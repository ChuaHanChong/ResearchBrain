# MVP-LAM: Learning Action-Centric Latent Action via Cross-Viewpoint Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> MVP-LAM, a latent action model from Seoul National University and Microsoft Research Asia, learns action-centric latent actions by leveraging multi-view videos with a novel cross-viewpoint reconstruction objective. This approach yields latent actions with higher mutual information to ground-truth actions and improves robot manipulation success rates by up to 20.8% on the SIMPLER benchmark.

## Problem

- The high cost and difficulty of acquiring large-scale, action-labeled robot demonstration data for training generalist policies.
- Existing latent action models (LAMs) are susceptible to exogenous noise, particularly viewpoint variation, leading to learned latent actions that are not truly informative about the underlying actions.
- Viewpoint changes, common in human videos, entangle camera motion with action-driven transitions, causing LAMs to overfit to viewpoint-specific visual cues.

## Method

- **MVP-LAM** employs a shared latent action encoder across time-synchronized multi-view videos, extracting visual features using a frozen DINOv2 encoder.
- It introduces a novel cross-viewpoint reconstruction objective, where the decoder reconstructs a future observation from one viewpoint using a latent action derived from a *different* synchronized viewpoint.
- The total objective combines this cross-viewpoint loss with a standard self-viewpoint reconstruction loss and VQ-VAE specific quantization losses, incentivizing viewpoint-independent action features.

## Results

- MVP-LAM achieved the highest estimated mutual information I(Z;A)I(Z; A)I(Z;A) (**1.96** bits vs. UniVLA's **1.42** bits) between latent actions and ground-truth actions on the Bridge V2 dataset, indicating improved action-centricity.
- VLA models pretrained with MVP-LAM latent actions improved average success rates on the SIMPLER benchmark from **39.6%** (UniVLA) to **60.4%** and achieved a **94.1%** average success rate on LIBERO suites, outperforming several state-of-the-art methods.
- The model demonstrated robustness to viewpoint perturbations and synchronization errors, maintaining higher action information and accurate future observation prediction even under challenging conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maximizing the mutual information between latent actions and ground-truth actions requires reducing capacity spent on viewpoint-specific factors beyond actual state changes.
> - Cross-viewpoint reconstruction explicitly discourages latent actions from encoding viewpoint-specific noise, forcing them to capture only the shared, intrinsic dynamics of the action.
> - Including diverse multi-view human manipulation videos during training further enhances action-centricity by prioritizing motion as the dominant source of visual transition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a significant challenge in robot learning: the bottleneck of acquiring large-scale, action-labeled robot demonstration data for training generalist policies. Unlike foundation models in other domains that leverage abundant, readily available data, robot learning is constrained by the cost and difficulty of collecting trajectories with low-level action labels, which typically requires human teleoperation. This limitation has motivated research into learning from videos, particularly human manipulation videos, as a scalable alternative to acquire transferable priors for manipulation-relevant dynamics.

Within this context, a line of research focuses on "latent action learning," where compact representations of video frame transitions, termed "latent actions," are learned from unlabeled videos and subsequently used as pseudo-action labels for pretraining Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models or defining reusable skills. Existing latent action models (LAMs), such as LAPA, Moto, and UniVLA, primarily operate on single-view videos. However, a fundamental issue with these methods is that visual transitions can be influenced by factors other than the agent's actions, known as exogenous noise, which can cause latent actions to become uninformative about the true underlying actions. Viewpoint variation, particularly prevalent in egocentric human videos, is identified as a critical source of such noise, as it can entangle camera motion with action-driven transitions, leading LAMs to overfit viewpoint-specific cues.

This paper extends the field of latent action learning by specifically tackling the issue of viewpoint variation to improve the "action-centricity" of learned latent actions. It builds upon prior work in learning visual priors and latent actions from videos but distinguishes itself by explicitly leveraging multi-view video data and a novel cross-viewpoint reconstruction objective during LAM training. While other research has explored learning view-invariant representations from multi-view data for observation representations or policy learning (e.g., TCN, MV-MWM, R3M), or mitigating exogenous noise with additional supervision or object-centric approaches (e.g., LAOM, slot decomposition), this work proposes an unsupervised method that directly addresses viewpoint-induced noise in latent action learning without requiring explicit action labels, 3D reconstruction, or external models beyond a visual encoder. It aims to develop a "universal latent action model" that can learn robust, action-centric representations from diverse, noisy real-world video data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a latent action model that learns discrete latent actions highly informative about ground-truth actions, specifically designed to mitigate the adverse effects of viewpoint variation. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck in Robot Learning:** The high cost and slow pace of collecting action-labeled robot demonstrations is a fundamental limitation for training generalist robot policies. Learning from readily available, unlabeled human manipulation videos presents a promising alternative. However, this necessitates methods to extract meaningful action information from video data that lacks explicit action labels.
2. **Improving the Informativeness of Latent Actions:** For effective VLA pretraining and downstream control, latent actions, used as pseudo-action labels, must accurately reflect the underlying ground-truth actions. The authors define "action-centric" latent actions as those that preserve high mutual information with the true actions. This is a critical requirement because if latent actions encode spurious information, they provide ineffective supervision.
3. **Mitigating Exogenous Noise, Particularly Viewpoint Variation:** A significant challenge in learning action-centric latent actions from real-world videos is the presence of exogenous noise. Visual transitions can be influenced by factors other than the agent's actions (e.g., background movement, lighting changes). The paper specifically focuses on "viewpoint variation," which entangles camera motion with true action-driven state changes. This is particularly problematic for human videos, which often feature substantial viewpoint changes, leading conventional latent action models to encode viewpoint-specific cues rather than focusing on the action itself.
4. **Developing a Viewpoint-Robust Latent Action Learning Mechanism:** Existing latent action approaches largely focus on single-view videos, making them susceptible to viewpoint-dependent factors. The motivation is to design a training objective that explicitly encourages the latent action to capture intrinsic transition information, irrespective of the camera viewpoint, thereby reducing its reliance on viewpoint-specific visual cues.
5. **Enabling More Effective and Data-Efficient VLA Pretraining:** By learning more action-centric latent actions, the researchers aim to provide higher quality pseudo-labels for VLA pretraining. This is expected to lead to improved performance on various downstream manipulation tasks, potentially requiring less pretraining data and demonstrating better generalization across different manipulation benchmarks.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the limitations of current latent action models by making them robust to viewpoint changes, thereby yielding more reliable and informative pseudo-labels from diverse, unlabeled video data to accelerate the development of generalist robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Multi-ViewPoint Latent Action Model (MVP-LAM), addresses the challenge of learning action-centric latent actions from multi-view videos by employing a cross-viewpoint reconstruction objective. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**Problem Formulation:**
The input is a sequence of images $\{I_t\}_t^T$. For each timestep $t$, an image $I_t$ is assumed to be captured from a camera pose $v_t$. Visual observations $o_t = f(I_t)$ are extracted using a frozen visual encoder, such as DINOv2. The future observation $o_{t+1}$ is taken at a fixed temporal stride $H$.
A standard Latent Action Model (LAM) encodes frame-to-frame transitions by predicting a continuous latent $e_t = E_\theta(o_t, o_{t+1})$, which is then vector-quantized into a discrete codebook entry $z_t = \text{Quantize}(e_t)$. A decoder $D_\theta$ reconstructs the next observation $\hat{o}_{t+1} = D_\theta(o_t, z_t)$. The objective for a standard LAM includes a reconstruction loss (MSE) and VQ-VAE specific quantization and commitment losses.

**Action-Centric Latent Action:**
The core idea is to learn a latent action $Z_t$ that is highly informative about the underlying ground-truth action $A_t$, maximizing mutual information $I(Z_t; A_t)$. Viewpoint variation ($V_t, V_{t+1}$) is identified as noise that can be spuriously encoded into $Z_t$, reducing its capacity for action-relevant information. A theoretical lower bound derivation suggests that reducing $I(Z_t; V_t, V_{t+1} | S_t, S_{t+1})$ (capacity spent on viewpoint-specific factors beyond state changes) is beneficial for maximizing $I(Z_t; A_t)$ for a fixed latent capacity.

**Multi-ViewPoint Latent Action Model (MVP-LAM):**
MVP-LAM leverages time-synchronized multi-view videos. While the explanation primarily covers a two-view case, it is designed to extend to more views.
1. **Feature Extraction:** Given synchronized image pairs $\{(I_{v_1,t}, I_{v_2,t})\}_t^T$, visual features $o_{v,t} = f(I_{v,t})$ are extracted using a frozen DINOv2 encoder.
2. **Latent Action Encoding:** For each viewpoint $v \in \{v_1, v_2\}$, an encoder $E_\theta$ computes a continuous latent $e_{v,t} = E_\theta(o_{v,t}, o_{v,t+1})$, which is then vector-quantized into a discrete latent action $z_{v,t} = \text{Quantize}(e_{v,t})$. Crucially, the encoder parameters $E_\theta$ are shared across all viewpoints.
3. **Reconstruction Objectives:** MVP-LAM optimizes two complementary reconstruction terms:
 - **Self-viewpoint Reconstruction ($L_{self}$):** This term is standard, where the decoder $D_\theta$ attempts to reconstruct the next observation $o_{v,t+1}$ from the current observation $o_{v,t}$ and the latent action $z_{v,t}$ derived from the *same* viewpoint.
 $L_{self} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{v \in \{v_1,v_2\}} \|o_{v,t+1} - D_\theta(o_{v,t}, z_{v,t})\|_2^2$
 - **Cross-viewpoint Reconstruction ($L_{cross}$):** This is the novel component. The decoder attempts to reconstruct the next observation $o_{v,t+1}$ from the current observation $o_{v,t}$ and a latent action $z_{\tilde{v},t}$ derived from a *different* synchronized viewpoint ($\tilde{v} \neq v$).
 $L_{cross} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{v,\tilde{v} \in \{v_1,v_2\}, v \neq \tilde{v}} \|o_{v,t+1} - D_\theta(o_{v,t}, z_{\tilde{v},t})\|_2^2$
4. **Full Objective:** The total MVP-LAM objective is $L_{MVP-LAM} = L_{self} + L_{cross} + L_{quant} + L_{commit}$, where $L_{quant}$ and $L_{commit}$ are standard VQ-VAE losses.
**Mechanism for Action-Centricity:** The cross-viewpoint reconstruction objective discourages the latent actions $z_{v,t}$ from encoding viewpoint-specific factors. If $z_{v,t}$ contains information unique to viewpoint $v$, using it to reconstruct $o_{v,t+1}$ from viewpoint $\tilde{v}$ would lead to higher reconstruction error. By minimizing $L_{cross}$, the model is incentivized to learn latent actions that capture only the shared, viewpoint-independent dynamics—i.e., the underlying action. This implicitly reduces $I(Z_t; V_t, V_{t+1} | S_t, S_{t+1})$.

**Implementation and Training:**
- MVP-LAM uses the UniVLA LAM architecture as its base.
- Training data includes time-synchronized multi-view robot trajectories from Open X-Embodiment (OXE) and multi-view human manipulation videos from EgoExo4D. Proprioceptive inputs or action labels are not used during LAM training.
- For VLA pretraining, a Prismatic-7B VLM is trained to predict MVP-LAM latent actions using a cross-entropy objective.
- For VLA finetuning, the pretrained VLM is converted into a VLA by finetuning with LoRA to predict ground-truth robot actions, using a multi-head attention mechanism to decode continuous actions from discrete VLM outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several key findings regarding the action-centricity, manipulation performance, and robustness of MVP-LAM.

#### 4.1. Action-Centricity of Latent Actions (RQ1)

- **Mutual Information (MI) Estimation:** MVP-LAM achieved the highest estimated mutual information $I(Z; A)$ between its latent actions and ground-truth actions compared to UniVLA, LAPA, and Moto. Using three different estimators (KSG, BA, MINE) on the Bridge V2 dataset, MVP-LAM consistently showed higher MI, with KSG reporting 1.96 bits for MVP-LAM compared to 1.42 for UniVLA, 1.02 for LAPA, and 0.74 for Moto. This indicates that MVP-LAM’s latent actions preserve more information about the underlying actions.
- **Linear Probing:** A simple linear layer trained to predict ground-truth actions from frozen latent actions demonstrated that MVP-LAM achieves the lowest Normalized Mean Squared Error (NMSE) on the in-distribution Bridge V2 dataset (0.793). It also generally performed well on out-of-distribution (OOD) LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Long), being competitive or outperforming UniVLA. While LAPA showed better OOD performance on some LIBERO suites, MVP-LAM consistently learned more action-predictive latents on the training distribution (Bridge V2).
- **Minimality of Latent Actions:** Inverse linear probing (predicting latent actions from ground-truth actions) indicated that MVP-LAM achieves lower NMSE in Bridge V2 and LIBERO-Long compared to UniVLA, suggesting that its latent actions are not only action-informative but also potentially more minimal, focusing on action-relevant information.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness for Downstream Manipulation (RQ2)

- **SIMPLER Benchmark:** VLA models pretrained with MVP-LAM latent actions achieved superior manipulation performance on the SIMPLER benchmark. MVP-LAM increased the average success rate from 39.6% (UniVLA) to 60.4% across four tasks, showing gains on all tasks. It also yielded the best average success rate compared to LAPA, OpenVLA, Octo, and $\pi_0$.
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** On the LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), the VLA pretrained with MVP-LAM achieved a 94.1% average success rate. This outperformed UniVLA (92.5%) under the same Bridge V2 pretraining and was competitive with state-of-the-art VLAs like $\pi_0$ (94.2%), despite MVP-LAM using substantially less pretraining data (e.g., $\le$60k robot trajectories vs. $\ge$970k for OXE used by OpenVLA). Notably, MVP-LAM achieved higher success on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite (90.8%) compared to $\pi_0$ (85.2%).

#### 4.3. Robustness to Viewpoint Perturbations (RQ3)

- **Qualitative Analysis of Attention Maps:** Visualizations showed that MVP-LAM’s decoder attention maps concentrated on task-relevant regions (gripper, manipulated objects) and remained stable even when inferring latent actions from viewpoint-perturbed transitions. In contrast, UniVLA’s attention was more diffuse and shifted significantly with viewpoint changes.
- **Quantitative Metrics under Perturbation:** MVP-LAM maintained higher MI, lower linear probe NMSE, and lower decoder reconstruction MSE (in DINOv2 feature space) when latent actions were inferred from viewpoint-perturbed transitions. This indicates that MVP-LAM’s latent actions better preserved action information and could still predict the subsequent observation accurately under viewpoint changes, demonstrating robustness. Pixel-decoding LAMs (LAPA, Moto) showed substantial degradation in pixel-level quality (PSNR) under perturbation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Human Video Dataset Inclusion:** Including human multi-view videos in MVP-LAM training improved action-centricity on Bridge V2 (lower NMSE, higher MI) compared to training solely on robot data. This suggests that the higher diversity in motions and scenes from human videos helps MVP-LAM prioritize motion as the dominant source of transition, leading to more action-centric latents.
- **Cross-Viewpoint Reconstruction ($L_{cross}$):** Removing the $L_{cross}$ term significantly reduced action-centricity, as evidenced by lower MI and higher NMSE. This confirms that the cross-viewpoint reconstruction objective, rather than merely using multi-view data with self-reconstruction, is crucial for learning action-centric latent actions.
- **Robustness to Synchronization Error:** MVP-LAM exhibited robustness to synthetic synchronization errors (lags of 2 or 4 frames). MI and NMSE remained consistent across different lag values, suggesting that MVP-LAM does not require frame-perfect multi-view alignment.

#### 4.5. Scaling Effects

- MVP-LAM’s action-centricity (measured by MI and NMSE) consistently improved with an increase in the number of viewpoints, the dataset ratio, and the model size. Increasing the number of viewpoints yielded the largest improvement, highlighting the importance of viewpoint diversity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MVP-LAM research presents several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of robot learning and embodied AI:

1. **Advancement in Action-Centric Latent Action Learning:** The core contribution is the development of a method that effectively learns latent actions highly correlated with ground-truth actions, even in the absence of action labels and in the presence of challenging viewpoint variations. By demonstrating increased mutual information with actual actions, MVP-LAM provides a more reliable foundation for downstream tasks.
2. **Improved VLA Pretraining from Unlabeled Video:** MVP-LAM's action-centric latent actions serve as higher-quality pseudo-labels for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model pretraining. This leads to substantial improvements in downstream manipulation performance, as evidenced by better success rates on SIMPLER and LIBERO benchmarks. This is crucial for bridging the gap between abundant unlabeled video data and the data requirements of robust robot policies.
3. **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** A notable impact is MVP-LAM's ability to achieve competitive, and in some cases superior, performance with substantially less pretraining data compared to other state-of-the-art VLA models. For instance, it outperforms some baselines even when pretrained on an order of magnitude less data, highlighting its efficiency in leveraging available data.
4. **Robustness to Real-World Viewpoint Noise:** By explicitly addressing viewpoint variation through its cross-viewpoint reconstruction objective, MVP-LAM yields latent actions that are more robust to viewpoint perturbations. This is vital for real-world application where camera perspectives can vary significantly (e.g., in human videos). Its demonstrated robustness to synchronization errors further increases its practical applicability to "in-the-wild" multi-view datasets.
5. **Scalability and Generalizability:** The observed scaling effects with the number of viewpoints, dataset size, and model capacity indicate that MVP-LAM is designed for large-scale data and model growth. Its ability to leverage diverse multi-view human videos, alongside robot data, positions it as a step towards a "universal latent action model" that can learn generalizable skills across different embodiments and environments.
6. **Reduced Reliance on Costly Supervision:** By learning effective latent actions in an unsupervised manner from videos, MVP-LAM reduces the dependency on expensive and labor-intensive action-labeled robot demonstrations, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for developing more capable robot systems.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that MVP-LAM still relies on multi-view videos, which, while more feasible than action-labeled robot data, requires more instrumentation than single-view capture. The VLA evaluation is currently limited to simulation environments. Future work could focus on training MVP-LAM on weakly synchronized or pseudo-paired multi-view videos to relax strict synchronization requirements and conduct real-world robot experiments to validate its performance beyond simulation. Scaling MVP-LAM with even larger multi-view datasets and increasing bottleneck capacity are also identified as promising directions.
