# HUGS: Guiding Unified Dexterous Grasp Synthesis Across Modes and Scales via Learned Human Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> HUGS introduces a human-prior-guided framework for unified dexterous grasp synthesis, enabling robots to generate diverse, physically plausible grasps across various object scales and contact modes. The framework, developed at Tsinghua University, achieves improved success rates and efficiency in generating millions of grasps by learning object-conditioned human preferences for contact modes and wrist poses.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous grasp synthesis methods frequently struggle to achieve both high success rates and broad grasp diversity, often relying on manually predefined contact expectations and coarse heuristic initializations.
- The high-dimensional optimization space for dexterous grasping is inefficient without adequate object-specific guidance, frequently leading to suboptimal solutions.
- Conventional approaches that directly retarget individual human grasps to robotic hands lack scalability and generalization to broad, diverse object sets.

## Method

- **HUGS** introduces a three-stage framework that decomposes complex grasp synthesis into predicting ==contact mode==, initializing ==wrist pose==, and optimizing robot configuration.
- A novel, compact human grasp dataset (**HUGS-Human**) was collected and released, comprising 1.8K grasps on 304 objects, specifically covering a broad spectrum of object scales and contact modes.
- ==Object-conditioned human priors== for contact modes (predicted by an MLP) and wrist pose initializations (generated by a ==diffusion model==) are learned from HUGS-Human to guide robot-specific ==force-closure optimization==.

## Results

- The learned human prior achieved lower KL divergence (**0.300** vs **0.612**) and higher soft precision/recall in contact mode prediction on held-out objects compared to scalar-scale rules, indicating superior geometric awareness.
- HUGS consistently outperformed heuristic baselines, adaptively synthesizing **3.2 million** robotic grasps across **157,000 scenes** with higher success rates and more efficient budget allocation for objects ranging from **2 cm to 30 cm**.
- A lightweight grasp generator trained on HUGS-synthesized data achieved an **F1 score of 0.934** for contact-mode availability and demonstrated successful cross-scale grasping of **24 diverse objects** in real-world LEAP Hand demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging generalized, reusable object-conditioned human priors offers superior adaptivity and scalability for robotic grasp synthesis compared to direct human imitation or fixed manual heuristics.
> - A relatively compact human grasp dataset can effectively capture diverse object-specific human grasp preferences across a broad range of object scales and contact modes.
> - Decomposing the complex grasp synthesis problem into prior-guided discrete contact mode prediction and continuous wrist pose initialization, followed by force-closure-aware optimization, improves efficiency and success while ensuring physical plausibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous grasping, encompassing a range of interactions from delicate two-finger pinches to robust bimanual holds, presents a significant challenge in robotics, particularly when extended across diverse object scales. Developing generalizable grasp generation models necessitates extensive training data, which is often impractical to acquire in large quantities from real-world robotic operations. Consequently, scalable grasp synthesis methods are critical for advancing dexterous manipulation capabilities.

Existing approaches to dexterous grasp synthesis often address the high-dimensional optimization space (due to numerous degrees of freedom and multi-contact interactions) by imposing constraints. These constraints typically involve manually predefined contact regions and heuristic wrist pose initializations. However, such methods frequently encounter a trade-off: loose heuristics can lead to inefficient synthesis and suboptimal grasp quality, while overly restrictive heuristics can severely limit the diversity of generated grasps. Most prior work concentrates on specific scenarios, such as single-hand full-finger grasps or bimanual grasps, often linking contact modes rigidly to object scale ranges, thereby limiting intra-scale diversity.

Human demonstrations are recognized as a valuable knowledge source for anthropomorphic robotic hands. However, conventional methods that directly retarget individual human grasps to robotic hands are limited in their scalability. These approaches typically generate grasps for the exact objects demonstrated or for highly similar ones, hindering generalization to broad, diverse object sets. Some research has explored learning object-conditioned contact maps from human data, but these efforts have largely been confined to single-hand full-finger grasps and require high-fidelity human contact predictions that may not align with robotic hand kinematics.

This work introduces a paradigm that departs from direct retargeting. Instead, it proposes learning generalized and reusable object-conditioned human priors. These priors aim to abstract human grasp preferences into high-level guidance for subsequent robot-specific optimization. This approach seeks to overcome the limitations of both manual heuristics and direct imitation by offering object-aware guidance that can generalize to unseen objects, thereby supporting scalable synthesis while maintaining physical plausibility through force-closure-aware optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, termed HUGS (Human-prior-guided framework for Unified dexterous Grasp Synthesis), that facilitates scalable and efficient dexterous grasp synthesis across a wide range of contact modes and object scales. This is motivated by several identified limitations in current methodologies:

- **Balancing Success Rate and Diversity:** Existing dexterous grasp synthesis methods frequently struggle to achieve both a high success rate and broad grasp diversity. This is primarily attributed to their reliance on manually predefined contact expectations and heuristic initialization strategies, which are often coarse and fail to exploit object-specific geometric information.
- **Exploiting Human Knowledge Effectively:** While human demonstrations offer a natural source of knowledge for anthropomorphic robotic hands, current methods often retarget these demonstrations directly. This one-to-one transfer approach limits the scalability of grasp synthesis, as it ties generated grasps closely to the specific objects demonstrated, impeding generalization to a larger, more diverse object space.
- **Addressing High-Dimensional Optimization:** The synthesis of dexterous grasps involves navigating a high-dimensional search space. Without adequate guidance, optimization can be inefficient or converge to suboptimal solutions. Manual heuristics provide some constraint but lack the adaptivity to generate suitable grasps for varied object geometries and sizes.

Against this backdrop, the key insight driving HUGS is to leverage human demonstrations not for direct imitation, but to learn **generalized and reusable object-conditioned human priors**. These priors are designed to capture human preferences for specific object geometries, offering high-level guidance for robotic grasp optimization. This "Human Prior + Robot Optimization" paradigm is motivated by three perceived advantages:

1. **Adaptivity:** The learned human prior aims to provide object-aware global guidance, directing the optimization process towards more plausible regions of the search space, which contrasts with the fixed nature of hand-crafted heuristics.
2. **Scalability:** By generalizing to unseen objects, the learned prior enables the scalable synthesis of large and diverse robotic grasp datasets, even from a relatively compact set of initial human demonstrations.
3. **Physical Plausibility:** Integrating the human prior with force-aware optimization ensures that the synthesized grasps account for robot-specific kinematics and task physics, allowing for the refinement and correction of any coarse errors present in the initial human prior.

To achieve these objectives, the research also explicitly aims to collect and publicly release a novel, compact human grasp dataset (HUGS-Human) that specifically covers a broad spectrum of object scales and contact modes, thereby providing the necessary foundation for learning these generalizable human grasp priors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HUGS framework addresses the problem of dexterous grasp synthesis by decomposing the complex, hybrid discrete-continuous optimization into a three-stage process, guided by a learned human prior.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

A grasp `g` is defined by the robot's joint configuration `Q`, wrist pose `T`, and contact configuration `c`. The objective is to minimize a force-closure energy function `Φ(g)` while satisfying constraints such as joint limits, object surface contacts, and collision-free hand-object and hand-hand configurations. The contact configuration `c` is treated as a discrete variable influencing the number and location of contact points, while `T` is a global continuous variable whose initialization critically impacts optimization. HUGS formulates the grasp synthesis problem as `π(Q, T, c | o) = π(Q, T | T₀, c, o) π(T₀ | c, o) π(c | o)`, where `π(c | o)` and `π(T₀ | c, o)` are predicted by the learned human prior.

#### 3.2. Contact-Mode Representation

To simplify the combinatorial search space, HUGS employs a compact discrete representation for contact modes, inspired by prevalent human grasping strategies executable by dexterous robot hands. These four modes are:
1. **Single-Two:** A single-hand two-finger grasp, primarily for small objects.
2. **Single-Three:** A single-hand three-finger grasp, the minimum for force closure under frictional point contacts.
3. **Single-Full:** A denser single-hand full-finger grasp, requiring a larger accessible object surface area.
4. **Both-Full:** A bimanual full-finger grasp, designed for large objects requiring broader surface coverage.

#### 3.3. Compact Human Grasp Dataset (HUGS-Human)

Recognizing the limitations of existing human grasp datasets for learning generalizable priors, the authors collected a new dataset, HUGS-Human.
- **Collection:** Participants grasped and lifted tabletop objects from various supporting poses, with encouragement for diverse human-plausible contact modes and wrist poses.
- **Scale:** The dataset comprises 304 canonical objects, 525 posed objects, and 1.8K distinct human grasps. It provides broad coverage of object scales, from very small to very large objects (half-diagonal lengths from 2 cm to over 30 cm).
- **Annotation:** Each sample includes object mesh, object pose, hand wrist pose (defined by the index metacarpophalangeal (MCP) position and dorsal-hand wrist orientation), MANO pose, and an manually annotated contact mode.
- **Observed Patterns:** The dataset reveals that humans adapt contact modes and wrist poses based on object geometry and scale; a single object can support multiple plausible contact modes; and contact modes and feasible wrist poses are strongly coupled.

#### 3.4. Object-Conditioned Human Grasp Prior Learning

HUGS learns an object-conditioned prior for contact modes `π(c | o)` and wrist initializations `π(T₀ | c, o)`.
- **Input:** A centered tabletop-frame object point cloud.
- **Architecture:** A MinkowskiEngine Sparse3DConv backbone encodes the point cloud into a global object feature `f₀`.
 - **Mode Head:** An MLP with softmax predicts `π(c | o)`. It is supervised by the empirical contact-mode distribution of demonstrations for the same posed object using soft-label cross-entropy.
 - **Diffusion Head:** A mode-conditioned diffusion model generates `π(T₀ | c, o)`. It is conditioned on the object feature `f₀` concatenated with a learnable contact mode embedding `f_c`. The model is trained on individual human wrist poses associated with their contact modes.
- **Data Augmentation:** Training incorporates random vertical rotations, small rotations around x/y axes, uniform scale augmentation, and Gaussian noise to point coordinates.

#### 3.5. Human-Prior-Guided Grasp Synthesis

During synthesis, the learned human prior guides the robot-specific optimization.
- **Object Rescaling:** The input object point cloud is first rescaled by a human-to-robot hand scale ratio (e.g., 1.4 for LEAP Hand, 1.0 for Shadow Hand) to align the relative object size perceived by the robot with that in human demonstrations.
- **Budget Allocation:** The prior predicts `π(c | o)`. For each contact mode, 4K wrist pose candidates are drawn from `π(T₀ | c, o)`, ranked by approximate diffusion likelihood, and the top 2K are kept. Farthest-pose selection then retains `K` diverse initializations, which are mapped to the robot hand and perturbed. A total optimization budget `B` is allocated across modes as `B_c = π(c | o)B`, with a maximum cap per mode.
- **Grasp Optimization:** Given a contact mode `c` and a wrist initialization `T₀`, a bi-level optimization process refines the robot hand configuration `(Q, T)` to minimize force-closure energy `Φ(g)` (Eq. 1). This energy is formulated as a sum of residuals over disturbance directions, with each residual determined by a quadratic program (QP). For bimanual grasping, the objective includes a weighted sum of global bimanual and per-hand force-closure terms to promote both overall stability and individual hand quality.
- **Simulation Filtering:** Optimized grasps are validated in MuJoCo simulation. Successful grasps must lift the object to a specified height (0.1m) with stable pose (position deviation < 5cm, rotation deviation < 15°) and remain collision-free. All validated grasps, across contact modes, are retained for dataset diversity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HUGS focused on its ability to improve large-scale multi-mode synthesis through better contact mode allocation and wrist initialization, and its capacity to support downstream model distillation for real-world deployment. Experiments were conducted using both the Shadow Hand and LEAP Hand, involving 3.2 million synthesized robotic grasps over 157,000 scenes, spanning object half-diagonal lengths from 2 cm to 30 cm.

#### 4.1. Contact Mode Prediction

A comparison between the geometry-aware human prior and scalar-scale rules (derived solely from object diagonal length) for predicting contact mode preferences on held-out objects revealed that the human prior achieved a lower KL divergence (0.300 vs. 0.612) and higher soft precision (0.873 vs. 0.805) and soft recall (0.964 vs. 0.892). This indicates that the human prior effectively captures object-specific geometric information beyond just scalar size for mode prediction.

#### 4.2. Efficient Multi-Mode Grasp Synthesis

The impact of human-prior guidance on synthesis success and budget allocation was evaluated against several heuristic baselines:
- **Heur-Fix:** Performed well only for object scales suited to its fixed Single-Full mode, demonstrating the necessity of contact-mode adaptation.
- **Heur-Single:** Showed improved robustness over Heur-Fix, but its single scale-dependent mode strategy limited its ability to cover multiple valid strategies within the same scale.
- **Heur-Multi:** Provided more mode diversity but suffered from inefficient allocation, wasting attempts on object-specific mismatches (e.g., single-hand attempts on large objects).
- **HUGS:** Consistently outperformed all heuristic baselines. It adaptively allocated optimization budget based on object geometry, smoothly transitioning from sparse single-hand modes for small objects to full-hand and bimanual modes for larger ones. HUGS also demonstrated the ability to handle object-specific exceptions within the same scale, leading to higher success rates compared to Heur-Multi.
- **HUGS-Single:** Outperformed Heur-Single, isolating the benefit of the human-prior-guided wrist initialization even when contact modes were pre-assigned.

#### 4.3. Grasp Diversity

Evaluation of grasp diversity, measured by the explained-variance ratio of the first principal component of grasp poses, indicated that HUGS produced lower pose dispersion. This suggests that HUGS concentrates on human-preferred wrist regions, leading to more natural grasps. In contrast, heuristic sampling often yielded higher dispersion partly due to unnatural or reversed wrist poses, which are less suitable for anthropomorphic robots.

#### 4.4. Supervision for Online Grasp Generators

The utility of HUGS-synthesized grasps for supervising learning-based grasp generation networks was assessed. Identical lightweight generators trained on HUGS data and Heur-Multi data were compared.
- The generator trained on HUGS data achieved a high F1 score of 0.934 for contact-mode availability prediction.
- In simulation, the HUGS-trained generator demonstrated a higher grasp success rate compared to the Heur-Multi-trained one. This indicates that HUGS produces training data that is more conducive to learning and offers better tolerance to errors in generated grasps.
- However, success rates for very small and very large objects showed a noticeable drop, suggesting that cross-scale, cross-mode grasp generation remains a challenging area.

#### 4.5. Real-World Cross-Scale Grasping Demonstrations

Real-world demonstrations using LEAP Hands, guided by a generator trained on HUGS-synthesized LEAP grasps, showcased adaptive mode selection. The system successfully grasped 24 diverse objects, ranging from a 0.5 cm screw to a 30 cm bucket. The generator adaptively selected appropriate contact modes and grasp poses. For instance, the system demonstrated choosing a top-down single-hand grasp for an object in clutter (where bimanual grasping was blocked) and a bimanual grasp for the same object when loaded (requiring more force). This highlights the practical significance of diverse generated grasps for accommodating deployment-time constraints. The authors noted that real-world performance was still subject to perception errors from calibration and depth reconstruction.

#### 4.6. LEAP Hand Simulation Results (Appendix C.1)

Additional simulations on the LEAP Hand showed consistent trends with the Shadow Hand experiments. HUGS adaptively reallocated synthesis budget and improved success rates over heuristics, confirming the transferability of the learned human prior to different robot hands, even with differing physical sizes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents HUGS, a framework that advances dexterous grasp synthesis by integrating learned human priors with robotic optimization. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

**Key Contributions:**

- **Novel Framework:** HUGS introduces a human-prior-guided approach for unified dexterous grasp synthesis, effectively moving beyond manually designed heuristics. This paradigm shifts from direct retargeting of human demonstrations to learning generalizable object-conditioned priors over contact modes and wrist poses, which then guide robot-specific force-closure-aware optimization.
- **New Dataset:** The creation and public release of HUGS-Human, a compact yet diverse human grasp dataset (1.8K grasps over 304 objects), is a valuable contribution. This dataset is specifically designed to support the learning of generalizable human grasp priors across a broad range of object scales and contact modes.
- **Scalable Grasp Synthesis:** The framework demonstrates scalable synthesis, generating 3.2 million robotic grasps across 157,000 scenes. These grasps span objects with half-diagonal lengths from 2 cm to 30 cm and include diverse contact modes from two-finger to bimanual grasps. This represents a significant step towards creating large-scale datasets with multiple plausible grasp modes per object, which is essential for training robust learning-based systems.
- **Improved Performance:** Quantitative evaluations show that the learned human prior substantially improves contact-mode prediction, synthesis success rates, efficiency, and grasp diversity compared to heuristic-based methods. The adaptivity of HUGS in allocating optimization budget based on object geometry leads to more successful and appropriate grasps.
- **Downstream Utility and Real-World Applicability:** The research demonstrates that models trained on HUGS-synthesized data can effectively supervise online grasp generators. Furthermore, real-world demonstrations with LEAP Hands validate the adaptive selection of contact modes and successful grasping of objects across a wide range of scales, from small screws to large containers, even under varying task constraints (e.g., clutter, heavy loads).

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Robotic Dexterity:** HUGS can contribute to more dexterous and versatile robotic manipulation capabilities by enabling robots to generate a wider array of physically plausible and situation-appropriate grasps.
- **Reduced Manual Engineering:** By learning from human priors, the framework reduces the need for laborious manual definition of grasp heuristics and contact regions, potentially streamlining the development of new robotic grasping systems.
- **Foundation for Learning:** The large-scale, diverse, and high-quality synthetic grasp datasets generated by HUGS provide a robust foundation for training advanced learning-based grasp generation models, potentially leading to more generalizable and robust real-time grasping solutions.
- **Adaptive Manipulation in Complex Environments:** The ability to adaptively select contact modes and grasp poses based on object geometry and environmental or task constraints (e.g., presence of clutter or object weight) holds promise for deploying robots in more complex and unstructured real-world environments.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also suggest avenues for future research:
- **Human Data Scale:** The reliance on a compact human dataset means that predictions may degrade for objects significantly out-of-distribution. Future work could explore scaling to larger egocentric interaction datasets and advanced techniques for mode discovery and annotation.
- **Online Grasp Generation:** The current online generator is preliminary and less robust than the offline synthesis pipeline, particularly for geometrically irregular objects. Further research is needed to develop stronger generative models and geometry representations for real-time deployment.
- **Contact Diversity:** The four discrete contact modes, while prevalent, do not exhaustively cover all human grasping behaviors. Expanding to denser taxonomies or detailed contact maps could enable more nuanced functional grasp synthesis.
- **Complete Grasping Systems:** Real-world demonstrations highlighted sensitivities to calibration, segmentation, and depth reconstruction errors. Developing robust, end-to-end closed-loop execution systems, tactile adaptation, and collision-aware motion planning remains crucial for reliable real-world deployment.

In conclusion, HUGS represents a significant advancement in dexterous grasp synthesis, demonstrating that learned human priors can effectively guide the generation of diverse and physically plausible robot grasps across a broad spectrum of object scales and contact modes. This work establishes a valuable framework and dataset for future research in robotic manipulation.
