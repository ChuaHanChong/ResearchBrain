# EgoAfford: Task-Oriented Affordance Grounding via Egocentric Referring Segmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced EgoAfford, a benchmark for task-oriented affordance grounding in multi-step tabletop tasks, along with EgoLens, a model that jointly infers remaining task plans and grounds role-specific part-level actions. EgoLens achieved 0.700 gIoU and 0.486 cIoU on generated data, outperforming commercial visual-language models by 14.7 percentage points in gIoU, and demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer with 0.666 gIoU on real-world images.

## Problem

- Existing 2D affordance formulations primarily focus on single-target functional regions, often failing to explicitly assign distinct semantic roles (e.g., direct object, instrument, destination) to multiple objects involved in complex actions.
- Prior affordance methods typically operate from a single-step perspective, predicting action possibilities for a given action or instruction rather than inferring a remaining procedure based on the overall task goal and current state.
- Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for referring segmentation are not designed to decompose complex tasks into sequential steps or simultaneously segment fine-grained, part-level action components for the *next* immediate action.

## Method

- The work formulates multi-step ==task-oriented affordance grounding== as a joint problem of generating a remaining plan (sequence of actions) and performing part-level grounding of three role-specific components (direct object, instrument, destination) for the immediate next action.
- A new benchmark, **EgoAfford**, was created, comprising 15,537 synthetically generated images with human-verified part-level mask annotations, designed to connect visual observations, semantic object roles, and multi-step planning. It also includes an EgoAfford-Real dataset of 102 manually captured images.
- **EgoLens**, a reference multimodal large language model based on the ==Qwen2.5-VL backbone==, was developed with ==replicated, role-specific mask decoders== to jointly predict the remaining task plan and fine-grained masks for the direct object, instrument, and destination of the next action.

## Results

- EgoLens achieved **0.700** gIoU and **0.486** cIoU for end-to-end segmentation on the EgoAfford generated test set, demonstrating a **14.7** percentage point improvement in gIoU over the highest-performing commercial VLM pipeline (Gemini 3 Flash Preview + SAM2).
- The model exhibited robust zero-shot transfer capabilities, attaining **0.666** gIoU and **0.455** cIoU on the EgoAfford-Real dataset without training on real images, representing a **9.7**-point gIoU improvement over GPT 5.5 + SAM2.
- Ablation studies confirmed the crucial contributions of EgoLens's design components, with box-coordinate prediction causing the largest performance drop (**6.8** points gIoU, **8.2** points Semantic F1) when removed, highlighting the importance of the spatial interface.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating task planning with fine-grained perception is critical for robust task-oriented affordance grounding, as evidenced by significant performance increases when models are provided with a reference next step.
> - Explicitly defining and grounding semantic roles (direct object, instrument, destination) at a part-level greatly enhances the actionability and precision of affordance predictions for complex, multi-object manipulation tasks.
> - A hybrid approach combining LLM-driven scene generation and automatic annotation with rigorous human verification is effective for creating large-scale, semantically rich, and precisely annotated datasets for embodied AI research.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a critical area at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and robotics, specifically in the domain of affordance learning and task-oriented manipulation. Affordance, defined as the action possibilities an environment offers an agent, is a fundamental concept for bridging perception and interaction in artificial intelligence systems.

Prior research in affordance learning has made progress in localizing functional object regions associated with elemental actions. Early datasets like UMD part affordance and IIT-AFF provided pixel-wise affordance labels for specific categories, leading to end-to-end detectors such as AffordanceNet. Subsequent efforts broadened the scope to full scenes (ADE-Affordance), purpose-driven detection (PADv2), and large-scale grounding from human-object interactions (AGD20K). Extensions to 3D object surfaces were explored with 3D AffordanceNet and LASO. More recently, the community has shifted towards open-ended language instructions and generalizable affordance grounding, utilizing large vision-language models (AffordanceLLM, InstructPart, RoboAfford++, RAGNet). Some works, like WorldAfford and SeqAfford, have expanded affordance grounding beyond isolated object-action pairs to multiple objects and sequences of actions.

Despite these advancements, existing 2D affordance formulations primarily exhibit two limitations:
1. **Single-target focus**: Most approaches ground a single action-conditioned functional region without explicitly assigning roles to multiple participating objects.
2. **Single-step perspective**: Even bimanual and scene-level formulations predict affordances for a given action or instruction rather than inferring a remaining procedure based on task progress.

In parallel, referring expression segmentation has evolved from early modular designs like MAttNet to end-to-end cross-modal architectures such as LAVT and CRIS, grounding natural language expressions to pixel-level masks. The advent of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) introduced reasoning segmentation capabilities, exemplified by LISA, which connects MLLMs to mask decoders for segmentation from queries requiring world knowledge. Further works like PixelLM, GSVA, GLaMM, OMG-LLaVA, Sa2VA, LENS, and UniPixel have enriched this paradigm. However, these MLLM-based models typically produce masks for explicitly or implicitly referred targets in a single-shot manner, and are not designed to decompose complex tasks into steps and simultaneously segment the fine-grained, part-level action components of the *next* step while inferring task progress.

This research addresses these gaps by introducing a benchmark, EgoAfford, and a reference model, EgoLens, that jointly couples semantically aligned egocentric observations, remaining-plan inference, and role-specific part grounding within the context of multi-step tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to advance task-oriented affordance understanding for complex multi-step tabletop tasks by integrating perception with multi-step planning. This goal is motivated by two key limitations observed in prevailing affordance grounding formulations:

1. **Lack of Multi-Object Role Assignment**: Current 2D formulations often focus on grounding a single functional region for a given action. However, complex tasks involve multiple objects that play distinct semantic roles (e.g., the object being manipulated, the tool used, and the destination). The motivation is to move beyond single-target grounding by explicitly representing each action step with three key components:
 - **Direct Object**: The object directly manipulated.
 - **Instrument**: The tool used to act upon the direct object.
 - **Destination**: The target location or object for transfer.
 Grounding these roles at a part-level is crucial not only for identifying *where* an action is afforded but also *how* multiple objects collaboratively participate in the action.

2. **Absence of Task-Oriented Planning**: Most existing methods predict affordances for a single, given action or instruction, rather than inferring a sequence of actions conditioned on the overall task goal and current state. The motivation is to integrate affordance grounding with task progress by requiring a model to:
 - Given an egocentric observation and a high-level task goal, infer the remaining procedure or plan.
 - Subsequently, ground the three components (direct object, instrument, destination) of the *immediate next action* at a part-level.
 This links affordance grounding to the broader context of task execution and state transitions, moving beyond isolated action predictions.

To achieve these objectives, the authors articulate three specific contributions:
- Formulating multi-step task-oriented affordance grounding as a joint problem of remaining-plan generation and part-level grounding of direct objects, instruments, and destinations.
- Instantiating this formulation in the EgoAfford benchmark, designed to connect visual observations, semantic roles of objects, and multi-step planning.
- Presenting EgoLens, a reference multimodal large language model, developed to jointly predict the remaining plan and the role-specific part-level component masks for the next action.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves defining a novel problem formulation, constructing a synthetic dataset with human verification, establishing a benchmark protocol, and developing a reference model.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task requires a model to take an egocentric tabletop image `I` and a natural language task description `T` as input. The model must produce two outputs:
1. **Plan**: A sequence of natural language action steps `{T1, ..., Tn}` that completes `T`.
2. **Segmentation Masks**: Three fine-grained, part-level masks `{Mo, Ms, Md}` for the components of the *next* action step:
 - **Direct Object (Mo)**: The manipulated object (or its container if material is inside).
 - **Instrument (Ms)**: The object used as a tool (absent if a bare-hand action). A "loaded instrument" carrying material is still annotated as the instrument.
 - **Destination (Md)**: The target location or object of a transfer (absent if no transfer).
Each mask represents the *functional part* of the component (e.g., a blade, a spout), excluding parts serving only for grip. If multiple instances of a component exist with the same semantics, all are segmented. An absent component corresponds to an all-zero mask.

#### 3.2. EgoAfford Dataset Generation

Due to the absence of suitable existing datasets, a generative pipeline was developed to create semantically aligned image series:
- **Task Metadata Generation**: Multiple commercial LLMs (Gemini 3 Flash Preview, GPT 5.5, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Claude Opus 4.8) were prompted to generate diverse task seeds (anchor objects and scenarios). For each, LLMs created complex tasks with step-by-step decompositions. They then enumerated all required objects and composed textual descriptions of the scene state *before* each step, specifying object positions and statuses.
- **Image Synthesis**: Image generation models (primarily GPT-Image-2, with some FLUX-2) synthesized images from these scene descriptions. A manual screening process identified and corrected state inconsistencies, regenerating images as needed or marking them as invalid.

#### 3.3. Annotation Pipeline

To provide part-level mask annotations, an LLM-based automatic pipeline was coupled with human correction:
- **Automatic Annotation**: For each action step's component list, Grounded-SAM localized objects with bounding boxes. An annotation LLM (GPT-5.5) then linked each action component to its bounding box and placed a point prompt on the functional part. SAM2 used this to produce an initial part-level mask.
- **Human Verification**: These automatic annotations served as initial proposals and were not directly used for training due to hallucination and limited spatial grounding. Human annotators verified component-role assignments, inspected, and refined every proposed mask by re-prompting SAM2 with corrected points. Before human correction, the GPT-5.5–SAM2 pipeline achieved 0.696 gIoU and 0.523 cIoU, indicating insufficient reliability without human intervention.
- **Dataset Size**: EgoAfford comprises approximately 15,537 images from 2,000 generated scenes. The metadata contains 4,123 unique object names (1,188 clustered types) and 391 unique verbs (357 clustered types).

#### 3.4. Benchmark Protocol

- **Dataset Splits**: EgoAfford is split into training (1,900 scenes, 15,049 images) and test sets (100 scenes, 488 images) at the scene level. An additional test set, EgoAfford-Real, consists of 102 manually captured images from 26 tasks, annotated using the same protocol, to evaluate generalization to real imagery.
- **Admissible Candidates**: For evaluation, task states may have multiple valid next actions. Two annotators independently described and provided role masks for admissible candidate actions. The union of these candidates formed the ground truth. Planning evaluation also used manually reviewed pairwise precedence constraints over reference steps.
- **Metrics**:
 - **Segmentation**:
 - **gIoU (global Intersection over Union)**: Average per-mask IoU over the test set. For empty ground truths, an empty prediction yields 1, a non-empty prediction yields 0.
 - **cIoU (accumulated Intersection over Union)**: Ratio of globally accumulated intersection to union. Empty-empty pairs contribute nothing; false positives contribute only to union.
 - **NE-mIoU (Non-Empty mean IoU)**: IoU averaged only over samples where the ground-truth component is present, used for component-wise analysis.
 - During evaluation, the ground truth yielding the highest gIoU among admissible candidates is selected.
 - **Planning**:
 - **Semantic Precision, Recall, F1**: Computed using an off-the-shelf cross-encoder for pairwise semantic similarity between predicted and reference steps. Hungarian algorithm identifies maximum-weight one-to-one assignments (similarity > 0.6 threshold).
 - **First Step Similarity**: Cross-encoder similarity between the first predicted step and the selected ground-truth next-step description.
 - **Constraint Satisfaction Ratio (CSR)**: Fraction of satisfied precedence constraints, where both endpoint steps are matched and appear in the correct relative order.
 - **Coverage Score**: Equivalent to Semantic Recall, indicating inclusion of steps from the canonical reference plan.

#### 3.5. EgoLens Model Architecture and Training

- **Architecture**: EgoLens is built upon the LENS architecture, utilizing a 3B Qwen2.5-VL backbone. This model consumes vision-language tokens and learnable queries. The hidden states of these queries pass through a 4-layer transformer and dedicated decoders that produce SAM2 prompt embeddings for mask prediction. To align with the three-component formulation, the LENS decoder is replicated into three parallel, role-specific branches for the direct object, instrument, and destination masks, ensuring an architectural binding between mask and role.
- **Training**: EgoLens is trained with supervised learning using a unified prompt template that includes the task description and output constraints. The model generates a CoT-style planning trace (`<think>` tags) and a structured answer (`<answer>` tags) containing remaining steps and textual descriptions with bounding boxes for the next step's components.
 - The planning trace is teacher-forced during training as a semantic prefix, visible to the transformer and mask queries, but excluded from the language-modeling loss (`Llm`).
 - The language-modeling loss supervises only the tokens within the `<answer>` tags.
 - The segmentation loss (`Lseg`) uses a combination of BCE and Dice losses for non-empty masks and BCE only for empty masks.
 - The total loss is `L = λlm Llm + λseg Lseg`, with `λlm = λseg = 1.0`.
 - Training parameters: 40 epochs, AdamW optimizer, learning rate 3 × 10−5, 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs, per-device batch size 16, 4 gradient-accumulation steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated EgoAfford, EgoLens, and various baselines across three settings: full task, reference-step segmentation, and task-only segmentation, as well as on the EgoAfford-Real dataset.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on the Full Task

On the full task (Table 1), EgoLens demonstrated the strongest performance in end-to-end segmentation, achieving 0.700 gIoU and 0.486 cIoU. This performance indicates the effectiveness of the EgoAfford dataset for supervising the joint task. EgoLens also improved gIoU by 14.7 percentage points compared to the highest-performing commercial-VLM pipeline (Gemini 3 Flash Preview + SAM2), despite using a compact 3B backbone. In planning performance, EgoLens obtained the highest Constraint Satisfaction Ratio (CSR) and coverage score, and the second-highest first-step similarity and Semantic F1.

Among the open-source MLLM baselines (Sa2VA, UniPixel, LENS, OMG-LLaVA), none consistently returned all three role-specific masks in a single forward pass under the full-task protocol, leading to omitted gIoU scores for these models. Sa2VA showed the strongest planning scores among these baselines. Component-wise analysis (Table 7) for EgoLens revealed substantial non-empty localization accuracy: NE-mIoU of 0.668 for direct objects, 0.614 for instruments, and 0.568 for destinations, indicating that performance is not solely driven by empty-mask recognition.

#### 4.2. Disentangling Next-Step Inference and Grounding

To assess the impact of next-step inference on grounding, a comparison was made with and without providing a reference next step (Table 3). When the reference next step was provided, gIoU increased for all baselines, highlighting that next-step inference is a significant source of error. The segmentation-only variant, EgoLens-Seg, achieved the best grounding performance with a reference step, reaching 0.839 gIoU and 0.668 cIoU. An oracle analysis (Table 8) further confirmed that replacing a commercial VLM's predicted step with the ground-truth step for EgoLens-Seg significantly raised gIoU from 0.682 to 0.839, quantifying the error propagation from planning.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on EgoAfford-Real

EgoLens demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer capabilities on the EgoAfford-Real dataset (Table 2), achieving the best gIoU (0.666) and cIoU (0.455) without training on real images. This represented a 9.7-point gIoU improvement over GPT 5.5 + SAM2. The performance drop from the generated test set (0.700/0.486 to 0.666/0.455 gIoU/cIoU) was modest, suggesting a manageable transfer gap. EgoLens also attained the highest first-step similarity, CSR, and coverage on the real set. For EgoLens-Seg, replacing GPT-5.5's predicted next step with the ground-truth step improved gIoU/cIoU from 0.606/0.329 to 0.724/0.472, indicating that next-step inference also remains an important error source on real observations.

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5) illustrated EgoLens's ability to align next-step predictions with ground truth candidates and localize corresponding functional parts, contrasting with issues observed in baselines such as inconsistent next steps or imprecise spatial prompts.

#### 4.4. Model Design Ablations

An ablation study on EgoLens's design (Table 4) revealed that all components contributed to its performance. The box-coordinate prediction had the largest effect on gIoU (6.8 points drop) and Semantic F1 (8.2 points drop), underscoring the importance of the box-based spatial interface. Removing textual component names reduced gIoU by 2.8 points, indicating their value as semantic supervision. Explicit task rules were particularly important for pixel-level grounding, with a 5.5-point cIoU drop when removed. The teacher-forced planning trace also provided useful intermediate semantics, as its removal decreased metrics. Role-specific decoders improved pixel-level mask quality, although task-specific supervision accounted for a substantial portion of overall performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes contributions to the fields of affordance grounding, task planning, and multimodal perception, with several potential impacts:

1. **Advancing Task-Oriented Affordance Grounding**: By formulating multi-step task-oriented affordance grounding as a joint problem of planning and role-specific part-level segmentation, the research addresses critical limitations of prior work that largely focused on single-step, single-object scenarios. This integrated approach is more aligned with the demands of complex, real-world manipulation tasks.

2. **Introducing a Novel Benchmark (EgoAfford)**: EgoAfford provides a new and comprehensive benchmark for studying joint perception and planning. Its features—including task-conditioned multi-object scenes, semantically aligned step-by-step observations, egocentric perspective, and fine-grained role-specific part grounding—distinguish it from existing datasets. The combination of a large generated dataset with human verification and a smaller, manually captured real-world test set (EgoAfford-Real) offers a scalable research platform while allowing for evaluation of generalization capabilities.

3. **Establishing a Reference Model (EgoLens)**: EgoLens serves as a strong reference model for the EgoAfford benchmark. Its architecture, which integrates a multimodal large language model with role-specific mask decoders, demonstrates competitive performance in both planning and fine-grained segmentation. The experimental results, particularly the comparative analysis with commercial VLMs and the ablation studies, characterize the challenges of the task and provide insights for future model development.

4. **Enabling Embodied AI and Robotics**: The structured, spatially grounded outputs of EgoLens (plan and role-specific masks) can directly inform embodied execution in robotic systems. By identifying direct objects, instruments, and destinations at a functional part level, the system can provide explicit, spatially grounded arguments for reusable manipulation skills. This could facilitate the development of more capable and generalizable robotic agents that can interpret high-level commands (e.g., `pour(source, destination)`, `cut(target, tool)`) and execute them using low-level controllers, leading to more robust and flexible manipulation policies.

5. **Diagnostic Tool for MLLMs**: The benchmark serves as a diagnostic tool for evaluating the capabilities of multimodal large language models in complex, multi-modal reasoning tasks that require both language understanding, visual perception, and sequential decision-making. The observed performance gaps between different MLLMs and the impact of providing explicit step-by-step guidance highlight areas for improvement in model architectures and training strategies.

In summary, EgoAfford and EgoLens provide a foundational contribution for integrating perception and planning in the context of multi-step tabletop tasks, paving the way for more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems.
