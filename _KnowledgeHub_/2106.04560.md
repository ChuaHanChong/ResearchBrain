# Scaling Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Google Research's Brain Team systematically explored the scaling properties of Vision Transformers (ViTs) with up to two billion parameters on three billion images, demonstrating that ViT performance follows a double-saturating power law with compute and achieving a new ImageNet top-1 accuracy of 90.45%.

## Problem

- Understanding if scaling laws observed in natural language processing Transformers apply to Vision Transformers (ViTs) and how their performance scales with model size, data, and compute.
- Addressing practical challenges of memory consumption and training stability when attempting to scale ViTs to billions of parameters.
- Determining the optimal compute allocation and architectural configurations for ViTs to achieve the best possible visual representation quality.

## Method

- Conducted an extensive ==empirical scaling study== of Vision Transformers, systematically varying model sizes (up to 2 billion parameters), dataset sizes (up to 3 billion images), and compute budgets (up to 10,000 TPUv3 core-days).
- Introduced architectural and training refinements including ==decoupled weight decay== for the classification head and memory-efficient pooling alternatives (==Multihead Attention Pooling==) to the `[class]` token.
- Adapted and optimized the ==Adafactor optimizer== for memory efficiency and utilized the proprietary ==JFT-3B== dataset for large-scale pre-training, yielding the **ViT-G/14** model.

## Results

- The ViT-G/14 model (nearly **2 billion parameters**) achieved a new state-of-the-art **90.45% top-1 accuracy** on ImageNet.
- Demonstrated strong few-shot transfer capabilities, reaching **84.86% top-1 accuracy** on ImageNet with only **10 examples per class**.
- Confirmed that proposed memory-efficient techniques and modified optimizers allowed training of 2-billion parameter models to fit on single TPUv3 cores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Transformer performance follows a double-saturating power law with total training compute, similar to NLP models, but with high-end performance ceilings.
> - Achieving optimal performance requires simultaneously scaling model size, data, and computational resources.
> - Larger Vision Transformers exhibit increased sample efficiency and benefit significantly from training on massive datasets, even beyond one billion images.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is a crucial contribution at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within deep learning: computer vision, natural language processing, and the study of scaling laws in neural networks.

**The Rise of Transformers in Computer Vision:** Prior to 2020, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) were the dominant architecture for computer vision tasks. The original Vision Transformer (ViT) paper by Dosovitskiy et al. (2021) was a paradigm shift, demonstrating that Transformer architectures, which achieved state-of-the-art results in NLP, could also be effectively applied to image recognition. This opened a new frontier for research, prompting questions about how these new vision models behave under different conditions, particularly at scale. This paper directly addresses these questions, serving as a foundational study for the field.

**Scaling Laws from NLP to Vision:** The concept of "scaling laws" gained significant traction in NLP with papers like Kaplan et al. (2020), which systematically analyzed how the performance of Transformer language models relates to compute, model size, and dataset size. These studies revealed power-law relationships, demonstrating that larger models, trained on more data with more compute, often lead to predictable performance improvements and even increased computational efficiency at scale. This understanding informed the development of massive models like GPT-3. This paper directly extends this crucial line of inquiry to the vision domain. By studying ViTs, the authors investigate whether similar scaling principles apply to visual data, which has different statistical properties and typically uses supervised pre-training (as opposed to unsupervised in NLP). The findings provide a direct parallel, validating the generalizability of scaling laws across modalities.

**Large-Scale Visual Representation Learning:** The paper also fits within a broader trend of training increasingly large models on massive datasets to learn general visual representations. Previous efforts in this space often focused on scaling CNNs (e.g., EfficientNets, BiT-L), often requiring complex architectures or hybrid approaches. This work shows that pure Transformers can also benefit significantly from scaling, pushing the boundaries of what was previously achievable with ViT architectures. It also contributes to the ongoing discussion about how to best leverage large datasets, demonstrating that Vision Transformers, especially larger ones, can benefit from data even beyond a billion images.

**Practical Advancements in Training Large Models:** Beyond theoretical scaling laws, the paper also addresses practical challenges associated with training multi-billion parameter models. It introduces specific architectural and training refinements that reduce memory consumption and improve training stability, making such large-scale experiments feasible. These methodological contributions are valuable for the entire deep learning community attempting to train models at the extreme end of scale.

In essence, this work consolidates the Vision Transformer's position as a scalable and highly performant architecture, bridging critical insights from NLP scaling laws to computer vision, and providing practical guidance for future large-scale model development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the "Scaling Vision Transformers" paper are multifaceted, revolving around a comprehensive understanding of how Vision Transformers behave at varying scales of model, data, and computational resources.

1. **Characterize Scaling Properties of Vision Transformers:** The core motivation is to systematically investigate and characterize the relationships between error rate, data, and compute for Vision Transformers (ViT). While scaling laws had been established for Transformer language models, it was unknown if these findings translated to the vision domain, which presents distinct challenges (e.g., typically supervised pre-training, different data modalities). The authors aim to fill this knowledge gap by scaling ViT models and data both up and down.

2. **Understand the Performance-Compute Frontier:** A key objective is to map out the "performance-compute frontier" for ViT models. This involves identifying the optimal allocation of computational budget, model size, and training duration to achieve the best possible representation quality on various tasks. The paper seeks to provide empirical evidence and mathematical formulations (like power laws) to describe this frontier.

3. **Refine ViT Architecture and Training for Scale:** To enable the extensive scaling experiments, a practical objective was to refine the existing ViT architecture and its training recipe. This includes:
 - Reducing memory consumption: Large models can quickly exceed available hardware memory. The authors aimed to find ways to make ViTs more memory-efficient.
 - Increasing accuracy: Beyond just fitting models, they sought to improve the overall accuracy of the resulting models, particularly for downstream transfer tasks.
 - Optimizing training hyper-parameters: Discovering subtle but significant choices in hyper-parameters (e.g., weight decay, learning rate schedules) that drastically improve performance, especially for few-shot transfer.

4. **Achieve New State-of-the-Art Results:** A tangible goal was to push the performance boundaries of Vision Transformers and achieve new state-of-the-art results on benchmark tasks like ImageNet. This serves as a validation of the insights gained from their scaling studies and demonstrates the practical utility of their refined methodologies.

5. **Evaluate Few-Shot Transfer Capabilities:** The authors specifically emphasize the importance of few-shot transfer performance, motivated by similar trends in large-scale NLP models (like GPT-3). They aim to demonstrate that large Vision Transformers learn highly transferable visual representations capable of strong performance even with very limited examples per class on new tasks.

In summary, the paper is driven by a desire to bring the rigorous empirical study of scaling laws from NLP to vision, providing both theoretical insights into ViT behavior at scale and practical improvements that enable the training of unprecedentedly large and performant vision models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "Scaling Vision Transformers" is primarily empirical, involving a systematic and extensive set of experiments across varying dimensions of model size, dataset size, and computational budget.

#### 3.1. Experimental Design and Scope

The authors conducted a large-scale study spanning:
- **Model Sizes:** ViT models ranging from five million to two billion parameters, encompassing various configurations (e.g., ViT-Ti/16, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16, up to the newly introduced ViT-G/14).
- **Dataset Sizes:** Training datasets ranging from one million images (public ImageNet-21k) to three billion images (proprietary JFT-3B).
- **Compute Budgets:** Ranging from below one TPUv3 core-day to beyond 10,000 core-days.
All models were trained on Google's TPUv3 infrastructure, allowing consistent measurement of "TPUv3 core-days" as the unit of compute.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Protocols

To assess the quality of learned representations, models were evaluated using:
- **Few-Shot Transfer:** Measuring accuracy by training a linear classifier on frozen pre-trained weights with a very small number of examples per class (e.g., 1, 5, or 10 shots). This evaluates the quality and transferability of the learned feature extractor.
- **Fine-tuning Transfer:** Fine-tuning the entire pre-trained model on the target dataset (e.g., ImageNet, ImageNet-v2, ReaL, ObjectNet) and evaluating top-1 accuracy. This assesses the model's ability to adapt to specific downstream tasks.
- **VTAB-1k Benchmark:** Transfer learning results on a diverse set of 19 tasks to measure generalized visual representation learning.

#### 3.3. Key Architectural and Training Refinements

The ability to conduct such large-scale experiments was heavily reliant on several critical improvements to the ViT architecture and its training protocol:

- **Decoupled Weight Decay for the "Head":** They discovered that applying a stronger L2 regularization (weight decay) specifically to the final linear prediction layer ("head") of the model, while keeping a lower weight decay for the rest of the model ("body"), drastically improves few-shot transfer performance. This suggests that high weight decay on the head encourages a more robust and separable representation in the body, akin to SVMs.

- **Memory Efficiency via Removing `[class]` Token:** The original ViT used an extra learnable `[class]` token to aggregate information for the final classification, which led to memory inefficiencies on TPUs due to padding (token dimension padded to multiples of 128). The authors investigated alternatives like Global Average Pooling (GAP) and Multihead Attention Pooling (MAP) over patch tokens. They found MAP to perform similarly to the `[class]` token while being significantly more memory-efficient. They also removed the final non-linear projection layer from the head for simplicity and further memory saving.

- **Scaling Up Data with JFT-3B:** The research extensively utilized the proprietary JFT-3B dataset, a much larger (nearly 3 billion weakly-labeled images) version of the JFT-300M dataset. This provided ample data for training very large models without overfitting. Crucially, they performed **deduplication** to remove near-duplicates of evaluation set images from JFT-3B to ensure fair comparison.

- **Memory-Efficient Optimizers:** Traditional optimizers like Adam store per-parameter state (moments), leading to significant memory overhead (e.g., 2x for Adam). To address this for 2-billion parameter models, they made two modifications:
 - **Adam with Half-Precision Momentum:** Storing the first momentum in bfloat16 (half-precision) while keeping the second momentum in full precision, reducing overhead from 2x to 1.5x.
 - **Modified Adafactor Optimizer:** Adafactor, known for its sublinear memory cost, was adapted. This involved re-introducing half-precision first momentum, disabling learning rate scaling relative to weight norms, and clipping the second momentum (beta2) at 0.999. This reduced memory overhead to a negligible 0.5x.

- **Improved Learning Rate Schedules:** To facilitate the study of varying training durations from a single run, they explored learning rate schedules with a "cooldown" phase. The reciprocal square-root schedule was chosen, which allows for indefinite training and evaluation at multiple durations without retraining from scratch.

- **Systematic Model Dimension Selection ("Shapefinder"):** Given the complex interplay of ViT architectural parameters (depth, width, heads, MLP-width, patch-size) and hardware-specific optimizations (XLA compiler), they employed an "extensive simulation" or "Shapefinder" to empirically determine which model configurations would fit into memory and their relative training speed on TPUv3. This systematic exploration guided the selection of optimal large model shapes like ViT-g/14 and ViT-G/14.

These methodological advancements collectively enabled the comprehensive empirical study, allowing the authors to push ViT scaling to unprecedented levels and uncover fundamental scaling behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings and results from its extensive empirical study on scaling Vision Transformers:

#### 4.1. Scaling Laws for Vision Transformers

- **Power-Law Relationship with Saturation:** The most significant finding is that the relationship between representation quality (measured as ImageNet fine-tune and 10-shot error rate) and total training compute roughly follows a **(double-saturating) power law**. On a log-log plot, the Pareto frontier of performance versus compute is largely linear over two orders of magnitude.
 - **High-End Saturation:** At the higher end of compute, the models' error rate does not tend to zero, suggesting an "irreducible error" or fundamental performance ceiling for ImageNet. This manifests as an additive constant `c` in the power law `E = aC^-b + c`.
 - **Low-End Saturation:** At the lower end of the compute spectrum, the performance of smaller models is better than predicted by a pure power law. This indicates that even trivial solutions can achieve non-zero accuracy, leading to a shift in the x-axis `d` in `E = a(C + d)^-b + c`.
- **Simultaneous Scaling is Crucial:** To stay on the performance-compute frontier and achieve the best representation quality, it is essential to scale compute, model size, and data size **simultaneously**. Smaller models or models trained on insufficient data saturate and fall off the optimal frontier even with more compute.
- **Generalizability of Scaling Laws:** The observed double-saturation power law was confirmed to apply not only to their large-scale private JFT-3B dataset but also to the public ImageNet-21k dataset, indicating the generality of these scaling conclusions.

#### 4.2. Benefits of Larger Models

- **Increased Sample Efficiency:** Bigger models are significantly more sample efficient, meaning they can achieve the same level of error rate with fewer "seen images" (total batch size × steps during pre-training). For example, the ViT-Ti/16 model required nearly 100 times more images to match the 10-shot representation quality of the ViT-L/16 model. This suggests that for a given compute budget, training a larger model for fewer steps might be preferable if sufficient data is available.
- **Stronger Data Hunger for Large Models:** Large models benefit from additional data, even beyond one billion images (up to 3 billion images in JFT-3B). Conversely, smaller models do not benefit from increasing dataset size beyond a certain point, indicating their limited capacity.

#### 4.3. New State-of-the-Art Performance

- **ImageNet Top-1 Accuracy:** The largest trained model, **ViT-G/14 (nearly two billion parameters)**, achieved a new state-of-the-art of **90.45% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet**, surpassing previous models like ViT-H/14 and EfficientNet-L2 based approaches.
- **Exceptional Few-Shot Transfer:** ViT-G/14 demonstrated impressive few-shot learning capabilities:
 - **84.86% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet with only 10 examples per class.** This significantly outperforms previous ViT models and competitive self-supervised learning approaches.
 - **69.52% accuracy with just a single example per class on ImageNet.**
- **Robust Generalization:** ViT-G/14 also showed strong performance on other benchmarks:
 - **83.33% on ImageNet-v2,** a robust generalization test set.
 - **90.81% on ImageNet ReaL** and **70.53% on ObjectNet**.
 - **78.29% on VTAB (light)**, a diverse benchmark of 19 tasks, with consistent results across tasks using a single hyperparameter setting.

#### 4.4. Efficacy of Methodological Improvements

The paper indirectly validates the efficacy of their architectural and training refinements:
- **Decoupled Weight Decay:** Visually demonstrated to significantly improve few-shot transfer performance.
- **Memory Savings:** The removal of the `[class]` token and the use of modified Adafactor optimizer were crucial enablers, allowing the ViT-G/14 model to fit on a single TPUv3 core for data-parallel training (entire model fits on single core).
- **JFT-3B Dataset:** Showed a consistent ~1% improvement in ImageNet transfer performance for both small and large models compared to JFT-300M, even without increasing model scale.

Overall, the main findings provide both fundamental insights into the scaling behavior of Vision Transformers and concrete evidence of their superior performance when scaled appropriately with optimized training practices.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Scaling Vision Transformers" paper holds significant importance and potential impact across several dimensions of deep learning research and application:

#### 5.1. Guiding Future Model Design and Resource Allocation

- **Empirical Roadmap for ViT Development:** The most direct impact is providing an empirical roadmap for designing and training future generations of Vision Transformers. The discovered power laws offer concrete guidance: instead of arbitrarily increasing model size or compute, researchers can predict performance trade-offs and optimize resource allocation more effectively. This reduces the need for trial-and-error, making large-scale model development more efficient.
- **Confirming Cross-Modal Scaling:** The work largely confirms that the scaling laws observed in NLP Transformers also apply to Vision Transformers. This suggests a potential universality in how large neural networks learn and benefit from scale across different modalities, reinforcing fundamental principles that can inform the development of multi-modal foundational models.
- **Highlighting Data Importance:** The findings emphasize that while model size and compute are critical, large ViTs are "data-hungry" and continue to benefit significantly from even extremely large datasets (billions of images). This underscores the ongoing importance of collecting and curating massive, diverse datasets for future AI advancements.

#### 5.2. Advancing State-of-the-Art in Computer Vision

- **New Performance Benchmarks:** Achieving 90.45% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet is a significant milestone, setting a new state-of-the-art for the benchmark. This demonstrates the immense potential of Vision Transformers when scaled, pushing the boundaries of image classification performance.
- **Strong Few-Shot Learning Capabilities:** The exceptional few-shot transfer results (e.g., 84.86% with 10 examples per class on ImageNet) are particularly impactful. They indicate that these large ViTs learn highly robust and generalizable visual representations. Such models can drastically reduce the data requirements for new downstream tasks, making high-performance vision models accessible even for applications with limited labeled data. This capability is crucial for practical deployment where large task-specific datasets are often unavailable.

#### 5.3. Practical Contributions to Large-Scale Training

- **Memory Efficiency Innovations:** The detailed discussion of memory-saving techniques (e.g., modified Adafactor, removing `[class]` token) provides practical solutions for training multi-billion parameter models. These techniques are readily transferable to other large neural network architectures, reducing the computational barrier for researchers and engineers attempting similar large-scale experiments.
- **Hyperparameter Insights:** The finding regarding decoupled weight decay for the head layer is a specific, actionable insight that can significantly improve the transfer performance of pre-trained models, especially in low-data regimes.

#### 5.4. Broader Societal and Environmental Considerations

- **Energy Consumption and Amortization:** The authors explicitly address the high energy cost of training such massive models. Their argument for "amortization" is significant: by providing scaling laws, they aim to guide future developers to more efficiently design models, potentially reducing wasted compute. Furthermore, highly transferable pre-trained models can be reused across many downstream tasks, where fine-tuning is much less energy-intensive than training from scratch. This perspective encourages a more sustainable approach to large-scale AI development by maximizing the utility of a single, expensive pre-training run.
- **Data Bias and Responsible AI:** The mention of "sensitive category association analysis" and human efforts to remove offensive content from the JFT-3B dataset highlights a growing awareness of responsible AI practices in large-scale data collection and model training, setting a positive example.

In conclusion, "Scaling Vision Transformers" is a landmark paper that not only pushes the boundaries of performance in computer vision but also provides fundamental scientific insights into the scaling behavior of modern deep learning models. Its findings offer a strategic blueprint for future research, emphasizing efficient resource utilization and the development of highly transferable, general-purpose visual intelligence.
