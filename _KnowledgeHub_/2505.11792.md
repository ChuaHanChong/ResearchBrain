# Solver-Informed RL: Grounding Large Language Models for Authentic Optimization Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework called Solver-Informed Reinforcement Learning (SIRL) enhances large language models to generate authentic and executable optimization models by using direct, objective feedback from optimization solvers as rewards. The SIRL-Qwen2.5-7B model demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on public benchmarks, achieving a macro average accuracy of 62.1%, comparable to a much larger 671B-parameter model.

## Problem

- Optimization modeling requires substantial domain expertise to translate natural language problem descriptions into precise mathematical formulations and executable code for solvers, limiting its widespread adoption.
- Large language models (LLMs) often struggle with formal correctness, logical consistency, and 'hallucinations' when generating structured outputs like mathematical models and executable code for optimization.
- Existing LLM-based approaches for optimization either rely on sensitive prompting or offline fine-tuning methods that often fail to guarantee functional correctness and solution feasibility.

## Method

- Introduced Solver-Informed Reinforcement Learning (**SIRL**), an RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with ==Verifiable Rewards==) framework that leverages objective feedback from optimization solvers (syntax correctness, feasibility, solution quality).
- Developed an instance-enhanced self-consistency method for data synthesis, incorporating structural information from solver-generated LP files for more robust ground truth selection.
- Designed a novel 'Partial KL' surrogate function for RL training to balance exploration in LLM reasoning steps with strict adherence to formal mathematical and code syntax.

## Results

- The SIRL-Qwen2.5-**7B** model achieved state-of-the-art performance with a macro average accuracy of **62.1%** across benchmarks, performing comparably to the **671B**-parameter Deepseek-V3 model (**61.0%**).
- The instance-enhanced self-consistency method consistently achieved higher accuracy in data synthesis than value-based self-consistency, showing up to a **128.1%** relative improvement for Qwen2.5-**7B** on the OptMATH dataset.
- Ablation studies validated that the Partial KL surrogate function improved both pass@1 accuracy and execution rate, and the two-stage reward mechanism effectively fostered both foundational and advanced modeling skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct, objective feedback from optimization solvers can effectively ground LLMs, significantly mitigating hallucinations and ensuring the 'authenticity' (correctness, feasibility, usability) of generated optimization models.
> - Strategically applying KL divergence penalties (Partial KL) during RL training allows LLMs to explore diverse reasoning paths while maintaining strict adherence to formal mathematical and programmatic rules.
> - A two-stage, curriculum-based reward system is effective for progressively teaching LLMs both foundational optimization modeling skills and advanced techniques.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of large language models (LLMs) and optimization, addressing a critical bottleneck in applying powerful optimization solvers to real-world problems. The broader landscape is characterized by increasing efforts to automate complex, domain-specific tasks using LLMs, often through advanced reasoning and feedback mechanisms.

**The Challenge of Optimization Modeling:**
Optimization modeling is fundamental to decision-making in diverse fields, yet it demands substantial domain expertise to translate natural language problem descriptions into precise mathematical formulations and executable code for solvers (e.g., Gurobi, CPLEX). This translation process is often manual, error-prone, and a significant barrier to the wider adoption of optimization techniques.

**Emergence of LLMs in Optimization:**
The advent of LLMs has opened promising avenues for automating this process. Existing approaches largely fall into two categories:
1. **Prompt-based or Agent-based Methods:** These leverage the inherent capabilities of powerful, frozen foundation LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Deepseek-V3) by guiding them with sophisticated prompts, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, or multi-agent frameworks (e.g., Chain-of-Experts, OptiMUS). While accessible, their performance is sensitive to prompt design and the capabilities of the underlying foundation model, and they do not adapt the model's parameters directly for the task.
2. **Offline Learning Approaches:** This involves fine-tuning open-source LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, Qwen) using techniques like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or alignment methods (e.g., DPO, KTO) on specialized datasets (e.g., ORLM, OptMATH, LLMOpt). While these methods adapt model parameters and can capture stylistic patterns, they often struggle to *guarantee functional correctness or solution feasibility*. Their training objectives typically focus on mimicking demonstrations or aligning preferences, rather than objective, verifiable outcomes. This leads to issues like "hallucinations" where LLMs generate syntactically plausible but logically incorrect or unexecutable models.

**The Rise of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):**
Concurrently, a significant trend in the broader LLM research landscape is the use of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enhance LLMs' reasoning capabilities, leading to powerful Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) in domains like mathematics and code generation. A key technique here is RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), where external tools (code interpreters, compilers, theorem provers) act as objective verifiers, providing direct feedback signals (e.g., code execution success, mathematical proof validity) as rewards for the RL process. This moves beyond subjective human preferences (RLHF) to objective correctness. Noteworthy examples include DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI-O1, and Tulu-3, which leverage execution-based feedback to achieve high performance on challenging coding and mathematical benchmarks.

**SIRL's Unique Position:**
This paper, with its Solver-Informed Reinforcement Learning (SIRL) framework, uniquely positions itself by being the *first to apply the powerful RLVR paradigm directly to the complex, multi-step reasoning task of optimization modeling*. It bridges the gap left by previous LLM-for-optimization approaches by:
- **Introducing objective verification:** SIRL explicitly leverages classical optimization solvers (Gurobi, COPT, CPLEX) not just as execution engines, but as *objective verifiers*. This allows for rich feedback signals—syntax correctness, feasibility, and solution quality (relative to ground truth)—to be directly incorporated as rewards in the RL process.
- **Addressing authenticity:** Unlike methods relying on imitation, SIRL directly optimizes for "authenticity" (formally correct and usable models) by using verifiable rewards, thereby mitigating hallucinations and enhancing reliability.
- **Integrating advanced data synthesis:** It also contributes a novel instance-enhanced self-consistency method, which uses structural information from LP files to synthesize high-quality training data, further improving the learning process.

By drawing inspiration from the success of RLVR in general reasoning tasks and adapting it specifically for the unique demands of optimization, SIRL pushes the frontier of automated optimization modeling, offering a more robust and reliable path towards democratizing access to optimization technology.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the acknowledged difficulty and expertise required for optimization modeling, contrasted with the transformative potential of Large Language Models to automate or significantly assist this process.

**Motivation:**
1. **Bridging the Expertise Gap:** Optimization modeling is a fundamental tool for decision-making across diverse industries, but its effective application demands significant domain expertise in translating real-world problems into precise mathematical models and executable solver code. This expertise bottleneck limits the widespread adoption and accessibility of powerful optimization techniques.
2. **LLM Limitations in Formal Domains:** While LLMs have shown remarkable abilities in natural language understanding and generation, they frequently struggle with formal correctness, logical consistency, and "hallucinations" when generating highly structured outputs like mathematical models and executable code, which are critical for reliable optimization. Existing LLM-based approaches often fail to inherently guarantee the functional correctness and solver compatibility required for practical use.
3. **Inspiration from Large Reasoning Models (LRMs):** Recent advancements have demonstrated that Reinforcement Learning (RL) can significantly enhance LLMs' reasoning capabilities, particularly for tasks requiring multi-step logical inference and tool interaction (e.g., in mathematics and coding). This success, often driven by verifiable rewards from external tools, suggests a powerful paradigm applicable to the multi-stage nature of optimization problem-solving (problem analysis, mathematical modeling, code implementation).

**Key Objectives:**
Given these motivations, the authors set forth the following objectives for the SIRL framework:

1. **Introduce Solver-Informed Reinforcement Learning (SIRL):** Develop a novel framework that fundamentally improves the "authenticity" (correctness, feasibility, and usability) of LLM-generated optimization models. This involves leveraging RL with verifiable rewards directly from external optimization solvers.
2. **Utilize Optimization Solvers as Verifiers:** Employ classic optimization solvers (e.g., Gurobi, COPT) as automated verifiers to provide precise and comprehensive feedback signals. These signals include syntax correctness, model feasibility (by attempting to solve the model), and solution quality (by comparing objective values), which serve as direct, objective rewards for the RL process.
3. **Develop Instance-Enhanced Self-Consistency for Data Synthesis:** Create a robust method for synthesizing high-quality training data. This method integrates LLM generation with a novel self-consistency mechanism that leverages both final objective values and structural information (variable types, objective direction, constraints) extracted from the instance-level LP files generated by solvers. This aims to create a more reliable and diverse dataset for training.
4. **Design a Novel Surrogate Function (Partial KL):** Propose a specialized surrogate function within the RL framework, termed "Partial KL," to reconcile the tension between promoting diverse exploration in LLM reasoning steps and ensuring strict adherence to formal mathematical formulation and solver code syntax.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Demonstrate through extensive experiments on diverse public benchmarks that the SIRL-trained models significantly outperform existing offline learning and agent-based methods in generating accurate, executable, and reliable optimization models, especially for models with fewer parameters.
6. **Publicly Release Code:** Make the developed code and framework publicly available to foster further research and adoption within the community.

In essence, the paper aims to empower LLMs to become reliable partners in optimization modeling, moving beyond mere linguistic assistance to truly generate functionally correct and solver-compatible solutions, thereby democratizing access to complex optimization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SIRL framework is built upon two core methodological pillars: an advanced data synthesis pipeline that leverages solver feedback, and a novel Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) training scheme.

#### 3.1. Data Synthesis Framework

The process for generating high-quality training data is critical for fine-tuning LLMs. SIRL's approach extends existing methods by integrating more rigorous, solver-informed verification:

1. **Initial Seed Data & Augmentation:** The process begins with curated seed data (e.g., from OR textbooks, industry scenarios). LLMs generate new problems by combining seed questions with contextual scenarios, followed by an augmentation phase to increase semantic and mathematical complexity.
2. **LLM-as-a-Judge Validation:** Generated problems are then validated for practical relevance and semantic consistency using an "LLM-as-a-judge" approach, ensuring the quality of the problem statements.
3. **Mathematical Model and Code Generation:** The LLM generates both the mathematical model and the corresponding executable code.
4. **Solver Execution and LP File Generation:** Crucially, this generated code is *executed by an external optimization solver* (e.g., Gurobi). This execution yields not only a solution (objective value, feasibility status) but also an LP file. The LP file provides a standardized, explicit, instance-level representation of the mathematical model, detailing variables, constraints, and objective function.
5. **Instance-Enhanced Self-Consistency (Novelty):** To ensure the correctness of the generated "question, answer" pairs, SIRL employs a novel instance-enhanced self-consistency method. Instead of relying solely on the final numerical objective value for consensus (as in standard self-consistency), this method incorporates structural information from the LP files. For a given problem, multiple LLM roles generate solutions. For each solution, the solver produces an objective value (O_r), optimization direction (D_r), and counts of binary (N_bin,r) and general integer (N_int,r) variables from the LP file. A weighted voting mechanism assesses consensus across all these features for each role's response, yielding a score S(r). The objective value from the response with the highest consensus score is selected as the definitive ground truth. This approach provides a more robust and reliable way to synthesize high-quality, formally correct data.
6. **Iterative Reflection and Refinement:** If code execution results in errors or an infeasible solution, an iterative reflection and refinement process is employed, prompting the LLM to analyze the root cause and regenerate or refine the model and code until a valid solution is found.
7. **Filtering for Challenging Samples:** Guided by the "Less is More" principle, the dataset is filtered to remove overly simple examples (where a strong baseline LLM achieves an 80% pass rate). This focuses training on more challenging and informative instances.

#### 3.2. SIRL: Solver-Informed Reinforcement Learning

The core of the framework is an RLVR approach that optimizes the LLM's policy using objective feedback from solvers.

1. **RLVR for Optimization Tasks:** The process of solving an optimization problem (problem analysis, mathematical modeling, code implementation) is treated as a multi-step reasoning process, analogous to Chain-of-Thought (CoT). The LLM generates a sequence of thoughts (`z`), comprising analysis (`<think>`), mathematical model (`<model>`), and executable code (`<python>`). The solver then executes the code to produce the objective value (`y`).
2. **Policy Optimization:** The LLM's policy (`π_θ`) is optimized using a robust policy gradient algorithm (REINFORCE++ with PPO-like clipping) to maximize an expected verifiable reward.
3. **Surrogate Function Design: Partial KL (Novelty):** A key innovation is the "Partial KL" surrogate function. It addresses the tension between allowing the LLM to explore diverse reasoning paths and enforcing strict adherence to formal syntax for mathematical models and code.
 - **Mechanism:** A KL divergence penalty (which discourages deviation from a reference policy) is *selectively applied*.
 - **Reasoning Steps (`<think>`):** The KL penalty is *omitted* for the initial reasoning steps. This encourages broader exploration, enabling the LLM to identify diverse problem interpretations and implicit constraints without being overly constrained by the reference model.
 - **Modeling & Code Segments (`<model>`, `<python>`):** The KL penalty *is applied* to the mathematical formulation and solver code segments. This ensures that the generated output remains well-structured, adheres to expected mathematical and programmatic formats, and prevents "policy collapse" into nonsensical generations, while still allowing gradual improvement guided by the rewards.
4. **Reward Design and Training Scheme:** A two-stage, rule-based reward system is implemented:
 - **Stage-1:** Focuses on foundational capabilities.
 - `R_format(z)`: Rewards for correct structural formatting (presence and order of `<think>`, `<model>`, `<python>` tags).
 - `R_exec(z)`: Rewards for code executability.
 - `R_accur(x, z, y*)`: Rewards for solution accuracy (objective value matching ground truth within tolerance).
 - **Stage-2:** Builds on Stage-1 by introducing a bonus for advanced modeling.
 - `R_bonus(x, z, y*)`: A bonus reward is given if the generated solution is correct *and* the model utilizes advanced modeling techniques (e.g., Big-M linearization, binary variables, nonlinear formulations), which are detected by analyzing the LP file. This incentivizes the LLM to learn and apply more sophisticated optimization strategies for complex problems.
 This curriculum-based approach allows for progressive skill acquisition, starting with fundamental correctness and then advancing to more sophisticated modeling.

By integrating solver-informed data synthesis with a tailored RLVR framework that includes a novel Partial KL regularization and a staged reward system, SIRL creates a powerful feedback loop that significantly enhances an LLM's ability to generate authentic and executable optimization models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on diverse public benchmarks demonstrate the significant efficacy of the SIRL framework, validating its core contributions.

#### 4.1. Instance-Enhanced Self-Consistency Performance

- The paper first evaluates the effectiveness of its novel instance-enhanced self-consistency method (inst\_sc) against a value-based self-consistency (val\_sc) and a single-pass generation (pass@1) on Qwen2.5 models (7B-Instruct and 32B-Instruct) across multiple datasets (NL4OPT, MAMO Easy/Complex, IndustryOR, OptMATH).
- **Key Finding:** Both self-consistency methods outperform the pass@1 baseline, confirming the benefit of multi-sample voting. Crucially, `inst_sc` consistently achieves higher accuracy than `val_sc`. The improvements are particularly substantial on more complex datasets, such as MAMO Complex (19.1% relative difference for 7B, 9.4% for 32B), IndustryOR (4.0% for 7B, 3.1% for 32B), and especially OptMATH (128.1% for 7B, 23.3% for 32B).
- **Significance:** This result validates the hypothesis that incorporating structural information from LP files (optimization direction, variable counts) provides a more robust measure of consensus than solely relying on final objective values, leading to higher-quality synthesized data.

#### 4.2. Main Results: Overall Model Performance (SIRL-Qwen2.5-7B)

- The SIRL-trained Qwen2.5-7B model (SIRL-Qwen2.5-7B) is compared against various baselines: powerful foundation LLMs (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, Deepseek-V3), agent-based approaches (Chain-of-Experts, OptiMUS), and offline-learning methods (ORLM-LLaMA-3-8B, LLMOpt-Qwen2.5-14B, OptMATH-Qwen2.5-7B). Performance is measured by pass@1 accuracy, with a strict criterion of relative error less than 1e-6.
- **Key Finding:** SIRL-Qwen2.5-7B achieves state-of-the-art performance, significantly outperforming all other models of comparable (7B/14B) or even larger (8B, 14B) parameter counts trained by offline learning or agent-based methods. For instance, it achieves 96.3% on NL4OPT, 90.0% on MAMO Easy, 62.1% on MAMO Complex, 33.0% on IndustryOR, and 29.0% on OptMATH.
- **Comparison to Large Foundation Models:** Remarkably, SIRL-Qwen2.5-7B achieves results comparable to the 671B-parameter Deepseek-V3 (e.g., Deepseek-V3 Macro AVG 61.0% vs. SIRL 62.1%), demonstrating exceptional efficiency in leveraging a much smaller model to achieve high performance through effective RLVR training.
- **Significance:** This unequivocally demonstrates the power of the SIRL framework in enhancing LLMs' capabilities for optimization modeling, yielding accurate and executable solutions across a wide range of problem complexities.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Surrogate Function Design (Partial KL)

- The study compares three surrogate function designs: Full KL (standard), Without KL (no regularization), and Partial KL (the proposed selective application).
- **Key Finding:** Partial KL consistently achieves the best performance in terms of both pass@1 accuracy and execution rate (ER) across challenging datasets (MAMO Complex, IndustryOR, OptMATH).
 - "Without KL" shows a dramatically lower execution rate (e.g., 72.5% on OptMATH vs. 88.1% for Partial KL), indicating that completely removing KL divergence, while promoting exploration, leads to a significant increase in invalid or unexecutable generations due to policy collapse or introduction of irrelevant constraints.
 - "Full KL" performs better than "Without KL" but is still surpassed by "Partial KL" in accuracy, suggesting it might overly constrain beneficial exploration in the reasoning stages.
- **Significance:** This confirms the effectiveness of Partial KL in strategically balancing the need for diverse reasoning exploration with the strict requirements of mathematical formulation and solver code syntax, leading to higher authenticity and reliability in generated models.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Reward Design (Two-Stage Rewards)

- The study compares the proposed two-stage reward mechanism against models trained with only Stage-1 rewards or only Stage-2 rewards.
- **Key Finding:** The integrated two-stage reward mechanism generally achieves superior performance across most benchmark tasks.
 - Stage-1 rewards alone perform strongly on simpler tasks (e.g., NL4OPT), indicating its efficacy in building foundational skills.
 - Stage-2 rewards alone, with its bonus for advanced strategies, show the best performance on the most challenging OptMATH dataset, but diminished performance on simpler tasks.
- **Significance:** This demonstrates that the two-stage curriculum successfully resolves the trade-offs inherent in single-stage reward systems, enabling the model to progressively learn both foundational optimization skills and advanced modeling techniques, leading to a more versatile and capable model.

In summary, the main findings highlight that SIRL, through its innovative solver-informed data synthesis, Partial KL surrogate function, and two-stage reward system, represents a significant leap forward in empowering LLMs to generate authentic, accurate, and executable optimization models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SIRL framework represents a significant advancement at the confluence of Large Language Models and Operations Research, with profound implications for both fields.

#### 5.1. Advancing LLM Capabilities for Formal Reasoning

- **Pioneering RLVR in Optimization:** SIRL is the first framework to successfully apply Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by directly leveraging external optimization solvers for feedback. This extends the successful paradigm of RLVR (previously seen in math and code generation with interpreters/compilers) into the complex, multi-stage reasoning domain of optimization modeling. It demonstrates that LLMs can be robustly trained to generate highly structured, formally correct outputs guided by objective, tool-based verification.
- **Mitigating Hallucinations and Enhancing Authenticity:** A major challenge with LLMs in formal domains is their propensity for hallucinations. SIRL's direct feedback loop from optimization solvers, coupled with the Partial KL strategy, inherently guides the LLM towards generating "authentic" models – those that are syntactically correct, semantically meaningful, feasible, and solvable. This moves beyond merely generating plausible text to generating functionally correct artifacts.

#### 5.2. Democratizing Access to Optimization

- **Reducing Expertise Barriers:** Optimization modeling traditionally requires deep domain expertise. By enabling LLMs to accurately translate natural language problems into executable models, SIRL significantly lowers the entry barrier for individuals and organizations without specialized operations research knowledge. This could empower more decision-makers to leverage powerful optimization techniques.
- **Accelerating Problem Solving:** Automating the modeling process drastically reduces the time and manual effort currently required. This can accelerate the deployment of optimization solutions in various industries (logistics, finance, manufacturing, healthcare), leading to more efficient resource allocation, cost savings, and improved decision-making.

#### 5.3. Novel Contributions to Data Synthesis and RL Design

- **Instance-Enhanced Self-Consistency:** The introduction of this method, leveraging structural information from LP files beyond just final numerical answers, offers a more robust and intelligent way to curate high-quality synthetic data for complex, multi-faceted tasks. This approach has broader applicability in other domains where LLM-generated outputs require rigorous structural validation.
- **Partial KL Surrogate Function:** This innovative design provides a blueprint for managing the trade-off between exploration in LLM reasoning and adherence to strict formal rules in specific output segments. It is a valuable contribution to the RL training methodology for LLMs in tool-augmented or structured generation tasks, where creativity needs to be balanced with correctness.
- **Two-Stage Reward Mechanism:** The curriculum-based reward system demonstrates an effective strategy for progressive skill acquisition, building foundational competence before incentivizing advanced techniques. This approach is highly relevant for training LLMs on complex tasks that require a hierarchy of skills.

#### 5.4. Practical and Industrial Impact

- The involvement of Cardinal Operations, a commercial entity, underscores the practical relevance and potential for real-world deployment of SIRL. This work directly addresses pain points in industrial optimization workflows, promising to make AI-driven optimization solutions more accessible and cost-effective for businesses.
- The open-sourcing of the code further encourages adoption, experimentation, and community-driven improvements, fostering innovation in the field.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

- SIRL opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated solver-informed feedback mechanisms, potentially incorporating sensitivity analysis, infeasibility diagnosis reports, or specific structural suggestions from solvers directly into the reward function.
- The framework could be extended to a wider array of optimization problem types beyond LP/MILP, including stochastic, robust, and meta-heuristic optimization problems.
- The principles of Partial KL and instance-enhanced verification could be generalized to other scientific modeling and engineering design tasks where LLMs generate code or formal specifications that must interact with external simulators or verification tools.

In conclusion, SIRL is a landmark contribution that moves LLM-based optimization modeling from a promise to a more reliable reality. By effectively grounding LLMs in the objective feedback of powerful optimization solvers, it not only enhances their reasoning capabilities but also promises to significantly impact the accessibility and application of optimization across industries.
