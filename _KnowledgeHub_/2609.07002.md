# WM-Craftnet: World Synesthesia Model for Generalizable and Robust Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> WM-Craftnet is a world-model-conditioned framework for dexterous in-hand manipulation on a 22-DoF five-finger hand. Its World Synesthesia Model, a Dreamer-style recurrent state-space model over proprioception, noisy depth, tactile sensing and actions, is trained with multimodal reconstruction and reward prediction, then supplies a detached recurrent feature as policy context rather than imagination. It reaches 753.3 return and 175/200 real-robot rotation success.

## Problem

- Tactile and visuotactile RL policies achieve strong in-hand rotation in controlled settings, but degrade under pose offsets, force disturbances and object variation.
- Methods that inject object identities or shape embeddings tie deployment to known annotations, limiting general-purpose manipulation across unseen object distributions.
- Simply concatenating depth and touch streams leaves the policy to learn contact dynamics from noisy fused inputs, and real wrist depth diverges from clean simulation.

## Method

- **World Synesthesia Model**: a ==Dreamer-style== multimodal ==RSSM== encoding proprioception, noisy depth, tactile contact and previous actions, trained with per-modality reconstruction, reward prediction and dynamics/representation KL terms.
- **Noisy-to-clean depth supervision**: the decoder reconstructs clean wrist-depth targets from augmented noisy inputs, giving ==denoised geometry==; auxiliary pose, value and ==basis-point-set shape heads== further structure the latent state.
- **WSM-conditioned asymmetric actor-critic**: the detached 512-dim deterministic state is projected to a 16-dim policy input for ==PPO==, and the pretrained WSM transfers as a reusable prior.

## Results

- On nine-object z-axis rotation, full WSM reaches **753.3** return and **1.293** rotation rate, versus **236.1** to **386.9** return for open-loop, blind, tactile-only and visuotactile baselines.
- On hardware, **175/200** trials succeed across 20 objects versus **33/200** to **53/200** for baselines; perturbation recovery rises to **14.1%** from **6.2%**, at **2.56 s** mean recovery.
- Ablations: LSTM/GRU history reach **621.0**/**497.8** return, noisy-depth supervision **708.0**, per-step encoder-decoder feature **667.6**, no auxiliary heads **688.1**; the pretrained prior gives **9.37** rad versus **3.28**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A world model can pay off purely as a recurrent state estimator supplying detached policy context, with no latent imagination or model-based policy optimization.
> - Training a latent model to map noisy sensor inputs onto clean targets bakes denoising into the representation, carrying sim-trained perception onto imperfect real hardware.
> - Predictive multimodal objectives beat plain recurrence: memory alone underperforms, and value or reward prediction supervision contributes more return than pose or shape heads.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Dexterous in-hand manipulation, which involves precisely controlling objects with a multi-fingered hand, is a fundamental capability for numerous robotic tasks, including object reorientation, tool use, and fine assembly. This domain presents significant challenges due to the inherent partial observability of the object, the need to manage intermittent fingertip contacts, and the requirement to adapt to dynamic changes in object pose, geometry, and external forces.

Existing learning-based methods, which leverage proprioception, tactile sensing, and vision, have achieved progress in sim-to-real in-hand control. Approaches like Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) infer object properties online from proprioceptive history for rotation, while methods such as DeXtreme combine domain randomization with vision-based pose estimation. Other works, including Touch Dexterity and AnyRotate, explore vision-free or dense tactile feedback for various rotation tasks. More complex reorientation strategies involve composing pre-trained skills or utilizing shape-specialized experts. However, these methods often exhibit limitations. Policies trained on constrained object sets or with fixed initial poses can overfit, leading to reduced robustness when confronted with variations in object pose, external disturbances, or novel objects. Some approaches attempt to incorporate object identities or shape embeddings, but this can constrain deployment to known annotations, limiting general-purpose manipulation.

A critical gap exists in developing policies that can robustly infer object geometry, pose, contact state, and slip risk from noisy and partial observations, especially when real-world sensor data, such as wrist-mounted depth inputs, are corrupted or inconsistent with clean simulation environments. Directly concatenating raw sensor streams often burdens the policy with the task of learning complex temporal contact dynamics from these noisy inputs. This work positions itself within the context of enhancing generalizability and robustness in dexterous in-hand manipulation by addressing these issues through a structured, predictive representation. It draws inspiration from both visuotactile fusion techniques and world model representations, which aim to provide deployable predictive features for control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables generalizable and robust dexterous in-hand manipulation. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and desired capabilities:

- **Robust State Inference from Noisy and Partial Observations:** A fundamental motivation is the need for a policy to infer critical object states—including pose, geometry, contact, and potential slip—from real-world partial and noisy sensor observations. Existing methods frequently struggle with the fidelity and completeness of real-world sensor data, leading to brittle performance.
- **Enhanced Robustness under Variability:** Current tactile and visuotactile reinforcement learning (RL) methods, while demonstrating in-hand rotation in controlled settings, often experience significant degradation in robustness when subjected to variations such as pose shifts, external force disturbances, and changes in object properties. A key objective is to improve policy resilience to these real-world challenges.
- **Addressing Overfitting to Narrow Object Sets:** Policies trained on limited sets of objects or specific initial poses tend to overfit to object-specific manipulation gaits. This work aims to overcome this limitation, enabling manipulation across a broader spectrum of objects without requiring explicit object identification or object-specific retraining.
- **Handling Real-World Sensor Data Imperfections:** The discrepancy between clean simulation data and noisy, shifted real-robot depth inputs poses a substantial barrier to sim-to-real transfer. A specific motivation is to develop a mechanism that can process noisy depth inputs to extract a clean geometric state relevant for control.
- **Improving Physical Inference for Contact-Rich Tasks:** The research is motivated by the hypothesis that a compact, action-conditioned latent state, informed by a combination of global geometry (vision), local contact evidence (touch), and historical actions, can significantly improve the physical inference required for dynamically changing, contact-rich manipulation tasks. This "synesthetic" reasoning is intended to allow the policy to anticipate contact transitions, object drift, and slip.
- **Developing Reusable Task Context:** The ability to pre-train and reuse a robust predictive model as a prior for downstream manipulation tasks is a significant motivation. This would allow for scalable learning across a large number of novel objects and manipulation skills, transferring fundamental physical understanding rather than just motor skills.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond fixed manipulation gaits and object-specific learning towards a more adaptable and intelligent robotic manipulation system that can interpret and react to the dynamic physical world through a robust, multimodal predictive representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WM-Craftnet introduces a world-model-conditioned framework that leverages a World Synesthesia Model (WSM) to achieve generalizable and robust dexterous in-hand manipulation. The methodology integrates recurrent latent dynamics, multimodal sensory inputs, predictive learning objectives, and an asymmetric actor-critic policy.

**World Synesthesia Model (WSM) Architecture:**
The core of WM-Craftnet is the WSM, implemented as a Dreamer-style Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM). This model is designed to learn compact, action-conditioned latent dynamics.
1. **Inputs:** The WSM encoder receives a single-step observation at each timestep $t$, comprising:
 - Proprioception ($p_t$): Normalized hand joint states.
 - Tactile sensing ($c_t$): Binary contact measurements.
 - Noisy depth image ($I_t$): A processed depth stream from a wrist-mounted camera.
 - Previous action ($u_{t-1}$): The joint-target command from the previous step.
 - An episode-start flag ($m_t$).
2. **Multimodal Encoding:** Proprioceptive and tactile streams are processed by Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) branches, while the depth stream is processed by a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) branch. The features from these branches are fused into a single encoded observation ($e_t$).
3. **Depth Preprocessing:** Input depth images ($I_t$) undergo clipping and cropping to focus on the hand-object workspace. Crucially, these inputs are augmented with temporally correlated dropout, Gaussian noise, and small random rotations during training. The WSM's objective is to reconstruct a *clean* (crop-only ground-truth) depth target ($I_{clean,t}$) from these noisy inputs, fostering the learning of a denoised geometric state.
4. **Recurrent Dynamics:** The RSSM updates its latent state based on the previous latent state ($z_{t-1}$, $h_{t-1}$), the previous action ($u_{t-1}$), and the current encoded observation ($e_t$). This update produces a stochastic latent state ($z_t$) and a deterministic recurrent state ($h_t$). The deterministic state ($h_t$) serves as the primary feature for the policy.

**WSM Training Objectives:**
The WSM is trained from a replay buffer, which stores records of proprioception, tactile data, noisy depth inputs, clean depth targets, previous actions, reward, and episode-start flags. The training optimizes a multimodal objective function:
$L_{wm} = L_{img} + L_{prop} + \lambda_c L_{tac} + \lambda_r L_{reward} + \lambda_{dyn} L_{dyn-KL} + \lambda_{rep} L_{rep-KL} + L_{aux}$
- **Reconstruction:** $L_{img}$ reconstructs the clean depth target ($I_{clean,t}$), $L_{prop}$ reconstructs proprioception, and $L_{tac}$ predicts tactile contact. This encourages the latent state to be geometry- and contact-aware.
- **Prediction:** $L_{reward}$ predicts the episode reward, providing a task-relevant signal for the dynamics model.
- **Regularization:** $L_{dyn-KL}$ and $L_{rep-KL}$ regularize the posterior and prior dynamics of the RSSM.
- **Auxiliary Heads ($L_{aux}$):** During simulation training, additional auxiliary heads predict object pose, value, and object shape (represented as a basis-point-set-to-mesh displacement vector). These heads provide supplementary supervision to enrich the learned state representation.

**World Model-Conditioned Manipulation Policy:**
The control policy is an asymmetric actor-critic model, trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
1. **Actor Input:** The actor receives the current deployable policy observation ($o_t$), the commanded rotation axis ($a$), and a projected version of the WSM's deterministic recurrent feature ($\psi(w_t)$, where $w_t = h_t$). Crucially, the WSM feature is detached from the PPO gradient path, ensuring that the world model provides context without direct policy gradient updates.
2. **Action Output:** The policy outputs a relative 22-DoF joint target command ($u_t$), which is then smoothed and executed by a low-level hand controller.
3. **Critic Input:** The critic, used for value estimation during training, additionally receives privileged simulator state information.
4. **Training Decoupling:** The world model optimization is decoupled from the PPO optimization. After each PPO epoch, replay chunks update the RSSM.

**Pretrained Synesthetic Prior:**
A significant aspect of the methodology is the ability to pretrain the WSM and reuse it as a "synesthetic prior."
- **Pretraining:** The WSM is initially trained on a contact-rich dataset from a 9-object z-axis rotation task.
- **Transfer:** The encoder, multimodal RSSM, depth/proprioceptive decoders, and reward predictor from the pretrained WSM are transferred to a new, more complex task involving 49 novel objects.
- **Downstream Adaptation:** During downstream PPO training on the new task, the transferred WSM components continue to adapt with new rollout data, while the actor-critic and task-specific auxiliary heads are learned for the target distribution. This enables the transfer of structured physical understanding across different object sets.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer Mechanisms:**
To bridge the sim-to-real gap, the approach employs:
- **System Identification:** Joint-level dynamics of the real robot hand are identified and matched in simulation by adjusting simulator PD gains.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive randomization is applied during simulation training, including object mass, friction coefficients, PD gains, observation and action noise, initial reset poses, external forces, and gravity direction, to enhance robustness to real-world uncertainties.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WM-Craftnet focused on four key areas: multi-object rotation and sim-to-real transfer, WSM mechanism analysis, performance on difficult rotation modes, and robustness under extreme conditions.

**Q1. Multi-object Rotation and Sim-to-Real:**
- **Z-axis Multi-object Rotation (Simulation):** On a nine-object set for z-axis rotation, WM-Craftnet with a full, pretrained WSM achieved the highest average return of 753.3 and a rotation rate (RotR) of 1.293, significantly outperforming all baselines. For comparison, the next best baseline (Touch Dexterity) achieved a return of 386.9 and RotR of 1.018. WM-Craftnet also demonstrated reduced off-axis motion (OffAxis 1.225) and angular velocity variation (AngVar 1.324). Ablations confirmed that tactile cues contribute to the learned state, with performance gradually decreasing as tactile input availability was reduced.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (Real Hardware):** WM-Craftnet showed strong transfer capabilities on a real 22-DoF hand for z-axis rotation. Across three seen objects (duck, cross block, corner block) and one unseen object (double-notched block), WM-Craftnet achieved high total rotation (e.g., 16.179 radians for duck) and high success rates (10/10 for seen, 8/10 for unseen). In contrast, baselines (Open-loop Replay, Touch Dexterity, In-Hand Rotation) and even "In-Hand Rotation + WSM-denoised depth" exhibited substantially lower rotation and success rates, particularly for the unseen object. This result indicates the value of both geometric denoising and the multimodal, temporally informed context provided by WSM.

**Q2. WSM Mechanism and Prior Analysis:**
- **Clean-Depth Supervision and Recurrence:** Comparative studies showed that reconstructing *clean* depth targets from noisy inputs (return 753.3) was more effective than reconstructing noisy depth (return 708.0). Similarly, conditioning the policy on the *recurrent* deterministic feature ($h_t$) yielded higher returns (753.3) compared to a per-step encoder-decoder feature (return 667.6), underscoring the importance of temporal memory.
- **Predictive vs. Recurrent Memory:** Full WSM, incorporating predictive multimodal training objectives, achieved a return of 753.3 and RotR of 1.293. This performance surpassed that of standard recurrent networks like LSTM (return 621.0, RotR 1.186) and GRU (return 497.8, RotR 1.095) that lacked the predictive losses, demonstrating that the predictive objective beyond mere recurrent memory is beneficial.
- **Auxiliary Head Contributions:** The inclusion of auxiliary heads in WSM training improved overall performance. The full WSM with all auxiliary heads achieved a return of 753.3, whereas without any auxiliary heads, the return was 688.1. The value head alone yielded the largest return increase among single-head variants, and the pose head contributed to lower angular velocity variation.
- **Latent State Diagnostics:** t-SNE visualizations of the WSM's recurrent states ($h_t$) revealed partially object-dependent regions, significant overlap across objects, and clear structuring by interaction phases. This suggests the learned representation captures both object-specific geometry and generalizable interaction dynamics.
- **Depth Reconstruction Quality:** WSM demonstrated the ability to reconstruct cleaner depth images from noisy inputs, effectively suppressing sensor artifacts while preserving essential hand-object geometry.
- **Reusable Synesthetic Prior:** Pretraining WSM on a 9-object z-axis task and reusing it as a prior for a 49-object downstream task significantly enhanced learning. The downstream policy achieved an average rotation of 9.37 radians per episode (vs. 3.28 for no-prior baseline), and the training fall rate decreased from 6% to 0.3%.

**Q3. More Difficult Rotation Modes:**
- **Y-axis Rotation (Tool-like Objects):** On nine elongated, tool-like objects requiring y-axis rotation (more challenging due to asymmetric contacts and larger moment arms), WM-Craftnet achieved a return of 272.7 and RotR of 1.025, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Blind RL Policy: return 177.8, RotR 0.545). It also exhibited the lowest fall rate.
- **X-axis Rotation (Strict Contact Sequencing):** For x-axis rotation (most difficult due to limited fingertip workspace and gravity), WM-Craftnet achieved a return of 432.8, an episode length of 435.4, the highest RotR (1.114), and the lowest fall rate. Baselines showed significantly lower performance, struggling to maintain grasp and progress rotation.

**Q4. Robustness under Extreme Conditions:**
- **Unseen-Object Zero-shot Transfer (Simulation):** WM-Craftnet consistently achieved the highest RotR and return across all three axes (x, y, z) when evaluated on held-out, unseen objects. For instance, on y-axis, it showed an 89.2% RotR gain over the strongest non-WSM baseline.
- **Perturbation-Recovery Test (Simulation):** Under severe simulated perturbations (difficult starts and external forces), WM-Craftnet demonstrated a recovery success rate of 14.1%, compared to 6.2% for the strongest baseline, and achieved the shortest mean recovery time of 2.56 seconds. Qualitative analysis showed the policy re-centering and stabilizing objects before resuming rotation.
- **Large-Scale Real-World Evaluation:** In a comprehensive hardware evaluation involving z-axis rotation on 20 real objects (9 seen, 11 unseen), WM-Craftnet succeeded in 175 out of 200 trials (87.5% success rate), significantly outperforming baselines which ranged from 33/200 to 53/200 successes.
- **Tool Use Behavior:** The framework also demonstrated its capacity for screwdriver translation (goal-conditioned adjustment to target positions) and rotation, indicating applicability beyond simple object reorientation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WM-Craftnet framework presents several advancements in the field of dexterous in-hand manipulation, with potential implications for robotic capabilities.

Firstly, the work addresses the challenge of **generalizability and robustness** in contact-rich manipulation. By learning a World Synesthesia Model (WSM) that integrates multimodal observations (proprioception, depth, tactile sensing, and actions) into a compact, predictive latent state, the system demonstrates an enhanced ability to cope with variations in object pose, geometry, external disturbances, and unseen objects. This signifies a move beyond policies that overfit to specific training conditions, enabling more adaptive and versatile manipulation.

Secondly, the methodology for **denoising geometric state from noisy inputs** is a notable contribution to sim-to-real transfer. The WSM's training objective to reconstruct clean depth targets from augmented, noisy depth inputs directly addresses a significant practical challenge in deploying learning-based policies on physical robots. This capability is shown to be crucial for successful real-robot operation, where sensor noise is prevalent.

Thirdly, the concept of a **reusable synesthetic prior** offers a pathway towards more scalable robot learning. The demonstration that a WSM pre-trained on a smaller set of objects can serve as an effective prior for downstream learning on a significantly larger and more diverse object set suggests that fundamental physical reasoning about object-hand interactions can be efficiently transferred. This could reduce the data and computational resources required to teach robots new manipulation skills across a wide range of objects.

Fourthly, the research extends robust manipulation capabilities to **more challenging rotation modes and unforeseen conditions**. The successful application to y-axis and x-axis rotations, which demand more complex contact re-arrangements and less stable support, indicates the framework's versatility. Furthermore, the quantitative and qualitative evidence for unseen-object transfer and perturbation recovery positions WM-Craftnet as a more resilient manipulation system. The strong performance in large-scale real-world evaluation further supports its practical applicability.

The potential impact of this work includes enabling robots to perform more dexterous and human-like manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, such as complex assembly, handling a wide variety of household items, or precise tool operation. By providing a policy that can infer critical object states and adapt to dynamic physical interactions, WM-Craftnet contributes to the development of autonomous robotic systems capable of performing intricate tasks with greater reliability and less human intervention.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the short-horizon nature of current tasks and limited recovery from severe perturbations, the advancements presented lay a foundation for future research exploring broader task distributions and more complex forms of tool use, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in dexterous robot manipulation.
