# SeeTraceAct: Visibility-Aware Latent Planning from Cross-Embodiment Demonstration Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> SEETRACEACT, a VLA framework, enhances robot skill learning from one-shot cross-embodiment demonstration videos by incorporating visibility-aware latent planning. The method improves precision on fine-grained manipulation tasks, achieving up to 4.4 percentage points higher success rates in simulation and a 12.5 percentage point increase in real-world average success over baselines, alongside introducing a new benchmark for reproducible research.

## Problem

- General-purpose Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot control are bottlenecked by the extensive and costly need for task- and embodiment-specific teleoperation data.
- Existing end-to-end demo-conditioned VLAs often struggle with precision-sensitive tasks that require accurate localization of small interaction regions or objects.
- There is a lack of standardized, publicly available benchmarks for evaluating demo-conditioned VLA policies, particularly for cross-embodiment scenarios, hindering reproducible research.

## Method

- SEETRACEACT integrates visibility-aware ==latent== planning into a ==diffusion==-based VLA architecture, leveraging a pre-trained video encoder and Perceiver Resampler to process demonstration videos.
- It introduces a 'visual latent plan' derived from learnable query tokens within the VLM, which is supervised by an auxiliary visibility-aware trace decoder during training.
- The trace decoder uses dual heads (regression and validity) to predict future end-effector 2D image coordinates and their visibility, effectively handling instances where the end-effector moves off-screen.

## Results

- SEETRACEACT achieved superior performance on the ROBOCASA-DC benchmark, with a 4.4 percentage point gain over the strongest baseline for precision-sensitive cross-embodiment tasks (**12.8%** vs. **8.4%**).
- On a real-world tabletop manipulation benchmark, it improved the average success rate from **37.5%** (baseline) to **50.0%** (SEETRACEACT) for unseen tasks conditioned on human demonstrations.
- A strong negative Spearman rank correlation (ρ = -0.63 against the strongest baseline) was found between the Target Interaction Ratio (TIR) and SEETRACEACT's performance gain, confirming its specific benefit for tasks requiring high precision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervising latent plans with visibility-aware 2D end-effector traces significantly enhances spatial grounding for precision-sensitive tasks and improves cross-embodiment transfer.
> - Explicitly accounting for partial visibility of end-effector trace points (e.g., when off-screen) via a dedicated validity head is crucial for effective supervision and robust representation learning.
> - Using an action-aware video encoder for demonstrations yields better performance compared to general-purpose encoders, suggesting the importance of task-relevant video representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a significant challenge within the field of robotic learning, specifically concerning the adaptation and scalability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for general-purpose robot control. VLAs have shown promise in enabling robots to understand and execute tasks based on visual and linguistic inputs by leveraging large pre-trained vision-language models. However, a primary bottleneck for their widespread deployment is the extensive need for task- and embodiment-specific teleoperation data for post-training. Collecting such data is resource-intensive, requiring specialized hardware and considerable human effort, thereby limiting the ability to scale VLAs across diverse tasks, robot embodiments, and environments.

This research focuses on an alternative approach: one-shot demo-conditioned VLAs. In this paradigm, a robot policy learns to execute an unseen task by observing a single demonstration video. This approach offers a potentially more scalable alternative to teleoperation. A key challenge in demo-conditioned learning, especially when demonstrations come from humans, is the "cross-embodiment gap," which makes direct translation of human motions to robot actions difficult.

Prior research in this area has explored various strategies to bridge this gap. Some methods, such as those employing kinematic retargeting (e.g., Demodiffusion, Okami, Ditto), aim to explicitly map human kinematics to robot kinematics. However, these often demand considerable embodiment- and task-specific engineering expertise, which may not be feasible for end-users. Consequently, there is growing interest in end-to-end approaches that directly condition robot policies on demonstrations without explicit retargeting. Examples include XSkill, UniSkill, Vid2Robot, and ViVLA, which learn video representations and utilize auxiliary objectives for demonstration grounding. While these end-to-end methods have demonstrated progress, the authors identify a common limitation: their struggle with precision-sensitive tasks that require accurately localizing small interaction regions, such as pressing a specific button or manipulating small objects.

SEETRACEACT positions itself within this landscape by building upon existing end-to-end VLA architectures (specifically GR00T N1.5) and addressing the identified limitation of imprecise spatial grounding. It introduces a mechanism, visibility-aware trace prediction, to enhance the robot's ability to precisely localize target regions and movements derived from demonstrations, thereby improving performance on precision-sensitive tasks and in cross-embodiment scenarios. The work also contributes a new benchmark, ROBOCASA-DC, to facilitate reproducible evaluation of demo-conditioned policies, encompassing both same- and cross-embodiment demonstrations, which is a gap in existing simulated benchmarks. The incorporation of visual traces in VLAs has been explored in other contexts (e.g., LLARVA, MolmoAct for autoregressive prediction, RT-Trajectory for sketches, TraceVLA for past traces, ThinkAct/FastThinkAct for latent plans), but SEETRACEACT specifically leverages visibility-aware trace prediction to ground one-shot demonstrations spatially, especially under partial visibility, which differentiates its approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to:

1. **Enhance Spatial Grounding in One-Shot Demo-Conditioned VLAs**: The core objective was to develop a framework that enables robots to perform precision-sensitive tasks by improving the spatial grounding of information extracted from one-shot demonstration videos. This directly addresses the observed limitation of existing end-to-end demo-conditioned VLAs, which struggle when successful task execution requires precise interaction with small target regions.

2. **Develop a Robust Latent Planning Mechanism for Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: The work aimed to create a visual latent plan that can summarize task-relevant motion from demonstrations, including those from a different embodiment (e.g., human demonstrations for a robot arm). This plan should then inform the robot's actions, effectively bridging the cross-embodiment gap without requiring explicit kinematic retargeting.

3. **Introduce Visibility-Aware Trace Prediction**: A specific objective was to design an auxiliary objective that supervises the prediction of future end-effector traces in the robot's camera views while explicitly accounting for partial visibility. This was motivated by the challenge of ill-posed regression targets when the end effector moves out of view in multi-camera setups, aiming to maintain effective supervision signal even under such conditions.

4. **Create a Reproducible Benchmark for Demo-Conditioned Robotic Learning**: A significant objective was to provide a standardized platform for evaluating demo-conditioned VLA policies. This motivated the development and release of ROBOCASA-DC, an extension of an existing simulation environment, to support both same-embodiment and cross-embodiment demonstration scenarios for reproducible research and fair comparison.

The motivations behind these objectives are rooted in the limitations of current robotic learning paradigms:

- **Costly Data Collection**: The dependence of general-purpose VLAs on large amounts of task-specific teleoperated robot data is a major impediment to their scalability and widespread adoption. Enabling learning from one-shot demonstrations, especially readily available human videos, offers a more practical and accessible alternative.
- **Precision Challenge in End-to-End Learning**: While end-to-end demo-conditioned methods avoid complex retargeting, they have not consistently achieved the precision required for fine-grained manipulation tasks. This suggests a need for mechanisms that explicitly encourage or constrain the policy to learn precise spatial relationships and movements from demonstrations.
- **Need for Robust Spatial Representation**: Accurately understanding *where* the robot needs to act in its visual field is crucial. Directly supervising predicted end-effector traces provides a strong signal for spatial grounding. The issue of objects or end-effectors moving out of frame is a practical challenge in multi-view robot perception, motivating the "visibility-aware" aspect.
- **Lack of Standardized Cross-Embodiment Benchmarks**: Prior evaluations often relied on real-world setups (difficult to reproduce), single-arm simulation demonstrations, or privately held human demonstration datasets. A public, episode-paired benchmark like ROBOCASA-DC is necessary to foster reproducible research and accelerate progress in cross-embodiment demo-conditioned learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEETRACEACT is a demo-conditioned VLA framework that integrates visibility-aware latent planning into a diffusion-based action generation architecture. The approach can be broken down into several components:

1. **Base Architecture**: The framework is built upon the GR00T N1.5 architecture, which combines a vision-language model (VLM) for processing observations and instructions, and a flow-matching-based action expert for continuous, high-frequency control.

2. **Demonstration Video Integration**:
 - **Video Encoding**: A pre-trained video encoder (V-JEPA 2) is used to process the demonstration video. This encoder is pre-trained on large-scale video data, including action-centric datasets.
 - **Feature Compression**: To manage the high dimensionality of video features, a Perceiver Resampler compresses the extracted features into a compact set of 32 "video tokens."
 - **VLM Input**: These video tokens are appended to the VLM's input sequence, alongside image tokens from current camera views and language instruction tokens. This causal attention setup allows video tokens to attend to image and language context.

3. **Visual Latent Plan**:
 - To specifically encode future task progression and condition action generation, SEETRACEACT introduces a separate set of "learnable query tokens." These tokens are appended to the VLM input after image, language, and video tokens.
 - The final hidden states corresponding to these query tokens form the "visual latent plan" (`z_t`). This plan acts as a compressed, context-dependent representation of the task derived from the demonstration.

4. **Visibility-Aware Trace Decoder (Training Only)**:
 - The visual latent plan (`z_t`) is decoded into future end-effector traces for each static camera view.
 - **Dual-Head Design**: To address the challenge of end-effector points moving off-screen (making 2D coordinates ill-posed regression targets), the trace decoder uses two heads:
 - **Regression Head**: Predicts the normalized 2D image coordinates of `N` future end-effector trace points.
 - **Validity Head**: Predicts a binary score indicating whether each trace point is visible (i.e., lies within the camera view).
 - **Inference**: This trace decoder component is explicitly *discarded* during inference, meaning the robot directly generates actions from the visual latent plan without needing to predict traces.

5. **Training Objectives**:
 - **Action Prediction Loss (`L_act`)**: This is the primary loss for robot control, implemented as a flow-matching objective. The action expert is trained to predict the target velocity field for an action chunk (a sequence of future actions) based on the VLM outputs (`ϕ_t`, `ψ_t`, `z_t`), current robot state (`q_t`), and a noised version of the ground-truth action chunk.
 - **Auxiliary Visual Trace Loss (`L_trace`)**: This loss supervises the visual latent plan `z_t` to encode future end-effector movements in the robot's image space. It consists of two parts:
 - **Masked Regression Loss (`L_reg`)**: An L1 loss applied only to visible trace points (masked by the ground-truth validity target `m_t+nΔ`), supervising the regression head's output.
 - **Validity Loss (`L_valid`)**: A binary cross-entropy loss applied to all `N` trace points, supervising the validity head's prediction against the ground-truth visibility `m_t+nΔ`.
 - **Overall Training Objective**: The total loss is a weighted sum: `L_total = L_act + λ_trace * L_trace`, where `λ_trace` controls the influence of the trace supervision, and `λ_valid` weights the validity loss within `L_trace`.

6. **Inference Procedure**:
 - At each control timestep, the VLM processes current camera views, language instruction, and the demonstration video to generate `ϕ_t`, `ψ_t`, and the visual latent plan `z_t`.
 - The action expert then uses these representations along with the current robot state `q_t` to predict an action chunk. This is done through an iterative denoising process (Euler integration over `K` steps), starting from random noise and progressively refining the action estimate.

7. **ROBOCASA-DC Benchmark**:
 - **Environment Extension**: ROBOCASA-DC extends the RoboCasa simulation environment by pairing standard Panda-arm robot trajectories with corresponding demonstration videos.
 - **Demonstration Types**: It includes both same-embodiment (Panda-arm videos) and cross-embodiment (GR-1 humanoid videos, collected via teleoperation in matched scenes as a proxy for human demos) demonstrations.
 - **Dataset Construction**: For training, 100 paired trajectories per task across 24 tasks are provided. For evaluation, 50 pre-defined unseen evaluation seeds per task, each with a corresponding demonstration, are used.
 - **Evaluation Protocol**: Policies are trained on a subset of "seen" tasks and evaluated on "unseen" tasks. At test time, a policy is conditioned on a single demonstration video (same- or cross-embodiment) for an unseen task, assessing its ability to generalize.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SEETRACEACT was conducted on both the newly introduced ROBOCASA-DC simulation benchmark and a real-world tabletop manipulation benchmark.

1. **Performance on ROBOCASA-DC**:
 - SEETRACEACT demonstrated superior performance across all four evaluation settings compared to competitive demo-conditioned VLA baselines (Vid2Robot, UniSkill, ViVLA).
 - **Category-balanced split**: In this split, SEETRACEACT achieved 23.0% and 11.6% average success rates for same-embodiment and cross-embodiment demonstrations, respectively, outperforming the next best baseline by 1.5 and 0.4 percentage points.
 - **Precision-sensitive split**: This split, designed to stress fine-grained spatial grounding, showed a more pronounced advantage for SEETRACEACT. It achieved 14.1% and 12.8% success rates for same-embodiment and cross-embodiment demonstrations, respectively. In the cross-embodiment setting, this represented a 4.4 percentage point improvement over the strongest baseline (ViVLA at 8.4%).
 - The results indicated that SEETRACEACT maintained its advantage even under harder conditions that require both precise spatial grounding and robust cross-embodiment transfer.

2. **Performance on Real-World Benchmark**:
 - On a real-world benchmark involving a Franka Panda arm conditioned on human demonstrations, SEETRACEACT also achieved the strongest performance across all four unseen tasks.
 - It improved the average success rate from 37.5% (achieved by the strongest baseline) to 50.0%, representing a 12.5 percentage point increase. This indicates that the benefits observed in simulation extend to real-world cross-embodiment demo conditioning.

3. **Enhanced Performance on Precision-Sensitive Tasks**:
 - A more direct analysis, training all models on all 24 ROBOCASA-DC tasks and evaluating per-task success rates, revealed a correlation between task precision sensitivity and SEETRACEACT's performance gains.
 - The Target Interaction Ratio (TIR), which measures the fraction of the image covered by the task-critical interaction region (smaller TIR means more precision-sensitive), was found to be negatively correlated with SEETRACEACT's improvement over baselines.
 - Specifically, there was a strong negative Spearman rank correlation between TIR and the gain over Vid2Robot (ρ = -0.80, p < 10^-5), UniSkill (ρ = -0.45, p < 0.05), and ViVLA (ρ = -0.52, p < 0.01). The correlation remained significant even when compared against the strongest baseline for each task (ρ = -0.63, p < 10^-3). These findings support the hypothesis that visibility-aware trace supervision is particularly beneficial for tasks requiring accurate localization of small interaction regions.

4. **Ablation Study**:
 - The ablation study, conducted in the cross-embodiment setting of ROBOCASA-DC, provided insights into the importance of SEETRACEACT's design choices:
 - **Trace Supervision**: Removing the auxiliary visual trace supervision significantly reduced performance (9.4% vs. 12.2% for full SEETRACEACT), demonstrating its crucial role in grounding demonstrations. This was only marginally better than the GR00T N1.5 backbone without any demonstrations (9.0%).
 - **Validity Head**: The largest performance degradation occurred when the validity head was removed (8.2%), underscoring the importance of visibility-aware supervision. Forcing the model to regress ill-defined off-screen coordinates was detrimental to representation learning.
 - **Action-aware Video Encoder**: Replacing the V-JEPA 2 video encoder with the backbone's SigLIP encoder also lowered performance (9.2%), suggesting that an action-aware video representation contributes to extracting task progression from demonstrations.
 - **2D vs. 3D Trace Supervision**: Using 3D trace supervision, while outperforming some other ablations (10.4%), still lagged behind the 2D image-space trace supervision (12.2%). This suggests that 2D traces provide a more suitable training signal, being directly aligned with the model's visual inputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic learning by addressing critical limitations in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and offering a more scalable approach to robot skill acquisition.

1. **Reduced Data Dependency**: SEETRACEACT offers a method for robots to learn new tasks from single, one-shot demonstration videos, including those from human demonstrators (cross-embodiment). This directly mitigates the bottleneck of requiring extensive, costly, and specialized robot teleoperation data, thereby making the deployment of general-purpose robot policies more practical and accessible.

2. **Improved Precision in Manipulation**: The introduction of visibility-aware latent planning and auxiliary trace prediction specifically enhances the robot's ability to achieve precise spatial grounding. This is particularly significant for precision-sensitive tasks that have proven challenging for prior end-to-end demo-conditioned approaches. The correlation between performance gains and task precision sensitivity (measured by TIR) suggests a clear pathway for developing more dexterous and capable robotic systems.

3. **Facilitation of Reproducible Research**: The release of ROBOCASA-DC provides a standardized and publicly available benchmark for evaluating demo-conditioned policies in simulation. This benchmark supports both same-embodiment and cross-embodiment scenarios, which is crucial for fostering fair comparisons, accelerating research, and building a cumulative body of knowledge in this area. It addresses a previous gap where human demonstration datasets for simulation were often not publicly released or varied widely.

4. **Advancement in Cross-Embodiment Learning**: By demonstrating robust performance with cross-embodiment demonstrations in both simulation and real-world settings, SEETRACEACT advances the feasibility of transferring skills from human activities to robotic platforms without complex kinematic retargeting. This brings closer the vision of robots learning directly from human examples observed in everyday life.

5. **Insights into Visual Planning**: The ablation study highlights the specific importance of visibility-aware trace supervision and the choice of video encoder, providing valuable insights for the design of future VLA architectures. The finding that 2D image-space traces are more effective than 3D traces suggests a stronger alignment between the visual learning signal and the robot's perception system.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges certain limitations, such as the evaluation being limited to tabletop single-arm benchmarks in the real world, and the need for future trace labels during training (though methods for acquiring these exist). The reported absolute success rates on ROBOCASA-DC, while outperforming baselines, also indicate that significant room for improvement remains in demo-conditioned robot learning. However, the identified importance of precise spatial grounding serves as a promising direction for future research efforts in this field.
