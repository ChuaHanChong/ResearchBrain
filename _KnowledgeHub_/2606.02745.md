# SeeTraceAct: Visibility-Aware Latent Planning from Cross-Embodiment Demonstration Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> SEETRACEACT, a VLA framework, enhances robot skill learning from one-shot cross-embodiment demonstration videos by incorporating visibility-aware latent planning. The method improves precision on fine-grained manipulation tasks, achieving up to 4.4 percentage points higher success rates in simulation and a 12.5 percentage point increase in real-world average success over baselines, alongside introducing a new benchmark for reproducible research.

## Problem

- General-purpose Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot control are bottlenecked by the extensive and costly need for task- and embodiment-specific teleoperation data.
- Existing end-to-end demo-conditioned VLAs often struggle with precision-sensitive tasks that require accurate localization of small interaction regions or objects.
- There is a lack of standardized, publicly available benchmarks for evaluating demo-conditioned VLA policies, particularly for cross-embodiment scenarios, hindering reproducible research.

## Method

- SEETRACEACT integrates visibility-aware ==latent== planning into a ==diffusion==-based VLA architecture, leveraging a pre-trained video encoder and Perceiver Resampler to process demonstration videos.
- It introduces a 'visual latent plan' derived from learnable query tokens within the VLM, which is supervised by an auxiliary visibility-aware trace decoder during training.
- The trace decoder uses dual heads (regression and validity) to predict future end-effector 2D image coordinates and their visibility, effectively handling instances where the end-effector moves off-screen.

## Results

- SEETRACEACT achieved superior performance on the ROBOCASA-DC benchmark, with a 4.4 percentage point gain over the strongest baseline for precision-sensitive cross-embodiment tasks (**12.8%** vs. **8.4%**).
- On a real-world tabletop manipulation benchmark, it improved the average success rate from **37.5%** (baseline) to **50.0%** (SEETRACEACT) for unseen tasks conditioned on human demonstrations.
- A strong negative Spearman rank correlation (ρ = -0.63 against the strongest baseline) was found between the Target Interaction Ratio (TIR) and SEETRACEACT's performance gain, confirming its specific benefit for tasks requiring high precision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervising latent plans with visibility-aware 2D end-effector traces significantly enhances spatial grounding for precision-sensitive tasks and improves cross-embodiment transfer.
> - Explicitly accounting for partial visibility of end-effector trace points (e.g., when off-screen) via a dedicated validity head is crucial for effective supervision and robust representation learning.
> - Using an action-aware video encoder for demonstrations yields better performance compared to general-purpose encoders, suggesting the importance of task-relevant video representations.
