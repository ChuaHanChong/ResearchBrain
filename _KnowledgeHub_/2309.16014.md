# Graph-level Representation Learning with Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures

> [!summary] Summary
> Graph-JEPA introduces the first Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for self-supervised graph-level representation learning, addressing limitations of existing methods by predicting latent representations of target subgraphs from context subgraphs. The architecture achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on various graph classification and regression benchmarks, demonstrating enhanced expressivity and up to 8x faster pre-training.

## Problem

- Existing contrastive graph self-supervised learning (SSL) methods rely on complex data augmentations and computationally expensive negative sampling.
- Generative graph SSL models often overfit to high-level details and may learn suboptimal representations for diverse downstream tasks, a problem exacerbated by graph data's non-Euclidean nature.
- Adapting Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) to graphs requires defining effective context/target signals and an optimal latent prediction task for capturing hierarchical graph concepts.

## Method

- **Graph-JEPA** partitions input graphs into 1-hop neighborhood-expanded ==subgraphs== using METIS, which are then embedded by a GINE model along with positional embeddings.
- It designates a context subgraph and multiple target subgraphs, processing them with separate Transformer encoders; the target encoder uses Exponential Moving Average (EMA) updates and a stop-gradient mechanism.
- A novel latent prediction objective transforms target subgraph embeddings into 2D hyperbolic coordinates, which a two-layer MLP predicts from the context embedding and target positional encoding.

## Results

- Graph-JEPA achieved state-of-the-art or competitive performance on 5 of 8 TUD graph classification datasets and strong results on ZINC regression, outperforming most existing graph SSL methods and demonstrating high expressivity (**98.77%** accuracy on EXP non-isomorphic graphs).
- Demonstrated significant computational efficiency during pre-training, being up to 2.5 times faster than Graph-MAE and 8 times faster than MVGRL on larger datasets.
- The novel latent prediction objective based on hyperbolic angles proved more effective than Euclidean or Poincaré distance-based objectives, inducing beneficial implicit hierarchical organization in the latent space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - JEPAs provide a robust self-supervised learning paradigm for graphs by predicting in the latent space, avoiding the complexities of negative samples, intricate augmentations, and direct raw data reconstruction.
> - Projecting high-dimensional latent graph embeddings onto 2D hyperbolic coordinates allows for implicitly learning hierarchical relationships between subgraphs without the computational and stability issues of full high-dimensional hyperbolic embeddings.
> - Applying stop-gradient and EMA updates to the target encoder, coupled with a simpler predictor network, is crucial for preventing representation collapse and ensuring stable, meaningful representation learning within the self-distillation framework.
