# RAMBO-RL: Robust Adversarial Model-Based Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Oxford Robotics Institute developed RAMBO-RL, a model-based offline reinforcement learning algorithm that addresses distributional shift and model exploitation by training an adversarial dynamics model. The method casts offline RL as a two-player zero-sum game, leading to state-of-the-art performance on D4RL MuJoCo locomotion benchmarks.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning policies struggle with distributional shift, leading to unreliable value estimates for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions.
- Model-based offline RL algorithms are prone to model exploitation, where policies exploit inaccuracies in the learned dynamics model for OOD state-action pairs.
- Prior model-based methods rely on explicit, often unreliable, uncertainty estimation to penalize OOD actions or lacked practical algorithms for theoretically grounded maximin formulations of offline RL.

## Method

- **RAMBO-RL** formulates offline RL as a ==two-player zero-sum game==, where an agent maximizes its value function and an adversarial environment model minimizes it by modifying its transition dynamics.
- An ==ensemble of neural networks== represents the environment model, which is updated adversarially to reduce the agent's value function while simultaneously being constrained to remain accurate on the observed dataset.
- The algorithm employs an ==alternating optimization scheme==, training the agent's policy using an off-policy actor-critic method with both real and synthetic data, and updating the adversarial model using a derived ==Model Gradient==.

## Results

- **RAMBO-RL** achieved the **highest total score** across D4RL MuJoCo locomotion domains, outperforming both existing state-of-the-art model-based and model-free baselines.
- The method demonstrated robust performance on datasets characterized by noise or suboptimality (e.g., Medium, Medium-Replay dataset types), securing the **best overall scores** in these categories.
- An ablation study confirmed that adversarial training is crucial, as removing adversarial updates resulted in an overall degradation of performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial training of the dynamics model itself can effectively introduce conservatism and mitigate model exploitation in offline RL, without requiring explicit uncertainty estimation.
> - Introducing pessimism gradually through an adversarial model, as RAMBO-RL does, can prevent policies from becoming trapped in poor local optima, outperforming methods that introduce aggressive pessimism early in training.
> - A practical and scalable algorithm can be derived to solve the theoretically-grounded maximin formulation of offline RL, enabling robust policy learning from static datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of offline reinforcement learning (RL), a sub-domain of RL focused on learning policies from static, pre-recorded datasets without further interaction with the environment. Offline RL addresses a significant limitation of traditional online RL, which requires extensive and often costly or dangerous exploration.

The broader landscape of offline RL is typically divided into two main categories: model-free and model-based approaches. Model-free methods train a policy directly from the fixed dataset, incorporating conservatism either into the value function or by directly constraining the policy to stay close to the data distribution. Examples include conservative Q-learning (CQL) and implicit Q-learning (IQL).

Model-based offline RL algorithms, conversely, learn an environment model from the dataset and then use this model to generate synthetic data for policy training. This approach offers the potential for broader generalization and adaptation to new tasks, but it faces the challenge of "model exploitation" – where policies may exploit inaccuracies in the learned model for out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs, leading to poor performance in the real environment. Previous model-based methods have attempted to address this by estimating model uncertainty and applying reward penalties for high-uncertainty regions (e.g., MOPO, MOReL). However, uncertainty estimation in neural networks can be unreliable. More recent approaches, like COMBO, have adopted model-free techniques to regularize the value function for OOD samples without explicit uncertainty estimation.

RAMBO-RL positions itself within the model-based offline RL paradigm, specifically as an approach that does not rely on explicit uncertainty estimation. It extends the concept of Robust Adversarial RL (RARL), which typically involves an adversarial policy perturbing the environment in online settings, to the offline domain. Unlike prior RARL work, RAMBO-RL proposes to make the environment model itself the adversary, modifying its transition dynamics rather than requiring a separate adversary policy. This work directly addresses a maximin formulation of offline RL previously only theoretically motivated, offering a practical algorithm for its solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a novel and robust model-based offline reinforcement learning algorithm that effectively addresses the problem of distributional shift and model exploitation without relying on explicit uncertainty estimation.

The key motivations behind RAMBO-RL are:

- **Addressing Distributional Shift in Offline RL:** Offline RL algorithms struggle when the policy attempts actions or visits states not adequately represented in the training dataset, leading to inaccurate value estimates and poor real-world performance. A core motivation is to introduce "conservatism" to prevent the policy from venturing into unreliably modeled regions.
- **Mitigating Model Exploitation:** In model-based offline RL, if the learned environment model is inaccurate in areas not covered by the dataset, a policy optimized within this model might exploit these inaccuracies, leading to high simulated performance but low real-world performance. The goal is to make the model inherently "pessimistic" about OOD regions.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Uncertainty Estimation:** Prior model-based offline RL methods often rely on estimating the uncertainty of the learned dynamics model to penalize OOD actions. However, neural network-based uncertainty estimates can be unreliable. A motivation for RAMBO is to introduce conservatism without this explicit uncertainty quantification.
- **Providing a Practical Algorithm for a Theoretically Grounded Problem:** The paper references a recent theoretical work that introduced a maximin formulation of offline RL with Probable Approximately Correct (PAC) guarantees and a pessimistic value function lower bound. A significant motivation for RAMBO-RL is to propose a practical, scalable algorithm to solve this theoretically well-founded problem, which was previously unaddressed algorithmically.
- **Adapting Robust Adversarial RL (RARL) to Offline Settings:** RARL has been used in online settings to improve policy robustness against environmental perturbations. The authors are motivated to adapt this adversarial training paradigm to the offline model-based context, but in a way that is specific to the limitations of offline data—by having the model itself act as the adversary, rather than learning a separate adversarial policy. This reinterpretation aims to simplify the adversarial setup for offline RL.

In summary, the work aims to create a robust offline RL agent by training an adversarial dynamics model that actively seeks to minimize the agent's value, while remaining accurate on observed data, thereby forcing the policy to be conservative in poorly understood regions of the state-action space.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RAMBO-RL formulates the offline RL problem as a two-player zero-sum game, where the agent aims to maximize its value function, and an adversarial environment model aims to minimize it. The approach alternates between optimizing the agent's policy and adversarially optimizing the environment model.

The core components of the methodology are:

- **Problem Formulation (Maximin Objective):** RAMBO-RL addresses Problem 1 from the theoretical work, defined as:
 $ \pi = \arg \max_{\pi \in \Pi} \min_{\hat{T} \in \mathcal{M}_D} V^{\pi}_{\hat{T}} $
 where $\mathcal{M}_D$ is a set of MDPs whose transition dynamics $\hat{T}$ are constrained to be similar to the Maximum Likelihood Estimate (MLE) $\hat{T}_{\text{MLE}}$ within the dataset $D$, specifically $E_D [TV(\hat{T}_{\text{MLE}}(\cdot|s,a), \hat{T}(\cdot|s,a))^2] \le \xi$. This constraint ensures that the adversarial model remains plausible on observed data, while allowing it to be pessimistic outside the data distribution.

- **Model Representation:** The environment dynamics model $\hat{T}_{\phi}$ (which includes both state transitions and rewards) is represented by an ensemble of neural networks. Each network outputs a Gaussian distribution over the next state and reward given the current state and action. Initially, these models are pre-trained using standard MLE on the dataset $D$.

- **Alternating Optimization:** The algorithm follows an iterative process akin to Robust Adversarial RL:
 1. **Agent Update:** The agent's policy ($\pi$) and value functions ($Q^{\pi}_{\phi}$) are trained using an off-policy actor-critic algorithm (Soft Actor-Critic, SAC, in this implementation). Policy optimization leverages an augmented dataset consisting of real data from $D$ and synthetic data generated from rollouts within the current adversarial model $\hat{T}_{\phi}$.
 2. **Adversarial Model Update:** The dynamics model $\hat{T}_{\phi}$ is updated adversarially to reduce the value function of the agent's current policy. This is done by minimizing a composite loss function $L_{\phi}$:
 $ L_{\phi} = \lambda V^{\pi}_{\phi} - E_{(s,a,r,s') \sim D} [\log \hat{T}_{\phi}(s', r|s,a)] $
 The first term, $\lambda V^{\pi}_{\phi}$, is the adversarial term (with $\lambda$ as a weighting constant), which aims to reduce the value of the policy. The second term is the standard MLE loss, ensuring the model remains accurate on data seen in $D$. The balancing act between these two terms is crucial: a small $\lambda$ ensures the MLE term dominates for in-distribution samples, while the adversarial term dominates for OOD regions.

- **Model Gradient Calculation:** To minimize the adversarial term $\lambda V^{\pi}_{\phi}$, the paper derives a "Model Gradient" (Proposition 2):
 $ \nabla_{\phi} V^{\pi}_{\phi} = E_{s \sim d^{\pi}_{\phi}, a \sim \pi, (s',r) \sim \hat{T}_{\phi}} [(r + \gamma V^{\pi}_{\phi}(s') - Q^{\pi}_{\phi}(s,a)) \cdot \nabla_{\phi} \log \hat{T}_{\phi}(s', r|s,a)] $
 This gradient resembles a policy gradient but applies to the model's parameters, modifying the likelihood of successor states and rewards. The advantage term $(r + \gamma V^{\pi}_{\phi}(s') - Q^{\pi}_{\phi}(s,a))$ quantifies how much a specific transition ($s,a \to s',r$) deviates from the expected value of taking action $a$ in state $s$. The policy's critic is used to estimate $V^{\pi}_{\phi}$ and $Q^{\pi}_{\phi}$. Samples for this gradient are generated by simulating the current policy in the current model.

- **Normalization:** To ensure consistent performance across domains, states in $D$ are normalized, and the advantage terms in the Model Gradient are normalized per minibatch.

- **Synthetic Data Generation:** Similar to Model-Based Policy Optimization (MBPO), synthetic $k$-step rollouts are generated from states in $D$ using the current model $\hat{T}_{\phi}$. This synthetic data is continuously added to a buffer $D_{\hat{T}_{\phi}}$ to train the agent.

The overall algorithm (Algorithm 1) proceeds by pre-training the model with MLE, then iteratively generating synthetic data, updating the agent with SAC using both real and synthetic data, and finally updating the model adversarially using the composite loss and the derived Model Gradient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RAMBO-RL was conducted on the widely used D4RL benchmarks, including MuJoCo locomotion tasks and AntMaze navigation problems. Two evaluation modes were presented: RAMBO (with limited online hyperparameter tuning) and RAMBO$_{OFF}$ (with offline hyperparameter selection via a heuristic).

- **D4RL Performance (MuJoCo):**
 - **Overall Superiority:** RAMBO achieved the highest total score across the MuJoCo locomotion domains, outperforming both existing state-of-the-art model-based (COMBO, MOPO, MOReL, RepB-SDE) and model-free (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC) baselines.
 - **Performance on Diverse Datasets:** RAMBO demonstrated strong performance on datasets characterized by noise or suboptimality (e.g., Medium, Medium-Replay dataset types), securing the best overall scores in these categories. For "Random" datasets, it was competitive but slightly outperformed by MOReL.
 - **Performance on High-Quality Datasets:** RAMBO's performance was observed to be lower than some baselines on "Medium-Expert" datasets, which contain a mix of suboptimal and expert data. The authors suggest that simpler methods like behavior cloning on the top trajectories might be more suitable for these high-quality datasets.

- **D4RL Performance (AntMaze):**
 - Model-based algorithms, including RAMBO, generally performed less effectively than model-free approaches on AntMaze tasks. This aligns with previous observations that model-based methods may struggle in these domains, potentially due to aggressive policies colliding with walls.
 - Despite the general difficulty for model-based approaches, RAMBO yielded positive scores for most AntMaze datasets, unlike several other model-based baselines that scored zero.

- **Offline Hyperparameter Tuning (RAMBO$_{OFF}$):**
 - RAMBO$_{OFF}$, which selected hyperparameters using an offline heuristic based on Q-value magnitude and stability, showed a slight performance degradation compared to RAMBO (online tuning). However, RAMBO$_{OFF}$ still achieved performance comparable to the best existing approaches on MuJoCo, indicating the viability of offline hyperparameter selection for this method.

- **Ablation of Adversarial Training:**
 - An ablation study where the adversarial updates were removed (i.e., $\lambda=0$) demonstrated an overall degradation in performance. This finding supports the hypothesis that adversarial training is crucial for mitigating model exploitation and achieving strong performance in model-based offline RL.
 - Notably, for some specific datasets, performance was optimal without adversarial training, suggesting problem types where regularization might be less critical.

- **Comparison to COMBO (Single Transition Example):**
 - On a custom "Single Transition Example" designed to highlight differences in pessimism introduction, RAMBO consistently outperformed COMBO.
 - Analysis revealed that COMBO introduces pessimism abruptly and can lead to policies becoming stuck in poor local maxima due to aggressively pessimistic Q-value estimates early in training.
 - In contrast, RAMBO introduces pessimism gradually as the adversarial model is trained. This gradual approach resulted in the policy being less prone to local optima, leading to improved overall performance in this specific task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RAMBO-RL represents a significant contribution to the field of offline reinforcement learning by introducing a novel, theoretically-grounded model-based approach that enforces conservatism through adversarial modification of transition dynamics.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The demonstration of RAMBO-RL outperforming current state-of-the-art algorithms on challenging D4RL benchmarks for various data distributions (especially noisy or suboptimal datasets) suggests its effectiveness and practical utility in real-world applications where data quality can be variable.
- **Novel Method for Enforcing Conservatism:** By adapting the Robust Adversarial RL paradigm to train the environment model itself as an adversary, RAMBO-RL offers an alternative to unreliable explicit uncertainty estimation. This novel formulation of adversarial model training provides a robust mechanism to prevent model exploitation for out-of-distribution actions.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The connection of the practical algorithm to a maximin problem formulation with PAC performance guarantees and a pessimistic value function lower bound provides a strong theoretical justification for the approach. This enhances confidence in the algorithm's reliability and its ability to learn conservative policies.
- **Potential for Broader Generalization:** As a model-based method, RAMBO-RL inherently holds the potential for better generalization to new states and tasks compared to purely model-free approaches. The adversarial training specifically aims to make this generalization more robust and less prone to inaccuracies.
- **Insights into Pessimism Introduction:** The comparison with COMBO highlighted that the *gradual* introduction of pessimism in RAMBO, through adversarial dynamics modification, can prevent policies from getting trapped in poor local optima. This insight could inform the design of future offline RL algorithms, suggesting that the timing and mechanism of conservatism might be as important as its mere presence.
- **Future Applications:** The authors propose extending RAMBO to image-space domains using latent variable models, aiding interpretability in deep RL by generating worst-case trajectories, and applying the ideas to online RL for improved robustness. These directions suggest that the core concept of an adversarial dynamics model could have broad applicability beyond the current scope.

Overall, RAMBO-RL advances the state of model-based offline RL by offering a robust, theoretically motivated, and practically effective algorithm that addresses core challenges in the field, with potential implications for safer and more reliable deployment of RL agents in data-constrained environments.
