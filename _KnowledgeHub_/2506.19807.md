# KnowRL: Exploring Knowledgeable Reinforcement Learning for Factuality

> [!summary] Summary
> KnowRL introduces a knowledge-enhanced reinforcement learning framework that mitigates factual hallucination in large language models by integrating process-oriented factual supervision. This approach significantly reduces incorrect response rates, such as a 20.3-point drop on SimpleQA, while preserving and enhancing reasoning abilities on benchmarks like GPQA and AIME by guiding models to perform fact-grounded thinking and recognize knowledge boundaries.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly 'slow-thinking' models with multi-step reasoning, frequently suffer from severe factual hallucination.
- Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches, focused on outcome-oriented rewards, often neglect the factual accuracy of intermediate reasoning steps, potentially reinforcing factually flawed thought processes.
- A 'Model Scaling Dilemma' exists where increasing model size and reasoning ability does not inherently reduce hallucination, indicating a need for targeted factual supervision.

## Method

- **KnowRL** constructs a training dataset from NqOpen, WebQuestions, and ComplexQuestions, filtered and refined using GPT-4o and Wikipedia to ensure verifiable, long ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== data.
- It adapts the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm with a ==composite reward function==, including a ==r_fact== for atomic factual accuracy of intermediate steps, ==r_format== for structural adherence, and ==r_correct== for final answer correctness with a positive incentive for ==refusal when uncertain==.
- A ==factuality-guided policy optimization== maximizes a ==clipped surrogate objective==, which shifts probability mass from hallucination-prone traces to knowledge-supported ones, regularized by ==entropy and KL divergence penalties== for stability.

## Results

- KnowRL achieved a **20.3-point** reduction in the Incorrect Rate on the SimpleQA dataset (from **78.00%** to **57.67%**) for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, with similar improvements on ChineseSimpleQA.
- Reasoning performance on GPQA and AIME 2025 benchmarks was maintained or slightly improved, demonstrating that factual reinforcement does not sacrifice complex reasoning capabilities (e.g., Skywork-OR1-7B-Preview improved GPQA scores from **37.37%** to **42.42%**).
- Training with KnowRL led to an increased 'Refusal Rate' and improved 'atomic fact accuracy' within the model's CoT, indicating the acquisition of knowledge boundary awareness and fact-grounded reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervising the factual accuracy of intermediate reasoning steps directly (process-oriented supervision) is crucial for reducing hallucination without compromising overall reasoning ability.
> - Incorporating a positive reward for models to explicitly refuse to answer when uncertain fosters knowledge boundary awareness, leading to more reliable outputs rather than fabricated ones.
> - The 'atomic fact accuracy' within the model's chain-of-thought increases steadily with training, demonstrating that explicit factual reinforcement grounds the internal reasoning process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is situated within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly those exhibiting "slow thinking" capabilities. Recent advancements, exemplified by models such as DeepSeek-R1, have demonstrated that leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL) to encourage extended Chains of Thought (CoT) can significantly enhance complex reasoning abilities. However, a critical challenge that has emerged with these models is their propensity for hallucination, where generated content, despite exhibiting sophisticated reasoning, lacks factual reliability. Existing literature shows that while scaling reasoning compute boosts problem-solving, it does not inherently align with factual accuracy; in some cases, it may even exacerbate hallucination rates on factuality benchmarks like SimpleQA.

The broader research landscape for LLMs is actively addressing this "reasoning-vs-hallucination" dilemma. Traditional RL approaches for LLMs often rely on outcome-oriented rewards, optimizing for the final answer without scrutinizing the intermediate reasoning steps. This black-box approach is recognized as a fundamental flaw because it can inadvertently reinforce fabricated or unfaithful reasoning paths if they lead to a correct final answer. Consequently, models struggle to recognize their "knowledge boundaries" and may invent information rather than admit uncertainty.

Current hallucination mitigation strategies, such as Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), face limitations when applied to slow-thinking models. RAG can suffer from retrieval efficiency bottlenecks given the extensive reasoning steps, while SFT, primarily relying on static knowledge injection, often leads to catastrophic forgetting and a degradation of inherent reasoning capabilities. More fundamentally, SFT encourages memorization rather than the generalized behavioral strategy of discerning known from unknown facts. Reinforcement Learning, conversely, is considered a suitable paradigm for shaping such dynamic behavioral strategies.

KnowRL positions itself as a response to this urgent need for an RL framework that can instill factual discipline directly into the model’s reasoning behaviors, thereby enabling it to identify knowledge boundaries and reduce hallucinations without compromising its reasoning prowess. It seeks to open the "black box" of reasoning by applying fine-grained, process-oriented factual supervision. This approach contrasts with prior work that often focuses on post-hoc verification or outcome-level alignment, aiming instead for intrinsic factual grounding throughout the generation process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a Reinforcement Learning framework, KnowRL, that effectively mitigates hallucinations in "slow-thinking" Large Language Models (LLMs) while simultaneously preserving or enhancing their complex reasoning capabilities.

The motivation behind KnowRL stems from several critical observations and challenges in the current landscape of advanced LLMs:

- **The Reasoning-Hallucination Paradox:** Recent "slow-thinking" LLMs, which use extended Chains of Thought (CoT) and RL to achieve sophisticated reasoning, exhibit a paradoxical behavior. Despite their high problem-solving abilities on complex tasks, they frequently generate factually incorrect information (hallucinations). This is evidenced by high scores on reasoning benchmarks like GPQA alongside low scores on factuality benchmarks like SimpleQA, indicating that enhanced reasoning compute does not automatically translate to factual reliability. This discrepancy suggests a fundamental misalignment between current training objectives and factual grounding.
- **Limitations of Outcome-Oriented RL:** Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically employs outcome-oriented rewards, which focus solely on the correctness of the final answer. This approach treats the model's internal reasoning process as a "black box." Consequently, if a model arrives at a correct answer through a sequence of fabricated or factually unsupported reasoning steps, the outcome-based reward can inadvertently reinforce these unfaithful pathways. This teaches the model to be "smart" at reasoning but "dishonest" about facts, perpetuating the hallucination problem.
- **Ignorance of Knowledge Boundaries:** A root cause of hallucination is the LLMs' inability to recognize their own "knowledge boundaries"—that is, distinguishing between what they genuinely know and what they do not know. Without explicit mechanisms to identify these boundaries, models tend to fabricate answers to uncertain questions rather than admitting ignorance, driven by the pressure to provide a response to secure a positive reward. This leads to a "snowball effect" where small factual errors in a long reasoning chain can lead to entirely fabricated conclusions.
- **Ineffectiveness of Existing Mitigation Strategies:** Current methods like Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) have demonstrated limitations for slow-thinking models. RAG can be computationally intensive and inefficient for very long reasoning chains. SFT, while injecting knowledge, often leads to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned capabilities and encourages memorization rather than dynamic, generalized factual verification behavior. It fails to instill the critical strategy of "knowing what you know and what you do not know."
- **Need for Process-Oriented Factual Supervision:** The authors identify a gap in current RL frameworks for LLMs: the lack of dense, process-level factual supervision. They argue that guiding the model's behavior at each step of its reasoning process with direct factual feedback is essential to correct erroneous thought pathways and to explicitly teach the model to identify its knowledge boundaries. The motivation is to shift the RL objective from merely "getting the answer right" to "reasoning faithfully" and, crucially, to learn to say "I don't know" when information is missing.

In summary, KnowRL is motivated by the need to develop a more disciplined and self-aware LLM that can perform complex reasoning with high factual reliability by integrating fine-grained factual supervision into the reinforcement learning process itself.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KnowRL is a Knowledge-enhanced Reinforcement Learning framework designed to address LLM hallucinations by integrating factual supervision directly into the reasoning process. The framework builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm and modifies the reward structure to incorporate fine-grained factual checks.

The methodology can be broken down into the following components:

#### 3.1. Data Construction

- **Source Data:** The training data is derived from NqOpen, WebQuestions, and ComplexQuestions, serving as factual question sources.
- **Filtering and De-duplication:** Initial filtering removes trivial queries, followed by semantic de-duplication to ensure data diversity.
- **Entity Extraction:** GPT-4o is used to perform entity extraction and quality refinement on the remaining data. Detailed prompts for this process are provided in the appendix.
- **Knowledge Matching:** To overcome limitations of incomplete knowledge coverage in existing datasets, extracted entities are explicitly matched against a comprehensive Wikipedia dump (20231101.en) to retrieve relevant factual contexts for each question.
- **Length Constraints:** Finally, length constraints are applied to screen samples, ensuring a robust dataset for stable training.

#### 3.2. KnowRL Training Framework Overview

As depicted in Figure 3, the training process is iterative:
1. A prompt is sampled.
2. The current policy generates a group of candidate rollouts (reasoning traces and answers).
3. These rollouts undergo format checks and detailed factual verification against an external knowledge base.
4. A composite reward is calculated for each rollout.
5. Advantages are computed based on these rewards.
6. The policy is optimized using a surrogate loss and regularized loss.
7. This process is repeated to update the model parameters.

#### 3.3. Reward Function Design

For a given rollout `o = (o_think, o_answer)` generated by the model, the composite reward `R_total(o)` consists of three components:

- **Factuality Reward (`r_fact(o)`):**
 - Inspired by FactScore, GPT-4o-mini is employed to decompose the reasoning trace `o_think` into `M` atomic facts, denoted as `Φ(o_think) = {f_1..., f_M}`.
 - Each atomic fact `f_j` is then verified against its most relevant knowledge set `K_x` from the external knowledge base. `K_x` is retrieved using `sentence-transformers/gtr-t5-large`.
 - A verification score `v(f_j, K_x) ∈ {0, 1}` is assigned (1 if supported, 0 otherwise).
 - `r_fact(o)` is calculated as the proportion of supported facts: `(1/M) * Σ v(f_j, K_x)` if `M > 0`, and `0` if `M = 0`. This dense reward provides step-level factual supervision.

- **Format Reward (`r_format(o)`):**
 - A binary reward that checks if the model's output adheres to the required `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>` structure.
 - `+1` for a valid format, `-1` otherwise.

- **Correct Reward (`r_correct(o)`):**
 - Evaluates the final answer `o_answer` using GPT-4o-mini as an evaluator model.
 - `+2` if the answer is correct.
 - `+1` if the model explicitly refuses to answer (e.g., states "I don't know"). This positively incentivizes recognition of knowledge boundaries.
 - `-1` if the answer is incorrect.

The **composite reward** `R_total(o)` is the sum of these three components: `R_total(o) = r_format(o) + r_correct(o) + r_fact(o)`.

#### 3.4. Factuality-Guided Policy Optimization

KnowRL adapts the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm for policy updates.
- **Rollout Generation:** For each prompt `x`, the current policy `π_θ_old` generates `G` candidate rollouts `{{o^(g)}}_G=1`.
- **Advantage Construction:** The `R_total` scores for these rollouts, `R_x = {R_g}`, are summarized by their sample mean `μ_x` and standard deviation `σ_x`. The credit assigned to each trajectory `g` is a group-relative advantage `A_g = (R_g - μ_x) / (σ_x + ε)`, where `ε` prevents division by zero. A positive `A_g` indicates an above-average factual reward, providing a signed learning signal.
- **Surrogate Objective:** A PPO-like clipped surrogate objective `J_hat(θ)` is used to maximize the product of the importance ratio (`ϱ_g = π_θ(o^(g)|x) / π_θ_old(o^(g)|x)`) and the advantage `A_g`. This objective explicitly transfers probability mass from hallucination-heavy traces (negative `A_g`) to knowledge-supported traces (positive `A_g`).
- **Regularized Loss:** To balance optimization with exploration and prevent divergence from a reference policy `π_ref`, an entropy bonus (`β_H * E_H`) and a KL anchor (`β_KL * E_KL`) are added to the negative surrogate objective. The final loss `L_KnowRL = -J_hat(θ) + β_H * E_H + β_KL * E_KL` is minimized.
- **Training Loop:** Each gradient step involves sampling prompts, generating rollouts, performing knowledge checks to yield composite rewards, converting rewards into group-relative advantages, and updating the policy by maximizing the factual surrogate objective while minimizing the regularized loss. This process ensures that gradients increase the probability of knowledge-supported reasoning and decrease that of hallucination-prone trajectories.

#### 3.5. Experimental Settings

- **Models:** Skywork-OR1-7B-Preview (RL-trained) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (distillation-trained). Scalability evaluated on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B.
- **Baselines:** Self-Refine (prompt engineering), SFT, DPO, FactTune-FS, TruthRL (post-training methods).
- **Training:** All models fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with specific hyperparameters (e.g., `lora_rank=128`, `learning_rate=1.0e-5`).
- **Evaluation Datasets:**
 - **Hallucination:** TruthfulQA (ROUGE, BLEU), SimpleQA, ChineseSimpleQA (Incorrect Rate, Refusal Rate, Precision on Answered Questions (PAQ), F1 score). Subsets of 300 examples used for efficiency.
 - **Reasoning:** GPQA, AIME 2025 (accuracy). Extended to OlympiadBench (mathematics, physics).
- **Decoding:** All models evaluated with temperature set to 0 for main results. Multi-run evaluations use temperature 0.6 with 5 responses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of KnowRL demonstrates its efficacy in mitigating hallucinations and enhancing factual reliability in slow-thinking LLMs, largely without compromising complex reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Hallucination Mitigation

- **Significant Reduction in Incorrect Rate:** KnowRL consistently and substantially reduces the Incorrect Rate on hallucination benchmarks. For the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B model, the Incorrect Rate on SimpleQA decreased by 20.3% (from 78.00% to 57.67%). Similar improvements were observed for the Skywork-OR1-7B-Preview model.
- **Increased Refusal Rate:** Concurrently with the reduction in incorrect answers, KnowRL leads to a notable increase in the Refusal Rate across models and datasets. This indicates that the model learns to identify its knowledge boundaries and explicitly states "I don't know" when uncertain, rather than fabricating responses. For instance, the DeepSeek-7B model's refusal rate on SimpleQA increased from 20.33% to 40.67%.
- **Cross-Lingual Transferability:** Despite training primarily on English-based knowledge, KnowRL showed consistent gains on ChineseSimpleQA. This suggests that the framework helps the model internalize a language-agnostic verification behavior, rather than merely memorizing language-specific facts.

#### 4.2. Reasoning Capability Preservation and Enhancement

- **Maintenance of Performance on Complex Tasks:** Unlike some baseline methods that suffer from catastrophic forgetting, KnowRL largely preserves or even enhances performance on complex reasoning benchmarks.
- **Improved GPQA Scores:** For the Skywork-OR1-7B-Preview model, KnowRL improved the GPQA score from 37.37% to 42.42%. For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, AIME 2025 performance remained stable while GPQA score saw marginal changes.
- **Performance on OlympiadBench:** Evaluation on OlympiadBench (mathematics and physics) showed that both DeepSeek-7B and Skywork-7B models maintained or improved their average accuracy after KnowRL training. DeepSeek-7B's average accuracy increased from 7.23% to 7.58%, and Skywork-7B improved from 4.65% to 5.75%.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics and Boundary Awareness

- **Improved Atomic Fact Accuracy:** During training, the accuracy of atomic facts within the model's Chain of Thought steadily increased.
- **Acquired Boundary-Awareness:** The completion length of generated responses dramatically dropped, especially on simple factual questions. This is attributed to the model learning to efficiently truncate wasteful hallucinations for unknown facts, while preserving extended reasoning chains for complex logical tasks. This behavioral shift reflects the model's acquisition of knowledge boundary awareness.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Importance of Factual Reward:** The factual reward (`r_fact`) was found to be critical. Using factual reward alone achieved the best performance on reasoning benchmarks like GPQA and AIME 2025, suggesting that `r_fact` encourages knowledge-grounded reasoning and reduces random errors.
- **Positive Refusal Incentives:** Explicitly giving a positive reward for refusals (`r_correct = +1` for refusal) was crucial for promoting and maintaining boundary awareness. Without this positive incentive (setting `R_refusal = -1`), the incorrect rate increased significantly as the model learned to "guess" to maximize scores rather than accepting refusal penalties.

#### 4.5. Robustness Analysis

- **Across Different RL Algorithms:** KnowRL's effectiveness was found to be robust across various RL algorithms, including BNPO, DAPO, and Dr.GRPO, in addition to the base GRPO. All tested algorithms significantly reduced the Incorrect Rate while maintaining or improving reasoning performance.
- **Scalability to Larger Models:** Experiments on the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B model demonstrated that KnowRL remains effective, consistently reducing hallucinations (e.g., SimpleQA Incorrect Rate from 83.00% to 68.33%) and improving reasoning (GPQA accuracy from 46.97% to 51.01%).
- **Evaluator Sensitivity:** KnowRL maintained robust performance when different evaluator models (GPT-4o-mini vs. Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) were used during training, indicating that its efficacy stems from the framework's design rather than reliance on a specific external judge.

#### 4.6. Multi-run Evaluation

- Multi-run evaluations (Avg@5, temperature 0.6) consistently supported the primary findings. KnowRL significantly improved refusal rates and reduced incorrect answers on hallucination benchmarks, while enhancing or maintaining reasoning performance on complex tasks. For example, AIME accuracy improved from 29.33% to 34.00% for DeepSeek-7B under stochastic decoding.

#### 4.7. Generative Diversity

- Evaluation on NoveltyBench showed only marginal variations in generative diversity after KnowRL training, suggesting that the LoRA-based training allowed the model to learn boundary-aware behaviors without substantially compromising its original generative capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The KnowRL framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the development and deployment of Large Language Models:

- **Addressing a Core LLM Challenge:** KnowRL offers a direct and effective technical pathway to mitigate the pervasive problem of hallucination in "slow-thinking" LLMs. By tackling the critical paradox where advanced reasoning capabilities often correlate with increased factual unreliability, it enhances the trustworthiness and reliability of these powerful models for real-world applications.
- **Paradigm Shift in RL for LLMs:** The work introduces a crucial paradigm shift from outcome-oriented to process-oriented supervision in Reinforcement Learning for LLMs. By providing dense, fine-grained factual feedback at each step of the reasoning process, KnowRL moves beyond treating the LLM as a black box. This approach allows for more targeted correction of reasoning paths and a deeper, intrinsic alignment with factual accuracy.
- **Enhanced Model Self-Awareness:** A key impact is enabling LLMs to recognize their own "knowledge boundaries." By incentivizing explicit refusals to answer questions when information is missing, KnowRL teaches models to be "self-aware" of their factual limitations. This reduces the tendency to fabricate answers, leading to more honest and dependable responses.
- **Improved Reasoning Quality:** Beyond simply reducing hallucinations, KnowRL's process-level supervision grounds reasoning in verifiable knowledge. This can lead to more robust, coherent, and less error-prone complex reasoning, as each step is factually constrained. The preservation and even enhancement of performance on benchmarks like GPQA and OlympiadBench confirm that factual discipline does not necessarily come at the cost of reasoning depth.
- **Generalizable Verification Behavior:** The demonstrated cross-lingual transferability of KnowRL (e.g., improvements on ChineseSimpleQA despite English-based training knowledge) suggests that the model learns a generalized, language-agnostic verification behavior. This implies that the benefits are not limited to memorizing specific facts but rather instill a broader cognitive strategy for factual grounding.
- **Robustness and Broad Applicability:** The framework's robustness across different RL algorithms and its scalability to larger model sizes indicate its broad applicability. This makes KnowRL a flexible solution that can be integrated into various slow-thinking LLM architectures and training pipelines.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** The observed multilingual hybrid reasoning phenomenon, where factual supervision in one language impacts performance in another, highlights a promising area for future fundamental mechanism studies. Furthermore, the framework opens avenues for extending factual verification to multimodal domains, addressing the complexity of real-world information that spans text, vision, and audio.

In conclusion, KnowRL represents a significant advancement in aligning LLM reasoning with factual accuracy. By fundamentally teaching models *how* to reason reliably and *when* to admit uncertainty, it contributes towards building more trustworthy, intelligent, and deployable AI systems.
