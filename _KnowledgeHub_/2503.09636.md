# Real-Time Neuromorphic Navigation: Guiding Physical Robots with Event-Based Sensing and Task-Specific Reconfigurable Autonomy Stack

> [!summary] Summary
> Neuromorphic Navigation is a reconfigurable perception-planning-control stack pairing event-camera (DVS) sensing with a nine-neuron spiking object detector, physics-informed trajectory planning, and a swappable robot-physics module, all running on a Jetson Nano. Deployed on a TurtleBot and a Bebop2 quadrotor, it cuts maneuver time, actuation energy, and compute energy against an RGB YOLO-based pipeline, e.g. 613 J versus 845 J in flight.

## Problem

- Frame-based cameras with dense deep perception impose latency, motion blur, and high power draw, blocking real-time navigation on resource-constrained robots under variable lighting.
- Prior neuromorphic work treats perception, planning, or control as isolated components, leaving no unified event-driven stack spanning sensing through actuation on one platform.
- Event-based SLAM and planning lean on hand-crafted heuristics that ignore robot dynamics, and are validated in simulation or per-platform rather than on embedded hardware.

## Method

- **Neuromorphic Navigation**'s ==spike-based object detector==: one 3x3 convolutional layer feeding nine ==leaky integrate-and-fire neurons==; threshold firing on dense event bursts, with min/max spike coordinates yielding bounding-box centers.
- ==Physics-aware planner== on Jetson Nano combines classical planners (==A*==, ==RRT== variants) with a swappable ==robot-physics module== (unicycle/differential-drive or quadrotor) and an energy-aware cost over thrust or rolling resistance.
- ==Physics-guided neural networks== embed dynamics and thrust penalties into the training loss (==RAMP-Net== style), emitting dynamically feasible, energy-efficient waypoints for the platform's onboard low-level controller.

## Results

- On Bebop2 the full stack cut maneuver time from **35.3 s** to **25.3 s**, actuation energy **845 J** to **613 J**, compute energy **93.5 mJ** to **69.2 mJ** versus RGB.
- TurtleBot repeated the trend: **63.4 s** to **49.8 s**, **1710 J** to **1373 J**, **93.6 mJ** to **74.5 mJ**; the perception-only ablation captured just part of that gain.
- Detector uses one conv layer plus **nine** neurons against YOLO's **60-65 M** parameters, tracks a gate moving **4 m/s** out to **9 m** depth; real flight burned **15%** more energy than simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Faster perception shortens maneuver time, which shortens path length and actuation energy: sensing latency cascades into physical energy cost, not merely compute cost.
> - Parameter-count reduction does not yield proportional energy savings on GPU edge hardware, since CUDA kernel setup dominates; dedicated neuromorphic silicon is required to realize the gap.
> - Fixing perception and control interfaces while swapping the physics model lets one stack serve ground and aerial robots; unsupervised spiking detectors trade labeled data for threshold tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic navigation in dynamic, real-world environments is a grand challenge in AI and robotics. Traditional approaches, which heavily rely on conventional frame-based sensors (e.g., RGB cameras, LiDAR) and deep learning models, face several fundamental limitations. These include high computational demands, significant latency, substantial power consumption, and vulnerability to varying lighting and motion blur. Such issues often prevent their effective deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms requiring real-time responsiveness and energy efficiency.

The broader research landscape is actively exploring alternative paradigms, with neuromorphic computing emerging as a highly promising avenue. Inspired by biological neural systems, neuromorphic systems leverage event-driven, asynchronous, and energy-efficient computation. Central to this shift are event-based vision sensors (DVS), which detect only changes in scene intensity at microsecond-level temporal resolution, drastically reducing data redundancy and power. Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) are the natural computational counterpart, processing these sparse, temporal event streams efficiently.

Prior work in neuromorphic robotics has often focused on isolated components:
- **Neuromorphic Perception:** Extensive research exists on using event cameras and SNNs for tasks like optical flow estimation, object detection, visual odometry, and SLAM.
- **Neuromorphic Planning:** Efforts have been made to integrate planning algorithms like RRT into neuromorphic frameworks, or to use physics-informed models for trajectory optimization.
- **Neuromorphic Control:** SNNs have been applied to control robotic manipulators, arms, and even bipedal robots, with studies exploring neuromorphic PID controllers and closed-loop spiking systems.

This paper distinguishes itself by addressing a critical gap: the lack of a **unified, end-to-end neuromorphic autonomy stack** that seamlessly integrates perception, planning, and control within a single, reconfigurable framework. While hybrid approaches exist (e.g., combining spiking layers with ANNs), and some studies explore embodied neuromorphic intelligence, this work explicitly presents a **task-specific, reconfigurable** solution that generalizes across diverse robotic platforms (ground and aerial). The authors highlight that existing methods often treat these components in isolation, rely on heuristic-based planning, or are platform-specific. By demonstrating a system that combines event-based sensing, SNN-based perception, **physics-informed planning**, and adaptive control on both a ground robot (Turtlebot) and an aerial robot (Bebop2 quadrotor), and deploying it on edge hardware (NVIDIA Jetson Nano), this research significantly advances the field towards practical, energy-efficient, and real-time robotic autonomy. It positions itself as a scalable solution that bridges the gap between theoretical neuromorphic concepts and their robust application in physical robots operating in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent limitations of conventional robotic navigation systems—namely, their high computational demands, latency, and power consumption—which hinder their deployment on resource-constrained platforms, especially in dynamic and visually challenging environments. The authors are driven to leverage the advantages of neuromorphic systems to overcome these challenges.

The key objectives of this work are clearly articulated through its contributions:

- **Develop Neuromorphic Perception for Real-Time Navigation:** To utilize event-based vision sensors (DVS) and Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) to achieve low-latency, energy-efficient perception and decision-making for robotic navigation. This aims to improve robotic perception and decision-making by focusing on essential, time-varying information.
- **Propose a Reconfigurable Neuromorphic Navigation Stack:** To design a modular autonomy framework that integrates perception, planning, and control, making it adaptable across different robotic platforms, specifically demonstrating with ground (TurtleBot) and aerial (Bebop2 quadrotor) robots. This addresses the need for task-specific design requirements across diverse applications.
- **Incorporate Physics-Informed Planning for Robust and Energy-Efficient Control:** To integrate physics-guided neural networks (PgNNs) and motion constraints into the planning process to optimize trajectories, ensuring they are dynamically feasible, robust, and energy-efficient.
- **Validate the Framework Experimentally in Dynamic Environments:** To conduct both simulations and real-world experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness and versatility of neuromorphic navigation in challenging, dynamic scenarios, such as obstacle avoidance by a ground robot and navigating through a moving gate with a drone.
- **Enable Edge Deployment:** To implement the proposed approach on resource-constrained hardware, specifically an NVIDIA Jetson Nano, to showcase its practical feasibility for real-time robotic applications.

In essence, the overarching goal is to pave the way for energy-efficient, scalable, and highly responsive autonomous navigation by fully exploiting the unique properties of neuromorphic sensing and computation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a "Neuromorphic Navigation Autonomy Stack" (Figure 6) that is modular, reconfigurable, and designed for real-time operation on edge hardware. Their approach systematically integrates event-driven sensing, neuromorphic perception, physics-aware planning, and adaptable control.

#### 3.1. Event-Driven Perception, Localization, and Mapping

- **Sensors:** The core sensing layer relies on a Dynamic Vision Sensor (DVS) camera (specifically, DAVIS346) and an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU). The DVS asynchronously records pixel-level brightness changes as discrete events (x, y, t, p), offering microsecond resolution and significantly reduced data redundancy compared to frame-based cameras. An Intel Realsense Depth Camera is also mentioned as part of the "Neuromorphic Sensing" input in Figure 6, providing supplementary depth information for mapping.
- **Neuromorphic Perception (Spike-based Object Detector):** The sparse DVS event stream is processed by a single-layer Spiking Neural Network (SNN).
 - **Event Processing:** The event rate of a moving object is directly proportional to its speed, leading to denser event bursts. These bursts cause spiking neurons to accumulate charge faster and fire when their membrane potential (V(t)) crosses a threshold (V_th). The Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron model is utilized (Equation 2).
 - **Object Localization:** After a neuron fires, object localization is achieved by extracting a bounding box based on the spatial distribution of spiking events, calculating the minimum and maximum coordinates (Equations 5, 6). A weighted neighborhood function with a 3x3 kernel is used for event grouping. The SNN model is fine-tuned with parameters like β=0.1 and V_th=1.75 for reliable tracking at object velocities of up to 4 m/s.
 - **Mapping:** IMU data and event-based feature tracking are used to incrementally build a dynamic map of the environment. For initial mapping and validation, an Optitrack Motion Capture system is used to provide ground truth. The asynchronous nature of event updates allows for real-time refinement during local replanning.

#### 3.2. Physics-Aware Planning and Decision Making

- **Edge Processing:** All high-level navigation logic is executed on an NVIDIA Jetson Nano, enabling a combination of neuromorphic perception with physics-informed planning.
- **Robot Physics Module:** A configurable kinematic or dynamic model of the robot (e.g., unicycle for ground robots, quadrotor dynamics for aerial robots) is integrated. This ensures that generated motion commands are dynamically feasible and respect the robot's velocity, acceleration, and turning limits. This modularity allows for "replaceable robot physics."
- **Adaptive Planning Algorithms:** The framework supports various planning algorithms (e.g., A*, RRT variants, or custom algorithms like those used in). Crucially, planning updates can occur whenever new perceptual events arrive, enabling rapid re-planning in dynamic environments.
- **Energy-Aware Planning:** For ground robots (TurtleBot), energy consumption is related to rolling resistance and motor efficiency (Equation 8), minimizing velocity fluctuations. For aerial robots (quadrotors), power consumption is modeled based on thrust force (Equation 10), and the objective function minimizes an integrated cost balancing flight time and energy (Equation 11).
- **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PiNNs/PgNNs):** For aerial robots, the framework incorporates PiNNs (or PgNNs) by embedding physical constraints directly into the loss function during learning (Figure 11, top). This ensures that learned trajectories are physically feasible, energy-efficient, and resilient to disturbances.
- **Command Generation:** The planner outputs high-level trajectory or waypoint information to the robot's onboard low-level controller for actuator command generation.

#### 3.3. Modular and Reconfigurable Architecture

- A key aspect is the generic design, allowing for the substitution of robot physics modules or planning algorithms without fundamental changes to the core stack. This ensures flexibility and scalability across different robotic platforms and missions.
- The architecture ensures that as long as the perception component provides real-time event data and the planner respects the chosen physics model, the system remains operational.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Ground Robot:** A custom TurtleBot-based platform equipped with a DAVIS346 DVS camera, NVIDIA Jetson Nano, and an OpenCR board for motor control (Figure 7).
- **Aerial Robot:** A Parrot Bebop2 quadrotor equipped with a DVS camera and tracked by an OptiTrack motion capture system. An NVIDIA Jetson Edge Processor handles event-based data streams and coordinates real-time tasks (Figure 8).

By combining these elements, the methodology aims to create a robust, energy-efficient, and highly adaptable navigation system that fully leverages the benefits of neuromorphic computing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling findings through both simulated environments and real-world demonstrations, validating the efficacy and advantages of the proposed neuromorphic navigation framework.

#### 4.1. Neuromorphic Perception Effectiveness

- **Real-time Object Detection:** The SNN-based object detector successfully localizes moving objects (e.g., gates) even at varying depths, where event density decreases (Figure 5). The single-layer SNN, using a 3x3 weighted neighborhood, proved capable of robust tracking.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** Compared to conventional deep learning object detectors like YOLO (60-65 million parameters), the neuromorphic detection approach is extremely lightweight, using only one 3x3 convolutional layer and 9 spiking neurons (Table 3). This dramatically reduces model complexity and memory footprint.

#### 4.2. Ground Robot (TurtleBot) Performance

- **Simulation:** In dynamic environments with moving obstacles, the TurtleBot simulation demonstrated continuous local map updates and collision-free path planning. The event-based strategy exhibited low path tracking error (Figure 9b), highlighting the benefits of asynchronous sensing for responsiveness.
- **Real-world Navigation:** The TurtleBot successfully navigated an indoor environment with static obstacles, demonstrating efficient obstacle avoidance and dynamic path replanning. The system used event-based depth inference (divergence method) and contrast maximization for ego-motion separation (Figure 12). Figure 13 shows the robot's smooth, corrected trajectory around obstacles, validating the real-time adjustments enabled by the event-driven pipeline.
- **Energy Consumption:** Actuation energy for the ground robot is primarily influenced by rolling resistance and motor efficiency. Simulation models helped identify optimal velocities to minimize energy, crucial for energy-aware planning.

#### 4.3. Aerial Robot (Bebop2 Quadrotor) Performance

- **Physics-Informed Planning:** Simulations and analyses demonstrated the importance of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PiNNs) for aerial robots. RAMP-Net, which uses PiNNs, achieved significantly lower RMSE in trajectory tracking across various patterns (Random, Circle, Lemniscate) compared to traditional controllers (PID, Nominal, GP-MPC), especially at higher speeds (Figure 11, bottom). This shows that integrating physical constraints improves robustness and accuracy in dynamic conditions.
- **Energy-Aware Maneuvers:** Energy models for quadrotors (Equation 10, 11) guided planning to balance flight time and energy efficiency. Optimal velocities were identified that minimize power consumption (Figure 10).
- **Real-world Dynamic Navigation:** The Bebop2 successfully navigated through a suspended, moving ring using neuromorphic vision and physics-informed control (Figure 14). The drone dynamically adjusted its altitude and approach angle to track and pass through the ring (Figure 15).
- **Simulation vs. Real-world Discrepancies:** While successful, the real-world flight trajectory showed sharper and more abrupt movements compared to simulation, attributed to non-idealities in motor control and physical constraints. The real-world actuation energy for the Bebop2 was approximately 15% higher (613 Joules) than its simulated counterpart (532 Joules), highlighting the gap between idealized models and practical deployment.

#### 4.4. Performance Comparison (Latency, Energy, Success Rate)

- **Overall Superiority:** Table 2 quantitatively demonstrates the significant advantages of the full neuromorphic stack over RGB-based navigation.
 - **Bebop2:** Full Neuromorphic Stack achieved a maneuver time of 25.3 ± 2.0s, actuation energy of 613 ± 14 J, and compute energy of 69.2 ± 3.5 mJ. This is a substantial improvement over RGB-Based Navigation (35.3 ± 5.2s, 845 ± 35 J, 93.5 ± 4.5 mJ).
 - **TurtleBot:** Similar trends were observed, with the full neuromorphic stack significantly reducing maneuver time (49.8 ± 2.0s vs. 63.4 ± 4.7s for RGB) and actuation energy (1373 ± 12 J vs. 1710 ± 28 J for RGB).
- **Cascading Effect:** The findings indicate a direct correlation: faster neuromorphic perception leads to reduced maneuver time (due to quicker adjustments), which in turn shortens the overall path length and thus lowers actuation energy.
- **Compute Energy Nuance:** Despite the drastically lower parameter count of the neuromorphic perception SNN, the reduction in *compute* energy was not as pronounced as expected when deployed on the NVIDIA Jetson Nano. This is primarily attributed to the reliance on standard CUDA kernels and their associated setup time. The authors point out that dedicated neuromorphic hardware is needed to fully realize ultra-low compute energy.

In summary, the main findings confirm that the proposed neuromorphic navigation framework delivers significant improvements in real-time performance, energy efficiency, and adaptability across different robotic platforms in dynamic environments, validating its core design principles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in robotic autonomy, demonstrating a practical and scalable approach to real-time navigation using neuromorphic systems. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

#### 5.1. Energy Efficiency and Sustainability

- The most direct impact is the significant reduction in actuation and computational energy consumption compared to traditional frame-based systems. This is crucial for resource-constrained platforms (e.g., small drones, mobile robots operating on battery power) where extended operational duration is vital.
- By enabling lower power consumption, the framework contributes to more sustainable robotics, reducing the environmental footprint of autonomous systems.

#### 5.2. Real-Time Responsiveness and Robustness

- Leveraging event-based vision and SNNs enables ultra-low latency perception. This allows robots to react almost instantaneously to dynamic changes in their environment, crucial for high-speed obstacle avoidance, agile maneuvering, and operation in rapidly changing scenarios (e.g., navigating through moving objects, unpredictable human interactions).
- The event-driven approach inherently provides high dynamic range and resilience to varying lighting conditions and motion blur, making robots more robust in challenging real-world environments where conventional cameras often struggle.

#### 5.3. Scalability and Adaptability

- The reconfigurable autonomy stack, with its modular design, is a major contribution. It allows the core neuromorphic perception and physics-informed planning principles to be applied across diverse robotic platforms, from ground vehicles to aerial drones, by simply swapping out the robot physics module. This reduces development time and increases the applicability of the technology.
- This generic framework paves the way for a "plug-and-play" approach to robot autonomy, making it easier to upgrade hardware or switch between different robot types without extensive redesigns.

#### 5.4. Advancement in Neuromorphic Computing Applications

- The work provides a concrete, end-to-end demonstration of how neuromorphic principles can be effectively integrated into a complete robotic system, moving beyond isolated proofs-of-concept.
- It highlights the practical benefits of SNNs for real-world perception and control, pushing the boundaries of neuromorphic AI in physically embodied systems. The extremely low parameter count for perception is a strong indicator of the efficiency potential.

#### 5.5. Future Trajectory for Autonomous Systems

- **Dedicated Neuromorphic Hardware:** The findings underscore the clear need for dedicated neuromorphic hardware to fully unlock the compute energy efficiency of SNNs, as current GPU-accelerated implementations on platforms like Jetson Nano still incur overheads. This work serves as a strong motivation for further investment and development in such hardware.
- **Advanced AI Integration:** The authors envision future integration with advanced AI, such as large language models (LLMs), to enable interactive, human-in-the-loop decision-making for complex robotic tasks. This suggests a future where neuromorphic speed and efficiency combine with high-level cognitive reasoning.
- **Multi-robot Systems and Reinforcement Learning:** Future work is outlined to expand the framework to multi-robot systems and incorporate reinforcement learning for even more adaptive planning, further extending the impact on complex autonomous operations.

In conclusion, this paper does more than just demonstrate neuromorphic navigation; it provides a foundational, highly adaptable framework that addresses core limitations of current robotic systems. Its significance lies in its clear path towards more energy-efficient, robust, and agile autonomous robots, thereby inspiring continued innovation in biologically inspired AI and robotics for complex, real-world deployment.
