# GRIT: Teaching MLLMs to Think with Images

> [!summary] Summary
> GRIT enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) from UC Santa Cruz and eBay to generate visually grounded reasoning chains by interleaving natural language with explicit bounding box coordinates. This method teaches MLLMs to "think with images" using a lightweight reinforcement learning approach on a dataset of only 20 image-question-answer triplets, achieving strong performance on various visual reasoning tasks without dense human annotations.

## Problem

- Existing open-source visual reasoning models primarily generate reasoning content using pure natural language, often lacking explicit integration of visual information, making their reasoning opaque and difficult to verify.
- MLLMs face technical challenges in directly generating visual elements like bounding box coordinates within a textual reasoning chain and maintaining context across sequences interleaving numerous visual elements.
- Obtaining human-annotated data that explicitly incorporates visual evidence in each reasoning step is exceptionally scarce and expensive, hindering the training of truly visually grounded models.

## Method

- Introduces a ==grounded reasoning paradigm== where MLLMs generate reasoning chains that seamlessly interleave natural language text with explicit bounding box coordinates, acting as pointers to specific image regions.
- Employs ==GRPO-GR==, a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, to train MLLMs to adhere to this paradigm, utilizing a ==composite reward system== that incentivizes correct answers and proper formatting of interleaved text and bounding boxes.
- Achieves high ==data efficiency== by training models with as few as 20 unique image-query-answer triplets from existing datasets, eliminating the need for expensive human annotations of intermediate reasoning steps or bounding box labels.

## Results

- GRIT-trained models consistently outperformed all baselines in both GPT-as-judge answer accuracy and grounding IoU across diverse in-domain and out-of-domain visual reasoning datasets (e.g., VSR, TallyQA, GQA, MathVista-mini, MME), despite training on only **20** image-query-answer triplets.
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated GRIT models exhibit flexible reasoning strategies, dynamically grounding critical image regions when necessary and correctly avoiding groundings for non-existent entities.
- GRIT models achieved significantly higher Vision-Language Reasoning Cross-Modal Correlation, indicating strong semantic alignment between generated text and image regions, and analysis showed generated bounding boxes actively increased visual attention in subsequent reasoning steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically GRPO-GR, effectively teaches Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to unify visual grounding and textual reasoning into a single generative process without requiring dense, step-by-step annotations.
> - The act of generating bounding boxes actively enhances the model's attention to relevant visual input in subsequent reasoning segments, leading to more visually consistent and coherent thought processes.
> - Interleaved visual-textual reasoning, where models 'think with images' by dynamically mixing bounding boxes and natural language, can be achieved in an open-source MLLM via lightweight RL, democratizing a capability previously seen in proprietary systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements in reasoning models, particularly with Large Language Models (LLMs) and their extension to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models are designed to articulate their problem-solving process through "reasoning chains," which involve step-by-step deliberations prior to producing a final answer. Early work in this area, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, primarily focused on language-only tasks, with models "thinking aloud" to demonstrate their reasoning. Subsequently, these techniques were adapted for vision-language tasks.

However, a key limitation in existing open-source MLLMs designed for visual reasoning is their tendency to generate reasoning chains consisting solely of natural language. This approach often results in reasoning that is insufficiently grounded in the visual input, lacking explicit connections between textual thoughts and specific image regions. While proprietary systems like ChatGPT-o3/4 have demonstrated capabilities that involve "thinking with images," allowing for dynamic interaction between visual and textual information, an open-source approach to achieve this interleaved visual-textual reasoning has been largely absent.

Previous attempts to integrate visual cues into CoT reasoning for vision-language tasks include multi-stage prompting methods and those leveraging external tools like scene graphs. Some works have aimed to learn visually grounded CoT with minimal supervision, for instance, by using self-generated bounding boxes with auxiliary MLLMs for supervision, though these often separate the grounding and reasoning phases. Other pioneer works fine-tuned models on datasets with detailed, dense annotations that explicitly link textual rationales to corresponding bounding boxes. However, such high-quality, dense annotations are resource-intensive to create and severely scarce.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has been applied to develop visual reasoning models, extending from language-only models to MLLMs for tasks like visual question-answering. These RL-based methods, such as R1-OneVision, R1-V, and Vision-R1, often address specific reasoning challenges (e.g., diagram reasoning, math problems, symbolic reasoning). While some, like VLM-R1, apply RL to grounding-heavy tasks (e.g., referring expression comprehension), their outputs typically consist of bounding boxes as final answers, with an implicit reasoning process rather than an interpretable, interleaved trace of text and visual grounding. These methods frequently treat visual grounding and textual reasoning as separate components or do not integrate them tightly into a single generative process.

GRIT addresses these gaps by proposing a novel, open-source method that enables MLLMs to generate reasoning chains that explicitly interleave natural language with bounding box coordinates. This approach aims to mirror human cognitive processes where visual evidence directly informs thought. By using an RL framework with a specific reward structure, GRIT seeks to eliminate the need for dense, step-by-step annotations for reasoning chains or explicit bounding box labels, demonstrating data efficiency and fostering a unified generative process for both grounding and reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the GRIT research is to enhance the visual reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by enabling them to integrate visual information explicitly into their reasoning processes. Current open-source MLLMs predominantly generate reasoning chains using only natural language, which can result in explanations that lack clear visual grounding and verifiability. This limitation makes it difficult to ascertain which specific parts of an image a model is attending to when formulating its thoughts, hindering interpretability. Humans naturally refer to visual elements when reasoning about images, and the paper aims to empower MLLMs to similarly "think with images."

The authors identify several key challenges in achieving this capability:
- **Technical Integration:** MLLMs are primarily designed to generate language tokens. Integrating visual elements, such as bounding box coordinates, directly within a language-based reasoning chain presents a technical hurdle. Furthermore, models struggle with maintaining context across multiple visual elements interleaved within a single input sequence.
- **Data Scarcity:** There is a significant scarcity of high-quality, human-annotated datasets that explicitly incorporate visual evidence, such as bounding box labels, within each step of a detailed reasoning process. Creating such data is laborious and expensive, especially given that for complex multimodal questions, there may not be a single "correct" reasoning path.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of the GRIT method are:
- **Propose a Novel Grounded Reasoning Paradigm:** Introduce a paradigm where MLLMs generate reasoning chains that interleave natural language text with explicit bounding box coordinates. These coordinates would highlight specific regions of the input image that the model references during its deliberation, making its thought process more transparent and visually grounded.
- **Develop a Data-Efficient Reinforcement Learning Approach (GRPO-GR):** Create a reinforcement learning algorithm, GRPO-GR, that can effectively train MLLMs for this grounded reasoning paradigm. A core objective is to achieve this data efficiency by focusing on robust rewards related to final answer accuracy and the *format* of the grounded reasoning output, thereby eliminating the need for expensive, dense annotations of reasoning chains or explicit bounding box labels for intermediate steps. This minimizes the data requirement to basic image-question-answer triplets.
- **Unify Grounding and Reasoning Abilities:** Enable MLLMs to inherently integrate their existing but often disconnected grounding (identifying objects/regions) and reasoning (logical deduction) faculties into a coherent, unified process.
- **Preserve Model Versatility:** Ensure that models trained with GRIT retain their broad capabilities for various vision-language tasks, including general visual question answering (VQA) and grounding-heavy tasks like referring expression comprehension.
- **Demonstrate Cross-modal Coherence:** Establish that the generated bounding boxes are semantically correlated with the interleaved natural language thoughts.
- **Investigate the Impact of Grounding on Reasoning:** Analyze whether the explicit generation of bounding boxes enhances the model's subsequent attention to relevant visual information during its reasoning process.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond purely textual reasoning in MLLMs by providing them with a mechanism to "think visually," making their decision-making process more interpretable, verifiable, and robust by explicitly connecting their thoughts to specific parts of an image.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GRIT (Grounded Reasoning with Images and Text) method enables MLLMs to generate reasoning chains that integrate both natural language and explicit visual grounding information, specifically bounding box coordinates.

#### 3.1. Grounded Reasoning Paradigm

The GRIT framework operates on a grounded reasoning paradigm where, given an image (I) and a textual question (q), the MLLM generates a two-part output: a reasoning chain (c) followed by a final answer (a).
- **Reasoning Chain Structure:** The reasoning chain (c) begins with a `<think>` token and concludes with a `</think>` token, followed by a potential `<rethink>` and `</rethink>` segment. Crucially, this chain is designed to interleave natural language text (T) with optional bounding box coordinates (B).
- **Dynamic Generation:** At each token generation step, the model dynamically decides whether to produce a natural language token or a sequence of bounding box coordinates.
- **Internal Understanding of Grounding:** When bounding box coordinates are generated, the model *does not* receive additional pixel inputs or cropped image regions corresponding to these boxes. Instead, it relies on its internal understanding of the original input image, informed by the newly generated coordinates, to continue its reasoning process. This approach is intended to be more efficient than alternatives requiring multi-turn generation with additional pixel inputs.
- **Visualization:** The resulting reasoning chain, when rendered, can be visualized as interleaved text alongside highlighted regions (cropped using the generated bounding boxes) from the input image, as depicted in Figure 1 of the paper.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning with GRPO-GR

To train MLLMs to produce these grounded reasoning chains, GRIT employs a novel reinforcement learning algorithm called GRPO for Grounded Reasoning (GRPO-GR), which is built upon the Group-Relative Policy Optimisation (GRPO) algorithm.
- **RL Formulation:**
 - The MLLM acts as a policy $\pi_\theta$ that generates the output sequence (c, a) given an input (I, q).
 - During training, for each (I, q) pair, a group of N candidate completions ($o_1, \dots, o_N$) is sampled from the current policy.
 - For each completion $o_i$, a composite task reward $r_i = R(q, o_i)$ is computed.
 - These rewards are then used to calculate a group-normalized advantage: $A_i = (r_i - mean\{r_1, \dots, r_N\}) / (std\{r_1, \dots, r_N\} + \delta)$, where $\delta$ is a small constant for numerical stability.
- **Composite Task Reward Components:** The task reward $r_i$ is composed of three components, designed to encourage desired grounded reasoning behavior and accurate answers:
 1. **Grounded-reasoning-format reward ($r_{format}$):** This component incentivizes the correct structural format of the reasoning output.
 - **Special Token Format Signal ($s_{st}$):** Rewards the correct usage and order of special reasoning-format tokens (`<think>`, `</think>`, `<rethink>`, `</rethink>`). Each correctly placed token pair contributes 0.5 to the reward.
 - **Bounding Box Format Signal ($s_{bf}$):** Rewards the inclusion of at least one syntactically valid bounding box within the reasoning chain (detected via regex matching a quadruplet of integers). A reward of 0.5 is assigned if one or more such bounding boxes are present.
 - *Design Philosophy:* This format reward is crucial because it encourages the *structure* and *presence* of visual grounding elements without constraining the specific textual content or semantic accuracy of the grounded regions themselves. This design eliminates the need for data with detailed reasoning chain annotations or explicit bounding box labels.
 2. **Grounded-target-counting reward ($r_{count}$):** (Optional) This reward is applied specifically for training examples from visual counting-related datasets. It assigns a reward of 0.5 if the number of bounding boxes generated within the reasoning chain exactly matches the ground-truth count for the target object. This encourages the model to systematically generate the correct quantity of bounding boxes as part of its counting reasoning.
 3. **GPT-aided answer-accuracy reward ($r_{ans}$):** This component assesses the correctness of the final natural language answer.
 - **GPT Score ($s_{GPT}$):** A binary correctness score (0 or 1) assigned by a GPT-4o judge, which evaluates the question, predicted answer, and ground truth triplet.
 - **BLEU Score ($s_{BLEU}$):** The sentence-level BLEU-1 similarity between the predicted answer and the ground truth, weighted by 0.1 to account for its sensitivity to length mismatch.
- **Optimization Objective:** The policy parameters $\theta$ are updated by maximizing the GRPO objective $J_{GRPO}(\theta)$. This objective balances maximizing the expected reward within each sampled group against maintaining proximity to a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ (typically the initial pre-trained model) through a KL divergence penalty, promoting stable learning.

#### 3.3. Training Implementation

- **Base MLLMs:** Two state-of-the-art MLLMs, Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL-3-2B, were used as base models.
- **Training Data:** GRIT demonstrates data efficiency by using a small dataset of only 20 unique image-query-answer triplets. These were sourced from the Visual Spatial Reasoning (VSR) and TallyQA datasets (10 from each). Critically, this training data only consists of image-query-answer triplets, without needing any explicit bounding box or reasoning chain annotations.
- **Training Configuration:** Models were trained for 200 steps with a total batch size of 128. During GRPO-GR training, 4 candidate reasoning traces were generated per input sample. The learning rate was set to 2e-6, using the AdamW optimizer and a Cosine scheduler. All training was performed on 8 NVIDIA A100 (80GB) GPUs with Deepspeed Zero2, with each model requiring approximately 12 hours of training.

#### 3.4. Testing Data

Models were evaluated on curated testing sets from six diverse public datasets: VSR (spatial relation), TallyQA (object counting), GQA (compositional spatial questions), MME (diverse tasks including counting/position), MathVista-mini (mathematical reasoning), and OVDEval (open-vocabulary object grounding). For VSR, TallyQA, and GQA, ground-truth bounding box annotations for critical image regions were utilized, with manual refinement for VSR and GQA. OVDEval, unlike others, primarily required grounding as the direct answer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research investigated the accuracy of grounded reasoning, the interaction between bounding boxes and natural language, and the effects of scaling training data.

#### 4.1. Grounded Reasoning Accuracy (Table 1)

GRIT-trained models (Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3) demonstrated improved performance compared to several baselines across seven diverse testing sets, as measured by GPT-as-judge answer accuracy (ACC) and grounding IoU (GIoU).
- **Overall Performance:** GRIT-trained models consistently achieved higher ACC and GIoU scores. This indicates a successful unification of grounding and reasoning abilities, which were inherently present but often disconnected in the base MLLMs.
- **Data Efficiency and Generalization:** Despite being trained on only 20 image-question-answer triplets, GRIT-trained models showed improvements on in-domain datasets (VSR, TallyQA) and generalized effectively to out-of-domain data (GQA, MathVista, MME, OVDEval). For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-GRIT achieved 72.9 ACC on VSR and 47.8 ACC on TallyQA, significantly outperforming its few-shot fine-tuning baseline (59.7 ACC on VSR, 44.5 ACC on TallyQA). Similarly, InternVL3-GRIT showed substantial gains.
- **Grounding Capabilities:** The models trained with GRIT, while not directly optimized for grounding IoU, outperformed baselines in locating question-relevant image regions during their reasoning. On the OVDEval dataset, which is grounding-heavy, GRIT models achieved more accurate detection results than zero-shot MLLMs (e.g., InternVL3-GRIT 56.0 GIoU vs. InternVL3 Direct Query 55.1 GIoU).
- **Baseline Limitations:**
 - Off-the-shelf MLLMs (Direct Query, Chain-of-Thought, One-shot ICL) exhibited rigidity, often generating either only bounding boxes or just a final answer. When prompted for interleaved output, their performance deteriorated significantly in either answer accuracy or grounding IoU, suggesting a disconnect and potential interference between their inherent grounding and reasoning capabilities.
 - Few-shot Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) showed more balanced performance than zero-shot baselines but still achieved considerably lower scores than GRIT-trained models, suggesting that SFT might primarily learn surface-level output forms rather than deeply integrated reasoning.

#### 4.2. Interaction Between Bounding Boxes and Natural Language

- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 3):** GRIT-trained models exhibited flexible reasoning strategies. They sometimes provided an answer first and then reflected on it with visual evidence, or conversely, grounded critical image regions initially before analyzing them in subsequent reasoning. The models also dynamically determined when grounding was necessary, avoiding erroneous groundings for non-existent entities.
- **Cross-Modal Correlation (Figure 4):** A "Vision-Language Reasoning Cross-Modal Correlation" metric, using GPT-4o as a judge, was introduced to assess the coherence between generated image regions and interleaved natural language. GRIT-trained models significantly outperformed Zero-shot ICL and Few-shot SFT baselines in this metric, demonstrating a stronger correlation between selected image regions and textual reasoning. A gap remained when compared to human-written reasoning chains, indicating areas for potential improvement.
- **Influence of Grounding on Subsequent Reasoning (Figure 5):** An analysis of attention scores to input visual tokens during the generation of "rethink" segments revealed that the presence of bounding boxes in the preceding "pre-rethink" segment led to significantly higher average attention scores to visual tokens. This suggests that explicit grounding actions (generating bounding boxes) facilitate stronger visual attention in subsequent reasoning, potentially benefiting the image consistency of the overall process.

#### 4.3. Effect of Scaling Training Data (Figure 6)

Experiments with increasing training data volume (20, 500, and 7,000 image-question-answer triplets) showed that answer accuracy generally improved with more data for GRIT-trained models.
- **In-domain vs. Out-of-domain:** Improvements were more pronounced for in-domain testing data (VSR, TallyQA) than for out-of-domain data (GQA, MathVista-mini).
- **Diminishing Returns:** The rate of performance growth diminished with increasing data, suggesting that continued improvement might require exponentially larger datasets. This finding aligns with observations from other studies that RL with verifiable rewards tends to bias existing reasoning patterns rather than fundamentally altering them, with pre-training playing a significant role.
- **Implication:** For substantial improvements in out-of-domain performance, increasing the diversity of training data may be more critical than simply scaling up data volume.

#### 4.4. Ablation on Counting-related Training Data and Reward (Appendix C, Table 3)

An ablation study comparing a GRIT model trained with 10 VSR + 10 TallyQA samples (with counting reward) against one trained with 20 VSR samples (without counting reward) revealed:
- **Impact on Grounding:** Excluding counting data and its associated reward led to a significant decrease in grounding IoU.
- **Impact on Generalization:** This exclusion also negatively affected answer accuracy on out-of-domain data.
These results underscore the importance of including counting-related training data and the specific grounded-target-counting reward within the GRIT method for both grounding accuracy and broader generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GRIT method presents several significant contributions to the field of multimodal artificial intelligence and holds potential for broad impact.

- **Enabling Grounded Reasoning in MLLMs:** GRIT introduces a novel paradigm that allows Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform visually grounded reasoning by explicitly interleaving natural language text with bounding box coordinates. This moves beyond purely textual reasoning, offering a more transparent and verifiable thought process where the model's visual references are made explicit. This capability is crucial for tasks requiring precise interaction with visual information.

- **Exceptional Data Efficiency:** A key impact of GRIT is its remarkable data efficiency. It achieves complex grounded reasoning abilities using as few as 20 image-question-answer triplets, eliminating the need for expensive, dense, and time-consuming annotations of intermediate reasoning steps or explicit bounding box labels. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying such sophisticated MLLMs, making advanced visual reasoning more accessible.

- **Unification of Grounding and Reasoning:** The method successfully unifies the grounding (locating objects/regions) and reasoning (logical deduction) abilities within MLLMs. Previously, these capabilities often operated separately or interfered with each other in baselines. GRIT demonstrates that their integration through a single generative process enhances the performance of both individual abilities, leading to more coherent and accurate outputs.

- **Improved Interpretability and Verifiability:** By explicitly showing the image regions a model is attending to during its reasoning, GRIT enhances the interpretability of MLLM outputs. Users can visually inspect the model's "thought process," allowing for better understanding, debugging, and trust in complex decision-making scenarios where accountability is important.

- **Robustness and Flexibility:** GRIT-trained models learn to dynamically assess when visual grounding is necessary, leading to more robust behavior that avoids spurious groundings for non-existent entities. The flexibility in reasoning strategies, adapting to diverse queries by sometimes grounding first, sometimes reasoning and then reflecting, indicates a more human-like cognitive process.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies important directions for future work, particularly the need for data diversity over mere volume to improve out-of-domain generalization. It also points to the foundational role of model pre-training in achieving higher performance. The observed gap between GRIT's cross-modal correlation and human performance suggests avenues for further enhancing the semantic coherence between generated bounding boxes and textual reasoning.

- **Broad Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across various VQA tasks (spatial reasoning, counting, compositional questions, mathematical reasoning) and grounding tasks demonstrates its broad applicability. This versatility makes GRIT a valuable contribution for developing MLLMs capable of tackling a wide range of real-world vision-language challenges.

In summary, GRIT represents a significant step towards MLLMs that can genuinely "think with images," offering a data-efficient, interpretable, and unified approach to visual reasoning.
