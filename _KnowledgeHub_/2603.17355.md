# OnlineHMR: Video-based Online World-Grounded Human Mesh Recovery

> [!summary] Summary
> OnlineHMR recovers world-grounded 3D human meshes from streaming monocular video. A two-branch design pairs a causal, KV-cached sliding-window HMR transformer with human-centric incremental MASt3R-SLAM, using soft human masks, EMA camera-trajectory smoothing, velocity regularization, and metric-depth rescaling. It matches offline chunk-based accuracy on EMDB while running fully causally at 0.30 s per-frame delay.

## Problem

- Video HMR models such as TCMR, MPS-Net and GLoT estimate the center frame of a 16-frame window, needing future frames and recomputing overlapping features wastefully.
- WHAM is online only in camera coordinates; its global trajectory module leans on DPVO or DROID-SLAM, which retro-optimize past camera poses using future observations.
- Human3R streams via a recurrent state but reconstructs local human motion inaccurately and jitters, since 4D-scene pretraining offers far less human-specific data.

## Method

- ==Sliding-window learning==: stride-1 overlapping windows where the last frame self-attends and cross-attends to earlier ==ViT== features, plus ==velocity and acceleration losses== penalizing pelvis-relative joint jitter.
- ==KV-cache inference==: keys and values of past frames held in a FIFO cache, so each streaming frame is processed causally in constant time while reproducing training-time window fusion.
- ==Human-centric incremental SLAM==: SAM2 soft masks (dilation plus Gaussian blur) suppress dynamic-region features in ==MASt3R-SLAM==, ==EMA== quaternion and translation correction with velocity clamping smooths poses, ==MoGe-V2== depth sets metric scale.

## Results

- On EMDB-1 it reaches **46.0** PA-MPJPE and **9.0** Accel, best among online methods, while streaming at **0.30 s** average per-frame delay against offline TRAM's **115.95 s**.
- World-grounded EMDB-2: WA-MPJPE100 of **93.5** beats online Human3R at **112.2** and offline WHAM at **135.6**, RTE **2.2%**, though W-MPJPE **310.4** trails Human3R's **267.9** due to scale error.
- Ablations: velocity regularization cuts EMDB-1 Accel from **15.7** to **9.0** and Jitter from **70.1** to **33.7**; soft masking lowers MASt3R-SLAM ATE from **1.22** to **0.83**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Causality can be retrofitted cheaply: train with parallel overlapping windows, then serve with a FIFO KV cache, no bidirectional-to-causal distillation required.
> - Smoothing the camera instead of the body indirectly regularizes world human motion, avoiding learned motion priors that break on out-of-distribution or vehicle-borne subjects.
> - Hard segmentation masks leak boundary features into SLAM matching while blurred soft-confidence masks do not; Accel and Jitter also miss perceived instability, motivating spectrogram-based evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human Mesh Recovery (HMR) is a field focused on reconstructing 3D human body pose and shape from visual inputs. Initially, HMR research primarily focused on estimating human meshes within camera coordinates from single images or short video clips. Recent advancements have extended this capability to "world-grounded" HMR, which reconstructs absolute human translation, orientation, and scale within a consistent world coordinate system over time. This global understanding of human dynamics is crucial for applications such as motion analysis, retargeting for humanoids, and virtual reality (VR).

Despite progress in world-grounded HMR, the majority of existing methods operate offline. These approaches typically require processing the entire video clip, often relying on future frames or global optimization techniques (e.g., TCMR, MPS-Net, GLoT, TRAM). While some methods, like WHAM, claim online inference, their global trajectory estimation components frequently depend on offline frameworks such as DPVO or DROID-SLAM, which use future frames for correction. Other online attempts, such as Human3R, have been noted for lacking accuracy in local human motion and maintaining smooth, coherent motion over time. This reliance on offline processing introduces significant latency, heavy computational costs, and memory demands, rendering these methods impractical for real-time or interactive applications like augmented reality (AR), VR, and telepresence.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a fully online framework for world-grounded HMR. It aims to develop a system that can process streaming video inputs causally—meaning predictions for a given frame rely only on current and past observations—while simultaneously ensuring high fidelity, temporal consistency, and efficiency. The work situates itself as a step towards making world-grounded HMR viable for real-world deployment in dynamic and interactive environments, building upon lessons from streaming inference techniques and human-camera-scene joint modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **OnlineHMR**, a fully online framework capable of recovering 3D human body pose and shape (represented by the SMPL parametric model) from monocular streaming videos, specifically in world coordinates. This objective is motivated by the limitations of existing HMR approaches, which are predominantly offline and thus unsuitable for real-time interactive applications.

To achieve this overarching goal, the authors identify and aim to satisfy four essential criteria for online processing:

1. **System-level causality:** The entire processing pipeline, including both the camera-coordinate HMR module and the global trajectory estimation module, must operate strictly incrementally. This means that the prediction for any given frame (i) should depend solely on current and past input frames, without any access to future frames or recourse to offline global optimization. This criterion is crucial for enabling low-latency, immediate responses necessary for interactive systems.

2. **Faithfulness:** The reconstructed 3D human meshes must accurately capture the body geometry and pose details for each individual frame. This ensures that the generated motion trajectories are reliable and that the spatial alignment of the human body within the world coordinate system is physically plausible. Maintaining high fidelity under online constraints is a core challenge addressed by the proposed methodology.

3. **Temporal consistency:** The reconstructed human motion must be smooth and coherent over time. The framework aims to preserve geometric and kinematic continuity, which involves mitigating abrupt changes, avoiding long-term drift in position or orientation, and preventing excessive damping that might smooth out natural human movements excessively. This ensures that the recovered motion appears natural and realistic.

4. **Efficiency:** Each new frame must be processed with constant-time complexity. This is vital for supporting continuous streaming inference, where a constant processing rate per frame translates to minimal latency and allows for continuous operation without accumulating delays. This criterion differentiates true online systems from those that might achieve real-time average throughput but still incur significant delays for individual frames due to batch processing or chunk-based optimizations.

The motivation for addressing these criteria stems from the practical demands of applications such as AR/VR, telepresence, and human-robot interaction. Existing offline methods, which require full video sequences or future frames, cannot provide the instantaneous feedback required by these interactive scenarios. Furthermore, their computational and memory costs for long sequences are often prohibitive. By developing a truly online, world-grounded HMR system, OnlineHMR seeks to enable a new generation of human-centric perception applications that are currently limited by the architectural constraints of conventional HMR techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OnlineHMR is designed as a fully online framework to recover the SMPL parametric human model in world coordinates from streaming monocular RGB video. The methodology is built upon a two-branch architecture that combines online camera-coordinate HMR with a human-centric incremental SLAM system.

**Parametric Human Model Preliminary:**
The system uses the SMPL model, which represents a human mesh M_i as a function of pose (θ_i ∈ R^(23x3) axis-angle rotations) and shape (β_i ∈ R^10) parameters. The HMR network initially predicts these parameters, along with a root rotation R_root_i and translation t_root_i, to produce a camera-coordinate mesh M_c_i. To transform this into the world coordinate system (M_w_i), a rigid transformation matrix P_i = [q_c_i, s ⋅ t_c_i] is applied, where q_c_i and t_c_i are the unit quaternion rotation and translation of the camera pose, and 's' is a metric scale factor.

**Camera Coordinates Online HMR:**
This branch focuses on recovering temporally smooth human motion in the camera frame while adhering to online processing constraints.

- **Sliding Window Learning:** During *training*, the input video sequence is divided into short, overlapping windows (N frames, step size 1). Features are extracted using a ViT backbone. Intra-window information fusion is performed using attention, where the last frame's query performs self-attention and cross-attention to preceding frames within its window. The aggregated information is fed to an SMPL head to regress body model parameters for the last frame. Standard frame-level HMR losses (3D keypoints, 2D keypoints, SMPL parameters, 3D vertices) supervise this process.
- **Inter-window Temporal Modeling:** To enhance temporal consistency and suppress abrupt motion changes, velocity regularization terms (L_v for joint velocity, L_a for acceleration relative to pelvis position) are added to the loss function, penalizing sudden shifts in the continuous sequence of sliding window outputs.
- **KV Cache Inference:** To achieve *causal, streaming inference* at test time, a key-value (KV) cache mechanism is employed. Extracted features (keys and values) from previous frames are stored. For the current frame 'i', its query q_i attends to its own key k_i and value v_i (self-attention), and also to the cached keys k_prev and values v_prev from preceding frames (cross-attention). This allows temporal information fusion without accessing future frames, enabling constant-time per-frame processing.

**Human Centric Incremental SLAM:**
This branch is responsible for estimating the camera trajectory in world coordinates.

- **Human → Camera (Masking for SLAM):** Standard SLAM systems perform poorly in human-centric videos due to dynamic human regions violating static feature assumptions. To address this, SAM2 is used to segment the human region in each input frame. Instead of a hard mask, a *soft confidence mask* C_soft_i is generated by applying dilation and Gaussian blur to the human segment C_h_i. This soft mask minimizes the influence of deformable human textures and motion dynamics on feature extraction for SLAM, ensuring that the SLAM system primarily relies on static background features. MASt3R-SLAM is used as the underlying SLAM system.
- **Camera → Human (Trajectory Smoothing):** To impose smoothness constraints on the camera trajectory and, indirectly, on world-grounded human motion, an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) correction is applied to the incremental camera poses from SLAM. For translation, a history buffer with exponentially decaying weights (w_m) is used to calculate a history-weighted average (t_bar_i). A velocity-dependent clamping threshold (τ) prevents large jumps in the translation update (Δt_i). For rotation, an EMA is applied to quaternions (q_i), ensuring consistent hemisphere alignment before linear interpolation (SLERP approximation). This EMA correction helps suppress high-frequency jitters in the camera trajectory.
- **Metric Scale Recovery:** The SLAM output is typically "up-to-scale" rather than metrically scaled. To convert it to a metric world coordinate system, the metric depth estimation model MoGe-V2 is used to predict per-frame metric depth. A scaler 's' is calculated using the SLAM depth map, explicitly ignoring human region pixels during this calculation to avoid distortion from dolly zoom effects or blurriness of human regions in SLAM depth. This scaler is kept consistent across the sequence and applied to SLAM translation.

**Frequency Domain Metric:**
To quantify motion naturalness and jittering effects more effectively than traditional Accel or Jitter metrics, a novel frequency-domain metric based on spectrogram analysis is proposed. The Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) is applied to flattened 3D joint positions, yielding a time-varying frequency distribution. Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Correlation (Corr) based on spectrogram statistics are used as dependent metrics, aiming to capture visual perceived temporal instability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of OnlineHMR was conducted using 3DPW and EMDB-1 datasets for camera-coordinate HMR, and EMDB-2 for world-coordinate HMR. The model was trained on BEDLAM, 3DPW, and H3.6M.

**Faithfulness & Consistency (Camera Coordinates):**
On the 3DPW and EMDB-1 datasets, OnlineHMR achieved competitive performance compared to both per-frame and video-offline HMR methods, while significantly outperforming other video-online approaches.
- **Accuracy:** OnlineHMR demonstrated PA-MPJPE values of 43.7 mm on 3DPW and 46.0 mm on EMDB-1. For MPJPE and PVE, it reported 69.9 mm and 83.7 mm on 3DPW, and 74.0 mm and 86.1 mm on EMDB-1, respectively. These figures are comparable to highly optimized chunk-based offline methods like WHAM and TRAM, and notably superior to other online methods such as TRACE (PA-MPJPE: 50.9, 71.5) and Human3R (PA-MPJPE: 44.1, 48.5).
- **Temporal Consistency:** The Accel metric for OnlineHMR was 6.4 mm on 3DPW and 9.0 mm on EMDB-1, indicating strong temporal smoothness. This is significantly better than other online methods (e.g., TRACE: 28.6, 25.5), and close to the best offline methods (e.g., TRAM: 4.9, 4.9). The authors categorize WHAM as an offline method in this context due to its VO component performing sliding window local optimization that utilizes future observations.

**Faithfulness & Consistency (World Coordinates):**
On the EMDB-2 dataset, OnlineHMR demonstrated strong performance in global human trajectory and motion recovery.
- **Overall Metrics:** OnlineHMR achieved a PA-MPJPE of 40.1 mm, WA-MPJPE_100 of 93.5 mm, RTE of 2.2%, and ERVE of 12.4 mm/frame. These results are competitive with and often superior to existing offline and online methods. For instance, its WA-MPJPE_100 is better than most listed offline methods (except TRAM and JOSH) and significantly better than other online methods (TRACE: 529.0, JOSH3R: 220.0, Human3R: 112.2).
- **W-MPJPE Discrepancy:** The W-MPJPE_100 (310.4 mm) was observed to be relatively higher than some offline methods (e.g., TRAM: 222.4, JOSH: 174.7). The authors attribute this to inaccuracies introduced by metric scale conversion. They clarify that good WA-MPJPE performance coupled with a lagging W-MPJPE suggests that while the overall shape of the trajectory and human motion is well reconstructed after sequence alignment, the incremental estimation of scale might not be perfectly consistent.
- **Visualizations:** Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 4, 5) illustrate that OnlineHMR produces faithful reconstructions of human trajectories compared to ground truth and concurrent work like Human3R. The method also demonstrates generalization to multi-individual scenarios (Fig. 6).

**Efficiency:**
Efficiency was evaluated based on Frames-Per-Second (FPS) and Average Delay per frame.
- **FPS:** OnlineHMR achieved 3.3 FPS on a single RTX 6000 Ada GPU. The authors note that FPS can vary significantly based on factors like server occupancy, video length, content, and the nature of SLAM processing (incremental vs. global optimization). They suggest viewing FPS as a secondary reference, as offline methods can often batch process frames in parallel if not streaming.
- **Average Delay:** OnlineHMR reported an average delay of 0.30 seconds per frame, which is significantly lower than offline methods (e.g., TRAM: 115.95 s, WHAM (w/ DPVO): 26.18 s). Among online methods, OnlineHMR had a slightly higher average delay than TRACE (0.04 s) and Human3R (0.21 s), but it offers better accuracy in both camera and world coordinates.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Velocity Regularization:** Implementing velocity regularization during training resulted in improved temporal consistency. Accel decreased from 8.9 mm to 6.4 mm on 3DPW and from 15.7 mm to 9.0 mm on EMDB-1. The independent Jitter metric also improved from 32.3 to 19.5 on 3DPW and from 70.1 to 33.7 on EMDB-1.
- **Soft Human Mask:** Using a soft human mask for SLAM input images significantly improved camera trajectory estimation. The Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) for MASt3R-SLAM decreased from 1.22 (Vanilla) to 0.96 (Hard Mask) and further to 0.83 (Soft Mask). Similarly, for human reconstruction metrics, the soft mask led to better WA-MPJPE (93.5 vs. 119.6 for vanilla) and RTE (2.2 vs. 4.1 for vanilla). This indicates that the soft mask effectively mitigated incorrect feature matching caused by dynamic human regions.
- **EMA Correction:** Qualitative results (Fig. 10 in Supplementary) indicate that EMA correction on camera translation and rotation reduces jittering effects on both camera and world-coordinate human translations.

**Frequency Domain Metric (Supplementary):**
When evaluated with the proposed frequency-domain metrics (RMSE_norm and Corr_norm), OnlineHMR demonstrated comparability to the offline baseline TRAM in capturing frequency amplitude and pattern distribution similarity with ground truth, suggesting its ability to produce natural-looking motion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OnlineHMR represents a methodological advancement in the field of human mesh recovery by proposing a fully online framework for world-grounded human motion reconstruction from monocular videos. This work addresses a critical limitation of most existing HMR methods, which are typically offline and therefore impractical for real-time interactive applications.

The primary significance lies in its capability to satisfy four essential criteria for online processing: system-level causality, faithfulness, temporal consistency, and efficiency. By adhering to strict causality, OnlineHMR enables immediate feedback and low latency, which is fundamental for applications such as AR/VR, telepresence, and human-robot interaction. The framework's design, incorporating a causal key-value cache inference mechanism and a curated sliding-window learning strategy, directly contributes to achieving these online properties without substantial degradation in reconstruction quality.

The technical innovations, including the human-centric incremental SLAM with soft masking and Exponential Moving Average (EMA) correction for camera trajectory smoothing, enhance the robustness and accuracy of world-grounded human motion estimation in challenging, dynamic environments. The soft masking approach effectively mitigates the interference of moving human figures on SLAM's static feature assumptions, while EMA correction refines the camera trajectory, indirectly improving the temporal smoothness and physical plausibility of the human motion. The introduction of a frequency-domain metric for evaluating motion naturalness offers a more nuanced assessment of temporal stability compared to traditional metrics.

The impact of OnlineHMR could extend to various domains requiring real-time, accurate 3D human perception. In **AR/VR**, it could facilitate more immersive and responsive user experiences by integrating digital avatars with physical environments. In **telepresence** and **remote collaboration**, it could enable more natural and engaging interactions. For **robotics**, understanding human motion in real-time and world coordinates is crucial for safe and efficient human-robot collaboration. Furthermore, the framework's competitive performance compared to chunk-based offline methods, despite its online constraints, suggests a viable path for deploying high-fidelity HMR in production-level systems.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as challenges with abrupt camera switches or multi-camera inputs, the presented OnlineHMR framework marks a step toward bridging the gap between sophisticated 3D human reconstruction techniques and their practical deployment in streaming, interactive scenarios.
