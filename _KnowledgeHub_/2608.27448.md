# TTPO: Test-Time Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> TTPO is a label-free test-time training objective for LLM reasoning that routes rollouts by agreement with a majority-vote pseudo-label: agreeing rollouts get dense on-policy self-distillation from an answer-conditioned teacher, disagreeing ones get grouped RL penalties, both refined by token-level weighting and masking. Without labels it matches label-supervised OPSD and lifts Qwen3-1.7B from 38.0% to 45.2% across five competition math benchmarks.

## Problem

- RLVR and on-policy self-distillation both need ground-truth answers for reward verification or teacher conditioning, so neither can run at test time on unlabeled data.
- Swapping ground truth for majority-vote pseudo-labels is fragile under dense supervision: one wrong label corrupts the teacher and misleads every token, not just one trajectory.
- Existing label-free test-time RL stays at coarse sequence-level rewards and is highly sensitive to consensus quality, giving no per-step guidance on competition-level problems.

## Method

- ==Asymmetric routing objective==: agreeing rollouts trained by ==forward-KL on-policy self-distillation== from a ==pseudo-label-conditioned thinking-mode teacher==, disagreeing rollouts penalized by ==grouped RL advantages==.
- Positive-branch ==token weighting== via ==Soft-OR== of normalized ==student entropy== and ==teacher-student KL==, down-weighting positions where the student is already confident and aligned.
- Negative-branch ==token masking== scores tokens by ==negative log-probability times normalized certainty== and keeps the ==top-50% median cut==, so penalties hit confident errors, not locally valid steps.

## Results

- Label-free test-time training on Qwen3-1.7B reaches **45.2%** average versus **38.0%** base, **41.9%** for OPSD-TTT and **40.2%** for test-time RL; 4B hits **61.1%**, 8B **65.3%**.
- With labeled data available, pseudo-label TTPO still edges label-supervised OPSD: **40.1%** vs **39.7%** at 1.7B, **58.6%** vs **58.4%** at 4B, **62.6%** vs **61.7%** at 8B.
- Ablations drop AIME 2026 from **46.5%** to **43.3%** without positive weighting and **45.4%** without negative masking; non-thinking evaluation gains reach **+25.2%** to **+36.4%** versus OPSD's **+3.5%** to **+7.1%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Errors in self-generated labels are asymmetric: disagreement signals genuine wrongness more reliably than agreement signals correctness, so penalties tolerate noise that distillation cannot.
> - Noisier supervision can beat exact supervision when it preserves a healthy positive-negative split; ground truth starves both branches on problems the model rarely solves.
> - Self-improvement is sustainable when consensus quality rises with the model, letting a system exceed the accuracy of its own initial supervision signal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has seen substantial advancements in mathematical reasoning capabilities, often facilitated by techniques such as extended chain-of-thought generation and post-training methods. Among these, Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has been prominent, providing sequence-level outcome rewards. However, RLVR's reward signals are often coarse, applying a single scalar reward to an entire sequence and thus providing limited supervision for individual reasoning steps.

A complementary approach, On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), addresses this by providing dense, token-level supervision. OPSD typically conditions the model (acting as a teacher) on the ground-truth answer to re-score the student's own rollouts token by token. Recent research has explored combining RLVR and OPSD signals through various mechanisms like auxiliary losses or credit redistribution. A significant limitation of these methods is their inherent reliance on ground-truth answers for reward verification or teacher conditioning.

This dependency poses a challenge in Test-Time Training (TTT) scenarios, where a model is adapted to unlabeled test data at inference time, and ground-truth labels are not available. Prior TTT efforts for LLM reasoning, such as TTRL (Test-Time Reinforcement Learning), utilize majority-vote pseudo-labels to derive rewards for RL-based training. While TTRL enables label-free reasoning improvement, it is susceptible to errors when the majority vote is incorrect, and it still relies on coarse, sequence-level rewards. Follow-up work, like Hi-TTRL and SCRL, has attempted to mitigate TTRL's sensitivity to consensus quality but remains rooted in RL-only approaches.

The natural extension of using pseudo-labels in OPSD has been attempted, but dense supervision, such as that provided by OPSD, significantly magnifies the impact of pseudo-label errors. A corrupted pseudo-label, when used to condition a teacher, can mislead the model at every token, in contrast to a corrupted sequence-level reward which misleads once per trajectory.

This paper addresses the challenge of providing robust, dense, token-level supervision in a label-free TTT setting. It builds upon the understanding that while pseudo-labels are frequently erroneous on complex problems, the nature of these errors can be exploited. Specifically, it observes an asymmetry where rollouts that disagree with a (potentially incorrect) pseudo-label are often genuinely wrong regardless of the pseudo-label's correctness. This insight motivates a differential treatment of positive (agreeing) and negative (disagreeing) samples. The work also incorporates advancements in token-level weighting and masking, which aim to make training more efficient by focusing gradients on informative or problematic tokens, a concept explored in prior work like TIP and STAPO. By integrating these components, TTPO aims to bridge the gap between powerful, label-dependent post-training methods and the practical constraints of label-free test-time adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective methodology for Test-Time Policy Optimization (TTPO) that enables large language models to improve their mathematical reasoning abilities without access to ground-truth labels. The method aims to leverage dense, token-level supervision, similar to On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), while remaining robust to the inherent noise and frequent errors present in self-generated pseudo-labels.

The motivation for TTPO is multifaceted, arising from several limitations of existing post-training and test-time training (TTT) approaches:

1. **Reliance on Ground-Truth Labels in Advanced Post-Training Methods**: Current state-of-the-art methods for improving LLM mathematical reasoning, such as Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), fundamentally rely on ground-truth answers. RLVR uses these labels for reward verification, while OPSD conditions its teacher model on them. This dependency renders these methods unusable in a TTT setting, where labels are unavailable at inference time.
2. **Fragility of Pseudo-Labels for Dense Supervision**: In the absence of ground truth, majority-vote pseudo-labeling is a natural alternative for generating self-supervision. While TTRL successfully uses pseudo-labels for sequence-level RL rewards, extending this to dense, token-level supervision (e.g., by replacing ground truth with pseudo-labels in OPSD) proves fragile. The paper highlights that a corrupted pseudo-label, when used to guide a teacher for token-level distillation, can propagate errors across every token of every rollout, leading to detrimental training. On competition-level problems, pseudo-labels are observed to be frequently incorrect (e.g., approximately 85% of prompts on AIME 2026 for Qwen3-1.7B).
3. **Asymmetry of Pseudo-Label Errors**: A critical observation forms the core motivation for TTPO's design. The authors found that even when a majority-vote pseudo-label is incorrect, a substantial portion of the rollouts that *disagree* with this pseudo-label are *also genuinely wrong* (approximately 79% in their observations). This implies that penalizing a rollout simply because it disagrees with the pseudo-label remains largely correct, irrespective of whether the pseudo-label itself is accurate. In contrast, distilling towards a pseudo-label (especially an incorrect one) directly introduces error into the teacher, misguiding subsequent tokens. This asymmetry suggests that different types of self-supervision (penalties versus distillation) should be applied selectively based on whether a rollout agrees or disagrees with the pseudo-label.
4. **Need for Signal-Efficient and Noise-Robust Mechanisms**: Given the high rate of pseudo-label errors, a robust TTT method must efficiently extract useful learning signals while minimizing the propagation of noise. This necessitates not only an asymmetric objective but also fine-grained control over which tokens receive gradients. Over-penalizing locally correct tokens within an overall incorrect trajectory or over-distilling already-converged tokens can waste computational resources and introduce noise.
5. **Achieving Self-Evolution**: The ideal TTT system should be capable of self-improvement, where the model's enhanced performance on test problems leads to the generation of higher-quality pseudo-labels, which in turn further improves the model. This virtuous cycle, or "self-evolution," is a key desideratum for TTT, distinguishing it from static, ground-truth-based training.

In summary, the motivation for TTPO is to enable robust, dense, token-level self-supervision in a label-free TTT context by acknowledging and strategically exploiting the asymmetric nature of pseudo-label errors, employing differential treatment for agreeing and disagreeing rollouts, and incorporating token-level gradient conditioning for efficiency and noise reduction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TTPO (Test-Time Policy Optimization) is designed as an asymmetric training objective to enable dense, token-level self-supervision for LLMs in a label-free test-time training (TTT) setting. It combines On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) for rollouts that agree with a majority-vote pseudo-label and Grouped Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization (GRPO) for rollouts that disagree, complemented by token-level selection mechanisms.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Problem Setup

The system operates on a set of test-time problems without ground-truth labels. For each problem $x$, $K$ trajectories $\{y_1, \ldots, y_K\}$ are sampled from the policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot | x)$. Final answers $a_k$ are extracted from these trajectories.

**Majority-vote Pseudo-labeling**: Answers are clustered by mathematical equivalence. The largest cluster determines the pseudo-label $\hat{a}$, with $c$ being its consensus count. Trajectories are then partitioned into:
- **Positive samples ($P$)**: Rollouts where $a_k \equiv \hat{a}$.
- **Negative samples ($N$)**: Rollouts where $a_k \not\equiv \hat{a}$.

**Answer-conditioned Teacher**: Following OPSD, a teacher is constructed by conditioning the same model on the pseudo-label $\hat{a}$ as privileged information. The teacher's token probabilities are $q^{(\hat{a})}_t = \pi_\theta(\cdot | [x; \hat{a}]_{\text{teacher}}, y_{<t})$ (without gradient propagation), while the student's are $p_t = \pi_\theta(\cdot | x_{\text{student}}, y_{<t})$ (with gradient propagation).

#### 3.2. Asymmetric Design Rationale

The core of TTPO's design is the observation that majority-vote pseudo-labels are frequently incorrect (e.g., ~85% of the time on AIME 2026 for Qwen3-1.7B). However, when the pseudo-label is wrong, a significant majority of rollouts disagreeing with it are also genuinely incorrect (~79%). This allows for a robust penalty on disagreeing rollouts. In contrast, distillation toward an incorrect pseudo-label via a teacher can mislead at every token. Therefore, TTPO applies different signals where they are most reliable:
- **OPSD for positive samples**: When a rollout agrees with the pseudo-label, even if the pseudo-label is incorrect, the teacher is conditioned on the same answer the student produced. This reduces the update to a beneficial "thinking-mode-to-non-thinking-mode" distillation.
- **GRPO for negative samples**: Penalties are applied to rollouts that disagree with the pseudo-label. This is reliable because the disagreement itself often indicates an error, regardless of the pseudo-label's correctness.

#### 3.3. Positive Samples: OPSD Branch

For each positive sample $y_k \in P$, the loss is based on OPSD's forward KL divergence with per-token weighting:
$L_{\text{OPSD}}(k) = \frac{1}{T_k} \sum_{t=1}^{T_k} w(t) \cdot \text{KL}(q^{(\hat{a})}_t \parallel p_t)$
where $T_k$ is the number of valid response tokens and $w(t)$ is the token weight.

**Token Weighting**: This mechanism aims to focus distillation on tokens that offer high learning value. It combines two normalized signals using a Soft-OR operation:
- Student entropy $H(t)$: Indicates student uncertainty.
- Teacher-student divergence $\Delta(t) = \text{KL}(q_t \parallel p_t)$: Indicates where the student deviates from the teacher.
The weight $w(t) = \hat{H}(t) + \hat{\Delta}(t) - \hat{H}(t) \cdot \hat{\Delta}(t)$ is high when at least one signal is high (student is uncertain or confidently wrong) and low when both signals are low (student is confident and aligned with the teacher). This down-weights positions where the student has already converged.

#### 3.4. Negative Samples: GRPO Branch

For negative samples $y_k \in N$, GRPO is applied. Each trajectory receives a binary reward $r_k=1$ if its answer matches the pseudo-label, and $r_k=0$ otherwise. Advantages $A_k$ are computed group-relatively over all $K$ rollouts, resulting in negative advantages for negative samples, leading to a penalty:
$L_{\text{GRPO}}(k) = - \frac{A_k}{T_k} \sum_{t=1}^{T_k} m(t) \cdot \log \pi_\theta(y_k^{(t)} | x, y_k^{(<t)})$
where $m(t)$ is a binary token mask.

**Token Masking**: Since GRPO acts only on negative samples in this asymmetric framework, the usual counterbalance from positive-advantage gradients is absent. This necessitates actively reducing "False Penalties" on locally correct tokens within failed trajectories. A scoring function $s(t) = -\log \pi_\theta(y_k^{(t)} | x, y_k^{(<t)}) \cdot (1 - \hat{H}(t))$ is used, where the negative log-probability primarily ranks tokens, and normalized certainty $(1 - \hat{H}(t))$ enhances scores for confidently wrong outputs. A binary mask $m(t) = \mathbb{1}[s(t) \geq \text{median}(\{s(t')\}_{t'=1}^{T_k})]$ selects the top 50% of tokens by score. This focuses penalties on confident errors and their immediate consequences, avoiding damage to valid computational skills.

#### 3.5. Unified Objective

The final TTPO objective combines both branches, with a weighting factor $\lambda$ for the GRPO loss:
$L_{\text{TTPO}} = \frac{1}{|P|} \sum_{k \in P} L_{\text{OPSD}}(k) + \lambda \left( \frac{1}{|N|} \sum_{k \in N} L_{\text{GRPO}}(k) \right)$
The training procedure involves iterative steps of majority voting, teacher/student forward passes, calculating OPSD loss on positive samples, GRPO loss on negative samples, and then updating the policy parameters.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

TTPO is evaluated on Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B models, fine-tuned with LoRA. Two settings are considered:
1. **OpenThoughts setting**: Models are trained on labeled data, but TTPO itself does not use labels for its training, only for comparison with label-dependent baselines.
2. **TTT setting**: Models train directly on the test set without any labels.
Baselines include label-supervised OPSD and GRPO, and label-free TTRL and OPSD-TTT (self-distillation using the model's own deterministic output as privileged information). Evaluation is performed on five competition-level math benchmarks (AIME 2025/2026, HMMT 2025/2026, BRUMO 2025) using Avg@12 accuracy with temperature 1.0. For TTPO, $K=64$ trajectories are sampled, with $K_{\text{train}}=8$ (50% positive, 50% negative) selected for gradient updates, and $\lambda=0.1$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several findings across different experimental settings and ablation studies, demonstrating the effectiveness and characteristics of TTPO.

#### 4.1. Performance with Labeled Training Data (OpenThoughts Setting)

In the OpenThoughts setting, where label-supervised methods have access to ground-truth labels, TTPO (which uses only majority-vote pseudo-labels) achieved performance comparable to or exceeding label-supervised OPSD.
- **Qwen3-1.7B**: TTPO achieved an average accuracy of 40.1%, surpassing OPSD (39.7%) and GRPO (35.7%). The base model scored 34.6%.
- **Qwen3-4B**: TTPO (58.6%) slightly outperformed OPSD (58.4%) and GRPO (57.5%). The base model was 56.0%.
- **Qwen3-8B**: TTPO (62.6%) again slightly exceeded OPSD (61.7%) and GRPO (61.2%). The base model achieved 58.6%.
This indicates that TTPO's asymmetric objective, combined with pseudo-labels, can effectively substitute for ground-truth annotations without sacrificing performance, even in environments where ground truth is available. Notably, TTPO on Qwen3-4B (58.6% average) matched the performance of the Qwen3-8B base model, suggesting an amplification of reasoning capacity.

#### 4.2. Label-free Test-Time Training (TTT Setting)

In the purely label-free TTT setting, TTPO consistently and substantially outperformed existing label-free baselines.
- **Qwen3-1.7B**: TTPO achieved 45.2% average accuracy, representing a 7.2 percentage point absolute gain over the base model (38.0%). This was +3.3 points higher than OPSD-TTT (41.9%) and +5.4 points higher than TTRL (40.2%).
- **Qwen3-4B**: TTPO reached 61.1% average, outperforming OPSD-TTT (59.4%) and TTRL (58.8%).
- **Qwen3-8B**: TTPO attained 65.3% average, surpassing OPSD-TTT (63.7%) and TTRL (63.0%).
The performance gap over TTRL indicates the value of dense, token-level distributional guidance from the teacher, while the gap over OPSD-TTT (which also uses self-distillation but on deterministic outputs) highlights the effectiveness of TTPO's asymmetric design in exploiting negative samples through selective RL penalties. TTPO on Qwen3-4B (61.1%) again surpassed the Qwen3-8B base model (60.7%).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Token-level Selection

Ablations demonstrated the individual contributions of token-level selection mechanisms:
- **Removing positive-sample weighting**: Reduced AIME26 accuracy from 46.5% to 43.3%, indicating that down-weighting already-converged positions prevents dilution of gradients from informative tokens.
- **Removing negative-sample masking**: Reduced AIME26 accuracy from 46.5% to 45.4%, suggesting that selective penalization of confident errors is crucial to avoid collateral damage to locally correct reasoning steps and reduce noise from anomalous tokens.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on Update Strategy

A comparison of different update strategy combinations validated the asymmetric design:
- The full TTPO (Forward KL on Positive, GRPO on Negative) with 48.9% on AIME26 significantly outperformed all alternatives.
- Using only FKL on positive samples (46.7%) outperformed FKL on all samples (46.3%), indicating the robustness of "thinking-to-non-thinking" distillation even with potentially incorrect pseudo-labels.
- GRPO was found to be better suited for negative samples due to its robustness to pseudo-label errors in identifying genuinely wrong trajectories. Applying GRPO to positive samples or FKL to negative samples led to poorer performance, confirming the non-triviality of the asymmetric assignment.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Privileged Information and Teacher Thinking Mode

- Using a thinking-mode teacher combined with a short answer as privileged information offered the best trade-off (46.5% accuracy). This setup allows the teacher to reason with a light hint, effectively distilling its thinking process.
- A full trajectory as privilege information degraded performance (41.1%), as it might constrain the teacher too much, turning it into a prefix completion task rather than independent reasoning.
- A non-thinking teacher generally performed worse, especially with only a short answer as privilege (33.6%), due to a smaller teacher-student gap and higher sensitivity to pseudo-label noise.

#### 4.6. Cross-Benchmark Generalization

Training TTPO on one benchmark (e.g., AIME 2026) consistently led to performance improvements on other, unseen benchmarks (HMMT 2026, BRUMO 2025). This demonstrates that TTPO fosters generalizable reasoning capabilities rather than overfitting to problem-specific patterns.

#### 4.7. Upper Bound with Labeled Supervision and Self-Evolution

- **Ground Truth vs. Pseudo-labels**: Surprisingly, TTPO with majority-vote pseudo-labels outperformed TTPO trained with ground-truth answers. The analysis suggested that on hard problems, ground-truth labels result in very few positive samples, effectively starving both the FKL and GRPO branches. Majority-vote labels, being easier to match, ensure a healthy positive-negative split, keeping both branches active and promoting exploration.
- **Sustainable Self-Evolution**: During training, TTPO's average accuracy (Avg@12) rose steadily towards the initial majority-vote accuracy (Maj@12). Critically, Maj@12 also continued to increase as the model improved. This demonstrated a self-evolving cycle where model enhancements lead to more accurate pseudo-labels, which in turn raise the ceiling for further training, allowing the model to surpass its initial supervision quality.

#### 4.8. Non-Thinking Evaluation

When evaluated with thinking mode disabled, TTPO achieved significantly larger gains over the base model (+25.2% for 1.7B, +30.6% for 4B, +36.4% for 8B) compared to OPSD (+7.1% for 1.7B, +5.8% for 4B, +3.5% for 8B). This suggests that TTPO more effectively transfers reasoning capabilities from the thinking-mode teacher to the student's non-thinking distribution, possibly due to the GRPO branch actively penalizing poor reasoning patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Test-Time Policy Optimization (TTPO) presents several significant implications for the advancement and deployment of large language models, particularly in domains requiring complex reasoning.

1. **Enabling Label-Free Fine-Tuning**: TTPO provides a robust method for test-time training (TTT) that eliminates the need for ground-truth labels during adaptation. This is critical for real-world applications where obtaining high-quality, dense labels for specific tasks, especially for complex reasoning, is often impractical, costly, or impossible.
2. **Robust Dense Supervision in TTT**: The work successfully addresses a key challenge in TTT: how to leverage dense, token-level supervision, previously thought to be fragile without ground-truth labels, under noisy pseudo-labels. By introducing an asymmetric objective and token-level selection, TTPO demonstrates that such supervision can be not only robust but also highly effective.
3. **Efficiency in Learning from Noisy Labels**: The core insight regarding the asymmetric nature of pseudo-label errors (i.e., disagreeing rollouts are often genuinely wrong regardless of pseudo-label accuracy) offers a novel perspective on learning from noisy self-generated data. This approach minimizes the "blast radius" of pseudo-label errors, ensuring that learning signals are applied where they are most reliable, thereby improving training efficiency and stability.
4. **Self-Evolving LLMs**: The demonstration of a "self-evolving" cycle, where improved model performance leads to higher-quality pseudo-labels which in turn further enhance training, signifies a step towards truly autonomous and continually improving LLM agents. This capacity for sustainable self-improvement can extend the lifetime and adaptability of models without external human intervention.
5. **Enhanced Reasoning Transfer to Non-Thinking Mode**: TTPO's ability to achieve significantly larger gains in non-thinking evaluation, compared to OPSD, indicates a more effective internalization of reasoning capabilities. This suggests that the model is learning more fundamental reasoning skills rather than merely mimicking thinking-mode outputs, making the improvements more broadly applicable during inference.
6. **Potential for Smaller Model Performance Amplification**: The observation that TTPO can enable smaller models (e.g., Qwen3-4B) to match or exceed the performance of larger, untuned base models (e.g., Qwen3-8B) highlights its potential for improving computational efficiency. This could allow for the deployment of more capable reasoning models within tighter resource constraints.
7. **Generalizable Reasoning Improvements**: The evidence of cross-task generalization indicates that TTPO is fostering underlying reasoning abilities rather than just overfitting to specific problem sets. This implies that the trained models acquire transferable skills that benefit performance across different but related reasoning tasks.

The implications of TTPO extend beyond mathematical reasoning, suggesting a framework for self-supervised learning that could be adapted to other complex generative tasks where verifiable outcomes can generate noisy pseudo-labels, such as code generation or logical deduction. This work contributes to making advanced LLM capabilities more accessible and robust in practical, label-scarce environments.
