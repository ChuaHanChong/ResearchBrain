# SpaCE-10: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models in Compositional Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> SpaCE-10, a new benchmark, evaluates Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in compositional spatial intelligence by defining 10 atomic spatial capabilities and 8 complex question-answer types across over 800 real indoor scenes. Evaluations showed current MLLMs achieved a maximum of 55.0% accuracy, significantly below human performance of 91.2%, with particular struggles in counting and integrating multiple spatial skills.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks lack comprehensive and systematic evaluation of compositional spatial intelligence, often focusing on isolated or less complex spatial capabilities.
- Many current benchmarks are limited in terms of scenario diversity, scale, modalities, and question types, hindering thorough assessment of advanced MLLMs.
- A clear framework was missing to explicitly define and systematically combine atomic spatial capabilities for rigorous evaluation of real-world spatial reasoning.

## Method

- Introduced SpaCE-10, a comprehensive benchmark featuring ==10 atomic spatial capabilities== and ==8 compositional QA types==, built upon 811 diverse real indoor scenes from multiple 3D datasets.
- Developed a dataset of over **5,000** high-quality question-answer pairs, supporting both ==2D imagery and 3D point cloud inputs==, annotated through a five-stage hierarchical pipeline combining human expertise and advanced LLMs.
- Conducted extensive evaluations on nearly **50 MLLMs**, encompassing both open-source and closed-source models, to establish current performance levels in compositional spatial intelligence.

## Results

- InternVL3.5-241B-A28B was the top open-source MLLM with **55.0%** overall accuracy, outperforming leading closed-source models like GPT-5 (**53.4%**) and GPT-4o (**49.0%**).
- 2D MLLMs generally outperformed 3D MLLMs on the benchmark, suggesting greater proficiency in inferring spatial intelligence from image-based inputs.
- MLLM accuracy declined significantly for multiple-answer questions and when more atomic capabilities were integrated into a task, showing an average accuracy decrease of up to **42.9%** for functional reasoning tasks requiring multiple integrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs are significantly behind human performance in compositional spatial intelligence, with the best model achieving 55.0% accuracy compared to human experts' 91.2%.
> - Integrating multiple atomic spatial capabilities into tasks leads to substantial drops in MLLM accuracy, highlighting a fundamental difficulty in compositional reasoning.
> - The 'Counting' (C5) capability consistently emerged as the weakest across all evaluated MLLMs, indicating a persistent challenge in discrete numerosity, especially in complex spatial contexts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has experienced rapid advancements, demonstrating remarkable capabilities in various multimodal tasks and progressively narrowing the performance gap with human intelligence. This progress has, in turn, fueled an intensified pursuit of higher machine intelligence, particularly in understanding and interacting with the real world. A critical aspect of real-world intelligence is spatial reasoning, which encompasses a multitude of capabilities, such as recognizing objects, localizing them in space, understanding their relationships, and planning actions based on this understanding.

However, the authors identify a significant gap in the existing research landscape: current benchmarks struggle to comprehensively and systematically evaluate the *compositional spatial intelligence* of MLLMs. Earlier benchmarks (e.g., Azuma et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2022; Yan et al., 2023) primarily focused on evaluating atomic or less-combined spatial capabilities, such as object recognition and basic localization. While more recent benchmarks (e.g., Yang et al., 2024; Linghu et al., 2024; Jia et al., 2025) have begun to explore more compositional questions, they often fail to explicitly define the underlying spatial capabilities or to systematically combine them in a way that truly reflects real-world compositional reasoning. Furthermore, many existing benchmarks are limited in terms of the diversity and scale of their scenarios (often fewer than 400 scenes), modalities, and question types, thereby hindering a thorough evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs.

SpaCE-10 directly addresses these shortcomings by offering a novel, comprehensive benchmark. It distinguishes itself by:
1. **Explicitly defining atomic spatial capabilities:** Introducing a pool of 10 core atomic spatial capabilities (C1-C10) that form the building blocks of spatial intelligence.
2. **Systematically combining capabilities:** Designing 8 compositional QA types, each engineered to integrate multiple atomic capabilities, thereby evaluating complex, real-world reasoning.
3. **Enhanced diversity and scale:** Utilizing over 800 real indoor scenes from multiple 3D datasets, significantly expanding the scenario coverage compared to previous benchmarks.
4. **Multi-modal and multi-type evaluation:** Supporting both 2D imagery and 3D point cloud inputs, and including various question formats, such as multi-choice and multi-answer questions, to provide a more holistic assessment.

By providing a granular yet comprehensive framework for evaluating compositional spatial intelligence, SpaCE-10 positions itself as a critical tool for advancing MLLM research, moving beyond isolated capabilities to assess a model's ability to integrate and apply diverse spatial knowledge in complex scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind SpaCE-10 stems from a fundamental question posed by the authors: "Can current MLLMs master these spatial capabilities and compose them seamlessly in real-world scenarios?" This query arises from the observation that even seemingly simple real-world tasks, like a home robot retrieving a watch, necessitate the on-the-fly composition of numerous spatial capabilities, from object recognition and localization to understanding spatial relations and planning.

The key objectives of this research are therefore multi-faceted:
1. **To establish a comprehensive evaluation framework:** The primary objective is to develop SpaCE-10 as a benchmark that can systematically and explicitly assess the compositional spatial intelligence (CSI) of MLLMs, covering a wide spectrum of spatial reasoning tasks.
2. **To explicitly define and categorize spatial capabilities:** To formalize and introduce 10 atomic spatial capabilities (C1-C10) that underpin general spatial intelligence, providing a clear lexicon for researchers.
3. **To design compositional tasks:** To create 8 meticulously designed QA types that demand the integration of multiple atomic capabilities, thereby mirroring the complexity of real-world spatial reasoning.
4. **To generate high-quality, diverse, and controllable data:** To construct a dataset of over 5,000 question-answer pairs across 811 diverse indoor scenes, ensuring variety in scenarios, modalities (2D and 3D), and question formats, using a robust hierarchical annotation pipeline.
5. **To identify strengths and limitations of current MLLMs:** To conduct extensive evaluations of a wide range of MLLMs to pinpoint their current performance levels, specific strengths, and critical weaknesses in compositional spatial intelligence.
6. **To provide actionable insights for future MLLM development:** Based on the evaluation findings, to offer valuable directions and guidance for the MLLM community to develop more capable models with advanced spatial intelligence.

Ultimately, the motivation is to push the boundaries of machine intelligence closer to human-level understanding and interaction with the physical world, by providing the necessary tools to rigorously measure and advance spatial reasoning in MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of SpaCE-10 employs a rigorous and innovative methodology centered around a hierarchical annotation pipeline to ensure the generation of high-quality, diverse, and controllable question-answer (QA) pairs for compositional spatial evaluation.

**Benchmark Design:**
SpaCE-10 is constructed upon 811 real indoor scenes sourced from four prominent 3D datasets: ScanNet++, ScanNet (SCN), 3RScan (3RS), and ARkitScene (ARK). These datasets offer a rich variety of environments, from living rooms and classrooms to bathrooms and kitchens.
The benchmark defines:
- **10 Atomic Spatial Capabilities (C1-C10):** These are fundamental skills, including Object Recognition (C1), Spatial Localization (C2), Spatial Relationship (C3), Size Comparison (C4), Counting (C5), Function Knowledge (C6), Multi-view Fusion (C7), Forward Thinking (C8), Reverse Reasoning (C9), and Situated Observation (C10).
- **8 Compositional QA Types:** These categories combine multiple atomic capabilities to form complex reasoning tasks. They include Entity Quantification (EQ), Scene Quantification (SQ), Size Assessment (SA), Object-Object Spatial Relationship (OO), Object-Scene Spatial Relationship (OS), Entity Presence (EP), Functional Reasoning (FR), and Spatial Planning (SP). Each QA type is designed to cover at least five atomic capabilities, demonstrating their compositional nature.

**Hierarchical Annotation Pipeline:**
The annotation process involves a five-stage, semi-automated pipeline that leverages both human expertise and advanced LLMs (GPT-4o), requiring over 150 hours of human expert effort.

1. **Data Preparation (Stage 1):** Human experts (3 individuals) manually collect 4-6 snapshots of 3D point cloud scans from various directions for each of the 800+ indoor scenes, dedicating over 38 hours to ensure high-quality visual representation.
2. **Structural Data Generation (Stage 2):** An automated pipeline processes these visual inputs to create structured scene descriptions. This involves:
 - Selecting 10 keyframes from scene videos using CLIP vision encoder and k-means clustering.
 - Generating 2D captions for each keyframe via GPT-4o, detailing appearance, size, and spatial relationships.
 - Refining these 2D captions using GPT-4o as an "inspector" to remove inaccuracies and redundancies.
 - Generating 3D captions by combining the manually collected 3D snapshots with keyframes, capturing global scene-level spatial information.
 - Refining 3D captions with the GPT-4o inspector.
 - Applying a rule-based extractor to distill structural data from the refined captions for subsequent QA generation.
3. **Basic QA Generation (Stage 3):** This stage generates over 10,000 "basic" QA pairs focusing on atomic capabilities.
 - EQ uses a template-based method based on semantic labels.
 - SP questions are manually designed by human experts.
 - Other QA types (SA, OO, OS, EP, FR) are generated by GPT-4o using the structured data.
4. **Quality Verification (Stage 4):** Three human experts manually filter the generated QA pairs. This intensive process, consuming over 112 hours, checks for incorrect options, invalid answers, missing data, or questions referring to non-existent objects, ensuring only high-quality QA pairs are retained (resulting in over 8,000 pairs). An annotation interface designed for 3D snapshot viewing (not 2D images) aids efficiency and consistency.
5. **Cross-Capability Integration (Stage 5):** A template-based strategy integrates atomic capabilities into the final compositional QA types, yielding over 5,000 high-quality QA pairs.
 - For SA, OO, OS, C7 (Multi-view Fusion) is integrated by designing options to refer to different objects across the scene, necessitating holistic spatial perception.
 - For EP, C7 (Multi-view Fusion) and C9 (Reverse Reasoning) are integrated by expanding questions to multiple objects and reversing the query (e.g., "which object does not exist").
 - For FR, C7 (Multi-view Fusion), C9 (Reverse Reasoning), and C10 (Situated Observation) are integrated by varying central objects and structuring options to avoid prior knowledge leakage, emphasizing functional understanding and contextual observation.

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation involved nearly 50 MLLMs, encompassing 4 closed-source and approximately 46 open-source models, ranging from 1B to 241B parameters. This includes specialized 3D MLLMs (LEO, GPT4Scene) and generalist 2D MLLMs (GPT-5, GPT-4o, InternVL, Qwen, etc.). Models were typically evaluated using 8 input frames (for 2D MLLMs), while 3D MLLMs like LEO received sampled point clouds. The LMMs-Eval framework was used for evaluation, with GPT-4o-2024-11-20 employed for response and answer alignment, and accuracy as the primary metric. Human performance was established by averaging the scores of 6 human experts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of nearly 50 Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on the SpaCE-10 benchmark yielded several critical findings regarding their compositional spatial intelligence:

**Human vs. MLLMs:**
A stark performance disparity was observed between humans and even the most advanced MLLMs. Human experts achieved an average score of 91.2% on SpaCE-10, demonstrating robust compositional spatial intelligence. In contrast, the best open-source MLLM achieved only 55.0%, and the top-performing closed-source model (GPT-5) reached 53.4%. This significant margin highlights that current MLLMs are still far from human-level competence in understanding and reasoning about complex spatial scenarios.

**3D MLLMs vs. 2D MLLMs:**
The results indicated that 2D MLLMs (models primarily designed for image-based understanding) significantly outperformed 3D MLLMs (models directly processing point cloud data) on SpaCE-10. For instance, LEO-7B, a 3D MLLM, scored a mere 11.1%, and GPT4Scene-7B achieved 34.5%. This performance is notably lower than 2D MLLMs of comparable scale. The authors suggest that current 3D MLLMs might be limited by their focus on specific objects rather than entire scene processing and potentially sacrifice multimodal conversational abilities to integrate 3D scans. This finding underscores the greater potential of 2D MLLMs in visual spatial intelligence comprehension.

**Open-Source vs. Close-Source MLLMs:**
While closed-source models like GPT-5 (53.4%) and GPT-4o (49.0%) showed strong performance, especially in perception tasks (e.g., SA, OO, FR for GPT-5; EQ for GPT-4o), the gap with open-source models has significantly narrowed. InternVL3.5-241B-A28B emerged as the top open-source model, achieving an impressive 55.0% overall accuracy, outperforming several closed-source counterparts and leading across most tasks. This indicates rapid advancements in the open-source community, challenging the dominance of proprietary models in compositional spatial intelligence. GPT-4o notably struggled with Spatial Planning (SP), scoring the lowest among all tasks, suggesting limitations in scene-level planning.

**Single-Answer vs. Multiple-Answer Questions:**
MLLMs demonstrated a marked decline in performance when confronted with multiple-answer questions compared to single-answer tasks. Smaller models (e.g., InternVL2.5-1B and 2B) often saw their accuracy plummet from over 30% on single-answer tasks to below 5% on multiple-answer questions, sometimes even below random chance. While larger models exhibited greater robustness, this trend suggests that smaller models might overfit to the single-answer format, whereas larger models may have acquired more fundamental compositional spatial intelligence, albeit still insufficient for complex multi-choice scenarios. An exception was noted in the Qwen series, where smaller models performed relatively better in multiple-answer tasks.

**Atomic Capability vs. Compositional Capabilities:**
The study rigorously demonstrated the impact of integrating multiple atomic capabilities on model performance. As more capabilities were incorporated into QA types, MLLM accuracy significantly dropped. For instance, integrating C7 (Multi-view Fusion) into SA, OO, and OS tasks led to average accuracy decreases of 19.1%, 7.6%, and 21.0%, respectively. The addition of C7 and C9 (Reverse Reasoning) to EP tasks resulted in a substantial 41.0% drop, and integrating C7, C9, and C10 (Situated Observation) into FR tasks caused an even larger 42.9% decline. These findings unequivocally highlight the limitations of current MLLMs in integrated spatial intelligence and validate the necessity of SpaCE-10's compositional evaluation approach.

**Spatial Capability Breakdown:**
A detailed analysis of atomic capabilities revealed consistent patterns:
- **Strengths:** C4 (Size Comparison) was consistently the strongest atomic capability across almost all models, suggesting a relative proficiency in continuous magnitude judgments.
- **Weaknesses:** C5 (Counting) was uniformly the weakest capability, even for top models like InternVL3.5-241B (37.5% vs. human 89.9%). This indicates a persistent challenge in discrete numerosity, especially under occlusion, clutter, or across multiple views.
- **Accuracy vs. Capability:** The study found that high overall task accuracy does not necessarily equate to high mastery across all atomic capabilities. Models with similar overall accuracies could have different underlying capability profiles. This distinction underscores the value of capability-based analysis, as relying solely on accuracy can be misleading about a model's true spatial reasoning generalizability.
- **Scaling Effects:** While increasing model parameters generally improved overall performance, this scaling did not uniformly enhance all capabilities. Counting (C5) remained a significant bottleneck, suggesting that parameter scaling alone is insufficient for qualitative improvements in this area. Models like GPT-4o and GLM-4.5V achieved comparatively superior scores in C5, indicating that architectural design, specialized training strategies, and data diversity might play a more crucial role for certain capabilities than raw model size.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SpaCE-10 represents a significant advancement in the evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with profound implications for the future direction of AI research in spatial intelligence. The paper's contributions and potential impacts can be summarized as follows:

**1. A Comprehensive and Granular Evaluation Standard:**
SpaCE-10 stands as the most comprehensive and diverse benchmark for compositional spatial intelligence to date. By explicitly defining 10 atomic spatial capabilities and systematically integrating them into 8 compositional QA types, it offers an unprecedented level of granularity and realism in evaluating MLLMs. Its coverage of over 800 real indoor scenes, support for both 2D imagery and 3D point cloud inputs, and inclusion of multi-choice and multi-answer questions establish a new, rigorous standard for the field. This benchmark will likely become a go-to resource for researchers aiming to assess and compare MLLM performance in spatial reasoning.

**2. Identification of Critical MLLM Limitations and Bottlenecks:**
The extensive evaluations on nearly 50 MLLMs, ranging from open-source to closed-source, revealed that even the most advanced models lag significantly behind human performance in compositional spatial intelligence. Crucially, the study pinpointed specific weaknesses:
- **Compositional Reasoning Deficiencies:** MLLMs struggle significantly when required to integrate multiple atomic capabilities, indicating a foundational challenge in combining diverse spatial knowledge on the fly.
- **Counting as a Major Bottleneck:** The consistently low performance across all models in the Counting (C5) capability highlights it as a critical and persistent challenge, particularly under complex real-world conditions like occlusion or clutter. This suggests that current visual processing and reasoning mechanisms are insufficient for robust discrete numerosity in spatial contexts.
- **Multi-Answer Complexity:** The marked drop in accuracy for multiple-answer questions reveals limitations in complex reasoning and robustness, especially for smaller models.

**3. Guiding Future MLLM Development:**
The findings provide invaluable insights and clear directions for the MLLM community:
- **Capability-Aware Training:** The divergence between high overall accuracy and varying atomic capability mastery suggests that future efforts should move beyond simple task completion rates. Instead, training methodologies should be "capability-aware," specifically designed to shore up weak atomic capabilities like counting, situated observation (C10), and spatial relationships (C3).
- **Targeted Data Curation:** For persistent challenges like counting, the authors recommend curating specialized training datasets that emphasize scenarios involving occlusion, crowding, and multi-view consistency. This data-centric approach can directly address specific deficiencies.
- **Beyond Scaling:** While model scaling generally improves performance, it was shown to be insufficient for overcoming certain bottlenecks (e.g., counting). This indicates that architectural innovations, novel training strategies (e.g., post-training tailored to capability composition), and enhanced data diversity might be more critical than simply increasing model size for qualitative leaps in spatial intelligence.
- **Focus on 2D MLLMs for Spatial Reasoning:** The superior performance of 2D MLLMs over their 3D counterparts suggests that image-based models hold significant potential for spatial intelligence, possibly by avoiding the complexities of directly processing point cloud data while still inferring rich 3D spatial information.

**4. Advancing Embodied AI and Real-World Applications:**
Ultimately, improving compositional spatial intelligence is paramount for the development of truly intelligent agents capable of operating effectively in the real world. Benchmarks like SpaCE-10 are instrumental for advancing embodied AI, robotics, and other applications where machines need to perceive, understand, and interact with physical environments in a human-like manner. By elucidating current limitations and offering concrete pathways for improvement, this work paves the way for MLLMs that can achieve more robust, generalized, and human-level spatial reasoning.

In conclusion, SpaCE-10 is not merely another benchmark; it is a meticulously crafted diagnostic tool that provides a fine-grained understanding of MLLMs' spatial intelligence, identifying crucial areas for research and development, and thereby offering an invaluable hint for future progress toward human-level machine intelligence.
