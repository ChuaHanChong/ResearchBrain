# SpaCE-10: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models in Compositional Spatial Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> SpaCE-10, a new benchmark, evaluates Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in compositional spatial intelligence by defining 10 atomic spatial capabilities and 8 complex question-answer types across over 800 real indoor scenes. Evaluations showed current MLLMs achieved a maximum of 55.0% accuracy, significantly below human performance of 91.2%, with particular struggles in counting and integrating multiple spatial skills.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks lack comprehensive and systematic evaluation of compositional spatial intelligence, often focusing on isolated or less complex spatial capabilities.
- Many current benchmarks are limited in terms of scenario diversity, scale, modalities, and question types, hindering thorough assessment of advanced MLLMs.
- A clear framework was missing to explicitly define and systematically combine atomic spatial capabilities for rigorous evaluation of real-world spatial reasoning.

## Method

- Introduced SpaCE-10, a comprehensive benchmark featuring ==10 atomic spatial capabilities== and ==8 compositional QA types==, built upon 811 diverse real indoor scenes from multiple 3D datasets.
- Developed a dataset of over **5,000** high-quality question-answer pairs, supporting both ==2D imagery and 3D point cloud inputs==, annotated through a five-stage hierarchical pipeline combining human expertise and advanced LLMs.
- Conducted extensive evaluations on nearly **50 MLLMs**, encompassing both open-source and closed-source models, to establish current performance levels in compositional spatial intelligence.

## Results

- InternVL3.5-241B-A28B was the top open-source MLLM with **55.0%** overall accuracy, outperforming leading closed-source models like GPT-5 (**53.4%**) and GPT-4o (**49.0%**).
- 2D MLLMs generally outperformed 3D MLLMs on the benchmark, suggesting greater proficiency in inferring spatial intelligence from image-based inputs.
- MLLM accuracy declined significantly for multiple-answer questions and when more atomic capabilities were integrated into a task, showing an average accuracy decrease of up to **42.9%** for functional reasoning tasks requiring multiple integrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs are significantly behind human performance in compositional spatial intelligence, with the best model achieving 55.0% accuracy compared to human experts' 91.2%.
> - Integrating multiple atomic spatial capabilities into tasks leads to substantial drops in MLLM accuracy, highlighting a fundamental difficulty in compositional reasoning.
> - The 'Counting' (C5) capability consistently emerged as the weakest across all evaluated MLLMs, indicating a persistent challenge in discrete numerosity, especially in complex spatial contexts.
