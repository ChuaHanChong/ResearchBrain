# InTraGen: Trajectory-controlled Video Generation for Object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called InTraGen enables the generation of videos with precisely controlled object interactions by employing a multi-modal interaction encoding pipeline with explicit object ID injection. This approach produces more realistic and trajectory-adherent video content, outperforming existing baselines in depicting complex multi-object scenes.

## Problem

- Current Text-to-Video (T2V) models struggle to generate realistic object interactions due to ambiguous text prompts and inadequate temporal reasoning.
- Existing trajectory-guided methods, often relying on sparse optical flow, fail to distinguish individual objects or maintain their identities during close interactions.
- There is a need for fine-grained control over dynamic, static, and interactive information for multiple objects within generated video scenes.

## Method

- **InTraGen** integrates a ==Diffusion Transformer== with a novel ==multi-modal== interaction encoding pipeline to guide video generation.
- The encoding mechanism combines sparse pose encoding for dynamic object motion with unique object ID maps to preserve individual identities and model static entities.
- The framework introduces new synthetic video interaction datasets (ViN Dataset) and a novel Matching Trajectory Evaluation Metric (MTEM) for training and evaluating interaction quality.

## Results

- InTraGen reduced the MTEM score to 9.40% from 22.17% (Open-Sora-Plan) and improved FID from **40.65** to **26.37**, indicating higher trajectory adherence and visual quality.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the object ID encoding component significantly enhances performance across all video evaluation metrics, validating its importance.
- User studies rated InTraGen's interaction realism (**3.24** ± **1.41**) substantially higher than baselines, demonstrating strong capabilities in simulating diverse interaction environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding unique object identities, alongside motion information, is crucial for generating realistic and consistent object interactions in videos.
> - Leveraging trajectory-based control signals can effectively mitigate the limitations of text ambiguity and temporal reasoning deficiencies in T2V generation.
> - Synthetic datasets with diverse interaction scenarios and ground truth trajectories are vital for training and evaluating models focused on fine-grained object interaction control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced substantial advancements, primarily driven by diffusion models and the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, which has gained prominence over U-Net-based models for tasks involving complex temporal patterns. Text-to-Video (T2V) generation, enabling video creation from text descriptions, represents a significant area of interest, with models like OpenAI’s Sora and Meta’s Movie Gen demonstrating advanced capabilities. However, open-source models continue to face challenges in achieving comparable quality, particularly concerning realistic object interactions.

Current T2V methods encounter two primary limitations: text ambiguity and temporal reasoning deficiency. Text ambiguity arises because video captioning models often fail to capture the detailed dynamics and complex interactions within a video, providing generalized descriptions that lack granular temporal information. Furthermore, text encoders used in generative models, such as T5 and CLIP, are predominantly trained on static images. This training paradigm limits their capacity to comprehend and translate temporal cues from text prompts into coherent video sequences, frequently resulting in a misrepresentation of event timing and object appearance sequences.

To address these limitations, researchers have explored incorporating additional control signals beyond text. While control modalities like Canny edges and depth maps have proven effective in image generation (e.g., ControlNet), applying similar signals to the video domain is more complex due to motion dynamics. Trajectories, which define object coordinates across frames, represent a more suitable control signal for video. They can be extracted from existing videos or specified by users.

Existing trajectory-guided T2V approaches, such as those conditioning diffusion models on sparse optical flow, often overlook the distinction between dynamic (moving) and static (stationary) objects. These methods also face difficulties in precisely associating unique trajectories with individual objects, leading to issues with object identity and appearance consistency during interactions. Sparse optical flow, while capturing movement, does not inherently account for stationary objects or the specific identity of each object involved in an interaction.

This work, InTraGen, positions itself within this landscape by introducing a pipeline designed to improve trajectory-based video generation specifically for object interaction scenarios. It aims to overcome the identified challenges by explicitly modeling dynamic, static, and interactive information while uniquely associating distinct trajectories with individual objects. This focus on "interaction-level control" seeks to advance the realism and controllability of generated videos, particularly in complex multi-object scenes, by providing more precise guidance than prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust trajectory-controlled video generation framework, InTraGen, that can produce videos featuring realistic and accurately controlled object interactions. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and leads to a set of defined objectives:

**Motivations:**

1. **Addressing Text Ambiguity in T2V:** Current text-to-video generation models often rely on video captions that are generated from large-scale datasets. These captions frequently lack the specificity required to describe complex object interactions and precise temporal sequences. This inherent ambiguity in text prompts hinders the ability of generative models to produce detailed and accurate interactive scenes.
2. **Mitigating Temporal Reasoning Deficiency:** Text encoders used in contemporary video generation models are typically pre-trained on image-text pairs, limiting their capacity to interpret and reason about temporal information within text prompts. This deficiency can lead to generated videos where objects appear prematurely or events unfold without the intended temporal progression, failing to capture the dynamic nature of interactions.
3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Trajectory-Guided Methods:** While trajectory-based control signals offer a promising avenue for enhancing video generation, current methods often rely on sparse optical flow. This approach has limitations, as it primarily captures dynamic motion and struggles to differentiate between closely interacting objects or account for static objects. Such methods frequently lead to issues like objects losing their identity, shape-shifting, or exhibiting inconsistent appearances during interactions.
4. **Enabling Interaction-Level Control:** There is a need for a generation framework that provides fine-grained control over how multiple objects interact within a scene, encompassing dynamic movement, static presence, and specific interactive events like collisions, crossings, or falling. Achieving this level of control requires a mechanism that can uniquely identify and track individual objects throughout the video.

### 3. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations, the research outlines the following key objectives:

1. **Propose a Novel Trajectory-Controlled Video Generation Model (InTraGen):** To design and implement a framework specifically engineered to leverage trajectory information for generating videos with rich and realistic object interactions.
2. **Introduce a Multi-Modal Interaction Encoding Pipeline:** To develop a mechanism that can effectively capture and integrate dynamic, static, and interactive information from input trajectories. This includes an object ID injection mechanism to ensure unique identification of interacting entities.
3. **Create Comprehensive Video Interaction Datasets:** To address the scarcity of datasets suitable for evaluating models on complex object interactions, the research aims to generate four new synthetic video datasets with diverse interaction scenarios and ground truth trajectory information.
4. **Develop a Novel Trajectory Quality Metric:** To establish a quantitative measure that can accurately assess the adherence of generated videos to input trajectories and the quality of depicted object interactions.
5. **Demonstrate Improvements in Visual Fidelity and Quantitative Performance:** To validate the effectiveness of the proposed InTraGen model by showing measurable enhancements in the realism of generated videos and their adherence to specified interaction conditions.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The InTraGen pipeline integrates a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture with a novel multi-modal interaction encoding mechanism and utilizes custom-designed datasets for training and evaluation.

#### 4.1. Model Backbone and Preliminary

The model utilizes a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) as its core denoising network, following architectures seen in works like Open-Sora-Plan and Latte. Videos are first encoded into a latent space using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), and the diffusion process involves iteratively denoising these latents. The DiT architecture employs separate spatial and temporal transformers to manage computational complexity and focus on distinct dimensions of video data. Text conditioning is integrated into the spatial transformer via cross-attention, as current video captioning models do not provide frame-by-frame textual descriptions, which could lead to temporal inconsistencies if used with temporal transformers.

#### 4.2. Object Interaction Modeling

The method conceptualizes object motion into three components:
- **Dynamic Information:** Refers to moving objects and their motion.
- **Static Information:** Pertains to stationary objects, acknowledging that objects can transition between static and dynamic states.
- **Interactive Information:** Encompasses interactions between objects, such as collisions, bouncing, or visual interactions like crossing or overlapping.

The research emphasizes that previous methods often failed to adequately capture these distinctions, particularly the unique association of trajectories with individual objects during interactions.

#### 4.3. Multi-modal Interaction Encoding

This is a central component designed to capture the dynamic, static, and interactive information from input trajectories to guide the video generation model.

- **Sparse Pose Encoding:** Inspired by motion-based diffusion models, sparse optical flow is used to encode dynamic interactions. Trajectories (sequences of coordinates) are transformed into point differences (speed and direction). These are then mapped to the RGB domain using a color wheel, representing different speeds and directions. A Gaussian blur is applied to disperse the flow, facilitating model capture. Acknowledged limitation: sparse pose primarily represents moving objects and can merge when objects are in close proximity, making individual distinction challenging.
- **Object ID Encoding:** To address the limitations of sparse pose, an object ID map is introduced to encode stationary and interactive information. Instead of encoding speed, unique colors are assigned to represent unique object IDs. This mechanism allows the model to differentiate objects even when they are close, and it maintains the identity of static objects throughout the video. The colors of object ID maps remain constant over time for each object.
- **Multi-Modal Interaction Encoder:** This module comprises two independent encoders—one for sparse poses and one for object ID maps. Their outputs are then merged through a fusion network. A VAE pre-processes the interaction information, encoding it into a latent space to align with the visual tokens of the DiT.

#### 4.4. Video Interaction (ViN) Dataset

To overcome the challenges of evaluating object interactions in real-world datasets (static content, noise, lack of ground truth), a new synthetic dataset, ViN Dataset, was created. It aims to provide high control and ground truth object locations.

- **Composition:** Four subsets:
 - **MOVi-Extended:** 20k videos, 256x256 resolution, 65 frames, generated using Kubric/Blender. Features 1-10 objects, including scenarios of objects leaving the scene, transitioning between movement and stillness, and receding.
 - **Pool:** 20k videos, 1080x1080, 120 frames, generated using Blender (Bullet physics engine). Supports complex interactions with up to 16 objects, including multiple direction changes due to collisions and objects falling into pockets.
 - **Dominoes:** 5k videos, 1080x1080, 152 frames, generated using Unity (PhysX engine). Focuses on scaling the number of tracked objects (up to 20), featuring simple scenes and interactions.
 - **Football:** 5k videos, 1080x1080, 230 frames, generated using Unity. Features a goalkeeper, player, and ball, with structured backgrounds resembling real-world settings.
- **Data Generation:** Captions are generated using SharedGPT4Video, dense optical flow estimated with Unimatch, and trajectories extracted using YoloV7 where ground truth is unavailable.
- **Real-world Complement:** A subset of Panda-70M (50K cleaned videos) and SoccerNet (18K video clips) are also used to balance synthetic and real-world data, with estimated sparse flow, optical flow, and trajectory data.

#### 4.5. Trajectory Evaluation (MTEM)

A novel quantitative metric, Matching Trajectory Evaluation Metric (MTEM), is proposed to assess how closely generated videos follow input trajectories.

- **Trajectory Extraction:** From generated videos, an object detection model extracts coordinates and IDs per frame. If an object temporarily disappears, its last known coordinates are assigned to missing frames to ensure continuous trajectories.
- **Metric Calculation:** The ground truth trajectories and extracted trajectories are represented as nodes in a complete bipartite graph. The edge weight between any two trajectories is the L2 distance between their corresponding point sequences across all frames. The Hungarian matching algorithm is then applied to find the minimum-weight matching, yielding a similarity score reflecting trajectory adherence.

#### 4.6. Implementation Details

The model is built on Open-Sora-Plan, based on Latte. It employs a dense optical flow pre-training strategy similar to MotionCtrl. Training is conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for 12,000 steps with the Adam optimizer and a learning rate of 2 × 10⁻⁵.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both quantitative and qualitative results to demonstrate the performance of InTraGen, including comparisons with baselines, ablation studies, and user evaluations.

#### 5.1. Quantitative Comparison

InTraGen's performance was assessed using the proposed Matching Trajectory Evaluation Metric (MTEM) along with standard generative model metrics: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS), and Fréchet Image Distance (FID).

- **Comparison with Open-Sora-Plan:** InTraGen showed improvements across all metrics compared to the Open-Sora-Plan baseline. The MTEM score decreased from 22.17% (Open-Sora-Plan) to 9.40% (InTraGen), indicating a reduction in the deviation from specified trajectories. Similarly, FID, a measure of feature distribution similarity, improved from 40.65 to 26.37. SSIM increased from 95.42% to 96.51%, and PSNR from 24.42 to 26.35, suggesting better visual quality. LPIPS, measuring perceptual similarity, decreased from 21.39% to 12.61%, reflecting higher visual fidelity.
- **Ablation Study (Impact of Object ID):** A variant of InTraGen that excluded the object ID component (retaining only sparse pose) was also evaluated. This variant showed improved performance over Open-Sora-Plan but was inferior to the full InTraGen model. Specifically, the "Sparse pose" variant achieved an MTEM of 10.38% and an FID of 36.65, demonstrating that the explicit incorporation of object ID maps during training contributes to further improvements in all video evaluation metrics.

#### 5.2. Qualitative Comparison

Qualitative results were presented through generated video frames and comparisons with other advanced video generation models.

- **Object Interaction Realism:** InTraGen generated object interactions such as crossing, collision, and falling that closely followed input trajectories across the MOVi-Extended, Pool Game, and Dominoes datasets. The visual outputs indicated high fidelity and consistent object behavior.
- **Real-world Datasets:** The model also demonstrated control and interaction capabilities on real-world datasets like Panda-70M and SoccerNet, producing aesthetically consistent videos.
- **Comparison with Commercial Models:** In a specific example from the dominoes dataset, InTraGen produced more realistic results in an interactive scenario compared to I2VGen-XL, LUMA, and SEINE, which reportedly struggled in depicting the scene accurately.
- **Multi-view Generation:** The model demonstrated potential for multi-view video generation, capable of producing consistent video from various camera perspectives given a single fixed trajectory.

#### 5.3. User Studies

Two user studies were conducted to assess multi-object interaction realism and adherence to predefined trajectories. Participants rated videos on a scale from 0 (no realism) to 5 (highly realistic).

- **Interaction Realism on MOVi-Extended:** InTraGen (sparse pose + object ID) achieved a realism score of 3.24 ± 1.41, outperforming the sparse pose-only variant (2.01 ± 1.76) and Open-Sora-Plan (0.66 ± 1.16). Original videos scored 4.91 ± 0.35.
- **Controlled Movement and Interaction Realism:** Across MOVi-Extended, Domino, and Pool datasets, InTraGen demonstrated strong performance in both interaction realism and adherence to trajectory, with scores suggesting high capabilities in simulating diverse domain-specific environments.

#### 5.4. Limitations of Tora Comparison

A comparison with Tora, another trajectory-based diffusion model, indicated that Tora experienced object identity issues, with objects mistakenly exchanging trajectories when in close proximity. In contrast, InTraGen maintained stable trajectory paths and object identities even during close approaches, collisions, and overlaps.

#### 5.5. Identified Failing Cases

Despite its overall performance, the model exhibited limitations in certain complex scenarios. When too many objects were introduced in close proximity, the model sometimes struggled to distinguish between them, potentially due to the sparsity of textual tokens and their inability to describe the scene in sufficient detail, impacting temporal consistency. Additionally, in some instances, the model was observed to generate extraneous objects along a trajectory that would later merge into a single entity.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The InTraGen framework represents an advancement in the field of video generation, particularly in addressing the challenges associated with creating controlled and realistic object interactions. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced Controllability and Realism:** By introducing a multi-modal interaction encoding pipeline with explicit object ID injection, InTraGen provides a more granular and precise control mechanism over object trajectories and interactions than previous methods. This leads to generated videos that more accurately adhere to specified movements and exhibit higher visual fidelity and realism in complex multi-object scenes. This is crucial for applications requiring specific object behaviors, such as simulations or character animations.

2. **Mitigation of Core T2V Limitations:** The work directly addresses the fundamental challenges of text ambiguity and temporal reasoning deficiency prevalent in text-to-video generation. By leveraging trajectories as an explicit control signal, InTraGen provides the necessary temporal and spatial guidance that ambiguous text prompts and image-trained text encoders often fail to deliver, leading to more coherent and contextually accurate video outputs.

3. **Development of New Research Resources:** The creation of four diverse, high-quality synthetic video interaction datasets (Pool, Dominoes, Football, MOVi-Extended) and the introduction of the Matching Trajectory Evaluation Metric (MTEM) are valuable contributions to the research community. These resources provide standardized benchmarks and evaluation tools for future development in controllable video generation, enabling more robust testing and comparison of models.

4. **Advancement of Diffusion Transformer Models:** InTraGen's successful integration with the Diffusion Transformer architecture further validates the capabilities of DiT-based models for handling the complex temporal patterns required for video generation. This work contributes to the ongoing development of efficient and powerful generative models for video.

5. **Potential for Practical Applications:** The ability to generate videos with controlled and realistic object interactions holds potential for various applications:
 - **World Simulators:** Creating intuitive and controllable virtual environments for training AI agents or testing physical scenarios.
 - **Content Creation:** Empowering artists and creators with tools for generating dynamic scenes with precise object movements and interactions, reducing the manual effort in animation.
 - **3D Reconstruction and Robotics:** The demonstrated multi-view generation capability suggests potential applications in generating consistent video data from multiple camera poses, which could benefit research in 3D scene understanding and robot perception.
 - **Educational Tools:** Developing interactive simulations for educational purposes where object behaviors need to be clearly demonstrated.

In conclusion, InTraGen contributes to the maturation of video generation technology by offering a solution to synthesize complex object interactions with unprecedented control and realism. Its novel methodology and accompanying resources are likely to influence future research directions in controllable video generation and its diverse applications.
