# Human-Object Interaction via Automatically Designed VLM-Guided Motion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified physics-based framework is introduced for Human-Object Interaction (HOI) synthesis, leveraging a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to automatically design goal states and reward functions. This approach generates natural, long-horizon interactions with diverse object types, achieving higher completion rates and precision compared to existing baselines in multi-task scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing HOI synthesis methods rely on extensive motion capture data or labor-intensive manual reward engineering, limiting their scalability and generalizability across diverse interactions.
- Task-centric policies often overfit to specific behaviors or produce biomechanically unrealistic motions, struggling with complex, contact-rich interactions and dynamic objects.
- Prior interaction representations, such as 'chain-of-contacts,' fail to capture continuous motion dynamics, whole-body coordination, and evolving spatio-temporal relationships, leading to suboptimal or jittery behaviors.

## Method

- A VLM-Guided ==Relative Movement Dynamics== (RMD) Planner utilizes GPT-4V to translate high-level instructions and environmental context into structured interaction plans, specifying global spatial goals and dynamic patterns.
- RMD, a novel bipartite graph representation, encodes fine-grained spatial and temporal relationships between human and object parts, categorized by relative motion patterns (stationary, approaching, separating, unstable).
- An automatic policy learning framework formulates HOI as goal-conditioned reinforcement learning, using VLM-generated RMD plans to construct comprehensive goal states and design composite reward functions, balancing task and motion style objectives.

## Results

- The framework achieved an average completion rate of **75.1%** for static, **71.2%** for dynamic, and **53.8%** for hybrid multi-task HOI scenarios on the Interplay dataset, outperforming baselines which ranged from **21.3%** to **47.8%**.
- It demonstrated superior or comparable performance in single-task scenarios, with notable superiority in tasks requiring post-interaction recovery (e.g., **62.0%** completion rate for 'Lie' task vs. UniHSI **23.2%** and InterPhys **2.7%**).
- A user study indicated higher ratings for motion realism (4.0 ± **0.4**) and task consistency (4.1 ± **0.3**) compared to baselines, and ablations confirmed the critical role of VLM and RMD components in performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can effectively bridge high-level semantic instructions with low-level physics-based control, enabling fine-grained, interaction-aware motion guidance for complex HOI.
> - The Relative Movement Dynamics (RMD) representation, by explicitly encoding relative motion patterns between human and object parts, allows for more expressive and generalizable HOI policies compared to simpler contact-based abstractions.
> - Automating the generation of goal states and reward functions via VLM guidance eliminates the need for manual reward engineering, addressing a major bottleneck in physics-based reinforcement learning for HOI.
