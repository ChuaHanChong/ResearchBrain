# Human-Object Interaction via Automatically Designed VLM-Guided Motion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified physics-based framework is introduced for Human-Object Interaction (HOI) synthesis, leveraging a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to automatically design goal states and reward functions. This approach generates natural, long-horizon interactions with diverse object types, achieving higher completion rates and precision compared to existing baselines in multi-task scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing HOI synthesis methods rely on extensive motion capture data or labor-intensive manual reward engineering, limiting their scalability and generalizability across diverse interactions.
- Task-centric policies often overfit to specific behaviors or produce biomechanically unrealistic motions, struggling with complex, contact-rich interactions and dynamic objects.
- Prior interaction representations, such as 'chain-of-contacts,' fail to capture continuous motion dynamics, whole-body coordination, and evolving spatio-temporal relationships, leading to suboptimal or jittery behaviors.

## Method

- A VLM-Guided ==Relative Movement Dynamics== (RMD) Planner utilizes GPT-4V to translate high-level instructions and environmental context into structured interaction plans, specifying global spatial goals and dynamic patterns.
- RMD, a novel bipartite graph representation, encodes fine-grained spatial and temporal relationships between human and object parts, categorized by relative motion patterns (stationary, approaching, separating, unstable).
- An automatic policy learning framework formulates HOI as goal-conditioned reinforcement learning, using VLM-generated RMD plans to construct comprehensive goal states and design composite reward functions, balancing task and motion style objectives.

## Results

- The framework achieved an average completion rate of **75.1%** for static, **71.2%** for dynamic, and **53.8%** for hybrid multi-task HOI scenarios on the Interplay dataset, outperforming baselines which ranged from **21.3%** to **47.8%**.
- It demonstrated superior or comparable performance in single-task scenarios, with notable superiority in tasks requiring post-interaction recovery (e.g., **62.0%** completion rate for 'Lie' task vs. UniHSI **23.2%** and InterPhys **2.7%**).
- A user study indicated higher ratings for motion realism (4.0 ± **0.4**) and task consistency (4.1 ± **0.3**) compared to baselines, and ablations confirmed the critical role of VLM and RMD components in performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can effectively bridge high-level semantic instructions with low-level physics-based control, enabling fine-grained, interaction-aware motion guidance for complex HOI.
> - The Relative Movement Dynamics (RMD) representation, by explicitly encoding relative motion patterns between human and object parts, allows for more expressive and generalizable HOI policies compared to simpler contact-based abstractions.
> - Automating the generation of goal states and reward functions via VLM guidance eliminates the need for manual reward engineering, addressing a major bottleneck in physics-based reinforcement learning for HOI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human-Object Interaction (HOI) synthesis is a fundamental area for advancements in embodied AI systems, particularly for applications in animation, simulation, and robotics. The existing research in HOI learning can be broadly categorized into two paradigms:

The first paradigm involves motion tracking policies that imitate reference demonstrations. While capable of reproducing specific interaction patterns, these methods are often limited by their reliance on high-quality motion capture (MoCap) data, which presents scalability challenges. Additionally, their strict adherence to reference trajectories restricts their ability to generate novel interactions outside the training distribution.

The second paradigm focuses on task-centric approaches, developing specialized policies for individual interactions such as sitting or carrying. This category faces two primary challenges. Firstly, it typically requires labor-intensive manual reward engineering by domain experts, a process that is demanding due to the complex and contact-rich nature of realistic HOI scenarios. This manual process inherently limits the generalizability of policies across different interaction types. Secondly, policies trained with artificially designed, single-objective reward mechanisms can overfit to specific behavioral patterns, potentially leading to task-compliant but biomechanically unrealistic motions that violate natural human kinematics.

Recent efforts have attempted to automate reward function generation using Large Language Models (LLMs), such as Eureka and Grove. However, these methods often employ iterative search paradigms that can be sample-inefficient and computationally expensive. UniHSI advanced this by introducing a "chain-of-contacts" abstraction to unify reward design, treating interactions as discrete sequences of contact events. While this approach simplifies the conceptualization of contact, it overlooks the continuous motion dynamics of interactions, struggles to model whole-body coordination, and lacks the capacity to accommodate dynamic objects. Consequently, it often produces suboptimal or jittery behaviors, especially in dynamic interaction scenarios.

This work positions itself to address the aforementioned limitations by proposing a unified physics-based HOI framework. It seeks to overcome the reliance on expensive MoCap data, eliminate manual reward engineering, improve generalizability across diverse interaction types and objects (static, dynamic, articulated), and enable the synthesis of natural, long-horizon human-like motions. The paper introduces a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-guided approach to automatically design goal states and reward functions, aiming to bridge the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level physics-based control, particularly for complex, multi-task interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified physics-based framework for Human-Object Interaction (HOI) synthesis that can generate natural, long-horizon interactions with diverse object types, including static, dynamic, and articulated objects. The motivation behind this objective stems from several limitations observed in existing HOI learning paradigms:

- **Addressing Scalability and Generalizability Issues:** Current methods frequently rely on extensive motion capture data, which is costly to acquire and limits the variety of interactions that can be synthesized. Task-specific policies, while effective for predefined interactions, require substantial manual reward engineering for each new task, hindering their scalability and generalizability to new scenarios or object types. The authors aim to bypass these limitations by automating the objective formulation process.
- **Eliminating Manual Reward Engineering:** A significant bottleneck in physics-based reinforcement learning for HOI is the need for expert-designed reward functions. These functions are complex to craft, especially for contact-rich interactions, and their manual tuning restricts the adaptability of policies. The research is motivated by the need for an automated mechanism to construct goal states and reward functions, thereby reducing development effort and improving the robustness of the learning process.
- **Enabling Long-Horizon and Diverse Interactions:** Existing approaches often struggle with synthesizing long-term, multi-step interactions or handling a broad spectrum of object dynamics. Methods that focus solely on static scenes or predefined object trajectories lack the capability to produce coherent motions when interacting with dynamic or articulated objects that physically react to human actions. This work aims to enable physics-based characters to perform extended sequences of interactions with varied object types, including those that move or have configurable parts.
- **Leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Semantic Guidance:** While LLMs have shown promise in reward generation, their reliance on text alone may limit their ability to provide fine-grained, spatio-temporal guidance for physical interactions. The authors are motivated to harness the superior spatial awareness and motion imagination capabilities of VLMs, which integrate both visual and textual inputs, to generate semantically grounded and interaction-aware motion guidance directly.
- **Developing a Principled Interaction Representation:** Previous abstractions, such as "chain-of-contacts," are identified as insufficient because they only model transient point-contact constraints and fail to capture the evolving spatio-temporal relationships crucial for dynamic and coordinated interactions. A core motivation is to introduce a more principled and structured representation that can encode fine-grained spatial and temporal relationships between human and object parts throughout an interaction. This representation should empower VLMs to reason more effectively about physical interactions and produce structured, executable plans aligned with underlying motion dynamics.
- **Creating a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** The absence of a dataset specifically designed for long-horizon static and dynamic interaction tasks across varied scene contexts motivates the creation of Interplay. This dataset is intended to facilitate systematic evaluation of the proposed framework in complex, multi-task scenarios.

In summary, the key motivation is to overcome the limitations of manual reward engineering and data dependency in HOI synthesis by introducing an VLM-guided, physics-based framework that automatically designs fine-grained spatio-temporal goal states and reward functions, enabling robust and natural long-horizon interactions with diverse object types.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates two main components: a VLM-Guided Relative Movement Dynamics (RMD) Planner and a VLM-Guided Motion Policy learning framework.

#### 3.1. VLM-Guided RMD Planner

The planner leverages GPT-4V, a Vision-Language Model, to translate high-level textual instructions and environmental context into a sequence of structured interaction plans.

- **Inputs:** The VLM planner receives three types of input:
 - A high-level textual instruction (**I**) describing the desired task.
 - A top-view contextual image (**C**) of the environment, providing visual scene information.
 - A set of modular prompts designed to guide the VLM in task decomposition and output generation.
- **Relative Movement Dynamics (RMD) Representation:** To address the limitations of simplistic abstractions like "chain-of-contacts," the authors introduce RMD, a novel representation for encoding fine-grained spatial and temporal relationships between human body parts and object components during an interaction.
 - **Formalization:** RMD is formalized as a bipartite graph **B = (V, E, w)**.
 - **Vertices (V):** Comprise human body parts (**P_H**) and object parts (**P_O**). Human parts include 15 key regions (e.g., pelvis, head, hands, feet). Object parts are inferred by the VLM based on the object and instruction (e.g., `basket_left_side`, `seat_cushion`).
 - **Edges (E):** Connect a human part **p_h_i** to an object part **p_o_j**.
 - **Weights (w):** Each edge **e_ij** has an associated weight **w_ij ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}** that characterizes the relative-motion pattern:
 - **w_ij = 0:** Stationary contact (parts remain relatively fixed).
 - **w_ij = 1:** Approaching motion (distance between parts decreases).
 - **w_ij = 2:** Separating motion (distance between parts increases).
 - **w_ij = 3:** Unstable or unclear relative trend (no consistent pattern).
- **Modular Prompt Design:** The VLM is guided by structured prompts divided into sections:
 - **Scene-context Understanding:** Helps interpret the scene and determine target destinations using seven canonical relative spatial relationships (e.g., `box(front)`).
 - **RMD Concept Definition:** Explains the RMD concept, its semantics, instantiation method, and formal definition.
 - **Specific Plan Instance:** Provides a templated RMD-based plan example to illustrate the correspondence with real-world interactions.
 - **Idea Outline:** Outlines the approach, specifying input/output, and how tasks are decomposed into sub-steps with consistent movement dynamics.
 - **Plan Rules:** Defines rules for task interaction boundaries, output formatting (JSON), and criteria for determining movement dynamics.
 - **Reference Example:** Includes a complete input-output example for one-shot prompting.
- **Planner Output:** The VLM generates an interaction plan **D** as a sequence of **N** structured steps, **D = {G_1, G_2..., G_N}**. Each step **G_i** is a triplet specifying the global spatial goals and dynamic pattern: **G_i = {T_H, T_O, B}**, where **T_H** and **T_O** are target locations for the human and object root, respectively, and **B** is the RMD graph.

#### 3.2. Automatic Policy Learning via VLM-Guided RMD

The framework formulates physics-based HOI policy learning as goal-conditioned reinforcement learning. The learned policy **π(a_t | s_t, g_t)** produces joint-level torques to actuate the agent.

- **Goal State Construction:** For each planned interaction step **G_i = {T_H, B, T_O}**, the framework automatically constructs a comprehensive goal representation **g_t**:
 - **RMD State (s_RMD_t):** For each edge **e_ij ∈ E**, relative position-velocity pairs (**˜p_ij, ˜v_ij**) between human and object parts are computed in the agent-centric frame. The edge weight **w_ij** is encoded as a one-hot vector **w'_ij**. These features are concatenated to form **s_RMD_t**.
 - **Destination State (d_t):** Absolute target positions for the human root (**p_h_tar**) and object root (**p_o_tar**) are derived from **T_H** and **T_O** by mapping spatial-relationship tokens (e.g., `front`) to displacement vectors relative to the object's bounding box center. These are then transformed into the agent-centric frame to yield **d_t = {d_h_t, d_o_t}**.
 - **Local Perception (h_t):** A fixed-resolution heightmap is included to capture local geometry and aid obstacle avoidance.
 - **Object State (o_t):** Object dynamics are described by bounding box vertices, rotation, linear velocity, and angular velocity.
 - The complete goal state **g_t = {s_RMD_t, d_t, h_t, o_t}** is formed by concatenating these components.
- **Reward Function Design:** The framework automatically designs a composite task reward **r_G** to guide the agent:
 - **Human Root Destination Reward (r_h_d):** Encourages the human root position **x_h_t** to approach **d_h_t**, using an exponential decay function based on distance.
 - **Object Root Destination Reward (r_o_d):** Encourages the object root position **x_o_t** to approach **d_o_t**, similarly using an exponential decay.
 - **RMD Alignment Reward (r_RMD):** This is a sum of individual alignment rewards **r_rmd(·)** for each edge **e_ij ∈ B**, weighted by **λ_ij**. The **r_rmd(·)** term is defined differently based on **w_ij**:
 - **w_ij = 0 (stationary contact):** Rewards vanishing relative velocity or orthogonal orientation between relative position and velocity.
 - **w_ij = 1 (approaching):** Rewards spatial proximity and alignment of relative velocity with the direction of relative position, encouraging approach to a target speed **v_⋆**.
 - **w_ij = 2 (separating):** Rewards increasing separation between parts and maintaining leaving speed along the displacement direction close to **v_⋆**.
 - **w_ij = 3 (unstable):** Provides a neutral constant reward of 1.
 - **Overall Task Reward (r_G):** Defined as **r_G = λ_RMD · r_RMD + λ_h · r_h_d + λ_o · r_o_d**. Adaptive weighting strategies dynamically adjust coefficients (**λ**) to balance contributions and facilitate accurate progress assessment.
 - **Task Transition:** Once **r_G** exceeds a threshold (0.9), the policy transitions to the next planned interaction step **G_i+1**.
- **Style Reward (r_S):** A discriminator network, trained on motion clips from existing datasets (SAMP, OMOMO, CIRCLE), provides a style reward **r_S(s_t, s_t+1)** to encourage natural, human-like behaviors, based on a window of 10 consecutive states.
- **Total Reward:** The final reward for learning is **r_t = α_task r_G + α_style r_S**.
- **Interplay Dataset:** A novel dataset is introduced to support the methodology, featuring thousands of long-horizon static and dynamic interaction plans across diverse indoor scene contexts. This dataset includes processed 3D assets, scene layouts, top-view images, and natural-language instructions.
- **Implementation:** Experiments are conducted in Isaac Gym using PyTorch, with a 15-rigid-body, 28-joint humanoid controlled by PD controllers. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed framework in both long-horizon multi-task and single-task scenarios, demonstrating its performance against existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Evaluation in a Long-Horizon Multi-Task Scenario

- **Settings:** Experiments were conducted using the newly introduced Interplay dataset, split into static-interaction, dynamic-interaction, and hybrid setups. Baselines (InterPhys, TokenHSI, UniHSI) were adapted to support multi-task evaluation where possible, with UniHSI specifically supporting only static object interactions.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Completion Rate:** Percentage of trials where all sub-step tasks are completed, and the character returns to a natural standing pose within 20 cm of the target.
 - **Sub-step Completion Ratio:** Ratio of completed sub-steps to total planned sub-steps.
 - **Sub-step Precision:** Average distance between human/object parts and their targets.
- **Results:** The proposed framework achieved higher performance across all metrics compared to baselines (Table 2).
 - **Completion Rate:** Ours (75.1% static, 71.2% dynamic, 53.8% hybrid) significantly outperformed InterPhys (21.3% static, 47.8% dynamic, 27.5% hybrid), TokenHSI (25.2% static, 52.5% dynamic, 36.0% hybrid), and UniHSI (37.2% static).
 - **Sub-step Completion Ratio:** Ours (86.2% static, 84.3% dynamic, 71.8% hybrid) also showed superior performance.
 - **Sub-step Precision:** Ours exhibited lower (better) precision values (7.7 cm static, 13.0 cm dynamic, 11.2 cm hybrid).
- **Analysis:** The framework’s unified formulation of interaction and fine-grained spatio-temporal guidance enabled robust execution and seamless transitions in multi-task scenarios. Baselines often struggled with task switching or specific aspects of interaction (e.g., UniHSI with getting up after interaction).
- **Ablation (VLM vs. LLM):** Replacing the VLM (GPT-4V) with an LLM (under purely textual conditions) resulted in a noticeable performance drop (e.g., Completion Rate of 39.9% for hybrid tasks compared to 53.8% with VLM). This indicated the VLM's critical role in spatial awareness and motion imagination for generating semantically meaningful and spatially grounded plans. Further ablations in Appendix E reinforced the robustness of the VLM planner across different VLM choices and prompt designs.

#### 4.2. Evaluation in a Single-Task Scenario

- **Settings:** Six interaction tasks were selected from InterPlay: reaching, sitting, lying down (static HOI), and carrying, pushing, opening (dynamic HOI). A new "leaving" step was introduced to task completion criteria, requiring the agent to return to a neutral standing pose after interaction. Baselines (AMP, InterPhys, TokenHSI, UniHSI) were re-implemented with adapted goal states and reward functions for fair comparison.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Completion Rate:** New metric measuring completion of interaction AND return to neutral state.
 - **Success Rate:** Traditional metric for successful interaction with the object.
 - **Precision:** Average distance between human part and target object position in successful trials.
- **Results:** The proposed method demonstrated higher or comparable performance across all single-task metrics (Table 3).
 - For tasks involving recovery (e.g., getting up after sitting or lying), the method showed notable superiority in Completion Rate. For instance, in "Lie" task, Ours achieved 62.0% Completion Rate, significantly higher than UniHSI (23.2%) and InterPhys (2.7%).
 - For other tasks, the method maintained strong performance, e.g., "Carry" Completion Rate of 88.3% compared to InterPhys (67.8%) and TokenHSI (71.2%).
- **Analysis:** The RMD representation's ability to guide body parts’ relative movements improved recovery processes and overall motion quality. UniHSI, lacking temporal dynamics, struggled with post-interaction movements, while InterPhys often produced unnatural or unstable motions due to insufficient fine-grained guidance.
- **Ablation (RMD Components):**
 - **Multi-one:** Simplifying object representation (treating as a single entity) led to a performance drop, indicating the importance of fine-grained geometric information.
 - **One-one:** Restricting RMD to model only one human-object part interaction per sub-step disrupted whole-body coordination, leading to a significant performance decline.
 - **Without kinematic (˜p_ij) or dynamic (w'_ij) encoding:** Removing these components further degraded performance, confirming their crucial role in capturing spatio-temporal relationships.
- **Qualitative Assessment (Figure 3 & Appendix D):** Visualizations showed that the proposed method produced natural, human-like motions, outperforming baselines that exhibited unnatural movements or incomplete interactions.
- **User Study (Appendix F):** A user study with 20 participants rated the proposed method higher in both Motion Realism (4.0 ± 0.4) and Task Consistency (4.1 ± 0.3) compared to baselines (InterPhys, TokenHSI, UniHSI).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of Human-Object Interaction (HOI) synthesis, with several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Unified and Scalable Framework:** The introduction of a unified physics-based HOI framework, leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs), represents a significant step towards scalable and generalizable HOI synthesis. By supporting diverse object types—static, dynamic, and articulated—within a single framework, it addresses a key limitation of prior methods that often specialized in specific interaction types or object categories. This unification can streamline the development of embodied AI systems.
- **Automated Objective Formulation:** The proposed VLM-Guided Relative Movement Dynamics (RMD) system automates the generation of goal states and reward functions for reinforcement learning. This eliminates the labor-intensive and expert-dependent process of manual reward engineering, which has historically been a major bottleneck. The automation has the potential to accelerate research and development in physics-based character control and embodied AI by making it more accessible and efficient.
- **Fine-Grained Spatio-Temporal Guidance:** RMD, as a structured spatio-temporal bipartite representation, enables VLMs to provide fine-grained, interaction-aware motion guidance. This allows the system to move beyond symbolic planning to directly inform dynamic skill composition, resulting in more expressive and generalizable HOI motion policies. This fine-grained control is crucial for synthesizing natural and biomechanically plausible motions, particularly for complex and contact-rich interactions.
- **Long-Horizon Interaction Capabilities:** The framework demonstrates proficiency in synthesizing long-horizon interactions, which is essential for real-world applications requiring agents to perform multi-step tasks. The ability to manage sequences of interactions with seamless transitions improves the robustness and utility of synthesized behaviors.
- **Bridging High-Level Semantics and Low-Level Control:** The work effectively bridges high-level semantic instructions with low-level physics-based control. By empowering VLMs to translate abstract commands into detailed, executable interaction plans, it enhances the autonomy and adaptability of virtual agents and robots.
- **New Dataset for Evaluation:** The creation of the Interplay dataset specifically for long-horizon static and dynamic interaction tasks provides a valuable resource for the research community. It enables systematic evaluation of HOI frameworks in complex, multi-task scenarios, fostering further advancements in the field.
- **Potential for Embodied AI Applications:** The ability to synthesize natural and physically plausible human-object interactions has direct applications in animation (for more realistic character behaviors), simulation (for training and testing robots in virtual environments), and robotics (for developing intelligent agents capable of complex manipulation and navigation in human-centric environments).
- **Foundational Abstraction (RMD):** RMD is positioned as a mid-level relational abstraction. Its graph formulation is naturally extensible, allowing for future integration of additional entities (e.g., multiple agents, tools) and constraints while maintaining a consistent policy-learning interface. This suggests a potential for broader impact beyond current HOI settings.

While the framework currently focuses on single-agent scenarios and does not address multi-agent interactions or very long-horizon tasks requiring deep temporal reasoning, the identified extensions to real-world applications (e.g., distillation into real-world controllers, robustness improvements) and future work (e.g., diffusion-based priors, tighter VLM-controller coupling, multi-agent settings) indicate a trajectory towards addressing these limitations and expanding its impact.
