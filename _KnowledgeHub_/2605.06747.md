# HumanNet: Scaling Human-centric Video Learning to One Million Hours

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanNet introduces a one-million-hour human-centric video dataset designed to provide a large-scale, diverse, and richly annotated resource for embodied intelligence systems. This corpus, which includes both first-person and third-person perspectives with interaction-centric annotations, demonstrated in a validation study that pretraining with 1,000 hours of its egocentric video matched or slightly surpassed models trained with 100 hours of real-robot data.

## Problem

- Embodied intelligence systems are bottlenecked by a scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and richly annotated human activity data for physical interaction models.
- Existing datasets for physical interaction are significantly smaller, often limited to specific benchmark tasks or robot platforms, hindering the development of general-purpose embodied intelligence.
- A "mismatch in scale" exists between the vast data available for vision and language models and the comparatively small, specialized datasets for physical interaction.

## Method

- Developed **HumanNet**, a one-million-hour human-centric video corpus, by systematically curating and processing heterogeneous video content through a ==three-stage pipeline== (collection, processing, annotation).
- Incorporated ==viewpoint diversity== by including both ==first-person (egocentric)== and ==third-person (exocentric)== perspectives to capture complementary information for embodied learning.
- Enriched the dataset with ==interaction-centric annotations==, such as captions, detailed motion descriptions, and hand/body-related signals, to support motion-aware and interaction-aware learning.

## Results

- HumanNet comprises **one million hours** of human-centric video, making it the largest dataset of its kind, featuring diverse fine-grained activities, human-object interactions, and long-horizon behaviors.
- A validation study showed that pretraining with **1,000 hours** of egocentric human video from HumanNet matched, and in some task groups slightly surpassed, a model pretrained with **100 hours** of real-robot CoBot data.
- The HumanNet-pretrained model substantially narrowed the performance gap between a generic web-scale language-vision initialization and a high-resource robot-specialized initialization (**LingBot** trained on **20,000 hours** of robot data).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggressively scaling human-centric video, combined with systematic curation and rich, interaction-centric annotations, offers a viable path to overcome data limitations for general-purpose embodied intelligence.
> - The integration of both egocentric and exocentric viewpoints provides a more comprehensive understanding of human interaction with the physical world, capturing both actor-centered intent and scene context.
> - Large-scale egocentric human video can serve as a scalable and cost-effective substitute for real-robot data in pretraining embodied foundation models, providing transferable action-centric visual representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied intelligence systems increasingly relies on extensive and diverse datasets for training. In fields such as vision and language modeling, foundational models have achieved significant advancements by leveraging massive internet-scale corpora of text, images, and multimodal web data. However, the domain of physical interaction, which is central to embodied intelligence, currently faces a bottleneck due to a scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and richly annotated human activity data. Existing datasets for physical interaction models are typically orders of magnitude smaller, often focus on a limited set of benchmark tasks, and are frequently tied to specific robot platforms, control interfaces, or sensing configurations. This disparity in data scale is identified as a primary impediment to the progression of general-purpose embodied intelligence.

Human-centric video has emerged as a promising alternative, building on its historical role in visual representation learning, temporal reasoning, and action understanding. Prior research has demonstrated the utility of human activity and instructional video corpora, including large-scale community resources such as Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS, Ego-Exo4D, and EgoSchema, for recognition, forecasting, narration, and multimodal understanding, particularly from egocentric and paired exocentric perspectives. Datasets focusing on structured interactions, like HOI4D, have highlighted the value of dense hand-object supervision. Recent studies have also shown that human-centered data can contribute to improvements in robot learning and representation learning. However, these existing corpora often suffer from limitations in duration, fragmentation across collection efforts, or optimization for a narrow range of downstream tasks.

This work builds upon a lineage of efforts that recognize the potential of human data for robot learning. EgoScale demonstrated that scaling egocentric human data can lead to predictable performance gains in dexterous manipulation. EgoVerse introduced the concept of a shared ecosystem for continuously growing egocentric robot-learning data. Ego-Exo4D further motivated the integration of first-person and third-person views to capture both actor-centered intent and scene-centered physical context. The Being-H line of work advocates for human interaction traces as a scalable substrate for cross-embodiment learning when combined with unified representations. Other systems co-train imitation policies using aligned human egocentric traces and robot demonstrations, and open vision-language-action stacks increasingly integrate heterogeneous robot logs with human video at a foundation-model scale.

HumanNet positions itself within this landscape by addressing the fundamental need for a dataset that is not only substantially larger in scale but also systematically curated across viewpoints and enriched with a detailed annotation taxonomy. The paper argues for a data-centric approach to overcome the limitations of current embodied learning systems, emphasizing aggressive scaling of human-centric video while treating curation, viewpoint diversity, and annotation taxonomy as primary scientific contributions. This work seeks to bridge the gap between internet-scale perception and embodied action learning by providing a readily available and structured resource for training physical AI models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the fundamental data limitation currently impeding the progress of general-purpose embodied intelligence systems. The authors observe a significant disparity between the vast, scalable data infrastructure available for vision and language models and the comparatively small, specialized datasets used for physical interaction models. This "mismatch in scale" is identified as a critical bottleneck.

Specifically, the key objectives and underlying motivations for introducing HumanNet are:

1. **Creation of a Scalable, Diverse Human-centric Video Corpus:** The core objective is to develop a massive video dataset that captures human interaction with the physical world at an unprecedented scale. Motivated by the observation that humans naturally perform a wide range of complex activities in diverse real-world settings (manipulation, tool use, locomotion, navigation, social coordination, multi-step procedures), the authors aim for a corpus of one million hours. This scale is intended to provide broad coverage over activities, environments, objects, body motions, and interaction styles, extending beyond the narrow task families often found in smaller datasets.
2. **Incorporation of Viewpoint Diversity:** The dataset is designed to include both first-person (egocentric) and third-person (exocentric) perspectives. This is motivated by the understanding that each viewpoint provides complementary information crucial for embodied learning. First-person video offers insights into action execution, contact dynamics, hand-object relations, and actor intent, while third-person video facilitates observation of full-body motion, posture, interaction context, surrounding agents, and scene-level dynamics. The objective is to enable models to learn from both actor-centered and observer-centered cues.
3. **Provision of Rich, Interaction-Centric Annotations:** Beyond raw video, a significant objective is to enrich the dataset with specific annotations that are relevant to physical interaction. These include captions, detailed motion descriptions, and hand/body-related signals. The motivation here is to move beyond generic visual data and provide supervision that enables motion-aware and interaction-aware learning, connecting pixels to geometric, kinematic, and semantic understanding of human actions.
4. **Establishment of a Systematic Data Curation Paradigm:** The project aims to introduce a principled methodology for transforming unstructured internet video into a structured, pretraining-ready resource. This involves treating human-centric filtering, temporal structuring, viewpoint diversity characterization, and annotation enrichment as core design principles. The motivation is to ensure that the massive scale of the dataset is coupled with high quality and utility for downstream tasks like representation learning, activity understanding, motion generation, and human-to-robot transfer, rather than simply accumulating raw footage.
5. **Validation of Egocentric Human Video as a Robot Data Substitute:** An explicit motivation is to explore and empirically validate whether large-scale egocentric human video can serve as a scalable and cost-effective substitute for robot-specific data in pretraining embodied foundation models. This objective is driven by the practical limitations and high costs associated with collecting real-robot data at scale. The authors seek to demonstrate that transferable visual and action-centric representations can be learned from human video, narrowing the "embodiment gap" to robot learning.

In summary, the work is motivated by the recognized gap in data scale for embodied AI and seeks to fill it by constructing a very large, comprehensively annotated, and systematically curated human-centric video dataset, with an explicit focus on its utility for accelerating the development of general-purpose embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing HumanNet centers on the principle of aggressively scaling human-centric video while maintaining a focus on systematic curation, viewpoint diversity, and a comprehensive annotation taxonomy. This approach is intended to provide a robust data substrate for embodied learning.

The authors define "human-centric video" as footage where human activity is the primary organizing signal, depicting physically meaningful behaviors such as object manipulation, tool use, navigation, assembly, operation of appliances, object transport, social coordination, or multi-step procedures that result in visible environmental state changes. This definition explicitly excludes passive or weakly grounded video where human motion is incidental or lacks temporal coherence relevant for action understanding.

The dataset design adheres to four key principles:
1. **Scale:** To achieve long-tail coverage of diverse activities, environments, body motions, and interaction styles.
2. **Viewpoint Diversity:** To incorporate both first-person and third-person perspectives, allowing models to learn from complementary actor-centered and observer-centered cues.
3. **Physical Relevance:** To preserve cues essential for embodied learning, including hand-object proximity, full-body motion, state changes, action ordering, procedural structure, and scene context.
4. **Pretraining Readiness:** To ensure the dataset is organized for modern large-scale training pipelines, with features like chunking, metadata indexing, quality filtering, caption labels, motion annotations, and potential alignment with text or structured labels.

**Scalable Data Sources:**
The corpus is designed to be heterogeneous by construction, indexing data across source type, viewpoint, physical activity content, and available supervision signals. It integrates controlled and semi-structured collections, which offer cleaner motion and richer metadata, with community, web-scale, and domain-specific sources to maximize diversity and long-tail coverage. Interaction content emphasizes physically grounded behaviors such as manipulation, tool use, and locomotion. Scene context is retained to account for varying object priors, action affordances, and occlusion patterns. Metadata, including narrations, timestamps, task descriptions, and pseudo-labels (e.g., hand tracks, body pose, motion categories, contact estimates), is tracked to support flexible training mixtures.

**Data Pipeline:**
The construction of HumanNet follows a three-stage pipeline: data collection, data processing, and annotation. This modular design allows for independent auditing, extension, or rerunning of each stage.

1. **Data Collection:**
 - **Keyword Discovery:** An initial set of seed keywords is expanded iteratively through keyword-based crawling and cleaning, channel-level crawling, and integration of existing data sources, forming a comprehensive keyword repository.
 - **Content Search & Retrieval:** This repository guides the retrieval of candidate videos from various platforms, general web search engines, direct crawling, open-source datasets, and a stream of self-collected recordings. The self-collected data complements web-scale sources by providing controlled first- and third-person footage for underrepresented activities.
 - **Initial Filtering:** Channel-level and source-level filtering is applied to remove off-topic, low-quality, or passively observational content, as well as duplicate and unusable recordings.

2. **Data Processing:**
 - **Deduplication and Normalization:** Near-identical copies are removed, and video parameters (frame rate, resolution, format) are unified.
 - **Content Filtering:** Clips are retained only if they contain meaningful human action and observable motion.
 - **Quality Filtering:** Recordings with severe motion blur, heavy occlusion, static framing, or other visual defects are discarded.
 - **Scene Splitting:** Long videos are segmented at visual changes to ensure that unrelated activities are not combined within a single sample.
 - **Video Clipping:** The processed videos are then clipped into fixed-granularity segments suitable for annotation.

3. **Annotation:**
 - **Geometric Supervision:** 3D hand and body pose detection is used to recover fine-grained motion structure. Monocular SLAM estimates camera trajectories for eligible first-person clips. A motion retargeting module aligns recovered human motion to a unified humanoid skeleton, identifying "robot-ready" clips based on retargeting error and valid-frame coverage.
 - **Semantic Supervision:** An LLM-assisted captioning module generates video captions, motion descriptions, and activity classifications, which are normalized against any existing source metadata.
 - **Review:** Privacy-sensitive content, unsafe material, and license constraints undergo review within the pipeline.

**Statistical Analysis:**
The corpus is analyzed along two axes: semantic coverage (activities, objects, scenes) and distributional structure (pose and motion signals). This analysis provides insights into the lexical, scene, and category-level composition, highlighting a broad vocabulary of physically grounded manipulation, diverse environments, and a pronounced long-tail distribution of activity categories. Statistical distributions of pose scores, motion scores, and motion lengths are analyzed to demonstrate the quality of retained clips and the heterogeneity of motion patterns.

**Validation of Egocentric Data:**
To validate the utility of egocentric human video for embodied policy learning, a controlled post-training comparison was conducted using the LingBot-VLA architecture. The study fixed the policy architecture and a downstream corpus of 100 tasks (34 hours of robot interaction data) while varying the pretraining source:
- A Qwen-based VLM (baseline).
- Qwen VLM adapted with 100 hours of real-robot CoBot data.
- Qwen VLM adapted with 1,000 hours of egocentric human video from HumanNet.
- LingBot, where the Qwen backbone is trained with 20,000 hours of real-robot data (serving as a high-resource baseline).
The performance metric used was validation loss across five held-out task groups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HumanNet project resulted in the creation of a large-scale human-centric video corpus and provided empirical validation of its utility for embodied learning.

**Dataset Characteristics:**
- **Scale:** HumanNet comprises one million hours of human-centric video, making it the largest dataset of its kind.
- **Content Diversity:** The corpus spans both first-person and third-person perspectives, capturing a wide array of fine-grained activities, human-object interactions, tool use, and long-horizon behaviors across diverse real-world environments.
- **Annotations:** The dataset is enriched with interaction-centric annotations including captions, motion descriptions, and hand and body-related signals. This allows for motion-aware and interaction-aware learning.
- **Curation Paradigm:** The dataset was constructed through a systematic data curation pipeline incorporating human-centric filtering, temporal structuring, viewpoint diversity, and annotation enrichment as core design principles. This process transforms heterogeneous internet video into a structured resource for various downstream applications.
- **Statistical Composition:**
 - Lexical, scene, and category-level analysis (Figure 2, 5) indicates that the action vocabulary is predominantly composed of physically grounded manipulation verbs applied to everyday objects.
 - The scene hierarchy distributes clips across a wide range of indoor and outdoor environments, avoiding concentration in a single domain.
 - The activity-category distribution exhibits a pronounced long-tail, suggesting that the large scale enables the capture of rare but physically informative behaviors, such as folding deformable objects or operating unfamiliar appliances, which would be underrepresented in smaller datasets.
 - Analysis of pose-score distributions shows a concentration at the high-confidence end after quality filtering, indicating suitability for dense pose, hand, and motion supervision.
 - Motion-score and motion-length distributions are heavy-tailed but well-bounded, demonstrating a mix of short, focused interaction units alongside longer and more vigorous segments required for temporal context and procedural learning. Specific categories like athletic and outdoor activities show longer, higher-magnitude motion, while daily activities tend towards shorter, finer-grained segments.

**Validation Study Results:**
A controlled post-training comparison using the LingBot-VLA architecture was conducted to evaluate the transferable value of egocentric human video for embodied policy learning. The study utilized a fixed policy architecture and a 34-hour downstream robot interaction corpus across 100 tasks. The pretraining sources were varied: a Qwen VLM, Qwen adapted with 100 hours of real-robot CoBot data, Qwen adapted with 1,000 hours of egocentric human video from HumanNet, and LingBot (Qwen + 20,000 hours of real-robot data).

The key findings from this validation study were:
- **Narrowing the Gap:** The egocentric-pretrained variant (1,000 hours from HumanNet) consistently narrowed the gap in validation loss between a generic web-scale language-vision initialization (Qwen) and a robot-specialized initialization (LingBot with 20,000 hours of robot data). This suggests that first-person human video effectively captures actor-centered cues, hand-object contact patterns, and procedural structure that remain beneficial after transfer to robot post-training.
- **Performance vs. Real-Robot Data:** The model initialized with 1,000 hours of egocentric video from HumanNet matched, and in several task groups slightly surpassed, the model initialized with 100 hours of real-robot CoBot data in terms of validation loss.
- **Closing the Gap to High-Resource Baseline:** The HumanNet-pretrained model also substantially closed the performance gap to the LingBot baseline, which was trained with 20,000 hours of real-robot data.

These results empirically support the central claim that large-scale egocentric human video, as curated in HumanNet, is not merely a supplementary visual corpus but a scalable and cost-effective substitute for robot data, capable of providing transferable action-centric visual representations for downstream robot learning, particularly when teleoperated robot data is limited.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HumanNet dataset and its associated methodology contribute to the field of embodied intelligence by addressing a significant bottleneck: the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and richly annotated data for training physical interaction models.

**Addressing the Data Bottleneck:** The primary significance lies in providing a one-million-hour corpus of human-centric video. This scale is intended to offer a foundation for scaling embodied foundation models, reducing the sole reliance on robot-specific data which is often costly and difficult to collect at comparable volumes. The dataset aims to provide the breadth necessary for representations to learn invariant physical structures across heterogeneous settings and viewpoints.

**Enabling Embodied Foundation Models:** By supplying a massive, structured dataset rich in human activity and interaction, HumanNet is positioned to serve as a critical substrate for the development of general-purpose embodied foundation models. Its diverse content and interaction-centric annotations are designed to facilitate the training of models capable of robustly understanding and generating physical behavior.

**Broadening Downstream Applications:** The dataset is designed to support a range of downstream tasks within physical AI:
- **Video and VLM Pretraining:** It can be used to pretrain video encoders and video-language models with enhanced human activity, contact, and motion structure, beyond what generic internet video provides.
- **World-Action Model Training:** HumanNet is suitable for training world-action models that jointly capture environment dynamics and the actions that drive them, enabling learning of action-conditioned forward dynamics and grounding language in physically executable behavior.
- **Motion-Aware Representation Learning:** The combination of first-person and third-person views, along with motion annotations, supports the development of representations that align appearance, language, and motion, moving beyond frame-by-frame analysis.
- **Human-to-Robot Transfer:** While not directly reporting new transfer experiments, the paper posits that the corpus can significantly widen the human data side of human-to-robot transfer pipelines, supplying scalable priors that can be mapped to robot-relevant state and action representations.
- **Multimodal Objectives for Physical AI:** The data supports various multimodal learning objectives such as masked or predictive video modeling, language-video alignment, procedural boundary prediction, weakly supervised hand-object learning, and caption-conditioned activity modeling.

**Methodological Contribution:** Beyond its scale, HumanNet introduces a systematic data curation paradigm that treats filtering, viewpoint characterization, quality control, and privacy review as integral design choices. This structured approach for transforming heterogeneous web video into a pretraining-ready resource represents a methodological advancement for dataset creation in embodied learning.

**Cost-Effectiveness and Scalability:** The empirical validation demonstrates that 1,000 hours of egocentric human video pretraining from HumanNet can match or exceed the performance of 100 hours of real-robot data pretraining. This suggests that human-centric video offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative to acquiring extensive robot data, thereby democratizing access to large-scale pretraining resources for robot learning.

**Limitations and Ethical Considerations (as noted by authors):** The authors acknowledge several limitations and ethical considerations. These include the inherent embodiment gap between human and robot behaviors, the introduction of noise at such a large scale, potential uneven coverage or biases in the data, and serious privacy and safety concerns associated with collecting and distributing human-centric video. These factors underscore the dual-use potential of such a dataset, benefiting assistive systems and robotics while also raising concerns about surveillance-adjacent perception systems and inherited societal biases.
