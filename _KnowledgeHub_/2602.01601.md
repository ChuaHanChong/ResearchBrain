# Adaptive Rollout Allocation for Online Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> The Variance-Informed Predictive (VIP) allocation strategy optimizes online reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs) with verifiable rewards by adaptively distributing computational rollouts based on dynamically predicted prompt success probabilities. This method consistently boosts performance in mathematical and tool-augmented reasoning tasks, yielding improvements such as +12.3 Pass@32 on AIME benchmarks for Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with a minimal training overhead of under 1.2%.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) based methods for large language model (LLM) post-training, particularly those utilizing verifiable rewards (RLVR), are computationally expensive due to sampling inefficiency.
- Existing group-based RL algorithms like Dr. GRPO and RLOO employ fixed, uniform rollout allocation, which inefficiently expends computational budget on uninformative or trivially solvable prompts.
- Previous adaptive allocation strategies often lack a principled, dynamic control mechanism that can continuously adjust to the model's evolving capabilities or require pre-sampling large batches.

## Method

- VIP introduces a theoretical framework that establishes a direct relationship between ==gradient variance== in group-based RL and a prompt's success probability.
- A Gaussian Process (GP) model is employed to recursively predict and update per-prompt success probabilities using prompt embeddings and historical rollout outcomes, adapting to the model's evolving capabilities.
- An adaptive budget allocator, formulated as a convex optimization problem, minimizes total expected gradient variance across a mini-batch by determining optimal rollout counts for each prompt, subject to a total budget constraint.

## Results

- VIP consistently improved sampling efficiency and achieved higher performance in mathematical reasoning (e.g., +12.3 Pass@32 for Qwen2.5-Math-**1.5B**) and tool-augmented reasoning tasks (e.g., +7.2 EM for RLOO+VIP on Bamboogle).
- The strategy simultaneously enhanced both answer accuracy and retrieval quality in tool-augmented reasoning, demonstrating its effectiveness in complex, multi-faceted tasks.
- The computational overhead introduced by VIP was minimal, accounting for only **0.58%** to **1.12%** of total training time, with its relative overhead decreasing for larger LLM backbones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The gradient variance for on-policy components in Dr. GRPO and RLOO is proportional to p(1-p), where p is the prompt success probability, justifying adaptive allocation based on prompt informativeness.
> - A Gaussian Process effectively models the non-stationary nature of prompt success probabilities during RL training, providing robust and adaptively calibrated uncertainty estimates for resource allocation.
> - Minimizing total gradient variance via an adaptive, variance-informed rollout allocation strategy leads to enhanced sampling efficiency and superior performance compared to uniform or heuristic methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the contemporary landscape of large language model (LLM) development, particularly focusing on post-training methods that align base LLMs with specific human intentions and task-oriented behaviors. Two primary post-training paradigms are identified: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). While SFT is computationally efficient, its effectiveness is often limited by the quality and comprehensiveness of curated datasets.

The research specifically addresses RL-based methods, which are noted for their ability to explore broader action spaces and achieve superior performance on complex tasks. Within RL, the paper distinguishes between Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The authors focus on RLVR, where rewards are determined by rule-based verifiers, thereby reducing reliance on human input and mitigating concerns about reward miscalibration often found in RLHF.

A central challenge in RL methods, including RLVR, is their significant computational cost, particularly sampling efficiency. Algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) require training a separate value model, which can be memory-intensive. To counter this, "critic-free" or group-based RL methods, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), Dr. GRPO, and RLOO, have emerged. These methods estimate advantage as a relative measure within a group of rollouts, trading speed for memory efficiency. A drawback of these methods is the necessity of generating a large number of rollouts (e.g., 16) for each training example to achieve stable training, which exacerbates computational overhead and can lead to memory-bound systems. This highlights a critical need for improved sampling efficiency in RL training.

Previous work on optimizing the performance-cost trade-off in RL has included hybrid SFT-RL approaches and adaptive strategies for data selection. Some methods filter out "non-informative" problems (accuracy close to 0 or 1) or those with low advantage, while others use pre-rollout estimations of prompt informativeness to dynamically allocate resources or prioritize problem difficulty. However, these methods often require pre-sampling large batches or lack a principled, adaptive control mechanism that can dynamically adjust to the model's evolving capabilities. This paper identifies a gap in existing literature regarding a crucial metric for dynamic adaptation and a corresponding adaptive control mechanism for group-based policy optimization processes like GRPO. The proposed Variance-Informed Predictive (VIP) allocation strategy aims to fill this gap by introducing a principled framework for efficient and adaptive rollout allocation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observation that sampling efficiency constitutes a significant bottleneck in reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of verifiable rewards (RLVR). Existing group-based policy optimization methods, such as GRPO, Dr. GRPO, and RLOO, often employ a fixed, uniform allocation of rollouts for all training prompts. This uniform approach implicitly assumes that all prompts are equally informative for policy updates, which is frequently not the case. Such an assumption can lead to suboptimal utilization of computational resources, as budget might be spent on prompts that offer minimal gradient signal (e.g., prompts that are either trivially easy or currently impossible for the model to solve). This inefficiency can impede the overall training progress and limit the performance gains achievable within a given computational budget.

The key objectives of the Variance-Informed Predictive (VIP) allocation strategy are:

1. **To provide a theoretical understanding of gradient variance:** The research aims to rigorously analyze the gradient variance in prominent group-based RL algorithms, specifically Dr. GRPO and RLOO. This involves establishing a mathematical relationship between the gradient variance contributed by a particular prompt and the probability that the current model successfully solves that prompt. This theoretical foundation is intended to justify the adaptive control of budget allocation during training.
2. **To develop a predictive mechanism for prompt success probabilities:** Recognizing that the true success probability of a model on a given prompt is not directly observable before performing rollouts and that this probability drifts as the model parameters evolve, the paper seeks to create a dynamic prediction system. This system, based on a Gaussian Process (GP) model over prompt embeddings, is designed to recursively update its beliefs about per-prompt success probabilities by leveraging both historical rollout outcomes and the inherent similarity structure among prompts.
3. **To formulate and solve an optimization problem for adaptive rollout allocation:** Based on the predicted per-prompt success probabilities, which are translated into gradient variance estimates, the objective is to design a method for optimal rollout allocation. This involves framing the allocation task as a convex optimization problem, where the goal is to minimize the total expected gradient variance across a mini-batch of prompts, subject to a hard computational budget constraint and defined lower and upper bounds on per-prompt rollouts. The research aims to develop an efficient algorithm to solve the continuous relaxation of this problem and a heuristic for deriving a feasible integer solution.

By achieving these objectives, VIP aims to overcome the limitations of uniform or heuristic allocation strategies, thereby enhancing the sampling efficiency and overall performance of group-based RL methods in LLM training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Variance-Informed Predictive (VIP) allocation strategy is introduced as a principled framework for efficient and adaptive rollout allocation in group-based policy optimization. The methodology integrates theoretical analysis, statistical modeling, and optimization techniques to dynamically manage computational resources.

#### 3.1. Gradient Variance Analysis

The initial step involves a theoretical analysis of the gradient variance for group-based RL algorithms, specifically Dr. GRPO and RLOO. The authors focus on the on-policy component of the gradient, as this is directly influenced by new rollouts. For on-policy samples, the behavior and target policies are assumed to coincide, simplifying the gradient analysis.
The per-prompt gradient contribution is represented by a random variable `~G`, which is the sum of advantage-weighted average log-likelihood gradients. To make the variance analysis tractable, the L2 norm of this gradient contribution is studied.
A key assumption, **Assumption 4.1**, is made: (i) rollouts for a given prompt are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) in terms of reward (`~R`) and projected gradient (`~Z`), and (ii) `{ ~Z_j }` and `{ ~R_j }` are uncorrelated up to second order. This assumption is justified by the i.i.d. nature of rollouts and supported empirically by statistical tests provided in the appendix.
Based on this, Proposition 4.2 for Dr. GRPO and Proposition 4.3 for RLOO are derived. These propositions establish that the variance of the per-prompt projected gradient estimator is directly proportional to `p(1-p)`, where `p` is the success probability of the prompt, and `σ_Z^2` (the variance of the projected gradient `~Z`). Specifically:
- **Dr. GRPO:** `Var(~G) = ((n-1)/n^2) * 4 * σ_Z^2 * p(1-p)`
- **RLOO:** `Var(~G) = (1/(n-1)) * 4 * σ_Z^2 * p(1-p)`
These derivations highlight that the gradient variance is dependent on the number of rollouts `n` and the prompt's success probability `p_q`.

#### 3.2. GP Prior and Recursive Posterior Update for Success Probability

The success probability `p_q` is not directly observable and evolves with model training. VIP employs a non-parametric Gaussian Process (GP) model to predict `p_q,t` for each prompt `q` at iteration `t`.
- **Modeling:** The success probability `p_q,t` is modeled as `sigmoid(g_t(x_q))`, where `g_t(x_q)` is a latent function over prompt embeddings `x_q`. `g_t` is assumed to follow a GP with a mean function `m_t(·)` and an RBF kernel `K(·, ·)`.
- **Initialization:** At the first iteration, a zero-mean prior `g_1 ~ GP(0, K)` is used.
- **Prediction:** At the beginning of each iteration `t`, the current prior mean `m_t` is used to predict `^p_q,t = sigmoid(m_t(x_q))` for all prompts in the mini-batch `B_t`. These predictions inform the rollout allocation.
- **Sequential Updates:** After `n_q` rollouts are performed for each `q` in `B_t`, observed rewards `~R_q,j` are used to compute a clipped average reward `~R_q`. This is then converted to an empirical success probability `^p_q,t` and subsequently to an observed latent value `^g_q,t = sigmoid^-1(^p_q,t)`. Clipping is applied to `~R_q` to avoid infinite latent values. The observed latent values `^g_B_t` are then used to update the GP's posterior mean for all prompts (both queried and unqueried), which becomes the prior mean `m_t+1` for the next iteration. This Bayesian update leverages both recent observations and the similarity structure captured by the kernel.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Budget Allocation for Gradient Variance Minimization

Given the predicted success probabilities `^p_q` (and assuming `σ_Z^2` is constant across prompts, also supported by statistical tests in the appendix), the framework determines the optimal number of rollouts `n_q` for each prompt `q` in the mini-batch.
- **Optimization Problem:** The objective is to minimize the sum of per-prompt gradient variances:
 `min sum_{q in B_t} Var(~G_q)` subject to `sum_{q in B_t} n_q = C` (total budget) and `L <= n_q <= U` (per-prompt bounds).
 Let `a_q = 4 * σ_Z^2 * ^p_q * (1 - ^p_q)`. The problem becomes:
 - **Dr. GRPO:** `min sum_{q in B_t} a_q * (n_q-1)/n_q^2`
 - **RLOO:** `min sum_{q in B_t} a_q / (n_q-1)`
- **Continuous Relaxation and Solution:** The problem is a nonlinear integer optimization problem. It is first solved in its continuous relaxation (where `n_q` are real numbers). Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 show that for both Dr. GRPO and RLOO, the objective function is convex and decreasing. The KKT conditions lead to a closed-form expression for the optimal `n_q` as a function of a Lagrange multiplier `λ`. This `λ` can be found efficiently using bisection, ensuring that the total budget constraint is met.
- **Heuristic Rounding:** Since `n_q` must be integers, a heuristic rounding procedure is applied. The continuous solutions `n*_q` are initially rounded down. The remaining budget is then allocated iteratively to prompts that offer the largest marginal decrease in the objective function for an additional rollout, respecting the upper bounds `U`.

The methodology is designed to be computationally efficient, with the overhead for GP updates and allocation being negligible compared to model-forward and rollout costs, particularly for larger models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of the VIP framework demonstrates its effectiveness in improving sampling efficiency and performance across different tasks and LLM backbones.

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvements

VIP consistently improved sampling efficiency and achieved higher performance compared to uniform or heuristic allocation strategies in all evaluated benchmarks.

#### 4.2. Mathematical Reasoning Task

Experiments were conducted on the DAPO-MATH-17k dataset with evaluation on AIME2024 and AIME2025 benchmarks, using three backbone LLMs: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, under two rollout budget settings (8×Q and 16×Q).
- **Overall Gains:** Integrating VIP with Dr. GRPO and RLOO consistently yielded improvements in Pass@32 and Mean@32 metrics across all three base models and both budget settings.
- **Model-Specific Impact:** The relative performance gain from VIP was observed to be more substantial for the smaller models (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) compared to the Qwen2.5-Math-7B model. For instance, RLOO+VIP improved Pass@32 by +12.3 points and Mean@32 by +6.3 points over RLOO for Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B at 8×Q. This suggests that VIP's budget-aware variance reduction is particularly beneficial for models that might otherwise underutilize the rollout budget due to their inherent capabilities.

#### 4.3. Tool-Augmented Reasoning Task

The VIP allocation strategy was applied to teach LLMs to use a retrieval tool, training on the MuSiQue dataset and evaluating on the Bamboogle benchmark and MuSiQue test set. The Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model was used.
- **Coupled Improvements:** VIP led to simultaneous improvements in both answer accuracy (Exact Match, EM) and retrieval quality (F1@5, Precision@5). For example, Dr. GRPO+VIP increased EM from 20 to 23.2 on Bamboogle, while also raising F1@5/Precision@5 by +0.051/+0.060. RLOO+VIP demonstrated larger absolute gains (EM from 10.4 to 17.6, F1@5 from 0.190 to 0.264, Precision@5 from 0.225 to 0.294).
- **Mechanism Insight:** The parallel improvements in retrieval metrics indicate a reduction in false positives and improved ranking of relevant contexts, while EM gains suggest better integration of retrieved evidence into final answers. These results support VIP's role as a sample-efficient and domain-agnostic mechanism in complex reasoning tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies investigated the contributions of VIP's two core components: the Gaussian Process (GP) variance predictor and the adaptive budget allocator.
- **Component Importance:** RLOO+VIP achieved the highest performance across metrics compared to ablated variants. Replacing the adaptive allocator with heuristic strategies (Inverse Accuracy, Inverse Variance) resulted in substantial performance drops. Substituting the GP predictor with Ridge Regression yielded milder but consistent degradations.
- **Primary Benefit:** These findings confirm that VIP's primary benefit stems from its variance-aware budget allocation, with the calibrated uncertainty provided by the GP predictor further enhancing allocation effectiveness.

#### 4.5. Runtime Comparison

The computational overhead introduced by VIP was measured and found to be minimal.
- **Low Overhead:** The total computational overhead added by VIP was approximately 1.12% for the 1.5B-parameter model and 0.83% for the 7B-parameter model, including pre-training cached computations. Excluding cached computations, the overhead further reduced to 0.79% and 0.58%, respectively.
- **Scalability:** The relative overhead was observed to decrease as model size increased, indicating that VIP's overhead becomes less significant compared to the dominating model-forward and rollout costs in larger-scale training.

#### 4.6. Success Probability Prediction Quality

The quality of the GP predictor was evaluated against Moving Average and Ridge Regression baselines over training steps for Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B.
- **Predictor Accuracy:** The GP predictor consistently maintained a lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) throughout training for both model sizes. This indicates that the GP model provides a more accurate and adaptive estimation of prompt success probabilities, especially under the non-stationary dynamics inherent in RL training where policy changes lead to drifting success probabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Variance-Informed Predictive (VIP) allocation strategy represents a contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for large language models, particularly in contexts involving verifiable rewards. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Core Bottleneck:** The work directly targets the critical issue of sampling inefficiency in group-based reinforcement learning algorithms used for LLM training. By moving beyond fixed or heuristic rollout allocations, VIP offers a principled method to optimize the use of computational budgets, which is a major constraint in the development and fine-tuning of large models. This is particularly relevant as the scale of LLMs and their training costs continue to increase.
- **Principled and Theoretically Grounded Approach:** Unlike many adaptive strategies that rely on heuristics, VIP is built upon a rigorous theoretical analysis of gradient variance in group-based RL methods (Dr. GRPO and RLOO). The derivation of variance formulas explicitly linking rollout counts and prompt success probabilities provides a strong foundation for the allocation strategy. The use of convex optimization to minimize this variance under budget constraints further solidifies its theoretical robustness. This shifts the paradigm from ad-hoc adjustments to a more mathematically informed resource management.
- **Enhanced Training Efficiency and Performance:** Empirical results demonstrate that VIP consistently leads to improved sampling efficiency and higher final model performance across diverse tasks, including mathematical reasoning and tool-augmented reasoning. The observed gains, especially for smaller LLM backbones, suggest that VIP can help maximize the potential of models that might otherwise struggle to efficiently utilize available training resources. For complex tasks like tool-augmented reasoning, VIP's ability to simultaneously improve answer accuracy and retrieval quality highlights its broader applicability.
- **Adaptive and Dynamic Resource Management:** The integration of a Gaussian Process (GP) model for predicting prompt success probabilities allows VIP to adapt to the non-stationary nature of RL training. As the model's capabilities evolve, the GP predictor continuously updates its belief about prompt solvability, enabling the allocation strategy to dynamically re-distribute rollouts to the most informative prompts. This dynamic adaptation ensures that computational effort is concentrated where it can yield the greatest learning signal.
- **Low Computational Overhead:** The practical utility of VIP is further supported by its minimal computational overhead. The additional runtime for the GP model and optimization problem is shown to be a small fraction of the total training time, especially for larger models where rollout generation dominates the cost. This ensures that the benefits of adaptive allocation are not negated by excessive overhead.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a basis for future research. The authors explicitly mention exploring its integration with non-verifiable or noisy rewards, which would expand its applicability to RLHF and other alignment paradigms where reward signals are less precise. This could lead to more robust and efficient training methods across a wider spectrum of LLM fine-tuning scenarios.

In summary, VIP represents a step towards developing more adaptive, resource-efficient, and principled training pipelines for large language models. By intelligently allocating scarce computational resources based on predicted informativeness, it offers a method to accelerate training and improve the final performance of LLMs in verifiable reward settings.
