# RAFT-3D: Scene Flow using Rigid-Motion Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> RAFT-3D estimates dense pixelwise 3D motion (SE3 scene flow) from stereo or RGB-D frame pairs by extending RAFT's iterative optical-flow architecture with rigid-motion embeddings and a differentiable Dense-SE3 layer that softly groups pixels into rigid objects, achieving state-of-the-art scene flow without any object-instance supervision.

## Problem

- Prior deep scene-flow pipelines rely on object detection or instance segmentation to decompose scenes into rigid parts, needing box/mask supervision unavailable for novel objects.
- Detection and segmentation modules are non-differentiable, blocking end-to-end training without extra bounding-box or instance-level annotation.
- Point-cloud-based scene flow methods handle sparse Lidar data well but ignore scene rigidity structure, which prior work shows is critical for accuracy.

## Method

- ==Rigid-motion embeddings==: per-pixel learned vectors giving a soft, differentiable grouping of pixels into rigid objects via embedding-distance affinities, requiring no mask supervision.
- ==Dense-SE3 layer==: a differentiable ==Gauss-Newton optimization== layer enforcing geometric consistency between correspondence, embeddings, and per-pixel SE3 motion each iteration.
- ==bi-Laplacian embedding optimization==: a closed-form ==Cholesky-based smoothing== layer that regularizes embeddings within motion boundaries to better aggregate large rigid objects.

## Results

- On FlyingThings3D (FlowNet3D split), RAFT-3D raises 3D accuracy delta below 0.05 from the prior best **34.3%** to **83.7%**, and EPE-3D drops to **0.064** from **0.156**.
- On the KITTI leaderboard, RAFT-3D reaches scene-flow error **5.77**, beating the next-best published method DRISP's **6.31** despite using no object-instance supervision.
- Ablations show **16** update iterations, neighborhood radius **256px**, and inverse-depth revisions each improve 3D accuracy, e.g. radius 256 gives **87.1%** versus **38.7%** at radius **8**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing hard object segmentation with soft, differentiably learned rigid groupings lets geometric structure emerge from flow supervision alone, no instance labels needed.
> - Embedding a classical Gauss-Newton optimizer as a differentiable network layer lets gradients directly shape a structured output space like SE3 fields.
> - Bounded local neighborhoods can outperform full-image optimization, since real rigid objects have limited spatial extent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Scene flow, the problem of estimating pixelwise 3D motion between video frames, is a fundamental task in computer vision with critical applications in robotics (path planning, collision avoidance), virtual reality, and motion modeling. It represents a more complex generalization of optical flow (2D pixel motion) and stereo (3D point reconstruction from two views).

Historically, scene flow estimation evolved from traditional energy minimization approaches, similar to early optical flow methods. A key insight in these classical methods was the exploitation of **piecewise rigid motion priors**, recognizing that many real-world scenes consist of collections of rigidly moving objects (e.g., cars in a driving scene). This prior allowed scene flow algorithms to outperform those that treated optical flow and stereo separately.

The advent of deep learning transformed optical flow and stereo estimation, with numerous neural network architectures achieving superior performance. Integrating deep learning into scene flow, however, presented unique challenges. Early deep learning approaches for scene flow often relied on **pre-trained object detection or instance segmentation networks** to first decompose the scene into potential rigid objects. While effective for well-defined categories (like vehicles in autonomous driving datasets), this approach suffered from several limitations:
1. **Requirement for instance supervision:** Training these auxiliary networks demanded extensive, pixel-level ground truth annotations (bounding boxes, instance masks), which are costly and time-consuming to obtain.
2. **Lack of generalization to novel objects:** The reliance on pre-defined object categories meant these systems struggled to estimate motion for previously unseen or atypical rigid objects.
3. **Non-differentiable components:** Object detection/segmentation modules often introduced non-differentiable elements, making end-to-end training of the entire scene flow pipeline difficult without complex workarounds or separate training stages.

RAFT-3D directly addresses these limitations. By building upon the highly successful RAFT architecture for optical flow, Teed and Deng extend its iterative refinement paradigm to 3D motion. The core innovation of **rigid-motion embeddings** allows RAFT-3D to implicitly learn and leverage the piecewise rigid motion prior without any explicit object instance supervision. This makes the entire process end-to-end differentiable and more generalizable. The paper positions RAFT-3D as a significant advancement by demonstrating that a structured, iterative deep learning approach can effectively enforce geometric consistency and rigid-motion priors, leading to superior accuracy while sidestepping the data-intensive requirements of prior deep learning methods. It bridges the gap between the implicit learning capabilities of deep networks and the explicit geometric reasoning required for robust 3D motion estimation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RAFT-3D is to **estimate pixelwise 3D motion (scene flow)** between a pair of stereo or RGB-D video frames using a novel deep architecture. Specifically, it aims to output a dense field of **SE3 transformations**, assigning a rigid body motion (rotation and translation) to each pixel.

The key motivations driving this research are:

1. **Overcoming limitations of existing deep learning scene flow methods:** As discussed, prior deep learning approaches often required explicit object instance supervision (e.g., object detection, segmentation masks) to decompose scenes into rigid components. This introduced several problems:
 - **Data dependency:** The need for extensive, expensive object-level annotations.
 - **Generalization:** Difficulty in handling novel or unknown objects not present in training datasets.
 - **Training complexity:** Non-differentiable components hindering end-to-end training.
 RAFT-3D aims to circumvent these issues by implicitly learning rigid-motion priors.

2. **Leveraging the power of rigid motion priors:** The motivation is to retain the strong inductive bias that many real-world scenes consist of rigidly moving objects, which has been shown to be crucial for accurate 3D motion estimation. RAFT-3D seeks to integrate this prior directly and differentiably within a neural network.

3. **Extending the success of RAFT to 3D:** Given the state-of-the-art performance of RAFT for 2D optical flow, the authors were motivated to generalize its robust iterative refinement framework, correlation volume mechanism, and recurrent update operator to the more complex domain of 3D rigid body transformations (SE3).

4. **Developing an end-to-end differentiable architecture:** The goal is to create a single, unified network that can learn to estimate dense SE3 motion fields from raw image and depth data, allowing for direct supervision from ground truth 3D scene flow without intermediate, separately trained modules.

To achieve these objectives, the paper introduces two core innovations: **rigid-motion embeddings** for soft grouping of pixels into rigid objects, and the **Dense-SE3 layer**, a differentiable optimization layer that enforces geometric consistency and updates the per-pixel SE3 motion based on these embeddings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RAFT-3D builds upon the core principles of the RAFT architecture but significantly adapts them for 3D scene flow estimation. The network takes a pair of RGB-D images (I1, Z1) and (I2, Z2) as input and outputs a dense transformation field T ∈ SE(3)H×W, where each pixel is assigned an SE3 rigid body transformation.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture Overview

The architecture is iterative and recurrent, refining the SE3 motion field over multiple steps. It comprises:
- **Feature Extraction:** Two encoders process the input images. A shared-weight feature encoder (fθ) extracts dense 128-dimension feature vectors at 1/8 resolution. A separate context encoder, a pre-trained ResNet50 with skip connections, extracts semantic and contextual information from the first image, also at 1/8 resolution. This change from original RAFT is motivated by the need for a larger receptive field and semantic understanding for rigid object grouping.
- **Correlation Volume Construction:** A 4D correlation volume is constructed by computing the dot product between all-pairs of feature vectors from the two images. This volume, organized into a pyramid, encodes visual similarity across potential correspondences.
- **Recurrent Update Operator (GRU):** This is the core iterative component. At each iteration, it takes the current estimate of the SE3 motion field, indexes into the correlation volume to retrieve features, and processes these along with other motion-related features (flow field, twist field, depth residual) using a GRU-based unit. The GRU then outputs:
 - **Rigid-motion embedding map (V):** Per-pixel vectors representing soft grouping of pixels into rigid objects.
 - **Revision map (rx, ry, rz):** Predicted corrections to optical flow and inverse depth.
 - **Confidence maps (wx, wy, wz):** Weights for the revisions.
- **Dense-SE3 Layer:** This novel differentiable optimization layer takes the outputs of the GRU (embeddings, revisions, confidences) and computes an update for the SE3 motion field.
- **SE3 Upsampling:** The low-resolution (1/8) SE3 field is upsampled to the full input resolution using a convex upsampling method adapted for Lie groups (logarithm mapping to Lie algebra, convex combination, then exponential mapping back).

#### 3.2. Rigid-Motion Embeddings and Dense-SE3 Layer

This is the central innovation.
- **Rigid-Motion Embeddings:** Each pixel i is assigned an embedding vector vi. The similarity (affinity) between two pixels i and j, aij, is computed as a sigmoid of the negative L2 distance between their embeddings. High affinity implies they likely belong to the same rigid object.
- **Dense-SE3 Objective Function:** The Dense-SE3 layer formulates an objective function (Equation 15) that seeks to minimize the reprojection error for each pixel i and its neighbors j (within a specified radius Ni). Critically, this error is weighted by the affinity aij. This means pixels with similar embeddings (high affinity) strongly contribute to finding a single SE3 transformation Ti that explains their relative motion.
 E(δ) = Σi∈Ω Σj∈Ni aij * e^2_ij(δi)
 where e^2_ij(δi) is the squared reprojection error between the predicted correspondence of pixel j (under transformation Ti) and its revised correspondence from the GRU, weighted by confidence.
- **Efficient Optimization:** Instead of solving a single, massive optimization problem for the entire image, the problem is decomposed. Because each term in the objective only involves a single Ti, the optimization for each pixel's SE3 can be solved independently. The authors apply a single unrolled Gauss-Newton update at each iteration of the GRU. Leveraging Equation 10 (H = Σ w * J^T J, b = Σ w * J^T r(x)), the Hessian (H) and gradient (b) for each 6-variable SE3 transformation can be constructed efficiently without explicitly forming large Jacobian matrices, making it feasible to implement directly in CUDA.

#### 3.3. bi-Laplacian Embedding Optimization

To improve the coherence of embeddings, especially for large objects, a differentiable bi-Laplacian optimization layer is introduced. This layer smooths the embedding vectors within motion boundaries. The GRU predicts edge weights (wx, wy) that determine where smoothing should occur (weights near zero at boundaries prevent smoothing across them). The optimization (Equation 17) balances smoothness with fidelity to the original predicted embeddings. This linear system can be solved efficiently using sparse Cholesky decomposition, and the gradients are derived to ensure differentiability end-to-end.

#### 3.4. Supervision Strategy

RAFT-3D is supervised using ground truth optical flow and inverse depth change. The network outputs a sequence of SE3 transformations {T1..., TK}. For each Tk, the induced optical flow and inverse depth change (f^k_est) are computed (Equation 18) and compared to the ground truth (fgt) using an L1 loss (Equation 19). A weighted sum over the iterative predictions (γ=0.9) is used. Crucially, **no direct supervision is applied to the rigid-motion embeddings**. They are implicitly learned by differentiating through the Dense-SE3 update layer, allowing the network to discover meaningful groupings that lead to geometrically consistent 3D motion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RAFT-3D achieves state-of-the-art performance on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating significant improvements over existing methods and baselines.

#### 4.1. FlyingThings3D Dataset

- **Significant Accuracy Gains:** On the FlyingThings3D dataset, using the two-view evaluation split from FlowNet3D, RAFT-3D improved the best published accuracy (δ < 0.05, which measures the percentage of pixels with 3D endpoint error below 0.05 units) from 34.3% to a remarkable **83.7%**. The 3D EPE (Endpoint Error) also dropped dramatically from 0.156 to 0.064.
- **Superior to RAFT Baselines:** Compared to various RAFT extensions designed for 3D (e.g., 2D flow backprojected, 2D flow + depth change, direct 3D flow prediction), RAFT-3D consistently and substantially outperformed them, especially on 3D metrics. This highlights the effectiveness of RAFT-3D's structured representation (SE3 field) and analytical geometric constraints.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations (Fig. 2) demonstrate that RAFT-3D effectively segments scenes into rigidly moving regions, producing piecewise constant SE3 motion fields, even without explicit supervision for these segments.

#### 4.2. KITTI Dataset

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** On the challenging KITTI dataset for autonomous driving, RAFT-3D achieved an overall scene flow error of **5.77**, outperforming the previous best published method, DRISF (6.31).
- **No Instance Supervision Advantage:** This achievement is particularly notable because RAFT-3D achieved its superior performance despite using *no object instance supervision*, unlike DRISF which relies on Mask-RCNN pre-trained on Cityscapes and fine-tuned with bounding box and instance mask supervision. This validates RAFT-3D's ability to implicitly learn rigid motion priors without expensive annotations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Iterations:** Performance improved with more iterations up to about 16, showing the benefit of iterative refinement.
- **Neighborhood Radius:** A radius of 256 pixels for the Dense-SE3 objective yielded the best performance, indicating that a moderately large, but not full-image, neighborhood is optimal for capturing rigid objects.
- **Revision Factors:** Including inverse depth revisions in addition to optical flow revisions (Flow + Inv. Depth) significantly improved 3D metrics, emphasizing the importance of depth consistency.
- **bi-Laplacian Smoothing:** The bi-Laplacian optimization layer provided qualitative improvements by smoothing embedding vectors within motion boundaries and resulted in slight improvements in threshold metrics, confirming its utility for coherent object grouping.

#### 4.4. Efficiency

RAFT-3D has 45M trainable parameters (with 40M from the ResNet50 backbone). Inference on 540x960 images using a GTX 1080Ti GPU with 16 updates takes approximately 386ms, requiring 1.6GB of GPU memory, indicating reasonable computational efficiency for its performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RAFT-3D represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D scene flow estimation, with profound implications for research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Technical Breakthroughs

- **End-to-End Differentiable Rigid Motion Learning:** The most critical contribution is the development of an end-to-end differentiable architecture that implicitly learns and leverages rigid-motion priors without requiring explicit object instance supervision. This eliminates a major bottleneck in data annotation and simplifies the training pipeline significantly.
- **Novel Geometric Integration:** The introduction of rigid-motion embeddings coupled with the differentiable Dense-SE3 layer provides an elegant and effective way to enforce geometric consistency (SE3 transformations) within a deep learning framework. This iterative, optimization-driven approach within the neural network is a powerful paradigm for solving complex geometric vision problems.
- **Extension of Iterative Correspondence:** Successfully generalizing the RAFT model's iterative refinement and correlation volume concept from 2D optical flow to 3D SE3 motion demonstrates the robustness and versatility of this architectural pattern for dense correspondence tasks.

#### 5.2. Superior Performance and Generalizability

- **State-of-the-Art Accuracy:** Achieving unprecedented accuracy on FlyingThings3D and outperforming all published methods on KITTI establishes RAFT-3D as the new benchmark in scene flow estimation.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** By decoupling rigid motion learning from explicit object categories, RAFT-3D is inherently more robust to novel objects and diverse scene compositions, which is a major advantage for real-world deployment in dynamic and unpredictable environments.

#### 5.3. Impact on Downstream Applications

- **Robotics and Autonomous Driving:** Accurate and dense 3D motion estimation is foundational for tasks like path planning, collision avoidance, simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), and predictive modeling of agent behavior. RAFT-3D's improved accuracy and robustness can lead to safer and more intelligent autonomous systems.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality:** High-fidelity scene flow is crucial for realistic motion parallax, consistent object interactions, and dynamic scene reconstruction in AR/VR applications.
- **Motion Modeling and Analysis:** Researchers in biomechanics, surveillance, and computer graphics can benefit from more precise 3D motion fields for analysis, synthesis, and animation.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The success of RAFT-3D's implicit learning of rigid components could inspire similar approaches for other complex structural priors in computer vision, reducing reliance on expensive semantic annotations. The underlying principles of differentiable geometric optimization on Lie manifolds, facilitated by tools like LieTorch, are likely to find broader application in various 3D vision problems. The high accuracy achieved without semantic supervision suggests a promising path towards more truly "geometric" deep learning models.

In conclusion, RAFT-3D is a landmark paper that advances the state-of-the-art in scene flow by introducing a geometrically sound, end-to-end differentiable architecture. Its ability to implicitly learn rigid motion from raw data represents a significant step forward, promising to enhance the capabilities of numerous real-world applications requiring robust 3D scene understanding.
