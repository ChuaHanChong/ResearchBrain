# PhysX-Anything: Simulation-Ready Physical 3D Assets from Single Image

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysX-Anything is a framework that generates simulation-ready physical 3D assets, complete with geometry, articulation, and explicit physical properties, directly from a single image. It leverages a fine-tuned Vision-Language Model and a novel, efficient 3D representation to produce assets deployable in physics simulators for tasks like robotic policy learning.

## Problem

- Existing 3D content generation primarily focuses on visual aesthetics, lacking crucial physical properties and articulation required for interactive simulations and robotics.
- The verbose nature of detailed 3D geometry poses a challenge for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) due to their inherent token budget limitations.
- Current physical 3D datasets often have limited object diversity and insufficient physical annotations, hindering robust training of generative models.

## Method

- Developed PhysX-Anything, a unified VLM-based generative framework that extracts geometry, articulation, and physical attributes from a single image to produce simulation-ready 3D assets.
- Introduced a novel 3D physical representation utilizing coarse voxel grids with sparse index serialization and continuous range merging, achieving a 193x token reduction for VLM processing.
- Designed a global-to-local generative pipeline that employs a multi-round VLM conversation for overall physical description and part-level geometry, followed by a controllable flow transformer for geometry refinement.

## Results

- PhysX-Anything outperformed state-of-the-art methods on physical and geometric metrics, achieving a 20.35 PSNR for geometry and reducing absolute scale error from 43.44 to 0.30 on PhysX-Mobility.
- Evaluations on in-the-wild images and user studies confirmed robust generalization, with VLM-based metrics yielding a 0.94 score for geometry and kinematic parameters.
- Generated assets were directly imported into MuJoCo-style simulators and successfully used for contact-rich robotic policy learning tasks, validating their physical plausibility and utility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-round VLM conversation effectively integrates high-level physical understanding with detailed part-level geometry generation from a single image.
> - Efficient voxel-based representations can overcome VLM token limitations for 3D by compressing explicit structural information into a manageable sequence.
> - Generating assets directly in standard formats like URDF and XML enables seamless integration into physics engines, providing truly functional outputs for embodied AI and robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D content generation has witnessed rapid advancements, driven by progress in deep learning, particularly diffusion models and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Historically, much of this research has focused on generating visually appealing 3D models from various inputs, such as text, images, or even multiple views. Methods like DreamFusion, Get3D, and recent feed-forward approaches like LGM and 3DTopia excel at creating high-quality 3D geometry and visual appearance, but often treat objects as static, rigid entities.

However, a critical gap exists between these visually impressive static 3D assets and the requirements of applications in robotics, embodied AI, and interactive simulations. These downstream applications demand more than just visual fidelity; they require 3D assets that possess accurate physical properties (e.g., density, absolute scale, material properties) and articulated structures (e.g., joint constraints, motion ranges). Most existing 3D generation methods either completely overlook these crucial physical and articulation attributes or provide only partial information, making their direct deployment in physics engines or simulators challenging, if not impossible.

Several lines of research have attempted to address these limitations:
- **Part-aware Generation:** Works like OmniPart focus on modeling object hierarchies and fine-grained structures, but generally still lack explicit physical attributes necessary for simulation.
- **Articulated Object Generation:** Approaches like URDFormer and Articulate-Anything aim to generate articulated objects. However, many of these are retrieval-based, relying on existing model libraries, which limits their ability to synthesize novel structures and generalize to "in-the-wild" images. Others, like DreamArt or URDF-Anything, may require specific inputs (e.g., video, point clouds) or struggle with robustness for complex textures or multiple movable parts, and often still lack comprehensive physical attributes.
- **Physical Deformation Learning:** Some methods, such as PhysGen3D, Vid2Sim, and PhysTwin, attempt to learn the physical deformation behavior of 3D assets. Yet, these often simplify assumptions, such as homogeneous materials, or neglect other essential physical attributes needed for realistic simulation.
- **Early Physical 3D Generation:** The authors' previous work, PhysXGen, made an important step by proposing a framework for generating 3D assets with core physical properties like absolute dimension and density. However, even PhysXGen fell short of providing assets that were directly "plug-and-play" in standard physics simulators like MuJoCo, limiting their immediate utility for complex, contact-rich tasks in embodied AI.

PhysX-Anything strategically positions itself to bridge these disparate efforts by introducing the "first simulation-ready (sim-ready) physical 3D generative paradigm." Its unique contribution lies in simultaneously achieving four critical criteria from a *single in-the-wild image*: generating objects with **articulation**, **physical properties**, **strong generalization capabilities**, and **direct simulation-readiness** (Table 1). By exporting assets in standard formats like URDF and XML, PhysX-Anything directly enables their deployment in widely used physics engines, thus significantly advancing the practical utility of generated 3D content for downstream applications. This work represents a crucial step towards creating functional digital twins of real-world objects, seamlessly integrating perception with actionable simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PhysX-Anything stems from the growing demand for functional, physically-grounded 3D assets in applications such as robotics, embodied AI, and interactive simulations. While existing 3D generation methods have excelled in visual fidelity, they largely neglect the crucial physical and articulation properties required for these real-world applications. This omission creates a substantial gap, as assets lacking explicit physical attributes (e.g., density, absolute scale, material) and articulation structures (e.g., joint types, motion limits) cannot be directly deployed or meaningfully interacted with in physics simulators.

The key objectives of PhysX-Anything are designed to systematically address this critical gap:

1. **Generate Simulation-Ready Physical 3D Assets from a Single Image:** The overarching goal is to create a generative framework that, given only a single real-world image, can produce high-quality 3D assets complete with explicit geometry, detailed articulation structures, and essential physical attributes, all directly exportable in formats suitable for standard physics engines (e.g., URDF, XML). This aims to make 3D asset creation more accessible and automated for simulation-driven tasks.

2. **Develop a Unified VLM-based Generative Model:** The authors aim to leverage the powerful capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to jointly predict and integrate geometry, articulation, and physical properties. This holistic approach is motivated by VLMs' strong reasoning and multi-modal understanding abilities, which are crucial for interpreting an image and synthesizing its complex physical characteristics.

3. **Overcome VLM Token Budget Limitations for Detailed Geometry:** A significant technical challenge in using VLMs for 3D generation is the intrinsic tension between their limited token budgets and the verbose nature of detailed 3D geometry representations (e.g., raw meshes). A core objective is to design a novel, efficient 3D representation that can substantially compress geometric information into a manageable token sequence without sacrificing explicit structural detail or requiring complex, task-specific tokenizers.

4. **Construct a Diverse and Rich Physical 3D Dataset:** Existing physical 3D datasets suffer from limited diversity in object categories and often lack comprehensive physical annotations. A key objective is to build a new, large-scale dataset, PhysX-Mobility, that significantly expands category coverage and provides rich physical annotations to enable robust training and generalization of the generative model.

5. **Validate Direct Deployability and Utility in Downstream Tasks:** The ultimate motivation is to ensure that the generated assets are truly functional. Therefore, an objective is to rigorously validate that these "sim-ready" assets can be directly imported and utilized in standard physics simulators for contact-rich tasks, specifically demonstrating their potential in robotic policy learning. This validation step is crucial to confirm the practical impact and unlock new possibilities for embodied AI and robotics.

By pursuing these objectives, PhysX-Anything seeks to move beyond merely visual 3D content generation towards a paradigm of physically-grounded, functional 3D asset creation that can directly empower a wide range of real-world AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysX-Anything introduces a novel, unified VLM-based generative framework designed to produce simulation-ready physical 3D assets from a single input image. The methodology can be broken down into three key components: the global-to-local generative pipeline, a new efficient 3D physical representation, and a sophisticated VLM and physical representation decoder.

#### 3.1. Global-to-Local Generative Pipeline

The core of PhysX-Anything operates through a multi-round conversation with a fine-tuned Vision-Language Model (VLM). This pipeline follows a global-to-local strategy:
1. **Image Input:** Given a single real-world image of an object.
2. **Round 1: Overall Physical Description:** In the first conversation round, the VLM takes the image and a text prompt (e.g., "Please analyze the given image of an object and output its structured description") to generate comprehensive *overall physical information*. This includes high-level details such as the object's name, category, absolute dimensions, constituent parts, material properties, affordances, and the kinematic group information (articulation structure) in a structured, VLM-friendly JSON-style format.
3. **Subsequent Rounds: Part-Level Geometry:** For each individual part identified in the overall description, subsequent conversation rounds (from 2 to N+1, where N is the number of parts) are initiated. In these rounds, the VLM is prompted (e.g., "Based on the structured description of l_0, generate its 3D voxel grid") to generate detailed *geometric information* for that specific part. Crucially, to mitigate context forgetting due to overly long prompts, only the shared overall information from Round 1 is retained as context when generating per-part geometry. This ensures consistency while allowing for independent generation of detailed shapes.
4. **Physical Representation Decoding:** Once all overall and part-level information (including geometry and physical attributes) is generated in the textual physical representation, a dedicated decoder module processes this information to synthesize the final high-quality, simulation-ready 3D assets.

#### 3.2. Novel 3D Physical Representation

A central innovation of PhysX-Anything is its highly efficient 3D physical representation, designed specifically to overcome the VLM's limited token budget while preserving explicit geometric structure. Unlike prior VLM-based methods that used text-serialized mesh representations (leading to excessive token lengths) or VQ-GANs (requiring special tokens and new tokenizers), PhysX-Anything adopts a voxel-based approach:
- **Coarse Voxel Representation:** The VLM operates on a coarse 32x32x32 voxel grid to capture the overall geometry. Converting meshes directly to these coarse voxels already yields a significant 74x reduction in token count compared to raw mesh representations.
- **Sparse Voxel Index Serialization:** To further compress tokens and eliminate redundancy in sparse voxel data, the 32x32x32 grid is linearized into a sequence of indices from 0 to 32^3 - 1. Only the indices of *occupied* voxels are serialized.
- **Contiguous Range Merging:** Neighboring occupied indices are then merged into continuous ranges, represented by hyphens (e.g., "199-216"). This final step achieves an impressive *193x token reduction* compared to the original mesh format.
- **Explicit Structure & No Special Tokens:** This representation maintains explicit structural information without introducing any additional special tokens or requiring a customized tokenizer during fine-tuning, simplifying the training and deployment process.
- **Physical Attributes Integration:** For overall information, a tree-structured, JSON-style format is used, which is VLM-friendly and allows for richer textual descriptions and physical attributes (e.g., material, density, absolute scale). Kinematic parameters (motion direction, axis location, range) are also converted into the voxel space to ensure consistency between structure and geometry.

#### 3.3. VLM and Physical Representation Decoder

The system utilizes Qwen2.5 as the foundation VLM, which is fine-tuned on the custom PhysX-Mobility dataset.
- **VLM Fine-tuning:** The VLM is fine-tuned to understand and generate the proposed physical representation through the multi-round dialogue process.
- **Controllable Flow Transformer for Geometry Refinement:** The coarse voxel representation generated by the VLM is then fed into a *controllable flow transformer*, inspired by ControlNet. This transformer-based control module guides a pre-trained structured latent diffusion model (e.g.,) to synthesize *fine-grained, high-fidelity voxel geometry*. The training objective (L_geo) ensures that this transformer accurately predicts the fine-grained target voxel representation conditioned on the noisy sample, image condition, time step, and the coarse voxel guidance.
- **3D Asset Generation:** From the fine-grained voxel representation, a sparse VAE decoder generates various 3D assets, including mesh surfaces, radiance fields, and 3D Gaussians.
- **Part Segmentation:** A nearest-neighbor algorithm is applied to segment the reconstructed mesh into individual part-level components, based on the voxel assignments for each part.
- **Physical-Format Decoder:** Finally, the physical-format decoder combines all the generated information—global structural information, absolute scale, affordances, material properties, and the refined part-level geometries—to output simulation-ready physical 3D assets. These assets are exported in six commonly used formats, crucially including URDF and XML files, making them directly importable into standard physics engines like MuJoCo.

#### 3.4. PhysX-Mobility Dataset

To address the limited diversity and annotation quality of existing physical 3D datasets, the authors constructed **PhysX-Mobility**. This new dataset:
- Collects assets from PartNet-Mobility.
- Features *carefully annotated physical attributes* for each object.
- Significantly expands category diversity, covering 47 common real-world object categories (e.g., toilets, fans, cameras, coffee machines, staplers), more than doubling the categories of prior physical 3D datasets.
- Includes over 2,000 common real-world objects with rich physical annotations, providing a robust training ground for the VLM.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating an efficient representation, a powerful VLM, a refinement network, and a meticulously curated dataset, allows PhysX-Anything to generate high-quality, truly simulation-ready 3D assets from single images.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhysX-Anything demonstrates strong performance and robust generalization through extensive experiments on its newly constructed PhysX-Mobility dataset and in-the-wild images. The key findings and results are summarized below:

#### 4.1. Evaluation on PhysX-Mobility

- **Superior Quantitative Performance:** PhysX-Anything consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods, including URDFormer, Articulate-Anything, and PhysXGen, across a comprehensive suite of geometric and physical metrics (Table 2).
 - **Geometric Fidelity:** Achieves the highest PSNR (20.35), lowest CD (14.43), and highest F-score (77.50), indicating superior geometric accuracy compared to all baselines.
 - **Dramatic Improvement in Absolute Scale:** The error in absolute scale is drastically reduced from 43.44 (PhysXGen) to a mere 0.30, representing over a 99% relative improvement. This highlights the VLM's ability to infer accurate real-world dimensions.
 - **Enhanced Physical Attributes and Descriptions:** PhysX-Anything attains the highest scores for Material (17.52), Affordance (14.28), Kinematic parameters (VLM) (0.83), and Description (19.36). The high description score underscores the VLM's inherent strength in generating coherent, part-level textual descriptions reflecting a deep understanding of object structure and function.
- **Qualitative Superiority:** Visual results (Fig. 5) clearly demonstrate that PhysX-Anything generates more faithful geometry, articulation, and physical attributes. It exhibits superior generalization, especially when compared against retrieval-based methods, and produces significantly more plausible physical attributes than even PhysXGen.

#### 4.2. In-the-Wild Evaluation

To assess real-world applicability and generalization, experiments were conducted on approximately 100 in-the-wild images collected from the Internet, covering diverse everyday object categories.

- **VLM-based Evaluation:** Automated evaluation using VLMs (e.g., GPT-5, as contextually implied by "VLM-based evaluation") confirms robust generative performance. PhysX-Anything achieves substantially higher scores on both Geometry (VLM) (0.94) and Kinematic parameters (VLM) (0.94) compared to all competing methods (Table 4), indicating superior generalization to unseen real-life inputs.
- **User Studies:** Human perception of generated quality was assessed through user studies involving 14 volunteers rating outputs on a 0-5 scale. PhysX-Anything's results aligned much better with human preferences (Table 3), achieving significantly higher scores for Geometry (Human) (0.98) and all physical attributes, including Absolute scale (0.95), Material (0.84), Affordance (0.94), Kinematic parameters (0.98), and Description (0.96). This confirms its robust performance in generating realistic and physically plausible assets in diverse real-world scenarios (Fig. 6).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies validated the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed 3D representation:
- **Representation Efficiency:** Comparing different voxel-based representations (Voxel, Index, Ours) showed that the proposed method (voxel-based with sparse index serialization and continuous range merging) achieved the best performance (Table 5). This representation allows PhysX-Anything to capture complete and detailed geometry for complex structures, whereas less efficient representations suffered from noticeable degradation due to token budget constraints. Qualitative results (Fig. 7) further illustrate the robustness for geometrically challenging objects.

#### 4.4. Robotic Policy Learning in Simulation

A crucial finding is the direct utility of PhysX-Anything's generated assets in downstream applications:
- **Direct Deployability:** The generated simulation-ready 3D assets (e.g., faucets, cabinets, lighters, eyeglasses) can be *directly imported* into a MuJoCo-style simulator without further manual intervention.
- **Physically Plausible Behavior:** These assets were successfully used for contact-rich robotic policy learning tasks, such as faucet switch manipulation, door opening and closing, eyeglass temple folding, lighter snapping open, laptop closing, and handle manipulation (Fig. 8). This validates that the assets exhibit highly physically plausible behavior and accurate geometric structures, providing a new direction for robotics policy learning.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that PhysX-Anything not only sets a new state-of-the-art in physical 3D asset generation but also delivers truly simulation-ready outputs, capable of direct deployment in real-world simulation and robotics environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysX-Anything represents a significant leap forward in the field of 3D content generation, particularly at the intersection of computer vision, embodied AI, and robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Gap between Static 3D and Functional Simulation:** The most profound contribution of PhysX-Anything is its ability to bridge the long-standing gap between visually appealing, static 3D models and the functional, physically-grounded assets required for interactive simulations and real-world AI applications. By producing "sim-ready" assets directly from a single image, it transforms 3D generation from a purely visual task into a functional content creation paradigm.

- **Empowering Embodied AI and Robotics:**
 - **Automated Environment Generation:** The capability to generate physically accurate and articulated objects automatically from images will revolutionize the creation of training environments for embodied AI agents and robots. This eliminates the laborious and expensive manual process of designing and annotating 3D assets for simulators.
 - **Realistic Interaction and Policy Learning:** Robots require realistic simulations for training complex manipulation and interaction policies. PhysX-Anything provides diverse, interactive objects with correct physical properties and articulation, enabling more robust and generalizable robotic policy learning in simulation, as demonstrated by the MuJoCo experiments.
 - **Digital Twins for Real-World Scenarios:** The generation of accurate physical properties and articulation allows for the creation of more faithful digital twins of real-world objects, which can be invaluable for testing, validation, and control strategies in robotics.

- **Advancing 3D Generative Models with VLMs:**
 - **Efficient Geometry Encoding:** The novel 3D representation, which achieves a 193x token compression while preserving explicit geometric structure and avoiding special tokens, is a major technical advancement. This innovation unlocks the full potential of VLMs for generating complex 3D geometry, making end-to-end learning more feasible and scalable.
 - **Holistic Multi-Modal Generation:** By unifying geometry, articulation, and physical attributes within a single VLM-based pipeline, PhysX-Anything demonstrates a powerful approach to holistic multi-modal generation, moving beyond single-aspect (e.g., geometry-only) generative tasks.

- **Enriching 3D Datasets for Research:** The creation of PhysX-Mobility, a new dataset with expanded category diversity and rich physical annotations, is a valuable contribution to the research community. This dataset will serve as a crucial resource for training and evaluating future models in physical 3D generation, accelerating progress in the field.

- **Broadening Downstream Applications:** Beyond robotics and embodied AI, PhysX-Anything's capabilities can impact a wide range of applications, including:
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Enabling the rapid creation of interactive and physically consistent virtual environments.
 - **Gaming and Entertainment:** Automating the generation of complex, functional assets for game development.
 - **Industrial Design and Prototyping:** Potentially assisting in the rapid generation of physically accurate 3D models for design iteration and simulation.

In conclusion, PhysX-Anything represents a pivotal step towards autonomous content creation for the next generation of intelligent systems. By making physically-grounded, simulation-ready 3D assets easily accessible from everyday images, it not only pushes the boundaries of generative AI but also lays a robust foundation for future research and development across 3D vision, embodied AI, and robotics.
