# A Comprehensive Survey of Self-Evolving AI Agents: A New Paradigm Bridging Foundation Models and Lifelong Agentic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper defines and systematically reviews the emerging paradigm of self-evolving AI agents, which bridge static foundation models with dynamic lifelong adaptability. It introduces a unified conceptual framework and a comprehensive taxonomy of evolution techniques, mapping the progression towards continuous self-improvement in AI systems.

## Problem

- Most existing AI agent systems rely on manually configured settings that remain static post-deployment, limiting their adaptability to dynamic, continuously evolving real-world environments.
- Current surveys on AI agents have not sufficiently covered the crucial aspects of agent self-evolution and continual adaptation, leaving a gap in the literature for lifelong, autonomous AI systems.
- Manual reconfiguration of agents in ever-changing scenarios is impractical and does not scale, necessitating autonomous adaptation mechanisms.

## Method

- The paper formally defines =="Self-Evolving AI Agents"== and proposes a ==unified conceptual framework== (System Inputs, Agent System, Environment, Optimisers) to abstract their ==iterative feedback loop==.
- It introduces =="Three Laws of Self-Evolving AI Agents"== (==Endure==, ==Excel==, ==Evolve==) as guiding principles for safe, performance-preserving, and autonomous optimization.
- The survey systematically categorizes and reviews existing ==evolution and optimization techniques== for ==single-agent and multi-agent systems==, as well as domain-specific applications.

## Results

- The survey establishes a unified conceptual framework for self-evolving agents, comprising System Inputs, Agent System, Environment, and Optimisers, providing a consistent lens for analysis.
- It yields a detailed taxonomy of agent evolution and optimization techniques, categorizing methods for single-agent and multi-agent systems and by specific components targeted for improvement.
- The paper outlines a fundamental paradigm shift in AI development towards continuous, autonomous adaptation, emphasizing the critical role of rigorous, multi-dimensional evaluation and continuous safety monitoring.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-evolving AI agents represent a new paradigm, bridging the gap between static foundation models and lifelong agentic systems capable of continuous adaptation.
> - The progression of LLM-based systems moves from Model Offline Pretraining (MOP) through online adaptation (MOA) and multi-agent orchestration (MAO) to the ultimate vision of Multi-Agent Self-Evolving (MASE).
> - Effective self-evolution requires a structured feedback loop, where optimizers continuously refine agent components (LLM behavior, prompts, memory, tools, workflows, communication) based on environmental feedback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) have ignited significant interest in AI agents capable of addressing complex, real-world tasks. However, the authors identify a critical limitation: most existing agent systems rely on manually configured settings that remain static post-deployment. This inherent rigidity restricts their capacity to adapt to dynamic and continuously evolving environments, such as shifting user intents, changing task requirements, or varying external information sources.

Existing surveys on AI agents, as the authors note, typically focus on either general agent architectures and functionalities, specific components like planning or memory, or domain-specific applications. While these provide valuable insights, they "have not been sufficiently covered" in the context of agent self-evolution and continual adaptation. The authors argue that these capabilities are "central to the development of lifelong, autonomous AI systems," thereby leaving a "critical gap in the literature."

This survey directly addresses this void by offering a "focused and systematic review of techniques that enable agents to evolve and improve themselves based on interaction data and environmental feedback." It introduces "self-evolving AI agents" as a "new paradigm bridging Foundation Models and Lifelong Agentic Systems," thereby providing a much-needed comprehensive and integrative perspective on this emergent research direction.

The paper further situates this work within a broader historical progression of LLM-based systems:
- **Model Offline Pretraining (MOP):** The initial stage of training models on static data.
- **Model Online Adaptation (MOA):** Introduction of post-deployment updates like fine-tuning or RLHF.
- **Multi-Agent Orchestration (MAO):** Coordination of multiple LLM agents for complex tasks without modifying core model parameters.
- **Multi-Agent Self-Evolving (MASE):** The ultimate vision of lifelong, self-improving agents that continuously refine their internal components based on environmental feedback.

By clearly defining MASE and tracing its lineage from earlier paradigms, the survey not only consolidates disparate research efforts but also articulates a clear trajectory for future AI development. It acknowledges a concurrent survey by Gao et al. (2025b) but differentiates its own contribution by offering a more "comprehensive and integrative perspective" grounded in a unified conceptual framework. This strategic positioning establishes the survey as a foundational reference for understanding the mechanisms and challenges of building adaptive and autonomous AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the inherent limitations of current AI agent systems when confronted with dynamic and evolving real-world environments. While LLMs have endowed agents with impressive reasoning and planning capabilities, their reliance on static, manually configured setups renders them ill-equipped for continuous adaptation. The authors highlight the impracticality and scalability issues associated with manual reconfiguration in ever-changing scenarios, such as new product introductions in customer service or the emergence of novel algorithms in scientific research.

To address this critical gap, the paper aims to explore and define the emerging paradigm of "Self-Evolving AI Agents"—autonomous systems capable of continuous self-improvement and adaptation. The core objectives are multifaceted:

1. **Formalize the Concept:** To formally define "Self-Evolving AI Agents" and propose a set of "Three Laws of Self-Evolving AI Agents" (Endure, Excel, Evolve). These laws act as guiding ethical and practical principles, prioritizing safety and stability during modification (Endure), preserving or enhancing performance (Excel), and enabling autonomous optimization (Evolve).
2. **Map the Evolution of LLM-centric Learning:** To delineate the progression of LLM-based systems from static pretraining (MOP) to dynamic, lifelong self-evolving agentic systems (MASE), providing a clear conceptual roadmap for the field.
3. **Introduce a Unified Conceptual Framework:** To present a high-level framework that abstracts the feedback loop underlying self-evolving agentic systems. This framework, comprising System Inputs, Agent System, Environment, and Optimisers, aims to provide a systematic foundation for understanding and comparing diverse evolution and optimization approaches.
4. **Systematically Review Existing Techniques:** To conduct a comprehensive review of current evolution and optimization techniques across various dimensions, including single-agent, multi-agent, and domain-specific settings, focusing on how different components of the agent system (e.g., foundation models, prompts, memory, tools, workflows, communication) are targeted for improvement.
5. **Address Critical Considerations:** To provide a dedicated discussion on evaluation methodologies, safety, and ethical considerations, recognizing their paramount importance for ensuring the effectiveness, reliability, and responsible deployment of self-evolving agents.
6. **Identify Future Directions:** To pinpoint key open challenges and outline promising research avenues, thereby fostering future exploration and advancing the development of more adaptive, autonomous, and lifelong agentic systems.

Ultimately, the authors are motivated to provide researchers and practitioners with a structured and systematic understanding of this new paradigm, laying the groundwork for AI systems that are not just intelligent but also continuously adaptive, resilient, and sustainable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a comprehensive and systematic review methodology to synthesize the rapidly growing body of literature on self-evolving AI agents. Their approach is structured to provide both a conceptual foundation and a detailed categorization of existing techniques.

The core of their methodology involves the introduction of a **unified conceptual framework** for self-evolving agentic systems. This framework abstracts the iterative feedback loop inherent in such systems, breaking it down into four interconnected components:
1. **System Inputs (I):** This component defines the contextual information and data provided, distinguishing between "task-level optimization" (improving overall performance on a task using datasets) and "instance-level optimization" (enhancing performance on specific examples).
2. **Agent System (A):** The central component subject to optimization, which can be a single agent or a multi-agent collective. It is further decomposed into elements like the underlying LLM, prompting strategy, memory module, and tool-use policy.
3. **Environment:** The external context in which the agent operates, providing inputs and generating feedback signals. This can range from benchmark datasets with predefined metrics (e.g., accuracy, F1) to dynamic, real-world settings. The environment is crucial for delivering quantifiable performance measures, often augmented by LLM-based evaluators when ground truth is unavailable.
4. **Optimisers (P):** The engine of self-evolution, responsible for refining the agent system based on environmental feedback. Optimisers are characterized by their "search space" (S), which defines the configurable aspects of the agent, and their "optimization algorithm" (H), encompassing strategies like rule-based heuristics, gradient descent, Bayesian optimization, MCTS, reinforcement learning, or evolutionary algorithms.

This framework serves as the organizing principle for the entire survey, enabling a structured comparison and understanding of diverse approaches. Building upon this, the authors systematically categorize and review existing techniques along several dimensions:

- **Single-Agent Optimisation:** This section delves into methods that enhance individual agents by focusing on:
 - **LLM Behaviour Optimization:** Training-based (SFT, RL) and test-time (feedback-based, search-based) strategies.
 - **Prompt Optimization:** Edit-based, generative, text gradient-based, and evolutionary approaches.
 - **Memory Optimization:** Short-term (context management) and long-term (persistent knowledge) mechanisms.
 - **Tool Optimization:** Training-based, inference-time, and tool functionality creation/adaptation.
- **Multi-Agent Optimisation:** This section expands to systems with multiple agents, covering:
 - **Manually Designed Systems:** Parallel, hierarchical, and debate paradigms.
 - **Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Systems:** Prompt optimization, topology optimization (code-level workflows, communication-graph topologies), unified optimization (code-based, search-based, learning-based), and LLM backbone optimization (reasoning-oriented, collaboration-oriented).
- **Domain-Specific Optimisation:** This part highlights tailored strategies for specialized fields such as biomedicine (medical diagnosis, molecular discovery), programming (code refinement, debugging), and financial and legal research.
- **Evaluation:** A dedicated section reviewing benchmark-based (tool-driven, web navigation, multi-agent, GUI/multimodal, domain-specific) and LLM-based (LLM-as-a-Judge, Agent-as-a-Judge) evaluation paradigms, alongside safety, alignment, and robustness considerations.

By meticulously structuring their analysis around this conceptual framework and detailed categorization, the authors provide a rigorous and accessible overview of this complex and rapidly evolving research landscape.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's comprehensive analysis reveals several key findings and results that collectively paint a clear picture of the current state and future trajectory of self-evolving AI agents.

First, the paper successfully establishes a **unified conceptual framework** comprising System Inputs, Agent System, Environment, and Optimisers. This framework effectively abstracts the iterative feedback loop, providing a consistent lens through which to analyze and compare diverse self-evolution techniques across different levels of agent complexity. This is a significant contribution for organizing a nascent and rapidly expanding field.

Second, the systematic review yields a detailed **taxonomy of agent evolution and optimization techniques**. This taxonomy categorizes methods based on whether they target single-agent or multi-agent systems, and further by the specific component being optimized (e.g., LLM behavior, prompts, memory, tools, workflows, communication mechanisms).
- **Single-agent optimization** highlights the dual approach of improving LLM behavior through both training-based (SFT, RL) and test-time (feedback, search) methods, alongside the critical role of prompt engineering (edit-based, generative, gradient-based, evolutionary), dynamic memory management (short-term coherence, long-term knowledge retention via RAG), and sophisticated tool integration (training, inference, and even autonomous tool creation).
- **Multi-agent optimization** reveals a shift from manually designed coordination (parallel, hierarchical, debate) to automated self-evolution. This includes optimizing prompts for individual agents, evolving system topologies (code-level workflows, communication graphs), and unified approaches that jointly optimize multiple aspects (prompts, topology, LLM backbone) using advanced search and learning algorithms. The trend is towards "agentic supernets" and "layered neural networks" that can dynamically configure multi-agent teams.
- **Domain-specific optimization** demonstrates that general principles must be tailored to specific contexts. In biomedicine, agents are optimized for diagnostic dialogues and molecular discovery, often integrating specialized tools (e.g., cheminformatics) and knowledge graphs. In programming, the focus is on iterative code refinement and debugging through self-feedback, collaborative workflows, and tool-enhanced frameworks. For financial and legal research, agents are designed for decision-making under uncertainty and rule-grounded reasoning, leveraging multi-agent collaboration and external data sources.

Third, the survey underscores a fundamental **paradigm shift** in AI development. It meticulously maps the evolution from static Model Offline Pretraining (MOP) to Model Online Adaptation (MOA), then to Multi-Agent Orchestration (MAO), and ultimately to Multi-Agent Self-Evolving (MASE). This progression reflects a move towards systems that are not merely pre-trained or fine-tuned but are capable of continuous, autonomous adaptation over their entire operational lifetime. The introduction of the "Three Laws of Self-Evolving AI Agents" (Endure, Excel, Evolve) provides a principled framework for governing this continuous self-improvement, emphasizing safety, performance preservation, and autonomous optimization.

Finally, the authors emphasize the crucial role of **rigorous, multidimensional evaluation**. They detail a variety of benchmark-based evaluations for different agent types (e.g., tool-driven, web navigation, multi-agent, GUI, domain-specific) and the emerging "LLM-as-a-Judge" and "Agent-as-a-Judge" paradigms. A key finding is that safety, alignment, and robustness are not one-off concerns but continuous requirements that must be monitored and addressed throughout an agent's evolutionary lifecycle, particularly as agents become capable of complex, multi-step malicious behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the future direction of AI research and its practical applications, establishing itself as a foundational document for the emerging field of self-evolving AI agents.

First, its primary significance lies in providing a **systematic and comprehensive understanding** of self-evolving AI agents. By synthesizing a vast and rapidly expanding body of literature, the authors offer a much-needed structured overview that clarifies terminology, categorizes diverse approaches, and highlights the overarching trends. This serves as an invaluable resource for both new researchers entering the field and seasoned practitioners seeking to navigate its complexities, enabling more informed design and development decisions.

Second, the introduction of the **unified conceptual framework** (System Inputs, Agent System, Environment, Optimisers) is a crucial intellectual contribution. It offers a standardized lens for analyzing and comparing different self-evolution strategies, fostering a more coherent and directed research agenda. This framework moves beyond mere description to provide an analytical tool that can guide the design of future adaptive systems.

Third, the paper’s articulation of the **MOP→MOA→MAO→MASE paradigm shift** provides a compelling roadmap for the evolution of LLM-based systems. By presenting MASE as the ultimate goal—systems capable of lifelong, autonomous learning—the survey inspires a vision for AI that is resilient, adaptable, and increasingly autonomous. This vision has **far-reaching implications** across numerous domains:
- **Scientific Discovery:** Self-evolving agents could autonomously generate hypotheses, design experiments, and iterate on research workflows, accelerating breakthroughs.
- **Software Engineering:** Agents could co-evolve development pipelines, integrating new tools and adapting to changing requirements.
- **Human-AI Collaboration:** Systems could learn individual preferences and continuously personalize interaction styles, leading to more natural and effective partnerships.
- **Physical World Interaction:** Through robotics and IoT, self-evolving agents could perceive environmental changes, act upon them, and incorporate real-world feedback into their evolutionary loops, paving the way for truly embodied AI.

Fourth, the emphasis on the **"Three Laws of Self-Evolving AI Agents" (Endure, Excel, Evolve)** introduces essential ethical and safety guardrails. By mandating continuous monitoring for safety and stability (Endure), preservation or enhancement of performance (Excel), and autonomous optimization (Evolve), the survey encourages responsible development. This is critical for building public trust and ensuring that as AI systems become more autonomous, they remain aligned with human values and operational safety standards. This proactive approach to ethics is vital as agents transition from controlled environments to complex, open-ended real-world deployment.

Finally, the identification of **key open challenges and promising future research directions** serves as a direct call to action for the research community. By pinpointing areas such as multimodal optimization, autonomous tool creation, real-world evaluation, and balancing efficiency with effectiveness, the survey guides future innovation, aiming to overcome current limitations and realize the full potential of lifelong agentic systems. This ensures that the field progresses not just rapidly, but also sustainably and responsibly, building AI that can truly "live, learn, and last."
