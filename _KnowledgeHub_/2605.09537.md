# Drift is a Sampling Error: SNR-Aware Power Distributions for Long-Horizon Robotic Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Chongqing Institute of Green and Intelligent Technology introduce Context-Aware Power Sampling (CAPS), a training-free, inference-time framework that addresses instruction drift in long-horizon robotic tasks by re-conceptualizing it as a systematic sampling error. CAPS uses an adaptive dual-process system with global power distributions and an SNR-based metacognitive control to iteratively refine action trajectories, achieving up to 18% higher success rates in real-world benchmarks compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from "instruction drift" in complex, long-horizon robotic tasks, where robots lose contextual grounding with the ultimate goal.
- Prior solutions like prompt engineering are limited by open-loop generation, and inference-time scaling methods often rely on inefficient parallel sampling and re-ranking for continuous action spaces.
- Local greedy sampling is prone to collapsing into "Negative Pivotal Windows" – irreversible local optima that lead to globally erroneous actions despite high local probability.

## Method

- Context-Aware Power Sampling (**CAPS**) is a training-free, inference-time framework that re-frames inference as sampling from a global power distribution to sharpen the probability landscape for long-horizon consistency.
- It employs an adaptive dual-process system that switches between "fast intuition" (greedy execution) and "slow deliberation" (global optimization via MCMC) using a Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) based metacognitive control.
- When drift risk is detected by a drop in SNR (high entropy), a Metropolis-Hastings (MCMC) algorithm is activated for iterative trajectory refinement, using randomized resampling and power distribution-based acceptance checks.

## Results

- CAPS achieved a **15.2%** increase in average success rate on RoboTwin 1.0, reaching **47.4%**, and outperformed TACO by **6.1%**.
- On the Libero-long benchmark, CAPS demonstrated a **97.6%** average success rate, significantly outperforming OpenVLA (**49.8%**) and RoboMonkey (**56.5%**).
- Real-world evaluation on XLeRobot showed CAPS improved the average success rate by **18.0%** over TACO (**53.0%**), achieving **71.0%**, with its adaptive strategy incurring only **2.15x** latency compared to **8.5x** for an 'Always-On' variant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instruction drift in long-horizon robotics can be fundamentally re-conceptualized as a systematic sampling error inherent in local greedy strategies, leading to "myopia of single-step prediction."
> - Applying global power distributions mathematically sharpens the model's probability landscape, enabling the system to "see through" locally tempting but globally detrimental actions.
> - An adaptive, context-aware mechanism, triggered by an increase in Shannon entropy (drop in SNR), allows for efficient allocation of computational resources, activating deliberation only at critical decision points.
