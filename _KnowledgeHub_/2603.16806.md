# DexGrasp-Zero: A Morphology-Aligned Policy for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> DexGrasp-Zero introduces a universal policy for dexterous robotic grasping, leveraging a morphology-aligned graph representation and hand-agnostic motion primitives for zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization. This approach, integrating physical property injection, achieved an 85.0% grasping success rate on unseen hands in simulation and an 82% average success rate on real-world robot platforms.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning policies for dexterous grasping are hand-specific, necessitating expensive re-training and data collection for each new robotic hand.
- Current cross-embodiment transfer methods, relying on intermediate action representations, often introduce errors or generate kinematically infeasible commands due to limitations in respecting hand-specific constraints.
- Prior graph-based representations for dexterous hands suffered from misalignment and information loss, failing to adequately capture anatomical semantics or complete topological relationships.

## Method

- Developed **DexGrasp-Zero**, a universal policy utilizing a ==morphology-aligned graph representation== for hand states and a ==hand-agnostic motion primitive space== for actions.
- Introduced a ==Morphology-Aligned Graph Convolutional Network (MAGCN)== that integrates ==layer-wise injection of URDF-derived physical properties== (e.g., joint limits, link lengths) to ensure kinematically feasible and stable grasps.
- Employed a fixed, hand-specific mapping to convert hand-agnostic motion primitives into physical joint commands, augmented with a ==feasibility penalty== based on an activation mask to guide the policy towards physically executable actions.

## Results

- The DexGrasp-Zero policy achieved an **85.0%** zero-shot grasping success rate on two unseen hands in simulation, marking a **59.5** percentage point improvement over the state-of-the-art CrossDex baseline.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer in real-world environments, achieving an **82%** average success rate across **three** heterogeneous physical robot platforms with **10** unseen objects, comparable to intra-hand oracle policies.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of morphology-aligned motion primitives, layer-wise physical property injection, and the activation mask with feasibility penalty for achieving high performance and generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning hand morphology at the graph node level (e.g., fingertip, proximal) and defining control in a hand-agnostic motion primitive space enables effective zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer across diverse robot hands.
> - Integrating static physical properties derived from URDF files directly into each layer of a graph neural network (MAGCN) allows the policy to adaptively compensate for kinematic and actuation variations, leading to more precise and stable grasps.
> - Explicitly guiding the policy with an activation mask and a corresponding feasibility penalty on executable primitive axes is critical for learning physically realizable actions that generalize to unseen hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is witnessing rapid advancements in dexterous hand hardware, leading to a proliferation of morphologically heterogeneous robotic hands. A significant challenge in this landscape is the development of robotic policies that can generalize across different hand embodiments. Current reinforcement learning (RL) policies for dexterous grasping are typically specific to the morphology on which they are trained. This specificity necessitates expensive re-training and data collection whenever a new robotic hand is introduced, which impedes the practical application and widespread adoption of dexterous manipulation systems.

Existing research approaches to cross-embodiment dexterous grasping generally fall into two categories:
1. **Open-loop grasp pose generation:** These methods generate static grasp postures but often lack the robustness required for real-time perturbations due to the absence of closed-loop feedback.
2. **RL-based policy transfer using intermediate action representations:** This common strategy involves training policies to predict simplified intermediate action targets (e.g., fingertip displacements or abstract body poses) which are then retargeted to the specific kinematics of each hand. A critical limitation of this approach is that the retargeting step may introduce errors or generate commands that violate the kinematic or actuation constraints of the target hand, thus hindering effective zero-shot generalization to new hardware.

Furthermore, while graph neural networks (GNNs) have been explored for representing dexterous hands, prior graph-based methods have often suffered from misalignment in cross-hand representations. Some approaches use kinematic links as nodes, which may lack anatomical semantic grounding. Others model only sparse keypoints, leading to information loss regarding topological relationships. Issues such as splitting a single anatomical unit with multiple degrees of freedom into multiple nodes can also hinder semantic alignment across hands with varying DoF distributions.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing an end-to-end, single universal policy that directly maps morphology-aligned graph representations to feasible and physically consistent actions. It aims to overcome the representation mismatch at both state and action levels that has constrained previous methods, enabling zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer without the need for hand-specific retargeting models or extensive re-learning. The paper emphasizes the potential for leveraging underlying morphological and kinematic regularities shared across diverse hands, despite their superficial differences, through a graph-based abstraction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and transferable policy for dexterous grasping that can achieve zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization to unseen robotic hands without requiring re-learning or fine-tuning. This objective is motivated by several key challenges and observations:

1. **Increasing Diversity of Dexterous Hand Hardware:** The rapid evolution of dexterous robotic hands demands policies that are not tethered to a single hardware design. The ability to transfer skills across diverse morphologies is crucial for the practical deployment of these technologies.
2. **Heterogeneity in Hand Kinematics and Physical Constraints:** Different dexterous hands exhibit substantial variations in their kinematic structures, degrees of freedom (DoF), joint limits, and link geometries. These differences make it difficult to align the input observations and output control commands for a universal policy, posing a fundamental obstacle to cross-embodiment learning.
3. **Limitations of Existing Transfer Paradigms:** Current methods that rely on intermediate motion targets and subsequent hand-specific retargeting steps often lead to kinematically infeasible actions or introduce errors due to the difficulty of respecting embodiment-specific constraints. This "lossy" conversion process limits their effectiveness for zero-shot generalization.
4. **Underlying Morphological Regularities:** Despite the superficial differences, dexterous hands share fundamental anatomical and kinematic regularities. The inter-joint relationships can be naturally modeled as a graph structure. The authors hypothesize that by explicitly extracting and leveraging these shared semantics, a more transferable policy can be learned.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of DexGrasp-Zero are:

- To introduce a **morphology-aligned graph representation** that semantically aligns the kinematic keypoints of diverse hands to anatomically grounded nodes.
- To define a **hand-agnostic motion primitive space** based on tri-axial orthogonal motions (flexion, abduction, axial rotation) for each node, enabling consistent control semantics across different morphologies.
- To design a **Morphology-Aligned Graph Convolutional Network (MAGCN)** that encodes this graph-based representation for policy learning.
- To integrate a **Physical Property Injection mechanism** into MAGCN. This mechanism is designed to fuse hand-specific physical constraints, derived from URDF files (e.g., link lengths, actuation limits), directly into the graph features, enabling the policy to adaptively compensate for these variations.
- To demonstrate that a single policy, jointly trained on a set of diverse hands, can achieve high zero-shot grasping success rates on novel, unseen hand hardware in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexGrasp-Zero proposes a universal grasping policy designed for zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer, operating within a reinforcement learning framework. The methodology centers on a morphology-aligned graph representation for both state and action spaces, coupled with a specialized graph neural network architecture.

**Problem Formulation:**
The research defines a unified cross-embodiment learning setting where a single policy ($\pi_\theta$) is trained on a set of diverse training hands ($H_{train}$) and evaluated without fine-tuning on unseen test hands ($H_{test}$). The policy outputs an intermediate, hand-agnostic action representation ($\alpha^h_t$), which is then converted into physical joint commands ($\alpha^{h}_{physical,t}$) for a specific hand `h` via a fixed, hand-specific mapping ($M_h$). The objective is to maximize the expected discounted return across training hands, with the constraint of zero-shot generalization to test hands.

**Morphology-Aligned State and Action Graph Representation:**

1. **Morphology-Aligned State Representation:**
 - Each dexterous hand `h` is abstracted into a graph $G_h = (V_h, E_h)$. The nodes ($V_h$) correspond to six anatomical unit types: fingertip, distal, middle, proximal, metacarpal, or wrist, allowing for structural and semantic alignment despite varying node counts ($N_h$) across embodiments.
 - Edges ($E_h$) represent kinematic relationships between these anatomical units.
 - **Node Features ($X^h_{node}$):** For each node $v_i$, a feature vector $x^h_i$ captures its dynamic state, including:
 - Distance vector from the node to the closest object surface point ($d^h_i$).
 - Joint angles ($\theta^h_i$) and velocities ($\dot{\theta}^h_i$) expressed in motion-primitive axes.
 - Binary contact indicator ($c^h_i$) and contact force magnitude ($f^h_i$).
 - One-hot encodings for finger semantic class (e.g., thumb, index) and node type (e.g., fingertip, wrist).
 - **Edge Connectivity ($A_h$):** An adjacency matrix encodes the kinematic connections.
 - **Global Features ($x^h_g$):** A vector summarizing object-centric and wrist-level information, such as wrist-to-object displacement, wrist linear/angular velocities, and object linear/angular velocities in the wrist frame.
 - The complete state representation is $s_h = (X^h_{node}, A_h, x^h_g)$.

2. **Hand-Agnostic Motion-Primitive Space:**
 - A universal action space ($\alpha^h_{prim}$) is defined to ensure control-semantic alignment across heterogeneous hands. It comprises two main components:
 - **6-DoF Wrist Motion Command:** Incremental changes in wrist Cartesian position ($\Delta p^h_w$) and orientation ($\Delta \theta^h_w$).
 - **Node-Level Articulation Commands:** For each node $v_i$, three orthogonal motion primitives (FLEXion, ABDuction, Axial ROTation) are defined, inspired by human hand biomechanics. Each primitive outputs an incremental displacement ($\Delta_{flex}, \Delta_{abd}, \Delta_{rot}$).
 - The full motion-primitive space $\alpha^h_{prim}$ concatenates wrist commands and all node-level primitive commands.

3. **Mapping to Executable Physical Commands ($M_h$):**
 - A fixed, hand-specific mapping $M_h$ converts the hand-agnostic motion primitives ($\alpha^h_{prim}$) into embodiment-specific physical joint commands ($\Delta q_h$). This mapping is a deterministic, sparse linear operator derived from the hand's kinematic specification.
 - It selects valid node-primitive pairs for each physical DoF, respects joint ordering, and applies sign corrections. It is implemented as an indexing rule, determined by applying unit joint excitations in simulation and matching induced motion semantics to corresponding primitives.

**DexGrasp-Zero Policy Design (MAGCN):**

1. **Morphology-Aligned Graph Encoding Module:** This module processes the state graph, encoding node features and adjacency into node embeddings ($E^h_{node}$) and global features into a global embedding ($E^h_g$) using a GCN backbone.

2. **Physical-Property Graph Construction:**
 - URDF files of each hand are parsed to construct a physical-property graph ($G^h_{physical}$), which is topology-aligned with the state graph.
 - **Physical Node Features ($X^h_{physical}$):** Each node $v_j$ in this graph is augmented with static mechanical priors, including normalized joint limits, velocity bounds, 3D axis directions for primitives, damping coefficients, and link vectors. These are encoded into a physical embedding matrix ($E^h_p$).
 - **Activation Mask ($M^h_{activation}$):** A binary mask indicates which motion primitives are physically realizable for each node, reflecting mechanical constraints. This mask is used to condition the decoder and for a reward penalty.

3. **Layer-wise Physical Injection in MAGCN:** The core innovation in the MAGCN architecture is the injection of these URDF-derived physical embeddings ($E^h_p$) at every layer of the GCN encoder. This layer-wise fusion (concatenating current node representation with physical priors) enables the policy to adaptively compensate for varying link lengths and actuation limits, thereby contributing to precise and stable grasping.

4. **Decoding Module:**
 - **Node-level Primitives:** Node embeddings are concatenated with the corresponding row from the activation mask ($M^h_{activation}$) and decoded by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) into the three motion primitive scalars for each node.
 - **Wrist 6-DoF Command:** The global embedding and the wrist node embedding are concatenated and decoded by another MLP to produce the wrist position and orientation changes.
 - These outputs are combined to form the full motion-primitive space $\alpha^h_{prim}$.

**Reward Design:**
The reward function combines a standard grasping reward ($r_{grasp}$) that encourages stable contact, with an additional "feasibility penalty" ($r_{pen}$). This penalty term is designed to suppress outputs on inactive primitive axes, leveraging the activation mask, ensuring that the policy learns to generate physically executable actions.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
To bridge the gap between simulation and real-world deployment, a teacher-student distillation strategy is employed. A privileged teacher policy is trained in simulation with access to ideal contact and force information. This teacher policy's actions are then used to train a student policy via behavior cloning. The student policy, using MAGCN as its backbone, is augmented with an LSTM to process historical proprioceptive and visual observations, enabling it to implicitly infer unobservable privileged information (like contacts and forces) in real-world scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DexGrasp-Zero was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, covering cross-hand transfer, single-hand transfer, and ablation studies.

**Simulation Evaluations:**

1. **Cross-Hand Zero-Shot Transfer (Multi-Hand Training):**
 - A single DexGrasp-Zero policy, jointly trained on four diverse hands (Allegro, Shadow, Ability, Schunk) using the YCB dataset, demonstrated high performance across both seen and unseen embodiments.
 - The full model achieved an average success rate of **92.0%** on the four training hands and **85.0%** on two unseen hands (LEAP and Inspire).
 - This performance substantially outperformed the state-of-the-art CrossDex multi-object baseline, which achieved 52.5% on seen hands and 26.5% on unseen hands. DexGrasp-Zero improved the zero-shot transfer success rate by 59.5 percentage points.

2. **Cross-Hand Zero-Shot Transfer (Single-Hand Training):**
 - When trained on a single hand using the GraspXL benchmark, DexGrasp-Zero policies still exhibited zero-shot transfer capabilities to other embodiments.
 - For example, policies trained on Shadow or Schunk hands achieved a 0.94 success rate on the unseen Inspire hand, surpassing the GraspXL specialist policy (0.91) which is designed for single-hand training.
 - The transfer performance was observed to be influenced by the morphological and dynamic similarities between the source and target hands.

3. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Without motion primitives:** Removing the morphology-aligned motion primitives led to a significant performance drop, from 92.0% to 56.5% on seen hands and from 85.0% to 34.0% on unseen hands. This indicates the crucial role of motion primitives in facilitating structural and semantic alignment.
 - **Early fusion (of physical priors):** Replacing the proposed layer-wise physical injection with a one-shot feature concatenation (early fusion) resulted in substantially worse performance (from 92.5% to 50.0% on seen hands) and exhibited unstable learning dynamics, highlighting the importance of layer-wise integration.
 - **Without $G_{physical}$ priors:** Omitting the URDF-derived physical-property priors reduced performance from 92.0% to 88.5% on seen hands and from 85.0% to 80.5% on unseen hands, demonstrating the utility of these constraints for generalization, particularly in zero-shot scenarios.
 - **Without $M_{activation}$ and $r_{pen}$:** Disabling the activation mask conditioning and its corresponding feasibility penalty decreased unseen hand performance from 85.0% to 63.0%. This suggests that explicitly guiding the policy on executable primitive axes is critical for preventing invalid actions that do not transfer.

4. **URDF Prior Sensitivity:**
 - Perturbing the URDF-derived link lengths (scaling by factors of 2 or 1/4) in simulation led to a drop in success rate (e.g., from 0.92 to 0.85 or 0.87), indicating that the policy functionally leverages the encoded kinematic scale.

5. **Backbone Comparison:**
 - Comparing MAGCN (using a GCN backbone) with a Graph-Transformer backbone, the GCN consistently yielded better and more stable performance across multiple seeds. This was attributed to the GCN's stronger kinematic inductive bias and simpler message-passing structure, which appears more amenable to RL optimization.

6. **Generalization to Non-Anthropomorphic End-Effectors:**
 - The policy, trained solely on anthropomorphic hands, successfully transferred zero-shot to the non-anthropomorphic 8-DoF Barrett Hand in simulation, achieving a 0.70 success rate on YCB objects. This indicates the extensibility of the morphology-aligned representation beyond human-like hand designs.

**Real-World Evaluations:**

1. **Zero-Shot Grasping on Real Robots:** The distilled policy was deployed on three heterogeneous physical robot platforms (Kinova arm with LEAP hand, Kinova arm with Inspire hand, and Piper arm with Revo2 hand) with 10 unseen objects.
 - The policy achieved an **82% average success rate** across these platforms.
 - Performance was observed to be comparable to an intra-hand oracle policy, for instance, 88% success on LEAP compared to the oracle's 90%.
 - Consistent with simulation results, removing the URDF-derived physical-property priors resulted in a performance drop (e.g., 0.88 to 0.84 on LEAP, 0.72 to 0.62 on Revo2), further affirming their importance for real-world generalization.
 - The Revo2 hand exhibited the lowest success rate, which the authors attributed to its smaller size hindering stable grasping of larger objects.
 - Observed failure modes included "empty grasps" on small objects due to the lack of tactile feedback, and embodiment-specific difficulties related to limited dexterity or enclosure capacity of certain hands.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexGrasp-Zero research presents a notable advancement in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation, particularly concerning the challenge of cross-embodiment generalization.

1. **Enabling Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration of robust zero-shot transfer capabilities for dexterous grasping policies across morphologically diverse robot hands. This directly addresses the current limitation where policies are typically hand-specific, necessitating costly retraining for each new hardware iteration. By enabling a single policy to operate on multiple, unseen hands, the work offers a pathway to reduce development costs and accelerate the deployment of dexterous manipulation systems.

2. **Novel Representation and Control Paradigm:** The introduction of a morphology-aligned graph representation for hand states, combined with a hand-agnostic motion primitive action space, provides a universal framework for representing and controlling diverse dexterous hands. This abstraction, grounded in anatomical and biomechanical principles, offers a semantically consistent interface that may be extended to a wider array of robotic end-effectors, as hinted by its successful transfer to the non-anthropomorphic Barrett Hand.

3. **Enhanced Robustness through Physical Property Integration:** The integration of URDF-derived physical constraints directly into the policy's learning process through layer-wise physical injection in MAGCN is a key methodological advancement. This mechanism allows the policy to adaptively account for hand-specific mechanical properties like link lengths and actuation limits, leading to more precise, stable, and kinematically feasible grasps. This enhances the overall robustness and reliability of the learned policies in varied real-world scenarios.

4. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The validation of DexGrasp-Zero on multiple physical robot platforms with unseen objects underscores its practical potential. The performance achieved in real-world settings, comparable to intra-hand oracle policies, suggests that the method is not only theoretically sound but also robust enough for deployment outside of simulation. This may encourage wider adoption in industrial automation, service robotics, and research involving diverse robotic hardware.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** By open-sourcing the code and datasets, this research provides a valuable resource for the robotics community. It may serve as a foundation for further investigations into more generalized robotic manipulation tasks, adaptive control for heterogeneous systems, and the development of foundation models for robotics that can abstract away hardware-specific details. The findings also highlight areas for future work, such as the incorporation of robust tactile feedback for handling small objects and refining policies for hands with limited dexterity.
