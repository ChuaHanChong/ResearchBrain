# OmniVLA: Physically-Grounded Multimodal VLA with Unified Multi-Sensor Perception for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> OmniVLA introduces a framework that augments Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by integrating infrared, millimeter-wave radar, and acoustic sensors through a novel "sensor-masked image" representation. This enables robots to perform tasks requiring perception beyond standard RGB vision, achieving an 84% average success rate on challenging real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation rely solely on RGB camera input, limiting their ability to perform tasks requiring perception beyond the visible spectrum.
- Robots struggle with tasks needing cues like temperature, hidden object detection, or sound localization, which are crucial for complex physical interactions.
- Effectively interpreting and utilizing heterogeneous sensor data (e.g., thermal images, raw radar signals, acoustic arrays) within VLA models presents challenges due to diverse formats and data scarcity.

## Method

- Developed a "sensor-masked image" representation where preprocessed 2D heatmaps from infrared, mmWave radar, and acoustic sensors are spatially aligned and overlaid onto semantically segmented regions of RGB images.
- Integrated these sensor-masked images into an adapted VLA backbone, keeping the pre-trained vision encoder frozen and adding lightweight, trainable MLP projection layers for each sensor modality.
- ==Utilized Vision-Language Models (VLM)== like GPT-4o and segmentation models like Grounded SAM 2 to dynamically generate task-relevant semantic masks for precise sensor information blending.

## Results

- OmniVLA achieved an **84%** average success rate on real-world tasks requiring thermal, mmWave, and acoustic perception, demonstrating a **59%** improvement over RGB-only VLA models and a **28%** improvement over raw multi-sensor fusion.
- The framework demonstrated data-efficient learning, achieving comparable success rates with approximately **50%** fewer training episodes than raw multi-sensor baselines for thermal perception tasks.
- OmniVLA exhibited robust generalization to unseen tasks after multi-sensory pre-training, outperforming baselines by up to **59%** in few-shot scenarios for novel manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating novel non-RGB sensors into VLA models significantly expands robotic perception, enabling tasks fundamentally impossible with visual input alone.
> - The "sensor-masked image" representation offers a data-efficient and uniform method to leverage pre-trained RGB vision encoders for diverse, heterogeneous sensor inputs while preserving RGB-like statistics.
> - Anchoring non-visual cues to specific objects via VLM-generated semantic masks facilitates physically-grounded spatial intelligence and effective action generation for manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of robotic manipulation, multi-modal perception, and large-scale foundation models, specifically Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The broader landscape has seen significant advancements in VLA models, which leverage extensive vision-language pretraining to enable robots to follow human instructions and generalize to new tasks with remarkable proficiency. These models map user prompts and camera observations to robot actions, promising a future of generalist robotic policies.

However, the authors accurately identify a critical limitation: the vast majority of existing VLA models rely *solely* on RGB camera input. While powerful for tasks discernible by visual cues, this reliance inherently restricts robots from performing tasks that require perception beyond the visible spectrum. For example, sensing temperature, detecting hidden objects, or localizing sound sources are beyond the capabilities of RGB-only systems. Previous efforts to extend VLA capabilities have focused on integrating depth information (for better spatial understanding), tactile perception (for physical interaction), or speech (for human-robot interaction). While valuable, these works largely haven't explored the integration of *novel* sensing modalities such as infrared (thermal), millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar, and acoustic microphone arrays. These modalities offer unique physical information: infrared reveals temperature, mmWave penetrates occlusions for hidden object localization, and acoustic arrays provide spatial sound awareness.

The paper effectively positions OmniVLA as a pioneering effort to bridge this gap. It differentiates itself from other multi-sensor fusion works, particularly those in autonomous driving or RF perception. While these fields have successfully combined sensors like mmWave radar and cameras for environmental understanding (e.g., 3D object detection, resilience in adverse weather), they typically focus on perception tasks rather than direct manipulation. Furthermore, their architectures are often highly specialized and lack the generalization and instruction-following capabilities inherent in VLA models for robotic action generation. Some RF-based works also rely on RFID tags, which limits their applicability.

OmniVLA's key contribution to this landscape is its novel approach to integrating these diverse, non-RGB sensors into a VLA framework for robotic manipulation. It tackles the challenges of heterogeneous sensor data, the need for a uniform representation, and data scarcity, all while aiming to preserve the strong generalization capabilities of existing VLA backbones. By introducing "sensor-masked images," OmniVLA offers a data-efficient and generalizable strategy for fusion that aligns new sensor information with the RGB-centric understanding of pre-trained vision models, thereby pushing the boundaries of what generalist robots can perceive and do.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of OmniVLA is to **equip Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with unified multi-sensor perception, enabling robots to perform manipulation tasks that require information beyond standard RGB vision.** This overarching goal is driven by the recognition that while current VLA models are powerful, their reliance on RGB cameras severely limits their ability to interact intelligently with the physical world in ways that humans, and indeed many advanced robotic applications, demand.

The authors articulate several specific motivations and challenges that OmniVLA aims to address:

1. **Expanding Perception Capabilities:** The core motivation is to move "beyond-RGB" perception. RGB cameras are inherently limited; they cannot see temperature contrasts, penetrate opaque materials, or spatially localize sounds. Integrating infrared, mmWave, and acoustic sensors allows robots to leverage these unique physical cues for tasks like finding a cold drink, locating an item hidden inside a box, or uncovering a ringing phone buried under clothes. This directly enhances the robot's ability to tackle challenging, real-world scenarios.

2. **Effective Interpretation of Heterogeneous Sensor Information:** A significant challenge lies in how VLA models, typically trained on vast amounts of RGB data, can effectively interpret and utilize highly diverse sensor data formats (e.g., thermal images, raw radar signals, acoustic arrays). Naively feeding raw sensor streams or training separate encoders for each modality often leads to poor performance, data inefficiency, and increased system complexity. The motivation is to find a way for the VLA model to seamlessly integrate and act upon this varied information.

3. **Scalable and Uniform Representation:** Sensors differ widely in their physical format, field of view, and resolution. Developing a system that requires a unique fusion model for every specific sensor hardware combination is not scalable. The motivation is to create a *uniform, hardware-agnostic representation* that can handle diverse sensor inputs, making the approach generalizable and compatible with future sensor additions.

4. **Data-Efficient Learning:** Training large-scale multi-modal models typically requires immense datasets. However, real-world multi-modal sensor data, particularly paired with robotic action demonstrations, is far scarcer than web-scale image-text data. The motivation is to develop a *data-efficient approach* that can quickly adapt existing RGB-pretrained VLA models to leverage new sensor modalities without requiring prohibitively large amounts of new training data.

5. **Physically-Grounded Spatial Intelligence:** Ultimately, the aim is to empower robots with a deeper, physically-grounded understanding of their environment, enabling them to perform manipulations guided by real-world physical properties rather than just visual appearance.

To address these challenges, the authors draw inspiration from human perception, where non-visual cues are often anchored to the visual scene. This inspiration underpins their core methodological innovation: the "sensor-masked image" representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OmniVLA's methodology is structured around two main components: **sensor-masked image generation** and a **multi-sensor Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model architecture** designed to leverage this novel representation.

#### 3.1. Sensor-Masked Image Generation

This component focuses on preprocessing and unifying diverse raw sensor data into a format digestible by an RGB-pretrained VLA model.

- **Sensor Data Preprocessing:**
 - **Uniform 2D Spatial Representation:** All raw sensor measurements are converted into a camera-like 2D spatial representation.
 - **Infrared Camera:** Outputs raster images directly (infrared intensity over image coordinates).
 - **mmWave Radar and Microphone Array:** These provide complex signal samples from array elements. To achieve a consistent 2D spatial mapping (azimuth-elevation heatmaps), conventional **delay-and-sum beamforming** is applied. This technique processes the raw signals to create image-like heatmaps, where intensity corresponds to the strength of mmWave reflections or sound sources at specific angular positions.
- **Segmentation Mask Processing:**
 - **VLM-Generated Prompts:** The task description (e.g., "Find my ringing phone") and the RGB image are fed into a cloud-based Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4o. The VLM generates descriptive segmentation prompts for objects relevant to the task (e.g., "black phone", "cardboard boxes"). These prompts are initialized at the beginning of a task and updated asynchronously in the background to accommodate scene changes, minimizing real-time latency.
 - **Semantic Segmentation:** The VLM-generated prompts and the RGB image are then input into a segmentation model, Grounded SAM 2 (which combines Segment Anything Model and Grounding DINO). This model generates precise binary masks (0-1 matrices) for the task-related objects in the RGB image.
- **Sensor-Masked Image Blending (The Core Innovation):**
 - **Spatial Alignment:** A one-time calibration aligns each sensor modality's 2D heatmap with the RGB camera frame (rotation and cropping), ensuring rough spatial correspondence.
 - **Overlaying Information:** The preprocessed sensor heatmaps are then *overlaid onto the masked regions* of the RGB image. The formula used is:
 `I_m_i = mask ⊙ (α * I_c_i + (1 - α) * I_RGB) + (1 - mask) ⊙ I_RGB`
 where `I_m_i` is the sensor-masked image for modality `i`, `mask` is the segmentation mask, `I_c_i` is the calibrated sensor image, `I_RGB` is the original RGB image, and `α` is a blending hyper-parameter (defaulted to 1). This process selectively replaces or blends RGB pixels within the segmented object regions with the corresponding sensor information, while leaving the rest of the RGB image untouched.
 - **Benefits of this Representation:**
 - **Spatially Grounded and Semantically Aligned:** Sensor information is directly linked to specific objects in the RGB image, facilitating robotic manipulation.
 - **RGB-like Statistics:** The output image maintains statistics close to RGB images, allowing reuse of pre-trained vision encoders.
 - **Uniform Interface:** Provides a consistent image-native format across diverse sensors, reducing hardware-specific complexities.

#### 3.2. Multi-Sensor Vision-Language-Action Model Architecture

The second part integrates these sensor-masked images into an adapted VLA model.

- **Base VLA Adaptation:** OmniVLA extends an existing RGB-pretrained VLA backbone (SmolVLA by default) by modifying its architecture. The design is generalizable and compatible with various VLA models.
- **Vision Encoder:** The generated sensor-masked images are fed into the *frozen* vision encoder of the pre-trained VLA model. Freezing the vision encoder is a common practice that leverages its rich, pre-learned visual feature representations, crucial for data efficiency.
- **Lightweight Projection Layers (MLPs):** For *each* sensor modality, a dedicated, lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) module processes the output of the vision encoder. These MLPs project the encoded sensor image features into a token space that is aligned with the language and RGB image tokens. This modular design allows flexible sensor integration and specialized processing for each modality.
- **LLM Backbone:** The projected tokens from all active sensors are concatenated with language tokens derived from the task description (processed by a language tokenizer). This combined stream of tokens is then fed into the Large Language Model (LLM) backbone.
- **Action Expert:** The LLM backbone conditions a diffusion-based or flow matching-based Action Expert (inherited from the base VLA model, e.g., SmolVLA or π0). This expert module is responsible for generating continuous robotic actions, chunk by chunk, from the processed multimodal input.
- **Training Strategy:**
 - Training starts from the pretrained weights of the base VLA model.
 - The vision encoder is kept frozen.
 - All other weights, including the individual MLP projectors for each sensor, are made trainable.
 - The sensor-specific MLPs are initialized using weights from the base VLA model's pre-trained RGB projection layer, leveraging existing visual feature mappings as a strong prior.
 - These modules are then co-fine-tuned with the unfrozen backbone weights using collected demonstration datasets.

#### 3.3. Hardware Implementation

The research utilizes a custom robot arm prototype featuring a SO101 manipulator. This arm is equipped with standard RGB cameras (top-down, front, arm-mounted), augmented with a comprehensive multi-modal sensor suite: a depth camera, an infrared thermal camera, an mmWave radar sensor, and a six-microphone circular array. This setup allows for real-world data collection and evaluation of tasks requiring diverse sensory inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OmniVLA demonstrates significant advancements in equipping robots with multi-sensory perception for complex manipulation tasks. The key findings are categorized into overall performance, learning efficiency, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Multi-Sensory Task Performance

- **Superior Success Rates:** OmniVLA achieved an impressive average task success rate of **84%** across challenging real-world tasks requiring thermal, mmWave, and acoustic perception. This includes tasks like distinguishing and picking a cold drink, opening a box with a hidden object, and uncovering a ringing phone.
- **Outperformance Against Baselines:**
 - OmniVLA significantly outperformed the **VLA-RGB baseline** (standard RGB-only VLA model), which achieved only a **25%** average success rate. This represents a substantial **59% improvement**, clearly demonstrating the necessity and effectiveness of non-RGB sensors for these tasks.
 - OmniVLA also substantially outperformed the **VLA-RAW baseline** (VLA model using raw sensor images/heatmaps without the sensor-masked overlay), which achieved a **56%** average success rate. This **28% improvement** highlights the critical role of OmniVLA's sensor-masked image representation in boosting performance.
- **Improved Task Scores:** Beyond binary success rates, OmniVLA achieved a higher average task score of **0.90**, compared to 0.55 for VLA-RGB and 0.73 for VLA-RAW, indicating more precise and complete task execution.
- **Base Model Compatibility:** The approach was successfully tested with two different foundational robotic VLA models (SmolVLA and Pi0). While SmolVLA performed better (84% vs. 64% average success rate for Pi0), this demonstrates the generalizability of OmniVLA's architecture across different VLA backbones.

#### 4.2. Learning Efficiency

- **Reduced Data Requirement:** OmniVLA demonstrated superior data efficiency. It achieved similar success rates with approximately **50% fewer training episodes** compared to the VLA-RAW baseline for the thermal task. This finding underscores the effectiveness of the sensor-masked image representation in enabling data-efficient learning, which is crucial given the scarcity of multi-modal robotic demonstration data.

#### 4.3. Generalization Performance

- **Strong Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** OmniVLA was evaluated on its ability to generalize to unseen tasks after multi-sensory pre-training. A pre-trained OmniVLA model, fine-tuned with only 25 few-shot demonstration episodes on novel tasks (e.g., different drink types, foam boxes instead of cardboard, towels instead of T-shirts), showed robust generalization.
- **Outperformance in Few-Shot Generalization:**
 - OmniVLA significantly outperformed the **OmniVLA-Base baseline** (no multi-sensory pre-training, only few-shot fine-tuning) by **59%** on average success rate for unseen tasks.
 - It also surpassed the **Pretrained VLA-RAW baseline** (pre-trained with raw sensor images but no sensor-masked overlay) by **28%** on average success rate.
- **Stage-wise Improvements:** A detailed stage-wise analysis revealed that OmniVLA improved performance in both Stage 1 (selecting the correct target to interact with) and Stage 2 (completing the subsequent manipulation). This suggests that the sensor-masked overlay helps in learning better selection policies (due to spatially aligned cues) and that multi-sensory pre-training provides transferable manipulation priors for improved control.

In summary, the results conclusively show that OmniVLA's unique sensor-masked image representation and its adaptation of VLA models provide a robust, data-efficient, and generalizable solution for extending robotic perception and manipulation capabilities beyond the visible spectrum.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OmniVLA represents a pivotal advancement in the field of robotic manipulation and multimodal AI, with significant implications and potential impact across several domains:

1. **Breaking the RGB-Only Barrier in Robotics:** The most immediate and profound significance of OmniVLA is its success in enabling robotic manipulation tasks that are fundamentally impossible for RGB-only VLA systems. By effectively integrating infrared, mmWave, and acoustic sensors, OmniVLA moves robots beyond purely visual perception, opening up a vast new range of capabilities. This is a critical step towards creating truly general-purpose robots that can interact with the physical world in a more comprehensive, human-like, and even super-human way.

2. **Novel and Data-Efficient Multimodal Representation:** The introduction of "sensor-masked images" is a highly impactful methodological contribution. This unified, spatially grounded, and semantically aligned representation elegantly solves several challenges:
 - It allows the reuse of powerful, pre-trained RGB vision encoders, leveraging billions of parameters learned from existing datasets and dramatically reducing the need for costly "from-scratch" training on new sensor modalities.
 - It provides a uniform interface for diverse sensors, simplifying the integration of new hardware and promoting scalability.
 - Its design (overlaying information onto relevant masked regions of RGB) makes learning highly data-efficient, requiring significantly fewer expert demonstrations compared to approaches using raw sensor data. This addresses a major bottleneck in robotics research.

3. **Enhanced Robotic Capabilities for Real-World Tasks:** OmniVLA empowers robots to perform complex tasks in unstructured and uncertain environments where visual information alone is insufficient. Examples include:
 - **Search and Rescue:** Locating individuals based on thermal signatures or faint sounds through debris.
 - **Manufacturing and Logistics:** Identifying hidden defects, sorting objects inside opaque containers, or confirming contents without direct visual access.
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Retrieving objects hidden from view or sensing environmental conditions beyond human sight.
 - **Object Interaction:** Manipulating objects based on their temperature, internal structure, or acoustic properties, leading to more nuanced and intelligent interactions.

4. **Generalizable and Flexible Framework:** The modular architecture and the compatibility with different VLA backbones (demonstrated with SmolVLA and Pi0) underscore OmniVLA's potential as a flexible and generalizable framework. This means the research can be built upon by the wider robotics community, integrating with future advancements in VLA models and adapting to various robotic platforms and sensor configurations.

5. **Accelerating Robotics Research and Development:** By open-sourcing the project (https://github.com/GuoHeyu/OmniVLA), the authors are fostering further research and development in multimodal robotics. This allows other researchers and developers to build upon OmniVLA's foundations, iterate on its design, and explore new applications, potentially accelerating the development of more capable and intelligent robotic systems.

In conclusion, OmniVLA is a significant stride towards creating robots with truly comprehensive perception and manipulation skills. It provides a robust, efficient, and scalable solution for integrating novel sensor modalities into VLA models, enabling robots to "see" and interact with the physical world in ways previously limited to science fiction. This work paves the way for a new generation of versatile and perceptive robots that can better understand and navigate the complexities of our environment.
