# A General Framework for Inference-time Scaling and Steering of Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A general inference-time framework, Feynman-Kac (FK) steering, utilizes interacting particle systems to guide diffusion models, improving sample quality and controllability. This training-free method applies to both continuous and discrete data with arbitrary reward functions, often outperforming fine-tuned models and best-of-N baselines in efficiency and performance.

## Problem

- Existing methods for steering diffusion models (e.g., fine-tuning) are computationally expensive and lack flexibility for adapting to diverse or dynamic user preferences.
- Inference-time steering techniques are often restricted to continuous-state models or differentiable reward functions, limiting their application to discrete data like text or complex real-world objectives.
- Simple inference-time approaches like Best-of-N sampling are inefficient, wasting computational resources on low-reward samples.

## Method

- Introduces **FK Steering** (Feynman-Kac Steering), a general inference-time framework that leverages ==Feynman-Kac Interacting Particle Systems (FK-IPS)== to guide diffusion models.
- The method uses ==multi-particle resampling== during reverse diffusion, iteratively denoising, scoring particles based on ==intermediate reward estimates==, and duplicating high-scoring paths.
- It is designed to work with arbitrary (==differentiable or non-differentiable==) reward functions and is applicable to both ==continuous and discrete== state-space diffusion models.

## Results

- FK steering enables a **0.8B**-parameter Stable Diffusion model to outperform a **2.6B**-parameter fine-tuned model (SDXL-DPO) on prompt fidelity and aesthetic quality.
- Text diffusion models show substantial improvements in fluency, with MDLM's perplexity reduced from **79.2** to **37.2** through trigram steering, and linguistic acceptability enhanced.
- The method effectively controls rare attributes like text toxicity, increasing the toxicity rate from **0.3%** to over **50%** for MDLM, facilitating "red-teaming" efforts.
- Empirical analysis confirmed that "MAX" potential offers higher prompt fidelity, and learned intermediate rewards significantly boost class-conditional image generation and toxicity control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - FK steering allows smaller diffusion models to achieve performance on par with or superior to much larger, fine-tuned models, without requiring additional training.
> - The framework's flexibility, through choices of potential functions and intermediate reward estimation methods, enables nuanced control over sample quality and diversity trade-offs.
> - Particle-based resampling effectively prunes unpromising generation trajectories, making FK steering more computationally efficient than generating and discarding many samples (Best-of-N).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have emerged as powerful generative models, producing impressive results across various modalities including images, video, and text. However, a persistent challenge remains: guiding these models to generate samples with precise, user-specified properties or to align with specific preferences. This paper addresses this crucial control problem, positioning its contribution within the broader landscape of methods aimed at improving the controllability and quality of diffusion model outputs.

Existing approaches to steer generative models generally fall into two categories:

1. **Fine-tuning:** Methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO) involve retraining or fine-tuning the diffusion model using reward functions that encode desired properties. While effective, fine-tuning is computationally expensive, requires significant training resources, and ties the model to a single, specific reward function, making it inflexible for dynamic control requirements. Moreover, these methods can be prone to mode collapse, reducing sample diversity.

2. **Inference-time Steering:** These approaches modify the generation process at inference time without altering the trained model weights.
 - **Reward Gradient-based Guidance:** Techniques such as classifier guidance or universal guidance efficiently steer continuous-state diffusion models by adding gradients from a reward function to the model's score estimate. However, this approach is limited to differentiable reward functions and continuous state spaces, restricting its applicability to many real-world scenarios, particularly in discrete-state text generation.
 - **Best-of-N (BoN) Sampling:** This simple method generates multiple independent samples and selects the highest-reward one. While generic and applicable to any reward function, it is computationally inefficient as it allocates significant resources to generating low-reward samples that are ultimately discarded.
 - **Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) and Feynman-Kac Interacting Particle Systems (FK-IPS):** More advanced particle-based methods, like Twisted Diffusion Sampler (TDS) and Soft Value-based Decoding in Diffusion Models (SVDD), have utilized FK measures for conditional sampling in diffusion models. These works have laid groundwork but often come with their own limitations, such as restricting guidance to differentiable rewards or continuous states, or by employing specific resampling strategies that may limit sample diversity (e.g., SVDD's strong resampling).

This research introduces "Feynman-Kac (FK) steering" as a novel inference-time framework that generalizes and significantly extends existing particle-based methods. It aims to overcome the limitations of both fine-tuning (cost, inflexibility) and prior inference-time steering techniques (differentiability constraints, computational waste). By providing a flexible, plug-and-play framework, FK steering seeks to advance the state-of-the-art in controllable diffusion model generation, making it more practical and broadly applicable across diverse tasks and model architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general, flexible, and efficient framework for steering diffusion models at inference time, enabling them to generate samples that possess user-specified properties or align with predefined reward functions. The authors are motivated by several limitations of existing approaches:

1. **High Training Costs of Fine-tuning:** Current state-of-the-art methods often rely on expensive fine-tuning processes, which are impractical for rapidly adapting models to new preferences or rewards. These methods also result in models tied to a single reward, necessitating retraining for each new steering objective.
2. **Restrictions of Gradient-based Guidance:** While efficient, gradient-based steering is confined to continuous-state diffusion models and requires differentiable reward functions. This severely limits its application to discrete data (like text) or scenarios with complex, non-differentiable human preference scores.
3. **Inefficiency of Best-of-N Sampling:** Simple "best-of-n" strategies, despite their generality, are wasteful. They generate many samples that do not meet the desired criteria, expending significant computational resources on ultimately discarded outputs.
4. **Limited Scope of Prior Particle-based Methods:** While some previous work has explored particle-based methods (SMC/FK-IPS) for diffusion models, they have often been specialized instances with specific choices of potentials, samplers, or requirements for reward differentiability.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the paper are:

- To propose a unifying framework, Feynman-Kac (FK) steering, that leverages Feynman-Kac interacting particle systems (FK-IPS) to enable flexible, inference-time steering of diffusion models with arbitrary (differentiable or non-differentiable) reward functions.
- To demonstrate that FK steering is applicable to both continuous-state (e.g., images) and discrete-state (e.g., text) diffusion models, thus broadening the scope of controllable generation.
- To introduce and evaluate various novel choices for potential functions, intermediate reward estimation methods, and proposal generators within the FK steering framework, showing how these choices impact performance and diversity.
- To empirically demonstrate that FK steering can achieve significant improvements in sample quality (e.g., prompt fidelity, linguistic acceptability) and controllability (e.g., toxicity, class-conditional generation), often outperforming both expensive fine-tuned models and less efficient inference-time baselines (like best-of-n and gradient guidance).
- To show that FK steering can enable smaller diffusion models to achieve performance comparable to or exceeding much larger, fine-tuned models, thereby offering a more compute-efficient pathway to high-quality controlled generation.

Ultimately, the motivation is to provide a versatile, training-free, and scalable mechanism for tailoring diffusion model outputs to diverse user needs and preferences, without sacrificing efficiency or generalizability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology presented is **Feynman-Kac (FK) steering**, a flexible inference-time framework for guiding diffusion models using Feynman-Kac Interacting Particle Systems (FK-IPS). This approach is designed to sample from a "tilted distribution" $p_{target}(x_0) \propto p_\theta(x_0) \exp(\lambda r(x_0, c))$, where $p_\theta(x_0)$ is the original diffusion model's output distribution, $r(x_0, c)$ is a user-defined reward function, $c$ is context (e.g., text prompt), and $\lambda$ controls the strength of the reward tilt.

FK steering operates through a multi-particle resampling process during the reverse diffusion (denoising) steps:

1. **Particle Sampling:** The process begins by initializing $k$ parallel diffusion processes, referred to as "particles" ($x_T^i$ for $i=1 \dots k$), from the model's prior.
2. **Iterative Denoising and Scoring:** At each reverse diffusion step $t$ (from $T$ down to $0$), each particle $x_t^i$ is evolved to $x_{t-1}^i$ using a *proposal generator* $\tau(x_{t-1} | x_t, c)$. Subsequently, all $k$ particles are evaluated and scored using *potential functions* $G_t$. These potentials are designed to assess the "promisingness" of a particle's current state, based on intermediate reward estimates.
3. **Resampling:** Based on these scores, particles are resampled. High-scoring particles are duplicated, while low-scoring particles are eliminated. This effectively "steers" the population of particles towards paths that are more likely to lead to high-reward final samples $x_0$. This resampling step ensures that computational resources are concentrated on more promising generation trajectories, improving efficiency over simple best-of-n.

**Theoretical Foundation:** FK-IPS defines a sequence of distributions $p_{FK,t}$ that iteratively tilts the base diffusion model's path distribution $p_\theta(x_{T:t} | c)$ using a product of potentials $G_s$. The crucial condition is that the total product of all potentials across all time steps must equate to the desired exponential tilt: $\prod_{s=T}^0 G_s(x_{T:s}, c) = \exp(\lambda r(x_0, c))$. This ensures that the final set of particles at $t=0$ constitutes an empirical approximation of the target distribution.

The flexibility of FK steering lies in its various design choices, which the paper systematically explores:

- **Choice of Proposal Generator $\tau$:**
 - **Base Diffusion Model:** The simplest choice is to use the original diffusion model's transition kernel $p_\theta(x_{t-1} | x_t, c)$. This is typically the most compute-efficient.
 - **Gradient-based Guidance:** For continuous-state models with differentiable rewards, a gradient-guided transition kernel can be used to pre-steer particles, potentially improving efficiency further.
 - **Discrete Normalization:** For discrete diffusion models, the transition kernel can be explicitly tilted by normalizing the product of $p_\theta(x_t | x_{t+1}, c)$ and $G_t$.
- **Choice of Potential Functions $G_t$:** While various potentials can satisfy the overall product constraint, their specific form influences how particles are steered and diversity. The paper investigates:
 - **DIFFERENCE:** $G_t = \exp(\lambda (r_\phi(x_t, c) - r_\phi(x_{t+1}, c)))$, favoring particles with increasing intermediate rewards. This is common in prior FK-IPS applications.
 - **MAX:** $G_t = \exp(\lambda \max_{s=t \dots T} r_\phi(x_s, c))$, favoring particles that have achieved the highest reward at any point up to time $t$.
 - **SUM:** $G_t = \exp(\lambda \sum_{s=t \dots T} r_\phi(x_s, c))$, favoring particles with the highest accumulated rewards over their path.
 - **Interval Resampling:** To balance exploration and computational cost, resampling is performed only at a subset of time steps, with $G_t = 1$ for other steps.
- **Choice of Intermediate Rewards $r_\phi(x_t, c)$:** This is a crucial aspect, as estimating the reward of a partial, noisy state $x_t$ is challenging. The paper explores:
 - **Rewards at Expected $x_0$:** The reward function $r$ is evaluated on the diffusion model's current best estimate of the final, denoised sample $x_0$ (i.e., $\hat{x}_t \approx E[x_0 | x_t]$). This is "off-the-shelf" and requires no extra training.
 - **Many-Sample $r_\phi$:** Multiple full samples $x_0$ are generated from $p_\theta(x_0 | x_t, c)$, and their rewards are aggregated (e.g., log-average-exp) to estimate the expected reward from $x_t$. This is more accurate but more computationally intensive.
 - **Learned $r_\phi$:** A separate model is trained to predict the (log-expected) reward from an intermediate state $x_t$, leveraging data samples and the forward diffusion process. This requires pre-training but can yield more accurate intermediate reward estimates.

FK steering also incorporates **adaptive resampling** based on the Effective Sample Size (ESS) to maintain particle diversity, only resampling if ESS drops below a certain threshold. The framework is shown to encompass prior particle-based diffusion steering methods like TDS and SVDD as specific instances, highlighting its generality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that FK steering consistently provides significant improvements in sample quality and controllability across various tasks and modalities, often outperforming strong baselines.

#### 4.1. FK Steering for Sample Quality

- **Text-to-Image Diffusion Models:**
 - **Outperformance of Fine-tuned Models:** FK steering a smaller 0.8B parameter model (Stable Diffusion v2.1) with as few as $k=3$ particles can outperform a 2.6B parameter fine-tuned model (Stable Diffusion XL-DPO) on prompt fidelity (GenEVAL score) and aesthetic quality (ImageReward, HPS scores). This is achieved with faster sampling and crucially, no additional training. Even with just $k=2$ particles, FK steering beats DPO and DDPO fine-tuned models.
 - **Efficiency:** FK steering achieves these gains with fewer computational operations (FLOPs) and faster sampling times compared to larger, fine-tuned models.
 - **Complementary to Fine-tuning:** Applying FK steering to *already fine-tuned* models (e.g., SDXL-DPO) further enhances their performance, indicating its complementary nature.
 - **Superior to Best-of-N:** FK steering consistently outperforms Best-of-N sampling with the same number of particles, highlighting its more efficient allocation of computational budget by pruning unpromising trajectories early.
 - **Interaction with Gradient Guidance:** While FK steering with the base model as a proposal generator outperforms gradient guidance alone, combining FK steering with gradient-based proposal generation (when applicable) yields even higher performance, albeit with increased computational cost.
 - **Effect of Particle Scaling:** Increasing the number of particles ($k$) linearly improves the average prompt fidelity and human preference alignment scores across all generated samples, not just the best one.

- **Text Diffusion Models:**
 - **Improved Fluency:** FK steering significantly improves fluency metrics like perplexity (GPT2-XL) and linguistic acceptability (CoLA) for text diffusion models (SSD-LM and MDLM), which traditionally lag behind autoregressive models. For MDLM, trigram steering dramatically reduces perplexity (37.2 vs 79.2).
 - **Outperforms Baselines:** FK steering consistently outperforms Best-of-N sampling for the same particle count. Notably, FK steering SSD-LM with $T=500$ steps performs better across all fluency metrics than SSD-LM run with 10 times more inference steps ($T=5000$).

#### 4.2. Studying Potential Choices in FK Steering

- **Max Potential for Prompt Fidelity:** For text-to-image steering with ImageReward, the "MAX" potential (favoring particles that have achieved the highest reward up to that point) generally leads to higher prompt fidelity scores compared to "DIFFERENCE" or "SUM" potentials.
- **Diversity Trade-off:** The "MAX" potential, while yielding higher average rewards, tends to reduce particle diversity more aggressively than the "DIFFERENCE" potential due to its stronger emphasis on maximizing the observed reward at any step. Diversity can be increased by using more sampling steps.

#### 4.3. Studying Different Choices of Intermediate Rewards for Controllability

- **Controlling Text Toxicity (Red-Teaming):** FK steering effectively manipulates rare attributes like toxicity. For MDLM, FK steering with $k=8$ particles increases the toxicity rate from a base of 0.3% to 64.7% (measured by the steering classifier) and 51.7% (by a holdout classifier), outperforming both gradient guidance and best-of-n.
 - **Improved Intermediate Rewards:** Using "learned" intermediate rewards (for SSD-LM) or "many-sample" intermediate rewards (for MDLM) significantly boosts the ability to generate toxic text, demonstrating the value of better reward estimates at intermediate steps.
- **Class-Conditional Image Generation:** For ImageNet class-conditional generation, learning the intermediate reward function ($r_\phi$) yields substantial improvements in class-conditional probabilities compared to using rewards evaluated only at the denoised state.
- **Compute Trade-offs:** The choice between using more particles ($k$) versus investing in better intermediate reward models (learned or many-sample) presents a computational trade-off, where each can enhance performance.

#### 4.4. Correlation of Intermediate and Final Rewards

- The study shows a strong correlation between intermediate rewards $r_\phi(x_t)$ and the final reward $r(x_0)$, particularly as inference progresses. Learned intermediate potentials show an even stronger correlation, validating their effectiveness in guiding the steering process.

In summary, FK steering is a powerful, flexible, and efficient inference-time framework that delivers substantial improvements in both the quality and controllability of diffusion model outputs, often surpassing computationally intensive fine-tuning methods and more basic inference-time techniques.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Feynman-Kac (FK) steering represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact stem from its unique combination of flexibility, efficiency, and performance:

1. **Democratizing Controllable Generation:** By enabling powerful inference-time steering without costly fine-tuning, FK steering makes sophisticated control over diffusion model outputs more accessible. This means researchers and practitioners can quickly adapt off-the-shelf diffusion models to new preferences or tasks using generic reward functions, dramatically reducing the computational burden and time-to-solution associated with training-based methods. This "plug-and-play" capability is highly impactful for diverse applications.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Model Scale and Performance:** The finding that FK steering can enable smaller diffusion models (e.g., 0.8B parameter SDv2.1) to outperform much larger, fine-tuned models (e.g., 2.6B parameter SDXL-DPO) is profound. This suggests a new paradigm where computational resources can be reallocated from training larger models to intelligent inference-time steering, potentially leading to more efficient development and deployment of high-performing generative AI.

3. **Enhanced Flexibility and Generalizability:** FK steering's ability to work with arbitrary reward functions (differentiable or not) and both continuous and discrete state-space diffusion models vastly expands the scope of controllable generation. This versatility is critical for real-world scenarios where rewards can be complex (e.g., human preference scores) or data is discrete (e.g., text, molecular structures).

4. **Improved Sample Quality and Controllability:** The consistent and significant improvements in prompt fidelity, aesthetic quality, linguistic acceptability, and precise attribute control (e.g., toxicity, ImageNet classes) demonstrate FK steering's effectiveness across modalities. This directly addresses one of the primary weaknesses of unguided diffusion models – their failure modes in adhering to specific user desires.

5. **Applications in Responsible AI:** The demonstrated capability for "red-teaming" (generating rare, undesirable attributes like toxic language) is highly impactful for responsible AI development. It provides a robust tool for proactively identifying and understanding model vulnerabilities, which is crucial for building safer and more aligned generative systems before deployment.

6. **Broader Scientific Applications:** The particle-based nature of FK steering holds promise for complex scientific domains like protein design. Dynamically allocating particles to promising regions in a vast search space could enable the generation of a large number of diverse, high-quality candidates for molecular design, drug discovery, or materials science. It can also serve as a better sample generator for preference learning algorithms.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

Despite its strengths, FK steering, like other reward-guided methods, fundamentally relies on the availability of strong, accurate reward functions. The development of more robust automated evaluation and reward modeling techniques remains a critical area for future research to unlock the full potential of such steering frameworks.

Future work could explore dynamic particle allocation, where the number of particles or computational budget is adaptively varied during inference based on the uncertainty or promisingness of paths. Further research into the fundamental limits of inference-time particle scaling and the nuanced compute-performance trade-offs will also be crucial for optimizing these methods.

In conclusion, FK steering offers a powerful, flexible, and efficient paradigm for enhancing the control and quality of diffusion model outputs. By making sophisticated steering accessible and adaptable, it is poised to significantly impact the development, application, and safety of generative AI technologies across diverse fields.
