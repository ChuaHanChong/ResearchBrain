# LDA-1B: Scaling Latent Dynamics Action Model via Universal Embodied Data Ingestion

> [!summary] Summary
> LDA-1B, a 1.6-billion-parameter robot foundation model, learns generalizable interaction dynamics from over 30,000 hours of diverse embodied data by employing a universal data ingestion paradigm and predicting structured DINO latent features. This approach enables superior performance in simulation and real-world complex manipulation tasks, including contact-rich and dexterous operations.

## Problem

- Robot foundation models predominantly rely on Behavior Cloning, which is limited by the scarcity of high-quality expert demonstrations and struggles to leverage vast amounts of suboptimal or actionless embodied data.
- Existing Unified World Models (UWMs) fail to scale effectively due to treating heterogeneous data uniformly and performing inefficient pixel-space predictions, which conflate dynamics learning with appearance modeling.
- A lack of standardized, large-scale datasets that unify varying-quality embodied data with consistent formats and aligned action representations hinders progress in scalable robot learning.

## Method

- LDA-1B introduces a "universal embodied data ingestion" paradigm, which assigns distinct learning roles to heterogeneous data types (high-quality, low-quality, actionless videos) within a unified framework.
- The model utilizes a Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) architecture to jointly denoise action chunks and predict future visual states in a structured DINO latent space, moving beyond pixel-level reconstruction.
- The authors compiled EI-30K, a large-scale dataset of over 30,000 hours of real/sim robot and human demonstrations, unified into the LeRobot format with aligned hand-centric action representations.

## Results

- LDA-1B achieved a 55.4% success rate on the challenging RoboCasa-GR1 benchmark in simulation, significantly outperforming strong baselines like GR00T-N1.6 (47.6%) and demonstrating the efficacy of DINO latent spaces.
- In real-world tests on Galbot G1 and Unitree G1 robots, LDA-1B showed superior performance across gripper and dexterous manipulation tasks, achieving, for example, 60% success on "Flip Box" compared to 20% for GR00T and 90% on "Flip Bread" versus 10% for baselines.
- The model demonstrated data-efficient finetuning, improving success rates by 10% when incorporating low-quality teleoperation data, and exhibited monotonic performance scaling with increasing data volume and model parameters up to 1.6 billion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Assigning distinct learning objectives to different qualities of embodied data (e.g., actionless videos for visual forecasting, low-quality for dynamics) enables more efficient and scalable model training than uniform data treatment.
> - Predicting DINO latent features for visual states, rather than raw pixels, allows the model to disentangle interaction-relevant dynamics from irrelevant visual appearance, which is crucial for scalable and robust generalization.
> - A unified diffusion-based architecture with learnable task embeddings and register tokens can effectively integrate various objectives (policy, dynamics, visual planning) and handle diverse input/output structures from heterogeneous data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robot foundation models represents a significant area of research in robotics, drawing inspiration from the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). Current approaches primarily leverage large-scale behavior cloning (BC), where models imitate expert actions from high-quality demonstrations. Examples include models like π0, RDT, and InternVLA. However, these methods are limited by their reliance on expert data, often discarding a substantial portion of heterogeneous embodied data that contains rich physical interaction dynamics but may be of mixed or lower quality. This constraint fundamentally limits their scalability and ability to generalize.

An alternative paradigm is the Unified World Model (UWM) formulation, which jointly optimizes dynamics, policy, and video generation. While UWMs, such as UWM and UVA, have the potential to utilize diverse data, existing instantiations face challenges in scaling to foundation-model levels. These limitations include a coarse approach to data usage, where heterogeneous data are often treated uniformly without differentiating roles based on quality or supervision. Furthermore, UWMs typically operate in pixel space for future state prediction, which can entangle dynamics learning with redundant appearance modeling. Minor visual variations can dominate training objectives, hindering the learning of interaction-relevant dynamics and reducing training efficiency at scale.

Another critical gap in the landscape is the lack of standardized, large-scale datasets that unify varying-quality data with consistent formats and aligned action representations. Existing embodied datasets are often fragmented, with inconsistencies in data formats, sensor configurations, action representations, and annotation quality.

This work, LDA-1B, aims to address these limitations by introducing a robot foundation model that scales a UWM formulation through a universal data ingestion strategy. It proposes to leverage diverse data by assigning distinct roles based on quality, utilizing a structured latent space (DINO) for dynamics prediction to avoid pixel-space issues, and unifying various data sources into a standardized, large-scale dataset (EI-30k). By doing so, LDA-1B seeks to enable scalable learning of transferable dynamics and action representations beyond the constraints of behavior-cloning-centric methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable robot foundation model that can effectively learn generalizable interaction dynamics from a diverse range of embodied data. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Behavior Cloning:** Existing robot foundation models predominantly rely on behavior cloning, which requires high-quality expert demonstrations. This approach inherently limits scalability because it discards vast amounts of heterogeneous embodied data containing valuable physical interaction dynamics but lacking expert-level action labels or being of mixed quality. The motivation is to move beyond this limitation to leverage all available data efficiently.

2. **Addressing Scaling Challenges in Unified World Models (UWMs):** While UWMs offer a promising framework for jointly modeling policy, dynamics, and visual generation, they have struggled to scale to foundation-level models. This is largely due to:
 - **Coarse Data Usage:** Current UWM instantiations often treat all heterogeneous embodied data uniformly, failing to differentiate their roles based on quality or type of supervision. This leads to underutilization of transferable dynamics knowledge embedded in diverse data. The motivation is to implement a "universal data ingestion" strategy that assigns distinct, complementary roles to data of varying quality (e.g., actionless videos for visual forecasting, low-quality trajectories for dynamics, high-quality for policy and dynamics).
 - **Pixel-Space Prediction:** Many UWMs predict future states directly in pixel space. This entangles dynamics learning with redundant appearance modeling, making large-scale training inefficient. Subtle visual variations (illumination, texture, background clutter) can dominate the training objective, diverting the model from learning essential interaction-relevant dynamics. The motivation is to shift from pixel-space to a structured, semantic latent space for visual prediction.

3. **Standardizing and Aggregating Embodied Data:** The robotics community lacks large-scale, ready-to-use datasets that unify diverse, varying-quality embodied data with consistent formats and aligned action representations. Existing datasets are fragmented and heterogeneous, posing a significant obstacle to large-scale data aggregation and unified training. The motivation is to create a standardized, comprehensive embodied interaction dataset that facilitates scalable research.

4. **Enabling Stable Scaling and Generalization:** The overarching motivation is to develop a framework (model architecture, data strategy, and representation) that allows for stable training at the billion-parameter scale while maximizing data utilization. This aims to achieve superior generalization and robustness across a wide range of settings, including simulation and real-world environments, contact-rich manipulation, dexterous manipulation, and long-horizon tasks, ultimately leading to more capable generalist robots.

In summary, the research seeks to build a more data-efficient and scalable robot foundation model by addressing the shortcomings of current approaches in data utilization, representation learning, and architectural design within a unified world model framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LDA-1B model employs a multi-faceted approach involving a novel data ingestion strategy, a large-scale unified dataset, a structured latent representation, and a multimodal diffusion transformer architecture.

**Universal Data Ingestion via Multi-task Co-training:**
LDA-1B adopts a universal data ingestion regime that allows heterogeneous embodied data to contribute to learning based on their quality and type of supervision. This is achieved through co-training multiple unified objectives within a single diffusion model:
- **High-quality robot and human demonstrations:** Used for all objectives: policy learning, forward dynamics, inverse dynamics, and visual forecasting.
- **Lower-quality trajectories (suboptimal/noisy actions):** Primarily used for dynamics and visual forecasting, where action optimality is not a strict requirement.
- **Actionless human manipulation videos:** Used exclusively for the visual forecasting objective, providing supervision for instruction-conditioned future state prediction.
This role-aware data usage is designed to prevent overfitting to expert-only behaviors and enable scalable learning of transferable dynamics and action representations.
To implement differentiated objectives, four learnable task embeddings and two learnable register tokens (one for action, one for visual state) are introduced. Task embeddings condition the denoising process, while register tokens act as placeholders for absent modalities in specific tasks. The model is trained using a flow-matching objective to denoise actions and observations, with losses selectively activated based on the task.

**Embodied Interaction Dataset (EI-30k):**
A large-scale dataset, EI-30k, was assembled, comprising over 30,000 hours of embodied interaction trajectories. This dataset integrates:
- 8.03k hours of real-world robot data
- 8.6k hours of simulated robot data
- 7.2k hours of human demonstrations with actions
- 10k hours of actionless human videos
All subdatasets are annotated with explicit quality labels.
The dataset undergoes a rigorous standardization process:
- **Data Unification:** All data are converted into the LeRobot format, providing a unified representation for observations, actions, and language. This includes end-effector poses, hand articulation, camera parameters, and metadata. Sequences are uniformly resampled to 10 Hz.
- **Aligned Action Representation:** Actions are represented in a unified hand-centric motion space based on end-effector motion (delta wrist poses and finger configurations). For grippers, this involves gripper width; for dexterous hands, keypoints in the wrist coordinate frame. Coordinate frames are manually aligned across datasets to ensure geometric consistency.
- **Quality Annotation and Cleaning:** Trajectories are systematically cleaned, with motion segments without meaningful hand-object interaction removed. Each trajectory is assigned a quality label based on action accuracy and annotation completeness, and language annotations are normalized using a vision-language model. Low-quality trajectories are preserved for quality-aware training.

**Representation of Predictive Targets:**
- **Visual Prediction:** Instead of pixel-space representations (e.g., VAE-based), LDA-1B uses latent features extracted from a pretrained DINOv3 encoder. DINO latents are intended to encode high-level semantic and spatial structure while suppressing background noise, thereby facilitating the learning of generalized scene dynamics.
- **Actions:** A unified hand-centric action space is used, consisting of delta wrist poses and finger configurations.
- **Temporal Dynamics:** Visual states (3 Hz) and actions (10 Hz) are organized as synchronized temporal streams with different sampling rates, balancing computational efficiency with fine-grained action dynamics.

**Architecture: Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT):**
LDA-1B employs an MM-DiT architecture to jointly denoise action chunks and predict future visual features within a unified diffusion framework.
- **Input Conditioning:** The model is conditioned on the current observation, language instruction, diffusion timestep, and task specification. Observations and language are encoded by a pretrained VLM (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct). Diffusion timesteps are sinusoidally embedded, and task information is represented by learned task embeddings. All conditioning signals are injected via adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN).
- **Modality Processing:** Actions (fixed-length chunks) and future DINO features are corrupted with Gaussian noise, then projected into token embeddings via modality-specific linear layers.
- **Transformer Backbone:** A shared Transformer backbone processes these tokens. Each MM-DiT block uses multimodal self-attention over concatenated action and visual tokens, enabling cross-modal interaction. Modality-specific QKV projections and FFNs are retained, while attention is shared. Language tokens are incorporated via cross-attention.
- **Output Heads:** Modality-specific output heads predict denoised action sequences and future visual features.

**Pre-training and Post-training:**
- **Pre-training:** The model is trained on 48 NVIDIA H800 GPUs for 400k iterations (4,608 GPU hours). The VLM and DINOv3 encoder parameters are kept frozen to preserve their pretrained generalization and representation quality, while the MM-DiT and action encoder/decoder are updated.
- **Data-Efficient Fine-tuning:** A lightweight post-training stage adapts the model to target embodiments and tasks. This stage also leverages mixed-quality teleoperation data, including low-quality trajectories, without requiring expert-level demonstrations, aiming to improve data efficiency and reduce collection costs. During fine-tuning, the VLM is unfrozen for end-to-end adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LDA-1B involved comprehensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, along with detailed ablation and scaling analyses.

**Simulation Experiments (RoboCasa-GR1):**
- **Performance:** LDA-1B achieved a success rate of 55.4% on the RoboCasa-GR1 benchmark, outperforming strong baselines like GR00T-N1.6 (47.6%) and a reproduced GR00T-EI10k (51.3%), which was trained on a high-quality subset of EI-30k. This indicates that joint learning of actions and dynamics within a unified model provides benefits beyond data quality and parameter scaling.
- **Ablation Study:**
 - UWM variants (0.1B, 1B, 1B with MM-DiT) showed limited success (14.2% to 20.0%), suggesting architectural constraints and VAE-derived pixel-space representations fundamentally limit their performance.
 - Replacing pixel-space VAE latents with DINO representations in LDA led to a substantial performance gain (20.0% to 55.4%), highlighting the importance of semantically structured latent spaces.
 - Removing the MM-DiT architecture or reducing model size to 0.5B parameters resulted in performance drops (6.5% and 4.7% respectively), confirming the efficacy of the multi-expert design and its scaling behavior.
- **Task-Level Results:** LDA consistently outperformed GR00T, especially in contact-rich and cluttered rearrangement tasks requiring precise placement and closing actions (e.g., "PnP Can To Drawer Close" (71% vs. 13% for GR00T)). It also showed improved robustness in object grasping and placement accuracy, anticipating post-action consequences and avoiding collisions.

**Real-World Experiments (Galbot G1, Unitree G1):**
- **Gripper Manipulation (Galbot G1):** LDA-1B consistently outperformed π0.5 and GR00T across diverse categories:
 - **Pick & Place:** Achieved 80-90% success rates, demonstrating effective few-shot adaptation to a new robot embodiment.
 - **Contact-rich & Long-horizon Manipulation:** Showed significantly wider performance gaps. For "Clean the Rubbish" (coordinated dual-arm, tool usage, sequential transfer), LDA-1B achieved 35% success, while baselines failed (0%). This suggests improved anticipation of action-induced state transitions and temporal consistency.
- **Dexterous Manipulation (Unitree G1 with BrainCo, Galbot G1 with SharpaWave):**
 - **Low-DoF hands (BrainCo):** Achieved 80% success on "Pull Nail" (precise motion, stable contact), outperforming π0.5 (0%) and GR00T (40%).
 - **High-DoF hands (SharpaWave):** Achieved 90% success on "Flip Bread" (high-dimensional control, continuous contact), significantly outperforming π0.5 and GR00T (both 10%). This suggests strong latent priors from human data pretraining for dexterous control.
- **Generalization Ability:** On a pick-and-place task, LDA-1B maintained high success rates (60% for novel objects/backgrounds, 40% for OOD positions) under visual and spatial perturbations, indicating its ability to focus on task-critical affordances over visual noise.
- **Data-Efficient Fine-tuning:** LDA-1B improved success rates by 10 percentage points on tasks like "Place the pen into the box" and "Bimanually remove the lid" when incorporating low-quality trajectories (typically harmful to baselines), demonstrating effective utilization of noisy data.

**Analysis of Design Choices for Model Scaling (Agibot World):**
- **Effectiveness of Universal Data Ingestion:** The full co-training framework (policy, dynamics, visual forecasting) exhibited consistent improvement in action prediction error as additional heterogeneous data was introduced. Even after action-labeled data was exhausted, adding 10k actionless videos continued to reduce error. Partial co-training or policy-only objectives yielded unstable or less robust scaling.
- **Effectiveness of Latent Representation:** UWM, with its VAE-derived pixel-space latents, quickly saturated, whereas LDA, operating in a semantically structured DINO latent space, scaled smoothly with increased model capacity and richer training objectives.
- **Effectiveness of Model Size Scaling:** Scaling LDA from 0.1B to 0.5B and then to 1B parameters led to monotonic reductions in action prediction error, demonstrating effective absorption of additional capacity with sufficient heterogeneous supervision.

**Analysis of Multi-task Learning:**
- **Qualitative Analysis of Latent Forward Dynamics:** Visualizations showed that LDA generates coherent future-state predictions that respect physical constraints (object permanence, contact continuity, motion consistency) and focus on task-relevant objects, suggesting a dynamics-aware latent world model.
- **Action-Conditioned Attention:** Attention maps, conditioned on action primitives, consistently highlighted regions causally relevant to the commanded interaction (e.g., leading edge of a mug for "Push Right," drawer surface for "Push Close"), suppressing background clutter.
- **Gradient Similarity Analysis:** Gradient cosine similarities among training objectives showed that objectives with matched targets aligned strongly from early training, and even objectives with different prediction targets showed positive alignment by 400k iterations, suggesting LDA organizes heterogeneous objectives into compatible feature subspaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of LDA-1B represents an advancement in the field of robot foundation models, primarily by demonstrating a scalable alternative to behavior-cloning-centric pretraining. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Enabling Scalable General-Purpose Robot Models:** LDA-1B addresses a fundamental limitation of existing robot foundation models by providing a framework that can effectively scale with both data quantity and model capacity. Its universal embodied data ingestion paradigm, coupled with a structured latent space, offers a viable pathway for building more capable and generalized robot agents. This can accelerate the development of generalist robots applicable across diverse tasks and environments.

2. **Maximal Utilization of Heterogeneous Embodied Data:** The proposed method for assigning distinct, complementary roles to data of varying quality (high-quality, low-quality, actionless videos) allows for the exploitation of previously underutilized data sources. This significantly improves data efficiency, reducing the reliance on costly, meticulously curated expert demonstrations and making training feasible with naturally occurring, imperfect data. The EI-30k dataset itself is a contribution, offering a standardized, large-scale resource for the community.

3. **Improved Dynamics Understanding and Generalization:** By learning dynamics in a semantically structured DINO latent space rather than a pixel space, LDA-1B develops a more abstract and robust understanding of how actions causally propagate through a scene. This leads to policies that exhibit superior generalization across novel objects, backgrounds, and out-of-distribution conditions, as well as enhanced robustness in contact-rich, dexterous, and long-horizon manipulation tasks. This suggests a step towards robots that can better anticipate consequences and recover from errors.

4. **Advancements in Dexterous Manipulation:** The substantial performance gains observed in dexterous manipulation tasks, particularly with high-DoF hands, highlight the potential of leveraging large-scale human data to provide strong latent priors for fine-grained control. This could lead to more agile and precise robotic manipulation capabilities, expanding the range of tasks robots can perform.

5. **New Paradigm for Fine-tuning:** The demonstration of data-efficient fine-tuning, where low-quality trajectories actually boost performance rather than degrade it, offers a practical benefit for real-world deployment. This reduces the burden of collecting perfectly expert-level data for new tasks, making robot deployment more agile and cost-effective.

6. **Framework for Multi-task Learning:** The detailed analysis of gradient similarities and action-conditioned attention provides insights into how the MM-DiT architecture can effectively integrate diverse objectives and selectively ground visual attention to task-relevant regions based on action commands. This contributes to the understanding of multi-task learning for embodied AI.

Future directions include jointly learning visual representations with latent dynamics, extending to richer sensory modalities beyond egocentric RGB, and developing methods to automatically optimize data roles. These advancements could further unlock the potential of large-scale, heterogeneous data for robotics, fostering broader adoption of scalable, data-driven robot foundation models within the research community and industry.
