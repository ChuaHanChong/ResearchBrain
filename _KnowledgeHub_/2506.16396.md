# GoalLadder: Incremental Goal Discovery with Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A method called GoalLadder, developed by researchers at the University of Oxford, enables reinforcement learning agents to learn visual tasks from a single natural language instruction by incrementally discovering goals using Vision-Language Models. It mitigates noisy VLM feedback with an ELO-based rating system and improves VLM query efficiency through a learned visual embedding space, achieving approximately 95% success rates across continuous control tasks with about 4500 VLM queries for Metaworld.

## Problem

- Manually designing effective reward functions for Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents in visual environments is resource-intensive and requires significant domain expertise.
- Existing VLM-based approaches for generating reward functions from natural language instructions often suffer from noisy feedback, especially in spatial reasoning, or are computationally expensive due to frequent VLM queries.
- Prior VLM-based methods struggle with generalizability of reward signals and maintaining robustness to VLM errors without relying on environment modifications.

## Method

- **GoalLadder** incrementally discovers states closer to a ==language-specified task goal== using an iterative process involving VLM evaluations.
- It employs an ELO-based rating system to robustly rank candidate goals based on pairwise VLM comparisons, effectively absorbing noisy VLM feedback.
- A variational autoencoder (VAE) trained on unlabelled environment observations defines the reward function as the negative Euclidean distance from the current state's latent embedding to the highest-rated goal's embedding, enabling generalization and VLM query efficiency.

## Results

- GoalLadder achieved a mean final success rate of approximately **95%** across a range of continuous control tasks, nearly matching and in some cases surpassing Oracle performance with ground-truth rewards.
- The method demonstrated superior VLM query efficiency, requiring around 4500 VLM queries for Metaworld tasks, in contrast to other preference-based methods needing substantially more labels.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the ELO rating system in handling noisy VLM feedback and showed that a small, task-specific VAE for reward definition significantly outperformed large, general-purpose visual encoders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An ELO-based rating system can effectively absorb and aggregate noisy feedback from Vision-Language Models, leading to a robust ranking of task sub-goals.
> - Defining reward functions as distances in a learned, compact visual embedding space (VAE) allows for efficient reward signal generalization to unseen states, significantly reducing the need for continuous VLM queries.
> - An incremental discovery process, guided by a VLM, can progressively identify and refine task-relevant sub-goals from a single natural language instruction.
