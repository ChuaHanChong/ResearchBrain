# LASeR: Learning to Adaptively Select Reward Models with Multi-Armed Bandits

> [!summary] Summary
> LASeR (Learning to Adaptively Select Reward models) introduces a framework that uses Multi-Armed Bandits to dynamically select the most suitable reward model for each batch during iterative LLM training. This adaptive approach improved LLM alignment, achieving higher accuracy on reasoning tasks, better instruction-following capabilities, and up to 3x faster training compared to single-RM and ensemble baselines.

## Problem

- Single, fixed Reward Models (RMs) often lack generalization across diverse tasks, are prone to 'reward hacking,' and can provide unreliable feedback during LLM alignment.
- Ensemble-based RM approaches are computationally prohibitive due to the need to run multiple large models simultaneously and can suffer from conflicting preference signals.
- Manually selecting optimal RMs or designing effective aggregation strategies for ensembles is a labor-intensive and inefficient process.

## Method

- ==LASeR== frames the problem of selecting an RM from a pool of candidates as a contextual ==Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB)== problem, primarily utilizing the ==LinUCB algorithm==.
- It dynamically selects a single RM for each mini-batch during iterative LLM fine-tuning (e.g., DPO) and uses its feedback to update the LLM.
- The MAB's reward signal is defined as the ==negative normalized training loss== of the LLM from that batch, enabling it to learn which RMs provide the most informative guidance for different contexts.

## Results

- LASeR achieved an absolute average accuracy of **76.32%** on reasoning benchmarks (StrategyQA, GSM8K, MMLU) with Llama-3-8B, outperforming the 'Best RM' baseline by **1.45%** and the 'RM Score Ensemble' by **2.67%**.
- It demonstrated superior AlpacaEval win rates on instruction-following tasks, significantly beating 'Random RM Selection' (**78.33%**) and 'RM Score Ensemble' (**72.69%**) baselines.
- The method provided a **2x** to **3x** speedup in wall-clock training time compared to ensemble and sequential baselines while delivering higher performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically selecting a single, context-appropriate RM per batch mitigates the computational overhead and conflicting signals inherent in traditional ensemble methods.
> - The MAB effectively balances exploration of different RMs with exploitation of those performing well, adapting its selection strategy as the LLM policy evolves.
> - Using the downstream LLM's training loss as the MAB's reward provides a robust and direct signal for assessing the quality and utility of a selected RM in a given context.
