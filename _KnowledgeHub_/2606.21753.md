# Scene-Level Heterogeneous Physics Simulation with 3D Gaussian Splats

> [!summary] Summary
> RAF (Representation Abstraction Framework) makes captured 3D Gaussian Splatting assets physically interactive by translating 3DGS, virtual meshes and fluids into one unified particle set, simulating them in a solver-agnostic multi-solver kernel (MPM, SPH, PBD, rigid, articulated) against a reconstructed static collision mesh, then reverse-translating deformation gradients back into per-asset visuals rendered in Unreal Engine 5. Five previously impossible scene-level heterogeneous scenarios each simulate in under 10 minutes.

## Problem

- Production physics engines operate on particles and meshes, so they cannot natively ingest 3DGS covariance, spherical-harmonic and opacity attributes, leaving photorealistic captures as passive non-interactive renderables.
- Prior physics-for-3DGS solvers are monolithic silos: closed systems containing only Gaussian particles, incapable of two-way interaction with external meshes, fluids or rigid bodies.
- Those solvers demonstrate only object-centric dynamics in a void or on an ideal plane, unable to collide with complex pre-existing static scene geometry.

## Method

- ==Representation abstraction layer== converts every dynamic asset into one particle list: 3DGS by ==opacity-field thresholding== plus ==Poisson-disk filling== with invisible ghost particles, meshes by ==barycentric-weight binding==, fluids as ==SPH particles==.
- ==Unified simulation kernel==, built on the Genesis solver, dispatches rigid, articulated, ==MPM==, ==SPH== and ==PBD== solvers in fixed sequence, coupled by ==SDF impulse response== and grid-mediated momentum exchange.
- ==Visual state recoupling== reverse-translates physical state per asset: Gaussians update covariance as F Sigma_rest F transpose and rotate SH coefficients, meshes skin from particle positions, then serialize into Unreal Engine 5.

## Results

- **Five** scenarios cover SPH fluid on virtual mesh, SPH on MPM 3DGS soft body, articulated arm manipulation, PBD cloth on captured statue, rigid bodies in imported 3DGS basket, each under **10 minutes**.
- Ablating the unified kernel lets SPH sauce and MPM donut particles pass through each other; removing the static collision mesh collapses cloth onto an ideal plane, penetrating the captured statue.
- Against KiriEngine photogrammetry on identical multi-view images, the planar-Gaussian pipeline preserves sharp bulldozer treads and table slats where the photogrammetric mesh is blobby and geometrically distorted.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bridging representations beats rebuilding solvers: abstracting assets to particles inherits an entire industrial multi-solver ecosystem instead of reimplementing physics for each new visual representation.
> - The deformation gradient is a general interface: any local affine map can drive Gaussian covariance, mesh skinning weights, or raw point positions alike.
> - Caution: the pipeline is offline and cinematic, density mismatch across representations needs invisible ghost particles, and object-level separation in captured scenes stays unsolved.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of photorealistic rendering and physical simulation, specifically addressing a limitation within the rapidly evolving field of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). 3DGS has emerged as a state-of-the-art technique for real-time, photorealistic novel-view synthesis, representing scenes with explicit Gaussian splats. While excelling in rendering static scenes or replaying pre-captured motion, 3DGS assets have largely remained "passive renderables," lacking the ability to physically interact within dynamic environments.

Traditional production-grade physics engines (e.g., Unreal Engine, Houdini) are mature, powerful, and capable of simulating complex, heterogeneous interactions between various physical representations such as particles, meshes, and rigid bodies. However, these engines are representation-agnostic and do not natively support the novel 3DGS format.

Prior research attempting to integrate physics with 3DGS often followed a "siloed" approach, developing proprietary, monolithic physics solvers specifically for 3DGS. Examples include methods like PhysGaussian, DecoupledGaussian, Gaussian Splashing, and GausSim. These prior methods were fundamentally limited in two main ways: they were homogeneous, meaning they could only simulate interactions between GS particles and were incompatible with external standard computer graphics (CG) assets (like meshes or fluids); and they were isolated, typically operating in void environments or on ideal planes, thus incapable of interacting with complex, pre-existing 3D static collision geometry. This restriction to "toy" scenarios prevented their scalability and general application in complex virtual worlds.

This research aims to bridge this "representation gap" by enabling 3DGS assets to participate in complex, scene-level, heterogeneous physical simulations, leveraging existing, robust physics engines instead of developing another specialized solver. It seeks to transform 3DGS assets from passive visual elements into active, physically interactive components within unified, high-fidelity rendering environments like Unreal Engine 5.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assets to participate in scene-level, heterogeneous, multi-solver physical simulations. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in the existing research landscape:

1. **Bridging the Representation Gap:** 3DGS has achieved photorealistic rendering, but its explicit Gaussian representation is not understood by traditional physics engines, which operate on physical representations like particles and meshes. This fundamental representation gap prevents 3DGS assets from being physically interactive.
2. **Overcoming "Siloed" Physics Solutions:** Previous attempts to integrate physics with 3DGS have relied on developing proprietary, monolithic solvers specifically for the 3DGS representation. These "siloed" approaches suffer from two critical flaws:
 - **Homogeneity:** They create closed systems where only GS particles can exist, making them incapable of interacting with external, standard CG assets such as meshes, fluids, or rigid bodies. This limits the complexity and realism of possible interactions.
 - **Isolation:** They typically operate in simplified environments, such as a void or an ideal plane, and lack the ability to support complex, pre-existing 3D static collision geometry from captured real-world scenes. This restricts their applicability to "toy" scenarios rather than complex virtual worlds.
3. **Enabling Heterogeneous and Scene-Level Interactions:** The motivation is to move beyond isolated object-centric simulations to enable complex, two-way interactions between deformable 3DGS assets, standard CG assets (e.g., fluids, meshes), and large-scale captured static environments. This includes supporting multi-solver architectures (e.g., MPM, SPH, PBD) within a single simulation context.
4. **Achieving Production-Grade Fidelity:** The goal extends to integrating these physically interactive 3DGS assets into industrial-strength rendering pipelines, such as Unreal Engine 5, to leverage advanced rendering features like global illumination and ray-traced shadows for cinematic-quality results.

In essence, the research aims to transform 3DGS assets from static visual elements into fully functional, interactable components within "physical sandboxes" by developing a general and scalable solution that integrates them into complex, scene-level simulations without reinventing physics solvers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel **Representation Abstraction Framework (RAF)** designed to bridge the gap between 3DGS assets and industrial-strength heterogeneous physics engines. The framework operates in three main stages: (1) heterogeneous asset preparation and abstraction, (2) a unified simulation kernel, and (3) heterogeneous visual recoupling and rendering.

#### 3.1. Asset Preparation and Abstraction

This initial stage processes diverse input assets and translates them into two core components for the simulation kernel: a single static collision mesh (M\_static) and a unified particle list.

- **Populating the Static World (M\_static):** M\_static defines all non-moving geometry for dynamic object interaction. It is a composite mesh formed from both captured real-world data and optional imported assets. For in-the-wild scenes, multi-view images are processed through a captured asset pipeline, leveraging techniques like Gaussian segmentation and object-scene restoration (using planar-based Gaussian and surface reconstruction) to extract static geometry. Users can also augment this with custom geometry, which is then merged into a final watertight M\_static.
- **Populating the Dynamic World (Unified Particle List):** All dynamic assets are processed by a representation abstraction layer and converted into a unified physical particle list.
 - **Captured 3DGS assets:** Dynamic 3DGS objects (G\_dyn) are converted into a set of physical particles (X\_gs). For pre-processed assets, dense points are used. For standard 3DGS captures, the object's interior is defined by computing and sampling a continuous opacity field d(x) (Equation 1). Each resulting particle position x\_j becomes a physical entity p\_j, encapsulating geometric, kinematic, and constitutive parameters (e.g., position, mass, velocity, model type, Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio) (Equation 2). Concurrently, the visual attributes (covariance, spherical harmonics, opacity) are stored in a visual asset cache for later recoupling. Particle filling involves discretizing the opacity field onto an Eulerian grid, sampling internal cells using Poisson Disk Sampling, and optionally injecting ghost particles for density homogenization during multi-physics coupling (Supplementary S1).
 - **Virtual meshes:** Dynamic meshes are converted into particles using Poisson disk sampling (Equation 3). A binding between the template mesh and its particles is established by pre-computing barycentric weights (Equation 4), stored in the visual asset cache.
 - **External particle systems (e.g., fluid):** Inputs such as SPH fluids are loaded directly as initial particle positions, with their model type set (e.g., SPH).
 - **Kinematic assets (e.g., robot arm):** These are treated as standard virtual mesh assets (rigid bodies) with kinematically prescribed motion, acting as one-way colliders without being physically solved by the kernel.

#### 3.2. Unified Simulation Kernel

This stage serves as a modular, solver-agnostic physics kernel. It receives the complete static collision mesh (M\_static) and the unified particle list with initial physical states (x\_t, F\_t). The kernel integrates various specialized solvers, including Material Point Method (MPM) for soft bodies, Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) for fluids, Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) for cloth, rigid-body dynamics, and articulated-body dynamics.

The kernel's core functionality is robust management of heterogeneous interactions. This encompasses continuous force exchanges (e.g., SPH pressure on MPM particles) and discrete collision events. For discrete collisions and frictional contact between all entities (e.g., particle-mesh, rigid-body), a unified impulse-based mechanism is employed. This mechanism computes a response impulse to determine post-collision velocities, incorporating coefficients of restitution and Coulomb friction (Equations 5, 6).

Within each time step, solvers are executed iteratively in a specific sequence (rigid-body, articulated-body, then MPM, SPH, and PBD), with states propagated and refined to ensure consistent resolution of coupling forces and constraints (Supplementary S4, S5).
- **Rigid Body Dynamics (Supplementary S4.1):** Simulates articulated mechanisms using a Reduced Coordinate formulation, governed by the manipulator equation, with an implicit integration scheme for stability.
- **Material Point Method (MPM) (Supplementary S4.2):** Primary solver for continuum materials (elastoplastic solids, granular media). It uses Moving Least Squares MPM (MLS-MPM) with Affine Particle-in-Cell (APIC) for particle-to-grid (P2G) transfer (Equations S5, S6) and grid-to-particle (G2P) transfer (Equations S9, S10), and handles constitutive models and plasticity via SVD-based return-mapping.
- **Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) (Supplementary S4.3):** Simulates deformable objects like cloth, soft bodies, and Lagrangian fluids using an Extended Position-Based Dynamics (XPBD) formulation. It employs a predictor-corrector scheme to iteratively resolve geometric constraints (Equations S11-S13), including distance, bending, and volume constraints (Equations S14-S16). Position-Based Fluids (PBF) enforce constant density and handle viscosity (Equations S17, S18).
- **Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) (Supplementary S4.4):** A fully Lagrangian fluid solver, supporting Weakly Compressible SPH (WCSPH) or Divergence-Free SPH (DFSPH) for pressure calculation (Equations S21, S23), and modeling viscosity (Equation S24) and surface tension (Equation S25).

The coupling mechanisms between these diverse solvers are crucial:
- **SDF-based impulse response (Rigid-MPM, Rigid-SPH):** Uses signed distance fields (SDFs) of rigid geometries to modulate collision softness and friction, applying velocity-level impulses (Equations S26-S28).
- **Position-based penetration correction (Rigid-PBD):** Relies on geometric projection to resolve interpenetration for PBD particles, projecting particle positions outward along the contact normal (Equation S29).
- **Grid-mediated momentum exchange (MPM-SPH, MPM-PBD):** Achieves coupling via the MPM Eulerian grid acting as a momentum exchange medium, forcing velocity synchronization between grid nodes and interacting particles (Equations S30, S31).

#### 3.3. Heterogeneous Recoupling and Rendering

The final stage translates the sparse physical data from the simulation kernel back into high-fidelity, renderable visual assets. This involves asset-specific reconstruction using the updated physical state (x', F') and cached visual data.

- **3DGS assets:** The new Gaussian center (p'\_j) is set to the updated particle position (Equation 7). The new covariance (Σ'\_j) is computed by applying the physical deformation gradient (F'\_j) to the cached rest-state covariance (Σ\_rest,j) (Equation 8). Opacity (σ'\_j) is assumed invariant (Equation 9), and spherical harmonics coefficients (C\_j) are rotated based on F'\_j. This F-propagation to Σ is physically consistent (Supplementary S2).
- **Mesh assets:** The asset's template mesh and precomputed barycentric weights are retrieved. New deformed vertex positions (v'\_i) are calculated by applying these weights to the new particle positions (x'\_k) (Equation 10).
- **Particle assets:** For fluid assets (SPH), the physical particle position (p'\_j) directly serves as its visual representation.

The recoupled visual data is then serialized into asset-aware formats (e.g..ply for 3DGS.abc for meshes.pbd caches for particles). These files are loaded into Unreal Engine 5 using custom 3DGS plugins and native engine support for other formats. This enables co-simulated assets to be rendered with advanced features like Lumen global illumination and ray-traced shadows. The system is designed for offline, cinematic-quality production, prioritizing accuracy and visual fidelity over real-time performance (Supplementary S3).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors validated their framework through a series of complex scenarios and ablation studies, designed to demonstrate capabilities unattainable by prior "siloed" methods.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Showcase of New Capabilities

Five scenarios were presented, each showcasing distinct capabilities of the framework:

1. **Scene-Level Composition (Fluid on Virtual Mesh):** A virtual fluid (SPH) was poured into an imported virtual mesh bowl, demonstrating interaction with both the bowl and a static 3DGS garden scene. This validated the framework's ability to compose and simulate interactions between standard CG assets and captured 3DGS environments.
2. **Heterogeneous Multi-Solver Coupling (Fluid on GS Soft Body):** A high-viscosity fluid (SPH) interacted with a captured 3DGS soft-body (MPM donut) on a plate. This showcased complex multi-solver, two-way interaction between physically distinct asset types.
3. **Articulated Dynamics (Robot Arm Manipulation):** A kinematically-driven robotic arm (articulated-body solver) manipulated a virtual rigid body (cube) on a table. This demonstrated the integration of rigid and articulated body dynamics within the framework.
4. **Complex Geometry Interaction (Cloth on Captured Statue):** A virtual cloth (PBD solver) realistically draped and slid over the complex, non-convex geometry of a captured 3DGS statue. This directly addressed and refuted the "ideal plane" limitation of previous works, showing robust interaction with detailed static environments.
5. **Interaction with Imported 3DGS Assets (Rigid-body Collision in 3DGS Container):** Multiple virtual rigid bodies (fruits) interacted with each other and with the complex geometry of an imported 3DGS asset (a basket). This highlighted the framework's ability to incorporate and simulate interactions with imported 3DGS objects.

Collectively, these qualitative demonstrations confirmed the framework's capacity for unifying various solvers and heterogeneous assets (captured GS scenes, objects, imported assets, virtual meshes/particles) within complex, scene-level compositions, integrating both SOTA physics and rendering.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study and Comparative Analysis

Ablation studies were conducted to scientifically validate the necessity of core components, as direct numerical comparisons with prior siloed methods were deemed inapplicable due to fundamental differences in scope.

##### 4.2.1. Ablation Study

- **Validating the Unified Kernel:** Comparison with "Ours w/o unified simulation kernel" (where SPH and MPM solvers do not interact) showed that without the heterogeneous coupling mechanism, fluid particles (sauce) passed through the soft-body particles (donut) in a physically implausible manner. The full model correctly simulated the SPH-on-MPM collision and deformation, demonstrating the critical importance of the unified kernel.
- **Validating Scene-Level Interaction:** Comparison with "Ours w/o static collision mesh" revealed that without the complex M\_static, the PBD-cloth solver defaulted to an "ideal plane," causing the cloth to pass through the captured statue. The full method, utilizing the statue's high-fidelity collision mesh, produced realistic draping and sliding, confirming the essential role of the scene-level pipeline for integration into captured environments.
- **Validating Representation Abstraction:** Comparison with "Ours w/o representation abstraction" showed only sparse underlying simulation particles, lacking continuous surfaces and photorealistic appearance. The full method's recoupling layer effectively translated this sparse physical state into a high-fidelity visual result, demonstrating its necessity for photorealistic rendering.

##### 4.2.2. Comparison with a Photogrammetry Pipeline

To justify the use of 3DGS, the framework was compared against a standard photogrammetry pipeline (KiriEngine) generating meshes from the same multi-view images.
- The photogrammetry baseline exhibited severe geometric artifacts, appearing "blobby" or distorted on fine structures (e.g., bulldozer treads, table slats).
- In contrast, the proposed pipeline, leveraging 3DGS with PGSR, preserved sharp and geometrically complete details. This comparison illustrated the fundamental trade-off: while photogrammetry produces physics-ready meshes, it often sacrifices visual fidelity and geometric detail. The framework bridges this gap by enabling high-fidelity 3DGS assets to participate in advanced physics, transforming them into interactive physical assets while maintaining visual quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Representation Abstraction Framework (RAF) proposed in this paper represents a significant advancement in integrating photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assets with advanced physical simulation.

1. **Bridging a Fundamental Representation Gap:** The core contribution is the development of the first end-to-end pipeline that effectively bridges the gap between the rendering-centric 3DGS representation and the physical representations understood by industrial-strength heterogeneous physics engines. This enables 3DGS assets, which are easy to capture and render with high fidelity, to become physically interactive.
2. **Unlocking Heterogeneous Scene-Level Interactions:** The framework directly addresses the limitations of prior "siloed" physics-for-3DGS methods. By abstracting diverse visual representations (3DGS, virtual meshes, fluids) into a unified physical particle set and incorporating static scene collision boundaries, it facilitates complex, two-way interactions between heterogeneous assets and real-world static environments. This capability, demonstrated through multi-solver coupling and interaction with complex geometries, was previously unattainable.
3. **Leveraging Production-Grade Tools:** Instead of developing another custom, limited physics solver, the approach intelligently integrates captured 3DGS assets into existing, robust, and mature physics engines (like the Genesis-world solver) and rendering platforms (Unreal Engine 5). This allows immediate access to advanced physics simulation capabilities and cinematic-quality rendering features, including global illumination and ray-traced shadows, for all co-simulated assets.
4. **Enabling New Forms of Content Creation:** The ability to simulate deformable 3DGS objects interacting realistically with fluids, rigid bodies, cloth, and complex captured scenes opens new avenues for creating dynamic virtual content. This could impact film production, game development, virtual reality experiences, and robotics simulation, where photorealism and physical plausibility are crucial.
5. **Scientific Validation:** The rigorous ablation studies confirm the necessity of the unified coupling kernel, the scene-level geometry handling, and the visual recoupling bridge, providing a scientific basis for the framework's design choices. The comparison with photogrammetry highlights the visual fidelity advantage of the 3DGS-based approach while achieving physics readiness.

The potential impact of this work is in transforming 3DGS from a technology primarily focused on novel-view synthesis of static scenes to a foundational element for building dynamic, interactive, and photorealistic virtual worlds. While currently targeting offline, cinematic-quality production, future extensions to real-time interactive scenarios and improved object-level separation in complex captured scenes are identified as promising research directions.
