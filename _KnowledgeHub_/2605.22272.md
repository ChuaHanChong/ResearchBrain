# Imagine2Real: Towards Zero-shot Humanoid-Object Interaction via Video Generative Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> The Imagine2Real framework enables zero-shot Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI) by converting human video demonstrations into dynamically feasible robot trajectories through a unified 4D point representation. It employs a Behavior Foundation Model (BFM)-based Keypoints Tracker for natural gait preservation and a progressive training strategy, successfully demonstrating diverse physical interaction tasks on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Problem

- High-fidelity 3D interaction data for humanoid robots is scarce, limiting the development of robust and versatile HOI policies.
- Existing video-to-motion methods face representation misalignment and rely on explicit geometric priors (e.g., CAD models) or struggle with jointly estimating robot and object motions from videos.
- Retargeting dense, low-level joint signals from human videos to robot embodiments is complex and error-prone, especially with object geometry; sparse tracking often compromises natural whole-body movements.

## Method

- A unified ==4D point trajectory representation== for robot and object motions is introduced to avoid reliance on explicit geometric priors and ensure inherent spatial alignment.
- A **Behavior Foundation Model (BFM)**-based ==Keypoints Tracker== is developed to track only sparse, interaction-critical points (robot base, hands, object) while constraining motion within the BFM's latent space to preserve natural gaits and stability.
- A progressive three-stage training strategy (BFM backbone, Keypoints Tracker, ==Interaction Adaptor==) leverages multi-scale datasets to build foundational priors before specializing in HOI, addressing data scarcity and simplifying reward engineering.

## Results

- The BFM-based Keypoints Tracker achieved significantly smoother and more natural motions, reducing action jitter (A_rate) to **0.22** compared to **1.65** for direct tracking, and improving mean per joint angle error (E_mpjae) to **0.25**.
- The Interaction Adaptor proved essential for physical object manipulation, increasing the success rate for 'Carry Box' from **0.00%** to **82.65%** and for 'Push Box' from **29.82%** to **64.91%** in sim2sim evaluations.
- Zero-shot real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot was successfully demonstrated for diverse tasks, including lifting various boxes (**0.5kg to 2kg**) and performing interactive gestures, validating the 'video-to-motion-to-real' paradigm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing both robot and object motions as unified 4D point trajectories effectively resolves representation misalignment and eliminates the need for explicit geometric models.
> - Constraining sparse keypoint tracking within the latent space of a pre-trained Behavior Foundation Model is crucial for maintaining natural whole-body gaits and generating smooth, stable actions, even with minimal control signals.
> - A progressive training framework, utilizing abundant non-interactive data to build a strong behavioral prior before fine-tuning for interaction, allows for robust zero-shot HOI with simplified reward structures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of whole-body Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI) is a critical area for advancing robotic capabilities, particularly for deploying humanoid robots in complex, unstructured environments like households and factories. While proprioceptive control has achieved notable advancements, extending these to sophisticated interactions remains challenging due to a significant bottleneck: the scarcity of high-fidelity 3D interaction data specifically for humanoid robots. This data limitation hinders the development of robust and versatile interaction policies.

Current HOI methodologies face a generalization ceiling. Scaling-based models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, aim for broad generalization through end-to-end training but are constrained by the absence of sufficiently massive, humanoid-specific datasets. In contrast, approaches incorporating Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) utilize sparse, high-quality data to learn specialized behaviors. However, these methods often encounter issues like mode collapse, which restricts them to specific task categories and necessitates extensive manual reward engineering for each new task. Motion tracking methods, while bypassing data scarcity by mimicking single trajectories, are primarily limited to rigid motion replay and lack the autonomy required for novel or dynamic scenarios.

A promising direction to address 3D data scarcity involves leveraging internet-scale video generation models to provide visual and physical priors for downstream motion trackers. While this pipeline has demonstrated potential in general motion tracking, its application to HOI introduces two specific and significant challenges:

1. **Representation Misalignment:** Extracting 3D motion from 2D videos commonly relies on strong geometric priors, such as explicit object CAD models, which are often unavailable in real-world settings. Furthermore, existing methods struggle to jointly estimate robot and object motions in a unified manner. Independent estimation using separate tools for robots (e.g., GVHMR) and objects (e.g., FoundationPose) leads to spatial and depth discrepancies, resulting in low-quality reference motions and tracking failures. This often forces researchers to resort to manual or heuristic methods for rigidly attaching objects to robot motions, which still require CAD models and can distort actual object movement.
2. **Retargeting Complexity:** Most motion tracking models depend on dense, low-level signals like joint positions. Obtaining these from human videos necessitates explicit retargeting of the extracted motion to a specific humanoid embodiment. In HOI contexts, this retargeting becomes exceptionally complex due to the introduction of object geometry. It requires intensive and time-consuming morphing to jointly align robot and object meshes, a process that inherently amplifies estimation errors from the video source. While tracking sparse keypoints offers an alternative to complex retargeting, existing sparse methods often compromise motion quality, struggling to achieve natural gaits without dense joint-level information or extensive reliance on mechanisms like AMP, which limit scalability to interaction tasks.

This work, Imagine2Real, aims to bridge these gaps by proposing a zero-shot HOI framework that directly confronts the issues of representation misalignment and retargeting complexity. It positions itself as a method to unlock flexible, geometry-free interaction by using unified 3D point trajectories and leveraging Behavior Foundation Models (BFM) to ensure natural movements from sparse control signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a zero-shot Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI) framework capable of achieving flexible and geometry-free interaction, leveraging video generative priors. This overarching goal is driven by several detailed motivations aimed at addressing the aforementioned challenges in the current HOI landscape:

1. **To Resolve Representation Misalignment:** A core motivation is to overcome the reliance on explicit geometric priors (e.g., CAD models) and the discrepancies arising from independently estimating robot and object motions from videos. The objective is to formulate robot and object motions using a unified, shared representation—specifically, 4D point trajectories. This unified representation aims to ensure that robot and object movements are inherently aligned within a common coordinate frame, thereby eliminating the need for geometric priors and improving the quality of reference motions.
2. **To Overcome Retargeting Complexity:** The complexities and error amplification associated with retargeting dense, low-level joint signals from human videos to specific humanoid embodiments, especially in the presence of objects, motivate the development of a simpler, more robust approach. The objective is to entirely bypass this complex and error-prone retargeting process by tracking only sparse, interaction-critical keypoints (specifically, the robot's base and hands, and the object itself). This minimal set of tracking points is intended to reduce computational overhead and prevent error propagation.
3. **To Maintain Natural Gaits with Sparse Signals:** A challenge with sparse keypoint tracking is often the degradation of whole-body naturalness and stability. Therefore, a key objective is to ensure that despite relying on sparse signals, the humanoid robot can still maintain stable, natural gaits and whole-body postural balance. This is motivated by the understanding that a naive policy aggressively optimizing for sparse targets would likely violate physical constraints. The chosen approach is to utilize the latent space of a pre-trained Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) as the search domain for the keypoints tracker. The BFM is intended to provide strong physical priors and constrain movements to be physically viable and natural.
4. **To Enable Robust Zero-shot Physical Deployment:** The ultimate motivation is to achieve successful deployment of learned HOI policies on a real humanoid robot without prior exposure to the specific real-world setup or task. This requires policies that are robust to noise and discrepancies between simulation and reality. A related objective is to simplify the training process by using straightforward tracking rewards, avoiding the complex, hand-engineered reward functions or advanced mechanisms (like object force curriculum or Physical State Initialization) typically required for learning complex interaction skills from scratch. This is facilitated by the progressive training strategy, which builds foundational priors from abundant non-interactive data before specializing on interaction tasks.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to create a more scalable, flexible, and data-efficient paradigm for humanoid robots to perform complex interactions with objects in unstructured environments, moving from "imagined" video interactions to physical reality with minimal human intervention.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Imagine2Real framework addresses zero-shot HOI through a video-guided tracking paradigm, which involves synthesizing an interaction video, extracting reference motions, and then controlling a robot to track these motions. The methodology is structured around a three-stage progressive training strategy for the HOI Tracker and a two-component real-world deployment pipeline.

#### 3.1. HOI Tracker Training (Progressive Strategy)

The training pipeline comprises three cascaded modules, designed to leverage multi-scale datasets and overcome the scarcity of high-fidelity interaction data.

#### 3.1.1. BFM Backbone

The initial stage involves training a Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) backbone. This BFM constructs a compact motion representation space to address the inherent difficulty of learning natural sparse keypoint tracking directly. When provided only sparse targets, naive policies tend to overfit and violate physical constraints like natural gaits.
- **Purpose:** To create a versatile and reusable latent motion space (32-dimensional) that internalizes humanoid forward dynamics and natural motion transitions.
- **Architecture:**
 - **Motion Encoder (E):** A 6-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) (512 hidden dimensions) maps a $\delta$-step motion subsequence $m_{t:t+\delta}$ to a latent code $z_t$.
 - **Autoregressive Predictor (P):** A Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) (256 hidden size) processes proprioceptive observation history ($o_{prop}_{0:t}$) and previous latent state ($z_{t-1}$) to estimate a latent prior $\hat{z}_t$. This predictor is optimized via a prediction loss to match the encoded reference motion.
 - **Decoder (D):** An MLP (6 layers, 1024 size) translates the current latent command $z_t$ and proprioceptive history into low-level action targets $a_t$.
- **Training:** Pre-trained on a large corpus of human motion data (~68.5h from AMASS, LAFAN1, 100STYLE). The predictor, once frozen, acts as an autonomous motor engine, outputting physically viable default motion priors that can be modulated by higher-level sparse tracking policies.

#### 3.1.2. Keypoints Tracker

Building on the frozen BFM, the Keypoints Tracker learns to track sparse reference motions.
- **Purpose:** To perform task-conditioned biasing on top of the BFM low-level backbone, tracking only a minimal set of critical points (robot base, hands, object) while maintaining balance. This bypasses the complex, error-amplifying retargeting process.
- **Mechanism:**
 - The frozen BFM Predictor ($P^*$) and Decoder ($D^*$) constitute a low-level backbone.
 - A latent residual planner (H) takes task commands (sparse keypoints $m_{kp}_{t:t+\delta}$), previous latent $z'_{t-1}$, and the current latent prior $\hat{z}_t$ to output a residual command $z_{res}_t$.
 - This residual is added to the latent prior to form the new latent command $z'_t = \hat{z}_t + z_{res}_t$.
 - The new latent command $z'_t$ is decoded by the frozen BFM Decoder ($D^*$) into a full-body action $a'_t$.
- **Training:** Trained on loco-manipulation data (~8.86h from LAFAN1, OMOMO, AMASS), which is more targeted than the BFM's diverse whole-body motion. Sparse keypoints are used as tracking targets.

#### 3.1.3. Interaction Adaptor

The final stage introduces the Interaction Adaptor to enable fine-grained object manipulation.
- **Purpose:** To bridge the dexterity gap, as the BFM backbone is trained on non-interactive motions, by learning residual joint-level actions.
- **Mechanism:**
 - Formulated as a residual policy ($\pi_{adapt}$).
 - Receives proprioceptive history, sparse robot keypoints motion, and target object motion.
 - Outputs a residual joint-level action $\Delta a_t$.
 - $\Delta a_t$ is added directly to the Keypoints Tracker's output $a'_t$ to form the final execution command $a_{int}_t = a'_t + \Delta a_t$.
- **Training:** Fine-tuned on limited HOI data (~0.43h from OMOMO, specifically box-carrying and pushing tasks). It uses straightforward tracking rewards, avoiding complex mechanisms due to the foundational priors built in previous stages.

#### 3.2. Real-world Deployment

The real-world deployment pipeline translates the imagined interactions into physical reality.

#### 3.2.1. Video-to-Motion Pipeline

This pipeline extracts unified 3D point trajectories from a synthesized interaction video.
- **Input:** Monocular RGB image (initial robot/object state) and a text instruction.
- **Video Generation:** A video generative model (e.g., Seedance 2.0 Fast) synthesizes an interaction video conditioned on the text instruction and initial image.
- **Motion Extraction:**
 - **Key Region Segmentation:** SAM3 is used to obtain precise masks for the robot's left hand, right hand, pelvis, and the target object.
 - **4D Point Tracking:** SpaTrackerV2 tracks pixels within these masks, yielding 4D point trajectories for each region.
 - **Robust Keypoint Filtering:** Tracked points are filtered based on visibility and confidence scores from SpaTrackerV2. Spatio-temporal outliers are removed. Valid point trajectories within each mask are geometrically averaged and smoothed to produce a stable reference trajectory for each keypoint.

#### 3.2.2. Mocap Deployment

During physical execution, a motion capture (mocap) system is utilized.
- **Real-time Global Positions:** The mocap system provides real-time global positions of the robot and object keypoints, serving as direct inputs for the control policy.
- **Scale Calibration:** Since video-generated motions inherently lack metric depth, the extracted trajectories represent relative motions. The mocap system is used to capture the precise global positions of keypoints at the initial frame, which are then used to scale and calibrate the generated trajectories to real-world dimensions. This method aims for higher calibration accuracy compared to monocular depth estimation or stereo vision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to evaluate the naturalness of gaits, success in HOI tasks with sparse information, and the feasibility of zero-shot real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

- **Dataset:**
 - **BFM Backbone:** Large-scale human motion data from AMASS, LAFAN1, and 100STYLE (~10,000 clips, 68.5 hours), retargeted to the robot via inverse kinematics.
 - **Keypoints Tracker:** Loco-manipulation motions from LAFAN1, OMOMO, and AMASS (~4,000 clips, 8.86 hours), filtering out difficult-to-represent actions.
 - **Interaction Adaptor:** Specific box-carrying and pushing tasks from OMOMO (~200 clips, 0.43 hours), retargeted via holosoma for morphological mismatch correction and auxiliary rewards. Inference requires no retargeted data.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Task Completion:** Success Rate (SR) (tracking errors below 0.2m), Tracking Errors (E$_{hands}$, E$_{base}$, E$_{obj}$) and final-frame errors (E$_{hands-f}$, E$_{base-f}$, E$_{obj-f}$).
 - **Motion Naturalness:** Mean Per Joint Angle Error (E$_{mpjae}$) (against ground-truth dense motion), Action Rate (A$_{rate}$) and Action Smoothness (A$_{smooth}$) (first and second derivatives of actions, lower values indicate smoother commands).
- **Training Details:** Policies trained in Isaac Gym using PPO, 8,192 parallel environments on an RTX 4090 GPU, Adam optimizer. Gaussian noise (5cm) injected into reference keypoints during the latter two stages to simulate 3D tracker noise.

#### 4.2. Sim2sim Evaluation (MuJoCo)

All quantitative metrics were evaluated in the MuJoCo simulator to verify policy generalization.

- **Ablation on Keypoints Tracking (Table 2):**
 - **Direct Tracking:** Achieved the lowest E$_{hands}$ and E$_{base}$ but at a significant cost to motion quality. It resulted in high jitter (A$_{rate}$=1.65, A$_{smooth}$=0.64) and unnatural whole-body postures (high E$_{mpjae}$), rendering it impractical for real-robot deployment.
 - **DAgger Baseline:** Showed marginal improvement over Direct tracking but still exhibited relatively high errors in motion naturalness and smoothness.
 - **Imagine2Real (Ours, BFM-based):** By constraining the search space to the BFM's latent space, this method significantly reduced A$_{rate}$ (0.22), A$_{smooth}$ (0.09), and E$_{mpjae}$ (0.25). This indicates that the BFM-based Keypoints Tracker effectively performs sparse tracking while preserving natural gaits and generating smooth, stable actions, albeit with slightly higher tracking errors for hands and base compared to the Direct method.

- **HOI Task Execution (Table 1):**
 - **Interaction Adaptor Necessity:** The Interaction Adaptor was crucial for successful physical object manipulation. For "Carry Box," the "w/o Adaptor" baseline had a 0.00% Success Rate, while "w/ Adaptor" achieved 82.65%. For "Push Box," the SR improved from 29.82% to 64.91% with the adaptor. This demonstrates the adaptor's role in bridging the gap between kinematic tracking and effective physical interaction.
 - **Task Performance:** The full framework ("w/ Adaptor") achieved high success rates for carrying and pushing boxes, with reasonable object and robot tracking errors. The relatively lower success rate for pushing large boxes was attributed to the absence of marker orientation input, which complicates fine hand adjustments during pushing.
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 3):** Time-lapse sequences illustrated natural whole-body coordination across various HOI tasks (carrying/pushing boxes, carrying a suitcase) and robust locomotion.

#### 4.3. Real-world Evaluation

The complete zero-shot deployment pipeline was validated on a real humanoid robot.

- **Setup:** Seedance 2.0 Fast for video generation, SAM3 for key region segmentation, SpaTrackerV2 for 4D point tracking, and deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot within a motion capture (mocap) system.
- **Zero-Shot Real-World Pipeline (Figure 4):** The robot successfully executed diverse physical tasks, including lifting various boxes of different sizes and weights (e.g., 0.5kg, 1kg, 2kg with a rolling ball inside). Furthermore, the generative capabilities of the video model enabled the robot to perform semantically rich interactive tasks, such as hitting an "Iron Man" pillar. This successfully validated the "video-to-motion-to-real" paradigm proposed by the framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Imagine2Real framework represents a step towards enabling zero-shot Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI) by directly addressing key limitations in existing video-driven robotic control methods. The significance of this work lies in its proposed solutions to critical challenges, leading to several potential impacts:

**Key Contributions:**

1. **Unified 4D Point Trajectory Representation:** The introduction of a unified 4D point trajectory representation for both robot and object motions is a notable contribution. This approach eliminates the dependency on explicit geometric priors (like CAD models) and effectively resolves the representation misalignment issues that arise from separate robot and object motion estimation. This provides a more flexible and robust foundation for interpreting complex interactions from video sources.
2. **BFM-based Keypoints Tracker:** The development of a Keypoints Tracker based on a Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) is significant. By tracking only sparse, interaction-critical points (robot base, hands, and object) within the latent space of a BFM, the framework bypasses the complex and error-amplifying retargeting process typically required for dense joint-level control. Critically, the BFM ensures that natural whole-body gaits and stability are maintained despite the sparse control signals, which has been a persistent challenge for sparse tracking methods.
3. **Progressive Training Framework:** The design of a progressive training strategy, which leverages multi-scale datasets across three cascaded stages (BFM backbone, Keypoints Tracker, Interaction Adaptor), is an effective approach to tackle data scarcity in HOI. This methodology builds foundational priors from abundant non-interactive data before specializing in interaction skills, enabling robust zero-shot HOI without resorting to complex, hand-engineered reward mechanisms or extensive task-specific data.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Generalization and Versatility for Humanoid Robots:** By allowing robots to learn interaction skills directly from generated videos without task-specific training data or explicit geometric models, Imagine2Real can significantly enhance the generalization capabilities of humanoid robots. This could enable deployment in a wider array of unstructured environments (e.g., homes, factories) where objects and tasks are diverse and constantly changing.
- **Reduced Data Requirements:** The framework addresses the critical bottleneck of high-fidelity 3D interaction data scarcity. By leveraging internet-scale video generative models and a progressive training approach, it reduces the need for extensive, manually collected humanoid-specific interaction datasets, making the development of new HOI skills more efficient.
- **Simplified Policy Development:** The use of simple tracking rewards and the inherent stability provided by the BFM-based approach simplifies the policy development process. This could lower the barrier for researchers and engineers to program complex humanoid interaction behaviors.
- **Foundation for Future Humanoid AI Systems:** This work contributes to the "video-to-motion-to-real" paradigm for robotics, offering a pathway for future humanoid AI systems to autonomously interpret and execute complex human demonstrations or instructions from diverse visual sources. The zero-shot capabilities pave the way for more intuitive and flexible human-robot collaboration.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

Despite its advancements, the framework currently has limitations. First, its real-world deployment relies on motion capture (mocap) systems, which are susceptible to marker occlusion during close-contact tasks. Future work could explore integrating on-device self-tracking systems using technologies like SLAM and multi-camera fusion to enhance robustness. Second, the current pipeline operates in an open-loop manner. While iterative prompting of the video model provides a pseudo-closed-loop approximation, it is inefficient. Achieving a true closed-loop system, where the video generator and motion tracker are jointly fine-tuned using physical feedback, remains a substantial challenge, especially given the proprietary nature of many capable video models and the current lack of physical consistency in open-source alternatives. Addressing these limitations could further enhance the autonomy and real-world applicability of zero-shot HOI.
