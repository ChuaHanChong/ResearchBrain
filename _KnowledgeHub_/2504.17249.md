# Demonstrating Berkeley Humanoid Lite: An Open-source, Accessible, and Customizable 3D-printed Humanoid Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> UC Berkeley researchers introduce an open-source, 3D-printed humanoid robot platform that costs under $5,000, featuring custom cycloidal gear actuators and demonstrating successful zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to hardware for locomotion tasks while enabling complex manipulation through teleoperation.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid robots are either expensive proprietary systems or limited research prototypes
- Lack of accessible, customizable platforms for broader robotics research and education

## Method

- Fully open-source hardware and software design using 3D-printed components
- Custom cycloidal gear actuators balancing cost, durability, and performance
- Modular architecture enabling easy reconfiguration and scaling

## Results

- Complete platform costs under $5,000 using standard components
- Successfully demonstrates complex tasks including writing and object manipulation
- Achieves high performance-per-dollar ratio compared to existing platforms
- Enables reconfiguration into various morphologies through modular design

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D-printed actuators remain stable after 60 hours of durability testing
> - Zero-shot policy transfer succeeds while using only 30% of torque capacity
> - Modular design requires only power and CAN communication for actuator integration

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work fits into the broader landscape of humanoid robotics research by directly addressing the need for more accessible and customizable platforms. Existing humanoid robots typically fall into three categories:

- **Commercial, Proprietary Systems:** These robots (e.g., Agility Robotics' Digit, Fourier Intelligence's GR1) are robust and capable, but are expensive and closed-source, limiting research possibilities.
- **Research Lab Designs:** While innovative and high-performing (e.g., Berkeley Humanoid, MIT Humanoid), these robots are usually produced in limited quantities and rely on advanced manufacturing techniques, restricting their accessibility to select institutions.
- **Open-Source Community Projects:** Projects like Poppy Humanoid and OP3 offer more accessibility by utilizing 3D printing and servo motors, but often lack backdrivability and are less suited for mid-to-large scale humanoid platforms.

The Berkeley Humanoid Lite aims to bridge the gap between these categories by providing a cost-effective, open-source platform that balances performance, accessibility, and customizability. The paper acknowledges and builds upon previous work in actuator design (e.g., open-source actuators with planetary gearing, belt-driven actuators, and 3D-printed cycloidal gearboxes) and integrates these concepts into a complete humanoid robot. By open-sourcing the hardware design, embedded code, and training frameworks, the authors hope to encourage broader participation in humanoid robot development and innovation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Addressing Lack of Accessibility:** The primary objective is to address the lack of open-source, cost-effective, and easily reproducible humanoid robot designs within the robotics community.
- **Democratizing Robotics Research:** The motivation is to democratize robotics research by providing a platform that researchers, educators, and hobbyists can readily access, modify, and build upon.
- **Advancing Humanoid Technologies:** By lowering barriers to entry, the project aims to accelerate advancements in fields like reinforcement learning for bipedal locomotion, human-robot interaction, and robotic manipulation.
- **Balancing Affordability, Modularity, and Reliability:** The goal is to design a humanoid platform that balances accessibility, modularity, and long-term reliability, overcoming limitations of existing solutions that often compromise one aspect for another.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Open-Source Design:** The core approach is to create a fully open-source hardware and software stack, including detailed instructions for replication, customization, and improvement.
- **3D-Printed Cycloidal Gear Actuators:** The robot leverages 3D-printed cycloidal gear actuators. This design balances cost-effectiveness with sufficient mechanical robustness, reducing dependency on expensive, proprietary actuators.
- **Modular and Scalable Design:** The platform employs modular and scalable design principles to enhance fabrication accessibility. All structural components can be 3D-printed using standard desktop printers, and all electronic and mechanical parts can be sourced from common online vendors.
- **Reinforcement Learning-Based Locomotion:** Experiments in locomotion and teleoperation are conducted to demonstrate the real-world viability of the platform. A reinforcement learning-based locomotion controller achieves zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to hardware, validating the effectiveness of the actuator design in dynamic tasks.
- **Teleoperation System:** A teleoperation system enables real-time manipulation tasks, further showcasing the robot's adaptability for research and education.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Cost-Effectiveness:** The total hardware cost is kept under $5,000 (based on U.S. market prices), making it significantly more affordable than many commercial humanoid platforms.
- **Durability of Actuators:** Extensive testing of the 3D-printed actuators validates their durability and alleviates concerns about the reliability of plastic components. A 60-hour durability test showed that the efficiency of the cycloidal gears remains stable and any backlash due to wear stays within reasonable limits.
- **Zero-Shot Policy Transfer:** Reinforcement learning-based locomotion experiments successfully demonstrated zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to hardware, highlighting the platform's suitability for research validation. Actuators also remained well within their capacity during tests utilizing only 30% of the torque limit.
- **Performance Factor:** The platform achieves a high performance factor (average peak torque normalized by robot weight and height) compared to other robots, while maintaining a low cost. This is quantified using a performance-per-dollar metric.
- **Teleoperation:** The robot is able to complete tasks, such as writing, unpacking and packing a box, solving a Rubik's Cube, and picking and placing blocks through teleoperation.
- **Modular Design:** The modular actuators require only power and CAN communication for use, allowing the robot to be easily reconfigured into a variety of morphologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Democratization of Humanoid Robotics:** By providing an accessible and customizable platform, the project has the potential to democratize the development of humanoid robotics, encouraging broader participation and innovation.
- **Accelerated Research:** The platform can accelerate research in areas such as reinforcement learning, human-robot interaction, and robotic manipulation by providing a readily available and modifiable testbed.
- **Educational Tool:** The 3D-printed actuator can serve as an effective teaching tool for engineering education, providing students with hands-on experience with motor actuator components and control algorithms.
- **Animatronics Platform:** The robot's modularity and lightweight design make it a promising platform for animatronics and entertainment applications, particularly in settings aimed at young audiences.

### 6. Conclusion

The Berkeley Humanoid Lite project represents a significant step towards making humanoid robotics research more accessible and collaborative. By focusing on open-source design, cost-effectiveness, and modularity, the authors have created a platform that can be readily adopted and adapted by a wide range of users. The successful demonstration of locomotion and teleoperation capabilities, along with the durability testing of the 3D-printed actuators, highlights the potential of this platform to accelerate innovation and broaden participation in the field of humanoid robotics.
