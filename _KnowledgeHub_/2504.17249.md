# Demonstrating Berkeley Humanoid Lite: An Open-source, Accessible, and Customizable 3D-printed Humanoid Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> UC Berkeley researchers introduce an open-source, 3D-printed humanoid robot platform that costs under $5,000, featuring custom cycloidal gear actuators and demonstrating successful zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to hardware for locomotion tasks while enabling complex manipulation through teleoperation.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid robots are either expensive proprietary systems or limited research prototypes
- Lack of accessible, customizable platforms for broader robotics research and education

## Method

- Fully open-source hardware and software design using 3D-printed components
- Custom cycloidal gear actuators balancing cost, durability, and performance
- Modular architecture enabling easy reconfiguration and scaling

## Results

- Complete platform costs under $5,000 using standard components
- Successfully demonstrates complex tasks including writing and object manipulation
- Achieves high performance-per-dollar ratio compared to existing platforms
- Enables reconfiguration into various morphologies through modular design

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D-printed actuators remain stable after 60 hours of durability testing
> - Zero-shot policy transfer succeeds while using only 30% of torque capacity
> - Modular design requires only power and CAN communication for actuator integration
