# Neural-ESO: A Dual-Pathway Architecture for Provably Robust Learning-Based Control

> [!summary] Summary
> An architecture called Neural-ESO integrates a predictive neural network with a corrective Extended State Observer, offering provable robustness and high-performance control for systems facing time-varying disturbances. This approach reduced quadrotor altitude tracking error to 0.37 cm in in-distribution tests and achieved a 53.4% error reduction in severe out-of-distribution scenarios through safe adaptation.

## Problem

- Learning-based control lacks formal stability guarantees and struggles with performance degradation under out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions.
- Traditional control methods like Extended State Observers (ESOs) provide stability but can benefit from incorporating prior knowledge about unknown disturbances for enhanced performance.
- Existing hybrid approaches often rely on simpler learning models, lack principled risk analysis, and do not provide mechanisms for robust adaptation to OOD scenarios.

## Method

- A dual-pathway architecture, **Neural-ESO**, combines a predictive neural network (NN) with a corrective ==Extended State Observer (ESO)==.
- The NN is trained offline using ESO estimates as 'teacher' signals to learn disturbance predictions, with a ==Lipschitz constraint== enforced via ==spectral normalization== to bound its sensitivity.
- Online, the NN provides feedforward predictions, while the ESO actively estimates and compensates for the NN's residual prediction errors, ensuring robustness and stability.

## Results

- Neural-ESO achieved a mean steady-state tracking error of **0.37 cm** on a quadrotor in in-distribution ground effect scenarios, significantly outperforming a nominal PD controller (**10.95 cm**) and a PD+ESO approach (**1.84 cm**).
- Under moderate out-of-distribution conditions (landing on an inclined slope), Neural-ESO maintained stable operation with a mean error of **1.53 cm**, demonstrating robustness where a purely learning-based method degraded.
- For severe OOD high-speed maneuvers, the Total Disturbance Retraining (TDR) mechanism enabled Neural-ESO to achieve an RMSE of **0.89 cm**, representing a **53.4%** reduction in tracking error compared to PD+ESO (**1.91 cm**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning disturbance estimates directly from an ESO provides model-informed predictions, maximizing the neural network's benefit in disturbance rejection without requiring direct access to underlying system dynamics.
> - Enforcing a Lipschitz constraint on the neural network is critical for formally bounding the coupling between learning and control components, enabling Uniform Ultimate Boundedness (UUB) stability guarantees via Lyapunov analysis.
> - The dual-pathway design, combined with a 'Total Disturbance Retraining' (TDR) mechanism, allows safe adaptation and performance improvement in out-of-distribution conditions by continuously updating the neural network with reliably estimated total disturbance data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: achieving high-performance control in the presence of unknown, time-varying disturbances. This challenge is particularly acute for systems deployed in real-world, dynamic environments, where disturbances can arise from model uncertainties, external factors like wind, or complex interactions (e.g., ground effect in aerial vehicles).

The existing research landscape for disturbance rejection control largely bifurcates into two main approaches:
- **Learning-based control:** This paradigm leverages deep neural networks (DNNs) to approximate system dynamics or model discrepancies, often referred to as direct residual learning. While flexible and requiring minimal assumptions on disturbance structure, these methods face several limitations. These include difficulties in providing formal stability guarantees, potential performance degradation or instability under out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions (where test scenarios differ significantly from training data), and instability during training transients before the learned model converges. These limitations hinder their deployment in safety-critical applications.
- **Control theory-based approaches:** Methods such as adaptive control and Extended State Observers (ESOs) offer formal stability guarantees and are widely used for disturbance estimation and rejection. However, these traditional methods can be improved if prior knowledge about the unknown disturbance is incorporated, for example, through model-informed or learning-informed priors. Recent efforts have explored combining ESOs with machine learning models, but these often rely on simpler models (e.g., linear regression, radial basis function networks) and generally lack a principled analysis of the risks introduced by the learning component or mechanisms to adapt when learned models become unreliable under OOD conditions.

This research aims to integrate the strengths of both paradigms by developing a learning-enabled disturbance-rejection framework that leverages neural network-based prediction for performance acceleration while maintaining robustness through a conventional observer-based correction mechanism and providing formal stability guarantees. It seeks to bridge the gap between the empirical success of learning-based methods and the theoretical guarantees of classical control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust learning-based control framework that can effectively reject time-varying unknown disturbances while providing formal stability guarantees and maintaining performance under out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions. The authors specifically address three fundamental research questions (RQs):

- **RQ1: What to learn?** The motivation here is to identify an optimal target for learning that maximizes the benefits of neural networks in disturbance rejection. Instead of directly learning residuals or full dynamics, the paper proposes a "disturbance-centric paradigm" where the neural network approximates Extended State Observer (ESO) estimates. This approach aims to provide faster and more model-informed predictions of disturbances, leveraging the robust estimation capabilities of ESOs as a "teacher" for the learning component.

- **RQ2: How to ensure stability?** A significant limitation of many learning-based control methods is the absence of formal stability guarantees, particularly concerning the interaction between learned components and the overall closed-loop system. The motivation is to overcome this by introducing a mechanism that ensures stability even when the neural network's predictions are imperfect. The paper aims to demonstrate that enforcing a Lipschitz constraint on the neural network, combined with a dual-pathway architecture where a conventional ESO actively compensates for neural network prediction errors, can guarantee closed-loop stability, specifically Uniform Ultimate Boundedness (UUB) of the error dynamics.

- **RQ3: How to generalize under OOD conditions?** Learning-based controllers often suffer from degraded performance or instability when deployed in environments that differ significantly from their training data. The motivation is to enable safe and effective adaptation to novel or unforeseen scenarios. The paper proposes a mechanism for "safe adaptation" by leveraging the combined estimates from both the neural network and the ESO (representing the total disturbance) as a training signal for retraining the neural network in a new domain, thereby improving generalization without compromising immediate operational safety.

In summary, the core motivation is to enhance the performance of disturbance rejection control using the predictive power of neural networks, without sacrificing the safety and robustness traditionally associated with control-theoretic methods, and to enable reliable operation in varied, potentially unknown environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, termed Neural-ESO, utilizes a dual-pathway architecture for learning-enabled disturbance rejection. The methodology integrates a predictive neural network (NN) component with a corrective Extended State Observer (ESO) component.

**A. System Dynamics and Control Law**
The approach considers a general robotic system described by an Euler-Lagrange formulation, which is then transformed into a state-space form suitable for ESO design. The total disturbance `f` is defined as `M^-1 * d(q, ̇q, t)`, encompassing model uncertainties and external disturbances. A nominal control law based on Proportional-Derivative (PD) control is initially defined, which is subsequently modified to actively cancel the estimated total disturbance.

**B. Neural-ESO Architecture**
The Neural-ESO is structured with two main pathways:

1. **Predictive Pathway (Feedforward Neural Network):**
 - **Data Collection with ESO:** In an offline training stage, a conventional ESO (Equation 6) is used as a "teacher." The system is operated under trajectory tracking with a properly tuned ESO, and a dataset is collected. This dataset comprises input vectors `z_k` (generalized position, velocity, and a one-sample delayed control input) and corresponding labels `y_k` (the disturbance estimates `x_3(t_k)` provided by the ESO). This disturbance-centric learning aims to provide model-informed predictions.
 - **Constrained DNN Training with Spectral Normalization:** A neural network `h(z; θ)` is trained to approximate the mapping from `z_k` to `y_k`. A critical aspect is enforcing a Lipschitz bound on the neural network, `||h||_Lip ≤ L_N`. This constraint is applied through spectral normalization (SN) to every weight matrix during the optimization process, which minimizes the prediction error (MSE loss). The Lipschitz constant `L_N` is a tunable parameter that influences the trade-off between model expressiveness and robustness.

2. **Corrective Pathway (Conventional ESO):**
 - The predictive pathway's neural network `f_N = h(z; θ*)` provides a feedforward disturbance estimate. However, a conventional ESO remains active online to estimate and compensate for `Δf = f - f_N`, which represents the residual disturbance or prediction error from the neural network. The Neural-ESO (Equation 7) formulates the total disturbance estimate as `f̂ = f_N + Δf̂`, where `Δf̂` is the ESO's estimate of the residual disturbance. This dual structure ensures that even if the neural network's prediction `f_N` is inaccurate, the ESO can correct the discrepancy, preventing over-reliance on the learning component.

**C. Neural-ESO Based Control Law**
The control law (Equation 9) is modified to incorporate the total estimated disturbance `f̂`, effectively canceling it in the nominal control `u_0`. This integrated estimate directly informs the feedback control action.

**D. Nonlinear Stability Analysis (UUB)**
A core contribution is the formal stability analysis using Lyapunov theory and a small-gain analysis to prove Uniform Ultimate Boundedness (UUB) of the closed-loop error dynamics.
- **Error Dynamics Derivation:**
 - **Tracking Error Dynamics:** The closed-loop dynamics for the tracking error `e = q* - q` are derived. It is shown that the tracking error `e_state = [e^T, ė^T]^T` is driven by the residual estimation error `f̃ = Δf - Δf̂`, establishing an Input-to-State Stable (ISS) subsystem (Equation 12).
 - **Observer Error Dynamics:** The error dynamics for the observer states `ẽ_obs = [x̃_1^T, x̃_2^T, f̃^T]^T` are also derived (Equation 16). This subsystem is driven by the derivative of the residual disturbance `Δḟ`. Due to pole placement, the observer is designed to be Hurwitz, making this subsystem ISS.
- **Coupling Quantification:** The analysis quantifies the coupling between the tracking and observer subsystems. Lemma 1 demonstrates that the residual derivative `Δḟ` is linearly bounded by a function of the tracking and observer error states, with interconnection gains (`γ_e`, `γ_ẽ`) proportional to the neural network's Lipschitz constant `L_N` (Equation 18). This establishes a critical link between the learning component's properties and the system's interconnected dynamics.
- **Composite Stability Proof (UUB):** A composite Lyapunov function `V(E) = V_e(e_state) + V_obs(ẽ_obs)` is constructed. By analyzing its time derivative `V̇`, the authors establish a Lyapunov-based small-gain condition (Equation 33). This condition requires that the intrinsic dissipation of each subsystem (controlled by gains `k_p`, `k_d`, `β_k`) dominates the energy injected through the interconnections. The crucial insight is that the Lipschitz constant `L_N` directly influences these interconnection gains; by bounding `L_N`, the required stabilization gains for the controller and observer are reduced, thus enabling UUB stability with moderate gains. This makes `L_N` a tuning knob for trading learning expressiveness for robustness.

**E. Total Disturbance Retraining (TDR) for OOD Adaptation**
Under Out-of-Distribution (OOD) conditions, where the initial neural network model `f_N` may become unreliable, a "Total Disturbance Retraining" (TDR) mechanism is employed. The combined estimate `f̂ = f_N + Δf̂` (representing the total disturbance in the new domain, safely reconstructed by the dual-pathway architecture) is collected as new training data. This data is then used to retrain the neural network, allowing it to adapt to the new operating conditions and improve performance without compromising stability during data collection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Neural-ESO framework was validated through a series of experiments on a quadrotor UAV, focusing on altitude control under ground effect, which acts as a significant aerodynamic disturbance. Three scenarios were tested: normal landing (in-distribution), landing in an OOD environment with a slope, and OOD high-speed near-ground maneuvers.

**A. Experiment 1: Normal Landing Subject to Ground Effect**
This experiment evaluated the performance in an in-distribution scenario, where training and testing conditions were similar.
- **Quantitative Results:** Compared to a nominal PD controller (10.95 cm mean error), PD+NN (2.49 cm mean error), and PD+ESO (1.84 cm mean error), the proposed Neural-ESO achieved the highest precision with a mean steady-state tracking error of 0.37 cm and a standard deviation of 0.12 cm (Table I).
- **Qualitative Observations:** Neural-ESO demonstrated faster settling times and smoother transient responses than PD+ESO (Figure 3), indicating the benefits of integrating learning for disturbance rejection in familiar environments. The PD+NN method still exhibited a noticeable residual error.

**B. Experiment 2: Landing in OOD Scenario (Inclined Slope)**
This experiment assessed the generalization and robustness of the learned network under conditions where the ground effect characteristics were altered by an inclined slope near the landing zone.
- **Robustness Demonstration:** The PD+NN method showed significantly larger oscillations and higher steady-state errors (mean 3.74 cm, std dev 1.88 cm) compared to its performance in Experiment 1, indicating degraded generalization (Table II, Figure 4). In contrast, the Neural-ESO successfully completed the landing, achieving a mean error of 1.53 cm with a std dev of 2.07 cm. Although its accuracy was lower than PD+ESO (mean 0.66 cm, std dev 0.10 cm) in this specific OOD test, its stability was maintained, demonstrating that the corrective ESO pathway compensated for inaccuracies in the neural prior.

**C. Sensitivity Analysis of the Lipschitz Constant (`L_N`)**
An ablation study on the Lipschitz constant `L_N` (Table III) revealed its role in governing the accuracy-robustness trade-off:
- **Small `L_N` (e.g., 0.2):** Led to underfitting, resulting in poorer in-distribution performance (2.73 cm mean error) but slightly improved OOD robustness (1.69 cm mean error).
- **Optimal `L_N` (e.g., 1.0):** Provided the best in-distribution accuracy (0.37 cm mean error) while maintaining acceptable robustness in OOD conditions (1.53 cm mean error).
- **Large `L_N` (e.g., 2.0):** Caused instability due to excessive sensitivity to noise and extrapolation errors, degrading both in-distribution (1.43 cm mean error) and OOD performance (2.59 cm mean error).
This analysis supports the theoretical finding that `L_N` acts as a tuning parameter for robustness.

**D. Experiment 3: OOD High-Speed Near-Ground Maneuvers**
This experiment challenged the framework with high-speed lemniscate trajectories near the ground, introducing severe non-stationary turbulence, representing an extreme OOD shift.
- **Limitations of Baseline Learning-based Control:** Without Total Disturbance Retraining (TDR), PD+NN exhibited substantial altitude deviations, with tracking error deteriorating to 5.47 ± 0.56 cm, worse than the baseline PD controller (4.08 ± 1.03 cm) (Figures 5, 6). This indicated that an inaccurate learned prior could degrade performance under severe OOD conditions. A single-pathway neural-observer method failed to maintain stability and could not complete the flight.
- **Robust Compensation under Severe Domain Shift:** Neural-ESO without TDR, while underperforming PD+ESO (1.91 ± 0.83 cm), still achieved an RMSE of 4.09 ± 0.21 cm, outperforming PD and PD+NN (w/o TDR). This demonstrated that the corrective ESO pathway continued to compensate for residual disturbances, maintaining stability even when the neural prior was significantly inaccurate due to extreme domain shift.
- **Performance Boosting via Total Disturbance Retraining:** After retraining the neural network using newly collected OOD flight data (TDR), the proposed framework achieved an RMSE of 0.89 ± 0.52 cm. This represented a 53.4% reduction in tracking error compared to PD+ESO (1.91 ± 0.83 cm) (Figure 6). This finding highlights the effectiveness and safety of the TDR mechanism in adapting to new, challenging environments.

Overall, the experiments validate the Neural-ESO's ability to achieve high precision in known environments, maintain stable operation under moderate OOD conditions, and adapt safely and effectively to severe domain shifts through retraining, consistently outperforming comparable baselines in relevant metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the design and deployment of robotic control systems:

- **Provably Robust Learning-Based Control:** A primary significance is the provision of formal stability guarantees (Uniform Ultimate Boundedness) for a learning-enabled control framework. This addresses a critical limitation of many deep learning-based control methods, which often lack theoretical assurances for safety and reliability, particularly in safety-critical applications. The rigorous Lyapunov-based small-gain analysis, which explicitly incorporates the Lipschitz bound of the neural network, provides a principled method for integrating learning components while preserving closed-loop robustness.

- **Dual-Pathway Architecture for Accuracy-Robustness Trade-off:** The proposed dual-pathway design, combining a predictive neural network with a corrective Extended State Observer (ESO), represents a balanced approach. The neural network component leverages data-driven insights to accelerate disturbance estimation and improve tracking accuracy, while the ESO acts as a safety net, compensating for prediction errors and preventing over-reliance on potentially inaccurate learned models. This architecture intrinsically manages the trade-off between performance (accuracy, settling time) and robustness (stability under uncertainty, OOD conditions). The explicit role of the Lipschitz constant `L_N` as a tuning parameter for this trade-off is a valuable practical insight.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Safe Adaptation to OOD Conditions:** The framework's ability to maintain stability and adapt performance under out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios is crucial for real-world robotic deployment. The "Total Disturbance Retraining" (TDR) mechanism allows the system to collect new-domain data safely (due to the active ESO correction) and retrain the neural network, thereby improving generalization and performance in previously unseen or altered environments. This contrasts with purely learning-based methods that often degrade or fail under OOD shifts.

- **Increased Operational Reliability for Safety-Critical Systems:** By combining high-performance learning with provable robustness and OOD adaptation, Neural-ESO contributes to increased operational reliability. This is particularly impactful for safety-critical systems like unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), where unexpected disturbances (e.g., ground effect, wind turbulence) can lead to instability or failure. The demonstrated success on a quadrotor platform navigating challenging ground-effect scenarios validates its practical applicability.

- **Foundation for Future Hybrid Control Systems:** This work provides a foundation for designing more sophisticated hybrid control systems that systematically integrate data-driven learning with model-based control theory. The approach of learning observer estimates rather than direct residuals offers a promising direction for future research in robust and adaptive control, encouraging further exploration of how to optimally blend the predictive power of machine learning with the guarantees of classical control.

In summary, Neural-ESO represents an advancement in creating intelligent, yet reliably operating, autonomous systems by offering a framework for learning-based control that is both high-performing and provably robust, even when faced with significant environmental changes.
