# MetaFold: Language-Guided Multi-Category Garment Folding Framework via Trajectory Generation and Foundation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> MetaFold provides a framework for language-guided multi-category garment folding in robotics by disentangling high-level trajectory generation from low-level action prediction. The system achieved up to 94% success rates in real-world experiments and outperformed existing baselines in handling diverse garments and language-conditioned tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable objects like garments faces challenges due to their high-dimensional state space and complex dynamics.
- Existing garment folding methods often lack generalization across diverse garment categories and struggle to adapt to varied user instructions, frequently relying on structural representations or specific demonstrations.
- Current robotic systems struggle to effectively bridge abstract, high-level task planning (understanding the goal) with concrete, low-level action execution for complex deformable object tasks.

## Method

- **MetaFold** introduces a disentangled framework separating language-guided point cloud trajectory generation for high-level planning from low-level action prediction using a fine-tuned ManiFoundation model.
- A new large-scale dataset, MetaFold, was created using a differentiable simulator to generate point cloud trajectories and language descriptions for various garment folding tasks.
- The framework incorporates a closed-loop control system that continuously acquires the garment's current state and iteratively updates planning and action execution, enhancing robustness to environmental disturbances.

## Results

- MetaFold consistently outperformed baseline methods like UniGarmentManip and GPT-Fabric, achieving superior rectangularity, area ratio, and success rates (e.g., **0.88** success rate for No-sleeve garments on MetaFold dataset) across various garment categories.
- The framework demonstrated robust language understanding and generalization capabilities, achieving higher success rates (e.g., **0.63** for 'unseen' instructions vs. **0.46** for baseline) in language-guided folding tasks.
- Real-world experiments using an xArm6 robot showed strong practical applicability, with Rectangularity between **0.85**-**0.94** and Success Rates ranging from 8/10 to 10/10 across different garment types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling high-level task planning (trajectory generation) from low-level action prediction significantly enhances robustness and generalization in robotic manipulation of deformable objects.
> - Utilizing complete point cloud trajectories for planning, rather than sparse keypoint representations, provides a more comprehensive spatial and temporal understanding crucial for precise garment deformation.
> - Integrating a large language model (LLaMA) for semantic feature extraction enables effective interpretation of diverse user instructions and open-vocabulary generalization for complex folding tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects, such as garments, presents a significant challenge in robotics due to their high-dimensional state space and complex dynamics. This difficulty contrasts with the intuitive ease with which humans perform tasks like clothes folding. The broader research landscape in this area has explored various strategies, including visual perception, dynamic control, and reinforcement learning, often focusing on identifying optimal grasping locations or aligning garments into predefined configurations.

Existing methods frequently rely on structural representations, such as key points or skeletal models, which limits their adaptability across diverse garment categories. Some approaches employ demonstration-based learning or heuristic policies, which restrict their generalization capabilities to specific object configurations or tasks. For instance, UniGarmentManip and Cloth Funnels have addressed category-level garment manipulation or canonicalized alignment, respectively. More recent work, like AdaFold, has explored model-based feedback loops for optimizing folding trajectories.

The integration of language understanding into robotic manipulation has also gained attention, with models leveraging large language models (LLMs) to interpret commands and guide actions, as seen in GPT-Fabric for simple fabric tasks or methods using graph dynamics for deformable objects. However, these often struggle with fine-grained control or rely on predefined key points, failing to account for intricate intermediate garment states.

Foundation models have shown promise in robotics, primarily for rigid or articulated objects. While some models, like ManiFoundation, have demonstrated capabilities in handling deformable objects by synthesizing contact points, they typically focus on low-level action prediction rather than high-level task planning for complex, multi-stage deformations.

This work addresses several gaps in this landscape. It proposes a disentangled framework inspired by human cognition, separating high-level task planning (future state prediction) from low-level action execution. By utilizing point cloud trajectories instead of structural representations, it aims to achieve broader generalization across garment categories and adapt to diverse language instructions without prior category-specific knowledge. Furthermore, it integrates a low-level action prediction foundation model with a high-level language-guided trajectory generation model to bridge the gap between abstract task understanding and concrete robotic actions, while also explicitly accounting for intermediate states through a closed-loop control mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified and generalizable framework, termed MetaFold, for language-guided multi-category garment folding in robotic manipulation. This framework aims to enable robots to fold a diverse range of garments according to varying user instructions with precision and adaptability.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent challenges in manipulating deformable objects:
- **Complex Dynamics**: Garments exhibit diverse forms of deformation, leading to a vast state space and complex dynamics. This complexity makes it difficult for robots to predict future configurations, plan precise manipulation points, and control force and positioning accurately.
- **Limited Generalization**: Many existing robotic manipulation methods are restricted to specific object configurations or garment categories, often relying on prior knowledge or extensive demonstrations. This limits their adaptability to new garment types or varied folding tasks.
- **Bridging High-level and Low-level Control**: Human manipulation of deformable objects effectively separates high-level task planning (understanding the goal) from low-level control (executing movements). Current robotic systems often struggle to replicate this disentanglement, leading to less robust or generalizable solutions.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of MetaFold are:
- To introduce a novel framework that disentangles task planning from action prediction. This hierarchical separation is intended to simplify the learning process by allowing each component to focus on its specific complexities, thereby enhancing model generalization.
- To propose MetaFold as an integrated framework that leverages language-guided point cloud trajectory generation for high-level planning and a low-level foundation model (ManiFoundation) for detailed action prediction, facilitating multi-category garment folding.
- To construct a large-scale, dedicated dataset of garment folding point cloud trajectories, each annotated with corresponding language descriptions, to support the training and evaluation of the proposed models.
- To implement a closed-loop control system that allows for continuous feedback and adjustment based on the garment's current state, thereby enhancing the robustness and precision of the manipulation process.
- To demonstrate, through extensive experimentation, the superiority of the proposed framework in terms of folding accuracy and generalization across various garment types and language instructions compared to existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MetaFold is designed as a unified, disentangled framework that separates high-level task planning from low-level action prediction for garment folding. The system operates in a closed-loop manner, integrating data generation, model training, and iterative manipulation.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The task is defined as generating a sequence of actions to fold a garment into a target point cloud configuration (P$_{goal}$), given its initial 3D point cloud (P) and language guidance (L). This is broken down into three sub-tasks:
1. **Point Cloud Trajectory Generation**: Given P and L, generate a trajectory T = {P$_i$}$_M$ representing the evolution of the point cloud over M frames.
2. **Action Prediction**: Given two successive point clouds (P, P') from T, the ManiFoundation model predicts an action 'a', defined as a set of contact syntheses (contact position 'p' and motion direction 's').
3. **Closed-Loop Manipulation**: A robot executes action 'a', manipulating the garment to a new configuration (P*). This process is iterated until P matches P$_{goal}$.

**B. Language-Guided Trajectory Generation**

1. **Data Generation**:
 - To train the point cloud trajectory generation model, a new dataset called MetaFold dataset was constructed.
 - Garment models from ClothesNetM were used as initial shapes.
 - The differentiable simulator DiffClothAI was employed to generate point cloud trajectories for garment folding.
 - A heuristic approach defined grasping and target points, guiding the simulator to move the grasping point along a trajectory.
 - Mesh vertices were extracted as point clouds and downsampled.
 - Language descriptions corresponding to different garment types and folding actions were added.
 - The MetaFold dataset comprises 3376 trajectories for 1210 garments, with 2664 trajectories for training and 712 for testing.

2. **Conditional Point Cloud Trajectory Generation**:
 - A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) serves as the core trajectory generation model, with Transformer encoder-decoder architectures.
 - **Input Processing**:
 - PointNet++ extracts spatial features (F$_P$ ∈ R$^{N \times 128}$) from the input point cloud (P ∈ R$^{N \times 3}$).
 - A pre-trained LLaMA (Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) processes the language description (L) to generate semantic features (R$^{1 \times 4096}$). An MLP reduces this to F$_L$ ∈ R$^{1 \times 128}$.
 - **CVAE Operation**: During training, F$_P$, F$_L$, and the ground-truth trajectory are used as conditional inputs to the CVAE encoder to optimize the latent variable 'z'. For both training and inference, 'z' is sampled, combined with F$_P$, and F$_L$ acts as a conditional input to the CVAE decoder to generate trajectory features (F$_T$). An MLP then converts F$_T$ into the generated trajectory (T ∈ R$^{M \times N \times 3}$).
 - **Design Rationale**: The model generates one folding stage at a time, simplifying the process and improving generalization. It is trained jointly across multiple garment categories to learn shared and distinct characteristics, enabling cross-category adaptability.

3. **Semantic Generalization**:
 - The pre-trained LLaMA model extracts semantic features from user-provided language guidance.
 - Predefined garment folding descriptions strengthen the encoding process.
 - During inference, LLaMA matches user input to these predefined descriptions, enabling open-vocabulary generalization by constraining conditions within a defined range.

**C. Closed-Loop Manipulation**
The framework employs a closed-loop strategy for robust garment folding.

1. **Point Cloud Acquisition**:
 - In simulation, point clouds are obtained by extracting mesh vertices.
 - In the real world, an RGB-D camera captures RGB images and depth maps. The Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM 2) is used to segment the garment, and its mask filters depth data to obtain the garment point cloud. This point cloud is then downsampled.
 - Users can specify folding methods via language descriptions.

2. **ManiFoundation Model**:
 - The trajectory generation model provides successive point clouds, creating a "flow."
 - The ManiFoundation model takes this point cloud flow as input and predicts contact syntheses (robot actions).
 - It was fine-tuned on a subset of garment data, using a loss function comparing predicted contact syntheses to ground truth.
 - An ensemble approach using 160 random seeds mitigates prediction variability. Predictions are grouped, and the arithmetic mean of point positions within the modal group is used to select the final contact point and force direction.

3. **Feedback Control**:
 - The robot executes the action predicted by ManiFoundation.
 - After a short interval, the garment reaches a new state. Its current point cloud is captured and fed back into the trajectory generation model to predict the subsequent trajectory.
 - This iterative feedback loop allows the framework to adapt to environmental disturbances and variations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in Isaac Sim, providing a simulation environment with accurate fabric dynamics. The evaluation utilized both the custom-generated MetaFold dataset and the CLOTH3D dataset, with approximately 500 different garments tested in total.

**A. Metrics**
- **Rectangularity (↑)**: Ratio of the final garment area to its bounding rectangle, measuring the quality of the folded shape.
- **Area Ratio (↓)**: Ratio of the final garment area to the initial garment area, indicating compactness of the fold.
- **Success Rate (↑)**: Percentage of trials where rectangularity exceeded a specified threshold AND area ratio fell below a certain threshold.

**B. Baselines**
- **Garment Folding (Q1)**: UniGarmentManip (demonstration-based SOTA) and GPT-Fabric (LLM-based keypoint selection).
- **End-to-End Comparison (Q2)**: 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) (direct action prediction from point cloud).
- **Language Guidance (Q3)**: Deng et al. (graph dynamics for language-conditioned manipulation, previous SOTA).

**C. Simulation Results and Analysis**

1. **Garment Folding (Q1 & Q2)**:
 - **Table I** presents the comparative results. MetaFold generally outperformed UniGarmentManip and GPT-Fabric across various garment categories (No-sleeve, Short-sleeve, Long-sleeve, Pants) on both the MetaFold and CLOTH3D datasets.
 - For instance, on the MetaFold dataset, MetaFold achieved a Rectangularity of 0.87 for No-sleeve garments compared to UniGarmentManip's 0.85 and GPT-Fabric's 0.78. Its success rates were consistently higher, e.g., 0.88 for No-sleeve compared to UniGarmentManip's 0.71 and GPT-Fabric's 0.34.
 - MetaFold demonstrated strong generalization on the CLOTH3D dataset in a zero-shot setting, matching or surpassing baseline performance.
 - Comparing with the end-to-end method DP3, MetaFold exhibited superior overall performance, particularly in Success Rate. This supports the hypothesis that disentangling task planning from action prediction leads to more robust and precise manipulation.

2. **Language-Guided Garment Folding (Q3)**:
 - **Table II** compares MetaFold with on language-guided tasks, using both "Seen" (training-encountered) and "Unseen" (novel) instructions.
 - MetaFold achieved higher Rectangularity and Success Rates, and lower Area Ratios, under both instruction types on both datasets. For "Unseen" instructions on the MetaFold dataset, MetaFold had a Success Rate of 0.63 compared to's 0.46.
 - The model demonstrated strong language understanding and generalization capabilities, successfully guiding folding sequences based on user inputs even for novel folding paths (e.g., "Bottom → Left → Right," as illustrated in **Figure 5**).

**D. Ablation Studies**
**Table III** details the ablation study results:
- **Ours w/o MF (without ManiFoundation)**: Showed significantly reduced performance (Success Rate 0.27 vs 0.86), indicating the ManiFoundation model's criticality for accurate contact point and force direction prediction.
- **Ours w/o CL (without Closed-Loop control)**: Exhibited a substantial drop in performance (Success Rate 0.07 vs 0.86, Area Ratio 0.60 vs 0.33), highlighting the necessity of feedback control for adapting to variations and disturbances.
- **Ours-5frames and Ours-15frames**: Demonstrated that the frequency of closed-loop execution impacts performance, with optimal results achieved when executed every 10 frames (the default "Ours" setting).
- **Ours-NextStep (single-step prediction)**: Performed worse than predicting an entire trajectory, suggesting that generating full trajectories provides more effective action sequences and planning.

**E. Real-World Experiments**
- Experiments were conducted using a uFactory xArm6 robot with an xArm Gripper and an overhead RealSense D435 camera.
- SAM2 was used for real-time garment point cloud acquisition from RGB-D data.
- The framework transferred directly to real-world settings due to a minimal sim-to-real gap in point cloud representation.
- **Table IV** and **Figure 6** present qualitative and quantitative real-world results, with 10 trials per garment type.
- High Rectangularity (0.85–0.94), low Area Ratio (0.24–0.45), and high Success Rates (8/10 to 10/10) were observed across Long-Sleeve, Short-Sleeve, No-Sleeve, and Pants garments, confirming the framework's practical applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MetaFold represents an advancement in robotic manipulation of deformable objects, specifically garments, through its disentangled framework and language-guided capabilities.

**Key significance points include:**
- **Modular and Generalizable Framework**: The core contribution lies in the disentanglement of high-level task planning (trajectory generation) from low-level action prediction. This modular design simplifies training, enhances model generalization across diverse garment categories, and allows the system to adapt flexibly to varying user instructions.
- **Novel Trajectory-Based Planning**: By generating complete point cloud trajectories instead of relying on limited keypoint representations or demonstrations, MetaFold provides a comprehensive spatial and temporal understanding of garment deformation, which is crucial for precise manipulation.
- **Multi-Category and Language-Guided Capabilities**: The framework successfully handles multi-category garment folding guided by natural language instructions, including "unseen" commands. This extends the utility of robotic systems beyond pre-programmed tasks to user-defined, semantic guidance.
- **Data Contribution**: The creation of the large-scale MetaFold dataset, comprising point cloud trajectories and language annotations for various garments and folding methods, provides a valuable resource for future research in deformable object manipulation and language-conditioned robotics.
- **Robustness through Closed-Loop Control**: The integration of a closed-loop feedback mechanism ensures continuous adaptation to environmental variations and execution errors, contributing to the robustness and reliability of the garment folding process in both simulation and real-world scenarios.
- **Practical Applicability**: The successful transfer of the framework from simulation to real-world robotic hardware, demonstrated by high success rates across different garment types, indicates its potential for practical applications.

**Potential impacts include:**
- **Advancing Robotic Manipulation**: MetaFold contributes to overcoming long-standing challenges in deformable object manipulation, particularly for textiles, by offering a principled approach to managing their complex dynamics.
- **Enhancing Human-Robot Interaction**: The language-guided nature of the framework improves human-robot collaboration, allowing users to intuitively instruct robots on complex tasks without specialized programming.
- **Automation in Various Sectors**: The technology has potential implications for automating tasks in industries such as apparel manufacturing, laundry services, logistics, and domestic robotics, where efficient and adaptable garment handling is required.
- **Foundational Research**: The methodology, particularly the disentangled architecture and trajectory-based planning, may serve as a foundation for future research in spatial intelligence and complex manipulation tasks involving other deformable materials.
