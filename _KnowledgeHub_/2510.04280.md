# A KL-regularization Framework for Learning to Plan with Adaptive Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Policy Optimization–Model Predictive Control (PO-MPC), a framework that unifies and extends MPPI-based reinforcement learning by reformulating policy learning as a KL-regularized problem with an adaptive prior. The approach demonstrates improved performance and sample efficiency in high-dimensional continuous control tasks by mitigating policy mismatch and reducing gradient variance through a learned intermediate prior.

## Problem

- A distribution mismatch often occurs between the learned sampling policy and the online planner's action distribution in MPPI-based RL, leading to unreliable value function estimates and degraded performance.
- Existing policy alignment strategies either lack direct constraints to the planner's distribution or rely on stale, high-variance planner data from replay buffers, hindering stable and efficient learning.
- Current methods typically employ a fixed regularization strength for policy alignment, which limits adaptability across tasks and can lead to premature convergence or insufficient exploration.

## Method

- The PO-==MPC== framework formulates sampling policy learning as a KL-regularized RL problem, maximizing expected return while penalizing the reverse KL divergence between the sampling policy and an adaptive prior policy.
- A key innovation is the introduction of a separate learned intermediate policy prior (π_θ_p), trained to approximate the online MPPI planner's distribution, thereby providing a stable and unimodal target for the sampling policy.
- The framework allows for flexible training objectives for the intermediate prior (e.g., forward or reverse KL divergence), enabling distinct inductive properties to guide the sampling policy towards either broader exploration or precise mode-matching.

## Results

- PO-MPC consistently matched or outperformed TD-MPC2 and BMPC across DeepMind Control Suite tasks, demonstrating significant performance gains in higher-dimensional HumanoidBench locomotion tasks.
- Intermediate λ values (e.g., λ=1 or λ=9) proved critical, enabling successful learning and convergence in several high-dimensional HumanoidBench environments where baseline methods failed.
- The choice of prior training objective demonstrated distinct effects: forward KL divergence enhanced exploration and accelerated convergence in tasks requiring broader action spaces, while reverse KL divergence facilitated precise behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - PO-MPC provides a unifying framework that shows existing state-of-the-art MPPI-based RL methods, such as TD-MPC2 and BMPC, as specific limiting cases of the proposed approach.
> - Intermediate KL-regularization strengths (λ), rather than extreme values, yield more robust performance and enable learning in challenging high-dimensional environments where baseline methods struggle.
> - Utilizing a learned intermediate policy prior significantly reduces the gradient variance in policy updates, which typically arises from using potentially outdated and multi-modal planner samples directly from the replay buffer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL), specifically focusing on planning-enhanced approaches that integrate learned policies with trajectory optimization. A key area of interest is Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, a sample-based method for solving stochastic optimal control problems.

Traditional MBRL approaches, such as TD-MPC and its successor TD-MPC2, leverage a learned world model and a learned policy to guide an MPPI planner. The planner refines actions, and the collected data is then used to update the policy and value function. However, a known limitation, termed "policy mismatch," arises because the sampling policy (which generates actions for the planner) and the planning distribution itself can diverge. This discrepancy leads to unreliable bootstrap estimates from the value function, particularly over short planning horizons, which can hinder long-term performance.

Prior attempts to address policy mismatch include explicitly aligning the learned policy with the planner's action distribution. Methods like Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control (BMPC) achieve this by minimizing the KL divergence between the learned policy and the planner's distribution. While these approaches aim to reduce mismatch, they face challenges:
1. **Fixed KL Penalty:** The regularization strength might be static, potentially limiting the policy's adaptability or causing premature convergence.
2. **High-Variance Targets:** Relying on planner samples stored in a replay buffer means the policy updates are based on potentially outdated planner statistics. This mixture of different planner versions can create a high-variance, multi-modal target distribution, making learning less stable.

This paper positions itself as a unifying framework, Policy Optimization–Model Predictive Control (PO-MPC), that encompasses and extends these existing MPPI-based methods. It frames policy learning as a KL-regularized reinforcement learning problem, where the planner's action distribution serves as an adaptive prior. By providing a common mathematical formulation, PO-MPC aims to clarify the underlying commonalities between disparate methods, organize design choices, and enable the systematic exploration of previously unstudied variants to overcome the aforementioned limitations. It connects to broader concepts in RL such as trust-region methods, expectation-maximization updates, and the perspective of RL as probabilistic inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the challenges in effective exploration and sample efficiency within MPPI-based MBRL, particularly in high-dimensional continuous control tasks. The core problem identified is the "policy mismatch" between the learned sampling policy and the online planner's action distribution.

**Detailed Problem Statement:**
- **Distribution Mismatch:** In MPPI-based RL, the learned sampling policy (π_θ_s) provides actions for trajectory generation and the bootstrap value function evaluates these trajectories. If the states and actions explored by the planner significantly deviate from those expected by the learned policy, the value function estimates become unreliable. This leads to poor value function learning and, consequently, degraded MPPI performance.
- **Limitations of Current Alignment Strategies:**
 - **Independent Updates (e.g., TD-MPC2):** Early methods update the sampling policy using standard policy gradients (e.g., deterministic policy gradients with entropy regularization) without direct constraint to the planner's distribution. This decoupling exacerbates the policy mismatch problem as the policy's local optima may not align with the planner's findings.
 - **Direct KL Alignment (e.g., BMPC):** More recent methods attempt to align the policy by minimizing KL divergence to the planner's distribution. However, they typically rely on planner data from the replay buffer. This data can be "stale," reflecting older versions of the planner's capabilities and potentially introducing high variance into the policy updates, as the aggregated data might form a multi-modal distribution instead of a stable, unimodal target.
 - **Fixed Regularization:** Existing approaches may use a fixed strength for policy alignment, which might not be optimal across different tasks or learning stages, potentially leading to premature convergence or insufficient exploration.

### 3. Key Objectives of the Research

1. **Unification and Generalization:** To introduce Policy Optimization–Model Predictive Control (PO-MPC) as a unifying framework for existing MPPI-based MBRL methods. This involves showing how current state-of-the-art algorithms emerge as special cases within this framework, thus clarifying the design space.
2. **Adaptive KL-Regularization:** To reformulate the sampling policy learning as a KL-regularized RL problem. The objective is to maximize the expected return while regularizing the sampling policy (π_θ_s) against an MPPI-induced prior (π_p), with the regularization strength controlled by a tunable hyperparameter, λ.
3. **Novel Algorithmic Variants:** To systematically explore new configurations within the PO-MPC framework, particularly by adjusting the KL-regularization strength (λ) beyond the extreme values (λ=0 or λ→∞) typically found in existing methods.
4. **Variance Reduction through Adaptive Priors:** To propose and evaluate the use of a *learned intermediate policy prior* (π_θ_p) to approximate the computationally expensive MPPI planner's distribution. This objective aims to shield the sampling policy from the high variance introduced by outdated planner samples stored in the replay buffer.
5. **Flexible Prior Training Objectives:** To demonstrate that alternative loss functions for training this intermediate prior (e.g., reverse KL vs. forward KL divergence) can embed distinct inductive properties into the resulting sampling policy, offering flexibility in guiding exploration and convergence.
6. **Empirical Validation:** To empirically demonstrate that the novel variants and intermediate regularization strengths within PO-MPC can yield performance improvements, including better sample efficiency and final performance, particularly in high-dimensional continuous control benchmarks.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The PO-MPC framework builds upon the architecture and principles of TD-MPC2, employing a learned world model, but introduces a refined approach to policy optimization for the sampling policy. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**A. Foundation in MPPI-based Reinforcement Learning:**
- **World Model:** Similar to TD-MPC2, PO-MPC utilizes a learned world model consisting of a state encoder (h_θ_h), a latent dynamics model (p_θ_d), and a reward function model (r_θ_r). The agent plans and learns within this learned latent representation space.
- **MPPI Planning (Section B.1):** At each environment step, the current state is encoded. MPPI then samples `M` noisy action sequences over a finite horizon `H`. These samples are a blend of actions from the learned sampling policy (π_θ_s) and a Gaussian planning policy. Each simulated trajectory in the world model is evaluated by an `H`-step estimated return, which includes a learned bootstrap action-value function (Q_πθs_θQ) for returns beyond the horizon. The nominal control sequence (mean `¯a` and standard deviation `σ` for each time step) is iteratively refined using a path-integral (desirability transform) based on the top-K performing samples. The first action of the final, refined distribution is executed in the environment.
- **Replay Buffer:** Transition information `(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_t+1, ¯a_t, σ_t)` is stored in a replay buffer. The `¯a_t` and `σ_t` represent the mean and standard deviation of the MPPI-derived planning policy (π_P) at that time step.
- **Lazy Reanalyze:** To mitigate the issue of stale planner data in the replay buffer, a subset of stored planning policies (`¯a`, `σ`) are periodically re-analyzed and updated using the current learned policy and value function.

**B. Sampling Policy Learning via KL-Regularized RL (Section 4.1, B.3):**
- **Objective Function:** The core of PO-MPC is to learn the sampling policy (π_θ_s) by maximizing a KL-regularized expected return. The objective (Equation 5) combines the standard reward maximization with a penalty for the reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the learned sampling policy and an adaptive prior policy (π_p). The regularization strength is controlled by the hyperparameter λ.
 `J(π_θ_s) = E[Σ (r(z_t, a_t) - λ KL[π_θ_s(·|z_t) || π_p(·|z_t)])]`
- **KL-Regularized Action-Value Function:** To optimize this objective, a specific KL-regularized action-value function (Q_πθs,λ_θQ) is introduced (Equation 7). Its Bellman equation incorporates the KL term:
 `Q_πθs,λ_θQ(z_t, a_t) = E[r(z_t, a_t) + γ (Q_πθs,λ_θQ(z_t+1, a) - λ log(π_θ_s(a|z_t+1)/π_p(a|z_t+1)))]`
 This action-value function is learned by minimizing its TD-error (Equation 16).
- **Policy Loss:** The sampling policy (π_θ_s) is updated to maximize its expected Q-value (from Q_πθs,λ_θQ), minimize the KL divergence to the prior (π_p), and maximize its entropy for exploration (Equation 18). Adaptive scaling parameters (S_KL, S_Q) are applied to the KL and Q-value terms to balance their magnitudes.

**C. Adaptive Prior Policy Design (Section 4.1, B.2):**
- **Intermediate Learned Prior (π_θ_p):** A key innovation is the introduction of a separate learned policy (π_θ_p) that acts as an intermediate prior for the sampling policy. This prior is explicitly trained to approximate the online planner's distribution (π_P) derived from MPPI.
- **Prior Training Objectives:** The intermediate prior can be trained using different KL divergence objectives:
 - **Reverse KL Minimization (Equation 8):** `J(θ_p) = E[(s,π_P)∼D [KL[π_θ_p(·|z_t) || π_P(·|z_t)]]]`. This objective trains π_θ_p to cover the modes of the planner's distribution, making it suitable for tasks requiring precise actions.
 - **Forward KL Minimization (Equation 9):** `J(θ_p) = E[(s,π_P)∼D [KL[π_P(·|z_t) || π_θ_p(·|z_t)]]]`. This objective trains π_θ_p to cover the support of the planner's distribution, promoting broader exploration.
 - An adaptive scale parameter (S_p) is used for these losses.
- **Benefits:** This learned prior provides a stable, unimodal target for the sampling policy, reducing the variance that would arise from directly using the potentially outdated and aggregated multi-modal samples from the replay buffer.

**D. Relationship to Prior Works:**
- **TD-MPC2:** This method is recovered when λ = 0, meaning the policy update focuses solely on maximizing the Q-value (and entropy regularization) without direct KL regularization towards the planner.
- **BMPC:** This method is recovered when λ → ∞ (meaning only the KL-divergence is optimized) and the prior is trained using reverse KL divergence.

**E. Experimental Setup (Section 5, Appendix A):**
- **Environments:** Evaluation was performed on 7 tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite (Humanoid and Dog domains) and 14 higher-dimensional tasks from the HumanoidBench locomotion suite.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against TD-MPC2 and BMPC (specifically, its strongest variant).
- **PO-MPC Variants:** Experiments tested various values for λ (0.1, 1, 9), the impact of using the learned intermediate prior (vs. raw replay buffer planner samples), and the choice between forward and reverse KL for training the intermediate prior.
- **Metrics:** Interquartile Mean (IQM) and 95% bootstrap confidence intervals of the final average return over 5 runs were used for evaluation. Learning curves were also provided.
- **Implementation:** The method builds on JAX implementations of TD-MPC2 and BMPC, inheriting their architectural choices and hyperparameter settings where applicable.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the DeepMind Control Suite and HumanoidBench locomotion suite yielded several findings regarding the performance and characteristics of the PO-MPC framework.

**A. Effect of KL-Regularization Strength (λ):**
- **Performance Across Tasks:** Regulating the proximity between the sampling and planning policies through λ led to performance improvements. PO-MPC with intermediate λ values matched or slightly outperformed baselines in lower-dimensional DMControl tasks and showed significant gains in higher-dimensional HumanoidBench tasks.
- **Intermediate λ Values:** Values such as λ=1 and λ=9 often outperformed both extreme cases (λ=0 corresponding to TD-MPC2, and λ→∞ corresponding to BMPC). In several high-dimensional environments (e.g., Balance S., Crawl, Pole, Run, Slide, Stair, and Walk in HumanoidBench), intermediate λ values enabled learning where baselines struggled or failed to converge to a solution.
- **Degradation at Extremes:** Performance tended to degrade when λ was very low (e.g., λ ≤ 0.1), indicating that insufficient regularization led to policy mismatch. Similarly, performance could decline when λ was very high (approaching infinity, as in BMPC), suggesting that overly strong regularization could cause premature mode collapse and hinder necessary exploration.
- **General Recommendation:** λ=1 demonstrated robust performance across most tasks, with larger λ values sometimes proving beneficial in particularly challenging high-dimensional environments.

**B. Impact of the Learned Intermediate Policy Prior (π_θ_p):**
- **Variance Reduction:** The introduction of a learned intermediate policy prior (π_θ_p) as a proxy for the planner's policy was found to be beneficial. On average, across HumanoidBench tasks, using this learned prior matched or surpassed the performance achieved by directly using planner samples from the replay buffer (Figure 2).
- **Theoretical Justification:** Theoretical analysis (Appendix G) and empirical evidence (Figure 7) suggest that this improvement stems from a reduction in gradient variance during policy updates. The learned intermediate prior provides a more stable, unimodal target, shielding the sampling policy from the noise and multi-modality inherent in outdated planner samples stored in the replay buffer.

**C. Influence of Prior Training Objective (Forward vs. Reverse KL):**
- **Inductive Properties:** The choice of loss function for training the intermediate policy prior (π_θ_p) was observed to embed distinct inductive properties into the resulting sampling policy (Figure 3).
- **Forward KL Divergence:** When π_θ_p was trained to minimize forward KL divergence (KL[π_P || π_θ_p]), it encouraged the prior to encompass the support of all sampled planning distributions. This approach generally enhanced exploration and could accelerate convergence in tasks requiring broad exploration (e.g., Stair), especially at lower λ values.
- **Reverse KL Divergence:** When π_θ_p was trained to minimize reverse KL divergence (KL[π_θ_p || π_P]), it biased the prior towards matching one of the modes of the planning policy distribution. This could accelerate convergence in tasks requiring precise, deterministic behavior (e.g., Balance S.) but might limit exploration in other settings.

**D. Overall Performance and Unification:**
- PO-MPC consistently achieved competitive or superior results compared to TD-MPC2 and BMPC, particularly in high-dimensional continuous control tasks.
- The framework successfully demonstrated that existing state-of-the-art methods are specific limiting cases of PO-MPC (TD-MPC2 as λ=0; BMPC as λ→∞ with reverse-KL distillation).

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of model-based reinforcement learning, particularly for continuous control tasks.

**A. Principled Unification of MPPI-based RL:**
- PO-MPC provides a novel, unifying framework that integrates various MPPI-based RL methods under a single theoretical umbrella of KL-regularized RL. This clarifies the relationships between existing state-of-the-art approaches (like TD-MPC2 and BMPC), which are shown to be special cases of PO-MPC. This unification helps researchers understand the design trade-offs and commonalities, fostering systematic exploration of the algorithmic design space.

**B. Enhanced Performance and Sample Efficiency:**
- The empirical results demonstrate that PO-MPC, especially with adaptively tuned KL-regularization strength (λ) and a learned intermediate prior, yields notable performance improvements. It consistently matches or surpasses baselines, with significant gains observed in high-dimensional and challenging control tasks. This advancement directly addresses the critical need for more sample-efficient and robust learning in complex environments.

**C. Mitigation of Key MBRL Challenges:**
- **Policy Mismatch:** The framework effectively tackles the policy mismatch problem by explicitly incorporating KL-regularization into the policy update. This ensures better alignment between the learned sampling policy and the planner's distribution, leading to more reliable value function estimates and improved performance.
- **Variance Reduction:** The introduction of a learned intermediate policy prior is a methodological advancement that significantly reduces the variance associated with using stale and potentially multi-modal planner samples from the replay buffer. This contributes to more stable and faster learning.
- **Guided Exploration:** The flexible control over regularization strength (λ) and the choice of prior training objective (forward vs. reverse KL) provide mechanisms for guided exploration. This allows tailoring the policy's behavior to task requirements, enabling it to either exploit known high-reward regions or explore broader action spaces more effectively.

**D. Future Research Directions:**
- The work opens several avenues for future research, including investigating more expressive policy distributions beyond Gaussian approximations, developing automated methods for tuning the λ hyperparameter (similar to adaptive entropy in SAC), and exploring strategies to more efficiently leverage simulated planning data for value function learning.

In summary, PO-MPC advances the state-of-the-art in MPPI-based reinforcement learning by offering a more robust, flexible, and unified approach. Its ability to improve performance and stability in high-dimensional continuous control tasks has implications for various applications requiring autonomous decision-making, such as robotics, where sample efficiency and reliable learning are paramount.
