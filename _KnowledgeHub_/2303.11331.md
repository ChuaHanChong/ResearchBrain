# EVA-02: A Visual Representation for Neon Genesis

> [!summary] Summary
> EVA-02 introduces a Transformer-based visual representation that achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse computer vision tasks while using moderate model sizes and exclusively publicly available training data. The model reached 90.0% ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy with 304M parameters, and EVA-02-CLIP-L achieved 80.4% zero-shot ImageNet-1K accuracy, outperforming larger open-sourced models.

## Problem

- The growing disparity between state-of-the-art large-scale models and their accessibility to the wider research community due to immense computational resources and reliance on private data.
- The high cost and limited transparency associated with training and fine-tuning multi-billion parameter models, hindering widespread experimentation and verification.
- The underestimated potential of smaller-sized plain Vision Transformers in achieving top-tier performance on diverse computer vision tasks.

## Method

- Developed a next-generation Transformer-based visual representation by incorporating architectural refinements from language models (e.g., ==SwiGLU FFN==, Sub-Layer Normalization, ==2D Rotary Position Embedding==) into plain Vision Transformers.
- Utilized a ==Masked Image Modeling (MIM) pre-training== strategy where models reconstruct strong, language-aligned vision features from a publicly accessible giant EVA-CLIP vision encoder, rather than raw pixels.
- Conducted extensive pre-training exclusively on publicly accessible datasets, such as ImageNet-21K and Merged-38M (a composite of IN-21K, CC12M, COCO, etc.), across various model scales.

## Results

- EVA-02-L (**304M parameters**) achieved **90.0%** fine-tuning top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, surpassing several larger and more resource-intensive models.
- EVA-02-CLIP-L reached **80.4%** zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, outperforming the previous largest open-sourced CLIP model (OpenCLIP-Giant, ~1.8B parameters) with approximately **1/6th** of the parameters and training data.
- Demonstrated superior transfer learning by achieving competitive and often state-of-the-art results on downstream tasks like object detection (COCO, LVIS) and semantic segmentation (COCO-Stuff-164K, ADE20K), including new SOTA on LVIS (**65.2 APbox** & **57.3 APmask**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Moderate-sized Vision Transformers, when robustly optimized, can achieve state-of-the-art performance with significantly fewer parameters and compute, challenging the 'bigger is always better' paradigm.
> - Architectural advancements originally explored in large language models, such as SwiGLU FFN and Rotary Position Embeddings, effectively transfer to vision Transformers, yielding performance improvements.
> - Pre-training via Masked Image Modeling (MIM) by regressing features from a strong, publicly accessible giant CLIP encoder is an effective strategy for learning highly transferable visual representations.
