# EVA-02: A Visual Representation for Neon Genesis

> [!summary] Summary
> EVA-02 introduces a Transformer-based visual representation that achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse computer vision tasks while using moderate model sizes and exclusively publicly available training data. The model reached 90.0% ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy with 304M parameters, and EVA-02-CLIP-L achieved 80.4% zero-shot ImageNet-1K accuracy, outperforming larger open-sourced models.

## Problem

- The growing disparity between state-of-the-art large-scale models and their accessibility to the wider research community due to immense computational resources and reliance on private data.
- The high cost and limited transparency associated with training and fine-tuning multi-billion parameter models, hindering widespread experimentation and verification.
- The underestimated potential of smaller-sized plain Vision Transformers in achieving top-tier performance on diverse computer vision tasks.

## Method

- Developed a next-generation Transformer-based visual representation by incorporating architectural refinements from language models (e.g., ==SwiGLU FFN==, Sub-Layer Normalization, ==2D Rotary Position Embedding==) into plain Vision Transformers.
- Utilized a ==Masked Image Modeling (MIM) pre-training== strategy where models reconstruct strong, language-aligned vision features from a publicly accessible giant EVA-CLIP vision encoder, rather than raw pixels.
- Conducted extensive pre-training exclusively on publicly accessible datasets, such as ImageNet-21K and Merged-38M (a composite of IN-21K, CC12M, COCO, etc.), across various model scales.

## Results

- EVA-02-L (**304M parameters**) achieved **90.0%** fine-tuning top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, surpassing several larger and more resource-intensive models.
- EVA-02-CLIP-L reached **80.4%** zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, outperforming the previous largest open-sourced CLIP model (OpenCLIP-Giant, ~1.8B parameters) with approximately **1/6th** of the parameters and training data.
- Demonstrated superior transfer learning by achieving competitive and often state-of-the-art results on downstream tasks like object detection (COCO, LVIS) and semantic segmentation (COCO-Stuff-164K, ADE20K), including new SOTA on LVIS (**65.2 APbox** & **57.3 APmask**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Moderate-sized Vision Transformers, when robustly optimized, can achieve state-of-the-art performance with significantly fewer parameters and compute, challenging the 'bigger is always better' paradigm.
> - Architectural advancements originally explored in large language models, such as SwiGLU FFN and Rotary Position Embeddings, effectively transfer to vision Transformers, yielding performance improvements.
> - Pre-training via Masked Image Modeling (MIM) by regressing features from a strong, publicly accessible giant CLIP encoder is an effective strategy for learning highly transferable visual representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself directly within the rapidly evolving landscape of large-scale vision and vision-language representation learning, which has seen a surge of interest in scaling up models, data, and compute budgets. This trend is largely driven by the empirical observation that increasing these factors often leads to improved performance in deep learning models, particularly Transformers.

However, the authors identify a critical and growing challenge within this landscape: the increasing disparity between state-of-the-art large-scale models and their accessibility to the wider research community. Many top-performing models require immense computational resources for training, fine-tuning, and evaluation, often relying on prohibitively expensive infrastructure and web-scale private training data. This lack of accessibility limits transparency, hinders independent verification of results, and restricts participation from researchers without access to such resources.

EVA-02 directly addresses this accessibility gap. Instead of solely chasing ever-larger models, the core contribution of this work is to demonstrate that state-of-the-art performance can be achieved with "moderate model sizes" and "significantly fewer parameters and compute budgets," all while utilizing *exclusively publicly accessible training data*. This is a crucial distinction from much of the contemporary large model research, which often leverages proprietary datasets or colossal computational budgets beyond the reach of most.

The paper is part of a broader movement towards making advanced AI models more democratic and widely usable. It contributes to the understanding that architectural refinements and optimized pre-training strategies can yield competitive results even at smaller scales, rather than solely relying on brute-force scaling. This positions EVA-02 as a practical and democratizing step in the field of computer vision, aiming to provide a "robust and effective recipe for making state-of-the-art models more affordable in practice." It stands in contrast to approaches that necessitate billions of parameters or extensive, private data collections, thereby fostering a more equitable research environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "EVA-02" project is to introduce a "next-generation Transformer-based visual representation" that can achieve robust, state-of-the-art performance across diverse visual tasks while maintaining moderate model sizes and significantly reduced computational requirements.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the identified limitations and challenges within the current landscape of large-scale visual representation learning:

- **Addressing the Accessibility Gap:** The authors explicitly state their motivation to bridge the growing gap between cutting-edge, resource-intensive models and what is practically affordable and accessible for the broader research and practitioner community. Training and evaluating multi-billion-parameter models demand enormous computational power and time, often leading to limited experimentation and optimization.
- **Democratizing State-of-the-Art:** By developing EVA-02 variants with fewer parameters and lower compute budgets, the authors aim to "democratize access to state-of-the-art vision models," enabling a wider range of researchers to conduct high-quality research without the need for extensive private infrastructure or resources. This aligns with a broader push for open science and equitable access to advanced AI tools.
- **Leveraging Publicly Accessible Data:** A significant motivation is to achieve top-tier performance using *exclusively publicly accessible training data*. This commitment enhances transparency, reproducibility, and fairness, making the research more verifiable and broadly applicable.
- **Undertapping Potential of Smaller ViTs:** The empirical investigation led the authors to conclude that "the smaller-sized plain ViTs are highly capable, and their potential has been significantly underestimated." This motivates a focused effort on optimizing these more compact architectures to unlock their full performance.
- **Developing a Robust and Effective Recipe:** Rather than introducing a fundamentally novel method, the core objective is to synthesize existing architectural advancements and pre-training strategies into a "robust and effective recipe" that can consistently yield state-of-the-art results at an affordable scale. This includes exploring architectural improvements from language models and a sophisticated masked image modeling (MIM) approach.
- **Fostering Efficient and Equitable Research:** Ultimately, the work is motivated by a desire to "enable a broader range of the research community to advance the field in a more efficient and equitable manner," providing a pathway to high-impact research without prohibitive overheads.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of EVA-02 is centered around developing robustly optimized plain Vision Transformers (ViTs) that learn transferable bidirectional visual representations. This is achieved through a two-pronged approach: architectural refinements borrowed from language models and a sophisticated Masked Image Modeling (MIM) pre-training strategy.

#### 3.1. Architectural Improvements (Transform Vision - TrV)

The authors build upon the plain ViT architecture, which is favored for its minimal visual structure prior and natural compatibility with masked modeling. They incorporate several micro-architectural advancements previously explored in large language models but less thoroughly in vision:

- **SwiGLU Feedforward Network (FFN):** They adopt the SwiGLU FFN, which uses a Gated Linear Unit (GLU) with SiLU activation. To maintain parameter and FLOP consistency with the baseline MLP, the FFN hidden dimension is reduced by a factor of 2/3. Empirical studies showed SwiGLU performed well when combined with a specific weight initialization.
- **Sub-Layer Normalization (Sub-LN):** This normalization scheme is utilized, with the inner attention Layer Normalization (LN) being deemed unnecessary and removed.
- **2D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE):** RoPE is incorporated for injecting positional information. Unlike conventional relative position embeddings, RoPE manipulates query (q) and key (k) vectors, making it compatible with high-performance multi-head self-attention (MHSA) operators.
- **Xavier Normal Weight Initialization:** All weights in the TrV blocks are initialized using Xavier normal initialization, which was found crucial for the performance of SwiGLU FFN.

These architectural modifications, collectively forming the "Transform Vision" (TrV) block, resulted in an overall improvement of 1.6 points in ImageNet-1K fine-tuning accuracy compared to baseline ViT configurations, demonstrating the efficacy of applying language model architectural insights to vision.

#### 3.2. Pre-training Strategy

The core of EVA-02's pre-training strategy is Masked Image Modeling (MIM), which reconstructs masked-out image content based on visible patches. The unique aspect here is the choice of the MIM target:

- **Target Representation from Giant CLIP Encoder:** Instead of reconstructing raw pixels or discrete visual tokens, EVA-02 is pre-trained to regress strong and robust *language-aligned vision features* from a *publicly accessible giant EVA-CLIP vision encoder* (a 1-billion-parameter model). The rationale is that a larger, more robust CLIP model provides more transferable target representations for MIM, leading to better pre-trained student models. Negative cosine similarity is used as the loss function.
- **Masking Scheme:** Block-wise masking with a 40% masking ratio is employed, consistent with prior MIM approaches.
- **Pre-training Data:**
 - Smaller models (EVA-02-Ti, -S, -B): Trained on ImageNet-21K (IN-21K), a publicly available dataset of 14.2 million images.
 - Largest model (EVA-02-L): Trained on "Merged-38M," a composite dataset comprising IN-21K, CC12M, CC3M, COCO, ADE20K, Object365, and OpenImages. All components of this 38-million-image dataset are publicly accessible.
- **Extensive Pre-training:** The authors found that a stronger teacher (EVA-CLIP) requires more extensive pre-training for the student model to fully master its knowledge. Extending the training schedule (e.g., from 300 to 1600 epochs for base-sized models) yielded significant performance gains.
- **Scaling:** Beyond architecture and pre-training duration, the approach also involves scaling model size and input resolution, and optionally incorporating intermediate fine-tuning with labels (e.g., on IN-21K) to further boost performance.

#### 3.3. Implementation and Efficiency

The pre-training code is built upon the open-sourced EVA implementation. It leverages DeepSpeed with ZeRO stage-0/-1 optimizer and fp16 precision for memory and compute efficiency, and xFormers for accelerated MHSA operations. Remarkably, this setup results in approximately 10% shorter wall-clock pre-training time compared to official BEiT series implementations, despite using a 1-billion-parameter teacher.

Four model variants are released: EVA-02-Ti (6M), -S (22M), -B (86M), and -L (304M), covering a range of model sizes suitable for various applications and resource constraints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EVA-02 demonstrates exceptional performance across a broad spectrum of computer vision tasks, often surpassing larger and more resource-intensive models.

#### 4.1. Image Classification (ImageNet-1K and Variants)

- **State-of-the-Art with Fewer Parameters:** EVA-02-L (304M parameters) achieves a phenomenal 90.0% fine-tuning top-1 accuracy on the ImageNet-1K validation set. This is a significant result as it outperforms several existing state-of-the-art models that utilize substantially more parameters (e.g., EVA at 1011M, InternImage-H at ~1080M, BEiT-3 at ~1900M) and sometimes private data or more extensive labeled data. Notably, it even surpasses its own 1011M-parameter EVA-CLIP teacher, highlighting that effective MIM can yield student models superior to their teachers in certain contexts.
- **Competitive Base-Sized Models:** EVA-02-B (86M parameters) achieves 88.6% top-1 accuracy, matching the performance of a ViT-B model distilled from a massive 4B-parameter teacher trained on proprietary JFT-4B data.
- **Strong Light-Weight Performance:** Contrary to the common belief that plain ViTs perform poorly in light-weight settings, EVA-02-Ti (6M) achieves 80.7% and EVA-02-S (22M) achieves 85.8% on IN-1K, outperforming several specialized lightweight networks with stronger inductive biases.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** EVA-02 models exhibit excellent robustness and generalization capabilities on various ImageNet-1K variants (e.g., ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-Adversarial, ObjectNet). EVA-02-L achieves the highest averaged accuracy and the smallest performance gap across these variants, indicating superior generalization without specialized fine-tuning. The models are also robust to different numerical precisions and optimizers during fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (EVA-02-CLIP)

- **Zero-Shot Classification Excellence:** By initializing the vision encoder of CLIP with MIM pre-trained EVA-02 representations, EVA-02-CLIP achieves remarkable zero-shot performance. EVA-02-CLIP-L (304M parameters) reaches 80.4% zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, outperforming the previous largest and best open-sourced CLIP model (OpenCLIP-Giant, ~1.8B parameters) with approximately 1/6th of its parameters and 1/6th of the image-text training data.
- **Broad Zero-Shot Transferability:** EVA-02-CLIP also demonstrates strong performance across 26 additional zero-shot classification benchmarks, with EVA-02-CLIP-L showing a non-trivial 1.2-point average improvement over OpenCLIP-H. It is also effective in zero-shot video classification.
- **Competitive Zero-Shot Retrieval:** While not as pronounced as in classification, EVA-02-CLIP achieves competitive results in zero-shot image and text retrieval on Flickr30K and COCO.

#### 4.3. Object Detection and Instance Segmentation (COCO, LVIS)

- **Superior Transfer Learning:** EVA-02 models consistently outperform same-sized and larger counterparts when used as backbones for object detection and instance segmentation tasks (e.g., with Cascade Mask R-CNN and ViTDet configurations). This is particularly evident on LVIS, a challenging large-vocabulary dataset.
- **SOTA with O365 Intermediate Fine-tuning:** With additional intermediate fine-tuning on Object365 (O365) detection annotations, EVA-02-L achieves highly competitive results on COCO and sets new state-of-the-art on LVIS (65.2 APbox & 57.3 APmask), even against methods with significantly larger models.
- **Minor Data Contamination Impact:** A case study showed that the inclusion of unlabeled images from development/test sets in MIM pre-training had a minor impact on final detection and segmentation performance, suggesting robustness against such contamination issues.

#### 4.4. Semantic Segmentation (COCO-Stuff-164K, ADE20K)

- **Strong Pure MIM Performance:** Pure MIM pre-trained EVA-02 models, without additional IN-21K intermediate fine-tuning, significantly outperform same-sized BEiTv2 models with UperNet segmenter.
- **New State-of-the-Art:** When combined with Mask2Former, EVA-02-L achieves new state-of-the-art results on both COCO-Stuff-164K (53.7 mIoU) and ADE20K (61.7 mIoU), demonstrating its effectiveness for pixel-level prediction tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of EVA-02 are multifaceted, extending beyond mere performance metrics to influence the broader dynamics of AI research:

- **Democratization of State-of-the-Art AI:** This is arguably the most profound impact. By demonstrating that top-tier performance can be achieved with "moderate model sizes" and publicly accessible data, EVA-02 lowers the entry barrier for high-quality research in computer vision. It enables researchers and practitioners globally, who lack access to supercomputing clusters or proprietary datasets, to participate in and contribute to the advancement of visual AI. This can accelerate innovation by fostering a more inclusive and diverse research community.

- **Revisiting Scaling Laws and Model Design:** EVA-02 challenges the prevailing "bigger is always better" narrative by proving that robust optimization and intelligent design choices (like borrowing from language model architectures and leveraging a strong, accessible teacher in MIM) can yield superior results more efficiently. It suggests that the potential of smaller, plain Vision Transformers has been "significantly underestimated," shifting focus towards smarter scaling and architectural efficiency rather than just raw parameter count.

- **Highly Transferable and Robust Representations:** The consistent state-of-the-art performance across a diverse range of downstream tasks (classification, detection, segmentation, and zero-shot capabilities) underscores the exceptional quality, transferability, and robustness of the visual representations learned by EVA-02. This makes it a highly versatile foundation model for numerous real-world applications.

- **Advancing Vision-Language Understanding:** The remarkable zero-shot performance of EVA-02-CLIP, outperforming much larger and more data-intensive open-sourced CLIP models, highlights the immense potential of language-aligned vision features. EVA-02-CLIP's ability to act as a "basic building block" for future multi-modal systems is significant, as it promises more robust and flexible AI capable of understanding and generating content across modalities.

- **A "Robust and Effective Recipe" for Future Research:** The paper explicitly positions itself as providing a "robust and effective recipe" rather than just a novel method. This detailed recipe, encompassing specific architectural modifications and a refined MIM pre-training strategy, offers clear guidance for future research efforts aiming to develop high-performance, resource-efficient vision models.

- **Commitment to Open Science:** The release of the complete suite of EVA-02 variants to the community underscores a strong commitment to open science. This facilitates reproducibility, allows for deeper analysis by other researchers, and serves as a direct contribution to the collective knowledge base, accelerating collaborative progress in the field.

- **Bootstrapped Learning Paradigm:** The proposed concept of "alternate training of the pure MIM visual representations as well as vision-language CLIP representations... in a bootstrapped manner" (Figure 3) points towards a powerful and scalable future research direction. This cyclical improvement between vision-only and vision-language pre-training could unlock new efficiencies and capabilities for developing even more powerful and integrated AI models.

In conclusion, EVA-02 represents a significant step towards making state-of-the-art visual AI more accessible and efficient. It provides a compelling argument for the underestimated potential of moderately sized models when robustly optimized and smartly pre-trained, thereby democratizing access to powerful AI and shaping the future direction of foundational model research.
