# Training-Free Reasoning and Reflection in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Wuhan University developed FRANK, a training-free approach that integrates reasoning and reflection capabilities into Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by strategically merging a vision-pretrained MLLM with a reasoning-specialized LLM. This method, which uses a novel layer-wise fusion guided by observed functional specialization, achieves state-of-the-art performance on various multimodal reasoning benchmarks, including outperforming GPT-4o on MMMU (69.2 vs 69.1) while preserving visual perception.

## Problem

- Endowing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with sophisticated, multi-step reasoning capabilities, similar to advanced text-only LLMs.
- The prohibitive computational costs and the scarcity of high-quality, verifiable multimodal reasoning datasets required for retraining or fine-tuning MLLMs for reasoning tasks.
- Existing MLLMs, despite strong perceptual abilities, often lack complex logical inference for tasks like visual math or interpreting diagrams.

## Method

- FRANK, a ==training-free approach==, merges a visual-pretrained MLLM with a reasoning-specialized LLM by combining their ==task vectors==.
- It employs a novel ==layer-wise fusion mechanism== that leverages the observed functional specialization within MLLM decoders (shallow for perception, deep for reasoning).
- An ==attention-guided exponential decay function== is used to derive modality priors, enabling an optimal closed-form solution for per-layer fusion weights that balance visual grounding and symbolic reasoning without additional training or data.

## Results

- FRANK-38B achieves state-of-the-art results on multimodal reasoning benchmarks, scoring **69.2** on MMMU (outperforming GPT-4o at 69.1), **56.8** on MMMU-Pro (+**8.8** vs. InternVL2.5-38B), and significant gains on MathVision (+**7.5**) and WeMath (+**8.7**).
- The method successfully preserves fundamental visual perception capabilities, with only slight drops on the MME benchmark while sometimes enhancing commonsense reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of layer-wise fusion and the attention-guided modality prior for optimal performance, and qualitative analysis reveals intrinsic self-correction and adaptive reasoning depth through generated reflection tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit layer-wise functional specialization, with shallow decoder layers primarily handling visual perception and deeper layers focusing on textual semantics and higher-level reasoning.
> - Capabilities from different fine-tuned models (e.g., vision-specific and reasoning-specific) can be effectively integrated via homologous model merging, viewing them as task vector additions from a common base.
> - Optimal per-layer fusion weights for combining visual and reasoning task vectors can be derived in a closed-form solution using Taylor approximation and by leveraging the empirical orthogonality of task vectors, eliminating the need for additional data or training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in complex reasoning tasks, exemplified by models like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI-o1, which often leverage reinforcement learning (RL) for multi-step problem-solving and self-correction. Simultaneously, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have emerged, integrating visual perception with language understanding to tackle tasks like visual question answering and image captioning.

However, a significant gap exists between these two domains: endowing MLLMs with sophisticated *reasoning* capabilities akin to powerful text-only LLMs presents formidable challenges. Current approaches often involve adapting computationally intensive RL techniques to MLLMs, which incurs prohibitive retraining costs and demands high-quality, verifiable multimodal reasoning datasets—data that is currently scarce. Previous attempts to integrate reasoning into MLLMs, such as LLaVA-Reasoner or plan-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (CCoT, TextCoT), often yield limited gains or require extensive fine-tuning. Similarly, recent RL-based MLLM reasoning works (e.g., MM-Eureka, Vision-R1, LMM-R1) still face the fundamental issues of high training costs and data dependency.

This paper positions itself as a critical advancement in this landscape by proposing a **training-free** solution. Instead of costly retraining, FRANK leverages the paradigm of **model merging**, a technique that efficiently combines capabilities from different fine-tuned models into a single one without accessing original training data. While model merging techniques like Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, and MetaGPT have been successful for text-only LLMs, or have used sub-optimal fixed fusion weights for multimodal settings (e.g., VLM-Merging), they typically do not account for the unique hierarchical structure and functional specialization within MLLMs.

FRANK innovates by specifically adapting model merging for MLLMs. It integrates two key insights:
1. **Homologous Model Merging:** It views pre-trained MLLMs and reasoning-specialized LLMs as variants fine-tuned from the same base LLM, allowing the application of task arithmetic principles.
2. **Layer-wise Functional Specialization in MLLMs:** It observes and explicitly leverages the empirical finding that shallow decoder layers in MLLMs primarily handle visual perception, while deeper layers focus on textual semantics and higher-level reasoning.

By combining these insights, FRANK offers a method that circumvents the major limitations of current MLLM reasoning development (cost and data scarcity). It distinguishes itself by providing a principled, layer-wise fusion mechanism that is both training-free and attuned to the intrinsic functional hierarchy of multimodal models, thereby significantly advancing the practical development of reasoning-capable MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **endow existing, off-the-shelf Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with advanced reasoning and reflection abilities without the need for any gradient updates, additional training, or extra supervision.**

The motivation for this ambitious objective stems from several critical observations and challenges in the current landscape of large models:

- **Growing Need for Multimodal Reasoning:** Real-world applications frequently demand capabilities that integrate visual understanding with complex logical inference. Examples include solving visual mathematics problems, interpreting intricate diagrams, and comprehending code snippets embedded within images. Existing MLLMs, despite their strong perceptual abilities, often fall short in these multi-step reasoning tasks.
- **Success of Reasoning-Focused LLMs:** Recent advancements in text-only LLMs, such as DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI-o1, have demonstrated remarkable prowess in complex logical reasoning, math, and program synthesis, often through reinforcement learning and iterative self-correction. This success inspires the desire to replicate similar capabilities in multimodal contexts.
- **Prohibitive Costs of Retraining MLLMs:** Extending reinforcement learning or other sophisticated training paradigms to large-scale MLLMs is computationally extremely expensive, making it impractical for most research groups and even many large organizations. The sheer size of these models makes full retraining resource-intensive and time-consuming.
- **Scarcity of High-Quality Multimodal Reasoning Datasets:** Effective training for reasoning capabilities, especially via methods like reinforcement learning, heavily relies on large volumes of diverse, high-quality, and verifiable datasets. For multimodal reasoning, such datasets are notably scarce, posing a significant bottleneck to development and scalability.

In summary, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between powerful unimodal reasoning and multimodal understanding, while simultaneously addressing the practical constraints of computational resources and data availability. Their objective is to create an efficient, accessible, and high-performing solution for integrating complex reasoning into MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FRANK (training-FRee ANd r1-liKe MLLM) introduces a novel, training-free approach that enhances MLLMs' reasoning and reflection abilities by strategically merging a visual-pretrained MLLM with a reasoning-specialized LLM. The core of FRANK lies in two key insights and a sophisticated layer-wise fusion mechanism.

**Core Insights:**

1. **Homologous Model Merging:** The method conceptualizes both the vision-fine-tuned MLLM and the reasoning-specialized LLM as variants of the *same base LLM* that have been fine-tuned on different tasks. Leveraging the "task arithmetic" hypothesis, which posits that the difference in weights (task vectors) between a fine-tuned model and its base model encapsulates task-specific adaptations, FRANK proposes to combine these task vectors to integrate multiple capabilities.
2. **Layer-wise Functional Specialization in MLLMs:** Drawing an analogy to hierarchical processing in the human brain, the authors observe a similar pattern in MLLM decoders. Empirical analysis (Figure 2) shows that **shallow decoder layers** allocate significantly more attention to visual tokens, focusing on perceptual grounding. Conversely, **deeper decoder layers** concentrate on textual semantics, indicating a specialization in higher-order cognitive functions and symbolic reasoning. This observation is crucial for guiding the merging process.

**Methodological Approach – FRANK's Mechanism:**

Based on these insights, FRANK employs a hierarchical weight merging strategy with fine-grained control:

1. **Layer-Wise Fusion Setup:** The MLLM decoder, comprising multiple transformer layers, is treated as a series of independently mergeable units. For each layer $l$, task vectors ($\tau^{(l)}_V$ for vision, $\tau^{(l)}_R$ for reasoning) are computed as the difference between the layer's parameters in the fine-tuned model and the base model.
 - The fusion process aims to create new, combined layer parameters ($\theta^{(l)}_f$) by linearly combining the base model parameters ($\theta^{(l)}_0$) with the weighted task vectors: $\theta^{(l)}_f = \theta^{(l)}_0 + \lambda_V \tau^{(l)}_V + \lambda_R \tau^{(l)}_R$.
 - The objective is to minimize the **Layer-Wise Average Loss Difference (LALD)**, which averages the "degradation" (quantified by Layer-Wise Task Loss Difference, LTLD) when using fused weights instead of the original fine-tuned weights for both visual and reasoning tasks.

2. **Taylor-Based Approximation and Closed-Form Fusion Weights:** To achieve efficient, data-agnostic fusion weights, FRANK approximates the LALD using a second-order Taylor expansion. This approximation relies on two critical assumptions, empirically validated:
 - **NTK Linearization:** In wide neural networks (like LLMs), small weight perturbations result in locally linear changes in model output, and the Hessian of the loss function can be approximated as an isotropic matrix ($\delta^{(l)}_t I_d$). This implies that the first-order Taylor term vanishes near convergence, leaving only the quadratic term.
 - **Task-Vector Orthogonality:** The task vectors for vision fine-tuning ($\tau^{(l)}_V$) and reasoning fine-tuning ($\tau^{(l)}_R$) are observed to be nearly orthogonal (cosine similarity close to zero across layers, as shown in Figure 3). This property significantly simplifies the mathematical derivation by minimizing cross-interference terms.
 - Under these assumptions, the authors derive a **closed-form solution** for the optimal per-layer fusion weights: $\lambda^{(l)}_t = \frac{\|\tau^{(l)}_t\|^2}{\|\tau^{(l)}_V\|^2 + \|\tau^{(l)}_R\|^2}$. This elegant solution means the fusion weights depend solely on the squared magnitudes of the task vectors, eliminating the need for held-out data, grid search, or additional training.

3. **Incorporating Modality Priors:** To further align with the observed functional hierarchy and optimize the balance between visual grounding and symbolic reasoning, FRANK introduces layer-dependent modality priors ($w^{(l)}_V, w^{(l)}_R$). These priors modify the fusion objective, leading to a refined closed-form solution: $\lambda^{(l)}_t = \frac{w^{(l)}_t \|\tau^{(l)}_b\|^2}{w^{(l)}_V \|\tau^{(l)}_V\|^2 + w^{(l)}_R \|\tau^{(l)}_R\|^2}$.
 - **Attention-Guided Decay Priors:** Crucially, these modality priors are not manually set. Instead, they are derived by analyzing the layer-wise visual attention patterns within the MLLM (how much attention text tokens give to visual tokens). An **exponential decay function** is fitted to these observed attention weights across layers. This data-driven, yet label-free, approach ensures that shallow layers (high visual attention) are weighted more towards preserving visual grounding, while deeper layers (low visual attention) are weighted more towards integrating reasoning capabilities. This mirrors the natural shift from perception to cognition observed in models.

In essence, FRANK performs a precise, layer-by-layer intelligent weight merge, guided by an understanding of how MLLMs process multimodal information, all without any gradient-based optimization during the merging process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments on challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of FRANK across different model scales.

**Quantitative Performance on Multimodal Reasoning Benchmarks:**

- **Superior Performance:** FRANK consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including strong proprietary models like GPT-4o, on a suite of five multimodal reasoning benchmarks: MMMU val, MMMU-Pro standard, MathVista testmini, MathVision testmini, and WeMath testmini.
 - **MMMU:** FRANK-38B achieves an accuracy of 69.2, surpassing InternVL2.5-38B by +5.3 points and even outperforming GPT-4o (69.1).
 - **MMMU-Pro:** FRANK-38B scores 56.8, a significant +8.8 points over InternVL2.5-38B.
 - **Math Benchmarks:** On MathVista, FRANK-38B achieves 73.1 (+1.2 vs. InternVL2.5), 39.7 on MathVision (+7.5), and 47.0 on WeMath (+8.7).
- **Scaling Law:** The results clearly indicate a positive scaling trend. Larger FRANK variants (FRANK-8B, FRANK-15B, FRANK-38B) show progressively higher performance, demonstrating that increased model capacity enables better absorption and utilization of the injected reasoning capabilities. While smaller FRANK models show some underperformance on MathVista compared to their baselines, larger models mitigate this, indicating that the benefits of fusion are fully realized with sufficient capacity.
- **Architecture Agnostic:** Consistent improvements were observed across both LLaMA-based (FRANK-8B) and Qwen-based (FRANK-15B, FRANK-38B) architectures, highlighting the generalizability of the layer-wise fusion method across different backbone models.

**Preservation of Visual Perception:**

- Evaluations on the MME benchmark, which assesses basic visual perception, showed that FRANK-15B (with the Modality Prior) incurs only very slight drops in perceptual performance compared to its non-reasoning vision-only baseline (NVIL-15B). This confirms that the method effectively integrates reasoning without significantly degrading the model's fundamental visual grounding abilities.
- Notably, in sub-tasks requiring commonsense reasoning, FRANK-15B even slightly surpassed NVIL-15B (85.0 vs 82.9), suggesting that the infused reasoning can sometimes complement and enhance perceptual understanding.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Effectiveness of Model Merging:** The ablation study on MMMU demonstrated that model merging successfully injects reasoning. Traditional merging baselines (VLM-Merging, Task Arithmetic, MetaGPT) showed modest gains.
- **Importance of Layer-Wise Fusion:** FRANK-15B without the Modality Prior (layer-only fusion) outperformed all traditional merging methods, validating the benefit of the closed-form, per-layer merging.
- **Crucial Role of Modality Prior:** The full FRANK-15B, incorporating the attention-guided exponential decay modality prior, achieved the highest scores (61.3% on MMMU). This significant additional gain (2.9 points over layer-only fusion) underscores that the learned prior is essential for optimally balancing visual grounding and symbolic reasoning across layers. Ablations with fixed/linear decay priors further confirmed the superiority of the attention-guided approach.

**Reflection Frequency and Output Length Analysis:**

- **Intrinsic Self-Correction:** A key qualitative finding is FRANK’s ability to perform self-correction. Unlike the non-reasoning baseline (NVIL-15B) which produced zero reflection tokens, FRANK-15B generated numerous reflection tokens (e.g., "Wait," "Hmm," "Mistake," "Alternatively," "Check") in its responses. This indicates that the fusion method intrinsically enables iterative self-correction during the reasoning process.
- **Adaptive Reasoning Depth:** Output length analysis on MMMU showed that FRANK models generate longer responses for harder tasks, demonstrating an inference scaling capability where reasoning depth adapts to problem difficulty. Larger models also naturally produced longer responses, further indicating their capacity to elaborate on complex reasoning.

**Case Studies:**

- Qualitative examples vividly illustrate the superiority of FRANK. While non-reasoning baselines often provide direct, incorrect answers, FRANK models (across all scales) produce detailed, step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning with explicit reflection tokens, leading to correct solutions. This visually confirms the enhanced reasoning and self-correction abilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of FRANK represents a significant step forward in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with profound implications for research and practical applications:

1. **Cost-Effective Multimodal Reasoning:** The most impactful contribution of FRANK is its **training-free** nature. By eliminating the need for extensive retraining, gradient updates, or additional supervision, FRANK drastically reduces the computational resources and time required to imbue MLLMs with reasoning capabilities. This democratizes access to advanced multimodal AI, making it feasible for researchers and developers with limited resources to deploy and experiment with highly capable models. It directly addresses the bottleneck of prohibitive retraining costs and the scarcity of high-quality multimodal reasoning datasets.

2. **Enhanced Model Capabilities:** FRANK effectively bridges the gap between powerful text-only reasoning LLMs and visually capable MLLMs. Its ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance on challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks, even surpassing proprietary models like GPT-4o in some cases, demonstrates its potent capacity to imbue complex logical and reflective reasoning while preserving crucial visual grounding.

3. **Increased Interpretability:** By explicitly leveraging the observed layer-wise functional specialization (shallow layers for perception, deep layers for reasoning) and deriving fusion weights through a Taylor-derived closed-form solution, FRANK offers a more interpretable mechanism for combining model capabilities. This contrasts with end-to-end fine-tuning, which often acts as a black box. Understanding how capabilities are integrated at different architectural depths can inform future model design.

4. **Scalability and Generalizability:** The method's effectiveness across different model sizes (8B, 15B, 38B) and different base architectures (LLaMA-based, Qwen-based) highlights its scalability and broad applicability. This suggests that FRANK could be a general solution for integrating new capabilities into a wide range of existing large pre-trained models.

5. **Practical Tool for AI Development:** FRANK provides a practical, efficient, and robust framework for developing sophisticated multimodal intelligence. Its ability to enable intrinsic self-correction (evidenced by reflection tokens) and adaptive reasoning depth based on task difficulty makes it a powerful tool for building more robust and human-like AI systems.

Looking ahead, FRANK's framework opens avenues for exploring more diverse modalities (audio, video) and dynamic fusion strategies. Its success underscores the potential of intelligent model merging as a viable alternative to costly large-scale training, paving the way for more accessible and capable multimodal AI.
