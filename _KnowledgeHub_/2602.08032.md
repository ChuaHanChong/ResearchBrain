# Horizon Imagination: Efficient On-Policy Rollout in Diffusion World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Technion and Microsoft Research introduce Horizon Imagination (HI), an on-policy imagination method for diffusion world models that enables parallel denoising of future observations for training discrete stochastic policies. This approach maintains control performance with half the denoising budget of autoregressive baselines, reducing training time by approximately 30%, and introduces stable action sampling to minimize action fluctuations during the imagination process.

## Problem

- High computational cost of diffusion models during inference limits their direct use as real-time controllers or for generating extensive simulated experience in RL.
- Current world model imagination methods for diffusion models are inherently sequential, leading to prohibitive computational overhead as each observation generation is a multi-step denoising process.
- Parallel denoising of future observations creates instability, as policies conditioned on intermediate, noisy observations can lead to frequent and destabilizing action changes as observations clarify.

## Method

- **Horizon Imagination** (HI) framework for ==on-policy imagination== in discrete stochastic policy settings, enabling parallel denoising of multiple future observations.
- A stable discrete action sampling method that utilizes a single multivariate uniform sample to minimize action changes from evolving policy distributions during parallel denoising.
- A novel Horizon schedule that decouples the total denoising budget from the effective decay horizon, allowing for independent control over these parameters and supporting sub-frame budgets.

## Results

- Horizon Imagination maintained control performance comparable to autoregressive baselines while utilizing half the denoising budget (B=16 vs. B=32), leading to a **30%** reduction in training time on Atari environments.
- The stable action sampling method showed near-optimal performance, resulting in at most one average action change over 16 denoising steps, significantly outperforming naive sampling which caused changes in most steps.
- Parallel configurations using the Horizon schedule achieved better Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) scores, particularly at low to medium budgets, with sub-frame budgets reaching quality comparable to baselines using 16 times the budget.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parallel denoising in diffusion world models can be stabilized and made efficient for on-policy imagination by carefully managing action sampling across evolving observation states.
> - Decoupling the denoising budget from the decay horizon in generation schedules provides greater flexibility in resource allocation and can lead to improved generation quality, especially at lower budgets.
> - Maintaining action consistency throughout the multi-step denoising process is critical for preventing instability and ensuring effective policy training in diffusion-based imagination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) often benefits from sample-efficient methods, and world models have emerged as a paradigm to address this by generating simulated experience for training controllers. Among generative models, diffusion-based models have demonstrated high fidelity in synthesizing various modalities, including images and video. Consequently, researchers have begun exploring the integration of diffusion models into world models for RL. This line of research, termed diffusion world models, aims to leverage the generative capabilities of diffusion models to create realistic and diverse simulated environments.

However, the application of diffusion world models to control tasks faces several computational challenges. Contemporary diffusion models, particularly large-scale architectures, are computationally intensive during inference. This characteristic limits their direct use in scenarios requiring lightweight and efficient controllers for real-time deployment. Existing approaches to integrate diffusion models into RL control frameworks typically fall into two categories:
1. **Direct world model use as policy/planner (e.g., Model Predictive Control):** Methods like Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024) utilize the world model directly for control, but this often results in high computational costs, making real-world deployment impractical.
2. **Training an independent controller via imagination:** Approaches such as those by Alonso et al. (2024) and Yang et al. (2024) train a separate controller using experience imagined from the world model. However, these methods typically rely on sequential imagination, where each observation is generated through a multi-step denoising process, interleaved with policy decisions. This sequential nature leads to significant computational overhead, as highlighted by runtime analyses in previous works (Alonso et al., 2024).

Furthermore, some concurrent multi-step generation efforts (Rigter et al., 2024; Jackson et al., 2024) involve policy-guided imagination using score-based models, but these are primarily limited to continuous action spaces. Other works (Ding et al., 2024) explore off-policy multi-step generation for offline RL settings, but do not comprehensively examine how generation quality is influenced by sampling configurations or budgets. The present work positions itself within this landscape by specifically addressing the efficiency challenges of *on-policy imagination* in *discrete stochastic policy* settings for diffusion world models, aiming to enable more practical training of deployable controllers. It introduces mechanisms to improve both the stability and computational efficiency of the imagination process itself.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more efficient approach for training discrete stochastic policies using diffusion world models. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Addressing Computational Inefficiency in Diffusion World Models for Control:** While diffusion models offer high generative fidelity, their large architectures and multi-step denoising processes lead to substantial computational costs at inference time. This makes them unsuitable for direct use as real-time controllers or for generating extensive simulated experience through sequential imagination, which is a common practice in model-based RL. The authors seek to mitigate this barrier to practical deployment.

2. **Improving Imagination Efficiency:** Current methods for world model imagination with diffusion models are inherently sequential. Each future observation needs to be generated before the subsequent action can be sampled, and this process is repeated for the entire imagined trajectory. Given that each observation generation is itself a costly multi-step denoising process, this sequential dependency results in prohibitive computational overhead. The paper aims to reduce this sequential burden by enabling parallel generation of multiple future observations.

3. **Stabilizing Parallel Denoising with Evolving Policies:** When multiple future observations are denoised in parallel, actions must be conditioned on intermediate, potentially noisy, observations. As denoising progresses and observations become clearer, the policy's action distributions may change. Naive sampling from these evolving distributions can lead to frequent, unnecessary action changes across denoising steps, which can destabilize the overall imagination process and degrade generation quality. A key objective is to devise a mechanism to maintain action consistency despite the evolving policy and observation states.

4. **Decoupling Denoising Budget from Effective Horizon:** Existing multi-step generation schedules, such as the Pyramidal schedule, often entangle the total denoising budget (number of denoising steps) with the effective horizon over which denoising is applied (decay horizon). This entanglement can lead to suboptimal generation quality at higher budgets and restricts flexibility in resource allocation. The research aims to introduce a sampling schedule that allows independent control over these parameters, facilitating fine-grained resource management and supporting "sub-frame budgets" where fewer denoising steps are allocated per observation than the number of frames generated.

By addressing these objectives, the authors motivate their work as a step towards making diffusion world models a more viable and practical tool for training sample-efficient and deployable RL agents, particularly in contexts where real-time performance and computational efficiency are critical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Horizon Imagination (HI), introduces an on-policy imagination process designed for discrete stochastic policies in diffusion world models. It focuses on parallel denoising of multiple future observations and incorporates specific mechanisms to enhance stability and efficiency.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning Setup

The framework operates within a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) where the agent interacts with an environment, observing `o_t` and taking actions `a_t` to receive `o_{t+1}`, `r_t`, and `d_t`. The agent's goal is to maximize expected return.

#### 3.2. World Model Agents

The overall agent architecture adheres to the typical world model paradigm, comprising four main components:
1. **Representation model:** An encoder-decoder (tokenizer) for converting raw observations into compact latent representations `z_t` and vice-versa. The encoder output is constrained to `[-1, 1]`. The tokenizer is trained using an MSE regression objective combined with a perceptual loss.
2. **Dynamics model (World Model):** A generative model `p(z_{t+1}, r_t, d_t | z_{\le t}, a_{\le t})` that learns environment dynamics. This consists of a denoiser `v_θ` for future observations and a lightweight reward-termination predictor.
3. **Controller:** An actor-critic network trained entirely on simulated experience.
4. **Replay buffer:** Stores real environment interactions used for training the representation and dynamics models.
The method is applicable to both online and offline training settings.

#### 3.3. Diffusion Framework

The authors employ the 1-Rectified Flow (RF) framework (Liu et al., 2023) for the denoiser `v_θ`. RF learns a time-dependent vector field `v_θ` to transport samples from a prior distribution `p_prior` to the data distribution `p_data` by minimizing a regression objective `L(θ) = E_{x_0,x_1,τ} ||v_θ(x_τ, τ) - (x_1 - x_0)||^2`.

#### 3.4. World Model Training (Denoiser `v_θ`)

The denoiser `v_θ` operates over sequences of latent observations `z_t`. During training, `h`-step trajectory segments are sampled from the replay buffer, encoded to `z_{1:h}`, and serve as clean targets. Noise samples `z_0` and denoising times `τ` are drawn for each observation. With a 20% probability, a clean prefix is provided to improve generation quality at inference. The denoiser is trained to approximate `E[z_{1,t} - z_{0,t} | z_{τ,1}..., z_{τ,t}, a_{<t}, τ_{\le t}]` for `1 \le t \le h` by minimizing the rectified flow regression objective. Critically, all denoiser outputs for a trajectory segment are computed in parallel in a single forward pass, ensuring causality by conditioning each output only on inputs up to its timestep.

#### 3.5. Horizon Imagination (World Model Inference)

The core of HI is parallel denoising of multiple observations, even before near-future observations are fully clean. This requires future actions to be conditioned on intermediate, noisy observations, and the policy is queried before each denoising step.

- **Minimizing Action Changes Over the Denoising Process:** To counter instability caused by frequent action changes due to policy stochasticity during parallel denoising, a stable sampling method is introduced for discrete actions. It leverages a single multivariate uniform sample `ω` and a random permutation `ρ` of actions. At each denoising time `τ`, the action `a_τ` is determined by scanning `ω`'s coordinates against conditional probabilities `α_i(π_τ)`. This ensures that if the policy distribution `π_τ` does not change, the action remains unchanged, minimizing unnecessary fluctuations (Proposition 1).

- **The Horizon Schedule:** A novel time schedule is proposed, designed as a matrix `K` of denoising times `τ` for `h` observations over `B` denoising steps. It introduces a `decay horizon` parameter `ν` that decouples the denoising budget `B` from the effective horizon over which denoising decays. This allows independent control over these parameters, supporting sub-frame budgets (where `B < h`). The schedule progresses denoising faster for near-future observations.

#### 3.6. Actor-Critic Training

The controller (actor-critic) is trained on data generated by HI. Since parallel generation requires the policy to operate on observations at varying noise levels, the policy must be trained across all noise levels.
- The actor-critic uses identical backbones with distinct linear heads.
- The critic operates on fully denoised inputs, providing bootstrapped value estimates in symlog space.
- The actor is trained using the REINFORCE objective, applied at every trajectory step and leveraging imagined interactions across all noise levels. Advantage terms are scaled using an exponential moving average (EMA).

#### 3.7. Implementation Details (Summarized from Appendix A)

- **Representation Model (Tokenizer):** A continuous autoencoder with a `tanh` activation on the encoder output to constrain latents to `[-1, 1]`. Uses a standard MSE loss combined with a perceptual loss.
- **World Model:** The denoiser `v_θ` is a causal Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture. Inputs are patchified, and 3D RoPE positional embeddings are used. Action conditioning is implemented via learned embeddings summed with denoising-time embeddings into AdaLN layers. The reward-termination network is a lightweight RNN with a CNN feature extractor, predicting rewards in symlog space and terminations via cross-entropy.
- **Actor-Critic:** The critic's objective is a regression in symlog space for λ-returns. Advantage scaling uses EMA of return quantiles.
- **Hyperparameters:** Fixed hyperparameters are used across all environments. Initial action biases are used in some Craftium environments for exploration. Replay buffer sampling is a mix of uniform and Beta(3,1) distribution to prioritize recent data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficiency and performance of Horizon Imagination.

#### 4.1. Empirical Analysis of Action Consistency

Two experiments were conducted to evaluate the stable action sampling mechanism:
- **Comparison to theoretical lower bound:** The stable sampling method empirically performed close to the theoretical lower bound, represented by the total variation distance `δ(p, q)`, across various numbers of actions (`N`). This suggests its near-optimal performance in minimizing changes when policy distributions evolve.
- **Comparison with naive sampling:** In a simulated denoising process, the proposed stable method resulted in at most one average action change over 16 steps, even with evolving policy distributions. In contrast, naive sampling led to action changes in most steps, exceeding half of all steps, especially with high-entropy policies. This validates the effectiveness of stable sampling in preventing unnecessary action fluctuations.

#### 4.2. Control Performance

The agent was benchmarked in an online RL setting on Atari 100K and Craftium environments, comparing HI with `(ν=4, B=16)` and `(ν=4, B=32)` against an autoregressive baseline `(ν=1, B=32)`.
- **Performance Maintenance with Reduced Budget:** Both HI baselines with `ν=4` maintained control performance comparable to the autoregressive baseline across all tested environments. Notably, the `(ν=4, B=16)` configuration, utilizing only half the denoising budget of the autoregressive baseline, achieved similar results.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Reducing the denoising budget from `B=32` to `B=16` on Atari environments shortened training time from approximately 27 hours to 19 hours, demonstrating a reduction in computational cost, though not a full 2x speed-up due to other unaffected training stages.
- **Impact of Budget in Complex Environments:** While a single denoising step per observation (`B=32` in the autoregressive case) generally sufficed for Atari, the `(ν=4, B=32)` baseline showed superior performance in Craftium/ChopTree-v0, a visually more complex environment, suggesting benefits from higher budgets in intricate settings.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Stable Action Sampling

An ablation study where the stable action sampling mechanism was replaced by naive sampling revealed a substantial drop in control performance. In some environments like Atari Boxing and Gopher, this replacement led to a prominent collapse in episode returns, underscoring the importance of action consistency for stable imagination and effective policy training.

#### 4.4. Parallel vs. Sequential Generation Quality

World model generation quality was assessed using Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and Mean Squared Error (MSE) under various `(ν, B)` configurations, using recorded actions.
- **Advantage of Parallel Configurations:** Parallel configurations (e.g., `4 \le ν \le 16`) consistently showed an advantage in FVD scores, particularly at low to medium budgets. In several instances, parallel variants with sub-frame budgets achieved generation quality comparable to baselines using 16 times the budget.
- **Performance at Extreme Budgets:** At very high budgets (`B \ge 128`), the autoregressive baseline (`ν=1`) generally performed best in terms of FVD, though parallel configurations remained strong.
- **MSE vs. FVD Trends:** Interestingly, MSE results sometimes suggested that increasing the denoising budget could lead to generated sequences drifting further from ground truth, even as perceptual quality (FVD) improved. This indicates a potential trade-off or different aspects of quality being captured.
- **Comparison to Pyramidal Schedule:** An experiment with the Pyramidal schedule (Chen et al., 2024) showed significant performance degradation at high budgets, attributed to the coupling of budget and decay horizon. This contrasts with the Horizon schedule, which maintained generation quality more robustly.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Visualizations

Qualitative visualizations of generated sequences demonstrated the visual output quality of the world model under different Horizon schedule configurations, including comparisons to ground truth and tokenizer reconstructions, providing visual evidence for the quantitative findings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers contributions that address a critical bottleneck in the application of diffusion world models to reinforcement learning, with several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Practicality of Diffusion World Models for RL:** The core contribution of Horizon Imagination (HI) is to significantly improve the computational efficiency of on-policy imagination in diffusion world models. By demonstrating that comparable control performance can be maintained with half the denoising budget and supporting sub-frame budgets, the work makes diffusion-based world models substantially more practical for training lightweight, deployable policies. This is a crucial step towards bridging the gap between the high generative fidelity of diffusion models and the efficiency demands of real-world RL applications.

2. **Increased Stability of Imagination Processes:** The introduction of the stable discrete action sampling mechanism directly tackles a previously unaddressed instability issue arising from policy-induced action fluctuations during parallel denoising. By ensuring consistent action selection even as observation states and policy distributions evolve, HI contributes to a more robust and reliable imagination process, which is fundamental for effective policy training.

3. **Flexible Resource Management in Generative World Models:** The Horizon schedule provides a novel way to disentangle the denoising budget from the decay horizon. This independent control allows practitioners to fine-tune the computational resources allocated to imagination based on specific task requirements and hardware constraints. The ability to use sub-frame budgets represents a new level of efficiency, offering more granularity than previous approaches.

4. **Broader Applicability:** The method's design, being training-agnostic and compatible with any pre-trained world model that supports observation-level time conditioning, implies a wide potential scope for integration into various existing and future diffusion-based RL frameworks. This could accelerate research and development in the field by providing a more efficient imagination primitive.

5. **Paving the Way for Scalable Diffusion-based RL Agents:** By addressing the computational overhead that has traditionally limited the scalability and practical deployment of diffusion world models, this work facilitates the development of more capable and efficient RL agents that can leverage the generative power of diffusion models without incurring prohibitive costs. This could lead to advancements in areas requiring high-fidelity environment modeling, such as complex robotics, autonomous systems, and highly visual environments.

In summary, Horizon Imagination offers a set of methodological advancements that collectively enhance the efficiency and stability of diffusion world models for RL. This contributes to making these advanced generative models more accessible and viable for the development of performant and deployable agents, thereby having a significant impact on the broader landscape of model-based reinforcement learning.
