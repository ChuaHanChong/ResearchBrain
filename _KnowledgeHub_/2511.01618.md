# Actial: Activate Spatial Reasoning Ability of Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Actial, a framework developed by researchers including those from Nanjing University and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, activates latent 3D spatial reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models by explicitly training them on foundational viewpoint understanding. It achieves 92.2% accuracy on its new Viewpoint-100K dataset and secures an 83.5% average score on CV-Bench, outperforming leading proprietary models such as GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with understanding fine-grained 3D spatial information and cross-view consistency, often defaulting to superficial 2D continuity.
- Existing MLLMs lack foundational spatial skills, such as precise viewpoint understanding and spatial transformations, leading to unreliable performance in real-world 3D scenarios.
- The inherent "visual-spatial intelligence" of MLLMs is underexploited due to a lack of targeted training strategies for 3D perception from 2D inputs.

## Method

- ==Actial== introduces a =="Viewpoint Learning"== task that simplifies 3D consistency evaluation into multiple-choice questions about relative horizontal translation and rotation between object-centric image pairs.
- A new ==Viewpoint-100K dataset==, comprising 100,000 object-centric image pairs with automatically generated question-answer pairs for relative viewpoints, is created to support this task.
- A ==two-stage fine-tuning strategy== is employed: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with ==hybrid cold-start initialization== for foundational knowledge injection, followed by Reinforcement Learning using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for generalization enhancement.

## Results

- Actial-7B achieved **92.2%** accuracy on the Viewpoint-100K dataset, a substantial improvement from the baseline's **12.9%**.
- On the CV-Bench, Actial-7B scored an average of **83.5%**, surpassing leading models like GPT-4o (**79.4%**) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (**77.7%**).
- The model demonstrated near-mastery of foundational viewpoints with **99.2%** accuracy on BLINK's multi-view component and showed comprehensive improvements across many subtasks on the challenging MMSI-Bench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs, despite primary training on 2D data, possess significant latent potential for 3D spatial perception that can be activated through targeted training.
> - Explicitly training MLLMs on basic spatial skills like viewpoint understanding effectively "activates" and substantially enhances their broader spatial reasoning capabilities across diverse tasks.
> - A hybrid cold-start initialization during SFT, combining new dataset training with human-assisted pseudo Chain-of-Thoughts, is crucial for preserving instruction-following and coherent reasoning while injecting foundational knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in processing and understanding 2D visual information, exhibiting capabilities from image captioning to complex visual question answering. Naturally, the research community is keen on extending these capabilities to more intricate 3D reasoning tasks, which are fundamental for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous navigation, and immersive virtual environments.

However, a critical gap exists: while MLLMs can achieve impressive 2D visual understanding, it remains largely unclear whether they can effectively capture the fine-grained *spatial information* and crucially, *cross-view consistency*, required for robust 3D reasoning. Previous studies have highlighted that current MLLMs often struggle with cross-view consistency, primarily relying on superficial 2D cues or "2D continuity" inherent in video data, rather than a true understanding of "3D consistency." 2D continuity refers to the seamless pixel-level transitions between frames, whereas 3D consistency implies the preservation of actual spatial and geometric relationships (depth, scale, object positions) across different views. The paper emphasizes that current models, largely trained on 2D data and constrained by factors like low frame rates, fail to perceive that images and videos are projections of 3D space, not just sequences of changing pixels.

Existing research has attempted to integrate MLLMs with 3D environments by incorporating rich 3D inputs, features, or explicit 3D priors. While these approaches demonstrate potential, many focus on high-level reasoning tasks, often overlooking the *foundational spatial skills* necessary for true 3D understanding, such as viewpoint estimation and spatial transformations. This paper directly addresses this oversight. It argues that the "visual-spatial intelligence" of MLLMs is largely underexploited due to a lack of targeted training on these fundamental concepts. By introducing "Viewpoint Learning," this work aims to provide MLLMs with the foundational spatial grammar that allows them to move beyond mere 2D pattern matching to a deeper, more accurate interpretation of 3D space from diverse 2D projections. Thus, "Actial" positions itself as a critical step towards activating latent 3D perception in MLLMs, bridging the gap between their current 2D prowess and the sophisticated 3D reasoning required for real-world interactive and autonomous systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **activate and significantly enhance the spatial reasoning ability of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**, particularly focusing on their capacity to understand and leverage 3D consistency from 2D visual inputs.

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging the 2D-3D Perception Gap:** Despite their advancements in 2D visual tasks, existing MLLMs demonstrate a profound struggle with detailed 3D spatial information and cross-view consistency. They often default to recognizing 2D continuity in videos rather than understanding the underlying 3D consistency, leading to unreliable performance in real-world 3D scenarios.
- **Unlocking Latent Spatial Intelligence:** The authors hypothesize that MLLMs possess a latent potential for 3D spatial perception, which remains largely untapped due to a lack of appropriate data utilization and targeted training strategies. The goal is to "exploit" this latent intelligence by correcting the models' conceptual understanding of visual input—teaching them to view images as projections of 3D space.
- **Addressing Foundational Skill Deficiencies:** Current MLLMs often lack foundational spatial skills, such as precise viewpoint understanding. As demonstrated by their near-random performance on certain spatial tasks (e.g., BLINK's multi-view task), models struggle with even basic viewpoint estimation. The authors posit that mastering these fundamental skills is a pivotal step towards achieving robust 3D reasoning.
- **Enabling Robust Real-World Applications:** Enhanced spatial reasoning is critical for the reliable deployment of MLLMs in domains requiring advanced 3D understanding, such as robotics (navigation, object manipulation), autonomous systems (self-driving cars, drones), and sophisticated 3D scene comprehension.
- **Simplifying Complex Tasks:** Rather than immediately tackling precise 3D camera pose regression, the paper aims to simplify the problem into a "Viewpoint Learning" task involving multiple-choice questions for relative translation and rotation, making it more tractable for MLLMs to learn foundational concepts.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to develop MLLMs that can genuinely "see" and "reason" in three dimensions, moving beyond superficial 2D pattern recognition to a deeper, geometrically grounded understanding of the world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named **Actial**, is designed to activate and enhance the spatial reasoning abilities of MLLMs through a novel task, a specialized dataset, and a two-stage fine-tuning strategy.

#### 3.1. Viewpoint Learning Task

At the core of Actial is "Viewpoint Learning," a foundational task focused on evaluating and improving an MLLM's understanding of 3D consistency. Recognizing that directly regressing precise camera poses is challenging for MLLMs, the problem is simplified into multiple-choice questions concerning relative horizontal translation and rotation between object-centric image pairs. The task considers two frames of reference: ego-centric (camera's perspective) and object-centric (object's perspective), and focuses on horizontal transformations inspired by the characteristics of the source dataset.

#### 3.2. Viewpoint-100K Dataset

To support Viewpoint Learning, the authors introduce the **Viewpoint-100K dataset**. This dataset is automatically generated from MVImgNet, a large-scale multi-view image dataset that includes real-captured frames with precise camera calibrations, depth maps, and point clouds.
- **Composition:** It comprises 100,000 object-centric image pairs, where each pair captures the same subject from distinct viewpoints.
- **QA Generation:** Corresponding ego-centric or object-centric question-answer pairs (QAs) are automatically derived using the provided camera parameters to compute relative translation and rotation.
- **Diversity:** The dataset features 10,813 distinct objects spanning 205 different object classes.
- **Challenge Design:** The horizontal angle difference between camera viewpoints is deliberately set between 20 and 100 degrees to ensure the task is challenging yet discriminable for both humans and models.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Fine-Tuning Strategy

To effectively activate spatial reasoning, Actial employs a two-stage fine-tuning approach:

**Stage 1: Foundational Knowledge Injection (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT)**
- **Objective:** To inject critical viewpoint understanding and mitigate the 2D-centric biases inherent in pre-trained MLLMs.
- **Method:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is performed on the **Viewpoint-100K dataset**. The goal is for the model to develop a correct understanding of spatial relationships and viewpoint transformations.
- **Hybrid Cold-Start Initialization:** A crucial component introduced to address two issues: (1) preserving the instruction-following capabilities and coherent reasoning process of the base MLLM, and (2) simultaneously learning viewpoint representations.
 - This involves combining the Viewpoint-100K dataset with **human-assisted pseudo Chain-of-Thoughts (CoTs)** as input for SFT.
 - Initially, a correct CoT template is manually constructed based on MLLM raw outputs. Then, Gemini 2.5 Pro is used to generate 1,000 pseudo CoTs for Viewpoint-100K, which are further refined.
 - A small interleave ratio (0.9:0.1 for Viewpoint-100K:pseudo CoTs) is used to prevent disrupting the model's learning of accurate viewpoint knowledge. The system prompt instructs the model to "think about the reasoning process."

**Stage 2: Generalization Enhancement (Reinforcement Learning - RL)**
- **Objective:** To preserve the acquired viewpoint-related knowledge while significantly improving the model’s generalization capacity across a broader range of spatial tasks, thereby mitigating potential overfitting from the single-task SFT.
- **Method:** Reinforcement Learning is applied using the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm.
- **Dataset:** Fine-tuning is performed on the **SAT dataset**, a synthetic dataset designed for spatial aptitude training, which covers both static and dynamic spatial reasoning.
- **Reward Mechanism:** Reward-based optimization encourages the model to generate its own reasoning chains and apply the spatial knowledge gained in the first stage, fostering deeper and more flexible understanding without direct supervision on intermediate steps.

#### 3.4. Baseline Model and Evaluation

The base MLLM used for fine-tuning is **Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct**. The model's performance is evaluated using the VLMEvalKit across multiple established benchmarks for spatial reasoning: 3DSRBench, CV-Bench, BLINK, and MMSI-Bench.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that the Actial approach significantly activates and enhances the spatial reasoning capabilities of MLLMs.

#### 4.1. Initial Baseline Performance and the Need for Activation

- Baseline MLLMs (e.g., Qwen base model) show extremely poor performance on viewpoint-related tasks, often near random guessing (e.g., 12.9% accuracy on Viewpoint-100K, comparable to random chance on BLINK's multi-view task). This confirms their reliance on superficial 2D cues and a lack of explicit 3D spatial understanding.
- The high KL divergence observed when directly applying RL (GRPO) to the baseline on Viewpoint-100K further indicated an entrenched bias towards 2D reasoning, emphasizing the necessity of targeted foundational knowledge injection.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Foundational Knowledge Injection (SFT)

- **Viewpoint-100K Performance:** Direct Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on Viewpoint-100K dramatically improved the model's accuracy on this task, reaching 92.2% (compared to 12.9% for the baseline). This demonstrates that MLLMs *can* learn foundational viewpoint understanding when explicitly trained.
- **Out-of-Domain Generalization:** Surprisingly, the SFT phase on viewpoint tasks led to performance improvements on *out-of-domain* tasks in CV-Bench, specifically for Height, Depth, and Relation. This highlights the foundational importance of viewpoint learning for enhancing the model's overall spatial ability, not just the specific task it was trained on.
- **Hybrid Cold-Start Initialization Impact:** The hybrid cold-start initialization, by incorporating pseudo CoTs during SFT, was crucial for maintaining the model's instruction-following capabilities and coherent reasoning process, leading to smoother training and more robust reasoning chains compared to SFT without it.

#### 4.3. Overall Performance with Two-Stage Fine-Tuning (Actial-7B)

- **CV-Bench and BLINK:** Actial-7B achieved substantial performance gains over the baseline on CV-Bench, with an average score of 83.5%, significantly outperforming the baseline (71.2%) and even surpassing leading proprietary models like GPT-4o (79.4%), Gemini-1.5-Pro (77.7%), and QwenVLMax on this benchmark. On BLINK's multi-view component, Actial achieved a remarkable 99.2% accuracy, indicating near-mastery of foundational viewpoints.
- **3DSRBench:** Actial-7B showed improvements over the baseline (47.7% vs. 45.8%) on 3DSRBench, demonstrating a harmonious balance of the various improvements, though remaining slightly behind the previous state-of-the-art on this specific benchmark.
- **MMSI-Bench (Additional Experiments):** Actial-7B, despite being a 7B parameter model, achieved comprehensive improvements across many subtasks on the challenging MMSI-Bench and showed performance comparable to, or even outperforming, larger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and GPT-4o on several subtasks. A noted limitation was its tendency to generate excessively long reasoning chains for multi-step reasoning (MSR), leading to truncation and lower scores on that specific subtask.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Insights

- **Knowledge Injection (SFT):** Generally beneficial, improving performance across most tasks. While some task-specific declines were observed due to limited data diversity, the overall positive impact, especially on out-of-domain tasks, validated its importance.
- **Generalization Enhancement (GRPO):** The GRPO phase successfully preserved the SFT improvements and addressed performance degradations from SFT-induced overfitting. It also facilitated additional gains on tasks where the model was already strong, thereby enhancing adaptability and robustness. However, it was noted that relying solely on result-based rewards could sometimes slightly dilute previously injected foundational knowledge in specific areas (e.g., some 3DSRBench metrics).

**Core Insights:** The experiments collectively confirm three key points: (1) Current MLLMs lack foundational spatial skills like viewpoint understanding. (2) Despite training on 2D data, they possess significant *latent potential* for 3D spatial perception. (3) Explicitly training MLLMs on basic spatial skills effectively *activates* and significantly enhances their broader spatial reasoning capabilities across diverse tasks. This "activation" often manifests as a sudden, rapid generalization once the model shifts its internal representation from 2D to 3D cues, as evidenced by rapid loss decreases during training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in addressing their inherent limitations in 3D spatial reasoning. The contributions of "Actial" carry substantial scientific and practical implications:

**Scientific Significance:**

- **Proof of Latent 3D Capacity:** The work provides compelling evidence that MLLMs, despite being predominantly trained on 2D data, possess latent capabilities for 3D spatial perception. This challenges the notion that these models are fundamentally restricted to 2D understanding and opens new avenues for unlocking their full potential.
- **Foundational Spatial Intelligence:** By introducing "Viewpoint Learning" and the Viewpoint-100K dataset, the authors establish a concrete, simplified task and benchmark for instilling foundational spatial knowledge. This approach shifts the focus from merely augmenting MLLMs with explicit 3D features to teaching them a conceptual understanding of 3D space from its 2D projections.
- **Effective Training Paradigm:** The proposed two-stage fine-tuning strategy, combining supervised fine-tuning for knowledge injection with reinforcement learning for generalization, offers a robust and effective paradigm for developing complex cognitive abilities in MLLMs. The hybrid cold-start initialization is a critical innovation for maintaining model coherence and reasoning integrity during this process.
- **Understanding MLLM Learning Dynamics:** The analysis of training metrics, such as the sudden drop in loss during SFT and the behavior of KL divergence during GRPO, provides valuable insights into how MLLMs acquire and generalize spatial knowledge, suggesting an "activation" moment when they shift from 2D to 3D interpretation.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**

- **Robotics:** Enhanced spatial reasoning is paramount for robots operating in unstructured environments. Actial's improvements in cross-view consistency and 3D understanding can lead to more accurate object localization, improved navigation, safer human-robot interaction, and more dexterous manipulation capabilities.
- **Autonomous Systems:** For self-driving cars, drones, and other autonomous agents, a deeper understanding of 3D spatial relationships in dynamic environments is crucial. Actial can contribute to more reliable perception of obstacles, scene layout, and the relative motion of objects, thereby increasing safety and operational efficiency.
- **3D Scene Understanding and Reconstruction:** This work can improve MLLMs' ability to interpret and reconstruct 3D environments from sparse or multi-view 2D inputs, benefiting applications in virtual reality, augmented reality, 3D content creation, and architecture.
- **Next-Generation Multimodal AI:** By equipping MLLMs with foundational spatial intelligence, this research paves the way for building more truly intelligent and physically grounded AI systems that can interact with and understand the world in a manner more akin to humans. This sets a precedent for how other complex, non-linguistic forms of intelligence might be "activated" in large foundation models.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The authors acknowledge that the current dataset scenarios are relatively constrained, being primarily object-centric and simplifying problems compared to more complex real-world settings or precise camera pose estimation. The identified issue of excessively long reasoning chains leading to output truncation on complex tasks like MMSI-Bench's MSR subtask also highlights a need for optimizing reasoning efficiency and managing output length. Future work will likely focus on expanding the diversity and complexity of viewpoint learning tasks, integrating more challenging 3D reasoning problems, and refining the reinforcement learning stages to ensure better retention of foundational knowledge while generalizing more robustly.

In conclusion, "Actial" provides a compelling argument and a practical methodology for cultivating essential 3D spatial skills in MLLMs, propelling them towards a more sophisticated and reliable understanding of our three-dimensional world, with profound implications for numerous AI applications.
