# Contact-Anchored Policies: Contact Conditioning Creates Strong Robot Utility Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Contact-Anchored Policies (CAP) introduces a framework that conditions robot policies on precise physical contact points rather than abstract language for manipulation tasks. This method achieves strong zero-shot generalization across diverse environments and robot embodiments, demonstrating high success rates with only 23 hours of demonstration data while outperforming existing language-conditioned baselines.

## Problem

- Natural language conditioning is often too abstract and imprecise to guide the concrete physical interactions required for robust robot manipulation.
- Current state-of-the-art vision-language-action (VLA) models are data-hungry, computationally intensive, and struggle with data-efficient generalization in physical domains.
- Developing and iterating on robot policies for diverse real-world scenarios is slow and costly due to reliance on extensive real-world evaluation.

## Method

- Robot policies (**CAP**) are conditioned on ==3D contact anchor== coordinates, which are precise points of physical interaction derived from expert demonstrations and tracked during inference.
- A ==Vector-Quantized Behavior Transformer (VQ-BeT)== architecture, pre-trained on a diverse dataset, learns to map visual inputs and contact embeddings to robot actions.
- **EgoGym**, a lightweight MuJoCo-based simulation suite, enables rapid iteration, policy refinement, and identification of real-world failure modes through ==procedurally generated== diverse scenes.

## Results

- Achieved **81-96%** zero-shot success rates on Pick, Open, and Close tasks in unseen real-world environments when using VLM-generated contact anchors, further improving to **90-98%** with VLM verifier-guided retries.
- Demonstrated zero-shot generalization across multiple robot embodiments (Stretch, Franka, XArm, UR3e) with consistent performance, including deployment and real-time inference on modern iPhones.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art baselines on key manipulation tasks, showing a **56%** improvement over π0.5-DROID on Franka for Pick and a **36%** improvement over AnyGrasp on Stretch, using substantially less training data (**23 hours**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precise physical contact points provide a more effective and data-efficient conditioning signal for robust robot manipulation than abstract language commands.
> - Modular robot utility models, each specializing in an atomic skill, can be chained by high-level controllers to accomplish complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - Lightweight, diverse simulations focused on identifying failure modes, rather than photorealism, can significantly accelerate the development and calibration of real-world robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a core tension in the contemporary field of robot learning: the struggle to achieve robust, generalizable physical manipulation when relying heavily on abstract language conditioning. The prevalent paradigm often involves training large, monolithic vision-language-action (VLA) models, frequently built upon large language model (LLM) backbones, to generalize across tasks, environments, and embodiments using language prompts. While powerful, this approach is often data-hungry, computationally intensive, and suffers from the inherent imprecision of language in guiding concrete physical interactions.

Contact-Anchored Policies (CAP) offers a significant counterpoint and alternative paradigm. Instead of language, CAP proposes conditioning policies on precise physical contact points. This shifts the focus from high-level, abstract linguistic understanding to a more embodied, physically grounded representation. This work fits into a broader movement towards:

- **Data-Efficient Robot Learning**: While frontier VLA models require thousands of hours of data, CAP achieves strong zero-shot generalization with only 23 hours of demonstration data, making advanced robot learning more accessible and sustainable for academic labs and smaller research groups.
- **Generalization of Atomic Skills**: Rather than aiming for a single, monolithic generalist policy that handles all tasks, CAP structures itself as a "library of modular utility models," each specializing in a fundamental manipulation skill (e.g., pick, open, close). This aligns with the "Robot Utility Models" concept (Etukuru et al., 2025) and suggests a path to building complex behaviors by chaining these robust, specialized models, similar to tool-calling in large language models.
- **Embodied AI and Physical Grounding**: By emphasizing contact, CAP explicitly grounds robot actions in physical interaction with the environment, which is crucial for real-world manipulation. It argues that physical understanding is primary, and language, while useful for high-level reasoning, may be too abstract for direct policy conditioning.
- **Efficient Simulation-in-the-Loop Development**: The introduction of EgoGym, a lightweight simulation benchmark, highlights the importance of rapid iteration and failure mode identification, moving away from photorealism towards diversity and speed for generalization-focused evaluation. This contrasts with efforts to create hyper-realistic simulations for direct sim-to-real transfer, focusing instead on using sim to guide real-world policy refinement.

By challenging the assumptions about the necessity of massive data and complex language models for generalization in manipulation, CAP carves out a pragmatic and potentially more effective niche, especially for tasks requiring precise physical interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Contact-Anchored Policies (CAP) are clearly articulated:

1. **Overcome the Limitations of Language Conditioning in Robotics**: The authors identify a "fundamental tension" where natural language, despite its utility for high-level task specification, is often too abstract and imprecise to guide the concrete physical understanding required for robust robot manipulation. Language models, while powerful, often carry "extraneous information" irrelevant to physical tasks, leading to inefficient inference and large model sizes. CAP aims to rectify this by replacing language with a more direct and physically grounded conditioning signal.
2. **Introduce Physical Contact as a Policy Conditioning Mechanism**: The core motivation is to propose "physical contacts as the policy medium" instead of natural language. By conditioning policies on 3D coordinates representing intended points of contact with objects, CAP seeks to provide the precise spatial awareness that is critical for successful manipulation, directly guiding the robot's interaction with the environment.
3. **Achieve Data-Efficient Generalization**: A key goal is to enable robust zero-shot generalization to novel environments and embodiments using significantly less data, compute, and model parameters compared to state-of-the-art large VLA models. The paper explicitly states using "only 23 hours of demonstration data," a stark contrast to the thousands of hours often required by larger models.
4. **Develop Modular and Refinable Robot Utility Models**: Instead of pursuing a single, monolithic generalist policy, CAP is structured as a library of modular utility models. This factorization is motivated by the desire for efficient iteration. The authors aim to create specialized policies for fundamental skills (e.g., pick, open, close) that can be individually refined and combined.
5. **Enable Rapid Development through Simulation-in-the-Loop**: The motivation for EgoGym is to accelerate the policy development cycle. Real-world evaluation is slow and costly. By creating a lightweight simulation benchmark focused on object and scene diversity rather than photorealism, the authors aim to rapidly identify failure modes, iterate on models and datasets, and calibrate policies *prior* to real-world deployment, thus establishing a powerful "real-to-sim iteration cycle."
6. **Increase Accessibility to General Robot Learning**: By demonstrating strong performance with reduced resource requirements, the work aims to provide a framework that empowers researchers with limited resources, particularly in academia, to study and contribute to the emergence of general behavior in robotics.

In essence, CAP seeks to redefine how robots are conditioned for precise manipulation by leveraging the inherent physicality of contact, leading to more efficient, robust, and accessible robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Contact-Anchored Policies (CAP) methodology is built around conditioning robot behavior on physical contact points, supported by a data-efficient collection strategy, a specialized policy architecture, and a simulation-in-the-loop development cycle.

#### 3.1. Contact-Anchored Policies (CAP) Core Idea

CAP replaces abstract language conditioning with concrete 3D coordinates representing the desired point of physical contact with an object. This "Contact Anchor" serves as a precise spatial guide for the robot's manipulation trajectory.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Annotation

- **Hardware Design:** To minimize the sim-to-real gap, a low-cost, 3D-printable gripper compatible with both handheld operation (for data collection) and robot mounting (for inference) was designed. An iPhone 13 Pro is rigidly mounted for synchronized RGB-D streams and 6-DoF camera poses via ARKit at 30Hz, ensuring consistent observation space.
- **Dataset:** Expert demonstrations were collected for three fundamental tasks: Pick (general object pickup), Open (drawers/cabinet doors), and Close (drawers/cabinet doors). The dataset comprises 20,365 demonstrations (23.1 hours) across 424 diverse environments, emphasizing varied lighting, clutter, and object forms.
- **Data Preprocessing:** RGB and depth images are resized to 224x224 and augmented with horizontal flips. The action space consists of delta end-effector (EE) pose (derived from iPhone camera pose) and continuous gripper aperture. Gripper state is estimated visually using SAM2, segmenting fingers and calculating centroid distance.
- **Hindsight Contact Labeling:** For training, contact anchors are generated automatically. For Pick and Open, contact (t=c) is defined when gripper aperture ceases to decrease. For Close, it's manually labeled during collection. The 3D coordinate centered between gripper fingers at t=c is the contact anchor (pc). This pc is then back-projected using camera odometry for all preceding timesteps (t<tc) and frozen for subsequent timesteps (t>tc), providing a consistent contact anchor throughout the trajectory.

#### 3.3. Policy Learning (VQ-BeT Adaptation)

- **Architecture:** Policy learning is formulated as a conditional imitation learning problem using a Vector-Quantized Behavior Transformer (VQ-BeT) (Lee et al., 2024). VQ-BeT is chosen for its autoregressive nature, which simplifies conditioning, and its efficiency in terms of model size and speed.
- **Input Conditioning:** The policy 𝜋(𝑎𝑡:𝑡+ℎ|𝑜𝑡−𝑘:𝑡, 𝑝𝑡−𝑘:𝑡) predicts actions based on a history of visual inputs and contact anchors (context length k=3).
 - **Visual Embedding:** A ResNet-50 backbone, pre-trained with MoCo (Chen* et al., 2021) on their dataset, embeds 224x224 RGB input into a feature vector (zv ∈ R256).
 - **Contact Embedding:** The 3D contact anchor (pt ∈ R3), expressed in the current camera frame, is linearly projected to a contact embedding (zc ∈ R256).
- **Observation Token:** These embeddings are concatenated to form the observation token st = [zv, zc], which is fed into VQ-BeT to predict tokenized actions. This joint conditioning allows the policy to adapt to diverse object geometry while anchoring its trajectory to the interaction point.

#### 3.4. Contact Prompting during Inference

- **Initial Anchor Generation:** Unlike training, inference requires an initial contact anchor (p0). This can be manually provided via a user click on the object in the RGB-D view or autonomously generated by querying an off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM) (e.g., Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5) with a text prompt. The 2D pixel (u,v) is deprojected using the depth map and camera intrinsics to obtain p0.
- **Anchor Tracking:** As the robot moves, the camera frame changes. The anchor (pt) is tracked in the camera frame using the robot’s forward kinematics (A⁻¹t A0 p0), ensuring the policy always perceives a consistent contact anchor in the world frame. After gripper closure, the anchor is frozen.

#### 3.5. Simulation-in-the-Loop Development (EgoGym)

- **Purpose:** EgoGym is a lightweight MuJoCo-based simulation suite designed for rapid iteration, checkpoint evaluation, and failure mode discovery during CAP development. It prioritizes speed and scene diversity over photorealism.
- **Procedural Generation:** Diversity is achieved through task-specific procedural scene generation:
 - **Pick:** Objects sampled from Objaverse (915 assets) with varying poses, arrangements, and random surface textures, plus distractors.
 - **Open/Close:** Articulated doors and drawers with randomized geometrical parameters.
- **Benefits:** Enables fast checkpoint evaluation, detects overfitting, provides training signals beyond validation loss (which poorly correlates with real-world performance), and helps in pre-deployment analysis and calibration of grasping thresholds. EgoGym serves as a crucial feedback loop for refining models and datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that Contact-Anchored Policies (CAP) achieve significant advancements in zero-shot generalization for robot manipulation, particularly concerning data efficiency and performance compared to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Environment Generalization

- **Core Tasks Performance:** On three fundamental manipulation tasks evaluated zero-shot in unseen real-world environments (5 scenes for Pick, 5 doors/5 drawers for Open/Close on Stretch 3 robot), CAP shows strong performance with oracle contact prompts:
 - **Pick:** 83.2% success rate.
 - **Open:** 81.0% success rate.
 - **Close:** 96.0% success rate.
- **VLM-Generated Contact Anchors:** CAP maintains comparable performance when initial contact anchors are autonomously generated by a VLM (Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5):
 - **Pick:** 81.0% success rate.
 - **Open:** 80.0% success rate.
 - **Close:** 97.0% success rate. This validates that human intervention is not strictly required for contact prompting.
- **Verifier-Guided Retries:** With VLM-generated anchors and an automated VLM verifier (GPT-4o) for retries, CAP achieves even higher success rates:
 - **Pick:** 90.4% success rate.
 - **Open:** 91.0% success rate.
 - **Close:** 98.0% success rate.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Embodiment Generalization

- CAP policies, trained on handheld gripper data, exhibit strong zero-shot transferability across multiple robot embodiments by simply adapting the gripper mount and inverse kinematic controller.
- **Pick Task Across Robots:**
 - Stretch (internal): 83%
 - Franka FR3: 79% (81% with VLM for contact)
 - XArm 6: 83%
 - Universal Robotics UR3e: 70% (lowest due to short reach).
- **External Evaluations:** Independent evaluations by external collaborators (Hello Robot, UCLA, Ai2) broadly confirmed internal success rates, demonstrating system robustness.
- **iPhone Deployment:** The small model size (52 million parameters) allows real-time deployment and inference on modern iPhones, enabling users to preview CAP's behavior in diverse real-world scenes.

#### 4.3. Baseline Comparisons

CAP significantly outperforms state-of-the-art task-specific and generalist baselines:
- **Pick:**
 - CAP-VLM (81%) vs. π0.5-DROID (25%) on Franka, showing a **56%** improvement.
 - CAP (83%) vs. AnyGrasp (47%) on Stretch, showing a **36%** improvement.
- **Open:**
 - CAP (81%) vs. Stretch-Open (58%) on Stretch, showing a **23%** improvement.
These results highlight CAP's superior generalization and precision in manipulation.

#### 4.4. Eliciting Complex Behaviors with Tool Calling

- By chaining CAP utility models with a high-level VLM controller (via tool calling), complex, long-horizon tasks can be achieved.
- **"Get coffee beans from the cabinet" task (Open, Pick, Drop, Close):** 6/10 trials successful.
- **"Clean up the table" task (sequence of Pick & Drop):** 10/10 trials successful.
This demonstrates that modular utility models can compose to solve sophisticated real-world problems.

#### 4.5. Understanding Real Performance via Simulation (EgoGym)

- **Sim-to-Real Correlation:** A single-blind study showed a strong correlation between CAP's performance in EgoGym-Pick and real-world evaluations, validating EgoGym as an effective proxy for real-world behavior.
- **Failure Analysis:** EgoGym rollouts provided detailed insights into failure modes (e.g., "low-lift failures"), which directly informed refinements to the CAP data processing pipeline, showcasing the value of simulation-in-the-loop development.
- **Robustness to Distractors:** CAP with oracle contact anchors maintains stable performance even with increasing distractor objects, while CAP + VLM and π0.5 models show performance degradation due to picking the wrong objects.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Contact Anchor Importance:** An RGB-only ablation for the Close task achieved only 58% success, significantly worse than CAP's 96%, strongly validating the crucial role of the contact anchor in improving manipulation performance.

Overall, the findings establish CAP as a highly effective, data-efficient, and generalizable framework for robotic manipulation, offering a compelling alternative to large language-conditioned models by prioritizing physical grounding and modularity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Contact-Anchored Policies (CAP) research presents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning, with several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Redefining Robot Conditioning for Physical Tasks:** CAP challenges the dominant paradigm of language-conditioned robot learning for physical manipulation. By demonstrating that precise physical contact points can be a more effective conditioning mechanism than abstract language for atomic manipulation skills, it opens new avenues for designing robot policies. This shift underscores the importance of embodied intelligence and direct physical grounding for real-world robotic tasks, where precision often trumps semantic breadth.

2. **Increased Accessibility to Advanced Robot Learning:** One of the most impactful aspects of CAP is its exceptional data efficiency. Achieving superior zero-shot generalization with only 23 hours of demonstration data, compared to the thousands of hours required by large proprietary VLA models, democratizes access to advanced robot learning. This significantly lowers the barriers to entry for academic researchers and smaller labs with limited resources, enabling them to conduct cutting-edge research and iterate rapidly without needing massive compute clusters or extensive datasets.

3. **Promotion of Modular Robot Utility Models:** The approach of structuring CAP as a "library of modular utility models" offers a powerful alternative to monolithic generalist policies. This modularity allows for specialized, robust performance on specific manipulation skills, which can then be chained together by high-level controllers (e.g., VLMs via tool-calling) to accomplish complex, long-horizon tasks. This paradigm can lead to more interpretable, debuggable, and extensible robotic systems.

4. **Validation of Lightweight Simulation for Generalization:** The development and successful integration of EgoGym demonstrate a pragmatic and effective strategy for simulation-in-the-loop development. By trading photorealism for speed and procedural diversity, EgoGym proves to be a reliable tool for rapidly identifying failure modes, correlating with real-world performance, and refining policies. This approach is highly significant for accelerating the development cycle in robotics, moving away from expensive and time-consuming real-world evaluations.

5. **Benchmarking and Open-Sourcing for Community Advancement:** The commitment to open-sourcing all model checkpoints, codebase, hardware designs, simulation environments, and datasets is a substantial contribution to the robotics community. This transparency and availability will enable other researchers to reproduce, build upon, and extend CAP, fostering collaborative progress and accelerating the development of more general-purpose robots.

6. **Enabling Practical Robot Deployment:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization across diverse environments and robot embodiments (including Stretch, Franka, XArm, UR3e) indicates a high degree of practical utility. The ability to deploy CAP on various robots with minimal adaptation, coupled with its small model size allowing real-time inference even on mobile devices like iPhones, makes it highly suitable for real-world applications and rapid prototyping.

In conclusion, Contact-Anchored Policies offers a compelling and resource-efficient framework for achieving robust, generalizable robot manipulation. By grounding policies in the physical reality of contact and embracing modularity and efficient simulation, CAP provides a potent new direction for robot learning, with the potential to significantly broaden participation and accelerate progress in the field.
