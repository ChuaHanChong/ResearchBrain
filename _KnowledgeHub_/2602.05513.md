# DECO: Decoupled Multimodal Diffusion Transformer for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation with a Plugin Tactile Adapter

> [!summary] Summary
> DECO, a decoupled multimodal diffusion transformer, and a plugin tactile adapter improve bimanual dexterous manipulation by effectively integrating vision, proprioception, and tactile sensing. The approach achieves an 82.50% average success rate across diverse tasks, demonstrating up to a 53.75% improvement on contact-rich tasks like assembly over visual-only baselines.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation policies often struggle in contact-rich tasks or under visual occlusions due to insufficient utilization of fine-grained tactile information.
- Traditional multimodal fusion for bimanual dexterous manipulation often couples sensory inputs, failing to leverage the distinct contributions and varying information density of modalities like vision, proprioception, and touch.
- A scarcity of large-scale, real-world datasets exists that combine bimanual dexterous manipulation with integrated tactile sensing and active vision.

## Method

- Introduces **DECO**, a Decoupled Multimodal Diffusion Transformer, using distinct conditioning pathways for vision, proprioception, and tactile signals to optimize their integration.
- Develops a two-stage training paradigm: first training a vision-based policy, then integrating tactile information via a lightweight plugin tactile adapter using cross-attention and LoRA for parameter efficiency.
- Collects and releases DECO-50, a large-scale real-world dataset comprising over 50 hours of bimanual dexterous manipulation data with active binocular vision, proprioception, and tactile feedback.

## Results

- DECO with the plugin tactile adapter (DECO.p) achieved an average success rate of **82.50%** across tasks, improving overall performance by **21.00%** compared to the Diffusion Policy (DP) baseline.
- On contact-rich tasks (Waste Disposal and Assembly), DECO.p demonstrated a **73.13%** average success rate, showing a **53.75%** improvement over the visual-only DP baseline for these specific tasks.
- The plugin tactile adapter (DECO.p) achieved performance comparable to a fully retrained model (DECO.ds) while optimizing an order of magnitude fewer parameters (**7.97** million vs. **89.41** million).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupled integration of multimodal sensory inputs, particularly tactile information via cross-attention, is more effective for robotic manipulation than coupled fusion, especially in contact-rich scenarios.
> - A parameter-efficient plugin adapter, leveraging pre-trained policies with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), can effectively introduce new sensory modalities like touch without requiring extensive retraining of the entire model.
> - Tactile sensing is crucial for high-dexterity, contact-rich tasks, enabling robots to detect fine contact, manage grasp force, and overcome visual occlusions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual dexterous manipulation is a complex challenge in robotics, requiring robots to coordinate two hands to interact with objects and environments in a manner analogous to human capabilities. Traditional robotic manipulation has often relied on rigid grippers and policies primarily conditioned on visual and proprioceptive inputs. Recent advancements have seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and diffusion-based policies (e.g., RT-1, RDT-1B, OpenVLA, Diffusion Policy) that have demonstrated progress in generalization for robot manipulation.

However, a notable limitation in many existing systems is their performance in contact-rich settings or under visual occlusions, where fine-grained interaction states and force-related signals become critical. While some studies have begun incorporating additional modalities like joint torque, end-effector force/torque, or tactile information into VLA frameworks, the method of integration often remains either straightforward or inadequately explored. Many visuo-tactile policies fuse modalities in a coupled manner, treating them with equal importance. This approach may not fully leverage the distinct contributions of vision, proprioception, and touch for action generation, particularly when visual information is dynamic or tactile signals are sparse.

Furthermore, the availability of large-scale datasets for bimanual dexterous manipulation with integrated tactile sensing is limited. Existing datasets often focus on single-arm or mobile manipulation, or bimanual manipulation with grippers, without providing the rich tactile feedback essential for high-dexterity tasks. Datasets like ActionNet and UniHand-2.0 have explored bimanual dexterous manipulation with vision and proprioception, but a comprehensive dataset that includes tactile sensing for such tasks has been less common. This research addresses these gaps by proposing a decoupled multimodal integration framework and releasing a corresponding dataset, aiming to advance the robustness and dexterity of robotic manipulation in complex, real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic policy capable of advanced bimanual dexterous manipulation by effectively integrating multimodal sensory inputs. The specific challenges and motivations addressed include:

- **Effective Multimodal Integration for Complex Tasks**: Bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks are often complex and rely on combining information from various sensors. The research aims to improve how a robot policy processes and utilizes inputs from vision, proprioception (robot's internal state), and tactile sensors. Current approaches often fuse these modalities in a coupled or uniform manner, which may not optimally account for their distinct roles and varying information density during manipulation. For instance, vision is crucial for global understanding, proprioception for motor control, and tactile sensing for local contact interactions.

- **Addressing Limitations of Coupled Multimodal Fusion**: The motivation stems from the hypothesis that different sensory modalities contribute unequally to action generation. Directly concatenating or uniformly mixing diverse modalities may lead to interference or dilute the unique insights each modality offers. For example, vision can change rapidly with an active camera, while tactile signals might be sparse (only active during contact). A decoupled approach is proposed to allow modality-specific conditioning, enabling a more structured and controllable integration that can exploit the strengths of each sensor.

- **Enhancing Performance on Contact-Rich Tasks**: A significant motivation is to improve robot performance on tasks that involve intricate physical contact, such as assembly or precise object handling. In these scenarios, vision alone may be insufficient due to occlusions or the need for fine force control, where tactile feedback becomes critical. The researchers aim to demonstrate that explicitly incorporating and judiciously utilizing tactile information can lead to substantial performance gains in such tasks.

- **Parameter-Efficient Adaptation of Policies**: Developing new policies from scratch for every new sensory modality or task can be computationally expensive. The research is motivated by the need for parameter-efficient methods to inject new sensory information, particularly tactile data, into existing or pre-trained visuomotor policies. This would allow for adaptable and scalable policy development without requiring extensive retraining of an entire large model.

- **Creating a Comprehensive Dataset for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation with Tactile Sensing**: A practical motivation is the scarcity of large-scale, real-world datasets that combine bimanual dexterous manipulation with tactile sensing and active vision. Such a dataset is considered essential for training and evaluating advanced multimodal policies in contact-rich environments.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Develop a decoupled multimodal Diffusion Transformer (DECO) to integrate visual, proprioceptive, and tactile signals via specialized conditioning pathways.
2. Introduce a plugin tactile adapter for parameter-efficient injection of tactile information into pre-trained policies.
3. Release DECO-50, a bimanual dexterous manipulation dataset with tactile and active vision, to support research in this domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DECO framework employs a two-stage training paradigm and a custom Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) block designed for decoupled conditioning.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The robot policy's objective is to predict a sequence of actions, an "action chunk" $A_t$, given current multimodal observations $O_t$. The observations include multiple images $I_{i,t}$, robot joint states $q_t$ (including dual arms, dual hands, and active camera), tactile information $T_t$, and a one-hot task condition $c$. The policy models a conditional distribution $p(A_t | O_t)$. During training, a rectified flow-matching loss $L_\theta$ is utilized, where the model learns to predict a velocity vector $v_\theta$ that maps noised action sequences $A^\tau_t$ back to the original action sequence $A^0_t$. Inference involves iteratively sampling flow matching timesteps and applying the predicted velocity to generate actions.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Paradigm

The training process for DECO is structured into two distinct stages:
1. **Stage 1: Vision-Based Policy Training**: A core policy is initially trained using only visual and proprioceptive inputs, along with task-level conditions. During this stage, the model learns fundamental manipulation skills based on these primary modalities.
2. **Stage 2: Tactile Integration with Plugin Adapter**: After Stage 1, the pre-trained vision-based policy parameters are frozen. Tactile sensing is then incorporated into the model via a lightweight plugin adapter. Only the parameters of this adapter, along with selected low-rank adaptation (LoRA) layers within the frozen policy, are optimized. This approach aims to efficiently adapt the policy to tactile-aware behaviors without requiring a full retraining of the entire model.

#### 3.3. Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) Block

The core of the velocity predictor is the MMDiT block, which implements a decoupled conditioning strategy for different modalities:

- **Vision and Action Token Interaction**: Binocular images are processed by a shared ResNet-34 backbone, and the resulting feature maps are flattened into visual tokens. These tokens, along with embedded noisy action sequences, are fed into a joint self-attention mechanism. Rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) are applied to visual tokens, and learnable positional embeddings to action tokens. Modality-specific linear projections generate query, key, and value (QKV) representations, which are then concatenated into a unified attention space. Self-attention is computed, and outputs are split back into their respective modalities. This direct interaction allows visual information to guide action generation.

- **Proprioceptive States, Diffusion Timesteps, and Task Conditions**: These signals are injected through Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) modulation. Modality-specific linear projections on both visual and action tokens are followed by AdaLN. The AdaLN parameters (scale, shift, and gate) are dynamically generated by neural networks conditioned on the proprioceptive states ($q_t$), diffusion timesteps ($\tau_i$), and one-hot task-level conditions ($c$). This mechanism allows these contextual signals to modulate the feature representations without altering the core attention structure.

- **Tactile Signals**: Tactile information is incorporated using a dedicated cross-attention module. This design choice enables tactile integration without modifying the self-attention mechanism of the pre-trained vision-based policy, aligning with the decoupled conditioning principle and facilitating plug-and-play functionality.

Finally, the processed action tokens are passed through a linear prediction head to output the action velocity required for the diffusion process.

#### 3.4. Tactile Adapter for Vision-Based Policy

The plugin tactile adapter is designed for parameter-efficient injection of tactile signals into a pre-trained vision-based DECO:

- **Tactile Encoder**: Raw tactile data from the robot's 17 tactile pads (covering finger tips, pulps, ends, and palm) are processed. The encoder uses two complementary approaches to produce region-level features:
 - Averaging raw values within each tactile pad.
 - Projecting raw values through a learnable linear layer.
 These features are concatenated, and a gating mechanism (using a sigmoid-activated learnable linear layer with sinusoidal activation) controls their relative influence, generating final tactile embeddings.

- **Cross-Attention and LoRA**: These tactile embeddings are integrated into the pre-trained vision-based DECO via cross-attention. To maintain parameter efficiency, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is applied. LoRA selectively fine-tunes only a small fraction of parameters within the attention layers of the pre-trained vision-action backbone, alongside optimizing the adapter's parameters. This allows the model to learn to attend to relevant tactile cues for contact-rich interactions while preserving the original visual manipulation capabilities.

#### 3.5. DECO-50 Dataset Collection

A large-scale dataset, DECO-50, was collected to support this research.
- **Hardware**: A bimanual teleoperation system was used, featuring a dual-arm robot equipped with Inspire RH56DFTP dexterous hands (6 DOF per hand, 17 tactile pads each with 1062 contact points), and an active camera mounted on a Unitree H1-2 robot.
- **Teleoperation**: Human motion was captured by a Vision Pro, with wrist poses and hand keypoints translated into robot arm commands via inverse kinematics and retargeted to robot hands.
- **Data Recording**: The system recorded binocular images, tactile readings, and proprioception (14 arm joints, 12 hand joints, 2 camera joints) at 30 Hz.
- **Tasks**: The dataset comprises 4 distinct bimanual dexterous manipulation scenarios with 28 sub-tasks:
 - **Pick and Place**: Basic bimanual coordination for moving and placing static objects.
 - **Material Sorting**: Dynamic interaction involving grasping moving objects from a conveyor belt and placing them into containers.
 - **Waste Disposal**: A contact-rich, multi-stage task involving picking and throwing trash, and opening/closing a bin lid.
 - **Assembly**: The most challenging contact-rich task requiring precise bimanual coordination and force control for assembling plugs and sockets.
- **Scale**: The dataset includes over 50 hours of data, approximately 5 million frames, and 8,000 successful trajectories, covering a variety of objects and contact scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated DECO against several baselines (ACT, DP, DP.t) on the DECO-50 dataset through real-world robot rollouts, totaling over 2,000 trials.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- DECO achieved an average success rate of 72.25% across all tasks, representing a 21.00% improvement over the baseline Diffusion Policy (DP).
- When augmented with the plugin tactile adapter (DECO.p), the average success rate further increased to 82.50%.
- On contact-rich tasks (Waste Disposal and Assembly), DECO.p demonstrated a 73.13% average success rate, a 20% gain over DECO without tactile and a 53.75% improvement over the visual-only DP baseline on these specific tasks.

#### 4.2. Task-Specific Analysis

- **Task 1: Pick and Place**: This task was generally well-handled by all methods, as it primarily relies on visual and proprioceptive feedback for static objects. DECO exhibited slightly superior performance (103/120 successful trials) compared to baselines. The addition of tactile information (DECO.p, 108/120) provided only limited gains, indicating its lesser criticality for this task.

- **Task 2: Material Sorting**: This task involved dynamic object interaction. DP showed relatively weaker performance (67/120) possibly due to slower inference speed affecting grasping of moving objects. Both DECO (101/120) and ACT (97/120) performed comparably better. Tactile data (DP.t, 77/120; DECO.p, 105/120) notably improved success rates on small, circular, and smooth objects (e.g., 00081-p-2x) that are prone to slipping, as tactile feedback aided in detecting contact and managing grasp force. For larger objects, tactile gains were limited.

- **Task 3: Waste Disposal**: This long-horizon task included two contact-rich phases: opening and closing the bin lid.
 - **Stage 1 (Open Lid)**: All methods performed reasonably well, with DECO.p (76/80) and DP.t (77/80) showing marginal advantages.
 - **Stage 2 (Pick and Throw Trash)**: Performance varied, with DECO (65/80) and DECO.p (72/80) outperforming DP (38/80) and DP.t (43/80).
 - **Stage 3 (Close Lid)**: This stage showed the most significant impact of tactile sensing. Vision-only policies often struggled to confirm lid closure, leading to premature hand withdrawal. DECO.p achieved 62/80 successful trials, a substantial improvement over DECO (48/80), DP (30/80), and ACT (21/80). Tactile signals assisted in detecting successful contact and monitoring applied force for secure lid closure.

- **Task 4: Assembly**: This was identified as the most challenging and contact-rich task. DECO.p (55/80) significantly outperformed DECO (37/80) and all baselines (ACT 10/80, DP 15/80, DP.t 15/80). Tactile sensing was crucial for detecting contact during picking and maintaining a stable plug-socket configuration during insertion, especially for smaller objects or those prone to visual occlusion. All models showed better performance on larger objects.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on two assembly object pairs to evaluate the tactile adapter's effectiveness and the importance of decoupled injection:

- **DECO.cs (Coupled Tactile, Trained from Scratch)**: This variant, which simply appended tactile embeddings to the existing conditioning mechanism, performed similarly to the vision-only DECO (e.g., total 21/40 on Stage 3 for both objects), suggesting that naive fusion of tactile data is insufficient and can fail to leverage its specific information.

- **DECO.ds (Decoupled Tactile, Trained from Scratch)**: This variant, which used the same tactile preprocessing and cross-attention as DECO.p but was trained from scratch, achieved performance comparable to DECO.p (e.g., total 29/40 on Stage 3 for both objects for both DECO.ds and DECO.p). This confirms that the decoupled injection mechanism (cross-attention) is effective in extracting and exploiting tactile information.

- **DECO.p (Plugin Tactile Adapter)**: This variant demonstrated performance comparable to DECO.ds, despite updating significantly fewer parameters. DECO.p required optimizing only 7.97 million trainable parameters out of a total model size of 91.02 million, which is an order of magnitude less than the full retraining required for DECO.ds (89.41 million trainable parameters). This highlights the parameter efficiency and effectiveness of the plugin adapter design.

Overall, the findings indicate that tactile information is crucial for contact-rich tasks, and its effective integration requires a structured, decoupled approach rather than simple concatenation. The plugin tactile adapter offers a parameter-efficient method to introduce tactile capabilities into pre-trained policies without compromising performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces several contributions that could influence the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Methodological Advancement in Multimodal Policy Design**: The proposed DECO framework, with its decoupled multimodal Diffusion Transformer architecture, presents a structured approach to integrating heterogeneous sensory inputs (vision, proprioception, tactile). By assigning specific conditioning pathways, it accounts for the distinct roles and information densities of different modalities, potentially leading to more robust and higher-performing manipulation policies. This design could inspire future research into specialized multimodal integration strategies for complex tasks.

- **Parameter-Efficient Adaptation for Enhanced Dexterity**: The plugin tactile adapter demonstrates a practical and efficient method for enhancing the capabilities of existing visuomotor policies. The ability to integrate tactile sensing with minimal additional trainable parameters (less than 10% of the model) significantly reduces the computational cost and data requirements associated with extending robot skills. This approach facilitates the widespread adoption of tactile sensing in robotics, as it allows for incremental skill acquisition and adaptation without requiring complete model re-training. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in tasks requiring fine force control and contact awareness.

- **Contribution of a Comprehensive Real-World Dataset**: The release of DECO-50 addresses a notable gap in available datasets for bimanual dexterous manipulation. By providing a large-scale, real-world dataset that combines active binocular vision, extensive proprioception, and rich tactile feedback across a diverse set of bimanual tasks, DECO-50 serves as a valuable resource. It enables researchers to train and benchmark advanced multimodal policies, particularly for contact-rich interactions, thereby fostering further progress in the field.

- **Empirical Insights into Modality Utility**: The experimental results provide empirical evidence on the varying importance of different sensory modalities across different types of manipulation tasks. The finding that tactile sensing is critical for contact-rich tasks (e.g., assembly, waste disposal) but less so for simpler tasks (e.g., pick-and-place, material sorting of large objects) offers practical guidance for sensor selection and policy design. This insight can inform decisions on hardware configurations and data collection strategies, optimizing resource allocation based on task requirements.

- **Advancement Towards Human-like Dexterity**: By enabling robots to effectively utilize tactile feedback for precise contact detection, force monitoring, and handling of visual occlusions, this work contributes to bridging the gap between current robotic capabilities and human-level dexterity. The improved performance on complex assembly and contact-rich tasks indicates progress towards robots that can operate more reliably and adaptably in unstructured environments, potentially impacting applications in manufacturing, service robotics, and assistive technologies.

Future work outlined includes exploring temporal modeling for long-horizon tasks and applying the tactile adapter to larger-scale visual-language-action models, suggesting further avenues for enhancing generalizability and robustness in robotic manipulation.
