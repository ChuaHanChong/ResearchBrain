# Robust Policy Expansion for Offline-to-Online RL under Diverse Data Corruption

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University, Tencent, and Sun Yat-sen University developed Robust Policy EXpansion (RPEX), an Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning method to combat data corruption during both offline pre-training and online fine-tuning. RPEX mitigates corruption-induced policy heavy-tailedness, achieving an average score gain of 32.7% over vanilla IQL on D4RL Medium-Replay tasks under diverse corruption types.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies often perform suboptimally due to limited state-action space coverage in static datasets, necessitating online fine-tuning.
- Existing Offline-to-Online (O2O) RL methods largely assume clean offline data and online interactions, which is unrealistic as real-world data is prone to diverse corruption (e.g., in states, actions, rewards, dynamics).
- Data corruption not only affects Q-targets but also significantly exacerbates policy heavy-tailedness, leading to inefficient and uncontrolled online exploration by assigning non-zero probabilities to inferior actions.

## Method

- The proposed Robust Policy EXpansion (**RPEX**) method extends the ==Policy Expansion== (PEX) framework by integrating Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) into the action selection mechanism.
- RPEX modifies action selection probabilities by adding an IPW term to the standard Q-value-based weighting, where this term is clipped and inversely proportional to the action's policy probability and its advantage (Q-V).
- This IPW mechanism adaptively penalizes low-quality actions (with negative advantages), especially those with low policy probabilities, thereby rectifying heavy-tailed action distributions that result from data corruption.

## Results

- RPEX achieved substantial performance gains, showing an average score gain of **32.7%** over vanilla IQL and **28.7%** over vanilla RIQL on D4RL Medium-Replay tasks under random, adversarial, and mixed data corruption.
- The method consistently outperformed baselines across diverse corruption types (state, action, reward, dynamics) and challenging environments like AntMaze, achieving the highest scores in 11 out of 12 settings against RIQL-based methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the IPW component of RPEX effectively reduces policy heavy-tailedness, and also highlighted the importance of state normalization and specialized policy extraction methods (AlignIQL for state attacks) for robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Data corruption in O2O RL settings introduces significant heavy-tailedness into the policy's action distributions, which actively hinders efficient online exploration by directing the agent towards low-quality actions.
> - Addressing this policy heavy-tailedness is as crucial as, if not more important than, robustifying Q-functions alone for achieving robust and efficient O2O performance in corrupted environments.
> - Inverse Probability Weighting provides an effective mechanism to adaptively mitigate corruption-induced policy heavy-tailedness, selectively reducing the likelihood of poor, corruption-driven actions during online fine-tuning.
