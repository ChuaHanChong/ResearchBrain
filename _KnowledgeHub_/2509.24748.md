# Robust Policy Expansion for Offline-to-Online RL under Diverse Data Corruption

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University, Tencent, and Sun Yat-sen University developed Robust Policy EXpansion (RPEX), an Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning method to combat data corruption during both offline pre-training and online fine-tuning. RPEX mitigates corruption-induced policy heavy-tailedness, achieving an average score gain of 32.7% over vanilla IQL on D4RL Medium-Replay tasks under diverse corruption types.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies often perform suboptimally due to limited state-action space coverage in static datasets, necessitating online fine-tuning.
- Existing Offline-to-Online (O2O) RL methods largely assume clean offline data and online interactions, which is unrealistic as real-world data is prone to diverse corruption (e.g., in states, actions, rewards, dynamics).
- Data corruption not only affects Q-targets but also significantly exacerbates policy heavy-tailedness, leading to inefficient and uncontrolled online exploration by assigning non-zero probabilities to inferior actions.

## Method

- The proposed Robust Policy EXpansion (**RPEX**) method extends the ==Policy Expansion== (PEX) framework by integrating Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) into the action selection mechanism.
- RPEX modifies action selection probabilities by adding an IPW term to the standard Q-value-based weighting, where this term is clipped and inversely proportional to the action's policy probability and its advantage (Q-V).
- This IPW mechanism adaptively penalizes low-quality actions (with negative advantages), especially those with low policy probabilities, thereby rectifying heavy-tailed action distributions that result from data corruption.

## Results

- RPEX achieved substantial performance gains, showing an average score gain of **32.7%** over vanilla IQL and **28.7%** over vanilla RIQL on D4RL Medium-Replay tasks under random, adversarial, and mixed data corruption.
- The method consistently outperformed baselines across diverse corruption types (state, action, reward, dynamics) and challenging environments like AntMaze, achieving the highest scores in 11 out of 12 settings against RIQL-based methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the IPW component of RPEX effectively reduces policy heavy-tailedness, and also highlighted the importance of state normalization and specialized policy extraction methods (AlignIQL for state attacks) for robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Data corruption in O2O RL settings introduces significant heavy-tailedness into the policy's action distributions, which actively hinders efficient online exploration by directing the agent towards low-quality actions.
> - Addressing this policy heavy-tailedness is as crucial as, if not more important than, robustifying Q-functions alone for achieving robust and efficient O2O performance in corrupted environments.
> - Inverse Probability Weighting provides an effective mechanism to adaptively mitigate corruption-induced policy heavy-tailedness, selectively reducing the likelihood of poor, corruption-driven actions during online fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in complex decision-making tasks, but its deployment in real-world environments without reliance on simulators often faces challenges. Directly applying online RL in such settings can be expensive and unsafe due to its inherent exploratory nature. To mitigate these issues, Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL) was developed, enabling policy learning from pre-collected static datasets without further environmental interaction. However, offline datasets typically exhibit limited coverage of the state-action space, which can result in suboptimal policies.

A hybrid paradigm, Offline-to-Online RL (O2O RL), has emerged as a promising approach. This paradigm involves pretraining a policy on offline data and subsequently fine-tuning it through online interactions. This approach combines the benefits of leveraging existing data with the adaptability of online learning, analogous to pretraining and fine-tuning in other machine learning domains.

The majority of existing O2O RL methods, such as Nakamoto et al. (2023), Zhou et al. (2025), and Zhang et al. (2023), have primarily focused on scenarios where offline datasets are derived from simulators or clean environments, assuming relatively accurate data collection. In practical real-world applications, however, data is often susceptible to noise or even malicious corruption. Such corruption can occur during data collection by human annotators or sensors, and during online interactions. Previous research in Robust RL has predominantly focused on either the theoretical properties of robust offline RL or its certification under data corruption, with an emphasis on either test-time robustness (learning from clean data but defending against attacks during evaluation) or training-time robustness (learning from corrupted datasets and evaluating under clean conditions). For instance, Yang et al. (2023) highlighted the performance degradation of offline RL algorithms when exposed to corrupted offline data and introduced RIQL to enhance robustness by addressing heavy-tailed Q-targets.

Within the O2O RL context, existing works generally aim to address the conservatism of offline policies and enable efficient online exploration, such as calibrating value functions (Nakamoto et al., 2023) or using policy expansion (Zhang et al., 2023). However, the specific challenge of learning robust and efficient O2O policies under *both* offline and online data corruption across various data components (states, actions, rewards, dynamics) remains largely unaddressed. This paper positions itself by identifying and targeting this unexplored area, aiming to bridge the gap between robust offline RL and efficient O2O fine-tuning in the presence of continuous data corruption.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient Offline-to-Online (O2O) reinforcement learning method capable of effectively handling data corruption during both the offline pretraining phase and the online fine-tuning phase. This encompasses corruption in states, actions, rewards, and environmental dynamics.

The motivation for this work stems from two key observations:
1. **Prevalence of Data Corruption:** Real-world data, whether pre-collected or generated through online interactions, is often noisy, erroneous, or subject to malicious tampering. For example, human-annotated trajectories for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models may contain unintentional errors or deliberate corruptions. Such practical realities necessitate robust learning algorithms for O2O RL to be safely and effectively deployed.
2. **Impact of Corruption on Policy Behavior:** The authors observe that data corruption not only amplifies the heavy-tailedness of Q-targets, as previously noted in robust offline RL literature (e.g., RIQL), but also significantly exacerbates the heavy-tailedness of the policy itself. This policy heavy-tailedness is identified as a critical issue because it substantially impairs the efficiency of online exploration. Unlike intentional exploration mechanisms (e.g., entropy maximization), this heavy-tailedness introduces uncontrolled stochasticity, wherein the policy assigns non-zero probabilities to inferior or "bad" actions in an unpredictable manner. This uncontrolled exploration can lead to the collection of low-quality trajectories during online fine-tuning, thereby degrading overall performance. The paper provides empirical evidence, such as Kurtosis values of policies under various attacks, to support this observation, noting that RIQL, while robustifying Q-functions, can paradoxically increase policy heavy-tailedness.

Therefore, the core motivation is to address the dual challenge of data corruption affecting both offline and online phases by developing a mechanism that specifically mitigates the heavy-tailed behavior of the policy, thereby enabling more efficient and robust online exploration and fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Robust Policy EXpansion (RPEX), extends the Policy Expansion (PEX) framework by incorporating Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) to enhance robustness against data corruption in Offline-to-Online (O2O) Reinforcement Learning.

**Preliminaries and Problem Statement:**
The work operates within the standard Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework. Offline RL is defined as learning from a static dataset $D_{offline}$ without interaction, while O2O RL aims to obtain an optimal policy $\pi_{\theta}$ with minimal online interactions, leveraging a pretrained offline policy $\pi_{\beta}$ and value functions $Q_{\phi}, V_{\psi}$.
Data corruption is modeled by injecting random or adversarial noise into states ($s$), actions ($a$), rewards ($r$), and next states ($s'$) in both the offline buffer ($\hat{D}_{offline}$) and the online replay buffer ($\hat{D}$). This means the agent interacts with a clean environment, but the recorded transitions are corrupted before being stored and used for policy updates. Examples of random attacks (observation, action, reward, dynamics) are provided, as are adversarial dynamics attacks.

**Implicit Q-learning (IQL) and Robust IQL (RIQL):**
The base offline RL algorithms used are IQL and RIQL.
- **IQL:** Addresses Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) actions by using the state-conditional upper expectile of the action-value function $Q(s,a)$ to estimate the value function $V(s)$. The value objective ($L_V$) is learned using a special quadratic loss ($L_{\tau^2}(u) = |\tau - \mathbb{1}(u<0)|u^2$), the Q-function ($L_Q$) is learned via MSE loss, and the policy ($L_{\pi}$) is extracted using Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR), minimizing $-\exp(\alpha(Q(s,a) - V(s))) \log \pi_{\beta}(a|s)$.
- **RIQL:** Enhances IQL's robustness, particularly to dynamics attacks, by replacing the quadratic loss in the Q-function update with the Huber loss ($l^H_{\delta}(\cdot)$) and employing quantile-based Q estimators.

**Policy Expansion (PEX):**
PEX (Zhang et al., 2023) is a technique for O2O RL that mitigates performance degradation by utilizing a composite policy set $\Pi = [\pi_1, \ldots, \pi_K]$. Actions are selected based on the potential utilities (critic values) of policies within this set. For a policy set with two policies, $\Pi = [\pi_{\beta}, \pi_{\theta}]$ (a frozen offline pretrained policy and an online learnable policy), the probability of selecting an action $a_i$ from $\pi_i$ is given by $P_w[i] = \frac{\exp(Q_{\phi}(s, a_i)/\alpha)}{\sum_j \exp(Q_{\phi}(s, a_j)/\alpha)}$, where $\alpha$ is a temperature parameter.

**Robust Policy EXpansion (RPEX) - The Core Contribution:**
RPEX builds upon PEX by introducing Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) into the action selection mechanism. The motivation is to alleviate the heavy-tailedness induced by data corruption in the policy, which hinders online exploration.
The modified action selection probability in RPEX is:
$P_w[i] = \frac{\exp(Q_{\phi}(s, a_i)/\alpha) + \kappa w^{\pi_i}_{ipw}}{\sum_j \exp(Q_{\phi}(s, a_j)/\alpha) + \kappa w^{\pi_j}_{ipw}}$, where $\kappa$ is a coefficient.
The IPW term is defined as: $w^{\pi_i}_{ipw} = \text{CLIP}\left(\frac{Q_{\phi}(s, a_i) - V_{\psi}(s)}{\pi_i(a_i|s)}, \text{MIN}, \text{MAX}\right)$.
The clipping range (e.g., MIN=-10000, MAX=100) is designed to primarily penalize low-quality actions while mitigating the impact of large positive outliers.

**Intuition and Theoretical Justification:**
- **Intuition:** For low-quality actions ($Q-V < 0$), the IPW term significantly penalizes them, making them less likely to be selected. For high-quality actions ($Q-V > 0$), the clipped IPW values ensure that the weights are predominantly influenced by the Q-function. This adaptive weighting mechanism aims to eliminate exploration inefficiency caused by the heavy-tailed distribution of action probabilities under corruption.
- **Theoretical Justification (Proposition 6.1):** The authors show that the RPEX action selection objective (Eq. 6) maximizes a term combining standard maximum entropy RL objectives with a regularization term: $\kappa \frac{Q_{\phi}-V}{\pi_i(a_i|s)} P_w(i|s, a_1, a_2)$. This regularization term is interpreted as a rectification mechanism. It penalizes "bad" actions (where $Q-V < 0$) by reducing their selection probability, especially when their original probability $\pi_i(a_i|s)$ is low, indicating a corruption-induced anomaly. Conversely, good actions ($Q-V > 0$) are encouraged within the clipping limits.

#### 1.1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of reinforcement learning (RL) in real-world environments without reliance on simulators is a significant objective in the field. Traditional online RL often faces challenges related to cost and safety due to its inherent exploratory nature. Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL) emerged as an alternative, enabling policy learning from pre-collected datasets without further environment interaction. However, offline datasets typically suffer from limited coverage of the state-action space, frequently leading to the acquisition of suboptimal policies.

A recent development, Offline-to-Online RL (O2O RL), has gained prominence as a promising paradigm. This approach involves pretraining a policy on offline data and subsequently fine-tuning it through online interactions. While existing O2O RL methods have largely focused on mitigating the conservatism of offline policies through online exploration and improving sample efficiency, they predominantly assume that offline datasets and online interactions are relatively clean, often derived from simulators.

In contrast, real-world data collection, whether by human annotators or sensors, and subsequent online interactions are frequently susceptible to unpredictable noise or malicious corruption. Prior research on robust RL has primarily addressed robust *offline* RL, focusing on either training-time robustness (learning from corrupted datasets) or test-time robustness (learning from clean data but robust to evaluation-time attacks). For example, works like RIQL have investigated the robustness of Implicit Q-learning (IQL) under various data corruption scenarios in the offline setting, noting the emergence of heavy-tailed Q-targets under dynamics corruption.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the robustness of O2O RL under *diverse data corruption* occurring in *both* the offline pretraining and online fine-tuning phases remains largely unexplored. This is a crucial aspect for the practical deployment of O2O RL in real-world applications. The work therefore extends the scope of robust RL to the O2O paradigm, addressing a scenario where both historical data and live interactions are compromised.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient Offline-to-Online (O2O) reinforcement learning method capable of effectively operating under data corruption occurring in both the offline pre-training and online fine-tuning phases. The motivation for this objective stems from several observations regarding the challenges posed by data corruption in real-world RL deployments:

1. **Ubiquity of Data Corruption**: In practical scenarios, data collected for RL (e.g., by human annotators, sensors) is often noisy, incomplete, or even maliciously corrupted. This applies to both pre-collected offline datasets and data gathered during online interactions. Existing O2O RL approaches largely assume clean data, which limits their applicability in such real-world settings.

2. **Degradation of O2O Performance**: The presence of corrupted data in either the offline or online phase, or both, severely degrades the performance of conventional O2O RL algorithms. This suggests that current methods lack the necessary resilience to withstand realistic data imperfections.

3. **Heavy-tailed Policy Behavior**: The authors observe that data corruption not only induces heavy-tailed behavior in Q-targets (as identified in prior work on robust offline RL like RIQL) but also significantly exacerbates heavy-tailedness in the *policy itself*. This phenomenon is quantified using Kurtosis values, which show a substantial increase in policies trained under corrupted conditions compared to clean data. This policy heavy-tailedness is particularly pronounced under dynamics attacks, and notably, robust offline methods like RIQL, while mitigating Q-target heavy-tailedness, can inadvertently increase policy heavy-tailedness.

4. **Impaired Online Exploration**: The key insight motivating this work is that this corruption-induced heavy-tailedness in the policy substantially impairs the efficiency of online exploration. During the online fine-tuning phase, the offline-pretrained policy is used to guide exploration. A heavy-tailed policy, while superficially resembling some exploration mechanisms (e.g., entropy maximization), introduces uncontrolled stochasticity. It assigns non-zero probabilities to inferior or out-of-distribution actions due to corruption, rather than intentional exploration, thereby leading to the sampling of poor-quality or longer trajectories and hindering the discovery of optimal behaviors.

5. **Need for Robust Online Exploration**: The paper thus identifies a critical need for mechanisms that can alleviate policy heavy-tailedness during online exploration in O2O settings to facilitate more efficient and robust fine-tuning. This forms the direct motivation for integrating Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) into the online exploration strategy.

#### 3.1. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Robust Policy EXpansion (RPEX), addresses the challenges of data corruption in Offline-to-Online (O2O) Reinforcement Learning by integrating Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) into the Policy Expansion (PEX) framework.

**A. Core Components and Problem Setup:**

- **Offline Pre-training**: RPEX begins by utilizing an offline policy (π_β) and value functions (Q_ϕ, V_ψ) pretrained on a potentially corrupted offline dataset using an offline RL algorithm such as IQL or RIQL.
- **Online Fine-tuning**: The objective is to obtain an optimal policy (π_θ) through limited online interactions. Crucially, the online interaction phase is also assumed to be subject to data corruption, affecting transitions stored in the online replay buffer.
- **Policy Expansion (PEX)**: RPEX builds upon PEX, which employs a composite policy set Π = [π_β, π_θ]. Here, π_β is the frozen offline policy, and π_θ is a new, learnable online policy. Action selection in PEX is typically based on the estimated utility (Q-value) of actions generated by policies in Π, using a softmax-like distribution over actions: P_w[i] = exp(Q_ϕ(s, a_i)/α) / Σ_j exp(Q_ϕ(s, a_j)/α).

**B. Robust Policy Expansion (RPEX) with Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW):**

The core novelty of RPEX lies in modifying the action selection mechanism of PEX by incorporating IPW to mitigate policy heavy-tailedness:

- **Modified Action Selection**: The probability of selecting an action *a_i* from policy π_i is updated to:
 P_w[i] = (exp(Q_ϕ(s, a_i)/α) + κ * w_π_i_ipw) / (Σ_j exp(Q_ϕ(s, a_j)/α) + κ * w_π_j_ipw)
 where κ is a coefficient, and *w_π_i_ipw* is the Inverse Probability Weight for policy π_i, defined as:
 w_π_i_ipw = CLIP((Q_ϕ(s, a_i) - V_ψ(s)) / π_i(a_i|s), MIN, MAX)
 The clipping range (MIN, MAX) is set to (-10000, 100), asymmetrically penalizing negative advantages more.

- **Intuitive Explanation of IPW's Role**:
 - For **low-quality actions** (where Q(s, a_i) - V(s) < 0), the term (Q_ϕ - V) / π_i(a_i|s) will be a large negative value if π_i(a_i|s) is small. The IPW weight *w_π_i_ipw* will predominantly be determined by the negative clipping bound (MIN). This effectively penalizes low-quality actions, especially those that have low probability under the policy (suggesting they might be sampled due to corruption-induced heavy-tailedness rather than deliberate exploration).
 - For **high-quality actions** (where Q(s, a_i) - V(s) > 0), the IPW weight *w_π_i_ipw* will be clipped to MAX (100). In this case, the *exp(Q_ϕ(s, a_i)/α)* term primarily influences the action selection probability. This allows the mechanism to still favor high-value actions guided by the Q-function.
 - Overall, IPW adaptively adjusts action selection probabilities to reduce the influence of heavy-tailed actions that stem from corruption, thereby enabling more efficient exploration.

**C. Theoretical Justification (Proposition 6.1):**

The paper provides a theoretical justification by demonstrating that the modified action selection (Eq. 6) maximizes an objective function comprising two main terms:
1. **Maximum Entropy RL Objective**: E [Σ_i P_w(i|s, a_1, a_2) (Q_ϕ(s, a_i) - α log P_w(i|s, a_1, a_2))]. This term encourages both high rewards and broad exploration.
2. **Regularization Term**: E [Σ_i P_w(i|s, a_1, a_2) * κ * (Q_ϕ - V) / π_i(a_i|s)]. This term acts as a rectification mechanism. It penalizes "bad" actions (Q-V < 0), especially those with low probabilities (π_i(a_i|s)), which are likely products of corruption. Conversely, it encourages "good" actions (Q-V > 0), although their influence is controlled by the clipping mechanism.

**D. Algorithm Summary (Algorithm 1):**

The RPEX algorithm proceeds as follows:
1. **Offline Pre-training**: An offline RL algorithm (IQL or RIQL) is used to train a policy π_β and value functions Q_ϕ, V_ψ on a corrupted offline dataset. State normalization is applied.
2. **Initialization**: An online policy π_θ is initialized. The policy set is defined as Π = [π_β (frozen), π_θ].
3. **Online Interaction**: The agent collects online samples by selecting actions using the RPEX action selection mechanism (Eq. 6) from the composite policy Π. These online transitions are then subjected to simulated corruption before being added to an online replay buffer.
4. **Online Policy Updates**: The online policy π_θ and value function Q_ϕ are trained using batches from both the offline and online buffers. The underlying online RL algorithm for updates can be IQL or RIQL.
 - Specific techniques like state normalization and policy extraction methods (AlignIQL for state attacks, AWR otherwise) are incorporated.
 - The Updates-to-Data (UTD) ratio is carefully managed, with a high UTD ratio for action corruption but a standard ratio for other corruption types.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RPEX was conducted primarily on D4RL Medium-Replay tasks under various data corruption scenarios, including random, adversarial, and mixed attacks. The performance is reported as average normalized scores over multiple random seeds.

**A. Performance under Random Corruption (Medium-Replay Tasks):**

- **Overall Improvement**: RPEX demonstrated substantial performance gains. When using IQL as the base offline RL algorithm, IQL-RPEX achieved an average score gain of 32.7% compared to vanilla IQL. With RIQL as the base, RPEX (RIQL-RPEX) yielded an average improvement of 28.7% over vanilla RIQL.
- **Across Corruption Types**: RPEX consistently exhibited high performance, achieving the highest scores in 11 out of 12 settings when compared to RIQL-based methods. This was particularly evident under state, action, and reward corruption, where RPEX significantly outperformed baseline methods.
- **Impact of IPW**: The integration of IPW into PEX was effective, leading to an approximate 13.6% improvement in O2O performance over the vanilla PEX implementation.
- **Policy Heavy-tailedness Mitigation**: Visualizations (Figure 3) demonstrated that RPEX, by incorporating IPW, significantly reduced the heavy-tailedness of the action distributions generated by the offline pretrained policy under reward attack, making them closer to the clean policy's distribution. This correlated with improved training curves and higher normalized returns.

**B. Ablation Studies:**

- **Updates-to-Data (UTD) Ratio**: A high UTD ratio, while often beneficial in uncorrupted O2O settings for accelerating learning, was found to have a nuanced effect under data corruption. It primarily improved robustness against *action corruption*, likely due to the supervised learning-like nature of IQL-style policy updates. However, for other types of corruption, a high UTD ratio could degrade overall performance, possibly due to amplified Bellman backup errors from corrupted data.
- **Policy Type**: In contrast to offline settings where deterministic policies are sometimes preferred for robustness, deterministic policies were observed to degrade O2O performance. This is attributed to less efficient exploration when noise is merely added to deterministic actions.
- **State Normalization**: Applying state normalization consistently and significantly enhanced O2O performance across corruption types.
- **Hyperparameter κ (IPW Weight)**: RPEX's performance was relatively insensitive to the choice of κ, indicating robustness in its hyperparameter settings, although marginal improvements could be achieved through fine-tuning.
- **Policy Extraction Method**: The choice of policy extraction method was critical. AlignIQL (Eq. 8) yielded superior performance under state observation attacks, while the standard AWR method (Eq. 3) performed better in other scenarios.
- **Offline Buffer Retention**: The presence or absence of the offline dataset during online fine-tuning had minimal impact on RPEX's performance, demonstrating its robustness in terms of data requirements during the online phase.
- **Clipping Range for IPW**: The performance of RPEX was largely stable across a range of clipping values for the IPW weights, with minor differences observed except in extreme cases, further suggesting the method's robustness.

**C. Additional Experiments:**

- **Mixed and Adversarial Attacks**: RPEX consistently outperformed RIQL and RIQL-PEX under both random mixed and adversarial attack settings on the Hopper-MR task.
- **AntMaze Tasks**: On the challenging AntMaze-large and AntMaze-diverse tasks, RPEX significantly outperformed RIQL-PEX, achieving an average improvement of approximately 30.7% under dynamics and observation corruption. This highlights RPEX's effectiveness in sparse-reward, difficult exploration environments under corruption.
- **Varying Corruption Levels**: RPEX demonstrated robust performance across varying levels of online corruption (including the non-corrupted setting), outperforming RIQL-PEX by an average of about 16.8%. This suggests that RPEX's mechanism to mitigate heavy-tailedness is generally beneficial, even in scenarios with less severe or no corruption, where it can still rectify suboptimal exploration tendencies.
- **Training Cost**: While RPEX introduces additional computational overhead compared to PEX due to IPW calculations, this overhead is modest (approximately 8.1% increase over PEX) for a substantial performance gain (approx. 13.6% over PEX).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper carries several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of reinforcement learning:

- **Addressing a Critical Real-World Challenge**: The work highlights and directly addresses the largely unexplored challenge of data corruption in Offline-to-Online (O2O) RL, affecting both the offline datasets and online interactions. This aligns RL research more closely with real-world deployment scenarios where data quality cannot always be guaranteed, thereby enhancing the practical applicability of O2O RL.
- **Novel Insight into Policy Heavy-tailedness**: The paper makes a significant conceptual contribution by identifying policy heavy-tailedness as a key issue induced by data corruption, beyond the previously acknowledged heavy-tailedness in Q-targets. This insight is crucial for understanding the mechanisms by which corruption degrades O2O performance and for developing targeted mitigation strategies.
- **Robust and Efficient O2O Policy Learning**: RPEX, the proposed method, offers a simple yet effective solution by leveraging Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) within the Policy Expansion (PEX) framework. This design effectively mitigates policy heavy-tailedness, leading to more efficient online exploration and superior O2O performance under diverse corruption scenarios (random, adversarial, mixed attacks across states, actions, rewards, and dynamics).
- **Demonstrated State-of-the-Art Performance**: The extensive experimental results on D4RL datasets, including challenging AntMaze tasks, demonstrate that RPEX achieves competitive or superior O2O performance compared to existing baselines under a wide range of data corruption, establishing a new benchmark for robust O2O RL.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: By being the first to propose a robust O2O algorithm that specifically addresses both offline and online data corruption, this work lays a foundation for future research. It encourages further exploration into robust RL in more realistic and complex scenarios, potentially inspiring new techniques for handling various forms of data imperfections in sequential decision-making.

**Limitations:**

A noted limitation of RPEX is its reliance on access to exact action probabilities for the Inverse Probability Weighting mechanism. This requirement could hinder its direct integration with probability-agnostic models, such as certain diffusion models that are gaining traction in RL for their generative capabilities. Future work might explore extensions of RPEX or alternative robust O2O strategies that do not impose this constraint, thereby broadening the applicability of robust O2O RL to a wider array of policy representations.
