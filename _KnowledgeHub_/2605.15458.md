# Video Models Can Reason with Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Davis and Microsoft Research developed VideoRLVR, a framework that leverages verifiable rewards to train video diffusion models. This method enables models to generate visual trajectories that consistently adhere to task-specific rules, improving rule-consistent visual reasoning across tasks like Maze, FlowFree, and Sokoban, while also achieving a 40% reduction in training latency.

## Problem

- Existing video diffusion models prioritize perceptual plausibility and temporal coherence but struggle to reliably generate visual trajectories that satisfy explicit spatial, temporal, or logical constraints.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) to deterministic flow-matching models is challenging due to the lack of a tractable stochastic transition density needed for policy gradients.
- Video reinforcement learning is computationally intensive, and sparse binary rewards in complex reasoning tasks offer insufficient feedback for effective learning.

## Method

- Introduced **VideoRLVR**, a systematic framework using rule-based feedback for verifiable visual reasoning, adapting an ==SDE-based formulation== to convert deterministic denoising dynamics into stochastic transitions, enabling ==Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== on flow-matching models.
- Implemented an ==Early-Step Focus== strategy that restricts gradient backpropagation and SDE injection to the first few denoising steps, reducing training latency by approximately 40% without performance loss.
- Designed ==dense, decomposed, task-aware reward functions== derived from rule-based verifiers, providing fine-grained feedback for partial progress in complex reasoning tasks.

## Results

- VideoRLVR consistently improved success rates on all three reasoning domains: Maze by **6.1%**, FlowFree by **5.5%**, and Sokoban by **3.2%** over supervised baselines, outperforming extended supervised training and frontier LLMs.
- The Early-Step Focus strategy reduced training latency by approximately **40%** while maintaining equivalent F1 scores and success rates compared to full trajectory optimization.
- Achieved significant generalization, increasing the average score on out-of-domain VBVR tasks from **26.2 to 60.2**, demonstrating transferable visual reasoning abilities competitive with larger models despite using a smaller backbone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verifiable reinforcement learning can effectively teach video models to perform rule-consistent visual reasoning, moving beyond mere perceptual imitation.
> - Concentrating RL optimization on early denoising steps is efficient since they primarily determine coarse structure and reward-relevant aspects, and dense, decomposed reward functions are essential where sparse success signals would otherwise fail training.
> - Direct optimization within a visual latent space for visual reasoning tasks can be more effective than reasoning over tokenized representations, as highlighted by comparisons with large language models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have broadened their utility from content generation to sophisticated reasoning systems. This shift is partly attributed to their ability to externalize problem-solving through sequences of intermediate states. This development prompts a corresponding inquiry into whether video models, analogous to LLMs reasoning through tokens, can reason through sequences of visual frames. Videos inherently offer a foundation for this concept, where each frame can represent an intermediate visual state within a goal-directed process. This interpretation views generated videos not merely as motion synthesis but as temporally ordered chains of visual states that encode a visual reasoning trajectory, particularly in domains such as navigation, puzzle-solving, and embodied planning.

Despite this potential, contemporary video diffusion models primarily focus on perceptual quality, temporal coherence, and plausible motion. While some large-scale video models have demonstrated nascent visual reasoning capabilities, these abilities are not reliably elicited or verifiable under standard training objectives. A fundamental challenge is the discrepancy between perceptual plausibility and objective correctness. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on ground-truth solution videos can teach models the visual form of valid trajectories, but it does not directly optimize for the correctness of sampled outputs, potentially leading to imitative patterns that fail to satisfy underlying task rules.

This situation draws a parallel to the development of reasoning-oriented LLMs, where pre-training establishes broad generative competence, SFT teaches the format of reasoning traces, and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) provides the critical third stage for optimizing objective correctness. The current work identifies this RLVR component as a missing element in the training paradigm for video reasoning models.

Prior research has explored reinforcement learning for diffusion and flow-matching models, typically to align generative models with human preferences, perceptual objectives, or task-specific rewards, such as aesthetics, text rendering, image fidelity, or motion quality. Simultaneously, a growing body of work investigates reasoning in video generation models, viewing them as potential world simulators or physical reasoners, yet often highlighting their limitations in reliably producing rule-correct trajectories. Verifiable reinforcement learning, which has been instrumental in LLMs by providing scalable supervision through objective correctness signals (e.g., mathematical equivalence or executable code tests), offers a precedent. This paper extends this paradigm from language outputs to video trajectories, where verification involves visual, temporal, and process constraints, addressing the gap in direct optimization of video models for rule-based reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the observed mismatch between the increasing perceptual realism of video generation models and their current inability to reliably perform verifiable reasoning. While these models excel at generating visually plausible and temporally coherent sequences, they often fail to satisfy explicit spatial, temporal, or logical constraints inherent in complex tasks. This limitation is particularly pronounced in problem domains that require goal-directed planning, puzzle-solving, or adherence to specific rules.

The key objectives and motivations of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Transition Video Models Beyond Perceptual Imitation to Verifiable Reasoning:** The primary goal is to move video diffusion models from being primarily optimized for plausible generation to being capable of verifiable reasoning. This involves enabling them to generate visual trajectories that not only look realistic but also objectively satisfy predefined logical and physical rules of a task.
2. **Introduce a Systematic RLVR Framework for Video Models:** The paper aims to develop and present VideoRLVR, a practical and systematic recipe for integrating reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards into the optimization process of video diffusion models. This framework is intended to serve as the "missing piece" in the training paradigm, analogous to how RLVR has enhanced reasoning in LLMs.
3. **Address Challenges of Applying RL to Deterministic Flow-Matching Models:** Standard flow-matching models employ deterministic ODE solvers, which complicate the application of likelihood-ratio policy gradients due to the lack of a tractable stochastic transition density. A key objective is to adapt an SDE-based formulation to introduce stochastic transitions, thereby making flow-matching models compatible with GRPO-style optimization.
4. **Enhance Training Efficiency for Video RL:** Video reinforcement learning is computationally intensive, requiring generation and backpropagation through high-dimensional spatio-temporal latents over numerous denoising steps. A motivation is to develop strategies that reduce this computational burden without sacrificing performance, focusing on the most critical phases of the generation process.
5. **Design Robust Reward Mechanisms for Sparse Reasoning Tasks:** In many complex reasoning tasks, successful outcomes are rare, leading to sparse binary reward signals that provide insufficient feedback for learning. The objective is to design dense, decomposed reward functions that break down overall task success into verifiable structural components, offering more informative feedback for partial progress and facilitating learning in low-success-rate environments.
6. **Evaluate Performance on Objective, Rule-Verifiable Benchmarks:** The research aims to rigorously evaluate the effectiveness of VideoRLVR on a multi-task suite of procedurally generated reasoning domains (Maze, FlowFree, Sokoban) where solution correctness can be objectively checked by rule-based verifiers. This evaluation is critical to demonstrate improved in-domain rule-based correctness.
7. **Assess Transferability of Learned Reasoning Abilities:** A further objective is to determine whether the visual reasoning abilities acquired through VideoRLVR generalize beyond the specific training domains to out-of-domain reasoning tasks, thereby demonstrating the broader applicability and robustness of the approach.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to bridge the gap between advanced video synthesis and the demand for models that can engage in reliable, verifiable visual reasoning, thereby expanding the capabilities and potential applications of generative video models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce VideoRLVR, a systematic recipe for optimizing video diffusion models with rule-based feedback to enable verifiable visual reasoning. The approach formulates video reasoning as the generation of verifiable visual trajectories and comprises three main components: an SDE-GRPO optimization backbone, an Early-Step Focus strategy for efficiency, and dense decomposed rewards.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Video reasoning is defined as a conditional generation task where a model generates a temporal sequence of visual states $V = \{I_0, I_1, \ldots, I_{F-1}\}$ given an initial image $I_0$ and a textual instruction $T$. The generated sequence must satisfy task-specific correctness criteria, distinguishing it from standard video synthesis focused on perceptual quality. The reverse denoising process of flow-matching video generation is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) over latent variables. In this MDP, the state at denoising step $k$ is the noisy video latent $x_{t_k}$, and the action is the model velocity prediction $\hat{v}_{\theta}(x_{t_k}, t_k, c)$. A challenge arises because standard flow matching uses a deterministic ODE solver, which hinders the direct application of likelihood-ratio policy gradients due to the absence of a tractable stochastic transition density.

#### 3.2. SDE-GRPO for Video Reasoning

To overcome the deterministic nature of ODE solvers, the authors adopt an SDE-based formulation, converting the deterministic denoising dynamics into stochastic transitions.
- **Stochastic Denoising Transitions:** For a discretized denoising schedule, a Gaussian transition $\pi_{\theta}(x_{t_{k+1}} | x_{t_k}, c) = \mathcal{N}(x_{t_{k+1}}; \mu_{\theta}(x_{t_k}, t_k, c), \sigma_k^2 I)$ is defined, where $\mu_{\theta}$ is the mean update and $\sigma_k^2$ is the SDE transition variance. This enables closed-form log-probabilities for policy gradients.
- **GRPO Objective:** Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is utilized to estimate relative advantages from groups of sampled outputs, eliminating the need for a separate critic. For a group of $G$ sampled videos, verifier-derived rewards $R(V, c)$ are computed and normalized to obtain advantages $A_i$. A dimension-normalized log-ratio $\log \rho_{i,k}$ is computed for each sample $i$ and denoising step $k$. The policy loss $L_{\text{policy}}$ uses PPO-style clipping: $L_{\text{policy}} = -\mathbb{E}_{i,k}[\min(\rho_{i,k}A_i, \text{clip}(\rho_{i,k}, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)A_i)]$.
- **Regularization:** An additional KL penalty $L_{\text{KL}} = \mathbb{E}_k[\frac{1}{D}\frac{\|\mu_{\theta} - \mu_{\text{ref}}\|_2^2}{2\sigma_k^2}]$ regularizes the policy against a reference model (the SFT checkpoint), preserving the generative prior. The final objective is $L_{\text{VideoRLVR}} = L_{\text{policy}} + \beta L_{\text{KL}}$.

#### 3.3. Early-Step Focus for Efficient Video RL

To mitigate the high computational cost of video RL, an Early-Step Focus strategy is introduced.
- **Rationale:** Early denoising steps (high-noise) primarily determine coarse structure, object placement, and long-range planning, while later steps (low-noise) refine local appearance.
- **Strategy:** During RL optimization, full denoising trajectories are sampled for generation and reward evaluation. However, stochastic perturbation, log-probability computation, and gradient backpropagation are restricted to the first $L$ (e.g., 10) out of $K$ (e.g., 20) denoising steps. This reduces training latency by approximately 40% while preserving performance. The policy loss is adjusted to $L_{\text{ESF}} = -\mathbb{E}_{i,k \le L}[\min(\rho_{i,k}A_i, \text{clip}(\rho_{i,k}, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)A_i)] + \beta L_{k \le L, \text{KL}}$.

#### 3.4. Verifiable Reward Design and Acquisition

A crucial aspect of VideoRLVR is the automatic parsing and evaluation of generated videos using rule-based verifiers.
- **Data Acquisition:** Task instances are synthesized using rule-based planners that generate initial configurations, solve them with valid action sequences, and render the resulting state trajectories into videos. Environment metadata (grid layouts, object states, etc.) is retained for automatic verification and dense reward computation. Each discrete environment action is mapped to a unique frame transition, making videos directly interpretable as reasoning trajectories.
- **Task-aware Reward Function:** A dispatcher identifies the task $T(c)$ for each conditioning input $c$ and applies the corresponding task-specific reward function $R_{T(c)}(V,c)$.
- **Dense Reward Formulations:** Instead of sparse binary success rewards, dense decomposed rewards are designed to provide informative feedback for partial progress, particularly in low-success-rate domains.
 - **Maze:** $R_{\text{maze}} = R_{\text{conn}} \cdot R_{\text{wall}}$, a multiplicative reward for start-to-goal path connectivity and absence of wall violations.
 - **FlowFree:** $R_{\text{ff}} = \lambda_{\text{valid}}R_{\text{valid}} + \lambda_{\text{pres}}R_{\text{pres}} + \lambda_{\text{conn}}R_{\text{conn}} + \lambda_{\text{fill}}R_{\text{fill}}$, an additive combination of path validity, endpoint preservation, 4-connected color regions, and grid coverage.
 - **Sokoban:** $R_{\text{sok}} = \lambda_{\text{state}}R_{\text{state}} + \lambda_{\text{proc}}R_{\text{proc}}$, a combination of final-state box placement on target cells and process validity (fraction of valid transitions under movement rules).

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Tasks:** Maze (spatial connectivity, obstacle constraints), FlowFree (globally consistent non-overlapping paths), and Sokoban (object interaction, irreversible transitions, long-horizon reasoning). These tasks are procedurally generated to ensure rule-verifiability, scalability, and varied complexity.
- **Dataset:** A multi-task suite of 30,000 training samples (10,000 per task) and 3,000 held-out test samples (1,000 per task). Samples consist of an input image, text instruction, and an 81-frame ground-truth video (480x832 resolution) with varied color themes.
- **Base Model:** Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, a state-of-the-art video generation model.
- **SFT Baseline:** The base model fine-tuned on ground-truth solution videos using the standard flow matching objective, serving as the initial and reference policy for RL.
- **Training Configuration:** VideoRLVR is initialized from the SFT checkpoint and trained for 1 epoch. Key hyperparameters include group size $G=16$, $K=20$ denoising steps, $L=10$ early steps for backpropagation, learning rate $5 \times 10^{-6}$, and KL coefficient $\beta=0.04$.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Trajectory alignment metrics (Precision, Recall, F1 at pixel-, cell-, or action-level) measuring alignment with reference solutions, and Symbolic Success Rate, verifying whether generated videos satisfy underlying task rules.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VideoRLVR primarily focuses on its ability to improve rule-consistent visual reasoning on verifiable benchmarks, its efficiency, and its generalizability.

#### 4.1. RLVR Consistently Outperforms Supervised Baselines

VideoRLVR demonstrates consistent improvements in success rates across all three reasoning domains compared to the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Epoch 5 checkpoint used for initialization.
- **Maze:** VideoRLVR improves the success rate by 6.1%.
- **FlowFree:** VideoRLVR improves the success rate by 5.5%.
- **Sokoban:** VideoRLVR improves the success rate by 3.2%.
The RL-optimized model also surpasses the performance of several proprietary (Sora 2, Kling V3, Veo 3.1) and open-source (CogVideoX1.5, HunyuanVideo, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B) video generation models on these specific visual reasoning tasks, indicating the efficacy of targeted verifiable reinforcement learning.

#### 4.2. Superior Scaling on High-Complexity Tasks

VideoRLVR exhibits greater robustness and scaling advantages on more challenging tasks compared to extended supervised learning.
- **Extended SFT vs. VideoRLVR:** When compared to an SFT Epoch 10 checkpoint (representing further supervised training), VideoRLVR maintains a performance edge.
 - On Maze, VideoRLVR establishes a 3.2% margin over SFT Epoch 10 and shows less performance degradation with increasing maze grid size.
 - On FlowFree, VideoRLVR improves over SFT Epoch 10 by 5.4%, whereas continued SFT yielded minimal improvement.
 - On Sokoban, VideoRLVR improves by 3.4% over SFT Epoch 10, while continued SFT showed a slight degradation.
These trends suggest that verifiable rewards provide an optimization signal distinct from and more effective than that derived solely from additional imitation training, particularly in difficult contexts.

#### 4.3. Comparison with LLMs

The study benchmarked frontier LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5.5 Pro, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Pro) on the Maze task.
- **Performance Gap:** LLMs showed a notable performance decay on maze tasks. For instance, GPT-5.5 Pro achieved a success rate of 66.0%, significantly lower than VideoRLVR's 72.2%.
- **Representation Bottleneck:** This divergence suggests a representation bottleneck for LLMs, which reason over tokenized renderings, whereas video models operate directly on a visual latent space that preserves visual topological relationships crucial for visual reasoning tasks. Direct visual trajectory generation and optimization appear more effective for these types of problems.

#### 4.4. Out-of-Domain (OOD) Results

VideoRLVR was evaluated on the out-of-domain split of VBVR, a benchmark designed for broader visual reasoning assessment.
- **Generalization:** VideoRLVR substantially improved performance over a 5B baseline model, increasing the average score from 26.2 to 60.2, with gains observed across all VBVR-OOD reasoning categories (Abstract, Knowledge, Perception, Spatial, Transition).
- **Competitiveness with Larger Models:** VideoRLVR achieved an average score of 60.2, competitive with the larger 14B VBVR-Wan2.2 model's 61.0, despite utilizing a smaller 5B backbone and less training data. This indicates that VideoRLVR learns transferable visual reasoning abilities that generalize beyond its specific training domains.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Group Size:** Performance generally scales positively with group size up to G=16, attributed to more stable estimation of group-relative advantages. Beyond G=16, diminishing returns are observed, and computational costs increase linearly. G=16 was selected as a practical trade-off.
- **Early-Step Focus (ESF):** Restricting gradient backpropagation and SDE injection to the first 10 denoising steps (out of 20 total) yielded nearly identical F1 scores and success rates as computing over the full trajectory. Crucially, this strategy reduced training time by approximately 40%. This confirms that early denoising steps carry the majority of reward-relevant structural signal for visual reasoning.
- **KL Constraint:** The KL-divergence constraint (controlled by $\beta$) was found to be essential. Without it ($\beta=0$), the model could exhibit "reward-hacking" behavior, producing visually implausible patterns that satisfy verifier criteria without maintaining generation quality. A constant penalty of $\beta=0.04$ successfully anchored optimization to the original visual quality.
- **Dense Reward Design:** Sparse binary success rewards were sufficient for tasks where the baseline success rate was already decent (e.g., Maze). However, for high-complexity, low-success-rate tasks (e.g., FlowFree, Sokoban), sparse rewards led to extreme gradient sparsity and an ineffective cold-start. Dense decomposed rewards addressed this by providing partial credit for intermediate structural properties, enabling learning before full task success became frequent.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Case Studies

Qualitative examples from Maze, FlowFree, and Sokoban illustrate that while the SFT baseline often captures the visual format of tasks, it frequently fails to satisfy underlying rules (e.g., disconnected paths, inconsistent color connectivity, invalid object transitions). In contrast, the VideoRLVR-optimized model consistently generates solutions that satisfy symbolic constraints checked by verifiers, producing connected paths, coherent grid solutions, and valid object transitions while preserving overall visual structure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the fields of video generation and artificial intelligence, offering implications for both fundamental understanding and practical applications.

**Advancement in Video Generation Capabilities:** The introduction of VideoRLVR marks a notable step in moving video diffusion models beyond purely perceptual imitation. Previously, models primarily focused on generating visually plausible and temporally coherent videos. This work demonstrates a method to enhance their capacity for objective, rule-consistent visual reasoning, addressing a critical limitation in their utility for tasks requiring logical correctness.

**Filling a Research Gap in Video Model Training:** By drawing an analogy to the success of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in reasoning-oriented Large Language Models (LLMs), the paper identifies and addresses a "missing piece" in the training paradigm for video reasoning models. VideoRLVR provides a systematic and practical recipe for applying RL with verifiable rewards to video models, establishing a new framework for optimizing their logical abilities.

**Enhanced Efficiency for Video Reinforcement Learning:** The Early-Step Focus strategy is a significant methodological contribution. Video RL is computationally intensive due to the high-dimensional nature of video data. By demonstrating that restricting optimization to the early denoising phases can reduce training latency by approximately 40% without compromising performance, the paper makes video RL more computationally feasible and scalable for complex tasks.

**Robustness in Sparse-Reward Environments:** The design of dense decomposed rewards is crucial for tackling the challenges of learning in tasks with inherently sparse success signals, where full success is rare. By providing fine-grained, verifiable feedback on partial progress, this approach enables effective learning even in complex domains like FlowFree and Sokoban, which would otherwise suffer from severe gradient sparsity.

**Demonstrated Generalizability of Reasoning Skills:** The positive results on out-of-domain benchmarks like VBVR-OOD suggest that VideoRLVR not only improves performance on specific training tasks but also cultivates transferable visual reasoning abilities. This indicates that the models are learning more fundamental logical and structural invariants rather than merely overfitting to training data.

**Rethinking Visual Reasoning Paradigms:** The comparison with state-of-the-art LLMs on visual maze tasks highlights a potential "representation bottleneck" for language models when faced with inherently visual reasoning problems. The superior performance of VideoRLVR suggests that directly operating within and optimizing a visual latent space can be more effective for visual reasoning than attempting to solve such tasks through language-token representations alone. This finding could influence future research directions in multimodal AI, suggesting that task-specific modalities might offer inherent advantages.

**Potential for Broader Applications:** The ability of video models to generate verifiable reasoning trajectories has significant implications for various applications:
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Generating correct action sequences for navigation, manipulation, and planning in complex environments.
- **Automated Design and Simulation:** Creating valid structural designs or simulating physical processes that adhere to specific rules.
- **Educational Tools:** Developing interactive puzzle-solving or problem-solving environments where correctness can be automatically verified.
- **Content Generation with Constraints:** Producing videos that meet explicit logical or factual criteria, moving beyond purely aesthetic considerations.

In conclusion, this work provides a foundational step towards building more intelligent and reliable video generation systems that can reason with verifiable consistency, opening new avenues for research and application in the broader field of artificial intelligence.
