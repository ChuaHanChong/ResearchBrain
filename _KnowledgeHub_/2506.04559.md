# Reasoning-Aligned Perception Decoupling for Scalable Multi-modal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The RAPID framework from Southern University of Science and Technology, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, and Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab introduces a method to decouple perception from reasoning in multi-modal large language models (MLLMs). It leverages a novel Visual Perception Optimization (VPO) algorithm to align MLLM-generated textual descriptions with downstream reasoning tasks, achieving an 11.2% accuracy improvement on multi-modal reasoning benchmarks by integrating powerful external text-only LLMs without costly retraining.

## Problem

- Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) lag behind text-only LLMs in complex reasoning tasks, often due to outdated or less capable internal LLM components.
- Upgrading the internal LLM components of MLLMs is prohibitively expensive, requiring extensive and costly vision-language alignment retraining (trillions of tokens), making frequent updates impractical.
- Existing MLLM improvement methods are either constrained by the base LLM's inherent capabilities or focus solely on caption quality without explicit alignment to final reasoning success.

## Method

- The **RAPID** (Reasoning-Aligned PerceptIon Decoupling) framework introduces a modular pipeline that separates multi-modal perception from textual reasoning, with an MLLM generating query-relevant captions and tentative solutions.
- A powerful external text-only LLM (e.g., Qwen3-8B) then performs the reasoning based on these textual outputs, enabling independent upgrades of the reasoning component without MLLM retraining.
- Visual Perception Optimization (VPO) is a novel policy gradient reinforcement learning algorithm designed to align the MLLM's perceptual output with the correctness of the final reasoning outcome.

## Results

- RAPID achieved an average accuracy of **53.2%** (an **11.2%** improvement over the baseline MLLM) on multi-modal reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MathVista, MMMU) when applying Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** with Qwen3-**8B**.
- The framework demonstrated an improved performance-to-size trade-off, with RAPID-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** (**15B** parameters total) outperforming larger monolithic MLLMs like MM-Eureka-**32B**.
- VPO-optimized MLLMs generated higher-quality, more comprehensive, and query-relevant captions, validated by GPT-4o and human evaluations, with the training process being highly cost-effective (under $300 for significant gains).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling multi-modal perception from textual reasoning allows MLLMs to efficiently leverage state-of-the-art text-only LLMs, bypassing costly vision-language alignment retraining for reasoning upgrades.
> - Optimizing MLLM perceptual outputs directly for downstream reasoning success, rather than just descriptive quality, is crucial for improving multi-modal reasoning performance.
> - The VPO algorithm, with its reward mechanism and caption penalty, effectively trains MLLMs to produce reasoning-aligned and comprehensive textual representations without reward hacking, and generalizes across different LLM backbones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have led to significant progress in text-based reasoning, particularly in complex domains such as mathematics and science. Models like OpenAI-o1 and Qwen3 have demonstrated capabilities in step-by-step reasoning that surpass previous generations. However, multi-modal large language models (MLLMs), such as Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma3, and InternVL3, have not achieved comparable reasoning proficiency. This performance gap is often attributed to the MLLMs’ reliance on older or less capable internal LLM components.

A significant challenge in the development of advanced MLLMs is the prohibitive cost associated with upgrading their internal LLM modules. Integrating a state-of-the-art LLM into an existing MLLM typically requires a complete and costly vision-language alignment retraining process, often involving trillions of tokens. This expense renders frequent updates impractical for many research and development efforts.

Existing approaches to improve MLLM reasoning capacity generally fall into two categories:
1. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) or Knowledge Distillation on Existing MLLMs**: Methods like VL-Rethinker, MM-EUREKA, NoisyRollout, R1-OneVision, Vision-R1, and ReVisual-R1 attempt to enhance performance through RL or supervised fine-tuning on reasoning data. However, their ultimate success is constrained by the inherent reasoning capabilities of their underlying base LLMs, which may not be state-of-the-art.
2. **Caption-then-Reason Pipelines**: Prior work, including studies by Tiong et al. (2022), Guo et al. (2022), Hu et al. (2022), Gou et al. (2024), and Lu et al. (2025), explores decoupling visual perception from textual reasoning. These pipelines typically use vision-language models (VLMs) or MLLMs to generate captions from visual inputs, which are then fed to a separate LLM for reasoning. Existing efforts primarily focus on improving the quality or relevance of caption generation, for instance, by selecting query-relevant image patches or enhancing captioning datasets.

This work, RAPID (Reasoning-Aligned PerceptIon Decoupling), distinguishes itself from previous caption-then-reason pipelines by two key innovations:
1. It expands the generated textual representation to include both a detailed, query-relevant caption and a *tentative solution*. This comprehensive output is designed to capture all essential visual information for subsequent reasoning.
2. It introduces a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, Visual Perception Optimization (VPO), which explicitly aligns the MLLM's perceptual output with the *correctness of the final reasoning task outcome*. This differs from prior methods that do not optimize captions based on downstream reasoning success. By modularizing the MLLM's reasoning component, RAPID enables flexible replacement with any powerful, external, text-only LLM reasoner, offering a path to overcome the costly retraining cycle inherent in monolithic MLLM architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the advanced reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art text-only LLMs and the relative limitations of current multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) in complex reasoning tasks, particularly in multi-modal math and science. While text-based LLMs have demonstrated significant breakthroughs in emulating deliberate, step-by-step reasoning, MLLMs often lag due to their reliance on outdated or less sophisticated internal LLMs.

The critical problem identified by the authors is the prohibitive cost and computational expense associated with upgrading these internal LLM components within MLLMs. Integrating a new, more powerful LLM typically necessitates a full vision-language alignment retraining process, which involves vast amounts of data and computational resources, making frequent updates impractical. This scenario raises a fundamental question: Can MLLMs efficiently leverage the advanced reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art text-only LLMs without undertaking redundant and costly vision-language retraining?

To address this challenge, the key objectives of the RAPID framework are:

1. **To introduce a Perception-Reasoning Decoupling pipeline**: This pipeline aims to redefine the MLLM's role by focusing its primary function on multi-modal perception. The MLLM is tasked with converting complex multi-modal inputs (images and queries) into comprehensive textual representations. This modularization is intended to allow the reasoning component to be flexibly replaced by any advanced external LLM, thereby circumventing the burdensome and expensive retraining of vision-language alignment.
2. **To propose Visual Perception Optimization (VPO)**: This novel policy gradient algorithm is designed to align the MLLM's perceptual outputs with the objective of generating correct final answers. VPO employs a reinforcement learning feedback loop, rewarding the MLLM based on the correctness of answers generated by the external LLM reasoner using the MLLM's perceptual outputs as context. This ensures that the MLLM learns to produce faithful and query-relevant captions that are optimized for downstream reasoning.
3. **To enable scalable multi-modal reasoning**: By combining the decoupling pipeline and VPO, the goal is to establish a novel inference-time scaling paradigm. Once an MLLM is trained and optimized with VPO, it can be paired with progressively more powerful external LLM reasoners without requiring any further retraining of the MLLM itself. This "plug-and-play" capability aims to provide consistent performance improvements and a practical solution for the rapid evolution of both MLLMs and reasoning LLMs.
4. **To achieve significant performance gains on multi-modal reasoning benchmarks**: The research seeks to empirically demonstrate that RAPID can yield notable improvements in accuracy on challenging multi-modal reasoning tasks compared to existing methods, while maintaining computational efficiency in the training process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Reasoning-Aligned PerceptIon Decoupling (RAPID) framework addresses the challenges of multi-modal reasoning through a two-pronged approach: a modular pipeline for decoupling perception and reasoning, and a reinforcement learning algorithm for optimizing the perceptual output.

#### 3.1. Perception-Reasoning Decoupling

This pipeline separates the processing of multi-modal inputs into two distinct stages:

- **Perception Stage**: An MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL series) functions as a perception module. Given an image (I) and a query (q), the MLLM generates a textual representation, denoted as O_p. The authors explored various strategies for generating O_p to capture essential visual information for downstream reasoning:
 - `none`: An empty set, serving as a baseline.
 - `cap`: A standard image caption generated using a generic prompt.
 - `qcap`: A query-relevant caption generated with a prompt incorporating the user query.
 - `sol`: A "tentative solution," where the MLLM attempts to solve the problem directly, providing this output as context rather than a final answer.
 - Combinations: `cap+sol` and `qcap+sol`.
 Initial experiments indicated that `cap+sol` generally provided better results, but `qcap+sol` demonstrated greater potential when coupled with the subsequent optimization step, becoming the default choice for the full RAPID system. The inclusion of a tentative solution alongside captions is a key distinguishing feature from prior caption-then-reason methods.

- **Reasoning Stage**: A powerful text-only LLM reasoner (e.g., R1-Distilled-7B or Qwen3-8B) receives the original query (q) and the consolidated perceptual outputs (O_p). These inputs are structured by a specific reasoning prompt (P_r) before being fed to the LLM. A core advantage of this decoupled architecture is that the textual outputs form a universal interface, enabling independent upgrades of the LLM reasoner without necessitating costly retraining of the MLLM or its vision-language alignment components.

#### 3.2. Visual Perception Optimization (VPO)

To address the challenge that the MLLM's generated perceptual outputs (captions and tentative solutions) might not be optimally aligned with the correctness of the final reasoning outcome, the authors introduce Visual Perception Optimization (VPO). VPO is a novel policy gradient algorithm implemented through a reinforcement learning (RL) feedback loop.

- **Objective Design**: VPO is inspired by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The MLLM, acting as the visual captioning policy (π_θ), generates a group of G caption rollouts for a given image-query pair. The policy is updated using a clipped surrogate loss based on a normalized advantage (ˆA_i), which quantifies how much better a given rollout's reward is compared to the group's baseline, along with a KL-penalty to stabilize training.

- **Reward Design**: Unlike standard RL for text generation where the model's output is the final answer, in VPO, the MLLM generates an intermediate caption. To obtain a reward signal for this caption:
 1. For each caption rollout (o_i), the external reasoning LLM is prompted to generate a final answer (y_i).
 2. The reward (ˆR_i) is then determined by comparing this generated answer (y_i) against the ground-truth answer (y_gt). A binary reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) is used.
 3. **Reward Hacking Prevention**: The authors observed instances of "reward hacking" where the MLLM would directly generate a solution instead of a descriptive caption. To counteract this, a penalty (λ) is applied to the reward if the rollout (o_i) leads to a correct answer but does not contain a discernible caption (checked via a `hasCap(·)` function, itself implemented with few-shot prompting). This mechanism encourages the MLLM to genuinely produce descriptive captions rather than shortcuts.

- **MLLM Optimization with GRPO**: In addition to VPO for captions, the tentative solutions generated by the MLLM are improved using GRPO. This uses a simpler, rule-based reward function based on the correctness of the tentative solution. In experiments, the MLLM is typically optimized with GRPO first, followed by VPO.

VPO offers two advantages:
1. **Reasoning-Aligned Captions**: It directly optimizes MLLMs to produce captions that are functionally aligned with downstream reasoning, as dictated by the final answer's correctness.
2. **LLM-Agnostic Generalization**: The optimization is LLM-agnostic, meaning the optimized MLLM generates natural language outputs that can be consumed by any LLM reasoner. This enables a one-time MLLM alignment that generalizes across various LLM backbones in a "plug-and-play" fashion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of RAPID demonstrates its effectiveness across several multi-modal reasoning benchmarks and highlights its architectural advantages.

**Overall Performance Gains:**
- RAPID consistently achieved notable performance improvements on a diverse set of multi-modal reasoning benchmarks, including MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, MMMU, WeMath, DynaMath, and LogicVista.
- For instance, applying RAPID to Qwen2.5-VL-7B (a 7-billion parameter MLLM) and pairing it with Qwen3-8B (an 8-billion parameter LLM) resulted in an average accuracy of 53.2%, representing an 11.2% improvement over the original Qwen2.5-VL-7B model (42.0%).
- When RAPID was applied to Qwen2.5-VL-72B and paired with GPT-OSS-120B, it achieved the highest average accuracy of 58.0% among all compared models, surpassing several proprietary MLLMs and state-of-the-art open-source MLLMs.

**Performance-Size Trade-off:**
- RAPID demonstrated an improved trade-off between performance and total model size. For example, Qwen2.5-VL-7B enhanced with RAPID (totaling 15B parameters, MLLM + LLM) outperformed larger models like MM-Eureka-32B, InternVL3-38B, and Ovis2-34B.
- Similarly, Qwen2.5-VL-32B with RAPID surpassed VL-Rethinker-72B and InternVL3-78B, indicating that the decoupled approach can achieve higher performance with relatively smaller overall model footprints.

**Comparison with Other Methods:**
- RAPID outperformed existing caption-then-reason methods such as ECSO and OmniCaptioner, which the authors attribute to the synergistic use of tentative responses and the VPO optimization algorithm.
- The method maintained comparable performance with the original MLLMs on general benchmarks (e.g., MME, MMBench-v1.1, MM-Vet, SEED-Image, MMStar, RW-QA, MMT-Bench, BLINK) when evaluated in a "non-thinking" mode, suggesting that RAPID provides a targeted enhancement for reasoning abilities without degrading general vision-language capabilities.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Decoupling Impact**: The perception-reasoning decoupling pipeline alone provided a substantial 5.5% average accuracy gain over the baseline MLLM, highlighting the immediate benefit of leveraging a more powerful external LLM for reasoning.
- **GRPO Effectiveness**: Applying GRPO to optimize the MLLM's tentative solutions further improved performance by an additional 3.0%.
- **VPO Contributions**: VPO, even without the caption penalty, contributed a further 1.7% gain. The inclusion of the caption penalty, which prevents the MLLM from "reward hacking" by outputting direct solutions instead of detailed captions, added another 1.0%, bringing the total VPO contribution to 2.7% over the GRPO-only model. The caption penalty was empirically shown to be critical for maintaining high ratios of valid caption generation during training.
- **Complementarity of Components**: Both GRPO and VPO were found to be complementary, with sequential application yielding distinct performance boosts. Removing either component resulted in suboptimal performance.
- **Captioning Strategy**: While standard captions (`cap+sol`) initially outperformed query-relevant captions (`qcap+sol`) before VPO, this trend reversed after VPO application. This suggests that `qcap` is more amenable to optimization under VPO due to its query guidance.
- **Reward LLM Selection**: The choice of LLM for computing rewards during VPO training was critical. R1-Distilled-7B performed optimally, striking a balance. Stronger LLMs like Qwen3-8B inadvertently incentivized shorter, less detailed captions, while weaker ones (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) led to over-long and potentially inaccurate ones.
- **Reward Input Content**: Using only the caption (`qcap`) for reward computation (instead of `qcap+sol`) during VPO training yielded better results. This was because including tentative solutions allowed the reasoning LLM to shortcut, relying on solutions rather than evaluating caption quality.

**Generalization and Scaling with Different LLMs:**
- The performance gains from VPO generalized effectively to new, unseen LLM reasoners at inference time. The gap between VPO-trained and baseline MLLMs was sustained or widened when paired with more powerful LLMs, confirming the LLM-agnostic nature of the VPO optimization.
- RAPID exhibited scalability, with absolute performance generally trending upward when paired with more capable LLMs. Qwen3-8B was identified as the optimal LLM for the inference stage, balancing performance and model size.

**Caption Quality Evaluation:**
- Pairwise comparisons, conducted by GPT-4o acting as a judge and validated by human assessments, indicated that VPO-optimized MLLMs generated higher-quality captions. These captions were more comprehensive, accurate, and contained details relevant to answering the query, demonstrating VPO's effectiveness in aligning perceptual outputs with reasoning requirements.

**Generalization Across MLLMs:**
- RAPID's decoupling pipeline and VPO components demonstrated consistent gains when applied to other MLLMs, such as InternVL3-8B, suggesting broad generalizability across different MLLM architectures.

**Cost Analysis:**
- RAPID training was found to be highly cost-effective, requiring less than $300 for significant performance improvements (e.g., ~$75 for GRPO and ~$214 for VPO for a 3B model). This cost is significantly less than 0.1% of the estimated cost for pre-training or fine-tuning an MLLM with new LLM alignment, making it an accessible solution for researchers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RAPID framework presents a significant advancement in the development of multi-modal reasoning models, offering solutions to several current limitations and opening new avenues for research and application.

**Addressing the MLLM Reasoning Gap:** The most direct impact of RAPID is its ability to bridge the performance gap between state-of-the-art text-only LLMs and existing MLLMs in complex reasoning tasks, particularly in scientific and mathematical domains. By enabling MLLMs to leverage external, highly capable LLM reasoners, RAPID allows multi-modal systems to immediately benefit from the rapid advancements in textual reasoning without waiting for monolithic MLLM architectures to catch up.

**Cost-Effective Scalability and Democratization of Advanced Reasoning:** RAPID circumvents the prohibitively high computational cost of repetitive vision-language alignment retraining. This "plug-and-play" inference-time scaling paradigm allows for continuous performance improvement simply by swapping in more powerful LLM reasoners, without requiring any retraining of the MLLM perception module. This efficiency makes advanced multi-modal reasoning more accessible to smaller research teams and individual practitioners, thereby democratizing the development and deployment of sophisticated multi-modal AI.

**Refined Role of MLLMs:** The work redefines the primary role of MLLMs from end-to-end reasoners to sophisticated "perceptual modules." This dedicated focus on generating high-quality, reasoning-aligned textual representations of visual input is critical, as the quality of this intermediate representation directly influences downstream reasoning success. The Visual Perception Optimization (VPO) algorithm introduces a novel mechanism for explicitly aligning these perceptual outputs with the objective of correct final answers, which is a significant methodological contribution.

**Potential as a Data Engine:** The modular nature of RAPID and its ability to generate high-quality reasoning trajectories could be leveraged as a "data engine." The meticulously aligned textual outputs from the perception module, paired with expert reasoning, can generate vast amounts of synthetic, high-quality multi-modal reasoning data. This synthetic data could then be used to train future, more unified MLLM architectures, addressing the bottleneck of scarce high-quality multi-modal reasoning datasets.

**Broad Applicability and Robustness:** The demonstrated generalizability of RAPID across different MLLM backbones (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3) indicates its robust applicability across the MLLM ecosystem. Furthermore, the preservation of general vision-language capabilities on standard benchmarks confirms that RAPID's targeted enhancements for reasoning do not compromise the model's broader functionalities.

In conclusion, RAPID provides a practical, efficient, and scalable solution for advancing multi-modal reasoning. Its decoupling strategy and novel optimization technique represent a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to fully harness the power of modern LLM reasoners, making sophisticated multi-modal AI more attainable and adaptable to future innovations.
