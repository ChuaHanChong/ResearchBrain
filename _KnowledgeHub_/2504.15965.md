# From Human Memory to AI Memory: A Survey on Memory Mechanisms in the Era of LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab present a systematic survey mapping human memory concepts to AI memory mechanisms in large language models. The paper introduces a three-dimensional, eight-quadrant (3D-8Q) taxonomy for AI memory, classifying it by object, form, and time, and uses this framework to categorize and analyze existing memory solutions in LLMs.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) face limitations in retaining and utilizing information over extended interactions due to finite context windows, hindering prolonged coherence and personalized conversations.
- A systematic and unified framework for understanding and developing AI memory, particularly one drawing parallels to human memory, was lacking in the rapidly evolving field of LLMs.
- Existing categorizations of AI memory often relied solely on time (short-term vs. long-term), neglecting crucial dimensions like the memory's object (what is remembered) and form (how it is stored).

## Method

- The authors developed a systematic survey approach that first meticulously details human memory mechanisms to serve as a blueprint for AI memory design.
- They proposed a novel 3D-8Q memory taxonomy for AI, organizing memory based on its object (personal or system), form (parametric or non-parametric), and time (short-term or long-term).
- Existing research and systems in LLM memory were systematically reviewed and categorized according to the proposed 3D-8Q framework, demonstrating its utility in structuring the field.

## Results

- Successfully established parallels between human memory categories (sensory, working, explicit, implicit) and their functional equivalents in LLM-driven AI systems.
- Introduced a novel and comprehensive three-dimensional, eight-quadrant (3D-8Q) memory taxonomy for AI, classifying memory by Object (personal/system), Form (non-parametric/parametric), and Time (short-term/long-term).
- Systematically categorized a broad range of existing AI memory research and systems within the 3D-8Q framework, detailing specific examples for personal and system memory across different forms and durations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A comprehensive taxonomy for AI memory requires considering the object (personal/system), form (parametric/non-parametric), and time (short-term/long-term) dimensions, moving beyond simple temporal classifications.
> - Drawing explicit parallels between human memory categories (sensory, working, explicit, implicit) and AI memory mechanisms provides a bio-inspired framework for designing more robust and human-like AI systems.
> - Current AI memory research, when organized under the proposed 3D-8Q taxonomy, reveals clear areas for improvement and future focus, such as multimodal memory, stream memory, and comprehensive integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have revolutionized artificial intelligence, enabling capabilities in language understanding, generation, and complex reasoning that were previously unimaginable. However, despite their impressive capabilities, current LLMs face inherent limitations, particularly concerning "memory" or the ability to retain and utilize information over extended interactions. Traditional LLMs operate within a finite "context window," meaning they can only process and remember information presented within a limited number of preceding tokens. This fundamental constraint hinders their ability to engage in prolonged, coherent, and personalized conversations, or to perform multi-step, dynamic tasks that require recalling past actions or user preferences.

The broader research landscape is actively exploring various mechanisms to augment LLM memory, moving beyond the simple context window. Existing approaches include retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems that pull relevant information from external knowledge bases, fine-tuning models on specific data, and designing more sophisticated agent architectures. While these methods address aspects of memory, a unified and principled framework for understanding and developing AI memory, especially one drawing parallels to the highly evolved human memory system, has been largely lacking.

This survey paper directly addresses this gap. It positions itself as a systematic review that not only summarizes existing memory mechanisms for LLMs but critically analyzes their relationship to human memory. By proposing a novel, multi-dimensional taxonomy (3D-8Q), the work brings much-needed structure and clarity to a rapidly evolving and often fragmented field. It aims to inspire the development of more robust, flexible, and human-like memory systems for LLMs, moving beyond ad-hoc solutions to a more comprehensive and biologically informed design paradigm. This contributes to the foundational understanding of "AI memory" and provides a conceptual roadmap for future research, pushing LLMs towards greater intelligence, personalization, and autonomy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey paper stems from the recognition that while previous research has touched upon memory mechanisms in AI, there is a significant lack of a comprehensive and systematic review that explicitly connects LLM memory to its human counterpart. The authors identify that existing work often categorizes memory solely based on time (short-term vs. long-term), neglecting other crucial dimensions such as the *object* of memory (what is remembered) and the *form* of memory (how it is stored).

To address this critical gap, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Systematically Define and Relate AI Memory to Human Memory:** The first objective is to thoroughly analyze the categories of human memory (sensory, working, explicit, implicit, and their sub-types) and establish direct parallels and relationships with memory mechanisms observed or engineered in LLM-driven AI systems. This comparative analysis aims to provide biological inspiration for AI memory design.
2. **Propose a Comprehensive Memory Taxonomy:** The paper aims to introduce a novel and systematic classification method for AI memory. This method, termed the "three-dimensional, eight-quadrant (3D-8Q) memory taxonomy," organizes AI memory based on three core dimensions:
- **Object:** Whether the memory pertains to *personal* user interactions or *system*-internal processes.
- **Form:** Whether the memory is stored *parametrically* (within the model's weights) or *non-parametrically* (externally).
- **Time:** Whether the memory is *short-term* (transient, current session) or *long-term* (persistent, cross-session).
3. **Systematically Organize and Summarize Existing Work:** Based on the proposed 3D-8Q taxonomy, the paper intends to review and categorize current memory-related research in the LLM era. This involves dissecting the field into "personal memory" (enhancing personalization) and "system memory" (improving complex task execution) and then further subdividing based on the form and time dimensions.
4. **Identify Open Problems and Future Directions:** Finally, the survey aims to highlight the existing challenges and limitations in current AI memory research and to outline promising avenues for future development in the field of LLM-driven AI systems.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the need for a unified, biologically inspired, and functionally comprehensive framework for understanding, classifying, and advancing memory capabilities in LLMs, thereby steering AI technology towards greater intelligence, adaptability, and human-centric design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a structured, survey-based approach to systematically analyze and classify memory mechanisms in LLM-driven AI systems. Their methodology is primarily conceptual and analytical, rather than experimental.

The core of their approach involves:

1. **Human Memory as a Blueprint:** The survey begins by meticulously detailing the intricate mechanisms of human memory. It categorizes human memory into short-term (sensory and working memory) and long-term memory (explicit—episodic and semantic; and implicit—procedural). For each category, the paper describes its function, duration, and the underlying cognitive processes of encoding, storage, and retrieval, along with related processes like consolidation, reconsolidation, reflection, and forgetting. This foundational understanding serves as the primary source of inspiration and a benchmark for AI memory.

2. **Drawing Parallels between Human and AI Memory:** The authors then explicitly map human memory concepts to their counterparts in LLM-driven AI systems. For instance, human sensory memory is paralleled with the initial processing of inputs by AI; working memory with KV-Caches and intermediate thought processes; explicit memory with non-parametric external databases (episodic) and parametric knowledge within model weights (semantic); and implicit memory with learned skills and parametric adjustments. This comparison highlights similarities and areas for bio-inspired design.

3. **Developing the 3D-8Q Memory Taxonomy:** This is the central methodological contribution. The authors propose a novel classification framework for AI memory based on three fundamental dimensions:
- **Object Dimension:** Distinguishes between *personal memory* (user-specific interactions and data) and *system memory* (internal processes, reasoning steps, tool usage).
- **Form Dimension:** Differentiates between *non-parametric memory* (external, retrievable databases like vector stores or knowledge graphs) and *parametric memory* (knowledge encoded within the LLM's trainable weights).
- **Time Dimension:** Categorizes memory as *short-term* (transient, within current interaction session) or *long-term* (persistent, across multiple sessions or over extended periods).
 These three dimensions combine to create an eight-quadrant taxonomy, providing a comprehensive grid for classifying any memory mechanism in an LLM system.

4. **Categorization and Review of Existing Research:** The bulk of the survey involves populating this 3D-8Q taxonomy with existing research. The authors systematically review relevant papers and systems, organizing them under two main sections:
- **Personal Memory:** This section is further subdivided into:
 - *Contextual Personal Memory (Non-Parametric):* Covering multi-turn dialogue handling (Quadrant I) and memory retrieval-augmented generation (Quadrant II), including detailed discussions on memory construction, management, retrieval, usage, and benchmarks.
 - *Parametric Personal Memory:* Discussing memory caching for acceleration (Quadrant III) and personalized knowledge editing (Quadrant IV).
- **System Memory:** This section is subdivided into:
 - *Contextual System Memory (Non-Parametric):* Focusing on reasoning & planning enhancement (Quadrant V) and reflection & refinement mechanisms (Quadrant VI).
 - *Parametric System Memory:* Examining KV management & reuse (Quadrant VII) and parametric memory structures (Quadrant VIII).
 For each sub-category, the authors provide examples of specific models, frameworks, and techniques from the literature, illustrating how they fit into the proposed taxonomy.

5. **Identifying Open Problems and Future Directions:** Based on their comprehensive review and analysis, the authors synthesize the current limitations and articulate several promising research directions. These include the transition from unimodal to multimodal memory, static to stream memory, specific to comprehensive memory, exclusive to shared memory, individual to collective privacy, and rule-based to automated evolution.

This systematic decomposition and re-composition of the field, guided by bio-inspired principles and a novel taxonomy, allows the authors to provide a structured, in-depth, and forward-looking analysis of memory in LLM-driven AI systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's main findings and results are primarily conceptual and organizational, providing a comprehensive framework and structured overview of memory mechanisms in LLM-driven AI systems, largely inspired by human cognition.

1. **Established Parallels Between Human and AI Memory:** The survey thoroughly details human memory categories (sensory, working, explicit, implicit) and successfully maps them to their functional equivalents in AI systems.
- **Sensory Memory (Human) ↔ Initial Information Processing (AI):** AI systems briefly hold raw input data (text, images) before further processing, analogous to human sensory memory.
- **Working Memory (Human) ↔ Real-time Context & Processing (AI):** AI working memory includes short-term contextual information in multi-turn dialogues (personal memory), intermediate reasoning steps (system memory), and KV-Caches for efficient inference (parametric short-term memory).
- **Explicit Memory (Human) ↔ Accessible Knowledge (AI):** AI explicit memory encompasses non-parametric long-term storage of user data (analogous to episodic memory) and parametric knowledge embedded in model parameters (semantic memory).
- **Implicit Memory (Human) ↔ Learned Procedures & Skills (AI):** AI implicit memory includes non-parametric learning from past experiences and successes/failures (procedural memory) and task-related knowledge internalized within model parameters.

2. **Proposed 3D-8Q Memory Taxonomy:** The central conceptual result is the introduction of a novel, comprehensive taxonomy for AI memory, categorized along three dimensions:
- **Object Dimension:** Memory is classified as *personal* (user-centric, e.g., preferences, interaction history) or *system* (AI-centric, e.g., internal reasoning, planning, tool use).
- **Form Dimension:** Memory is classified as *non-parametric* (stored externally, e.g., databases, vector stores) or *parametric* (encoded within the LLM's trainable parameters).
- **Time Dimension:** Memory is classified as *short-term* (transient, within a single session) or *long-term* (persistent, across sessions).
 These three dimensions combine to form eight distinct quadrants, each representing a specific type of AI memory with a defined role and function (e.g., Quadrant II: Personal, Non-Parametric, Long-Term, akin to Episodic Memory; Quadrant VII: System, Parametric, Short-Term, for KV Management & Reuse).

3. **Comprehensive Categorization of Existing Work:** The survey systematically organizes a vast array of existing research and systems within the proposed 3D-8Q framework:
- **Personal Memory:**
 - **Non-Parametric:** Multi-turn dialogue context (Quadrant I, e.g., ChatGPT) and long-term personal memory for personalization via RAG (Quadrant II, e.g., MemoryBank, ChatGPT Memory). This section details construction (key-value, graph, vector formats), management (deduplication, reflection, updating), retrieval (SQL, fuzzy, dense retrieval), and usage in applications like recommender systems. Numerous benchmarks are also identified.
 - **Parametric:** Memory caching for inference acceleration (Quadrant III, e.g., Prompt Cache) and personalized knowledge editing (Quadrant IV, e.g., Character-LLM, AI-Native Memory) to embed user-specific information.
- **System Memory:**
 - **Non-Parametric:** Short-term memory for reasoning and planning enhancement (Quadrant V, e.g., ReAct, Reflexion) and long-term memory for reflection and refinement of strategies (Quadrant VI, e.g., Buffer of Thoughts, Voyager).
 - **Parametric:** Short-term KV (Key-Value) cache management and reuse for inference efficiency (Quadrant VII, e.g., vLLM, RAGCache) and long-term parametric memory structures (Quadrant VIII, e.g., Memorizing Transformer, MemoryLLM) for integrating new knowledge and forming a continuously evolving knowledge base.

4. **Identification of Open Problems and Future Directions:** The paper concludes by outlining critical areas for future research:
- Transition from unimodal to **multimodal memory**, integrating diverse data types.
- Shift from static to **stream memory**, enabling real-time, continuous adaptation.
- Development of **comprehensive and collaborative memory** systems that integrate various memory types.
- Evolution from exclusive to **shared memory** among different AI systems.
- Addressing the complex issue of **collective privacy** in data sharing.
- Advancing from rule-based to **automated evolution** of AI systems.

These findings collectively provide a structured understanding of the current state of AI memory research, identify its limitations, and offer a clear conceptual roadmap for future advancements, significantly contributing to the theoretical foundation and practical development of LLM-driven AI systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the context of Large Language Models and AI agents. Its potential impacts are multi-faceted:

1. **Structuring and Unifying a Fragmented Field:** The most immediate and significant impact is the introduction of the "three-dimensional, eight-quadrant (3D-8Q) memory taxonomy." The concept of "memory" in AI, especially with LLMs, has been loosely defined and explored through various ad-hoc techniques. This taxonomy provides a much-needed, comprehensive, and systematic framework for categorizing, understanding, and comparing diverse memory mechanisms. It moves beyond simple temporal distinctions, integrating *object* and *form*, which are critical for the design of practical and robust AI systems. This structured view can help researchers pinpoint gaps, avoid redundant efforts, and foster more holistic approaches to memory design.

2. **Guiding Future Research and Development:** By clearly mapping human memory principles to AI memory and identifying open problems, the paper acts as a conceptual roadmap for future research. It encourages bio-inspired innovation, suggesting that a deeper understanding of human cognition can unlock more effective and efficient AI memory architectures. The outlined future directions, such as multimodal memory, stream memory, comprehensive memory, and shared memory, provide concrete, high-impact areas for the research community to focus on. This guidance is invaluable for both academic researchers seeking novel problems and industry labs aiming to build more capable AI products.

3. **Enhancing LLM Capabilities and Applications:** The survey underscores how advanced memory systems are foundational for next-generation LLM applications. Improved memory directly translates to:
- **Enhanced Personalization:** AI systems can remember individual user preferences, history, and personality traits across sessions, leading to more tailored and satisfying interactions (e.g., in personal assistants, recommender systems).
- **Improved Reasoning and Planning:** AI agents can retain intermediate thoughts, past successful strategies, and failures, enabling more complex, multi-step problem-solving and continuous self-improvement.
- **Increased Efficiency and Adaptability:** Parametric memory optimization (like KV caching) improves inference speed and reduces computational costs, making LLMs more practical for real-world deployment. Adaptive memory systems also allow LLMs to continually learn and evolve.

4. **Promoting Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** By explicitly drawing parallels between neuroscience and AI, the paper fosters interdisciplinary dialogue. It invites cognitive scientists to contribute their understanding of biological memory to AI design and encourages AI researchers to explore new paradigms inspired by human intelligence. This cross-pollination of ideas can lead to breakthroughs that might not emerge from purely domain-specific research.

5. **Addressing Practical Challenges:** The discussion of privacy (shifting from individual to collective privacy) acknowledges the societal implications of advanced AI memory systems. This foresight is crucial for developing ethical and secure AI technologies as they become more integrated into daily life.

In conclusion, "From Human Memory to AI Memory" is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a foundational piece that redefines the conversation around memory in AI. By providing a structured framework, drawing biological inspiration, and outlining critical future challenges, it sets the stage for the development of more intelligent, adaptive, and human-aligned LLM-driven AI systems.
