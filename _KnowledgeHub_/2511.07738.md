# From Exploration to Exploitation: A Two-Stage Entropy RLVR Approach for Noise-Tolerant MLLM Training

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage entropy-guided Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) method dynamically optimizes Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), transitioning from an exploration phase with entropy maximization to an exploitation phase with entropy minimization. This approach consistently demonstrates improved robustness against annotation noise and enhances overall performance across various MLLM tasks and backbones.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for MLLMs heavily relies on high-quality labeled data, which is often scarce and prone to annotation noise in real-world applications.
- Existing noise-handling methods (external-signal-based, internal-signal-based, static entropy-based) suffer from limitations such as inconsistent performance, limited effectiveness, or issues with policy convergence and response diversity.
- Current entropy optimization in RLVR is static, failing to dynamically balance the trade-off between exploration (for diversity) and exploitation (for confident predictions) across different training stages.

## Method

- The research introduces "Two-Stage Entropy-Guided GRPO," an RLVR approach built upon ==Group Relative== Policy Optimization (GRPO) that incorporates dynamic token-level entropy optimization.
- The method employs a unified objective function where a time-varying scalar coefficient `λ(τ)` switches between positive and negative values to guide entropy.
- Training proceeds in two phases: an initial "Exploration" phase (entropy maximization) to promote diversity and resist noisy labels, followed by an "Exploitation" phase (entropy minimization) to consolidate knowledge and refine accuracy.

## Results

- The "GRPO w. Two." method consistently outperformed standard GRPO and single-stage entropy variants across diverse noise levels (**0%** to **100%** noisy labels) on GUI grounding, fine-grained classification, and open-vocabulary object detection tasks.
- For GUI grounding with Qwen2.5-VL-**3B**, the method maintained **80.2%** accuracy at **50%** noise, showing a **4.8%** absolute gain over standard GRPO at **100%** noise and **4.2%** at **50%** noise.
- Training dynamics showed entropy steadily increasing during the exploration phase (up to **400%** of initial value) and then rapidly decreasing during exploitation (to **20%** of peak), confirming the intended dynamic shift in uncertainty management.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dynamic, phased approach to entropy optimization effectively manages the exploration-exploitation trade-off in noise-tolerant RLVR, outperforming static entropy regularization.
> - Starting with entropy maximization during exploration is crucial for resisting overfitting to noisy labels and for robust advantage estimation within GRPO by promoting diverse outputs.
> - Subsequent entropy minimization allows the model to consolidate acquired knowledge and refine prediction accuracy by concentrating probability mass on high-reward trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), specifically as applied to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). RLVR has demonstrated efficacy in tasks requiring reasoning, such as mathematical problem-solving, formal verification, and code generation, and has been extended to multimodal domains including image classification, object grounding, and medical reasoning. A notable algorithm in this area is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), used in models like DeepSeek-R1.

A significant challenge for RLVR in real-world applications is its reliance on high-quality labeled data, which is frequently scarce and contains annotation noise. Existing methods to address this issue fall into three categories:
1. **External-Signal-Based Methods:** These approaches use external tools (e.g., compilers) or other LLMs (e.g., LLM-as-a-Judge) to provide verifiable signals. Their performance can be inconsistent due to variations in LLM capabilities and task-specific tool limitations. Test-time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) is an example that generates noisy pseudo labels via majority voting.
2. **Internal-Signal-Based Methods:** These methods derive rewards directly from model outputs (e.g., random or format rewards) without relying on labeled data. While flexible, their effectiveness is limited as internal signals may not align well with specific task objectives.
3. **Entropy-Based Methods:** These methods utilize entropy to guide training, often by minimizing predictive entropy to exploit prior knowledge or by using self-certainty signals. However, existing approaches tend to apply entropy optimization in a static manner, either purely maximizing or purely minimizing it, potentially leading to convergence issues or suppression of necessary response diversity.

This paper identifies the limitations of these existing methods, particularly under noisy supervision where static entropy optimization can be problematic. It positions its contribution as a dynamic, two-stage entropy optimization method that schedules exploration and exploitation phases, aiming to enhance noise tolerance and robustness in RLVR for MLLMs, addressing a recognized gap in the effective application of RLVR in real-world, noisy data environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the noise tolerance of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) training. This objective is motivated by several key challenges observed in real-world scenarios:

1. **Dependency on High-Quality Labeled Data:** RLVR methods, while effective, are highly dependent on datasets with accurate and clean labels. However, real-world datasets are often characterized by substantial annotation noise, making the direct application of standard RLVR challenging.
2. **Limitations of Existing Noise-Handling Approaches:**
 - **External-Signal-Based Methods:** These methods, which use external signals like compilers or LLM-as-a-Judge, suffer from inconsistent performance due to varying LLM capabilities and the task-specific nature of tools.
 - **Internal-Signal-Based Methods:** Approaches relying on internal signals (e.g., random or format rewards) often have limited effectiveness because these signals may not sufficiently align with specific task objectives.
 - **Static Entropy-Based Methods:** Current entropy-based methods (e.g., EMPO, one-shot RL) typically apply either pure entropy minimization or pure entropy maximization throughout training.
 - **Pure entropy minimization** can lead to the model becoming overconfident in potentially incorrect labels and suppressing the response diversity that is crucial for the reward ranking signal in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This can trap the model in sub-optimal deterministic behaviors.
 - **Pure entropy maximization**, while promoting diversity and mitigating overfitting to noisy labels, can make policy convergence difficult as the probability mass is not encouraged to concentrate on high-reward actions.
3. **Need for Dynamic Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** The research identifies that a static approach to entropy optimization overlooks its dynamic role across different training stages. An effective strategy would dynamically manage the trade-off between exploration (generating diverse outputs to discover correct solutions, especially in the presence of noise) and exploitation (producing confident outputs based on acquired knowledge).

Therefore, the motivation is to develop a novel RLVR training method that explicitly guides the model from an exploration phase, where diversity is encouraged to resist overfitting to noisy labels and support robust advantage estimation, to an exploitation phase, where confidence is promoted to consolidate learned knowledge and refine accuracy. This dynamic scheduling is hypothesized to provide superior robustness and performance in noisy supervision settings compared to static approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named "Two-Stage Entropy-Guided GRPO," builds upon Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) and, specifically, the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm. It introduces a token-level entropy optimization strategy that dynamically shifts between maximization and minimization phases during training.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

The foundation of the approach is GRPO, an RLVR algorithm that uses rule-based verification functions (e.g., exact answer matching) to assign binary rewards.
- **Response Sampling:** For each input `x`, the current policy `π_θ(·|x)` samples `K` responses `{y_1..., y_K}`.
- **Reward Assignment:** Each response `y_i` receives a binary reward `R(y_i, y*)` based on correctness.
- **Group-wise Advantage Estimation:** GRPO's key innovation is normalizing rewards within each group to reduce variance. The advantage `A_i` for a response `y_i` is computed as `(r_i - mean(r(y_1:K))) / std(r(y_1:K))`. This normalization helps in estimating the relative value of different responses within a group.
- **Policy Gradient:** The policy is updated using a gradient `∇_θ L_GRPO` that incorporates these advantages.
- **Clipped Surrogate Objective:** In practice, a clipped surrogate objective is used to constrain updates and maintain stability, preventing aggressive parameter changes relative to the old policy.

#### 3.2. Token-Level Entropy

The method utilizes token-level entropy as a granular measure of uncertainty.
- **Definition:** For an input `x` and partially generated tokens `y_<t`, the per-token entropy `H_t(x, y)` is calculated as the negative sum of `π_θ(v | x, y_<t) log π_θ(v | x, y_<t)` over all vocabulary tokens `v`.
- **Sequence Entropy:** The token-level entropy for an entire sequence `y` is the average of `H_t(x, y)` over all `T` tokens.
- **Entropy Loss:** The `L_entropy` term is defined as the negative expected value of this token-level entropy across the sampled responses, averaged over `K` responses. A positive `L_entropy` term (when minimizing) encourages confident, low-entropy outputs, while a negative `L_entropy` (when maximizing) encourages diverse, high-entropy outputs.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Entropy-Guided GRPO Framework

The core of the proposed method is a dynamic scheduling of the token-level entropy optimization, transitioning from exploration to exploitation.
- **Unified Objective Function:** The total loss `L_total` is defined as `L_GRPO + λ(τ) L_entropy`, where `λ(τ)` is a scalar coefficient that varies with the training step `τ`.
- **Phase 1: Exploration (τ ≤ τ_switch):**
 - In the initial phase of training (up to a predefined switch step `τ_switch`), `λ(τ)` is set to a positive constant `+λ_max`.
 - This positive coefficient leads to **entropy maximization**, encouraging the model to produce more diverse outputs.
 - **Purpose:** This promotes exploration, mitigates premature convergence to noisy labels by preventing overconfidence in potentially incorrect signals, and ensures sufficient intra-group variation, which is essential for GRPO's informative advantage estimation.
- **Phase 2: Exploitation (τ > τ_switch):**
 - After the switch point `τ_switch`, `λ(τ)` is set to a negative constant `-λ_min`.
 - This negative coefficient leads to **entropy minimization**, encouraging the model to produce confident and deterministic outputs.
 - **Purpose:** This shifts the optimization objective towards consolidating the knowledge acquired during the exploration phase and refining prediction accuracy, allowing the policy to concentrate probability mass on high-reward trajectories.
- **Hyperparameters:** `λ_max` and `λ_min` are positive constants, and `τ_switch` is the critical hyperparameter determining the transition point.
- **Algorithm:** The training process iteratively samples responses, computes rewards and advantages, calculates the GRPO loss and the dynamically weighted entropy loss, and updates the model parameters using AdamW.

The methodology is designed to provide a controlled trade-off between exploration and exploitation, leveraging the benefits of both entropy maximization and minimization at different stages of training, thereby enhancing robustness against annotation noise and promoting stable convergence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments across various MLLM backbones, noise settings, and tasks to evaluate the Two-Stage Entropy-Guided GRPO method.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Analysis

- **Overall Robustness Across Tasks:** The proposed "GRPO w. Two." method consistently outperformed standard GRPO and single-stage entropy variants (GRPO w. Min., GRPO w. Max.) across diverse noise levels (0% to 100% noisy labels) on GUI grounding (ScreenSpot) and fine-grained classification (Pets37) tasks.
 - For GUI grounding with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, "GRPO w. Two." maintained 80.2% accuracy at 50% noise, only 2% below GRPO trained on clean labels. It achieved a 4.8% absolute gain over standard GRPO at 100% noise and 4.2% at 50% noise.
 - For fine-grained classification, the two-stage method balanced the strengths of minimization and maximization, delivering robust performance across noise levels.
- **Performance Across Different MLLM Backbones:** The benefits of the two-stage method extended across different model sizes and families (Qwen2-VL-2B, Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and InternVL-3.5-2B on GUI grounding).
 - Larger backbones, such as Qwen2-VL-7B, showed more significant improvements (e.g., 8.6% gain at 50% noise) with the two-stage schedule compared to smaller models.
 - The method's effectiveness was confirmed beyond the Qwen family, with consistent outperformance over standard GRPO for InternVL-3.5-2B across all noise levels.
- **Generalizable Findings on OVOD:** The approach also demonstrated effectiveness on the open-vocabulary object detection (OVOD) task (COCO dataset). With 50% label noise, "GRPO w. Two." improved Qwen2-VL-2B's mAP by 3.53 points (from 15.94 to 19.47) over standard GRPO, matching the best performing single-stage entropy variant.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Impact on Reasoning Traces:** A qualitative comparison on a GUI grounding task illustrated the effects of different entropy regimes:
 - **GRPO w. Min.:** The policy quickly collapsed to a single, confident decoding path, often converging to an incorrect coordinate when labels were noisy, as identical reasoning traces led to unchecked propagation of noisy rewards.
 - **GRPO w. Max.:** Generated diverse reasoning paths, including trajectories consistent with the true label, which helped reduce susceptibility to misleading reward signals. However, this diversity alone did not always lead to the highest accuracy due to a lack of consolidation.
 - **GRPO w. Two.:** Produced reasoning traces that were sufficiently diverse to resist noise during the exploration phase but also coherent enough to pinpoint the correct GUI region after the exploitation phase, indicating a balanced approach.

#### 4.3. Discussions and Ablation Studies

- **Noisy Data Scaling:** When incrementally adding noisy samples to a fixed set of clean samples, standard GRPO performance peaked and then declined. In contrast, the two-stage method maintained robust accuracy (80.0%-82.8%) across all noise scaling levels, demonstrating superior stability and effective exploitation of noisy data benefits while mitigating risks.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** The two-stage method achieved competitive OOD performance on ScreenSpot-Pro, OS-World-G, and MMBench-GUI L2 benchmarks, which differ significantly from the training distribution. For instance, on MMBench-GUI L2, it achieved 60.6% accuracy on clean data and 57.4% at 50% noise, outperforming standard GRPO and single-stage entropy methods. This suggests improved knowledge transfer.
- **GRPO Tolerance to Data Noise:** The paper observed that standard GRPO exhibited moderate robustness to noisy labels, a phenomenon hypothesized to stem from GRPO's group-wise advantage normalization. If a model's prior ability leads all `K` rollouts to predict the actual correct answer (even if the provided label is noisy), all responses receive the same zero reward, leading to zero normalized advantages and thus no learning signal from that group. This "self-gating" effect provides a robust baseline. Ablation studies confirmed this robustness across preprocessing variations (e.g., coordinate formatting, image scaling).
- **Training Dynamics of Entropy:** Figure 3 illustrated the training dynamics of token-level entropy for the two-stage method. Entropy steadily increased during Phase 1 (exploration) to about 400% of its initial value, then rapidly decreased during Phase 2 (exploitation) to about 20% of the peak, stabilizing confident predictions. This confirmed the intended dynamic shift.
- **Switching Points Analysis:** Varying the `τ_switch` (e.g., 500, 700, 800, 900 steps) for GUI grounding showed robustness to the exact switching point. Switching at step 800 (80% of total training steps) yielded the best balance, suggesting a critical timing for phase transition.
- **Stage-wise Entropy Scheduling:** The order of entropy optimization was critical. "Max. then Min." consistently outperformed "Min. then Max." across all noise levels. Starting with entropy maximization supports exploration and diversity for GRPO's advantage estimation, while subsequent minimization consolidates high-reward behaviors. Applying entropy maximization only to noisy subsets or minimization only to clean subsets was less effective than a unified, phased schedule, especially when the true label status is unknown.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the proposed two-stage entropy-guided RLVR method provides enhanced robustness against annotation noise, improves overall performance across various tasks and models, and facilitates better generalization, driven by its principled dynamic management of exploration and exploitation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Two-Stage Entropy-Guided GRPO method addresses a fundamental challenge in the application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): the pervasive issue of annotation noise in real-world datasets. This work has several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** By dynamically guiding MLLM training from exploration to exploitation using token-level entropy, the method significantly improves the robustness of RLVR against noisy labels. This allows MLLMs to maintain high performance even when trained on datasets with substantial annotation errors, making RLVR a more reliable and practical fine-tuning approach for real-world applications where perfectly clean data is often unavailable or prohibitively expensive to obtain.
2. **Improved Generalization:** The research demonstrates that the phased entropy strategy not only enhances in-domain performance under noise but also contributes to better out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. This suggests that the model learns more robust and transferable representations, leading to better performance on unseen tasks and distributions. This is critical for deploying MLLMs in diverse and dynamic environments.
3. **Principled Exploration-Exploitation Management:** The work provides a principled and empirically validated framework for managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off in RLVR using entropy. It moves beyond static entropy regularization, demonstrating that the optimal role of entropy changes throughout the learning process. This understanding can inform the design of future RL algorithms, particularly those operating in environments with imperfect reward signals.
4. **Broader Applicability of RLVR:** By mitigating the strict requirement for high-quality labels, the method broadens the applicability of RLVR to a wider range of multimodal tasks and domains where data annotation is inherently noisy (e.g., user interface interactions, fine-grained classification with ambiguous categories, or open-vocabulary detection). This could accelerate the development and deployment of MLLMs in areas such as intelligent agents, robotics, and scientific discovery.
5. **Insights into GRPO's Noise Tolerance:** The research also provides valuable insights into the inherent robustness of the GRPO algorithm itself, attributing some of its noise tolerance to its group-wise advantage normalization mechanism. This understanding can help in further optimizing and designing more noise-resilient RL algorithms.
6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The success of dynamic entropy scheduling encourages further investigation into adaptive regularization techniques in reinforcement learning. Future work could explore more complex scheduling functions, meta-learning approaches for `λ(τ)` and `τ_switch`, or other forms of uncertainty-aware policy optimization for learning from imperfect data.

Overall, this work represents a step towards making advanced MLLM training more resilient to real-world data imperfections, thereby increasing the practical utility and impact of these powerful models across various scientific and engineering applications.
