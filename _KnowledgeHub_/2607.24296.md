# PAC-DP: PAC-Bayesian Diffusion Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> PAC-DP introduces a theoretically grounded regularization method for Diffusion Policies (DPs) by integrating PAC-Bayesian learning theory, aiming to improve policy generalization, especially in finite-data regimes. The approach demonstrates superior denoising accuracy, better generative modeling of action distributions, and higher task success rates in simulation, alongside successful transfer to physical robot hardware.

## Problem

- Diffusion Policies (DPs) exhibit limitations in generalization performance, particularly in scenarios with limited training data, leading to overfitting.
- The standard empirical denoising objective used for DPs can result in memorization of training data, failing to robustly capture the true underlying data distribution.
- Existing methods to improve DP performance primarily rely on architectural modifications or larger datasets, lacking theoretical guidance for the training objective itself.

## Method

- Formulating a novel training objective for DPs that combines the standard empirical denoising loss with a ==Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence regularizer==, derived from ==PAC-Bayesian theory==.
- Replacing the deterministic noise prediction network in DPs with a ==Bayesian Neural Network (BNN)== to learn a distribution over network parameters, enabling the incorporation of the KL-divergence term.
- Parameterizing the prior and posterior distributions over network weights as ==factorized Gaussians== to ensure tractability of the KL term, facilitating gradient-based optimization.

## Results

- PAC-DP achieved a **67%** reduction in Noise Prediction Error (NPE) and a substantially tighter variational Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) bound on the PushT task, leading to a stable task success rate of **93.7%** compared to **86.1%** for standard DP.
- Across Robomimic tasks, PAC-DP consistently yielded the lowest NPE and NLL, with notable gains in task success rate (e.g., **0.738** vs. **0.401** for DP on ToolHang) especially in low-data regimes (**20** demonstrations).
- Learned PAC-DP policies demonstrated robust real-world transfer, achieving a **100%** success rate across **200** trials on a Franka Emika Panda robot for four manipulation tasks without replanning or online feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - PAC-Bayesian regularization directly addresses generalization by balancing empirical fit with model complexity, preventing overfitting observed in standard DPs and Bayesian DPs (BDP) without this regularization.
> - Improved denoising accuracy and better generative modeling of action distributions (lower NPE and tighter NLL bounds) directly translate to higher task success rates in robotic control.
> - Introducing parameter uncertainty only into the denoising network, while keeping conditioning layers deterministic, yields superior performance compared to fully Bayesian architectures, suggesting the importance of stable observation representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robotic imitation learning, specifically focusing on Diffusion Policies (DPs). Diffusion Policies have emerged as a method for complex manipulation tasks by modeling multimodal action distributions through iterative denoising. Despite their empirical success, the generalization properties of DPs, particularly in scenarios with limited training data, remain an area of investigation.

The broader landscape of Diffusion Models (DMs), from which DPs derive, has seen theoretical and empirical studies indicating that minimizing an empirical denoising objective alone may not guarantee generalization. Prior work on DMs has shown that models can exhibit memorization of training data, generating samples closely tied to the training set rather than truly novel instances, especially under conditions of finite data or insufficient regularization. This suggests a potential limitation in the capacity of the standard denoising objective to fully capture the underlying data distribution, which the authors observe also applies to DPs.

Existing approaches to improve DP performance primarily involve architectural modifications, such as geometry-aware scene representations, hierarchical or goal-conditioned policies, or the use of large-scale, diverse datasets. While these methods can enhance empirical performance, they often improve generalization indirectly through design choices in the model architecture or data collection. They provide limited theoretical guidance regarding the formulation of the training objective itself.

PAC-Bayesian learning theory offers a principled framework for analyzing the generalization of stochastic predictors by bounding the expected test performance in relation to empirical risk and the divergence between posterior and prior parameter distributions. This framework has been applied in various robotics contexts, including controller synthesis, motion planning, and imitation learning, primarily for post-hoc analysis or other control paradigms. However, its direct application as a regularization principle for training diffusion-based policies, where optimization revolves around a denoising generative objective rather than conventional supervised losses, has been less explored.

This work positions PAC-DP as an advancement that addresses the generalization limitations of DPs by introducing a theoretically grounded regularization directly into the training objective. It aims to complement existing architectural and data-centric approaches by providing a principled method for optimizing the learning process itself, thereby offering stronger generalization guarantees, particularly in data-scarce robotic learning environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed limitations in the generalization performance of Diffusion Policies (DPs) in robotic imitation learning. While DPs are effective for complex manipulation tasks, the authors highlight that their generalization properties are not fully understood, especially in finite-data regimes. This is a practical concern in robotics, where collecting large datasets of expert demonstrations can be resource-intensive and time-consuming.

The standard training approach for DPs involves minimizing a denoising objective. However, research on general Diffusion Models (DMs) suggests that relying solely on an empirical denoising objective can lead to issues like memorization of training data and a failure to robustly capture the true underlying data distribution. The authors observed similar challenges in DPs, motivating the search for learning principles that offer stronger generalization guarantees.

The existing methods for enhancing DP performance, such as modifying network architectures or expanding training datasets, are acknowledged as effective but are characterized as indirect approaches to generalization. These methods improve performance through structural or data-driven means rather than by directly modifying the theoretical underpinnings of the training objective to guarantee generalization. The paper argues that a theoretical framework for designing the training objective is needed.

Against this background, the key objectives of the PAC-DP research are:

1. **To establish theoretical guarantees on the expected generalization performance for diffusion-based imitation learning:** This involves leveraging Probably Approximately Correct-Bayesian (PAC-Bayesian) learning theory to derive a generalization bound specific to DPs.
2. **To formulate a novel training objective for DPs:** This objective is designed to augment the standard empirical denoising loss with a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence regularizer. This regularizer quantifies the complexity of the learned model by measuring the divergence between its posterior and prior parameter distributions.
3. **To integrate this objective into the DP framework using Bayesian Neural Networks (BNs):** By modeling the noise prediction component of the DP as a BN, the framework can learn a distribution over network parameters, allowing the KL-divergence term of the PAC-Bayes bound to be incorporated directly into the optimization process.
4. **To empirically validate the proposed PAC-DP framework:** This validation is conducted across three complementary perspectives:
 - **Denoising Performance:** Assessing the quality of score-matching using the standard noise-prediction objective.
 - **Generative Modeling Performance:** Quantifying how well the model captures the action distribution using variational negative log-likelihood bounds.
 - **Robotic Task Success Rates:** Evaluating downstream control performance on simulated manipulation benchmarks, with a particular focus on low-data training regimes where regularization benefits are expected to be most pronounced.
5. **To demonstrate the real-world deployability of PAC-DP:** This involves validating the learned policies on a physical robotic system.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond empirical fixes for DP generalization by providing a theoretically grounded regularization method that directly influences the training objective, thereby improving robustness and performance in practical robotic learning scenarios, especially with limited data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of PAC-DP involves integrating PAC-Bayesian learning theory with Diffusion Policies (DPs) by modeling the noise prediction network as a Bayesian Neural Network (BNN).

**A. Diffusion Policy Preliminaries:**
The work begins by establishing the foundation of Diffusion Policies, which model the conditional distribution of future robot action sequences (x₀) given observations (o) using conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs). The core principle involves a forward diffusion process that progressively adds Gaussian noise to the action sequence, transforming x₀ into a noisy version xₜ. A neural network, ϵ_θ(xₜ, t, o), is then trained to predict this injected noise by minimizing the standard denoising objective, L_DP(θ), which is the expected squared difference between the true noise and the predicted noise. This learned denoiser implicitly defines a reverse diffusion process used for generating action sequences during inference, often accelerated by methods like Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM).

**B. PAC-Bayesian Generalization Bounds:**
To address the generalization limitations of L_DP, the authors introduce PAC-Bayesian learning theory. Unlike traditional learning that seeks a single, deterministic set of parameters, PAC-Bayesian learning considers a distribution over model parameters, Q(w). A predictor is formed by sampling parameters from this distribution, and the objective is to learn a posterior distribution Q(w) whose expected performance generalizes beyond the training data.
The framework defines empirical risk, R̂(Q), as the expected loss over the training dataset, given a weight distribution Q. The population risk, R(Q), is the expected loss over the true, unknown data distribution. The PAC-Bayes-kl inequality is utilized, which states that with high probability, the population risk is bounded by the empirical risk and a term involving the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the learned posterior Q(w) and a predefined prior P(w) over the model parameters. This bound inherently balances fitting the training data with maintaining a simpler posterior distribution, serving as a regularization mechanism.

**C. PAC-DP Formulation:**
1. **Bayesian Neural Network (BNN) Integration:** The deterministic noise prediction network ϵ_θ is replaced with a Bayesian version, ϵ_w(xₜ, t, o), where `w` represents stochastic weights sampled from the learned posterior distribution Q(w).
2. **Empirical Risk for PAC-DP:** The standard denoising objective L_DP(w) is reinterpreted as the empirical risk of this stochastic predictor, ˆR(Q) = E_w∼Q[L_DP(w)].
3. **PAC-DP Objective Derivation:** This empirical risk is then substituted into the PAC-Bayes bound (Equation 5 in the paper), leading to the proposed training objective, L_PAC-DP. This objective combines the empirical denoising term (ˆR(Q)) with a regularization term derived from the KL divergence D_KL(Q||P). A weighting coefficient, λ, is introduced to control the influence of the regularization term.
4. **Parameterization of Q and P:** To maintain tractability of the KL term, both the prior P(w) and posterior Q(w) distributions over network weights are parameterized as factorized Gaussians, Q(w) = N(μ, diag(σ²)). The mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) of the posterior are learnable parameters. For robust training, σ is reparameterized as log(1 + exp(ρ)), where ρ is an unconstrained learnable parameter.
5. **Reparameterization Trick:** During training, network weights are sampled from the posterior using the reparameterization trick (w = μ + σ ⊙ ξ, where ξ ∼ N(0, I)), enabling gradient-based optimization of μ and ρ.
6. **Loss Function Handling:** The authors acknowledge that the theoretical PAC-Bayes-kl inequality assumes bounded loss functions, while L_DP (squared error) is unbounded. However, in practice, L_DP typically drops below one early in training, making the clipped and unclipped losses nearly identical for most of the optimization. Therefore, the original denoising objective is used with the PAC-Bayesian regularization. The KL divergence for factorized Gaussians has a closed-form expression, allowing for efficient computation.

**D. Training and Inference:**
- **Training (Algorithm 1):** The process involves iteratively sampling data (x₀, o), timestep (t), and noise (ϵ), constructing xₜ. Policy parameters `w` are sampled from Q. The Bayesian network ϵ_w predicts noise. The full L_PAC-DP objective is computed, and gradients are back-propagated to update the posterior parameters (μ, ρ).
- **Inference (Algorithm 2):** Actions are generated by iteratively denoising a Gaussian noise sample. The policy parameters `w` can either be set to the deterministic posterior mean E[Q]=μ or sampled from Q to retain stochasticity. DDIM is used for accelerated sampling.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
- **Baselines:** Standard DP (deterministic network, standard denoising loss) and Bayesian DP (BDP, BNN but without PAC-Bayesian regularization).
- **Environments:** PushT and five Robomimic tasks (Lift, Can, Square, Transport, ToolHang).
- **Observations:** State-based.
- **Architecture:** U-Net from, with most layers converted to variational Bayesian variants, but conditional projection layers kept deterministic after ablation studies.
- **Metrics:** Noise Prediction Error (NPE) on a held-out test set, Variational Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) Lower Bound (information-theoretic measure of generative modeling quality), and Task Success Rate (SR) over 1,000 rollouts.
- **Data Regimes:** Evaluations conducted with 20, 40, 80, and 200 demonstrations.
- **Real-World Validation:** Deployment on a Franka Emika Panda robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of PAC-DP, comparing it against a standard Diffusion Policy (DP) and an ablated Bayesian Diffusion Policy (BDP), across various metrics and experimental settings.

**A. PushT Task Optimization Dynamics:**
Evaluation on the PushT task with 200 demonstrations showed distinct optimization behaviors:
- **Noise Prediction Error (NPE):** DP and BDP exhibited a gradual increase in NPE on validation data as training progressed, indicating overfitting and degradation in generalization. PAC-DP, in contrast, maintained a consistently low NPE, converging to 0.060 ± 0.002, a 67% reduction compared to DP (0.184 ± 0.008) and BDP (0.191 ± 0.009). This suggests superior generalization in predicting noise.
- **Variational Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) Bound:** DP and BDP quickly saturated near -3.24, suggesting limited information retention about the underlying distribution. PAC-DP achieved and maintained a substantially tighter bound of -8.275 ± 0.142, indicating a more informative and better learned generative model of the action distribution.
- **Task Success Rate (SR):** These improvements translated to downstream control. PAC-DP converged to a stable SR of 93.7% ± 1.0%. DP and BDP both showed a decline in SR after initial training, ending at 86.1% ± 0.7% and 90.2% ± 0.4%, respectively. This indicates a direct link between improved denoising model generalization and policy execution success. The BDP's improvement over DP in SR, despite not in other metrics, highlighted the partial benefit of Bayesian parameterization, but PAC-DP's overall superiority demonstrated the importance of the PAC-Bayesian regularizer.

**B. Robomimic Benchmark Performance:**
Across five Robomimic tasks (Lift, Can, Square, Transport, ToolHang) with 200 demonstrations:
- **Consistent Metric Improvements:** PAC-DP consistently achieved the lowest NPE and variational NLL lower bounds across all tasks, reinforcing its improved denoising accuracy and probabilistic modeling capabilities.
- **Task Success Rate Translation:** PAC-DP demonstrated significant gains in SR for several tasks, most notably the challenging ToolHang task, increasing SR from 0.401 (DP) and 0.544 (BDP) to 0.738. For simpler tasks like Lift and Can, all methods achieved near-perfect performance, limiting the observable gains in SR despite metric improvements. For Square and Transport, while NPE was reduced, SR did not always show proportional gains, which the authors attributed to PAC-DP optimizing the denoising objective rather than directly task SR.

**C. Low-Data Regime Evaluation:**
Experiments with varying numbers of demonstrations (20, 40, 80, 200) underscored PAC-DP's benefits in data-limited scenarios:
- **Enhanced Performance with Limited Data:** With 20 demonstrations, PAC-DP outperformed baselines in all tasks, with the largest improvements seen in PushT and Can.
- **Diminishing Returns with More Data:** As the number of demonstrations increased, the performance gap between PAC-DP and baselines generally decreased, confirming that the regularization is most effective when data is scarce.
- **Complex Task Benefits:** On the ToolHang task, PAC-DP significantly outperformed baselines even with 200 demonstrations (85.8% SR vs. 64.3% for DP and 72.0% for BDP), suggesting that its principled regularization remains beneficial for tasks requiring long sequences of fine manipulations.

**D. Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis:**
PAC-DP demonstrated robustness across a range of hyperparameters (λ and ρ).
- **Consistent Performance:** Average performance across different configurations was close to the best-performing one for most tasks, indicating that PAC-DP does not require highly specific tuning.
- **Importance of Regularizer:** The comparison with BDP showed that PAC-DP consistently achieved higher average SR, especially on more challenging tasks (e.g., Transport, ToolHang), and reduced variability across hyperparameter settings. This reinforces that the performance gains are primarily due to the PAC-Bayesian regularizer, not just the Bayesian parameterization.

**E. Bayesian Network Ablations:**
An ablation study on the ToolHang task compared two BNN architectures: one where both denoising and conditioning networks were Bayesian (Full BN), and the proposed architecture where only the denoising network was Bayesian, keeping conditioning deterministic (Ours).
- **Deterministic Conditioning Advantage:** The proposed architecture consistently outperformed the Full BN model (up to 85.8% SR vs. 27.8% for Full BN), suggesting that introducing parameter uncertainty into the conditioning layers degrades the quality of the observation representation. A stable, deterministic conditioning signal appears beneficial for the Bayesian denoising network.

**F. Real-World Experiments:**
PAC-DP policies were deployed on a physical Franka Emika Panda robot for Lift, Can, Square, and ToolHang tasks.
- **100% Success Rate:** PAC-DP successfully completed all 200 real-world trials (5 trajectories x 10 executions x 4 tasks), achieving a 100% success rate without replanning or online feedback. This confirmed that the policies learned in simulation with PAC-DP can transfer directly to real hardware with high reliability.

In summary, the results indicate that PAC-DP, through its PAC-Bayesian regularization, consistently improves denoising accuracy and generative modeling, leading to higher task success rates, particularly in low-data regimes and for complex manipulation tasks. The benefits are attributed to the regularization principle itself, and the policies demonstrate successful transfer to real-world robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on PAC-DP contributes to the field of robotic imitation learning by addressing generalization challenges in Diffusion Policies (DPs) through a theoretically grounded framework.

**A. Theoretical Advancement in Diffusion Policies:**
The primary significance lies in formulating diffusion-based imitation learning within a PAC-Bayesian framework. This establishes a principled connection between DP optimization and statistical learning theory, moving beyond empirical heuristics for regularization. By deriving a novel training objective that mathematically combines the standard denoising loss with a PAC-Bayesian complexity regularizer (KL-divergence term), the research provides a theoretical foundation for improving DP generalization. This is a notable departure from previous approaches that primarily focused on architectural or data-centric improvements.

**B. Enhanced Generalization and Robustness in Robotics:**
The empirical results consistently demonstrate that PAC-DP yields improved generalization, particularly in finite-data regimes and for complex manipulation tasks where collecting extensive expert demonstrations is impractical. This is evidenced by:
- **Improved Denoising Accuracy:** Lower Noise Prediction Error (NPE) indicates a better-learned score function.
- **Superior Generative Modeling:** Tighter variational Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) bounds suggest that PAC-DP models the underlying action distribution more effectively.
- **Higher Task Success Rates:** Direct translation of these improvements into more reliable and successful robotic task execution, crucial for practical deployment.
The ability to achieve higher success rates with fewer demonstrations could significantly reduce the data collection burden in robotics.

**C. Practical Applicability and Real-World Deployment:**
The successful deployment of PAC-DP policies on a physical Franka Emika Panda robot, achieving a 100% success rate across multiple tasks without online feedback, underscores its practical relevance. This confirms that the theoretically motivated regularization not only improves performance in simulation but also produces robust policies that transfer effectively to real-world hardware. This reliability is critical for robot systems operating in unstructured or dynamic environments.

**D. Future Research Directions:**
The work opens several avenues for future research, including:
- **Tighter Generalization Bounds:** Investigating data-dependent PAC-Bayesian priors could lead to even tighter and less conservative generalization bounds, potentially further boosting performance.
- **Extension to Complex Robotic Systems:** Applying the PAC-Bayesian framework to vision-based and multi-task diffusion policies could provide substantial benefits in large-scale robotic learning, where model complexity and data diversity are high, making regularization even more critical.

In summary, PAC-DP offers a principled and effective method for improving the generalization and robustness of diffusion-based robot policies. Its theoretical grounding and demonstrated empirical and real-world performance suggest a potential impact on making robot learning more efficient, reliable, and applicable in data-constrained and complex manipulation scenarios.
