# Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition for Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition (BFD) is introduced as a framework to quantitatively disentangle positional, semantic, and global bias factors within Vision Transformer attention mechanisms. Applying BFD reveals that self-supervised DINOv2 models preserve 2D spatial topology while enriching semantic content, offering a mechanistic explanation for their superior holistic shape processing compared to supervised ViTs.

## Problem

- Existing interpretability methods for Vision Transformers (ViTs) often highlight where a model attends or what concepts it learns, but lack a mechanistic understanding of *how* information is processed within the self-attention mechanism.
- The precise informational factors (e.g., positional, semantic) mediating token communication in ViT self-attention remain opaque, making it difficult to understand the rationale behind attention patterns.
- A clear mechanistic explanation for the superior holistic shape processing observed in self-supervised models like DINOv2, differentiating them from supervised counterparts, is currently lacking.

## Method

- Introduced ==Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition (BFD)==, a two-stage analytical framework that statistically factorizes raw token activations and then spectrally decomposes attention mechanisms.
- The first stage uses an ==ANOVA-style decomposition== to isolate statistically orthogonal factors for each token: a global layer effect, a positional effect, and a content residual.
- The second stage analyzes the query-key interaction of attention heads using ==Singular Value Decomposition== to identify bi-orthogonal communication modes, then projects the previously isolated factors onto these modes to quantify their relative contributions.

## Results

- Content-based interactions (content-content and content-position coupling) accounted for the **majority** of attention energy in both architectures, with DINOv2 allocating **significantly more** energy to content-position coupling across all layers and distributing it across more modes.
- DINOv2 models preserved the **2D spatial grid topology** of positional information throughout **all layers**, maintaining neighborhood structure, whereas supervised ViTs progressively collapsed this spatial manifold into a **quasi-1D structure**.
- DINOv2's content factors showed **progressive semantic enrichment** with depth, indicating a genuine transformation and accumulation of information, while supervised ViTs exhibited **limited content evolution** across layers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BFD provides a principled framework to quantitatively attribute the communication strength of attention modes to specific informational factors, moving beyond qualitative interpretations of attention maps.
> - Self-supervised ViTs (DINOv2) exhibit a 'dual preservation' mechanism, maintaining 2D spatial topology of positional embeddings while concurrently enriching semantic content across layers, which explains their holistic shape processing.
> - Individual bi-orthogonal modes within attention heads demonstrate strong functional specialization, acting as pure operators primarily dedicated to content-content, position-position, or content-position interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as foundational models in modern computer vision, powering diverse applications from medical imaging to large-scale generative models. Despite their widespread success, the precise mechanisms by which ViTs process information, particularly within their self-attention layers, remain largely opaque. This opacity presents both a practical imperative for understanding systems deployed in safety-critical settings and a scientific opportunity to compare their internal computations with human visual processing.

The paper situates itself within the evolving field of interpretability. Early interpretability efforts, such as attribution methods (e.g., saliency maps), focused on *where* a model attends in an input. While offering initial intuitions, these methods often highlight broad regions without clarifying *what* visual patterns drive decisions or *how* signals are combined. This led to a shift towards concept-based methods, which aim to identify *what* concepts a model has learned (e.g., "visual atoms" or latent concepts). While providing valuable insights into encoded information, these techniques remain *representation-centric*, explaining *what* is learned but not *how* that information arises computationally.

This paper directly addresses a critical gap in this landscape: the need for *mechanistic interpretability* of the attention mechanism itself. Self-attention is the core computational primitive in ViTs, mediating token communication. Existing analyses often interpret attention through superficial spatial patterns, showing *which* tokens attend to *which others*, but failing to reveal the *content* or *reason* behind these exchanges. This has led to skepticism about raw attention maps as genuine explanations.

The current work aligns with a recent "mechanistic turn" in interpretability that probes the *underlying query-key interactions* directly, often using spectral analysis, to identify dominant interaction modes. However, even these methods often leave the nature of these modes ambiguous – are they driven by position, content, or global trends? This paper provides a principled *factor attribution framework* to disentangle these sources, bridging the gap between identifying dominant modes and understanding *what informational factors* mediate them.

Crucially, this work extends interpretability paradigms previously developed for Large Language Models (LLMs) to the vision domain. The explicit transfer of techniques from language (e.g., Song and Zhong) highlights a growing convergence in interpretability research across different modalities, recognizing shared computational structures in transformer architectures. By comparing supervised ViTs with self-supervised models like DINOv2, the paper also contributes to understanding how different training objectives shape internal representations and lead to emergent properties like holistic shape processing, a known advantage of self-supervised methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper is motivated by a desire to move beyond superficial explanations of Vision Transformers and achieve a principled, mechanistic understanding of their internal operations. Specifically, the authors pose and address three core questions:

1. **Understanding Informational Factors in Attention:** The primary objective is to determine *what informational factors* – positional, semantic, or global bias – mediate token communication within the self-attention mechanism. Furthermore, the authors aim to investigate how the composition of these factors varies across different layers of the network and between models trained with supervised versus self-supervised objectives. This addresses the ambiguity in interpreting attention maps, which currently only show *where* attention focuses, not *what* information is being exchanged.

2. **Investigating Functional Specialization of Attention Heads and Modes:** A second key objective is to examine whether attention heads and their constituent singular modes exhibit *functional specialization*. This asks if different heads or individual modes within a head specialize in distinct types of operations, such as purely semantic interactions (content-content), purely spatial interactions (position-position), or localization-aware semantic interactions (content-position coupling). This delves into the modularity versus distributed nature of attention computations, which has significant implications for mechanistic explanations.

3. **Explaining Holistic Shape Processing in Self-Supervised Models:** The third objective is to uncover *what structural properties* of intermediate-layer representations differentiate self-supervised models (like DINOv2) from their supervised counterparts. Specifically, the researchers seek to understand how these properties – particularly the preservation of positional structure and the enrichment of semantic content – contribute to the superior *holistic shape processing* abilities observed in self-supervised ViTs. This aims to provide a mechanistic explanation for a known behavioral difference between training paradigms.

The overarching motivation for these objectives stems from both practical and scientific imperatives. Practically, as ViTs are increasingly deployed in safety-critical applications and form the backbone of large-scale generative models, a deeper understanding of their inner workings is crucial for ensuring reliability, trustworthiness, and for guiding future model design. Scientifically, ViTs represent highly scalable and capable vision models, offering an opportunity to investigate whether their internal computations align with principles of human visual processing, contributing to broader theories of intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is the introduction of **Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition (BFD)**, a novel two-stage analytical framework designed to disentangle the informational factors mediating attention in Vision Transformers.

**Stage 1: Statistical Factorization (ANOVA-based)**
The first stage focuses on decomposing the raw token activations into statistically orthogonal components. Building on the understanding that positional information is often injected additively at the input layer, the authors adapt an ANOVA-style decomposition to isolate three distinct factors for each token embedding $f_l^{(p)}(x)$ at layer $l$:
- **Layer effect ($\mu_l$):** The global mean activation across all images and token positions at a given layer. This captures overall layer-specific biases.
- **Positional effect ($\mu_p^{(p)}$):** The average activation for a specific token position $p$ across all images, independent of image content. This isolates the fixed positional bias.
- **Content residual ($\mu_c^{(p)}(x)$):** The remaining activation component, which is unique to a specific input image $x$ and token position $p$, after removing the global and positional biases. This component is designed to isolate input-specific semantic information.

By construction, these three factors are statistically orthogonal in expectation over the joint distribution of images and token positions, meaning they represent independent sources of variation.

- **Validation of Factor Isolation:** To ensure the efficacy of this decomposition, the authors perform a sanity check using linear probes. These probes are trained to decode spatial coordinates from both the raw block activations and the isolated content factor ($\mu_c^{(p)}(x)$). High decoding accuracy from raw activations confirms positional information is present, while chance-level accuracy from the content factor validates that positional information has been successfully removed and disentangled.

**Stage 2: Bi-Orthogonal Spectral Decomposition**
The second stage analyzes the self-attention mechanism itself. For a given attention head, the query-key interaction is central to information exchange. The authors characterize this bilinear interaction, $(AW_Q)(AW_K)^T$, by computing the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the effective interaction matrix $W = W_Q W_K^T$.
- **Bi-orthogonal Modes:** The SVD yields triplets $(u_i, \sigma_i, v_i)$, where $u_i$ and $v_i$ are orthonormal left and right singular vectors (bases for the query and key subspaces, respectively), and $\sigma_i$ quantifies their coupling strength. These triplets define *bi-orthogonal modes*, which represent independent communication channels. A mode is activated when a query aligns with $u_i$ and a key with $v_i$.
- **Projected Codes:** Token activations are projected onto these singular vectors to obtain "projected codes" ($z_Q = AU$, $z_K = AV$), representing the alignment of token activations with each communication mode.

**BFD: Coupling Factors and Modes**
The two stages are then combined. Each of the three statistically orthogonal factors (layer, position, content) from Stage 1 is projected onto the query and key modes identified in Stage 2. This allows for the quantification of "energy" ($\xi^{(i)}$) for each factor on the $i$-th mode, effectively attributing how much of a mode's communication strength is mediated by position, content, or global bias. Normalized energy ($\bar{\xi}^{(i)}$) then quantifies the relative dominance of each factor within a given mode. This approach operationalizes a precise understanding of *what information* flows through *which attention channels*.

**Experimental Setup**
The framework is applied to two representative Vision Transformers:
- A supervised **ViT-B/16** from the `timm` library.
- A self-supervised **DINOv2-B/14** (with registers).
Both models have 12 transformer blocks and similar patch tokenization. The analysis is conducted across all 12 layers using 5,000 images sampled from the ImageNet validation set.

**Additional Analyses:**
- **Geometric Preservation of Positional Structure:** Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to the positional factor ($\mu_p^{(p)}$) across layers to visualize how the 2D spatial grid topology is maintained or distorted in the embedding space.
- **Content Enrichment with Depth:** Pearson correlations are computed between flattened representations across different layers for both full activations and content factors to assess how semantic content evolves and enriches through the network.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The application of the Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition (BFD) framework to ViT and DINOv2 models yielded several key findings:

1. **Validation of Factor Isolation:**
 - The ANOVA-based factorization successfully disentangles positional and content information. Linear probes could decode spatial coordinates from raw block activations with high accuracy (especially in early layers) but performed at chance-level when trying to decode position from the content residual factor ($\mu_c^{(p)}(x)$), confirming its content-specific nature.
 - **Divergence:** DINOv2 exhibited higher positional information accessibility via linear probes through intermediate layers compared to the supervised ViT, suggesting self-supervised training better preserves explicit spatial structure.

2. **Characterizing Information Flow in Attention (Addressing Q1):**
 - **Content-Dominance:** In both architectures, content-based interactions (content-content and content-position coupling) accounted for the majority of attention energy, especially in deeper layers. This confirms that contextual integration in ViTs is primarily driven by semantic computation rather than mere positional structure.
 - **DINOv2's Richer Interactions:** DINOv2 allocated significantly more energy to content-position coupling across all layers compared to the supervised ViT. Moreover, DINOv2 distributed this content-based interaction energy across a broader spectrum of singular modes (higher *stable rank*), indicating richer, more distributed communication channels and a more diverse set of interaction patterns. In contrast, the supervised ViT concentrated its energy into fewer dominant modes.
 - **Spectral Signature:** DINOv2 maintained a higher stable rank and exhibited lower alignment between query and key modes (more asymmetric specialization), particularly in intermediate layers. This "higher rank, asymmetric modes, and high content-position energy" combination formed a unique quantitative fingerprint for self-supervised attention.

3. **Functional Specialization of Attention Heads and Modes (Addressing Q2):**
 - **Strong Mode Purity:** Individual bi-orthogonal modes within attention heads showed strong functional specialization. When visualized using ternary plots (representing contributions from layer-, position-, and content-related interactions), modes clustered near the vertices, indicating that they predominantly implement specialized operations.
 - **Head Differentiation:** This specialization was observed at both the mode level (individual singular vectors) and the head level (aggregating energy across all modes of a head). Heads differentiated into content-content, content-position, and position-position operators.
 - **DINOv2's Enhanced Specialization:** DINOv2 demonstrated slightly greater mode purity and more pronounced differentiation in intermediate layers, suggesting that self-supervised training leads to a clearer functional segregation of attention operations. This finding supports the notion that per-head mechanistic analyses are meaningful, as BFD can identify pure computational primitives.

4. **Dual Preservation for Holistic Shape Processing (Addressing Q3):**
 - **Positional Preservation in DINOv2:** PCA visualization of the positional factor ($\mu_p^{(p)}$) revealed a striking difference: DINOv2 preserved the 2D spatial grid topology throughout all layers, maintaining an approximately planar manifold. The tokens retained their neighborhood structure.
 - **Positional Collapse in ViT:** In contrast, the supervised ViT progressively collapsed the spatial manifold into a quasi-1D structure by mid-layers, with tokens condensing along a single principal axis and obliterating local spatial relationships.
 - **Semantic Enrichment in DINOv2:** Analysis of layer-layer Pearson correlations showed that DINOv2's content factors ($\mu_c^{(p)}(x)$) underwent a steady, gradual transformation across layers. This indicates a progressive enrichment of semantic content, where token meanings are updated by accumulating information from other object parts. The similarity between early and late-layer content factors was significantly lower than for full activations, suggesting a genuine transformation beyond persistent positional signals.
 - **Limited Semantic Evolution in ViT:** The supervised ViT, however, showed content factor correlations closely mirroring those of full activations, with limited evolution across depth. This suggests that ViT performs more local refinement rather than integrating semantic content into broader, contextually enriched representations.
 - **Conclusion:** The authors propose that DINOv2's superior holistic shape processing arises from this "dual preservation" mechanism: maintaining a stable 2D spatial topology while simultaneously enriching semantic content through intermediate layers. This allows for coordinated spatio-semantic integration that is largely absent in supervised models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition for Vision Transformers" makes significant contributions to the field of mechanistic interpretability and offers profound implications for understanding, designing, and improving Vision Transformers.

**Significance:**
- **Principled Mechanistic Interpretability:** The introduction of BFD provides a novel, principled framework that goes beyond merely visualizing attention maps to quantitatively disentangle the informational factors (position, content, global bias) mediating token communication. This advances the interpretability field from "where" and "what" to "how" information is processed within ViTs.
- **Cross-Domain Methodological Transfer:** By adapting and contextualizing a successful interpretability paradigm from Large Language Models (LLMs) to Vision Transformers, the work highlights common computational principles across modalities and fosters interdisciplinary research.
- **Mechanistic Explanation for Emergent Behavior:** The framework provides a concrete, quantitative explanation for why self-supervised models like DINOv2 exhibit superior holistic shape processing. The "dual preservation" of spatial topology and progressive semantic enrichment offers a compelling computational motif for this behavior, addressing a long-standing question in self-supervised learning.
- **Quantifying Functional Specialization:** BFD provides a rigorous method to quantify the functional specialization of attention heads and individual singular modes. The finding of "mode purity" suggests that attention mechanisms are not entirely black boxes but contain interpretable, specialized computational primitives, which can simplify future mechanistic analyses.
- **Revealing Training Paradigm Differences:** The work systematically elucidates the distinct computational profiles of supervised versus self-supervised training. DINOv2's richer, higher-rank, and more content-position coupled interactions, alongside its unique representational geometry, provide a "quantitative fingerprint" of self-supervision.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved Model Design:** Understanding *how* positional and semantic information is processed and combined, particularly in self-supervised models, can directly inform the design of future ViT architectures. Researchers can leverage these insights to engineer models that better preserve spatial structure, facilitate content enrichment, or optimize for specific types of interactions (e.g., more effective content-position coupling).
- **Enhanced Interpretability Tools:** BFD provides a powerful analytical tool for researchers to diagnose and understand complex behaviors in ViTs. It can be extended to analyze other transformer variants, different datasets, or specific tasks, offering a deeper lens into model failures or successes.
- **Guiding Self-Supervised Learning Research:** The identified "dual preservation" mechanism in DINOv2 offers a concrete target for self-supervised learning objectives. Future algorithms could be designed to explicitly encourage this co-evolution of stable spatial structure and transforming semantic content, potentially leading to more robust and human-aligned visual representations.
- **Bridging AI and Cognitive Science:** By providing a mechanistic understanding of how artificial systems process visual information, this research offers a quantitative basis for comparing AI computations with human visual processing. This can inform theories in cognitive science about the neural substrates of perception and help develop AI models that are more aligned with biological intelligence.
- **Development of More Robust and Trustworthy AI:** For safety-critical applications, a deeper understanding of internal computations is paramount. BFD can contribute to building more transparent and auditable ViTs, allowing developers to ensure that decisions are based on semantically meaningful features rather than spurious correlations or unintended positional biases.

In summary, this paper represents a significant step forward in the mechanistic interpretability of Vision Transformers, providing a robust framework to understand the interplay of positional and semantic information within their attention mechanisms, with far-reaching implications for both fundamental AI research and practical applications.
