# Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition for Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition (BFD) is introduced as a framework to quantitatively disentangle positional, semantic, and global bias factors within Vision Transformer attention mechanisms. Applying BFD reveals that self-supervised DINOv2 models preserve 2D spatial topology while enriching semantic content, offering a mechanistic explanation for their superior holistic shape processing compared to supervised ViTs.

## Problem

- Existing interpretability methods for Vision Transformers (ViTs) often highlight where a model attends or what concepts it learns, but lack a mechanistic understanding of *how* information is processed within the self-attention mechanism.
- The precise informational factors (e.g., positional, semantic) mediating token communication in ViT self-attention remain opaque, making it difficult to understand the rationale behind attention patterns.
- A clear mechanistic explanation for the superior holistic shape processing observed in self-supervised models like DINOv2, differentiating them from supervised counterparts, is currently lacking.

## Method

- Introduced ==Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition (BFD)==, a two-stage analytical framework that statistically factorizes raw token activations and then spectrally decomposes attention mechanisms.
- The first stage uses an ==ANOVA-style decomposition== to isolate statistically orthogonal factors for each token: a global layer effect, a positional effect, and a content residual.
- The second stage analyzes the query-key interaction of attention heads using ==Singular Value Decomposition== to identify bi-orthogonal communication modes, then projects the previously isolated factors onto these modes to quantify their relative contributions.

## Results

- Content-based interactions (content-content and content-position coupling) accounted for the majority of attention energy in both architectures, with DINOv2 allocating significantly more energy to content-position coupling across all layers and distributing it across more modes.
- DINOv2 models preserved the 2D spatial grid topology of positional information throughout all layers, maintaining neighborhood structure, whereas supervised ViTs progressively collapsed this spatial manifold into a quasi-1D structure.
- DINOv2's content factors showed progressive semantic enrichment with depth, indicating a genuine transformation and accumulation of information, while supervised ViTs exhibited limited content evolution across layers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BFD provides a principled framework to quantitatively attribute the communication strength of attention modes to specific informational factors, moving beyond qualitative interpretations of attention maps.
> - Self-supervised ViTs (DINOv2) exhibit a 'dual preservation' mechanism, maintaining 2D spatial topology of positional embeddings while concurrently enriching semantic content across layers, which explains their holistic shape processing.
> - Individual bi-orthogonal modes within attention heads demonstrate strong functional specialization, acting as pure operators primarily dedicated to content-content, position-position, or content-position interactions.
