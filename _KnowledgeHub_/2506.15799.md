# Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent Space Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, Diffusion Steering via Reinforcement Learning (DSRL), enables rapid, autonomous adaptation of pre-trained diffusion policies for robotic control by learning to manipulate their latent noise input space. This approach achieves high sample efficiency and black-box compatibility, making it practical for real-world fine-tuning of large generalist robot policies such as π0.

## Problem

- Behavioral cloning policies, particularly those based on diffusion models, struggle to adapt autonomously in novel, open-world environments without collecting new human demonstrations.
- Traditional reinforcement learning is often prohibitively sample-inefficient for complex, high-dimensional robotic domains, making real-world online adaptation impractical.
- Finetuning large, complex diffusion policies directly with standard RL methods is computationally intensive, numerically unstable, and requires white-box access to model weights.

## Method

- **DSRL** reinterprets a pre-trained ==diffusion policy== as a ==deterministic transformation== from a ==latent-noise input space== to the physical action space.
- Frames the problem as learning a separate, smaller ==reinforcement learning policy== that operates on this latent-noise space to ==steer== the fixed, pre-trained diffusion policy.
- Introduces ==Noise-Aliased DSRL (DSRL-NA)==, which uses a ==dual-critic== setup to efficiently propagate value information across aliased noise samples and effectively incorporate ==offline data== for improved sample efficiency.

## Results

- Achieved **5-10x** improvement in sample efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art RL methods for diffusion policies in simulation.
- Demonstrated rapid real-world adaptation, improving a single-task pick-and-place success rate from **20%** to nearly **100%** with less than **50 episodes** (approx. **3500 online steps**).
- Successfully adapted a **3.3B** parameter generalist robot policy (π0) in both simulation and real-world tasks, marking the first successful real-world RL-based fine-tuning of this model with approximately **10,000 online steps**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The latent noise input space of a diffusion policy offers sufficient expressivity to steer its behavior for robotic control.
> - By treating the diffusion policy as a black-box transformation within the environment, standard RL can be applied efficiently to a smaller latent-noise policy without finetuning the large base model.
> - Leveraging 'noise aliasing' (where different noise inputs can produce similar physical actions) allows for improved sample efficiency by transferring value information between physical and latent-noise action spaces.
