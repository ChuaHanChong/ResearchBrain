# PhyLatent: Learning Dynamics-Relevant Representations for JEPA World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Nanyang Technological University researchers introduced PhyLatent, a method that enhances Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) for world models by explicitly addressing local latent space pathologies critical for robotic control. PhyLatent reduced specific dynamics-relevant collapse rates on OGBench-Cube by up to 85.8% and improved Model Predictive Control success by 8.1 to 17.0 percentage points on manipulation and navigation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing JEPA world models, despite preventing global latent collapse, often fail to guarantee a reliable local state space for control and planning tasks.
- Three specific failure modes impede JEPA world models: Physical Invariance Collapse (sensitivity to visual nuisances), Physical Identifiability Collapse (physically distinct states mapped to similar latents), and Counterfactual Dynamics Collapse (indistinguishable predicted futures for different actions).

## Method

- **PhyLatent** augments JEPA training with three dedicated pathways: ==Static Visual Invariance Constraint (SVIC)==, ==Physical State Grounding (PSG) and Future Representation Alignment (FRA)==, and ==Counterfactual Action Separation Constraint (CASC) and Latent Denoising (LD)==.
- These pathways introduce auxiliary objectives that explicitly enforce physical invariance, state identifiability, and counterfactual dynamics separation within the learned latent space.
- The auxiliary modules are active only during training and are discarded for inference, maintaining the original JEPA world model architecture for deployment.

## Results

- PhyLatent reduced Physical Invariance Collapse by **51.7%** (to **7.53%**), Physical Identifiability Collapse by **85.8%** (to **0.95%**), and Counterfactual Dynamics Collapse by **45.1%** (to **4.62%**) on the OGBench-Cube task.
- Model Predictive Control (MPC) success rates improved from **70.00%** to **78.10%** on OGBench-Cube and from **81.00%** to **98.00%** on TwoRooms, demonstrating enhanced planning capabilities.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component of PhyLatent contributes to mitigating specific collapse types and improving overall planning success, with the full PhyLatent objective consistently achieving the best performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global non-collapse of latent representations, while important, is insufficient for creating robust world models; explicit local structural properties related to physical dynamics are critical for effective control.
> - Targeted training objectives can systematically mitigate specific dynamics-relevant collapse modes, leading to latent spaces that more accurately reflect physical states and action outcomes.
> - By regularizing physical invariance, ensuring state identifiability, and separating counterfactual futures, JEPA world models become more reliable foundations for Model Predictive Control without requiring pixel-level reconstruction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of representation learning for embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on world models derived from Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). JEPAs, as introduced by LeCun (2022), aim to learn compact latent representations for future prediction without requiring explicit reconstruction of pixel-level details. This paradigm has been extended to action-conditioned world models, such as V-JEPA 2 (Assran et al., 2025) and LeWorldModel (Maes et al., 2026a), where learned latent spaces are intended to support control and planning tasks. LeWorldModel, for instance, integrates latent prediction with isotropic Gaussian regularization to enable Model Predictive Control (MPC) directly within the learned representation space.

A known challenge in joint-embedding models is the prevention of representation collapse, where the learned latent space degenerates into trivial or low-rank solutions. Various methods, including contrastive objectives, asymmetric prediction, momentum targets, and variance-covariance regularization, have been developed to address this global collapse (e.g., Grill et al., 2020; Chen & He, 2021; Bardes et al., 2022). LeJEPA (Balestriero & LeCun, 2025) and LeWorldModel employ Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) to maintain a globally non-degenerate embedding distribution.

Concurrently, research into dynamics-aware representations within latent world models and control-oriented representation learning explores properties such as planning, temporal continuity, controllability, transition prediction, and bisimulation (e.g., Hafner et al., 2019; Jonschkowski & Brock, 2015). These efforts recognize that effective state representations should capture task-relevant dynamics while filtering out irrelevant visual information. Sensorimotor World Models (Ivashkov et al., 2026) utilize inverse dynamics regularization to align latent representations with actions.

PhyLatent positions itself at the intersection of these two research directions. It acknowledges the success of existing JEPA world models in preventing global latent collapse but identifies a complementary limitation: a globally non-collapsed latent space does not inherently guarantee the preservation of local physical and counterfactual structures crucial for robust control and planning. The work addresses this gap by proposing explicit mechanisms to ensure that the learned latent geometry accurately reflects physical states and action consequences, thereby enhancing the reliability of JEPA world models for downstream tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work is the observation that current JEPA world models, despite employing regularization techniques like Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) to prevent global latent collapse, do not inherently guarantee a reliable state space for world modeling. While the overall distribution of latents may be non-degenerate, the local geometry of this space can still be problematic for accurately representing physical states and action outcomes, which are essential for control and planning tasks.

The authors identify three specific failure modes in JEPA world models that compromise their utility as reliable state spaces:

1. **Physical Invariance Collapse:** This occurs when visual appearance-only changes in observations lead to disproportionately large shifts in the latent representation, even though the underlying physical state of the environment remains unchanged. For instance, a change in lighting or texture might drastically alter a latent vector, making it appear physically different from its unperturbed counterpart. This implies that the representation is sensitive to irrelevant visual nuisances rather than being invariant to them.

2. **Physical Identifiability Collapse:** This failure mode describes situations where physically distinct states are mapped to nearby regions within the latent space. Despite their physical differences, the model's representation treats these states as being similar, leading to potential confusion during state estimation or planning. A globally dispersed latent manifold might still locally fold physically distant states into close proximity, violating the principle that distinct physical states should correspond to distinct latent representations.

3. **Counterfactual Dynamics Collapse:** This arises when different actions, which would lead to distinct and separated future trajectories in the real world, result in predicted future latent representations that are insufficiently separated. For effective Model Predictive Control (MPC), a world model must be able to differentiate the outcomes of alternative actions to make informed decisions. If the predicted futures for different actions collapse onto similar latent regions, the planner cannot reliably discriminate between these actions, hindering its ability to select optimal policies.

The primary objective of PhyLatent is to systematically address these three identified failure modes. By introducing specific training objectives, the work aims to explicitly enforce physical invariance, ensure physical identifiability, and preserve counterfactual dynamics separation within the learned latent space of JEPA world models. The motivation is to move beyond mere global non-collapse and imbue the latent representations with the precise local structural properties necessary for robust prediction and effective planning in complex, action-conditioned environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhyLatent is designed as a dynamics-relevant representation learning method that augments the training of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) for world models. It builds upon the LeWorldModel (Maes et al., 2026a) architecture and extends its training objectives.

**Base Architecture:**
The core of the system is an action-conditioned JEPA world model. Given an observation $o_t$, the next observation $o_{t+1}$, and an action $a_t$, an encoder $E$, a projector $P$, and an action-conditioned predictor $F_\theta$ operate as follows:
- $z_t = P(E(o_t))$
- $z_{t+1} = P(E(o_{t+1}))$
- $\hat{z}_{t+1} = F_\theta(z_t, a_t)$
The predictor is trained to minimize the mean squared error between its output $\hat{z}_{t+1}$ and the stop-gradient future latent $sg(z_{t+1})$, forming the primary prediction loss $L_{pred}$. LeWorldModel additionally applies Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) to prevent global latent collapse.

**PhyLatent's Three Training Pathways:**
PhyLatent introduces three complementary training pathways, each addressing one of the identified collapse modes. These pathways use auxiliary heads and modules that are active only during training and are discarded for inference, maintaining the original JEPA world model architecture for deployment.

1. **Physical Invariance Pathway (Static Visual Invariance Constraint - SVIC):**
 - **Objective:** To ensure that latent representations remain stable despite appearance-only changes in observations.
 - **Mechanism:** An augmented observation sequence $O_{aug}$ is created by applying visual perturbations (e.g., brightness, color) to the original sequence $O$, without altering the underlying physical state. This $O_{aug}$ is encoded to $Z_{aug}$. SVIC then minimizes the normalized mean-squared distance ($d_N$) between $Z_{aug}$ and a stop-gradient copy of the original encoded sequence $sg(Z)$. This loss ($L_{inv}$) encourages the encoder to produce similar latents for perceptually varied but physically identical states.

2. **Physical Identifiability Pathway (Physical State Grounding - PSG and Future Representation Alignment - FRA):**
 - **Objective:** To ensure that physically distinct states are separated in the latent space and that the predicted future representations retain task-relevant physical structure.
 - **Physical State Grounding (PSG):** A shared state head $H_s$ is applied to both the encoded latent sequence $Z$ and the predicted future sequence $\hat{Z}$. This head is supervised by task-specific, normalized physical state targets $\bar{S}$. The loss ($L_{state}$) encourages both encoded and predicted latents to correspond to these ground-truth physical variables, thereby grounding the abstract latent space in concrete physical reality.
 - **Future Representation Alignment (FRA):** This component further aligns predicted future sequences $\hat{Z}$ with observed target futures $Z_+$ through two sub-branches:
 - **Projection Branch:** A shared projection head $\phi$ maps both $\hat{Z}$ and $sg(Z_+)$ to a common space, minimizing $d_N(\phi(\hat{Z}), sg(\phi(Z_+)))$ ($L_{proj}$).
 - **Action-Query Branch:** An action-query module $\Psi$ processes $\hat{Z}$ and $sg(Z_+)$ conditioned on the mean context-action embedding $\bar{c}_a$. It minimizes $d_N(\Psi(\hat{Z}, C_{a_{ctx}}), sg(\Psi(Z_+, C_{a_{ctx}})))$ ($L_{act}$).
 - The combined FRA loss is $L_{align} = L_{proj} + L_{act}$. This ensures consistency and structure in the predicted future content and how it relates to the action context.

3. **Counterfactual Dynamics Pathway (Counterfactual Action Separation Constraint - CASC and Latent Denoising - LD):**
 - **Objective:** To ensure that futures resulting from different actions are distinguishable and that the local structure around future latents is regularized.
 - **Counterfactual Action Separation Constraint (CASC):** For each batch sample, a counterfactual action sequence $A_{cf}$ is generated by permuting actions across the batch and adding Gaussian noise. This $A_{cf}$ is used to predict a counterfactual future $\hat{Z}_{cf}$. The objective is to enforce a separation margin between $\hat{Z}_{cf}$ and the factual predicted future $sg(\hat{Z})$, proportional to the difference between factual and counterfactual actions. This loss ($L_{sep}$) is applied only when action differences exceed a median threshold, ensuring that significantly different actions lead to separated predicted outcomes.
 - **Latent Denoising (LD):** This technique regularizes the local geometry of the predicted future latent space. Noise $\epsilon$ (Gaussian) scaled by $\sigma$ (sampled uniformly) is added to a stop-gradient copy of the target future latent $sg(Z_+)$, creating $\tilde{Z}_+$. A denoising head $D_\omega$ is then trained to predict this noise $\hat{\epsilon}$ given $\tilde{Z}_+$, $\hat{Z}$, action conditioning, and $\sigma$. The loss ($L_{denoise}$) minimizes MSE($\hat{\epsilon}, \epsilon$), implicitly regularizing the predicted future's local structure without pixel reconstruction.

**Full Training Objective:**
The complete PhyLatent objective is a weighted sum of the baseline JEPA prediction loss, SIGReg, and the auxiliary losses from the three pathways:
$L_{PhyLatent} = L_{pred} + 0.09 L_{SIGReg} + \lambda_{inv}L_{inv} + \lambda_{state}L_{state} + \lambda_{align}(L_{proj} + L_{act}) + \lambda_{sep}L_{sep} + \lambda_{denoise}L_{denoise}$.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Tasks:** Evaluated on OGBench-Cube, PushT, Reacher (DeepMind Control Suite), and TwoRooms.
- **Metrics:** Dynamics-relevant collapse rates (physical invariance, physical identifiability, counterfactual dynamics) on Cube, and Model Predictive Control (MPC) success rates across all tasks.
- **Model:** ViT-Tiny encoder, latent dimension 192, 3-step observation history. Predictor uses 6 transformer blocks.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer, 10 epochs, batch size 32, bfloat16 precision.
- **MPC Configuration:** Prediction horizon of 5, receding horizon of 5, action block size of 5. Samples 100 candidate action sequences, retaining the top 10 elites.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that PhyLatent enhances the dynamics-relevant structure of latent representations in JEPA world models and improves performance on various control tasks.

**Dynamics-Relevant Latent Structure on OGBench-Cube:**
PhyLatent significantly reduces all three identified dynamics-relevant collapse rates on the OGBench-Cube task, indicating a more reliable and structured latent space:
- **Physical Invariance Collapse:** Reduced from 15.60% for JEPA + SIGReg to 7.53% for PhyLatent. This represents a 51.7% reduction in cases where appearance-only changes cause larger latent shifts than real physical transitions.
- **Physical Identifiability Collapse:** Decreased from 6.71% to 0.95%. This marks an 85.8% reduction in instances where physically distant states are mapped to close latent regions, ensuring better distinction between unique physical configurations.
- **Counterfactual Dynamics Collapse:** Lowered from 8.41% to 4.62%. This indicates a 45.1% improvement in maintaining sufficient separation between predicted futures resulting from different actions.

**Planning Performance Across Tasks:**
The improvements in latent structure translate into enhanced planning success using Model Predictive Control (MPC):
- **Cube:** MPC success rate improved from 70.00% (JEPA + SIGReg) to 78.10% (PhyLatent), an 8.1 percentage point increase.
- **TwoRooms:** Success rate rose substantially from 81.00% to 98.00%, a 17.0 percentage point gain.
- **Reacher:** Showed a minor improvement from 78.33% to 79.33%.
- **PushT:** Experienced a slight decrease in success from 77.67% to 75.33%.
These results suggest that the principles of PhyLatent are broadly beneficial, particularly for tasks like manipulation (Cube) and navigation (TwoRooms).

**Ablation Study:**
An ablation study on OGBench-Cube investigated the contribution of each pathway:
- **Removing Static Visual Invariance Constraint (SVIC):** Led to an increase in physical invariance failure rate from 7.53% to 9.93%, demonstrating the necessity of this constraint for robustness to appearance changes.
- **Removing Physical State Grounding (PSG) and Future Representation Alignment (FRA):** Resulted in an increase in counterfactual dynamics failure to 6.84% (from 4.62%) and a drop in planning success from 78.10% to 74.00%. This indicates the importance of physical grounding and representation alignment for predictable dynamics.
- **Removing Counterfactual Action Separation Constraint (CASC) and Latent Denoising (LD):** Produced the largest decline in planning success, reducing it to 73.33%. This highlights the critical role of explicitly structuring counterfactual futures for effective action selection.
The complete PhyLatent objective consistently achieved the highest planning success while maintaining low failure rates across all three diagnostics.

**Qualitative Observations:**
Qualitative comparisons of MPC rollouts on Cube illustrate the practical impact. While the baseline JEPA + SIGReg model might initially engage with an object, it often fails to maintain consistent interaction, leading to task failure. In contrast, PhyLatent enables the robot to sustain interaction with the cube throughout the trajectory, successfully moving it towards the target, aligning with its higher reported success rate.

**Robustness and Specific Task Analysis:**
- **Robustness to Identifiability Thresholds:** The improvement in physical identifiability collapse was found to be robust across various threshold settings for physical and latent distances, indicating that the measured reduction in collisions is not dependent on a specific diagnostic configuration.
- **PushT Analysis:** Despite PhyLatent reducing all three collapse rates on PushT (Inv. Fail. from 1.33% to 0.20%; Id. Fail. from 1.15% to 0.52%; CF Fail. from 4.25% to 2.38%), this did not translate to higher MPC success. This suggests that while PhyLatent improves fundamental representation properties, contact-rich tasks like pushing may also rely on other factors, such as precise local contact transitions or the granularity of the planning objective, which are not solely addressed by the learned latent geometry.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "PhyLatent: Learning Dynamics-Relevant Representations for JEPA World Models" makes a significant contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence and representation learning for world models. Its primary significance lies in empirically demonstrating that preventing global representation collapse, a common objective in self-supervised learning, is insufficient to guarantee a dynamics-relevant state space necessary for robust control and planning in complex environments.

**Advancement in JEPA World Models:** PhyLatent introduces a systematic approach to explicitly instill three crucial properties—physical invariance, physical identifiability, and counterfactual dynamics separation—into the latent space of JEPA world models. By achieving substantial reductions in diagnosed collapse rates and notable improvements in Model Predictive Control (MPC) success on several tasks, the work establishes that targeted constraints on local latent structure are vital for learning effective dynamics. This moves JEPA world models beyond abstract pixel prediction towards more functionally useful state representations for action-conditioned tasks.

**Improved Reliability for Planning and Control:** The enhanced latent geometry provided by PhyLatent directly benefits downstream planning algorithms. A latent space where similar physical states are invariant to visual nuisances, distinct physical states are clearly separated, and alternative action outcomes are discriminable, provides a more reliable foundation for an MPC planner to explore future trajectories and select optimal actions. This leads to more robust and effective control in robotic manipulation and navigation scenarios.

**Generalizability of Principles:** The consistent improvements on tasks like OGBench-Cube (manipulation) and TwoRooms (navigation), alongside competitive performance on Reacher and PushT, suggests that the principles embedded in PhyLatent are broadly applicable across different types of visual control tasks. This indicates a step towards more generalized learning principles for world models rather than task-specific architectural tweaks.

**Implications for Future Research:**
The work highlights a critical area for future research by identifying a limitation: PhyLatent currently relies on task-specific physical targets for auxiliary supervision during training. Addressing this limitation by investigating more broadly available supervision signals, or exploring methods to infer such dynamics-relevant structure without explicit ground truth physical states, could further enhance the autonomy and scalability of world model learning. This could involve self-supervised or unsupervised approaches to inferring physical properties, thereby making the framework applicable to an even wider range of complex, uninstrumented environments.

In summary, PhyLatent refines the understanding of what constitutes a "good" latent space for world models, emphasizing that explicit structural integrity related to physical dynamics is as crucial as global non-collapse. This provides a clear direction for developing more capable and reliable AI agents for real-world interaction.
