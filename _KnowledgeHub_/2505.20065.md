# SafeDPO: A Simple Approach to Direct Preference Optimization with Enhanced Safety

> [!summary] Summary
> LG AI Research developed SafeDPO, a direct preference optimization method for large language model safety alignment that derives a provably equivalent, tractable objective from a hard-constrained problem. It achieves high harmlessness (97-100%) while maintaining helpfulness, simplifying the training process by removing auxiliary models.

## Problem

- Existing preference-based LLM alignment methods, such as RLHF and DPO, do not inherently enforce strict safety constraints, allowing preferred responses to still be unsafe.
- Prior safety alignment techniques are often computationally and conceptually complex, requiring auxiliary models, multi-stage optimization, or extensive hyperparameter tuning.
- Many approaches rely on relaxed expected-cost safety formulations, which fail to strictly guarantee the complete elimination of unsafe outputs, a critical requirement for safety-sensitive applications.

## Method

- Redefining the hard-constrained safety alignment problem to derive a closed-form optimal policy by using a ==cost-augmented== reward function that assigns negative infinity to unsafe responses.
- Introducing a safety-aware transformation for preference data that swaps or discards pairs to align with the theoretical preference distribution where safe responses are always preferred over unsafe ones.
- Incorporating an optional safety margin (∆) into the objective to provide additional control over safety enforcement and strengthen the learning signal without altering the set of optimal solutions.

## Results

- SafeDPO achieved high harmlessness, with a ratio of **97%** (model-based) and **100%** (GPT-4) on PKU-SafeRLHF-**30K**, while maintaining competitive helpfulness scores.
- The method significantly reduces computational complexity, requiring fewer network components and achieving substantially faster training times (e.g., 1388 seconds vs. 32957 seconds for policy training compared to SafeRLHF).
- SafeDPO demonstrated strong safety performance and scalability across model sizes (**1.5B** to **13B** parameters) but showed a higher over-refusal rate (**12.4%**) on the XSTest benchmark, indicating a trade-off for strict safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The original hard-constrained safety alignment problem admits a closed-form optimal policy that strictly assigns zero probability to unsafe responses.
> - A safety-aware transformation of empirical preference data can yield a tractable objective that is provably equivalent to the intractable objective derived from the hard-constrained optimal policy.
> - Introducing a safety margin parameter (∆) can enhance optimization dynamics and learning signals for safety without altering the theoretical set of optimal solutions for the policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The widespread deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) has introduced a challenge regarding the generation of unsafe or harmful content, alongside their demonstrated capabilities. This necessitates effective safety alignment techniques that balance helpfulness with strict safety constraints.

The current landscape of LLM alignment primarily relies on preference alignment methods. These include Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which typically involves training a separate reward model and then fine-tuning the policy, and Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs), such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which directly optimize the policy using preference data, thereby simplifying the pipeline by removing the need for an explicit reward model. While these methods have proven effective for maximizing helpfulness, they do not intrinsically enforce safety constraints, meaning preferred responses may still be unsafe.

To address this gap, several safety alignment methods have been proposed, including SafeRLHF, SACPO, and CAN. These approaches typically extend preference-based training by integrating safety information through auxiliary models (e.g., reward or cost models), multi-stage optimization processes, or relaxed constrained objectives. A common characteristic of these methods is their increased computational and conceptual complexity, often stemming from the need for additional models, training phases, or the tuning of multiple hyperparameters. Furthermore, many existing safety alignment methods address the safety problem through relaxed expected-cost formulations, which do not strictly guarantee the elimination of unsafe outputs, a critical requirement in safety-sensitive applications.

This work addresses the limitations of existing safety alignment methods by revisiting the original hard-constrained safety objective. It aims to develop a simpler, more theoretically grounded, and computationally efficient approach that explicitly excludes unsafe responses, in contrast to methods that rely on approximations or auxiliary models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a lightweight and theoretically grounded method for safety alignment in LLMs that effectively addresses the challenge of balancing helpfulness and safety. The method aims to enforce strict safety constraints while maintaining competitive helpfulness, with a focus on simplicity and computational efficiency.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the existing safety alignment landscape:

- **Complexity of Existing Methods**: Current safety alignment approaches often introduce significant complexity. Methods like SafeRLHF, SACPO, and CAN frequently require auxiliary reward or cost models, involve multi-stage training pipelines, or necessitate extensive hyperparameter tuning. This complexity increases the computational burden, memory requirements, and development effort. The authors are motivated to explore if a simpler, single-stage approach can achieve comparable or superior performance.
- **Insufficient Enforcement of Hard Safety Constraints**: Many prior works replace the hard safety constraint (where unsafe responses must have zero probability under the policy) with a relaxed expected-cost formulation. While computationally convenient, these relaxations do not strictly guarantee the absence of unsafe outputs. In safety-critical applications, even minor violations can lead to considerable risks. This work is motivated by the need to strictly enforce the safety constraint, ensuring that unsafe responses are excluded by design.
- **Quest for Theoretical Tractability**: The authors are motivated to analyze the original hard-constrained optimization problem directly. By doing so, they aim to determine if this problem admits a closed-form optimal policy and if a provably equivalent, tractable objective can be derived. This theoretical exploration seeks to provide a more principled foundation for safety alignment, moving beyond surrogate relaxations.
- **Efficiency and Practicality**: The objective includes developing a method that is practical for real-world LLM deployment. This implies a solution that minimizes computational overhead (e.g., no need for reward models, cost models, or online sampling) and relies primarily on readily available data (preference data and binary safety indicators). The goal is to integrate safety alignment into existing preference-based training methods with minimal modifications and additional hyperparameters.

In summary, the key objectives are to: (1) demonstrate that the original safety-constrained objective admits a closed-form optimal policy; (2) derive a provably equivalent and tractable objective for direct optimization; (3) propose SafeDPO as a lightweight, single-stage algorithm for safety alignment; and (4) empirically validate that SafeDPO achieves competitive safety-helpfulness trade-offs with enhanced efficiency compared to existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of SafeDPO is based on a re-examination of the hard-constrained safety alignment problem, aiming to derive a tractable objective that preserves the properties of the original problem without relying on auxiliary models or multi-stage training. The approach proceeds in three main steps:

1. **From Hard Constraint to Closed-Form Policy**:
 - The authors begin by analyzing the conventional safety alignment objective (Equation 6), which maximizes helpfulness while imposing a hard constraint: `c(x, y) <= 0` for all responses `y` generated by the policy `π_θ`. This constraint means that unsafe responses must receive zero probability.
 - To incorporate this hard constraint directly, a "cost-augmented reward" `r_c(x, y)` is defined. If a response `y` is safe (`c(x, y) <= 0`), `r_c(x, y)` is equal to the original helpfulness reward `r(x, y)`. If `y` is unsafe (`c(x, y) > 0`), `r_c(x, y)` is set to `-∞`.
 - Substituting `r_c(x, y)` into the KL-regularized objective (Equation 8) yields a reduced optimization problem. Under mild assumptions (Feasibility of Safe Responses and Bounded Reward, detailed in Section 4 and Appendix A), the optimal solutions of this reduced problem are shown to converge to those of the original hard-constrained problem (Proposition 4.2).
 - The optimal policy for this `r_c`-augmented objective is then derived in closed form (Equation 9): `π*(y|x) = (1/Z(x)) * π_ref(y|x) * exp((1/β)r_c(x,y))`. This formulation ensures that `π*(y|x) = 0` for any unsafe response, thereby strictly excluding unsafe outputs from the policy's support by construction.
 - This closed-form policy induces a theoretical preference distribution based on `r_c`, leading to an intractable DPO-style objective (Equation 10) because `r_c` is not directly observable.

2. **From Intractable Form to Tractable Objective via Safety-Aware Transformation**:
 - To make the intractable objective tractable, the authors introduce a safety-aware transformation `T` applied to the empirical preference data `D`. The dataset entries are augmented with binary safety indicators (`h_w, h_l`) for the preferred (winner) and non-preferred (loser) responses.
 - The transformation rules are designed to align with the behavior of `r_c`:
 - If the preferred response (`y_w`) is safe (`h_w=0`), the pair remains unchanged.
 - If `y_w` is unsafe (`h_w=1`) but the non-preferred response (`y_l`) is safe (`h_l=0`), the pair is swapped, making the safe `y_l` the new winner and the unsafe `y_w` the new loser. This ensures that safe responses are always preferred over unsafe ones.
 - If both responses are unsafe (`h_w=1` and `h_l=1`), the pair is discarded, as optimal policy assigns zero probability to such responses.
 - The authors prove that optimizing over this transformed empirical distribution `T(D)` (Equation 11) is provably equivalent to optimizing the intractable objective under the theoretical distribution induced by `r_c` (Proposition 4.3). This forms the core `L_SafeDPO(θ)` objective.

3. **Introduction of a Safety Margin (∆)**:
 - To provide additional control over safety enforcement during training and further leverage safety information, an optional safety margin `∆ >= 0` is introduced. The `L_SafeDPO(θ)` objective is augmented with a term `-(h_l - h_w)∆` (Equation 12).
 - This margin term selectively applies to pairs where a safe response is compared against an unsafe one (after transformation, `h_w=0` and `h_l=1`, so `(h_l - h_w) = 1`). It increases the log-probability gap between safe and unsafe responses, strengthening the learning signal that favors safe outputs.
 - Crucially, Proposition 4.4 demonstrates that introducing this `∆` term does not alter the *set of optimal solutions* of the SafeDPO objective. It provides a tunable mechanism to enhance safety enforcement without changing the theoretical optimum.
 - The framework is extensible to other Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs), forming "SafeDAA" by applying the same transformation and margin.

**Experimental Approach**:
Experiments were conducted on the PKU-SafeRLHF-30K benchmark. A shared reference model (Alpaca-7B SFT) was used. SafeDPO was compared against DPO variants (DPO-HELPFUL, DPO-HARMLESS, DPO-SAFEBETTER), SafeRLHF, SACPO, P-SACPO, MoCAN, and PeCAN. Evaluation metrics included helpfulness, harmlessness (using model-based reward/cost models), and harmless ratio (proportion of safe responses). Evaluation was performed using both model-based evaluators (e.g., `beaver-7b-unified-reward/cost`) and GPT-4/GPT-5.1 as automatic judges. Ablation studies investigated the impact of the `∆` parameter and robustness across model scales (1.5B to 13B). Over-refusal behavior was assessed on the XSTest benchmark.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the performance, efficiency, and robustness of SafeDPO:

- **Enhanced Safety Performance**: SafeDPO demonstrated a substantial improvement in safety. On the PKU-SafeRLHF-30K benchmark, it achieved a harmless ratio of approximately 97% under model-based evaluation and 100% under GPT-4 evaluation. This indicates a high rate of suppressing unsafe generations. Furthermore, SafeDPO attained the highest harmlessness score, suggesting not only a reduction in unsafe responses but also stronger safety margins in borderline cases. Simple dataset filtering (DPO-SAFEBETTER) did not achieve comparable safety levels, highlighting the necessity of an explicit safety-aware optimization mechanism.

- **Competitive Helpfulness**: Despite its strict safety enforcement, SafeDPO maintained competitive helpfulness. Under model-based evaluation, its helpfulness scores were comparable to or slightly surpassed other safety alignment methods. Under GPT-4 evaluation, SafeDPO achieved the highest helpfulness score, although the paper notes that GPT-4 judges may implicitly favor safer responses, potentially conflating safety with helpfulness. Human evaluation indicated that SafeDPO's helpfulness was comparable to SafeRLHF, with both being lower than SFT when helpfulness was defined as directly answering a query, even if the query had harmful intent.

- **Effectiveness and Sensitivity of the ∆ Parameter**:
 - Even without an explicit safety margin (∆ = 0), SafeDPO achieved a high harmless ratio, indicating the primary effectiveness of the safety-aware pair transformation.
 - As ∆ increased, the harmless ratio remained consistently high, suggesting robustness to the choice of the margin. The theoretical prediction that ∆ does not alter the optimal solution was supported by empirical observations that ∆ primarily influenced optimization dynamics rather than final safety performance.
 - Applying the ∆ margin to standard DPO variants did not achieve safety levels comparable to SafeDPO, reinforcing that the safety-aware transformation is the key mechanism.
 - Excessively large ∆ values (e.g., 50) were observed to degrade helpfulness performance, which was attributed to potentially unstable or degenerate behavior from aggressive unlikelihood-style updates in finite training regimes.

- **Robustness Across Models and Scales**: SafeDPO exhibited scalability across various model sizes. Experiments with models ranging from 1.5B to 13B parameters consistently showed strong safety performance alongside maintained or improved helpfulness. This suggests the method's applicability to both smaller and larger LLMs.

- **Over-Refusal Behavior on XSTest Benchmark**:
 - On the XSTest benchmark, designed to probe excessive conservativeness, SafeDPO achieved a 100% harmless ratio, indicating complete elimination of unsafe generations.
 - However, this strict safety came with a higher over-refusal rate (12.4%) compared to other methods (1–4%). This behavior aligns with SafeDPO's hard-constrained formulation, where the model may adopt conservative responses in ambiguous cases where benign prompts (e.g., "kill a Python process") lexically resemble harmful queries.
 - This finding highlights a trade-off: SafeDPO prioritizes strict safety guarantees, potentially at the cost of increased conservativeness on ambiguous prompts.

- **Efficiency**: The appendix details several efficiency benefits. SafeDPO requires fewer network components (only the policy and reference policy) compared to SafeRLHF (which needs reward, cost, and associated value functions), leading to lower memory overhead. Its single-stage, offline optimization nature also results in significantly faster training times (e.g., 1388 seconds for policy training versus 32957 seconds for SafeRLHF's policy training). Data efficiency is also improved as SafeDPO does not require explicit harmlessness preference labels, only binary safety indicators. Finally, SafeDPO introduces only one additional hyperparameter (∆) compared to the multiple hyperparameters required for SafeRLHF.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of large language model safety alignment through its theoretical rigor and empirical performance, with several potential impacts:

- **Theoretical Foundation for Hard-Constrained Safety**: The work provides a principled theoretical framework for hard-constrained safety alignment. By demonstrating that the original constrained objective admits a closed-form optimal policy and deriving a provably equivalent tractable objective, SafeDPO offers a more direct and transparent approach to safety guarantees. This theoretical grounding can inform the development of future alignment algorithms.

- **Algorithmic Simplicity and Efficiency**: SafeDPO significantly reduces the complexity associated with safety alignment. By eliminating the need for auxiliary reward models, cost models, and online sampling, it streamlines the training process. This translates into lower computational resource requirements (memory and time) and reduced engineering overhead, making advanced safety alignment more accessible and scalable for researchers and practitioners. Its minimal additional hyperparameter (∆) further simplifies deployment.

- **Enhanced Safety with Competitive Helpfulness**: Despite its minimalist design, SafeDPO achieves substantial improvements in safety by effectively suppressing unsafe generations, while maintaining helpfulness. This demonstrates that robust safety does not necessarily necessitate complex, multi-component systems, but can be achieved through a carefully reformulated objective. The hard-constrained nature of SafeDPO offers a stronger guarantee against unsafe outputs compared to methods based on expected-cost relaxations, which is critical for deployment in sensitive domains.

- **Impact on Real-World LLM Deployment**: The ability of SafeDPO to achieve competitive safety performance with enhanced efficiency can accelerate the deployment of safer LLMs in practical applications. Its reduced resource demands and simplified implementation lower the barrier for integrating safety considerations into LLM development pipelines.

- **Guidance for Future Research**: The work highlights the inherent trade-off between strict safety enforcement and potential over-refusal on ambiguous but benign prompts, as demonstrated by the XSTest benchmark results. This observation sets a direction for future research to mitigate over-conservativeness without compromising safety guarantees. The generalizability of the SafeDPO framework to other Direct Alignment Algorithms (SafeDAA) also opens avenues for exploring new instantiations and further improving alignment techniques. The simplicity of SafeDPO could establish it as a robust baseline for these future investigations.
