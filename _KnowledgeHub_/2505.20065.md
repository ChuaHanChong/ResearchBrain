# SafeDPO: A Simple Approach to Direct Preference Optimization with Enhanced Safety

> [!summary] Summary
> LG AI Research developed SafeDPO, a direct preference optimization method for large language model safety alignment that derives a provably equivalent, tractable objective from a hard-constrained problem. It achieves high harmlessness (97-100%) while maintaining helpfulness, simplifying the training process by removing auxiliary models.

## Problem

- Existing preference-based LLM alignment methods, such as RLHF and DPO, do not inherently enforce strict safety constraints, allowing preferred responses to still be unsafe.
- Prior safety alignment techniques are often computationally and conceptually complex, requiring auxiliary models, multi-stage optimization, or extensive hyperparameter tuning.
- Many approaches rely on relaxed expected-cost safety formulations, which fail to strictly guarantee the complete elimination of unsafe outputs, a critical requirement for safety-sensitive applications.

## Method

- Redefining the hard-constrained safety alignment problem to derive a closed-form optimal policy by using a ==cost-augmented== reward function that assigns negative infinity to unsafe responses.
- Introducing a safety-aware transformation for preference data that swaps or discards pairs to align with the theoretical preference distribution where safe responses are always preferred over unsafe ones.
- Incorporating an optional safety margin (∆) into the objective to provide additional control over safety enforcement and strengthen the learning signal without altering the set of optimal solutions.

## Results

- SafeDPO achieved high harmlessness, with a ratio of **97%** (model-based) and **100%** (GPT-4) on PKU-SafeRLHF-**30K**, while maintaining competitive helpfulness scores.
- The method significantly reduces computational complexity, requiring fewer network components and achieving substantially faster training times (e.g., 1388 seconds vs. 32957 seconds for policy training compared to SafeRLHF).
- SafeDPO demonstrated strong safety performance and scalability across model sizes (**1.5B** to **13B** parameters) but showed a higher over-refusal rate (**12.4%**) on the XSTest benchmark, indicating a trade-off for strict safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The original hard-constrained safety alignment problem admits a closed-form optimal policy that strictly assigns zero probability to unsafe responses.
> - A safety-aware transformation of empirical preference data can yield a tractable objective that is provably equivalent to the intractable objective derived from the hard-constrained optimal policy.
> - Introducing a safety margin parameter (∆) can enhance optimization dynamics and learning signals for safety without altering the theoretical set of optimal solutions for the policy.
