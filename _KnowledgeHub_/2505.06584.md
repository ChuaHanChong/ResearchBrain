# JAEGER: Dual-Level Humanoid Whole-Body Controller

> [!summary] Summary
> The JAEGER framework introduces a dual-level whole-body controller for adult-sized humanoid robots, capable of robustly integrating both root velocity and local joint angle tracking. This approach, which includes a novel control architecture and training strategy, demonstrates superior tracking accuracy and real-time performance on Unitree H1 and H1-2 platforms.

## Problem

- Developing robust and versatile whole-body controllers for complex adult-sized humanoid robots is challenging due to high-dimensional action spaces and hardware limitations.
- Existing methods struggle to effectively combine coarse-grained (root velocity) and fine-grained (joint angle) control without compromising balance or introducing instability.
- Training instability and interference arise from simultaneously controlling heterogeneous body parts, such as the upper body's pose tracking and the lower body's balance and locomotion.

## Method

- The **JAEGER** framework utilizes a dual-level control architecture, reformulating whole-body control as a ==Dec-POMDP== with independent lower-body (Gated Transformer-XL) and upper-body (MLP) controllers.
- An efficient three-layer MLP retargeting network converts human motion data into smooth and accurate humanoid robot poses at high frequencies (up to 1 kHz) for training and teleoperation.
- A curriculum learning strategy employs supervised initialization with KL regularization for the lower body, followed by PPO-based reinforcement learning, to ensure stable and optimal policy convergence.

## Results

- JAEGER's MLP-based retargeting processed frames at **0.00064** s/frame (over 1 kHz) with a joint position error of **0.13567** m/frame, showing improved speed, accuracy, and smoothness compared to baselines.
- In root-based mode on H1, JAEGER achieved linear velocity error of **0.1504** m/s and angular velocity error of **0.0937** rad/s, outperforming HumanPlus and ExBody in velocity and upper-body tracking.
- In pose-based mode on H1, JAEGER demonstrated significantly lower joint angle tracking errors (**0.0686** rad for upper-body, **0.0658** rad for lower-body), achieving approximately 30-**60%** of the errors seen in other state-of-the-art methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling whole-body control into specialized lower and upper-body agents effectively reduces learning complexity and mitigates mutual interference, leading to more robust and accurate control.
> - Supervised initialization for the upper body combined with KL regularization for the lower body is crucial for stable training, preventing conservative behaviors and guiding the controller to optimal solutions.
> - Lightweight MLP networks can achieve superior real-time performance and smoothness in human-to-robot pose retargeting, which is essential for low-latency applications like teleoperation and high-quality training data generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Whole-body control (WBC) for adult-sized humanoid robots, such as the Unitree H1-2, presents challenges due to hardware constraints and the high dimensionality of the robotic action space. The current research landscape in WBC has seen advancements, often leveraging learning from human motion data to enable versatile robot movements.

Existing WBC methodologies generally fall into three categories:
- **Root velocity tracking:** This approach focuses on coarse-grained control, where the robot tracks a desired velocity without adhering to a specific reference pose. Examples include aspects of ExBody.
- **Kinematic position tracking:** This method focuses on accurately reproducing a given trajectory of reference positions, often for global keypoints. OmniH2O relies on this.
- **Local joint angle tracking:** This also aims for accurate reproduction of reference poses, but through direct control of joint angles. HumanPlus primarily uses this approach. ExBody combines joint angle tracking for the upper body with root velocity tracking for the lower body, while ExBody2 refines this by focusing on lower-body feasible poses and local keypoint tracking.

A challenge in the field has been the effective integration of both coarse-grained (e.g., root velocity) and fine-grained (e.g., joint angle or kinematic position) control within a single, versatile framework. Prior works like OmniH2O and HumanPlus derive velocity tracking capabilities from reference pose trajectories. However, direct imitation of human poses can sometimes hinder a humanoid's balance and robustness due to embodiment differences. HOVER is a concurrent work that also integrates various interfaces (root velocity, joint angles, keypoint positions) into a unified framework through distillation.

The current paper positions itself within this context by addressing the integration challenge through a novel dual-level control architecture and a structured training approach. It proposes decoupling upper and lower body control, reformulating the WBC problem as a multi-agent system, to manage the complexity and heterogeneity of these body parts. This approach aims to improve robustness and versatility, distinguishing it from single-controller or solely pose-imitating methods. The work also emphasizes the importance of an efficient retargeting network for real-time applications and robust training.

The field of humanoid locomotion also increasingly relies on learning algorithms, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL), which have demonstrated robustness over traditional model-based control methods. The use of large motion datasets like AMASS is a common strategy to learn diverse motion skills. JAEGER builds upon these advancements by utilizing AMASS data and integrating supervised learning with RL within a curriculum learning framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a more robust and versatile whole-body controller for adult-sized humanoid robots, specifically addressing the challenges of training such policies.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Training Challenges for Robust and Versatile WBC:** The inherent complexity of robotic action spaces and hardware constraints make achieving effective whole-body control a significant challenge. Traditional single-controller approaches often struggle to train policies that are both robust and versatile, capable of diverse movements.
2. **Integrating Coarse-grained and Fine-grained Control:** A versatile WBC controller should ideally integrate both root velocity tracking (coarse-grained control, for overall movement and navigation) and local joint angle tracking (fine-grained control, for precise pose reproduction). Existing methods have struggled to effectively combine these, with some focusing on one aspect or deriving velocity control indirectly from pose imitation, which can sometimes compromise balance. JAEGER aims to support both modes directly.
3. **Mitigating the Curse of Dimensionality and Interference:** The high dimensionality of the whole-body action space and the heterogeneous functionalities of the upper and lower bodies can lead to training instability. Interactions between upper and lower body control can hinder convergence, with, for example, upper body tracking becoming overly conservative to ensure lower body stability. The paper seeks to alleviate the "curse of dimensionality" and reduce mutual interference.
4. **Improving Fault Tolerance:** Decoupling control for different body parts could enhance fault tolerance, allowing one part of the robot to continue functioning even if another encounters an issue.
5. **Developing an Efficient Retargeting Mechanism:** Leveraging human motion datasets (like AMASS) is crucial, but requires efficient and accurate retargeting of human poses to humanoid robot poses. This process needs to be real-time and produce smooth, accurate joint angles suitable for robot control, especially for low-latency applications like teleoperation.
6. **Establishing an Effective Training Strategy:** Given the complexity of WBC training, a structured approach is necessary to ensure convergence to optimal or near-optimal solutions, avoiding overly conservative behaviors. This includes addressing the initial instability observed when simultaneously training lower-body root velocity tracking and upper-body pose tracking.

In summary, the authors aim to overcome these limitations by proposing a dual-level, multi-agent control architecture, an efficient MLP-based retargeting network, and a structured curriculum learning strategy combining supervised initialization with reinforcement learning, to achieve accurate and stable control across various movement modes on challenging adult-sized humanoid platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The JAEGER framework comprises three main components: a retargeting network, a dual-level controller, and a curriculum learning procedure.

#### 3.1. Retargeting Network

- **Purpose:** To efficiently convert human poses from a dataset into humanoid robot poses for training and real-time teleoperation. It aims for low latency, accuracy, and smoothness.
- **Data Preprocessing:** A two-stage optimization approach (similar to H2O) is initially used to process the AMASS dataset, generating a large set of human and humanoid pose pairs. This data is filtered to remove frames with discontinuous motions resulting from unstable Inverse Kinematics (IK) solutions, ensuring high-quality training data.
- **Mapping Network:** A lightweight three-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) is trained to learn the mapping between the preprocessed human and humanoid pose pairs.
- **Advantages:** This MLP-based approach provides significantly improved real-time performance (operating at up to 1 kHz) compared to traditional optimization-based IK methods. It also ensures smooth output, even with input noise, by acting as a continuous nonlinear function approximator, which helps eliminate frame-to-frame jitter observed with IK.

#### 3.2. Dual-level Controller

- **Formulation:** The whole-body control problem is reformulated as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP) with two independent agents: one for the lower body (policy $\pi_{lower}$) and one for the upper body (policy $\pi_{upper}$).
- **Rationale:** This decoupling aims to decompose the action space, reducing learning complexity, and allowing each controller to specialize in its tasks. It also enhances fault tolerance.
- **Shared Information:** The two controllers share observations and rewards to facilitate effective coordination.
- **Observation:** At each timestep $t$, the controllers receive a history ($n$-step) of proprioceptive states ($o_t$), previous actions ($a_{t-1}$), root velocity tracking targets ($v_t$), and pose tracking targets ($g_t$).
- **Action Space:** The outputs of the policies, $a_{lower,t}$ and $a_{upper,t}$, are concatenated to form the target joint positions for Proportional-Derivative (PD) controllers, which execute the actions on the robot.
- **Command Modes:**
 - **Root-based mode:** Coarse-grained control, involving root velocity commands (linear forward, sideways, and angular turning velocities) for the lower body and reference joint angle commands for the upper body.
 - **Pose-based mode:** Fine-grained control, providing reference joint angle commands for the entire body.
- **Lower-body Controller:** Instantiated as a Gated Transformer-XL architecture (three layers of transformer blocks). This choice is motivated by its performance in locomotion tasks requiring high balance control and its ability to process long observation/action histories (15 timesteps) to encode environmental information and adapt dynamically. Gating mechanisms replace standard residual connections to improve learning stability and speed in RL.
- **Upper-body Controller:** Implemented as a lightweight 3-layer MLP. This simpler architecture is deemed sufficient as the upper body's primary task is to follow reference poses, without the complex balance control requirements of the lower body. It takes a 3-timestep observation history.

#### 3.3. Curriculum Learning

- **Motivation:** Training a whole-body controller with simultaneous root velocity tracking for the lower body and pose tracking for the upper body from scratch using RL is unstable due to interfering objectives.
- **Stages:**
 1. **Base Policy Training:** The lower-body controller is trained independently first, with upper-body joints fixed. This creates a base policy ($\pi_{base}$) for either locomotion or joint angle tracking, depending on the target mode.
 2. **Supervised Initialization:** To address the upper-body controller's tendency to be overly conservative and its interference with lower-body stability:
 - Supervised learning is applied to the upper body controller to directly mimic reference poses, minimizing $L_{SL}(\pi_{upper}) = \|\pi_{upper}(x_{t,3}) - g_{upper,t}\|$.
 - KL regularization is applied to the lower-body controller, $L_{KL}(\pi_{lower}) = D_{KL}(\pi_{lower}(\cdot | x_{t,15})\|\pi_{base}(\cdot | x_{t,15}))$, to prevent significant deviation from the pre-trained base policy.
 - During this stage, the pose amplitude is reduced to half its original value to simplify the task. This initialization provides a solid starting point for subsequent RL training, promoting faster learning and reducing convergence to suboptimal local optima.
 3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Training:**
 - Building upon the initialized policy, the controller's performance is further improved using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm.
 - The pose magnitude is restored to its original range.
 - A detailed reward function is designed (Table 1), incorporating:
 - Tracking rewards for DoF position (upper and lower body), feet height, linear velocity, angular velocity, and roll/pitch.
 - Penalty rewards for torque limits, DoF position/velocity limits, and stumble events (Table 11).
 - Regularization terms for DoF acceleration, action rates (lower and upper body), action smoothness, torque, feet air time, feet contact force, and orientation (Table 11).
 4. **Policy Distillation:** After training policies for both root-based and pose-based modes separately, they are distilled into a single network using imitation learning for unified control. For root-based mode, the lower-body reference pose command is set to zero.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in the IsaacGym simulator on two Unitree humanoid robot platforms: the 19-DoF H1 and the 21-DoF H1-2. Performance was evaluated against HumanPlus, ExBody, and OmniH2O using a curated subset of the AMASS dataset, measuring Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for various tracking metrics. JAEGER's retargeting method was applied as a preprocessing step for all baselines.

#### 4.1. Retargeting Analysis

- **Real-time Performance:** JAEGER's MLP-based retargeting method significantly outperformed baselines, achieving an average processing time of 0.00064 s/frame, compared to HumanPlus (0.00200 s/frame) and H2O (9.05409 s/frame). This rate (over 1000 Hz) is suitable for low-latency teleoperation.
- **Accuracy:** JAEGER achieved joint position error (0.13567 m/frame) comparable to H2O (0.14476 m/frame) and superior to HumanPlus (0.16908 m/frame), which copies angles without considering structural differences.
- **Smoothness:** The MLP-based method demonstrated superior joint angle smoothness, measured by the Root Mean Square of angular acceleration. For example, left shoulder pitch was 0.0069 rad/s$^2$ for JAEGER versus 0.0143 rad/s$^2$ for H2O. This indicates a more continuous and less jittery output, beneficial for real-world deployment.

#### 4.2. Root-based Mode Evaluation

- JAEGER demonstrated superior performance across both H1 and H1-2 platforms compared to HumanPlus and ExBody.
- For H1, JAEGER achieved linear velocity error $E_{root-lin-vel}$ of 0.1504 m/s (vs. 0.1696 m/s for HumanPlus, 0.2417 m/s for ExBody), angular velocity error $E_{root-ang-vel}$ of 0.0937 rad/s (vs. 0.3105 rad/s for HumanPlus, 0.4176 rad/s for ExBody), and upper-body joint angle error $E_{upper-j}$ of 0.1093 rad (vs. 0.2187 rad for HumanPlus, 0.1689 rad for ExBody).
- For H1-2, JAEGER also showed lower errors in velocity tracking and significantly lower upper-body joint angle error (0.0933 rad) compared to HumanPlus (0.2547 rad).
- The H1-2 robot generally exhibited larger tracking errors across all methods, attributed to its heavier upper body, which increases the difficulty of lower-body training.

#### 4.3. Pose-based Mode Evaluation

- JAEGER achieved higher tracking accuracy in both upper-body and lower-body pose tracking compared to HumanPlus and OmniH2O on both H1 and H1-2.
- JAEGER's joint angle tracking errors were approximately 30-60% of those observed in other methods. For H1, $E_{upper-j}$ was 0.0686 rad (vs. 0.2199 rad for HumanPlus, 0.2217 rad for OmniH2O) and $E_{lower-j}$ was 0.0658 rad (vs. 0.1284 rad for HumanPlus, 0.2404 rad for OmniH2O).
- JAEGER also demonstrated comparable accuracy in root tracking errors (velocity, height, roll, pitch, yaw), exhibiting the lowest errors for root roll, pitch, and yaw on H1, indicating effective balance maintenance.
- OmniH2O, being a keypoint position tracking method, showed certain advantages in root tracking but performed worse in joint angle tracking.

#### 4.4. Component Ablation Studies

- **JAEGER w.o. Init (without supervised initialization):** This variant performed worse than the full JAEGER model, particularly in upper-body tracking for the H1-2 in pose-based mode ($E_{upper-j}$ of 0.4088 rad vs. 0.1192 rad for JAEGER). This highlights the importance of supervised initialization for model convergence to an optimal solution.
- **JAEGER Single (single Transformer for whole body):** This variant generally struggled with upper-body joint tracking. For instance, in root-based mode on H1, $E_{upper-j}$ was 0.5377 rad for JAEGER Single compared to 0.1093 rad for JAEGER. In the pose-based mode on H1, $E_{upper-j}$ was 0.2469 rad for JAEGER Single vs. 0.0686 rad for JAEGER. This suggests that a single policy controlling the entire body may lead to interference between upper and lower body movements, potentially sacrificing the performance of one task to improve another. The dual-level controller allows separate controllers to focus more effectively on their respective tasks.

These findings collectively confirm the effectiveness of JAEGER's dual-level controller architecture, the supervised initialization strategy within curriculum learning, and the MLP-based retargeting network.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The JAEGER framework presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid whole-body control, particularly for adult-sized robots.

1. **Enhanced Versatility and Robustness:** By effectively integrating both root velocity tracking (coarse-grained) and local joint angle tracking (fine-grained), JAEGER enables a more versatile range of movements for humanoid robots. The dual-level control architecture, which decouples the upper and lower bodies, contributes to a more robust policy by reducing interference during training and allowing each body part to specialize. This robustness is critical for real-world deployment on complex platforms like Unitree H1-2.
2. **Improved Tracking Accuracy:** The results demonstrate JAEGER's ability to achieve significantly lower tracking errors for both joint angles and root velocities compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. This precision is important for executing intricate tasks and mimicking human motions more faithfully.
3. **Efficient and Smooth Retargeting:** The MLP-based retargeting network provides high-frequency (1 kHz) and smooth pose adaptation, which is crucial for low-latency teleoperation and for generating high-quality training data without jitter. This advancement can facilitate more responsive human-robot interaction and accelerate the development of imitation learning techniques.
4. **Effective Curriculum Learning Strategy:** The proposed curriculum learning approach, including supervised initialization and KL regularization, addresses the inherent instability of training complex whole-body controllers. This strategy provides a generalizable method for more efficient convergence to optimal policies, a common challenge in reinforcement learning for robotics.
5. **Addressing Sim-to-Real Gap Challenges:** While the paper mentions limitations in deploying some complex poses, the comprehensive reward engineering and domain randomization employed in training contribute to improving the sim-to-real transferability of the learned policies, as evidenced by real-world demonstrations.

The potential impact of JAEGER extends to various applications, including:
- **Advanced Teleoperation:** The high-frequency, low-latency retargeting and precise control could enable more intuitive and responsive human-to-humanoid teleoperation for complex tasks.
- **Humanoid Dance and Expressive Motion:** The ability to accurately track diverse human poses allows for more natural and expressive humanoid robot movements, such as performing various dance routines.
- **Complex Locomotion and Manipulation:** The robust control policies, especially for the lower body's balance and velocity tracking, coupled with precise upper-body control, could enable humanoids to perform more complex tasks involving locomotion while simultaneously manipulating objects.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that JAEGER currently does not support a wider range of commands, such as keypoint positions, which might limit its versatility in some applications. Furthermore, certain complex poses, particularly those requiring prolonged single-foot support or large amplitudes, remain challenging for real-world deployment despite being feasible in simulation. The framework also relies on extensive reward engineering, which can be a time-consuming aspect of reinforcement learning development. Addressing these limitations could be areas for future research.
