# ManipDreamer3D : Synthesizing Plausible Robotic Manipulation Video with Occupancy-aware 3D Trajectory

> [!summary] Summary
> ManipDreamer3D presents a framework for generating realistic robotic manipulation videos with physically plausible actions by integrating 3D scene understanding and optimal 3D trajectory planning. The approach yields synthetic data suitable for training robot policies, exhibiting improved video quality and precise adherence to planned robot and object trajectories.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality, diverse training data for robotic manipulation policies, as real-world data collection is time-consuming and expensive.
- Existing generative video models for robotics often lack 3D spatial awareness, leading to physically implausible trajectories, collisions, or inefficient actions in synthesized videos.
- Many current methods for synthetic data generation require significant manual intervention, such as manual object selection or explicit guidance, limiting their scalability and autonomy.

## Method

- Reconstructs a 64x64x64 3D occupancy map of the static scene from a single input image, leveraging neural surface reconstruction for continuous representation.
- Plans locally optimal and physically plausible 3D manipulation paths using a three-stage process involving the A* algorithm and CHOMP-inspired gradient-descent optimization for collision avoidance and smoothness.
- Employs a parameter-free trajectory-guided video synthesis scheme that projects 3D robot and object trajectories into 2D mask maps, then dynamically edits the latent space of a diffusion model to control video generation.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance in video quality, with a Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 93.98 (DiT version) and superior PSNR/SSIM metrics compared to existing generative models.
- Demonstrated significantly reduced trajectory errors for both the robot end-effector (15.38) and the manipulated object (16.59) using the DiT version, indicating precise adherence to planned 3D paths.
- Successfully generated videos showcasing fine-grained manipulation control, including adherence to specific object 'affordance areas' and ensuring physically safe, collision-free robot movements after trajectory optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit 3D scene understanding via occupancy maps and optimal 3D trajectory planning is critical for generating physically plausible and collision-free robotic manipulation videos.
> - Optimizing A*-generated trajectories with objectives for collision avoidance, path length, and smoothness significantly enhances the safety and realism of simulated robot movements.
> - A latent-editing strategy that dynamically overlays 3D-projected object and end-effector information onto the initial latent provides precise, fine-grained control over diffusion-based video generation without modifying the model architecture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in ManipDreamer3D addresses a critical bottleneck in robotic manipulation: the **scarcity of high-quality, diverse training data**. The ability to train robust and generalizable robotic policies is heavily reliant on large datasets, which are notoriously time-consuming, labor-intensive, and hardware-constrained to collect in the real world. This paper positions itself within the evolving landscape of **synthetic data generation for robotics**, particularly focusing on **video generation for robotic manipulation**.

Current approaches to mitigating data scarcity generally fall into a few categories:
- **Data Augmentation:** Techniques like replaying recorded trajectories with diverse visual augmentations (e.g., altering objects, textures, backgrounds, camera viewpoints) are common (Mandi et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2023; Fang et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2025a). While useful, these methods are limited to variations of existing demonstrations and do not generate entirely novel manipulation sequences with new trajectories.
- **Scene Reconstruction in Simulation:** Methods like Re3Sim (Han et al. 2025) reconstruct realistic scenes in simulation to synthesize plausible demonstrations. ORV (Yang et al. 2025b) uses 4D semantic occupancy to generate realistic videos from real or simulated data. These approaches aim for higher fidelity but can still struggle with maintaining perfect geometric and physical consistency with the real world.
- **Modeling Robotic Actions with Generative Models:** Recent advancements leverage diffusion models to generate realistic robotic videos. Examples include This&That (Wang et al. 2025a), which conditions generation on object and target gestures, and RoboMaster (Fu et al. 2025), which jointly models robot and object trajectories.

ManipDreamer3D directly confronts several key limitations of these existing generative methods:
1. **Lack of 3D Spatial Awareness:** Many prior works, especially diffusion models, primarily rely on 2D trajectories or sparse 2D control signals. This inherent 2D ambiguity often leads to generated videos containing physically implausible trajectories, lacking collision avoidance, or exhibiting inefficient execution. Robotic actions, by nature, are planned and executed in 3D space.
2. **Heavy Reliance on Manual Intervention:** Existing methods often require manual object selection or explicit manual guidance for control (e.g., 2D Gaussian maps for object/target gestures). This limits autonomy and scalability.
3. **Geometric and Physical Inconsistency:** Generated scenes often fail to maintain consistency with real-world environments, leading to inaccuracies in object size, placement, or contact states, which makes them less suitable for training robust robotic policies.

The paper bridges the gap between sophisticated 3D environment understanding and state-of-the-art video generation techniques. It integrates concepts from **robotic trajectory planning** (similar to Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) or Vision-Language-Action (VLA) tasks but adapted for global optimal paths in occupancy space) with **trajectory-controlled video generation** (extending diffusion models that guide motion using sparse bounding boxes or 2D Gaussian maps). By explicitly incorporating 3D occupancy maps and optimized 3D trajectories, ManipDreamer3D moves beyond superficial visual realism to achieve **physical plausibility and geometric consistency**, which are critical for the utility of synthetic data in robotic policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind ManipDreamer3D stems from the fundamental challenge of **data scarcity in robotic manipulation**. Training robust and generalizable robot policies demands vast amounts of diverse demonstration data, which is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to collect in real-world settings. While synthetic video generation offers a promising alternative, existing methods fall short in producing videos that are both visually realistic and physically plausible for real robotic execution.

The key objectives of ManipDreamer3D are meticulously designed to address these limitations:

1. **Generate Plausible and Physically Consistent Robotic Manipulation Videos:** The primary goal is to synthesize videos where robotic actions are not only visually convincing but also adhere to physical laws, ensuring collision avoidance, smooth motion, and realistic object interactions. This moves beyond merely aesthetic video generation to creating data that is genuinely useful for robot policy training.
2. **Incorporate 3D Spatial Awareness into Video Generation:** A core objective is to overcome the "3D spatial ambiguity" inherent in 2D trajectory-based control methods. By reconstructing a 3D occupancy map of the scene, the framework aims to enable 3D-aware planning, making the generated trajectories physically viable.
3. **Automate 3D Trajectory Planning with Minimal Human Intervention:** A significant motivation is to reduce the need for manual annotations or explicit human guidance. The system should autonomously plan optimal 3D end-effector trajectories based on a single input image and text instruction, rather than relying on manually specified 2D keyframes or object gestures. This enhances the scalability and usability of the generation process.
4. **Achieve Fine-Grained and Precise Control over Robotic Actions:** The framework seeks to enable comprehensive control over manipulation, including keypoint, full-trajectory, and even affordance-level control (i.e., specifying precise contact points on objects), which is crucial for learning intricate manipulation skills.
5. **Develop an Efficient Trajectory-to-Video Synthesis Scheme:** The objective is to seamlessly integrate the planned 3D trajectories into existing diffusion-based video generation models without requiring complex auxiliary modules or significantly altering the model architecture, ensuring efficiency and compatibility.
6. **Improve Video Quality and Trajectory Adherence:** Quantitatively and qualitatively demonstrate superior visual fidelity, temporal consistency, and, crucially, a much lower trajectory error for both the robot end-effector and the manipulated object compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

In essence, ManipDreamer3D aims to provide a robust, autonomous, and physically grounded framework for generating high-quality synthetic robotic manipulation data, thereby accelerating the development of more capable and generalizable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ManipDreamer3D proposes a novel, end-to-end framework for generating 3D-aware robotic manipulation videos. The methodology is structured around four main components: 3D Occupancy Map Reconstruction, Optimal Trajectory Planning, 3D Trajectory Data Curation (for training), and Trajectory-Guided Video Synthesis.

#### 3.1. 3D Occupancy Map Reconstruction

To enable effective 3D motion planning, the method first builds an accurate 3D representation of the static scene from a single third-view observation image ($I_0$). This involves a three-step process:
1. **Initial Point Cloud Generation:** The VGGT model (Wang et al. 2025b), known for its 3D scene understanding capabilities, is used to generate an initial, albeit potentially discontinuous, point cloud in camera coordinates.
2. **Neural Surface Reconstruction:** To address discontinuities and recover a continuous surface, a neural surface reconstruction technique (Huang et al. 2023) is applied to the raw point cloud.
3. **Occupancy Grid Discretization:** The reconstructed surface is then uniformly resampled to create a denser point cloud, which is finally converted into a 64x64x64 occupancy grid. Each voxel in this grid indicates the presence or absence of matter, balancing precision with computational efficiency for path searching.

#### 3.2. Optimal Trajectory Planning

Building on the 3D occupancy map, ManipDreamer3D plans a locally optimal and physically plausible manipulation path using a three-stage method:
1. **Initial Trajectory Generation (A* Algorithm):** An initial, heuristic trajectory ($P_{init}^3$) is generated using the A* algorithm, applied sequentially for three distinct phases of a pick-and-place task:
 - **Approaching Stage ($P_1$):** From the end-effector's 3D start position to the object's initial position.
 - **Manipulating Stage ($P_2$):** While grasping the object, from its initial position to the target destination.
 - **Back-idle Stage ($P_3$):** Returning the end-effector to a safe, initial position.
2. **Trajectory Optimization (CHOMP-inspired):** The A*-generated sub-trajectories are further optimized using a gradient-descent approach, inspired by the CHOMP algorithm, to enhance safety and smoothness. The optimization minimizes a composite objective function consisting of:
 - **Collision Loss ($L_{col}$):** Uses a signed distance field (SDF) of the scene to penalize paths too close to obstacles.
 - **Path Length Loss ($L_{len}$):** Minimizes the total length of the trajectory for efficiency.
 - **Smoothness Loss ($L_{acc}$, $L_{cur}$):** Comprises an acceleration loss (penalizing sharp changes in velocity) and a curvature loss (penalizing sharp turns) to ensure realistic robot motion.
 The optimization is performed using the Adam optimizer, yielding optimized sub-trajectories ($P_{opt}^3$).
3. **Path-aware Time Reallocation:** To ensure the trajectory's speed profile aligns with real robot mechanics (e.g., accelerate then decelerate), a post-processing step redistributes trajectory points. The number of points in each sub-trajectory is reassigned proportionally to its arc length, and points are repositioned via interpolation to match a predefined velocity profile (e.g., a sine wave). This ensures realistic temporal dynamics.

#### 3.3. 3D Trajectory Data Curation (for training the video generation model)

To train the video generation model, 3D trajectories for both the robot and the object are extracted from existing real-world videos:
1. **3D Scene Reconstruction:** VGGT is used to generate temporally consistent 3D point clouds and camera parameters for each frame of the training videos.
2. **End-Effector Localization:** A fine-tuned YOLO model detects gripper fingers in 2D. The 2D bounding box centers are mapped to 3D coordinates, and their midpoint defines the 3D gripper location.
3. **Object Detection and Segmentation:** The grasping initiation moment is identified. Qwen-VL (Bai et al. 2023) with an LLM-parsed instruction and the gripper's 2D center guides visual grounding to identify the object. SAM (Kirillov etal. 2023) then generates precise 2D object masks. This process extracts high-fidelity 3D trajectories for training.

#### 3.4. Trajectory-Guided Video Synthesis

This component transforms the planned 3D trajectories into a suitable format for controlling video generation via a diffusion model:
1. **3D-to-2D Trajectory Representation:**
 - **Latent Editing Preparation:** The latent code of the first video frame is extracted using the VAE of the video diffusion model. Pooled latent vectors represent the manipulated object and end-effector (with biases for gripper states).
 - **2D Mask Projection from 3D Trajectories:** The 3D object and gripper trajectories are projected into 2D mask maps for each frame. Object and gripper are modeled as spheres (with radii based on object dimensions or predefined fixed size for gripper). Their depth is estimated relative to the camera, and then standard perspective projection converts 3D spheres to 2D circles (masks), with scale reflecting distance.
 - **Temporal Latent Construction:** The initial first-frame latent is kept for the first frame. For subsequent frames, the object and gripper latent vectors are overlaid within their projected 2D mask regions onto the first latent, creating a dynamically edited latent video sequence.
2. **Conditioned Video Generation:** Unlike methods that use ControlNet or injection modules, ManipDreamer3D directly concatenates this constructed dynamic latent with the noisy video latent along the channel dimension. This replaces the traditional static first-frame condition with a dynamic, trajectory-aware video condition. For UNet-based models (like SVD), the latent is directly used. For DiT-based models (like CogVideoX-5B), a temporal pooling step aligns the latent dimensions. This "latent-editing" strategy is parameter-free, maintains the original diffusion model framework, and minimizes distribution shift, ensuring precise control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ManipDreamer3D demonstrates superior performance across both quantitative metrics and qualitative observations, showcasing its effectiveness in generating high-quality, physically plausible robotic manipulation videos with precise trajectory control.

#### 4.1. Superior Video Quality and Trajectory Accuracy

The quantitative evaluations highlight ManipDreamer3D's advancements:
- **Common Video Quality Metrics (FVD, PSNR, SSIM):**
 - **ManipDreamer3D (SVD) Version:** Achieves state-of-the-art results among SVD-based models with an FVD of 143.33 (lower is better), PSNR of 22.75 (higher is better), and SSIM of 0.807 (higher is better). This outperforms DragAnything (FVD 158.42) and This&That (FVD 148.69).
 - **ManipDreamer3D (DiT) Version:** Further elevates performance, achieving an FVD of 93.98 and SSIM of 0.847, significantly surpassing DiT-based RoboMaster (FVD 147.31, SSIM 0.803).
- **Trajectory Accuracy (TrajError):** This is a critical metric for robotic applications.
 - **ManipDreamer3D (SVD) Version:** Demonstrates significantly reduced trajectory errors for both the robot end-effector (17.40) and the manipulated object (18.77) compared to SVD-based competitors (e.g., This&That had 62.07 for robot, 37.12 for object).
 - **ManipDreamer3D (DiT) Version:** Achieves even more precise trajectory adherence with errors of 15.38 for the robot and 16.59 for the object, outperforming RoboMaster (16.47 for robot, 24.16 for object). This superior accuracy is attributed to the explicit modeling of both gripper and object spatial positions, unlike methods relying on ambiguous 2D gestures or implicit modeling.
- **VBench Metrics:** Evaluation using VBench, a comprehensive benchmark for video generative models, reveals balanced strengths:
 - The SVD model leads in temporal stability (97.98 Flickering score) and consistency metrics (95.39 Subject Consistency, 96.57 Background Consistency).
 - The DiT variant maintains competitive Imaging Quality (68.65) and robust motion handling (98.70 Smoothness).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Improvements

Visual comparisons (e.g., Figure 5) show that ManipDreamer3D better preserves the original shape and appearance of objects during manipulation, whereas baselines like This&That often exhibit noticeable shape distortions. This qualitative advantage aligns with the quantitative metrics, confirming superior visual quality and realism.

#### 4.3. Key Components Analysis

- **Precision Manipulation Control:** Experiments demonstrate ManipDreamer3D's capability for fine-grained control. By specifying different "affordance areas" (contact points) on an object (e.g., a pot), the model successfully generates manipulation videos that accurately adhere to these specific contact conditions (Figure 7). This highlights its potential for teaching intricate robot skills.
- **Impact of Trajectory Optimization:** The trajectory optimization process is shown to be crucial for generating safe and plausible robot behaviors. Without optimization, initial A*-generated paths might lead to collisions (e.g., the gripper moving too close to the sink edge). The optimized paths (Figure 6) ensure collision avoidance and physically safer movements, which is vital for downstream robot policy learning, where unsafe behaviors are undesirable.

In summary, ManipDreamer3D sets a new benchmark in robotic manipulation video generation by effectively integrating 3D scene understanding and optimal trajectory planning into diffusion models. Its ability to produce physically plausible, high-quality videos with precise trajectory control significantly advances the field towards more practical applications in robot learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManipDreamer3D represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation and synthetic data generation, with profound potential impacts on various aspects of robotics research and deployment:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity in Robotic Policy Learning:** This is the most direct and crucial impact. By enabling the autonomous generation of realistic and physically plausible robotic manipulation videos, ManipDreamer3D offers a scalable solution to the persistent problem of data scarcity. This synthetic data can significantly reduce the need for expensive, time-consuming, and often hazardous real-world robot demonstrations, accelerating the development of robust and generalizable robotic policies.

2. **Enhancing Realism and Physical Plausibility:** Unlike previous methods that often overlook 3D spatial constraints, ManipDreamer3D's occupancy-aware 3D trajectory planning ensures that generated videos depict collision-free, smooth, and kinematically valid robot movements. This physical consistency makes the synthetic data much more valuable for training real robots, as policies learned from such data are less likely to encounter impossible or unsafe situations in deployment.

3. **Reducing Human Intervention and Increasing Autonomy:** The framework's ability to plan 3D trajectories autonomously from a single image and text instruction drastically reduces the manual effort required to create diverse manipulation scenarios. This moves away from laborious 2D keyframe annotations or manual object selections, making the data generation process more efficient and scalable.

4. **Enabling Fine-Grained Control for Complex Skills:** The demonstrated capability for precision manipulation control, including conditioning on specific object "affordances" (different grasp points), is critical for teaching robots complex, dexterous manipulation skills. This level of control allows researchers to synthesize highly specific and nuanced interactions that would be difficult to capture or specify manually.

5. **Advancing Motion-Controlled Video Generation:** Beyond robotics, the novel trajectory-to-video synthesis scheme, which seamlessly integrates dynamic 3D-projected information into diffusion models without auxiliary modules, contributes to the broader field of motion-controlled video generation. This approach offers a powerful and efficient way to guide video synthesis with precise spatial and temporal information.

6. **Improved Safety and Generalization for Embodied AI:** By providing high-fidelity supervision data that accurately reflects physical realities, ManipDreamer3D can contribute to training more robust and safer robot policies. Policies trained on data that includes diverse, physically plausible interaction scenarios are more likely to generalize to novel situations and handle unexpected variations in real environments.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies limitations, such as its current focus on rigid-body interactions. This opens clear avenues for future work to explore more complex physical phenomena like object articulation, deformation, and compliance-aware manipulation, further extending the realism and utility of synthetic data.

In essence, ManipDreamer3D provides a powerful tool that moves synthetic data generation for robotics from merely "realistic-looking" to "physically plausible and robot-actionable," thereby bridging a crucial gap towards more autonomous and capable robotic systems.
