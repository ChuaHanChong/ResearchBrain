# ReconVLA: Reconstructive Vision-Language-Action Model as Effective Robot Perceiver

> [!summary] Summary
> ReconVLA introduces a reconstructive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that enhances visual attention grounding by implicitly training the VLA to reconstruct the "gaze region" of the manipulated object in a latent space. This approach achieves a 3.95 average subtask length on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark and demonstrates improved generalization to unseen objects in real-world robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Robotic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from dispersed visual attention, hindering precise object interaction.
- This lack of precise visual grounding causes inaccurate manipulation, struggles with long-horizon tasks, and poor generalization to novel environments.
- Existing visual grounding paradigms (explicit or Chain-of-Thought) often rely on external models or are challenging to train effectively, failing to fundamentally improve VLA's intrinsic attention.

## Method

- A ==reconstructive learning== framework is integrated into **ReconVLA**, training it to perform ==latent visual reconstruction== of the "gaze region" (target object).
- A lightweight ==diffusion transformer denoiser==, conditioned on the VLA's internal ==visual tokens==, learns to predict noise added to latent "scene tokens" derived from the gaze region.
- A large-scale visual pretraining dataset, automatically annotated with gaze regions via ==Grounding DINO==, is used to enhance the VLA's generative capabilities and generalization to diverse manipulation tasks.

## Results

- ReconVLA achieved the highest average subtask completion length of **3.95** on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, surpassing other grounding paradigms.
- Visualizations demonstrated concentrated attention on target objects, translating to a **79.5%** success rate for "stack block" compared to **59.3%** for the baseline.
- Real-world experiments showed superior generalization, with ReconVLA successfully manipulating unseen objects where other Vision-Language-Action models failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit visual grounding, achieved by training the VLA to reconstruct the task-relevant "gaze region," effectively refines the model's internal attention.
> - This reconstructive learning paradigm inherently forces the VLA to encode precise visual information, improving fine-grained perception for manipulation tasks.
> - Pretraining the VLA on a large, diverse dataset of robot trajectories with automatically annotated gaze regions is essential for building robust visual generation capabilities and enhancing generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

ReconVLA operates within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to bridge perceptual understanding (vision), linguistic comprehension (language), and physical execution (action) in robotic agents. This field builds heavily on the success of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) (e.g., LLaVA, OpenFlamingo, PaliGemma) that have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in integrating visual and textual information.

The core challenge addressed by ReconVLA is the "dispersed visual attention" observed in many current VLA models. While VLAs can process multimodal inputs and generate actions, their internal mechanisms for *focusing* on relevant visual information—especially in cluttered environments or for precise manipulation—are often suboptimal. This limitation can lead to misidentification of target objects or inaccurate action execution.

The paper positions ReconVLA in contrast to several existing paradigms for improving visual grounding in robotics:
- **Explicit Grounding:** Methods like RoboGround (Huang et al. 2025) and VIP (Li et al. 2025) use external object detectors (e.g., LISA, YOLOv11) to segment or crop target objects, which are then fed into the VLA as additional visual inputs. ReconVLA argues that these methods rely on "external expert models" and do not fundamentally enhance the VLA's *intrinsic* visual grounding capabilities or attention allocation. They provide pre-grounded information rather than teaching the VLA to ground itself.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Grounding:** Approaches like ECoT (Zawalski et al. 2024) and GraspVLA (Deng et al. 2025) instruct the VLA to first output bounding box coordinates (a "thought" step) before generating actions. ReconVLA suggests that outputting precise coordinates along with actions can be challenging to train and may not effectively guide the model's attention.
- **Generative Methods for Manipulation:** A separate line of research (e.g., UniPi, SuSIE, GR-1, 3D-VLA, GEVRM) focuses on using generative models, often diffusion models, to predict future states (images or subgoals) to aid in robotic planning. While ReconVLA also uses a generative component (diffusion transformer), its purpose is distinct: it reconstructs the *current gaze region* to refine *perception* and *attention grounding*, rather than predicting future states for planning. This highlights a shift from future-oriented generation for planning to current-oriented generation for improved perception.

ReconVLA's "implicit grounding" paradigm aims to overcome the limitations of these existing methods by directly supervising the VLA's visual outputs to reconstruct the "gaze region" (the target manipulated object). This approach is novel in how it leverages reconstructive learning to guide the internal attention mechanisms of the VLA, fostering a more fundamental improvement in visual perception and, consequently, precise manipulation. It draws inspiration from "reconstructive visual instruction tuning" (Wang et al. 2024), adapting this concept to the action-oriented domain of VLAs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ReconVLA stems from an empirical observation: traditional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit **dispersed visual attention**. Instead of focusing sharply on the specific objects relevant to an instruction or task, their attention maps are widely distributed across the scene. This lack of precise visual grounding leads to several critical issues for robotic manipulation:
1. **Imprecise Manipulation:** When attention is scattered, the robot struggles to accurately identify and interact with the target object, especially in cluttered environments or when fine-grained control is required. This can result in manipulating incorrect objects or failing to achieve precise grasps.
2. **Difficulty in Long-Horizon Tasks:** Many complex robotic tasks involve a sequence of sub-tasks, each requiring interaction with a different object. Dispersed attention makes it harder for the VLA to correctly identify and switch focus to the next relevant object, hindering its ability to complete long-horizon tasks reliably.
3. **Limited Generalization:** Without robust visual grounding, VLAs may struggle to adapt to new environments or unseen objects, as their understanding of "what to focus on" is not deeply integrated.

To address these limitations, the **key objectives** of ReconVLA are:
- **To guide visual attention grounding:** Develop a mechanism that forces the VLA model to concentrate its visual attention on the correct target regions within an image.
- **To learn fine-grained representations:** Enable the VLA to extract detailed, region-specific visual information, essential for precise interaction.
- **To enhance visual grounding capabilities:** Fundamentally improve the VLA's internal ability to associate specific parts of the visual input with the language instruction and desired action.
- **To achieve precise manipulation:** Translate improved visual grounding into more accurate and reliable robotic actions.
- **To boost generalization:** Ensure the model can effectively ground and manipulate objects in diverse scenes and with unseen targets, moving beyond its training data.

The paper proposes an **implicit grounding paradigm** to achieve these objectives. Instead of explicitly providing bounding boxes or cropped images, ReconVLA implicitly encourages focused attention by introducing an auxiliary task: reconstructing the "gaze region" (the target manipulated object) from the VLA's internal visual outputs. This reconstructive process acts as a strong supervisory signal, prompting the VLA to learn representations that inherently prioritize relevant visual information, akin to how humans naturally focus their gaze on critical areas.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReconVLA introduces a novel reconstructive learning framework built upon existing VLA architectures, designed to enhance visual grounding through an implicit supervision mechanism.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Standard VLA Architecture

ReconVLA builds upon a standard VLA model, typically comprising:
- **Vision Encoder (E):** Processes input images (I) into image tokens ($h_I$).
- **Tokenizer (T):** Converts text instructions (S) into text tokens ($h_S$).
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** The core transformer-based model that takes concatenated image and text tokens and generates action tokens (a) in an autoregressive manner.
- **Action Detokenizer (Q):** Maps the action tokens into executable robotic actions (A).

The baseline VLA is constructed using LLaVA-7b (Liu et al. 2024b) as the VLM backbone, which itself leverages Qwen2-7b (Yang et al. 2024a) as the LLM and SigLIP-so400m-patch14-384 (Zhai et al. 2023) as the vision encoder.

#### 3.2. Reconstructive Vision-Language-Action Model (ReconVLA)

The core innovation of ReconVLA lies in its **reconstructive part**, which operates in parallel with the action generation part.

**Reconstruction Target: The Gaze Region:**
Inspired by human eye gaze, ReconVLA focuses its reconstructive efforts on the "gaze region"—the specific part of the image corresponding to the object currently being manipulated. This is crucial because it ensures the model learns to attend to task-relevant areas, rather than just any salient feature. This mechanism also implicitly aids in sub-task planning for long-horizon tasks by dynamically shifting focus between different sub-goals.

**Latent Visual Reconstruction Process:**
Instead of reconstructing raw RGB pixels, ReconVLA performs reconstruction in a latent space, following best practices in generative modeling.
1. **Visual Tokenizer (F):** A frozen variational autoencoder (VAE) (Kingma and Welling 2022, as used in Rombach et al. 2022) extracts low-level, detailed visual features of the gaze region ($I'$) into latent "scene tokens" ($z_0 = F(I')$). The VAE's high fidelity in capturing fine-grained image features is key here.
2. **Diffusion Denoiser (D):** The denoiser is a lightweight diffusion transformer (Peebles and Xie 2022). Its task is to predict the noise ($\epsilon$) added to a noisy version of the scene tokens ($z_t$) at different diffusion timesteps (t).
3. **Conditioning on Reconstructive Tokens:** The denoiser's prediction is conditioned on "reconstructive tokens" ($h_R$), which are derived from the VLA's internal visual outputs ($h_R = LLM(h_I)$). This is the crucial link: the VLA's internal representation *guides* the reconstruction. This implicit conditioning forces the VLA to encode the necessary information about the gaze region into its visual outputs.
4. **Loss Function:** The overall training objective for ReconVLA is a combination of the standard VLA action prediction loss ($L_{action}^{VLA}$, cross-entropy) and a diffusion-based visual reconstruction loss ($L_{visual}^{VLA}$):
 $L_{ReconVLA} = L_{action}^{VLA} + L_{visual}^{VLA}$
 The visual loss minimizes the squared difference between the predicted noise and the actual noise: $L_{visual}^{VLA}(h_R, I') = E_{t,\epsilon} ||D(z_t; h_R, t) - \epsilon||^2$.

**Instruction-Visual Token Alignment:** To ensure the VLA's visual tokens properly attend to instructions, text tokens are prepended to image tokens, allowing causal attention to fuse information.

#### 3.3. Visual Pretraining

A key insight is that VLA models, typically fine-tuned for understanding and action, lack robust visual *generation* capabilities needed for reconstruction. To address this, ReconVLA incorporates a large-scale visual pretraining stage.

**Dataset Curation:**
- A diverse, large-scale dataset is created from open-source robotic datasets: BridgeData V2 (Walke et al. 2023, 2024), LIBERO (Liu et al. 2024a), and CALVIN (Mees et al. 2021).
- **Automatic Gaze Region Annotation:** Grounding DINO (Liu et al. 2024c), a state-of-the-art open-vocabulary object detector, is fine-tuned and used to automatically segment and crop the gaze region corresponding to the instructed manipulation target for each image-text pair. This process generates pairwise (entire image, gaze region image) samples.
- The resulting dataset comprises over 100k trajectories and 2 million samples, significantly boosting the model's capacity for generalized visual reconstruction.

**Training during Pretraining:** During this stage, gradient backpropagation occurs for *both* the reconstruction loss and the action loss. This ensures that the model develops strong visual reconstruction capabilities without degrading its action generation performance, maintaining consistency with the overall optimization target. This pretraining enhances the VLM's generative abilities, making it more robust to diverse environments and tasks.

Finally, after pretraining, the model is finetuned on specific downstream tasks to align its improved vision-language comprehension and reconstruction abilities with precise manipulation in the target action space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReconVLA's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments in simulation (CALVIN benchmark) and real-world setups.

#### 4.1. Paradigm Comparison (Table 1)

- **Baseline:** Achieves an average length of 3.36 out of 5 subtasks on CALVIN ABC→D.
- **Explicit Grounding (EG):** Improves slightly over baseline (3.61 average length), suggesting that providing pre-grounded information helps, but the redundancy of concatenating entire and cropped images limits performance.
- **Chain-of-Thought Grounding (CG):** Performs significantly worse (0.63 average length), indicating that directly outputting bounding box coordinates before actions is insufficient and challenging for VLA models to learn effectively.
- **Implicit Grounding (IG, ReconVLA):** Achieves the highest success rates across all subtasks and the best average length (3.95). This confirms the superiority of ReconVLA's implicit grounding approach, highlighting its ability to precisely attend to visual information without extra inputs or complex CoT outputs.

#### 4.2. In-depth Analysis (Figure 4)

- **Attention Visualization:** Qualitative analysis shows that ReconVLA's attention maps are highly concentrated on the target object (gaze region), unlike the dispersed attention of the baseline. For example, in the "put watermelon into yellow bowl" task, ReconVLA correctly focuses on the watermelon, enabling successful task completion, whereas the baseline's attention is scattered, leading to failure.
- **Precise Manipulation:** In challenging tasks like "stack block," ReconVLA significantly boosts the success rate from 59.3% (baseline) to 79.5%, demonstrating that its gazing mechanism translates directly into enhanced action accuracy.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 2)

- **Reconstructive Part:** Removing the reconstructive part ("Recon. $\times$") significantly decreases performance (3.42 average length), confirming its importance for enhancing holistic visual attention.
- **Gaze Region:** Reconstructing the entire image instead of just the gaze region ("Gaze Region $\times$" but "Recon. $\checkmark$") yields lower performance (3.85 average length) compared to using the gaze region (3.95). This shows that focusing reconstruction on the target object (gaze region) is more effective for guiding visual attention and precise manipulation than reconstructing the entire image.
- **Pretraining:** Disabling pretraining ("Pretrain Splits $\times$") leads to a notable drop in performance (3.85 average length compared to 3.95 with pretraining). This validates that the large-scale pretraining dataset is crucial for the model's generative capabilities and generalization, especially in unseen test environments.

#### 4.4. Comparison with State-of-the-arts (Table 3 & 4)

- **CALVIN ABC→D (Unseen Backgrounds):** ReconVLA achieves the highest average length (3.95) and competitive success rates (e.g., 64.1% on the 5th subtask). It outperforms generative methods like GR-1 (40.1% on 5th subtask) and CLOVER (45.4%) by substantial margins, demonstrating that strong perception of current observations is more beneficial than just predicting future images. It also surpasses large VLA models like OpenVLA (43.5%) and UniVLA (56.5%) on the last subtask, highlighting the effectiveness of its implicit grounding.
- **CALVIN ABCD→D (Seen Backgrounds):** ReconVLA maintains competitive performance, achieving an average length of 4.23 and high success rates (70.5% on the 5th subtask), on par with or slightly better than top models like GR-1 (4.21 average length, 73.1% on 5th subtask) and RoboFlamingo (4.08 average length, 66.0% on 5th subtask).

#### 4.5. Multi-task Experiments in the Real World (Figure 5 & 6)

- **Seen Tasks:** ReconVLA consistently outperforms OpenVLA and PD-VLA across four real-world tasks (Put Fruit into Bowl, Stack Bowls, Flip Cups, Bus Table), achieving success rates near or above 90% for "Put Fruit into Bowl" and "Stack Bowls." It shows significant improvements in fine-grained manipulation tasks like "Flip Cups" and "Bus Table," where other VLAs struggle.
- **Unseen Tasks:** Critically, for tasks involving unseen target objects not present in training data, ReconVLA maintains a respectable success rate, while OpenVLA and PD-VLA exhibit nearly 0% success. This is a strong testament to ReconVLA's enhanced visual generalization capabilities due to its large-scale mixed-data pretraining and implicit grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReconVLA model presents a significant advancement in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, primarily by tackling the fundamental issue of dispersed visual attention, which limits the precision and generalization of robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Robotic Precision:** By implicitly guiding the VLA to focus its attention on task-relevant "gaze regions," ReconVLA enables significantly more precise manipulation. This is crucial for tasks requiring delicate handling, accurate placement, or interaction in cluttered environments, moving robots closer to human-level dexterity. The ability to "stack blocks" with higher accuracy or "flip cups" effectively showcases this enhanced precision.

2. **Improved Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** The large-scale pretraining dataset, coupled with the reconstructive learning objective, endows ReconVLA with superior generalization capabilities. Its ability to successfully manipulate *unseen objects* in real-world experiments is a major breakthrough. This means robots can adapt to novel items or environments without extensive re-training, drastically reducing deployment costs and increasing versatility. This is a critical step towards truly general-purpose robotic agents.

3. **More Robust Long-Horizon Task Execution:** The implicit grounding mechanism facilitates better sub-task planning and sequential manipulation. By consistently focusing on the correct target for each step of a multi-stage task, ReconVLA demonstrates higher success rates and average completion lengths in complex long-horizon benchmarks like CALVIN, indicating improved task coherence and adaptability.

4. **Novel Implicit Grounding Paradigm:** ReconVLA introduces and validates an "implicit grounding" approach that contrasts with prevalent explicit and chain-of-thought methods. This paradigm offers a more integrated and computationally efficient way for VLAs to learn visual grounding, as it doesn't rely on external expert models or necessitate sequential bounding box outputs. It proposes that by forcing the VLA to reconstruct what it "sees" as important, it fundamentally refines its internal perception and attention allocation. This could inspire new directions in visual grounding research for robotics.

5. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The robust performance in diverse real-world tasks, including handling variations in objects and backgrounds, highlights ReconVLA's potential for immediate practical deployment. From industrial assembly to service robotics, any application requiring a robot to understand and interact precisely with its environment stands to benefit.

6. **Contribution of Large-Scale Pretraining Data:** The curated pretraining dataset, derived from multiple open-source robotic datasets and automatically annotated with gaze regions, is itself a valuable contribution. It provides a blueprint for how to leverage existing data to enhance generative capabilities in VLA models, specifically for reconstruction tasks.

In conclusion, ReconVLA represents a significant step towards creating more effective and capable robot perceivers. By addressing the critical issue of visual attention grounding through an elegant reconstructive learning paradigm and comprehensive pretraining, it pushes the boundaries of precision, generalization, and practical applicability for Vision-Language-Action models. The implications are far-reaching for the development of autonomous robots that can reliably operate in complex, dynamic, and unstructured human environments.
