# Agentic Robot: A Brain-Inspired Framework for Vision-Language-Action Models in Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Agentic Robot, a framework from a multi-institutional collaboration including Jilin University, Harvard, and MIT, proposes a brain-inspired architecture for robust long-horizon robotic manipulation. It introduces the Standardized Action Procedure (SAP) to coordinate a planning LRM, a VLA executor, and a temporal VLM verifier, achieving an average success rate of 79.6% on the LIBERO benchmark and substantial gains on long-horizon tasks through closed-loop self-verification and error recovery.

## Problem

- Current robotic manipulation systems struggle with error accumulation and lack effective recovery mechanisms, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Static plan-following agents suffer from compounding error propagation, while end-to-end visuomotor policies lack introspection and fail to recover from unexpected states.
- Achieving reliable and robust performance for multi-step, sequential manipulation in dynamic, uncertain environments remains a significant challenge for embodied AI.

## Method

- **Agentic Robot** introduces a brain-inspired framework based on a ==Standardized Action Procedure== (SAP) that orchestrates a closed perception-reasoning-execution-verification loop.
- The framework integrates three specialized components: a GPT-4o-based Large Reasoning Model (LRM) for subgoal planning, an OpenVLA model as a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) executor, and a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (VLM) for perception-based verification.
- The VLM verifier performs a two-stage assessment (subgoal completion, then stuck/still trying diagnosis) and triggers predefined recovery actions upon failure detection, enabling autonomous self-correction.

## Results

- Agentic Robot achieved an average success rate of **79.6%** across all LIBERO benchmark suites, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- On challenging LIBERO-Long tasks, the framework reached a **61.6%** success rate, a **6.1%** improvement over the previous state-of-the-art and **12.1%** better than OpenVLA.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all components contribute to performance, with fine-tuned verification (**26.5%** success rate drop without it) and subgoal decomposition (**8.1%** drop) providing the most substantial improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A brain-inspired, structured coordination protocol, the Standardized Action Procedure (SAP), can significantly enhance reliability and interpretability in robotic manipulation.
> - Explicit, perception-based subgoal verification and autonomous recovery mechanisms are crucial for mitigating error accumulation and improving robustness in long-horizon tasks.
> - Modularizing an agent into specialized components (planner, executor, verifier) allows for tailored models and improves overall system reliability when coordinated by a well-defined protocol.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Executive Summary

The paper "Agentic Robot: A Brain-Inspired Framework for Vision-Language-Action Models in Embodied Agents" introduces a novel architecture for robust long-horizon robotic manipulation. Traditional approaches, whether static planners or end-to-end visuomotor policies, struggle with error accumulation and lack effective recovery mechanisms in complex sequential tasks. Addressing these limitations, the authors propose Agentic Robot, a framework inspired by Standardized Operating Procedures (SOPs) in human organizations and cognitive processes. Its core innovation is the Standardized Action Procedure (SAP), a coordination protocol that governs the interactions among three specialized components: a large reasoning model (LRM) for planning, a vision-language-action (VLA) executor, and a temporal vision-language model (VLM) verifier. This closed-loop, SAP-driven design enables dynamic self-verification and error recovery. Evaluated on the challenging LIBERO benchmark, Agentic Robot achieves state-of-the-art performance with an average success rate of 79.6%, significantly outperforming previous methods, particularly on long-horizon tasks. This work underscores the potential of structured, brain-inspired coordination for reliable autonomous systems.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied artificial intelligence, particularly robotic manipulation, has witnessed rapid advancements due to powerful foundation models like Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models have shown remarkable capabilities in interpreting natural language instructions and bridging high-level reasoning with low-level physical control. However, a significant challenge remains: achieving reliable performance in "long-horizon" tasks. These tasks, such as setting a table or assembling furniture, demand extended sequences of coordinated actions, robust error detection, and effective recovery mechanisms, which current systems often lack.

The paper identifies two main categories of existing approaches and their limitations:
1. **Static plan-following agents:** These systems generate a fixed sequence of actions without adaptive feedback. They suffer from compounding error propagation, where small initial deviations can lead to catastrophic failures as the task progresses.
2. **End-to-end visuomotor policies:** These policies directly map observations to actions without intermediate reasoning. They lack introspection and often fail to recover from unexpected states, especially when encountering scenarios outside their training distribution.

"Agentic Robot" directly addresses these weaknesses by drawing inspiration from established principles of reliability in human systems. It looks to **Standardized Operating Procedures (SOPs)** in human organizations, which provide structured coordination protocols to minimize errors and enable effective collaboration. Similarly, it takes inspiration from **natural cognition**, where complex behaviors arise from specialized neural circuits (e.g., prefrontal cortex for planning, motor cortices for execution, sensory-motor loops for continuous verification) interacting through well-defined patterns.

This work positions itself as a brain-inspired solution that integrates high-level reasoning with low-level control, while explicitly incorporating mechanisms for self-verification and error recovery. By introducing the Standardized Action Procedure (SAP), it aims to provide the structured coordination protocols missing in prior work, thereby enhancing system reliability and interpretability in complex, long-horizon manipulation scenarios, which is a critical step towards deploying autonomous systems in unstructured real-world environments.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Agentic Robot" is to overcome the inherent limitations of current robotic manipulation systems in executing **long-horizon tasks** reliably. The authors are motivated by the observation that existing approaches—whether static planners or end-to-end visuomotor policies—suffer from **error accumulation** and a **lack of effective verification and recovery mechanisms**. These deficiencies severely hinder their performance and reliability in real-world scenarios that demand extended reasoning and precise, sequential actions.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Enhance Reliability and Robustness:** To develop a framework that can reliably perform complex, multi-step manipulation tasks by systematically mitigating error propagation and enabling autonomous recovery from failures.
2. **Improve Interpretability and Modularity:** To create a highly modular framework with well-defined component interfaces, allowing for better understanding of the robot's decision-making process and enabling independent optimization of its sub-components. This contrasts with opaque end-to-end systems.
3. **Introduce Structured Coordination:** To propose a novel coordination protocol, the Standardized Action Procedure (SAP), which formalizes the interactions between planning, execution, and verification phases, inspired by human SOPs and cognitive cycles. This structured approach aims to ensure consistent information exchange and progress monitoring.
4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** To demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the proposed framework by achieving superior performance on challenging benchmarks, particularly those involving long-horizon tasks, thereby validating the brain-inspired and SAP-driven design.
5. **Enable Self-Verification Without External Supervision:** To allow the robot to introspectively assess its progress and detect failures using only its sensory inputs, without requiring external human intervention or ground-truth state information.

Ultimately, the motivation is to advance the state of the art in embodied AI by creating robotic systems that are not only capable of complex tasks but also inherently more reliable, adaptable, and robust in dynamic and uncertain environments.

### 4. Methodology and Approach: Agentic Robot and Standardized Action Procedure (SAP)

The core of the proposed solution is **Agentic Robot**, a brain-inspired framework that redefines long-horizon manipulation as a closed perception-reasoning-execution-verification loop. This loop is orchestrated by the **Standardized Action Procedure (SAP)**, a novel coordination protocol that structures component interactions.

#### 4.1. Standardized Action Procedure (SAP)

Inspired by human Standardized Operating Procedures (SOPs) and natural cognitive cycles, SAP is the central organizing principle. It defines a complete agentic loop, establishing explicit protocols for information exchange, progress monitoring, and error recovery across the system's components. Its fundamental principles are:
1. **Modular Decomposition:** Complex tasks are systematically broken down into manageable and verifiable subgoals.
2. **Structured Coordination:** Interactions between components follow predefined workflows and interfaces, preventing communication breakdowns.
3. **Adaptive Verification:** Systematic checkpoints are integrated to enable early error detection and recovery actions.

#### 4.2. Architecture: Three Specialized Components

Agentic Robot integrates three distinct, specialized components, each operating within the SAP framework.

### 5. Planner: LRM for Subgoal Generation

- **Role:** This module serves as the high-level reasoning unit. It takes a high-level task instruction and, optionally, an initial visual observation, then decomposes it into a structured sequence of executable subgoals.
- **Implementation:** A state-of-the-art large multimodal reasoning model, specifically **GPT-4o**, is used. This allows the planner to process both text and images for better visual grounding.
- **Atomic Skill Library:** Subgoals are derived from a predefined "Atomic Skill Library" (e.g., "pick up [object]," "place [object] in/on [location]"), ensuring compatibility with the executor and maintaining interpretability.
- **SAP Protocol:** The planner's operation is guided by a structured, SAP-compliant prompt architecture, including a task preamble, the complete skill library, and few-shot examples to ensure consistent and semantically coherent subgoal generation.

### 6. VLA Executor: Reactive Visuomotor Policy

- **Role:** This component acts as the core visuomotor interface. It translates each discrete subgoal generated by the planner and real-time visual observations into continuous, low-level robotic control signals.
- **Implementation:** The framework utilizes **OpenVLA**, an open-source, pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. OpenVLA directly connects natural language subgoals with visual inputs to generate 7-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) Cartesian displacements and binary gripper states for the robot.
- **SAP Protocol:** Subgoals adhere to the structured format of the Atomic Skill Library. Although the executor itself is stateless, its robustness is significantly enhanced by the SAP verification loop, which detects failures and triggers recovery actions. The executor operates at a frequency of 10 Hz.

### 7. Verifier: Perception-based Subgoal Assessment and Recovery

- **Role:** This crucial module provides continuous feedback by assessing the success of each subgoal through visual analysis. It enables autonomous progression to the next subgoal or triggers recovery actions upon failure.
- **Implementation:** A fine-tuned **Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (VLM)** is employed as the verifier model. It processes a sliding buffer of recent image pairs from both third-person and wrist-mounted cameras to capture visual dynamics.
- **Two-Stage Assessment Protocol (SAP-driven):**
 - **Primary Check:** The verifier determines if the current subgoal is complete (Yes/No) based on the image sequence and the subgoal instruction.
 - **Secondary Check (if 'No'):** If the subgoal is not complete, a diagnosis module determines if the robot is "Stuck" (e.g., stationary arm, failed grasp, oscillating behavior) or "StillTrying."
 - **Recovery:** If "Stuck," a predefined recovery action is triggered (e.g., lifting the gripper or reorienting the wrist). The system then re-executes the current subgoal. After a maximum number of recovery attempts, the task is marked as failed.
- **SAP Protocol:** The verification prompt follows SAP's structured format. Verification is performed periodically (every 20 frames, or 0.5 Hz) to optimize responsiveness and efficiency, achieving a balance between accuracy and computational load. A small, annotated dataset (approx. 500 triplets) is used to fine-tune the VLM for subgoal-level introspection, demonstrating effective adaptation with limited supervision.

#### 7.1. SAP Operational Framework

The entire system operates as a sequence of agentic steps, each constituting a complete SAP cycle: perception -> planning -> execution -> verification. This closed-loop design, with its explicit interfaces and standardized information flow, ensures dynamic self-correction and significantly enhances the robustness and interpretability of robotic manipulation, particularly for long-horizon tasks.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Agentic Robot was thoroughly evaluated on long-horizon manipulation tasks within simulated embodied environments, utilizing the **LIBERO benchmark suite**.

#### 8.1. Experimental Setup

- **Perception:** Dual-camera system (static agent-view and wrist-mounted eye-in-hand cameras) providing synchronized RGB observations.
- **Action Space:** 7-dimensional continuous control vector (6-DoF end-effector + binary gripper state).
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations focused on four challenging LIBERO subsets: LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and particularly LIBERO-Long, which involves extended sequential manipulations. Each subset comprises 10 distinct tasks with 50 human demonstrations each.
- **Baselines:** The performance was benchmarked against state-of-the-art generalist policies, including Diffusion Policy, Octo-Base, OpenVLA, TraceVLA, and SpatialVLA. These baselines represent diverse model paradigms (diffusion-based, transformer-based, and large-scale VLA models).
- **Implementation Details:** Agentic Robot integrated a GPT-4o-based planner, an OpenVLA-based executor, and a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct verifier. The recovery mechanism involved raising the gripper to a safe position upon failure detection, and verification was performed every 20 frames.

#### 8.2. Overall State-of-the-Art Performance

Agentic Robot achieved **state-of-the-art performance** with an impressive **79.6% average success rate** across all LIBERO benchmark suites (Table 1). This significantly surpassed all baselines, demonstrating its exceptional versatility. Crucially, Agentic Robot consistently ranked among the top-3 performers in all task categories, indicating strong cross-domain generalization unlike specialized approaches that might excel in one domain but falter in others.

#### 8.3. Long-Horizon Manipulation Analysis

The framework's most significant gains were observed in the **LIBERO-Long** tasks, which explicitly test multi-step sequential manipulations. Agentic Robot achieved a **61.6% success rate**, a substantial **6.1% improvement over SpatialVLA**, the previous state-of-the-art on these challenging tasks.
Analyzing individual LIBERO-Long tasks (Table 2), Agentic Robot consistently outperformed OpenVLA, showing an average improvement of **12.1%**. The most significant gains were in tasks with lower baseline success rates, such as:
- **Bowl-Drawer:** 24% improvement over OpenVLA.
- **Mug-Mug:** 19% improvement.
- **Soup-Sauce:** 21% improvement.

These results confirm that the explicit subgoal decomposition, verification, and recovery mechanisms effectively reduce cascading failures, which are a major limitation for other methods in long-horizon scenarios.

#### 8.4. Verification and Recovery Effectiveness

A key innovation is the explicit subgoal-level verification and recovery. As illustrated in Figure 3, baselines like OpenVLA might continue executing indefinitely even when stuck (e.g., gripper colliding with the table after a failed grasp), leading to task failure. In contrast, Agentic Robot's visual verifier detects such failure states and triggers a simple recovery action (lifting the gripper), allowing the robot to retry and successfully complete the subgoal. This closed-loop error detection dramatically enhances system robustness.

#### 8.5. Verification Frequency Analysis

The study investigated the impact of verification frequency (every 10, 20, or 50 steps) on performance and efficiency (Figure 4). While shorter tasks (Spatial, Object, Goal) were less sensitive to frequency, LIBERO-Long tasks showed considerable sensitivity: reducing verification to every 50 steps resulted in a 6% drop in success rate. The **20-step interval was identified as optimal**, maintaining peak success rates while significantly reducing computational overhead (e.g., 48% less computational load and over 15 seconds faster per episode compared to 10-step verification on long tasks).

#### 8.6. Ablation Study

A systematic ablation study on LIBERO-Long tasks quantified the contribution of each component (Table 3, Figures 5 & 6):
- **No Visual Input (to Planner):** A 4.4% drop in success rate (to 57.4%), highlighting the importance of visual context for instruction grounding and ambiguity resolution.
- **No Recovery Mechanism:** A 2.1% drop (to 59.7%), confirming the value of even minimal corrective behaviors in mitigating error accumulation.
- **No Fine-tuned VLM (Verifier):** The most substantial drop, a **26.5% decrease** (to 35.3%). This underscores that generic VLMs are insufficient for accurate subgoal-level introspection, and domain adaptation is crucial. The fine-tuned verifier enables sequential verification and successful task completion, whereas a zero-shot verifier often fails to detect subgoal completion, leading to repetitive, failing execution.
- **No Subgoal Decomposition (Vanilla OpenVLA):** An 8.1% drop (to 53.7%). This strongly supports the central hypothesis that breaking complex tasks into smaller, verifiable atomic skills significantly enhances execution reliability and verification accuracy. Without decomposition, the system attempts the full task in one shot, leading to undetected intermediate failures and overall task failure.

The ablation study confirmed that all components provide measurable benefits, with fine-tuned verification and hierarchical planning contributing the most substantial improvements.

### 9. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Agentic Robot" framework represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI, particularly for robotic manipulation in complex, long-horizon tasks.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control with Robustness:** The framework effectively combines the symbolic reasoning capabilities of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) with the reactive control of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, critically enhanced by the continuous, perception-based verification of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This modular design, governed by SAP, provides a principled way to manage task complexity.
2. **Introduction of Standardized Action Procedure (SAP):** SAP is a novel and impactful contribution. By formalizing component interactions and enforcing structured workflows for planning, execution, and verification, it addresses a fundamental limitation of prior open-loop or end-to-end systems: the accumulation and propagation of errors. This brain-inspired coordination protocol demonstrably enhances system reliability and interpretability.
3. **Superior Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks:** The state-of-the-art results on the LIBERO benchmark, especially the substantial improvements (e.g., 24% on Bowl-Drawer, 21% on Soup-Sauce) on long-horizon tasks, provide strong empirical evidence for the effectiveness of SAP-driven coordination and subgoal-level verification. This performance gain is crucial for real-world applicability.
4. **Autonomous Error Recovery:** The integration of an introspective, perception-based verifier that enables autonomous detection of failures and triggers recovery actions (even simple ones like lifting the gripper) is vital. This closed-loop feedback mechanism allows the robot to self-correct without external supervision, a critical capability for deployment in dynamic and uncertain environments.
5. **Interpretability and Debugging:** The modular nature of Agentic Robot, with clear component interfaces and explicit subgoal definitions, inherently offers greater interpretability compared to monolithic end-to-end policies. This facilitates debugging and understanding *why* a task failed, which is essential for developing trustworthy autonomous systems.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Reliable Robotic Deployment:** The increased robustness and error recovery capabilities could accelerate the deployment of autonomous robots in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to household assistance, where long-horizon, multi-step tasks are common.
- **Inspiration for Future Architectures:** The SAP framework provides a blueprint for designing more robust and generalizable agentic systems, encouraging future research to explore structured coordination protocols beyond just end-to-end learning.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** This work contributes to the broader understanding of how biologically inspired design principles, such as modularity, hierarchical planning, and continuous self-monitoring, can be effectively translated into artificial intelligent agents.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations, primarily related to the **sim-to-real gap**. Transferring Agentic Robot to physical platforms will require addressing challenges like real-world sensor noise, lighting variations, occlusions, and actuation delays. The verifier's robustness to visual domain shift also needs extensive evaluation and potentially real-image fine-tuning.

Future work could focus on:
- **More Sophisticated Recovery Strategies:** Incorporating advanced recovery actions such as policy rollback, re-grasping, or online subgoal regeneration to handle a wider range of failure scenarios.
- **Adaptive Verification Scheduling:** Instead of fixed verification intervals, dynamically adjusting the frequency based on factors like task complexity, motion intensity, or the LLM's uncertainty quantification to optimize computational efficiency without sacrificing safety.
- **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning:** Addressing specific limitations observed in tasks requiring fine-grained spatial coordination (e.g., "Moka-Moka" task), potentially through temporal structure modeling, spatial intent prediction, or memory-aware policies that consider previous placements.
- **Autonomous Skill Library Expansion:** Exploring how LLMs could automatically populate or expand the Atomic Skill Library, reducing the reliance on expert-defined primitives and enhancing generalization to novel tasks.

In conclusion, Agentic Robot offers a compelling, brain-inspired framework that moves beyond the limitations of current robotic control by emphasizing structured coordination, proactive verification, and autonomous recovery. Its success in simulation lays a strong foundation for future research aimed at building truly reliable and adaptable embodied AI systems for real-world applications.
