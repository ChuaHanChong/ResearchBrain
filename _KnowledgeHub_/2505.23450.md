# Agentic Robot: A Brain-Inspired Framework for Vision-Language-Action Models in Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Agentic Robot, a framework from a multi-institutional collaboration including Jilin University, Harvard, and MIT, proposes a brain-inspired architecture for robust long-horizon robotic manipulation. It introduces the Standardized Action Procedure (SAP) to coordinate a planning LRM, a VLA executor, and a temporal VLM verifier, achieving an average success rate of 79.6% on the LIBERO benchmark and substantial gains on long-horizon tasks through closed-loop self-verification and error recovery.

## Problem

- Current robotic manipulation systems struggle with error accumulation and lack effective recovery mechanisms, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Static plan-following agents suffer from compounding error propagation, while end-to-end visuomotor policies lack introspection and fail to recover from unexpected states.
- Achieving reliable and robust performance for multi-step, sequential manipulation in dynamic, uncertain environments remains a significant challenge for embodied AI.

## Method

- **Agentic Robot** introduces a brain-inspired framework based on a ==Standardized Action Procedure== (SAP) that orchestrates a closed perception-reasoning-execution-verification loop.
- The framework integrates three specialized components: a GPT-4o-based Large Reasoning Model (LRM) for subgoal planning, an OpenVLA model as a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) executor, and a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (VLM) for perception-based verification.
- The VLM verifier performs a two-stage assessment (subgoal completion, then stuck/still trying diagnosis) and triggers predefined recovery actions upon failure detection, enabling autonomous self-correction.

## Results

- Agentic Robot achieved an average success rate of **79.6%** across all LIBERO benchmark suites, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- On challenging LIBERO-Long tasks, the framework reached a **61.6%** success rate, a **6.1%** improvement over the previous state-of-the-art and **12.1%** better than OpenVLA.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all components contribute to performance, with fine-tuned verification (**26.5%** success rate drop without it) and subgoal decomposition (**8.1%** drop) providing the most substantial improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A brain-inspired, structured coordination protocol, the Standardized Action Procedure (SAP), can significantly enhance reliability and interpretability in robotic manipulation.
> - Explicit, perception-based subgoal verification and autonomous recovery mechanisms are crucial for mitigating error accumulation and improving robustness in long-horizon tasks.
> - Modularizing an agent into specialized components (planner, executor, verifier) allows for tailored models and improves overall system reliability when coordinated by a well-defined protocol.
