# Robust Quadrupedal Locomotion via Risk-Averse Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Shanghai AI Lab developed Risk-Averse Legged Locomotion (RALL), a framework for quadrupedal robots that uses risk-sensitive policy learning to enhance robustness in uncertain environments. This approach explicitly models environmental uncertainty through distributional value functions and optimizes for worst-case outcomes, significantly improving stability and success rates, such as achieving a 92.7% success rate on a 0.4m high platform compared to 14.4% for baseline methods.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for quadrupedal locomotion struggle with truly uncertain scenarios, like abrupt terrain changes or unexpected external forces.
- Conventional RL optimizes for expected returns, potentially leading to policies that tolerate rare but catastrophic failures if they contribute to higher average performance.
- Current robustness strategies, relying on privileged information, external sensors, or extensive domain randomization, often have limitations in explicitly accounting for future risk events.

## Method

- Introduces **Risk-Averse** Legged Locomotion (RALL), a framework that injects rare risk penalization terms into the reward function to distort value distributions, focusing on future risk events.
- Employs an Implicit Quantile Network (IQN) to learn the full distribution of returns and optimizes the policy using Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) to prioritize worst-case outcomes.
- Integrates a risk-aware meta controller that dynamically adapts risk preferences (risk-averse vs. risk-neutral) based on real-time environmental risk assessed by the Interquartile Range (IQR).

## Results

- RALL significantly improved "Time to Fall" (TTF) metrics in simulation across various payload scenarios and achieved a **92.7%** success rate when walking down a 0.4m high platform, substantially outperforming baselines.
- Qualitative real-world experiments on a Unitree Aliengo robot confirmed enhanced stability, rapid recovery from disturbances, and effective traversal of challenging terrains, without reliance on external sensors.
- The risk-aware meta controller, by adaptively switching policies based on IQR-derived risk, achieved the highest overall returns across all outcome quantiles (worst-case, middle-case, and best-case scenarios).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the distribution of returns, rather than just the expected return, allows policies to proactively mitigate rare but catastrophic failures in uncertain environments.
> - Rare but impactful risk penalties, even with low probability, can effectively shape the value distribution to highlight and train against worst-case scenarios without significantly affecting average expected returns.
> - The Interquartile Range (IQR) derived from the learned quantile distribution provides a robust, proprioception-based, real-time measure of environmental uncertainty, enabling adaptive risk-aware control.
