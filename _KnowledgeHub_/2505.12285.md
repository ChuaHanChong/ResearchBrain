# CALM: Co-evolution of Algorithms and Language Model for Automatic Heuristic Design

> [!summary] Summary
> The CALM framework introduces a co-evolutionary approach for automatic heuristic design, enabling both the heuristics and the underlying language model to adapt and improve. It achieves superior performance across various optimization problems using a compact 7B LLM on a single GPU, outperforming state-of-the-art methods through a combination of verbal and numerical guidance.

## Problem

- Manual heuristic design for optimization problems is labor-intensive and demands extensive expert knowledge.
- Existing automatic heuristic design (AHD) methods, including early LLM-based approaches, typically rely on static language models, neglecting to adapt the LLM's generative capabilities based on performance feedback.
- Traditional AHD approaches often constrain the search space by depending on human-defined, problem-specific components.

## Method

- CALM (==Co-evolution== of Algorithms and the Language Model) employs a hybrid approach integrating verbal guidance (prompt engineering with evolutionary operators) and numerical guidance (reinforcement learning for LLM fine-tuning).
- The framework iteratively generates, evaluates, and assigns rewards to heuristics, using this feedback to update the LLM's parameters via GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) to enable continuous adaptation.
- It incorporates advanced prompt generation operators (injection, replacement, diversity-aware crossover, simplification) and a collapse mechanism to manage search space, ensure diversity, and escape local optima.

## Results

- CALM consistently outperformed all baseline methods, achieving the lowest average optimality gap (**0.71%**) on Online Bin Packing, a **38.69%** average improvement over FunSearch, and superior results on CVRP and OP.
- The framework demonstrated state-of-the-art performance using a compact Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct-INT4 model, running locally on a single **24GB** GPU, showcasing significant resource efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the reinforcement learning module (GRPO) was responsible for the largest performance gains, while CALM's verbal guidance alone, when paired with GPT-4o-mini, matched or exceeded existing LLM-based AHD baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-evolution of the language model and heuristic designs, driven by both verbal and numerical gradients, significantly enhances the LLM's ability to discover high-quality heuristics.
> - Reinforcement learning, coupled with a fine-grained reward function, allows smaller, locally fine-tuned language models to achieve and surpass the performance of larger, static, API-based commercial models in specialized AHD tasks.
> - Carefully designed verbal guidance, including sophisticated evolutionary operators and a collapse mechanism, is critical for effective exploration, exploitation, and preventing premature convergence in LLM-driven evolutionary search.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of optimization problems, prevalent in various real-world applications such as logistics, scheduling, and transportation, traditionally relies on the manual design of heuristics. This process is often labor-intensive and demands significant expert knowledge. To address these limitations, Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD) has emerged. Early AHD approaches, such as Genetic Programming (GP), aimed to automate heuristic generation but often remained dependent on human-defined, problem-specific components, thereby restricting the search space and flexibility.

The recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have introduced new possibilities for AHD. In this paradigm, LLMs function as heuristic generators, integrated within evolutionary computing (EC) frameworks. These systems typically operate by iteratively evaluating LLM-generated heuristics through a simulation framework, with superior heuristics then informing subsequent prompt generation in a feedback loop.

Existing LLM-based AHD methods, exemplified by works like FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo, and MCTS-AHD, primarily guide the evolution of heuristics through textual prompt manipulations, which the authors refer to as "verbal gradients." A significant limitation of these approaches is that they typically keep the underlying LLM's parameters fixed. This practice neglects the potential to fine-tune and enhance the LLM's generative capabilities based on the explicit performance feedback derived from the heuristics it designs.

This paper positions itself as a development that addresses this gap. It aims to advance LLM-based AHD by introducing a hybrid framework that not only employs verbal guidance but also integrates "numerical gradients" to adapt the LLM itself. By enabling the LLM to co-evolve with the heuristic search process, the proposed method seeks to leverage the rich information contained in "prompt-response-performance" triplets generated during the evolutionary loop, thereby allowing the LLM to internalize characteristics of successful heuristics and improve its future generations. This work directly contrasts with and builds upon prior LLM-based AHD literature by introducing a dynamic, adaptive LLM component.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an automatic heuristic design framework, named CALM (Co-evolution of Algorithms and the Language Model), that overcomes the limitations of existing LLM-based AHD methods by enabling the simultaneous evolution of both the heuristic designs and the underlying Large Language Model.

The primary motivations and specific objectives are:

- **Bridging the Gap in LLM-based AHD:** Current LLM-based AHD methods predominantly rely on static LLMs, guiding heuristic evolution solely through prompt engineering (verbal guidance). This approach overlooks the opportunity to enhance the LLM's intrinsic heuristic generation capability based on performance feedback. CALM aims to address this by introducing a mechanism for LLM adaptation.
- **Introducing Numerical Guidance:** The paper proposes to supplement verbal guidance with "numerical gradients." This is achieved by fine-tuning the LLM using reinforcement learning (RL), where the quality of generated heuristics provides a direct signal for model updates. The iterative evolutionary process naturally yields "prompt-response-performance" triplets, which are highly informative data points for RL.
- **Enabling Co-evolution of LLM and Heuristics:** A core motivation is to allow the LLM to "co-evolve" alongside the heuristic designs. This means the LLM learns to generate better heuristics over time by internalizing the characteristics of high-performing solutions, rather than merely being steered by textual prompts. This adaptive capability is expected to unlock a new dimension of performance improvement and adaptability.
- **Developing Advanced Prompt Generation Operators:** To enhance the efficacy of verbal guidance within the evolutionary loop, CALM aims to introduce a suite of sophisticated evolutionary operators. These include fine-granularity mutation operators (injection and replacement) to encourage meaningful and diverse heuristic variations while maintaining structural coherence, and a diversity-aware crossover operator to balance exploration and exploitation.
- **Implementing a Robust Search Mechanism:** Recognizing the common challenge of premature convergence in evolutionary algorithms, the research aims to integrate a "collapse mechanism." This mechanism is designed to detect stagnation in the search process and proactively reset the population, thereby enabling the system to escape local optima and reinitiate effective exploration.
- **Designing a Fine-Grained Reward Function:** A specific objective is to develop a carefully crafted reward function for the reinforcement learning component. This function must provide fine-grained guidance to the LLM, effectively penalizing infeasible outputs and reinforcing improvements over existing heuristics, while also accounting for novelty and relative performance.
- **Achieving High Performance with Resource Efficiency:** A practical objective is to develop a framework that can discover high-performing heuristics using compact LLMs and limited computational resources (e.g., a single consumer-grade GPU). This would contrast with prior methods that often rely on more powerful, API-based commercial LLMs, thereby democratizing access to cutting-edge AHD.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CALM framework represents a hybrid approach to Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD), integrating both verbal and numerical guidance to enable the co-evolution of algorithms and the underlying language model.

#### 3.1. CALM Framework Overview

CALM maintains a pool of heuristics, each characterized by its natural-language idea, source code, and performance score. In each iteration, the framework performs the following steps:
1. **Operator Selection:** A feasible evolutionary operator is chosen to guide the generation of a new prompt (`q`).
2. **Response Generation:** The local LLM (`π_θ`) samples `G` candidate responses based on `q`.
3. **Heuristic Evaluation:** The generated heuristics are executed and evaluated in a predefined simulation environment.
4. **Reward Assignment:** Based on the evaluation results, rewards are assigned to each response.
5. **LLM Fine-tuning:** The LLM's parameters (`θ`) are updated using a reinforcement learning algorithm (GRPO) based on these rewards (numerical guidance).
6. **Heuristic Pool Update:** New feasible heuristics are added to the pool.
This iterative process continues for `T` rounds, after which the best-performing heuristic discovered is returned.

#### 3.2. Prompt Generation (Verbal Guidance)

CALM employs a suite of evolutionary operators to generate prompts, guiding the LLM to produce diverse and improved heuristics.

- **Heuristic Sampling Method:** For most operators, heuristics are sampled from the top-performing pool based on an inverse proportionality to their performance rank. For the crossover operator, a hybrid approach is used.
- **Fine-Granularity Mutation Operators:**
 - **Injection:** Prompts the LLM to incorporate a new component into an existing heuristic. It uses compact summaries instead of full code, stores global descriptions of previously injected components to promote diversity, and focuses on granular modifications. Its sampling probability is increased during population expansion.
 - **Replacement:** Prompts the LLM to rewrite an existing component with specific instructions. Beyond rewriting hyperparameters, CALM introduces two novel instructions: (1) converting instance-independent decision rules to instance-dependent ones, and (2) transforming fragments that assign equal credit to those that differentiate credit based on contextual performance.
- **Diversity-Aware Crossover:** This operator balances exploitation and exploration. It randomly chooses between two parent selection strategies: (1) performance-based, where both parents are sampled by performance rank, or (2) diversity-based, where the first parent is performance-ranked, and the second is sampled with probability inversely proportional to its diversity rank (measured by unique idea tokens relative to the first parent). This ensures at least one high-quality parent while promoting structural novelty.
- **Simplification Operator:** Addresses the accumulation of redundant or verbose components by prompting the LLM to create a more concise and effective version of a given heuristic.
- **Initialization Operator:** Used when the heuristic pool is empty, prompting the LLM to generate initial heuristics without requiring existing ones.

#### 3.3. Collapse Mechanism

To counteract issues like inbreeding and premature convergence, CALM introduces a proactive collapse mechanism.
- **Detection:** It tracks a "no-breakthrough counter" (`c_n`) which increments if a round fails to yield a globally superior heuristic after the population reaches its target size.
- **Trigger:** Collapse is probabilistically triggered if `random(0, 1) < c_n * δ_0` or if `c_n ≥ C` (a hard cap), where `δ_0` controls the growth rate of the collapse probability.
- **Reset:** Upon collapse, the population is reset, retaining only the original seed algorithm and the current best-performing heuristic as new seeds. During early repopulation, selection constraints are temporarily relaxed to allow novel but potentially suboptimal components to propagate.

#### 3.4. Reward Function (Numerical Guidance)

The reward function `r_ϕ(q, o | h_new, h_t_base)` provides fine-grained feedback for GRPO-based learning.
- **Infeasible Responses:** Penalizes responses that fail to form a valid heuristic with a hierarchy of negative rewards (e.g., -1.0 for idea omission, -0.75 for randomness). This gradient encourages progress towards syntactic and semantic correctness.
- **Feasible Responses:** Rewards are based on the new heuristic's performance relative to the best base heuristic in the prompt (`h_t_base`) and its novelty.
 - If `h_new` improves over `h_t_base`: `1 + ∆(h_new, h_t_base)`, where `∆` measures the relative performance gap.
 - If `h_new` underperforms `h_t_base`: `0.5 * r_rand * ∆(h_new, h_t_base)` (scaled negative reward).
 - If `h_new` has identical performance to an existing base heuristic: `0.8 * r_rand` (small penalty to discourage trivial reproduction).
 - For heuristics generated by the initialization operator (no base reference), the reward is zero.
This design ensures feasible heuristics are prioritized, and genuine improvements yield positive rewards.

#### 3.5. Reinforcement Learning (GRPO)

CALM employs GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) for efficient LLM fine-tuning.
- GRPO snapshots the current model (`π_θ`) as a reference (`π_ref`) and an old policy (`π_old`).
- For each batch of prompts, it samples `G` responses, computes rewards, and normalizes them to determine advantages for each token.
- Model parameters are updated by maximizing an objective function incorporating a ratio of new to old policy probabilities, a clipping mechanism, and a KL divergence regularization term to ensure stable updates.
- GRPO uses the group mean reward as a baseline, eliminating the need for an auxiliary value network and reducing memory requirements.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

CALM is built on Unsloth, an open-source framework for efficient LLM training. It utilizes an INT4-quantized Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model, fine-tuning only 1.15% of its weights. The fine-tuning process is performed locally on a single 24GB GPU, making the approach resource-efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CALM demonstrated its effectiveness across various challenging optimization tasks, often outperforming state-of-the-art (SOTA) baselines with greater resource efficiency.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Across Tasks

CALM consistently outperformed all baseline methods in terms of heuristic quality, achieving superior results on Online Bin Packing (OBP), Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP), and Orienteering Problem (OP), and competitive performance on the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP). This was achieved while running entirely on a local computer with a single 24GB GPU using a compact Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-INT4 model.

- **Online Bin Packing (OBP):** CALM achieved the lowest average optimality gap (0.71%) across all tested instances, including both in-domain and out-of-domain scales. It notably achieved a 0.00% gap on the 1k_500 test set, indicating optimal solutions. CALM’s improvement over FunSearch was 38.69% on average, significantly higher than EvoTune's reported improvements.
- **Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP):** CALM-constructed heuristics achieved the lowest optimality gaps among LLM-based baselines on the N=100 and N=200 (out-of-domain) test sets. At N=200, CALM surpassed the Neural Combinatorial Optimization (NCO) baseline POMO, which typically requires per-scale training.
- **Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP):** CALM consistently outperformed all LLM-based baselines across all test sets (N=50, N=100, N=200), including in-domain and out-of-domain scenarios.
- **Orienteering Problem (OP):** CALM delivered superior performance compared to all other LLM-based baselines on out-of-domain scales and outperformed EoH and MCTS-AHD on the in-domain scale.

#### 4.2. Efficacy of Verbal Gradient

An ablation study demonstrated the strength of CALM’s verbal guidance mechanisms (prompt designs, operators, collapse) in isolation. When CALM was run using the GPT-4o-mini API (without GRPO-based fine-tuning), it achieved performance comparable to or superior to the recent MCTS-AHD approach across multiple tasks. For example, it secured the lowest optimality gaps on two OBP datasets, matched MCTS-AHD on CVRP, consistently surpassed MCTS-AHD on OP, and closely tracked or outperformed MCTS-AHD on TSP. These results indicate that CALM's prompt engineering alone establishes it as a top-tier LLM-based AHD method.

#### 4.3. Power of Reinforcement Learning (RL)

A critical finding from ablation studies was that disabling the GRPO module resulted in the largest performance drop across nearly all settings. Furthermore, the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-INT4 model, when enhanced with GRPO and CALM’s custom reward function, was able to outperform the GPT-4o-mini-based heuristic (which did not use GRPO). Training curves illustrated that while CALM's heuristics might initially lag due to the larger LLM's head start, GRPO-driven adaptation allowed the model to converge and ultimately outperform baselines. This highlights the transformative impact of RL in enabling smaller, locally fine-tuned models to compete with and exceed the performance of larger, fixed API-based models.

#### 4.4. Impact of Reward Design

The proposed reward function, which attributes credit based on the generated heuristic's novelty and performance relative to the best base heuristic in the prompt, proved more effective than alternative schemes. Alternative reward functions, such as a purely performance-based reward or a binary improvement reward, either underperformed the no-RL baseline on some problems or delivered inferior results compared to CALM's designed reward, confirming its effectiveness in guiding the LLM's learning process.

#### 4.5. Impact of Collapse Mechanism

The collapse mechanism generally enhanced the heuristic search process. However, experiments showed that overly strict collapse triggers (e.g., very low `δ_0` and small `C`) could hinder performance by prematurely resetting the search before sufficient breakthroughs occurred. This suggests that a balanced tolerance for stagnation is important for optimizing the collapse mechanism's benefit.

#### 4.6. Impact of Evolutionary Operators

Ablation studies confirmed that all proposed evolutionary operators contributed positively to heuristic quality. Removing the simplification operator caused the most significant performance drop in both OBP and OP tasks, suggesting its crucial role in reducing redundancy and complexity. Crossover, injection, and replacement also proved critical, with their removal notably degrading performance. Furthermore, the diversity-aware aspect of the crossover operator was essential; performing crossover without diversity-based selection yielded worse results than not using crossover at all.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CALM framework represents a notable advancement in the field of Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD) with several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Novelty in LLM-based AHD:** CALM is presented as the first framework that combines prompt evolution (verbal guidance) with on-the-fly Large Language Model (LLM) adaptation (numerical guidance) for AHD. This departure from fixed-model approaches allows the LLM to learn and improve its heuristic generation capabilities continuously, rather than merely being steered by static prompts.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The framework demonstrates the discovery of heuristics that consistently outperform existing state-of-the-art baselines across a range of challenging optimization problems, including Online Bin Packing, Traveling Salesman Problem, Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem, and Orienteering Problem. This indicates a robust and effective approach capable of generating high-quality solutions.
- **Resource Efficiency and Accessibility:** A key impact is CALM's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance using a compact, INT4-quantized 7B LLM running locally on a single 24GB GPU. This contrasts with many prior LLM-based AHD methods that rely on more powerful, often API-based, commercial models. This resource efficiency significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, startups, and educational institutions with limited computational budgets, thereby democratizing access to advanced AHD capabilities.
- **Transformative Power of Reinforcement Learning:** The research highlights the substantial impact of reinforcement learning (GRPO) in enhancing LLM-based AHD. The results demonstrate that a smaller, locally fine-tuned model, when integrated with an effective RL strategy and reward design, can surpass the performance of larger, general-purpose LLMs used in a static manner. This suggests that targeted adaptation of LLMs can unlock considerable performance gains in specialized domains.
- **Robustness of Verbal Guidance:** Even without the reinforcement learning component, CALM's prompt engineering and evolutionary operators, when paired with a capable LLM API, were shown to match or exceed prior best results. This underscores the effectiveness of the verbal gradient designs and their contribution to the overall framework's success.
- **Advancements in Evolutionary Algorithm Design:** The introduction of fine-granularity mutation operators, a diversity-aware crossover, and a collapse mechanism demonstrates refined strategies for managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off and preventing premature convergence in LLM-driven evolutionary search. These methodological innovations are valuable for the broader field of evolutionary computing.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, including scaling CALM's paradigm to larger models and exploring mechanisms for LLM adaptation in scenarios where explicit performance feedback from generated heuristics might be less direct or absent. It also suggests possibilities for better integration with other advanced techniques like reflection.

In summary, CALM advances automated algorithm discovery by enabling a symbiotic co-evolution between LLMs and algorithms, demonstrating a path toward more powerful, accessible, and efficient AHD systems. This could accelerate innovation in various application domains reliant on complex optimization.
