# TraKDis: A Transformer-based Knowledge Distillation Approach for Visual Reinforcement Learning with Application to Cloth Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Imperial College London researchers developed TraKDis, a Transformer-based knowledge distillation approach, enabling robots to perform complex cloth manipulation using only visual input. This framework achieved up to 21.9% higher performance compared to existing visual reinforcement learning methods in simulation and demonstrated effective real-world transfer.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable objects like cloth is challenging due to intricate dynamics, high elasticity, and complex configuration spaces, making visual state inference difficult.
- Visual RL for cloth manipulation faces hurdles from high-dimensional observations, partial observability due to self-occlusion, and the inability of traditional Markovian RL to leverage historical information effectively.
- Existing effective RL approaches often require privileged access to precise state information (e.g., cloth particle positions) which is unavailable in real-world visual settings.

## Method

- The Decision Transformer (DT) architecture was used to re-frame ==reinforcement learning== as a sequence modeling problem, allowing the agent to capture contextual relationships and long-term dependencies.
- A novel knowledge distillation framework, **TraKDis**, was developed to transfer expertise from a state-privileged teacher agent to a vision-based student agent.
- A pre-trained CNN encoder estimates low-dimensional cloth states from RGB images, and the student policy's weights are initialized by copying from the teacher to accelerate learning.

## Results

- TraKDis achieved the highest performance across all simulated cloth manipulation tasks, outperforming existing visual RL baselines by up to **21.9%** and exhibiting the lowest standard deviation.
- The proposed knowledge distillation method, specifically via weight initialization, allowed the student policy to converge in approximately **0.6%** of the training steps required by typical baselines.
- The framework exhibited robustness to noisy state estimations and successfully transferred to a real UR5 robot, achieving a performance of **0.895** in a real-world cloth folding task without any fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Re-framing RL as sequence modeling with Decision Transformers enables agents to leverage long-term historical information, which improves control in dynamic, partially observable environments like cloth manipulation.
> - Knowledge distillation is highly effective when the student policy's weights are initialized from a pre-trained privileged teacher, drastically reducing training time and improving the final performance of the visual agent.
> - Robust state estimation from visual data, achieved through a pre-trained CNN encoder and image augmentation, is critical for successfully distilling knowledge from a state-based teacher to a vision-based student.
