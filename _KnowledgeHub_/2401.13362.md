# TraKDis: A Transformer-based Knowledge Distillation Approach for Visual Reinforcement Learning with Application to Cloth Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Imperial College London researchers developed TraKDis, a Transformer-based knowledge distillation approach, enabling robots to perform complex cloth manipulation using only visual input. This framework achieved up to 21.9% higher performance compared to existing visual reinforcement learning methods in simulation and demonstrated effective real-world transfer.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable objects like cloth is challenging due to intricate dynamics, high elasticity, and complex configuration spaces, making visual state inference difficult.
- Visual RL for cloth manipulation faces hurdles from high-dimensional observations, partial observability due to self-occlusion, and the inability of traditional Markovian RL to leverage historical information effectively.
- Existing effective RL approaches often require privileged access to precise state information (e.g., cloth particle positions) which is unavailable in real-world visual settings.

## Method

- The Decision Transformer (DT) architecture was used to re-frame ==reinforcement learning== as a sequence modeling problem, allowing the agent to capture contextual relationships and long-term dependencies.
- A novel knowledge distillation framework, **TraKDis**, was developed to transfer expertise from a state-privileged teacher agent to a vision-based student agent.
- A pre-trained CNN encoder estimates low-dimensional cloth states from RGB images, and the student policy's weights are initialized by copying from the teacher to accelerate learning.

## Results

- TraKDis achieved the highest performance across all simulated cloth manipulation tasks, outperforming existing visual RL baselines by up to **21.9%** and exhibiting the lowest standard deviation.
- The proposed knowledge distillation method, specifically via weight initialization, allowed the student policy to converge in approximately **0.6%** of the training steps required by typical baselines.
- The framework exhibited robustness to noisy state estimations and successfully transferred to a real UR5 robot, achieving a performance of **0.895** in a real-world cloth folding task without any fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Re-framing RL as sequence modeling with Decision Transformers enables agents to leverage long-term historical information, which improves control in dynamic, partially observable environments like cloth manipulation.
> - Knowledge distillation is highly effective when the student policy's weights are initialized from a pre-trained privileged teacher, drastically reducing training time and improving the final performance of the visual agent.
> - Robust state estimation from visual data, achieved through a pre-trained CNN encoder and image augmentation, is critical for successfully distilling knowledge from a state-based teacher to a vision-based student.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation of deformable objects, particularly cloth and other fabrics, presents significant challenges due to their intricate dynamics, high elasticity, and complex configuration spaces. While rigid-body manipulation is relatively well-established, deformable object manipulation remains an area of active research.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising tool for robotic manipulation, including cloth tasks. However, many successful RL applications for cloth manipulation often rely on privileged access to precise state information, such as exact particle positions of cloth keypoints, which is difficult to obtain in real-world scenarios. Approaches that infer states from visual data (e.g., RGB images) are more practical but face hurdles with cloth due to high self-occlusion and a lack of consistent, trackable features. Furthermore, visual data is inherently high-dimensional, making direct training of visual RL policies more challenging.

Previous works in visual RL for cloth manipulation frequently employ asymmetric actor-critic architectures, where the actor uses high-dimensional observations (RGB images) and the critic learns from lower-dimensional environment states. While effective in some rigid and deformable object tasks, a limitation of these Markov Decision Process (MDP) based actor-critic methods is their reliance on the Markov assumption, which can lead to a loss of historical information in dynamic environments. This loss can compromise the controllability and observability of the control agent, particularly in tasks like cloth manipulation where current states often depend on sequences of past states.

The Transformer architecture, initially successful in Natural Language Processing (NLP) for sequence modeling, has recently been extended to vision and reinforcement learning. Transformers offer advantages in processing information with large capacity and long memory, making them suitable for predicting complex trajectory sequences and capturing contextual relationships. This makes them a candidate for addressing the historical information loss in dynamic RL scenarios.

Knowledge Distillation (KD) is a transfer learning technique that transfers knowledge from a larger, often more complex "teacher" model to a smaller, simpler "student" model. In the context of robotic control, KD has been used to transfer knowledge from state-based policies (which perform well but require privileged information) to vision-based policies (which are more practical for real-world deployment). This paper leverages KD in a specific one-to-one fashion to bridge the gap between a privileged state-based teacher agent and a vision-based student agent, but notably within a Transformer-based framework to address the temporal dependencies inherent in cloth manipulation.

This work specifically addresses the challenge of visual reinforcement learning for cloth manipulation by integrating the strengths of Transformer models for sequential decision-making with a novel Knowledge Distillation approach to transfer expertise from a state-privileged agent to a vision-only agent, aiming to overcome the limitations of prior visual RL and actor-critic methods in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient approach for robotic cloth manipulation using visual feedback, overcoming the limitations of current methods. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and motivations:

1. **Address Challenges of Visual Reinforcement Learning for Cloth Manipulation:**
 - **High Dimensionality of Visual Data:** RGB images, while practical in the real world, present a high-dimensional observation space that complicates direct RL training compared to low-dimensional state information.
 - **Intricate Cloth Dynamics and Partial Observability:** Cloth exhibits complex and high-dimensional states, and visual observations are often partially observable due to self-occlusion and the lack of distinct, trackable features. This makes it difficult to infer accurate cloth states from images alone.
 - **Limitations of Markovian Models:** Traditional RL methods, particularly actor-critic architectures, often rely on the Markov assumption, where decisions are made based solely on the current state. In highly dynamic environments like cloth manipulation, historical information (a sequence of past states) is crucial for effective control, and its loss can degrade performance.

2. **Leverage Transformer Architecture for Sequential Decision-Making:**
 - The authors are motivated to adopt a Transformer-based RL approach because of its capacity to process information with a long memory and its ability to capture global contextual relationships. This is hypothesized to allow the agent to better understand complex trajectory sequences and mitigate issues related to the loss of historical information, which is a known limitation of prior MDP-based methods.

3. **Propose a Novel Knowledge Distillation (KD) Framework (TraKDis):**
 - The goal is to transfer the robust performance of a "privileged agent" (teacher) that has access to complete cloth state information to a "vision-based agent" (student) that only receives RGB image input. This aims to bridge the "domain gap" between low-dimensional state information and high-dimensional visual observations.
 - **Facilitate Knowledge Transfer with Pre-trained State Estimation:** A key motivation is to minimize the discrepancy between visual observations and state inputs during distillation. This is achieved by designing and pre-training a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder to estimate low-dimensional cloth states directly from RGB images.
 - **Improve Distillation Efficiency and Performance via Weight Initialization:** Recognizing the benefits of transfer learning in large models (like LLMs), the authors propose initializing the student policy's weights by copying them from the pre-trained privileged teacher policy. This is expected to significantly boost both training efficiency and the final performance of the vision-based agent.

4. **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Robustness:**
 - The research aims to show that TraKDis outperforms existing state-of-the-art visual RL techniques in various cloth manipulation tasks within simulation.
 - It also seeks to validate the agent's robustness by evaluating its performance in noisy environments, demonstrating its ability to adapt to uncertainties in state estimation.
 - Finally, a motivation is to showcase the practical applicability and efficiency of the proposed method through real-robot experiments.

In summary, the core motivation is to enable robots to perform complex cloth manipulation tasks using only visual input by developing an intelligent learning framework that can effectively learn from high-dimensional, partially observable, and temporally dependent data, leveraging the strengths of Transformers and efficient knowledge distillation techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, TraKDis, decomposes the visual reinforcement learning problem for cloth manipulation into two distinct stages: training a privileged agent (teacher) and then performing knowledge distillation to a vision-based agent (student).

**A. Stage 1: Privileged Agent Training (Teacher Model)**

1. **Problem Formulation:** The cloth manipulation environment is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), defined by `<A, S, O, P, R>`, where `A` are actions, `S` are states, `O` are observations, `P` are transition functions, and `R` are rewards. The agent aims to maximize cumulative discounted rewards.
2. **RL Framework: Decision Transformer (DT):** The Decision Transformer (DT) model is adopted as the reinforcement learning framework. DT re-frames RL as a sequence modeling problem, where the model processes a sequence of trajectory data `(return-to-go, state, action)` to predict the next action. This architecture allows for processing information with a long memory and capturing contextual relationships over a sequence of past states, thereby addressing the limitations of Markovian assumptions.
3. **Training Procedure:**
 - **Offline Training:** The privileged agent is initially trained using an offline dataset. This dataset is collected from a human-designed heuristic expert algorithm that has complete access to the full dynamics and state information (e.g., cloth particle locations) of the cloth in a simulated environment (SoftGym suite). The training objective is a cross-entropy loss (`L_CE`) to maximize the likelihood of predicting ground-truth actions from the expert policy.
 - **Online Fine-tuning:** To improve performance beyond the pre-collected dataset distribution, online fine-tuning is applied. During this phase, samples from the training buffer are gradually replaced with new data collected by the partially trained agent, enhancing its exploration ability. An entropy loss (`L_en`) is added to the total loss function (`L = L_CE - λL_en`) to encourage exploration.
 - **Input for Privileged Agent:** The privileged agent takes privileged cloth state information as input.

**B. Stage 2: Knowledge Distillation for Learning Visual Control Agent (Student Model)**

This stage involves transferring the knowledge from the trained privileged agent (teacher) to a vision-based agent (student) that only receives RGB images.

1. **Pre-trained CNN Encoder for State Estimation (`F_c`):**
 - **Purpose:** To bridge the domain gap between high-dimensional visual observations and low-dimensional state information, a dedicated Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder (`F_c`) is designed. This CNN takes a 32x32x3 RGB image (`I_t`) as input and outputs an estimated low-dimensional state representation (`S'_t`) of the target cloth.
 - **Training:** The CNN encoder is trained using data collected from the simulation, where ground-truth state information is available, allowing for supervised learning of state estimation.
 - **Image Augmentation:** Image augmentation techniques (e.g., flipping, cropping) are applied *only during the training of the CNN encoder*. The goal is to implicitly improve the robustness of the visual RL agent by enhancing the CNN encoder's ability to provide stable state estimations under varying visual conditions.

2. **Knowledge Distillation via Weight Initialization:**
 - **Student Model Architecture:** The student model employs a Decision Transformer architecture, similar to the privileged teacher model. The key difference is that the student policy takes the estimated states (`S'_t`) from the pre-trained CNN encoder as input, rather than ground-truth states.
 - **Weight Transfer Initialization:** To facilitate knowledge transfer and improve training efficiency, the student model is initialized by copying the weights directly from the pre-trained privileged teacher model. This leverages the knowledge already acquired by the teacher.
 - **Distillation Process:** During knowledge distillation:
 - The parameters of the pre-trained CNN encoder (`F_c`) and the teacher policy are frozen.
 - The student policy (DT model) is updated by minimizing an imitation loss (`L_distill`).
 - The loss is calculated as the cross-entropy (`L_CE`) between the actions generated by the teacher policy (using ground-truth states `a_t`) and the actions predicted by the student policy (using estimated states `a'_t`). This process effectively teaches the student to imitate the teacher's robust actions based on its visual perception.
 - **Training Efficiency:** The student policy is designed to converge with significantly fewer steps (10k steps) compared to a typical RL training process, owing to the efficient initialization and distillation process.

**C. Experimental Setup and Evaluation:**

- **Tasks:** Three vision-based cloth manipulation tasks from the SoftGym suite are used: "Cloth Fold Image," "Cloth Fold Diagonal Pinned Image," and "Cloth Fold Diagonal Unpinned Image."
- **Evaluation Metric:** Normalized performance score, calculated as `(s_t - s_0) / (s_opt - s_0)`, where `s_t` is current performance, `s_0` is initial performance, and `s_opt` is optimal performance.
- **Baselines:** TraKDis is compared against state-of-the-art methods: Teacher (state-based DT), DMFD (image-based LfD), Behavior Cloning, CURL (contrastive visual RL), and DT-image (direct DT on images).
- **Ablation Studies:** Variants of TraKDis are tested to isolate the contribution of each component: `No-Aug` (no image augmentation in CNN), `No-Pretrain` (no weight copy from teacher), and `No-Distill` (direct imitation of heuristic expert without KD framework).
- **Robustness Test:** Gaussian noise is introduced to the estimated states (`S'_t`) during training and evaluation to assess the model's tolerance to estimation inaccuracies.
- **Real-world Validation:** A UR5 robot with a parallel gripper and an Intel RealSense D435i camera is used to perform the "Cloth Fold Unpinned" task in a real environment to demonstrate practical applicability without further fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance, robustness, and efficiency of the TraKDis framework.

**A. Comparison Experiments (Performance against Baselines):**

- **Teacher Performance:** The privileged Decision Transformer (DT) teacher model, trained with full cloth state information, exhibited performance comparable to or superior to the heuristic expert policy, demonstrating its effectiveness as a source of knowledge.
- **TraKDis Outperforms Baselines:** In all three visual cloth manipulation tasks ("Cloth Fold Image," "Cloth Fold Diagonal Pinned Image," "Cloth Fold Diagonal Unpinned Image"), TraKDis consistently achieved the highest performance compared to other state-of-the-art image-based baselines (DMFD, Behavior Cloning, CURL, and DT-image).
 - **Cloth Fold Image:** TraKDis achieved a performance of 0.583, which corresponds to 78% of the teacher's performance. This represents a 21.9% improvement over other baselines.
 - **Cloth Fold Diagonal Pinned Image:** TraKDis attained 0.916, showing a 13.8% improvement.
 - **Cloth Fold Diagonal Unpinned Image:** TraKDis reached 0.944, a 8.3% improvement. Notably, in the two diagonal fold tasks, TraKDis's performance surpassed both the expert and teacher's performances, suggesting that the distillation process, potentially combined with the DT's inherent capabilities and robust state estimation, can lead to highly effective policies even with visual inputs.
- **Robustness (Low Standard Deviation):** TraKDis exhibited the lowest standard deviation across the three tasks, indicating higher reliability and consistency in its performance compared to other methods.

**B. Ablation Studies (Contribution of Components):**

- **Effectiveness of Image Augmentation:** Pre-training the CNN encoder with image augmentation techniques was found to lead to more robust state estimation, which, in turn, stabilized the training of the student policy.
- **Significance of Weight Initialization:** Copying weights from the privileged teacher agent to initialize the student model demonstrated a significant improvement in both training efficiency and final performance. The student policy converged within 60 episodes (approximately 6000 steps), which is reported as only 0.6% of the training steps required by some baselines.
- **Necessity of Distillation Framework:** Ablation variants without weight copy (`No-Pretrain`) or without the full distillation setup (`No-Distill`) showed either slow performance improvement or performance degradation, reinforcing the importance of the proposed knowledge distillation framework. The `No-Pretrain` and `No-Distill` variants exhibited either slower convergence or failed to reach comparable performance levels, highlighting that direct imitation or training without this specific initialization is less effective.

**C. Robustness to Noisy State Estimation:**

- **Tolerance to Noise:** The model demonstrated an ability to adapt to added Gaussian noise in the estimated states during training, converging effectively within 60 epochs. While adding noise did not intrinsically improve the final performance, it enhanced the model's robustness by reducing the standard deviation with more training steps.
- **Higher Tolerance:** Retraining the student model with explicitly added noise resulted in a higher tolerance for inaccurate state estimation when the model was later evaluated under varying noise levels. This indicates that the method can be made more resilient to real-world sensor inaccuracies.

**D. Real-world Experiment:**

- **Real-world Applicability:** In the real-world "Cloth Fold Unpinned" task using a UR5 robot, TraKDis achieved a performance of 0.895. This result is comparable to the simulation performance of 0.940, indicating the practical effectiveness of the method in real-world scenarios without any fine-tuning or re-training for the real environment.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that TraKDis effectively leverages Decision Transformers and a specialized knowledge distillation approach to achieve superior performance in visual cloth manipulation tasks. The method is efficient in training, robust to environmental uncertainties, and directly applicable to real robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation and reinforcement learning, with potential impacts across various applications.

1. **Advancement in Visual Reinforcement Learning for Deformable Object Manipulation:** TraKDis provides a robust and high-performing framework for a notoriously challenging problem: manipulating deformable objects like cloth using only visual input. By demonstrating superior performance over existing state-of-the-art methods, it pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in this domain. This is critical for tasks where explicit state information is unavailable or difficult to obtain in real-world settings.

2. **Efficient and Effective Knowledge Transfer:** The proposed knowledge distillation approach, which combines a pre-trained CNN encoder for state estimation and weight initialization from a privileged teacher, offers an efficient strategy for transferring complex skills. The drastic reduction in training steps for the vision-based student agent (converging in 0.6% of typical steps) is a substantial achievement, making the development of visual policies more practical and less computationally intensive. This could accelerate the deployment of visual RL agents in other complex manipulation tasks.

3. **Validation of Transformer Architectures in Robotic Control:** The successful application of the Decision Transformer for sequential decision-making in a dynamic, partially observable environment like cloth manipulation highlights the benefits of sequence modeling over traditional Markovian approaches. Transformers' ability to process long historical contexts mitigates the issue of historical information loss, offering a pathway to more intelligent and adaptable robotic control policies for complex, dynamic tasks.

4. **Enhanced Robustness to Real-world Imperfections:** The demonstrated ability of TraKDis to handle noisy state estimations and maintain performance, particularly when specifically trained with noise, is crucial for real-world deployment. Robotic systems often operate with imperfect sensors, and a method that can adapt to such uncertainties is more likely to succeed outside of controlled laboratory environments.

5. **Practical Applicability in Real-world Scenarios:** The successful real-world experiment, without any fine-tuning, validates the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of TraKDis. This indicates its potential for direct application in practical settings such as:
 - **Domestic Robotics:** Assisting with household chores like folding laundry or making beds.
 - **Healthcare:** Robotic assistive dressing for patients or elderly individuals.
 - **Industrial Automation:** Automating tasks in garment manufacturing, textile handling, or packaging industries where deformable materials are prevalent.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work also identifies clear avenues for future improvements, such as enhancing data efficiency (reducing reliance on large offline datasets), closing performance gaps in highly complex tasks, and optimizing model size for faster operational speeds and reduced memory footprint. Furthermore, integrating TraKDis with recent advancements in large-scale pre-trained visual encoders could enable it to tackle even more challenging scenarios, including manipulating complex-shaped garments (e.g., shirts, trousers) or multi-goal manipulation tasks.

Overall, TraKDis represents a significant step towards developing robust, efficient, and practical visual reinforcement learning solutions for challenging deformable object manipulation tasks, with broad implications for future robotic applications.
