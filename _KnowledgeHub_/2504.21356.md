# Nexus-Gen: Unified Image Understanding, Generation, and Editing via Prefilled Autoregression in Shared Embedding Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Nexus-Gen, developed by Zhejiang University and Alibaba Group, unifies image understanding, generation, and editing within a single 7B-parameter framework, introducing a "prefilled autoregression" strategy to mitigate error accumulation in continuous embedding prediction. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks and releases a new high-quality instruction-guided image editing dataset.

## Problem

- Unified multimodal large language models (MLLMs) often achieve lower image synthesis quality compared to specialized diffusion models.
- Autoregressive generation of continuous image embeddings in MLLMs using diffusion decoders suffers from severe error accumulation, leading to degraded image quality.
- Many existing unified models lack robust and validated capabilities for complex downstream tasks, particularly high-fidelity fine-grained image editing.

## Method

- Nexus-Gen proposes a unified architecture integrating a vision encoder (from Qwen2.5-VL), a 7B autoregressive model, and a ==diffusion-based vision decoder== operating within a shared continuous image embedding space.
- It introduces a novel =="prefilled autoregression" strategy== that initializes and pre-fills input sequences with learned image embeddings during inference, preventing error propagation and aligning training with inference.
- A dedicated ==dual-conditioning editing decoder== is developed, combining 81-token semantic embeddings from the autoregressive model with 324-token fine-grained embeddings from the input image for robust detail preservation during editing.

## Results

- Achieves leading scores across multimodal understanding benchmarks (MME, SEED-Bench, MMMU, TextVQA) with its **7B** autoregressive component, without additional parameters or performance loss.
- Attains a state-of-the-art overall score of **0.81** on the GenEval benchmark for image generation after instruction tuning, showing particular strength in compositional understanding (e.g., "Two Object," "Counting").
- Demonstrates superior performance on the ImagePulse benchmark for image editing, with a CLIP-T score of **0.324** and strong fidelity preservation (L1 **0.134**, CLIP-O **0.909**, DINO-O **0.834**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "prefilled autoregression" strategy effectively mitigates error accumulation during the autoregressive prediction of continuous embeddings, resulting in significantly lower MSE loss compared to naive autoregression.
> - A specialized dual-conditioning editing decoder is critical for high-fidelity image editing, substantially improving the preservation of non-edited regions compared to a general generation decoder.
> - An optimal token quantity (81 tokens) for autoregressive generation balances semantic coverage and ease of prediction, while a higher token count (324 tokens) from the input image is beneficial for detail preservation in editing tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal generative modeling has rapidly advanced, driven by the success of large language models (LLMs) and diffusion models. This paper positions itself at the forefront of efforts to unify these capabilities, particularly focusing on integrating image understanding, generation, and editing within a single framework.

Currently, the research landscape for unified multimodal large language models (MLLMs) broadly follows two dominant paradigms:
1. **Autoregressive models coupled with Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) or VQ-VAEs:** Models like Chameleon, Janus-Pro, and Emu3 primarily use autoregressive models to predict visual embeddings, which are then decoded into images by VAE-based decoders. While these models excel in image understanding due to their MLLM backbone, their image synthesis quality often lags behind state-of-the-art diffusion models, largely due to a lack of pixel-level image modeling capabilities.
2. **Autoregressive models utilizing diffusion models for image generation:** Approaches like SEED-X, Emu2, MetaMerph, and even the closed-source GPT-4o, leverage powerful pre-trained diffusion models as vision decoders. These methods generally achieve superior image quality compared to VAE-based approaches. However, they face a critical challenge: severe error accumulation during the autoregressive generation of continuous image embeddings. Additionally, their extensibility and validated capability for complex downstream tasks like fine-grained image editing are often limited or not fully explored.

Nexus-Gen directly addresses these limitations, seeking to combine the strengths of both paradigms while mitigating their weaknesses. It aims to achieve high-fidelity image generation (a strength of diffusion models) by integrating a diffusion model as its vision decoder. Simultaneously, it tackles the persistent problem of autoregressive error accumulation for continuous embeddings through its novel "prefilled autoregression strategy." Furthermore, Nexus-Gen significantly advances the unification goal by explicitly incorporating robust image editing capabilities, a task that many existing unified models have not adequately validated or optimized for. By establishing a shared continuous image embedding space, Nexus-Gen facilitates a more synergistic interplay between language reasoning and visual synthesis, positioning it as a crucial step toward achieving more generalized and capable multimodal AI, moving closer to the vision of "unified general intelligence" for complex tasks like visual reasoning and reasoning-enhanced image generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Nexus-Gen is to develop a unified multimodal model capable of seamlessly performing image understanding, generation, and editing tasks within a single, coherent framework. The motivations driving this research stem from several recognized limitations and opportunities in current MLLM development:

1. **Bridging Modality Gaps and Joint Optimization:** A fundamental challenge in MLLMs is integrating diverse visual tasks (understanding, generation, editing) and jointly optimizing them to harness the full potential of multimodal data, especially interleaved text-image corpora. Existing models often excel in one area but falter in others or struggle with synergistic learning across modalities.
2. **Improving Image Synthesis Quality:** While MLLMs have shown strong image understanding capabilities, their image generation quality, particularly in VAE-based architectures, remains inferior to specialized diffusion models. A key motivation is to bridge this quality gap within a unified setup.
3. **Mitigating Autoregressive Error Accumulation:** When autoregressive models predict continuous image embeddings (a common approach in models using diffusion decoders), the iterative nature of prediction leads to a compounding of errors, significantly degrading the quality of generated images. Addressing this "error accumulation phenomenon" is a critical technical motivation.
4. **Enhancing Image Editing Capabilities:** Many unified models have not adequately addressed or demonstrated robust image editing functionality. There's a clear need for a framework that can faithfully execute editing instructions while preserving details in non-edited regions, leveraging both semantic understanding and fine-grained control.
5. **Efficient Data Utilization and Cross-Modal Learning:** Unification offers the advantage of efficient data utilization and improved cross-modal representation learning from vast multimodal datasets. The motivation is to create a system that can effectively learn from and leverage these diverse data types for multiple tasks.
6. **Advancing Towards Unified General Intelligence:** The overarching motivation is to move towards more generalized AI systems that can seamlessly switch between and integrate various multimodal tasks, enabling complex visual reasoning and interaction paradigms.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose specific innovations: a unified image embedding space, a novel prefilled autoregression strategy, a multi-stage training approach, and the curation of a large-scale, high-quality multimodal dataset.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Nexus-Gen's methodology is built upon three core components: a vision encoder, an autoregressive model, and a vision decoder, all operating within a shared continuous image embedding space.

#### 3.1. Architecture (Figure 1a)

- **Unified Image Embedding Space:** This is the central design element, acting as an interface between the autoregressive model and the diffusion vision decoder. It's intended to preserve information integrity and versatility across understanding, generation, and editing tasks.
- **Vision Encoder:** The vision transformer from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is adopted. It encodes input images into unified image embeddings. The choice of Qwen2.5-VL ensures implicit alignment with textual representation due to its multimodal pretraining. Dynamic resolution capabilities allow for controlling the number of image tokens (e.g., 25, 81, 324 tokens), enabling a trade-off between capturing fine details and ease of autoregressive generation. An optimal count of 81 tokens was determined for output images.
- **Autoregressive Model:** This component, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, is responsible for unified multimodal reasoning. It takes projected text embeddings (from a text tokenizer) and encoded visual embeddings (from the vision encoder) as input. It then predicts output embeddings, which can either be text embeddings (converted to logits by a text projector) for understanding tasks or image embeddings (mapped to the unified space via a vision projector) for generation and editing tasks.
- **Vision Decoder:** The diffusion transformer from FLUX.1-Dev is adapted for high-fidelity image decoding. It's specialized for different tasks:
 - **Generation Decoder (Figure 1c):** Designed for text-to-image generation, it reconstructs images from the 81-token semantic image embeddings produced by the autoregressive model, using a two-layer MLP projector for embedding alignment.
 - **Editing Decoder (Figure 1d):** Crucial for image editing, it employs a dual conditioning mechanism. It combines the 81-token semantic embeddings from the autoregressive model (for semantic changes) with 324-token fine-grained embeddings directly encoded from the input image (for preserving unedited details). A joint attention layer projects these heterogeneous inputs to the diffusion transformer.

#### 3.2. Prefilled Autoregression (Figure 2)

This novel strategy directly addresses the "error accumulation" issue observed when autoregressive models predict continuous embeddings. In naive autoregression, errors from predicted tokens can propagate and amplify when fed back as input for subsequent predictions. Nexus-Gen's solution involves:
- **Initialization:** During training, all image tokens are initialized with `N_E` learnable embeddings with positional encoding.
- **Prefilling:** During inference, after predicting a "beginning of image" (BOI) token, the input sequence for subsequent predictions is "prefilled" with these learned special image embeddings, rather than using the model's own potentially biased predictions. This strategy unifies the training and inference dynamics, preventing error propagation and ensuring consistent performance.

#### 3.3. Dataset Curation

To facilitate unified multi-task learning, the authors curated a large-scale dataset of 26.3 million samples encompassing image understanding, generation, and editing tasks.
- It combines and re-labels samples from existing public datasets (e.g., Cambrian-7M for understanding, Journey DB, Laion-HR for generation, HQ-Edit, UltraEdit for editing).
- **ImagePulse Dataset:** A significant contribution is the construction of a new, high-quality image editing dataset called ImagePulse. This dataset aims to overcome aesthetic quality and visual fidelity limitations of existing editing datasets. It includes three subsets: Change-Add-Remove (object manipulation), Style Transfer (applying new styles), and FaceID (identity-preserving edits with high variation). ImagePulse samples feature pristine image pairs synthesized by FLUX.1-Dev and semantically rich editing instructions generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B.
- **Bilingual Annotations:** To support Chinese-English capabilities, additional Chinese generation and editing samples were incorporated through re-annotation.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

The training involves separate objectives for the autoregressive model and the vision decoders:
- **Autoregressive Model:** Uses a composite loss function:
 - `L_Text`: Standard cross-entropy loss for text tokens.
 - `L_Image`: Combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Cosine Similarity loss for image embeddings, ensuring both detail fidelity and semantic coherence.
- **Vision Decoders (Generation & Editing):** Both utilize the MSE loss function of flow matching, commonly used in diffusion models, to optimize image reconstruction given conditions.

#### 3.5. Multi-stage Training Strategy

A three-stage training process is employed (Figure 1b, c, d):
1. **Multi-Task Pretraining for Autoregressive Model:**
 - **Unified Pretraining:** On the full 26.3 million samples, to establish visual embedding prediction and preserve text prediction.
 - **Aesthetic Fine-tuning:** On 4.3 million high-quality samples to optimize visual output quality.
2. **Conditional Adaptation for Generation Decoder:** Fine-tuned on two million high-quality image generation samples using an image reconstruction objective, aligning its inputs with the unified image embedding space.
3. **Conditional Adaptation for Editing Decoder:** Fine-tuned on the ImagePulse dataset to handle dual-stream inputs (81-token semantic + 324-token fine-grained embeddings).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Nexus-Gen demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across all three target tasks: image understanding, generation, and editing, primarily with a 7B-parameter autoregressive model.

#### 4.1. Image Understanding (Table 1)

- Nexus-Gen 7B achieves leading scores across a comprehensive suite of multimodal understanding benchmarks including MME, SEED-Bench, MMMU, TextVQA, VQAv2, and RealWorldQA.
- It significantly outperforms other unified models, especially in MME-Cognition and TextVQA.
- Crucially, the integration of image generation and editing capabilities does not incur a significant loss in the base model's image understanding performance, and it achieves this without introducing additional parameters to the core MLLM.

#### 4.2. Image Generation (Table 2)

- On the GenEval benchmark, Nexus-Gen (multi-task jointly trained) achieves an overall score of 0.77.
- After generative-task-specific instruction tuning (Nexus-Gen*), it reaches a state-of-the-art overall score of 0.81.
- Nexus-Gen* shows particular strengths in metrics related to object properties such as "Two Object," "Counting," and "Position," indicating improved capability in compositional generation.

#### 4.3. Image Editing (Table 3)

- Evaluated on the newly introduced ImagePulse benchmark, Nexus-Gen consistently exhibits superior performance across all metrics:
 - **CLIP-T (CLIP image-text similarity):** Higher score (0.324) indicates better alignment between the edited image and the target caption.
 - **L1 (pixel-level difference):** Lower score (0.134) signifies closer pixel fidelity to the ground truth.
 - **CLIP-O and DINO-O (image-image cosine similarity):** Higher scores (0.909 and 0.834 respectively) demonstrate better preservation of overall content and structure compared to the ground truth.
- These results highlight Nexus-Gen's enhanced ability to accurately execute editing instructions while maintaining visual fidelity and detail preservation in unaltered regions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Token Quantity Trade-offs (Figures 3, 4):** While 324 tokens (higher resolution) allow the vision decoder to reconstruct images with superior detail consistency, the autoregressive model struggles to accurately predict such an extensive token sequence for *generation*, leading to semantic repetition and compromised quality. 81 tokens were found to be the optimal balance for the autoregressive model's generation task. The editing decoder, however, benefits from utilizing 324 tokens from the input image to preserve details.
- **Necessity of Editing Decoder (Figure 5):** The dedicated editing decoder, with its dual conditioning (81-token semantic from AR model + 324-token fine-grained from input image), is proven essential for high-fidelity image editing. It significantly outperforms the generation decoder in preserving details in non-edited regions, a critical aspect of realistic editing.
- **Impact of Prefilled Autoregression (Figure 6):** This strategy effectively mitigates error accumulation. It achieves a significantly lower MSE loss during inference (0.43) compared to naive autoregression (0.5543), demonstrating its success in aligning training and inference dynamics and improving the quality of continuous embedding generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Nexus-Gen represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of unified multimodal AI, demonstrating several key areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Towards Unified General Intelligence:** By seamlessly integrating image understanding, generation, and editing within a single, coherent framework, Nexus-Gen takes a substantial step towards building truly general-purpose multimodal models. This unification fosters efficient data utilization and deeper cross-modal representation learning, which is crucial for complex AI tasks.
2. **Innovative Solution to a Core Technical Challenge:** The proposed "prefilled autoregression strategy" is a notable technical contribution. It effectively resolves the long-standing problem of error accumulation during the autoregressive prediction of continuous embeddings, a challenge that has hampered the performance of many diffusion-based MLLMs. This solution has implications for a broader range of autoregressive models dealing with continuous output spaces.
3. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Multiple Modalities:** Achieving SOTA results across diverse and challenging benchmarks for understanding, generation, and editing with a relatively compact 7B-parameter model is a strong validation of Nexus-Gen's architectural and training innovations. This demonstrates efficiency and effectiveness, making it a competitive candidate against larger, more specialized models.
4. **Enhanced Image Editing Capabilities:** The novel dual-conditioning editing decoder significantly improves fidelity and control in image editing, allowing for both semantic changes and meticulous preservation of unedited regions. This capability opens doors for more sophisticated and user-friendly image manipulation tools.
5. **Valuable Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to releasing all models, datasets (including the new ImagePulse dataset), and source code is a major contribution to the open science community. The ImagePulse dataset, specifically designed for high-quality instruction-guided image editing, addresses a critical gap in publicly available resources and will undoubtedly facilitate further research and benchmarking in this area.
6. **Foundation for Advanced Multimodal Reasoning:** The unified image embedding space, by bridging LLM reasoning and diffusion synthesis, lays a robust foundation for future research into more complex interleaved tasks, such as in-context learning for visual tasks, and sophisticated step-by-step vision-language reasoning, enabling richer human-AI interaction.
7. **Industry-Academia Synergy:** The collaboration between Zhejiang University and Alibaba Group highlights the power of combining academic foundational research with industrial resources and application-oriented development, leading to practical and high-impact AI solutions.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as sensitivity to prompt variations and unexplored visual reasoning capacities, Nexus-Gen provides a strong baseline and a clear pathway for future advancements in robust, general-purpose multimodal AI.
