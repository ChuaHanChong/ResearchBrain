# Multi-Modal World Model for Physical Robot Interactions: Simultaneous Visual and Tactile Predictions for Enhanced Accuracy

> [!summary] Summary
> SPOTS is a dual-pipeline action-conditioned world model that predicts future scene frames and tactile forces simultaneously, pairing a stochastic video generator with a vector-based tactile predictor joined by cross-modal fusion layers. On robot-pushing data containing visually identical objects with hidden friction patches, it lowers scene MAE from 0.0104 to 0.0091 and cuts tactile prediction error roughly threefold against a tactile-only baseline.

## Problem

- Video-prediction world models for robot pushing condition only on pixels and actions, leaving friction, mass and contact dynamics as unresolved latent variables.
- Tactile prediction models and video models are developed separately, so neither exploits the correlation between touch and sight during contact-rich interaction.
- Existing vision-touch fusion targets classification, grasp success or modality translation, not forward prediction, and public pushing datasets contain no tactile stream.

## Method

- **SPOTS** runs two modality-specific predictors, a ==stochastic video generator== for scenes and the vector-based ==action-conditioned tactile network== for forces, joined by ==crossover links==.
- **Multi-Modal Fusion Model** layers, two ==linear layers with batch normalisation and tanh==, sit before each pipeline's ==LSTM chain==, giving every modality its own fused representation.
- Two ==magnetic-taxel== pushing datasets are released: 5,500 household-cluster trials, plus 1,000 train and 600 test trials of one visually identical box with relocated sandpaper friction patches.

## Results

- On the visually identical edge-case subset SPOTS-small reaches MAE **0.0091** and PSNR **79.26** versus vision-only SVG at **0.0104** and **77.53**, over ten training runs.
- Tactile prediction MAE is **0.0167** for SPOTS against **0.0452** for the tactile-only ACTP baseline and **0.2584** for the single-pipeline SVTG variant.
- Replacing live tactile input with no-contact values returns SPOTS to **0.0108**, matching SVG, while occluding vision flattens tactile spike predictions entirely.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal prediction pays off mainly under physical ambiguity and at long horizons; when appearance already reveals dynamics, extra sensing buys almost nothing.
> - Heterogeneous modalities are better kept in separate pipelines with limited crossover than forced into one shared latent space, which sacrifices modality-specific fidelity.
> - Gains come from live cross-modal input, not from richer training signal, so sensor dropout at inference erases the advantage entirely.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: predicting the outcomes of robotic actions, often referred to as learning a "world model," in complex environments. Existing approaches for learning world models in robotics have primarily focused on visual observations and action inputs to generate video-based predictions. However, these methods frequently overlook the role of tactile feedback, which is crucial for understanding physical interactions, particularly in contact-rich scenarios.

Human perception of physical interaction relies significantly on the integration of both visual and tactile sensations, with tactile cognition playing a role in tasks such as grasping, object manipulation, and object pushing. Humans also employ predictive models to effectively execute these complex interaction tasks. In robotics, physical interaction tasks are commonly addressed through methods like estimating physical properties, interactive planning, learning-based planning, or deep neural network video prediction models, often benchmarked with object-pushing datasets (e.g., BAIR). More recently, tactile predictive models have shown performance advantages over reactive tactile systems in physical robot interaction tasks.

However, a gap exists in the thorough exploration of forward predictive models that integrate both vision and tactile modalities. Current multi-modal research in robotics often focuses on task-specific applications, such as classifying grasp success, identifying product defects, translating between material properties, or object classification, where a combined multi-modal feature vector is used. These approaches typically treat vision and tactile sensation as separate modalities within broader systems. This paper identifies a need for more general multi-modal prediction frameworks that capture the correlation between visual and tactile sensing to predict future sensory states across both modalities. By developing a dual-pipeline multi-modal architecture, this research aims to overcome the limitations of single-modality approaches, which can result in a higher number of latent variables and increased prediction uncertainty. The work directly addresses the need for robust, modality-aware visuo-tactile prediction architectures that can capture physical dynamics across sensing modalities, especially in contact-rich and partially observable interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that current robotic world models, largely reliant on visual data, often lack a comprehensive understanding of physical interactions. While vision provides extensive contextual information, it frequently falls short in situations where crucial physical properties—such as friction, contact location, or material compliance—are not visually inferable. This limitation leads to increased prediction uncertainty and reduced robustness in physical robot interaction tasks.

The work is bio-inspired by the mammalian brain's ability to seamlessly integrate touch and sight, demonstrating cross-connections that facilitate the fusion of tactile and visual information for environment perception. This biological precedent suggests that a multi-modal approach in robotics could enhance an agent's predictive capabilities.

The key objectives and motivations of this study are detailed as follows:

- **Improve Cause-and-Effect Understanding**: The central goal is to enhance a robot's understanding of physical cause-and-effect relationships during physical robot interaction (PRI). Without this understanding, a robot cannot effectively distinguish between advantageous and disadvantageous actions, hindering efficient and safe task performance.
- **Integrate Tactile and Visual Information**: The research aims to investigate the benefits of integrating tactile and visual information within predictive perception systems. The hypothesis is that such integration will improve an agent’s predictions across both modalities (vision and touch).
- **Develop a Dual-Pipeline Multi-Modal Architecture**: To achieve robust integration, the authors propose a novel bio-inspired dual-pipeline architecture, termed SPOTS (Simultaneous Prediction of Optical and Tactile Sensations). This architecture is designed to explicitly preserve modality-specific inductive biases while facilitating cross-modal interaction, rather than forcing all sensory data into a single, potentially compromising, latent space.
- **Address Physically Ambiguous Interaction Regimes**: A key motivation is to specifically target scenarios where physical properties are visually ambiguous or indistinguishable. The research aims to demonstrate that visuo-tactile prediction provides the greatest benefits in these regimes, where underlying latent physical properties (e.g., friction, contact dynamics) cannot be inferred from vision alone. Conversely, it seeks to clarify that improvements might be limited when object dynamics are visually unambiguous.
- **Enhance Prediction Accuracy and Robustness**: The objective is to show that simultaneous prediction of vision and touch improves prediction accuracy, robustness, and long-horizon prediction accuracy, especially under conditions of physical ambiguity.
- **Improve Tactile Prediction Quality**: The study also aims to demonstrate that visual input can enhance tactile prediction quality, generalisation to unseen physical properties, and robustness under sensory occlusion.
- **Create Novel Datasets**: To facilitate the investigation of these objectives, the authors developed two novel tactile-visual robot-pushing datasets. One dataset specifically focuses on visually identical objects with varying physical properties to isolate physical ambiguity, while the other mirrors existing robot-pushing benchmarks with household object clusters. These datasets are intended for unsupervised learning.
- **Lay Foundation for Advanced Control**: The overarching motivation is that an improved multi-modal world model, capable of accurate forward predictions, can benefit other aspects of physical robot interaction, including grasping, in-hand manipulation, human-robot interaction, and soft tissue manipulation, and ultimately inform model predictive control (MPC) systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research focuses on developing a multi-modal forward perception model that improves both visual and tactile prediction by integrating both modalities. The approach involves adapting a stochastic video prediction baseline and creating several architectural variants to explore different integration strategies.

**Problem Formulation:**
The core problem is defined as predicting future frames, which can be either video (`v_i`) or tactile (`t_i`) or both (`f_i = {v_i, t_i}`), given a sequence of past context frames (`f_0:c-1`), past robot trajectory (`x_0:c-1`), and planned future robot actions (`a_c-1:H-2`). The objective is to minimize the difference between predicted and observed frames using loss functions (L1 or L2) in either pixel space or tactile reading space. For simultaneous prediction, a weighted sum of vision and tactile losses is minimized (weights `α=β=1`).

**Base Architecture:**
The Stochastic Video Generation (SVG) model is chosen as the baseline for video prediction. SVG is preferred due to its generalizability, simplicity, and fewer assumptions about input data compared to other video prediction models, making it suitable for multi-modal adaptation. It decomposes video prediction into a frame prediction network, a prior network, and a posterior network (used during training) to handle uncertainty through latent variable estimation (`z`), which aims to represent unknown physical properties and produce sharper predictions. An LSTM-based method is selected over transformer-based approaches due to the relatively smaller dataset sizes, where LSTMs are generally more data-efficient.

**Multi-Modal Model Architectures:**
The study investigates four main types of architectures for integrating tactile and visual data:

1. **SVG (Baseline)**: The original stochastic video generation model, which processes only visual input.
2. **SVG-TE (Tactile-Enhanced Stochastic Video Generation)**: This model integrates tactile data by encoding flattened past tactile frames (`t_0:c-1` transformed from R^4x4x3 to R^48) into a feature vector. This vector is then concatenated with robot action data, learned latent variables, and the scene feature vector, and fed into the LSTM chain of the Frame Prediction Model. In this architecture, tactile features condition the video prediction but do not influence the latent variable computation.
3. **SVTG (Stochastic Video and Tactile Generation)**: This model employs a single prediction pipeline to forecast both visual and tactile sensations simultaneously. It concatenates the scene and tactile data *before* encoding. Tactile data is reshaped from its vector format (`R^48`) into an image-like representation (`R^64x64x3` with three channels for normal, shear x, and shear y forces) to be compatible with the visual processing pipeline.
4. **SPOTS (Simultaneous Prediction of Optic and Tactile Sensations)**: This bio-inspired model utilizes a dual-pipeline architecture, with separate prediction modules for each modality.
 - **Scene Prediction Pipeline**: Uses the SVG's frame predictor model.
 - **Tactile Prediction Pipeline**: Employs a specialized Action-Conditioned Tactile Prediction (ACTP) network, which is designed for magnetic-based tactile data.
 - **Cross-Modal Interaction**: Crossover connections between the encoded tactile and scene data allow each pipeline to leverage information from the other modality.
 - **Multi-Modal Fusion Model (MMFM)**: Each pipeline incorporates an MMFM layer (two linear layers with batch normalization and tanh activation) positioned before the LSTM chain to process encoded scene and tactile values. This allows for modality-specific optimal multi-modal representations.
 - **SPOTS-SOP (SPOTS Scene-Only Prior)**: A variant where only scene data is used as input for the learned prior network, exploring if latent variable estimation is beneficial for tactile prediction.
 - **SPOTS-small**: A version of SPOTS with a reduced parameter count, comparable to the vision-only SVG, to assess if performance improvements are due to architectural design rather than increased model capacity.

**Datasets:**
Two novel object-pushing datasets were collected using a Franka Emika Panda robot with a Xela uSkin magnetic tactile sensor on the pushing fingertip and an Intel RealSense D345 camera. Data was synchronized and recorded at 10 Hz. Straight-line pushes were used to ensure consistent tactile contact and continuous interaction.

1. **Household Object Clusters Pushing Dataset**: Comprises 5,500 4-second pushing trials involving clusters of household objects (primarily from the YCB dataset). Test data included both "seen" (new clusters from training objects) and "unseen" (objects not in training) object clusters, each with 250 trials. A semi-automated reset system facilitated data collection.
2. **Visually Identical Dataset**: Contains 1,000 training and 600 test interactions with a single, heavy object (1.1 kg). Friction was varied by applying 60-grit sandpaper to different locations on its contact surface. This design specifically isolates physical ambiguity. The test set included 550 pushes with unseen friction locations and 50 with seen locations. Colored markers were used to quantify object location and orientation for evaluation.
 - **Edge Case Subset**: A subset of four critical test cases from the Visually Identical Dataset, where initial conditions are identical but varying friction locations lead to distinct final object positions, used for qualitative analysis.

**Evaluation Metrics:**

- **Scene Prediction**: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for pixel-wise visual fidelity. Qualitative analysis focused on object location accuracy in physically ambiguous interactions.
- **Tactile Prediction**: Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for quantitative assessment. Qualitative analysis involved examining individual taxel force trajectories, focusing on the accurate prediction of force transients, peaks, and changes in magnitude.

**Training and Test Procedures:**
Scene images were resized to 64x64x3. All models were trained end-to-end using the stochastic video prediction framework in PyTorch on two NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs. The learned prior network replaced the inference network during test time.

**Ablation Studies:**
To confirm the source of performance gains, experiments included:
- **Tactile Anaesthetisation**: Replacing live tactile data with "no contact" values during inference to assess the reliance on active tactile input.
- **Visual Occlusion**: Analyzing tactile prediction performance when visual input was systematically removed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research systematically evaluated various uni-modal and multi-modal architectures on two novel robot-pushing datasets, focusing on different interaction regimes.

**Performance on Visually Unambiguous Object Clusters (Household Object Clusters Dataset):**
- In scenarios where object dynamics are visually distinguishable, such as the household object clusters, the performance differences between tactile-enabled and vision-only models (SVG, SVG-TE, SVTG, SPOTS, SPOTS-small, SPOTS-SOP) were minimal.
- SPOTS-small, despite having a parameter count comparable to the baseline SVG, demonstrated the best overall performance in terms of MAE, PSNR, and SSIM across both the full prediction horizon and the final time step (i+5).
- While most models performed similarly for objects seen during training, SPOTS-based models consistently outperformed SVG and SVG-TE for clusters of *unseen* objects. This indicated improved generalization capabilities for multi-modal architectures even when visual ambiguity was not explicitly engineered.
- SVTG generally showed slightly lower performance compared to SVG and SPOTS models in this dataset.

**Performance under Physical Ambiguity (Visually Identical Dataset and Edge Case Subset):**
- In the visually identical dataset, designed to isolate physical ambiguity, tactile-enabled models demonstrated significant improvements. SPOTS and SPOTS-small achieved lower MAE and MSE compared to the vision-only SVG baseline. SPOTS-small exhibited the lowest average error.
- Qualitative analysis on the visually identical edge-case subset revealed that tactile-enabled models generally predicted object locations more accurately. The SVG model struggled with understanding physical properties. SVTG, despite sometimes having lower pixel-level metrics, qualitatively showed the most accurate object localization, indicating a strong perception of physical interaction.
- Model size had a limited impact on physical interaction perception; both SPOTS and SPOTS-small delivered comparable, strong predictions. This suggests architectural design was more influential than parameter count.

**Long-Horizon Prediction and Error Accumulation:**
- Over extended prediction horizons (up to 15 frames, beyond the 5 frames used in training), tactile-enabled models, particularly SPOTS, degraded more gracefully than the vision-only SVG.
- SVG’s errors compounded significantly at longer horizons due to its limited access to physical interaction cues. In contrast, tactile-enabled models leveraged contact information to maintain more stable and accurate long-term predictions.
- SVTG, despite initial underperformance, maintained competitive performance relative to SVG over extended periods due to its improved modeling of cause-effect dynamics.

**Ablation via Sensory Removal (Tactile Anaesthetisation):**
- When tactile input was replaced with "no contact" values (tactile anaesthetisation), the performance of tactile-enabled models (SVG-TE, SVTG, SPOTS variants) reverted to levels comparable to the non-tactile SVG model.
- This result indicates that the performance benefits of multi-modal models are contingent on actively receiving live tactile input, which allows them to update their internal understanding of the system in real-time. The learning process itself, when exposed to tactile data, did not inherently enhance visual perception without the continuous tactile input.

**Tactile Prediction Performance:**
- The SPOTS models (SPOTS, SPOTS-small, SPOTS-SOP) significantly outperformed both the tactile-only baseline ACTP and the single-pipeline SVTG in tactile prediction accuracy (lower MAE).
- The SVTG model, which attempts to predict tactile sensations using an SVG-style architecture for image-like tactile data, showed poor performance, indicating that its rigid architecture was not well-suited for both modalities.
- Qualitatively, SPOTS models produced good estimates of changes in tactile sensation, including predicting spikes at correct time steps and with approximately correct scale, dramatically outperforming ACTP and SVTG. Post-spike predictions in SPOTS could sometimes linger at high/low values.
- The similar performance across SPOTS, SPOTS-small, and SPOTS-SOP suggests that the dual-pipeline structure itself is the primary driver of tactile prediction success, with minor architectural variations having negligible impact.

**Impact of Visual Input on Tactile Prediction (Visual Occlusion):**
- Experiments where visual input was occluded showed that multi-modal systems failed to predict spikes in tactile sensation, resulting in flat tactile data. This indicates that multi-modal models heavily rely on visual features for accurate tactile predictions, demonstrating the bi-directional benefits of cross-modal integration.

**Discussion and Limitations:**
- Performance gains on the household cluster dataset were modest, consistent with the hypothesis that tactile–visual integration benefits are most evident under physical ambiguity.
- The results suggest that separating heterogeneous sensory modalities into modality-specific pipelines with limited cross-modal interaction provides a more effective bias-variance trade-off than forcing a shared latent representation.
- While SPOTS achieved strong quantitative scores, SVTG occasionally showed slightly better qualitative localization, indicating a trade-off between visual fidelity and physical interaction understanding depending on the downstream task.
- Limitations include the modest performance gains in visually unambiguous settings and the relatively small dataset size for household clusters, which could benefit from scaling up.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the development of robotic world models by demonstrating the advantages of a multi-modal approach that integrates both visual and tactile information. The core significance lies in empirically showing that simultaneous prediction of tactile and visual signals enhances prediction accuracy and robustness, particularly in physically ambiguous interaction scenarios where visual information alone is insufficient.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Validated Multi-Modal World Modeling**: The study provides evidence that tactile input can improve video prediction accuracy, and conversely, visual input can enhance tactile prediction accuracy during physical robot interactions. This bidirectional benefit is a key finding for developing more comprehensive robot perception systems.
- **Effectiveness in Ambiguous Scenarios**: The work highlights the critical role of tactile–visual integration in physically ambiguous interaction regimes (e.g., varying friction properties of visually identical objects). In these contexts, the proposed multi-modal architectures substantially outperform vision-only baselines by enabling the robot to infer latent physical properties.
- **Dual-Pipeline Architecture (SPOTS) Validation**: The bio-inspired dual-pipeline SPOTS architecture, which maintains modality-specific prediction pipelines with cross-modal interactions, proved to be an effective design. It achieved improved performance over strong uni-modal baselines (SVG for vision, ACTP for tactile) while preserving the fidelity specific to each modality. This architectural insight suggests a general design principle for handling heterogeneous sensory data in multi-modal prediction.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization**: The multi-modal approach demonstrated improved robustness, allowing one modality to partially compensate for missing or degraded information in the other (as shown by tactile predictions relying on visual input). Furthermore, SPOTS models showed better generalization to unseen object clusters, indicating broader applicability beyond specifically ambiguous cases.
- **Unsupervised Learning Potential**: The use of novel, unsupervisedly collected tactile-visual datasets for robot pushing aligns with the broader goal of scalable and cost-effective model development, reducing the need for extensive human annotation.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy**: By providing robots with a more robust and accurate understanding of physical cause-and-effect relationships, this work can lead to more autonomous and reliable robotic systems capable of operating in complex, unstructured real-world environments.
- **Improved Manipulation Capabilities**: The findings are directly applicable to a wide range of physical interaction tasks beyond object pushing, including precision grasping, in-hand manipulation, soft tissue manipulation, and tactile exploration, where subtle physical cues are paramount.
- **Advanced Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)**: A better understanding of physical interactions can contribute to safer and more intuitive human-robot collaboration, as robots become more adept at predicting and responding to physical contact.
- **Foundation for Model Predictive Control (MPC)**: The developed multi-modal predictive models can serve as forward models within MPC frameworks, enabling robots to anticipate future sensory states and select optimal actions in real-time. This could lead to proactive control strategies, such as proactive slip control.
- **Future Multi-Modal Robotics Research**: The proposed architecture and findings offer a strong foundation for future research. This includes exploring additional sensory modalities (e.g., audition, temperature, vibration, texture), scaling to larger datasets with transformer-based models, and integrating tactile reasoning modules (like slip detection) directly into world models.
- **Bridging Perception-Action Gap**: Ultimately, integrating multi-modal world models directly into policy learning or model-based control loops represents a crucial step towards closing the perception-action loop in physical robot interaction, enabling more intelligent and adaptive robotic behavior.
