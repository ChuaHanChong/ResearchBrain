# MAPL: Multi-Objective Preference Learning for Robot Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> MAPL introduces a framework for learning robot locomotion rewards directly from high-level natural language objectives, addressing the complexity of manual reward engineering. This approach decomposes complex tasks into distinct objectives, leveraging LLMs to generate per-objective preference rankings, which are then linearly combined to guide policy training. The framework achieved locomotion policies with performance comparable to expert-designed rewards across various terrains, demonstrating enhanced stability and velocity tracking.

## Problem

- Designing effective reward functions for complex robot locomotion tasks using Reinforcement Learning (RL) is challenging, often requiring meticulous manual engineering to balance numerous competing objectives.
- Imprecise reward specifications can lead to suboptimal policies or unintended behaviors like "reward hacking."
- Existing LLM-based preference learning methods typically solicit a single, overarching judgment between behaviors, which is problematic for multi-objective tasks and can lead to less reliable preferences and noisy supervision signals.

## Method

- **MAPL** decomposes the complex locomotion task into semantically grounded, independent objectives: velocity tracking, stability, and motion smoothness, defined exclusively in natural language.
- It queries a Large Language Model (LLM) to elicit separate ==preference rankings== for pairs of downsampled trajectories for each distinct objective, mitigating implicit trade-offs.
- A transformer-based preference scoring model is trained using these multi-objective LLM-generated rankings, with shared parameters and a distinct output head for each objective, whose scores are then linearly combined to form the reward signal for policy training.

## Results

- MAPL consistently achieved higher final tracking rewards and convergence rates across all four tested terrains (flat plane, wave, stairs, obstacle) compared to LAPP and LLM Reward baselines, with policies performing comparably to expert-designed rewards.
- Ablation studies confirmed that incorporating all three objectives (velocity, stability, smoothness) was necessary for optimal locomotion, achieving nearly twice the evaluation reward of the best two-term variant with reduced variance.
- Policies trained with MAPL rewards showed tighter clustering around stable upright positions and consistently achieved the lowest velocity tracking error across all terrains and commanded velocities, demonstrating improved stability and generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex robot locomotion tasks into separate, semantically meaningful objectives (e.g., velocity, stability, smoothness) for LLM-based preference learning yields more accurate and reliable supervision signals than single, monolithic judgments.
> - The integration of a potential difference formulation in the reward signal significantly boosts training speed and final returns for policies learned from LLM-generated preferences, improving sample efficiency and stability.
> - A multi-objective preference learning approach can produce robot locomotion policies with stability and performance comparable to, or sometimes exceeding, those trained with manually engineered expert rewards, effectively reducing the reliance on domain expertise.
