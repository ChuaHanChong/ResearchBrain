# Fathom-DeepResearch: Unlocking Long Horizon Information Retrieval and Synthesis for SLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Fractal AI Research developed FATHOM-DEEPRESEARCH, an agentic system with two specialized 4-billion-parameter models, to enhance open-source Small Language Models (SLMs) in multi-turn information retrieval and synthesis tasks. The system achieves state-of-the-art performance in its category, outperforming some proprietary models on deep research benchmarks and improving tool-use efficiency and stability.

## Problem

- Open-source Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit a substantial performance gap compared to proprietary systems in complex, multi-turn agentic information-seeking tasks.
- Existing open-source frameworks suffer from unstable multi-turn RL training (reward hacking, inefficient tool calling) and lack high-quality, verifiable, scalable datasets for multi-turn tool-augmented reasoning, particularly for very recent information.
- Current approaches primarily optimize for closed-ended queries, neglecting the sophisticated synthesis capabilities required for open-ended, exploratory investigations.

## Method

- The FATHOM-DEEPRESEARCH system consists of two specialized Qwen3-4B models: ==Fathom-Search==-4B for search-enabled reasoning and Fathom-Synthesizer-4B for information synthesis and report generation.
- Fathom-Search-4B is trained on DUETQA, a multi-agent self-play generated dataset for live web-search dependent QA, using Reward-Aware Policy Optimization (RAPO) to stabilize multi-turn RL and a steerable step-level reward function to mitigate reward hacking and promote efficient, diverse tool usage.
- Fathom-Synthesizer-4B is fine-tuned on the DEEPRESEARCH-SFT dataset, generated via a "Plan-then-Write" protocol from GPT-5, to produce structured, citation-dense reports from multi-hop traces, with context extended to 65,536 tokens.

## Results

- Fathom-Search-**4B** (Stage-2) achieves **52.1%** avg accuracy across DeepSearch benchmarks (surpassing GPT-4o w/search's **46.5%**), and Fathom-DeepResearch scores **45.47%** on DeepResearch-Bench, outperforming proprietary systems like Claude-3.7-Sonnet w/Search (**40.67%**) and Perplexity-DeepResearch (**42.25%**).
- RAPO significantly improves training stability and efficiency, leading to higher accuracy (e.g., **39.0%** on WebWalker vs. GRPO's **33.8%**) and shorter average reasoning traces (5,000 tokens vs. 9,000 for GRPO).
- The steerable step-level reward function dramatically enhances performance (e.g., **64.8%** vs. **58.2%** on FRAMES) and enables controlled growth in response length, fostering deeper exploration without redundant tool calls, extending reliable tool-calling beyond 20 steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RAPO stabilizes multi-turn RL for tool-augmented LLMs via example pruning, advantage scaling, and replay buffers, while a fine-grained step-level reward function guides efficient exploration and verification, jointly preventing collapse, tool spamming, and reward hacking.
> - Multi-agent self-play can generate high-quality, search-dependent datasets (DUETQA) that force models to utilize external tools for answers, addressing the scarcity of suitable training data.
> - A "Plan-then-Write" synthesis protocol, combined with context extension, enables SLMs to generate comprehensive, structured, and citation-dense reports from complex investigative traces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Singh et al. is positioned at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of **agentic AI** and **tool-integrated reasoning** for Large Language Models. Recent advancements have significantly enhanced LLM capabilities in diverse tasks like mathematical reasoning and code generation, ushering in a "paradigm shift towards agentic intelligence." This shift enables models to autonomously observe, reason, and interact with complex, dynamic environments, primarily through the integration of external tools.

**DeepResearch Agents**, a prominent sub-field, leverage tool-use to excel in sophisticated, long-horizon information retrieval and synthesis tasks, often exhibiting super-human performance. These agents transcend the limitations of static parametric knowledge by dynamically partitioning queries, coordinating multiple tool interactions (e.g., web search, specialized APIs), and integrating heterogeneous information sources to formulate evidence-supported conclusions.

However, a critical challenge exists: a **substantial performance gap** between proprietary implementations (e.g., OpenAI's DeepResearch, Gemini Deep Research) and open-source alternatives. Existing open-source frameworks suffer from several fundamental limitations that Fathom-DeepResearch aims to address:
- **Lack of sustained tool proficiency:** Open-source models struggle with the prolonged and nuanced tool usage required for high-uncertainty reasoning.
- **Training pipeline constraints:** This includes the absence of high-quality, verifiable, and scalable datasets for multi-turn tool-augmented reasoning, algorithmic instability in reinforcement learning (RL) with tools, and inefficient tool-calling behaviors.
- **Overemphasis on closed-form problem solving:** Many existing approaches prioritize definitive answers, neglecting the crucial information synthesis capabilities needed for open-ended investigative queries.

Fathom-DeepResearch directly tackles these deficiencies, aiming to develop robust DeepResearch architectures within the open-source domain. By introducing innovative methodologies for data generation, reinforcement learning optimization, and sophisticated synthesis, this work endeavors to bring open-source SLMs closer to the advanced, agentic capabilities currently dominated by proprietary systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Fathom-DeepResearch is to **unlock long-horizon information retrieval and synthesis for open-source Small Language Models (SLMs)**. To achieve this, the authors introduce an end-to-end agentic system composed of two specialized 4-billion-parameter models: Fathom-Search-4B for search-enabled reasoning and Fathom-Synthesizer-4B for information synthesis and report generation.

The motivation for this work stems from four critical limitations observed in the current landscape of open-source tool-augmented LLMs:

1. **Training Instability of GRPO in Multi-Turn Tool Interaction:** While Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) shows promise for single-turn tasks, it falters in multi-turn, tool-augmented environments. External tool responses induce distribution shifts and decoding instability, causing group-relative advantages to saturate and leading to unstable gradient updates and training collapse.
2. **Reward Hacking and Inefficient Tool Calling:** Traditional sparse, correctness-only rewards for RL training lead to "reward hacking." Agents learn to increase tool usage indiscriminately (tool spamming) without improving accuracy or efficiency, often collapsing into repetitive, identical calls. Furthermore, standard RL offers limited control over the cognitive behaviors (e.g., exploration vs. verification) developed by the policy, amplifying pre-existing biases from supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
3. **Limited Training Data Characterized by High and Hard-to-Reduce Intrinsic Information Uncertainty:** Existing QA datasets often allow models to bypass genuine search or retrieve information from easily discoverable sources (e.g., Wikipedia). They fail to expose models to the real-world challenges of noisy, heterogeneous web data, especially for information published very recently. Current synthetic data generation pipelines are often expensive, brittle, and lack scalability or topical diversity.
4. **Challenges in Handling Open-Ended Queries:** Most research optimizes models for closed-ended queries with well-defined answers. However, real-world applications frequently involve open-ended, exploratory queries that demand extensive multi-turn exploration to uncover diverse perspectives and require sophisticated synthesis of comprehensive, evidence-grounded responses. This synthesis capability is a significant unmet need.

By addressing these motivations, Fathom-DeepResearch aims to create a more robust, steerable, and performant open-source solution for deep information seeking and synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Fathom-DeepResearch system is bifurcated into two specialized models, Fathom-Search-4B and Fathom-Synthesizer-4B, both built upon the Qwen3-4B base model.

#### 3.1. Fathom-Search-4B: Search-Enabled Reasoning

Fathom-Search-4B is optimized for evidence-based investigation through live web search and targeted webpage querying, enabling long-horizon tool use (beyond 20 calls). Its capabilities stem from three core advances and a two-stage training regimen:

1. **DUETQA: A DeepSearch Dataset, Generated via Multi-Agent Self-Play:**
 - **Goal:** Create a verifiable, search-dependent, multi-hop QA dataset that models cannot answer from parametric knowledge alone.
 - **Generation:** Utilizes two frontier web search-enabled LLMs (M1, M2 from OpenAI, 2025a) as proxy web-crawling agents, and a non-search LLM (M3, GPT-4o) for obfuscation and baseline verification.
 - **Key Design Goals:**
 - **Live Web-Search Dependency:** Questions are crafted such that at least one hop contains information post-2024-01-01, making them unanswerable without current web search.
 - **Diverse Source Domains:** Requires querying heterogeneous web sources beyond common encyclopedic sites.
 - **Steerable Theme Control:** Questions are grounded in 5-7 sampled themes from a manually curated taxonomy of over 200 topics.
 - **Data Obfuscation:** M3 paraphrases questions to mask intermediate hops, converting specific dates to intervals, precise numerics to qualitative magnitudes, and named entities to indirect descriptors, preventing shortcut reasoning.
 - **Multi-Agent Verification:** A QA pair is retained only if M1 and M2 (search-enabled) produce the same correct answer, while M3 (non-search) fails, guaranteeing search indispensability and non-triviality.

2. **RAPO (Reward-Aware Policy Optimization):**
 - **Problem:** GRPO's instability in multi-turn RL due to vanishing group-relative advantages when reward variance is low (e.g., all rollouts succeed or fail).
 - **Solution:** RAPO is a zero-overhead extension of GRPO to stabilize training:
 - **Dataset Pruning:** Prompts effectively solved (SolveRate ≥ 0.9) are pruned at the end of each epoch, maintaining a curriculum focused on harder examples and preventing batches from being dominated by saturated groups.
 - **Advantage Scaling:** Token-level advantages of "Good groups" (those with σR > 0) are rescaled inversely with their batch frequency, preserving gradient magnitude even when informative groups are sparse.
 - **Replay Buffer:** A per-prompt buffer stores recent successful trajectories. If all rollouts for a prompt fail in the current epoch, one trajectory is randomly replaced with a successful one from the buffer, reintroducing variance and anchoring updates to high-quality references.

3. **Steerable Step-Level Reward Design for Search Tools:**
 - **Problem:** Vanilla sparse rewards lead to reward hacking and inefficient tool usage.
 - **Solution:** A fine-grained, steerable reward function that classifies each tool call by its cognitive behavior and marginal utility using a GPT-4.1 LLM-as-judge:
 - `search_urls` calls are classified as `UNIQUESEARCH` or `REDUNDANTSEARCH`.
 - `query_url` calls are classified as `EXPLORATION`, `VERIFICATION`, or `REDUNDANTQUERY`.
 - The reward penalizes redundant calls (ρ) for correct rollouts, promoting efficiency. For incorrect rollouts, it credits genuine, non-redundant exploration (`∆S`) and information seeking (`∆Q`), even if the final answer is wrong.
 - **Steerability:** Exposes knobs (CS, CQ, Bv) to control the saturation thresholds for novel searches, novel queries, and the verification depth per claim, allowing explicit steering of exploration and verification behaviors.

4. **Training Recipe:**
 - **Stage 1:** 10 epochs on DUETQA with RAPO and a vanilla trajectory-level reward (Eq. 4) to instill correct tool-calling and strict format adherence.
 - **Stage 2:** 2 additional epochs with RAPO on a mixed dataset (DUETQA, filtered math data from S1, filtered MUSIQUE) using the Steerable Step-Level Reward (Eq. 8) to extend the tool-use horizon beyond 20 calls and enable steerable cognitive behaviors.
 - Uses Serper API for web search and a retrieval toolchain with Jina-AI, TRAFILTURA, and CRAWL4AI. Base model: Qwen3-4B, full 40,960-token context.

#### 3.2. Fathom-Synthesizer-4B: Information Synthesis

Fathom-Synthesizer-4B is a planning and synthesis model, also built on Qwen3-4B, designed to convert multi-hop DeepSearch traces into structured, citation-dense DeepResearch Reports.

1. **DEEPRESEARCH-SFT Dataset:**
 - **Goal:** Train the synthesizer via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using a "Plan-then-Write" protocol.
 - **Generation:** Synthetic dataset distilled from GPT-5, providing supervision along three axes:
 - **Question Decomposition:** Decomposes the input question into ordered sub-questions, forming the report scaffold.
 - **Section Mapping:** Maps every piece of retrieved evidence (URLs, passages) to one or more report sections, ensuring citation accuracy and preventing omissions.
 - **Planning for Insights:** Specifies an analysis strategy for synthesizing gathered evidence into higher-level insights.
 - The training target includes a private `<think>` block containing the plan (decomposition, mapping, insights), followed by the public report.
 - **Report Structure:** Reports follow a fixed format: Executive Summary, Main Body (organized by decomposed sections), and a deduplicated "Sources used" list. Sections are citation-dense, with inline citations strictly from URLs explored by Fathom-Search-4B.
 - **Scale:** 2,500 training questions generated via the Seeded Question mode from 100 open-ended real-world questions spanning diverse themes.

2. **Training Recipe:**
 - Fine-tuning Qwen3-4B for 5 epochs on DEEPRESEARCH-SFT.
 - **Context Extension:** Uses YaRN RoPE scaling to extend the effective context from 40,960 to 65,536 tokens, allowing the synthesizer to ingest the full investigation trace for high-quality, section-aligned synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Fathom-DeepResearch demonstrates significant advancements in open-source agentic capabilities, rivaling and often surpassing strong closed-source and open-source baselines across various benchmarks.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Open-Weights Category:**
 - **DeepSearch Benchmarks (SimpleQA, FRAMES, WebWalker, Seal0, MuSiQue - Table 1):** Fathom-Search-4B (Stage-2) achieves an average accuracy of 52.1%, establishing itself as the leading open-source model. It significantly outperforms all other open-source contenders, such as II-Search-4B (47.3%) and WebSailor-3B (44.0%). Notably, it surpasses GPT-4o with search (46.5%) and demonstrates substantial gains on challenging tasks like FRAMES, WebWalker, and Seal0.
 - **General Reasoning Benchmarks (HLE, AIME-25, GPQA-Diamond, MedQA - Table 1):** The model achieves an average accuracy of 53.8%, outperforming all open-source models in this category and even surpassing GPT-4o (without search: 53.6%, with search: 54.1%), demonstrating strong generalization to diverse reasoning tasks.
 - **DeepResearch-Bench (Open-ended citation-driven report generation - Table 2):** Fathom-DeepResearch scores 45.47% overall, outperforming several proprietary closed-source systems, including Claude-3.7-Sonnet w/Search (40.67%), Grok Deeper Search (40.24%), and Perplexity-DeepResearch (42.25%). This underscores its competitiveness in complex, end-to-end deep research tasks, including comprehensiveness, depth, instruction-following, readability (RACE), factuality, and citation accuracy (FACT).

2. **Efficacy of RAPO for Policy Optimization:**
 - **Ablation Study (Table 3):** RAPO consistently outperforms GRPO during Stage-1 training across DeepSearch benchmarks. For instance, on WebWalker, RAPO achieves 39.0% accuracy compared to GRPO's 33.8%. Critically, RAPO leads to more efficient reasoning traces with significantly fewer average tokens (5,000 vs. 9,000 for GRPO), indicating its success in mitigating tool-spamming behavior and stabilizing gradient updates (Figure 8, bottom).

3. **Benefits of Steerable Step-Level Reward:**
 - **Ablation Study (Table 4):** The steerable step-level reward, used in Stage-2 training, dramatically improves performance compared to the vanilla trajectory-level reward. It achieves superior accuracy across all DeepSearch tasks (e.g., 64.8% vs. 58.2% on FRAMES, 50% vs. 43.2% on WebWalker). This reward system also enables a controlled growth in response length (14,500 avg. tokens vs. 5,500), fostering deeper information exploration and verification without inflating reasoning traces with redundant tool calls (Figure 6, Figure 8 top). This validates its ability to mitigate reward hacking and steer cognitive behaviors effectively.

4. **Efficient Long-Horizon Tool Interaction:**
 - The system demonstrates higher accuracy and efficient long-horizon tool interaction compared to other open-source DeepSearch models, as illustrated by the accuracy vs. average tool calls plot (Figure 7). This highlights the ability of Fathom-DeepResearch to reliably extend tool-calling beyond 20 calls when warranted, a significant leap for open-source agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Fathom-DeepResearch represents a pivotal step forward for open-source Large Language Models, significantly narrowing the performance gap with proprietary systems in complex agentic tasks. Its contributions have several profound implications:

- **Democratizing Advanced Agentic AI:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance in the open-weights category, Fathom-DeepResearch makes highly sophisticated deep research capabilities accessible to a broader research community and wider range of applications. This fosters innovation and reduces reliance on opaque, costly proprietary models.
- **Enabling Robust, Long-Horizon Information Seeking:** The system's ability to reliably perform tool-calling beyond 20 steps and synthesize comprehensive, citation-dense reports is crucial for tackling real-world, open-ended investigative queries. This unlocks new possibilities for AI applications in domains requiring extensive research, such as law, business intelligence, scientific discovery, and policy analysis.
- **Advancing Reinforcement Learning for Tool-Augmented LLMs:** The methodological innovations – DUETQA, RAPO, and the steerable step-level reward – address fundamental challenges in training LLMs for multi-turn tool interaction. DUETQA provides a scalable pipeline for high-quality, search-dependent data; RAPO offers a stable and efficient optimization algorithm for multi-turn RL; and the steerable reward provides unprecedented control over agent cognitive behaviors, mitigating common issues like reward hacking and inefficient tool use. These contributions are foundational for developing more controllable, interpretable, and efficient AI agents.
- **Foundation for Future Agentic Systems:** The structured "Plan-then-Write" protocol for synthesis, enabled by DEEPRESEARCH-SFT, paves the way for AI agents that can not only retrieve information but also critically analyze, synthesize, and present it in a human-understandable, evidence-backed format. This is a critical step towards more autonomous and reliable AI assistants.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The authors acknowledge certain limitations. While RAPO effectively stabilizes multi-turn RL, its reliance on trajectory replacement in the replay buffer can anchor learning to low-entropy traces, limiting test-time scaling when the steerable step-level reward is absent and question difficulty increases. This indicates that while it prevents collapse, it might hinder adaptation to even more extended reasoning horizons under vanilla reward schemes. Furthermore, the current system depends on synchronous training pipelines, which, despite their simplicity, can be inefficient and brittle at scale. Future work will likely involve transitioning to asynchronous frameworks to improve efficiency and robustness, further pushing the boundaries of agentic AI.
