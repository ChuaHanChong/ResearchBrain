# Chain-of-Model Learning for Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new learning paradigm called Chain-of-Model (CoM) enables large language models to scale incrementally and adapt elastically during inference. Implemented as CoLM and CoLM-Air, this approach integrates multiple sub-models within a single structure, demonstrating comparable performance to traditional models while significantly accelerating prefilling for long contexts.

## Problem

- Current LLM scaling policies require training new models from scratch, leading to high computational costs and inefficient knowledge acquisition.
- Existing LLM architectures operate at a fixed capacity, lacking dynamic adaptability for flexible deployment across diverse computational environments.
- The high computational cost of prefilling (generating key-value caches for long prompts) and the inability to seamlessly switch between different LLM scales pose practical deployment challenges.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Chain-of-Model (CoM)== paradigm, built upon ==Chain-of-Representation (CoR)== and ==Chain-of-Layer (CoL)==, to integrate multiple sub-models of varying capacities within a unified structure.
- Instantiates CoM into ==Chain-of-Language-Model (CoLM)== for Transformer architectures, reformulating linear layers, attention, and feed-forward networks with causal dependencies across distinct 'chains' of representation.
- Develops ==CoLM-Air==, a specialized variant that implements a key-value (KV) sharing mechanism to accelerate prefilling and facilitate seamless switching between different LLM scales and capabilities.

## Results

- CoLM models achieve competitive average accuracy on commonsense reasoning tasks, performing comparably to or slightly better than standard Transformer baselines of similar parameter counts.
- Chain expansion allows for incremental model growth, with experiments showing that adding a new chain to pre-trained models can lead to performance improvements (e.g., TinyLLaMA-v1.1 gained **0.92%** average accuracy).
- CoLM-Air demonstrates up to **3.0x** faster prefilling for 1M tokens (and an impressive **27x** faster when combined with MInference) compared to standard LLaMa models, highlighting a major efficiency gain for long-context processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating hidden states as causally dependent sub-representations (CoR) allows for inherent multi-scale encoding, enabling incremental model scaling and elastic inference within a single architecture.
> - The Chain-of-Layer (CoL) property, when applied to Transformer components, ensures that information flows progressively across chains, allowing a unified model to contain multiple distinct sub-models.
> - Sharing key and value caches across different chains in CoLM-Air significantly reduces computational overhead for prefilling long sequences and supports dynamic, runtime adaptation of model scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on "Chain-of-Model Learning" directly addresses some of the most pressing challenges in the current landscape of large language models (LLMs). The field has witnessed an exponential growth in LLM parameter sizes, leading to state-of-the-art performance but also imposing immense computational burdens for training and inflexibility in deployment. This paper positions itself as a novel paradigm to tackle these issues.

**Scaling Laws and Efficiency:** The advent of LLMs and the recognition of "scaling laws" (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) have firmly established that increasing model size, data, and compute generally leads to better performance. However, existing scaling strategies often require training models from scratch for each new scale or capacity, which is highly inefficient. The paper critiques this "train from scratch" approach and the fixed capacity of traditional dense Transformers or even Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models (Jacobs et al., 1991; Shazeer et al., 2017) that activate a fixed scale of parameters. CoM aims to provide a more efficient, incremental scaling mechanism.

**Elastic Inference and Continual Learning:** The work is motivated by "elastic inference" (Cai et al., 2020; Devvrit et al., 2023) and "continual training" (Rusu et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2024). Elastic inference seeks to offer dynamic inference capabilities, where a model can adapt its size or computational load to different deployment environments. Continual training, on the other hand, focuses on incrementally acquiring new knowledge without forgetting previously learned information, similar to human intelligence. CoM attempts to integrate these two concepts into a unified architecture. Unlike traditional methods that might involve sampling sub-networks or training separate models, CoM inherently builds multiple sub-models within a single structure.

**Distinction from MoE:** The paper explicitly distinguishes CoM from Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, a popular method for sparsely activating parameters in large models (Mistral AI Team, 2024; Lepikhin et al., 2020). While MoE focuses on having multiple experts with *equivalent* capabilities and selectively activating a few for each token, CoM aims to create a sequence of *nested experts* (chains) where each subsequent expert builds upon and integrates the capabilities of its predecessors, progressing from "weak to strong." This makes CoM orthogonal to MoE, suggesting potential for hybrid architectures in the future.

**Memory and Computational Optimization:** Practical aspects of LLM deployment, such as prefilling acceleration (Agrawal et al., 2023) and efficient fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2022), are critical. The proposed KV sharing mechanism in CoLM-Air directly addresses the prefilling bottleneck by allowing keys and values to be computed once and shared, reducing computational costs for long contexts. This places CoLM-Air alongside other efforts in optimizing KV cache management (Jiang et al., 2024; Brandon et al., 2024).

In essence, this research represents a significant step towards developing "next-generation foundation models" that are not only powerful but also more efficient to train, flexibly deployable, and capable of incremental knowledge acquisition, addressing the sustainability and practicality concerns arising from the current LLM scaling trajectory.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the Chain-of-Model (CoM) paradigm stems from the increasing computational and deployment challenges posed by the explosive growth of Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors identify two primary inherent issues with existing LLM scaling approaches:

1. **Inefficiency in Knowledge Acquisition:** Current LLM scaling policies, unlike human intelligence which acquires new knowledge incrementally, typically require training a completely new model from scratch when scaling up. This process is computationally expensive, time-consuming, and leads to significant inefficiencies as it cannot leverage or retain knowledge from previously trained, smaller scales. This results in redundant computation and a lack of continuous learning.
2. **Lack of Dynamic Adaptability in Inference:** Existing LLM architectures (e.g., dense Transformers or Mixture-of-Experts models) activate a fixed set of parameters during inference. This inflexibility means they cannot dynamically adapt their problem-solving capabilities or computational footprint to varying deployment environments or specific task requirements. Deploying LLMs often necessitates either using a full-sized, expensive model or training and deploying multiple separate models of different sizes, which is also resource-intensive and complex to manage.

Motivated by the concepts of **elastic inference** and **continual training**, the authors propose CoM to address these issues. Their key objectives are to:

- **Introduce Scaling Efficiency:** Develop a learning paradigm that allows for incrementally scaling up model size by adding new "chains" (sub-representations) based on previous models, thereby avoiding the need to train from scratch and retaining existing knowledge. This aims to make the training process more efficient and akin to how intelligence accumulates.
- **Enhance Inference Flexibility:** Provide a unified model that can offer multiple sub-models at varying sizes. This "elastic inference" capability would allow dynamic adaptation to different computational budgets, latency requirements, or performance needs in diverse deployment scenarios, without the overhead of maintaining separate models.
- **Incorporate Causal Relationships:** Design the model architecture such that hidden states at each layer explicitly incorporate a causal relationship between sub-representations. This ensures that information flows progressively, allowing smaller scales to be nested within larger ones.
- **Accelerate Prefilling and Enable Seamless LM Switching:** Address specific practical challenges in LLM deployment, such as the high computational cost of prefilling (generating keys and values for long prompts) and the inability to seamlessly switch between different LLMs or different scales of the same LLM without re-computation. The CoLM-Air variant aims to tackle this through a KV sharing mechanism.
- **Mitigate Catastrophic Forgetting:** Ensure that when the model is progressively scaled or fine-tuned, it retains the knowledge of its earlier, smaller scales, preventing performance degradation on tasks associated with those scales.

In summary, the paper aims to create a more modular, efficient, and adaptable LLM architecture that can grow incrementally and operate elastically, bridging the gap between massive computational demands and flexible deployment needs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper's methodology revolves around introducing a novel learning paradigm called **Chain-of-Model (CoM)**, built upon the concept of **Chain-of-Representation (CoR)**. This paradigm is then specifically applied to the Transformer architecture to create **Chain-of-Language-Model (CoLM)** and its variant, **CoLM-Air**.

#### 3.1. Chain-of-Representation (CoR)

The authors define CoR as formulating any hidden state `x ∈ R^D` as a concatenation of `n` sub-representations, `ξ(x, n) = {x_1..., x_n}`. Each `x_i` is a "chain" and has its own dimension `d_i`. Activating the first `i` chains (`x_≤i`) allows the representation to encode information at "scale i" with a dimension `sum(d_≤i)`. When `n=1`, CoR is equivalent to a standard representation. This concept enables the encoding of multiple scales within a single representation.

#### 3.2. Chain-of-Layer (CoL)

To process CoR features while maintaining the multi-scale property, the authors introduce CoL. A layer `y = f_θ(x)` is a CoL if its input `x` and output `y` are both CoR, and each output chain `y_i` is conditioned *only* on the input chains `x_≤i`. This imposes a causal dependency. CoL possesses three key properties:
- **Generality:** Any standard layer is a CoL when `n=1`. This means CoL generalizes existing architectures.
- **Causality:** To compute `y_i`, only parameters `θ_≤i` and input `x_≤i` are needed. This enables dynamic computation at varying scales.
- **Compositionality:** Stacking multiple CoL layers preserves the CoL property, allowing for generalization from layer-level to model-level.

#### 3.3. Chain-of-Model (CoM)

A model is defined as a CoM if all its layers conform to the CoL property. This allows CoM to integrate multiple sub-models at different scales within one unified model. It enables progressive scaling by adding chains while retaining previous capabilities.

#### 3.4. Chain-of-Language-Model (CoLM) Architecture

The CoM framework is instantiated within the Transformer architecture to create CoLM. This involves reformulating each Transformer component:

- **Chain-of-Linear Layer:** The fundamental linear transformation `y = Wx + b` is modified. Instead of one large weight matrix `W`, it's replaced by `n` sub-linear layers. Each `y_i` is computed as `W_i * x_≤i + b_i`. The dimensions `D_x_i` and `D_y_i` for each chain are determined by a hyper-parameter `C = {c_1..., c_n}`. An efficient block-wise sparse kernel is developed to optimize the computation of this layer, particularly in distributed training, by reducing all-reduce operations.
- **Chain-of-Attention (MHA/GQA):** All Linear layers (Q, K, V, O) within the Multi-head Attention (MHA) module are replaced with Chain-of-Linear layers. To maintain the CoL causality, the design ensures that each attention head only contains single-scale information. This is achieved by setting `sum(C)` equal to the number of heads (`h`), so `c_i` represents the number of heads allocated to chain `i`.
- **Chain-of-Feed-Forward Network (FFN):** Similarly, all Linear layers within the FFN are replaced by Chain-of-Linear layers, using the same chain hyper-parameter `C` as in attention.
- **Normalization (LayerNorm/RMSNorm):** Normalization is applied independently to each chain, ensuring the normalized features preserve CoR properties and potentially improving training efficiency.
- **Embedding:** The embedding layer remains standard during training. For inference at scale `i`, only the dimensions corresponding to the first `i` chains are activated.

#### 3.5. CoLM-Air with KV Sharing

A specialized variant, CoLM-Air, is introduced to enhance flexibility, particularly for prefilling. In CoLM-Air, a **KV sharing mechanism** is implemented: all keys and values are computed *only within the first chain* and then shared (and repeated, if necessary, to match query heads, similar to Grouped Query Attention (GQA)) across all other chains. This design, while potentially slightly impacting performance, provides significant benefits like prefilling acceleration and seamless LLM switching.

#### 3.6. Objective Function

For training, a multi-chain cross-entropy loss is proposed. Instead of a single classification head, individual classification heads `W_i` are built for each scale `i`, and the loss `Loss_i = L(W_i * x_≤i)` is computed. During pre-training, a standard cross-entropy loss is used for faster convergence, and the multi-chain loss is applied during fine-tuning.

#### 3.7. Training and Evaluation

- Pre-training uses SlimPajama dataset (600B tokens) with LLaMA-2 tokenizer, FSDP2, BFloat16, and AdamW optimizer.
- Evaluation covers commonsense reasoning tasks (EleutherAI LM Evaluation Harness) for performance, and specific experiments for chain expansion, elastic inference, prefilling speed, and chain tuning (GLUE benchmark).

This comprehensive methodology builds a foundation for multi-scale language models that are designed for both training efficiency and inference flexibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that the Chain-of-Model (CoM) family, including CoLM and CoLM-Air, successfully achieves its objectives of providing comparable performance to standard Transformers while significantly enhancing flexibility and efficiency in various scenarios.

1. **Comparable Performance:**
 - On commonsense reasoning tasks (HellaSwag, Obqa, WinoGranda, ARC-e/c, Boolq, Piqa), CoLM models with configurations like `{16, 16}` chains and appropriate dimensions achieve average accuracy competitive with or even slightly better than a standard Transformer baseline (e.g., CoLM {16, 16} Dims 2560 (1.11B Params) achieves 45.27% average, slightly above Baseline {32} Dims 2048 (1.10B Params) at 45.11%).
 - **CoLM-Air**, which incorporates the KV sharing mechanism, shows a slight performance degradation compared to CoLM without KV sharing. For example, CoLM-Air {16, 16} Dims 2560 achieves 44.80% average, which is marginally lower than CoLM's 45.27% for the same configuration. This indicates a trade-off between performance and the significant flexibility gained from KV sharing.
 - The choice of chain configuration matters; `{16, 16}` generally outperforms `{8, 8, 8, 8}` in terms of raw accuracy, suggesting that a balanced distribution or fewer, larger chains might be more effective for overall performance, though more chains offer finer granularity for elastic inference.

2. **Chain Expansion:**
 - This feature allows incrementally increasing model capacity by adding new chains to an already trained model.
 - Experiments show that expanding TinyLLaMA-v1.1 and LLaMA-3.2-1B with an additional chain (incurring ~0.8B new parameters and fine-tuning for 20K steps) leads to performance improvements. TinyLLaMA-v1.1 saw a 0.92% average improvement, and LLaMA-3.2-1B saw a 0.14% improvement.
 - This validates CoM's ability to scale models progressively without training from scratch, preserving prior knowledge, and boosting performance with limited additional computation.

3. **Elastic Inference:**
 - CoLM-Air is demonstrated to effectively offer multiple sub-models within a single framework. For a CoLM-Air {16, 16} Dims 2048 model (0.86B params), activating only "Chain 1" provides a smaller sub-model (0.33B params) with an average accuracy of 41.13%. Activating "Chain 1 + 2" uses the full model, achieving 43.90%.
 - The smaller sub-model, while expectedly lower in performance, retains competitive capabilities relative to its size, showcasing the flexibility to choose a model scale based on specific inference requirements from a single trained entity.

4. **Prefilling Acceleration:**
 - CoLM-Air significantly reduces the time required for prefilling (generating KV caches for prompts), especially for longer sequence lengths.
 - For 1M tokens, CoLM-Air yields approximately **1.6x faster prefilling** with `{16, 16}` chains and **3.0x faster** with `{8, 8, 8, 8}` chains compared to standard LLaMa models of similar parameter sizes.
 - When combined with MInference, a separate prefilling acceleration technique, CoLM-Air achieves an impressive **27x speedup** (e.g., from 1920ms to 70.0ms for LLaMa-8B, 1M tokens) for the `{8, 8, 8, 8}` configuration. This highlights a major practical advantage for long-context LLM applications.

5. **Chain Tuning:**
 - CoM's causal architecture enables "Chain Tuning," where only the latter chains are fine-tuned while freezing the initial ones.
 - On the GLUE benchmark, fine-tuning only ~42% of the model parameters (0.8B out of 1.9B in an expanded model) leads to substantial performance gains (average accuracy jumped from 48.46% to 67.79%).
 - This approach not only reduces tuning costs but also helps mitigate catastrophic forgetting and allows for seamless transfer of KV caches in CoLM-Air, further enhancing practical adaptability.

Overall, the findings strongly support the utility and effectiveness of the CoM paradigm in addressing critical challenges related to LLM scalability, efficiency, and deployment flexibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Chain-of-Model (CoM) learning paradigm presents a highly significant advancement in the field of large language models, with profound potential impacts on how these models are designed, trained, and deployed.

#### 5.1. A Paradigm Shift for LLM Scaling

CoM offers a novel conceptual framework that goes beyond simply increasing model dimensions, layers, or employing sparse activation (like MoE). By incorporating causal relationships into hidden states through Chain-of-Representation (CoR) and Chain-of-Layer (CoL), it redefines how scaling can be achieved. This paradigm shift could lead to more inherently structured and efficient deep learning models, not just for LLMs but potentially for other foundation models as well (as hinted by its generality to "any ML models").

#### 5.2. Enhanced Training Efficiency and Reduced Resource Waste

The ability to perform "Chain Expansion" means that new, larger models can be incrementally grown from existing, well-trained smaller models. This directly tackles the "train from scratch" inefficiency that plagues current LLM development. By preserving existing knowledge and only training additional parameters for new chains, CoM can significantly reduce the computational resources, energy consumption, and time required to develop more capable models. This has substantial economic and environmental implications for large-scale AI research and development.

#### 5.3. Unprecedented Inference Flexibility and Adaptability

CoM enables "elastic inference" by integrating multiple sub-models of varying capacities within a single foundation model. This allows developers to dynamically select the appropriate model size at inference time based on real-time computational constraints, latency requirements, or desired performance levels. This flexibility is crucial for deploying LLMs across diverse hardware environments, from resource-constrained edge devices to powerful cloud servers, optimizing resource utilization without compromising core capabilities.

#### 5.4. Practical Solutions for LLM Deployment Challenges

- **Prefilling Acceleration:** CoLM-Air's KV sharing mechanism provides a concrete and substantial solution to the bottleneck of prefilling long contexts, a critical issue for many real-world LLM applications. The observed speedups (up to 27x with MInference) can dramatically improve user experience and reduce inference costs.
- **Efficient Fine-tuning (Chain Tuning):** The ability to fine-tune only specific chains (e.g., latter chains) while freezing others offers a cost-effective and memory-efficient approach to adapt LLMs for downstream tasks. This not only reduces the parameters needing optimization but also helps in mitigating catastrophic forgetting, preserving the general knowledge of the foundational model.
- **Seamless LLM Switching:** CoLM-Air's design supports dynamic switching between different scales of LLMs without re-computing keys and values, which is a novel capability that simplifies complex multi-model deployment strategies.

#### 5.5. Bridging Continual Learning and LLM Scaling

The CoM framework explicitly addresses continual learning principles, allowing models to incrementally acquire and retain knowledge. This aligns more closely with biological learning and paves the way for more robust and long-lived AI systems that can continuously evolve and improve without periodic, expensive overhauls.

#### 5.6. Future Research Avenues

The paper explicitly highlights that CoM is orthogonal to other scaling architectures like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), suggesting the exciting possibility of combining these paradigms for even more powerful and efficient models. Furthermore, the discussion on applying CoM to model depth (layers) and the challenges involved opens new research directions for optimizing hierarchical learning in deep networks. Addressing the infrastructure-level challenges for Chain-of-Linear's tensor parallelism is also a critical area for future engineering impact.

In conclusion, "Chain-of-Model Learning" offers a powerful and elegant solution to several critical challenges facing large language models. By enabling efficient, incremental scaling and flexible deployment, it holds the promise of fundamentally changing how future foundation models are developed, making them more adaptable, sustainable, and broadly applicable across a multitude of AI applications.
