# Chain-of-Model Learning for Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new learning paradigm called Chain-of-Model (CoM) enables large language models to scale incrementally and adapt elastically during inference. Implemented as CoLM and CoLM-Air, this approach integrates multiple sub-models within a single structure, demonstrating comparable performance to traditional models while significantly accelerating prefilling for long contexts.

## Problem

- Current LLM scaling policies require training new models from scratch, leading to high computational costs and inefficient knowledge acquisition.
- Existing LLM architectures operate at a fixed capacity, lacking dynamic adaptability for flexible deployment across diverse computational environments.
- The high computational cost of prefilling (generating key-value caches for long prompts) and the inability to seamlessly switch between different LLM scales pose practical deployment challenges.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Chain-of-Model (CoM)== paradigm, built upon ==Chain-of-Representation (CoR)== and ==Chain-of-Layer (CoL)==, to integrate multiple sub-models of varying capacities within a unified structure.
- Instantiates CoM into ==Chain-of-Language-Model (CoLM)== for Transformer architectures, reformulating linear layers, attention, and feed-forward networks with causal dependencies across distinct 'chains' of representation.
- Develops ==CoLM-Air==, a specialized variant that implements a key-value (KV) sharing mechanism to accelerate prefilling and facilitate seamless switching between different LLM scales and capabilities.

## Results

- CoLM models achieve competitive average accuracy on commonsense reasoning tasks, performing comparably to or slightly better than standard Transformer baselines of similar parameter counts.
- Chain expansion allows for incremental model growth, with experiments showing that adding a new chain to pre-trained models can lead to performance improvements (e.g., TinyLLaMA-v1.1 gained **0.92%** average accuracy).
- CoLM-Air demonstrates up to **3.0x** faster prefilling for 1M tokens (and an impressive **27x** faster when combined with MInference) compared to standard LLaMa models, highlighting a major efficiency gain for long-context processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating hidden states as causally dependent sub-representations (CoR) allows for inherent multi-scale encoding, enabling incremental model scaling and elastic inference within a single architecture.
> - The Chain-of-Layer (CoL) property, when applied to Transformer components, ensures that information flows progressively across chains, allowing a unified model to contain multiple distinct sub-models.
> - Sharing key and value caches across different chains in CoLM-Air significantly reduces computational overhead for prefilling long sequences and supports dynamic, runtime adaptation of model scale.
