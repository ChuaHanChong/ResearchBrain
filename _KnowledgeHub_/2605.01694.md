# Latent State Design for World Models under Sufficiency Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> This work redefines world model research as the design of latent states under specific "sufficiency constraints" for an agent's intended functions. It proposes a functional taxonomy and an evaluation framework, concluding that effective world models are those whose latent states precisely match task demands, often necessitating strategic information compression rather than maximal preservation.

## Problem

- The diverse field of world modeling lacked a unified functional understanding and a clear, unambiguous categorization of existing methods.
- Current world model categorization often relied on architectural similarities or application domains, which obscured critical distinctions in the purpose and design of latent states.
- Evaluating world models solely on general metrics like reconstruction fidelity could be misleading if the latent state was designed for a different specific purpose, such as control or planning.

## Method

- Proposed a functional taxonomy comprising six distinct roles for latent states (e.g., predictive embedding, recurrent belief state) based on what information they are designed to provide.
- Developed an evaluation framework with seven axes (e.g., Representation, Prediction, Planning) to assess world models based on how well their latent states satisfy their intended sufficiency constraints.
- Formulated three propositions to formally clarify relationships between different types of sufficiency, such as the distinction between predictive and control sufficiency, and passive versus counterfactual dynamics.

## Results

- Successfully developed and presented a functional taxonomy of six distinct latent state roles, offering a more informative classification of world models than traditional architectural groupings.
- Established three propositions formalizing critical distinctions, revealing that, for instance, a latent state can support accurate prediction without providing control-relevant information.
- Created a multi-axis evaluation framework and a "Preserve/Discard/Enable" matrix that clarified design trade-offs and identified current research gaps, particularly in causal/counterfactual reasoning and robust uncertainty handling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An actionable world model's effectiveness is determined by its latent state construction precisely matching the task's demands, not by preserving the most information; strategic information compression is critical.
> - Predictive sufficiency (accurate future observation prediction) does not inherently imply control sufficiency (providing useful distinctions for reward or action choice).
> - Passive predictive sufficiency cannot identify counterfactual dynamics without additional assumptions or interventions, highlighting the need for intervention-sensitive mechanisms for causal reasoning.
