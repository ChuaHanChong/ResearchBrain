# Temporal Representation Alignment: Successor Features Enable Emergent Compositionality in Robot Instruction Following

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Temporal Representation Alignment (TRA), a method for robot instruction following that enables emergent compositional generalization. TRA uses an auxiliary temporal alignment loss to structure state, goal, and language representations, allowing robots to perform novel, multi-step tasks without explicit planning and achieving substantial performance gains on out-of-distribution compositional tasks in both real-world and simulated environments.

## Problem

- Existing robot learning methods for compositional generalization in long-horizon tasks often face issues of instability, data inefficiency, or struggle with out-of-distribution state-goal pairs.
- Developing robotic policies that can effectively combine previously learned behaviors to solve novel, complex tasks using both visual goals and natural language instructions.
- Many current approaches for compositional behavior rely on explicit subtask planning or complex reinforcement learning frameworks, which can be challenging for real-world deployment.

## Method

- Develops ==Temporal Representation Alignment== (**TRA**), which augments goal-conditioned behavioral cloning with two contrastive representation alignment objectives.
- Introduces a symmetric InfoNCE loss that aligns current state representations with future state representations, implicitly encoding successor features to enable 'stitching' of short-horizon behaviors.
- Integrates a CLIP-style contrastive InfoNCE loss to align future state/goal representations with corresponding natural language instructions, grounding language commands in the structured representation space.

## Results

- TRA demonstrated substantial improvements in compositional generalization on out-of-distribution real-world robot manipulation tasks, achieving over **40%** higher success rates in certain categories compared to baselines.
- The method significantly outperformed explicit offline RL approaches like AWR, surpassing it by **45%** on instruction following and **25%** on goal reaching tasks requiring behavior stitching.
- TRA showed improved generalization for rarely-seen skills and achieved strong performance on compositional 'stitch' environments within the OGBench simulation benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Emergent compositionality in robotics can arise from structuring representation spaces through temporal and multimodal alignment, rather than solely relying on explicit planning or value functions.
> - An auxiliary objective enforcing temporal consistency between state representations, akin to successor features, is sufficient to enable generalization to novel compositional tasks.
> - Combining goal-conditioned and language-conditioned learning with representation alignment allows for robust instruction following for complex, long-horizon tasks, even with infrequently observed skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of compositional generalization in long-horizon sequential decision-making for robotics. Compositionality, the ability to combine previously learned behaviors to solve novel, complex tasks, is recognized as a fundamental aspect of intelligent systems. Existing approaches to achieve this in robot learning include hierarchical learning, explicit subtask planning, and dynamic programming-based methods. However, these techniques often face issues of instability or data inefficiency, particularly in real-world robotics applications, limiting their scalability.

Biological systems, such as humans and animals, demonstrate robust compositional abilities, attributed to mechanisms like transitive inference, successor representations, causal models, and planning with world models. These biological theories suggest the importance of learning structured representations of the environment to facilitate inferring actions that lead to specific goals.

In the context of artificial intelligence and robotics, recent advancements in large-scale datasets have enabled the development of policies capable of being commanded with image goals and natural language instructions. These policies are commonly trained using goal- and language-conditioned imitation learning, reinforcement learning, or other supervised methods, sometimes enhanced by techniques like hindsight relabeling. Concurrently, research in compositional generalization has explored how to generalize to new behaviors formed by known sub-behaviors. This has led to hierarchical and planning-based methods that explicitly decompose tasks, with more recent efforts leveraging pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) for subtask generation.

The present work contributes to the field of representation learning for states and tasks in decision-making. Prior research has explored multimodal pre-trained representations (e.g., from images and language) for downstream tasks, and in reinforcement learning, representations are often trained to predict future states, rewards, or actions. Some methods have also introduced structural constraints on representations to enable planning or enforce metric properties.

This research distinguishes itself from prior contrastive representation learning methods (e.g., VIP, GRIF, R3M) by focusing on emergent compositional generalization capabilities. It achieves this by aligning representations across time and task modalities (goals and language) through an auxiliary loss. Critically, these learned representations are not directly employed for policy extraction or defining a value function during training or inference, a departure from many reinforcement learning or value-function-based approaches that explicitly utilize a learned value function for compositional behavior.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a method for automatically learning task representations that enable emergent compositionality in robot instruction following. The authors aim to demonstrate that an auxiliary objective focusing on temporal alignment between current and future state representations can facilitate compositional generalization without relying on explicit subtask planning or complex reinforcement learning frameworks.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
1. **Limitations of Existing Compositionality Approaches:** Current techniques for achieving compositional behavior in robotics are frequently unstable or demand extensive data, posing practical challenges for deployment in real-world settings.
2. **Biological Inspiration for Structured Representations:** Biological intelligence suggests that structured representations of the world are crucial for compositional capabilities, guiding the inference of actions toward goals. The authors seek to translate this theoretical insight into a practical algorithmic approach for robot learning.
3. **Desire for Emergent Compositionality:** Instead of explicitly decomposing tasks or relying on hierarchical structures, the research is motivated by the potential for compositional abilities to emerge from fundamental representation learning principles. This could simplify policy learning and improve generalization to novel, multi-step tasks.
4. **Integration of Modalities:** The work aims to combine the benefits of goal-conditioned and language-conditioned supervised learning with temporal representation alignment, enabling robots to follow instructions specified through either goal images or natural language for complex, long-horizon tasks.
5. **Efficiency and Robustness:** By focusing on representation learning as an auxiliary objective, the authors hypothesize that compositional generalization can be achieved in a more data-efficient and robust manner than methods requiring explicit planning or extensive online reinforcement learning.

Specifically, the research aims to answer the following questions:
- Can the proposed Temporal Representation Alignment (TRA) method enable zero-shot composition of sequential tasks without additional rewards or planning mechanisms?
- Does TRA demonstrate improved compositional generalization compared to existing imitation learning and offline reinforcement learning baselines?
- How effectively does TRA capture and enable skills that are infrequently observed within the training dataset?
- Is temporal alignment alone, as an auxiliary representation learning objective, sufficient to achieve effective compositional generalization?

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, Temporal Representation Alignment (TRA), involves structuring a shared representation space for states, goals, and language instructions. This structuring aims to make the space more amenable to compositional generalization, particularly for long-horizon tasks that can be broken down into known subtasks.

The approach builds upon goal-conditioned behavioral cloning (BC) but augments it with two contrastive representation alignment objectives:
1. **Goal-Conditioned Behavioral Cloning (GCBC):** A policy $\pi(a | s, g)$ is trained to select actions to reach a goal $g$ from expert demonstrations. The standard behavioral cloning loss, $L_{BC}$, is used. The paper highlights that for compositional tasks (e.g., $s \to w \to g$), directly conditioning on a distant goal $g$ when the intermediate waypoint $w$ is not explicitly known can be challenging, as the state-goal pair $(s, g)$ might be out of the training distribution.
2. **Temporal Alignment Loss:** To address the challenge of composing behaviors, TRA introduces a temporal alignment objective. This objective, formulated as a symmetric InfoNCE (Noise-Contrastive Estimation) loss, $L_{NCE}$, aligns the representation of a current state $\phi(s_i)$ with the representation of a future state $\psi(s_i^+)$. This process aims to implicitly encode the "successor features" concept, where representations of states are aligned with representations of states reachable in the future. By aligning representations of intermediate waypoints with representations of distant goals, the policy can effectively "stitch" together learned short-horizon behaviors. The future state $s_i^+$ is sampled a geometrically distributed number of steps ($k \sim Geom(1-\gamma)$) after the current state $s_i$.
3. **Language-Task Alignment Loss:** To enable instruction following with natural language, TRA integrates an additional CLIP-style contrastive alignment loss. This loss, also an InfoNCE objective, connects the future state/goal representations $\psi(g_i)$ with the corresponding language instruction representations $\xi(\ell_i)$. This grounds language commands into the structured representation space, allowing the policy to be conditioned on language instructions.

The overall TRA objective function, $L_{TRA}$, is a sum of these three components:
$L_{TRA} = L_{BC} + L_{NCE}(\text{states}) + L_{NCE}(\text{goals, language})$

Here:
- $L_{BC}$ trains the policy $\pi$ to imitate expert actions, conditioned on either the future state representation $\psi(s_i^+)$ or the language instruction representation $\xi(\ell_i)$.
- $L_{NCE}(\text{states})$ uses encoders $\phi$ for current states and $\psi$ for future states/goals to align them temporally.
- $L_{NCE}(\text{goals, language})$ uses encoder $\psi$ for goals and $\xi$ for language to align task representations across modalities.

**Key aspects of the methodology:**
- **Auxiliary Loss:** The temporal alignment loss acts as an auxiliary objective. It structures the representation space but is not directly used for policy extraction or test-time inference as a value function or planner. The policy continues to be a goal- or language-conditioned behavioral cloning policy.
- **Data Generation:** For each sampled observation $s_t$, a future state $s_{t+k}$ is sampled from the same trajectory, where $k$ is drawn from a geometric distribution based on the discount factor $\gamma$. Language annotations are also augmented by rephrasing to increase diversity.
- **Architecture:** The policy network typically employs a ResNet-34 architecture, with an MLP head for action prediction. The state, goal, and language encoders ( $\phi$, $\psi$, $\xi$) map inputs to a shared latent space.
- **Theoretical Justification:** The paper provides a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1) to formalize the intuition behind TRA. It presents bounds on the compositional generalization error, showing its relationship to the in-distribution imitation error under certain conditions. This suggests that the learned representations enable better generalization to longer horizons.
- **Simulation Adaptation:** For environments without language instructions (e.g., OGBench), the language-task alignment component ($L_{NCE}(\text{goals, language})$) is removed, and the policy is trained using only state and goal representations, $\pi(a|\phi(s), \psi(g))$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Temporal Representation Alignment (TRA) was evaluated through real-world robotic manipulation tasks and simulated reinforcement learning benchmarks.

**Real-World Evaluation:**
The evaluation used a 7 DoF WidowX250 manipulator arm and an augmented version of the BridgeDataV2 dataset. Compositional generalization was tested on 13 held-out tasks categorized into four scenes with increasing difficulty:
- **Set A (One-Step Tasks):** Non-compositional tasks, primarily for baseline comparison (e.g., opening a drawer). TRA's performance was competitive but not statistically significantly better than baselines for these single-step tasks.
- **Set B (Task Concatenation):** Tasks requiring multiple sequential actions where the order might not be strictly critical (e.g., "put the spoons on towels").
- **Set C (Semantic Generalization):** Tasks requiring manipulation of objects of the same semantic type (e.g., "put the food items in a container").
- **Set D (Tasks with Dependency):** The most challenging tasks, where subtasks have strict completion dependencies (e.g., "open the drawer and then take the mushroom out of the drawer").

**Key Real-World Results:**
- **Emergent Compositionality:** TRA demonstrated substantial improvements in compositional generalization on OOD (out-of-distribution) tasks.
 - For **language-conditioned tasks**, TRA achieved success rates of 88.9% in Set B, 83.3% in Set C, and 60% in Set D. In contrast, the best-performing baselines achieved 30% (LCBC) in Set B, 43.3% (AWR) in Set C, and 33.3% (Octo) in Set D. This represents over 40% improvement in certain categories.
 - For **goal-conditioned tasks**, TRA achieved 60% in Set C and 43.3% in Set D, outperforming best baselines at 46.7% and 20% respectively.
- **Qualitative Observations:** TRA-trained policies produced smoother trajectories and successfully composed subtasks, whereas baseline methods often failed to correctly ground instructions or execute multi-step behaviors. For example, in a "remove bell pepper + sweep towel" task, TRA successfully grasped and moved the pepper before sweeping, while a baseline policy might nudge the pepper and fail.
- **Comparison with Offline RL (AWR):** TRA significantly outperformed AWR, a method explicitly designed for compositional generalization through "stitching" via a learned value function. TRA surpassed AWR by 45% on instruction following and 25% on goal reaching tasks. AWR policies often terminated after completing only one subtask of a multi-step instruction, indicating difficulty in stitching behaviors from an offline dataset without explicit temporal alignment in representation learning.
- **Performance on Rarely-Seen Skills:** TRA exhibited better generalization for rarely-seen skills, maintaining a higher success rate from Set C to Set D (83.3% to 60%) compared to AWR (43.3% to 6.67%).
- **Sufficiency of Temporal Alignment:** An ablation study comparing TRA with TRA augmented by AWR (using AWR as an additional policy learning metric) showed no statistically significant difference, and even a marginal decrease in efficacy for the combined approach. This finding suggests that the temporal alignment mechanism itself is the primary driver for compositional generalization, rather than explicit value functions or reinforcement learning objectives.

**Simulation Evaluation (OGBench):**
TRA's compositional behavior was also validated on 7 different environments from the OGBench offline RL benchmark, including "stitch" datasets designed for compositional generalization and general goal-reaching tasks.
- **Key Simulation Results:** TRA outperformed other imitation and offline RL methods (GCBC, CRL, GCIQL, GCIVL, QRL) on several "stitch" environments, which require compositional generalizations. For instance, in "antmaze medium stitch," TRA achieved 60.7% success, compared to 45.5% for GCBC and 52.7% for CRL. In environments not explicitly designed for stitching, traditional offline RL methods sometimes showed stronger performance, but TRA still represented an improvement over baseline GCBC.

**Failure Cases:**
The study noted that TRA shares common failure modes with other Gaussian policies, particularly struggling with multimodal behaviors, early grasping, or incorrect reaching. Its primary benefits were observed in compositionally OOD tasks, with less pronounced improvements on in-distribution tasks. Challenges with depth reasoning, leading to premature grasping or late release, were also identified.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Temporal Representation Alignment (TRA) as a method for enabling emergent compositionality in robot instruction following. The findings demonstrate that by using an auxiliary temporal alignment loss to structure state, goal, and language representations, robots can achieve robust compositional generalization without explicit planning or complex reinforcement learning setups.

The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:
- **Paradigm Shift in Compositional Learning:** The research suggests that sophisticated generalization properties, traditionally attributed to reinforcement learning or explicit planning algorithms, can emerge from structured supervised learning with well-designed, temporally consistent representations. This challenges existing assumptions and opens new avenues for simpler, more data-efficient approaches to complex robotic behaviors.
- **Enhanced Robot Capabilities:** TRA significantly improves the ability of robots to perform long-horizon, compositionally novel tasks specified through both language and goal images. This capability is critical for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured environments where task specifications can be varied and complex.
- **Reduced Reliance on Complex RL:** By demonstrating that a supervised learning framework, augmented with an auxiliary representation alignment objective, can achieve strong compositional generalization, the method potentially reduces the need for computationally intensive or data-hungry reinforcement learning strategies, particularly for real-world robotics where data collection is expensive and often offline.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work highlights the critical role of representation learning in unlocking advanced agent capabilities. It provides a strong baseline and motivation for exploring further structural constraints on representation spaces to address remaining limitations, such as scenarios where behavioral cloning entirely fails or for handling multimodal behaviors more robustly.
- **Scalability to More Complex Settings:** While the current implementation uses a ResNet architecture, the underlying principle of temporal representation alignment can be integrated with more advanced architectures like transformers or diffusion policies, suggesting a path toward scaling to even more complex tasks and domains.
- **Complementary to Other Methods:** The authors suggest that TRA could be combined with other approaches, such as hierarchical task decomposition using VLMs or other planning methods, to further enhance performance and address current limitations, potentially creating hybrid systems that leverage the strengths of both representation learning and explicit reasoning.

In summary, this paper provides evidence that focusing on the temporal consistency of learned representations can be a powerful and efficient means to achieve compositional generalization in robotics, offering a practical and theoretically grounded alternative or complement to existing methodologies.
