# Infusing fine-grained visual knowledge to Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A research collaboration between CTU and Google DeepMind introduces a fine-tuning pipeline for Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs) that infuses fine-grained visual knowledge without causing catastrophic forgetting of general-purpose visual understanding or cross-modal retrieval capabilities. The method combines parameter and embedding regularization with a dual-component validation strategy, achieving up to +29.9 mAP@20 in-domain on fine-grained tasks like SOP while largely preserving out-of-domain performance (e.g., -0.1 mAP@20 and -0.9 R@1).

## Problem

- Fine-grained open-set visual retrieval tasks often yield suboptimal performance when using general-purpose Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs) directly.
- Standard fine-tuning of VLM vision encoders on domain-specific data leads to catastrophic forgetting, significantly degrading general-purpose visual understanding and cross-modal (image-text) alignment.
- The necessity of maintaining multiple specialized VLM instances for different tasks creates operational inefficiencies in terms of computational resources, storage, and model management.

## Method

- The authors propose a fine-tuning pipeline for the VLM's visual encoder that combines two regularization techniques: ==L2 parameter regularization== and ==embedding regularization==, the latter distilling embeddings towards a frozen pre-trained encoder using samples from an external generic dataset (ImageNet).
- A total objective function weights domain-specific classification loss with both parameter and embedding regularization losses to balance specialization and knowledge retention.
- A principled, ==dual-component validation strategy== averages in-domain retrieval metrics (e.g., mAP@20 on fine-grained dataset) and out-of-domain retrieval metrics (e.g., mAP@20 on a generic dataset like ImageNet) to guide hyperparameter tuning and early stopping.

## Results

- The proposed method successfully mitigated catastrophic forgetting, achieving **+29.9 mAP@20** in-domain on Stanford Online Products (SOP) while only incurring **-0.1 mAP@20** out-of-domain for image-image and **-0.9 R@1** for text-image retrieval on SigLIP, a substantial improvement over standard fine-tuning's **-6.0 mAP@20** and **-12.8 R@1** drops.
- The combined regularization approach consistently outperformed various baselines, including L2-SP, WISE-FT, and variants of LDIFS, in achieving a superior balance between in-domain specialization and out-of-domain knowledge retention across five diverse fine-grained datasets and two VLM architectures (SigLIP, TIPS).
- The study demonstrated that ImageNet serves as an effective external dataset for embedding regularization, outperforming LAION in maintaining a strong trade-off between in-domain adaptation and out-of-domain retention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parameter regularization alone (L2-SP) is largely insufficient to prevent catastrophic forgetting of out-of-domain performance, especially in fine-grained retrieval contexts.
> - Embedding regularization, particularly when applied to embeddings from an *external generic dataset* (like ImageNet), is crucial for maintaining general-purpose knowledge and cross-modal alignment.
> - A rigorous, dual-component validation strategy that jointly optimizes for in-domain specialization and out-of-domain retention is essential for developing robust and adaptable foundation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs), fine-grained visual recognition, continual learning, and catastrophic forgetting mitigation. The landscape is currently dominated by the impressive capabilities of large-scale contrastively pre-trained VLMs such as CLIP, SigLIP, and TIPS. These models have revolutionized many visual and multimodal tasks by learning powerful, general-purpose representations (embeddings) that facilitate effective cross-modal and visual retrieval. They have become foundational tools across a wide array of applications, from general-purpose image search to specialized domains.

However, a critical challenge arises when these general-purpose VLMs are applied to *fine-grained open-set visual retrieval* tasks. These tasks demand extreme discriminability, differentiating between visually similar but semantically distinct categories, like specific clothing items, car models, or species. While the pre-trained VLM vision encoders are strong, they typically do not achieve optimal performance on these specialized fine-grained tasks without further domain-specific fine-tuning.

The standard practice of fine-tuning the vision encoder on domain-specific, expertly labeled datasets significantly boosts in-domain performance. Yet, this approach introduces a severe drawback: *catastrophic forgetting*. This phenomenon leads to a substantial degradation of the model’s general-purpose capabilities, including its performance on out-of-domain visual tasks and, critically, its cross-modal (image-text) alignment. This forces practitioners to manage and deploy multiple specialized models for different tasks, increasing computational overhead, storage requirements, and overall operational complexity.

The paper directly addresses this critical limitation by drawing inspiration from the continual learning literature. Continual learning is a subfield of machine learning focused on enabling models to learn new tasks sequentially without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. Existing approaches in this area include partial fine-tuning (e.g., freezing layers, layer-wise learning rates), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods (e.g., Visual Prompt Tuning, LoRA, adapters), and regularization techniques that constrain parameter or embedding drift (e.g., L2-SP, LDIFS, ARF, WiSE-FT). This work positions itself by systematically analyzing and combining such regularization techniques within the specific context of fine-grained VLM adaptation.

Crucially, the authors highlight a methodological shortcoming in much of the prior work: the neglect of principled validation strategies when balancing conflicting objectives like in-domain specialization and out-of-domain knowledge retention. They argue that this oversight often leads to models that are either overly specialized or insufficiently adapted. By proposing a rigorous validation framework, this work not only offers a practical solution but also advances the methodological standards in the field, making it easier to reliably evaluate and reproduce results for models that must maintain diverse capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a fine-tuning strategy that can effectively "infuse" fine-grained visual knowledge into Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs) without compromising their inherent general-purpose visual understanding and cross-modal retrieval capabilities. In essence, the goal is to achieve an optimal balance between domain-specific specialization and the retention of broad, pre-trained multimodal knowledge.

This objective is motivated by several pressing issues observed in the deployment and adaptation of powerful VLMs:

- **Suboptimal Performance on Fine-Grained Tasks:** While VLMs excel at general visual understanding, their direct application to highly specialized fine-grained open-set visual retrieval tasks (e.g., identifying specific product variations or intricate biological species) often yields suboptimal results compared to models explicitly fine-tuned on relevant domain-specific data.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting from Standard Fine-Tuning:** The current best practice of fine-tuning VLM vision encoders on domain-specific datasets, while boosting in-domain performance, inevitably leads to catastrophic forgetting. This means the model loses its ability to perform well on general visual tasks and, critically, its alignment with the text encoder for cross-modal retrieval.
- **Operational Inefficiencies:** The degradation of generalizable capabilities necessitates maintaining multiple specialized models for different tasks. This practice introduces significant operational burdens, including increased computational resources for training and inference, higher storage requirements, and greater complexity in model management and deployment. A single model capable of both specialized and general tasks would offer substantial efficiency gains.
- **Lack of Balanced Fine-Tuning Solutions:** Existing fine-tuning and catastrophic forgetting mitigation techniques often address only parts of the problem, or their effectiveness has not been thoroughly evaluated in the specific VLM fine-grained retrieval context. There is a clear need for a strategy that can robustly achieve both specialization and retention simultaneously.
- **Methodological Gaps in Validation:** A critical, yet commonly overlooked, problem in prior literature is the absence of rigorous validation strategies for accurately predicting both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization performance during fine-tuning. Without a proper validation framework, the selection of hyperparameters and optimal model checkpoints becomes unreliable, hindering reproducibility and robust generalization.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to provide a more principled and effective approach to adapting foundational VLMs, making them more versatile and efficient for a wider range of real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel fine-tuning pipeline explicitly designed to adapt the vision encoder of a pre-trained VLM to fine-grained visual domains while preserving its original general-purpose knowledge. The core of their approach lies in combining two complementary regularization techniques, inspired by continual learning, along with a robust validation methodology.

**Vision Encoder Focus:** The pipeline exclusively leverages the visual encoder of the VLM, typically a Vision Transformer (ViT), for fine-tuning. The text encoder of the VLM is kept frozen throughout the process, a crucial design choice that highlights the goal of maintaining visual-text alignment without requiring additional text data during fine-tuning.

**Domain Learning with Class-Level Supervision ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{domain}}$):**
To adapt the model to the fine-grained domain, a classification-based representation learning approach is used. A cosine classifier is appended to the vision encoder, and the model is trained with a normalized cross-entropy loss. This loss encourages the model to learn discriminative features for the specific classes within the fine-grained dataset. Importantly, this classifier is discarded at inference time, as the task is open-set retrieval on unseen classes.

**Regularization Towards Pre-trained Knowledge:**
To counteract catastrophic forgetting, two distinct regularization approaches are employed:

1. **Parameter Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{pretr.params}}$):** This technique, inspired by L2-SP, constrains the fine-tuned network's parameters ($\theta_{\text{ft}}$) to remain close to the parameters of the original pre-trained network ($\theta_{\text{pre}}$). An L2 loss penalizes the deviation of each network parameter from its initial pre-trained value. This method aims to prevent large shifts in the model's learned weights, thereby preserving its foundational knowledge.

2. **Embedding Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{pretr.embed.}}$):** Drawing inspiration from LDIFS, this regularization explicitly restricts the drift of the learned representations. It distills the embeddings produced by the fine-tuned network ($f_{\theta_{\text{ft}}}(\mathbf{x})$) towards the embeddings generated by the *frozen pre-trained network* ($f_{\theta_{\text{pre}}}(\mathbf{x})$). A critical distinction from prior work is that this loss is applied to images sampled from an *external generic dataset*, specifically ImageNet, rather than the fine-tuning domain's data. These generic batches are interleaved with the fine-tuning domain batches in a round-robin fashion. To optimize computational efficiency, the target embeddings from the frozen pre-trained encoder are extracted offline once before training. This ensures that the fine-tuned model's embedding space remains broadly aligned with the original VLM's semantic space, even as it specializes in a new domain.

**Total Objective Function:**
The final loss function is a weighted combination of these three components:
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{domain}} + \lambda_{\text{emb}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pretr.embed.}} + \lambda_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{pretr.params.}}$
The weights $\lambda_{\text{emb}}$ and $\lambda_{\theta}$ are crucial for balancing the trade-off between domain adaptation and knowledge retention.

**Principled Hyperparameter Tuning and Validation:**
A significant methodological contribution of this work is its rigorous validation strategy. Recognizing that optimizing for both in-domain performance and out-of-domain retention requires careful consideration, the authors propose a dual-component validation framework:
1. **In-domain component:** Standard validation set from the fine-grained dataset, measuring relevant retrieval metrics (e.g., mAP@20).
2. **Out-of-domain component:** A held-out subset of a large, generic dataset (e.g., ImageNet training set), evaluated with the same retrieval metric.
The final validation score is computed as the *average* of these two components. This composite metric is then used for both hyperparameter tuning (grid search for $\lambda_{\text{emb}}$, $\lambda_{\theta}$) and early stopping during training. This approach ensures that the model selection process explicitly encourages a robust balance across conflicting objectives.

**Experimental Settings:**
The method is evaluated on two prominent VLMs (SigLIP and TIPS, both with ViT-Base/16 backbones) and five diverse fine-grained open-set retrieval datasets (Stanford Online Products, InShop, Stanford Cars196, iNaturalist 2018, Food2k). Performance is measured using mAP@20 for image-image retrieval and Recall@1 for text-image retrieval on datasets like COCO and Flickr30k. Several baselines, including standard fine-tuning, L2-SP, WISE-FT, and variants of LDIFS, are used for comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive empirical evaluation demonstrating the effectiveness of its proposed fine-tuning method. The key findings are:

1. **Catastrophic Forgetting is a Significant Problem:** Standard fine-tuning, while achieving the highest in-domain performance (e.g., +31.0 mAP@20 on SOP for SigLIP), leads to severe catastrophic forgetting. This is evidenced by substantial drops in out-of-domain image-image retrieval (e.g., -6.0 mAP@20) and particularly in text-image retrieval performance (e.g., -12.8 R@1 on average for SOP fine-tuning), as visually depicted in Figure 1 and detailed in Table 1. This reaffirms the core motivation for the research.

2. **Individual Regularization Techniques Have Distinct Effects:**
 - **Parameter Regularization (L2-SP):** While helpful in curbing in-domain overfitting, parameter regularization alone (without embedding regularization) proved largely insufficient to retain out-of-domain performance. Figure 3 shows that regardless of the regularization strength, out-of-domain accuracy still rapidly drops, indicating that constraining network parameters does not adequately prevent embedding distribution drift. This contradicts some prior work, possibly due to the specific nature of fine-grained retrieval tasks versus standard classification.
 - **Embedding Regularization (LDIFS-inspired):** This technique effectively prevents out-of-domain catastrophic forgetting, with strong regularization nearly freezing performance at initial values. However, this comes at the cost of reduced in-domain adaptation, meaning a very high embedding loss weight can degrade performance on the fine-grained task (Figure 3).

3. **Combined Regularization Achieves Optimal Balance:** The proposed method, which combines both parameter and embedding regularization with a principled validation strategy, consistently achieves a superior balance between in-domain adaptation and out-of-domain knowledge retention.
 - Across all five fine-grained datasets and both SigLIP and TIPS models, our method significantly mitigates out-of-domain forgetting while still achieving strong in-domain specialization. For instance, fine-tuning SigLIP on SOP, our method yields +29.9 mAP@20 in-domain, while only incurring -0.1 mAP@20 out-of-domain for image-image and -0.9 R@1 for text-image, compared to standard fine-tuning's -6.0 and -12.8 drops, respectively (Table 1).
 - Crucially, the model retains strong visual-text alignment without using any text data or the original text encoder during fine-tuning.
 - The optimal regularization weights ($\lambda_{\text{emb}}, \lambda_{\theta}$) were found to vary depending on the specific pre-trained model and target dataset, underscoring the importance of the proposed robust hyperparameter tuning methodology.

4. **Superiority Over Baselines:** A direct comparison against various baselines (Table 2, fine-tuning SigLIP on SOP) further highlights the advantages of the proposed approach:
 - **Standard fine-tuning:** Best in-domain, worst out-of-domain.
 - **L2-SP (parameter regularization only):** Slightly better out-of-domain than standard, but still significant drops, and a slight in-domain performance reduction.
 - **(Our) LDIFS variant (embedding regularization only):**
 - With *in-domain* targets: Limits out-of-domain forgetting but significantly cripples in-domain adaptation.
 - With *generic* targets (ImageNet): Offers much better trade-off than in-domain targets, confirming the importance of diverse, unrelated data for preserving general semantics.
 - **WISE-FT (parameter averaging):** Maintains good out-of-domain generalization but sacrifices more in-domain specialization compared to our method.
 - **Our method** consistently achieved the highest "In-Out Avg." score, demonstrating the best overall balance.

5. **Importance of External Dataset Choice:** The study on external datasets for embedding regularization (Table 3) confirmed ImageNet as an effective choice, yielding a better "In-Out Avg." compared to LAION. This suggests that a comprehensive, generic dataset is crucial for effectively anchoring the pre-trained capabilities.

6. **Validation Strategy is Key:** The paper implicitly and explicitly demonstrates that the proposed dual-component validation (averaging in-domain and out-of-domain metrics) is vital for selecting optimal hyperparameters and achieving robust, reproducible results. This addresses a critical methodological gap in prior work.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the fields of Vision-and-Language Models, fine-grained visual recognition, and continual learning, with substantial potential impact on both academic research and practical applications.

1. **Enhanced VLM Adaptability and Efficiency:** The most immediate impact is the development of a robust fine-tuning method that allows powerful general-purpose VLMs to be specialized for fine-grained visual domains without suffering catastrophic forgetting of their broad, pre-trained knowledge. This means practitioners can adapt a single foundation model for specific tasks while retaining its general capabilities and cross-modal alignment. This directly addresses the operational inefficiencies of managing multiple highly specialized models, reducing computational costs, storage requirements, and deployment complexity. It allows VLMs to be truly "infusable" with new knowledge, making them more versatile and cost-effective.

2. **Advancement in Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation:** By systematically analyzing and combining parameter and embedding regularization techniques, the paper provides novel empirical insights into their distinct functionalities and interactions within the VLM fine-tuning context. The finding that parameter regularization alone is insufficient for out-of-domain retention, especially for fine-grained tasks, and that embedding regularization with generic data is crucial, advances the understanding of how to effectively preserve knowledge in deep neural networks. This contributes directly to the broader field of continual learning, offering practical strategies for maintaining model flexibility over time.

3. **Methodological Rigor for Model Evaluation:** The introduction of a principled, dual-component validation framework is a critical methodological contribution. By explicitly evaluating and averaging both in-domain specialization and out-of-domain generalization performance, the authors provide a robust mechanism for hyperparameter tuning and early stopping. This addresses a widely overlooked issue in prior work, enhancing the reproducibility and reliability of research on adapting foundation models. This methodology can serve as a standard for future studies aiming to balance conflicting objectives in model adaptation.

4. **Bridging General and Specialized AI:** This work is a crucial step towards bridging the gap between powerful, general-purpose foundation models and their effective, nuanced application in highly specialized domains. It enables these large models to perform at state-of-the-art levels on fine-grained tasks while maintaining their general intelligence, which is a key requirement for developing more human-like and adaptable AI systems.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The findings and proposed methodology lay a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
 - Exploring more advanced regularization techniques or adaptive weight learning for the regularization terms.
 - Investigating the optimal choice and sampling strategies for the external generic dataset used in embedding regularization.
 - Applying this methodology to other modalities or multi-task learning scenarios beyond visual retrieval.
 - Further analyzing the theoretical underpinnings of why certain regularization types behave differently in various fine-tuning contexts.

The provision of code and model checkpoints further facilitates research and adoption by the wider community, ensuring that these advancements can be built upon effectively. Overall, this paper makes a substantial contribution to making powerful VLMs more practical, adaptable, and robust for real-world applications demanding both broad understanding and fine-grained discrimination.
