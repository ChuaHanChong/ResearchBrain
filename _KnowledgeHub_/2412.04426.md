# Towards Fast Safe Online Reinforcement Learning via Policy Finetuning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Marvel, an offline-to-online (O2O) framework for Safe Reinforcement Learning that employs policy finetuning, addressing challenges of inaccurate Q-estimations and Lagrangian mismatch. Marvel combines Value Pre-Alignment (VPA) and an Adaptive PID (aPID) controller, leading to policies that achieve higher rewards while satisfying safety constraints with significantly fewer online interactions across ten safety environments.

## Problem

- Learning safe policies from scratch in online Safe Reinforcement Learning (RL) is often costly, time-consuming, and risky due to extensive environmental interactions.
- Offline Safe RL, while avoiding online interaction, often suffers from lower performance, strong reliance on dataset quality, and vulnerability to out-of-distribution actions.
- Existing offline-to-online (O2O) techniques from standard RL are not directly applicable to Safe RL due to erroneous Q-estimations (from objective mismatch and cost sparsity) and Lagrangian mismatch.

## Method

- Proposed **Marvel**, a ==policy-finetuning based offline-to-online (O2O)== Safe RL framework, that integrates ==Value Pre-Alignment (VPA)== and an ==Adaptive PID (aPID)== control mechanism.
- **VPA** re-aligns offline-pretrained reward and cost ==Q-functions== to match online learning objectives and promote exploration, using only the offline dataset.
- **aPID** replaces standard ==dual ascent== for Lagrange multiplier updates during online finetuning, dynamically adjusting gains to efficiently manage safety constraints and stabilize training.

## Results

- Marvel consistently achieves superior or comparable performance across ten Bullet Safety Gym and Safety Gymnasium environments, obtaining higher rewards while effectively maintaining costs below safety thresholds.
- The framework significantly reduces the required online interactions, achieving near-optimal performance within hundreds of gradient updates (e.g., **120,000 interactions**) compared to **millions** for baselines.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role and synergy of both VPA and aPID for Marvel's efficiency, stability, and robust constraint satisfaction, with the adaptive nature of aPID being particularly crucial.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-aligning Q-functions with VPA is crucial for overcoming the inherent conservatism and inaccuracies of offline-trained Q-values, enabling effective online finetuning and exploration.
> - An adaptive PID controller is essential for rapidly and precisely managing safety constraints during online finetuning, especially after a more optimistic, VPA-driven warm start.
> - The synergistic combination of VPA and aPID effectively addresses both erroneous Q-estimations and Lagrangian mismatch, providing a robust and efficient pathway for fast safe online learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) involves an agent learning optimal behaviors through trial-and-error interactions with an environment to maximize cumulative rewards. While standard RL has achieved successes, its application in real-world scenarios, particularly in safety-critical domains such as robotics, autonomous vehicles, and healthcare, often requires adherence to safety constraints. This necessity led to the development of Safe Reinforcement Learning (Safe RL), which aims to optimize rewards while ensuring that predefined safety limits (e.g., on cumulative costs or specific states/actions) are not violated.

Solving online Safe RL from scratch typically involves extensive interactions with the environment, which can be prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, or risky in real-world systems. To mitigate these issues, Offline Safe RL emerged as an approach to learn safe policies from static datasets without requiring online interactions. However, offline methods have their own limitations, including potentially limited performance due to reliance on fixed dataset quality and susceptibility to out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, which can restrict their generalization across varying scenarios.

A successful paradigm in unconstrained RL, known as offline-to-online (O2O) RL, leverages policies (including Q-functions) pretrained on offline data to accelerate subsequent online finetuning. This approach reduces the need for extensive environmental interactions compared to training from scratch. While O2O RL has gained prominence in standard RL and imitation learning, its potential for facilitating faster and safer online learning in the *constrained* setting of Safe RL has not been extensively investigated through policy-finetuning-based frameworks. Existing work in O2O Safe RL, such as Guided Online Distillation, often relies on large pretrained models like Decision Transformers, which differs from policy-finetuning with typically smaller-scale policy networks.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a policy-finetuning based O2O Safe RL framework. The work identifies and tackles specific challenges that arise when attempting to adapt general O2O RL techniques to the safe RL context, thereby aiming to improve the efficiency and practicality of online safe policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to design an effective offline-to-online approach for Safe RL that addresses the limitations of both online and offline Safe RL, thereby enabling fast online safe policy learning with minimal environmental interactions.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in the current landscape of Safe RL:

1. **High Costs and Risks of Online Safe RL:** Learning safe policies directly in real-world environments often involves prohibitive costs and risks associated with extensive exploration and potential safety violations during initial learning phases. This limits the practical applicability of many online Safe RL methods.
2. **Limitations of Offline Safe RL:** While offline Safe RL offers a solution by enabling learning from static datasets, it typically suffers from several drawbacks. These include generally lower performance compared to online methods, heavy dependence on the quality and coverage of the offline dataset, and vulnerability to extrapolation errors when encountering states or actions not present in the training data (out-of-distribution actions).
3. **Untapped Potential of O2O Paradigm in Safe RL:** The pretraining-and-finetuning paradigm has demonstrated significant success in accelerating learning in unconstrained O2O RL. The authors hypothesize that a similar approach could provide a safe "warm start" and expedite online finetuning for Safe RL, effectively mitigating the issues of extensive online interactions. However, this direction has not been fully explored in Safe RL literature.

The authors identify two specific, unique challenges that prevent the direct application of existing O2O algorithms from standard RL to the Safe RL setting:

1. **Erroneous Q-estimations:**
- **Objective Mismatch:** Offline Safe RL algorithms often introduce specific regularizations or penalties (e.g., pushing up cost estimates for OOD actions) to ensure safety and avoid unsafe explorations beyond the dataset. This leads to a different Q-function optimization objective compared to standard online Safe RL. Consequently, Q-functions pretrained offline may not accurately estimate values for state-action pairs encountered during online finetuning.
- **Offline Cost Sparsity:** Offline datasets for Safe RL typically contain a majority of safe, zero-cost state-actions. This, combined with conservative extrapolation in offline algorithms that assign high costs to OOD actions, results in a pretrained cost Q-function that is overly conservative. It may erroneously assign high values to OOD actions and low values to in-distribution actions. This "inertia" can hinder online learning by discouraging active exploration, forcing the policy to remain in low-cost, low-reward regions, even when safe opportunities for higher rewards exist.
2. **Lagrangian Mismatch:** Many online Safe RL algorithms use a primal-dual approach with Lagrange multipliers to enforce safety constraints. The initial values for these multipliers, particularly those derived from offline safe RL, often do not align with the requirements of online finetuning. For instance, an offline multiplier might be vastly different from what is needed online (e.g., 1500 offline vs. 0.65 online in an example environment). Improper initialization can lead to either extensive constraint violations (if too low) or training stagnation (if too high and overly conservative), significantly degrading online learning performance even with accurate Q-functions.

Therefore, the key objective is to propose a framework that directly addresses these two challenges to enable efficient and safe online policy learning via finetuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a framework named **Marvel** (warM-stArt safe Reinforcement learning with Value prE-aLignment), which integrates two primary components to address the identified challenges: Value Pre-Alignment (VPA) and Adaptive PID Control (aPID). Marvel is designed as a policy-finetuning based approach for O2O Safe RL.

The overall methodology involves three phases:

1. **Offline Pretraining Phase:**
- An initial safe policy (π₀) and associated reward (Q) and cost (Qc) Q-functions are learned from an offline dataset (D_off).
- The paper uses CPQ (Constraints Penalized Q-learning) as the exemplary offline safe RL algorithm for this phase, though the framework is compatible with other actor-critic safe RL methods.

2. **Pre-Finetune Phase (Value Pre-Alignment - VPA):**
- **Objective:** To mitigate the "Erroneous Q-estimations" challenge arising from objective mismatch and offline cost sparsity.
- **Mechanism:** VPA re-aligns the pretrained Q-functions (Q and Qc) to better match the true Q-values of the offline policy (π₀) under the online learning objective. This is performed *using only the offline dataset* (D_off), thus incurring no additional online interaction costs.
- **Technical Details:** VPA adopts a Fitted Q Evaluation (FQE) approach. It minimizes Bellman error-like objectives for both reward (Eq. 8) and cost (Eq. 9) Q-functions. Crucially, these objectives include *entropy regularization terms* (α_VPA log π₀(a'|s') and α_VPA_c log π₀(a'|s')) for both reward and cost Q-functions. These entropy terms encourage a more optimistic estimation of rewards and costs for state-action pairs where the pretrained policy is "uncertain," thereby promoting exploration. The offline policy π₀ remains fixed during VPA to preserve learned knowledge.
- **Theoretical Justification:** Theorem 1 demonstrates that VPA can yield increasingly accurate Q-function estimates. The overall Q-estimation error (in weighted L2-norm) is bounded by a statistical error term (decreasing with dataset size) and an iteration error term (decaying exponentially with VPA steps). The theory suggests using a larger α_VPA for reward Q (to encourage exploration) and a smaller α_VPA_c for cost Q (for more accurate cost estimation). Empirically, Spearman's rank correlation coefficients show that VPA significantly improves the alignment of both reward and cost Q-value rankings with actual returns/costs, particularly for in-distribution actions.

3. **Online Finetuning Phase (with Adaptive PID Control - aPID):**
- **Objective:** To address the "Lagrangian Mismatch" challenge and to effectively manage the safety constraint violations that might arise from VPA's more optimistic Q-estimations.
- **Mechanism:** The online finetuning uses a Lagrangian-based online safe RL algorithm (SAC-lag in experiments) but replaces its standard dual ascent for Lagrange multiplier updates with an Adaptive PID (aPID) controller.
- **Technical Details:**
 - **PID Formulation:** A discrete, position-form PID controller updates the Lagrange multiplier (λ_t). It uses an exponentially moving average (EMA) smoothed violation (~e_t = c_t - c_th) for the proportional term (K_p ~e_t), an integral term (I_t) that accumulates raw violations (with an anti-windup mechanism, I_t = max(0, I_{t-1} + K_i e_t)), and a derivative term (D_t) calculated as a lagged difference of EMA-smoothed costs (D_t = max(0, ~c_t - ~c_{t-d})).
 - **Adaptive Gain Schedule:** A core innovation is the adaptive adjustment of the PID gains (K_p, K_i, K_d).
 - K_p and K_i are increased when the average cost (calculated over a short buffer) exceeds the threshold and decreased when below, to accelerate correction or improve stability, respectively.
 - K_d is increased when the standard deviation of costs is large, providing damping against volatility.
 - Gains are clipped within predefined minimum and maximum bounds.
- **Synergy:** VPA encourages exploration and provides a beneficial warm-start, potentially increasing initial cost violations. aPID then rapidly and appropriately pushes down these costs by dynamically adjusting the Lagrange multipliers, guiding the policy towards high-reward, low-cost regions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Marvel was conducted across ten environments from Bullet Safety Gym and Safety Gymnasium, using CPQ for offline pretraining and SAC-lag for online finetuning. The primary evaluation criterion was the ability to quickly achieve high rewards while satisfying cost constraints with minimal online interactions.

The main findings are:

1. **Superior Performance and Efficiency:** Marvel consistently demonstrates superior or comparable performance compared to all tested baselines (Warm Start, SO2, JSRL, PEX, From Scratch, Cal-QL, SAC with offline replay buffer, and a smaller Decision Transformer-based approach). It achieves higher returns while effectively keeping costs below the predefined threshold. Crucially, Marvel finds safe, high-reward policies very quickly, often achieving near-optimal performance within a few gradient updates (e.g., 20 steps in BallCircle and CarCircle), significantly reducing the required online interactions (e.g., 120,000 interactions vs. 1.5 million for typical online methods).
2. **Effective Constraint Satisfaction:** Marvel maintains its cost below the safety threshold across all evaluated environments, demonstrating its robustness in adhering to safety constraints while maximizing rewards.
3. **Limitations of Baselines:**
- **Naive Warm Start:** Directly finetuning offline pretrained policies and Q-functions (Warm Start) is largely ineffective, often leading to performance stagnation or degradation due to conservatism and erroneous Q-estimations.
- **Q-estimation and Exploration Methods (JSRL, PEX, SO2):** JSRL and PEX, which use offline policies for exploration rather than direct finetuning, struggle significantly. SO2, which partially addresses Q-estimation inaccuracies, shows some improvement but lacks consistent performance across environments, even with aPID. These results underscore the necessity of Marvel's explicit solutions for Q-estimation and Lagrangian mismatch.
- **Other Baselines:** Cal-QL and SAC with an offline replay buffer also fail to consistently achieve satisfactory outcomes or maintain safety, violating constraints in several cases.
4. **Enhanced Stability:** Marvel exhibits superior stability during training compared to other baseline algorithms, with smoother learning curves for both reward and cost.
5. **Compatibility:** Marvel demonstrates flexibility by performing effectively when integrated with different offline safe RL pretraining methods, such as BEAR-lag, highlighting its broad applicability.
6. **Ablation Study Insights:**
- **Synergy of VPA and aPID:** The best performance is achieved when both VPA and aPID are utilized in concert. VPA alone with traditional dual ascent significantly slows down learning and cost reduction. aPID alone (without VPA) performs similarly to naive finetuning, confirming the importance of addressing erroneous Q-estimations.
- **Adaptive vs. Static PID:** The adaptive nature of aPID is crucial. Replacing aPID with a standard PID controller leads to increased cost fluctuations and less precise control, particularly when costs are near the limit, potentially resulting in substantial safety violations.
- **VPA Design:** Applying VPA to both reward (Q) and cost (Qc) Q-networks simultaneously is more effective than applying it to only one, and incorporating the entropy term further enhances performance by promoting beneficial exploration. Finetuning pretrained Q-networks during VPA is also found to be superior to training new Q-networks from scratch in VPA, as it leverages valuable prior knowledge.
- **Robustness to Offline Quality:** Marvel consistently achieves near-optimal performance quickly, regardless of the initial quality or effectiveness of the offline pretrained policy or dataset, demonstrating robustness to variations in offline data.
7. **Cumulative Cost vs. Max Reward Analysis:** Marvel demonstrates its efficiency by achieving the best maximum reward for a given cumulative cost and incurring the least cumulative cost to reach a specific reward level.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Safe Reinforcement Learning by introducing Marvel, the first policy-finetuning based framework for Offline-to-Online (O2O) Safe RL. The work identifies and systematically addresses two specific challenges—erroneous Q-estimations and Lagrangian mismatch—that hinder the effective application of O2O principles to constrained settings.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Bridging the Gap:** Marvel effectively bridges the gap between offline and online safe RL, offering a practical pathway to combine the benefits of learning from static data with the performance enhancements of online interaction.
2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Safety:** By enabling fast and safe online policy learning with minimal environmental interactions, Marvel significantly improves the efficiency of deploying Safe RL algorithms. This is particularly crucial for real-world applications where interaction costs are high, and safety is paramount.
3. **Increased Practical Applicability:** The framework's modularity and compatibility with a variety of existing offline and online safe RL approaches enhance its practical appeal. This allows researchers and practitioners to integrate Marvel with their preferred base algorithms.
4. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundational policy-finetuning approach for O2O Safe RL, distinct from large model-based guiding methods. It is expected to stimulate further research into techniques for efficiently transferring knowledge from offline datasets to accelerate safe online learning.
5. **Real-world Impact:** The ability to develop high-performing, safe policies more quickly and with fewer risky interactions has direct implications for a wide range of safety-critical applications, including robotics, autonomous navigation, industrial control, and medical systems, facilitating the broader adoption of Safe RL in practical settings.

In conclusion, Marvel represents a step towards making Safe RL more practical and deployable by providing a robust and efficient method for leveraging offline data to accelerate online safe learning.
