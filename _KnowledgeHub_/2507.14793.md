# Flow Equivariant Recurrent Neural Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Flow Equivariant Recurrent Neural Networks (FERNNs), a class of models designed to explicitly encode continuous, time-parameterized symmetries (flows) in sequential data. FERNNs demonstrate enhanced training efficiency, superior length generalization, and zero-shot generalization to unseen flow velocities on tasks like next-step prediction and action recognition.

## Problem

- Geometric deep learning frameworks for static data lack a formal approach for continuous, time-parameterized transformations or 'flows' in dynamic sequential data.
- Standard recurrent neural networks, including Group Equivariant RNNs (G-RNNs), fail to capture continuous motion symmetries, exhibiting 'lagging' behavior in hidden states when processing moving stimuli.
- There was no formal mathematical framework for defining and building sequence models that are inherently 'flow equivariant'.

## Method

- A rigorous mathematical definition of ==flow equivariance== for sequence models is established, treating flows as ==one-parameter Lie subgroups==.
- **FERNNs** are proposed, which lift hidden states and inputs into a higher-dimensional space of flow generators and static symmetry groups.
- **FERNNs** employ a novel ==recurrence relation== where each hidden state corresponding to a specific flow velocity operates in its own ==moving reference frame==, ensuring equivariant transformation of internal states.

## Results

- **FERNNs** achieved significantly lower validation loss in fewer training iterations and up to an **order of magnitude lower MSE** on next-step prediction tasks on Flowing MNIST compared to G-RNNs.
- Demonstrated exceptional length generalization, maintaining high accuracy on sequences up to **70 time-steps**, substantially longer than training sequences, while G-RNNs degraded rapidly.
- Exhibited zero-shot generalization to unseen flow velocities and improved action recognition with induced camera motion on Moving KTH, achieving **0.716** accuracy versus G-RNN's **0.665**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing group-equivariant RNNs are not flow-equivariant, failing to correctly transform their internal representations in response to continuous input motion.
> - Explicitly designing recurrent architectures to respect time-parameterized symmetries leads to substantial improvements in learning efficiency and generalization capabilities for dynamic data.
> - The concept of each RNN 'bank' operating within its own moving reference frame, defined by a specific flow velocity, is crucial for achieving flow equivariance.
