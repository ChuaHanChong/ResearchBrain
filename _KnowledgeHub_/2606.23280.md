# Causal Reward World Models: Zero-shot Reward Design for Automated Skill Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Causal Reward World Models (CRWM) introduces a framework for Automated Reward Design that integrates causal knowledge to enable zero-shot generation of reward functions for robotic skill learning. This approach pre-trains a universal causal model to guide large language models (LLMs), achieving performance comparable to iterative methods in unseen Dexterity tasks and robust generalization to new robot embodiments, without requiring costly feedback loops.

## Problem

- Traditional reward engineering is time-consuming and demands extensive human expertise for autonomous skill acquisition.
- Existing LLM-based Automated Reward Design (ARD) methods rely on correlation, leading to inefficient iterative refinement and susceptibility to spurious correlations, causing "Optimization Collapse" or "Specification Gaming."
- Current ARD approaches design reward functions on a case-by-case basis, neglecting the potential for shared causal structures across tasks within similar dynamical systems.

## Method

- Develops a ==Causal Reward World Model== (**CRWM**) that captures fundamental causal relationships among reward-relevant variables by pre-training on multi-task offline data.
- Refines a coarse structural prior using micro-level trajectories through a joint optimization module, incorporating Explicit Mechanism Decoupling (EMD) to model state-dependent transient interactions.
- Integrates the distilled CRWM into an LLM-guided process, enabling the LLM to perform "causal pruning" and generate executable Python reward functions in a single pass for unseen tasks.

## Results

- CRWM achieved success rates (e.g., **0.92** on "Catch Over 2 Underarm", **0.87** on "Lift Underarm") on unseen Dexterity tasks, comparable to or surpassing state-of-the-art iterative ARD baselines, in a zero-shot setting (0 Evolutionary Search Iterations).
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to different robot embodiments and physical environments, achieving high success rates (e.g., **0.89** on ManiSkill2 "OpenCabinetDoor", **0.80** on "TurnFaucet") without task-specific adaptation.
- In sim-to-real experiments, CRWM-guided rewards exhibited high Pairwise Distinguishability Accuracy (average AUC ", **96.5%**) in discerning successful from failed human-teleoperated trajectories on a physical mobile manipulator, significantly outperforming a pure semantic LLM baseline (average AUC ", **53.9%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling causal relationships, rather than purely semantic correlations, is essential for mitigating common reward failure modes like Optimization Collapse and Specification Gaming in ARD.
> - A universal, task-irrelevant causal prior, pre-trained from diverse multi-task interaction data, can enable robust zero-shot reward generation without the need for iterative environmental feedback.
> - Decoupling stable causal structures from transient, state-dependent physical interactions through mechanisms like EMD significantly improves the accuracy and stability of the learned causal model for complex robotic systems.
