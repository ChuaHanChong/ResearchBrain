# Causal Reward World Models: Zero-shot Reward Design for Automated Skill Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Causal Reward World Models (CRWM) introduces a framework for Automated Reward Design that integrates causal knowledge to enable zero-shot generation of reward functions for robotic skill learning. This approach pre-trains a universal causal model to guide large language models (LLMs), achieving performance comparable to iterative methods in unseen Dexterity tasks and robust generalization to new robot embodiments, without requiring costly feedback loops.

## Problem

- Traditional reward engineering is time-consuming and demands extensive human expertise for autonomous skill acquisition.
- Existing LLM-based Automated Reward Design (ARD) methods rely on correlation, leading to inefficient iterative refinement and susceptibility to spurious correlations, causing "Optimization Collapse" or "Specification Gaming."
- Current ARD approaches design reward functions on a case-by-case basis, neglecting the potential for shared causal structures across tasks within similar dynamical systems.

## Method

- Develops a ==Causal Reward World Model== (**CRWM**) that captures fundamental causal relationships among reward-relevant variables by pre-training on multi-task offline data.
- Refines a coarse structural prior using micro-level trajectories through a joint optimization module, incorporating Explicit Mechanism Decoupling (EMD) to model state-dependent transient interactions.
- Integrates the distilled CRWM into an LLM-guided process, enabling the LLM to perform "causal pruning" and generate executable Python reward functions in a single pass for unseen tasks.

## Results

- CRWM achieved success rates (e.g., **0.92** on "Catch Over 2 Underarm", **0.87** on "Lift Underarm") on unseen Dexterity tasks, comparable to or surpassing state-of-the-art iterative ARD baselines, in a zero-shot setting (0 Evolutionary Search Iterations).
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to different robot embodiments and physical environments, achieving high success rates (e.g., **0.89** on ManiSkill2 "OpenCabinetDoor", **0.80** on "TurnFaucet") without task-specific adaptation.
- In sim-to-real experiments, CRWM-guided rewards exhibited high Pairwise Distinguishability Accuracy (average AUC ", **96.5%**) in discerning successful from failed human-teleoperated trajectories on a physical mobile manipulator, significantly outperforming a pure semantic LLM baseline (average AUC ", **53.9%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling causal relationships, rather than purely semantic correlations, is essential for mitigating common reward failure modes like Optimization Collapse and Specification Gaming in ARD.
> - A universal, task-irrelevant causal prior, pre-trained from diverse multi-task interaction data, can enable robust zero-shot reward generation without the need for iterative environmental feedback.
> - Decoupling stable causal structures from transient, state-dependent physical interactions through mechanisms like EMD significantly improves the accuracy and stability of the learned causal model for complex robotic systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in reinforcement learning (RL) and embodied artificial intelligence: the design of effective reward functions for autonomous skill acquisition. Traditional reward engineering often requires substantial human expertise, leading to manual and time-consuming processes. Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have given rise to Automated Reward Design (ARD) paradigms, where LLMs synthesize executable reward functions from task descriptions. These LLM-based approaches aim to mitigate the reliance on human expertise by leveraging the semantic reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

However, existing LLM-based ARD methods are predominantly correlation-driven and rely on iterative environmental feedback for refinement. This approach presents several limitations:
1. **Inefficient Reasoning:** The iterative "trial-evaluation-mutation-feedback" loops required to refine reward hypotheses for each specific task are computationally expensive, as each iteration involves training and evaluating RL policies in a physical environment.
2. **Susceptibility to Spurious Correlations:** LLMs, primarily reasoning over semantic associations, may introduce semantically plausible but causally irrelevant reward components. Such spurious correlations can lead to "Optimization Collapse" (where noisy signals impede learning) or "Specification Gaming" (where agents exploit misaligned rewards to achieve high scores without genuine task completion).
3. **Lack of Reusability:** Current methodologies often design and optimize reward functions on a case-by-case basis, neglecting the potential for shared causal structures across tasks within similar dynamical systems.

The paper aims to bridge these gaps by incorporating explicit causal knowledge into the ARD process. While causal discovery methods exist, applying them directly to high-dimensional physical systems remains challenging due to dense interactions, incomplete observations, and limited interventions. Prior-guided methods, often relying on LLMs for initial topological knowledge, can still produce coarse or biased structural priors. Furthermore, existing world models in robotics primarily focus on predicting future observations or state transitions, with limited emphasis on the causal relationships among reward-relevant variables. This work positions itself by proposing a novel "white-box" paradigm for ARD that transitions from correlation-based, task-wise inference to the pre-training of a universal reward world model that captures fundamental causal relationships, thereby enabling zero-shot reward design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient method for automated reward design that overcomes the limitations of current LLM-based approaches. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Mitigate Spurious Correlations:** Address the problem where LLMs introduce semantically plausible but causally irrelevant reward components. Such components can lead to "Optimization Collapse" (e.g., in `ShadowHandLiftUnderarm`, where instantaneous lifting speed provides ambiguous signals causing gradient noise) and "Specification Gaming" (e.g., in `ShadowHandScissors`, where maintaining scissors slightly open accrues rewards without achieving the true goal). The motivation is to guide LLMs with reliable prior knowledge to prevent these issues.
2. **Enable Zero-Shot Reward Design:** Eliminate the need for computationally expensive and time-consuming iterative environmental feedback during reward function synthesis. Current methods require costly "trial-evaluation-mutation-feedback" loops to refine reward hypotheses. The goal is to allow LLMs to generate effective reward functions in a single pass for unseen tasks.
3. **Improve Computational Efficiency and Reduce Latency:** By achieving zero-shot reward generation, the objective is to significantly reduce the computational overhead associated with evaluating and refining reward functions, thereby accelerating the acquisition of new robotic skills.
4. **Leverage Inter-Task Reward Relationships:** Recognize that reward components across distinct tasks often exhibit shared causal structures within identical dynamical systems. The motivation is to explicitly model these causal topological relationships, moving beyond case-by-case reward design.
5. **Develop a Causal Reward World Model (CRWM):** Propose and construct a CRWM that explicitly captures the causal topological relationships between candidate reward components and task-targeted physical variables. This model is intended to serve as a universal, task-irrelevant causal prior.
6. **Provide Interpretability:** By explicitly constructing causal graphs, the work aims to offer a "white-box" paradigm, allowing for better understanding of why specific reward components are included or excluded, contrasting with the often opaque nature of correlation-based methods.

In essence, the research is motivated by the hypothesis that precisely capturing the causal relationships among reward components and task objectives through multi-task pre-training can enable the inference of reward function consequences without extensive empirical training, leading to more efficient, robust, and generalizable automated reward design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Causal Reward World Model (CRWM) framework operates in three synergistic phases to distill reward-relevant causal knowledge from multi-task offline data and guide zero-shot reward generation.

#### 3.1. Phase 1: Structural Prior Extraction via Causal Foundation Models

This initial phase aims to generate a coarse structural prior.
1. **Hierarchical Data Collection:** The authors construct an offline dataset, $D_{off}$, split into macroscopic ($D_{macro}$) and microscopic ($D_{micro}$) streams.
 - **Reward Component Normalization:** From various ARD frameworks, physically meaningful reward components are extracted (e.g., target distance, joint velocity). Task-specific names for semantically equivalent quantities (e.g., `scissors_position`, `pot_position`) are normalized into a global pool of $d$ shared structural variables $V = \{v_1, \dots, v_d\}$.
 - **Interventional Data Generation:** $N$ diverse weight vectors $\omega^{(n)}$ are uniformly sampled to form different reward functions. Training and executing policies with these functions serves as interventional manipulations, akin to Pearl’s do-operations.
 - **Dataset Components:**
 - $D_{macro} = \{(\omega^{(n)}, F^{(n)})\}_{n=1}^N$: Records final fitness scores $F^{(n)}$ for each reward weight configuration $\omega^{(n)}$. This macro-level data provides task-level evidence for how reward-component weights affect task success.
 - $D_{micro} = \{(s^{(n)}_0, s^{(n)}_1, \dots, s^{(n)}_T)\}_{n=1}^N$: Records dense, per-step trajectories of structural variables $s_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$, capturing instantaneous values of physical variables. This fine-grained data is reserved for modeling transient interactions and calibrating the coarse prior.
2. **Structural Prior Extraction:** The pre-trained causal foundation model LimiX is applied to $D_{macro}$ to estimate causal relationships among reward components.
 - LimiX outputs a continuous-valued raw causal effects matrix $M_{raw} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$.
 - This is converted into a coarse structural prior $\langle M_{prior}, M_{conf} \rangle$:
 - $M_{prior}$: Encodes the directed topology (initial edge existence and directions).
 - $M_{conf} \in^{d \times d}$: Represents normalized absolute effect magnitudes, serving as a structural confidence matrix. Larger absolute causal effects correspond to higher confidence.
 The authors acknowledge that this coarse prior may still contain biased causal effects and structural hallucinations.

#### 3.2. Phase 2: Joint Optimization Module for CRWM Distillation

This phase refines the coarse structural prior using micro-level trajectories, distilling the final CRWM skeleton $M^*_{inv}$. This addresses the limitations of static topologies and inaccuracies in the initial prior.
1. **Unified Optimization Objective:** The distillation is driven by minimizing a loss function (Eq. 4):
 $L = E_{(s_{t-1},s_t)\sim D_{micro}} [\|s_t - M_{inst}(s_{t-1})s_{t-1}\|^2_2] + \lambda h(M_{inv}) + L_{soft}$.
 - $M_{inv}$: A learnable causal skeleton that will converge to $M^*_{inv}$ (the CRWM).
 - $\theta$: Parameters for a state-dependent transient component.
 - The first term is a reconstruction loss, comparing observed transitions in $D_{micro}$ with predictions from a state-dependent instantaneous topology.
 - $\lambda h(M_{inv})$: An acyclicity regularization term (DAGMA) applied to $M_{inv}$ to ensure it forms a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), preventing circular dependencies in the reward-relevant causal prior.
 - $L_{soft}$: The Confidence-Aware Soft Fusion loss, described below.
2. **Explicit Mechanism Decoupling (EMD):** This module constructs the state-dependent instantaneous topology $M_{inst}(s_{t-1})$ (Eq. 6):
 $M_{inst}(s_{t-1}) = M_{inv} \odot (1_{d \times d} + M_{trans}(s_{t-1}; \theta))$.
 - $M_{trans}(s_{t-1}; \theta)$: A transient component parameterized by an MLP, which accounts for short-lived physical interactions (e.g., brief contacts or collisions) based on the previous state $s_{t-1}$.
 - The Hadamard product ($\odot$) ensures that $M_{trans}$ modulates the strengths of existing edges in $M_{inv}$ but cannot introduce new, unrelated edges, thus stabilizing the learned skeleton. This decoupling allows $M_{inv}$ to capture the stable reward-relevant structure while $M_{trans}$ handles state-dependent dynamic variations during reconstruction.
3. **Confidence-Aware Soft Fusion:** This term (Eq. 7) calibrates the coarse structural prior:
 $L_{soft} = \gamma \|M_{conf} \odot (M_{inv} - M_{prior})\|^2_F$.
 - It penalizes deviations of the learnable skeleton $M_{inv}$ from the initial coarse prior $M_{prior}$.
 - The loss is weighted by the structural confidence matrix $M_{conf}$, meaning stronger regularization is applied to high-confidence edges from the prior, while low-confidence edges are more flexible to be updated by micro-level data.
4. **Optimization:** The objective is solved using the Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) to balance reconstruction accuracy, DAG acyclicity, and prior calibration. Upon convergence, $M_{inv}$ becomes $M^*_{inv}$, the final CRWM.

#### 3.3. Phase 3: Causal Automated Reward Design (Causal-ARD)

This final phase converts the distilled CRWM into a format usable by LLMs to guide zero-shot reward generation.
1. **Causal Graph to Prompt Translation:**
 - To translate the continuous $M^*_{inv}$ matrix into structured text, one-dimensional 2-means clustering is applied to the absolute edge causal effects ($W = \{|M^{*,i,j}_{inv}|\}$).
 - An adaptive threshold $\tau$ (midpoint of cluster centroids) is used to distinguish dominant causal effects from numerical noise.
 - Active causal edges exceeding $\tau$ are extracted and serialized into explicit topological triplets (source, target, causal effect).
 - These triplets are embedded into system instructions to create a "Causal Topology Prompt" for the LLM.
2. **Causal Pruning:** When prompted with the textual task description, raw observation space, and the Causal Topology Prompt, the LLM generates a Python reward function.
 - The LLM performs "causal pruning" by assigning zero coefficients to reward terms associated with identified spurious correlates (i.e., variables or sub-paths that lack a valid causal path to the 'score' node according to the CRWM).
 - Reward components whose required embodiment-specific variables are not present in the observation space are also excluded.
3. **Executable Output:** This process yields an executable Python reward function in a single pass for unseen environments, bypassing iterative trial-and-error and enabling zero-shot deployment with the underlying RL algorithm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate the CRWM across various dimensions, including zero-shot task performance, computational efficiency, generalization capabilities, and real-world applicability.

**Zero-Shot Task Performance and Efficiency (Dexterity Tasks):**
- **Performance:** Experiments on 6 unseen Dexterity continuous control tasks (out of 20, 14 used for pre-training) demonstrate that CRWM-guided reward generation achieves performance comparable to or surpassing state-of-the-art iterative ARD baselines (Eureka, URDP). For instance, CRWM achieved Success Rates (SRs) of 0.92 on "Catch Over 2 Underarm" and 0.87 on "Lift Underarm."
- **Efficiency:** CRWM achieves this performance in a zero-shot setting (Evolutionary Search Iterations, ESI = 0), meaning it generates executable reward functions in a single pass without feedback-driven refinement. In contrast, URDP required 1-4 ESIs and Eureka consistently required 4 ESIs to converge, each ESI involving costly large-scale RL environment interactions.
- **LLM Guidance:** The "ZS-LLM" baseline, relying purely on semantic reasoning without causal guidance, consistently showed weak performance (e.g., near-zero SR on dynamic and long-horizon tasks), highlighting the insufficiency of semantic reasoning alone for robust reward generation in physical control tasks. CRWM's causal pruning mechanism is shown to provide necessary guidance.

**Ablation Experiments (Dexterity Tasks):**
Ablation studies investigated the contributions of key CRWM components:
- **Necessity of Structural Prior:** "CRWM w.o. Prior" (removing the coarse structural prior) significantly reduced average SR (from 0.85 to 0.36), indicating that offline transition data alone is insufficient for stable structure estimation.
- **Effect of EMD:** "CRWM w.o. EMD" (removing the state-dependent interaction term) led to unstable performance and a substantial drop in average SR (from 0.85 to 0.35), suggesting EMD improves the stability and accuracy of the learned structure by capturing transient physical interactions.
- **Confidence-Aware Soft Fusion:** "CRWM w.o. Soft-Fusion" (replacing soft regularization with a rigid mask) degraded performance (average SR dropped from 0.85 to 0.38), demonstrating the importance of flexible integration between the prior and data.
- **Learned vs. LLM-Inferred Prior:** "Causal-ARD w. LLM-Prior" (using an LLM-inferred semantic causal topology instead of CRWM) performed substantially worse (average SR dropped from 0.85 to 0.09). This indicates that the performance gain originates from the CRWM's learned causal topology rather than the LLM's inherent semantic causal inference ability.
- **DAGMA Acyclicity:** Removing the DAGMA acyclicity regularization (`CRWM w.o. DAG`) resulted in a decline in average SR (e.g., 0.88 to 0.81 on Kettle), suggesting its role in regularizing the learned causal skeleton.

**Cross-Embodiment and Cross-Environment Generalization (ManiSkill2):**
- CRWM, trained on the full 20-task Dexterity suite, demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to three unseen ManiSkill2 tasks (different robot embodiments, physics environments, and manipulation types).
- It achieved high SRs (e.g., 0.89 on "OpenCabinetDoor," 0.80 on "TurnFaucet," 0.64 on "PickCube") without any interaction data or task-specific adaptation for these new environments. This indicates that CRWM captures transferable causal structures across substantial domain shifts.

**Sim-to-Real Experiments (CASBOT W1 Mobile Manipulator):**
- To evaluate practical feasibility, human-teleoperated kinematic trajectories were used with CRWM-guided reward functions for "PickCube," "LiftUnderarm," and "Scissors" tasks.
- CRWM-guided rewards achieved high Pairwise Distinguishability Accuracy (average AUC ≈ 96.5%) in distinguishing successful from failed trajectories. This contrasts with the ZS-LLM baseline, which showed limited distinguishability (average AUC ≈ 53.9%). These results suggest that CRWM supports zero-shot reward generation that remains valid on physical hardware.

**Prevalence of Reward Failure Modes:**
- A quantitative analysis across 20 Dexterity tasks showed that baseline ARD methods (Eureka, URDP) frequently exhibited "Optimization Collapse" (21.2% for Eureka, 12.6% for URDP) and "Specification Gaming" (10.4% for Eureka, 4.7% for URDP).
- CRWM-guided rewards, through causal pruning, consistently showed improved alignment between reward signals and task outcomes, mitigating these failure modes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of Automated Reward Design (ARD) for reinforcement learning. The proposed Causal Reward World Model (CRWM) introduces a novel approach with several potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Zero-Shot Reward Design:** The most immediate impact is the capability for zero-shot reward generation. By providing a causally informed prior, CRWM allows LLMs to synthesize executable reward functions for unseen tasks in a single pass without requiring iterative environmental feedback. This eliminates the need for computationally intensive and time-consuming "trial-and-error" loops, fundamentally changing the efficiency of robotic skill acquisition.
2. **Reduced Computational Costs and Design Latency:** The ability to generate effective reward functions without costly evolutionary search iterations significantly reduces the computational overhead and wall-clock time required to design rewards for new skills. This can accelerate research and deployment of autonomous systems by making reward engineering less of a bottleneck.
3. **Improved Robustness and Interpretability:** By explicitly modeling causal relationships and guiding LLMs to perform causal pruning, CRWM inherently mitigates problems like "Optimization Collapse" and "Specification Gaming," which plague correlation-driven methods. This leads to more robust reward functions that align better with true task objectives. Furthermore, the "white-box" nature of explicit causal graphs enhances interpretability, providing insights into why certain reward components are effective or spurious.
4. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated strong generalization across unseen tasks, different robotic embodiments (Shadow Hand vs. ManiSkill2 manipulators), and varying physical environments suggests that CRWM captures transferable, reward-relevant causal structures. This "train-once, infer-anywhere" paradigm could lead to more widely applicable ARD systems, reducing the need for extensive retraining for every new robot or task variation.
5. **New Paradigm for ARD:** This work shifts the ARD paradigm from a purely semantic, correlation-based, task-wise inference to a causal-based approach grounded in pre-trained world models. This paradigm has the potential to influence future research directions in grounding LLM reasoning in physical realities and causal mechanisms.
6. **Practical Applicability:** The validation on real-world hardware, demonstrating high alignment accuracy for CRWM-guided rewards with human-teleoperated trajectories, indicates the practical utility of the approach for physical robotic systems.

While the approach requires an initial offline pre-training cost, the benefits of zero-shot deployment and robust causal reasoning suggest a significant step towards more autonomous and efficient robotic skill generation, particularly in complex manipulation tasks. Future work would focus on reducing this pre-training cost and expanding the scope of observable physical variables to enhance the model's versatility.
