# Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization (ARPO) trains Large Language Models to act as agents in multi-turn tool interaction scenarios. The method leverages observed increases in token entropy after tool calls to adaptively branch sampling, which leads to superior performance on 13 reasoning and deep search benchmarks while achieving comparable accuracy with half the tool-call budget of previous methods.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) are primarily effective for single-turn reasoning tasks.
- Current trajectory-level agentic RL algorithms struggle to effectively balance an LLM's long-horizon reasoning with dynamic, multi-turn tool interactions, often overlooking crucial stepwise behaviors.
- LLMs exhibit significant uncertainty (high token entropy) immediately following external tool-call feedback, which prior RL methods do not adequately address for fine-grained exploration.

## Method

- ARPO introduces an ==entropy-based adaptive rollout mechanism== that dynamically branches exploration paths at high-uncertainty tool-call steps, balancing global trajectory sampling with fine-grained step-level exploration.
- It employs an ==Advantage Attribution Estimation== method, specifically a soft setting, to enable LLMs to better internalize the advantage differences of stepwise tool-use interactions within branched trajectories.
- The framework integrates a ==hierarchical reward design==, inspired by Tool-Star, which considers correctness, adherence to tool invocation format, and a bonus for effective multi-tool collaboration.

## Results

- ARPO consistently outperformed trajectory-level RL algorithms (GRPO, Reinforce++, DAPO) across **13** benchmarks in computational reasoning, knowledge reasoning, and deep search, demonstrating an average accuracy improvement of approximately **4%**.
- The algorithm achieved superior overall accuracy using only **half** the tool-call budget compared to GRPO during training, highlighting significant efficiency gains through its selective exploration strategy.
- ARPO-aligned models demonstrated robust scaling and generalization, achieving high Pass@K scores (e.g., **61.2%** Pass@5 on GAIA for Qwen-14B) and showing effectiveness on deep search tasks with limited training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs consistently show a sharp increase in token entropy immediately after receiving feedback from external tools, signaling significant uncertainty and critical decision points for exploration.
> - Balancing broad trajectory exploration with adaptive, step-level exploration guided by this observed uncertainty is crucial for efficiently training LLM agents for complex multi-turn tool interaction.
> - Attributing advantage at a fine-grained, stepwise level, particularly within branched reasoning paths, facilitates better learning of complex tool-use strategies compared to relying solely on trajectory-level rewards.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and Agentic AI.

**Evolution from RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** The authors begin by acknowledging the success of "large-scale Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)" in enhancing LLM capabilities for single-turn reasoning tasks. This refers to a prominent paradigm where LLMs are fine-tuned using human feedback or verifiable outcomes (e.g., correctness of a mathematical solution), exemplified by models from OpenAI, DeepSeek-AI, and Qwen. RLVR has significantly improved LLMs' alignment with human preferences and task-specific performance.

**Emergence of Agentic Reinforcement Learning:** However, the paper highlights that real-world reasoning often demands more than single-turn interactions. This leads to the domain of "Agentic Reinforcement Learning (Agentic RL)," where LLMs are trained to act as agents, engaging in dynamic, multi-turn interactions with external tools and environments. This paradigm shift, from static task-solving to dynamic agent-environment reasoning, is a critical frontier for endowing LLMs with greater autonomy and problem-solving versatility, moving beyond mere text generation to proactive problem-solving.

**Limitations of Current Agentic RL Approaches:** The core innovation of ARPO is framed by addressing a significant limitation in existing agentic RL methods. Current approaches, typically "trajectory-level algorithms" like GRPO or DAPO, sample complete tool-use trajectories and assign rewards based on the final outcome. While some efforts have focused on designing sophisticated reward functions to mitigate issues like tool overuse and sparse rewards, the authors argue that these methods often "overlook a crucial aspect of training LLM-based agents: the multi-turn interaction loops between the LLM and the tool environment." They emphasize the need to discover effective *stepwise* tool-use behaviors, which trajectory-level methods struggle to capture due to their emphasis on full rollout comparisons rather than fine-grained, step-level exploration.

**Inspiration from Entropy-Based RL:** To address this, the paper draws inspiration from "entropy-based RL studies," which quantify the uncertainty in a model's generation process. By applying this to LLM-based agents, the authors identify a critical behavioral pattern: a "sharp rise in token entropy following each tool call." This observation forms the empirical basis for ARPO, suggesting that tool interaction introduces significant uncertainty, which is currently underexplored by existing RL methods.

Thus, ARPO contributes to the broader landscape by moving beyond coarse-grained, trajectory-level RL for LLM agents, proposing a finer-grained, uncertainty-aware approach that more effectively aligns with the interactive nature of tool-use. It aims to develop LLM agents that are not only capable of long-horizon planning but also adept at adaptive, multi-turn interactions with external tools.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization (ARPO) stems from a critical observation regarding the behavior of Large Language Models (LLMs) when interacting with external tools in multi-turn reasoning scenarios. While large-scale Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has significantly advanced LLM capabilities in single-turn tasks, its application to complex, dynamic, multi-turn tool-use remains challenged.

**The core motivations are:**
1. **Inadequate Balance in Current RL for Multi-Turn Tool Use**: Existing RL algorithms, primarily trajectory-level methods, struggle to effectively balance an LLM's intrinsic long-horizon reasoning abilities with its proficiency in dynamic, multi-turn interactions involving external tools. They often treat tool interactions as discrete events within a complete trajectory, rather than as a source of dynamic feedback that can significantly alter the model's reasoning path.
2. **Observed Uncertainty Post-Tool Interaction**: A key empirical finding that motivates ARPO is the observation that LLMs exhibit "highly uncertain behavior, characterized by an increase in the entropy distribution of generated tokens, immediately following interactions with external tools." This indicates that external tool-call feedback introduces substantial uncertainty into the LLM's reasoning process, highlighting latent behaviors and decision points that current trajectory-level RL methods tend to overlook. Such methods overemphasize complete roll-out sampling, neglecting the exploration of fine-grained behavior at each tool-use step.

**Based on these motivations, the key objectives of ARPO are:**
1. **Develop a Novel Agentic RL Algorithm for Multi-Turn LLM Agents**: To propose and implement an agentic RL algorithm specifically "tailored for training multi-turn LLM-based agents" that can effectively handle the complexities of dynamic tool interactions.
2. **Incorporate Entropy-Based Adaptive Rollout**: To design a mechanism that leverages the observed uncertainty (high token entropy) after tool usage. This mechanism should dynamically balance global trajectory sampling with "step-level sampling," thereby promoting exploration at steps with high uncertainty after tool usage. The goal is to encourage the policy model to "adaptively branch sampling during high-entropy tool-call rounds."
3. **Enable Internalization of Stepwise Advantage Differences**: To integrate an "advantage attribution estimation" method that allows LLMs to better internalize the advantage differences associated with stepwise tool-use interactions, especially in scenarios where trajectories might share initial segments but diverge later due to branching.
4. **Achieve Superior Performance and Efficiency**: To demonstrate that ARPO can consistently outperform traditional trajectory-level RL algorithms across various challenging benchmarks (computational reasoning, knowledge reasoning, deep search) while also offering a more "scalable solution for aligning LLM-based agents with real-time dynamic environments," notably by achieving improved performance with a reduced tool-use budget.

In essence, ARPO aims to equip LLM agents with a more sophisticated and efficient learning mechanism that is sensitive to the dynamic nature and inherent uncertainties of multi-turn tool-augmented reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ARPO introduces a principled agentic RL algorithm designed to guide LLMs in exploring step-wise tool-use behaviors under entropy-based guidance. Its methodology comprises three core components: Entropy-based Adaptive Rollout, Advantage Attribution Estimation, and a solid Theoretical Foundation.

#### 3.1. Entropy-based Adaptive Rollout

This is the cornerstone of ARPO, designed to balance broad trajectory exploration with fine-grained, uncertainty-driven exploration at critical interaction points.
1. **Rollout Initialization**: Given a global rollout size `M`, the LLM first generates `N` complete trajectories through standard trajectory-level sampling. The remaining `M - N` budget is reserved for partial (branching) sampling. The initial entropy of the first `k` tokens in each of these `N` trajectories is computed (`H_initial`).
2. **Entropy Variation Monitoring**: During agentic reasoning, after each tool invocation and reception of feedback, the model generates `k` additional tokens. The entropy of these new tokens (`H_t`) is measured. A normalized change in entropy (`∆H_t = Normalize(H_t - H_initial)`) is calculated. A positive `∆H_t` signals increased uncertainty, indicating a potentially fruitful point for further exploration.
3. **Entropy-based Adaptive Beaming**: This mechanism dictates branching behavior. A partial sampling probability `P_t = α + β · ∆H_t` is computed, where `α` is a base probability and `β` is a stability entropy value. If `P_t` exceeds a predefined threshold `τ`, the model initiates `Z` additional partial reasoning paths (branches) from the current node. Otherwise, it continues along the current trajectory. This adaptively focuses exploration resources on high-uncertainty regions.
4. **Termination**: The rollout process continues until the total number of forked paths (`ˆZ`) reaches the partial sampling budget (`M - N`), or all paths produce a final answer. If the budget isn't fully utilized, additional independent trajectory-level samples are generated to meet the `M` total.

This adaptive rollout mechanism significantly reduces computational complexity compared to naive branching, claiming a reduction from O(n^2) to between O(n log n) and O(n^2) per rollout, by selectively exploring high-entropy steps.

#### 3.2. Advantage Attribution Estimation

The adaptive rollout naturally creates trajectories with shared initial segments and divergent branched paths. ARPO proposes two settings to handle advantage assignment in this structure:
1. **Hard Advantage Estimation**: Explicitly differentiates shared and individual tokens. Individual tokens receive normalized rewards (`ˆA_i,t`), while shared tokens are assigned the average advantage across all `d` trajectories containing that shared segment (`ˆA_shared_i,t = (1/d) Σ ˆA_i,t`).
2. **Soft Advantage Estimation**: Integrates this distinction latently within the policy optimization framework, specifically by adapting the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective. The importance sampling ratio `r_i,t(θ)` naturally assigns identical weights to shared prefix tokens across branched trajectories, effectively aligning their advantage contributions. The paper notes that the soft setting achieves consistently higher rewards and greater stability in practice and is the default for ARPO.

**Hierarchical Reward Design**: ARPO leverages a reward function inspired by Tool-Star, which considers correctness, adherence to tool invocation format, and a bonus (`r_M`) for effective multi-tool collaboration (e.g., using both search and Python). This comprehensive reward encourages both accurate and efficient tool usage.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Foundation

The paper provides a theoretical justification for its adaptive partial rollout mechanism by introducing the **Generalized Policy Gradient (GPG) Theorem**. This theorem allows for the dynamic segmentation of a Transformer-based policy's output tokens into "macro actions" (`MA_i`), where each `MA_i` can be a segment of tokens. The theorem asserts that policy optimization can be effectively conducted using these macro actions, generalizing the traditional Policy Gradient Theorem which operates on single-token actions. This theoretical framework provides a robust basis for ARPO's ability to perform partial rollouts and branch at macro-level steps, particularly around tool calls.

**Implementation Details**: In practice, ARPO builds upon the VERL framework. Tool invocation results are excluded from loss calculations to prevent bias, focusing only on text reasoning and tool request tokens. Training adheres to a cold-start SFT with RL paradigm, combining supervised fine-tuning (using LLaMAFactory and Tool-Star/STILL datasets) with a subsequent RL phase. Different configurations (batch sizes, rollout parameters, entropy weights, KL divergence coefficients) are applied for Deep Reasoning and Deep Search tasks, with deep search employing more extensive web browsing capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across 13 challenging benchmarks in computational reasoning, knowledge reasoning, and deep search domains consistently demonstrate ARPO's superiority over existing trajectory-level RL algorithms.

#### 4.1. Token Entropy Analysis (Pilot Experiment)

- **Observation**: A sharp and consistent increase in token entropy (uncertainty) is observed in the LLM immediately following each tool-call feedback.
- **Implication**: This critical finding validates ARPO's foundational hypothesis: external tool-calls introduce significant uncertainty, which is largely overlooked by trajectory-level RL methods that prioritize complete rollout sampling. This highlights the potential for fine-grained, step-level exploration.
- **Type of Tool Impact**: Search engine feedback introduces more uncertainty than Python interpreter feedback, likely due to the less structured and more diverse nature of search results.

#### 4.2. Performance on Mathematical & Knowledge-Intensive Reasoning (Table 1)

- **Superiority**: ARPO consistently outperforms all compared trajectory-level RL algorithms (GRPO, Reinforce++, DAPO) across 10 datasets. It achieves an average accuracy improvement of approximately 4% across various LLM backbones (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B).
- **Ineffectiveness of Prompting**: Tool-integrated prompting methods (TIR Prompting) show limited or even negative impact compared to direct reasoning, suggesting that mere prompt engineering is insufficient for guiding complex tool-use behaviors.
- **Limitations of Trajectory-Level RL**: While traditional RL methods show improvements over direct reasoning, they don't fully unlock the potential of tool-integrated reasoning, especially in multi-turn scenarios, confirming the need for ARPO's step-level focus.

#### 4.3. Performance on Deep Search Tasks (Table 2)

- **Exceptional Generalization**: ARPO demonstrates remarkable performance even with relatively smaller models (Qwen3-8B and 14B) and a limited training dataset (only 1K RL samples), significantly surpassing direct reasoning baselines and even some larger/closed-source models like GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1-671B on specific benchmarks (e.g., GAIA, HLE).
- **Importance of Step-Level Exploration**: ARPO consistently beats GRPO, with notable improvements (e.g., 6% on GAIA and WebwalkerQA), underscoring that its balanced global and step-level sampling is crucial for complex deep search scenarios requiring frequent tool invocations and adaptive reasoning.

#### 4.4. Quantitative Analysis (Tool-Call Efficiency & Sampling at Scale)

- **Tool-Call Efficiency (Figure 7)**: ARPO achieves superior overall accuracy using only *half* the tool-call budget required by GRPO during training. This significant efficiency gain is attributed to ARPO's selective exploration strategy, branching only at high-entropy tool-call steps, thus optimizing resource allocation.
- **Sampling at Scale (Pass@K, Figure 6)**: ARPO shows consistent improvements and robust scaling trends across Pass@3 and Pass@5 metrics. The Qwen-14B model, aligned with ARPO, achieves remarkable Pass@5 scores (e.g., 61.2% on GAIA, 59% on xbench-DR), indicating that ARPO efficiently expands the sampling space, leading to both inference efficiency and sampling diversity.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies of Browser Agents (Table 3)

- **Necessity of Browser Agents**: The study confirms that relying solely on web snippets is insufficient for deep search tasks. Web content fetching and browsing capabilities are essential for performance.
- **Impact of Browser Agent Capability**: Performance scales positively with the capability of the external browser agent, demonstrating that more powerful search agents enable better information integration and key detail extraction.

#### 4.6. Scaling Analysis of ARPO Hyperparameters (Figure 8)

- **Entropy Value (∆H_t)**: Performance peaks at a moderate entropy value (0.4), indicating that a balanced integration of entropy as a clue for partial sampling is optimal. Over-reliance on entropy (e.g., at 1.0) can reduce sampling diversity.
- **Initial Sampling Size (N)**: An optimal balance in the global-to-partial sampling ratio (e.g., N=8 for M=16) is crucial for performance. Complete global sampling (N=16 for M=16) degrades performance.
- **Global Rollout Size (M)**: Increasing the global rollout size consistently enhances model performance, confirming ARPO's scalability.

In summary, ARPO not only addresses a critical limitation in current LLM agent training but also provides a more efficient, robust, and scalable solution, particularly for complex, multi-turn tasks involving external tools.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization (ARPO) marks a significant advancement in the field of training Large Language Models (LLMs) to act as sophisticated agents, particularly in dynamic, multi-turn reasoning environments augmented with external tools. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging a Critical Gap in Agentic RL**: ARPO directly addresses the inadequacy of existing trajectory-level RL algorithms in balancing LLMs' long-horizon reasoning with their proficiency in multi-turn tool interactions. By shifting the focus to fine-grained, step-level behavior learning, ARPO unlocks new potential for LLMs to engage more effectively and adaptively with external environments. This is a crucial step towards creating truly autonomous and intelligent LLM agents.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Reduced Costs**: One of ARPO's most impactful findings is its ability to achieve superior performance using only *half the tool-use budget* compared to traditional methods like GRPO. In scenarios involving frequent API calls to external tools (e.g., search engines, code interpreters), the computational and financial costs can be substantial. ARPO's entropy-based adaptive rollout mechanism intelligently focuses exploration on high-uncertainty decision points, drastically reducing redundant tool calls. This efficiency makes agentic RL more practical and scalable for real-world deployment.

3. **Robustness and Generalizability Across Domains**: The comprehensive evaluation across 13 challenging benchmarks in computational reasoning, knowledge reasoning, and deep search domains demonstrates ARPO's broad applicability and robust performance. Its consistent outperformance of strong baselines, even on highly complex tasks like GAIA and HLE, and its effectiveness across different LLM backbones (Qwen, Llama series), highlight its strong generalization capabilities. This suggests ARPO can be a foundational algorithm for training versatile LLM agents across a wide spectrum of applications.

4. **Novelty in Exploration Strategy**: The core innovation of ARPO lies in its entropy-based adaptive rollout mechanism. By quantifying token entropy after tool usage and using this as a signal for dynamic branching and selective exploration, ARPO introduces a novel and intelligent way for agents to explore their action space. This uncertainty-aware exploration is more aligned with human-like problem-solving, where difficult or ambiguous steps warrant more focused attention and alternative strategies.

5. **Improved Internalization of Stepwise Advantage**: The Advantage Attribution Estimation, particularly in its stable soft setting, enables LLMs to better understand and internalize the impact of individual tool-use steps on overall success. This fine-grained feedback is essential for learning complex, multi-step reasoning strategies, moving beyond a simple pass/fail outcome for an entire trajectory.

6. **Theoretical Justification**: The introduction of the Generalized Policy Gradient (GPG) Theorem provides a strong theoretical underpinning for ARPO's macro-action segmentation and partial rollout strategy. This adds confidence in the algorithm's design principles and opens avenues for further theoretical development in agentic RL for Transformers.

7. **Catalyst for Advanced LLM Applications**: By creating more capable and efficient LLM agents, ARPO could accelerate the development of advanced applications in areas such as scientific discovery, complex problem-solving, automated web research, and intelligent personal assistants. Agents trained with ARPO could autonomously navigate complex digital environments, integrate information from diverse sources, and perform multi-step reasoning with unprecedented efficiency and accuracy.

In conclusion, ARPO represents a significant stride in developing more sophisticated and practical LLM agents. Its innovative approach to uncertainty-aware, step-level exploration and efficient resource utilization positions it as a key technique for scaling agentic AI and enabling LLMs to tackle increasingly complex, real-world challenges.
