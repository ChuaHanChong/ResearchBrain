# Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization (ARPO) trains Large Language Models to act as agents in multi-turn tool interaction scenarios. The method leverages observed increases in token entropy after tool calls to adaptively branch sampling, which leads to superior performance on 13 reasoning and deep search benchmarks while achieving comparable accuracy with half the tool-call budget of previous methods.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) are primarily effective for single-turn reasoning tasks.
- Current trajectory-level agentic RL algorithms struggle to effectively balance an LLM's long-horizon reasoning with dynamic, multi-turn tool interactions, often overlooking crucial stepwise behaviors.
- LLMs exhibit significant uncertainty (high token entropy) immediately following external tool-call feedback, which prior RL methods do not adequately address for fine-grained exploration.

## Method

- ARPO introduces an ==entropy-based adaptive rollout mechanism== that dynamically branches exploration paths at high-uncertainty tool-call steps, balancing global trajectory sampling with fine-grained step-level exploration.
- It employs an ==Advantage Attribution Estimation== method, specifically a soft setting, to enable LLMs to better internalize the advantage differences of stepwise tool-use interactions within branched trajectories.
- The framework integrates a ==hierarchical reward design==, inspired by Tool-Star, which considers correctness, adherence to tool invocation format, and a bonus for effective multi-tool collaboration.

## Results

- ARPO consistently outperformed trajectory-level RL algorithms (GRPO, Reinforce++, DAPO) across **13** benchmarks in computational reasoning, knowledge reasoning, and deep search, demonstrating an average accuracy improvement of approximately **4%**.
- The algorithm achieved superior overall accuracy using only **half** the tool-call budget compared to GRPO during training, highlighting significant efficiency gains through its selective exploration strategy.
- ARPO-aligned models demonstrated robust scaling and generalization, achieving high Pass@K scores (e.g., **61.2%** Pass@5 on GAIA for Qwen-14B) and showing effectiveness on deep search tasks with limited training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs consistently show a sharp increase in token entropy immediately after receiving feedback from external tools, signaling significant uncertainty and critical decision points for exploration.
> - Balancing broad trajectory exploration with adaptive, step-level exploration guided by this observed uncertainty is crucial for efficiently training LLM agents for complex multi-turn tool interaction.
> - Attributing advantage at a fine-grained, stepwise level, particularly within branched reasoning paths, facilitates better learning of complex tool-use strategies compared to relying solely on trajectory-level rewards.
