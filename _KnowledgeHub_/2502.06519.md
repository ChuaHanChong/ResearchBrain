# SIREN: Semantic, Initialization-Free Registration of Multi-Robot Gaussian Splatting Maps

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and Tsinghua University developed SIREN, a method for registering multi-robot Gaussian Splatting maps without requiring initial pose estimates or raw image data. It leverages open-vocabulary semantic information to robustly align maps, demonstrating up to 465x smaller translation errors and superior photometric quality compared to baselines on various real-world and benchmark datasets.

## Problem

- Multi-robot systems need to merge high-fidelity 3D scene representations for large-scale mapping and complex tasks.
- Existing radiance field registration methods often depend on strong initial pose estimates, raw camera data, or struggle in unstructured environments.
- Traditional map representations lack the detailed visual and semantic information crucial for advanced robotic applications like dexterous manipulation.

## Method

- **SIREN** introduces a three-stage pipeline (Semantic, Coarse, Fine) to register ==Gaussian Splatting (GSplat)== maps by first distilling open-vocabulary semantic features into the maps.
- It employs a robust, initialization-free coarse registration that matches semantically augmented Gaussians, utilizing a closed-form solution with ==RANSAC== for geometric alignment.
- A subsequent fine photometric registration step refines the alignment using ==novel-view synthesis== from semantically filtered rendered images, followed by ==bundle adjustment==.

## Results

- SIREN variants consistently achieved the lowest geometric errors, with up to **465x** smaller translation errors and **1.14x to 8.89x** smaller rotation errors than baselines on standard radiance field and mobile robot datasets.
- The method demonstrated superior photometric quality (higher PSNR, SSIM, lower LPIPS) and accurate, artifact-free fused maps compared to baselines in qualitative evaluations.
- It successfully fused maps from heterogeneous robots with limited workspaces (manipulators, drones, quadrupeds), enabling collaborative mapping of larger environments without external assistance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Open-vocabulary semantics can effectively guide initialization-free map registration by identifying feature-rich regions and deriving robust correspondences between Gaussians.
> - The combination of robust coarse geometric registration and high-fidelity photometric refinement is essential for achieving accurate and photorealistic fusion of radiance field maps.
> - Synthetically rendered images from a combined Gaussian Splatting map can be used for post-registration finetuning, improving visual fidelity and removing artifacts without additional real-world data collection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "SIREN" addresses a challenge in 3D scene representation and multi-robot mapping within the field of robotics. Traditionally, robots have relied on map representations such as point clouds and voxel maps for tasks like navigation and manipulation. However, these representations often lack the detailed visual and semantic information required for fine-grained robotic tasks.

Recent advancements in radiance fields, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting (GSplat), have provided more expressive and high-fidelity 3D scene representations. These models are typically trained from monocular images, often collected by a single robot in a single deployment.

The broader research landscape, however, frequently requires mapping large-scale environments or operating with limited-resource robots (e.g., limited battery life, limited workspace), necessitating the fusion of maps from multiple robots or multiple deployments. While previous work has explored fusing radiance field maps, these methods often require either a strong initial estimate of inter-map correspondences or direct access to camera poses and raw images, which may not be practical or available in real-world robotic scenarios. Furthermore, many existing methods struggle in unstructured environments.

SIREN positions itself by introducing a method for multi-robot GSplat map registration that explicitly aims to overcome these limitations. It contributes to the field by offering an initialization-free and semantics-grounded approach, distinguishing itself from prior work that relies on geometric cues, photometric loss, or external pose information. It leverages the inherent semantic information within scenes to improve the robustness and accuracy of map registration, a novel application of semantic radiance fields in this context. Compared to point cloud registration methods (e.g., ICP, RANSAC-GR) and more recent GSplat-specific methods (e.g., GaussReg, PhotoReg), SIREN seeks to provide a more robust solution that does not require camera poses or initial inter-map transforms, addressing a gap in autonomous and large-scale 3D mapping for robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SIREN is to develop a robust and accurate registration algorithm for multi-robot Gaussian Splatting (GSplat) maps. This algorithm is designed to operate under challenging conditions, specifically with zero access to camera poses, original images, or any initial inter-map relative transforms that are typically required by other registration methods.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several practical limitations and requirements in real-world robotics:

- **Scalability for Large Environments:** Single robots often have constraints such as limited battery life or workspace. To map large-scale areas, multiple robots or multiple deployments are necessary. Fusing the local maps generated by these individual robots into a coherent global map is a fundamental challenge.
- **High-Fidelity Scene Representation:** Traditional map representations (point clouds, voxels) often lack the expressiveness for fine-grained visual details and semantic information, which are increasingly important for advanced robotic tasks (e.g., dexterous open-vocabulary manipulation). Radiance fields like GSplat offer this high fidelity, but their fusion poses unique challenges.
- **Practical Operational Constraints:** In many real-world scenarios, robots may not be able to share their camera poses, raw images, or precise initial relative transformations due to privacy concerns, communication bandwidth limitations, or simply the nature of heterogeneous robot deployments. Methods that depend on these inputs have limited applicability.
- **Robustness in Unstructured Environments:** Prior radiance field registration methods often perform poorly in complex, unstructured real-world environments. A robust registration technique is needed to ensure reliability in diverse operational domains.
- **Leveraging Semantic Information:** Many real-world scenes inherently contain rich semantic information (e.g., objects like vehicles, furniture). The authors observe that this information is often unexploited in map registration. SIREN is motivated by the insight that explicitly leveraging semantics can significantly improve the robustness and accuracy of identifying correspondences and aligning maps, especially in initialization-free settings.

To achieve its objectives, SIREN proposes to harness semantics in three critical ways:
1. **Identification of Feature-Rich Regions:** Use semantics to locate informative areas in local maps where the registration problem is inherently better posed, thereby eliminating the need for initialization.
2. **Robust Correspondence Derivation:** Utilize robust semantic features to identify candidate correspondences between individual Gaussians across different maps, forming a reliable basis for geometric optimization.
3. **Photometric Refinement with Semantic Filtering:** Leverage the novel-view synthesis capabilities of GSplat maps for photometric refinement, using a semantics-based image filter to ensure reliable image-to-image registration and achieve a high-accuracy, non-rigid transformation for a photorealistic fused map.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SIREN operates through a three-stage Semantic, Coarse, and Fine (SCF) registration pipeline: (a) Semantic feature extraction and matching, (b) Coarse Gaussian-to-Gaussian geometric registration, and (c) Fine photometric registration. The process aims to register a source GSplat map to a target GSplat map, extendable to multiple local maps.

**A. Semantic Feature Extraction and Matching**

This initial stage establishes semantic understanding within the GSplat maps and identifies potential correspondences.

- **Semantic Gaussian Splatting (Semantic Distillation):** SIREN avoids training auxiliary models for semantic feature dimensionality reduction, unlike some prior works. Instead, it simultaneously trains a semantic field `ψ` alongside the GSplat model. This `ψ` field maps 3D points to `d`-dimensional semantic features (e.g., CLIP embeddings). It is parameterized by a multi-resolution neural hashgrid. The training loss `L` combines the standard photometric `L_gs` with terms that penalize `L2` distance and reward cosine-similarity (`ϕ`) between ground-truth and predicted semantic feature maps (`I_f`, `ˆI_f`). Crucially, SIREN leverages GSplat's accurate depth estimates to back-project points from the image plane to 3D, feeding these into `ψ` to predict semantic features for each pixel without requiring a separate proposal network.
- **Feature Extraction (Semantic Localization):** After training, each Gaussian in the GSplat map is augmented with a semantic attribute by querying the semantic field `ψ` at its mean `μ`. Open-vocabulary queries are used to compute a semantic relevancy score for each Gaussian, based on the cosine similarity between the Gaussian's semantic feature and the semantic embedding of a natural-language query versus a generic/null query. This identifies "feature-rich" areas. Gaussians can be post-processed (inflated or deflated) based on geometric or semantic distances to refine the set.
- **Feature Matching:** Candidate correspondences `E` are identified by matching Gaussians between the source (`P`) and target (`Q`) maps. This is done by computing the cosine similarity between their semantic embeddings. Each source Gaussian is matched to a random set of `M` target Gaussians within a specified geometric distance. The sampling probability can be uniform or proportional to cosine similarity. Efficient data structures like KD-trees are employed for speed. Geometric descriptors like Fast Point Feature Histograms (FPFH) can optionally augment this process.

**B. Coarse Gaussian-to-Gaussian Registration**

This stage computes an initial, robust non-rigid transformation using the semantically matched Gaussians.

- **Optimization Problem Formulation:** The problem is formulated as minimizing a cost function (Equation 2, 3) over scale (`s_c`), rotation (`R`), and translation (`t`) parameters. The cost function includes two terms: one minimizing the `L2` distance between transformed Gaussian means and target Gaussian means, and another minimizing the Frobenius norm difference between transformed covariance matrices and target covariance matrices.
- **Robustness with Weights and RANSAC:** A weight `w_ij` is introduced for each correspondence `(i,j) ∈ E`, proportional to the cosine similarity of their semantic embeddings. This reduces the influence of outliers (false correspondences). The RANSAC algorithm is used to further improve robustness by iteratively updating correspondences and computing optimal transformations.
- **Closed-Form Solution:** The optimization problem (Equation 3) is designed to be efficiently solvable in closed-form. The derivation (detailed in Appendix A) proceeds by first solving for optimal translation `t`, then reformulating the problem into a trace-minimization form. This allows for separate optimization of rotation `R` and scale `s_c`. The optimal `R` is found using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) (Equation 4), and the optimal `s_c` is then computed (Equation 5), followed by the optimal `t` (Equation 6). This approach is based on a method similar to Umeyama's algorithm for point set registration.

**C. Fine Photometric Registration**

This final stage refines the coarse transformation using visual information for high-accuracy and photorealistic map fusion.

- **Image Generation and Matching:**
 - **Identification of Overlap:** SIREN leverages the semantic submap and the coarse registration result to identify regions of overlap and corresponding camera poses between the source and target maps.
 - **Novel-View Synthesis:** GSplat's novel-view synthesis capabilities are used to render images at these corresponding poses from both maps.
 - **Semantic Filtering:** To ensure reliable image matching, rendered image pairs are filtered using image-level semantics from vision foundation models (e.g., CLIP). The cosine similarity between image embeddings determines which pairs are "sufficiently similar" for further processing.
- **Image Registration and Triangulation:** For the filtered image pairs, features are extracted using robust learned feature extractors like NetVLAD (global descriptors) and SuperPoint (local features), then matched. From these correspondences, the relative pose of the camera and estimated 3D locations of feature points are computed, providing an initial estimate of camera poses in a common reference frame.
- **Bundle Adjustment:** The initial camera pose estimates and 3D feature point locations are refined jointly through non-linear optimization, typically using iterative methods like Levenberg-Marquardt. This process minimizes reprojection errors, improving the accuracy of the camera poses in a common frame `B`.
- **Final Transformation Calculation:** With the refined camera poses in frame `B` and their corresponding poses in the source (`B_s`) and target (`B_t`) local maps, a final optimal transformation (scale `s_f`, rotation `R`, translation `t`) is computed by solving another registration problem (Equation 7). As `β_ij` approaches zero, this problem also approximates a closed-form solution (Equation 8), similar to the coarse registration, allowing composition of transformations to get the final transform between `B_s` and `B_t`. The source map is then transformed and merged with the target map to create a composite GSplat map.
- **Finetuning (Post-Registration):** After registration, the composite map can optionally be finetuned using new or existing data, or, as demonstrated, using *synthetically rendered images* from the combined GSplat map itself. This helps remove artifacts like "floaters" and further improves visual fidelity without real-world interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SIREN's performance was evaluated against several baselines, including GSplat-specific registration methods (GaussReg, PhotoReg) and classical point-cloud registration methods (RANSAC-GR, FGR, ICP, Colored-ICP). Evaluations were conducted on standard radiance field benchmarks (Mip-NeRF360 dataset) and real-world data collected by heterogeneous robotic platforms (quadruped, drone, manipulator). Metrics included geometric errors (Rotation Error (RE), Translation Error (TE), Scale Error (SE)) and photometric quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), along with computation time (CT).

**A. Mip-NeRF360 Dataset (Playroom, Truck, Room scenes)**

- **Geometric Evaluation (Table I):**
 - SIREN-R (with RANSAC) and SIREN-NR (without RANSAC for coarse registration) consistently achieved low geometric errors.
 - In the Playroom and Truck scenes, SIREN-R achieved the lowest rotation errors (1.14x to 8.89x smaller than baselines) and translation errors (6x to 46x smaller).
 - In the Room scene, SIREN-NR achieved the lowest translation and scale errors, with SIREN-R performing second-best.
 - Overall, SIREN variants achieved the lowest geometric errors across nearly all metrics and scenes.
 - While classical methods (RANSAC-GR, FGR) sometimes showed faster computation times, SIREN generally delivered higher accuracy.
- **Photometric Evaluation (Table III, Figure 3 & 4):**
 - SIREN-R achieved the best photometric performance (highest PSNR, SSIM, lowest LPIPS) in the Playroom scene.
 - In the Room scene, SIREN-NR achieved the best photometric performance, followed by SIREN-R.
 - In the Truck scene, RANSAC-GR showed high mean PSNR/SSIM, but Figure 3 and the high standard deviations revealed a critical failure mode where large parts of the scene were missing or poorly registered, indicating that mean photometric scores alone can be misleading without visual inspection.
 - Qualitative results (Figure 4) demonstrated that SIREN generated high-fidelity, accurately registered fused maps with precise geometric detail, in contrast to artifacts, duplicate objects, or significant misalignments observed in baselines like PhotoReg, GaussReg, and Colored-ICP.

**B. Mobile-Robot Mapping (Kitchen, Workshop, Apartment scenes)**

- **Geometric Performance (Table II):**
 - SIREN variants consistently achieved the best geometric performance across all mobile robot mapping scenarios.
 - In the Kitchen scene, SIREN-NR achieved significantly lower errors (approx. 160x RE, 465x TE, 488x SE) compared to the best baseline. SIREN-R followed closely.
 - In the Workshop scene, SIREN-R showed improvements of 415x RE and 1287x TE, with SIREN-NR having the lowest scale error (2962x improvement).
 - In the Apartment scene, SIREN-R again achieved the lowest errors across all geometric metrics.
 - GaussReg had the fastest computation time, but SIREN's accuracy gains were substantial.
- **Photometric Performance (Table IV, Figure 6):**
 - SIREN variants generally achieved superior photometric scores (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) in all scenes.
 - Qualitative evaluation (Figure 6) showed SIREN-R generating composite maps consistent with ground-truth, while GaussReg and PhotoReg maps contained conspicuous artifacts, blurry renderings, or duplicate objects due to inaccurate registration.

**C. Tabletop Mapping with Multiple Manipulators (Figure 7 & 8)**

- SIREN was qualitatively demonstrated to enable the fusion of GSplat maps created by two Franka manipulators with limited individual workspaces.
- This allowed the robots to collectively map a broader environment, overcoming individual workspace limitations without human assistance or hardware improvisation.
- The fused map created by SIREN provided a higher-fidelity reconstruction over the entire scene.

**D. Ablations (Tables V & VI)**

- The ablation study highlighted the importance of both coarse Gaussian-to-Gaussian and fine photometric registration steps.
- The fine registration step was shown to notably improve rotation errors (to sub-degree levels), achieve about 2x to 100x smaller translation errors, and generally reduce scale errors.
- Photometric performance also significantly improved with the fine registration step, especially in Playroom and Room scenes.
- The coarse registration, relying on semantics, was deemed crucial for providing a sufficiently good initialization, enabling the fine registration procedure to converge accurately, unlike uninitialized ICP variants or less robust global registration methods.

**E. Finetuning (Table VII, Figure 9)**

- Post-registration finetuning of the fused map, using *synthetically rendered images* from the combined GSplat map (not real-world data), further improved photometric quality.
- In less than 90 seconds, finetuning reduced the gap between the fused map's photometric scores and a ground-truth map by approximately 20% to 40%.
- Qualitative results (Figure 9) showed that finetuning effectively removed floaters and other artifacts, resulting in higher fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SIREN offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on multi-robot mapping and 3D scene representation:

- **Initialization-Free Registration:** A primary contribution is the elimination of the requirement for prior knowledge of inter-map relative poses or access to raw camera images and poses for initialization. This significantly enhances the autonomy and practicality of multi-robot systems in unknown or dynamic environments.
- **Semantics-Grounded Robustness:** By leveraging open-vocabulary semantics in its core design, SIREN addresses a critical challenge in map registration: robustly identifying correspondences. This semantic grounding makes the registration process less susceptible to noise, sparse features, or repetitive textures that can mislead purely geometric or photometric methods.
- **High-Fidelity GSplat Map Fusion:** SIREN enables the accurate and photorealistic fusion of Gaussian Splatting maps, which represent 3D scenes with high visual detail and efficiency. This preserves the advantages of GSplat for downstream tasks requiring fine-grained visual information.
- **Applicability Across Heterogeneous Robot Platforms:** The demonstration across manipulators, drones, and quadrupeds suggests broad applicability in diverse robotic tasks and hardware platforms, from industrial manipulation to outdoor exploration. This versatility is a key advantage for real-world deployment.
- **Enabling Large-Scale and Collaborative Mapping:** By allowing independent submap creation and subsequent robust fusion, SIREN facilitates collaborative mapping efforts, enabling robots with limited individual capabilities (e.g., small workspace, limited battery) to collectively map large or complex environments that would be infeasible for a single robot.
- **Reduced Computational Overhead:** The method's design, particularly its efficient semantic distillation and closed-form solutions for coarse registration, aims to mitigate the high computational costs often associated with radiance field processing. While slower than some point-cloud methods, it outperforms them in accuracy.
- **Post-Registration Refinement:** The demonstration of finetuning fused maps using synthetic data highlights a practical approach to further enhance map quality and remove artifacts (like floaters) without requiring additional real-world data collection, extending the utility of the generated maps.

The potential impact of SIREN lies in advancing autonomous robotics by providing a reliable and practical solution for high-fidelity, large-scale 3D mapping in multi-robot systems. This can benefit applications such as:
- **Robot Navigation:** More accurate and visually rich maps for complex navigation paths and dynamic environments.
- **Robotic Manipulation:** Enhanced 3D understanding for dexterous manipulation tasks, especially open-vocabulary manipulation requiring semantic grounding.
- **Scene Understanding and Editing:** Improved foundation for 3D scene analysis and modification.
- **Augmented Reality/Virtual Reality:** High-fidelity 3D reconstructions for immersive experiences.

While the method relies on embedded semantics and assumes the scene possesses relevant features, and floaters can persist, SIREN's robust, initialization-free, and semantics-grounded approach addresses significant limitations in the current state of 3D map registration, paving the way for more capable and autonomous robotic systems.
