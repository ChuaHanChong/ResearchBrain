# LAP: Language-Action Pre-Training Enables Zero-shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Princeton University and Physical Intelligence introduced Language-Action Pre-training (LAP), a method that represents low-level robot actions in natural language, enabling zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer with a 2x relative improvement over prior Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The LAP-3B model also achieves faster and more data-efficient fine-tuning for new real-world tasks, requiring approximately 2.5x fewer demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with zero-shot transfer to previously unseen robot embodiments, requiring costly and time-consuming per-embodiment fine-tuning.
- The
- distributional mismatch
- between VLM pre-training (language) and typical robotic action representations (raw continuous values or arbitrary tokens) degrades the VLM's generalization capabilities for motor control.
- The immense diversity of robot embodiments makes it impractical to collect exhaustive training data for every possible configuration, necessitating policies that generalize from limited embodiment diversity.

## Method

- Introduced Language-Action Pre-training (**LAP**), which deterministically parses continuous low-level robot actions into structured natural language descriptions, called
- language-actions
- .
- Trained a Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone (PaliGemma-3B) to auto-regressively predict these language-action tokens as a conditional language modeling task.
- Combined the VLM with a knowledge-insulated ==diffusion==-based action expert responsible for high-frequency continuous action execution during real-time robot control.

## Results

- LAP-3B achieved an average zero-shot success rate exceeding **50%** across three previously unseen robot embodiments and six manipulation tasks, demonstrating a **2x** relative improvement over prior VLAs.
- The method showed significantly faster fine-tuning, requiring approximately **2.5x** fewer real-world demonstrations to reach comparable performance on challenging new tasks.
- LAP models exhibited favorable scaling with increasing model size (Gemma3 backbones), consistently showing monotonic performance improvement where baselines saturated or degraded early.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing robot actions in natural language mitigates the distributional mismatch, allowing VLMs to better leverage their vast pre-trained semantic and visual knowledge for robust control.
> - Language-action supervision promotes the learning of more transferable, embodiment-agnostic control representations, which is crucial for achieving true zero-shot generalization across diverse robot hardware.
> - The language-action format naturally supports co-training with Vision-Question-Answering (VQA) tasks, further enhancing cross-embodiment performance and accelerating fine-tuning for new tasks.
