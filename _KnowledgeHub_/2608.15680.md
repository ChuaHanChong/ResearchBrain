# Robo-Dopamine 2.0: History-Conditioned and OOD-Aware Process Reward Modeling for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Robo-Dopamine 2.0 is a history- and OOD-aware pairwise process reward model for VLA manipulation policies. It conditions predictions on query-dependent temporal panels (expert history, rollout history, or source-aligned reference panels) and a signed progress space separating valid, robustness-preserving, and failure states, trained via a Signed-Hop curriculum. It raises mean visual order consistency to 0.986 and reaches 86.8% RoboTwin RL success and 71/80 real-world insertions.

## Problem

- Existing visual reward models use static before-after observation pairs, causing temporal aliasing where visually similar states map to different latent progress due to hidden contacts or repeated phases.
- Prior reward models are largely endpoint- or success-centric and lack explicit supervision for distinguishing semantics-preserving variations from true task-invalid failures.
- Successful in-distribution demonstration trajectories alone provide limited supervision for learning failure-aware and recovery-aware progress distinctions needed for OOD robustness.

## Method

- **Robo-Dopamine 2.0**'s ==history-conditioned pairwise reward formulation== using same-episode expert history, observed rollout history for online queries, or source-aligned successful-reference panels for synthetic OOD queries while preserving actual queried endpoints.
- ==OOD-aware signed progress space== (**Signed-Hop** target) that organizes positive, robustness-preserving, and negative branch states into one unified pairwise regression target with a pair-dependent normalization floor.
- **Signed-Hop Curriculum** with transition-aware replay: two-stage training that first learns coarse global ordering from large-Hop pairs, then refines local calibration while replaying 25% large-Hop examples.

## Results

- Reference panels raise mean visual order consistency from 0.967 to **0.986** and OOD-robust VOC from 0.906 to **0.958** versus the static baseline on the same checkpoint.
- Under a fixed 400K pairwise budget, Signed-Hop with 25% replay reaches **0.9872** mean VOC versus 0.9858 for a matched-pool shuffled control and 0.9866 with no replay.
- Downstream RL reaches **86.8%** mean RoboTwin success (versus 72.2% controlled baseline) and **71/80** real-world repeated insertions versus 48/80 for sparse event reward.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward-model context provenance should match query type: expert history for standard pairs, rollout history online, and source-aligned panels for synthetic OOD edits, rather than one fixed context scheme.
> - Encoding failure and recovery as signed, depth-indexed coordinates within one pairwise interface lets a single regression head unify valid progress, robustness, and failure without separate classifiers.
> - Curriculum ordering from coarse global transitions to fine local calibration, with partial replay of large transitions, improves progress calibration more than shuffled training on the identical data pool.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen substantial progress with the advent of large-scale imitation learning and vision-language-action (VLA) models, enabling visuomotor policy generalization across diverse demonstrations. However, challenges persist in long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks. Policies derived from VLA models often exhibit vulnerabilities to compounding errors, encounter difficulties with unseen scene changes, and struggle with recovering from off-trajectory states.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is recognized as a method to refine these pretrained VLA policies through interaction. A critical dependency for effective RL in robotics is the availability of dense, task-relevant, and reliable reward signals that generalize beyond the distribution of initial demonstrations. Traditional reward generation methods present limitations: sparse terminal rewards lead to inefficient exploration, while manually engineered dense rewards are resource-intensive, brittle, and typically specific to a single task.

Recent efforts have focused on learning dense visual or language-conditioned reward models. These models aim to provide continuous feedback from observations and task descriptions. However, existing robotic reward models, particularly those based on static before-after observations, frequently face two main issues:
1. **Temporal Ambiguity:** Visually similar states can correspond to different levels of latent progress due to factors such as hidden contact events, repeated task phases, or reordered subgoals. This makes it difficult for a static reward model to accurately gauge progress without contextual information.
2. **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Execution:** Current models often struggle to distinguish between robustness-preserving variations (e.g., minor occlusions that do not impede task progress) and genuine task-invalid failures (e.g., failed grasps or wrong-object interactions). Successful in-distribution trajectories provide limited supervision for learning these nuanced distinctions, which are crucial for robust policy recovery.

Robo-Dopamine 2.0 addresses these limitations by introducing a reward modeling framework that integrates temporal context and explicit awareness of OOD states. It aims to improve progress estimation under conditions of temporal ambiguity and OOD execution, thereby providing more informative and reliable reward signals for refining robotic manipulation policies. This work builds upon prior process reward modeling by incorporating mechanisms to handle execution history and explicitly represent failure and recovery states within a unified reward formulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop Robo-Dopamine 2.0, a history-conditioned and Out-of-Distribution (OOD)-aware process reward model designed to generate dense and reliable reward signals for robotic manipulation. The motivation stems from the acknowledged deficiencies of existing reward modeling approaches in addressing complex manipulation scenarios.

The specific objectives and underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Mitigating Temporal Ambiguity in Progress Estimation:**
 - **Motivation:** Current reward models that rely on static before-after observation pairs struggle to accurately assess progress when visual states are ambiguous. This can occur due to hidden contact events, where the robot interacts with an object without clear visual cues, or during repeated task phases (e.g., multiple insertions of similar objects) where the visual appearance of the robot and object may be similar at different points in cumulative progress. Reordered subgoals within a long-horizon task can also confuse static models.
 - **Objective:** To overcome this, Robo-Dopamine 2.0 aims to incorporate execution context by developing a history-conditioned pairwise reward formulation. This involves providing the reward model with a sequence of prior observations (temporal panels) alongside the queried endpoints. For standard queries, this context comes from the same expert episode; for online queries, it comes from the observed rollout history. Critically, for synthetic OOD queries, it uses phase-aligned successful-reference panels to provide a nominal successful progression context without altering the edited OOD endpoints.

- **Improving Robustness by Distinguishing Semantics-Preserving Variations from Task-Invalid Failures:**
 - **Motivation:** For policies to be truly robust, they must differentiate between minor, non-critical variations in the environment or execution (e.g., partial occlusions, background changes) that do not impede task progress and genuine failures that require corrective action (e.g., wrong object interaction, failed grasp, incomplete release). Existing models, often trained predominantly on successful trajectories, lack sufficient supervision to make these distinctions, especially in OOD scenarios.
 - **Objective:** Robo-Dopamine 2.0 introduces an OOD-aware signed progress space. This space is designed to explicitly organize execution states into positive progress, robustness-preserving variations, and negative (task-invalid failure) states. By assigning signed values (positive for valid progress, negative for failure, and same value for robust variations), the model can learn to represent these categories within a unified pairwise training interface. This is enabled by structured OOD data construction, which involves generating counterfactual trajectories with specific negative (e.g., wrong object) or robust (e.g., occlusion) edits.

- **Optimizing Learning of Fine-Grained Progress Ordering and Calibration:**
 - **Motivation:** Learning a coherent global ordering of progress, failure, and recovery states is essential, but refining fine-grained differences, especially for subtle state changes or near-zero progress, is also crucial for precise policy control. Naive training on all pairs might lead to an imbalanced focus or suboptimal calibration.
 - **Objective:** To address this, the research introduces a Signed-Hop Curriculum with transition-aware replay. This curriculum progressively learns execution ordering, initially focusing on coarse-grained transitions between successful, robust, and failed states (large-Hop examples). Subsequently, it refines fine-grained progress differences (small-Hop and zero-Hop examples) while replaying a fraction of the large-Hop examples to maintain global coherence. This structured training approach aims to improve both global ordering and local calibration of the reward model.

Overall, the motivation for Robo-Dopamine 2.0 is to enhance the efficacy of RL in complex robotic manipulation by providing a reward function that is robust to common challenges in real-world environments, such as temporal aliasing and out-of-distribution states, thereby enabling policies to learn more effectively from interaction and recover from errors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Robo-Dopamine 2.0 integrates several methodological innovations: a history-conditioned pairwise reward formulation, an OOD-aware signed progress space, and a Signed-Hop curriculum for training.

#### 3.1. History-Conditioned Pairwise Reward Formulation

The core of Robo-Dopamine 2.0 is its General Reward Model (GRM), which predicts signed relative progress ($\hat{y}_{ba}$) given an instruction ($c$), task-reference anchors ($R$), ordered temporal context ($C_{ba}$), and an ordered pair of multi-view states ($X_b$, $X_a$). The model builds upon existing pairwise reward interfaces but enhances them with temporal context.

- **Inputs:** The GRM takes multi-view states ($X_b$, $X_a$) as actual queried endpoints. Reference anchors ($r_s$, $r_g$) denote the task start and goal. The crucial addition is $C_{ba}$, which provides temporal context.
- **Temporal Context ($C_{ba}$):** This context is composed of panels from two windows: `W_pre` (observations before $\min(t_b, t_a)$) and `W_mid` (observations between $\min(t_b, t_a)$ and $\max(t_b, t_a)$). These windows are uniformly sampled into panels, which are then integrated into the input layout.
 - **Input Layouts:** The model uses `I_static` (only task references, $X_b$, $X_a$) and `I_ref` (adds `M_pre` and `M_mid` panels). Panels are ordered, with padding for empty cells and prompts indicating panel counts.
- **Context Provenance:** The source of $C_{ba}$ varies based on the query type:
 - **Standard Training Pairs:** Use temporal panels from the *same expert episode* as the queried states.
 - **Synthetic OOD Pairs:** Utilize panels *exclusively from the aligned successful source episode* (source-only reference panels), while $X_b$ and $X_a$ remain the *edited observations*. This ensures the context reflects ideal progression while the queried points show OOD variations.
 - **Online Inference:** Panels are constructed dynamically from the *observed rollout history* during active policy execution, requiring no source selection or index alignment.

#### 3.2. Structured OOD Data Construction

To enable OOD awareness, the method constructs a dataset of counterfactual trajectories.
- **Data Sources:** Successful trajectories are drawn from simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa, LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0) and real-robot data (AgiBot World). Camera streams are mapped to canonical view slots (head, left-wrist, right-wrist).
- **Edit Operator:** A task- and phase-aware edit operator $E_z(\tau, c)$ is applied to a successful source trajectory $\tau$ and instruction $c$ to generate an aligned counterfactual trajectory $\tilde{\tau}_z$. $z$ denotes the edit type, belonging to either $Z_{rob}$ (semantics-preserving robust operators) or $Z_{neg}$ (task-invalid negative operators).
 - **Negative Operators:** Include wrong-object interaction, empty grasp, non-release, and target replacement. These invalidate task-relevant conditions.
 - **Robust Operators:** Include partial occlusion, background variation, irrelevant distractors, and non-target color changes. These alter appearance without changing task semantics.
- **Editing Process:** The pipeline involves normalizing instructions, assigning semantically compatible operators, localizing intervention to phase-valid temporal windows, constructing multi-reference editing requests for each frame and camera, generating edited observations using an instruction-following image editor (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image), and retaining extensive metadata for provenance. The editor is constrained to preserve robot pose, object geometry, lighting, and other invariants while performing the specified edit.
- **OOD Context during Training:** For synthetic OOD pairs, task-reference anchors and all temporal panels are drawn from the *aligned successful source episode*. The edited observations appear only as $X_b$ and $X_a$, ensuring that the context represents successful progression.

#### 3.3. OOD-aware Signed Progress Space

A key innovation is the representation of progress in a signed space.
- **Progress Definition:** For a successful source trajectory of length $T$, the progress of state $s_i$ is $g_i = i / (T-1)$.
- **Signed Potential Function ($\Phi$):**
 - Positive state ($P_i$) and robustness-preserving robust state ($R_i$) are assigned $\Phi(P_i) = \Phi(R_i) = g_i$. This means robust variations have the same progress value as their successful counterparts.
 - Task-invalid negative state ($N_i$) is assigned $\Phi(N_i) = -g_i$. The magnitude $|-g_i|$ indexes failure depth by the aligned source phase, and the negative sign explicitly denotes task-invalid execution.
- **Signed Hop Target ($H_{ba}$):** This measures the normalized fraction of available potential range. For a pair $(X_b, X_a)$ with potentials $\phi_b = \Phi(X_b)$ and $\phi_a = \Phi(X_a)$, and a binary negative-branch indicator $b(X)$ (0 for valid, 1 for negative), the Hop target is:
 $H_{ba} = \frac{\Delta_{ba}}{1 - \phi_b}$ if $\Delta_{ba} > 0$
 $H_{ba} = \frac{\Delta_{ba}}{\phi_b - L_{ba}}$ if $\Delta_{ba} < 0$
 $H_{ba} = 0$ if $\Delta_{ba} = 0$
 where $\Delta_{ba} = \phi_a - \phi_b$, and $L_{ba} = -\max\{b(X_b), b(X_a)\}$ (0 for valid-only, -1 if either is negative). This normalization handles positive progress, regression, and transitions into/out of failure states. The regression label $y_{ba} = 100 \cdot \text{clip}(H_{ba}, -1, 1)$ is used for training.
- **Training:** The GRM is trained with mean-squared regression ($\mathcal{L}_{pair}$). Training data includes valid pairs ($P_i \to P_j, R_i \to R_j, P_i \leftrightarrow R_i$), failure pairs ($U_i \to N_j$), recovery pairs ($N_i \to U_j$), and within-failure pairs ($N_i \to N_j$).

#### 3.4. Signed-Hop Curriculum

To improve optimization of fine-grained progress ordering, a two-stage curriculum is employed:
- **Stage 1 (Global Progress):** Samples 200K examples from $D_{large}$, comprising the top 40% of examples by $|H_{ba}|$ within each pair family. This stage focuses on learning the coarse geometry of progress, failure, and recovery.
- **Stage 2 (Local Calibration with Replay):** Samples 200K examples primarily from $D_{fine}$ (the remaining small-Hop and zero-Hop examples) to refine local calibration and robust invariance. A fraction $\rho=0.25$ of large-Hop examples from $D_{large}$ are replayed to maintain global understanding.

#### 3.5. Closed-Loop Reward Framework

For downstream RL, the trained GRM is frozen and used to provide dense rewards.
- **Online Potential Estimation:** At each rollout step $t$, the model computes three normalized predictions: forward-anchored ($\hat{h}_F^t$), backward-anchored ($\hat{h}_B^t$), and incremental ($\hat{h}_I^t$). Crucially, online temporal panels are constructed *from the observed rollout history* at that moment, without relying on source-aligned expert data.
- **Online Branch Gate and Floor:** An online branch gate $\hat{b}_t$ indicates if the current state is estimated to be in the negative branch. This determines the normalization floor ($L_F^t, L_B^t, L_I^t$) for inverse normalization.
- **Potential Fusion:** The three predictions are inverted using the $\Gamma$ function (inverse of Hop normalization) to yield three potential estimates ($\hat{\Phi}_F^t, \hat{\Phi}_B^t, \hat{\Phi}_I^t$). These are then averaged to produce a final fused potential $\hat{\Phi}_t = \text{clip}((\hat{\Phi}_F^t + \hat{\Phi}_B^t + \hat{\Phi}_I^t)/3, -1, 1)$.
- **RL Reward:** The reward supplied to the RL algorithm is $r_{GRM}^t = r_{task}^t + \gamma_{RL}(\hat{\Phi}_{t+1} - \hat{\Phi}_t)$. This potential-based reward shaping integrates the GRM's dense, history-conditioned, and OOD-aware progress signal with the environment's sparse task reward.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Robo-Dopamine 2.0 encompassed offline performance metrics and downstream reinforcement learning tasks in both simulation and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Offline Results

- **Visual Order Consistency (VOC):** Trajectory-level VOC, measured by Spearman correlation between predicted progress and ground-truth order, was used across five benchmark families: in-distribution success (ID), temporal memory (Temp.), OOD-negative (Neg.), OOD-robust (Rob.), and OOD-temporal (OOD-T).
 - **Impact of Panels:** Robo-Dopamine 2.0 with prebuilt 3x3 temporal panels achieved an average VOC of 0.986, an improvement over its static input variant (0.967) and the GRM-8B-Pro baseline (0.958). Specifically, OOD-robust VOC improved from 0.906 (static) to 0.958 (panel). This indicates that training-aligned temporal context enhanced progress estimation.
- **Task-Completion Classification Accuracy:** Robo-Dopamine 2.0-8B achieved 96.1% task-completion accuracy on 60 episodes, outperforming several large language models including GPT-5.5 (81.1%), Gemini-3-Pro (77.2%), Claude-Opus-4.8 (79.4%), Qwen3.5-397B-A17B-fp8 (76.1%), and RoboBrain 2.0 (61.7%). This metric assesses the model's ability to classify task success.
- **Temporal Context and OOD Ablations:**
 - Structured panels provided the largest VOC gains, with 3x3 panels increasing average VOC by 0.033 for GRM and 0.027 for Robo-Dopamine 2.0 compared to no-panel OOD training.
 - OOD supervision also improved panel VOC compared to no-OOD training, though the panel contribution was more substantial.
 - The QA200K mix had a smaller marginal effect once structured temporal context was enabled.
 - Optimal performance was observed with one or two between-state panels, suggesting a trade-off between additional temporal evidence and visual-token budget. Prebuilt panels consistently outperformed runtime construction variants.
- **Signed-Hop Curriculum:**
 - Under a fixed 400K pairwise reward budget, the Signed-Hop curriculum with 25% replay reached an average VOC of 0.9872. This exceeded both a matched-pool shuffled control (0.9858) and a no-replay variant (0.9866).
 - Gains were observed across all family benchmarks, particularly in OOD-robust and OOD-temporal cases. This suggests that the curriculum, by progressively learning global geometry before refining local calibration and replaying large-Hop examples, improved overall and OOD-specific ordering.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning

- **Simulation on RoboTwin:**
 - Robo-Dopamine 2.0 was used to shape rewards for OpenVLA-OFT policies optimized with GRPO on three RoboTwin 2.0 tasks.
 - **Baseline Comparison:** A sparse reward baseline trained for 1,000 epochs achieved 85.8% mean success. The reward-shaped conditions (C00-C11) were trained for 300 epochs.
 - **Ablation Results:**
 - Without OOD supervision or rollout-history panels (C00): 72.2% mean success.
 - With rollout-history panels (C01): 75.1% mean success, a 2.9 point increase over C00.
 - With OOD supervision (C10): 80.6% mean success, an 8.4 point increase over C00.
 - With both OOD supervision and rollout-history panels (C11): 86.8% mean success, a 6.2 point increase over C10 and 11.7 points over C01.
 - C11 surpassed the 1,000-epoch sparse baseline in mean success and on two of the three tasks, demonstrating complementary gains from both rollout history and OOD supervision in improving policy learning efficiency.

- **Real-World Repeated Insertions:**
 - ConRFT (consistency policy reinforced fine-tuning) was used on dual Franka arms for a repeated Insert Square task (K=4 attempts), which inherently creates visually similar states at different cumulative progress levels.
 - **Baseline Comparison:** An "Event Sparse" baseline (reward after each insertion) achieved 48/80 successful insertions and 8/20 full sequence successes over 300 online episodes. The C00-C11 conditions used 200 online episodes.
 - **Ablation Results:**
 - C00 (no OOD, no history): 46/80 insertions, 7/20 full successes.
 - C01 (history): 67/80 insertions, 13/20 full successes. This represents a substantial gain of 21 insertions and 6 full successes over C00, highlighting the benefit of rollout-history panels in handling temporal aliasing.
 - C10 (OOD): 50/80 insertions, 9/20 full successes.
 - C11 (full model): 71/80 insertions, 15/20 full successes. C11 achieved the highest performance, exceeding the Event Sparse baseline with fewer online episodes, indicating the combined effectiveness of the proposed components in a real-world, highly ambiguous task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Robo-Dopamine 2.0 presents a significant advancement in process reward modeling for robotic manipulation, addressing critical limitations of prior approaches. The research introduces a framework that enhances the robustness and informativeness of reward signals, which is fundamental for effective reinforcement learning in complex, real-world robotic tasks.

The primary significance of this work lies in its integrated approach to **temporal context awareness** and **out-of-distribution (OOD) state handling**. By conditioning pairwise progress predictions on execution history, Robo-Dopamine 2.0 effectively mitigates temporal ambiguity arising from visually similar states, hidden contact events, or repeated task phases. This enables the reward model to differentiate true progress from superficial visual changes, which is a common challenge in long-horizon manipulation.

The introduction of an **OOD-aware signed progress space** is also notable. This allows the model to explicitly represent and distinguish between valid task progression, robustness-preserving variations (e.g., minor occlusions), and task-invalid failures (e.g., wrong object interaction, failed grasps). This capability is crucial for developing policies that are not only capable but also robust, able to recover from unexpected situations or subtle deviations from demonstrated trajectories. The structured OOD data construction pipeline and prompt design further facilitate the learning of these distinctions, moving beyond success-centric supervision.

Furthermore, the **Signed-Hop Curriculum** enhances the training process by strategically learning coarse-grained transitions before refining fine-grained progress calibration, supported by transition-aware replay. This methodical approach to training contributes to a more accurate and globally consistent reward function.

The potential impact of Robo-Dopamine 2.0 is substantial for the field of robotic reinforcement learning:

- **Enhanced Policy Learning Efficiency and Robustness:** By providing dense, reliable, and context-aware reward signals, the framework can significantly accelerate policy learning and lead to more robust policies that can handle off-trajectory states and OOD scenarios more effectively. This was demonstrated by the improved success rates in both simulation (RoboTwin) and real-world (Repeated Insert Square) RL experiments, often outperforming sparse reward baselines with less online interaction.
- **Reduced Manual Reward Engineering:** The learned nature of the reward model reduces the need for costly and brittle manual reward engineering, facilitating the deployment of robots to new tasks or environments where dense supervision is otherwise difficult to obtain.
- **Improved Generalization:** The explicit modeling of OOD states and robustness-preserving variations supports better generalization of policies beyond the distribution of initial training data, a key requirement for generalist robot agents.
- **Unified Reward Interface:** The ability of the same pairwise interface to support both offline progress estimation and closed-loop reward shaping simplifies the integration of reward modeling into existing RL pipelines.

In conclusion, Robo-Dopamine 2.0 represents a comprehensive solution for generating more effective reward signals in complex robotic manipulation. Its focus on temporal context and OOD awareness addresses fundamental challenges in robot learning, paving the way for more capable and reliable autonomous systems. Future work, as noted by the authors, involves broadening the scope of tasks, OOD scenarios, and real-world evaluations to further solidify its capabilities.
