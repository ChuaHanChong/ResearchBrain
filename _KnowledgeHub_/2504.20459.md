# SAS-Prompt: Large Language Models as Numerical Optimizers for Robot Self-Improvement

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Arizona State University and Google DeepMind demonstrate that Large Language Models can function as numerical optimizers for robot self-improvement through a novel "Summarize, Analyze, Synthesize" (SAS) prompting technique, enabling robots to iteratively refine their behavior while providing natural language explanations for parameter adjustments.

## Problem

- Robot self-improvement typically requires expert demonstrations or complex optimization algorithms
- Existing LLM applications in robotics lack transparency in parameter selection and learning processes
- Traditional numerical optimization methods don't provide human-interpretable explanations for decisions

## Method

- SAS prompting framework that enables LLMs to perform numerical optimization through natural language reasoning
- Iterative learning loop where LLM analyzes robot execution traces and synthesizes improved control parameters
- Natural language justifications for parameter choices maintain explainability

## Results

- Achieved **39.4%** top-1 and **83.7%** top-10 accuracy in retrieving correct samples based on human instructions
- Successfully transferred learning from left-side table tennis hits to right-side hits through iterative improvement
- Demonstrated comparable or superior performance to traditional optimization methods like gradient descent and Nelder-Mead
- Maintained explainability through natural language justifications of parameter adjustments

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can perform effective numerical optimization "out-of-the-box" without additional training
> - Textual analysis of parameter-outcome relationships serves as an interpretable gradient for optimization
> - The combination of natural language reasoning and numerical optimization enables explainable self-improvement
