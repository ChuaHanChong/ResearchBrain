# SAS-Prompt: Large Language Models as Numerical Optimizers for Robot Self-Improvement

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Arizona State University and Google DeepMind demonstrate that Large Language Models can function as numerical optimizers for robot self-improvement through a novel "Summarize, Analyze, Synthesize" (SAS) prompting technique, enabling robots to iteratively refine their behavior while providing natural language explanations for parameter adjustments.

## Problem

- Robot self-improvement typically requires expert demonstrations or complex optimization algorithms
- Existing LLM applications in robotics lack transparency in parameter selection and learning processes
- Traditional numerical optimization methods don't provide human-interpretable explanations for decisions

## Method

- SAS prompting framework that enables LLMs to perform numerical optimization through natural language reasoning
- Iterative learning loop where LLM analyzes robot execution traces and synthesizes improved control parameters
- Natural language justifications for parameter choices maintain explainability

## Results

- Achieved **39.4%** top-1 and **83.7%** top-10 accuracy in retrieving correct samples based on human instructions
- Successfully transferred learning from left-side table tennis hits to right-side hits through iterative improvement
- Demonstrated comparable or superior performance to traditional optimization methods like gradient descent and Nelder-Mead

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can perform effective numerical optimization "out-of-the-box" without additional training
> - Textual analysis of parameter-outcome relationships serves as an interpretable gradient for optimization
> - The combination of natural language reasoning and numerical optimization enables explainable self-improvement

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several active areas within AI and robotics:

- **Large Language Models (LLMs) for Robotics:** This paper contributes to the growing body of work exploring the use of LLMs to enhance robot capabilities, including task planning, code generation, and reward function design.
- **In-Context Learning:** The paper builds upon the concept of in-context learning, where LLMs leverage examples and feedback to refine robot skills. However, it distinguishes itself by focusing on self-improvement without explicit human coaching, relying solely on robot execution data.
- **Explainable AI (XAI):** A key feature of the proposed approach is the explainability of the learning process. The LLM provides natural language justifications for its parameter choices, increasing the transparency of the self-improvement process.
- **Numerical Optimization:** The paper introduces the novel idea of LLMs as numerical optimizers. This connects LLMs to a fundamental area of machine learning and control, suggesting a new way to perform policy search and adaptation.
- **Robot Self-Improvement:** The paper addresses the challenge of enabling robots to learn and improve their performance through trial-and-error, a long-standing goal in robotics.

The research differentiates itself from existing approaches by:

- **Internal Optimization Loop:** Unlike methods that use LLMs for planning or reward design but rely on external optimization loops, this work implements the entire learning loop within the LLM.
- **No Expert Demonstrations:** Unlike imitation learning approaches, this work focuses on self-improvement without requiring human expert demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to demonstrate the ability of large language models (LLMs) to perform iterative self-improvement of robot policies. The primary motivation stems from the observation that LLMs possess an inherent capacity for numerical optimization, a property that can be harnessed for explainable robot policy search.

Specifically, the key objectives are:

- To show that LLMs can perform stochastic numerical optimization "out-of-the-box," without the need for additional retraining.
- To develop a novel prompting technique (SAS Prompt) that enables robots to understand, interpret, and learn from previous robot behavior to synthesize new, improved behavior.
- To create an explainable policy search method entirely implemented within an LLM.
- To validate the proposed approach through experiments in both simulation and on a real-world robot table tennis task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the "SAS Prompt" (Summarize, Analyze, Synthesize). This prompt leverages the LLM's ability to:

- **Summarize:** The LLM is prompted to summarize in-context examples of robot behavior, extracting key features and parameters.
- **Analyze:** The LLM analyzes the impact of control variables on robot behavior, identifying correlations and dependencies.
- **Synthesize:** The LLM generates a new set of control parameters based on the analysis, aiming to improve robot performance.

The process is iterative. The new control parameters are executed on the robot, and the resulting execution trace is added to the in-context examples, allowing the LLM to learn from its past experiences. The natural language capabilities of the LLM are used to provide justifications for parameter choices, increasing the transparency of the learning process.

The methodology is evaluated in the context of a robot table tennis task, both in simulation and on a real robot. The robot uses a lower-level controller (LLC), and the LLM generates attenuation parameters for this controller, modulating its behavior to achieve specific objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings:

- **LLMs as Numerical Optimizers:** The experiments demonstrate that LLMs can perform numerical optimization effectively, comparing favorably to traditional optimization methods like gradient descent, Adam, and Nelder-Mead, even without access to gradients. LLM performance was particularly strong with more dimensions.
- **SAS Prompt for Retrieval:** The retrieval experiments show that the SAS prompt can be used to retrieve the correct samples given an instruction from a human user. The system displays a general tendency to correctly translate human instructions into real-world actions. On average the LLM achieves 39.4%(Top-1), 68.89%(Top-5), and 83.70%(Top-10).
- **SAS Prompt for Self-Improvement:** The self-improvement experiments, conducted both in simulation and on a real robot, demonstrate that the SAS prompt can enable robots to learn and improve their performance iteratively. The robot successfully learned to hit the ball to the far right side of the table, starting from a narrow set of examples on the left side. The analysis is considered a textual gradient – it relates changes in parameter values to changes in task objectives and outcomes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has significant implications for the field of robotics and AI:

- **New Approach to Robot Learning:** The SAS prompt offers a novel approach to robot learning that leverages the capabilities of LLMs for both reasoning and optimization.
- **Explainable Robot Behavior:** The explainability of the learning process, provided by the LLM's natural language justifications, is crucial for building trust and understanding in human-robot interaction.
- **Simplified Robot Programming:** The use of natural language objectives makes robot programming more accessible to non-expert users.
- **Self-Improving Robots:** The ability of robots to learn and improve their performance through trial-and-error, without explicit human coaching, has the potential to revolutionize various applications, including manufacturing, logistics, and healthcare.
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** This work contributes to the emerging paradigm of using foundation models as core components of robotic systems, enabling more flexible and intelligent robot behavior.

The potential impact of this research extends to:

- **More Adaptive Robots:** Robots that can adapt to changing environments and task requirements without manual reprogramming.
- **More Accessible Robotics:** Simplifying robot programming, making it accessible to a broader range of users.
- **More Trustworthy Robots:** Increasing the transparency and explainability of robot behavior, building trust between humans and robots.

In conclusion, this paper presents a compelling and innovative approach to robot self-improvement, leveraging the unique capabilities of large language models. The SAS prompt provides a pathway towards a new generation of robots that are more intelligent, adaptable, and explainable.
