# Dreamitate: Real-World Visuomotor Policy Learning via Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Dreamitate" framework introduces a "synthesize-then-track-then-act" approach, fine-tuning a large-scale video diffusion model on human tool-use demonstrations to enable real-world robotic manipulation. This method achieves superior generalization and data efficiency by generating videos of human tool interaction and then tracking the tool's 6D pose for robot execution.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policies often exhibit poor generalization across diverse visual environments, objects, and task variations.
- Collecting sufficient ground truth robot action data is costly and time-consuming, leading to data scarcity for robust policy learning.
- The inherent "embodiment gap" makes it challenging to directly transfer skills from human videos to robot manipulators.

## Method

- A video diffusion model, pretrained on internet-scale data, is fine-tuned using small, task-specific datasets of human demonstrations performing manipulation tasks with customized tools.
- At inference, the model generates stereo videos depicting human tool-use from initial scene observations, serving as an explicit plan.
- An object tracking system extracts the 6D pose trajectory of the tool from the synthesized video, directly mapping these poses to robot end-effector actions.

## Results

- Dreamitate achieved significantly higher success rates across four real-world manipulation tasks, including 92.5% for Object Rotation (vs. 55% for baseline) and 92.5% for Table Top Sweeping (vs. 12.5% for baseline).
- The framework demonstrated superior data efficiency, maintaining high performance even when training data was reduced by two-thirds, outperforming the baseline which experienced substantial performance degradation.
- Qualitative and quantitative evaluations confirmed Dreamitate's enhanced robustness and generalizability to novel objects and environments compared to the Diffusion Policy baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging human demonstrations of tool use, rather than direct human body motion transfer, simplifies bridging the embodiment gap by focusing on tracking the rigid tool's trajectory.
> - Integrating a video generative model as a direct component of the visuomotor policy provides an interpretable, visual plan for robot actions.
> - Fine-tuning large-scale pretrained video models enables the policy to capitalize on extensive priors from internet data, significantly improving generalization and data efficiency with fewer task-specific examples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of visuomotor policies—systems that learn to map visual observations directly to robot actions—is a long-standing and challenging problem in robotics. A primary hurdle is the difficulty in developing policies that can generalize robustly across diverse visual environments, object variations, and task conditions.

Traditional approaches, particularly **Behavior Cloning (BC)** from human demonstrations, have gained popularity for manipulation tasks. In BC, a policy learns to regress from visual observations to desired robot actions, typically supervised by teleoperated robot data or human demonstrations. However, a major limitation of traditional BC is its demand for ground truth robot actions, which are difficult and costly to collect at scale, especially for diverse scenarios. This often leads to policies that struggle with generalization, multimodal behaviors, and high-precision tasks.

More recently, the advent of **large-scale video generative models**, pretrained on vast internet video datasets, has opened new avenues for policy learning. These models embed extensive priors about human behavior and world dynamics. Researchers have explored leveraging these models in several ways:
- **Synthesizing videos of human behavior:** While such videos capture a wide diversity of actions, directly transferring these human actions to robots is challenging due to the inherent **embodiment gap** – the fundamental differences in kinematics, dynamics, and physical interactions between a human hand and a robot manipulator.
- **Synthesizing videos of robot behavior:** This approach avoids the embodiment gap but is severely limited by the scale of available robot-specific demonstration data, which is orders of magnitude smaller than human video datasets. This scarcity makes policies trained this way less robust in unconstrained environments.
- **Video models as world simulators or for planning:** Another line of work utilizes video generative models as world models to predict future states given actions, or as components in longer-horizon planning frameworks, often in conjunction with language models.

"Dreamitate" positions itself uniquely by addressing the limitations of these existing paradigms. Instead of directly imitating human actions (which faces the embodiment gap) or generating robot actions from limited robot data, Dreamitate proposes a novel "synthesize-then-track-then-act" framework. Its core insight is to generate videos of *humans using common tools* to perform tasks. By focusing on tool use, the embodiment gap is dramatically simplified; the robot only needs to track and replicate the *tool's trajectory*, which is a rigid body with known kinematics, rather than the complex dynamics of a human hand. This approach leverages the vast and diverse priors from internet-scale human video data while simultaneously bypassing the complexities of human-to-robot motion transfer.

The paper differentiates itself from prior work that uses video prediction models as world models or simulators. Dreamitate integrates the video generation model *directly into the visuomotor policy*, where the predicted video serves as an explicit, interpretable action plan rather than merely an observation or a planning aid. This makes Dreamitate a significant contribution to the evolving landscape of foundation models in robotics, demonstrating a practical method for translating high-level human intent captured in generative models into precise robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Dreamitate" is to develop a highly generalizable visuomotor policy for real-world robotic manipulation. The authors aim to overcome the inherent limitations of traditional imitation learning methods, particularly their poor generalization capabilities and data inefficiency in diverse, real-world scenarios.

The key motivations driving this research stem from several critical challenges in robot learning:

1. **Poor Generalization of Existing Visuomotor Policies:** Traditional behavior cloning methods, while effective for specific tasks, often fail to generalize robustly to novel visual environments, unseen objects, or variations in task execution. This lack of generalization significantly limits their practical deployment in unstructured settings.
2. **Data Scarcity for Robot-Specific Actions:** Learning robust policies typically requires vast amounts of diverse training data. For robots, collecting ground truth action labels through teleoperation or costly robot demonstrations is a bottleneck, making it difficult to scale policies to a wide array of tasks and environments.
3. **The Embodiment Gap:** While large datasets of human videos exist, directly transferring human manipulation skills to robots is challenging due to the anatomical and kinematic differences between human hands and robot manipulators. Previous attempts to synthesize human behavior often struggle with this "embodiment gap."
4. **Underutilization of Internet-Scale Priors:** The immense knowledge embedded in video generative models, pretrained on massive datasets of internet videos showcasing diverse human behaviors, represents a powerful untapped resource for robotics. The challenge lies in effectively transferring this knowledge to robot control.

To address these motivations, Dreamitate proposes a specific solution: leveraging video generation not just for learning representations or simulating worlds, but as an *integral part of the policy that directly generates executable robot actions*. The authors' central "key insight" is that by generating videos of humans performing tasks *using common tools*, and then focusing on tracking the *tool's trajectory*, they can "effortlessly bridge the embodiment gap." The tool acts as a universal intermediary, whose manipulation by a human can be directly mapped to a robot's end-effector.

This approach offers several distinct advantages, which also serve as specific objectives for the framework:

- **Generalizability:** By fine-tuning a video generative model pretrained on vast internet data, Dreamitate aims to preserve and leverage this extensive prior knowledge, enabling the learned policy to generalize much better to unseen scenarios with less task-specific data.
- **Scalability:** The method relies on human demonstrations, which are significantly easier and cheaper to collect than teleoperated robot data, thus making dataset collection more scalable.
- **Interpretability:** During inference, the policy produces a synthetic video of the task execution *before* the robot acts. This video serves as an intermediate, human-interpretable plan, a crucial feature for debugging, human-robot collaboration, and understanding the policy's decision-making process, contrasting with opaque end-to-end black-box policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dreamitate operates on a novel "synthesize-then-track-then-act" framework, which involves fine-tuning a video diffusion model on human demonstrations and then using the generated videos to directly control a robot.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

The core framework is succinctly expressed as:
$a_t = \mathcal{T}(\hat{v}_t) \text{ where } \{\hat{v}_t\} = f_\theta(v_0)$
Here, $v_0$ is the initial stereo image of the scene, $f_\theta$ is the learnable generative video model, $\{\hat{v}_t\}$ represents the synthesized video frames of a human performing the task with a tool, and $\mathcal{T}$ is a tracking function that extracts the 6D pose trajectory $\{\mathbf{a}_t\}$ of the tool from the generated video. These actions $\mathbf{a}_t \in SE(3)$ (6D pose) are then executed by the robot.

#### 3.2. Data Collection for Fine-tuning

For each manipulation task, the authors establish a tabletop setup equipped with two calibrated stereo cameras. These cameras are positioned approximately 45 degrees apart (one top, one side view) to provide comprehensive visual coverage and facilitate 3D perception. Human demonstrators perform the specific manipulation tasks using custom-designed, 3D-printed tools. A critical aspect here is that these tools have known CAD models, which is essential for precise 3D tracking later in the pipeline. The demonstrations are captured as stereo video pairs. This human-centric data collection is inherently more scalable and less prone to robot-specific calibration issues compared to teleoperation.

#### 3.3. Video Generation (Fine-tuning)

The heart of Dreamitate is the video generative model.
- **Base Model:** The framework utilizes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), a powerful video diffusion model pretrained on a massive dataset of internet videos, as its foundation.
- **Fine-tuning:** This pretrained model is then fine-tuned on the small, task-specific dataset of human demonstration videos collected in the previous step. A separate model $f_\theta$ is fine-tuned for each distinct task (e.g., scooping, sweeping).
- **Stereo Video Generation:** To enable 3D action inference, the model is adapted to generate stereo videos. The input image embedding is modified based on the target viewing angle, ensuring that the output video contains frames from both camera perspectives. The model generates an odd number of frames (25 in their adaptation), with the first 13 frames corresponding to view 1 and the last 12 to view 2. The training objective minimizes the L2 difference between the synthesized stereo video frames and the ground truth stereo frames from the demonstrations.
- **Efficiency:** During fine-tuning, the encoder and decoder components of the SVD model are frozen, with only the spatial/temporal attention layers being updated. This makes the fine-tuning process more efficient and helps retain the powerful visual priors learned during large-scale pre-training.

#### 3.4. Track then Act (Inference Time)

At test time, when the robot is presented with a novel scene:
- **Input:** A stereo image pair of the current scene ($v_0$) is fed into the fine-tuned video model $f_\theta$.
- **Video Synthesis:** The model generates a stereo video $\{\hat{v}_t\}$ depicting a human performing the task with the specialized tool in that specific environment.
- **Tool Tracking:** The generated video serves as the intermediate plan. To extract executable robot actions, the Megapose object tracking system is employed. Given the known CAD model of the tool, Megapose accurately tracks its 6D pose (position and orientation) in each frame of the synthesized stereo video. The stereo pair and calibrated camera parameters enhance 3D consistency and accuracy.
- **Robot Execution:** The sequence of 6D tool poses extracted from the synthesized video forms the action trajectory $\{\mathbf{a}_t\}$. This trajectory is then directly executed by the robot arm, with the identical customized tool mounted on its end-effector. This entire data-collection and execution pipeline is described as "agent-independent" because it relies on tracking known tools rather than learning robot-specific action spaces.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Baseline

The approach is evaluated on four diverse real-world manipulation tasks: Object Rotation, Granular Material Scooping, Table Top Sweeping, and Push-Shape (a long-horizon task). To assess generalizability, training and evaluation datasets use non-overlapping sets of objects and varied environments (tabletops, lighting). The primary baseline for comparison is **Diffusion Policy**, a state-of-the-art behavior cloning method. Diffusion Policy is trained on the same collected human demonstration data, where the target trajectories are also obtained via Megapose tracking, ensuring a fair comparison of policy learning paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Dreamitate across four real-world manipulation tasks unequivocally demonstrates its superior performance, particularly in generalization, compared to the Diffusion Policy baseline.

#### 4.1. Generalization Performance Across Tasks

- **Object Rotation Task:** This task required precise grasping and controlled rotation of objects. Dreamitate achieved a remarkable 92.5% success rate, significantly outperforming Diffusion Policy, which managed only 55%. Qualitative results (Fig. 4) showed that Diffusion Policy often struggled to establish stable contact or maintain grasp, leading to slippage, whereas Dreamitate consistently made contact and selected appropriate grasping points.
- **Granular Material Scooping Task:** This task demanded precise 3D manipulation to scoop particles from one container to another, while ignoring distractors. Dreamitate achieved 85% success, considerably better than Diffusion Policy's 55%. This task highlighted Dreamitate's ability to accurately determine object poses for 3D manipulation, even with small target tools. Diffusion Policy, in contrast, was frequently distracted by other objects or misjudged container heights (Fig. 5).
- **Table Top Sweeping Task:** This challenging task involved using a brush to sweep particles to a target, avoiding obstacles, and presenting multi-modal distribution in demonstrations. Dreamitate maintained a strong 92.5% success rate. Diffusion Policy's performance plummeted to a mere 12.5%, often generating trajectories that collided with obstacles or failed to reach the target (Fig. 6). This result strongly supports Dreamitate's capacity to handle multi-modal behaviors and achieve broader generalization due to its internet-scale pre-trained foundation.
- **Push-Shape Task (Long Horizon):** A multi-step task requiring precise object pushing and rotation to match a target mask. Dreamitate achieved a mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) of 0.731 and an average rotation error of 8.0 degrees. Diffusion Policy's results were substantially worse, with an mIoU of 0.550 and a large rotation error of 48.2 degrees. Dreamitate demonstrated superior ability to adjust both position and orientation of the object (Fig. 7), a critical aspect where Diffusion Policy largely failed.

#### 4.2. Performance Scaling Curve (Data Efficiency)

A crucial experiment assessed the impact of training dataset size on generalization. When the training data for the rotation task was reduced to two-thirds and then one-third, Dreamitate consistently maintained high success rates. In stark contrast, Diffusion Policy's performance declined significantly with reduced data (Fig. 8). This finding is particularly significant, as it highlights Dreamitate's superior data efficiency and reinforces the benefit of leveraging extensive priors from internet-scale pre-trained video generation models. It suggests that Dreamitate can learn robust policies with fewer demonstrations, making it more practical for real-world deployment where data collection is often costly.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Insights

The qualitative results (Figures 4, 5, 6, 7, and supplementary materials) visually confirm the quantitative findings. Dreamitate's generated videos consistently illustrate plausible and effective task execution plans, which translate into successful robot behaviors. Conversely, Diffusion Policy often produced erratic or suboptimal trajectories, leading to failures. The interpretability offered by the synthesized video plans is a clear qualitative advantage.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that Dreamitate, by fine-tuning a video diffusion model to generate human tool-use videos for subsequent trajectory tracking, achieves significantly higher generalization, robustness, and data efficiency in real-world robot manipulation tasks compared to a strong behavior cloning baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Dreamitate framework represents a significant advancement in real-world visuomotor policy learning, with profound implications for the field of robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Paradigm for Robot Policy Learning:** Dreamitate introduces a novel "synthesize-then-track-then-act" paradigm that directly integrates large-scale video generation into the robot control loop. This deviates from traditional behavior cloning, world modeling, or representation learning approaches, proposing a new pathway for transferring complex human skills to robots.
- **Effective Bridging of the Embodiment Gap:** The paper offers an elegant and highly effective solution to the long-standing embodiment gap problem. By focusing on generating human demonstrations that utilize common tools and then tracking these tools, Dreamitate cleverly bypasses the complexities of directly mapping human body movements to robot kinematics. This "tool-centric" approach unlocks the vast potential of internet-scale human video data for robotic skill acquisition.
- **Unlocking Generalization through Internet-Scale Priors:** The most impactful finding is Dreamitate's superior generalization performance. By fine-tuning a model like Stable Video Diffusion, which has learned extensive priors about real-world dynamics and human interactions from massive internet datasets, Dreamitate can robustly adapt to novel visual environments, unseen objects, and varying task conditions with unprecedented reliability. This pushes the frontier of robot capabilities beyond narrowly defined training scenarios.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency and Scalability:** The ability of Dreamitate to achieve high performance with significantly fewer human demonstrations than traditional methods is a critical practical advantage. Collecting human demonstrations is considerably less arduous and costly than teleoperating a robot for hours. This data efficiency makes it more feasible to scale robot learning to a wider range of tasks and environments, accelerating the development and deployment of robotic systems.
- **Increased Interpretability and Trust:** The generated video of the planned execution provides a human-interpretable intermediate representation of the robot's intended actions. This transparency is invaluable for debugging, understanding policy failures, and fostering greater trust and collaboration in human-robot interaction contexts, moving away from opaque end-to-end black-box policies.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Dreamitate opens up several promising avenues for future research.
 - **Advanced Tool Tracking:** Improving robustness to occlusion and extending to non-rigid or deformable tools could expand the applicability.
 - **Real-time Closed-Loop Control:** Addressing the computational cost of video models for real-time applications is crucial for dynamic, responsive robot behavior. Advances in efficient video inference could make this a reality.
 - **Complex Multi-tool and Multi-step Tasks:** The framework could be extended to more intricate tasks involving multiple tools or longer, more sequential manipulation plans.
 - **Integration with Language Models:** Combining video generation with large language models could enable more abstract task specification and planning.

In conclusion, "Dreamitate" represents a significant leap forward in robot learning by demonstrating a practical and powerful method to leverage the immense knowledge within large-scale generative models for real-world robotic manipulation. By offering a novel way to bridge the embodiment gap, improving generalization, and enhancing data efficiency and interpretability, this work paves the way for a new generation of more capable, adaptable, and human-friendly robotic systems.
