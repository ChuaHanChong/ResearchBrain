# VCM: Vision Concept Modeling Based on Implicit Contrastive Learning with Vision-Language Instruction Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> The VCM framework introduces a self-supervised approach to vision concept modeling, enabling Large Vision-Language Models to adaptively compress visual tokens based on task instructions. It achieves up to 85% FLOPs reduction and improves average accuracy on VQA benchmarks by 1.3% while enhancing dense perception capabilities across various vision-language tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) process entire images at a low-level token granularity, leading to substantial computational costs and inefficiency, particularly for high-resolution inputs and video sequences.
- Prior vision token reduction methods often lack semantic grounding, fail to preserve crucial spatial relationships, or cannot precisely control the number of retained tokens, limiting their applicability.
- Current LVLM approaches do not effectively mimic human cognition's selective focus on high-level vision concepts, restricting their scalability and versatility in real-world scenarios requiring fine-grained understanding.

## Method

- VCM proposes a ==self-supervised framework== that leverages correlations between vision tokens and text priors (keyword counts in instructions/responses) to dynamically estimate the optimal vision concept length for a given task.
- A ==semantic alignment module==, pre-trained with a semantic alignment loss, adaptively extracts task-relevant keywords from instructions and responses, guiding the vision concept extraction process.
- A novel ==dynamic programming-based forward-backward algorithm== efficiently selects vision tokens with adaptive length and spatial alignment, further optimized by a parallelized segment merging operation that significantly speeds up feature processing.

## Results

- VCM achieved a computational cost reduction of up to **85%** in FLOPs for LLaVA-1.5-7B and improved average accuracy by **1.3%** over the vanilla model on **11** VQA benchmarks (e.g., **+4.9%** on VizWiz, **+5.7%** on SEED, **+2.5%** on MMStar) with **75%** fewer vision tokens.
- The framework enhanced dense perception capabilities, demonstrating improved performance in region-level VQA (RefCOCO), zero-shot image classification (CLIP ViT on COCO), open-vocabulary object detection (F-VLM on OV-COCO), and semantic segmentation (CatSeg on ADE-150/847).
- A crucial parallelized segment merging operation, integral to concept extraction, delivered a **100x** speedup, effectively eliminating a significant computational bottleneck for variable-length feature processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The required number of vision tokens for a task correlates with image-related keyword counts in the associated text, providing a viable pseudo-label for self-supervised learning of vision concept length.
> - Dynamically extracting and compressing only task-relevant vision concepts based on instruction fine-tuning significantly reduces computational costs while simultaneously enhancing the dense perception capabilities of LVLMs.
> - Implicit contrastive sampling through random keyword masking in instructions proves effective for robust vision concept modeling, enhancing learning and offering control over vision token length during inference.
