# Learning Social Navigation from Positive and Negative Demonstrations and Rule-Based Specifications

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed PioneeR, a framework for mobile robot social navigation that integrates density-based reward learning from positive and negative demonstrations with rule-based safety and goal-reaching specifications. This approach yielded higher success rates, up to 99.6% in challenging elevator co-boarding simulations, and demonstrated effective real-world deployment on a mobile robot in human-dense environments.

## Problem

- Mobile robots struggle with balancing adaptability to diverse human behaviors and ensuring strict safety in shared, dynamic environments.
- Classical navigation methods often rely on handcrafted rules, limiting their generalization in complex social contexts.
- Purely learning-based approaches, such as reinforcement or imitation learning, frequently lack explicit safety guarantees or require extensive training resources.

## Method

- PioneeR, a teacher-student framework, learns a composite reward function combining density-based rewards from positive and negative demonstrations with rule-based obstacle avoidance and goal-seeking terms.
- A teacher policy, utilizing short-horizon lookahead control and an adaptive weighting scheme for its reward components, provides robust supervision.
- An uncertainty-aware ==Mixture Density Network (MDN)== is distilled as a compact student policy via dataset aggregation (DAgger) for real-time operation and risk assessment.

## Results

- PioneeR achieved high success rates (**99.4%** in HR-RL, **99.6%** in HL-RR) in elevator co-boarding simulations, outperforming baselines like CVaR-BF and CrowdNav++ by up to **26.6%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the learned density reward for resolving congested interactions and the complementary benefits of rule-based priors for safety and efficiency.
- The MDN student policy achieved higher success rates (**98.0%** and **100.0%**) compared to an MLP, with its uncertainty estimates correlating with proximity to humans in risky frames, indicating potential hazards.
- Real-world experiments validated the framework's practical deployability, demonstrating successful single-policy navigation in diverse elevator co-boarding scenarios with human participants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating density-based rewards derived from both positive and negative demonstrations effectively encodes nuanced human navigation preferences, including both desired and undesired behaviors.
> - Combining data-driven adaptability from learned rewards with explicit rule-based safety and goal constraints provides a robust balance for navigation in dynamic, human-shared environments.
> - Uncertainty estimates from the student MDN policy can serve as valuable indicators of navigation risk, potentially enabling more conservative or risk-aware fallback strategies.
