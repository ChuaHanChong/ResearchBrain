# A Framework for Scalable Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Adversarial Reinforcement Learning in IsaacLab

> [!summary] Summary
> HARL-Adversarial (HARL-A) is presented as an extended IsaacLab framework for scalable heterogeneous multi-agent adversarial policy training in high-fidelity physics simulations. The framework successfully trains robust policies that achieve high win rates and demonstrate emergent specialized adversarial behaviors across diverse robotic morphologies.

## Problem

- Existing Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) frameworks lack robust support for heterogeneous agents in adversarial settings within high-fidelity physics simulations.
- Training instability and ineffective policy learning often arise in adversarial settings due to co-evolving agents and the collapse of value estimates with shared critics in zero-sum games.
- Real-world robotic applications demand agents capable of anticipating and counteracting opponent strategies, which current frameworks often fail to adequately address with heterogeneous agent complexities.

## Method

- Developed HARL-Adversarial (HARL-A) by extending the IsaacLab simulator and integrating competitive variants of HARL algorithms to support scalable multi-agent heterogeneous adversarial learning.
- Introduced team-specific critics within the framework to ensure agents receive non-trivial advantage signals aligned with their individual or team rewards, overcoming the critic collapse issue in zero-sum games.
- Implemented a suite of benchmark adversarial environments (e.g., Sumo, Soccer, 3D Galaga) featuring heterogeneous robots and multi-stage curriculum learning with a zero-buffer strategy for observations.

## Results

- Adversarial policies consistently achieved higher win rates against untrained opponents across various heterogeneous environments, demonstrating successful learning of effective competitive strategies.
- Emergent adversarial behaviors, such as targeting opponent vulnerabilities (e.g., Leatherback targeting Anymal legs) and strategic maneuvers (e.g., Anymal dragging rovers), were observed among heterogeneous agents.
- The zero-buffer strategy, while initially slowing early convergence, enabled seamless expansion of the state space in later curriculum stages, facilitating policy transfer and accelerating overall curriculum progression.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Team-specific critics are crucial for stabilizing adversarial training in heterogeneous multi-agent competitive environments, preventing value function collapse and ensuring effective policy updates.
> - High-fidelity adversarial multi-agent training can induce emergent specialized behaviors and dynamic role specialization among heterogeneous agents without explicit programming.
> - A curriculum learning approach, combined with a zero-buffer observation strategy, allows for seamless state space expansion and accelerates training efficiency across progressively complex adversarial tasks.
