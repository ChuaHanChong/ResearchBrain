# SkillVLA: Tackling Combinatorial Diversity in Dual-Arm Manipulation via Skill Reuse

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore and collaborators developed SkillVLA, a framework that enables dual-arm robots to reuse single-arm skills and dynamically adjust inter-arm cooperation. This approach enhances combinatorial generalization to unobserved task combinations and improves operational efficiency in long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Dual-arm manipulation presents combinatorial diversity, where the number of possible bimanual tasks grows quadratically with the repertoire of single-arm skills.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from "skill entanglement" as they treat both arm actions as a single, monolithic output, hindering flexible recombination of single-arm behaviors.
- This entanglement leads to poor generalization, resulting in near-zero success rates for existing models when confronted with novel combinations of familiar single-arm tasks.

## Method

- SkillVLA utilizes a two-level reasoning architecture where a high-level VLM generates per-arm sub-prompts, which then guide separate low-level VLMs and action experts for each arm.
- An adaptive cooperation estimator predicts a scalar `α` to dynamically control cross-attention between arm-specific action experts, enabling flexible switching between independent and cooperative behaviors.
- Specialized training objectives for the cooperation estimator, including a communication usefulness objective and VLM prior regularization, promote adaptive and stable inter-arm coordination.

## Results

- SkillVLA achieved a 51% success rate on novel combinatorial tasks involving unseen pairings of single-arm skills, a substantial improvement over baselines that showed near-zero success.
- The system demonstrated comparable performance to strong baselines on highly cooperative bimanual tasks while reducing average completion time by approximately 21% on long-horizon tasks through opportunistic parallelization.
- In continual learning scenarios, SkillVLA exhibited superior data efficiency, achieving strong performance for new cooperative skills with as few as 5 fine-tuning demonstrations, significantly outperforming baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling task instructions into per-arm natural language sub-prompts is effective in disentangling single-arm skills from high-level VLM representations.
> - An adaptive mechanism that dynamically controls inter-arm communication based on task requirements enables both independent skill execution and tight cooperation, avoiding constant skill entanglement.
> - Discretizing the cooperation gate to binary states (independent or cooperative) significantly enhances policy stability and robustness over long-horizon tasks compared to continuous gating.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly moving towards "generalist policies" capable of performing a wide range of tasks in diverse environments. A significant driver of this progress has been the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large-scale vision-language pretraining to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and generalize across visual scenes. Recent VLAs have shown impressive capabilities in bimanual manipulation, allowing robots with two arms to perform complex, coordinated actions.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in existing bimanual VLA formulations: they largely overlook the challenge of **combinatorial diversity**. In bimanual tasks, different pairings of single-arm behaviors can lead to qualitatively distinct tasks. For instance, "lift bag with left arm" and "shake cup with right arm" can be composed into a novel bimanual task "lift bag with left, shake cup with right." The number of such possible pairings grows quadratically with the number of single-arm skills, making it infeasible to explicitly train a model on every conceivable combination.

Current VLA designs often address bimanual control by treating the actions of both arms as a single, concatenated action vector. While this simplifies the modeling and can capture tight coordination when directly observed, it leads to **skill entanglement**. This means the model learns the joint distribution of actions as they appear in demonstrations, internalizing specific left-right correlations rather than independent, reusable single-arm skills. Consequently, these models struggle to generalize to "unseen combinations" of single-arm behaviors, resulting in near-zero success rates on novel recomposition tasks. Even approaches that factorize the control to some extent, like TwinVLA, may still suffer from implicit coupling due to uncontrolled cross-arm attention, leading to unpredictable interference.

**SkillVLA directly addresses this fundamental limitation.** It proposes a framework that explicitly enables "skill reuse"—the ability to recombine previously learned single-arm skills across novel left-right pairings. This paradigm shift from learning fixed bimanual primitives to learning and recomposing disentangled skills represents a significant advancement towards truly scalable and generalist bimanual robotic systems. By focusing on explicit skill identification, per-arm reasoning, and adaptive inter-arm communication, SkillVLA aims to unlock a new level of combinatorial generalization that is crucial for deploying versatile dual-arm robots in complex, open-ended environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a bimanual manipulation policy that exhibits **skill reuse**, thereby effectively tackling the challenge of **combinatorial diversity** inherent in dual-arm tasks.

The motivation for SkillVLA stems from several critical observations and limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in dual-arm robotics:

1. **Combinatorial Explosion in Dual-Arm Tasks:** While bimanual manipulation expands a robot's capabilities, it introduces a combinatorial challenge. Many dual-arm tasks can be viewed as compositions of single-arm behaviors. As the repertoire of single-arm skills grows, the number of possible left-right pairings increases quadratically, leading to an overwhelming number of potential bimanual tasks.
2. **Skill Entanglement in Current VLAs:** Mainstream bimanual VLA formulations typically predict the actions of both arms as a single, concatenated vector. This "monolithic" approach, while seemingly simple, leads to "skill entanglement." The model learns the joint probability distribution of arm actions from observed demonstrations, thereby tightly coupling the arms. This design prevents the policy from forming new bimanual behaviors by flexibly recombining single-arm skills that were never observed together during training. The paper formalizes this limitation, identifying both "action entanglement" (at the output level) and "latent entanglement" (in the shared latent representations of action-expert VLAs).
3. **Limited Generalization:** Due to skill entanglement, existing models exhibit poor combinatorial generalization. When confronted with novel left-right skill pairings—tasks that require recombining familiar single-arm behaviors in an unseen configuration—they often fail, frequently reverting to learned training-time patterns or exhibiting unstable behavior.
4. **Inefficiency in Continual Learning:** The inability to disentangle and reuse skills also hinders efficiency in continual learning. Acquiring a new bimanual skill often means relearning both arms from scratch, even if components of the skill (e.g., single-arm actions) are already known. An ideal model should be able to leverage existing single-arm skills and only learn the additional information related to inter-arm coupling.

To overcome these limitations, SkillVLA is motivated by the need for a framework that can:
- **Automatically identify latent skill structures** within demonstrations.
- **Perform reasoning at the skill level**, allowing for flexible invocation and recomposition.
- **Reuse discovered single-arm skills** across different left-right combinations to form novel, unobserved pairings.
- **Adaptively capture inter-arm dependence** only when genuine cooperation is required, otherwise allowing independent execution.
- **Improve data efficiency** in learning new cooperative skills by building upon existing single-arm knowledge.

In essence, the core motivation is to move beyond simply mimicking observed bimanual behaviors to understanding and recombining the underlying skills, thereby scaling bimanual robot capabilities more effectively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkillVLA proposes a novel framework explicitly designed to enable skill reuse and address combinatorial diversity in dual-arm manipulation. Its methodology is built upon a two-level reasoning architecture complemented by an adaptive cooperation estimator and strategic training objectives.

**Core Principles:**
1. **Disentangled Skill Identification:** Instead of monolithic dual-arm reasoning, SkillVLA aims to automatically identify and represent single-arm skills.
2. **Skill-Adaptive Action Generation:** The policy should be able to switch between independent per-arm execution and cooperative bimanual execution based on task demands.
3. **Leveraging VLM Generalization:** It utilizes pretrained Vision-Language Models for high-level semantic understanding and generalization.

**Architectural Components:**

1. **Two-Level Reasoning Pipeline:**
 - **High-Level VLM:** This module, based on a frozen pretrained VLM backbone (e.g., PaliGemma released with π0.5), captures global task intent from the input `x` (observation, instruction, robot state). Crucially, it generates **per-arm sub-prompts (u_L, u_R)**. These natural language prompts serve as explicit skill descriptors for what each arm should do (e.g., "pick the cake on the right"). This explicit linguistic decoupling is central to enabling flexible single-arm recomposition and tackling latent entanglement.
 - **Low-Level VLMs and Action Experts:** Below the high-level VLM, two **separate streams** are maintained, one for each arm. Each stream comprises:
 - A **Low-Level VLM**: Fine-tuned (e.g., via LoRA) to process the visual input `x` and its corresponding per-arm sub-prompt (u_L or u_R), producing a per-arm latent representation (z_L or z_R).
 - An **Action Expert**: Predicts the specific action (a_L or a_R) conditioned on its respective latent (z_L or z_R) and the arm's state.
 - **Adaptive Cross-Attention:** To support coordinated bimanual behaviors when needed, an adaptive cross-attention mechanism is introduced **between the action experts**. This allows information to flow between the arm-specific action experts, capturing inter-arm dependencies.

2. **Cooperation Estimator (Behavior Mode Identification):**
 - This is a critical component that controls the degree of interaction between the two arm-specific action streams. It attends to the high-level VLM's latent representation (z_H = f_H(x)) and predicts a scalar **α ∈**.
 - `α` quantifies the **degree of inter-arm cooperation** required (larger α implies stronger coupling). This `α` value gates the inter-arm message (`m_i`) produced by the cross-attention mechanism (`a_i ~ p(a_i | z_i, α * m_i)`).
 - When `α` is low, the arms operate largely independently, enabling skill recomposition. When `α` is high, cross-attention is fully active, allowing for tight coordination.

**Training Strategies:**

1. **Training the Cooperation Estimator:**
 - A "communication usefulness objective" derived from behavioral cloning (BC) is used.
 - The loss `L_coop` is designed to increase `α` when enabling cross-attention reduces the BC error (meaning communication is useful) and decrease `α` otherwise.
 - `L_coop = λ * (|L_on_BC - L_off_BC|)_sg * α`, where `L_on_BC` is BC loss with cross-attention enabled, `L_off_BC` is BC loss with cross-attention disabled, and `sg` denotes stop-gradient.

2. **Additional Cooperation-Level Learning Mechanisms:**
 - **VLM Prior (`L_prior`):** The cooperation estimator is regularized towards a prior coordination strength (`α_vlm`) obtained from an off-the-shelf VLM (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B) for the current scene and task. This leverages the VLM's commonsense knowledge about task-dependent cooperation.
 - **Sticky Gating (`L_sticky`):** Encourages temporal smoothness of `α` values to prevent erratic switching.
 - **Conservative Communication (`L_sup`):** A simple regularization term (`α_t`) encourages `α` to remain small unless explicitly beneficial, favoring disentangled operation.
 - **Cooperation Level Discretization:** Optionally, the continuous `α` can be discretized to `α ∈ {0, 1}`. The model predicts a probability (`y_hat`) of enabling cross-arm communication, trained with binary cross-entropy where the ground truth (`y_t`) is determined by whether cross-attention *actually* reduces BC error. This improves stability during inference.

**Implementation Details:**
- Uses pretrained PaliGemma checkpoints.
- High-level VLM is fine-tuned for subtask generation then frozen.
- Low-level VLMs are fine-tuned with independent LoRA adapters.
- Cross-attention module QKV projections are trained from scratch.
- Action sampling uses flow-matching, similar to π0.5.

This comprehensive approach allows SkillVLA to dynamically adapt its reasoning and action generation based on the task's coordination requirements, moving beyond the rigid, entangled control paradigms of previous bimanual VLA models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SkillVLA was rigorously evaluated on a real dual-arm robot across a range of tasks, demonstrating substantial improvements in combinatorial generalization, cooperative skill reproduction, long-horizon task efficiency, and continual learning data efficiency.

1. **Single-Arm Skill Reuse (Combinatorial Generalization):**
 - **Problem:** Existing VLAs (π0.5, π0-FAST, TwinVLA) show near-zero success rates on recomposition tasks where single-arm skills (e.g., "lift mug," "stir cake") are combined in novel left-right pairings that were not present in the training data. This highlights severe "skill entanglement" in baselines, causing them to revert to training-time patterns or become unstable.
 - **SkillVLA's Performance:** SkillVLA achieves a remarkable **51% success rate** on these unseen combinatorial tasks, a decisive improvement over baselines (0% for π0.5/π0-FAST, 0.04% for TwinVLA). This demonstrates its ability to reliably disentangle and recompose single-arm skills, significantly enhancing combinatorial generalization.

2. **Dual-Arm (Cooperative) Skill Reproduction:**
 - **Problem:** It's critical that skill reuse doesn't sacrifice the ability to perform tasks requiring tight inter-arm coordination.
 - **SkillVLA's Performance:** On highly cooperative tasks like SHAKE (shaking a cup with a cap) and BALL (lifting a ball), SkillVLA performs comparably to the strong baseline π0.5. This indicates that its adaptive communication mechanism is sufficiently expressive to support close coordination when required. For ALIGN (aligning blocks), SkillVLA even slightly outperforms baselines.
 - **Ablation Study (Cross-Attention):** Disabling cross-attention within SkillVLA ("SkillVLA w/o Attn.") leads to a significant performance drop, especially on tasks requiring tight low-level alignment (SHAKE, BALL), confirming the importance of this communication pathway for cooperative behaviors.

3. **Applications in Long-Horizon Settings (Mode Switching and Efficiency):**
 - **Problem:** Complex, multi-stage tasks often interleave independent per-arm actions with cooperative bimanual phases. Models need to infer the cooperation level and switch modes dynamically.
 - **SkillVLA's Performance:**
 - **Comparable Progress:** SkillVLA achieves progress scores comparable to strong baselines on long-horizon tasks like TUBES and COLLECT ITEMS.
 - **Enhanced Efficiency:** Crucially, SkillVLA reduces the average completion time by approximately **21%** compared to baselines. This is because it opportunistically parallelizes independent subtasks (e.g., transferring tubes with both arms simultaneously, `α=0`), while baselines tend to follow sequential, demonstration-like patterns.
 - **Coherent Mode Switching:** The cooperation gate (`α`) in SkillVLA evolves coherently with task progress, switching to `α=1` (cooperative mode) during inherently bimanual phases (e.g., moving a loaded rack) and remaining at `α=0` (independent mode) during parallelizable collection phases. This demonstrates its ability to infer the required cooperation level.
 - **Ablation Study (Continuous vs. Discretized Gating):** Using a continuous `α` leads to instability and higher fluctuations over long horizons, resulting in lower success and higher variance. Discretizing `α` to `{0, 1}` significantly improves robustness and stability by enforcing clearer mode switching.

4. **Reuse of Learned Skills in Continual Learning:**
 - **Problem:** Acquiring new, related cooperative skills often requires extensive demonstrations, even if single-arm components are already known.
 - **SkillVLA's Performance:** In continual learning scenarios (e.g., adapting single-arm LIFT BAG to dual-arm LIFT BAG, or single-arm SHAKE CUP to cooperative SHAKE CUP), SkillVLA exhibits superior data efficiency. For LIFT BAG, it achieves 20% success zero-shot and reaches near-peak performance with only **5 fine-tuning demonstrations**, whereas baselines (π0.5, TwinVLA) remain far from convergence even after 30 demonstrations. This highlights SkillVLA's ability to effectively leverage pretrained single-arm behaviors as a strong initialization and adapt them rapidly to new cooperative policies, significantly reducing the demonstration burden.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that SkillVLA effectively tackles skill entanglement and combinatorial diversity through its hierarchical reasoning, explicit skill descriptors, and adaptive cooperation mechanism. It not only achieves robust combinatorial generalization but also improves efficiency and data economy in complex bimanual tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SkillVLA makes several significant contributions to the field of robotics, with substantial potential impact on the development of generalist bimanual manipulation systems:

1. **Resolution of Skill Entanglement:** The most critical contribution is the explicit identification and effective mitigation of the "skill entanglement" problem in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for dual-arm manipulation. By demonstrating how monolithic VLA designs inherently limit combinatorial generalization, the paper sheds light on a fundamental bottleneck for scaling bimanual robot capabilities. SkillVLA provides a robust architectural solution that allows for genuine disentanglement and reuse of skills.

2. **Enhanced Combinatorial Generalization:** SkillVLA dramatically improves the robot's ability to perform novel bimanual tasks by recombining learned single-arm skills that were never jointly demonstrated. The leap from near-zero to 51% success on unseen combinations represents a paradigm shift, enabling robots to adapt to a much wider array of tasks without requiring explicit training for every permutation. This is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured environments where novel task compositions are common.

3. **Increased Data Efficiency in Continual Learning:** The ability of SkillVLA to rapidly acquire new cooperative skills with minimal demonstrations (e.g., 5 fine-tuning demonstrations) is highly impactful. This significantly reduces the data collection burden, a major bottleneck in robotics research. It enables a more efficient, "learn-once, reuse-many" approach to skill acquisition, fostering more scalable and adaptable robot learning pipelines.

4. **Improved Operational Efficiency and Autonomy:** By enabling the robot to intelligently identify cooperation levels and parallelize independent subtasks, SkillVLA leads to faster completion times (21% reduction in experiments). This has direct practical implications for robot throughput and productivity in industrial, service, or domestic settings where efficiency is key. The robot can autonomously choose the most efficient execution strategy, whether independent or cooperative, based on real-time task context.

5. **Foundation for Generalist Bimanual Agents:** SkillVLA lays important groundwork for developing truly generalist bimanual robotic agents. By decomposing complex bimanual tasks into reusable single-arm skills and an adaptive coordination mechanism, it moves closer to a system that can understand and execute a vast, open-ended vocabulary of human instructions, leading to more versatile and capable robots.

6. **Insights into Robot Reasoning:** The success of the two-level reasoning pipeline (high-level VLM for semantic decomposition, low-level VLMs for per-arm action generation) and the adaptive cooperation estimator offers valuable insights into how robots can effectively reason about and execute complex, coordinated behaviors. The coherent switching of the cooperation gate demonstrates a promising approach for dynamic task interpretation.

**Limitations and Future Work (as acknowledged by authors):**
The paper acknowledges that SkillVLA's reliance on pretrained VLMs for high-level reasoning ties its performance, latency, and cost profile to these foundation models. Future work will focus on:
- **Efficiency:** Distilling or compressing the VLM's reasoning into more lightweight policy components.
- **Richer Skill Representations:** Exploring skill representations beyond natural language, potentially allowing for more nuanced and abstract skill encoding.

Despite these limitations, SkillVLA marks a significant step forward in making bimanual robots more adaptable, efficient, and generalizable, paving the way for broader deployment in real-world applications.
