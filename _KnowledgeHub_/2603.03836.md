# SkillVLA: Tackling Combinatorial Diversity in Dual-Arm Manipulation via Skill Reuse

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore and collaborators developed SkillVLA, a framework that enables dual-arm robots to reuse single-arm skills and dynamically adjust inter-arm cooperation. This approach enhances combinatorial generalization to unobserved task combinations and improves operational efficiency in long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Dual-arm manipulation presents combinatorial diversity, where the number of possible bimanual tasks grows quadratically with the repertoire of single-arm skills.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from "skill entanglement" as they treat both arm actions as a single, monolithic output, hindering flexible recombination of single-arm behaviors.
- This entanglement leads to poor generalization, resulting in near-zero success rates for existing models when confronted with novel combinations of familiar single-arm tasks.

## Method

- SkillVLA utilizes a two-level reasoning architecture where a high-level VLM generates per-arm sub-prompts, which then guide separate low-level VLMs and action experts for each arm.
- An adaptive cooperation estimator predicts a scalar `α` to dynamically control cross-attention between arm-specific action experts, enabling flexible switching between independent and cooperative behaviors.
- Specialized training objectives for the cooperation estimator, including a communication usefulness objective and VLM prior regularization, promote adaptive and stable inter-arm coordination.

## Results

- SkillVLA achieved a 51% success rate on novel combinatorial tasks involving unseen pairings of single-arm skills, a substantial improvement over baselines that showed near-zero success.
- The system demonstrated comparable performance to strong baselines on highly cooperative bimanual tasks while reducing average completion time by approximately 21% on long-horizon tasks through opportunistic parallelization.
- In continual learning scenarios, SkillVLA exhibited superior data efficiency, achieving strong performance for new cooperative skills with as few as 5 fine-tuning demonstrations, significantly outperforming baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling task instructions into per-arm natural language sub-prompts is effective in disentangling single-arm skills from high-level VLM representations.
> - An adaptive mechanism that dynamically controls inter-arm communication based on task requirements enables both independent skill execution and tight cooperation, avoiding constant skill entanglement.
> - Discretizing the cooperation gate to binary states (independent or cooperative) significantly enhances policy stability and robustness over long-horizon tasks compared to continuous gating.
