# LadderSym: A Multimodal Interleaved Transformer for Music Practice Error Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> LadderSym introduces a multimodal interleaved Transformer for music practice error detection, integrating a two-stream encoder with layer-wise cross-attention and a symbolic-prompted decoder. The system improves error identification, particularly for missed notes in complex pieces, achieving a 54.7% F1 score for missed notes on MAESTRO-E and generalizing effectively to real-world beginner performances.

## Problem

- Existing music practice error detection systems offer coarse feedback, and explicit alignment methods are sensitive to substantial deviations, limiting their reliability for beginners.
- State-of-the-art latent alignment models like Polytune use late fusion, restricting inter-stream alignment and cross-modality comparison between practice and score audio.
- Relying solely on audio for music score representation introduces ambiguity in the frequency spectrum, making it difficult to detect errors like missed notes, especially with concurrent notes.

## Method

- **LadderSym** employs a ==Ladder encoder== with a two-stream Transformer that uses interleaved cross-attention modules before each Vision Transformer (ViT) block for continuous, layer-wise alignment and additive fusion of score and practice audio.
- A T5-based multimodal decoder is used, prompted with tokenized symbolic representations of the music score, to provide unambiguous reference information and reduce acoustic ambiguity.
- The model generates MIDI-like tokens detailing musical events with explicit error labels (Extra, Missed, Correct), trained end-to-end with weighted cross-entropy loss.

## Results

- LadderSym improved the Missed Notes F1 score on the MAESTRO-E dataset from **26.8%** (Polytune) to **54.7%** and increased Extra Notes F1 by 14.4 points to **86.4%**.
- On a real-world dataset of beginner piano performances, LadderSym achieved a Missed Notes F1 of **78.5%** compared to Polytune's **63.9%**, demonstrating improved generalization without finetuning.
- The model is parameter-efficient with 172 million parameters (vs. Polytune's 192 million) and exhibits lower latency, with encoder latency around **97ms** compared to Polytune's **129ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frequent, interleaved cross-attention between audio streams within the encoder enhances fine-grained temporal alignment and improves error detection accuracy without sacrificing asymmetric feature extraction.
> - Integrating unambiguous symbolic score prompts into the decoder significantly reduces ambiguity in score representation, especially benefiting the detection of challenging error types like missed notes.
> - The Ladder encoder design promotes an asymmetric division of labor, allowing the practice stream to retain strong local information while the score stream integrates cross-stream features more effectively, leading to specialized comparison.
