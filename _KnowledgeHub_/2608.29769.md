# Learning Agile Perceptive Traversal of Sparse 3D Structures for Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> A humanoid traverses monkey bars end-to-end from raw head-mounted solid-state lidar, with no intermediate map. An attention-based grid encoder plus GRU memory extracts the sparse bar returns, and three privileged subtask experts (jump-up, brachiation, jump-down) are merged by phase-scheduled DAgger distillation with regularized PPO refinement. Battery-sag, actuator-thermal and lidar-noise models plus passive hook hands enable transfer, giving 14/15 hardware successes at up to 0.5 m/s.

## Problem

- Elevation maps discard thin and overhanging geometry, while voxel grids recover full 3D but pay steeply in memory and compute as resolution rises.
- Raw-exteroception policies so far target dense terrain and large obstacles; agile contact-accurate interaction with centimeter-scale sparse structures is largely unexplored.
- Existing brachiators are purpose-built platforms or assume known bar positions, and none combines onboard perception with jumping onto and off the structure.

## Method

- ==AME-2 attention encoder== adapted to the native lidar scan grid, feeding a ==GRU memory==, with an ==auxiliary decoder== predicting the closest bar's centerline position and orientation.
- ==Phase-scheduled multi-teacher distillation==: three privileged experts cloned via ==DAgger==, then critic warm-up, then ==PPO== with a decaying behavior-cloning anchor and per-phase advantage standardization.
- ==Sim-to-real modeling stack==: first-order battery voltage-sag law rescaling stall torque and no-load speed, leaky-integrator actuator thermal proxy, ray-cone edge-bleed lidar noise, plus passive water-jet-cut hook end-effectors.

## Results

- Hardware succeeds on the full jump-up, brachiation, jump-down sequence in **14 of 15** trials across three ladders, reaching **0.5 m/s** brachiation, near human speeds.
- The attention encoder achieves the lowest behavior-cloning loss (**2.35e-2** with auxiliary loss vs **2.62e-2** CNN, **2.76e-2** MLP, **2.90e-2** blind) with **13.8k** parameters, roughly an order fewer than CNN.
- Sim-to-sim in MuJoCo holds **70-90%** full-sequence success over **1.65-1.90 m** rung heights; the ducking policy clears **1-5 cm** bars at every feasible clearance with **100%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Raw sparse sensor returns can drive explosive contact-accurate whole-body motion; attention plus recurrence substitutes for an explicit map at a fraction of the parameters.
> - Explicit geometric auxiliary supervision, here bar-centerline regression, meaningfully sharpens a recurrent perception backbone during distillation rather than merely regularizing it.
> - Near actuation limits, sim-to-real hinges on modeling platform non-idealities such as voltage sag and winding heating; ignoring them caused brownouts in practice.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic locomotion and manipulation in complex 3D environments often rely on the robot's ability to perceive and interpret its surroundings. Current approaches for whole-body control and navigation in legged robots frequently employ intermediate spatial representations, such as 2.5D elevation maps or voxel grids. Elevation maps, while computationally efficient and beneficial for generalization, have limitations in representing thin or overhanging structures. Voxel grids can capture full 3D geometry but incur significant memory and computational costs as resolution increases.

An alternative direction in robotic perception involves directly processing raw sensor data, such as depth images or point clouds, to inform policy decisions. Previous research in this area has primarily focused on tasks involving dense terrain or large obstacles for quadrupeds and humanoids, including parkour and collision avoidance. However, agile and contact-accurate interaction with sparse, thin 3D structures, such as monkey bars, remains a challenge largely unaddressed by direct raw sensor data approaches, especially for general-purpose humanoids using onboard sensing.

This work contributes to the landscape by specifically tackling the problem of agile, perceptive traversal of sparse 3D structures. It bridges the gap between general perceptive locomotion and tasks requiring fine-grained interaction with slender objects. The paper positions monkey-bar traversal as an "extreme instance" of this problem, stressing the need for precise perception, challenging exploration of contact sequences, and agile whole-body control within hardware constraints. By demonstrating a humanoid executing a full jump-up, brachiation, and jump-down sequence using onboard lidar, the research advances the capabilities of robots in dynamically interacting with complex, non-contiguous environments without relying on prior maps or detailed environmental models. It also builds upon and extends techniques from privileged distillation, sim-to-real transfer modeling, and attention-based neural map encoding to address these specific challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to perform agile perceptive traversal of sparse 3D structures using direct observations from onboard sensors. This objective is explored through the challenging task of monkey-bar traversal, which involves a sequence of dynamic maneuvers: jumping up to the structure, brachiating across sparse bars, and safely jumping down.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges inherent in this task:
1. **Fine-grained Perception of Thin Structures:** Sparse 3D structures, such as monkey bars with small radii (1–3 cm), yield few and intermittent returns in raw lidar scans. Traditional intermediate representations like elevation maps often fail to adequately capture such thin, overhanging geometry. The ability to extract task-relevant geometric information directly from sparse sensor data is therefore critical.
2. **Hard Exploration of Precise Contact Sequences:** Successfully traversing monkey bars requires discovering and executing precise contact sequences involving dynamic gripping and releasing, swinging, and controlled landings. This presents a difficult exploration problem for reinforcement learning algorithms, as successful contact states are rare and sensitive to execution accuracy.
3. **Agile Whole-Body Control under Tight Hardware Limits:** Humanoid robots performing dynamic maneuvers like jumping and swinging impose significant loads on actuators and batteries. Maintaining agile, accurate whole-body motions requires operating close to the platform's physical limits, necessitating robust control and careful consideration of thermal and power constraints.

To address these motivations, the authors established specific objectives:
- **Develop an end-to-end perceptive control system:** This system should operate directly on raw observations from a head-mounted solid-state lidar, avoiding intermediate spatial representations that discard critical information.
- **Design a perception backbone for sparse data:** The system needed an attention-based encoder with recurrent memory to process raw lidar scans, select task-relevant returns, and integrate information over time, given the sparsity of observations from thin bars.
- **Facilitate policy exploration and robustness:** A method for training policies capable of complex, multi-phase tasks was required. This involved using a teacher-student pipeline with privileged subtask experts for jumping up, brachiating, and jumping down, distilled into a single perceptive student policy.
- **Ensure reliable sim-to-real transfer:** Accurate modeling of hardware specificities such as battery-voltage sag, actuator thermal limits, and lidar noise was crucial for successfully transferring policies trained in simulation to a physical humanoid robot.
- **Equip the humanoid for robust interaction:** The robot needed appropriate end-effectors, such as passive hooks, to tolerate contact placement errors, manage dynamic loads, and enable efficient disengagement from bars.
- **Validate the approach on hardware:** Demonstrate the learned policy completing the full jump-up, brachiation, and jump-down sequence on a physical humanoid across diverse bar configurations, and show the perception backbone's reusability for other sparse obstacle avoidance tasks (e.g., ducking under thin overhead obstacles).

The overall motivation is to expand the range of dynamic and complex interaction tasks that general-purpose humanoids can perform autonomously in unstructured environments, moving beyond dense terrain navigation to agile interaction with fine-grained 3D elements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research combines hardware modifications, a multi-stage reinforcement learning pipeline, and detailed sim-to-real transfer modeling to enable a humanoid robot to traverse sparse 3D structures.

**A. Hardware and Sensor Configuration:**
- **Humanoid Robot:** The PM-01 humanoid robot was used as the platform.
- **Passive Hook End-Effectors:** Custom stainless-steel passive hooks were designed and integrated. These hooks replace the robot's hands, are capable of supporting the robot's full weight, tolerate contact placement error (60mm opening for 1-3cm bar radii), facilitate disengagement using the wrist-yaw joint (rotating the hook out of the bar plane), and feature symmetric geometry for bidirectional traversal.
- **Head-Mounted Solid-State Lidar:** A RoboSense E1R solid-state lidar with an integrated IMU was mounted on the robot's head. This sensor provides a dense 192x144 scan pattern over a 120°x90° field of view at 10 Hz. The solid-state nature reduces motion-induced scanning distortion during dynamic maneuvers, and its resolution is sufficient to resolve thin bars at jumping distances. The policy directly consumes a decimated subset of these returns.

**B. Learning Approach Overview:**
The system utilizes reinforcement learning (RL) within IsaacLab, employing the RSL-RL implementation of PPO. The learning process is divided into two main stages:
1. **Exploration (Privileged Experts):** Multiple "privileged teachers" are trained with access to ground-truth bar positions (unavailable to the real robot) to discover optimal contact modes for subtasks.
2. **Perception (Perceptive Student):** A single "perceptive student" policy, operating solely on onboard sensor data (raw lidar), learns to reproduce the expert behaviors through distillation and refinement.

**C. Privileged Teachers:**
- **Subtask Specialization:** Three separate privileged teachers are trained for: jumping up, brachiating, and jumping down. An additional teacher is trained for the ducking task.
- **Observations:** Teachers receive privileged information including ground-truth bar endpoints, contact states, base velocity, and simulated battery/thermal states.
- **Reward Conditioning:** The brachiation teacher is conditioned on goal-position commands, while jump-up and jump-down teachers use contact-based rewards to encourage discovery of distinct contact modes without human priors.
- **Architecture:** All teachers are MLP policies. The obstacle avoidance teacher uses a height map with an upward-directed raycast channel (teacher-only).

**D. Training Terrain and Curriculum:**
- **Procedural Generation:** Policies are trained on procedurally generated ladder environments with randomized geometry (bar radius, width, spacing) per environment. Random objects are scattered for spatial disturbance robustness.
- **Curriculum Learning:**
 - Jump-up/Jump-down: Curriculum progresses with increasing ladder height.
 - Brachiation: Curriculum progresses with increased bar spacing and reduced bar width.

**E. Reward Design:**
The reward function comprises two categories:
- **Base (Shared) Rewards:** These terms are common across all tasks and primarily act as limits and safety measures, penalizing undesired body contacts, hand/foot slip, filter clipping, joint position/velocity/torque limit excursions, high thermal integrator states, battery voltage sag, and high limb torque budgets.
- **Task-Specific Rewards:**
 - **Brachiation:** Rewards for goal tracking, dense hand/base progress, and penalties for stagnation.
 - **Jump-up:** Rewards for hand/torso tracking to the nearest bar, dense progress, hand contact at target radius, grasp load, and sustained grasp/stable hang bonuses.
 - **Jump-down:** Rewards for feet contact below bar height (landing), root height tracking, foot speed near ground (descent braking), penalties for bar contact after release, heading misalignment, and bonuses for stable landing.
 - **Ducking:** Follows a formulation similar to.

**F. Multi-Teacher Distillation (Student Training):**
The learned expert policies are distilled into a single perceptive student in three stages, with an active teacher `p` scheduled based on task progression:
1. **Distillation:** Pure behavior cloning (DAgger) using an MSE loss with truncated Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). The student policy learns to imitate the active teacher's actions.
2. **Critic Warmup:** The critic network is trained to regress unclipped Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) returns, while actor behavior cloning updates continue. This stabilizes the critic before full RL.
3. **Regularized PPO:** A gradual transition from imitation to reinforcement learning using the clipped PPO surrogate objective. A decaying behavior cloning anchor allows the policy to adapt to its perceptual limitations. Advantages are standardized per phase, and the value loss is weighted by clipped inverse return variance (PopArt-inspired) to handle differing reward scales across phases.

**G. Policy Architecture (Perceptive Student):**
- **Inputs:**
 - **Proprioception (`o_p_t`):** Positions and velocities of all actuated joints (except head-yaw), IMU angular velocity and projected gravity, and the previous action, with a 4-frame history.
 - **Command (`c_t`):** Planar goal position and a jump-down trigger.
 - **Lidar Observations (`o_e_t`):** Decimated E1R returns (36x35x4 for brachiation, 36x48x4 for ducking) refreshed at 10 Hz, where each grid node provides hit position relative to sensor and range. Brachiation crops rays to the upper-half workspace; ducking uses the full field of view.
- **Perception Encoder:** An attention-based map encoder (AME-2) adapted to process the raw point cloud directly on its 2D grid structure, rather than as an unordered set.
- **Memory Module:** A GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit) receives the perception features, along with an embedding of proprioception and command, to integrate information over time.
- **Action Head:** An MLP decodes the GRU's hidden state into 23 joint position targets (excluding head-yaw) at 50 Hz, which are tracked by joint PD controllers.

**H. Auxiliary Guidance Losses:**
To provide stronger gradient signals for the recurrent perceptive backbone during distillation, an auxiliary bar-centerline prediction loss is introduced. A decoder is trained on the GRU hidden state to predict the closest bar's relative position and orientation.

**I. Ducking Under Thin Obstacles:**
A separate, standalone ducking policy is trained. It reuses the AME-2 encoder and GRU memory from the brachiation policy without architectural modifications, consumes raw E1R observations, and is trained under the lidar noise model. It is distilled from a height-map-based privileged teacher.

**J. Sim-to-Real Transfer Modeling:**
Critical for transferring dynamic skills, three physical effects dominant at the platform's limits are modeled and randomized during training:
- **Battery Voltage Model:** A first-order lag model captures voltage sag under load, driven by the summed absolute joint torque. The saged voltage (`V_t`) rescales each motor's stall torque and no-load speed (`tau_sat`, `dot_q_max`), affecting actuator effort. A penalty for low battery voltage is included in the reward.
- **Actuator Thermal Model:** Each low-torque joint (18 in total) has a leaky integrator representing a winding-temperature proxy. It charges with the load ratio (`rho_j_t`) and leaks towards zero. This state is a privileged observation and penalized in the reward.
- **Lidar Noise Models:**
 - **Training-time Model (IsaacLab):** A custom Warp raycaster dithers ray directions within the beam cone, adds Gaussian range noise, and injects outliers via edge detection and background mixing (modeling mixed-pixel effect). It also randomizes mounting calibration, drops/corrupts returns, freezes frames (sensor delay), and gates ranges.
 - **Sim-to-Sim Validation Model (MuJoCo):** Explicitly approximates beam divergence by casting multiple stratified rays within the cone for each pixel. It uses inverse-square weighting for return fusion, models edge dropout based on range gaps, and introduces spurious returns. This higher-fidelity model aims to reproduce hardware-observed edge-bleed artifacts.

These detailed models and randomization help the policy to be robust to real-world imperfections and operate safely near hardware limits.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence of the system's capabilities through ablation studies, sim-to-sim validation, and real-world hardware deployment.

**A. Encoder and Auxiliary-Loss Ablation:**
- An ablation study compared different point-cloud encoders for the combined jump→brachiation→jump-down distillation task, evaluating their behavior cloning (BC) loss.
- The attention-based AME-2 encoder (adapted for point clouds) achieved the lowest BC loss across all subtask phases. It also demonstrated parameter efficiency, using roughly an order of magnitude fewer parameters than a CNN and two orders fewer than an MLP.
- The "blind" student (without lidar perception) performed worst, indicating that proprioception alone is insufficient for the task. This confirms the necessity of perception for interacting with sparse structures.
- The inclusion of the centerline auxiliary loss (predicting the closest bar's relative position and orientation) further lowered the BC loss in every phase, suggesting that explicit geometric supervision provides a beneficial gradient signal for the recurrent perceptive backbone during distillation.

**B. Sim-to-Sim Validation:**
- **Combined Traversal Policy:** The policy was evaluated in MuJoCo using the higher-fidelity beam-divergence lidar model (unseen during training).
 - Across a range of rung heights (1.65 m to 1.90 m) with 0.35 m bar spacing, the policy achieved success rates between 70% and 90% for the full sequence.
 - Success rates moderately degraded as the first bar height approached the upper limit of the robot's jumping capability.
- **Ducking Policy:** This policy was tested on cylindrical bars with diameters from 0.01 m to 0.05 m and underside clearances from 1.1 m to 1.5 m.
 - The policy demonstrated a 100% success rate, consistently detecting and clearing all obstacles it could physically fit under.

**C. Real-World Results:**
- **Full Traversal Sequence:** The policy was deployed on the physical PM-01 humanoid, executing the full jump-up → brachiation → jump-down sequence. The robot started from rest, and an operator specified a single goal position. Onboard pose estimation was provided by SE(3) lidar-inertial odometry.
 - Across 15 hardware trials, spanning three different ladder configurations (Ladder A, B, C, with varying height and spacing), the policy successfully completed the full sequence in 14 trials, resulting in a 93% success rate.
 - The single failure occurred during brachiation when a hook did not advance to the next bar after a successful jump-up.
 - Brachiation speeds of up to 0.5 m/s were achieved, which is comparable to reported human brachiation speeds.
 - Hardware measurements during a full sequence showed peak mechanical joint power of 2.11 kW during the jump-up, with the battery voltage dropping to 34.7 V. Low-torque actuators approached 93% of their torque limits. These observations validated the necessity and effectiveness of the battery and thermal models for sim-to-real transfer.
- **Ducking Under Thin Obstacles:** The ducking policy was tested in real-world scenarios involving 2 cm x 2 cm wooden bars mounted in random orientations.
 - In 10 passes with randomly placed bars, the robot successfully approached, passed beneath the obstacle without contact (e.g., a 1.2 m clearance), and recovered to a stable stance in all trials.

These results indicate that the learned perceptive control system, supported by robust sim-to-real transfer modeling, enables complex and dynamic interaction with sparse 3D structures on a physical humanoid robot using only onboard solid-state lidar.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in humanoid robotics, particularly in the domain of agile, perceptive interaction with complex 3D environments.

**Significance:**
- **Pioneering Humanoid Brachiation with Onboard Sensing:** This work represents the first reported demonstration of a general-purpose humanoid robot perceiving and executing a complete jump-up, brachiation, and jump-down sequence using only onboard sensing. Previous brachiation studies often relied on specialized platforms, simplified models, known bar positions, or human intervention for perception.
- **Validation of Raw Lidar Perception for Dynamic Tasks:** The study successfully demonstrates that raw solid-state lidar observations, processed by an attention-based encoder with recurrent memory, can provide sufficient information to support explosive, contact-accurate whole-body motions in complex 3D structures. This challenges the common reliance on dense, pre-processed spatial representations.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The detailed modeling of critical hardware phenomena (battery voltage sag, actuator thermal limits, and lidar noise) is shown to be crucial and effective for reliably transferring high-dynamic behaviors from simulation to a physical robot. This contributes to the broader field of robotic control, where accurately bridging the sim-to-real gap remains a significant challenge, especially for tasks pushing robots to their physical limits.
- **Efficient Learning for Complex Multi-Phase Tasks:** The phase-scheduled multi-teacher distillation scheme with PPO post-refinement proves to be an effective strategy for combining specialized subtask experts into a single, deployable policy, addressing the exploration challenges inherent in multi-stage dynamic behaviors.
- **Generalizability of Perception Backbone:** The demonstration that the same perception backbone (AME-2 encoder and GRU memory) can be directly reused for a distinctly different task—ducking under thin overhead obstacles—without architectural modifications highlights the versatility and robustness of the proposed perception system.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Humanoid Capabilities:** The capabilities demonstrated in this paper could enable humanoids to operate in more diverse and challenging environments, including disaster zones, construction sites, or even recreational settings, where agile interaction with sparse or fragile structures is required.
- **Reduced Reliance on Pre-computed Maps:** By operating directly on raw sensor data, the robot can potentially adapt more quickly to unknown or dynamic environments, reducing the need for extensive prior mapping or high-fidelity environmental models.
- **Advancements in Perception for Sparse Data:** The attention-based encoder's effectiveness in selecting task-relevant geometry from sparse lidar returns could inspire similar approaches for other robotic tasks involving fine-grained interaction with slender or occluded objects.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Methodologies:** The specific models for battery and thermal dynamics, alongside the fidelity of lidar noise modeling, offer practical insights and benchmarks for future sim-to-real transfer efforts in high-performance robotics.
- **Foundational Work for More Complex Behaviors:** This work lays a foundation for future research into more generalized and robust manipulation and locomotion skills for humanoids in 3D environments, including tasks requiring longer-horizon spatial memory or more diverse types of sparse structures.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The current system operates with a small set of separately trained task policies. Future work includes extending the system's robustness to substantially more diverse geometries and developing direct-lidar perception capabilities for partially observable structures that necessitate long-horizon spatial memory. These efforts would further enhance the robot's autonomy and adaptability in highly complex environments.
