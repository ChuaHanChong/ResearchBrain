# RoHIL: Robust Human-in-the-Loop Robotic Reinforcement Learning Against Illumination Variations

> [!summary] Summary
> The RoHIL framework enhances Human-in-the-Loop Reinforcement Learning policies to operate robustly under diverse illumination conditions by using an offline fine-tuning approach with world-model relighting and anti-forgetting mechanisms. It enables HIL-RL policy deployment costs to reduce from O(N) to O(1) and achieves 1.00 success rates on tasks like USB insertion even under extreme lighting variations, outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Human-in-the-Loop Reinforcement Learning (HIL-RL) policies, while effective in training environments, severely degrade when deployed in new workstations due to visual domain shifts caused by illumination variations.
- Current remedies are impractical: re-collecting demonstrations and retraining is costly (O(N)), simple image augmentations fail to capture complex light changes, and naive fine-tuning leads to catastrophic forgetting.
- The challenge is to make HIL-RL policies robust to significant illumination variations without incurring prohibitive retraining costs or losing proficiency in the original training environment.

## Method

- A world-model relighter (Cosmos-Transfer1-DiffusionRenderer) generates diverse and realistic illumination variations from existing source-workstation data, avoiding the need for new real-robot interaction.
- Illumination-Retention Replay (IRR) is a data-level mechanism that carefully mixes original-light policy data with relit policy and demonstration data in the replay buffer (with an optimal original-light fraction of "alpha"=0.75) to balance adaptation and retention.
- Anchored Bellman–Actor Regularization applies model-level anti-forgetting by adding feature and actor-side reference-action anchors to the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) objective, regularizing the visual encoder and policy towards a frozen source-workstation policy on expert demonstrations.

## Results

- The full RoHIL framework achieved 1.00 success rates on both source and shifted lighting conditions for the critical `usb_insertion` task after offline fine-tuning, significantly outperforming a standard SAC objective which resulted in lower success rates (0.57 source, 0.73 shifted).
- An ablation study demonstrated that the combination of both feature and actor-side reference-action anchors was necessary to achieve simultaneous high success rates (1.**00/1**.00) on source and shifted lighting, with individual anchors only partially addressing the problem.
- RoHIL consistently achieved the highest success rates across four real-robot manipulation tasks (e.g., RAM 1.00, USB 1.00, wiping 0.87, breaker 0.83) at a **60%** illumination shift, demonstrating a significant robustness advantage over five HIL-compatible baselines that experienced substantial performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating diverse and realistic illumination variations from existing real-robot trajectories via world-model relighting is an efficient way to create training data for illumination robustness without costly new physical interaction.
> - Combining both data-level (Illumination-Retention Replay) and model-level (Anchored Bellman–Actor Regularization) anti-forgetting mechanisms is crucial for stable adaptation to new illumination conditions while simultaneously preventing catastrophic forgetting of the source task.
> - Separate anchor terms targeting the visual encoder (feature anchor) and the actor's action distribution (reference-action anchor) address distinct failure modes, synergistically contributing to robust performance across illumination shifts and preserving source-workstation proficiency.
