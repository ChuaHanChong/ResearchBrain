# AR2-D2:Training a Robot Without a Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> AR2-D2 is an iOS app that collects robot manipulation demonstrations without any real robot: a user films themselves manipulating an object, LiDAR depth plus hand-pose tracking drive an AR Franka Panda whose end-effector mirrors the hand, and the hand is segmented out and inpainted to yield RGB and voxel training data. Five such demos plus 3k fine-tuning iterations train a real robot to comparable success as teleoperated data.

## Problem

- Kinesthetic teaching and teleoperation controllers demand specialized user training, making demonstration collection slow, labor-intensive, and hard to crowdsource to untrained people.
- Every existing collection method needs a physical robot, so throughput is capped by the number of expensive robots available in a lab.
- Lab-bound robots only see a handful of nearby objects; simulation alternatives scale but suffer sim-to-real gaps and limited trainable task variety.

## Method

- **AR2-D2** iOS app on Unity plus ==AR Foundation== projects a Franka Panda URDF into LiDAR-sensed 3D space; Apple hand-pose detection plus depth reconstructs 6D hand pose at 30 fps to drive the AR end-effector.
- Users specify key-points the end-effector should reach, with real-time AR playback and collision feedback so task feasibility and reachability are visually verified before recording.
- Post-processing pipeline segments the hand with ==Segment-Anything==, fills the hole via ==E2FGVI== video inpainting, and emits RGB video plus voxelized 3D scenes to train ==Image-BC== and ==PerAct== agents.

## Results

- Across 9 personalized objects and press/push/pick-up, AR2-D2 matched VR-teleoperated real-robot data (t=0.547, p=0.592), beating it on LEGO-train push (**50.0%** vs **30.0%**) and staying within **14.3%** elsewhere.
- User study (N=10, 300 demos) showed collection times of **22.1** and **29.5** seconds versus kinesthetic teaching's **41.6** and **61.4** seconds, significant at p below 8e-6, with equal demonstration success rates.
- Ablations fixed the optimum at **5** fine-tuning demos and **3k** iterations (~10 minutes); voxel PerAct beat Image-BC without fine-tuning, e.g. **40.0%** vs **0.00%** on pressing the buzzer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interface familiarity beats interface fidelity: recording your own hand needs zero training yet yields policy-grade data, so demonstration collection can decouple from robot hardware availability.
> - AR overlay is what makes hand video usable, since projecting the robot gives live kinematic and collision feedback that passive human-activity video cannot supply.
> - A small sensor-domain gap remains: a brief fine-tune on dummy objects with the target camera is still required, and 3D voxel inputs transfer far better than 2D images.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates within the field of robot learning, particularly imitation learning, where robots acquire skills by observing human demonstrations. The broader research landscape recognizes the importance of diligently gathered human demonstration datasets for advancing robot learning. Traditional methods for collecting such data include kinesthetic teaching, where a human physically guides a robot, and various teleoperation techniques using controllers such as keyboards, mice, game controllers, 3D mice, specialized master-slave interfaces, or virtual reality (VR) controllers.

Despite ongoing efforts in this area, the authors identify three primary challenges limiting the scalability and accessibility of robot data collection:
1. **Specialized User Training:** Existing methods often require human operators to undergo training to use complex interfaces or master specific kinesthetic teaching techniques.
2. **Resource Dependency:** Data collection is bottlenecked by the availability of physical robots, which are typically expensive and limited in number.
3. **Environmental Confinement:** Robots are often located in laboratories, restricting data collection to a limited set of objects and environments.
While simulation-based approaches offer scalability without real hardware, they face challenges related to the sim2real gap and the limited variety of tasks trainable in simulated environments.

AR2-D2 addresses these limitations by proposing a novel approach that removes the dependency on physical robots and specialized user training during the data collection phase. This positions the work as an attempt to democratize and scale the generation of high-quality robot demonstration data, particularly for manipulating diverse, personalized objects in varied real-world settings, thereby expanding the applicability and accessibility of imitation learning for robotics.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop a system for collecting robot demonstrations that overcomes significant barriers present in traditional methods, thereby enabling more accessible and scalable robot learning. This objective is broken down into three specific aims:
1. **Eliminate the need for specialized human training:** The system should allow individuals without prior robotics experience or training in specialized controllers to generate useful demonstrations.
2. **Remove the requirement for physical robots during data collection:** The process of collecting demonstrations should not depend on access to expensive and limited real robotic hardware.
3. **Enable manipulation of diverse objects:** By removing the constraints of a laboratory setting and physical robots, the system should facilitate the collection of demonstrations involving a wide variety of personalized objects in different environments.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the identified challenges in the current robot learning paradigm. The authors highlight that manually curated datasets are critical for machine learning, especially in robotics where human-generated demonstrations are indispensable for the success of imitation learning. However, the existing methods for collecting these demonstrations are characterized by:
- **High labor intensity and time consumption:** Kinesthetic teaching can be tedious and slow.
- **Skill acquisition requirements:** Teleoperation methods necessitate learning specialized control interfaces.
- **Scalability limitations:** The number of available robots constrains parallel data collection, and robots are costly.
- **Environmental constraints:** Robots are typically confined to laboratories, limiting the diversity of objects they can interact with.
- **Sim2real gap and task variety issues:** While simulation offers a scalable alternative, it introduces challenges in transferring learned policies to the real world and often has a limited range of trainable tasks.

By addressing these issues, AR2-D2 aims to democratize robot training, making it possible for a broader user base to generate personalized manipulation data for real robots, thereby accelerating the development and deployment of robotic systems for household and everyday tasks involving unique objects. The system is motivated by the potential to leverage ubiquitous mobile devices for data collection, expanding the reach of robot learning beyond specialized laboratories.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The AR2-D2 system is designed as an iOS application that allows users to record demonstrations of object manipulation without requiring a physical robot. The methodology encompasses the system's features, design, implementation, and the process for creating training data.

#### 3.1. AR2-D2 System Features

- **No physical robot required:** Users collect high-quality demonstration data using only a mobile device, eliminating the need for access to real robot hardware. This enables data collection from diverse environments.
- **Real-time control of AR robots in the real-world:** The system leverages LiDAR sensors, common in modern iPhones and iPads, to estimate the 3D layout of the environment. An Augmented Reality (AR) representation of a robot arm (specifically a Franka Panda) is projected into this physical space. Users can control this AR robot through various interactions, including pointing at 3D points, using GUI controls, or via AR kinesthetic control (mimicking hand movements). The AR robot's motions are designed to interact with the real environment, providing feedback on collisions.
- **Real-time visualization of task feasibility:** As users specify key-points for the robot's end-effector, AR2-D2 visualizes the AR robot's planned motion in real-time. This immediate feedback allows users to evaluate the feasibility and accuracy of their input and make adjustments if necessary.

#### 3.2. Design and Implementation

The AR2-D2 system comprises two main components: a phone application and a training data creation pipeline.

- **The Phone Application:**
 - Developed as an iOS application using the Unity Engine and AR Foundation kit.
 - Utilizes the mobile device's built-in camera sensors to acquire intrinsic and extrinsic camera values and depth estimations (via LiDAR).
 - The AR Foundation kit projects a URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) model of a Franka Panda robot arm into the physical environment.
 - Apple's human pose detection algorithm is employed to determine the user's 2D hand pose. This 2D pose, combined with the depth map, is used to reconstruct the 3D pose of the human hand.
 - The AR robot's end-effector continuously tracks and mimics the 6D pose of the human hand at 30 frames per second, providing an intuitive control mechanism.

- **Training Data Creation:**
 - After a user records a demonstration video of themselves manipulating an object, the system processes this video to generate data suitable for training real robots.
 - The human hand is segmented out and removed from the video using Segment-Anything.
 - The gap left by the missing hand is filled using a video in-painting technique (E2FGVI).
 - A final video is produced where the AR robot arm appears to manipulate the real-world object by moving to the key-points identified by the user's hand movements. This video serves as training data for visual-based imitation learning.
 - Additionally, the system leverages the captured depth estimations to generate a 3D voxelized representation of the scene over time, which can be used to train agents that rely on 3D inputs, such as Perceiver-Actor (PERACT).

#### 3.3. Agent Training and Evaluation

- **Behavior Cloning:** The authors utilize Perceiver-Actor (PERACT), a transformer-based, language-guided behavior cloning policy. PERACT takes a 3D voxel observation and a language goal as input and outputs discretized values for end-effector translation, rotation, and gripper state, which a motion planner then uses to execute the task.
- **Training Procedure:** Individual agents are trained for each task for 30,000 iterations. To address the domain gap between the mobile device's depth camera (iPhone/iPad) and the Kinect V2 sensor typically used by PERACT, the backbone of the PERACT architecture is frozen, and the rest is fine-tuned for an additional 3,000 iterations (approximately 10 minutes of training). This fine-tuning uses a small set of real-world demonstrations collected with a VR interface on "dummy objects."
- **User Study:** A within-subjects user study (N=10) was conducted to evaluate AR2-D2's usability. Participants collected demonstrations for pick-up and stacking tasks using AR2-D2 and four baseline methods (keyboard and mouse in simulation, 3D mouse in simulation, kinesthetic teaching with a real robot, and HTC Vive VR controller with a real robot). Metrics included data collection time, task success rate, and System Usability Scale (SUS) survey scores.
- **Real Robot Deployment Evaluation:** Policies trained with AR2-D2 demonstrations were deployed on a real Franka Panda robot to assess their ability to manipulate personalized objects. Demonstrations for press, push, and pick-up tasks were collected for nine personalized objects. The performance of AR2-D2 trained agents was compared against agents trained with real-world and simulation data, all fine-tuned using the same dummy object demonstrations.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presented two main categories of findings: the usability and efficiency of AR2-D2 in a user study, and the effectiveness of AR2-D2 generated data for training real robots.

#### 4.1. User Study Results

A within-subjects user study involving 10 participants compared AR2-D2 against four alternative demonstration collection techniques: keyboard and mouse (simulated robot), 3D mouse (simulated robot), kinesthetic teaching (real robot), and HTC Vive VR controller (real robot). Participants performed two tasks: picking up a cube and stacking three cubes.

- **Data Collection Time:** AR2-D2 demonstrated significantly faster data collection times compared to all other methods. For the pick-up task, AR2-D2 averaged 22.1 seconds, and for the stacking task, 29.5 seconds. Kinesthetic teaching, the next fastest method, averaged 41.6 seconds for pick-up and 61.4 seconds for stacking. Statistical analysis (t-tests with p-values of 7.587 × 10⁻⁶ and 7.514 × 10⁻⁶ respectively) confirmed a significant difference, indicating AR2-D2 is faster. The study noted that participants were consistently faster with AR2-D2 from the initial trials, exhibiting lower standard deviations (5.75s and 8.89s) compared to kinesthetic teaching (9.62s and 14.02s).
- **Task Success Rate:** AR2-D2 achieved a comparable task success rate to kinesthetic teaching, both of which had significantly higher success rates than the other baseline methods.
- **User Preference (SUS Score):** Users indicated a higher preference for AR2-D2 in the System Usability Scale (SUS) survey, with scores similar to kinesthetic teaching (within ±6% difference). The results suggest that users found AR2-D2 intuitive and required no prior training.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Deployment Results

AR2-D2 demonstrations were used to train behavior cloning agents (specifically PERACT) for deployment on a real Franka Panda robot across three manipulation tasks (press, push, pick up) involving nine personalized objects. These agents were fine-tuned for a short period (3,000 iterations, approximately 10 minutes) on dummy real-world objects to mitigate the sim-to-real domain gap.

- **Effectiveness with Personalized Objects:** Policies trained with AR2-D2 data successfully enabled a real robot to manipulate personalized objects that were only encountered by the AR robot during demonstration collection, without direct exposure to a physical robot or the specific real objects during the initial training phase.
- **Performance Comparison:**
 - AR2-D2's data generally outperformed policies trained using simulation data and real-world data collected without personalized objects. For instance, in some cases, AR2-D2 data achieved a 53.4% higher success rate compared to PERACT’s real-world data collection without personalized objects. This finding highlighted the benefit of collecting demonstrations with the specific target objects, even if through AR.
 - AR2-D2 demonstrations trained policies with accuracy comparable to, and in some instances exceeding, demonstrations collected directly from real robots, even when the real robot data collection included personalized objects. For tasks like pushing a LEGO train or picking up a paper bag, AR2-D2's data yielded better success rates. Across the remaining personalized objects, the performance gap was observed to be ≤ 14.3%. A t-test (t-value of 0.547, p-value of 0.592) indicated no statistically significant difference between AR2-D2 and real-robot data collection with personalized objects.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Fine-tuning:** An analysis on fine-tuning parameters revealed that using 5 fine-tuning demonstrations and 3,000 training iterations provided the most effective outcome for maximizing agent performance.
 - **Data Modalities:** When comparing different data modalities without fine-tuning, training with 3D voxelized inputs (for PERACT) consistently outperformed training with 2D image inputs (for Image-BC) across various tasks (e.g., 40% vs 0% for "Press the buzzer from the side", 33.34% vs 6.67% for "Pick up the queen piece"), indicating the advantage of 3D data for these agents.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The AR2-D2 system introduces a novel approach to robot demonstration collection with several implications for the field of robot learning:

- **Democratization of Robot Training:** A primary impact of AR2-D2 is its potential to democratize robot training. By eliminating the need for specialized user training or access to expensive physical robots, the system lowers the barrier to entry for individuals interested in generating robot demonstrations. This expands the pool of potential data contributors beyond robotics experts and specialized labs. The authors suggest that the widespread availability of compatible mobile devices (e.g., an estimated 1.36 billion iPhone users) could enable a large-scale collection of personalized manipulation data.
- **Increased Data Diversity and Scale:** Traditional methods often confine data collection to laboratory settings, limiting the variety of objects and environments encountered. AR2-D2 enables demonstration collection anywhere, allowing users to record interactions with diverse, personalized objects in their natural settings (e.g., household items). This capability facilitates the creation of richer, more varied datasets that can enhance the generalization capabilities of robot learning agents.
- **Accessibility and Flexibility:** The system's mobile app format offers unprecedented flexibility in data collection. Users can generate demonstrations at any time and in any location, circumventing logistical challenges associated with scheduling robot access or setting up specialized teleoperation stations. This flexibility can accelerate the data acquisition process for research and development.
- **Comparable Performance to Real-World Data:** The finding that AR2-D2's AR demonstrations can train real robots as effectively as, and sometimes surpass, data collected from real-world robot teleoperation is significant. It validates the utility of augmented reality as a robust proxy for real robot interactions in the context of data generation for imitation learning. This suggests that high-quality training data can be sourced without direct robot involvement, offering a cost-effective and scalable alternative.
- **Advancement in Behavior Cloning:** The work contributes to the practical application of behavior cloning by demonstrating an effective method for generating the necessary training data. The ability to provide both 2D visual and 3D voxelized data modalities further supports various behavior cloning architectures.
- **Future Research Avenues:** While the system currently requires a minor fine-tuning step to bridge the domain gap, the work points towards future research into better data augmentation techniques or integration with emerging hardware (e.g., AR head-mounted displays) to further eliminate this dependency and enhance the realism and utility of AR-generated data.

Overall, AR2-D2 represents a step towards making robot learning more accessible, scalable, and adaptable to real-world complexities, potentially accelerating the development of robots capable of performing a wider array of tasks in diverse, unconstrained environments.
