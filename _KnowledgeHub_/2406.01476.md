# DreamPhysics: Learning Physics-Based 3D Dynamics with Video Diffusion Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamPhysics introduces a framework for generating realistic 3D dynamics by integrating physics-based simulation with implicit physics knowledge from video diffusion priors. It achieves an average aesthetic quality score of 5.03, outperforming existing methods by leveraging a novel Motion Distillation Sampling technique and a KAN-based material field for learning physical properties.

## Problem

- Animating static 3D assets with realistic dynamics in simulated environments remains a significant challenge.
- Traditional physics-based simulations require manual and often inaccurate assignment of physical properties, leading to unnatural motions.
- Directly distilling deformation from video generative models frequently produces small, discontinuous, or non-physical movements in 3D objects.

## Method

- The framework learns a 'material field' to define physical properties for ==3D Gaussian Splatting== objects, guided by ==video diffusion== models, rather than directly predicting deformation.
- A differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) simulator is used to animate objects, ensuring physically consistent motions based on the learned material properties.
- Motion Distillation Sampling (MDS) is employed to specifically extract and emphasize motion priors from video diffusion models, mitigating color biases in the optimization process, and a KAN-based material field with frame boosting stabilizes parameter optimization across wide value ranges.

## Results

- DreamPhysics achieved the highest average aesthetic quality score of **5.03** across evaluation scenes, outperforming PhysDreamer (**4.84**) and DreamGaussian4D (**4.61**), while approaching the ground truth score of **5.13**.
- The system successfully generated realistic motions for diverse scenarios, including natural swaying of a ficus and accurate bouncing dynamics for a ball upon impact.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the KAN-based material field, Motion Distillation Sampling, and Frame Boosting each contribute to improved generation quality, robustness, and faster convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit physics knowledge within video diffusion models can be effectively utilized to drive realistic 3D dynamics when channeled through a physics-based simulation framework.
> - Learning physical properties for a simulator is more effective than directly distilling time-dependent deformations from video generative models for producing physically consistent 3D motions.
> - Optimizing physical parameters with wide value ranges benefits from KAN-based representations and broad temporal supervision (frame boosting), leading to more stable and robust results.
