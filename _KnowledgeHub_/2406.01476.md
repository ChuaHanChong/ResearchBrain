# DreamPhysics: Learning Physics-Based 3D Dynamics with Video Diffusion Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamPhysics introduces a framework for generating realistic 3D dynamics by integrating physics-based simulation with implicit physics knowledge from video diffusion priors. It achieves an average aesthetic quality score of 5.03, outperforming existing methods by leveraging a novel Motion Distillation Sampling technique and a KAN-based material field for learning physical properties.

## Problem

- Animating static 3D assets with realistic dynamics in simulated environments remains a significant challenge.
- Traditional physics-based simulations require manual and often inaccurate assignment of physical properties, leading to unnatural motions.
- Directly distilling deformation from video generative models frequently produces small, discontinuous, or non-physical movements in 3D objects.

## Method

- The framework learns a 'material field' to define physical properties for ==3D Gaussian Splatting== objects, guided by ==video diffusion== models, rather than directly predicting deformation.
- A differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) simulator is used to animate objects, ensuring physically consistent motions based on the learned material properties.
- Motion Distillation Sampling (MDS) is employed to specifically extract and emphasize motion priors from video diffusion models, mitigating color biases in the optimization process, and a KAN-based material field with frame boosting stabilizes parameter optimization across wide value ranges.

## Results

- DreamPhysics achieved the highest average aesthetic quality score of **5.03** across evaluation scenes, outperforming PhysDreamer (**4.84**) and DreamGaussian4D (**4.61**), while approaching the ground truth score of **5.13**.
- The system successfully generated realistic motions for diverse scenarios, including natural swaying of a ficus and accurate bouncing dynamics for a ball upon impact.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the KAN-based material field, Motion Distillation Sampling, and Frame Boosting each contribute to improved generation quality, robustness, and faster convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit physics knowledge within video diffusion models can be effectively utilized to drive realistic 3D dynamics when channeled through a physics-based simulation framework.
> - Learning physical properties for a simulator is more effective than directly distilling time-dependent deformations from video generative models for producing physically consistent 3D motions.
> - Optimizing physical parameters with wide value ranges benefits from KAN-based representations and broad temporal supervision (frame boosting), leading to more stable and robust results.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in 3D representations, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS), have facilitated significant progress in the reconstruction and generation of static 3D assets. However, the animation and interaction of these static 3D assets within simulated environments remain a considerable challenge. This capability is critical for numerous applications, including video games, virtual reality, and robotics.

Current approaches to animating static 3D objects generally fall into two categories:
1. **Physics-based simulation**: Methods that inject physical parameters into 3D objects and then predict motion using a physics-based simulator. This approach aims to produce realistic movements governed by physical laws. A primary limitation is the requirement for manual assignment of precise physical properties (e.g., Young's modulus, yield stress). Inaccurate manual settings often lead to unnatural simulation results. Examples include PhysGaussian (Xie et al. 2023) and work by Feng et al. (2024b).
2. **Learning deformation from video generative models**: Approaches that distill knowledge from pre-trained video generative models (e.g., video diffusion models) to learn time-dependent deformations of 3D objects. These models are trained on real-world video data, which inherently contains physical phenomena. While leveraging the rich priors of video models, these methods tend to produce 3D videos characterized by small and discontinuous motions. The authors hypothesize that this limitation stems from an inappropriate extraction and application of physics priors, rather than the video models themselves (Singer et al. 2023; Bahmani et al. 2023; Zhao et al. 2023).

This work positions itself as an effort to integrate the advantages of both categories while mitigating their respective shortcomings. It aims to extract physics knowledge from video generative models and apply it within a physics-based simulation framework to achieve realistic dynamic 3D synthesis. The paper also distinguishes itself from concurrent work like PhysDreamer (Zhang et al. 2024), which supervises physical property prediction using pre-generated ground-truth videos. The authors argue that existing video generative models may struggle to produce high-quality, desired ground-truth videos suitable for such supervision due to limitations in motion control. DreamPhysics proposes a method that operates directly with image- or text-conditioned optimization, without the need for pre-generated video ground truth.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for creating realistic dynamic 3D content by integrating the physical knowledge embedded in video generative models with physics-based simulation. The core motivation arises from the limitations observed in existing methods for animating 3D objects.

The challenges motivating this work include:
- **Inaccuracy of manual physical property assignment**: Traditional physics-based simulations rely on engineers or artists to manually assign precise physical properties to 3D objects. This process is complex, time-consuming, and prone to error. Even slight inaccuracies can lead to simulations that appear unnatural or unrealistic, hindering the creation of convincing dynamic 3D content for applications like video games and virtual reality.
- **Limitations of direct video diffusion distillation for deformation**: While video generative models possess implicit knowledge of real-world physics, direct distillation of their priors to predict 3D object deformation often results in motions that are either too subtle or lack temporal coherence. The generated movements may appear small, discontinuous, or fail to adhere to expected physical behaviors, indicating that the latent physics priors are not being effectively extracted or applied to drive robust 3D dynamics.
- **Need for automated and robust physical property learning**: To overcome the manual tuning bottleneck and leverage the vast data captured in video generative models, there is a clear need for a method that can automatically learn appropriate physical properties for 3D objects. This learning process should be guided by the implicit physics knowledge within video diffusion models.
- **Requirement for realistic and stable 4D content generation**: The ultimate goal is to generate 4D (3D + time) content that exhibits natural, realistic, and stable physical interactions, regardless of the input condition (text or image). This requires a robust optimization process for physical parameters that can handle their wide value ranges and the sequential nature of physical simulation.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the DreamPhysics framework are:
- To learn a "material field" that defines the physical properties of a 3D object from video diffusion models, rather than directly predicting its deformation.
- To utilize a physics-based simulator, specifically the Material Point Method (MPM), to animate the 3D object using these learned material properties, thereby ensuring physically consistent motions.
- To introduce a novel **Motion Distillation Sampling (MDS)** technique to specifically emphasize motion information from video diffusion priors during optimization, while mitigating potential color biases.
- To propose a **KAN-based material field** combined with **frame boosting** to facilitate stable and efficient optimization of physical parameters across their potentially large value ranges and to enhance supervision coverage over the simulated video sequence.
- To enable the generation of high-quality 4D content conditioned on either text descriptions or single images, eliminating the need for pre-generated ground truth videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamPhysics is a physics-based 3D animation framework designed to learn the physical properties of a 3D object represented by Gaussian Splatting (3D GS) using video diffusion priors. The overall process involves an iterative loop of physical parameter estimation, simulation, rendering, and optimization.

#### 3.1. Method Overview

1. **Input**: A static 3D GS scene `{G_i}` (either reconstructed or generated).
2. **Material Field Initialization**: Physical parameters `{θ_G_i}` for each Gaussian kernel `G_i` are initialized using a KAN-based material field `ϕ(x_i)`, where `x_i` is the center coordinate of `G_i`.
3. **Physics-Based Simulation**: The initialized physical parameters are fed into a Material Point Method (MPM) simulator. The simulator predicts a time-dependent state `{x_i(t), Σ_i(t), Ω_i(t)}` for each Gaussian kernel, representing its deformation and orientation over time.
4. **Dynamic Rendering**: The time-dependent states are rendered into an L-length video `V^(k)` at the current iteration `k`.
5. **Optimization with Motion Distillation Sampling (MDS)**: The rendered video `V^(k)` is used to distill motion priors from a pre-trained video diffusion model. MDS generates a distillation gradient that primarily focuses on motion information, minimizing color biases.
6. **Gradient Back-propagation**: The distillation gradient is back-propagated through the differentiable MPM simulator to update the parameters of the material field `ϕ`. This refines the physical properties for the next iteration `k+1`.
7. **Iteration and Convergence**: Steps 2-6 are repeated for approximately 30 iterations. Training stops when the optimized physical parameter values stabilize within a defined order of magnitude.

#### 3.2. Parameter Optimization with Motion Distillation Sampling (MDS)

The core idea is to leverage the implicit physical knowledge within video generative models without relying on their often imperfect ground-truth video generation capabilities.
- **Motivation for MDS**: Traditional Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), when applied to videos (SDS-T), might excessively focus on color details. Direct supervision with pre-generated videos (as in PhysDreamer) is also problematic due to the limited control and quality of current video generators. The goal is to isolate and emphasize motion features.
- **MDS Formulation**: MDS addresses color bias by computing the difference between the noise predicted by a video diffusion model for the entire rendered video sequence and the noise predicted for only its first frame. The first frame is considered to largely represent the color information of the video.
 The motion distillation sample `s_MDS` is formulated as:
 `s_MDS = ω(μ) * (ˆϵ_V(V_r(t); μ, y) - ˆϵ_V(V_r(0); μ, y))`
 Here, `ˆϵ_V(V_r(t); μ, y)` is the noise predicted by the video diffusion model for the rendered video sequence `V_r(t)` at noise timestep `μ` with condition `y`. `ˆϵ_V(V_r(0); μ, y)` is the noise predicted for the first frame `V_r(0)` of the sequence. `ω(μ)` is a weighting function for the noise timestep.
- **Gradient Calculation**: The gradient `s_MDS` is then used to update the target physical parameters `θ_G`. This involves back-propagating the gradient through the differentiable rendering process and the differentiable MPM simulator:
 `∇_θ_G L_MDS(θ_G, r(t)) ≜ E [ s_MDS * (∂V_r(t) / ∂(x, Σ, Ω)) * (∂(x, Σ, Ω) / ∂θ_G) ]`

#### 3.3. Parameter Estimation with Material Field

To address the challenges of optimizing physical parameters, which can have a wide range of values, DreamPhysics incorporates a KAN-based material field and a frame boosting strategy.
- **KAN-Based Triplane**:
 - **Challenge**: The large value range of physical properties (e.g., Young's modulus from 1e4 to 1e8) makes gradient updates difficult, potentially trapping parameters within specific magnitude ranges.
 - **Approach**: Spatial information is encoded using a tri-plane representation. Instead of traditional Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) are employed to extract features from these tri-plane projections. KANs are proposed to offer enhanced modeling capabilities for physics-related tasks. These extracted features are then combined to form the physical parameters `θ_G`.
 - **Gradient Propagation**: The gradients `L_MDS` are propagated to the material field parameters `ϕ`:
 `∇_ϕ L_ϕ(x, r(t)) ≜ E [ L_MDS(ϕ(x), r(t)) * (∂θ_G / ∂ϕ) ]`
- **Frame Boosting**:
 - **Challenge**: MPM, as a sequential model, is susceptible to gradient vanishing or exploding during back-propagation through time (BPTT). Truncated BPTT, while mitigating these issues, limits the supervision to only a subset of frames.
 - **Approach**: To maximize the coverage of supervised frames, the total `M × T` simulated frames are divided into `M` distinct groups. Each group consists of `T` frames sampled at equal intervals (e.g., for group `i`, frames `I_i, I_i+M..., I_i+M(T-1)`). These `M` groups are then alternately fed into the MDS supervision process.
 - **Boosted Loss**: This strategy results in a boosted motion distillation loss:
 `L_ˆϕ(x) = (1/M) * Σ_(i=1)^M L_ϕ(x, r(t_i))`
 This ensures that gradients are computed and applied across a more diverse set of temporal slices from the simulation, improving the robustness of the optimization process.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation**: Based on the `warp` implementation of MPM, with specific time steps and frame durations.
- **Video Diffusion Models**: ModelScope (Wang et al. 2023b) for text-to-video, and Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) (Blattmann et al. 2023) for image-to-video, typically generating 16 to 25 frames.
- **Dataset**: Seven static 3D scenes/objects, including plants, a beanie hat, a telephone cord, a sofa with pillows, and a ball, covering rotation and collision motions.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Aesthetic quality score from VBench (Huang et al. 2024b) to quantify naturalness and realism, and user studies for human preference.
- **Comparison**: PhysGaussian, PhysDreamer, and DreamGaussian4D.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DreamPhysics demonstrates its capabilities in generating realistic 3D dynamics and the effectiveness of its proposed components.

#### 4.1. 3D Dynamics Generation

- **Text-Conditioned Optimization**: When given a text prompt like "a ficus swaying in the wind," DreamPhysics successfully optimized the physical properties, such as Young's modulus, of a 3D ficus model. Before optimization, scenes with poorly set physical parameters (e.g., too low Young's modulus) resulted in excessive tilting and unnatural recovery. After optimization, the ficus exhibited natural swaying motions, with space-time slices illustrating realistic motion trajectories.
- **Image-Conditioned Optimization**: For an image of a ball undergoing a dropping process (collision motion), DreamPhysics adjusted its physical properties to prevent excessive deformation upon impact with the ground. The optimized simulation showed the ball dropping and bouncing realistically, maintaining its form, unlike simulations with inaccurately initialized properties.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Works

- **Quantitative Evaluation (Table 1)**: DreamPhysics was quantitatively compared against DreamGaussian4D, PhysGaussian, and PhysDreamer using the aesthetic quality score from VBench. On four common evaluation scenes, DreamPhysics achieved an average aesthetic quality score of 5.03, which was the highest among the compared methods and closest to the ground truth score of 5.13.
 - DreamGaussian4D, lacking physical constraints, exhibited the lowest score (4.61).
 - PhysDreamer (4.84), which relies on pre-generated videos as ground truth, scored lower than PhysGaussian (4.98), suggesting that the quality of such ground truth videos may be a limiting factor.
- **Qualitative Evaluation (Figure 4)**: Visualization of space-time slices corroborated the quantitative findings.
 - PhysGaussian, with its manually set physical properties, often produced motions described as "too extreme."
 - DreamGaussian4D generated consistent but less natural motions due to the absence of physical constraints.
 - PhysDreamer exhibited some energy dissipation, but DreamPhysics's results were noted to be more similar to the ground truth visualizations in terms of the amplitude and frequency of simulated motions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted across seven scenes to evaluate the contribution of each proposed component: KAN-based material field (+KAN), Motion Distillation Sampling (+L_MDS), and Frame Boosting (+Boost).
- **Baseline (Vanilla SDS-T)**: A baseline method using vanilla SDS-T loss without KAN or frame boosting yielded the lowest aesthetic quality score (4.86) and required the highest average training iterations (36.86). This indicated poor convergence and generation quality without the proposed enhancements.
- **Effect of KAN-based Material Field**: Equipping the model with a KAN-based material field (+KAN) improved the aesthetic score to 4.89 and reduced average iterations to 34.29. This suggested that KANs facilitate optimization and improve generation quality. Further ablation specifically on KAN (Figure 6, supplementary) showed that KAN layers enhanced robustness in collision scenarios, helping objects like a ball maintain and restore their shape, unlike MLP-based fields that made the ball appear "too soft."
- **Effect of Motion Distillation Sampling**: Incorporating Motion Distillation Sampling (+L_MDS) further enhanced the aesthetic score to 4.94, confirming its effectiveness in distilling motion priors from video diffusion models.
- **Effect of Frame Boosting**: Adding Frame Boosting (full DreamPhysics method) maintained a similar aesthetic score (4.93) but significantly reduced the average training iterations to 29.71. This demonstrates that frame boosting primarily contributes to faster parameter convergence without necessarily improving final quality beyond MDS.
- **Qualitative Ablation (Figure 5)**: Visualizations of space-time slices for the Alocasia scene showed that the baseline and KAN-only versions were less consistent with the ground truth compared to versions incorporating MDS and Frame Boosting, which produced 4D content closer to real-captured videos.

#### 4.4. User Study (Supplementary Material)

User studies involving 28 volunteers, including 5 professionals, affirmed these findings. Participants consistently preferred the results generated by DreamPhysics over comparison methods and ablation variants, indicating higher perceived realism and coherence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamPhysics addresses critical limitations in the field of 3D animation and dynamic content creation, offering a method to generate physically realistic 4D content from static 3D assets.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:
- **Enhanced Realism in 3D Animation**: By effectively extracting and applying physics knowledge from large-scale video diffusion models, DreamPhysics enables the automatic assignment of precise physical properties to 3D objects. This eliminates the need for manual tuning, which is often inaccurate and time-consuming, resulting in more natural and realistic motions in simulations.
- **Bridging the Gap between Generative Models and Physics Simulators**: The framework successfully combines the generative power of video diffusion models with the physical consistency of MPM simulators. This integration leverages the strengths of both paradigms, creating a more robust and versatile solution for dynamic 3D synthesis.
- **Novel Methodological Contributions**:
 - **Motion Distillation Sampling (MDS)** provides a targeted approach to distill specific motion priors from video diffusion models while mitigating irrelevant color biases. This is a crucial innovation for motion-focused generative tasks.
 - The **KAN-based material field** enhances the stability and convergence of physical parameter optimization, particularly for parameters with wide value ranges, leading to more robust results.
 - **Frame Boosting** improves the efficiency of training by ensuring broader temporal supervision coverage, which is beneficial for sequential physics simulations prone to gradient issues.
- **Versatile Content Creation**: DreamPhysics supports both text-conditioned and image-conditioned optimization, offering flexibility in how users can initiate dynamic content generation without relying on pre-generated ground-truth videos. This broadens its applicability for various creative and engineering workflows.
- **Impact on Various Applications**:
 - **Video Games and Virtual Reality**: Facilitates the creation of more immersive and believable virtual environments and characters that exhibit realistic physical interactions.
 - **Robotics Simulation**: Provides a tool for generating high-fidelity physical simulations, which is essential for training and testing robotic agents in diverse and dynamic scenarios.
 - **3D Content Production**: Streamlines the animation pipeline for artists and designers, allowing them to rapidly prototype and animate 3D models with physically plausible movements.

The acknowledged limitations, such as combining diverse motion types into a single simulator and handling complex scene-level interactions (e.g., dynamic shadows), point to future research directions. Despite these, DreamPhysics represents a substantial step towards more autonomous, realistic, and efficient 4D content generation, positioning it as a foundational contribution to the field of physics-integrated generative AI.
