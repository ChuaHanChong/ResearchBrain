# Opt2Skill: Imitating Dynamically-feasible Whole-Body Trajectories for Versatile Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Opt2Skill framework, developed by Georgia Tech researchers, integrates model-based trajectory optimization with reinforcement learning to enable robust whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoid robots. It generates dynamically feasible reference motions, including joint torques and contact forces, to train policies that achieve superior tracking accuracy and robustness on complex tasks, with successful sim-to-real transfer on the Digit humanoid.

## Problem

- Controlling high-dimensional, inherently unstable humanoid robots for complex loco-manipulation tasks remains challenging due to limitations in both model-based (computational complexity, sensitivity) and model-free (inefficient learning, unnatural motions, sim-to-real gap) approaches.
- Existing motion reference data, such as human motion capture or inverse kinematics, often lack dynamic feasibility and contact consistency, hindering the learning of high-fidelity, contact-rich behaviors for humanoid robots.
- Prior work combining trajectory optimization with RL for humanoids largely focused on locomotion alone or used reduced-order dynamics models, with no demonstrated hardware success for whole-body loco-manipulation.

## Method

- A whole-body trajectory optimization (TO) pipeline, leveraging Differential Dynamic Programming with full-order robot dynamics, generates diverse, dynamically feasible reference trajectories that explicitly include joint torques and contact forces.
- Reinforcement learning policies are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in an asymmetric actor-critic setup, where the actor uses noisy proprioception and partial references while the critic utilizes privileged simulation states and full reference information.
- Extensive domain randomization (robot dynamics, observation noise, control delays) and curriculum learning are integrated during training to ensure robust policy learning and facilitate reliable sim-to-real transfer to hardware.

## Results

- Opt2Skill policies achieved superior tracking performance in simulation, demonstrating lower average end-effector tracking errors (hand: 2.00 cm, foot: 5.23 cm) and reduced global drift compared to policies trained with human-retargeted or IK-based references.
- Ablation studies confirmed that explicitly including reference joint torques and contact forces in the reward function was crucial for stable and accurate force application in contact-rich tasks (e.g., table wiping), outperforming policies relying only on position or contact force references.
- Successfully transferred learned policies to the real Digit humanoid robot, demonstrating versatile loco-manipulation capabilities including flat-ground walking, stair/ramp traversal, desk object reaching (3.7-3.8 cm end-effector error), box pickup (3.6 cm error), heavy box handling, and door opening tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically feasible reference trajectories, particularly those explicitly containing joint torque and contact force information generated by full-order dynamics-based trajectory optimization, are critical for learning high-fidelity and robust contact-rich humanoid loco-manipulation skills.
> - An asymmetric actor-critic architecture, combined with comprehensive domain randomization and curriculum learning, is effective in bridging the sim-to-real gap, enabling robust policy transfer to real humanoid hardware without extensive fine-tuning.
> - Providing RL policies with high-quality, physically grounded reference data significantly improves learning efficiency, enhances tracking accuracy, and increases robustness to environmental disturbances and variations in complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are envisioned to operate in human-centered environments, performing a diverse range of complex tasks, often involving both locomotion and manipulation (loco-manipulation). However, controlling these high-dimensional, inherently unstable, and contact-rich systems remains a formidable challenge. The research landscape has primarily explored two distinct paradigms: model-based optimal control and model-free reinforcement learning (RL).

Model-based optimal control methods offer the advantage of precision, flexibility, and the ability to define exact motion objectives and constraints. They are foundational for generating specific, dynamically consistent motions. However, their primary limitations include high computational complexity, sensitivity to model inaccuracies, and the requirement for precise contact sensing, often restricting them to behaviors with simple, predefined contact sequences. Model Predictive Control (MPC), a variant of optimal control, typically relies on simplified robot models for real-time computation, leading to conservative motions, while full-body MPC still faces high computational demands.

In contrast, model-free reinforcement learning has emerged as a powerful tool for controlling high-dimensional robots, offering robustness to environmental uncertainties and the ability to learn complex behaviors from scratch. Yet, RL suffers from inefficient learning, the generation of unnatural or sub-optimal motions, the significant effort required for reward shaping, and the pervasive sim-to-real gap, which complicates real-world deployment.

To bridge the gap between these two approaches, a growing trend involves providing RL with motion references. Early attempts included manually designed references, which are often task-specific and lack generalization. Human motion capture (mocap) data has shown promise for humanoid imitation, enabling teleoperation and autonomous skills. However, the significant embodiment gap between humans and humanoids necessitates complex motion retargeting, often resulting in kinematically or dynamically infeasible motions that require extensive post-processing, thereby limiting the robot's ability to faithfully imitate and generalize.

More recently, researchers have started leveraging model-based trajectory optimization (TO) to generate high-quality reference data for RL-based motion imitation. This approach has seen success in quadrupedal locomotion (e.g.,) and, to a lesser extent, in humanoid locomotion using various simplified models (e.g., single rigid-body, centroidal dynamics, kino-dynamic models). However, applying full-order dynamics-based TO to guide RL for *whole-body loco-manipulation* in humanoids, particularly demonstrating *hardware success*, remained an open problem. Previous works often focused solely on locomotion or used reduced-order models that do not capture full dynamic details like joint torques.

Opt2Skill directly addresses this gap by presenting a novel end-to-end pipeline. It distinguishes itself by being the first to adopt *full-order dynamics-based TO* to generate reference trajectories that explicitly include *joint torque information* and *contact forces*. This rich, dynamically consistent data is then used to guide RL policies for *versatile whole-body loco-manipulation* on a high-DoF humanoid robot, Digit, with demonstrated success in real-world hardware. This work significantly advances the state-of-the-art by combining the precision and dynamic feasibility of optimal control with the robustness and adaptability of reinforcement learning for complex humanoid behaviors, moving beyond the limitations of kinematic or reduced-order model approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Opt2Skill framework are multifaceted, aiming to resolve core challenges in humanoid robot control:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Current Methods:** The paper is motivated by the inherent challenges of controlling humanoid robots—specifically, their high-dimensional and unstable dynamics, coupled with the complex, contact-rich nature of real-world loco-manipulation tasks. It seeks to surmount the limitations of both purely model-based methods (computational complexity, sensitivity to uncertainties) and purely RL methods (inefficient learning, unnatural motions, sim-to-real gap).

2. **Integrate Strengths for Robust Control:** A key objective is to combine the strengths of model-based trajectory optimization (TO) with reinforcement learning (RL). TO offers precise, dynamically feasible motion generation, while RL provides robustness and adaptability to real-world disturbances and uncertainties. The goal is to develop a hybrid approach that leverages the best of both worlds.

3. **Generate High-Quality, Dynamically Feasible Reference Motions:** The authors aim to produce superior reference motions that are not only kinematically but also *dynamically feasible* and *contact-consistent*. This involves using full-order robot dynamics, explicit contact constraints, and torque limits during trajectory optimization. A crucial motivation here is to generate trajectories that inherently contain critical dynamic information, such as *joint torques* and *interaction forces*, which are often absent from human motion capture or inverse kinematics (IK)-based references.

4. **Enhance RL Policy Learning:** By providing RL policies with high-quality, dynamically consistent reference motions, the objective is to improve data efficiency, simplify reward tuning, and prevent the generation of unnatural or physically implausible movements typically associated with learning from scratch. The torque and contact force information in the references is specifically intended to help the policy learn physically grounded control strategies for contact-rich tasks.

5. **Achieve Versatile Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation:** The framework is designed to enable a diverse set of complex loco-manipulation tasks on a humanoid robot. This includes challenging behaviors such as handling bulky objects, traversing uneven terrain (e.g., stairs, ramps), opening doors, and manipulating desk objects, which require intricate coordination between locomotion and manipulation.

6. **Facilitate Reliable Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A critical motivation is to develop policies that can be directly transferred from simulation to real-world hardware without requiring extensive online adaptation or fine-tuning. This is addressed through a combination of robust policy training, comprehensive domain randomization, and curriculum learning in simulation.

7. **Demonstrate Hardware Efficacy on Humanoid Platforms:** Ultimately, the researchers aim to provide empirical evidence of their framework's effectiveness through successful deployment on a real humanoid robot, specifically the Digit robot, thereby validating the practical utility and robustness of Opt2Skill in complex, real-world scenarios. This work also aims to be the *first* to showcase hardware success for humanoid loco-manipulation using full-order dynamics-based TO-guided RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Opt2Skill introduces an end-to-end pipeline that meticulously integrates model-based trajectory optimization with reinforcement learning to enable robust whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoid robots. The methodology can be broken down into two main stages: trajectory generation and policy training.

#### 3.1. Whole-Body Trajectory Optimization (TO)

The first stage focuses on generating high-quality, dynamically feasible, and task-specific reference motions offline.
- **Robot Model:** The framework utilizes a standard floating-base model of a humanoid robot, like the Digit, which includes an unactuated 6-DoF base and a set of actuated limbs.
- **Optimization Solver:** Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP), implemented within the Crocoddyl framework, is employed to solve the optimal control problem. DDP is chosen for its efficiency in handling complex, nonlinear dynamics.
- **Problem Formulation:** The TO problem is formulated to minimize a cost function that tracks task-space variables (e.g., end-effector positions, base velocities) while adhering to a comprehensive set of physical constraints. These constraints are crucial for dynamic feasibility:
 - **Full-Order Dynamics:** The equations of motion for the entire robot (mass matrix, Coriolis/centrifugal forces, joint torques, contact forces) are explicitly incorporated.
 - **Hybrid Dynamics:** The formulation accounts for both continuous dynamics during free motion and impact dynamics during contact events.
 - **Contact Constraints:** Rigid contact constraints (zero acceleration at contact points) are enforced for stance feet or other contact points, along with zero force for non-contacting parts.
 - **Joint and Torque Limits:** Physical limitations on joint positions and torque outputs are respected.
 - **Friction Cone Constraints:** Contact forces are constrained to remain within the friction cones at contact points, ensuring physical plausibility.
- **Task Specification:** The desired trajectories (ˆy) are defined based on the task:
 - For tasks requiring precise execution (e.g., walking, stair climbing), specific end-effector trajectories are enforced at each timestep.
 - For more flexible behaviors (e.g., box pickup, desk manipulation), sparse subgoals are defined, allowing the optimizer to generate smooth, natural transitions.
 - **Diverse Data Generation:** To ensure policy generalization, a diverse dataset of trajectories is generated by systematically varying motion parameters (e.g., gait phase, contact mode, walking speed, foot clearance, CoM position, target object location).
- **Output Data:** The optimized trajectories provide a rich set of information: joint angles and velocities, base position and orientation, linear and angular velocities, *joint torques*, and *contact forces*. This dynamically relevant information is critical for the subsequent RL stage.

#### 3.2. RL-Based Imitation of Dynamically Feasible Trajectories

The second stage involves training reinforcement learning policies to accurately track the TO-generated reference motions.
- **Problem Formulation:** The control problem is framed as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), where the agent operates with noisy and incomplete observations. The goal is to learn an optimal policy π that maximizes discounted rewards, primarily for accurate trajectory tracking.
- **RL Algorithm and Architecture:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to train an Actor-Critic architecture in the MuJoCo Playground simulator. Both actor and critic networks are multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs).
- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:** To facilitate sim-to-real transfer, an asymmetric actor-critic setup is employed:
 - **Critic:** Has access to "privileged state" information in simulation, including true proprioceptive states (base position, orientation, velocities, joint states, end-effector positions, contact forces, joint torques) and *all reference trajectory information*. This helps the critic accurately estimate value functions.
 - **Actor:** Operates on "noisy proprioception" (noisy base velocities, projected gravity, joint states) and *partial reference information* (reference velocities, joint positions, contact forces, torques). This mimics real-world sensor limitations and ensures robustness.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs joint-level target position *offsets* from a default standing pose for the actuated joints. These targets are then fed into a low-level Proportional-Derivative (PD) joint torque controller (u_t = K_p(a_t + q_j_dflt - q_j_t) - K_d * q_j_t), which computes and applies torques to the robot.
- **Reward Function:** A carefully designed reward function encourages:
 - **Task Rewards:** Accurate tracking of various components of the reference trajectory, including joint positions, base position/orientation/velocities, end-effector positions, and crucially, *joint torques* and *contact forces*.
 - **Regularization Rewards:** Penalties for high action rates, excessive torques, and joint accelerations, promoting smooth, physically plausible, and energy-efficient motions.
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** To enhance sim-to-real transfer, extensive DR is applied during training. This includes randomizing robot dynamics (motor strength, Kp/Kd factors, body mass, gravity, friction), observation noise (joint positions/velocities, base velocities, projected gravity), and control delays.
- **Curriculum Learning:** A noise curriculum is used, gradually increasing observation noise and penalty strength over training, which helps stabilize learning in early stages and improves generalization in later stages.
- **Deployment:** For real-world deployment, policies are given partial references from *new, unseen* offline-generated TO trajectories, demonstrating generalization without online TO or retraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents compelling findings across simulation and hardware experiments, highlighting the advantages of the Opt2Skill framework.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Different Datasets (Simulated Walking)

- **Superior Tracking Performance:** Opt2Skill policies, trained on dynamically feasible TO-generated trajectories, consistently achieved the best tracking accuracy across most metrics compared to policies trained with human-retargeted motions (Human) or Inverse Kinematics (IK)-based references.
 - Opt2Skill demonstrated lower average end-effector tracking errors (hand: 2.00 cm, foot: 5.23 cm) compared to Human (4.25 cm, 9.42 cm) and IK (5.47 cm, 5.24 cm) policies.
 - It also exhibited significantly lower global drift for long-term consistency (base position: 1.14%, lateral position: 0.06 mm/step, yaw: 0.02 mrad/step), outperforming baselines.
 - While the IK-based policy sometimes achieved lower joint angle errors, its higher task-space errors underscored the discrepancy between kinematic feasibility and dynamic consistency, a gap that Opt2Skill effectively closes.
- **Impact of Observation History:** An ablation study showed that an optimal observation history length (10 steps) for the actor's input significantly improved tracking accuracy, while histories that were too short or too long deteriorated performance.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Rough Terrain:** Opt2Skill-trained policies, even without specific test-time tuning, displayed superior robustness on challenging terrains. They maintained high success rates up to 12 cm stairs and showed greater stability on steeper slopes compared to the Human and IK baselines, which dropped sharply beyond 8 cm stairs. This indicates that training with dynamically feasible references generalizes better to varying environmental conditions.

#### 4.2. Role of Torque Information in Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation (Simulated Wiping Task)

An ablation study focusing on a table-wiping task demonstrated the critical role of joint torque and contact force information:
- **Pos-only Policy:** A policy tracking only end-effector positions failed to produce consistent contact forces, often undershooting desired forces or overshooting when no force was required, due to a lack of contact awareness.
- **Pos+F Policy (Contact Force Reference):** Adding reference contact force to the observation and reward improved average force tracking but still exhibited instability in certain conditions.
- **Pos+T Policy (Joint Torque Reference):** Policies trained with reference joint torques showed improved contact force tracking and better position tracking than Pos+F. This suggests that torque information provides dynamic insights into *how* to apply forces, leading to more physically consistent behavior.
- **Pos+F+T Policy (Combined):** The combination of both reference contact force and joint torque signals (Opt2Skill's full approach) achieved the *best overall force tracking* with competitive position tracking. It demonstrated the most stable and accurate force application across all tested scenarios, proving the complementary nature and critical importance of these dynamic signals for learning high-fidelity, contact-rich skills.
- **Adaptability to Environment Variations:** Policies trained with TO-generated references also showed adaptability to changes in table height (e.g., maintaining wiping behavior and adjusting forces for 0.9m and 1.0m table heights when trained for 0.95m).

#### 4.3. Hardware Experiments (Sim-to-Real Transfer)

Opt2Skill successfully transferred policies to the real Digit humanoid robot for a diverse set of loco-manipulation tasks, showcasing its practical applicability:
- **Locomotion:** Demonstrated accurate base position tracking during flat-ground walking and successful traversal of stairs and ramps (19.5 degrees slope), with plots confirming foot height elevation. The robot exhibited toe compliance on edges, a result of low PD gains.
- **Multi-Contact Manipulation:**
 - **Desk Object Reaching:** The robot successfully reached for an object beyond its support polygon, coordinating leg and upper-body movements, with end-effector tracking errors of 3.7 cm (left) and 3.8 cm (right).
 - **Box Pick-up:** The robot picked up a box from a lower shelf and placed it on a higher one. The policy handled moderate variations in target hand positions beyond the training range, achieving 3.6 cm tracking error for both hands. A small y-axis deviation was observed due to a size mismatch with the real box.
- **Additional Complex Tasks:** Successfully performed heavy box pickup (up to 4.9 kg), pickup+walk, door opening (heavy fire doors), and drawing+wiping tasks, further demonstrating versatility and robust compliant contact.

These hardware results confirm that Opt2Skill enables the robot to perform complex, everyday loco-manipulation behaviors with compliant contact and effective adaptation to environmental variations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Opt2Skill framework represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics control, with far-reaching potential impacts on research and real-world applications.

- **Pioneering Hybrid Control for Humanoids:** This work establishes a robust and scalable pipeline for integrating model-based trajectory optimization with reinforcement learning for high-dimensional humanoid loco-manipulation. By leveraging full-order dynamics-based TO, Opt2Skill pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in terms of dynamically feasible and contact-consistent reference motion generation, providing a blueprint for future hybrid control strategies.

- **Elevating the Quality of Learning Data:** The paper compellingly demonstrates that motion references derived from full-order dynamics TO are superior to those from human motion capture or inverse kinematics, especially for learning complex, contact-rich behaviors. The inclusion of dynamically consistent information, such as joint torques and contact forces, is shown to be critical for the fidelity, robustness, and transferability of learned policies. This finding shifts the paradigm for data generation in imitation learning, advocating for physically grounded data sources.

- **Enabling Versatile Humanoid Capabilities:** Opt2Skill empowers humanoid robots to perform a diverse and complex array of tasks that demand intricate whole-body coordination and interaction with the environment. From dynamic locomotion over challenging terrains (stairs, ramps) to multi-contact manipulation (desk object reaching, box handling, door opening, wiping), the framework significantly expands the operational repertoire of humanoids, moving them closer to fulfilling their promise of general-purpose utility in human-centric spaces.

- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful and extensive hardware demonstrations on the Digit humanoid robot underscore the practical viability of the Opt2Skill approach. The meticulously designed domain randomization and curriculum learning strategies effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap, a perennial challenge in robotics. This capability is crucial for accelerating the development and deployment of humanoid robots in real-world scenarios without prohibitive manual tuning.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens up several avenues for future research. It can inspire further investigations into automated generation of TO parameters for even greater versatility, exploring ways to combine multiple short-horizon reference trajectories for longer, more complex tasks, and potentially integrating online adaptive TO components with RL. The acknowledged complexity of the TO pipeline also highlights the need for continued research into simplifying high-fidelity data collection and improving sensor technologies for real-time force estimation.

In conclusion, Opt2Skill significantly advances the field by providing a robust, data-driven, and physically-grounded approach to humanoid loco-manipulation. Its contributions in generating high-quality dynamic references, improving RL policy learning, and demonstrating versatile hardware capabilities lay a strong foundation for the next generation of intelligent and adaptable humanoid robots.
