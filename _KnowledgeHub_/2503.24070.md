# HACTS: a Human-As-Copilot Teleoperation System for Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics and the Beijing Institute of Technology developed HACTS, a low-cost, kinematically equivalent teleoperation system that provides bilateral joint synchronization for robot arms. This system enables human operators to act as copilots, facilitating the collection of action-correction data which significantly improves robot learning models' failure recovery, generalization to novel scenarios, and human-in-the-loop reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Most existing robot teleoperation systems provide only unilateral control, lacking real-time feedback of the robot's current joint positions to the human operator.
- The absence of bilateral synchronization hinders effective human intervention in autonomous robot tasks and complicates the collection of valuable action-correction data.
- Current bilateral control solutions often rely on expensive, specialized hardware and complex algorithms, limiting their accessibility and widespread adoption in research.

## Method

- **HACTS** is designed as a low-cost, kinematically equivalent leader-follower system built with off-the-shelf servo motors (under $300) and 3D-printed components.
- The system implements real-time bilateral joint position synchronization, allowing the teleoperation device to mirror the robot's state and vice-versa.
- It features a manual override interface (e.g., a foot pedal) that enables human operators to seamlessly switch between autonomous robot execution and direct intervention, acting as a 'copilot'.

## Results

- HACTS-ACT models achieved higher success rates (e.g., **80%** for OpenBox) compared to full-ACT models (**60%**), demonstrating that intervention data is more valuable than an equivalent amount of pure expert demonstrations for imitation learning.
- Action-correction data collected via HACTS significantly improved failure recovery in in-distribution scenarios (e.g., UprightMug success from **10%** to **70%**) and enabled generalization to out-of-distribution static and dynamic environments (e.g., 30-**50%** success rates where baselines failed completely).
- RLPD-HACTS achieved an **80%** success rate in a challenging CloseBin task within 45 minutes of online fine-tuning, improving from an initial **50%** BC policy, and human intervention lengths decreased as the policy learned, indicating efficient human guidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bilateral joint position synchronization, without complex force or tactile feedback, is a simple yet effective mechanism for enabling precise human intervention and copilot functionality in robot teleoperation.
> - Action-correction data, derived from human interventions during robot failures or guidance in novel scenarios, is particularly valuable for improving robot learning policies, often outperforming equivalent amounts of pure expert demonstrations.
> - Utilizing accessible, low-cost hardware designs for teleoperation systems can democratize advanced robot learning research, fostering broader adoption and accelerating data-driven advancements.
