# Select Less, Reason More: Prioritizing Evidence Purity for Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CASIA, UCAS, and NTU developed an evidence-prioritized adaptive framework for Video LLMs that dynamically selects critical frames and performs localized re-sampling to improve long-form video reasoning. This method achieved new state-of-the-art performance among open-source models, demonstrating an 11.3% absolute improvement on the VideoMME benchmark compared to a similar foundational architecture.

## Problem

- Long-form videos present challenges for Video LLMs, as complex temporal and spatial relationships lead to information dilution when using uniform frame sampling.
- Existing textual-space reasoning approaches are static, unable to dynamically acquire visual information based on evolving knowledge gaps during the reasoning process.
- Current pixel-space agent methods lack robust reward mechanisms to ensure selected visual content genuinely contributes to answering questions (evidence purity) and often cannot access fine-grained temporal details.

## Method

- An ==evidence-prioritized== adaptive framework, guided by the "Select Less, Reason More" philosophy, dynamically selects relevant frames and performs localized re-sampling for fine-grained temporal detail.
- An Evidence-Aware Reinforcement Learning (EARL) phase refines the policy through sequential decision-making, iteratively alternating between textual reasoning and dynamic frame selection operations.
- A multi-component reward system in EARL explicitly enforces evidence purity and maximizes answer accuracy, incorporating action, relevance (via IoU), and IoU-constrained correctness rewards, with dynamic sensitivity adjustment across training stages.

## Results

- The method achieved new state-of-the-art performance among open-source Video LLMs across five benchmarks, including **59.8%** on LongVideoBench and **69.0%** on MVBench.
- It demonstrated substantial absolute improvements over the Qwen2.5-VL baseline, with an **11.3%** increase on VideoMME (from **53.6%** to **64.9%**) and on MVBench (from **62.6%** to **69.0%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of each component of the EARL framework, including the relevance reward and the IoU constraint in the correctness reward, for achieving optimal policy and performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically selecting a minimal, highly relevant set of frames combined with localized temporal refinement is more effective for high-quality long-form video reasoning than simply increasing the fixed visual context.
> - A rigorous reinforcement learning framework with a multi-component reward system that explicitly promotes evidence purity and links selection quality to answer correctness is crucial for optimizing visual input control in Video LLMs.
> - Intelligently managing visual input through an adaptive selection strategy offers a pathway to scalable and high-performance Video LLMs, especially for long content, by avoiding the prohibitive costs of processing massive fixed contexts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Video Large Language Models (Video LLMs) has demonstrated progress in video understanding by integrating visual feature extraction with advanced language model capabilities. However, a significant challenge persists in long-form video reasoning, primarily due to the inherent characteristics of long videos, which often involve complex long-range temporal and spatial relationships.

Current approaches to video reasoning can be broadly categorized. One prevalent method involves uniform frame sampling, which is often limited by diluting the visual context window with redundant information, thereby obscuring crucial evidence for precise, causality-based decision-making. To mitigate this, some methods have explored frame selection to pre-determine key frames, frequently using external tools such as text-visual similarity metrics. These approaches, often described as textual-space video reasoning, treat visual input as a static condition and lack the ability for models to dynamically request and acquire additional visual information as reasoning progresses. This static nature limits their capacity to resolve ambiguities in information-sparse regions or adapt to evolving information needs during reasoning.

More recently, research has advanced into pixel-space video reasoning, where models are designed to actively interact with video content to obtain necessary information. This domain includes multi-agent Video LLMs and end-to-end agent Video LLMs. Multi-agent systems, like VideoRAG, utilize external knowledge components and dual-channel architectures, but their reliance on decoupled components can limit unified, end-to-end optimization of reasoning and evidence selection policies. End-to-end agent methods, such as Pixel Reasoner, VITAL, and FrameMind, employ reinforcement learning to enable proactive, tool-harnessing interaction. While these methods allow models to select frames within a given interval to obtain new information, they generally supervise only coarse actions. A critical limitation in these existing end-to-end approaches is the absence of rigorous reward mechanisms to enforce evidence purity—that is, to ensure selected visual content genuinely contributes to answering the question. Furthermore, some methods, like Pixel Reasoner, restrict selection to pre-sampled frames, preventing access to finer temporal granularity.

This work positions itself within the landscape of pixel-space video reasoning, specifically addressing the dual limitations of existing end-to-end agent methods: the lack of robust reward mechanisms for enforcing evidence purity and, in some cases, the inability to access fine-grained temporal detail beyond initially sampled frames. The proposed framework aims to provide a unified, adaptive strategy that enables models to actively and intelligently manage their visual input to ensure evidence purity and acquire necessary temporal granularity, thereby enhancing reasoning capabilities in long-form videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance long-form video reasoning capabilities in Video LLMs by addressing the challenges of information dilution and the obscuring of critical evidence caused by static uniform frame sampling.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing video reasoning approaches:
- **Information Dilution in Long Videos:** Long-form videos present complex temporal and spatial relationships. The conventional method of uniformly sampling frames often results in the visual context window being filled with redundant information, thereby diluting crucial evidence necessary for accurate reasoning and causality-based decision-making. This redundancy can hinder the model's ability to focus on pertinent details.
- **Limitations of Textual-Space Reasoning:** While some methods employ frame selection to identify key frames, they typically operate within a textual-space reasoning paradigm. These approaches treat the visual input as a static starting condition and lack the ability for the model to dynamically request and acquire further visual information based on knowledge gaps identified during the reasoning process. This passive engagement limits their adaptability and effectiveness in complex scenarios.
- **Suboptimality of Existing Pixel-Space Reasoning Agents:** More recent pixel-space video reasoning models attempt to actively interact with videos. However, these models, including multi-agent and end-to-end agent systems, exhibit two key deficiencies:
 - **Lack of Evidence Purity Rewards:** Existing end-to-end agent methods generally supervise only coarse actions without rigorously rewarding whether the selected visual content genuinely contributes to answering the question or enforcing evidence purity. This means models may select frames that are not truly relevant, leading to contextual distraction and potentially incorrect inferences.
 - **Inability to Access Fine-Grained Temporal Detail:** In some cases, these models are restricted to selecting frames only from an initially pre-sampled set. This limitation prevents them from accessing finer temporal granularity when specific, localized details are crucial for accurate decision-making.

To address these limitations, the research proposes an evidence-prioritized adaptive framework guided by the philosophy "Select Less, Reason More." The core objectives are:
- To enable the model to dynamically select the minimal yet sufficient set of relevant frames to maximize answer accuracy and evidence purity.
- To integrate a mechanism for temporal refinement that performs localized re-sampling around selected key frames, thereby accessing finer temporal granularity necessary for accurate decision-making.
- To develop a rigorous reward mechanism within a reinforcement learning framework that explicitly enforces evidence purity, ensuring that selected visual content is genuinely contributory to the reasoning task.

By achieving these objectives, the work aims to transform Video LLMs into active interrogators of evidence, capable of dynamically controlling and purifying their visual input for improved long-form video reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is an evidence-prioritized adaptive framework for pixel-space video reasoning, built upon the "Select Less, Reason More" philosophy. This framework aims to dynamically identify critical frames and perform localized re-sampling for fine-grained temporal detail. The comprehensive training pipeline involves two main phases: Operation-Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Evidence-Aware Reinforcement Learning (EARL).

#### 3.1. Operation-Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The training initiates with an SFT phase using a dataset of question-answer pairs and their corresponding reasoning steps. These reasoning steps include both textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps and explicit frame selection actions, which act as callable tools. The model is trained to minimize the standard cross-entropy loss, allowing it to acquire baseline competence in multi-step, tool-augmented reasoning. However, SFT is recognized as limited by the quality of its expert data, as it cannot reliably differentiate between genuinely necessary and suboptimal frame selection actions. This limitation motivates the subsequent EARL phase.

#### 3.2. Evidence-Aware Reinforcement Learning (EARL)

The EARL phase is central to refining the model's competence into an adaptive reasoning policy. This phase frames the video reasoning task as a sequential decision-making process where the model iteratively alternates between textual reasoning and frame selection operations.
- **Dynamic Interaction:** After a frame selection operation, the model refines its visual context by dynamically performing localized frame re-sampling. To maintain efficiency, the model is restricted to a maximum of two dynamic frame selection operations per prompt. Each selection triggers local, uniform re-sampling of the original video, with up to 16 new frames.
- **Key-frame Annotation:** High-quality key frame annotations are crucial for supervising the relevance and purity of the model's frame selection decisions. A hybrid annotation approach is used: GPT-4o initially generates a preliminary set of key frame indices (F_key) based on video frames, questions, and answers, adhering to a size constraint. Human annotators then review and refine F_key by removing irrelevant frames, resulting in the ground-truth set F_gold. This process ensures the capture of visual evidence for the model's two-round frame selection.
- **Multi-component Reward System:** EARL is guided by a reward system specifically designed to promote "Select Less, Reason More" by enforcing evidence frame purity and maximizing final answer accuracy. The system comprises three components:
 - **Action Reward (r_action):** A small, fixed reward (1 if frames are selected, 0 otherwise) is provided for every frame selection action. This incentivizes the model to actively engage in frame selection and exploration rather than avoiding such actions due to uncertainty.
 - **Relevance Reward (r_relevance):** This reward encourages the selection of frames crucial for answering the query, directly promoting evidence purity. It is calculated as the Intersection over Union (IoU) between the model's selected frames (F_selected) and the ground-truth golden key frames (F_gold). The reward is a continuous value ranging from 0 to 1, guiding the model toward selecting the smallest, purest set of frames that maximally overlaps with the ground truth.
 - **Correctness Reward (r_correct):** This component links frame selection quality to the ultimate task objective and enforces evidence purity. When the model's predicted answer matches the ground truth, a differentiated positive reward is granted based on the IoU of the selected frames. If the IoU is 0.5 or greater, a reward of 1 is given; if the IoU is less than 0.5, a reward of 0.5 is given. An incorrect answer results in a reward of -1. This structure incentivizes both accurate answers and their derivation from a high-purity set of visual evidence.
- **Dynamic Adjustment of Reward Sensitivity:** To enhance learning stability, a dynamic adjustment mechanism is introduced, tailoring reward sensitivity to different training stages. Training progress (t/T) determines scaling factors α(t) for r_action and β(t) for r_relevance.
 - **Early Stages (Progress ≤ P):** α(t) is higher (α_early) and β(t) is lower (β_early), encouraging exploration and experimentation with frame selections without excessive focus on strict purity.
 - **Later Stages (Progress > P):** α(t) is reduced (α_late) and β(t) is increased (β_late), shifting focus to refining the model's ability to maximize purity and accuracy.
 The total reward is calculated as: `r_total = r_correct + α(t) · r_action + β(t) · r_relevance`. This dynamic balancing facilitates a gradual transition from exploration to refined, high-accuracy selection performance.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The base model used is Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. The SFT phase uses 3.8k samples and Open-R1, while the RL phase uses 8.3k samples and OpenRLHF. Videos are initially uniformly sampled to a maximum of 32 frames.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experiments across five video reasoning benchmarks to validate the effectiveness of the evidence-prioritized adaptive method.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

The proposed evidence-prioritized adaptive method achieves a new state-of-the-art performance among open-source Video LLMs across all five evaluated benchmarks: MLVU, VideoMME (without subtitles), LongVideoBench, LVBench, and MVBench.
- Specifically, the 7B model achieved 59.8% on LongVideoBench, 69.0% on MVBench, and 64.9% on VideoMME (Overall).
- Compared to the Qwen2.5-VL baseline (7B model, 32 frames), which shares a similar foundational architecture, the proposed method demonstrates substantial absolute improvements, for example, an 11.3% increase on VideoMME (from 53.6% to 64.9%) and on MVBench (from 62.6% to 69.0%).
- The method also outperformed existing open-source end-to-end agent Video LLMs, such as Pixel Reasoner (67.8% on MVBench) and FrameMind (64.2% on MVBench), highlighting the necessity of its novel reward mechanism for enforcing evidence purity and reasoning accuracy.
- In long-video reasoning scenarios, the adaptive selection mechanism combined with temporal refinement enabled the model to perform competitively with or surpass many long-video models that utilize significantly larger fixed visual contexts. For instance, on VideoMME (Long), the model achieved 57.8%, which is higher than LongVA (47.6% with 128 frames) and LongVILA (53.0% with 256 frames). This suggests that an intelligent, evidence-aware selection strategy can be more effective than simply increasing the quantity of fixed input frames.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of individual components of the framework.

- **Effectiveness of Evidence-Aware RL (EARL):** The EARL phase was found to be critical. Without EARL (i.e., using only SFT), the model's accuracy was substantially lower across benchmarks. For example, on LongVideoBench, accuracy dropped from 59.8% (with EARL) to 51.9% (SFT only). This demonstrates that EARL, with its multi-component reward system, effectively refines the policy learned during SFT by optimizing visual input control.
- **Effectiveness of Relevance Reward (r_relevance):** Removing the relevance reward (Ours w/o RR) led to performance degradation. Accuracy on LongVideoBench dropped from 59.8% to 56.8%, and on MLVU, it decreased from 49.3% to 47.1%. This indicates that r_relevance is indispensable for enforcing evidence purity, reducing irrelevant frames, minimizing noise, and preserving the quality of the visual context.
- **Effectiveness of IoU Constraint in Correct Reward (r_correct):** The IoU constraint within r_correct was evaluated by simplifying the correctness reward to a standard binary one (Ours w/o IoU). Its removal resulted in decreased overall accuracy, with LongVideoBench performance dropping from 59.8% to 57.8% and LVBench from 46.2% to 44.7%. This suggests that the IoU constraint is crucial for ensuring that correct answers are not only produced but are also derived from a high-purity and relevant set of visual evidence.
- **Effectiveness of Dynamic Adjustment (DA):** Ablating the dynamic adjustment mechanism (Ours w/o DA), by using fixed reward scaling factors, led to consistent accuracy drops across all benchmarks. This indicates that the dynamic adjustment mechanism is important for ensuring stable convergence and achieving the optimal policy by balancing early exploration with later refinement toward pure frame selection.

Overall, the findings suggest that the intelligent, evidence-aware selection strategy, supported by the EARL framework and its multi-component reward system, provides a more effective approach for long-form video reasoning compared to methods relying on static sampling or less-refined agent-based interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of video understanding by addressing critical limitations in long-form video reasoning within Video LLMs. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Addressing Information Dilution and Temporal Granularity:** The study successfully tackles the pervasive challenges of visual redundancy and the lack of fine temporal granularity inherent in long-form video processing. By introducing an adaptive framework, it offers a method to counter the information dilution that often hampers the performance of Video LLMs.
- **Novel Framework for Active Evidence Interrogation:** The proposed evidence-prioritized adaptive pixel-space video reasoning framework, guided by the "Select Less, Reason More" philosophy, represents a shift from passive video processing to an active, strategic evidence interrogation process. This new paradigm could lead to more efficient and targeted video analysis.
- **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for Video LLMs:** The core technical contribution of the evidence-aware reinforcement learning (EARL) framework, with its multi-component reward system (action, relevance, and IoU-constrained correctness rewards) and dynamic adjustment mechanism, provides a robust method for explicitly enforcing evidence purity and strategically managing visual context selection. This approach offers a model for designing more sophisticated reward functions in complex sequential decision-making tasks within multimodal AI.
- **New State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art accuracy among open-source Video LLMs across demanding benchmarks demonstrates the practical effectiveness of the proposed framework. This sets a new benchmark for performance in long-form video reasoning and validates the principles of evidence prioritization and temporal refinement.
- **Efficiency and Scalability for Long Videos:** The findings suggest that an intelligent, frames-aware method is more effective for high-quality reasoning than simply increasing the number of fixed input frames. This indicates a potential path toward building more scalable and high-performance Video LLMs, particularly for hour-long or even longer video content, without incurring prohibitive computational costs associated with processing massive fixed contexts.
- **Potential for More Robust and Explainable AI:** By focusing on selecting "pure" and relevant evidence, the framework could lead to more robust and potentially more explainable video reasoning systems. If a model's reasoning is tied to specific, demonstrably relevant frames, it may offer clearer insights into its decision-making process.

The work lays a foundation for future research in adaptive multimodal reasoning, potentially influencing the development of AI agents capable of more sophisticated, human-like interaction with and understanding of dynamic visual environments.
