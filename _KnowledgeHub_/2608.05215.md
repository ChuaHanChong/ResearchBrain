# VLAff: Vision-Language-Affordance Model for Unified Actionable Affordances

> [!summary] Summary
> VLAff, a Vision-Language-Affordance model, enables robots to learn manipulation skills by extracting unified visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances from egocentric human videos. It achieves state-of-the-art visual affordance prediction and robust zero-shot manipulation in real-world environments, accelerating robot policy learning.

## Problem

- High cost and time required for collecting robot-specific action-state datasets limit the scalability of robot skill learning.
- The embodiment mismatch between human demonstrations and robotic systems hinders direct transfer of manipulation skills from human videos.
- Existing affordance learning methods often focus on single modalities or rely on specialized sensor data, lacking a unified and comprehensive understanding of manipulation from diverse human videos.

## Method

- Created ==EgoAffordance==, a large-scale dataset of unified visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances by extracting object-centric interaction information from egocentric human videos.
- Developed **VLAff**, a Vision-Language-Affordance model, which integrates a ==VLM backbone==, ==DINOv2 encoder==, and specialized affordance tokens with decoders to simultaneously predict visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances.
- Applied VLAff for zero-shot manipulation in simulation and real-world robots, and for affordance-guided robot policy learning, demonstrating practical utility and transferability.

## Results

- EgoAffordance, a large-scale dataset of over **200,000 episodes** and millions of visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances, was successfully constructed from egocentric human videos.
- VLAff achieved state-of-the-art performance in visual affordance prediction across all metrics (e.g., IoU of **0.121**) and demonstrated superior real-world zero-shot manipulation (**68.0%** average success rate) compared to existing methods.
- Integration of all three affordance modalities provided the most efficient guidance for robot policy learning, with visual affordances identified as critical for successful manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly learning visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances enables the model to capture cross-modal correlations, leading to a more comprehensive and robust understanding of manipulation tasks.
> - Object-centric representation of affordances, normalized relative to a 3D anchor point, is crucial for creating embodiment-agnostic information transferable from human demonstrations to diverse robot platforms.
> - Visual affordance heatmaps provide fundamental cues for object interaction points and are essential for successful robotic manipulation learning, outweighing the impact of grasp pose and trajectory guidance in some contexts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of foundation models, robot learning, and affordance theory, specifically addressing the challenge of transferring manipulation skills from human demonstrations to robotic systems.

**Foundation Models in Robotics:** The paper acknowledges the success of large-scale foundation models in natural language processing and computer vision and their nascent application in robotics. However, it highlights limitations in directly applying these models to robot control, primarily due to the high cost and time required for collecting robot-specific action-state datasets, which often confine learning to constrained laboratory environments.

**Learning from Human Videos:** Human video datasets offer a scalable and diverse alternative to robot-specific data. Prior work has explored learning implicit visual representations from human videos for robot learning, which, while improving performance, are often difficult to interpret and directly translate into robot actions. More recent efforts have focused on extracting explicit action representations, such as contact-based affordances, hand poses, or 3D trajectories. However, these methods frequently concentrate on a single modality in isolation or are limited to 2D image space, lacking a comprehensive, unified understanding of manipulation.

**Affordance Learning:** Affordances, defined as opportunities for action provided by an environment, are recognized as a valuable bridge between human demonstrations and robot task understanding. Existing affordance research has identified visual interaction regions, grasp configurations, and hand trajectories. Nevertheless, these studies often rely on data acquired through specialized sensors in static environments or focus on individual affordance modalities without integration into a single framework. There has been a gap in developing a unified model that predicts visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances simultaneously from human video data.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** While VLA models have demonstrated progress in robotic manipulation through large-scale pretraining, they typically require robot-specific action labels and contend with the embodiment mismatch between humans and robots. This work proposes to mitigate this issue by learning actionable affordances as an intermediate, embodiment-agnostic representation.

In summary, this work addresses the need for a scalable, comprehensive, and embodiment-agnostic approach to robot skill learning from human demonstrations. It seeks to overcome the limitations of single-modality affordance learning and the embodiment gap in VLA models by developing a unified framework for extracting and learning visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances from large-scale egocentric human videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable scalable robot learning of manipulation skills by effectively leveraging large-scale egocentric human videos, thereby addressing the embodiment mismatch between humans and robots and the inherent lack of explicit action-state information in video data.

To achieve this overarching goal, the authors set forth three specific objectives:

1. **To develop a method for extracting actionable affordance data from human videos:** The motivation here stems from the observation that while human videos are abundant and diverse, they lack direct, executable action information for robots. Traditional methods often provide implicit representations or focus on isolated aspects of interaction. This objective aims to construct a pipeline that can robustly extract comprehensive, explicit, and object-centric representations of interaction, including *where* to interact (visual affordance), *how* to grasp (grasp affordance), and *how* to move (trajectory affordance). This is crucial for creating an embodiment-agnostic representation suitable for robot transfer.

2. **To train a large-scale, unified affordance knowledge model:** Previous work on affordances often focused on individual modalities, such as visual contact points or hand poses, in isolation. The motivation for a unified model is rooted in the hypothesis that manipulation is a multi-faceted action requiring integrated understanding of all these aspects. A model that simultaneously predicts visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances can learn cross-modal correlations, leading to a more complete and robust understanding of a manipulation task. This objective aims to build a foundation model, leveraging advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), that can generate all three types of actionable affordances from a visual observation and natural language instruction.

3. **To apply the learned affordance knowledge to downstream robotic manipulation tasks:** The ultimate motivation is to demonstrate the practical utility and transferability of the extracted and learned affordances to real-world robotic systems. This involves evaluating the model's performance in direct zero-shot manipulation scenarios, where robots execute tasks based solely on the model's predictions, and in affordance-guided robot learning, where the predicted affordances provide structured priors to accelerate reinforcement learning in target robot embodiments. This objective seeks to validate the proposed framework as an effective pathway for scalable robot skill acquisition.

In essence, the motivation is to create a robust and comprehensive system that can transform readily available human demonstration videos into directly applicable, multi-modal action guidance for robots, thereby overcoming key data and embodiment challenges in robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a framework that comprises three main components: (1) the construction of a large-scale actionable affordance dataset from egocentric human videos, named EgoAffordance; (2) the development of VLAff, a Vision-Language-Affordance model for unified prediction of affordances; and (3) the application of VLAff to robotic manipulation tasks.

**A. Affordance Extraction from Egocentric Human Videos (EgoAffordance Dataset)**

The core of the data generation process involves leveraging state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction and human-object interaction analysis techniques on raw egocentric human videos.

1. **Data Preparation:** The process begins with existing egocentric video datasets (e.g., Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS). For each video segment with associated language descriptions, frames are sampled around annotated interaction timestamps. A pre-trained Large VLM is then queried to extract key interaction information, including contact keyframes, action labels, object categories, and interacting hand information.

2. **Hand-Object Interaction Extraction:** For the identified contact keyframes, a hand-object detector is applied to obtain bounding boxes for hands and objects. Object masks and 2D hand keypoints are extracted. Contact regions are determined by computing the intersection between fingertip keypoints and object masks. These contact regions are then tracked across frames. For 3D hand reconstruction, MANO parameters, representing hand pose and shape, are extracted. To reduce visual bias from the egocentric perspective and create agent-agnostic scenes, ego segmentation is performed, followed by inpainting to remove the human hand.

3. **Ego-centric Structure-from-Motion (SfM):** To reconstruct the 3D scene and trajectory, monocular depth estimation is applied to the inpainted frames. Camera intrinsics are estimated if not provided by the dataset. Camera poses are then estimated, with dynamic regions (hands, objects) filtered out to ensure robust estimation. With estimated camera poses and depth maps, 3D hand trajectories are tracked, capturing both pre- and post-contact motion patterns.

4. **Object-Centric Formulation:** The extracted affordances are formulated in an object-centric manner. A 2D peak interaction point from the visual affordance heatmap is projected to 3D space using camera intrinsics and depth to obtain a 3D anchor point. The grasp pose (MANO parameters) and trajectory (sequences of 6D poses: 3D translation + 3D rotation) are then spatially transformed relative to this 3D anchor point. This normalization ensures the affordances are embodiment-agnostic, providing "where to interact," "how to grasp," and "how to move" relative to the object.

**B. VLAff: Vision-Language-Affordance Model**

VLAff is a large vision-language model-based unified foundation model designed to predict visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances simultaneously.

1. **Model Architecture:**
 - **VLM Backbone:** The core is a pre-trained Vision-Language Model, specifically Qwen2.5-VL, which processes the input image and language instruction.
 - **Vision Encoder:** An additional DINOv2 encoder is integrated to provide enhanced, fine-grained visual features, addressing VLM limitations in precise spatial grounding.
 - **Specialized Affordance Tokens:**
 - **Visual Affordance Token (`<SEG>`):** A special token added to the VLM vocabulary. Its embedding is used by a segmentation decoder to generate pixel-wise visual affordance heatmaps.
 - **Grasp Token (`<GRASP>`):** A token introduced for grasp pose prediction. The VLM learns to predict a 96-dimensional vector representing MANO hand parameters (global wrist rotation and 15 local joint rotations, each in 6D rotation representation). This is handled by a dedicated grasp decoder.
 - **Trajectory Tokens (`<p*>`):** To represent trajectories autoregressively, the 6D pose space (3D translation + 3D rotation) is discretized into bins, forming a vocabulary of trajectory tokens. The VLM generates sequences of these tokens, which are decoded back into continuous 6D poses.
 - **Decoders:**
 - **Visual Affordance Decoder:** Fuses DINOv2 features with the `<SEG>` token embedding to produce the final visual affordance heatmap.
 - **Grasp Decoder:** Decodes the `<GRASP>` token embedding into the 96-dimensional MANO parameters.
 - **Trajectory Decoder:** The VLM's generation mechanism itself acts as a trajectory decoder, predicting sequences of quantized trajectory tokens.

2. **Training Objectives:**
 - **Visual Affordance Loss:** Soft Dice Loss is used for heatmap prediction, addressing class imbalance.
 - **Grasp Affordance Loss:** Smooth L1 loss is applied to the predicted MANO parameters.
 - **Trajectory Affordance Loss:** Standard Cross-Entropy loss is used for the autoregressive prediction of trajectory tokens.
 - **Total Loss:** A weighted sum of these three losses is minimized during training.

3. **Trajectory Sampling Strategy (Inference):** To generate physically plausible 3D trajectories from 2D image and language inputs:
 - **In-Context Trajectory Guidance:** A high-performance VLM (GPT) acts as a high-level planner, providing a coarse 3D directional vector (e.g., push forward) in the ego-centric frame. This guidance is applied to the initial trajectory token by sampling its position around the contact point scaled by the VLM-provided direction.
 - **Sampling-Based Trajectory Selection:** Multiple trajectory candidates are sampled using nucleus sampling. The optimal trajectory is selected by balancing collision avoidance (checking waypoints against voxelized 3D scene) and directional alignment using a composite objective.

**C. Experimental Evaluation**

VLAff's performance is evaluated across visual affordance prediction, zero-shot manipulation in simulation and real-world, and affordance-guided policy learning. The system uses Fetch and PR2 robots for real-world experiments and IsaacGym for simulation tasks from PartManip, FrankaKitchen, and ManiSkill. Baselines include VRB, UAD, 3DOI, LISA for visual affordance, and RAM, VRB, GeneralFlow, VidBot for zero-shot manipulation. GraspNet is used for baseline gripper pose generation, while VLAff uses a retargeting strategy from predicted hand poses. For affordance-guided learning, a reward function combines sparse task success (VLM-determined) and dense affordance-based guidance rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted assess VLAff across visual affordance prediction, zero-shot manipulation, and affordance-guided robot learning.

**1. EgoAffordance Dataset:**
The researchers successfully constructed **EgoAffordance**, a large-scale dataset. This dataset comprises 204,025 episodes, featuring 5,782,431 visual affordance heatmaps and 11,612,524 grasp and trajectory affordances. This collection provides comprehensive actionable affordances extracted from diverse egocentric human videos, significantly expanding the scope of available data for unified affordance learning.

**2. Visual Affordance Prediction:**
VLAff demonstrated **state-of-the-art performance** in visual affordance prediction. Across all quantitative metrics (IoU, NSS, SIM, KLD), VLAff outperformed baseline methods such as VRB, UAD, 3DOI, and LISA.
- VLAff achieved an IoU of 0.121, NSS of 1.542, SIM of 0.142, and KLD of 2.517, consistently superior to the baselines.
- Qualitative results indicated that VLAff produced more fine-grained and accurate localization of interaction regions compared to existing methods, which sometimes generated discrete points or predicted entire objects rather than specific interaction spots.
- This superior performance was attributed to the integration of DINOv2's part-aware visual features and the extensive training on the large-scale, diverse EgoAffordance dataset. The results supported the hypothesis that jointly learning multiple affordance modalities improves visual affordance prediction.

**3. Zero-Shot Manipulation Performance:**
VLAff exhibited robust capabilities in zero-shot robotic manipulation across both simulated and real-world environments.
- **Simulation Tasks:** VLAff achieved an average success rate of 83.0% across 10 diverse manipulation tasks (e.g., opening cabinets, picking up objects). This performance was comparable to VidBot (85.0%), which was identified as a leading zero-shot manipulation framework. The slight edge of VidBot in simulation was attributed to its direct access to spatial input like depth information, which is beneficial in controlled simulation settings.
- **Real-World Tasks:** VLAff demonstrated **superior performance in real-world scenarios**, achieving an average success rate of 68.0% across 5 tasks (e.g., opening a drawer, picking up a bucket). This significantly surpassed VidBot's 52.0% and other baselines like RAM (24.0%), VRB (28.0%), and GeneralFlow (28.0%). This performance gap validated the benefit of training VLAff on large-scale real-world egocentric video datasets, which are suggested to better capture the complexities of real manipulation compared to other methods that struggled with estimating appropriate interaction regions in cluttered real environments.
- Identified failure cases primarily involved incorrect initial direction sampling during trajectory generation.

**4. Affordance-Guided Manipulation Learning:**
An ablation study on the "Open Fridge" task in a real-world environment investigated the contribution of each affordance modality to policy learning efficiency.
- **Complete Guidance:** The model utilizing all three affordance modalities (visual heatmap, grasp pose, and trajectory) achieved the most efficient learning and highest success rate. This indicated that these modalities provide complementary guidance.
- **Criticality of Visual Affordance:** The **visual affordance heatmap was found to be essential for manipulation learning**. When it was removed, the robot consistently failed to achieve successful manipulation, regardless of training duration. This underscores the heatmap's role in providing fundamental cues for object interaction points.
- **Role of Grasp Pose:** Grasp pose information was observed to play a less significant role in accelerating manipulation learning. This was attributed to the morphological differences between human hands (from which the grasp poses were derived) and robot grippers, suggesting that direct transfer of human hand configurations to robot grippers is challenging.
- **Trajectory Guidance:** While contributing to learning efficiency, trajectory information was not as critical as visual affordance.

In summary, VLAff demonstrated its effectiveness in generating actionable affordances, achieving state-of-the-art visual affordance prediction, robust zero-shot manipulation (particularly in real-world settings), and providing beneficial guidance for robot policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the fields of robotics, computer vision, and artificial intelligence.

**1. Enabling Scalable Robot Learning from Human Videos:** The most immediate impact lies in providing a practical framework for leveraging the vast and growing repository of human video data for robot learning. By addressing the embodiment mismatch and the challenge of extracting explicit actions, VLAff offers a pathway to bypass the costly and time-consuming process of collecting robot-specific action-state datasets, thereby scaling up robot skill acquisition to a broader range of tasks and environments.

**2. Introduction of the EgoAffordance Dataset:** The creation of EgoAffordance, a large-scale dataset comprising comprehensive actionable affordances (visual heatmaps, grasp poses, and trajectories) extracted from egocentric human videos, is a significant resource contribution. This dataset fills a notable gap in existing benchmarks, which typically focus on single modalities or are acquired in controlled laboratory settings. EgoAffordance is expected to stimulate further research in unified affordance learning, multi-modal understanding, and human-to-robot skill transfer.

**3. Unified and Comprehensive Affordance Representation:** The development of VLAff, a model that jointly learns and predicts visual, grasp, and trajectory affordances, represents an advancement over single-modality approaches. This unified representation provides a more complete understanding of manipulation, encoding "where," "how," and "motion" aspects of interaction. This integrated approach can lead to more robust and generalizable robot behaviors, as it captures the cross-modal correlations essential for complex tasks.

**4. Enhanced Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation:** The demonstrated superior performance of VLAff in real-world zero-shot manipulation tasks indicates its immediate practical applicability. This capability allows robots to execute novel tasks in unseen environments without task-specific training, a crucial step towards truly general-purpose robotic systems. The ability to generalize from human videos to diverse real-world scenarios has significant implications for deploying robots in unstructured environments such as homes, offices, and warehouses.

**5. Accelerated Affordance-Guided Robot Learning:** VLAff's ability to provide effective guidance for real-world reinforcement learning policies is another key impact. By offering structured priors in the form of predicted contact points and trajectories, VLAff can significantly accelerate the learning process for robots. This hybrid approach, combining large-scale pre-training with environment-adaptive fine-tuning, represents a robust strategy for bridging the sim-to-real gap and achieving high-performance robot control. The finding that visual affordances are essential for manipulation learning offers critical insights for designing future robot learning systems.

**6. Advancing Vision-Language Models for Embodied AI:** This work extends the capabilities of Vision-Language Models by enabling them to generate directly actionable, multi-modal control signals for robots. By integrating specialized affordance tokens and decoders, VLAff pushes VLMs beyond purely perceptual or descriptive tasks towards generative action planning, contributing to the broader goal of embodied artificial intelligence.

**Potential Future Work:** The acknowledged limitation regarding occasional generation of implausible trajectories suggests future research directions, particularly integrating 3D scene-aware model architectures to enhance physical plausibility. This would further solidify the reliability and safety of affordance-guided robot operations.

In conclusion, VLAff represents a substantial step towards autonomous robot learning from human-centric data, offering a scalable, comprehensive, and practically impactful solution for developing generalist manipulation skills in robotic systems.
