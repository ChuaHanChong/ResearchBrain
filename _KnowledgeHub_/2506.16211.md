# ControlVLA: Few-shot Object-centric Adaptation for Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ControlVLA introduces a novel framework that enables robots to acquire new manipulation skills from a few demonstrations by integrating pre-trained Vision-Language-Action models with object-centric representations. The method utilizes a ControlNet-style fine-tuning approach with zero-initialized layers, achieving a 76.7% success rate across diverse short-horizon tasks with 10-20 demonstrations and demonstrating robust performance on long-horizon and generalized tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies traditionally require extensive demonstrations (hundreds or thousands) for new tasks, making real-world deployment expensive and impractical.
- Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while general, remain data-intensive to fine-tune for specific downstream tasks and often lack explicit grounding to task-relevant objects.
- Existing object-centric methods still demand significant data and often lack the broad action prior provided by large VLA models, limiting their applicability in data-scarce scenarios.

## Method

- The framework pre-trains a general-purpose diffusion transformer-based VLA policy on a large, multi-task dataset to establish a strong action prior.
- It incorporates object-centric representations by segmenting and tracking task-relevant objects using GroundingDINO and SAM2, then extracting both positional and action-relevant geometrical features for these objects.
- A ControlNet-style fine-tuning strategy is applied, introducing a dual-attention structure with zero-initialized key-value projection layers for object-centric conditions, enabling stable and efficient few-shot adaptation of the pre-trained VLA model.

## Results

- ControlVLA achieved a **76.7%** overall success rate across 6 diverse short-horizon tasks with only 10-20 demonstrations, significantly outperforming baselines like Diffusion Policy (**20.8%**) and Octo (**1.6%**).
- On challenging long-horizon tasks, ControlVLA attained an average **60.0%** success rate with 25 demonstrations, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like π0 (**23.3%**) by approximately **3x**.
- The method showed promising generalization to unseen objects (**70.0%** success rate on novel toys) and backgrounds (**60.0%** success rate on unseen backgrounds) after training on limited data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating object-centric representations is crucial for data efficiency and task grounding in few-shot learning, as demonstrated by the significant performance drop when they are omitted.
> - Zero-initialization of new conditional layers, a ControlNet-style adaptation, is essential for stable fine-tuning, allowing the pre-trained model's prior knowledge to be preserved while new conditions are learned.
> - A strong action prior from large-scale pre-training is indispensable for robust and generalizable robotic policies, as training an object-centric policy from scratch leads to unstable execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is grappling with the significant challenge of enabling robots to learn complex skills efficiently and generalize them to new tasks and environments. This paper, ControlVLA, directly addresses a critical bottleneck: the high data requirements for training and adapting robotic manipulation policies, particularly in real-world settings where obtaining large, diverse datasets is prohibitively expensive or impractical.

Historically, robotic manipulation has relied on several approaches, each with limitations that ControlVLA seeks to overcome:

- **Simulation-augmented data:** Methods that leverage simulated environments to generate training data (e.g.,) often face the "sim-to-real gap," where policies trained in simulation do not translate effectively to the real world. They also frequently require *a priori* knowledge of object CAD models and precise 3D pose estimations, which are impractical in unstructured real-world scenarios.
- **Pre-built modules:** Some approaches rely on modular systems incorporating pre-built components for tasks like grasping or pose estimation. While functional, these lack extensibility and struggle with the complexity of real-world variations or novel objects.
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** Imitation learning has shown promise due to its scalability and ability to learn from demonstrations, reducing reliance on explicit knowledge. However, traditional IL often demands extensive demonstrations for each new task.
- **Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Recent advances, such as RT-1, RT-2, Octo, and $\pi_0$, have ushered in a new era of "foundation models" for robotics. These VLA models are pre-trained on massive datasets of diverse manipulation tasks, enabling impressive generalization capabilities across various scenarios. However, as the authors highlight, fine-tuning these general-purpose policies for downstream, specific tasks remains "data-intensive," often requiring substantial task- and environment-specific data to adapt to visual and action domains. This is the **direct gap ControlVLA aims to bridge.**
- **Object-centric Representations:** On a separate front, object-centric representations, which decompose complex scenes into manipulable entities, have demonstrated potential for improving data efficiency and policy robustness. By focusing on salient object properties, they reduce input complexity and enhance robustness to pose changes and noise. However, existing object-centric methods still typically require hundreds of demonstrations to learn a task and, crucially, often "lack an action prior from VLA models in data-scarce scenarios."

ControlVLA strategically positions itself at the intersection of these advancements. It combines the **rich, generalizable action prior provided by large-scale pre-trained VLA models** with the **data efficiency and robustness offered by object-centric representations.** The key innovation lies in *how* it integrates these two: by adapting the ControlNet architecture, originally from generative AI (image diffusion), for robotic control. This is explicitly stated as the *first effort* to apply ControlNet-style fine-tuning in robotic manipulation. This novel architectural adaptation allows for efficient, few-shot adaptation of powerful VLA models by "progressively integrating object-centric representations" without "compromising the generalization or action quality" of the pre-trained model. In essence, ControlVLA seeks to unlock the full potential of large pre-trained robot models by making them rapidly adaptable to new, data-limited real-world tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind ControlVLA is to **overcome the current barrier of high data requirements in real-world robotic manipulation**, thereby accelerating the practical deployment of robots in diverse and unstructured environments.

The key objectives of this research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enable Efficient Few-Shot Adaptation:** The primary objective is to develop a learning framework that allows robots to acquire new manipulation skills from a very limited number of demonstrations (specifically, 10-20 episodes) for specific tasks, a significant reduction compared to traditional methods requiring hundreds.
2. **Bridge Pre-trained VLA Models with Object-Centric Representations:** To achieve few-shot learning, the authors aim to combine the strengths of two promising but previously disparate approaches:
 - **Leverage Pre-trained VLA Models:** Utilize the rich, general-purpose action priors learned from large-scale, multi-task datasets to provide a strong foundation for new skills.
 - **Incorporate Object-Centric Representations:** Integrate task-relevant object information to enhance data efficiency and provide explicit grounding, focusing the policy on crucial elements and improving robustness.
3. **Develop a Stable and Effective Adaptation Mechanism:** Introduce a novel architectural design, inspired by ControlNet, that can inject new object-centric conditions into a pre-trained VLA model without overwriting its valuable prior knowledge. This involves using zero-initialized projection layers to ensure a smooth and stable fine-tuning process.
4. **Demonstrate Robustness and Extensibility:** Empirically validate the framework's ability to generalize to unseen objects and backgrounds, and its capability to handle complex, long-horizon tasks that involve sequential decision-making.

The motivation for these objectives stems directly from the limitations of existing methods. Current approaches either demand impractical amounts of data for real-world deployment, struggle with sim-to-real gaps, or cannot efficiently adapt powerful general-purpose policies to specific real-world contexts. ControlVLA is motivated by the vision of a robot that can rapidly learn new skills "in the wild" from minimal human input, making advanced robotic manipulation truly accessible and scalable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ControlVLA proposes a novel framework that integrates pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with object-centric representations via a ControlNet-style fine-tuning strategy. The methodology is structured into three main phases:

#### 3.1. VLA Model Pre-training

The process begins by pre-training a general-purpose policy, $\pi_g$, on a large-scale, multi-task manipulation dataset (e.g., the DROID dataset mentioned in the appendix). This phase establishes a strong, broad action prior, which is crucial for subsequent few-shot adaptation.
- **Architecture:** The $\pi_g$ model is implemented as a diffusion transformer, a type of generative model (specifically, a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model, DDPM, or a Denoising Diffusion Implicit Model, DDIM) known for its ability to capture complex and multimodal action distributions.
- **Inputs (Observations $O_t$):** At each time step $t$, the observation consists of a single-view RGB image ($I_t$), a language instruction ($\ell_t$), and the robot's proprioceptive state ($q_t$). These multimodal inputs are processed through pre-trained encoders (e.g., CLIP for vision and text) and projected into a shared embedding space.
- **Output (Actions $A_t$):** The model predicts a future action sequence.
- **Training Objective:** The policy is trained using a conditional denoising loss, learning to denoise a noisy action sequence back to the expert action sequence, conditioned on the observations.
- **Inference:** DDIM is used during inference to accelerate the sampling of actions for real-time control.

#### 3.2. Object-centric Representations

To enable efficient task-specific learning from few demonstrations, ControlVLA explicitly incorporates object-centric information. This involves:
- **Segmenting and Tracking Task-Relevant Objects:** The framework identifies fine-grained instance masks ($\{M_i\}$) corresponding to task-relevant objects. This is achieved by leveraging prompt-driven segmentation models:
 - **GroundingDINO:** Used for open-set object detection, grounding language instructions to specific objects in the image.
 - **SAM2:** Segment Anything Model (version 2) is used to generate high-quality instance masks from the detected object regions, ensuring consistent segmentation and tracking across frames.
- **Extracting Object-Centric Features:** For each identified object mask $M_i$, a function $f_\phi$ extracts an object-centric representation $z_i$, composed of two parts:
 - **Positional Feature ($z_i^{pos}$):** Encodes the mean coordinates of the object mask using sinusoidal positional encoding. This provides "where" the object is.
 - **Geometrical Feature ($z_i^{geo}$):** Obtained from a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) that operates directly on the object mask. Unlike typical pre-trained visual features, this CNN is trained from scratch to learn "actionable visual features" specifically informative for continuous control tasks. This provides "what" the object is in an actionable context.
- **Final Representation:** The positional and geometrical features are concatenated to form the complete object-centric representation $z_i$, and an ordered set $Z = \{z_i\}$ for all relevant objects serves as an additional condition for the expert policy.

#### 3.3. ControlNet-style Fine-tuning

This is the core novelty of ControlVLA, enabling efficient adaptation of the pre-trained $\pi_g$ to a task-specific expert policy $\pi_e$ using a limited set of demonstrations ($N_e = 10-20$).
- **Dual-Attention Structure:** The pre-trained VLA policy, being transformer-based, relies on cross-attention mechanisms to condition actions on observations. ControlVLA extends this by introducing a dual-attention structure:
 - One branch uses the original cross-attention based on general observations ($O$).
 - The second, *new* branch incorporates the object-centric representations ($Z$) as an additional condition.
- **Zero-Initialized Key-Value (KV) Projections:** Inspired by ControlNet, the critical design choice is to zero-initialize the weights ($W_z$) and biases ($B_z$) of the new KV-projection layers ($K_z, V_z$) associated with the object-centric conditions.
 - **Initial State Preservation:** At the very beginning of fine-tuning, with zero-initialized weights, the new object-centric attention branch effectively outputs zeros. This means the expert policy $\pi_e$ behaves identically to the pre-trained general-purpose policy $\pi_g$, preserving its rich prior knowledge and action quality.
 - **Gradual Adaptation:** As fine-tuning proceeds, non-zero gradients (as explained in Appendix C) are backpropagated through these zero-initialized layers. This allows the model to progressively learn to integrate the object-centric conditions, guiding the policy towards task-specific skills without corrupting the valuable pre-trained base.
- **Fine-tuning Objective:** The expert policy $\pi_e$ is fine-tuned using the same conditional denoising loss as pre-training, but now conditioned on both the original observations and the new object-centric representations.

This methodology ensures that ControlVLA effectively combines broad generalizability from large-scale pre-training with precise, data-efficient task grounding through object-centric features, all while maintaining stable training dynamics through the ControlNet-style initialization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ControlVLA's experimental results consistently demonstrate its superior performance and efficiency in few-shot robotic manipulation across diverse real-world tasks.

- **Overall Performance on Short-Horizon Tasks (Figure 4):**
 - ControlVLA achieves an impressive **76.7% overall task success rate** across 6 diverse short-horizon manipulation tasks (rigid pick-and-place, soft object manipulation, precise object handling, articulated object operation, deformable cloth folding, and liquid pouring).
 - This significantly **outperforms all tested baselines** by a wide margin: Diffusion Policy (20.8%), Octo (1.6%), ACT (5.0%), and VIOLA (0.0%).
 - ControlVLA demonstrated robust and efficient learning with only **10-20 demonstrations** per task, a stark contrast to traditional methods requiring over 100. Baselines like Octo and ACT struggled particularly with precise object manipulation and complex behaviors, highlighting the benefit of ControlVLA's object-centric grounding and stable fine-tuning.

- **Ablation Studies (Figure 4):**
 - The ablation studies confirm the critical contribution of each core component of ControlVLA:
 - **"w/o pretrain":** Training an object-centric Diffusion Policy from scratch resulted in severe execution jitter and significant performance drops. This underscores the necessity of a strong action prior from large-scale VLA pre-training.
 - **"w/o object-centric":** Fine-tuning the pre-trained model without object-centric representations yielded only marginal improvement over training Diffusion Policy from scratch, emphasizing that object-centric representations are vital for data efficiency and task grounding.
 - **"w/o zero-init":** Removing the zero-initialization for the additional object-centric condition layers caused a drastic drop in success rate. This validates the crucial role of ControlNet-style initialization in stabilizing fine-tuning and effectively integrating new conditions without disrupting prior knowledge.
 - The authors note that stripping all three components (pre-training, object-centric representations, and zero-initialization) effectively degenerates ControlVLA into the Diffusion Policy baseline, further reinforcing the impact of each element.

- **Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks (Table 2):**
 - ControlVLA was evaluated on two challenging long-horizon tasks (OrganizeMultiObjects and ReplaceObjectsInDrawer), each requiring sequential execution of multiple sub-goals and trained with only 25 demonstrations.
 - ControlVLA achieved an average **60.0% success rate** across these tasks (56.7% for OrganizeMultiObjects and 63.3% for ReplaceObjectsInDrawer).
 - It **significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines** like $\pi_0$ (23.3%, 16.7%) and Diffusion Policy (10.0%, 6.7%) by approximately 3x.
 - The stage-wise breakdown of success rates further demonstrated ControlVLA's consistent performance throughout extended action sequences, indicating its ability to reduce compounding errors.

- **Performance on Data Scaling (Figure 5):**
 - On the OrganizeToy task, ControlVLA achieved a high success rate of **80% with as few as 20 demonstrations**.
 - In contrast, baselines struggled to reach comparable performance even with 100 demonstrations, highlighting ControlVLA's exceptional data efficiency and rapid convergence.

- **Generalization on Unseen Objects and Backgrounds (Table 3, Figure 6):**
 - Trained on 20 demonstrations of the OrganizeToy task with a specific green toy and uniform background, ControlVLA showed promising generalization.
 - It achieved a **70.0% average success rate** across three novel objects (bread, banana, orange) and a **60.0% success rate** on a previously unseen background.
 - While slightly lower than the 90% in-domain success rate, these results demonstrate ControlVLA's ability to adapt to visual variations in new environments with minimal effort (only requiring an object prompt for mask extraction).

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that ControlVLA sets a new state-of-the-art for few-shot robotic manipulation, achieving high success rates with significantly reduced data requirements, extending effectively to complex tasks, and showing robust generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ControlVLA represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Breaking the Data Barrier for Robot Deployment:** The most immediate and critical impact of ControlVLA is its ability to enable effective robotic manipulation with **exceptionally few demonstrations (10-20 episodes).** This dramatically lowers the practical barrier to deploying robots in diverse real-world scenarios. In many industrial, service, or domestic settings, collecting hundreds or thousands of task-specific demonstrations is simply unfeasible due to cost, time, or safety concerns. ControlVLA makes rapid skill acquisition a reality, facilitating wider adoption of robots beyond highly structured factory environments.

2. **Unlocking the Potential of Large VLA Models:** ControlVLA effectively addresses the Achilles' heel of large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their data-intensive fine-tuning. By providing an efficient adaptation mechanism, ControlVLA allows the vast general knowledge embedded in these foundation models to be practically leveraged for specific downstream tasks. This synergy means robots can benefit from broad pre-training while still achieving the precision and specificity required for novel manipulation challenges.

3. **Pioneering ControlNet in Robotics:** ControlVLA is explicitly stated as the first application of a ControlNet-style architecture for fine-tuning policies in robotic manipulation. This is a significant methodological contribution. ControlNet has revolutionized conditional generation in computer vision, and its successful adaptation here opens up a new paradigm for "controllable" robot policies. This approach, which allows for stable integration of new conditions without disrupting core learned behaviors through zero-initialized layers, could inspire similar architectural innovations across various robot learning problems.

4. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated robustness to unseen objects and backgrounds, as well as the strong performance on long-horizon tasks, indicates that ControlVLA yields more generalizable and reliable policies. This is crucial for real-world applications where environments are dynamic and task specifications might involve multiple sequential steps. Reducing compounding errors in long-horizon tasks is particularly valuable.

5. **Accelerating Robot Development Cycles:** By significantly reducing the data collection and training time required for new manipulation tasks, ControlVLA could drastically shorten development cycles for robotic applications. This would allow researchers and engineers to rapidly prototype and iterate on new robot skills, fostering innovation and quicker deployment of advanced robotic capabilities.

6. **Foundation for More Capable Embodied AI:** ControlVLA's success in bridging high-level VLA priors with low-level object-centric grounding provides a robust blueprint for future embodied AI systems. As the authors themselves note in the limitations, extending this framework to bi-manual manipulation or truly "in-the-wild" environments with larger foundation models is a natural next step, suggesting ControlVLA could serve as a foundational piece for increasingly autonomous and adaptable robots.

In essence, ControlVLA moves the field closer to a future where robots can learn and adapt to new, complex tasks with unprecedented speed and efficiency, making them more versatile and accessible tools for a wide range of applications.
