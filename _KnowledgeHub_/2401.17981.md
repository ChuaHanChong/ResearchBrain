# From Training-Free to Adaptive: Empirical Insights into MLLMs' Understanding of Detection Information

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper empirically investigates how different training strategies influence Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) comprehension of infused textual detection information from object detection and OCR models. It demonstrates that fine-tuning a pre-trained MLLM for a single epoch effectively enables it to utilize this information, outperforming training-free methods and full retraining, leading to improved performance across various multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently struggle with fine-grained visual details, leading to issues like object hallucination, miscounting, and inaccurate text identification within images.
- Integrating the capabilities of specialized vision detection models (object detection and OCR) into MLLMs is challenging, particularly optimizing the fusion of their textualized outputs.
- Prior work largely relied on 'training-free' strategies for textual detection information infusion, lacking systematic investigation into the effects of adaptive training on MLLM comprehension.

## Method

- Developed a systematic experimental framework to compare three distinct strategies for infusing textual detection information into MLLMs: ==Training-free Infusion (TFI)==, ==Retraining Based Infusion (RBI)==, and ==Fine-tuning Based Infusion (FTBI)==.
- Transformed outputs from DINO (object detection) and PaddleOCRv2 (OCR) into compressed textual formats, using central coordinates and instruction sentences, which are then supplied as supplementary input to the MLLM's language backbone.
- Evaluated LLaVA-1.5 across **10** diverse multimodal benchmarks, covering comprehensive VQA, perceptual reasoning, text recognition, and fine-grained detail understanding.

## Results

- Training-free infusion (TFI) of detection information yielded inconsistent improvements and sometimes led to performance degradation on MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5.
- Retraining-based infusion (RBI) improved performance on specific benchmarks with detection information but notably degraded the MLLM's general image comprehension when detection data was not provided.
- Fine-tuning based infusion (FTBI) consistently outperformed LLaVA-1.5, TFI, and RBI across nearly all **10** evaluated benchmarks, achieving performance increases up to **3.99%** for 7B models and up to **6.71%** for 13B models over training-free methods, while also demonstrating robustness to changes in underlying detectors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training-free infusion of raw textual detection information often introduces noise and can degrade Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) performance.
> - Retraining MLLMs from scratch with continuous detection information infusion negatively impacts their ability to interpret original image encoder features.
> - Fine-tuning a pre-trained MLLM for a single epoch provides a balanced approach, preserving original image understanding while effectively utilizing new detection information and adapting to varying detector qualities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized natural language processing, leading to the development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that integrate visual understanding. MLLMs like GPT-4V have shown impressive general-purpose capabilities across various tasks. However, a significant limitation remains in their ability to accurately interpret fine-grained visual details, leading to issues such as object hallucination (e.g., miscounting objects, misidentifying text in images) and inaccurate localization.

Simultaneously, specialized vision detection models, including object detection (OD) and Optical Character Recognition (OCR), have achieved superior performance in recognizing specific fine-grained elements within images. This disparity has naturally led researchers to explore methods for integrating the strengths of vision detection models with MLLMs to enhance their visual comprehension.

Existing strategies for enhancing MLLMs' detection capabilities can be broadly categorized into:
1. **Expanding datasets:** Incorporating vast amounts of object detection or OCR data during MLLM training (e.g., InstructBLIP, ASM, LLaVA).
2. **Restructuring image encoders:** Modifying the MLLM's visual backbone to extract more fine-grained features (e.g., LION, Honeybee).
3. **Integrating pre-trained detection models at the output end:** Training MLLMs to interact with external detectors for bounding box prediction or segmentation (e.g., UNIFIED-IO, Lisa).
4. **Converting detection model outputs into text:** Transforming detection results (e.g., object labels, bounding box coordinates, OCR text) into natural language descriptions, which are then supplied as supplementary input to the MLLM's language backbone.

This paper specifically focuses on the *fourth category*, highlighting its simplicity and effectiveness. While this approach has gained popularity, the majority of prior work in this area has adopted a "training-free" strategy, directly feeding the textual detection information to pre-trained MLLMs without additional adaptation. The authors identify a critical gap: the limited research on the effects of *adaptive training* when infusing such textual detection information. This work directly addresses this gap by systematically comparing training-free methods with adaptive training strategies, positioning itself as a crucial empirical study for optimizing the fusion of explicit visual information into MLLMs. It aims to inform practitioners on the most effective ways to leverage external detection models for enhancing MLLMs' fine-grained understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to systematically investigate how different training strategies influence MLLMs’ ability to comprehend and utilize infused textual detection information. The authors pose a core research question: **"How does training influence MLLMs’ understanding of infused textual detection information?"**

The motivation stems from several observations:
- **MLLMs' Fine-Grained Limitations:** Despite impressive general capabilities, MLLMs often "hallucinate" or fail to accurately discern specific details like counts, precise locations, or embedded text within images.
- **Effectiveness of Vision Detectors:** Specialized object detection and OCR models excel at these fine-grained tasks.
- **Simplicity of Textual Infusion:** Converting detection outputs into text and feeding them to MLLMs is a straightforward and effective fusion method, often bypassing complex architectural modifications or extensive retraining.
- **Under-exploration of Adaptive Training:** Most existing work leveraging textual infusion is "training-free." However, prior research in pure-text LLMs (e.g., Retrieval-Augmented Generation, fine-tuning for specially formatted inputs) suggests that adaptive training can significantly enhance an LLM's comprehension of novel input formats and its ability to filter irrelevant information.
- **Potential for Enhanced Performance:** The authors hypothesize that adaptive training could push the performance of MLLMs beyond what is achievable with training-free integration, allowing them to better understand the specialized textual format, discard noise, and more effectively integrate this detailed visual information into their reasoning processes.

By addressing this question, the paper seeks to provide empirical insights and practical guidance for optimizing the integration of fine-grained visual understanding into MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors design a systematic experimental framework to compare three distinct training strategies for infusing textual detection information into MLLMs, primarily using LLaVA-1.5 as the representative MLLM, and DINO (object detection) and PaddleOCRv2 (OCR) as the source detection models.

#### 3.1. Text-Based Detection Information Construction

To standardize and compress the detection outputs into a format palatable for LLMs, the authors adopt a specific textual representation:
- **Object Detection Information:** For each detected object, DINO provides a class label and bounding box coordinates. This information is condensed by:
 - Selecting only the central coordinates (first two values of the bounding box).
 - Consolidating objects of the same category, effectively providing a count and a list of central coordinates for each category.
 - Prepending an instruction sentence (e.g., "Here are the central coordinates of certain objects in this image: ...").
- **OCR Information:** PaddleOCRv2 extracts textual content and its positional information. This is similarly transformed by:
 - Extracting the text string and its central coordinates.
 - Prepending an instruction sentence (e.g., "Here are the central coordinates of certain texts in this image: ...").
- **Compression:** This method effectively compresses the potentially verbose bounding box data, minimizing the impact on input sequence length. Information exceeding a token length of 1,024 is excluded.

#### 3.2. Studied Model Architecture (LLaVA-1.5 as Example)

The MLLM architecture generally consists of:
- **Image Encoder:** CLIP-ViT-L-336px to extract image-level features.
- **Input Projector:** A two-layer MLP to align image features with the text semantic space.
- **Backbone LLM:** Vicuna-1.5.
The generated textual detection information is passed through the LLM's embedding layers to obtain text features. Both the image-level features and the detection features are then concatenated and fed into the backbone LLM, allowing it to access both holistic image context and fine-grained details.

#### 3.3. Studied Infusion Strategies

The core of the methodology lies in comparing three training strategies:
- **Training-free Infusion (TFI):** The textual detection information is directly fed into a pre-trained MLLM (LLaVA-1.5) during inference, without any additional training on this specific input format. All original model parameters remain frozen.
- **Retraining Based Infusion (RBI):** The MLLM is trained *from scratch* using its original training pipeline (pre-training MLP, then instruction tuning with LoRA modules for the LLM backbone). Throughout this entire process, the textual detection information is continuously infused. This tests if "re-learning" with the new input format from the ground up is beneficial.
- **Fine-tuning Based Infusion (FTBI):** A well-trained, pre-existing MLLM (LLaVA-1.5) is fine-tuned for a *single additional epoch*. Crucially, the MLP module and the backbone LLM weights are largely frozen, and only LoRA modules introduced to the LLM are trained. This strategy aims to adapt the MLLM to the new input format efficiently without disrupting its established knowledge. Textual detection information is infused during this fine-tuning.

#### 3.4. Quantitative Evaluation Settings

The models are rigorously evaluated across 10 widely recognized multimodal benchmarks, covering diverse capabilities:
- **Comprehensive VQA:** VQAv2, GQA\* (a modified subset of GQA for unambiguous questions), MME.
- **Perceptual & Reasoning:** MMBench, SEED-Bench.
- **Text Recognition:** TextVQA, MME-Cognition (subset of MME focusing on text-in-image).
- **Fine-Grained Details / Hallucination:** MM-Vet (complex tasks with fine-grained details), POPE (object hallucination).
The overall performance improvement is measured as ∆, the average percentage improvement over the original LLaVA-1.5 model.

#### 3.5. Additional Experiments (in Appendix)

The paper includes a rich set of supplementary experiments to validate and deepen the core findings:
- Performance with other MLLMs (Qwen-VL, LLaVA-NeXT) to confirm generalizability.
- Testing different object detectors (YOLOv8, YOLOv5N, YOLOv11L, Co-DETR-LVIS) to assess the impact of detector quality and category range.
- Evaluating FTBI without detection-related data in the fine-tuning set.
- Experiments with unfrozen visual encoders during training.
- Detailed analysis of inference efficiency and text compression.
- Evaluation on DocumentVQA and VALSE benchmarks for specific linguistic phenomena and document understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key insights into the optimal strategies for infusing textual detection information into MLLMs:

**Lesson 1: The Original MLLM Struggles with Comprehending Textual Detection Information (Training-free Infusion - TFI).**
- **Results:** TFI-7B showed partial enhancement on some benchmarks (e.g., POPE, MME-Cognition, confirming benefit for OD/OCR tasks), but TFI-13B often exhibited discernible performance degradation on other tasks.
- **Analysis:** Directly feeding raw textual detection information without adaptation introduces "noise" or extraneous content that can adversely affect MLLM accuracy. Pre-trained MLLMs are not inherently equipped to effectively filter or optimally utilize this specially formatted, often redundant, information.

**Lesson 2: Retraining Has Adverse Effects on Comprehending ViT Features (Retraining Based Infusion - RBI).**
- **Results:** RBI models showed improvements over LLaVA-1.5 on some benchmarks (e.g., MMBench, SEED-Bench, POPE, TextVQA), particularly the 13B variant outperforming TFI-13B. However, when evaluated *without* detection information during inference (meaning they relied solely on ViT features), RBI models showed a noticeable performance lag compared to the original LLaVA-1.5.
- **Analysis:** Retraining the entire MLLM from scratch with continuous infusion of detection information detrimentally affects its ability to interpret features from its original image encoder (ViT). The model struggles to balance the importance of new detection information with its foundational understanding derived from image features, leading to a decline in general image comprehension tasks where fine-grained details might not be critical.

**Lesson 3: Suitable Fine-Tuning Achieves Good Trade-Offs Between ViT Features and Textual Detection Information (Fine-tuning Based Infusion - FTBI).**
- **Results:** Both 7B and 13B versions of FTBI consistently outperformed LLaVA-1.5, TFI, and RBI across almost all 10 benchmarks. FTBI models surpassed the original models by up to 3.99% (7B) and training-free models by up to 6.71% (13B). Crucially, when FTBI models were tested *without* detection information during inference, their performance was comparable to the original LLaVA-1.5, significantly outperforming RBI models under the same conditions.
- **Analysis:** Fine-tuning a pre-trained MLLM for just one additional epoch with infused textual detection information allows it to adapt effectively. This strategy preserves the MLLM's original understanding of ViT features while simultaneously enabling it to better comprehend and utilize the specially formatted detection information. It achieves a good balance, effectively filtering noise and integrating useful fine-grained details without sacrificing broader image comprehension. The appendix further confirms that the performance gain is due to the infused information, not just the extra fine-tuning epoch.

**Lesson 4: Suitable Fine-Tuning Helps MLLMs Better Understand Specially Formatted Detection Information (Detector Interchangeability).**
- **Results:** When the closed-set detector DINO was replaced with the open-set detector Grounding DINO, both TFI and FTBI models showed some performance reduction (due to Grounding DINO's lower accuracy introducing noise). However, the FTBI model consistently performed superior to the TFI model after the replacement.
- **Analysis:** This demonstrates that the fine-tuned MLLM (FTBI) learns to *understand* the format of the textual detection information itself, rather than simply relying on a specific detector's output characteristics or memorizing patterns. This "learned understanding" allows it to better mitigate noise from different or less accurate detectors and retain its performance benefits, enabling the MLLM to dynamically leverage updated or different detection models.

**Additional Insights from Appendix:**
- The findings generalize to other MLLMs like Qwen-VL and LLaVA-NeXT, validating the robustness of the FTBI strategy.
- The specific format devised for textual detection information makes the choice of the detection model less critical, as similar-performing detectors (e.g., DINO vs. YOLOv8) yield comparable MLLM enhancements.
- The FTBI strategy is robust to noisier inputs from lower-performance detectors (e.g., YOLOv5N vs. YOLOv11L), as it learns to filter out errors.
- Fine-tuning without explicit detection-related data still yields strong results, suggesting that the MLLM learns general principles of interpreting the formatted input.
- Unfreezing the visual encoder during training maintains consistency with the main findings, reinforcing the superiority of FTBI.
- Using detectors with broader category ranges (e.g., Co-DETR-LVIS) does not necessarily improve performance, as the increased scope might introduce more redundant or noisy information, underscoring the importance of filtering, which FTBI excels at.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper makes a significant contribution by empirically demonstrating the critical role of adaptive training, specifically fine-tuning, in optimizing the integration of external vision detection information into MLLMs. Its findings offer actionable insights for both researchers and developers working on multimodal AI systems:

1. **Bridging the Modality Gap More Effectively:** The research provides compelling evidence that simply injecting textualized visual details (training-free) is often suboptimal, and might even be detrimental. Instead, a targeted adaptive training phase allows MLLMs to truly "understand" and effectively leverage this supplementary information, overcoming their inherent limitations in fine-grained visual comprehension and reducing hallucinations.
2. **Practical Guidance for MLLM Enhancement:** The finding that fine-tuning with a relatively small amount of general VQA data (e.g., the MLLM's own instruction-tuning data) for just one additional epoch can yield substantial improvements is highly practical. It offers an efficient and accessible method for enhancing MLLMs without requiring massive new datasets or extensive retraining from scratch. This lowers the barrier for MLLM deployment in applications requiring detailed visual understanding.
3. **Improved Adaptability to Evolving Technologies:** The demonstration that fine-tuned MLLMs retain their benefits even when the underlying detection models are swapped highlights their learned adaptability. This means MLLMs enhanced via this strategy can seamlessly integrate with newer, more advanced object detectors or OCR models as they become available, leading to sustained performance improvements without costly re-training from scratch. This modularity is a crucial step towards robust and future-proof multimodal AI systems.
4. **Addressing the Noise Challenge:** The paper implicitly tackles the real-world challenge of imperfect detection models. By showing that fine-tuning enables MLLMs to filter out noise from less accurate detectors, it paves the way for more resilient MLLM applications that can operate effectively even with some level of erroneous input from upstream vision components.
5. **Pioneering Further Research:** As a pioneering systematic investigation into the impact of adaptive training for this specific mode of MLLM enhancement, this work opens several avenues for future research. This includes exploring more sophisticated fine-tuning techniques, designing specialized datasets for optimal adaptive training with detection information, or investigating how MLLMs can learn to dynamically request and integrate specific types of external information based on context.

In essence, this research moves beyond simply demonstrating the *possibility* of enhancing MLLMs with detection information to providing *empirical best practices* for doing so. By advocating for adaptive fine-tuning over training-free approaches, it contributes significantly to the development of more accurate, robust, and adaptable multimodal large language models capable of nuanced visual dialogue and reasoning.
