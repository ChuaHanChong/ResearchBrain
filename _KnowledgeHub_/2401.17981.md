# From Training-Free to Adaptive: Empirical Insights into MLLMs' Understanding of Detection Information

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper empirically investigates how different training strategies influence Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) comprehension of infused textual detection information from object detection and OCR models. It demonstrates that fine-tuning a pre-trained MLLM for a single epoch effectively enables it to utilize this information, outperforming training-free methods and full retraining, leading to improved performance across various multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently struggle with fine-grained visual details, leading to issues like object hallucination, miscounting, and inaccurate text identification within images.
- Integrating the capabilities of specialized vision detection models (object detection and OCR) into MLLMs is challenging, particularly optimizing the fusion of their textualized outputs.
- Prior work largely relied on 'training-free' strategies for textual detection information infusion, lacking systematic investigation into the effects of adaptive training on MLLM comprehension.

## Method

- Developed a systematic experimental framework to compare three distinct strategies for infusing textual detection information into MLLMs: ==Training-free Infusion (TFI)==, ==Retraining Based Infusion (RBI)==, and ==Fine-tuning Based Infusion (FTBI)==.
- Transformed outputs from DINO (object detection) and PaddleOCRv2 (OCR) into compressed textual formats, using central coordinates and instruction sentences, which are then supplied as supplementary input to the MLLM's language backbone.
- Evaluated LLaVA-1.5 across **10** diverse multimodal benchmarks, covering comprehensive VQA, perceptual reasoning, text recognition, and fine-grained detail understanding.

## Results

- Training-free infusion (TFI) of detection information yielded inconsistent improvements and sometimes led to performance degradation on MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5.
- Retraining-based infusion (RBI) improved performance on specific benchmarks with detection information but notably degraded the MLLM's general image comprehension when detection data was not provided.
- Fine-tuning based infusion (FTBI) consistently outperformed LLaVA-1.5, TFI, and RBI across nearly all **10** evaluated benchmarks, achieving performance increases up to **3.99%** for 7B models and up to **6.71%** for 13B models over training-free methods, while also demonstrating robustness to changes in underlying detectors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training-free infusion of raw textual detection information often introduces noise and can degrade Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) performance.
> - Retraining MLLMs from scratch with continuous detection information infusion negatively impacts their ability to interpret original image encoder features.
> - Fine-tuning a pre-trained MLLM for a single epoch provides a balanced approach, preserving original image understanding while effectively utilizing new detection information and adapting to varying detector qualities.
