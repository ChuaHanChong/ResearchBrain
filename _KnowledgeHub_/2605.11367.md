# 3D-Belief: Embodied Belief Inference via Generative 3D World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Johns Hopkins University developed 3D-Belief, a generative 3D world model that infers and updates explicit, uncertainty-aware, and semantically grounded 3D beliefs from partial observations. This model, leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting within a diffusion framework, significantly improves 3D object and room completion, and achieves higher success rates in open-vocabulary object navigation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing visual generative models primarily operate in 2D pixel space, lacking explicit 3D representations and uncertainty modeling required for embodied agents in 3D environments.
- Embodied agents struggle to maintain spatially consistent scene memory, reason about unobserved regions, and sequentially update their understanding of the 3D world under partial observability.
- Current generative world models often produce single, deterministic predictions, which are insufficient for robust decision-making when inherent uncertainty about unseen regions exists.

## Method

- The **3D-Belief** model formulates world modeling as embodied belief inference in 3D space, using 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) as its core explicit 3D scene representation.
- It employs a ==diffusion model== framework that applies noise and denoises directly to the 3D scene representation (Gaussian primitives), promoting global geometry modeling and multi-view consistency.
- The architecture integrates a U-ViT backbone with an MVS-style 3DGS predictor for geometric consistency and a CLIP-distilled semantic head for open-vocabulary semantic predictions of 3D regions.

## Results

- 3D-Belief achieved superior 2D visual quality, outperforming baselines in both scene memory (e.g., PSNR 28.81 in AI2-THOR) and scene imagination (e.g., FID 47.24 in AI2-THOR).
- It consistently surpassed baselines on the new 3D-CORE benchmark for 3D object and room completion, demonstrating improved geometry, semantics (e.g., BEV IoU of 0.484 for object completion at **55%** visibility), and long-horizon consistency (e.g., LPIPS 0.123 for object permanence).
- The model significantly enhanced open-vocabulary object navigation performance, achieving higher success rates (**59.17%** in simulation, **55.56%** in real-world) and efficiency compared to reconstruction, 2D imagination, and VLM-based baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D scene representations, such as 3D Gaussian Splatting, provide a strong prior for cross-view consistency and reduce drift compared to 2D-only generative models, improving performance in embodied tasks.
> - Modeling uncertainty through multi-hypothesis belief sampling is crucial for robust planning in partially observable environments, leading to improved task success.
> - Directly integrating open-vocabulary semantic information into the 3D belief state allows for efficient, task-relevant reasoning without continuous reliance on external Large Vision-Language Models during inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual generative models has shown progress in developing "world models" capable of predicting future visual frames or synthesizing novel views from existing observations. Approaches such as video diffusion models and view-synthesis methods focus on generating visually realistic outputs, often in pixel or latent space. These models can render unobserved parts of environments or changes induced by agent actions, demonstrating capabilities in visual prediction.

However, a recognized gap exists between these visually-oriented world models and the requirements of embodied agents operating in 3D environments under partial observability. Existing models primarily emphasize visual fidelity rather than the structured, uncertainty-aware representations necessary for decision-making in interactive tasks. Specifically, current methods often lack:
- **Explicit 3D representations:** Many models operate in 2D pixel space, requiring separate reconstruction modules to convert predictions into actionable 3D structures.
- **Uncertainty modeling in 3D:** Standard approaches typically produce a single, deterministic prediction, which is insufficient for handling the inherent uncertainty of unobserved regions in partially known environments.
- **Sequential belief updating:** While some models can process streaming observations, they often do not maintain an evolving, consistent 3D belief state that incorporates new evidence over time.
- **Semantic grounding:** Generative world models have largely focused on geometric and appearance generation, with limited capabilities for producing open-vocabulary semantic predictions of unobserved 3D regions that are crucial for task-relevant reasoning and planning.

This work positions itself to address these limitations by proposing "world modeling as embodied belief inference in 3D space." It aims to bridge the gap by developing a generative 3D world model that not only predicts visual appearances but also infers and updates explicit, actionable, and uncertainty-aware 3D beliefs of the environment, including unobserved regions, to support robust embodied agents. The authors highlight that existing methods typically cover only a subset of these identified capabilities, as detailed in their appendix.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a generative 3D world model capable of supporting embodied belief inference, enabling agents to operate effectively under partial observability in 3D environments. This objective is motivated by the recognized inadequacy of current world models, which primarily focus on visual realism or short-horizon prediction, for the complex reasoning and planning required by embodied agents.

To achieve this, the authors identify four key capabilities that a generative 3D belief model must possess:

1. **Spatially Consistent Scene Memory:** An embodied agent needs to build and maintain a persistent, geometrically and semantically consistent representation of the 3D world, particularly the regions it has already observed. This memory should ensure "object permanence," meaning observed objects and structures remain stable in the belief even when revisited or temporarily unobserved. This capability is crucial for long-horizon exploration and coherent understanding of the environment.

2. **Multi-hypothesis Belief Sampling:** Due to partial observability, multiple plausible configurations of the unobserved 3D world may exist. A robust belief model should not output a single, deterministic prediction but rather allow for sampling diverse hypotheses about the unseen parts of the environment. This capacity enables the agent to reason about uncertainty and consider various possibilities for effective planning.

3. **Sequential Belief Updating:** Embodied agents continuously acquire new observations as they move through an environment. The belief model must be able to sequentially update its internal representation of the 3D world, integrating new sensory data with prior beliefs while maintaining consistency with all historical observations. This ensures the agent's understanding of the world evolves dynamically and accurately.

4. **Semantically Informed Prediction:** For task-driven reasoning and planning, the agent's belief about the 3D world needs to contain semantic information. This means the model should not only predict geometry and appearance but also provide direct semantic predictions (e.g., object categories, room types) for both observed and unobserved regions. This allows agents to perform open-vocabulary queries and drive task-specific behaviors, such as searching for a particular object.

The motivation for integrating these capabilities stems from the demands of real-world embodied tasks like object navigation. Agents in unfamiliar environments must infer unseen structures, understand object locations, and update their world knowledge as they explore. A model that explicitly represents and updates these 3D beliefs, including uncertainties and semantics, would enable more robust, efficient, and intelligent decision-making compared to systems relying solely on 2D visual predictions or explicit 3D reconstructions of only observed areas.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, 3D-Belief, is a generative 3D world model designed to infer and sequentially update explicit, actionable 3D beliefs from partial egocentric observations. It is built upon a diffusion model framework and utilizes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its core 3D scene representation.

#### 3.1. Formulation

3D-Belief formalizes belief inference within the context of partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs). The belief `b(s_t)` represents the distribution of the true state `s_t` conditioned on past observations `o_{1:t}`. The model aims to predict and update a 3D representation `z_t = φ(s_t)` of the scene. This representation `z_t` is chosen to preserve crucial 3D structures and semantic information necessary for embodied tasks and to be parametrically suitable for model training.

Specifically, `z_t` is defined as a set of 3D Gaussian primitives `{g_k}^K_{k=1}`, where each primitive `g_k` includes parameters for mean (position `μ_k`), covariance (shape `Σ_k`), opacity (`α_k`), spherical harmonic (SH) appearance (`S_k`), and a semantic embedding (`e_k`). This explicit 3DGS representation is favored for its ability to carry semantic features and its fast differentiable rendering capabilities. The `z_t` is conceptually split into `z_t^o` (observed Gaussians) and `z_t^i` (imagined Gaussians).

The sequential belief update is formulated autoregressively: `z_{t+1} ~ p(z_{t+1} | o_{t+1}, z_t^o)`. This implies that `z_t^o` is expanded with new observations `o_{t+1}` to form `z_{t+1}^o`, while `z_t^i` is entirely re-imagined as `z_{t+1}^i` to account for potential conflicts with new evidence. This design facilitates training over short horizons and enables scaling to long-horizon planning by maintaining a constant per-step computational cost.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The 3D-Belief model consists of a shared U-ViT backbone that processes input images and two specialized heads:
- **3DGS Predictor:** For geometric consistency and 3D structure prediction, an MVS-style (Multi-View Stereo) 3DGS predictor is used. This predictor incorporates a multi-view Transformer and a cost-volume module. The cost volume stores cross-view feature matching scores over discretized depth candidates, guiding the prediction of depth and Gaussian parameters. These parameters are then lifted into 3D Gaussian primitives for rendering.
- **Semantic Head:** A lightweight semantic head projects features from the U-ViT backbone into per-pixel semantic maps. This head is trained through distillation from CLIP-style embeddings, enabling open-vocabulary text-based querying of the generated 3D scene representation at inference time.

#### 3.3. Diffusion Training

Unlike models that perform pixel-space diffusion frame-by-frame, 3D-Belief adopts a scene-level 3D diffusion approach. Diffusion is applied directly to the entire 3D scene representation, i.e., the Gaussian primitives `z`. This promotes global geometry modeling and multi-view consistency.

The training involves paired context images `o_{ctxt}` and target images `o_{trgt}`. The forward diffusion process adds noise to `o_{trgt}`. In the reverse process, the model learns to reconstruct a denoised scene `z_{τ-1}` conditioned on `o_{ctxt}`, the noisy `o_{trgt}` at diffusion time `τ`, and the respective camera parameters. This `z_{τ-1}` is then rendered into observation space (`o_{trgt, τ-1}`) using the 3DGS renderer `G(·)`. The neural network `Φ_θ` (the U-ViT backbone and its heads) is trained to predict `z_{τ-1}`.

#### 3.4. Training Objective

The model `Φ_θ` is trained with a composite loss function involving paired context and target observations:
- **RGB Loss (`L_rgb`):** An L2 loss is applied between the rendered RGB image and the ground-truth RGB image for both target and context views.
- **Semantic Loss (`L_sem`):** This loss aligns the rendered semantic feature map with features extracted from image patches of the ground-truth observations. Patches are sampled from the ground-truth images, and their features are computed using a frozen CLIP image encoder. The semantic feature at the corresponding pixel in the rendered semantic map is then supervised against these CLIP features.
- **Depth Loss (`L_depth`, optional):** When ground-truth depth maps are available, a masked L1 loss is applied between the rendered depth and the ground-truth depth, handling missing or invalid depth values.

The total loss is a weighted sum of these components, with additional feature-level alignment losses for both prediction-to-ground-truth and prediction-to-pretrained VGGT features, along with a depth smoothness loss as regularization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 3D-Belief encompassed three primary areas: 2D visual quality of belief prediction, 3D object and scene imagination, and belief-guided planning for object navigation tasks.

#### 4.1. Experiment 1: 2D Visual Quality of Belief Prediction

This experiment evaluated 3D-Belief's ability to faithfully memorize observed regions (scene memory) and plausibly imagine unobserved spaces (scene imagination) through visual rendering.

- **Evaluation Protocol:**
 - **Scene Memory (observed regions):** Models were conditioned on both ends of a trajectory to predict intermediate novel views. Metrics: LPIPS (lower is better), PSNR (higher is better), SSIM (higher is better).
 - **Scene Imagination (unobserved regions):** Models were conditioned only on the starting frame to generate views beyond the observed field of view. Metrics: FID (lower is better), FVD (lower is better).
- **Domains:** AI2-THOR (synthetic embodied environments) and RealEstate10K (real-world visual prediction benchmark).
- **Baselines:** DFoT, NWM (AI2-THOR); DFoT, GEN3C (RealEstate10K).
- **Results:**
 - **AI2-THOR:** 3D-Belief consistently outperformed baselines across both scene memory (PSNR 28.81, SSIM 0.928, LPIPS 0.0502) and scene imagination (FID 47.24, FVD 271.8).
 - **RealEstate10K:** 3D-Belief achieved superior PSNR (24.878), SSIM (0.8432), LPIPS (0.0663), and FVD (50.993), with competitive FID (26.465), compared to pretrained baselines.
 - **Qualitative Observations:** Baselines exhibited semantic drift and geometric distortion under viewpoint changes, failing to preserve plausible structures (e.g., open doors, consistent grass texture). 3D-Belief maintained semantic and scene consistency and better preserved pose alignment and rigid scene structure due to its explicit 3D belief representation.

#### 4.2. Experiment 2: 3D Object and Scene Imagination (3D-CORE Benchmark)

This experiment introduced 3D-CORE (3D COntextual REasoning), a benchmark designed to evaluate consistent 3D belief formation under partial observability, focusing on spatial expansion, semantic grounding in 3D, and long-horizon consistency.

- **Benchmark Tasks:**
 - **Object Completion (233 tasks):** Model completes 3D geometry and appearance of partially visible target objects. Metrics: BEV IoU, 3D IoU, Chamfer distance, SigLIP similarity, Recognition.
 - **Room Completion (263 tasks):** Model predicts unseen layout and semantics of a room from a single egocentric view. Metrics: Object Prediction F1, Occupancy Accuracy, Occupancy IoU.
 - **Object Permanence (474 tasks):** Model's 3D belief stability is tested over trajectories with large viewpoint changes returning to the start. Metrics: LPIPS, SigLIP similarity.
- **Baseline:** DFoT-VGGT (an "imagination-then-lift" baseline combining DFoT for 2D generation and VGGT for 3D reconstruction).
- **Results:** 3D-Belief consistently outperformed DFoT-VGGT across all 3D-CORE tasks.
 - **Object Completion:** 3D-Belief showed improved geometry and semantics across visibility levels, with higher BEV/3D IoU, lower Chamfer distance, and better SigLIP similarity and VLM recognition. For 55% visibility, 3D-Belief achieved BEV IoU of 0.484 (vs. 0.362 for baseline) and 3D IoU of 0.318 (vs. 0.243).
 - **Room Completion:** 3D-Belief produced substantially more accurate occupancy beliefs (Occ. Acc. 0.900 vs. 0.252; Occ. IoU 0.442 vs. 0.110), indicating stronger spatial belief estimation.
 - **Object Permanence:** 3D-Belief demonstrated better long-horizon consistency, achieving higher perceptual (LPIPS 0.123 vs. 0.555) and semantic (SigLIP 0.978 vs. 0.907) consistency when revisiting viewpoints, suggesting reduced drift and more stable object identity.

#### 4.3. Experiment 3: Belief-Guided Planning with Inference-Time Scaling (Object Navigation)

This experiment assessed 3D-Belief's utility as a mental 3D world model for embodied planning in open-vocabulary object navigation tasks.

- **Approach:** At each step, the agent updates its 3D belief, samples multiple plausible 3D hypotheses, evaluates candidate paths by rendering imagined observations along them, and scores rollouts using open-vocabulary semantic queries on the imagined belief.
- **Environments:** AI2-THOR simulator (135 unseen houses) and a real-world mobile manipulator (Hello Robot Stretch) in a mock apartment.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR%), Success-weighted by Path Length (SPL%), Success-weighted by Episode Length (SEL%), Inference Time (s/step), Token Cost (/step).
- **Baselines:**
 - **3D Reconstruction:** VGGT (with frontier-based or VLM-driven goal selection).
 - **Lifted 2D Imagination:** DFoT-VGGT, NWM-VGGT (using video prediction followed by 3D reconstruction).
 - **VLM Agents:** GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.0, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (end-to-end VLM agents).
 - **Policy Model:** SPOC (learned navigation policy).
- **Results (Simulation):** 3D-Belief consistently outperformed all baseline categories. It achieved the highest SR (59.17%), SPL (39.07%), and SEL (40.24%), indicating greater success and efficiency. VLM agents like Gemini 3.0 had competitive SR (45.00%) but higher token costs (7512.70 vs. 0 for 3D-Belief) and less efficient paths (SPL 37.81%).
- **Results (Real World):** 3D-Belief maintained its superior performance over the Gemini 3.0 agent, achieving a higher SR (55.56% vs. 23.08%) and improved efficiency (SEL 35.91% vs. 13.55%). This demonstrated the transferability and robustness of explicit, online-updatable 3D beliefs to real-world conditions.
- **Ablation Studies (Appendix A3.2.2):** Removing geometry from 3D-Belief significantly reduced performance (SR dropped from 45.83% to 17.50%). Using a single hypothesis instead of multi-hypothesis sampling also led to a performance drop (SR from 45.83% to 35.14%), indicating the importance of modeling uncertainty.
- **Discussion on 2D Rendering and Embodied Task Performance (Appendix A5):** The explicit 3D representation in 3D-Belief acts as a strong prior for cross-view consistency, reducing drift and improving perceptual/distributional metrics in 2D rendering. For embodied tasks, maintaining spatially consistent scene memory, multi-hypothesis sampling, and internal semantic prediction are identified as key factors for superior performance and reduced deployment costs compared to external VLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a conceptual framework for embodied belief inference through generative 3D world modeling, distinguishing it from prior work that primarily focuses on visual prediction or 2D latent space generation. The work emphasizes the necessity of explicit, uncertainty-aware, and semantically grounded 3D representations for embodied agents operating under partial observability.

The main contributions include:
1. **A novel conceptual framework:** This framework redefines world modeling for embodied agents, prioritizing explicit 3D belief inference, sequential updating, uncertainty modeling, and semantic grounding.
2. **The 3D-Belief model:** This is a generative 3D world model that instantiates the proposed framework. It predicts unseen regions in an explicit 3D representation (3D Gaussian Splatting) from partial observations and updates this belief online. Its architecture integrates a U-ViT backbone, an MVS-style 3DGS predictor, and a CLIP-distilled semantic head, enabling the generation of actionable, semantic, and uncertainty-aware 3D scene beliefs.
3. **The 3D-CORE benchmark:** This new benchmark provides a diagnostic tool for evaluating object- and scene-level 3D imagination and contextual reasoning capabilities, directly addressing limitations of existing metrics focused on 2D visual fidelity.
4. **Comprehensive evaluations:** The experiments demonstrate that 3D-Belief improves upon existing generative world models. It yields superior 2D visual quality for both scene memorization and imagination, outperforms baselines in explicit 3D scene imagination tasks on the 3D-CORE benchmark, and significantly enhances downstream open-vocabulary object navigation performance in both simulated and real-world environments.

The potential impact of 3D-Belief lies in its capacity to enable more robust and intelligent embodied agents. By providing a coherent and queryable 3D belief state that explicitly represents unobserved regions and associated uncertainties, it can facilitate better decision-making for tasks such as:
- **Assistive robotics:** Helping robots navigate and interact in complex, partially known home or care environments.
- **Object search and retrieval:** Allowing robots to efficiently locate target objects, even if initially occluded, by reasoning about plausible unseen structures.
- **Warehouse inspection and logistics:** Improving autonomous agents' ability to understand and navigate dynamic, large-scale industrial settings.

The explicit 3D representation and integrated semantics may reduce reliance on external Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for online semantic querying during planning, potentially leading to lower computational costs and faster inference in deployed systems.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that the current 3D-Belief model assumes a static world, which restricts its applicability to dynamic environments. Future work is suggested to incorporate dynamic world modeling to support a broader range of embodied tasks. Another direction is to enhance the controllability of 3D imagination by conditioning hypothesis sampling on high-level guidance, such as language descriptions or scene graphs of desired imagined 3D worlds.

**Broader Impacts:**
The ability to infer and predict unobserved regions in 3D raises considerations regarding safety and privacy. Plausible but incorrect beliefs could lead to unsafe robot behaviors if not handled carefully, emphasizing the need for conservative planning and collision checking in practical deployments. Additionally, explicit 3D scene representations in indoor settings may capture sensitive information, necessitating privacy-preserving data handling and clear distinctions between observed evidence and imagined scene completions. The work is presented as a research step, not a deployment-ready system, highlighting the ongoing need for ethical considerations in embodied AI development.
