# $μ$VLA: On Recurrent Memory for Partially Observable Manipulation in VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> μVLA introduces a minimal recurrent memory mechanism into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, achieving an average success rate of 0.84 on partially observable MIKASA-Robo training tasks, a substantial increase from 0.42 for the memoryless baseline. This fine-tuning approach integrates learnable memory tokens without degrading performance on fully observable LIBERO benchmarks, providing insights into recurrence's isolated contribution to memory-dependent robotic control.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often assume full observability, struggling with tasks where critical information is transient, occluded, or established in the past.
- Existing memory-augmented VLA models combine multiple complex mechanisms (recurrence, retrieval, hierarchical memory), making it difficult to isolate and understand the specific contribution of recurrence.
- A lack of controlled studies to calibrate the fundamental capabilities and limitations of minimal, in-backbone recurrence within robust pretrained VLA architectures.

## Method

- Introduced **μVLA** by minimally inserting learnable ==memory tokens== into a pretrained OpenVLA-OFT backbone, between proprioception and language inputs.
- Developed an episodic dataloader to maintain temporal integrity for recurrent state propagation during training and utilized Truncated Backpropagation Through Time (TBPTT) for cross-step gradient flow.
- Employed an attention-mask guard to prevent memory tokens from trivially copying predicted actions, forcing them to encode relevant observations, and used receding-horizon inference for continuous memory updates.

## Results

- Increased average success rate on five MIKASA-Robo training tasks from **0.42** (memoryless baseline) to **0.84** for the optimal μVLA variant (m=64, K=2).
- Achieved **96.2%** average success on the fully observable LIBERO benchmark, demonstrating that the recurrent channel does not degrade performance in Markovian settings.
- Inference-time diagnostics revealed that memory tokens exhibit phase-conditioned dynamics, with state changes aligning with task transitions, and attention concentrating on cue regions when visible.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimal recurrent memory can be effectively added via fine-tuning to a memoryless VLA, yielding substantial performance improvements on partially observable tasks without retraining the full backbone.
> - The optimal Truncated Backpropagation Through Time (TBPTT) length for recurrent training exhibits a U-shaped performance curve, with K=2 providing the best balance for credit assignment across timesteps.
> - While effective for matched memory semantics, the minimal in-backbone recurrence approach shows limitations in generalizing to held-out tasks with conceptually novel memory requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models represent a category of robotic control systems that interpret multimodal sequences of visual observations, language instructions, and proprioception to predict future actions. A fundamental assumption in many VLA models is that the current observation provides sufficient information for decision-making. This assumption, however, proves inadequate in scenarios characterized by partial observability, where critical information for task completion may be transient, occluded, or established at earlier stages of an episode and is therefore not immediately visible.

The broader research landscape for addressing partial observability in VLAs encompasses several approaches. One common method involves extending the input with a finite history, such as concatenating multiple frames or reusing cached activations and KV states. While this increases the immediate context, it remains bounded and lacks a mechanism to selectively retain crucial information over longer periods. A second category introduces external memory and retrieval systems, where past observations are stored and accessed as needed. These systems offer flexibility but introduce additional components and necessitate a separate policy for memory management. A third approach utilizes recurrence, where a latent state is propagated across timesteps, allowing the model to learn which information to retain.

The challenge identified by the authors within this landscape is that existing memory-augmented VLA models frequently integrate multiple mechanisms simultaneously—such as recurrence, retrieval, hierarchical memory, compression modules, auxiliary objectives, or task-specific architectural changes. This entanglement complicates the isolation and assessment of the individual contribution of recurrence itself to improved performance. This paper aims to address this gap by conducting a controlled study focused solely on recurrence within a robust, pretrained VLA backbone. It seeks to delineate the specific capabilities and limitations of minimal in-backbone recurrence, thereby providing a clearer understanding of its role as a fundamental component in handling partial observability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a controlled isolation study of recurrence within a strong, pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model backbone. The authors explicitly frame their work as an "intervention study" rather than the proposal of a new foundation-model architecture. This framing underscores their intent to calibrate the capabilities and identify the limits of a minimal, in-backbone recurrent mechanism.

The motivation for this focused investigation stems from several observations regarding the current state of memory-augmented VLA research:
1. **Entangled Contributions:** Previous works incorporating memory into VLAs often combine recurrence with other complex components, such as external retrieval mechanisms, hierarchical memory structures, auxiliary loss functions, or significant architectural modifications. This multi-component approach makes it difficult to ascertain whether observed performance gains are attributable to recurrence itself or to the synergistic or individual effects of the other integrated modules. The authors contend that without isolating recurrence, its specific contribution to solving partially observable tasks remains unclear.
2. **Need for Fundamental Understanding:** By isolating recurrence, the researchers aim to gain a fundamental understanding of what this primitive mechanism can achieve on its own when integrated into a standard transformer architecture. This includes identifying the conditions under which minimal recurrence is sufficient for handling partial observability and the regimes where more elaborate memory structures become necessary.
3. **Efficiency and Simplicity:** The proposed recurrent formulation is intentionally minimal: it introduces a small set of learnable memory tokens without auxiliary losses or architectural additions. A key motivation is to assess whether such a computationally light and architecturally simple modification can yield substantial improvements in partially observable tasks, especially when introduced at the fine-tuning stage of an already pretrained model.
4. **Calibration of Capability Envelope:** The study seeks to precisely calibrate the "capability envelope" of minimal in-backbone recurrence. This involves systematically varying parameters like memory width, backpropagation through time (TBPTT) length, and memory update rules to understand their impact on performance across different types of memory-dependent tasks. This calibration provides a baseline for future work, enabling researchers to better design and attribute gains from more complex memory systems.
5. **Robustness and Generalization:** A further motivation is to evaluate how such a recurrent system behaves not only on training tasks but also on held-out tasks with both matched and novel memory semantics. This investigation helps determine the extent to which learned recurrent behaviors generalize and whether they introduce any performance regressions on fully observable (Markovian) tasks, thus assessing the versatility of the approach.

In essence, the core motivation is to disentangle the impact of recurrence from other factors, providing a clear empirical basis for understanding its inherent strengths and limitations in VLA models for robotic manipulation under partial observability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce μVLA, a family of OpenVLA-OFT variants, designed to systematically investigate the contribution of recurrent memory. The approach is characterized by its minimal intervention and controlled experimental design.

#### 3.1. OpenVLA-OFT Backbone

The work builds upon OpenVLA-OFT, a fine-tuned version of OpenVLA. This backbone comprises a dual SigLIP/DINOv2 encoder paired with a Llama-2-7B model. It processes vision, language, and proprioception into a single bidirectionally-attended sequence and predicts an 8-step chunk of continuous actions using an L1 regression head.

#### 3.2. Memory Tokens and Recurrence

- **Insertion:** `m` learnable memory tokens (`M_t ∈ R^(m×d)`) are inserted into the OpenVLA-OFT input sequence, specifically between the PROPRIO and TEXT tokens: `[BOS] [VIS] [PROPRIO] [M_t] [TEXT] [ACT] [STOP]`. `d` represents the backbone's hidden dimension.
- **Initialization:** At the beginning of each episode (`t=0`), memory tokens are initialized from `M_init`, a shared learnable parameter.
- **Recurrent Update:** For subsequent timesteps (`t ≥ 1`), `M_t` is derived from the hidden states at the memory token positions (`M_{t-1}`) from the previous step's forward pass. This means a single forward pass simultaneously reads the previous memory state and computes the updated memory state for the next step.
- **Hyperparameter:** The memory width `m` is a variable hyperparameter in the μVLA family.

#### 3.3. Attention-Mask Guard

To prevent a degenerate solution where memory tokens could trivially copy predicted actions (since action tokens are part of the input sequence), a modification is made to the attention mask. The context-to-action block of the attention mask is set to zero. This ensures that memory tokens (and other prefix tokens) cannot attend to the action region, thereby forcing the recurrent state to encode task-relevant information from visual observations, proprioception, and language instructions. The action region, however, retains its ability to read the full context.

#### 3.4. Episodic Dataloader

The standard VLA training paradigm often shuffles individual (observation, action chunk) pairs, disrupting temporal order. To accommodate recurrence, the authors implement a "round-robin episodic dataloader."
- **Mechanism:** It maintains `B` independent streams (one per batch slot), each processing a single episode step-by-step. When an episode ends in a stream, a new episode is sampled.
- **Temporal Integrity:** Steps are yielded in a fixed round-robin order, ensuring that consecutive batches correctly advance each stream by one step, thereby preserving the temporal context necessary for recurrent state propagation.
- **Memory Reset:** A `is_first` boolean flag signals episode boundaries, allowing only the relevant streams' memory states to be reset to `M_init`.

#### 3.5. Training Protocol and Write Rules

- **Initialization and Fine-tuning:** μVLA models are initialized from a released OpenVLA checkpoint and fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapters of rank 32 on the backbone, action head, memory-position embeddings, and `M_init`.
- **Loss Function:** Training uses only the standard L1 regression loss for action prediction, with no auxiliary objectives.
- **Recurrent Write Rules:**
 - **Truncated Backpropagation Through Time (TBPTT):** The model accumulates the L1 chunk loss over `K` consecutive environment steps without detaching the memory chain. A single backward pass is then performed through this `K`-step recurrent graph. Memory is detached only at the truncation boundary, allowing gradients to flow across steps. `K` is a hyperparameter (tested at 1, 2, 8).
 - **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** This variant replaces end-to-end backpropagation through time with a detached low-pass filter: `M_{t+1} = α M'_t.DETACH + (1 − α) M_t.DETACH`. `M'_t` is the memory readout from the current forward pass. Both operands are detached, meaning gradients are local to a single step.

#### 3.6. Receding-Horizon Inference

Unlike standard OpenVLA-OFT deployment with open-loop `H`-step action chunking, μVLA employs receding-horizon control. At every environment step, the model is re-queried, predicts an `H`-step action chunk, but executes only the first action (`a^(t)_t`). This ensures the memory state is updated at every environment step, aligning inference with training cadence and enabling the capture of transient, decision-relevant cues that might otherwise be missed within a longer chunk.

#### 3.7. Benchmarks

- **MIKASA-Robo:** A tabletop manipulation benchmark designed for partial observability. Tasks fall into cue-recall, occlusion, and sequential/predictive memory categories. Five tasks were used for multi-task training, and 18 held-out environments (classified by "matched" and "novel" memory semantics) were used for generalization evaluation.
- **LIBERO:** A fully observable control suite used to assess if the recurrent channel introduced any performance regression in a Markovian setting.

#### 3.8. Diagnostics

To understand the internal mechanisms of the trained memory, the authors utilized several inference-time diagnostics:
- **Representation Dynamics:** Analyzing changes in memory token states (e.g., cosine distance between `M_t` and `M_{t-1}`).
- **Attention Rollouts:** Visualizing attention weights from memory tokens to visual observations.
- **Causal Noise and Freeze-First Interventions:** Introducing noise to or freezing the memory state to assess its functional importance.
- **Chunked-Inference and Phase-Length Sweeps:** Varying inference parameters to probe robustness to different temporal structures.
- **OOD Cue-Identity Tests:** Evaluating generalization to unseen cue properties.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research systematically evaluates μVLA variants across MIKASA-Robo and LIBERO benchmarks, addressing four specific research questions (RQs).

#### 4.1. RQ1: Can recurrent memory be added at the fine-tuning stage to a memoryless VLA pretrained on memoryless tasks, without retraining the backbone from scratch?

- **Positive Confirmation:** The study demonstrates that adding recurrent memory tokens through fine-tuning significantly improves performance on partially observable tasks. The average success rate (SR) on the five MIKASA-Robo training tasks increased from 0.42 (OpenVLA-OFT, original setup) and 0.48 (OpenVLA-OFT with episodic dataloader) to 0.84 for the strongest μVLA variant (m=64, K=2).
- **Impact of Episodic Dataloader:** Switching to the episodic dataloader alone, without recurrence (m=0), contributed a +6 percentage point (pp) increase to the training average (0.42 to 0.48), indicating its importance for maintaining temporal context.
- **Memory Bandwidth:** Increasing memory width from m=1 to m=64 (at K=8) provided an additional +3 pp gain, suggesting that while a single recurrent token carries useful information, the dominant factor for cue-recall is the recurrent optimization regime itself.
- **Active Memory Use:** Causal interventions confirmed that the policy actively utilizes the recurrent state during inference; replacing memory with noise sharply degraded SR on cue-recall tasks.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Does end-to-end fine-tuning of the recurrence benefit from cross-step gradient flow — TBPTT versus a per-step detach (K=1) versus a learning-free EMA write — and if so, what truncation length K is right?

- **U-shaped Performance for TBPTT:** Performance with varying TBPTT length `K` exhibited a U-shaped curve. K=2 achieved the highest average SR on training tasks (0.84), significantly outperforming K=1 (0.57) and K=8 (0.57). For cue-recall tasks like RememberColor5, K=2 reached 0.93 SR, compared to 0.35 at K=1 and 0.40 at K=8.
- **Interpretation of K Effects:**
 - **K=1 (No cross-step gradients):** The recurrent write receives no future supervision, leading to memory functioning primarily as a passive encoding of the current observation.
 - **K=8 (Long gradient chains):** Propagating gradients through extended recurrent chains in a large pretrained backbone appears to result in a more diffuse and lower-resolution recurrent representation.
 - **K=2 (Optimal Balance):** This length appears to strike a balance between sufficient credit assignment across timesteps and managing the complexity of gradient propagation, achieving a "sweet spot."
- **EMA Write Performance:** The EMA (Exponential Moving Average) write rule generally tracked the K=8 performance on the training average but lagged significantly behind K=2 on cue-recall tasks (e.g., RememberColor5: 0.44 for EMA vs. 0.93 for K=2), indicating that detached low-pass updates are insufficient for precise memory retention.

#### 4.3. RQ3: How does the trained memory behave at inference time on (a) held-out tasks that share the memory semantics of a training task versus (b) held-out tasks whose memory semantics conceptually differs from the training mixture?

- **Matched Memory Semantics (Held-out):** On held-out tasks with memory requirements similar to the training set, the performance gains transferred. The K=2 variant achieved an average SR of 0.23, compared to 0.07 for the memoryless OpenVLA-OFT (episodic). The first-frame oracle (which sees the initial observation) performed comparably on cue-recall tasks where the cue is static, but recurrence was superior on dynamic tasks like TakeItBack (0.99 vs 0.94 for oracle).
- **Novel Memory Semantics (Held-out):** On tasks with memory requirements conceptually different from the training mixture (e.g., RememberShape family, Rotate family), the recurrent family's performance remained close to the memoryless baselines. K=2 achieved 0.16 SR, while other recurrent variants clustered around 0.09–0.11, indicating limitations in generalization to entirely new memory structures with minimal in-backbone recurrence.
- **Attention Mask Guard Validation:** Removing the action-copy guard (full attention mask) degraded cue-recall performance (e.g., RememberColor5: 0.44 to 0.25) but improved motor-heavy environments. This supports the hypothesis that the guard prevents the recurrent state from degenerating into copying predicted actions.
- **Generalization Probes:**
 - **Phase-length Robustness:** K=2 showed the strongest in-distribution recall but degraded faster when cue-to-action delays exceeded the training range. K=8 exhibited lower absolute SR but maintained greater stability across longer delays.
 - **OOD Cue-Identity:** K=2 was the most robust to out-of-distribution color swaps, retaining 0.48 SR even with all colors replaced, suggesting a partially abstract cue encoding rather than mere memorization.

#### 4.4. RQ4: Does adding the recurrent channel introduce performance degradation on Markovian (MDP) tasks where memory is not required?

- **No Degradation on LIBERO:** On the fully observable LIBERO benchmark, μVLA (m=64, K=8) achieved 96.2% average success, which matches or exceeds the strongest non-recurrent VLA baselines (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%, MemoryVLA at 96.5%). This indicates that the recurrent channel does not negatively impact the host policy's performance in fully observable settings.
- **Importance of Receding-Horizon Inference:** The high LIBERO performance relies on receding-horizon inference; evaluating the same checkpoint with open-loop H-step chunked execution resulted in a collapse of performance on certain tasks (e.g., Long-10 from 95.8% to 5.4%), highlighting the critical alignment between memory update cadence during training and inference.

#### 4.5. Memory Diagnostics

- **Representation Dynamics:** The recurrent state of K=2 (m=64) exhibited phase-conditioned dynamics, with distinct spikes in the cosine distance between `M_t` and `M_{t-1}` aligning with task phase transitions (e.g., cue appearance/disappearance, task switches). This indicates that memory actively reorganizes based on salient environmental changes.
- **Attention Rollouts:** Visualizations showed memory attention concentrating on cue regions when visible and then diffusing after occlusion, consistent with its role in carrying latent information. This dynamic suggests that recurrent state changes could potentially act as a learned key-frame signal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a focused investigation into the capabilities of recurrence within Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, offering several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Isolation of Recurrence's Contribution:** The primary significance lies in providing, to the authors' knowledge, the first controlled isolation study of recurrence in a pretrained VLA backbone. By systematically removing confounding factors such as auxiliary losses, retrieval mechanisms, hierarchical memory, or architectural modifications, the study directly quantifies the contribution of minimal in-backbone recurrence. This disentanglement is crucial for a clearer understanding of fundamental VLA capabilities.

2. **Demonstrated Efficacy in Partial Observability:** The findings establish that even a small, learnable recurrent state, integrated minimally into a transformer's self-attention mechanism, can substantially improve performance on partially observable manipulation tasks. The significant increase in success rates on MIKASA-Robo training tasks (from 0.42 to 0.84) and transfer to matched held-out tasks (from 0.07 to 0.23) indicates that recurrence is a potent and practical ingredient for memory-dependent control.

3. **Benign Integration into Existing Architectures:** The successful fine-tuning of recurrence onto a pretrained, memoryless VLA backbone (OpenVLA-OFT) using LoRA, without retraining the entire backbone, demonstrates an efficient and accessible method for incorporating memory. Furthermore, the results on LIBERO show that this recurrent augmentation does not degrade performance on fully observable (Markovian) tasks, highlighting the versatility and robustness of the approach.

4. **Calibration of Capability Envelope and Identified Limits:** The study meticulously calibrates the performance of recurrence across different memory widths and TBPTT lengths, identifying optimal configurations (e.g., K=2) and explaining the trade-offs involved (e.g., between credit assignment and gradient propagation length). It also clearly delineates the limits of this minimal approach, particularly regarding generalization to novel memory semantics, where performance remains near baseline. This calibration provides a valuable reference point for future research, indicating when minimal recurrence might suffice and when more structured or complex memory systems are warranted.

5. **Diagnostic Tools for Understanding Memory:** The development and application of various diagnostic tools (representation dynamics, attention rollouts, causal interventions) offer insights into how the recurrent state is used by the model. Observing phase-conditioned memory dynamics and targeted attention on relevant cues provides empirical evidence of learned memory mechanisms, potentially guiding the design of more interpretable and controllable memory systems.

6. **Implications for Future VLA Design:** The research suggests that recurrence itself, as a foundational mechanism for retaining past information, is an "understudied ingredient" in VLA systems when isolated. This implies that future work could prioritize enhancing recurrence mechanisms or combining them intelligently with other components, rather than solely focusing on external or complex memory architectures from the outset. It provides a strong argument for re-evaluating the role of intrinsic, in-backbone memory.

In summary, this work provides empirical evidence for the significant and often underappreciated role of simple recurrent memory in improving VLA performance under partial observability, while also precisely identifying its current boundaries. It encourages a more focused exploration of recurrence as a core mechanism for building capable and robust robotic agents.
