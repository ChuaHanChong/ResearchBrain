# Pseudo-Unification: Entropy Probing Reveals Divergent Information Patterns in Unified Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MMLab@HKUST developed an information-theoretic framework to diagnose "pseudo-unification" in multimodal models, revealing that these models exhibit modality-asymmetric encoding and pattern-split generative responses, which hinders true cross-modal synergy. The analysis of ten state-of-the-art models demonstrated that most UMMs maintain separate creative (text) and fidelity (image) generation patterns.

## Problem

- Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) often fail to consistently transfer reasoning capabilities from language to image synthesis, resulting in divergent response behaviors across modalities, termed "pseudo-unification."
- Existing UMM evaluation methods primarily assess performance with task-specific datasets but lack model-internal insights into *why* synergy is elusive or why certain models unify better.
- Current model-internal analyses frequently overlook crucial prompt-response dependencies, which are essential for understanding generative behavior and inconsistencies in UMMs.

## Method

- An ==information-theoretic probing framework== was introduced to analyze internal information flow in UMMs, operating at both prompt (input encoding) and response (output generation) levels.
- The framework employs ==matrix-based Rényi entropy== to quantify intrinsic uncertainty and isotropy of representations and a ==conditional entropy proxy== to estimate input-output dependencies in generative processes.
- ==Kernel methods in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS)== are used to overcome challenges of direct entropy estimation in high-dimensional, variable-length transformer states.

## Results

- All **10** tested UMMs showed modality-asymmetric encoding, with text and image prompts processed differently regarding entropy levels and layer-wise compression strategies.
- Text prompt encoding was found to be largely structure-agnostic, with entropy patterns more influenced by model size and architecture than by reasoning complexity.
- Nearly all UMMs exhibited higher conditional entropy for text generation (indicating creative, diverse outputs) and lower conditional entropy for image generation (indicating fidelity-driven, deterministic outputs), with **Harmon** being an exception due to its MAE-based architecture.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pseudo-unification stems from a dual divergence within UMMs: modality-asymmetric encoding of inputs and pattern-split responses during generation.
> - UMMs exhibit structure-agnostic encoding within each modality, but systematic asymmetries emerge across modalities, indicating vision and language inputs follow distinct geometric trajectories internally.
> - A "creative text vs. fidelity image" divergence characterizes most UMMs, reflecting an inheritance of modality-specific optimization objectives rather than a unified generative logic.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) represents a significant direction in artificial intelligence, aiming to integrate the creative reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) with the fidelity-driven generation abilities of vision models. Historically, text generation and image synthesis have been optimized under distinct objectives. LLMs typically learn a creative response pattern through next-token prediction, prioritizing contextual plausibility. In contrast, text-to-image (T2I) models, trained on text-image pairs, emphasize fidelity to the input prompt. UMMs were conceived to unify these paradigms, theoretically enabling synergistic cross-modal reasoning and generation.

However, the paper identifies a discrepancy between this theoretical aspiration and practical outcomes, coining the term "pseudo-unification." This phenomenon describes the observed failure of UMMs to consistently transfer LLM-like reasoning to image synthesis tasks, resulting in divergent response behaviors across modalities. Existing research has approached UMM evaluation through data-driven probing using task-specific datasets or synergy benchmarks. While these methods assess performance, they often lack model-internal insight into *why* certain models unify better or why synergy remains elusive. Similarly, current model-internal analyses, often focusing on LLM representations layer-by-layer, frequently overlook the crucial prompt-response dependencies, which are essential for understanding generative behavior in UMMs.

This work contributes to the broader landscape by addressing these diagnostic limitations. It proposes an information-theoretic framework designed to provide model-internal insight into the mechanisms underlying pseudo-unification. By analyzing both input encoding and output generation, and critically, the dependencies between them, the paper aims to shift the understanding of UMMs from purely performance-based metrics to a mechanistic analysis of their internal information flow. This positions the research as foundational for developing UMMs that achieve genuine synergy rather than merely co-locating capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to diagnose the model-internal causes of "pseudo-unification" in Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs). Pseudo-unification refers to the observed phenomenon where UMMs, despite sharing parameters or architectures, fail to transfer the creative, reasoning-based generation abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to image synthesis, exhibiting divergent response patterns across modalities.

The motivation for this investigation stems from several observations and limitations in the current state of UMM research:

- **Observed Divergent Response Behaviors:** The authors highlight that UMMs often struggle with reasoning-based text-to-image generation. For example, a model might correctly answer a complex reasoning question with text but fail to generate a corresponding image that reflects the same understanding or knowledge. This indicates a lack of true synergy, where common representations do not translate to coherent cross-modal reasoning.
- **Performance Discrepancies in Model Scale:** The paper notes instances where smaller UMMs unexpectedly exhibit superior reasoning-based image generation compared to larger models. This counter-intuitive finding suggests that mere architectural scaling or increased parameter count does not inherently guarantee improved cross-modal reasoning or unification. Such discrepancies underscore the need for a deeper, model-internal understanding beyond external performance metrics.
- **High Training Costs and Rapid Architectural Proliferation:** The development and training of UMMs are resource-intensive processes. The rapid proliferation of diverse UMM architectures, without a clear understanding of their internal mechanisms for unification, necessitates a diagnostic framework. Such a framework can guide more efficient and effective model design, moving beyond trial-and-error.
- **Limitations of Existing Probing Methods:**
 - **Lack of Model-Internal Insight:** Current data-driven probing methods, which often rely on task-specific benchmarks, can identify whether synergy occurs but do not reveal the underlying internal reasons for its presence or absence. They cannot explain *why* certain models unify better than others internally.
 - **Neglect of Prompt-Response Dependencies:** While some studies analyze prompt representations layer-by-layer in LLMs, they typically do not investigate the dynamic dependencies between prompt encoding and response generation, especially across different modalities. Understanding these dependencies is crucial for diagnosing generative inconsistencies in UMMs.

To address these issues, the research aims to develop a general probing framework that can analyze internal information flow within UMMs. This framework is designed to disentangle unification into encoding consistency (how inputs are processed) and response coherence (how outputs are generated given inputs), thereby providing a novel diagnostic tool for understanding and ultimately improving multimodal synergy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces an information-theoretic probing framework designed to analyze the internal information flow within Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs), specifically focusing on how they encode inputs (prompts) and generate outputs (responses) across modalities.

#### 3.1. Two-Level Probing Framework

The framework operates at two complementary levels:

- **Prompt Level:** This level analyzes how text and image inputs are represented internally within the UMM. It involves computing:
 - **Embedding Entropy:** Measures the intrinsic uncertainty and isotropy of the initial embedding sequences for both text and image prompts. Higher entropy indicates a more isotropic and information-rich embedding space.
 - **Layer-wise Entropy of Hidden States:** Tracks the evolution of information content (entropy) across different layers of the UMM's transformer backbone for both modalities. This analysis reveals modality-specific differences in information preservation, compression strategies, and the formation of shared representations. For instance, it can indicate whether one modality's representation reaches a bottleneck earlier than another.
- **Response Level:** This level probes the UMM's generative behavior by examining the relationship between prompts and responses. It estimates:
 - **Conditional Entropy Proxy:** Quantifies the residual uncertainty in the generated response given the input prompt, measured across layers. This reflects whether the model maintains consistent reasoning and generative logic across modalities. Low conditional entropy suggests fidelity-driven generation, while high conditional entropy indicates creative or diverse responses.

#### 3.2. Addressing Challenges in Classic Entropy Estimation

A fundamental challenge in applying classical information theory to UMMs is the infeasibility of direct entropy estimation. Transformer-based models do not expose explicit joint probability densities, and their high-dimensional, variable-length representations preclude the use of density-based estimators. To overcome this, the authors reformulate information measures using kernel methods in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS).

- **Matrix-Based R´enyi Entropy:**
 - This method quantifies information content (intrinsic uncertainty and isotropy) directly from the similarity structure of representations in a kernel space.
 - Given an embedding sequence $Z = \{z^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^n \in \mathbb{R}^d$, a Gram (kernel) matrix $K \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is constructed using a Gaussian kernel, where $[K]_{ij} = \exp\left(-\frac{\|z^{(i)} - z^{(j)}\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$. The parameter $\sigma$ controls the bandwidth.
 - $K$ is normalized by its trace to form a probability matrix $A = K/\text{tr}(K)$.
 - The $\alpha$-order R´enyi entropy, $H_\alpha(K) = \frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log(\text{tr}(A^\alpha))$, is then computed. For practical purposes, $\alpha = 1.01$ is used to approximate Shannon entropy while maintaining numerical stability.
 - **Validation:** Experiments demonstrate that this matrix-based entropy increases monotonically with the number of independent information clusters in synthetic embedding sequences, confirming its sensitivity to representational diversity.

- **Conditional Entropy Proxy:**
 - To estimate conditional entropy $H(Z_r | Z_p)$ without explicit densities, the identity $H(Z_r | Z_p) = H(Z_p, Z_r) - H(Z_p)$ is leveraged.
 - $H_\alpha(Z_p)$ (prompt entropy) is calculated from the self-kernel matrix $K_{pp}$ of the prompt embedding sequence $Z_p$.
 - For the joint entropy $H(Z_p, Z_r)$, a block joint kernel matrix $K_{\text{joint}} = \begin{pmatrix} K_{pp} & K_{pr} \\ K_{rp} & K_{rr} \end{pmatrix}$ is constructed. $K_{pr}$ and $K_{rp}$ are cross-kernel matrices capturing similarities between prompt and response embeddings.
 - The joint entropy is estimated as $H_\alpha(K_{\text{joint}})$.
 - The conditional entropy proxy is then defined as $\hat{H}(Z_r | Z_p):= H_\alpha(K_{\text{joint}}) - H_\alpha(K_{pp})$.
 - **Validation:** Controlled experiments show that this proxy increases as the semantic dependency between $Z_p$ and $Z_r$ weakens (e.g., from identical sequences to independently sampled ones), confirming its ability to quantify residual uncertainty. Lower values indicate strong input-output dependency (fidelity), and higher values reflect greater randomness (creativity).

#### 3.3. Probing Framework and Setting

- **Model Selection:** Ten state-of-the-art UMMs were selected to represent diverse architectural designs, image generation paradigms, and model scales:
 - BAGEL (14B) and Bagel-RecA (14B): MoT-based, Flow Matching.
 - Harmon (1.5B) and Harmon-RecA (1.5B): All-in-one, Masked Autoencoder (MAE).
 - Janus-Pro (1B, 7B): All-in-one, VQ-VAE.
 - JanusFlow (1.3B): All-in-one, Flow Matching.
 - Show-o (1.3B) and Show-o2 (7B): All-in-one, Diffusion Loss and Flow Matching.
 - OmniGen2 (7B): Multimodal LLM + Diffusion model pipeline.
 This selection allows for examining the impact of different underlying mechanisms on unification.

- **Data Sources:**
 - **Text Prompts:** T2I-CoReBench, comprising 1,080 prompts covering composition and reasoning tasks (deductive, inductive, abductive).
 - **Image Prompts:** MMBench, containing 3,217 images for reasoning (attribute, logical, relational) and perception (coarse, single-instance, cross-instance) tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research identifies "pseudo-unification" as stemming from a dual divergence within UMMs: modality-asymmetric encoding and pattern-split responses.

#### 4.1. Modality-Asymmetric Encoding in Prompt Representations

The analysis of prompt representations reveals that UMMs exhibit structure-agnostic encoding within a modality but systematic asymmetries across modalities.

- **Text Prompt Analysis:**
 - **Effect of Prompt Length:** Embedding entropy monotonically increases with prompt length, indicating richer and more isotropic embeddings for longer inputs. However, absolute entropy levels vary significantly by architecture, with models sharing an LLM backbone showing similar baselines, suggesting the LLM prior's dominant role. Layer-wise entropy trajectories show model-dependent early compression: larger models often exhibit early entropy collapse (aggressive compression for cross-modal alignment), while smaller models show smoother, oscillatory growth. Middle-length prompts exhibit larger oscillations in deep layers, hypothesized to be an "alignment ambiguity zone."
 - **Effect of Prompt Type:** Embedding entropy levels and layer-wise entropy trajectories are nearly identical across different text prompt types (e.g., compositional vs. various reasoning tasks). This suggests a "structure-agnostic encoding" where reasoning cues are not specifically preserved or differentiated, and prompt engineering primarily elicits surface-level pattern matching. Encoding behavior is more strongly shaped by model size, with larger models showing aggressive compression and smaller ones maintaining higher deep-layer entropy.

- **Image Prompt Analysis:**
 - Across diverse image prompt types (perception-based vs. reasoning-based), all models exhibit nearly identical layer-wise entropy trajectories. This indicates that UMMs encode visual inputs in a structure-agnostic manner, primarily governed by architectural priors rather than the semantic or cognitive demands of the image content. Three patterns emerged: Harmon (gradual entropy growth, detail-preserving), OmniGen2 (moderate, stable entropy, consistent with its decoupled MLLM+diffusion design), and mainstream UMMs (e.g., BAGEL, Janus, Show-o) showing immediate high-entropy saturation (early construction of shared semantic space).

- **Cross-Modal Asymmetries (Pseudo-Unification in Encoding):**
 - Despite architectural unification, all UMMs exhibit systematic differences in how they process text and image prompts within their own architectures. The "unified" representation space remains heterogeneous:
 - **BAGEL series:** Text prompts show early-layer entropy collapse and moderate plateaus, while image prompts maintain high and stable entropy. This suggests disproportionate compression of text representations compared to visual pathways.
 - **Harmon series:** Text prompts show rapid early entropy rise, stabilizing with oscillations, while image prompts start lower and gradually ascend to a similar range. This implies an asynchronous, modality-asymmetric encoding strategy.
 - **Show-o and Janus series:** Both modalities show early entropy surges and plateaus, but with different absolute levels (e.g., text ≈ 8, image ≈ 9), indicating a design bias towards rapid saturation into a high-dimensional shared space with modality-specific offsets.
 - **OmniGen2:** Exhibits a hybrid pattern where text entropy rises slowly to a high level, while image entropy starts high and remains flat, with final entropy values becoming nearly identical.
 - These findings indicate that vision and language follow distinct geometric trajectories during encoding, which likely contributes to divergent generative behaviors downstream.

#### 4.2. Pattern-Split Response Generation

The analysis of response patterns reveals a "creative text vs. fidelity image" divergence in nearly all UMMs, indicative of pseudo-unification driven by misaligned generative patterns.

- **Creative Text vs. Fidelity Image Divergence:**
 - Almost all UMMs exhibit higher conditional entropy in text generation than in image generation.
 - **Text Generation (Higher Entropy):** This reflects a creative response pattern. The model samples from a broad semantic distribution, producing diverse and contextually plausible textual outputs, characteristic of LLM-like creativity.
 - **Image Generation (Lower Entropy):** This reflects a fidelity response pattern. The model tends to produce a single, deterministic visual output that strictly aligns with the prompt, suppressing randomness to ensure faithfulness. This behavior is typical of diffusion or autoregressive vision models.
 - This split indicates that UMMs largely inherit modality-specific optimization objectives rather than unifying their generative logic. They employ a "dual-track" response mechanism.

- **Harmon as an Exception:**
 - Harmon is the only model where image-generation conditional entropy initially exceeds that of text generation, with text entropy rising and eventually surpassing image entropy in final layers.
 - This unique pattern is attributed to Harmon's architecture, which employs a Masked Autoencoder (MAE) for images, functionally paralleling next-token prediction in text. Both modalities are grounded in a common inductive bias of contextual prediction—inferring masked parts from visible context.
 - This finding suggests that genuine unification may require aligning all modalities within a common contextual-prediction framework, treating both visual and textual generation as structured inference from partial observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides several significant contributions to the field of multimodal AI and has implications for future UMM development:

- **Deeper Understanding of UMM Limitations:** The work offers a model-internal diagnosis of "pseudo-unification," moving beyond external performance metrics to explain *why* current UMMs struggle to achieve true cross-modal synergy. It reveals that the issue is not merely a lack of capacity or larger models, but rather a fundamental misalignment in internal information flow and generative inductive biases.
- **Novel Methodological Framework:** The proposed information-theoretic probing framework, utilizing matrix-based R´enyi entropy and a conditional entropy proxy, addresses a critical theoretical gap. It enables the quantification of information content and dependencies in high-dimensional, implicit, and variable-length representations generated by Transformer-based UMMs, which was previously infeasible with classical entropy estimation. This methodology can be applied to a wider range of deep learning models for interpretability.
- **Guiding UMM Architectural Design:** The findings highlight that current architectures often lead to modality-asymmetric encoding and pattern-split responses. The identification of Harmon as an exception, where a shared contextual prediction paradigm facilitates more unified behavior, offers concrete guidance for future UMM design. This suggests that designers should prioritize consistency in generative logic and inductive biases across modalities, rather than merely integrating different modality-specific components.
- **Redefining Unification Objectives:** The research underscores that "real multimodal synergy requires consistency in information flow, not just shared parameters." This challenges the prevailing assumption that simply scaling models or sharing backbones guarantees unification. It encourages a shift in focus towards rethinking pre-training objectives and architectural principles that enforce unified entropy dynamics and symmetrical prediction tasks across modalities.
- **Advancing Evaluation Paradigms:** The study implicitly advocates for moving beyond "does it work?" evaluations, which often rely on aggregate task performance, towards "how and why does it unify?" analyses. This entails developing new diagnostic tools and benchmarks that specifically probe internal consistency and the mechanistic causes of synergistic behavior.
- **Implications for Prompt Engineering and Model Interpretability:** The observation that UMMs engage in "structure-agnostic encoding" for prompt types suggests limitations in how they process semantic nuances for reasoning tasks. Future work could explore how to enforce greater semantic sensitivity in encoding. Furthermore, the framework contributes to the broader effort of making large language and multimodal models more interpretable by providing tools to analyze their internal state transformations.

Overall, this paper provides a foundational analysis that can inform the next generation of UMMs, steering research towards models that achieve genuine, coherent multimodal reasoning and generation through principled design of information flow.
