# Unveiling Chain of Step Reasoning for Vision-Language Models with Fine-grained Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Chain of Step (CoS) reasoning, a framework for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that decomposes complex problems into fine-grained, structured steps using "Name-Thought-Reflection" templates and fine-grained reward mechanisms. This approach, leveraging a new ShareGPT-Step-300K dataset and iterative DPO, achieved average accuracy increases of 4.9% for LLaVA-NeXT and 3.2% for InternVL-2.5-MPO on challenging multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Chain of Thought (CoT) reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lacks fine-grained structure and human-like systematic thinking.
- Evaluating intermediate reasoning steps in VLMs is challenging, hindering effective reinforcement learning and robust inference.
- VLMs struggle to effectively integrate and leverage visual cues at each step of a reasoning chain, contributing to issues like hallucination.

## Method

- Introduced the **Chain of Step** (CoS) framework, which decomposes VLM reasoning into structured, fine-grained steps using "Name-Thought-Reflection" components.
- Constructed ShareGPT-Step-300K, a large-scale supervised fine-tuning dataset, by leveraging GPT-4o to generate detailed CoS reasoning from existing VQA and multimodal math datasets.
- Developed a Process Reward Model (PRM) trained with step-level annotations (Monte Carlo estimation, LLM-as-Judge) to provide fine-grained rewards for iterative Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) training of VLMs.

## Results

- The CoS framework improved average accuracy by **4.9%** for LLaVA-NeXT and **3.2%** for InternVL-2.5-MPO across six challenging multimodal benchmarks, setting new state-of-the-art results.
- PRM-based step-level beam search significantly outperformed other inference-time scaling methods, including Self-Consistency, by effectively refining reasoning paths.
- DPO training using PRM scores for both reasoning steps and final answers yielded the best performance, validating the importance of fine-grained rewards for VLM improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained, step-level reward signals are critical for effective reinforcement learning in VLMs, demonstrating superior performance compared to outcome-only rewards.
> - Optimal performance in selecting the best reasoning path is achieved by combining both intermediate step scores and final answer scores, rather than relying on either alone.
> - For visual complex reasoning tasks, the quality of individual reasoning steps holds more importance than merely increasing the overall reasoning chain length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research builds upon the tremendous success of Chain of Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs). CoT has revolutionized LLMs' ability to perform complex reasoning by generating explicit, detailed step-by-step thought processes before arriving at a final answer. This paradigm, when combined with large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, has led to groundbreaking advancements, exemplified by models like OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1.

Inspired by these successes in the NLP domain, the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has increasingly explored CoT reasoning to tackle more complex multimodal tasks. However, the integration of visual information into reasoning introduces significant challenges. Unlike pure textual reasoning, VLMs must effectively leverage visual cues at each step, and their CoT outputs often become coarse-grained, lacking structured, human-like systematic thinking. This coarse granularity makes it difficult to evaluate the quality of intermediate reasoning steps, which is crucial for effective reinforcement learning and robust inference-time scaling.

Existing VLM reasoning methods, such as Mavis, Visual-CoT, and Insight-V, have made strides in enhancing complex visual reasoning. However, many still rely on reasoning chains at a coarse-grained level, struggling to perform fine-grained structured inference or effectively assess intermediate steps. This paper identifies this gap: the need for a framework that enables VLMs to perform truly "Chain of Step" reasoning—decomposing problems into distinct, evaluable cognitive steps, much like humans. This fine-grained approach is envisioned to unlock better training efficiency through precise reward signals and more effective inference strategies, aligning with the "System-2 thinking" paradigm where models take additional time to perform more deliberate, step-by-step reasoning. The work positions itself as a baseline for future multimodal reasoning research by addressing these fundamental challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address the limitations of existing CoT reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically the lack of fine-grained, structured reasoning and the difficulty in evaluating intermediate steps. The authors aim to:

1. **Define and structure fine-grained reasoning for VLMs:** Move beyond coarse-grained Chain of Thought (CoT) to "Chain of Step" (CoS) reasoning, where the overall problem-solving process is decomposed into distinct, logically coherent, and progressive steps. This involves designing a clear template for each step.
2. **Enable accurate evaluation of intermediate reasoning steps:** Develop a mechanism to provide precise, fine-grained reward signals for individual reasoning steps. This is crucial for effectively training VLMs using reinforcement learning.
3. **Enhance VLM reasoning capabilities through reinforcement learning (RL) with fine-grained rewards:** Leverage the step-level evaluation to guide RL training, specifically using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), to improve the quality of reasoning trajectories.
4. **Facilitate effective inference-time scaling:** Utilize the fine-grained step evaluation to implement more robust inference strategies, such as step-level beam search, allowing models to refine their reasoning and improve performance with increased computational time.

The motivation stems from two fundamental challenges:
- **Defining "step":** How to systematically decompose complex vision-language reasoning into discrete, logically flowing steps, each with a clear purpose and connection to visual information.
- **Evaluating "step":** How to accurately assign a quality score or reward to each intermediate reasoning step, moving beyond just evaluating the final answer. This fine-grained feedback is critical for machine learning optimization, especially for reinforcement learning algorithms that depend on precise reward signals.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to unveil a simple, effective, and transparent framework that significantly advances VLMs' ability to perform human-level, systematic, and structured multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, termed Chain of Step (CoS), involves three main stages: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Process Reward Model (PRM) training, and Iterative Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with fine-grained rewards.

#### 3.1. Structured Reasoning Design

The core of CoS is a novel structured reasoning template. The entire reasoning chain is broken down into multiple consecutive cognitive steps. Each step comprises three components, separated by special tokens:
- **Name:** A concise summary or title of the current step, outlining its key objective.
- **Thought:** A detailed description of the reasoning process, including observations, inferences, and analyses pertinent to that step.
- **Reflection:** A crucial component that establishes connections between the current reasoning step, the visual content, and previous steps. This is designed to mitigate VLM hallucination by grounding reasoning in visual evidence.
Special tokens (e.g., `<|reasoning_start|>`, `<|reasoning_step_start|>`, `<|reasoning_proceed|>`, `<|reasoning_end|>`, and specific tokens for Name, Thought, and Reflection) are used to ensure a stable and parseable output format, which is essential for step-level evaluation.

#### 3.2. ShareGPT-Step-300K Dataset Construction

To enable VLMs to generate this structured reasoning, a large-scale supervised fine-tuning (SFT) dataset, ShareGPT-Step-300K, was created.
- **Data Generation:** Leveraging existing VQA, multimodal math, and reasoning datasets that typically only provide short answers, GPT-4o was prompted to generate detailed, step-by-step reasoning following the CoS template. By providing the question and its ground-truth answer as references, the generation process was simplified, leading to higher-quality reasoning.
- **Diversity:** The dataset encompasses 17 diverse tasks requiring various multimodal reasoning skills, including mathematical reasoning, scientific reasoning, chart and document analysis, and world knowledge.
- **Quality Control:** After initial generation, meticulous data cleaning and filtering were performed to remove samples that did not adhere to the required formatting standards, resulting in 300K high-quality step-level instances. The authors plan to release this dataset.

#### 3.3. Step-Level Annotation for Reward Model Training

To provide fine-grained reward signals, step-level process-annotated data was collected using two mainstream methods:
- **Monte Carlo (MC) Estimation (Math-Shepherd):** For a given problem with a golden answer and a step-by-step solution, a fine-tuned VLM decodes N subsequent reasoning paths from a specific intermediate step. The correctness of the step is inferred based on how many of these decoded paths lead to the correct final answer. N=16 was used as the default.
- **LLM-as-Judge (GPT-4o):** GPT-4o was employed to evaluate each reasoning step with one of three ratings: "Good" (logical, relevant, correct), "Neutral" (relevant but minor flaws or gaps), or "Bad" (flawed, wrong, misunderstanding). For solutions with a correct final answer, both "Good" and "Neutral" steps were marked as correct; for incorrect final answers, only "Good" steps were considered correct.
These methods collectively generated 200K step-annotated data instances, which were then used to train the Process Reward Model (PRM).

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning with Fine-grained Rewards

The final stage uses the trained PRM to enhance the VLM's reasoning capabilities through iterative Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
- **Process Reward Model (PRM) Training:** A PRM ($r_{process}$) was trained using InternVL-2.5-MPO-38B as the base model on the 200K step-annotated data. The objective was to estimate the correctness of individual reasoning steps using binary cross-entropy loss.
- **Iterative Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):**
 - **Initialization:** An SFT model (fine-tuned on ShareGPT-Step-300K) serves as the policy model ($\pi_\theta$) and reference model ($\pi_{ref}$).
 - **Path Generation and Scoring:** For each question, the policy model generates 16 reasoning paths. The trained PRM evaluates each path by calculating a weighted sum of the average step score and the final answer score.
 - **Preference Pair Selection:** Positive-negative pairs for DPO training are selected based on these scores. A threshold $t$ ensures a sufficient distinction between chosen (positive) and rejected (negative) paths.
 - **DPO Training:** The DPO objective function optimizes $\pi_\theta$ to prefer high-scoring paths over low-scoring ones.
 - **Iteration:** This process is repeated for multiple rounds (three rounds in experiments), where the policy model is updated, and new data is generated, while $\pi_{ref}$ remains fixed as the initial SFT model. This iterative approach progressively refines the model's reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results demonstrating the effectiveness and properties of the Chain of Step (CoS) framework.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- CoS consistently outperforms strong open-source VLMs across six challenging benchmarks (MathVista, MMStar, MMMU, M3CoT, AI2D, ChartQA).
- When applied to LLaVA-NeXT, CoS yields an average accuracy increase of 4.9%. For InternVL-2.5-MPO, it achieves a 3.2% average increase, establishing new state-of-the-art results.
- For weaker VLMs (like LLaVA-NeXT), both SFT and DPO contribute significantly to performance gains. For stronger VLMs (like InternVL2.5-MPO), SFT's initial improvement is limited, but iterative DPO provides a substantial boost, highlighting the critical role of reinforcement learning for powerful models.

#### 4.2. Significance of Reasoning Steps

- An ablation study on M3CoT reveals that combining both intermediate step scores and final answer scores for evaluation leads to optimal performance in selecting the best reasoning path (Best-of-16 accuracy). Relying solely on either the final answer or intermediate steps is suboptimal, indicating the value of fine-grained feedback. The default weight for step scores was set to 20%.

#### 4.3. Inference-Time Scaling Advantages

- Comparison of various inference-time scaling methods (Pass@N, Self-Consistency (SC), PRM-based Best-of-N, and PRM with Step-Level Beam Search (PRM-BS)) shows that:
 - PRM-selected answers (Best-of-N based on PRM scores) significantly outperform Self-Consistency.
 - PRM-BS consistently improves upon PRM, demonstrating the efficacy of guided, step-level beam search in refining reasoning paths during inference, even with the same sampling efficiency as Best-of-N.

#### 4.4. Efficacy of Fine-grained Rewards in RL Training

- An ablation on reward types for DPO training on LLaVA-NeXt-SFT shows that using PRM scores for both reasoning steps and answers (Step&Answer PRM) yields the best performance across benchmarks. This outperforms methods relying solely on ground-truth outcomes or PRM scores for only the final answer, unequivocally validating the importance of fine-grained rewards for effective RL training.
- Further validation with GRPO, a state-of-the-art RL algorithm, confirms that PRM-based fine-grained rewards consistently lead to better performance than outcome-only rewards.

#### 4.5. PRM Accuracy and Model Choice

- Evaluating PRM accuracy on seen and unseen data shows that stronger base models for PRM lead to more accurate judgments of intermediate steps. InternVL2.5-38B was selected as the PRM base model due to its superior performance in evaluating reasoning steps, which is critical for the CoS framework. The paper notes an intriguing observation (discussed in Appendix F) that larger PRMs might prioritize step-level accuracy more than final answer accuracy, as step evaluation is semantically more complex and less prone to "answer memorization."

#### 4.6. Reasoning Length vs. Quality

- Contrary to observations in pure LLM mathematical reasoning (where longer reasoning often correlates with better performance), this study finds that for visual complex reasoning, increasing reasoning length is not necessarily better.
- Outcome-based DPO tends to increase reasoning length over iterations. However, PRM-based DPO (considering step quality) initially reduces reasoning length to improve quality, and only later gradually increases it. This suggests that for visual tasks, ensuring high-quality, concise steps is more crucial than merely extending the reasoning chain.

#### 4.7. Superiority of Structured Reasoning

- A direct comparison between "No Reason" (QA only), "Direct Prompt" (unstructured CoT), and CoS reveals that models trained with reasoning-augmented data (Direct Prompt and CoS) outperform QA-only models. Crucially, CoS with PRM-based RL yields significantly greater improvements than both baselines and even outcome-only RL for unstructured CoT, highlighting the clear advantages of structured, fine-grained reasoning with precise reward signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and multimodal AI reasoning:

1. **Establishing a New Baseline for VLM Reasoning:** By introducing the Chain of Step (CoS) framework, the paper provides a clear, transparent, and highly effective approach to structured reasoning for VLMs. The consistent performance gains across diverse benchmarks solidify CoS as a strong baseline for future research.
2. **Highlighting Fine-grained Rewards:** The research empirically demonstrates the critical importance of fine-grained, step-level reward signals for effective reinforcement learning in VLMs. This moves beyond traditional outcome-based rewards, offering a more nuanced and powerful way to guide model learning and improve reasoning quality.
3. **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning Interpretability:** The structured "Name-Thought-Reflection" format promotes more interpretable and human-like reasoning processes in VLMs. The "Reflection" component, in particular, offers a mechanism to explicitly connect reasoning steps to visual content and previous thoughts, which can help in mitigating hallucination and improving trustworthiness.
4. **Insights into Reasoning Length vs. Quality:** The discovery that for visual complex reasoning, quality of steps is more important than mere length challenges existing assumptions from LLM research. This insight can guide future model design and training strategies, encouraging models to be more precise and efficient in their reasoning rather than simply verbose.
5. **Practical Tools and Resources:** The commitment to release the ShareGPT-Step-300K dataset and the Process Reward Model (PRM) will significantly benefit the research community, providing valuable resources for developing and evaluating new VLM reasoning techniques.
6. **Enhanced Inference Capabilities:** The proposed step-level beam search, enabled by fine-grained evaluation, offers a practical method for improving VLM performance at inference time without drastically increasing computational cost, making VLMs more robust in real-world applications.

In conclusion, this paper not only pushes the performance boundaries of VLMs on complex reasoning tasks but also offers fundamental insights into how these models can learn to think more systematically and interpretably. It paves the way for the development of more sophisticated and reliable multimodal AI systems.
