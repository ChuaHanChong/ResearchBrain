# Humanoid Policy ~ Human Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a cross-embodiment learning framework that leverages a large dataset of egocentric human demonstrations (PH²D) collected with consumer-grade VR to train robust humanoid robot manipulation policies. A unified Human Action Transformer (HAT) policy co-trained with human and robot data significantly improves generalization to novel objects, backgrounds, and placements by nearly 100% in out-of-distribution settings, and enables efficient few-shot transfer across different robot platforms.

## Problem

- The prohibitive cost and limited scalability of collecting diverse, high-quality data directly from real robots for training robust manipulation policies.
- The inherent human-humanoid embodiment gap, including visual and action differences, complicates leveraging abundant human video data for robot learning.
- Existing approaches often rely on modular perception or specialized, expensive hardware for human data collection, which limits scalability and can introduce compounding errors.

## Method

- Developed PH²D, a large-scale, task-oriented egocentric human demonstration dataset (~3.02M frames, ~27k demos) collected efficiently using consumer-grade VR devices.
- Designed the Human Action Transformer (HAT), a unified behavior policy co-trained on mixed human and robot data, utilizing a 54-dimensional state-action space common to humans and humanoid robots.
- Mitigated data domain gaps by carefully requesting human demonstrators to minimize whole-body movement and by aligning human action speeds with robot capabilities through interpolation.

## Results

- Co-training with human data (PH²D) achieves nearly 100% relative improvement in out-of-distribution generalization for tasks involving novel objects, backgrounds, and placements compared to robot-only training.
- The HAT policy demonstrates efficient few-shot transfer capabilities across heterogeneous humanoid platforms, substantially outperforming robot-only baselines in low-data regimes.
- Human data collection using consumer-grade VR is significantly more efficient than robot teleoperation, with dramatically shorter task completion times (e.g., 4.09s for human vs. 19.72s for robot in grasping tasks).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric, task-oriented human demonstrations, when treated as a distinct humanoid embodiment, can serve as a highly scalable and effective data source for robot learning.
> - A unified state-action space combined with direct, end-to-end policy learning (HAT) can effectively bridge the human-robot embodiment gap without complex modular perception or generative models.
> - Simple image augmentations and sufficiently large, diverse data are effective for bridging visual domain gaps between human and robot observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Humanoid Policy ∼ Human Policy" is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields in robotics and artificial intelligence: imitation learning, cross-embodiment learning, and the utilization of human demonstrations for robot control.

**Imitation Learning for Robot Manipulation:** Recent years have seen significant advancements in robotic manipulation, largely driven by imitation learning from real robot demonstrations. Efforts like scaling up data collection through teleoperation and controlling robot end-effectors directly have led to impressive dexterity. However, a persistent bottleneck remains: collecting structured, real-robot data is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale to internet-level magnitudes seen in computer vision (e.g., ImageNet) or natural language processing (e.g., GPT-4). This limitation severely constrains the robustness and generalization capabilities of current robot policies, hindering their deployment in diverse, open-world environments.

**Learning from Human Videos:** Leveraging the vast existing human video data for robot policy learning is a long-standing aspiration. Previous approaches generally fall into two categories:
1. **Aligning Observations:** Many works attempt to use internet-scale human videos by aligning observations. Due to discrepancies between human videos and robot egocentric views (e.g., viewpoints, object scales), these methods often rely on modular approaches with intermediate representations as surrogates for training, such as affordances, object keypoints, or other object representations. While foundational, these modular pipelines can introduce compounding errors.
2. **Aligning Actions:** Other approaches focus on transferring human demonstrations by specifically aligning actions. This is more challenging due to fundamental differences in embodiment, actuation, and control dynamics between humans and robots. Techniques include masking in egocentric views, aligning motion trajectories, or using specialized hardware for hand tracking. While methods like HumanPlus have explored remapping 3D human poses for teleoperation, they still require robot hardware during the human data collection phase, limiting scalability.

**Cross-Embodiment Learning:** A crucial trend in modern robot learning is cross-embodiment pre-training, where policies are trained across diverse robot platforms. This approach has shown promise in improving adaptability and generalization by learning from a broader spectrum of robot experiences. End-to-end approaches using cross-embodied robot data are gaining traction to reduce compounding perceptive errors and foster desired behaviors like retrying.

**The Paper's Unique Contribution:** This work uniquely distinguishes itself by:
- **High-Quality Human Data as Primary Source:** It proposes egocentric, task-oriented human demonstrations as a *scalable and direct* data source for *cross-embodiment* learning. The key insight is to model humans as merely a *different humanoid embodiment*, where robot actions are a transformation away from human actions.
- **Direct, End-to-End Modeling without Surrogates:** Unlike many prior works that rely on intermediate representations (affordances, keypoints) or complex generative models to bridge the human-robot gap, this paper advocates for and demonstrates direct, end-to-end training. It achieves this by carefully designing a *unified state-action space* for both humans and humanoid robots.
- **Scalability via Consumer-Grade Hardware:** It sidesteps the need for specialized, expensive hardware for human data collection by adapting consumer-grade VR devices, making data acquisition significantly more accessible and efficient.
- **Addressing Embodiment Gaps Systematically:** It tackles both data and modeling perspectives: collecting specifically "task-oriented" human data aligned with robot tasks, and developing a policy (HAT) that inherently treats humans and robots within a unified framework, handling visual and action domain gaps.
- **Contrast with Concurrent Works:** The paper positions itself against concurrent efforts like DexCap and EgoMimic. While DexCap uses 3D inputs and EgoMimic requires strict visual sensor alignment, this paper demonstrates scalability with diverse 2D visual inputs and robustness to camera variations, and achieves larger dataset scale and object diversity.

In essence, this research represents a significant step towards leveraging the abundance of human demonstrations to create more robust and generalizable robot policies, potentially accelerating the development of foundational models for robotics by tapping into a previously underutilized and highly scalable data source.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind "Humanoid Policy ∼ Human Policy" stems from the fundamental challenge in modern robot learning: the prohibitive cost and limited scalability of collecting diverse, high-quality data directly from real robots. While imitation learning has shown promise, the laborious nature of teleoperation and the difficulty in scaling robot demonstrations prevent the training of truly robust and generalizable policies capable of operating in unstructured, open-world environments.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Overcome Data Scarcity for Robot Learning:** The primary motivation is to find a more scalable, cost-effective, and efficient data source than real-robot teleoperation to enhance the robustness and generalization of humanoid manipulation policies. The authors propose egocentric human demonstrations as this alternative.

2. **Mitigate the Human-Humanoid Embodiment Gap:** To effectively leverage human data for robot learning, the inherent differences in physical structure, actuation, and visual appearance between humans and humanoid robots (the "embodiment gap") must be addressed. The paper aims to mitigate this gap from both data collection and modeling perspectives.

3. **Develop a Task-Oriented, Scalable Human Demonstration Dataset (PH²D):** A concrete objective is to collect a large-scale dataset of egocentric human demonstrations specifically tailored for robot manipulation tasks. This dataset, termed Physical Humanoid-Human Data (PH²D), must provide accurate 3D hand-finger poses and head poses, be task-oriented, and be collected using consumer-grade hardware to ensure scalability and affordability, directly aligning with robot execution requirements.

4. **Design a Unified Cross-Embodiment Policy (HAT):** The paper aims to develop a novel behavior policy, the Human Action Transformer (HAT), capable of learning directly from both human and robot data. A crucial objective here is to introduce a *unified state-action space* that models humans and humanoid robots as different embodiments within the same framework, allowing for end-to-end training without relying on complex modular perception or learning surrogates. This unified space must also be amenable to differentiable retargeting for robot deployment.

5. **Demonstrate Improved Generalization and Robustness:** A core objective is to empirically validate that co-training with human data, using the PH²D dataset and the HAT policy, leads to significant improvements in policy generalization (e.g., to novel objects, backgrounds, placements) and robustness (e.g., against perturbations) compared to policies trained solely on robot data. This validation includes showing better performance in out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) settings and efficient few-shot transfer to new robot platforms.

6. **Quantify Data Collection Efficiency:** The authors also aim to quantitatively demonstrate that using human data, even when collected with VR devices, is substantially more efficient in terms of time, labor, and cost compared to traditional robot teleoperation, thereby solidifying its viability as a scalable data source.

In essence, the research is motivated by the vision of training generalist humanoid robot policies using internet-scale data, much like large models in other AI domains, and believes that human demonstrations are the most promising pathway to achieve this vision efficiently.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in "Humanoid Policy ∼ Human Policy" is twofold: the creation of a novel human demonstration dataset (PH²D) and the development of a cross-embodiment learning policy (HAT) designed to effectively leverage this data alongside traditional robot demonstrations.

#### 3.1. PH²D: Task-oriented Physical Humanoid-Human Data

The authors address the challenge of scalable human data collection by adapting consumer-grade VR devices, aiming to provide high-quality, task-oriented egocentric human demonstrations.

- **Hardware Adaptation for Scalable Data Collection:**
 - **Consumer-Grade Devices:** To avoid specialized and expensive hardware, the researchers developed custom software for widely available VR devices:
 - **Apple Vision Pro + Built-in Camera:** A Vision OS App was developed to use the built-in camera for visual observations and Apple ARKit for accurate 3D head and hand poses.
 - **Meta Quest 3 / Apple Vision Pro + ZED Camera:** A web-based application utilizing OpenTelevision was developed to gather 3D head and hand poses. A 3D-printed holder allowed mounting ZED Mini Stereo cameras, introducing visual diversity at a low cost (<$700).
 - This setup enables convenient data collection anywhere, without requiring a physical robot, significantly boosting efficiency.

- **Data Collection Pipeline:**
 - **Task-Oriented Demonstrations:** Human operators wearing VR devices perform specific manipulation tasks (e.g., grasping, pouring) that directly overlap with robot execution goals. Each demonstration includes language instructions.
 - **Synchronization:** Proprioceptive inputs (3D poses) and visual inputs (egocentric videos) are synchronized by closest timestamps.
 - **Dataset Scale:** PH²D is a large-scale dataset, comprising approximately 3.02 million frames and 26,824 human demonstrations, complemented by smaller-scale robot data (668k frames, 1,552 demos). This scale surpasses comparable task-oriented egocentric human datasets.

- **Mitigating Domain Gaps in Data:**
 - **Action Domain Gap:**
 - **Whole-Body Movement:** Human manipulation often involves involuntary whole-body movement. To align with current humanoid robot dexterity, human data collectors were requested to sit in an upright position and minimize whole-body movement. Ablation studies confirmed this necessity.
 - **Speed Discrepancy:** Humans are generally more dexterous and faster than robots. This speed gap is addressed during training by interpolating human translation and rotations, effectively "slowing down" human actions by a factor of α_slow = 4, normalized by average task completion times.
 - **Visual Domain Gap:** Differences in camera sensors (e.g., tones) and end-effector appearance (human hand vs. robot gripper) exist. Instead of complex heuristics or generative methods, the authors found that basic image augmentations (color jittering, Gaussian blurring) combined with sufficiently large and diverse data were effective in bridging this gap.

#### 3.2. HAT: Human Action Transformer

The core of the modeling approach is the Human Action Transformer (HAT), which learns a cross-embodied robot policy by directly modeling human behaviors.

- **Unified State-Action Space (Key Innovation):**
 - The crucial design choice is a unified state-action representation shared by both bimanual humanoid robots and humans.
 - **Proprioceptive Observation:** A 54-dimensional vector comprising 6D rotations (using 6D representations for continuity) of the head, left wrist, and right wrist, along with the x/y/z positions of the left and right wrists and 10 finger tips.
 - **Action Space:** HAT predicts future hand-finger trajectories in this same human-centric representation space.
 - **Bijective Mapping:** For robots with 5-fingered dexterous hands, a direct bijective mapping exists between robot and human finger tips.

- **Policy Architecture:**
 - **Transformer-based:** HAT utilizes a transformer-based architecture, similar to Action Chunk Transformer (ACT), to predict chunks of future actions.
 - **Visual Backbone:** A frozen DinoV2 ViT-S encoder is used as the visual backbone for both human and robot observations. Freezing the backbone leverages robust pre-trained visual features, improving generalization and reducing computational load during training.

- **Training Strategy:**
 - **Co-training:** HAT is co-trained on a mixed dataset of robot demonstrations (D_robot) and human demonstrations (D_human). During training, state-action pairs are sampled from either source.
 - **Loss Function:** The policy (π) is trained using an L1 loss, with an additional weight (λ=2) on end-effector positions to emphasize their importance: L = ℓ₁(π(sᵢ), aᵢ) + λ ⋅ ℓ₁(π(sᵢ)EEF, aᵢ,EEF).

- **Deployment:**
 - **Retargeting:** For real-robot deployment, the predicted human-centric actions (wrist 6DoF poses and 3D hand keypoints) from HAT are converted into robot-specific actions by applying inverse kinematics and hand retargeting. This process is designed to be differentiable.

The methodology emphasizes end-to-end learning without complex intermediate modules or strong assumptions about visual alignment, by carefully unifying the data representation and action space. This direct modeling of humans as another embodiment, combined with scalable data collection, forms the core strength of this research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the significant advantages of co-training robot manipulation policies with human data using the proposed PH²D dataset and HAT policy.

1. **PH²D Scale and Quality:** The PH²D dataset is established as the largest task-oriented egocentric human dataset, featuring ~3.02M frames and ~27k demonstrations, which is significantly larger and more diverse than comparable datasets like DexCap and EgoMimic (Table 1). This scale is achieved through the efficient use of consumer-grade VR devices.

2. **In-Distribution (I.D.) Performance:** For tasks performed in settings closely matching the robot training data (I.D. settings), policies co-trained with human data show similar performance to those trained solely on robot data (Table 2). This indicates that even a small amount of high-quality robot data, when combined with robust visual foundation models like DinoV2, can achieve good performance in familiar environments. Human data does not degrade I.D. performance.

3. **Significant Improvement in Out-Of-Distribution (O.O.D.) Generalization:** This is a core finding. Co-training with human data drastically improves O.O.D. performance, achieving nearly 100% relative improvement in settings unseen by the robot data but presented in the human data (Table 2). This demonstrates the policy's enhanced robustness and ability to generalize beyond its direct robot experience.
 - **Background Generalization:** Human data significantly improves performance on the cup passing task across novel backgrounds (e.g., red, green, wooden tablecloths) not seen in robot training, increasing overall success rates by almost 50% (Table 7).
 - **Object Appearance Generalization:** Co-training with human data enhances the policy's ability to grasp objects with novel appearances (e.g., different types of boxes and cans) not present in the robot data (Table 6). Even for objects not directly seen in human data, the policy demonstrates more proactive and stable grasping behaviors, suggesting learning of broader visual priors.
 - **Object Placement Generalization:** The policy exhibits superior generalization to unseen object placements within the workspace. When robot data is concentrated in specific areas, human data allows the HAT policy to generalize to sparse or unvisited regions, effectively doubling the overall success rate in the vertical grasping task (Fig. 8).

4. **Effective Few-Shot Transfer across Heterogeneous Embodiments:**
 - HAT demonstrates the ability to unify heterogeneous human-centric data sources (humanoids and humans) into a generalizable policy.
 - When transferring to a distinct humanoid platform (Humanoid B with different arm configurations), co-trained policies (Humanoid B + A + Human) substantially outperform Humanoid B-only baselines, especially in low-data regimes (Fig. 5a, 5b). This highlights the approach's capability for rapid adaptation and transferring latent task structures across different robot morphologies.

5. **Superior Data Collection Efficiency:** Human data collection is significantly more efficient than robot teleoperation.
 - Task completion times for human demonstrations (even with VR) are dramatically shorter than robot teleoperation (e.g., 4.09s for human vs. 19.72s for robot in grasping tasks; 4.90s vs. 37.31s in pouring tasks) (Table 5).
 - In a direct comparison of sampling efficiency, policies trained with mixed robot and human data showed significantly better performance than robot-only data collected in the same unit of time, even for lab settings (Fig. 6). Human data collection offers advantages in safety, labor efficiency, setup flexibility, and higher demonstration success rates.

6. **Importance of Unified State-Action Space and Action Speed Alignment:**
 - Ablation studies confirm the criticality of both the unified state-action space and the "slowing down" (interpolation) of human actions. Without interpolation, predicted actions fluctuate between human-like fast speeds and robot-like slow speeds, leading to instability. Without a unified state space, the policy can distinguish embodiments too easily, leading to poor O.O.D. performance despite similar I.D. results (Table 3).
 - Minimizing human whole-body movement during data collection is also crucial for performance on current robot platforms, as these movements are difficult for robots to replicate (Table 4).

In summary, the main findings strongly support the paper's central hypothesis: high-quality, task-oriented egocentric human data, combined with a unified policy design, can serve as a highly effective and scalable data source for significantly improving the generalization and robustness of humanoid robot manipulation policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Humanoid Policy ∼ Human Policy" carries substantial significance and holds the potential for transformative impact on the field of robotics, particularly in the domain of humanoid manipulation and generalist robot learning.

1. **Enabling Scalable Data for Robot Learning:** The most immediate and profound impact is demonstrating a viable pathway to overcome the long-standing data bottleneck in robot learning. By effectively treating human demonstrations as a scalable, cross-embodiment data source and leveraging consumer-grade VR devices, the paper offers a practical solution to acquire the large, diverse datasets necessary for training highly capable robotic policies. This moves beyond the limitations of expensive, slow, and constrained robot-only data collection.

2. **Accelerating Generalist Robot Development:** The ability to learn from diverse human experiences, coupled with robot data, is a critical step towards developing "generalist" humanoid robots. These robots would be capable of performing a wide array of tasks in unstructured human environments, adapting to novel objects, backgrounds, and task variations, much like large language models generalize across textual domains. The improved generalization and robustness demonstrated for O.O.D. settings directly contribute to this goal.

3. **Simplified and Robust Policy Learning Paradigms:** The success of the Human Action Transformer (HAT) in directly modeling humans as a different humanoid embodiment, without relying on complex modular perception, generative methods, or strict visual alignment heuristics, simplifies the policy learning pipeline. This end-to-end approach is less prone to compounding errors and offers a more robust framework for bridging the embodiment gap, making it easier to develop and deploy future robot learning systems.

4. **Cost-Effectiveness and Accessibility:** The emphasis on consumer-grade VR devices for data collection drastically reduces the financial and logistical barriers to entry for robot learning research and development. This accessibility can democratize the creation of large robot datasets, enabling more researchers and practitioners to contribute to and benefit from advancements in the field.

5. **Foundational Contribution to Cross-Embodiment Learning:** The paper pushes the boundaries of cross-embodiment learning by integrating human data in a unified state-action space. This framework could inspire new research into how different agents (humans, various robot morphologies, even simulated agents) can collectively contribute to a shared knowledge base for control policies, leading to more adaptable and intelligent robotic systems. The successful few-shot transfer to a different humanoid platform (Humanoid B) underscores the learned policy's ability to abstract latent task structures.

6. **Future Directions for Language-Conditioned Policies:** The acknowledgment of language instructions in the PH²D dataset and the stated future plan to expand HAT to language-conditioned policies points to a significant future impact. Combining scalable human demonstrations with language models could enable robots to learn from verbal instructions and generalize to novel commands, greatly expanding their utility and natural human-robot interaction capabilities.

7. **Guidance for Hardware and Software Development:** The ablation studies offer valuable insights into design choices, such as minimizing whole-body movement in human demonstrations and the importance of speed alignment. These findings can guide the development of future robot hardware (e.g., more dexterous whole-body control) and software tools to further reduce the embodiment gap, maximizing the utility of human data.

In conclusion, "Humanoid Policy ∼ Human Policy" offers a compelling and practical solution to a core challenge in robotics. By demonstrating that "Humanoid Policy ∼ Human Policy" holds true, the paper opens new avenues for creating intelligent, adaptable, and generalist humanoid robots that can learn from the vast and diverse repository of human experience, fundamentally reshaping how we collect data and train robots for the real world.
