# VLM See, Robot Do: Human Demo Video to Robot Action Plan via Vision Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> SeeDo is a VLM agent pipeline that converts long-horizon human demonstration videos into robot task plans without extra training, using hand-speed-based keyframe selection, object tracking as visual prompts, and GPT-4o chain-of-thought reasoning, then executes plans via Code-as-Policies on a real UR10e arm.

## Problem

- Prior VLM-for-robotics work maps language instructions or single images to task plans, but human demonstration videos carry richer temporal and spatial cues that text alone cannot specify.
- Directly feeding long-horizon demonstration videos into pretrained VLMs yields poor results since they struggle to retain all-frame information and confuse temporal order and spatial relationships.
- Existing evaluation only reports final-state success rate, which cannot measure whether a generated plan follows the demonstrated step order or where in a long-horizon sequence it diverges.

## Method

- **Keyframe Selection module** detects ==hand keypoints==, plots hand speed over time, and picks speed-trough frames as keyframes, then VLM-filters out frames without hand-object interaction.
- **Visual Prompting module** runs an ==open-vocabulary detector== plus ==SAM2 tracking== to overlay mask contours, tracking IDs, and center coordinates onto keyframes as spatial cues for the VLM.
- **VLM Interpreter module** prompts ==GPT-4o== with ==chain-of-thought== steps (object listing, pick choice, reference object, drop direction) to produce task plans, executed via ==Code-as-Policies== language model programs.

## Results

- SeeDo achieves SSR of **80.40/66.50/52.48** percent on vegetable, garment, and wooden-block tasks, beating GPT-4o init+final baseline (**54.43/31.61/32.69**) and Gemini 1.5 Pro.
- Uniform-sampling ablation collapses SSR to near zero (**0.00-1.32** percent) versus hand-detection keyframe selection's **80.40** percent, showing keyframe quality dominates VLM video understanding.
- Removing visual prompting on the block-stacking task drops TSR/FSR from **21.62** to **0.00** percent and raises spatial error rate from **64.10** to **87.5** percent, confirming visual prompts fix spatial reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Heuristic, training-free signals (hand speed) can outperform learned or uniform sampling for extracting temporally meaningful keyframes from long videos.
> - Visual prompting (contours plus tracking IDs) transfers spatial grounding gains from manipulation and navigation literature into video-to-plan interpretation for VLMs.
> - Step-wise sequence metrics (like SSR) reveal partial-progress failure points that final-state-only success rate metrics hide, informing better diagnostic benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of embodied AI, robotics, and large Vision Language Models (VLMs), a rapidly evolving field driven by the recent advancements in multimodal foundation models. The broader landscape sees VLMs increasingly adopted in robotics for their robust common sense reasoning and impressive generalization capabilities.

Existing research has primarily explored several avenues for integrating VLMs into robotics:
- **Natural Language to Task Planning:** Many efforts utilize VLMs as interfaces to parse human language instructions and generate high-level task and motion plans for robots. This includes generating robot executable code or "language model programs" (LMPs) to orchestrate action primitives.
- **Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs):** Another line of work fine-tunes pretrained VLMs with robotics-specific data to learn direct mappings from visual inputs and language instructions to robot actions.
- **Perception and Affordance Grounding:** VLMs have also been leveraged for their general knowledge to identify salient objects, key points, or assign affordance functions, transforming complex robotic control into optimization problems.

However, the authors identify a crucial gap in this landscape: while language instructions are effective, they often prove insufficient for precisely describing complex, long-horizon tasks, becoming tedious and inefficient. In contrast, human demonstration videos offer a more intuitive, direct, and information-rich medium for such tasks, naturally capturing intricate temporal and spatial dependencies. Moreover, the sheer abundance of video data online holds immense potential for scaling robot learning.

The significant challenge lies in the substantial domain gap between human demonstrations and robot execution. Traditional imitation learning methods, while attempting to bridge this gap, frequently struggle with long-horizon tasks, demanding an increasing number of demonstrations and exhibiting poor generalization to new environments or object appearances.

"VLM See, Robot Do" directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel approach that leverages the powerful reasoning abilities of VLMs to *interpret human demonstration videos* directly into actionable robot task plans. Unlike previous works that primarily rely on language instructions or direct end-to-end policy learning from videos, SeeDo focuses on extracting a symbolic, interpretable plan from visual demonstrations. It moves beyond direct imitation learning, which often aims to learn a policy to mimic human motion, to a more cognitive approach where the VLM understands the *intent* and *steps* of the human demonstration to generate a structured plan. This decomposition of planning from direct action learning allows SeeDo to benefit from VLM's common sense knowledge for robust understanding and generalization, mitigating many challenges faced by traditional imitation learning in dynamic, real-world scenarios. The paper differentiates itself by prioritizing video input over language input for task specification, and by enhancing VLM's inherent visual and spatial reasoning deficiencies through specialized preprocessing modules.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to learn new, long-horizon tasks by observing human demonstration videos, mirroring a fundamental human learning capability. The authors aim to translate complex visual demonstrations into explicit robot task plans, thereby simplifying the process of teaching robots and making robot learning more intuitive and scalable.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Limitations of Language Instructions:** While widely used, natural language instructions often lack the precision required for complex tasks, especially those involving intricate spatial and temporal dependencies. For long-horizon tasks, providing detailed language instructions can become cumbersome and inefficient.
- **Richness and Accessibility of Video Data:** Human demonstration videos naturally convey sequential actions, object interactions, and spatial relationships that are difficult to articulate solely through text. Furthermore, the internet provides an almost inexhaustible supply of video data, representing a vast, untapped resource for robot learning if the domain gap can be effectively bridged.
- **Challenges of Direct Imitation Learning:** Existing imitation learning techniques often struggle with the significant embodiment gap between humans and robots. They typically require extensive datasets of robot-specific demonstrations and frequently exhibit poor generalization when faced with variations in environment, object appearances, or task specifics. For long-horizon tasks, the data requirements become prohibitively large.
- **Leveraging VLM Capabilities:** Vision Language Models possess rich common sense knowledge and strong zero-shot generalization abilities. The authors hypothesize that these inherent VLM strengths can be harnessed to understand human intent, interpret object relationships, and generate task plans that are robust to variations between demonstration and execution environments, thus sidestepping many of the generalization issues of end-to-end learning.
- **Addressing VLM Deficiencies:** While powerful, VLMs have known limitations in processing extensive visual sequences and accurately discerning fine-grained spatial relationships and object identities, particularly from raw video. A critical objective is to design a modular framework that augments VLMs to overcome these specific shortcomings in the context of robotic task planning.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a practical, VLM-centric framework that allows robots to "see" human demonstrations, reason about the underlying task logic, and "do" the task by executing a generated plan, offering a more flexible and generalizable alternative to current robot learning paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "VLM See, Robot Do" (SeeDo) methodology proposes a modular agent framework designed to interpret long-horizon human demonstration videos into natural language task plans executable by robots. The core idea is to enhance a VLM's capabilities for video understanding and spatial reasoning through a pipeline comprising three main modules: Keyframe Selection, Visual Prompting, and VLM Interpreter, followed by a Plan Execution stage.

1. **Keyframe Selection Module:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** VLMs have limited context windows and struggle to process all frames of a long-horizon video. Uniform frame sampling often misses critical action moments.
 - **Approach:** SeeDo employs a heuristic approach based on hand speed. Hand-object interactions are crucial in manipulation tasks, and hands typically slow down during picking or placing actions. A lightweight model detects hand keypoints in the video. The speed of these keypoints' center is plotted over time, and "troughs" (points of minimum speed) are identified as potential keyframes. These frames represent critical junctures of interaction. Further filtering is applied by the VLM Interpreter module to remove any irrelevant keyframes where no hand-object interaction is occurring.

2. **Visual Prompting Module:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Current VLMs often lack precise visual and spatial perception. They struggle with accurately determining object locations, understanding spatial relationships, and consistently differentiating visually similar objects over time.
 - **Approach:** This module enhances the VLM's visual grounding. First, the VLM is prompted to identify a list of objects present in the scene. Then, an open-vocabulary object detector (GroundingDINO) extracts bounding boxes for these identified objects in the initial video frame. The Segment Anything Model (SAM2) is then used to track these objects throughout the video, generating tracking IDs and mask contours. These mask contours, along with their corresponding tracking IDs, are rendered onto the selected keyframes. The center coordinates of these masks are also extracted and appended to the textual prompts provided to the VLM. This visual and textual prompting provides explicit cues, helping the VLM to precisely identify objects, track them, and understand their relative spatial positions, especially critical for distinguishing visually identical items (e.g., wooden blocks).

3. **VLM Interpreter Module:**
 - **Core VLM:** GPT-4o is chosen for its strong performance and general recognition, although the framework is designed to be VLM-agnostic.
 - **Reasoning Strategy:** A Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategy is employed to guide the VLM through a structured reasoning process for generating task plans. The CoT is designed to break down the interpretation of each pick-and-place subtask.
 - **Detailed CoT Steps (informed by visual prompts):**
 - **Step 1: Object List Acquisition:** The VLM is initially prompted to list all objects on the desk, crucial for tasks with multiple, similar items where it is instructed to repeat object names according to their quantity.
 - **Step 2: Invalid Keyframe Filtering:** The VLM analyzes keyframes from the selection module, identifying and discarding those where no hand-object interaction is detected.
 - **Step 3: Object Picked Identification:** For valid "pick" keyframes, the VLM, aided by visual prompts (contours, IDs), identifies which specific object from the object list is being manipulated.
 - **Step 4: Reference Object Selection:** For corresponding "place" keyframes, the VLM identifies a "reference object" (e.g., a container for storage, a supporting block for stacking) relative to which the picked object will be placed.
 - **Step 5: Spatial Relationship Reasoning:** Finally, the VLM determines the dominant relative spatial relationship between the picked object and the reference object (e.g., "in," "on top of," "to the left of"). The visual prompts, including contours and coordinates, are crucial here for disambiguating positions, especially with similar objects. The VLM is also robust enough to correct potential misidentifications from earlier steps.

4. **Plan Execution:**
 - The natural language task plans generated by SeeDo are translated into executable robot actions using Language Model Programs (LMPs). This involves mapping the high-level steps (e.g., "pick red block, drop on top of blue block") to calls of predefined, low-level robot action primitives.
 - **Real-world Deployment:** For physical execution, a segmentation model identifies objects in RGB images, and depth images provide 3D coordinates. A UR10e robot arm equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper executes the plan by moving to the identified 3D positions and performing grasping/releasing actions. This modular approach allows SeeDo to be compatible with various robot platforms and action primitive implementations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SeeDo was rigorously evaluated on a curated benchmark of long-horizon human demonstration videos across three diverse pick-and-place task categories, designed to test temporal, spatial, and object recognition capabilities:
- **Vegetable Organization Task:** Involves sorting 6 different plush vegetables into 4 distinct containers (38 demonstrations).
- **Garment Organization Task:** Focuses on organizing various types of garments (shirts, shoes, ties, umbrella) into separate boxes (30 demonstrations).
- **Wooden Block Stacking Task:** Challenges the models with visually similar objects (wooden blocks), demanding precise spatial understanding (39 demonstrations).

To comprehensively assess performance, the authors introduced three novel metrics:
- **Task Success Rate (TSR):** A strict metric requiring the generated plan to exactly match the demo's action sequence in both content and temporal order.
- **Final-state Success Rate (FSR):** Evaluates if the final configuration of objects matches the demonstration's end state, irrespective of the execution order.
- **Step Success Rate (SSR):** Measures partial completeness by calculating the ratio of correctly aligned (in temporal order) pick-drop steps from the generated plan to the ground truth steps.

**Main Results (Table I):**
SeeDo consistently demonstrated superior performance across all three task categories and evaluation metrics compared to state-of-the-art closed-source (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o variants) and open-source video VLMs (LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B, VILA).
- For **Vegetable Organization**, SeeDo achieved TSR of 60.53%, FSR of 60.53%, and SSR of 80.40%.
- For **Garment Organization**, SeeDo recorded TSR of 26.67%, FSR of 26.67%, and SSR of 66.50%.
- For **Wooden Block Stacking**, SeeDo showed TSR of 21.62%, FSR of 21.62%, and SSR of 52.48%.

These results highlight SeeDo's ability to interpret long-horizon tasks more accurately. While TSR and FSR are lower for more complex tasks (Garment, Blocks), SeeDo's consistently high SSR indicates that it successfully interprets a significant majority of individual task steps in correct temporal order, even when the complete plan might eventually fail due to accumulated errors. The GPT-4o Init+Final baseline, using only start and end frames, performed poorly on TSR/SSR, emphasizing the necessity of full video input.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Keyframe Selection (Table II):** Replacing SeeDo's hand-detection-based Keyframe Selection with uniform sampling (SeeDo Unif.) resulted in near-zero success rates across all metrics. This conclusively proved the critical role of intelligently selected keyframes in providing relevant context to the VLM, preventing crucial action information from being missed and avoiding VLM context limits.
- **Visual Prompting (Table III):** Ablating the Visual Prompting module (SeeDo w/o V.P.) significantly degraded performance, especially on the challenging Wooden Block Stacking task. TSR dropped to 0%, FSR to 0%, and SSR to 41.67%. The detailed error analysis showed a drastic increase in Vision Errors (42.86% vs. 20.51% for full SeeDo) and Spatial Errors (87.5% vs. 64.10%). This confirms that visual prompts are vital for enhancing the VLM's ability to differentiate visually similar objects and accurately understand their precise spatial relationships.

**Failure Case Analysis (Figure 5):**
SeeDo generally exhibited lower error rates across all three categories (Vision, Spatial, Temporal) compared to baselines. Its strength particularly lies in a significantly lower temporal error rate, demonstrating effective understanding of the action sequence. However, **spatial errors remained the predominant source of failure for SeeDo.** This is attributed to the inherent limitations in the spatial intelligence of current VLMs and occasional inaccuracies or mismatches within the visual prompting module's object tracking and VLM's textual descriptions.

**Real-world Deployment:**
The generated task plans were successfully deployed on a Universal Robots' UR10e cobot in real-world scenarios. While the evaluation metrics focused on plan accuracy, the real-world experiments demonstrated the practical viability of SeeDo's approach. The authors noted that hard-coded action primitives sometimes led to execution failures (e.g., blocks bouncing due to high drop distance), indicating areas for future improvement in robot adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "VLM See, Robot Do" research makes several significant contributions to the fields of robotics and embodied AI, with substantial potential impact:

**Significance:**
- **Novel VLM-Centric Framework:** SeeDo introduces a pioneering modular architecture that effectively leverages VLMs to interpret complex, long-horizon human demonstration videos into robot task plans. This is a crucial step towards making robots learn from readily available human-centric data.
- **Bridging Human-Robot Domain Gap with VLM Intelligence:** By employing VLMs for common sense reasoning and plan generation, SeeDo offers a more robust and generalizable alternative to traditional imitation learning. It shifts the paradigm from direct policy learning to interpretable plan generation, which is less susceptible to appearance shifts and embodiment differences between human demos and robot execution.
- **Enhanced VLM Capabilities for Robotics:** The paper demonstrates that by strategically integrating preprocessing modules—Keyframe Selection and Visual Prompting—VLM's inherent weaknesses in temporal understanding, fine-grained object differentiation, and precise spatial reasoning can be significantly mitigated for complex robotic tasks. This provides a blueprint for future VLM applications in embodied AI.
- **Comprehensive Benchmarking and Metrics:** The curation of a new, diverse benchmark of long-horizon pick-and-place tasks with human demonstration videos, coupled with novel, granular evaluation metrics (TSR, FSR, SSR, and error type analysis), provides a valuable resource for future research in this domain. These metrics offer a more nuanced assessment of task understanding than conventional success rates.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment of SeeDo-generated plans on a real robot arm (UR10e) validates the practical utility and robustness of the approach, moving beyond purely simulated environments.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Democratization of Robot Programming:** SeeDo could dramatically simplify the process of teaching robots new tasks. Instead of complex programming or extensive data collection, non-expert users could demonstrate tasks via video, making robotics more accessible to a wider audience and accelerating robot deployment in various settings (e.g., home, small businesses).
- **Leveraging Internet-Scale Data:** The ability to learn from human demonstration videos opens the door for robots to leverage the vast, continuously growing repository of instructional content available on the internet, leading to more generalized and capable robot behaviors without needing specialized robot data.
- **Foundation for More Complex Robotic Skills:** While currently focused on pick-and-place, the modular planning approach of SeeDo provides a strong foundation for extending to more complex manipulation tasks, sequential decision-making, and even collaborative human-robot interaction.
- **Advances in VLM-Robotics Integration:** The insights gained from SeeDo regarding VLM strengths and weaknesses, and how to effectively augment them, will inform the design of future VLMs specifically tailored for embodied intelligence, fostering the development of "spatially intelligent" and "temporally aware" models.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its significant advancements, the paper acknowledges several limitations, which also define exciting avenues for future research:
- **Limited Action Space:** The current work is confined to pick-and-place actions. Future work needs to expand SeeDo's capabilities to include a broader and more complex range of robot behaviors and behavioral logic.
- **Limited Spatial Intelligence:** Although significantly improved by visual prompting, VLMs still struggle with precise spatial reasoning, particularly in tasks requiring highly accurate relative positioning. This calls for fundamental advancements in VLMs' intrinsic spatial understanding.
- **Under-defined Spatial Positioning:** SeeDo currently describes relative positions using a high-level, limited set of spatial relations (e.g., "in," "on top of"). Future work aims to extract more precise, quantitative spatial positioning information from demonstration videos for fine manipulation tasks.
- **Adaptability of Action Primitives:** The reliance on hard-coded action primitives in real-world deployment sometimes led to failures due to a lack of adaptability. Future improvements would involve incorporating more adaptive or learnable robot policies to execute the plans.
- **Real-world Execution Evaluation:** The current evaluation focuses on the accuracy of the generated plan, not the success rate of the actual physical execution. Future work should integrate robust real-world execution success metrics.

Overall, "VLM See, Robot Do" represents a substantial step forward in enabling robots to learn intuitively from human demonstrations, paving the way for more capable, adaptable, and easily programmable robotic systems.
