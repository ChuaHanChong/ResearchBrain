# From Region Arrival to Instance-Level Grounding in Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced new object-instance-centric metrics and a plug-and-play refinement module, REALM, to address the "Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap" in Vision-and-Language Navigation. REALM consistently improved object navigation success and visual grounding across various VLN backbones in both simulated and real-world robot deployments, leading to more precise object-centric positioning.

## Problem

- Conventional Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) evaluation metrics (e.g., 3-meter radius success) primarily reward regional proximity to a waypoint, not precise object-level positioning.
- Agents deemed "successful" by existing metrics often stop far from the actual target instance or in orientations where the object is not visible, preventing downstream interaction.
- A lack of fine-grained, object-instance-centric metrics made it difficult to diagnose failures in achieving functional object perception beyond coarse region arrival.

## Method

- Developed **REALM** (Region-to-Entity Alignment for Last-3-Meter Navigation), a plug-and-play ==refinement module== that decouples fine-grained object approaching from long-horizon navigation.
- Introduced object-instance-centric evaluation metrics (==Object Navigation Success==, ==Grounding Success==, ==Oracle Grounding Success==) to precisely quantify navigation precision and visual grounding.
- Created **REVERIE-AIM**, a modified dataset with object-centric goals and short-horizon training samples, and incorporated a ==visibility-aware stop penalty== for training **REALM**'s refinement policy.

## Results

- Initial evaluations showed that over **80%** of navigations considered "successful" by conventional 3-meter metrics failed to achieve instance-level positioning (ONS@0.1m) or reliable target visibility (OracleGS).
- REALM consistently improved instance-centric metrics across **four** diverse VLN backbones in simulation, with ONS@0.1m seeing up to a **107%** relative gain (e.g., ETPNav-ZS from **7.07%** to **14.66%**) and OracleGS showing similar boosts.
- In real-world deployment on a Hello Robot Stretch, REALM substantially enhanced performance for the UniNaVid backbone, increasing ONS@0.5m from **8.33%** to **33.33%** and Grounding Success (GS) from **8.33%** to **25.00%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap" is a critical limitation in current VLN, where a high percentage of conventionally "successful" navigations fail to achieve instance-level positioning or reliable target visibility.
> - A decoupled refinement module is effective for bridging this gap, enabling existing VLN backbones to improve fine-grained object interaction without requiring architectural modifications or full retraining.
> - A visibility-aware stopping strategy is crucial for encouraging agents to continue moving until the target object is perceived, significantly improving the final viewpoint's alignment and groundability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) aims to develop embodied agents capable of navigating physical environments by following natural language instructions. Initially, much of the research focused on discrete environments where agents moved along predefined navigation graphs (e.g., R2R, RxR). A significant evolution in the field occurred with the introduction of continuous environments (VLN-CE), such as R2R-CE and RxR-CE, which enabled more realistic, free-form movement and introduced challenges related to low-level control and obstacle avoidance.

A more complex sub-task, Remote Embodied Visual Referring Expression in Real Indoor Environments (REVERIE), was introduced to require agents not only to navigate to a region but also to localize a specific target object within that region. REVERIE-CE further adapted this task to continuous environments. Existing methods for REVERIE-CE, such as Dynam3D and D3D-VLP, or adapted general VLN-CE paradigms like graph-based models (ETPNav), video-based models (UniNaVid), and zero-shot approaches (SmartWay), often achieve success rates based on stopping within a 3-meter radius of a pre-annotated endpoint.

This paper identifies a critical limitation in these conventional evaluation protocols: they primarily reward regional proximity and largely disregard the agent's final orientation or whether the target object is actually visible and localizable from the final viewpoint. This limitation means that an agent can be deemed successful by existing metrics while still being unable to perceive or interact with the intended object. This work positions itself as addressing this "Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap," which is crucial for bridging high-level language understanding with practical, object-level visual grounding necessary for downstream interaction tasks like inspection or manipulation.

While related problems of fine-grained target approaching have been explored in other embodied navigation tasks (e.g., switching strategies near targets, optimizing viewpoints via point-cloud scoring, or 6-DoF pose recovery from a reference image), these approaches often presuppose successful target localization or rely on known goal images, which do not directly apply to the VLN setting with free-form language instructions. This research differentiates itself by specifically tackling the grounding gap within the existing VLN evaluation framework and proposing a solution that is broadly applicable across diverse VLN backbones.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that conventional evaluation metrics in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), particularly for tasks like REVERIE-CE, are insufficient for real-world robotic applications. The current standard, which deems an agent successful if it stops within a 3-meter radius of a pre-annotated endpoint, often fails to ensure that the agent has achieved a functionally meaningful state for object interaction. This endpoint typically represents a navigable location *near* the target object, rather than the object instance itself. Furthermore, the agent's final orientation is not evaluated, potentially leading to scenarios where the agent stops facing a wall or away from the target, rendering the object invisible. This fundamental flaw means that a trajectory counted as "successful" may not enable the referred object to be perceived or grounded.

This limitation is formalized as the **Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap**: the discrepancy between achieving coarse-grained regional arrival and performing fine-grained object grounding. Bridging this gap is identified as essential for deploying language-conditioned navigation in any robotic system where subsequent perception, manipulation, or interaction with the referred object is required. Without reliable instance-level grounding, an agent cannot confirm an object's presence, inspect its state, or prepare for manipulation.

To address this, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Quantify the Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap:** Introduce novel, object-instance-centric metrics to precisely measure navigation precision (proximity to the target instance), target visibility, and final-view grounding capabilities, thereby diagnosing the extent of this gap in existing VLN methods.
2. **Develop a Solution to Mitigate the Gap:** Propose REALM (Region-to-Entity Alignment for Last-3-Meter Navigation), a plug-and-play refinement module designed to decouple fine-grained target approaching from long-horizon navigation. This module aims to reposition and reorient the agent to achieve a final state where the target is both proximate and visually accessible.
3. **Construct a Supporting Dataset and Training Strategy:** Create REVERIE-AIM, a modified dataset that replaces region-level endpoints with object-instance-level goals and provides a large number of short-horizon training samples specifically for the final approach stage. This is complemented by a visibility-aware stopping strategy within REALM to reduce premature termination and improve final viewpoint alignment.
4. **Validate Broad Applicability:** Evaluate REALM extensively across a diverse set of existing VLN backbones (graph-based, video-based, zero-shot, fine-tuned) to demonstrate its consistent effectiveness and broad applicability in improving proximity precision and visual grounding success in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on formally defining and quantifying the "Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap" and proposing a modular solution, REALM, along with a new dataset, REVERIE-AIM, and specific training techniques.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: The Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap

The paper highlights that standard VLN-CE evaluation relies on a Success Rate (SR) metric, where an episode is successful if the agent's stopping position is within 3 meters of a pre-annotated endpoint (p_ep). This endpoint, however, is merely a neighboring waypoint and not the target object itself. For practical embodied instruction following, the final agent state must satisfy two conditions: physical proximity to the actual target instance and visual groundability in the egocentric view with sufficient resolution. The failure of SR to ensure these conditions constitutes the Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Metrics

To provide a fine-grained diagnosis of agent performance, the paper introduces three object-instance-centric metrics, decoupling navigation from grounding:

- **Object Navigation Success (ONS):** Measures navigation precision as the minimum geodesic distance from the agent's stop position to navigable points around the target instance. Reported at thresholds τ=0.1m and τ=0.5m. This directly assesses proximity to the target object, unlike SR which measures proximity to a waypoint.
- **Grounding Success (GS):** Evaluates the accuracy of the predicted bounding box ($\hat{b}$) at the agent's final viewpoint against the ground-truth bounding box ($b^*$), independent of navigation success. An IoU of $\ge 0.5$ is required.
- **Oracle Grounding Success (OracleGS):** Determines if the ground-truth bounding box of the target instance is visible in the agent's final observation and occupies at least 1% of the camera frame area. This metric distinguishes failures due to suboptimal navigation (target not visible) from failures of the detection module itself (target visible but not detected).

#### 3.3. REALM Framework

REALM is presented as a plug-and-play, decoupled refinement module that operates in three stages:

1. **Navigation:** An upstream VLN policy ($\pi_{nav}$) follows the language instruction and navigates the agent to an initial stopping state ($s_{T_{nav}}$) where the target object is nearby according to conventional metrics.
2. **Refinement:** REALM's refinement policy ($\pi_{ref}$) takes over from $s_{T_{nav}}$. Using only egocentric RGB observations and the original instruction, it performs short-horizon object finding to reposition and reorient the agent to a refined state ($s_{T_{ref}}$) where the target is proximate, visible, and operable.
3. **Grounding:** A detection module (OWLv2) then predicts the bounding box of the target object from the agent's final observation at $s_{T_{ref}}$.

The decoupled design is justified by:
- The fundamental difference between long-horizon path planning (tens of meters) and short-horizon precise visual alignment (few meters), avoiding performance trade-offs.
- Ensuring architectural independence, allowing REALM to be compatible with diverse VLN backbones without requiring their modification.

#### 3.4. Refinement Policy (π_ref) Implementation

- **Architecture:** $\pi_{ref}$ is instantiated using UniNaVid, a video-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model. UniNaVid is selected for its temporal encoding capability suitable for sequential observations and strong pre-trained visual-linguistic representations.
- **Action Space:** The agent predicts a four-step action sequence (forward, turn left, turn right, stop) from a low-level action space.
- **Training Objective:** LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to the LLM backbone of UniNaVid, keeping the vision encoder frozen. The baseline imitation learning objective minimizes token-level cross-entropy ($L_{base}$).
- **Visibility-Aware Stop Penalty (VSP):** To address premature stopping, a specific penalty ($L_{vsp}$) is introduced. It quantifies how strongly the model favors the 'stop' action and applies a penalty only when the target is *not* visible. This encourages the agent to continue moving until the target is perceived, without penalizing correct stops when the target is visible. The total training objective combines imitation and VSP: $L_{total} = L_{base} + \lambda_{vsp} L_{vsp}$.

#### 3.5. Target Expression Extraction and Visual Grounding

After the refinement phase, a two-step process is used for object localization:
1. **Target Expression Extraction:** Given that instructions often contain both navigation cues and object descriptions, a BERT-Large extractive QA model, fine-tuned on GPT-4o-mini annotated instruction-phrase pairs, extracts the specific target noun phrase ($\hat{r}$) from the full instruction.
2. **Visual Grounding:** The extracted target phrase $\hat{r}$ is then passed as a text query to OWLv2, an open-vocabulary object detector, to predict the final bounding box ($\hat{b}$) in the agent’s final egocentric observation ($O_{T_{ref}}$).

#### 3.6. REVERIE-AIM Dataset Construction

To facilitate training and evaluation of instance-level grounding, the REVERIE-AIM dataset is constructed from the original REVERIE dataset through three stages:

1. **Continuous Trajectory Reconstruction:** Discrete navigation graph-based trajectories from REVERIE are remapped and interpolated into the Habitat continuous simulation environment, creating physically plausible and smooth continuous movements.
2. **Object-Centric Goal Alignment:** The original region-level goal annotations are replaced with precise instance-level object-centric endpoints. This is achieved by integrating the annotation protocol from the Habitat Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav) task, using Matterport3D scene hierarchy to map instance identifiers and transfer ObjectNav's sampling locations. This reduces the average minimum geodesic distance to the target from 1.32m to 0.34m.
3. **Short-Horizon Training Data Generation:** A dedicated set of approximately 180,000 short-horizon samples is generated for the refinement stage. Initial states are sampled by perturbing the second-to-last node of reference paths (spatial perturbation using truncated Gaussian, viewpoint augmentation with random heading angles). Shortest paths to target-surrounding locations are computed, and corresponding visual observation sequences and low-level action labels are extracted.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of REALM across various VLN backbones in both simulated and real-world environments, primarily focusing on its ability to bridge the Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap.

#### 4.1. Validation of the Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap

Initial evaluations using the newly proposed metrics (ONS, GS, OracleGS) on existing VLN backbones (ETPNav-FT) confirmed the existence and severity of the grounding gap. ETPNav-FT achieved a 34.67% Success Rate (SR) under the conventional 3-meter criterion but only 6.32% ONS@0.1m and 11.31% OracleGS. This indicates that over 80% of "successful" navigations, by standard metrics, failed to achieve actual instance-level positioning and reliable target visibility. This consistent discrepancy across various backbones highlights a systematic issue within current VLN evaluation protocols.

#### 4.2. Simulator Results

Table 2 details the performance of REALM across four diverse VLN-CE backbones (ETPNav-ZS, ETPNav-FT, UniNaVid-ZS, SmartWay) on the REVERIE-AIM val-unseen split.

- **Consistent Improvements:** REALM (Ours (Full)) consistently and significantly improved performance across all instance-centric metrics (ONS@0.1m, ONS@0.5m, GS, OracleGS) compared to all alternative move policies, including no refinement, random walk, heuristic CLIP-Search, and vanilla UniNaVid.
 - For **ETPNav-ZS**, REALM elevated ONS@0.1m from 7.07% (no move) to 14.66% (a 107% relative gain) and OracleGS from 6.99% to 16.52%.
 - For **ETPNav-FT**, ONS@0.1m increased from 6.32% to 17.19%, and GS rose from 4.46% to 8.48%.
 - For **UniNaVid-ZS**, ONS@0.1m improved from 13.61% to 14.96% and OracleGS from 12.20% to 16.37%.
 - For **SmartWay**, OracleGS saw a substantial boost from 4.69% to 15.92%.
- **Effectiveness of Visibility-Aware Stop Penalty (VSP):** The ablation study (Ours w/o VSP vs. Ours (Full)) demonstrated that the VSP component was crucial. Removing VSP consistently led to performance drops across all settings (e.g., ONS@0.1m fell by 2.53% for UniNaVid-ZS without VSP). This validates that the VSP successfully suppressed premature termination and encouraged decisive stops based on explicit object perception.
- **Gap to Human Performance:** Despite the significant improvements, a substantial performance gap remained between REALM and Human Eval. For example, on ONS@0.5m, REALM's best performance was 25.89% (ETPNav-FT + Ours (Full)) compared to Human Eval's 74.71%. This indicates ample room for future advancements.

#### 4.3. Real-world Results

To assess real-world applicability, REALM was deployed on a Hello Robot Stretch platform. Twelve diverse episodes were collected in various indoor environments.

- **Enhanced Real-world Performance:** The vanilla UniNaVid policy exhibited severe struggles in the physical environment, achieving an 8.33% ONS@0.5m, 8.33% GS, and 16.67% OracleGS, attributed to motor noise and limited exposure to REVERIE-style instructions during training. In contrast, the integration of REALM (UniNaVid + Ours) substantially improved performance across all metrics:
 - ONS@0.5m increased from 8.33% to 33.33%.
 - GS improved from 8.33% to 25.00%.
 - OracleGS also rose from 16.67% to 25.00%.

These gains indicate that REALM's visibility-aware strategy successfully corrects for upstream policy drift and aligns the robot's heading, effectively bridging the Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap to provide a more reliable perception interface for physical interaction in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions that have significant implications for the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation and embodied AI:

1. **Formal Identification of a Critical Gap:** The explicit identification and formalization of the "Last-3-Meter Grounding Gap" highlight a fundamental limitation in current VLN evaluation protocols. By demonstrating that high scores in conventional metrics do not guarantee functional object-level grounding, the work pushes the community toward more rigorous and task-relevant assessment criteria.
2. **Development of Instance-Centric Metrics:** The introduction of Object Navigation Success (ONS), Grounding Success (GS), and Oracle Grounding Success (OracleGS) provides a more comprehensive and diagnostic set of metrics. These metrics enable researchers to better understand where navigation failures occur (proximity vs. visibility vs. detection) and guide the development of more robust agents.
3. **Introduction of a Generalizable Solution (REALM):** REALM offers a practical, plug-and-play, and architecture-agnostic refinement module. Its decoupled design is a significant advantage, allowing it to be integrated with diverse existing and future VLN backbones without requiring retraining of the entire upstream system. This promotes reusability and accelerates research in fine-grained navigation.
4. **Creation of a Specialized Dataset (REVERIE-AIM):** The REVERIE-AIM dataset, with its continuous trajectories, object-centric goals, and dedicated short-horizon training samples, directly addresses the need for fine-grained supervision during the crucial last-meters approach. This dataset will likely serve as a valuable benchmark for future research focusing on precise object-centric navigation and grounding.
5. **Demonstrated Robustness and Real-World Applicability:** The extensive evaluations across four diverse VLN backbones, coupled with real-world deployment on a physical robot, validate REALM's effectiveness and broad applicability. The significant improvements observed in both simulation and the physical environment demonstrate its potential to make embodied agents more functional and reliable for real-world tasks requiring interaction with specific objects.
6. **Enabling Downstream Interaction Tasks:** By ensuring that an agent not only arrives in the vicinity of an object but also achieves a viewpoint where the object is visible and localizable, this work provides a critical perceptual interface. This is essential for enabling subsequent manipulation, inspection, or other forms of fine-grained interaction, moving VLN closer to truly useful robotic capabilities.

While acknowledging that a substantial gap remains between current agent performance and human capabilities, this work lays foundational groundwork by clearly defining a critical problem, providing tools for its measurement, and proposing an effective, generalizable solution. Future research can build upon these contributions by exploring more active perception strategies, sophisticated reasoning about partial observability, and more intelligent viewpoint selection for fine-grained object-centric navigation.
