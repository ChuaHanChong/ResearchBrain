# OSCAR: Omni-Embodiment Skeleton-Conditioned World Action Model for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> OSCAR, a video world model for robotics, leverages a large, diverse dataset and 2D kinematic skeleton rendering to predict future states based on robot actions. It demonstrates precise action following and generalization across various robot embodiments and human hands, achieving an FVD of 7.08 and an FID of 15.07, and strong correlation with real-world policy evaluation (Pearson r of +0.852).

## Problem

- Existing video world models frequently struggle with precise action following, which undermines the reliability of downstream policy evaluation.
- Limited diversity in current robot training datasets hinders models' ability to generalize across a wide array of tasks and environmental conditions.
- Many explicit-conditioning methods tend to overfit to specific robot appearances, restricting their generalization across different robot embodiments.

## Method

- A four-stage data pipeline was developed to curate, filter, semantically deduplicate, and caption a large-scale, diverse dataset encompassing robot and egocentric human videos.
- 2D kinematic skeleton rendering was adopted as a unified, texture-free conditioning representation, derived from URDF models for robots and MANO models for human hands.
- An existing 2B-parameter video ==Diffusion== ==Transformer== (DiT), Cosmos-Predict2.5, was finetuned to incorporate the rendered skeleton video as an additional, frame-aligned conditioned input stream.

## Results

- OSCAR achieved superior quantitative performance compared to seven baselines, including larger models, demonstrating an FVD of 7.08, FID of 15.07, and PSNR of 24.24.
- The model exhibited precise action following and high visual quality in generated videos across multiple robot embodiments and for human hand interactions.
- Virtual policy evaluations performed with OSCAR showed a strong correlation with real-world RoboArena policy rankings, achieving a Pearson r of +0.852 and a mean absolute success rate error of 1.73 percentage points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The use of standardized 2D kinematic skeleton rendering offers an effective balance between providing explicit action guidance and ensuring embodiment-agnostic generalization.
> - Integrating diverse human egocentric data, processed with the same skeleton representation, leads to positive transfer and improved performance on robot manipulation tasks.
> - Large-scale semantic deduplication and meticulous filtering of heterogeneous video data are essential for constructing a high-quality and generalizable training corpus for video world models.
