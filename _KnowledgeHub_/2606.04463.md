# OSCAR: Omni-Embodiment Skeleton-Conditioned World Action Model for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> OSCAR, a video world model for robotics, leverages a large, diverse dataset and 2D kinematic skeleton rendering to predict future states based on robot actions. It demonstrates precise action following and generalization across various robot embodiments and human hands, achieving an FVD of 7.08 and an FID of 15.07, and strong correlation with real-world policy evaluation (Pearson r of +0.852).

## Problem

- Existing video world models frequently struggle with precise action following, which undermines the reliability of downstream policy evaluation.
- Limited diversity in current robot training datasets hinders models' ability to generalize across a wide array of tasks and environmental conditions.
- Many explicit-conditioning methods tend to overfit to specific robot appearances, restricting their generalization across different robot embodiments.

## Method

- A four-stage data pipeline was developed to curate, filter, semantically deduplicate, and caption a large-scale, diverse dataset encompassing robot and egocentric human videos.
- 2D kinematic skeleton rendering was adopted as a unified, texture-free conditioning representation, derived from URDF models for robots and MANO models for human hands.
- An existing 2B-parameter video ==Diffusion== ==Transformer== (DiT), Cosmos-Predict2.5, was finetuned to incorporate the rendered skeleton video as an additional, frame-aligned conditioned input stream.

## Results

- OSCAR achieved superior quantitative performance compared to seven baselines, including larger models, demonstrating an FVD of 7.08, FID of 15.07, and PSNR of 24.24.
- The model exhibited precise action following and high visual quality in generated videos across multiple robot embodiments and for human hand interactions.
- Virtual policy evaluations performed with OSCAR showed a strong correlation with real-world RoboArena policy rankings, achieving a Pearson r of +0.852 and a mean absolute success rate error of 1.73 percentage points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The use of standardized 2D kinematic skeleton rendering offers an effective balance between providing explicit action guidance and ensuring embodiment-agnostic generalization.
> - Integrating diverse human egocentric data, processed with the same skeleton representation, leads to positive transfer and improved performance on robot manipulation tasks.
> - Large-scale semantic deduplication and meticulous filtering of heterogeneous video data are essential for constructing a high-quality and generalizable training corpus for video world models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" is identified as a significant component in the advancement of generalist robots, driven by recent progress in large-scale video diffusion models. These models predict future states based on robot action sequences, enabling capabilities such as policy evaluation, reinforcement learning in virtual environments, and action planning pretraining.

However, the paper identifies three challenges in developing generalizable action-conditioned world models for faithful robot policy evaluation:
1. **Precision in Action Following:** Generated videos must accurately reflect action conditions at both frame and pixel levels to provide meaningful signals for policy evaluation.
2. **Scenario Diversity:** Models require generalization across a wide range of tasks, environments, and action sequences for comprehensive policy evaluation.
3. **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** World models need to adapt to different robot embodiments rather than being limited to specific designs, to facilitate broader adoption.

Existing action-conditioned world models are categorized by their approach to action conditioning:
- **Latent-action methods** (e.g., AdaWorld, DreamDojo, IRASim, DreamZero, Ctrl-World): These compress robot states and actions into learned embeddings. While capable of handling multiple embodiments during training, they often result in imprecise action following due to the need for the model to infer spatio-temporal motion from a compressed latent representation.
- **Explicit-conditioning methods** (e.g., Kinema4D, ORV, EnerVerse-AC, Genie-Envisioner, VAP): These render actions into video streams that are spatially aligned with RGB frames. This approach can offer higher precision but often involves a trade-off with generalization. Dense pointmaps (Kinema4D) can be accurate for in-domain robots but may overfit to appearance, leading to degraded performance in out-of-distribution scenarios. End-effector or gripper-only renders (EnerVerse-AC, Genie-Envisioner) are more robust but may lack full-arm motion information. VAP uses skeleton rendering, which balances these aspects but has been limited by its dataset scale and lack of filtering.

In the context of policy evaluation, previous works such as SIMPLER, IRASim, WorldEval, EnerVerse-AC, WorldGym, Ctrl-World, GE-Sim, and Scalable Policy Evaluation have explored using simulations or video world models as proxies for real-robot policy evaluation. These efforts typically evaluate policies open-loop from pre-recorded actions or in closed-loop settings, with success rates assessed by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or human evaluators.

This work, OSCAR, positions itself as an advancement in explicit-conditioning methods by adopting 2D kinematic skeleton rendering for unified conditioning. It addresses the data scale and quality limitations observed in previous skeleton-based approaches like VAP by integrating a large-scale, standardized data pipeline. By doing so, OSCAR aims to achieve precise action following and cross-embodiment generalization, particularly for the application of robot policy evaluation, building upon the foundational capabilities of video diffusion models like Cosmos-Predict2.5.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general-purpose action-conditioned world model for robotics, named OSCAR, that achieves precise action following and generalizes across different robot embodiments. This model is specifically designed to enable reliable robot policy evaluation in virtual environments.

The motivation for this work stems from three interconnected challenges identified in existing video world models when applied to real-world robot evaluation:

1. **Limited Scenario Diversity in Current Robot Training Datasets:** Many existing robotics datasets offer a narrow distribution of environments, objects, tasks, or robot embodiments. This lack of diversity can hinder a world model's ability to generalize to novel situations and provide a comprehensive evaluation of generalist robot policies. For instance, datasets like AgiBot, despite their large scale, often contain significant redundancy in environments or tasks, which inflates clip counts without adding much scene diversity.
2. **Imprecise Action Following:** For a world model to provide meaningful signals for downstream policy evaluation, the generated video must precisely adhere to the given action conditions. This precision is required at both the frame-level (when an action occurs) and the pixel-level (where an action happens). Prior latent-action methods often struggle with this due to the implicit nature of their action signals, while some explicit-conditioning methods, such as dense pointmaps, can overfit to specific appearances.
3. **Poor Generalization Across Embodiments:** Most existing world models tend to focus on particular robot embodiments, limiting their broad adoption and utility for evaluating policies designed for various robot platforms. A truly generalist robot requires a world model capable of understanding and generating actions for different robot arms, grippers, or even human hands.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a two-pronged approach:

- **Large-scale Standardized Data Pipeline:** The motivation here is to overcome the data scarcity and heterogeneity issues prevalent in robotics. By curating, filtering, and deduplicating diverse robotics and egocentric human datasets, the aim is to create a clean, high-quality, and large-scale joint-training dataset. This dataset is designed to span a broad range of tasks, scenarios, actions, and robot embodiments, thereby enhancing the model's capacity for generalization.
- **Unified Kinematic Skeleton Rendering:** The motivation for this conditioning representation is to find a balance between precision and generalization. By using 2D kinematic skeleton rendering, the model receives explicit guidance on robot actions without tightly coupling motion with embodiment-specific appearance textures. This allows a single representation to generalize across different robot arms or human hands, where only the underlying kinematic specification needs to be updated for different embodiments.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to significantly reduce the cost and accelerate the iteration speed of robot policy development by enabling reliable and efficient policy evaluation within virtually generated worlds. This would minimize the need for extensive real-world robot deployments for testing, which are often time-consuming, expensive, and resource-intensive.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OSCAR is developed as an action-conditioned video world model primarily built upon the Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B model, a 2-billion parameter Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) utilizing a rectified-flow objective. The model employs a WAN 2.1 VAE to encode and decode video frames into and from a spatio-temporal latent representation.

The methodology for OSCAR integrates two main components: a large-scale standardized data pipeline and a unified conditioning representation based on skeleton rendering.

#### 3.1. Data Pipeline

A four-stage data pipeline was implemented to create a diverse, high-quality, and large-scale robotic video dataset. Starting with 2,165,359 source videos, the pipeline filtered down to 180,657 episodes.

- **Data Curation:** The process began by collecting data from both robotics and human video sources to ensure broad scene coverage.
 - **Robot Datasets:** Five robot datasets were curated: RH20T-cfg5 (Franka Panda), InternData-A1 (Franka Panda, synthetic), DROID (Franka Panda), RH20T-cfg7 (KUKA iiwa), AgiBot-Beta (AgiBot G1), and AIROA-MoMa (Toyota HSR). These totaled approximately 1.74 million episodes and 10.9 thousand hours.
 - **Human Datasets:** Two egocentric human video datasets, EgoDex and EPIC-Kitchens, were included, totaling about 428,000 episodes and 929 hours.
- **Data Filtering:** To mitigate noise and heterogeneity, four specific filters were applied:
 - **Length:** Episodes had to contain at least 70 video frames to ensure sufficient rollout duration.
 - **Static Camera:** Only episodes with camera movements below a certain threshold were kept, focusing on robot action rather than camera motion.
 - **Meaningful Action:** Episodes required a non-trivial manipulator action sequence to remove static clips.
 - **Visible Skeleton:** Episodes where the robot skeleton remained visible within video frames for a sufficient percentage were retained.
- **Semantic Deduplication:** This stage addressed redundancy in both visual scenes and action trajectories to expose the model to diverse environments.
 - **Visual Clustering:** SigLIP image embeddings were computed from five uniformly sampled frames per episode. Pairs with a cosine similarity above 0.95 were flagged for further verification.
 - **Trajectory Verification:** For visually similar pairs, a 64-step resampled manipulator trajectory was extracted. The root-mean-square (RMS) distance between these trajectories was calculated, and a pair was marked as a duplicate only if its RMS fell below an adaptive threshold. This prevents filtering clips with similar backgrounds but distinct motions.
- **Data Captioning:** All retained episodes were captioned using Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct. Input frames were sampled at 15 fps, with DROID episodes sampled at 1–2 fps due to their longer duration.

#### 3.2. Unified Conditioning: Skeleton Rendering

This method balances generalization and precision by providing explicit action guidance without tightly coupling with embodiment-specific appearance.

- **Skeleton Rendering Process:**
 - Given a URDF model (M) and joint configuration (q_t) at time *t*, forward kinematics yields SE(3) poses for each link.
 - A canonical point on each link (o_k) is projected onto 2D pixel coordinates using camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
 - These projected points and the kinematic tree’s edges are rasterized onto a black canvas (S_t). Line segments connect projected endpoints of edges, and small filled circles mark projected vertices. This results in a 2D visualization of the kinematic chain, devoid of texture.
- **Conditioning Injection:** The skeleton rendering (S_1:T), treated as a second RGB video stream, is fed through the same WAN 2.1 VAE to produce a skeleton latent (z_s). This latent is summed with the target video latent (z_v_t), and the combined representation is fed into the DiT for denoising.
- **Extension to Human Hands:** The same skeleton rendering technique is applied to human hands by substituting the robot's kinematic triple (M, q_t, o_k) with those of a MANO hand model. This allows incorporating broad human egocentric demonstrations into the training data, increasing diversity in scenarios, tasks, and action distributions.

#### 3.3. Training Details

OSCAR was finetuned on a single GH200 GPU in two stages:
- **Stage 1:** 15,000 iterations on the four robot embodiments (Franka Panda, KUKA iiwa, AgiBot G1, Toyota HSR).
- **Stage 2:** Continued training on the full robot-plus-human data mixture.
The AdamW optimizer was used with a learning rate of 3x10^-5 and a batch size of 16. Timesteps were sampled from a logit-normal distribution with reweighting. Batches were drawn using frequency-tempered weights (w_i ∝ n_frames,i^(1/3)) to balance different embodiment sources. The start frame for each 81-frame training window was biased towards grasp and release events. Classifier-free guidance was employed during training (0.2 probability of zeroing S_1:T) and inference (guidance scale w=6).

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol

- **Quantitative Comparison:** A self-curated benchmark of 200 robot manipulation clips from six datasets across four embodiments was used. Metrics included reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), temporal coherence (tLPIPS), distribution fidelity (FVD, FID, latent-ℓ2), and speed (FPS). Comparisons were made against seven baselines (text-only, latent-action, explicit-geometry).
- **Policy Evaluation:** OSCAR was deployed to evaluate real-robot policies from the RoboArena leaderboard, specifically seven open-source DROID generalist policies. Camera intrinsics were estimated with MoGe-v2 and extrinsics with CtRNet-X. Rollouts were performed autoregressively from the recorded first frame and given robot action. GPT-5 was used as a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to evaluate binary success rates and partial progress for each episode. Performance was assessed using Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV), Spearman ρ, Pearson r, and Success Rate Difference (SISR∆).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding OSCAR's performance in action-conditioned video generation and its utility for robot policy evaluation.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Baselines

OSCAR demonstrated competitive performance against existing world models across various metrics and embodiments.
- **Quantitative Superiority:** In quantitative comparisons (Table 2), OSCAR achieved the best or second-best results across most metrics, including reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), distribution fidelity (FVD, FID, latent-L2), and temporal coherence (tLPIPS). Notably, OSCAR, with its 2-billion parameter size, consistently outperformed Kinema4D, which is a much larger model (14 billion parameters). For instance, OSCAR achieved an FVD of 7.08 and an FID of 15.07, significantly better than Kinema4D's FVD of 17.07 and FID of 37.16. Its inference speed (FPS) was also competitive, ranking favorably among the top performers.
- **Qualitative Fidelity:** Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4, 5, 6) illustrated OSCAR's ability to generate videos with high visual quality and precise action following, surpassing many baselines.
- **Critique of Baseline Approaches:**
 - Text-only control (e.g., TesserAct, Cosmos-Predict2.5) proved least effective due to language's inherent imprecision in describing complex action sequences.
 - Latent-action guidance (e.g., IRASim, Ctrl-World) showed limitations in cross-embodiment generalization, as fixed kinematic layouts became out-of-distribution when transferred between different robot configurations (e.g., bimanual vs. single-arm setups).
 - Explicit, spatially aligned guidance (e.g., VAP, EnerVerse-AC, Genie Envisioner, Kinema4D) generally performed better. Among these, dense point-map methods (Kinema4D) exhibited a tendency to overfit in-distribution data, whereas OSCAR's skeleton condition offered a better balance between accuracy and generalization.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments focused on the impact of conditioning representation and data composition.
- **Conditioning Representation (Table 3, top block):** When trained purely on robotics datasets, skeleton rendering showed statistical equivalence in performance across all seven metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, tLPIPS, FVD, FID, L2 latent) compared to mesh rendering. However, skeleton rendering was chosen as the primary method due to its independence from robot-specific URDF assets and its ability to incorporate human data more flexibly. Latent action conditioning consistently underperformed, confirming its struggle with precise action cues.
- **Data Strategy (Table 3, bottom block):**
 - The inclusion of human data in the training mixture consistently improved the model's performance over robot-only training, indicating a positive transfer of learned visual and motion priors from human demonstrations to robot scenarios.
 - A warm-start strategy, where the model was initially trained on robot data and then continued with a robot-human mixture (Stage 2 of training), yielded the best overall results. This strategy led to the final model configuration that achieved the highest quantitative scores.

#### 4.3. Policy Evaluation

OSCAR was deployed for policy evaluation on the RoboArena leaderboard, testing seven DROID generalist policies.
- **Strong Correlation with Real-World Evaluation (Table 4, Figure 1):** Virtual policy evaluation using OSCAR demonstrated a significant correlation with real-world deployment outcomes. This was evidenced by high Pearson correlation (r = +0.852) and Spearman correlation (ρ = +0.750), coupled with a low Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV = 0.571) and Success Rate Difference (SISR∆ = 1.73 percentage points). These metrics indicate that OSCAR's virtual evaluations reliably reflect the relative performance of policies in the real world.
- **Effectiveness of Skeleton Conditioning for Policy Evaluation:** Among the tested conditioning representations, skeleton rendering provided the strongest correlation with real-world deployment outcomes, outperforming both latent-action and mesh renderings. This finding reinforces the suitability of skeleton rendering as a generalizable and precise action representation for world models intended for policy evaluation.
- **VLM Success Scoring:** The VLM (GPT-5) used for evaluating success rates in OSCAR rollouts was found to be conservative, tending to under-report success compared to human evaluators. However, it demonstrated high specificity in identifying failures (0.90) and its binary verdicts correlated well above chance with human labels (Pearson r = 0.58).

In summary, OSCAR not only establishes improved capabilities in generating action-conditioned videos with high fidelity and generalization across embodiments but also demonstrates practical utility as a proxy for real-world robot policy evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OSCAR represents a notable advancement in the field of action-conditioned world models for robotics, with several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Technological Advancement in World Models:** OSCAR introduces a precise action-conditioned video world model capable of cross-embodiment generalization. By leveraging a unified 2D kinematic skeleton rendering and a carefully curated large-scale dataset, the model achieves improved performance in action following, appearance quality, and motion consistency. This is particularly significant as it outperforms models with considerably larger parameter counts and computational resource requirements, demonstrating efficiency alongside high fidelity.
- **Reduced Cost and Accelerated Development for Robotics:** One of the most impactful contributions is the demonstration of a strong correlation between virtual policy evaluation using OSCAR and real-world evaluation on platforms like RoboArena. This paves the way for a future where robot policies can be primarily evaluated in virtually generated worlds. Such a capability could substantially reduce the significant costs, time, and physical resources associated with real-world robot experimentation and accelerate the iterative development cycle of robot policies.
- **Enhanced Generalization Across Robot Embodiments:** The use of 2D kinematic skeleton rendering as a unified conditioning representation allows OSCAR to generalize across diverse robot arms and even human hands. This cross-embodiment capability broadens the applicability of world models, making them more versatile tools for developing and testing generalist robot policies, rather than being confined to specific hardware configurations.
- **Robust Data Curation Methodology:** The development of a standardized, four-stage data pipeline for curating, filtering, and deduplicating raw robotics and egocentric human video datasets is a significant methodological contribution. This approach addresses the common challenge of data heterogeneity and redundancy in robotics, producing a clean, high-quality, and diverse training corpus crucial for training robust world models. The ability to integrate human egocentric demonstrations further enhances the model's understanding of manipulation tasks in varied contexts.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** OSCAR establishes a robust foundation for further research in embodied AI. By providing a reliable virtual testing ground, it can facilitate advancements in reinforcement learning, robot action planning, and the development of more sophisticated generalist robot behaviors. The release of code, data, and trained checkpoints further supports the research community in building upon this work.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges limitations, including the current data scale being constrained by the availability and quality of per-dataset camera calibration and kinematic annotations. Errors in these annotations directly impact skeleton-RGB alignment, limiting the usable raw video data. Additionally, the model's current 2B-parameter backbone, while efficient, suggests potential for further fidelity and generalization improvements by scaling to larger backbones, which would require increased computational resources. Addressing these limitations could lead to even more capable and broadly applicable robot world models.
