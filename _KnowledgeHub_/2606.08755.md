# Co-Evolving Skill Generation and Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> SAPO (Skill-Augmented Policy Optimization) is an online RL framework that validates candidate skills before storing them. It splits the rollout budget into matched base and skill-augmented groups under the same retrieval context, so the reward gap estimates a skill's marginal utility at no extra cost. That signal trains the policy as its own skill generator and scorer, lifting ALFWorld to 92.2 and WebShop success to 78.1 without proprietary APIs.

## Problem

- Prior skill-augmented RL methods insert newly generated skills straight into the retrievable bank without validating usefulness, assuming frontier-LLM skills are reliable.
- Existing utility tracking reads rollouts after storage and at set level, so it cannot isolate one skill's marginal contribution or catch skills going stale.
- Baselines pay repeated proprietary frontier-LLM calls for skill generation, roughly $30 per ALFWorld run, while many purchased skills carry negative utility.

## Method

- ==Matched-group prospective validation== splits the rollout budget into base rollouts under retrieved skills and skill-augmented rollouts adding one candidate; the reward gap gives marginal utility.
- ==Dual-bank curation== holds candidates in a temporary bank and promotes to long-term memory only on positive utility, top-fraction rank, and similarity below the novelty threshold.
- Utility-weighted generator training uses an asymmetric objective that suppresses negative-utility skills harder, plus ==KL distillation== so reduced-input generation likelihood scores skills for pruning and retrieval reranking.

## Results

- On Qwen2.5-7B, ALFWorld All **92.2** and WebShop success **78.1** beat SkillRL (**89.9**/**72.7**), Skill0 (**87.9**/**71.9**) and D2Skill (**87.8**/**73.4**).
- Search-augmented QA average reaches **47.8** versus **45.5** for SkillRL and **43.1** for EvolveR; with Qwen3-4B, ALFWorld All rises from **72.7** to **82.0**.
- Ablations drop ALFWorld/WebShop success to **90.6**/**75.0** without validation, **90.2**/**73.4** without generator training, **91.4**/**76.6** without likelihood scoring; GPT-5.4 and Claude-Opus-4.6 skills average near-zero utility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier-model provenance is no quality guarantee: generated skills have mixed utility, so validate before storing rather than diagnosing after retrieval.
> - Splitting an existing rollout budget into matched with-and-without groups turns routine sampling into free counterfactual credit assignment for any memory item.
> - Score utility against the current retrieval context, since a standalone-useful skill can be redundant once similar skills already occupy the prompt.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the enhancement of agentic Large Language Models (LLMs) through the integration of skills. Agentic LLMs are increasingly being applied to complex tasks requiring reasoning, tool use, and interaction with external environments. To improve these agents, recent work has explored the concept of "skills," which are defined as reusable procedural knowledge that equips LLMs with domain-specific capabilities and guides their behavior. Skills can be represented in various forms, such as natural-language instructions, executable programs, or memory units, and can be manually designed or automatically induced from an agent's experience.

Existing approaches to skill integration in LLM agents generally follow two main directions. One line of work maintains a fixed base LLM and focuses on refining external skill memory or task-specific context, as seen in methods like MCE. Another significant direction involves the co-evolution of skills alongside policy optimization, where a retrievable skill bank is updated during online reinforcement learning (RL). Examples include SkillRL, which updates a skill bank online; Skill0, which focuses on internalizing skill knowledge into the policy; and D2Skill, which manages multi-granularity skills using utility signals from rollouts.

Despite progress in skill-augmented RL, the paper identifies several limitations in existing methodologies. A primary concern is the common practice of integrating newly generated skills into a skill bank without explicit validation of their usefulness. Many methods rely on proprietary frontier LLMs for skill generation, implicitly assuming their outputs are reliable. However, the authors' preliminary findings challenge this assumption, demonstrating that even skills from advanced LLMs exhibit mixed utility, with many providing minimal benefit or even degrading performance. Once ineffective skills are stored, their negative impact is difficult to isolate due due to delayed feedback and the aggregate effect of multiple co-retrieved skills during policy rollouts. Furthermore, existing methods often incur substantial costs due to repeated API calls to proprietary LLMs for skill generation. The research aims to address these identified gaps by introducing a framework for pre-storage skill validation, context-dependent utility estimation, and policy-based skill generation and scoring, thereby enhancing skill quality, reducing costs, and improving the stability of agent learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from critical issues observed in existing skill-augmented reinforcement learning (RL) methods for language agents. The authors identified that current approaches often operate under an unreliable assumption: that newly generated skills, particularly those from proprietary Large Language Models (LLMs), are inherently useful and can be directly integrated into a skill bank.

The paper articulates three specific limitations in existing skill-augmented RL agents operating in online settings:

1. **Direct Injection of Unvalidated Skills:** Many contemporary methods, such as SkillRL, Skill0, and D2Skill, generate skills by analyzing trajectories using proprietary LLMs and then directly store these skills. The implicit assumption is that skills produced by powerful LLMs are reliable. However, the authors' preliminary experiments using GPT-5.4 demonstrated that even skills from frontier models exhibit highly mixed utility. A significant proportion of these skills offered little benefit or actively hindered agent performance. Storing such low-quality skills can lead to misleading retrieval decisions and slower policy learning, as any feedback on skill utility is often delayed until after the skill has already been stored and potentially influenced agent behavior.

2. **Lack of Context-Dependent Attribution for Individual Skills:** In current retrieval-based skill augmentation, skills are typically used in conjunction. Consequently, the observed performance feedback reflects the collective impact of multiple retrieved skills rather than the marginal contribution of any single skill. This makes it difficult to ascertain the individual value of a skill, especially its context-dependent utility (e.g., whether a skill is redundant when similar skills are already present, or if it provides unique complementary value). Tracking aggregate utility or training utility functions from set-level signals does not adequately address the need to evaluate an individual skill's marginal benefit within a specific context, which is crucial for effective skill maintenance and retrieval.

3. **High API Cost of Skill Generation:** Existing methods frequently rely on repeated calls to costly proprietary LLM APIs for skill generation during online RL training. This incurs significant financial overhead, especially given that a substantial number of these generated skills may turn out to be ineffective or harmful, as revealed by the authors' preliminary findings. Reducing this dependency without compromising skill quality is a practical challenge.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes three key objectives for its Skill-Augmented Policy Optimization (SAPO) framework:

1. **To estimate the marginal utility of newly generated skills without incurring additional rollout cost.** This objective aims to enable pre-storage validation of skills, allowing agents to filter out ineffective or harmful skills before they impact learning, all while utilizing the standard online RL rollout budget efficiently.
2. **To score a skill by its marginal contribution beyond the currently retrieved skill context.** The goal is to develop a method that can assess whether a candidate skill offers a genuinely useful, non-redundant contribution, thereby informing better skill maintenance and retrieval decisions.
3. **To reduce reliance on costly proprietary LLM APIs for skill generation without sacrificing skill quality.** This objective seeks to train the agent's policy itself to function as a skill generator, using the context-dependent utility signal as supervision, thereby making skill acquisition more autonomous and cost-effective.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Skill-Augmented Policy Optimization (SAPO)**, is an online reinforcement learning (RL) method designed to jointly manage skill induction, validation, and maintenance. It optimizes the agent policy using rewards from collected rollouts while simultaneously estimating the marginal utility of newly induced skills. This utility signal is then leveraged to curate the skill bank and train the policy to also serve as a skill generator, whose generation likelihood is subsequently reused for skill scoring, maintenance, and retrieval.

The SAPO framework consists of several integrated components.

### 4. Online Rollouts and Skill Induction

SAPO processes "skill induction units" ($X_i$), which can contain one or multiple semantically similar queries. For each unit, one candidate skill ($\hat{s}_i$) is generated. The framework maintains two skill banks: a `B_long` for validated, long-term skills and a `B_temp` for temporary candidate skills.

1. **Skill Retrieval:** For a given query $x_{i,j}$ within an induction unit $X_i$, SAPO first retrieves a set of relevant existing skills $S_{i,j}$ from both `B_long` and `B_temp`.
2. **Rollout Budget Allocation:** A total rollout budget of G trajectories is allocated per induction unit. This budget is split into two matched halves.
 - **Base Rollouts ($Y_{base_{i,j}}$):** The first half of the budget is used to generate rollouts conditioned solely on the retrieved skills $S_{i,j}$ (i.e., using the current policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot | x_{i,j}, S_{i,j})$). These trajectories provide evidence of the policy's current performance and highlight failure patterns.
 - **Candidate Skill Induction:** Based on the evidence from these base rollouts and the query context, the policy induces a candidate skill $\hat{s}_i$.
 - **Skill-Augmented Rollouts ($Y_{skill_{i,j}}$):** The remaining half of the rollout budget is then used to generate skill-augmented rollouts. These are generated under the identical task and retrieved skill context $S_{i,j}$, but with the newly induced candidate skill $\hat{s}_i$ additionally included (i.e., $\pi_\theta(\cdot | x_{i,j}, S_{i,j} \cup \{\hat{s}_i\})$).
3. **Policy Optimization:** Both the collected base and skill-augmented rollouts are used to optimize the agent policy using a standard RL algorithm, specifically GRPO.

### 5. Skill Utility Estimation and Bank Update

The matched pairs of base and skill-augmented rollouts enable direct, unbiased estimation of a candidate skill's utility without extra rollout cost.

1. **Intra-prompt Utility ($u(x_{i,j}, \hat{s}_i)$):** For each query $x_{i,j}$ and candidate skill $\hat{s}_i$, the utility is calculated as the average reward difference between the skill-augmented rollouts and the base rollouts. This quantity estimates the marginal benefit of adding $\hat{s}_i$ given the specific query and currently retrieved skills.
2. **Cross-prompt Utility ($U_{\hat{s}_i}$):** If an induction unit $X_i$ contains multiple queries ($K > 1$), the intra-prompt utilities are averaged across all queries in the unit to provide a cross-prompt utility. This assesses the skill's generalization across related tasks.
3. **Skill Promotion and Bank Update:** At the end of a defined temporary horizon ($T_{prom}$), candidate skills from `B_temp` are considered for promotion to `B_long`. A skill $s$ is promoted only if it satisfies three criteria:
 - Its cross-prompt utility $U_s$ is positive.
 - It ranks among the top $\rho$ fraction of skills in `B_temp` by utility.
 - It is sufficiently distinct from existing skills in `B_long` (i.e., its similarity to any existing long-term skill is below a novelty threshold $\gamma$). Discarded temporary skills are removed.

### 6. Policy as a Skill Generator and Scorer

SAPO reuses the utility signal to train the policy itself to both generate and score skills, reducing reliance on external LLMs.

1. **Skill Generator Training:** The policy is trained as a skill generator using an asymmetric utility-weighted objective, inspired by W-REINFORCE. This objective, $L_{gen}(\theta)$, reinforces the generation of positive-utility skills (based on intra-prompt utility $u(x_{i,j}, \hat{s}_i)$) and more strongly suppresses the generation of negative-utility skills. This directly improves the policy's ability to create useful skills.
2. **Policy-Based Skill Scoring:** The learned likelihood of the policy generating a skill, $\pi_\theta(\hat{s}_i | \dots)$, serves as an intrinsic quality score.
 - **Knowledge Distillation:** During training, a "full-mode" score (conditioned on base rollouts) is distilled into an "efficient reduced-mode" score (without rollout input). This allows efficient skill scoring during maintenance and retrieval without requiring additional rollouts.
3. **Long-Term Skill Maintenance:** When `B_long` reaches its capacity, outdated or low-value skills are removed. For each skill, its score is computed using the reduced-input likelihood (averaged over a set of relevant evaluation prompts and contextual skills, excluding the skill itself). Skills with low average scores are pruned.
4. **Retrieval-Time Skill Selection:** During task execution, SAPO first retrieves a candidate pool of skills using similarity-based retrieval. This pool is then reranked using the reduced-input skill-likelihood score. Skills are selected sequentially, with the score dynamically reflecting the marginal benefit of adding a skill given those already selected.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SAPO was evaluated across diverse interactive decision-making and search-augmented question answering (QA) tasks, utilizing Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-4B-Instruct as backbone models.

### 8. Performance on ALFWorld and WebShop

SAPO demonstrated superior performance compared to a range of baselines, including closed-source LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro), prompt-based and memory-based agentic methods (ReAct, Reflexion, Mem0, ExpeL, MemP, SimpleMem), general RL baselines (RLOO, GRPO), memory-augmented RL methods (MemRL, EvolveR, Mem0+GRPO, SimpleMem+GRPO), and prior skill-augmented RL methods (SkillRL, Skill0, D2Skill). On ALFWorld and WebShop, SAPO achieved the highest overall scores, with improvements observed across various subtasks. For instance, on ALFWorld, SAPO achieved an "All" success rate of 92.2% and a "Score" of 90.5, surpassing SkillRL's 89.9% and 85.2% respectively. This indicates that regulating the quality of skills entering the bank is beneficial for learning efficiency and final performance. Similar gains were observed when using the smaller Qwen3-4B-Instruct backbone.

### 9. Performance on Search-Augmented QA

In search-augmented QA tasks, encompassing both single-hop (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA) and multi-hop (HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue, Bamboogle) datasets, SAPO consistently achieved the best average performance. The improvements were evident on both in-domain (NQ, HotpotQA) and out-of-domain datasets, suggesting that the marginal-utility feedback effectively trains the skill generator to produce transferable search and reasoning strategies rather than domain-specific shortcuts.

### 10. Training Dynamics

Comparison of training curves between SAPO and SkillRL (using Qwen3-4B-Instruct) revealed two key aspects:
1. SAPO exhibited faster performance improvement during early training stages on both ALFWorld and WebShop, implying that its utility-based skill filtering mechanism provides more reliable guidance when the base policy is still developing.
2. SAPO consistently achieved higher peak validation performance, further supporting its robustness and generalizability across different backbone models. Analyses on individual ALFWorld subtasks also showed SAPO maintaining steadier performance with fewer fluctuations in later training stages, unlike SkillRL which sometimes declined after reaching peak performance.

### 11. Skill Utility

An analysis of generated skill utility compared SAPO (using Qwen3-4B-Instruct as its internal skill generator) with SkillRL (which utilized GPT-5.4).
1. Initially, skills generated by SAPO's smaller, open-weight model exhibited lower utility than those from GPT. However, through continuous training with utility feedback, SAPO's skill generator learned to produce more beneficial skills, eventually achieving utility levels comparable to, or slightly exceeding, those of the GPT-based generator used in SkillRL. This demonstrates the effectiveness of utility-based training for the skill generation component.
2. Even in later training stages, when the policy became stronger, the SAPO skill generator continued to produce skills with positive utility, indicating that beneficial skills can offer ongoing advantages beyond an already improved base policy.

### 12. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of SAPO's main components:
- **SAPO w/o Validation:** Removing the utility-based skill promotion mechanism and directly storing generated skills led to degraded performance on both ALFWorld and WebShop. This indicates that rigorous skill validation is crucial for maintaining skill bank quality and preventing the introduction of low-quality skills.
- **SAPO w/o Generator:** Disabling the utility-weighted skill generator training resulted in a performance drop, particularly in WebShop success rate. This highlights the importance of utility feedback in enabling the policy to generate more effective skills internally.
- **SAPO w/o Scoring:** Eliminating the likelihood-based skill pruning and retrieval-time reranking components also reduced performance. This suggests that the policy's learned skill-generation likelihood is effective for selecting valuable skills during retrieval and for removing outdated ones from the bank.
Overall, the full SAPO framework consistently achieved the best results across all metrics, confirming the synergistic benefits of its integrated components.

### 13. Hyperparameter Analysis

An analysis of the hyperparameter K (number of queries per skill induction unit) indicated that SAPO's performance was not highly sensitive to this specific choice, consistently outperforming SkillRL across tested K values. Using multiple related queries ($K > 1$) generally yielded better results than single-query induction, suggesting that richer evidence contributes to generating more reusable skills. A default setting of $K=4$ was selected for its balance of performance and rollout cost.

### 14. API Cost Analysis

The paper noted that SkillRL's reliance on proprietary LLMs (e.g., GPT-5.4) for skill generation incurred approximate costs of $30 per run for ALFWorld, excluding other computational expenses. SAPO avoids these recurring costs by training its own policy to generate skills, leveraging existing rollouts for utility signal extraction, thus eliminating the need for external proprietary LLM API calls for this function.

### 15. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses several challenges in the field of skill-augmented reinforcement learning (RL) for language agents, offering implications for future AI development.

**Improved Agent Performance and Robustness:** By introducing a mechanism for pre-storage skill validation and context-dependent utility estimation, SAPO demonstrably improves the quality of skills within an agent's bank. This leads to more reliable guidance for LLM agents, resulting in enhanced performance and faster learning on diverse interactive decision-making and search-augmented QA tasks. The observed stability in training dynamics further suggests that effective skill curation prevents the degradation of agent performance by low-quality or outdated skills.

**Enhanced Skill Management and Adaptability:** The framework introduces a nuanced approach to skill maintenance by evaluating the marginal utility of skills within their operational context. This allows for dynamic pruning of outdated or redundant skills and intelligent reranking during retrieval, ensuring that agents always leverage the most relevant and beneficial knowledge. The ability for skills to generalize across in-domain and out-of-domain tasks signifies a step towards more transferable and adaptable agent intelligence.

**Autonomous and Cost-Effective Skill Acquisition:** A significant contribution is training the agent's policy itself to serve as both a skill generator and a skill scorer. This eliminates the dependency on costly, proprietary frontier LLM APIs for skill induction during online RL. By learning to generate high-utility skills from its own experience and existing rollouts, the agent becomes more autonomous in its skill acquisition process, drastically reducing operational costs and making skill-augmented RL more accessible for researchers and practitioners without extensive computational budgets.

**Advancement in Foundational AI Research:** The method of deriving skill utility signals from existing rollouts for skill validation and generator training represents an efficient use of computational resources. This integration of skill generation, validation, and policy optimization within a single online RL framework contributes to the broader understanding of how agents can continuously learn, self-improve, and manage their knowledge base effectively in complex, dynamic environments. The principles of context-dependent utility and policy-based skill learning may extend beyond language agents to other areas of AI requiring continuous learning and knowledge management.
