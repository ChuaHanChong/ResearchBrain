# Can Multimodal Large Language Models Truly Understand Small Objects?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced SOUBench, the first comprehensive benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to evaluate their capabilities in understanding small objects across driving, aerial, and underwater scenarios. The evaluation revealed that current MLLMs, including advanced models like GPT-5.2, significantly lag human performance, with the best MLLM achieving 58.97% accuracy compared to a human baseline of 82.50%, and the introduced SOU-Train dataset effectively improved MLLM performance through fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lacked systematic exploration and evaluation of their performance in understanding small objects.
- Existing MLLM benchmarks did not specifically address the nuanced challenges of small object perception and interpretation across diverse scenarios.
- Preliminary experiments showed that even advanced MLLMs exhibited limitations in fundamental small object understanding (SOU) sub-tasks like enumeration, counting, and localization.

## Method

- Proposed **SOUBench**, the first comprehensive ==benchmark== specifically tailored for evaluating and enhancing MLLMs' ==small object understanding (SOU)== capabilities.
- Developed an ==automated Visual Question Answering (VQA) generation pipeline== to construct **SOU-VQA** (evaluation) and **SOU-Train** (fine-tuning) datasets, covering six SOU sub-tasks across driving, aerial, and underwater scenarios.
- Conducted an extensive evaluation of 15 state-of-the-art MLLMs and demonstrated the effectiveness of the SOU-Train dataset for ==supervised fine-tuning==.

## Results

- The best-performing MLLM, GPT-5.2, achieved an overall accuracy of **58.97%** on SOU-VQA, which is considerably lower than the human baseline of **82.50%**.
- Supervised fine-tuning using the SOU-Train dataset consistently improved the performance of the Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct model, showing gains up to **+1.71%** in the Driving scenario.
- Increasing model scale generally correlated with improved SOU performance, and cross-scenario generalization experiments demonstrated positive transfer effects, with models fine-tuned on one scenario improving performance in untrained environments (e.g., **+0.57%** for Driving after Underwater training).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs demonstrate weak capabilities in small object understanding tasks, exhibiting a substantial performance gap (e.g., ~23.53% overall) compared to human observers.
> - MLLM performance varies significantly across scenarios and sub-tasks, with particular difficulties observed in aerial environments and precise spatial reasoning for small objects.
> - Small object understanding appears to be a "scale-sensitive" rather than "domain-specific" task, suggesting MLLMs can capture a universal visual prior for small objects, enabling cross-scenario generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across a range of understanding tasks, encompassing image and video analysis, as well as complex reasoning in scientific domains. These models have also begun to influence established research paradigms, such as object detection and video anomaly detection, by shifting focus from visual space analysis to semantic understanding and reasoning.

Despite these advancements, the specific domain of small object understanding (SOU) by MLLMs has remained largely unaddressed. Traditional approaches to small object detection primarily focus on the singular task of identification, lacking the multi-task, comprehensive scenario understanding that MLLMs could potentially offer. Existing benchmarks for MLLMs cover diverse areas like science reasoning, image understanding, and autonomous driving, but none specifically target the nuanced challenges of small object perception and interpretation across multiple scenarios. This research endeavors to bridge this gap by introducing the first comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate and enhance MLLMs' capabilities in understanding small objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observation that while MLLMs excel in various cognitive tasks, their performance in scenarios involving small objects has not been systematically explored. Small object understanding is a foundational component for numerous real-world applications, including enhancing safety in autonomous driving systems, improving surveillance in remote sensing, and facilitating environmental monitoring in underwater exploration. The authors conducted preliminary experiments that indicated even advanced MLLMs, such as GPT-5.2, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, and Gemma3-4B, exhibited limitations in fundamental SOU sub-tasks, including object enumeration, counting, and localization. These preliminary findings underscored a significant gap in the capabilities of current MLLMs, motivating the need for a focused investigation.

To address this identified gap, the research established several key objectives:
1. **Introduce SOUBench:** To propose the first comprehensive benchmark specifically tailored for evaluating the small object understanding capabilities of MLLMs.
2. **Develop an Automatic VQA Generation Strategy:** To design an efficient and automated pipeline for generating Visual Question Answering (VQA) pairs, which are crucial for evaluating SOU tasks.
3. **Construct SOU-VQA:** To create a new, diverse evaluation dataset, SOU-VQA, comprising 18,204 VQA pairs, across six distinct sub-tasks, and within three dominant small object scenarios (Driving, Aerial, and Underwater).
4. **Evaluate State-of-the-Art MLLMs:** To conduct an extensive evaluation of 15 leading MLLMs to systematically assess their current performance and identify their weaknesses in SOU tasks.
5. **Develop SOU-Train:** To construct a multimodal VQA training dataset, SOU-Train, with 11,226 annotated VQA pairs, aimed at improving MLLMs' SOU capabilities through supervised fine-tuning.
6. **Demonstrate Training Effectiveness:** To validate that SOU-Train can effectively enhance MLLM performance in small object understanding tasks through empirical fine-tuning experiments.
7. **Provide Empirical Foundation:** To offer a robust empirical foundation and resources (datasets and code) to the research community for further development of MLLMs with improved small object understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research revolves around the development of SOUBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate and enhance MLLMs' small object understanding. SOUBench comprises an evaluation dataset (SOU-VQA) and a training dataset (SOU-Train), alongside a comprehensive evaluation framework.

**SOU-VQA Dataset Construction (Evaluation):**
The SOU-VQA dataset was meticulously constructed to cover three dominant small object scenarios:
- **Driving:** Utilizing the SODA-D dataset.
- **Aerial:** Incorporating data from VisDrone, AI-TOD, and SODA-A.
- **Underwater:** Based on the RUOD dataset, with original image resolutions adjusted to meet small object criteria as relevant dedicated datasets were scarce.
A small object was defined adhering to the COCO format, as an instance with an absolute area less than or equal to 1024 pixels. The final SOU-VQA dataset consists of 3,034 sampled images, 18,204 VQA pairs, and covers 38 distinct small object categories.

**Task Question Definition:**
Six sub-tasks were designed to comprehensively assess MLLM capabilities across multiple dimensions of understanding:
- **Foundational Perception:**
 - **Category Enumeration (CE):** Evaluates the ability to list distinct small object categories.
 - **Object Counting (OC):** Measures the ability to accurately count total small object instances.
- **Spatial Reasoning:**
 - **Category Recognition (CR):** Tests the recognition of a specific small object's category at given pixel coordinates.
 - **Object Location (OL):** Assesses the localization of small object bounding box(es) within a specified Red Rectangular Area (RRA).
- **Fine-Grained Understanding:**
 - **Specific Category Counting (SCC):** Evaluates the ability to count instances of a particular small object category.
 - **Peripheral Object Identification (POI):** Measures the ability to identify a small object based on its relative spatial position (e.g., topmost, leftmost).

**VQA Pairs Generation Pipeline:**
An automated and efficient pipeline was developed for generating VQA pairs. This process involved six sequential steps:
1. **Dataset Input:** Raw images and corresponding JSON annotation files were fed into the system.
2. **Preprocessing:** Data was filtered to retain only small objects (area ≤ 1024 pixels). Image resizing was applied to some datasets (e.g., RUOD) to ensure consistency with the small object definition.
3. **Annotation Extraction:** Ground-truth data, including category labels and bounding box coordinates for identified small objects, was extracted.
4. **QA Generation:** This module synthesized question-answer pairs for the six defined sub-tasks. For each question, one ground-truth answer and three incorrect options (distractors) were generated.
5. **Option Generation:** Distractors were generated ensuring they fell within a valid statistical range for the dataset but did not overlap with the correct answer. Options were then randomly shuffled (A, B, C, D) to mitigate position bias.
6. **QA Output:** The final VQA instances, combining visual data with generated text pairs, were produced.
A team of four students manually verified the generated VQA pairs to ensure accuracy and quality.

**SOU-Train Dataset Construction (Training):**
To facilitate improvement in MLLM SOU capabilities, the SOU-Train dataset was constructed. This dataset comprises 11,226 VQA training pairs, generated using a similar automated pipeline as SOU-VQA, but specifically structured for supervised fine-tuning (SFT).

**Experiment Setting and Evaluation:**
- **Models:** Evaluation was conducted on 15 state-of-the-art MLLMs, including 3 proprietary models (GPT-5.2, Gemini-3-flash-preview-nothinking, Grok-4-1-fast-no-reasoning) and 12 open-source models.
- **Testing Protocol:** Proprietary models were tested via their respective APIs. Open-source models were evaluated using VLMEvalKit with default parameters. Human performance was also recorded for a comparative baseline.
- **Metric:** Accuracy was used to report the final test performance.
- **Pre-prompts:** Due to the diverse origins and category definitions across datasets, specific pre-prompts were designed for each dataset to resolve ambiguities and provide consistent contextual information to the MLLMs.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning:** The Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct model was fine-tuned using the SOU-Train dataset. The LoRA (Low-rank Adaptation) method was applied with a lora\_rank of 8. The image\_max\_pixels was set to 4,194,304 (2048x2048) to accommodate high-resolution images. Training epochs were varied across different datasets to prevent overfitting.

**Exploration Experiments:**
Three additional experiments were conducted to gain deeper insights:
- **Model Scale Influence:** The InternVL3\_5 series (1B, 2B, 4B, 8B, 14B models) was used to investigate the relationship between model scale and SOU performance across scenarios.
- **Training Cost Impact:** The effect of varying training epochs on performance in Driving and Underwater scenarios was analyzed to identify optimal training durations.
- **Generalization Exploration:** Cross-scenario transfer experiments (e.g., fine-tuning on Underwater data and testing on Driving data) were performed to assess the generalizability of SOU capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on the proposed SOUBench yielded several findings regarding their small object understanding capabilities.

**Overall MLLM Small Object Understanding Capability:**
- Among the 15 evaluated MLLMs, GPT-5.2 achieved the highest overall accuracy on SOU-VQA at 58.97%, closely followed by InternVL3\_5-14B at 58.65%. Both scores are below 60%, indicating a general weakness in small object understanding.
- Performance varied across the three scenarios: MLLMs performed best in Driving (highest average of 65.59% by VLM-R1), followed by Underwater (63.73% by Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct), and Aerial (58.55% by InternVL3\_5-14B). The lower performance in Aerial scenarios suggests that highly cluttered backgrounds or extremely small objects with minimal visual detail pose greater challenges for MLLMs.
- Analysis of sub-tasks revealed particular difficulties, such as in the Object Location sub-task within the Driving scenario, where some MLLMs scored below 30%. This indicates a struggle with precise spatial reasoning for small objects.

**Performance Gap with Human Capabilities:**
- A notable performance gap exists between the best-performing MLLMs and human performance. Compared to the human baseline (82.50% overall), GPT-5.2 lagged by approximately 23.53%.
- Scenario-specific gaps were 19.33% in Driving, 24.12% in Aerial, and 14.84% in Underwater scenarios, further highlighting that current MLLMs do not yet achieve human-level understanding of small objects.

**Comparison of Open-source vs. Proprietary Models:**
- Proprietary models generally exhibited higher performance, with GPT-5.2 leading the benchmark.
- However, several open-source models, such as InternVL3\_5-14B (58.65%) and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (57.55%), demonstrated performance levels approaching those of proprietary models. This suggests a progressive closing of the performance gap by the open-source community in SOU tasks.

**Effectiveness of SOU-Train Dataset:**
- Supervised fine-tuning of the Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct model using SOU-Train consistently improved its performance across all evaluated scenarios.
- Performance gains included +1.71% in Driving, +1.03% (A-VisDrone), +0.77% (A-AITOD), +1.17% (A-SODA), and +1.27% in Underwater scenarios. These results empirically validate the effectiveness of the SOU-Train dataset in enhancing MLLMs' small object understanding capabilities.

**Influence of Model Scale on Performance:**
- An exploration using the InternVL3\_5 series (from 1B to 14B parameters) indicated a general positive correlation between increasing model scale and improved SOU performance across Driving, Aerial, and Underwater scenarios. This suggests that larger model capacities can help mitigate challenges inherent in SOU tasks.
- An exception was noted in the Driving scenario, where the 4B model performed worse than the 1B model, specifically due to lower performance in the Category Enumeration sub-task. This indicates that while scale generally helps, the relationship can be nuanced, requiring careful consideration of model architecture and specific task demands.

**Impact of Training Costs:**
- The study on training costs revealed that optimal training epochs varied by scenario (e.g., Epoch 9 for Driving vs. Epoch 3 for Underwater). This implies that MLLMs learn small object features at different rates depending on the complexity and visual characteristics of the environment.
- The authors suggest tailoring training costs to specific scenarios: higher epochs for high-entropy environments with dense interference, and early stopping for simpler scenes to prevent overfitting.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
- Cross-scenario generalization experiments demonstrated that models fine-tuned on one scenario (e.g., Underwater) exhibited positive transfer effects when applied to untrained scenarios (e.g., Driving). For instance, an Underwater-trained model improved Driving performance by +0.57%.
- These bidirectional performance gains suggest that SOU might be a "scale-sensitive" rather than a "domain-specific" task, implying that models can capture a universal visual prior for small objects. This finding points towards the feasibility of developing a single, generalized model for small object understanding across various environments, rather than requiring specialized models for each scenario.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field by introducing SOUBench, which is presented as the first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to assess their capabilities in understanding small objects. This development is significant because it provides a standardized framework for evaluating a previously underexplored area of MLLM performance.

The work's main findings highlight that current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit weak capabilities in small object understanding tasks, with a substantial performance gap compared to human observers. This empirical evidence establishes a clear baseline and identifies critical limitations that future research can aim to address. The introduction of the SOU-VQA evaluation dataset and the SOU-Train fine-tuning dataset, both generated through an effective automated VQA strategy, provides valuable resources for the community. These datasets can serve as a foundation for developing and testing new MLLM architectures and training methodologies.

The insights gained from the exploration experiments, particularly regarding the influence of model scale, optimal training costs for different scenarios, and the generalization potential across diverse environments, offer practical guidance for model development. The observation that small object understanding appears to be a "scale-sensitive" rather than a "domain-specific" task suggests a pathway towards building universal SOU models, potentially reducing the need for scenario-specific training.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted. Enhancing MLLMs' ability to accurately understand small objects holds direct implications for critical applications such as autonomous driving, where the reliable detection and interpretation of small road hazards or distant signs are crucial for safety. Similarly, improvements could benefit remote sensing for detailed environmental monitoring and underwater robotics for navigation and data collection in complex marine environments. By providing a benchmark and associated datasets, this research aims to stimulate innovation in MLLM development, fostering the creation of more robust, perceptive, and generally capable multimodal AI systems for real-world applications.
