# Dynamic Open-Vocabulary 3D Scene Graphs for Long-term Language-Guided Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DovSG is a mobile manipulation framework that enables robots to perform long-term, language-guided tasks in dynamic indoor environments using dynamic open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs. The system demonstrated 35% overall long-term task success, outperforming baselines at 5%, while also reducing memory consumption by 13 times and scene graph update times by 20-27 times.

## Problem

- Mobile robots struggle to perform multi-step, long-term tasks in real-world, dynamic environments where objects are frequently moved, added, or revealed.
- Existing robotic systems often assume static scenes, limiting their applicability and requiring frequent manual resets or interventions.
- A lack of robust, efficient methods for representing and continuously updating environmental knowledge in response to unpredicted changes.

## Method

- A comprehensive framework integrating dynamic open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs with language-guided task planning, navigation, and manipulation modules for mobile robots.
- Employs an open-vocabulary 3D object mapping pipeline using VLMs (Grounding DINO, SAM-2, CLIP) to build semantic 3D object representations and infer spatial relationships.
- Implements a localized and rapid dynamic scene adaptation mechanism that identifies and updates only affected portions of the 3D scene graph, rather than reconstructing the entire scene, through precise relocalization and voxel-index-based change identification.

## Results

- DovSG achieved 93-95% Scene Change Detection Accuracy and 88% Scene Graph Accuracy, outperforming LLM-based baselines by 28-54%.
- Demonstrated 35% overall long-term task success in dynamic environments, a substantial improvement over Ok-Robot's 5%, with consistently higher subtask success rates for navigation, pick-up, and place.
- Consumed 13 times less memory (0.15 GB vs 2 GB) and performed scene graph updates 20-27 times faster (1 minute vs 20-27 minutes) compared to baselines requiring full scene reconstruction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Localized updates to a 3D scene graph are crucial for efficient and robust long-term mobile manipulation in dynamic environments, significantly reducing computational overhead compared to full scene reconstruction.
> - Integrating state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for open-vocabulary perception and Large Language Models (LLMs) for task planning enables more flexible and adaptive robot behavior.
> - Precise visual relocalization combined with voxel-based change detection is effective for identifying scene changes and maintaining map consistency in dynamic settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards intelligent systems capable of operating autonomously in complex, human-centric environments. A significant hurdle in achieving this goal is enabling robots to perform long-term, multi-step tasks in dynamic settings where the environment frequently changes due to human activity or the robot's own actions. Traditional robotic systems often assume static or minimally changing scenes, which severely limits their real-world applicability. This paper directly addresses this critical limitation.

The research situates itself at the intersection of several key areas in robotics and artificial intelligence:
- **3D Scene Representation and Perception:** The paper acknowledges the progression from traditional dense 3D structures (e.g., point clouds, voxels) to the integration of 2D image-based vision-language models (VLMs) for open-vocabulary perception and language-guided object grounding. DovSG leverages state-of-the-art VLMs like Grounding DINO, Segment Anything-2 (SAM-2), and CLIP, moving beyond dense, redundant representations towards a more semantically rich and efficient object-centric understanding.
- **3D Scene Graphs for Memory and Reasoning:** Scene graphs (SGs) represent a powerful paradigm for structuring environmental knowledge by modeling objects as nodes and their spatial or semantic relationships as edges. This approach is superior to raw point clouds or voxels for reasoning and long-term memory. Previous works like ConceptGraphs and HOV-SG have advanced compact and efficient SG creation, but often under the assumption of static environments. DovSG builds on this foundation, specifically citing RoboEXP for its pioneering work in encoding action-conditioned logical associations and dynamic scene updates. DovSG extends these concepts by explicitly focusing on rapid, localized updates to the scene graph for mobile manipulation, a crucial aspect overlooked by many prior SG methods.
- **Large Language Models (LLMs) for Task Planning:** The advent of LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) and VLMs has revolutionized zero-shot planning in robotics, enabling robots to interpret high-level natural language commands and decompose them into actionable subtasks. DovSG integrates GPT-4o for its language-guided task planning, linking the abstract task commands to the structured knowledge within the 3D scene graph. This bridges the gap between human language instructions and robot executability, moving beyond predefined scripts.
- **Robust Visual Localization and SLAM in Dynamic Environments:** Accurate localization is paramount for any mobile robot, especially when interacting with a changing scene. The paper discusses visual relocalization techniques, from structure-based methods (e.g., LightGlue, MatchFormer) to learning-based methods (e.g., ACE, DSAC*). DovSG combines the strengths of these approaches (ACE for coarse, LightGlue + ICP for fine refinement) to achieve fast and accurate relocalization, which is essential for its dynamic scene adaptation capabilities.

DovSG distinguishes itself by developing a holistic framework that integrates these components synergistically. Its core innovation lies in the **dynamic and localized updating mechanism for 3D open-vocabulary scene graphs**. While other systems might reconstruct the entire scene or assume static conditions, DovSG's ability to selectively update portions of the scene graph based on new observations and robot interactions is a significant step forward. This enables continuous adaptation to unpredicted environmental changes, making robots truly viable for long-term deployments in human-inhabited spaces. By focusing on mobile manipulation tasks in real-world, dynamic settings, DovSG tackles a major unsolved problem that affects the practicality and generalizability of autonomous robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable mobile robots to perform complex, long-term tasks in real-world, dynamic environments. The authors explicitly state the formidable challenge posed by environments that frequently change due to human-robot interactions or the robot’s own actions.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing the "Static Scene" Limitation:** Traditional robotic systems fundamentally rely on the assumption of static or minimally changing environments. This severely restricts their applicability and robustness in real-world scenarios where adaptability to dynamic conditions is crucial for successful and sustained operation.
- **Enabling Long-term Autonomy:** Current robotic systems often require frequent resets or manual interventions when the environment changes, preventing true long-term task execution. The goal is to develop a system that can continuously adapt and operate without such interruptions, even when objects are moved, added, or revealed.
- **Facilitating Human-Robot Interaction:** In human-inhabited spaces, environments are inherently dynamic. For robots to collaborate effectively with humans or operate seamlessly in shared spaces, they must be able to perceive, understand, and adapt to changes made by humans.
- **Leveraging Advancements in AI and Robotics:** The recent breakthroughs in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) offer unprecedented capabilities for open-vocabulary perception and language-guided reasoning. The motivation is to integrate these powerful tools into a coherent robotic framework to enhance adaptability and task planning.

To achieve these objectives, the paper outlines specific contributions:
- **Proposing a Novel Robotic Framework:** Integrating dynamic open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs with language-guided task planning for accurate long-term task execution in dynamic environments.
- **Constructing Dynamic 3D Scene Graphs:** Developing scene graphs that capture rich object semantics and spatial relations, with the crucial ability to perform localized updates as the robot interacts, allowing efficient adaptation to incremental modifications without full scene reconstruction.
- **Developing a Flexible Task Planning Method:** Utilizing LLMs to decompose complex natural language tasks into manageable subtasks (e.g., pick-up, place, navigation), enhancing the robot’s flexibility and scalability.
- **Real-world Implementation and Validation:** Demonstrating the proposed DovSG system on real mobile robots in various dynamic environments, validating its performance in long-term tasks and fundamental subtasks.

In essence, the motivation stems from the gap between current robotic capabilities (often confined to static lab settings) and the requirements for practical, robust deployment in unpredictable real-world scenarios, particularly focusing on the crucial aspect of scene dynamism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DovSG (Dynamic Open-Vocabulary 3D Scene Graphs) is a comprehensive mobile manipulation framework designed to operate in dynamic indoor environments. It comprises five interconnected modules: Perception, Memory, Task Planning, Navigation, and Manipulation.

The overall approach involves:
1. **Home Scanning and Coordinate Transformation (III-A):**
 - **Data Collection:** An initial scan of the environment is performed using an RGB-D camera (Intel Realsense D455) to capture sequences of color and depth images.
 - **Pose Estimation:** DROID-SLAM is used to estimate camera poses, with actual depth data replacing DROID-SLAM’s depth prediction for true-scale accuracy.
 - **Scene Alignment:** The scene's coordinate system is normalized by detecting the "floor" using Grounding DINO and SAM-2, fitting a plane to the 3D floor points with RANSAC, and transforming all poses to align the floor with the global z=0 plane. This establishes a consistent reference frame for spatial relationships.

2. **Open-vocabulary 3D Object Mapping (III-B):**
 - **Open-Vocabulary 2D Segmentation:** For each incoming RGB-D frame, Recognize-Anything generates initial object classes. Grounding DINO refines these into bounding boxes, and Segment Anything-2 (SAM-2) provides precise object masks.
 - **Feature Extraction:** CLIP is used to extract visual features (from cropped and isolated mask images) and text features (from object class names) for each segmented object, which are then fused.
 - **3D Projection and Denoising:** Each masked 2D region is projected into 3D space, forming a point cloud. DBSCAN clustering (with adaptive ε) denoises these point clouds.
 - **Object Association:** Newly detected objects are associated with existing objects in the map based on a weighted sum of geometric (Nearest Neighbor rate), visual (CLIP cosine similarity), and text (CLIP cosine similarity) similarities. If no strong match is found, a new object is initialized.
 - **Object Fusion:** Associated objects are fused by incrementally updating their visual features (weighted average) and merging their point clouds, followed by down-sampling.

3. **3D Scene Graph Generation (III-C):**
 - Once the set of 3D objects (nodes) is established, their spatial relationships (edges) are inferred.
 - **Voxelization:** Object point clouds are voxelized to reduce computation and storage.
 - **Relationship Inference:** The system focuses on three key relationships: "on" (positional hierarchy), "belong" (attachment/ownership), and "inside" (containment). These are inferred based on the objects' spatial positions, aided by the floor-aligned coordinate system.

4. **Dynamic Scene Adaptation (III-D):** This is the core mechanism for handling scene changes.
 - **Relocalization and Refinement:**
 - An ACE (Accelerated Coordinate Encoding) scene-specific regression MLP is trained on initial mapping data for coarse global pose estimation of new observations.
 - LightGlue is used for local feature matching to identify the most similar historical image.
 - Multi-scale colored ICP (Iterative Closest Point) aligns new observations with the existing map, refining the pose for precise integration.
 - **Remove Obsolete Indices:** Voxels in the memory map are identified as obsolete (for deletion) if their projected depth or color from a new observation significantly differs from the stored values, indicating a change in the environment.
 - **Update Low-level Memory:** New RGB-D observations are processed and fused with the existing object set, updating object features and point clouds in the semantic memory. The new observations also become reference viewpoints for future relocalization.
 - **Update High-level Memory (Scene Graph):** A local, sub-graph-based update strategy is employed to avoid global reconstruction:
 - Identify "affected" objects (deleted, changed points) from the comparison of old and new object sets.
 - Identify and include parent/child objects related to "affected" objects, and remove associated edges.
 - For remaining "affected" objects and newly added objects ("need_process"), recompute their spatial relationships and update the scene graph edges.

5. **Language-guided Task Planning (III-E):**
 - GPT-4o is used to decompose complex, natural language long-term tasks into a sequence of executable subtasks.
 - Each subtask is structured as an "action_name" (e.g., Go To, Pick Up, Place) and corresponding "object_name"s, maintaining the original abstraction level.

6. **Navigation (III-F):**
 - **Localization:** The robot's precise pose is determined using the relocalization method (III-D1). Target objects are located by comparing their CLIP embeddings with scene object embeddings. For relational tasks (e.g., "A on B"), it identifies candidate pairs with shortest Euclidean distances.
 - **Mobile Control:** A* algorithm generates collision-free paths to the target location, and a PID controller ensures accurate path following.

7. **Manipulation (III-G):**
 - **Pick up:** The robot uses Grounding DINO and SAM-2 to segment the target object. AnyGrasp is used for grasp proposals, with the input point cloud cropped around the target for better focus. Grasps are filtered by cost. A heuristic grasp strategy (based on object bounding box dimensions to determine top-down vs. horizontal) is activated if AnyGrasp fails, optimizing gripper alignment.
 - **Place:** The target placement point is computed using the median coordinates (x, y) of the target object's point cloud and a calculated drop height (z_max = 0.1 + max(z) of points below the placement XY).

This methodology creates a tightly integrated system that prioritizes real-time adaptability and long-term autonomy in complex, dynamic environments, distinguishing itself from static-assumption or full-reconstruction approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments in real-world indoor environments using a UFACTORY xARM6 robotic arm on an Agilex Ranger Mini 3 mobile base, equipped with a RealSense D455 camera. The core of the evaluation focused on the system's ability to adapt to dynamic environments and successfully complete long-term tasks without manual resets. Environmental modifications were categorized into three levels: Minor Adjustment, Appearance (newly visible objects), and Positional Shift (significant relocation).

The evaluation employed three key metrics: Scene Change Detection Accuracy (SCDA), Scene Graph Accuracy (SGA), and overall Task Success Rate (including subtasks like pick-up, place, and navigation). Baselines included GPT-4o for scene graph evaluation and Ok-Robot along with ConceptGraphs for long-term task performance, memory usage, and update times.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Dynamic Scene Adaptation and Scene Graph Generation (Table I):**
 - **Scene Change Detection Accuracy (SCDA):** DovSG significantly outperformed the GPT-4o baseline. In "Minor Adjustment" scenarios, DovSG achieved 95.37% SCDA compared to GPT-4o's 41.44% (a nearly 54% difference). For "Appearance" and "Positional Shift" scenarios, DovSG maintained high accuracy (93.22% and 94.23% respectively), approximately 28% higher than GPT-4o. This superiority is attributed to DovSG's precise relocalization and voxel-index-based change identification, which effectively handles inconsistencies between historical and new observations that plague LLM-based approaches.
 - **Scene Graph Accuracy (SGA):** DovSG also demonstrated superior performance in generating accurate scene graphs. For "Minor Adjustment," DovSG achieved 88.75% SGA, compared to GPT-4o's 54.60%. While both methods faced challenges (DovSG occasionally decomposing a single object into multiple nodes; GPT-4o struggling with relationship recognition or redundant object definitions), DovSG consistently provided more accurate scene graphs.

2. **Long-Term Task and Subtask Success Rates (Table II):**
 - **Subtask Performance:**
 - **Navigation:** DovSG achieved a 94.01% success rate across all modification types, significantly higher than Ok-Robot's 80.48%. Ok-Robot's performance degraded particularly in "Positional Shift" scenarios, where the robot could be misled by historical object locations.
 - **Pick-up:** DovSG (81.28%) outperformed Ok-Robot (70.58%), showing a 10.7% improvement. This was attributed to DovSG's refined grasp strategies, including intelligent cropping for AnyGrasp and a heuristic grasp method that optimizes gripper alignment based on object geometry, reducing environmental interference.
 - **Place:** DovSG (86.81%) showed a 5.49% higher success rate compared to Ok-Robot (81.32%), benefiting from optimized lower and inclined placement positions.
 - **Overall Long-Term Task Success:** DovSG achieved a 35.00% success rate for complete long-term tasks, drastically outperforming Ok-Robot's 5.00%. Ok-Robot, designed for static environments, failed almost entirely in "Appearance" and "Positional Shift" scenarios because it could not adapt to significant scene changes or partially update its scene representation, leading to cascading errors in multi-step tasks. DovSG's dynamic adaptation capabilities were critical for its success in these complex, continuous scenarios.

3. **Efficiency Comparison (Table III):**
 - **Memory Consumption:** DovSG consumed significantly less memory (0.15 GB) compared to Ok-Robot (2 GB) for a 40 m² scene with 1200 RGB-D frames, representing a 13-fold reduction. This is comparable to ConceptGraphs (0.15 GB), both benefiting from efficient 3D scene graph representations.
 - **Update Time:** DovSG demonstrated vastly superior update times. Since Ok-Robot and ConceptGraphs cannot perform partial updates, the table lists their full scene reconstruction times. DovSG updated its scene graph in 1 minute, while ConceptGraphs required 27 minutes and Ok-Robot 20 minutes. This highlights DovSG's ability to perform local updates, making it 20 to 27 times faster in typical room scenarios.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that DovSG's dynamic scene graph and localized update mechanism provide a robust and efficient solution for mobile manipulation in dynamic real-world environments, significantly outperforming baselines that assume static scenes or rely on full scene reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DovSG represents a significant advancement in the field of mobile robotics, particularly in addressing the long-standing challenge of deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling True Long-term Autonomy:** By providing a mechanism for continuous and localized updates to the robot's understanding of its environment, DovSG moves beyond the limitations of systems that assume static scenes or require frequent manual resets. This capability is crucial for sustained operation in domestic, industrial, or service settings where environments are constantly changing due to human activity or the robot's own actions. The demonstrated 30% higher success rate in long-term tasks compared to static-assumption methods underscores this impact.

2. **Robustness to Environmental Dynamism:** The ability to accurately detect and adapt to minor adjustments, the appearance of new objects, or significant positional shifts is a game-changer. This robust adaptation ensures that tasks like navigation, picking, and placing remain effective even after unforeseen changes, which is a common failure point for current robotic systems. This makes robots more resilient and reliable in real-world, unpredictable scenarios.

3. **Efficient Knowledge Representation and Management:** DovSG's use of open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs provides a rich, semantic, and highly structured representation of the environment. More importantly, its efficient local update mechanism (20-27 times faster updates and 13 times less memory usage than baselines) prevents the computational and memory burden associated with full scene reconstruction. This efficiency is vital for real-time robotic operations and scalability to larger, more complex environments.

4. **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** As robots move into shared spaces with humans, their ability to understand and respond to human-induced changes is paramount for seamless interaction and collaboration. DovSG's dynamic adaptation enables robots to effectively track and react to items being moved, opened, or hidden by humans, paving the way for more natural and intuitive human-robot partnerships.

5. **Integration of State-of-the-Art AI:** The synergistic integration of advanced Vision-Language Models (Recognize-Anything, Grounding DINO, SAM-2, CLIP) for perception and Large Language Models (GPT-4o) for language-guided task planning, combined with robust SLAM and manipulation techniques, showcases a powerful holistic framework. This integration demonstrates how cutting-edge AI can be effectively leveraged to build more capable and intelligent robotic systems.

**Potential Applications:**
- **Household Robotics:** Robots capable of performing chores, organizing spaces, or assisting elderly individuals in dynamic home environments.
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** Autonomous handling of goods in warehouses where inventory is constantly changing.
- **Service Robotics:** Robots operating in dynamic public spaces, hospitals, or retail environments.
- **Manufacturing:** Flexible automation where assembly lines or workstations can be reconfigured.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that DovSG's visual relocalization performance relies on distinctive visual cues (textures, edges). Environments with sparse, minimal, or repetitive features could challenge localization accuracy. Future work includes exploring multi-sensor fusion for more robust localization, developing even more efficient memory update mechanisms, creating lightweight scene representations, and enabling more sophisticated collaboration between mobile robots and manipulators. These directions indicate a clear path towards overcoming current limitations and further enhancing the practicality and capabilities of mobile manipulation systems.

In conclusion, DovSG represents a significant step towards truly autonomous mobile robots that can reliably perform complex tasks in the unpredictable dynamism of the real world. Its impact will likely be seen in the development of more robust, adaptable, and practical robotic systems across various applications, fostering closer and more effective human-robot coexistence.
