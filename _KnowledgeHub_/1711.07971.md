# Non-local Neural Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Facebook AI Research and Carnegie Mellon University introduced non-local operations as a generic building block for deep neural networks, enabling direct capture of long-range dependencies in data. This approach consistently enhanced performance across diverse computer vision tasks, including a 1.0% top-1 accuracy improvement on Kinetics-400 and a 1.3 APbox gain on COCO object detection.

## Problem

- Traditional convolutional and recurrent networks primarily process local information, making it challenging to capture dependencies between spatially or temporally distant elements.
- Capturing long-range interactions indirectly through many layers leads to computational inefficiency, optimization difficulties, and less effective modeling of multi-hop dependencies.

## Method

- A generic **non-local operation** is introduced that directly computes interactions between any two positions in a feature map, capturing dependencies regardless of their spatial or temporal distance.
- The operation is instantiated with various ==pairwise functions== (e.g., ==embedded Gaussian==, ==dot product==) and wrapped into a ==residual block== for easy integration into existing deep architectures.
- Efficiency is managed through ==bottleneck design== and ==spatial/temporal subsampling== of the input feature map before calculating affinities.

## Results

- Demonstrated consistent performance improvements across various computer vision tasks, including video classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and pose estimation.
- Achieved a **1.0% top-1 accuracy** increase on Kinetics-400 with a single non-local block and a **1.3 APbox** gain on COCO object detection using Mask R-CNN, with minimal computational overhead.
- NL I3D models reached **76.0% top-1 accuracy** on Kinetics-400, outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods and showing strong complementarity with 3D convolutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The core benefit derives from the generic non-local interaction, with various pairwise function instantiations yielding similar performance improvements, rather than sensitivity to a specific form.
> - The embedded Gaussian non-local operation, when appropriately normalized, is mathematically equivalent to the self-attention mechanism, establishing a connection between non-local filtering and attention-based models.
> - Non-local operations are complementary to traditional convolutional and recurrent layers, effectively combining global context capture with local feature extraction for improved performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a fundamental limitation of conventional deep neural networks: the struggle to efficiently capture long-range dependencies. In the landscape of deep learning, two primary building blocks, convolutional and recurrent operations, have dominated for processing data like images and sequences, respectively.
- **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs):** Excel at capturing local patterns. However, to model relationships between spatially distant points, information must propagate through many stacked convolutional layers, leading to large effective receptive fields. This process is computationally expensive, prone to optimization difficulties, and less effective for multi-hop interactions.
- **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and LSTMs:** Designed for sequential data, they process information step-by-step, effectively creating a "local" temporal window at each step. Similar to CNNs, capturing long-range temporal dependencies requires many steps, suffering from issues like vanishing/exploding gradients and computational inefficiency.

The paper proposes **non-local operations** as a generic family of building blocks to directly overcome these limitations. This approach is not entirely novel in its underlying principle, drawing direct inspiration from the classical **non-local means** method in image processing, which computes a pixel's value based on a weighted average of *all* other pixels in the image, with weights determined by patch similarity. The paper effectively translates this classical idea into a modern deep learning context.

Crucially, this work also establishes a direct link to **self-attention mechanisms**, which gained significant traction, especially in Natural Language Processing (NLP) with models like the Transformer. The authors demonstrate that the embedded Gaussian version of their non-local operation, with proper normalization, is mathematically equivalent to the self-attention mechanism. This insight is significant as it shows that self-attention, often viewed through the lens of NLP, is a specific instance of a broader "non-local filtering" principle that can be applied to general computer vision tasks, including images and videos in space and spacetime.

The paper also distinguishes its approach from other related areas:
- **Graphical Models (e.g., CRFs):** While CRFs can model long-range dependencies through iterative inference, non-local operations are presented as a simpler, feedforward block.
- **Interaction Networks and Relation Networks:** These approaches also consider pairwise interactions but the authors emphasize that the core contribution of non-local operations is the direct, arbitrary-distance interaction, which they argue is orthogonal to the ideas of attention/interaction/relation.
- **3D Convolutional Networks (C3D, I3D):** For video, 3D convolutions extend local operations to the temporal dimension. The paper explicitly compares against and demonstrates complementarity with I3D models, positioning non-local operations as an alternative or an enhancement to 3D convolutions for capturing spatiotemporal dependencies.

By proposing a generic, plug-and-play non-local block that directly computes interactions between any two positions regardless of distance, this work introduces a fundamental architectural primitive that could potentially augment or replace existing local operations in many contexts, thereby significantly impacting the design principles of deep neural networks across various domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop an efficient, simple, and generic building block for deep neural networks that can directly capture long-range dependencies in data**.

The motivation stems from the inherent limitations of the predominant building blocks in deep learning:
- **Local Processing:** Both convolutional and recurrent neural networks process information primarily within a local neighborhood. For example, a convolutional filter operates on a small receptive field, and an RNN processes a current input in conjunction with the immediate past hidden state.
- **Indirect Long-Range Dependency Capture:** To capture relationships between distant elements (e.g., pixels far apart in an image, words far apart in a sentence, or objects across different frames in a video), these local operations must be applied repeatedly. This creates a "propagation chain" through many layers, accumulating information over distance.
- **Consequences of Indirect Capture:**
 - **Computational Inefficiency:** Stacking many layers to achieve a large receptive field adds significant computational cost.
 - **Optimization Difficulties:** Deep networks are harder to optimize, suffering from issues like vanishing/exploding gradients (a challenge that LSTMs and ResNets partially address but don't entirely eliminate for arbitrarily long distances).
 - **Difficulty with Multi-Hop Dependencies:** When information needs to travel back and forth or interact in complex ways over long distances, propagating through local layers becomes cumbersome and ineffective.

The authors aim to create a solution that offers several advantages:
- **Direct Interaction:** Capture dependencies between any two positions directly, bypassing the need for progressive signal propagation.
- **Efficiency:** Achieve strong results with only a few non-local layers, implying better computational efficiency for capturing long-range interactions.
- **Generality:** Be applicable across various data types (images, sequences, videos) and easily integrable into existing architectures.
- **Maintain Variable Input Sizes:** A practical design consideration for real-world applications.

By addressing these core limitations, the paper seeks to provide a more effective and fundamental mechanism for deep learning models to reason about complex, distant relationships in data, pushing the boundaries of what these networks can achieve.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the **non-local operation**, which generalizes the classical non-local means method.

#### 3.1. Formulation of the Non-local Operation

The generic non-local operation is defined as:
$y_i = \frac{1}{C(x)} \sum_{\forall j} f(x_i, x_j) g(x_j)$
Where:
- $i$ is the index for the output position.
- $j$ enumerates all possible positions in the input feature map.
- $x$ is the input signal (feature map).
- $y$ is the output signal, of the same size as $x$.
- $f(x_i, x_j)$ is a pairwise function that computes a scalar affinity/relationship between positions $i$ and $j$.
- $g(x_j)$ is a unary function that computes a representation of the input signal at position $j$.
- $C(x)$ is a normalization factor.

This formulation ensures that the response at any position $i$ considers information from *all* other positions $j$, regardless of their spatial or temporal distance, a stark contrast to local operations.

#### 3.2. Instantiations of Functions $f$ and $g$

For simplicity, $g(x_j)$ is generally instantiated as a linear embedding: $g(x_j) = W_g x_j$, implemented as a 1x1 convolution (or 1x1x1 for spacetime).
Various instantiations for the pairwise function $f(x_i, x_j)$ are explored:
- **Gaussian:** $f(x_i, x_j) = e^{x_i^T x_j}$. Uses dot-product similarity, with $C(x) = \sum_{\forall j} f(x_i, x_j)$.
- **Embedded Gaussian:** $f(x_i, x_j) = e^{\theta(x_i)^T \phi(x_j)}$, where $\theta(x_i) = W_\theta x_i$ and $\phi(x_j) = W_\phi x_j$. This version, with softmax normalization (where $1/C(x) \sum_{\forall j} f(x_i, x_j)$ becomes softmax), is shown to be equivalent to the self-attention mechanism used in Transformers.
- **Dot Product:** $f(x_i, x_j) = \theta(x_i)^T \phi(x_j)$, similar to embedded Gaussian but without the exponential (and thus no implicit softmax). $C(x) = N$, where $N$ is the number of positions.
- **Concatenation:** $f(x_i, x_j) = ReLU(w_f^T[\theta(x_i), \phi(x_j)])$, where embeddings are concatenated and projected. $C(x) = N$.

The paper emphasizes that the specific choice of $f$ is not highly sensitive, suggesting the generic non-local behavior is key.

#### 3.3. Non-local Block Design

To integrate into existing architectures, the non-local operation is wrapped into a "non-local block":
$z_i = W_z y_i + x_i$
- A residual connection (`+x_i`) is used, allowing the block to be inserted into pre-trained models without disrupting initial performance (if $W_z$ is initialized to zero).
- **Efficiency enhancements:**
 - **Bottleneck design:** The number of channels for $W_g, W_\theta, W_\phi$ is typically set to half the input channels, similar to ResNet bottlenecks.
 - **Subsampling:** The input $x$ can be spatially subsampled (e.g., via pooling) before computing pairwise affinities, reducing computational cost without altering the non-local nature.

#### 3.4. Integration into Architectures

Non-local blocks are designed to be "plug-and-play" components, easily inserted into any stage of a deep neural network, allowing the combination of both local (e.g., convolutions) and non-local information.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

The methodology is evaluated across diverse computer vision tasks:
- **Video Classification:** Extensive ablations on Kinetics and Charades datasets using ResNet-based backbones (C2D baseline, Inflated 3D ConvNet (I3D)). Experiments explore the number of non-local blocks, their placement, and the effect of space-only, time-only, or spacetime non-locality.
- **Object Detection/Instance Segmentation:** Applied to the Mask R-CNN framework on the COCO dataset.
- **Human Pose Estimation:** Also using Mask R-CNN on COCO.

This multi-task evaluation strategy rigorously demonstrates the generality and effectiveness of the proposed non-local operations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling results across multiple computer vision tasks, demonstrating the efficacy and generality of non-local neural networks.

#### 4.1. Generality and Robustness of Non-local Instantiations

- Ablation studies on Kinetics (Table 2a) show that different instantiations of the pairwise function $f$ (Gaussian, embedded Gaussian, dot-product, concatenation) yield very similar performance improvements (72.7% to 72.9% top-1 accuracy for R50 baseline 71.8%). This suggests that the *generic non-local interaction* itself, rather than the specific form of attention or similarity calculation, is the primary driver of the observed gains. The attentional (softmax) behavior, common in self-attention, is shown not to be essential in these applications.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness in Video Classification (Kinetics and Charades)

- **Consistent Improvement over Baselines:** Even a single non-local block (e.g., embedded Gaussian) provides a significant boost (e.g., +1% top-1 accuracy on R50 Kinetics, Table 2a).
- **Deeper Non-local Models Perform Better:** Increasing the number of non-local blocks (from 1 to 5 to 10) consistently improves accuracy (e.g., R50 Kinetics: 71.8% baseline -> 72.7% (1 block) -> 73.8% (5 blocks) -> 74.3% (10 blocks), Table 2c). This indicates that non-local blocks facilitate "multi-hop communication" of long-range dependencies.
- **Complementary to Model Capacity:** Non-local blocks' improvements are orthogonal to simply making networks deeper (e.g., 5-block R50 (73.8%) outperforms R101 baseline (73.1%) with fewer parameters and FLOPs, Table 2c).
- **Spacetime Non-locality is Key:** While space-only and time-only non-local operations improve performance, the combined spacetime version yields the best results (e.g., R101 Kinetics: 73.1% baseline -> 74.4% (space-only) -> 74.4% (time-only) -> 75.1% (spacetime), Table 2d). This highlights the ability to capture complex spatiotemporal relationships in videos.
- **Superiority and Complementarity with 3D Convolutions:**
 - NL C2D (non-local blocks added to 2D ConvNets) outperforms Inflated 3D ConvNets (I3D) while being more computationally economical (e.g., R101 Kinetics: NL C2D (75.1%) vs. I3D (74.4%) with 1.2x vs. 1.5x FLOPs, Table 2e).
 - Non-local operations are *complementary* to 3D convolutions: inserting non-local blocks into I3D models (NL I3D) further boosts performance substantially (e.g., R101 Kinetics: I3D (74.4%) -> NL I3D (76.0%), Table 2f).
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** NL I3D models achieve highly competitive, often state-of-the-art results on Kinetics and Charades datasets (Tables 3 and 4), surpassing previous RGB or RGB+flow methods, even matching heavily engineered competition winners *without* using optical flow or other auxiliary inputs.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness in Image Recognition (COCO Object Detection, Segmentation, Pose Estimation)

- **Object Detection and Instance Segmentation:** Adding a single non-local block to the Mask R-CNN backbone consistently improves AP metrics across different ResNet and ResNeXt models (e.g., +1.3 APbox for R101 baseline, Table 5). This demonstrates that even for static images, non-local dependencies are not fully captured by existing deep architectures and that non-local operations offer significant value at a minimal computational cost (<5% increase).
- **Keypoint Detection (Pose Estimation):** Inserting non-local blocks into Mask R-CNN's keypoint head and backbone significantly improves keypoint AP, especially for stricter metrics like AP75 (e.g., +2.4 points for R101, Table 6). This suggests better modeling of long-range dependencies among body parts.

Overall, the findings strongly support the hypothesis that non-local operations are a powerful and general building block capable of enhancing performance across diverse computer vision tasks by directly modeling long-range dependencies more effectively and efficiently than traditional local operations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Non-local Neural Networks holds significant implications for the field of deep learning and computer vision:

**A New Foundational Building Block:** This work establishes non-local operations as a generic and fundamental architectural primitive, akin to convolution or pooling layers. Its ability to be "plug-and-play" means it can be readily incorporated into almost any existing deep network architecture, offering a powerful tool for architects to enhance their models. This could lead to a paradigm shift in how long-range dependencies are considered and modeled in network design.

**Bridging Classical and Modern CV/DL:** By drawing inspiration from the classical non-local means method and explicitly connecting it to modern self-attention mechanisms, the paper creates a valuable bridge between traditional computer vision techniques and contemporary deep learning. This connection not only provides theoretical grounding for self-attention but also opens avenues for researchers to re-examine other classical algorithms for deep learning adaptations.

**Enhanced Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** The demonstrated improvements across a wide range of tasks—video classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and pose estimation—underline the broad applicability and effectiveness of non-local operations. This suggests that the problem of efficiently capturing long-range dependencies is pervasive and that the proposed solution offers a general remedy, potentially leading to more robust and accurate AI systems in practical applications.

**Rethinking Network Efficiency and Depth:** The paper shows that non-local operations can achieve better performance with fewer layers and FLOPs than deeper standard convolutional networks or even 3D convolutions. This challenges the notion that simply adding more local layers is the only path to better performance and encourages more intelligent architectural designs that directly address dependency modeling.

**Complementarity with Existing Techniques:** The finding that non-local operations are complementary to both 2D and 3D convolutions is crucial. It means non-local blocks are not a replacement but an *enhancement*, offering a way to capture different types of information (global vs. local) within a unified framework, leading to synergistic improvements.

**Catalyst for Future Research:** This work is likely to inspire extensive follow-up research. This could include exploring more sophisticated instantiations of $f$ and $g$, developing adaptive or attention-gated non-local operations, investigating optimal placement strategies within various network architectures, and applying non-local principles to other domains beyond vision, such as audio processing or time-series analysis, where long-range dependencies are also critical.

**Practical Impact and Open Science:** The release of open-source code by Facebook AI Research significantly lowers the barrier for adoption, enabling other researchers and practitioners to integrate non-local blocks into their models and validate its benefits, accelerating its impact on the field. This contributes to the reproducibility and progression of AI research.

In conclusion, "Non-local Neural Networks" provides a fundamental and widely applicable solution to a critical problem in deep learning, marking a significant step forward in designing more capable and efficient neural network architectures for complex data.
