# Coordinated Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Symmetry Equivariant Reinforcement Learning Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced SE-Policy, a Deep Reinforcement Learning framework that embeds strict symmetry equivariance into the actor and invariance into the critic for humanoid robots. This approach improved velocity tracking accuracy by 40.0% and enhanced spatio-temporal coordination on a Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating successful sim-to-real transfer and robustness across varied terrains.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies for humanoid robots often neglect inherent morphological symmetry, leading to uncoordinated, asymmetric, and suboptimal behaviors.
- Existing methods for integrating symmetry, such as data augmentation or loss regularization, often result in loose equivariance or introduce additional hyperparameters that complicate training.
- Strict equivariant methods, while theoretically promising, had not been extensively evaluated for their effectiveness and robustness on complex, agile real-world humanoid robots.

## Method

- ==Developed SE-Policy==, a DRL framework that integrates strict symmetry equivariance into the actor network's architecture and strict symmetry invariance into the critic network's architecture.
- Utilized the ESCNN framework to enforce these symmetries through parameter sharing within the neural network layers, eliminating the need for additional hyperparameters.
- Implemented a PPO-based training framework with a history encoder, an observation decoder, and a composite reward function, combined with curriculum learning and domain randomization for robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- SE-Policy achieved a velocity tracking error (TE-V) of **9.85** cm/s, which is **40.0%** lower than DreamWaQ (**16.43** cm/s) and **19.2%** lower than DreamWaQ-Regu (**12.19** cm/s).
- Demonstrated superior spatio-temporal coordination, achieving a spatial symmetry score (Spat-S) of **0.0** and the lowest temporal symmetry score (Temp-S) of **7.86**, indicating highly periodic and synchronized gaits.
- Successfully transferred policies trained in simulation to a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust locomotion and accurate command tracking on various unstructured terrains, including grass, slopes, and stones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strict architectural enforcement of symmetry equivariance in the actor leads to perfectly symmetric action generation (Spat-S of 0.0), directly ensuring coordinated movements.
> - The auxiliary symmetry-invariant critic plays a crucial role in effective optimization of equivariant policies, contributing significantly to both tracking performance and motion coordination.
> - Embedding symmetry into the DRL framework yields more natural, consistent, and human-like robot motions with superior temporal and spatial coordination, even on complex terrains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has demonstrated capabilities in various robotics control tasks, including those for quadruped, manipulator, bipedal, and humanoid robots. DRL facilitates the autonomous acquisition of complex skills in simulated environments, reducing the need for extensive domain-specific knowledge.

However, a recurring challenge in DRL-based robotics is the neglect of the inherent morphological symmetry present in many robot designs, particularly humanoids. This oversight can lead to DRL policies that produce uncoordinated, asymmetric, and suboptimal behaviors, which may detract from task performance and user experience. The human nervous system, by contrast, leverages bilateral symmetry for coordinated and balanced movements.

Previous research efforts to integrate morphological symmetry into DRL frameworks have explored various approaches:
- **Temporal Symmetry:** Some methods encourage periodicity in robot motions, for instance, by incorporating periodic phase signals into observations or reward functions, or by designing action spaces based on Central Pattern Generators (CPGs).
- **Spatial Symmetry:** Other approaches aim for policies that output equivariant actions under symmetric states. These include data augmentation techniques, where collected transitions are augmented with their symmetric copies to induce equivariance in the actor and invariance in the critic. Another strategy involves introducing regularization terms into the optimization objective of the actor and critic during training to penalize asymmetric actuation patterns. Additionally, some works have explored enforcing equivariance through hard constraints on neural network architectures.

Despite these developments, the optimal integration of symmetry equivariance into DRL for real humanoid robots remains an area of active investigation. Existing methods exhibit limitations:
- **Loose Equivariance:** Temporal symmetry methods and data augmentation often result in "soft" or "loose" equivariance due to insufficient policy constraints, yielding moderate performance.
- **Loss Regularization:** While effective, regularization methods introduce additional hyperparameters that require tuning, which can complicate the optimization process. These terms may also impede the primary policy optimization.
- **Strict Equivariance:** Methods that enforce strict equivariance through architectural constraints have primarily been evaluated in simulated classic control tasks. Their effectiveness and robustness when applied to complex, real-world humanoid robots, which demand high agility, have not been extensively explored.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a DRL framework that integrates strict symmetry equivariance directly into the neural network architecture of the actor and invariance into the critic, without the need for additional hyperparameters. The paper aims to demonstrate that such an approach can lead to improved coordination, naturalness, and task performance on a real humanoid robot, thereby advancing the state of DRL for complex robotic platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of existing Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methods in leveraging the inherent morphological symmetry of humanoid robots. Inspired by the bilateral symmetry of the human nervous system, which underpins coordinated and balanced movements, the authors identify a critical gap in current DRL approaches.

**Motivation:**
1. **Neglect of Morphological Symmetry:** Current DRL policies often treat humanoid robots as generic, black-box systems, failing to exploit their inherent bilateral symmetry. This leads to DRL policies that react inconsistently to symmetrically equivalent observations. For example, a robot might exhibit unequal movement styles or strengths in its symmetric joints during locomotion, indicating a limited understanding of its own body morphology.
2. **Suboptimal and Uncoordinated Behaviors:** The neglect of symmetry results in asymmetric and uncoordinated robot behaviors. These behaviors are often perceived as unnatural, can negatively impact user experience, and ultimately lead to suboptimal task performance, particularly in complex locomotion tasks requiring balance and agility.
3. **Limitations of Prior Symmetry Integration Methods:**
 - **Loose Equivariance:** Methods like temporal symmetric policies or data augmentation, which induce soft constraints, often yield only moderate performance because the policy constraints are insufficient to guarantee strict symmetry.
 - **Hyperparameter Dependence:** Loss regularization methods, while capable of improving symmetry, introduce additional hyperparameters that require delicate tuning. This tuning process can be time-consuming and may, in some cases, hinder the overall optimization process of the policy.
 - **Underexplored Real-Robot Effectiveness:** Strict equivariant methods, which enforce architectural constraints, have predominantly been validated in simulated classic control tasks. Their efficacy and robustness on real-world humanoid robots, which present significant challenges in terms of agility and dynamic stability, remain largely unexplored.

1. **Develop a Novel DRL Framework:** To propose a new DRL-based method, termed Symmetry Equivariant Policy (SE-Policy), designed for humanoid robot control tasks.
2. **Incorporate Strict Symmetry Equivariance and Invariance:** To embed strict symmetry equivariance within the actor network's architecture and strict symmetry invariance within the critic network's architecture. A primary objective is to achieve this integration without introducing any additional hyperparameters to the DRL training process.
3. **Generate Coordinated and Natural Motions:** To enable the humanoid robot to produce temporally and spatially coordinated, as well as natural, movements that reflect its symmetric morphology.
4. **Enhance Task Performance:** To achieve higher control performance, specifically in velocity tracking tasks, by improving accuracy and coordination compared to existing state-of-the-art DRL methods.
5. **Validate on Real Humanoid Robot:** To conduct extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world deployment using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating the superior performance, effectiveness, and broad applicability of the proposed method through sim-to-real transfer. This includes demonstrating robustness on various terrains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on integrating strict symmetry properties into a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework for humanoid robot locomotion. The approach formulates the problem within a standard Markov Decision Process (MDP) and develops a novel architecture, SE-Policy, based on an actor-critic paradigm.

**Problem Formulation:**
The interaction between the humanoid robot and its environment is modeled as an MDP, M = ⟨S, O, A, P, R, γ⟩, where $S$ is the state space, $O$ is the observation space, $A$ is the action space, $P$ is the transition probability, $R$ is the reward function, and γ is the discount factor. The objective is to learn a policy $\pi$ that maximizes the expected cumulative discounted return. Standard value ($V$) and state-action value ($Q$) functions are defined.

**Target Robot:**
The Unitree G1 humanoid robot, a 27-Degree of Freedom (DoF) platform, is used for experiments. The method is presented as a general training framework applicable to diverse control tasks and humanoid robots with morphological symmetry.

**Observation Space:**
The robot's observation $o_t \in R^{96}$ at time $t$ comprises several components:
- **Base angular velocity** ($\omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z$).
- **Projected gravity vector** ($g_x, g_y, g_z$) in the robot's body frame.
- **Velocity commands** ($c_x, c_y, c_\omega$) for desired linear (x, y) and angular (yaw) velocities.
- **Joint positions** ($\theta$) for all 27 joints, categorized into left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg, and waist.
- **Joint velocities** ($v$) for all 27 joints, similarly categorized.
- **Previous action** ($a_{t-1}$), representing target joint positions.
- **Phase input** ($\Phi_{sin}, \Phi_{cos}$), a sinusoidal clock signal to encourage periodic gaits.
A **height map** ($H$) providing robot-centric terrain information is used as privileged information exclusively for critic training.

**Action Space:**
The action $a_t \in A$ consists of target joint positions for the 27 DoF, used for position control.

**Symmetry in MDP:**
The paper leverages the reflection symmetry inherent in humanoid robot morphology. Symmetric transformation functions ($F_s, F_o, F_a$) are defined for states, observations, and actions, respectively, by swapping corresponding symmetric components (e.g., left arm and right arm joint angles, negating certain velocity components).
The following properties are established:
- **Invariance of Transition Probability:** $P(F_s(s')|F_s(s), F_a(a)) = P(s'|s, a)$.
- **Invariance of Reward Function:** $R(F_s(s), F_a(a)) = R(s, a)$.
These invariances imply that if symmetric actions are taken in symmetric states, the robot receives the same rewards and transitions to symmetric next states.
From these, the invariance of optimal critic functions ($Q^*, V^*$) is derived: $Q(F_s(s), F_a(a)) = Q(s, a)$ and $V(F_s(s)) = V(s)$.
Crucially, the **equivariance of the optimal policy** ($\pi^*$) is derived: $\pi^*(F_s(s)) = F_a(\pi^*(s))$, meaning symmetric states lead to symmetric optimal actions. This property is central to the SE-Policy design.

**Model Architecture (SE-Policy):**
The SE-Policy architecture, inspired by DreamWaQ (a PPO-based method for legged robots), comprises:
1. **History Encoder ($f_{en}$):** Inputs a temporal history of observations $o_{[t-h:t]}$ and outputs a latent feature $z$.
2. **Observation Decoder ($f_{de}$):** Predicts the next observation $o_{t+1}$ from $z$, trained via an autoencoder loss $L_{AE} = MSE(\hat{o}, o_{t+1})$.
3. **Policy Network ($f_{\pi}$ - Actor):** Outputs actions $a_t$ based on the current observation $o_t$ and the latent feature $z$.
4. **Value Network ($V$ - Critic):** Evaluates the policy by taking the height map $H_t$ and observation $o_t$ as input, outputting a value estimation.

**Equivariant Neural Network:**
All neural networks (encoder, decoder, actor, critic) are constructed using Linear layers and ReLU activations, following the ESCNN framework. This framework enforces equivariance through parameter sharing.
- The **actor** is designed to be symmetry equivariant: its output actions $a_t$ are strictly symmetric to actions generated from symmetric observations ($f_{\pi}(F_o(o_t), F_z(z)) = F_a(f_{\pi}(o_t, z))$). The symmetry transformation $F_z$ for the latent feature $z$ (assumed even-sized) is defined as swapping adjacent elements.
- The **critic** is designed to be symmetry invariant: $V(F_H(H_t), F_o(o_t)) = V(H_t, o_t)$, where $F_H$ swaps the left and right height map components.

**Training Framework:**
The training process largely follows the standard Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm:
- **Critic Training:** Uses a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss against target values (reward-to-go) from the trajectory buffer.
- **Actor Training:** Combines the autoencoder loss $L_{AE}$ with the PPO loss $L_{PPO}$, which uses a clipped surrogate objective and Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function (detailed in Appendix 1.1) is used, including:
 - Tracking rewards for linear and angular velocities.
 - Balance maintenance (e.g., penalties on z-axis velocity, pitch/roll angular velocities).
 - Posture maintenance (orientation and base height rewards).
 - Regularization terms (penalties on action oscillation, torque exceedance, hip/waist/arm movements) to encourage smooth and safe actions.
 - Gait-specific rewards (feet swing height, contact penalties).
- **Curriculum Learning:** The training progressively increases task difficulty by varying terrain types (flat, rough, discrete, slope), velocity commands, and sensor noise levels.
- **Domain Randomization:** To facilitate sim-to-real transfer, various physical parameters are randomized during training (detailed in Appendix 1.2), including ground friction, restitution, base mass, center-of-mass position, moment of inertia, motor strength, motor offset, Kp/Kd factors, and motor delay. This broadens the learned policy's robustness to discrepancies between simulation and reality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world deployment using the Unitree G1 humanoid robot to evaluate the performance of SE-Policy.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Robot:** Unitree G1, a 27-DoF humanoid robot.
- **Simulator:** NVIDIA Isaac Gym, a GPU-based physics simulator.
- **Baselines:**
 1. **DreamWaQ:** A state-of-the-art model-free DRL algorithm for legged robot locomotion.
 2. **DreamWaQ-Regu:** An enhanced DreamWaQ that includes a soft regularization term ($L_{reg} = \Vert\pi(F_o(o)) - F_a(\pi(o))\Vert^2$) in the policy updates to encourage symmetry. This represents a common approach to inducing equivariance.
 3. **SE-Policy (actor only):** An ablation study where the critic network of SE-Policy is replaced with a standard MLP critic, isolating the impact of the symmetry-invariant critic.
- **Training:** All methods were trained with consistent hyperparameters for over 5,000 iterations, approximately 4 hours, on NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs.
- **Evaluation Task:** The robot was tasked with tracking random velocity commands in the simulation environment ($|v_x| < 0.8 \text{m/s}$, $|v_y| < 0.8 \text{m/s}$, $|\omega| < 0.5 \text{rad/s}$).

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Tracking Error (TE):**
 - **TE-Velocity (TE-V):** Measures the average difference between commanded and actual robot body velocity.
 - **TE-Position (TE-P):** Measures the positional error between the robot's actual and desired positions.
 - **TE-Orientation (TE-O):** Measures the angular deviation between the robot's actual and desired orientations.
- **Temporal Symmetry (Temp-S):** Quantifies the average difference between joint actions and their symmetric joints' actions in the subsequent half-period, indicating motion periodicity and coordination. Lower values indicate higher temporal symmetry.
- **Spatial Symmetry (Spat-S):** Directly measures the equivariance property of the policy, computed as the difference between $\pi(o_{[t-h:t]})$ and $F_a(\pi(F_o(o_{[t-h:t]})))$. Lower values (ideally 0.0) indicate higher spatial symmetry.

1. **Superior Task Performance (Table 2, Figure 2):**
 - **Velocity Tracking:** SE-Policy achieved a TE-V of 9.85 cm/s, which is 40.0% lower than DreamWaQ (16.43 cm/s) and 19.2% lower than DreamWaQ-Regu (12.19 cm/s). This demonstrates a significant improvement in velocity tracking accuracy.
 - **Cumulative Errors:** SE-Policy consistently exhibited lower Tracking Error-Position (TE-P) and Tracking Error-Orientation (TE-O) over time compared to baselines (Figure 2), indicating better long-term trajectory adherence.
 - **Symmetry and Coordination:**
 - SE-Policy achieved a Spat-S of 0.0, confirming the successful implementation of strict symmetry equivariance in its actor architecture. DreamWaQ-Regu, relying on soft regularization, had a non-zero Spat-S (8.18), indicating a looser enforcement of spatial symmetry.
 - SE-Policy also obtained the lowest Temp-S (7.86), significantly lower than DreamWaQ (22.52) and DreamWaQ-Regu (16.58). This suggests that SE-Policy generates motions with higher symmetric periodicity and improved temporal coordination between symmetric body parts.

2. **Impact of Critic Invariance (SE-Policy (actor only) vs. SE-Policy):**
 - The ablation study showed that SE-Policy (actor only) performed worse than the complete SE-Policy. For instance, its TE-V was 12.2% higher and Temp-S was 17.0% higher than the full SE-Policy. This finding emphasizes that the symmetry-invariant critic is crucial for effective optimization of equivariant policies, contributing to both tracking performance and motion coordination.

3. **Locomotion Trajectory Visualization (Figure 3):**
 - **Accuracy:** SE-Policy (Figure 3c) generated robot trajectories that closely matched the ideal paths for various velocity commands, validating its superior tracking accuracy.
 - **Symmetry:** SE-Policy's trajectories displayed clear mirror symmetry with respect to the y=0m line, reflecting the enforced equivariance. In contrast, DreamWaQ (Figure 3a) showed significant deviations, and DreamWaQ-Regu (Figure 3b), despite some improvement, exhibited deviations and a tendency to turn left, indicating less robust spatial symmetry.

4. **Motion Visualization and Analysis (Figure 4):**
 - **Coordinated Foot Movements:** SE-Policy (Figure 4c) enabled highly periodic foot motions with consistent phase differences, identical amplitudes, and step sizes for both feet. This directly corresponds to its superior Temp-S score, indicating synchronized and coordinated locomotion.
 - **Asymmetry in Baselines:** DreamWaQ (Figure 4a) showed uncoordinated movements and inconsistent step sizes (e.g., 0.13m vs. 0.25m for the two feet). DreamWaQ-Regu (Figure 4b) achieved more consistent step sizes but still exhibited notable differences in motion styles and amplitudes between symmetric feet, suggesting that loss regularization, while improving spatial symmetry, may not fully address temporal coordination of symmetric joints.

5. **Real-World Experiment (Figure 5):**
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Policies trained in simulation were successfully deployed on the real Unitree G1 robot.
 - **Robustness and Accuracy:** Real-world results were consistent with simulations, showing that SE-Policy achieved higher tracking accuracy on velocity, position, and orientation compared to baselines. For example, SE-Policy successfully tracked a command within a boundary, while DreamWaQ failed.
 - **Traversability:** SE-Policy demonstrated effective traversability on various unstructured terrains, including grass, slopes, sand, and stones, confirming its robustness and practical applicability.

6. **Verification of Strict Equivariance (Figure 7, Appendix):**
 - Additional experiments explicitly demonstrated that for given observations and their symmetric counterparts, the actions produced by SE-Policy's actor were strictly symmetric ($a = F_a(F_o(o))$ after negation for clarity), confirming the architectural enforcement of strict equivariance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented on Symmetry Equivariant Policy (SE-Policy) carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of robotic control, particularly for humanoid robots:

1. **Advancement in Equivariant Reinforcement Learning:** SE-Policy introduces a method for integrating strict symmetry equivariance into the actor and invariance into the critic architectures of DRL policies. This is achieved without introducing additional hyperparameters, addressing a common challenge with regularization-based approaches. This architectural approach provides a robust and theoretically grounded way to leverage inherent morphological symmetries.

2. **Enhanced Robot Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated improvements in velocity tracking accuracy (up to 40% reduction in error) and superior spatial-temporal coordination translate to more effective and reliable robot operation. Coordinated motions contribute to better balance, stability, and overall control performance, which are critical for complex tasks and dynamic environments.

3. **More Natural and Human-like Motions:** By enforcing symmetry, SE-Policy generates robot motions that are inherently more natural and consistent with the robot's morphology. This not only improves task execution but can also enhance human-robot interaction and acceptance, as more intuitive and coordinated movements are often preferred.

4. **Improved Sample Efficiency (Implied):** While not explicitly quantified as a primary metric, equivariant networks are known to improve sample efficiency in DRL by reducing the hypothesis space and leveraging symmetries in the data. By encoding symmetry directly, the policy may generalize more effectively from less data, potentially shortening training times and reducing computational resources required.

5. **Stronger Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The combination of strict equivariance with curriculum learning and extensive domain randomization has been shown to facilitate successful transfer of policies from simulation to real hardware. This is a crucial step for deploying DRL-trained robots in practical applications, as it mitigates the "sim-to-real gap."

6. **Broad Applicability to Symmetric Robots:** Although demonstrated on the Unitree G1 humanoid, the SE-Policy framework is presented as a general training method. Its principles are applicable to any robot exhibiting morphological symmetry (e.g., other humanoid robots, bipedal robots, quadruped robots), suggesting a wide potential impact across various robot locomotion and manipulation tasks.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a baseline for strictly equivariant DRL on real humanoid robots. It opens avenues for future research into:
 - Applying SE-Policy to more complex and diverse humanoid tasks, such as navigation in highly cluttered environments, dynamic manipulation, or human-robot collaboration.
 - Investigating the interplay between different types of symmetries (e.g., rotational, translational) and their integration into DRL architectures.
 - Exploring methods to dynamically adapt or break symmetries when specific task requirements necessitate asymmetric behaviors.

In summary, SE-Policy represents a step in addressing fundamental challenges in DRL for complex robotic systems. By systematically embedding morphological symmetry, the work contributes to the development of more capable, robust, and natural-acting humanoid robots, moving closer to their effective deployment in real-world scenarios.
