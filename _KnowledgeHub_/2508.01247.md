# Coordinated Humanoid Robot Locomotion with Symmetry Equivariant Reinforcement Learning Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced SE-Policy, a Deep Reinforcement Learning framework that embeds strict symmetry equivariance into the actor and invariance into the critic for humanoid robots. This approach improved velocity tracking accuracy by 40.0% and enhanced spatio-temporal coordination on a Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating successful sim-to-real transfer and robustness across varied terrains.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies for humanoid robots often neglect inherent morphological symmetry, leading to uncoordinated, asymmetric, and suboptimal behaviors.
- Existing methods for integrating symmetry, such as data augmentation or loss regularization, often result in loose equivariance or introduce additional hyperparameters that complicate training.
- Strict equivariant methods, while theoretically promising, had not been extensively evaluated for their effectiveness and robustness on complex, agile real-world humanoid robots.

## Method

- ==Developed SE-Policy==, a DRL framework that integrates strict symmetry equivariance into the actor network's architecture and strict symmetry invariance into the critic network's architecture.
- Utilized the ESCNN framework to enforce these symmetries through parameter sharing within the neural network layers, eliminating the need for additional hyperparameters.
- Implemented a PPO-based training framework with a history encoder, an observation decoder, and a composite reward function, combined with curriculum learning and domain randomization for robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- SE-Policy achieved a velocity tracking error (TE-V) of **9.85** cm/s, which is **40.0%** lower than DreamWaQ (**16.43** cm/s) and **19.2%** lower than DreamWaQ-Regu (**12.19** cm/s).
- Demonstrated superior spatio-temporal coordination, achieving a spatial symmetry score (Spat-S) of **0.0** and the lowest temporal symmetry score (Temp-S) of **7.86**, indicating highly periodic and synchronized gaits.
- Successfully transferred policies trained in simulation to a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust locomotion and accurate command tracking on various unstructured terrains, including grass, slopes, and stones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strict architectural enforcement of symmetry equivariance in the actor leads to perfectly symmetric action generation (Spat-S of 0.0), directly ensuring coordinated movements.
> - The auxiliary symmetry-invariant critic plays a crucial role in effective optimization of equivariant policies, contributing significantly to both tracking performance and motion coordination.
> - Embedding symmetry into the DRL framework yields more natural, consistent, and human-like robot motions with superior temporal and spatial coordination, even on complex terrains.
