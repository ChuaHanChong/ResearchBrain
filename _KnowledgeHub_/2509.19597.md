# From Space to Time: Enabling Adaptive Safety with Learned Value Functions via Disturbance Recasting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California San Diego developed SPACE2TIME, a framework that enables autonomous systems to adaptively maintain safety in environments with unknown, spatially-varying disturbances by recasting spatial variations into a temporal context for offline-learned safety filters. In hardware experiments, this approach achieved a 100% success rate for a 6D Crazyflie drone, significantly outperforming baseline methods that yielded only 20% success.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based safety filters rely on static operational domain assumptions, leading to brittle and potentially unsafe behavior in environments with unknown, spatially-varying disturbances.
- Traditional safety analysis methods like Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability suffer from the curse of dimensionality, limiting their applicability to high-dimensional systems.
- Discrepancies between slow disturbance measurement rates and fast control loops mean unmodeled spatial variations can lead to safety violations if not actively anticipated.

## Method

- The **SPACE2TIME** framework reparameterizes spatially-varying disturbances as temporal variations, effectively treating the system's movement through space as a dynamic change in disturbance over time.
- An augmented system incorporating the disturbance's temporal rate of change allows for offline learning of a ==time-varying safety value function== using ==Physics-Informed Neural Networks== (e.g., DeepReach).
- During online operation, an adaptive safety filter uses current disturbance estimates and worst-case rate of change to dynamically calculate a "time to return" parameter, providing real-time safety guarantees via a ==Quadratic Program (QP)-based Control Barrier Function==.

## Results

- In 4D planar drone simulations, the SPACE2TIME (HJR-computed value functions) method achieved a **2%** safety violation rate, significantly outperforming baseline methods that showed **90-96%** violation rates.
- Hardware experiments with a 6D Crazyflie drone demonstrated a **100%** success rate (**5/5** trials) for SPACE2TIME, whereas the baseline method resulted in a **20%** success rate (**1/5** trials) with four crashes.
- The approach effectively balanced safety and performance, allowing for longer, safer navigation towards goals compared to overly conservative worst-case baseline designs which often confined the drone to small safe regions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial variations in disturbances, from the perspective of a moving system, can be effectively transformed into temporal variations, enabling their integration into time-varying safety value functions.
> - Learning a time-varying safety value function offline, parameterized by the expected disturbance change rate, allows for adaptive online deployment without requiring full *a priori* knowledge of the entire disturbance field.
> - Dynamically calculating an adaptive 'time to return' based on current disturbance and its estimated rate allows the safety filter to be realistic about present conditions while robustly anticipating future changes, reducing conservativeness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The widespread deployment of autonomous systems, particularly in safety-critical environments such as urban air mobility, necessitates robust methods for ensuring reliable, performant, and safe operation under variable environmental conditions. Current research in this area frequently employs safety filters, which minimally modify a nominal controller to guarantee safety without unduly compromising task performance. Prominent approaches for constructing these filters include Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) and Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) analysis. Recent advancements have sought to combine these paradigms, utilizing reachability-based value functions as barrier certificates to develop safety filters with formal guarantees.

However, existing methodologies face several practical limitations. Traditional HJR analysis is constrained by the curse of dimensionality, rendering it computationally intractable for high-dimensional systems. The systematic synthesis of valid CBFs for general nonlinear systems also remains an open problem. To address these scalability challenges, learning-based approaches have emerged to approximate safety value functions or barrier certificates directly from data. Nevertheless, these learning-based methods typically assume a static operational domain that is known beforehand. This assumption renders them brittle when faced with environmental conditions that shift during and across deployments, often leading to unsafe behavior or overly conservative policies designed for a static worst-case scenario.

A significant challenge arises from environments with spatially varying disturbances, such as wind in urban canyons, which are often unknown *a priori* and can differ significantly across deployments. Offline learning of safety value functions typically relies on a comprehensive joint system-environment model, which is infeasible under such unknown and variable conditions. Furthermore, disturbance measurements are frequently obtained at a slower rate than control inputs due to practical sensing and computational constraints. This slower update rate means unmodeled spatial variations can cause substantial changes between consecutive measurements, potentially leading to safety violations if not properly accounted for.

This research addresses the gap between scalable offline-learned safety value functions and their adaptive online deployment in evolving operational domains. It builds upon previous work on learning parameterized reachability value functions, particularly those that condition safety assurances on different disturbance bounds. However, prior work assumes constant disturbance bounds over space and time. This paper introduces a mechanism to explicitly account for spatially varying disturbances, reparameterizing them to enable their integration with offline-learned, time-varying safety value functions, thereby enhancing adaptability and safety guarantees in dynamic, uncertain environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the safe and adaptive deployment of offline-learned safety filters in autonomous systems operating under unknown, spatially-varying disturbances. The authors aim to overcome the inherent limitations of existing value function-based safety filters, which often rely on detailed *a priori* knowledge of all potential model mismatches or environmental disturbances.

The motivation for this work stems from several practical challenges encountered in real-world applications of autonomous systems:

1. **Unknown and Spatially-Varying Disturbances**: In safety-critical environments like urban canyons, industrial sites, or complex terrains, autonomous systems (e.g., drones) encounter disturbances (e.g., turbulent airflow, payload-drone interaction) that vary significantly across space. These disturbances are typically unknown before deployment and can change between missions. Existing methods often assume a fixed or spatially uniform worst-case disturbance, leading to either impractical conservatism or susceptibility to unsafe conditions.
2. **Inadequate Prior Information**: Current value function-based safety filters generally require precise priors on all possible sources of model mismatch, often expressed as disturbance bounds. Such comprehensive information is rarely available in real-world settings, making robust deployment difficult.
3. **Temporal Discrepancy in Measurements**: Disturbance measurements are frequently updated at a slower rate than the control loop. This discrepancy means that significant spatial variations in disturbances can occur between successive measurements, potentially leading to critical safety violations if the filter does not anticipate these changes.
4. **Limitations of Existing Adaptive Strategies**:
 - **Worst-Case Design**: Designing for the maximum possible disturbance across the entire operational domain (e.g., using a constant maximum disturbance bound) results in overly conservative behaviors, hindering task performance and utility.
 - **Perfect Oracle Information**: Assuming perfect knowledge of the spatially-varying disturbance field for offline value function computation is impractical, as such fields are unknown *a priori*. Pretraining for every conceivable disturbance landscape is intractable.
 - **Naive Parameterization**: Parameterizing learned value functions by *constant* disturbance magnitudes (e.g., using an ensemble of value functions for different fixed disturbance bounds) fails to guarantee safety in environments where disturbances change spatially and dynamically. These methods assume the observed disturbance bound will remain constant.
 - **Online Fine-tuning**: While online adaptation of disturbance models and value functions has been proposed, these approaches are typically limited to low-dimensional systems where dynamic programming is tractable. They are unsuitable for the high-dimensional systems where learning-based value functions are most beneficial, as training neural networks cannot occur within a real-time control loop.

To address these motivations, the key insight motivating SPACE2TIME is that spatial variations experienced by an autonomous system along its trajectory inherently manifest as temporal variations from the system's perspective. By reparameterizing these spatial variations into a temporal context, the authors propose to learn a time-varying safety value function offline. This function explicitly accounts for the evolution of disturbances over time, thereby implicitly capturing spatial variations along trajectories and enabling adaptive online safety filtering without requiring full *a priori* knowledge of the disturbance field. The goal is to develop a safety filter that is adaptable, formally certifiable, and amenable to offline learning for high-dimensional systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SPACE2TIME framework introduces a novel methodology to enable adaptive safety filtering by reparameterizing spatially varying disturbances as temporal variations, facilitating offline learning and online deployment.

**System Model and Background**:
The work considers a control- and disturbance-affine system defined as $\dot{x} = \tilde{f}(x, u, d) = f(x) + g(x)u + d$, where $x$ is the state, $u$ is the control input, and $d = w(x)$ represents a state-dependent disturbance from an unknown disturbance field $w(x)$. The system dynamics $\tilde{f}$ are assumed to be bounded and globally Lipschitz. The methodology builds upon Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability (HJR) analysis and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs). HJR defines a value function $V(x, t)$ whose zero level set characterizes the set of states from which a system can avoid a failure set $F$ and/or reach a target set $T$ despite worst-case disturbances. CBFs, particularly Viscosity-Based Control Barrier Functions (VB-CBFs) derived from HJR, are used to construct computationally efficient quadratic program (QP) based safety filters that minimally adjust a nominal control input $u_{nom}$ to ensure safety. For high-dimensional systems, the HJR value functions are approximated offline using self-supervised learning methods like DeepReach.

**Offline Safety Value Function Learning**:
The core innovation is to recast spatial variations in disturbance into temporal variations for offline learning.
1. **Disturbance Characterization**: The operational domain is assumed to have a maximum disturbance magnitude, $d_{max}$, and a known Lipschitz constant, $L_d$, that bounds the disturbance's maximum rate of change over space.
2. **Spatial to Temporal Reparameterization**: As the system moves through space, a spatially varying disturbance $w(x)$ causes the disturbance experienced by the system to change over time. This spatial variation is re-interpreted as a disturbance that grows linearly with time along the system's trajectory. The maximum temporal rate of disturbance change is derived as $\dot{d}_{max} = L_d M_{\tilde{f}}$, where $M_{\tilde{f}}$ is an upper bound on the system's dynamics $\tilde{f}$.
3. **Time-Varying Disturbance Set**: For offline learning, a time-varying disturbance set is defined as $D_{tv}(\dot{d}, t) = \{d \in R | |d| \leq \max\{0, d_{max} - |t|\dot{d}\}\}$. This formulation implies that as the system progresses in time towards the terminal time ($t=0$ in the backwards-in-time HJR formulation), the margin of disturbance magnitude that can be safely tolerated decreases.
4. **Augmented Dynamics**: The system's state is augmented to $z = [x, \dot{d}]$, where $\dot{d}$ is the disturbance's temporal rate of change. The dynamics are then extended to $\dot{z} = \hat{f}(z, u, \eta) = [f(x) + g(x)u + \eta; 0]$, where $\eta \in D_{tv}(\dot{d}, t)$ is the disturbance subject to the time-varying bounds. This allows the value function to explicitly depend on the expected rate of disturbance change.
5. **Control-Invariant Target Set**: A critical component is the definition of a target set $T$ that is control-invariant under worst-case disturbances. This set acts as a safe fallback region, ensuring that once reached, the system can remain safely within it.
6. **Learning the Value Function**: The reach-avoid value function $V(z, t)$ for the augmented system (6) is learned offline using a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) framework, specifically DeepReach. A modified reach-avoid control-invariant loss function is employed, $L(\theta) = E_{z,t}[\min(g(x) - V_\theta(z,t), \frac{\partial V_\theta}{\partial t} + H(\nabla_z V_\theta, z))]$, which empirically yields smoother gradients and better convergence for complex systems. This process results in an ensemble of K value functions, or a single value function parameterized by the disturbance rate $\dot{d}$.

**SPACE2TIME: Online Deployment of Temporally-Varying Value Functions**:
During online operation, the learned value functions are used within an adaptive safety filter.
1. **Online Disturbance Estimation**: The system estimates the current disturbance magnitude $\bar{d}$ and its directional derivative with respect to the dynamics, $D_{\tilde{f}}d = \nabla_x d^\top \tilde{f}$. In practice, the directional derivative is approximated using finite differences over a short history. To maintain robustness, the maximum directional derivative observed over a recent history horizon $H$ is chosen for the current control step, while the most recent disturbance magnitude estimate is used.
2. **Determining Time to Return ($t_{return}$)**: An adaptive time parameter, $t_{return}$, is calculated based on the current estimated disturbance $\bar{d}$ and the estimated worst-case disturbance rate $D_{\tilde{f}}d$: $t_{return} = \min(\frac{d_{max} - \bar{d}}{D_{\tilde{f}}d})$. This $t_{return}$ represents the estimated time until the disturbance reaches its maximum allowed magnitude, given its current rate of change. It is then used as the 'time' argument when querying the offline-learned value function $V(z, t_{return})$, where $z=[x, D_{\tilde{f}}d]$.
3. **Safety Filter Application**: A VB-CBF-based safety filter, which is a quadratic program (QP), is then solved online. This QP ensures that the chosen control input $u^*$ minimally deviates from the nominal controller $u_{nom}$ while satisfying the safety constraint derived from the value function $V(z, t_{return})$:
 $u^*(z, t_{return}) = \arg \min_{u \in U} \|u - u_{nom}\|_2^2$
 subject to $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(z, t_{return}) + \min_{\eta \in E} \nabla_z V(z, t_{return})^\top \hat{f}(z, u, \eta) \geq -\alpha(V(z, t_{return}))$, with $E = D_{tv}(D_{\tilde{f}}d, t_{return})$.
This process ensures that the system is realistic about the current disturbance state ($\bar{d}$) while being pessimistic and robust about future changes in disturbance (by using the worst-case rate $D_{\tilde{f}}d$), thereby reducing conservativeness compared to static worst-case approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SPACE2TIME framework was validated through extensive simulations and hardware experiments, demonstrating its ability to improve safety and goal-reaching performance compared to baseline approaches that do not explicitly account for disturbance variations.

**Simulation Experiments (4D Planar Drone)**:
- **Setup**: A planar drone navigating a simulated cityscape environment with obstacles, where spatially varying wind disturbances were modeled as an exponential function of altitude, intensifying towards urban canyon bottoms. Wind parameters were randomized for each trajectory.
- **Baselines Compared**:
 - **HJR Naive**: Uses an ensemble of HJR value functions, each computed for a different *constant* disturbance magnitude.
 - **HJR Naive Worst-Case**: Uses a single HJR value function computed assuming the maximum possible disturbance everywhere.
 - **DeepReach (DR) Naive**: Uses a learned value function parameterized by *constant* disturbance bounds.
 - **HJR Ours**: SPACE2TIME utilizing an ensemble of HJR-computed value functions conditioned on different *fixed disturbance rates*.
 - **DR Ours**: SPACE2TIME utilizing a learned value function parameterized by *disturbance rate*.
- **Performance Metrics**: Percentage of Safety Violations, Mean Goal Distance, and Mean Trajectory Length.
- **Key Findings**:
 - **Safety Violations**: The "HJR Ours" method achieved the lowest safety violation rate at 2%, significantly outperforming "HJR Naive" (96%) and "DR Naive" (90%). "DR Ours" also showed substantial improvement with a 34% violation rate, which the authors attribute partly to inaccuracies in the learned value function.
 - **Performance**: "HJR Ours" demonstrated the best goal-reaching performance (0.78 Mean Goal Distance) and trajectory length (993 steps), indicating it was able to navigate safely for longer and closer to goals. The "HJR Naive Worst-Case" achieved 0% safety violations but at the cost of high conservativeness, resulting in a large Mean Goal Distance (1.95) and often confining the drone to a small safe region, thus being non-performant.
 - **Qualitative Observations**: Trajectory visualizations confirmed that "DR Ours" safely navigated to goals, while "DR Naive" often crashed due to inadequate adaptation. "HJR Worst-Case" was overly conservative, frequently remaining in the initial safe region.
 - **Ablation Study (History Horizon H)**: Varying the history horizon $H$ (for estimating disturbance rate) showed minimal overall impact. For HJR Ours, larger $H$ slightly reduced violations. For DR Ours, a slight increase in crashes with larger $H$ was hypothesized to be due to increased conservativeness moving the drone to regions where the learned value function had higher error.

**Hardware Experiments (6D Crazyflie Drone)**:
- **Setup**: Crazyflie drones with OptiTrack motion capture, navigating physical obstacles mimicking urban canyons. Disturbances were "spoofed" (added to state observations) to ensure consistent and controlled wind profiles, emulating the simulated conditions for a 6D drone model.
- **Baselines Compared**: "DR Ours" (SPACE2TIME with learned value function parameterized by disturbance rate) vs. "DR Naive" (learned value function parameterized by constant disturbance bounds).
- **Key Findings**:
 - **Success Rate**: "DR Ours" achieved a 100% success rate (5/5 trials) without crashes. In contrast, "DR Naive" only achieved a 20% success rate (1/5 trials), with 4 out of 5 rollouts resulting in crashes.
 - **Qualitative Observations**: Hardware trajectories illustrated that "DR Ours" successfully adapted to increasing disturbances as the drone descended into urban canyons, maintaining safety. "DR Naive" failed to adapt sufficiently, leading to collisions.

In summary, the results indicate that SPACE2TIME's approach of reparameterizing spatial disturbance variations as temporal variations enables effective online adaptation for safety filters. This leads to significantly improved safety and better task performance compared to baselines that rely on constant disturbance assumptions, both in simulated and real-world conditions. The performance gap between HJR-based and DeepReach-based "Ours" methods highlights that the overall system performance is also influenced by the accuracy of the learned value functions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SPACE2TIME framework presents a notable advancement in the field of autonomous systems, particularly for their deployment in safety-critical and dynamically uncertain environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Adaptive Safety in Dynamic Environments**: The core contribution is bridging the gap between offline-learned safety certificates and their adaptive use in real-world scenarios characterized by unknown, spatially-varying disturbances. This capability is crucial for autonomous systems operating in complex environments like urban air mobility, industrial inspection, or disaster response, where environmental conditions are unpredictable and static worst-case assumptions lead to either unsafe operation or severe performance degradation.
2. **Reduced Conservativeness with Formal Guarantees**: By reparameterizing spatial disturbance variations into temporal variations, the method allows the safety filter to be "realistic about the present" and "pessimistic about the future." This adaptive strategy significantly reduces the inherent conservativeness of methods that rely on fixed maximum disturbance bounds, thereby enhancing the system's performance and maneuverability without sacrificing formal safety guarantees derived from reachability analysis and CBFs.
3. **Scalability for High-Dimensional Systems**: The framework leverages offline learning techniques (e.g., DeepReach) to approximate value functions. This approach is computationally scalable to high-dimensional systems, overcoming the "curse of dimensionality" that limits traditional dynamic programming methods in HJR analysis. This broadens the applicability of robust safety filters to more complex robotic platforms.
4. **Practical Validation**: Extensive validation in both high-fidelity simulations and hardware experiments using a 6D quadcopter model underscores the practical relevance and robustness of SPACE2TIME. The hardware results, in particular, demonstrate its efficacy in emulated real-world conditions where naive approaches failed consistently.
5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research directions. These include exploring methods to further reduce conservativeness, such as capturing more nuanced disturbance variations beyond a scalar temporal rate. Improving robustness to model uncertainty (beyond just disturbance uncertainty) is another critical area. Furthermore, extending the framework's compatibility to other learning paradigms, potentially including model-free reinforcement learning, could mitigate the current reliance on accurate dynamics models.

While the framework introduces some inherent conservativeness by compressing multi-dimensional spatial variations into a scalar temporal parameter and assumes accurate characterization of disturbance bounds or rates, it represents a substantial step toward practical, adaptive, and formally verifiable safety for autonomous systems in complex, real-world operational domains. Its impact could facilitate the broader adoption of autonomous technologies in environments where stringent safety requirements and dynamic conditions have historically been significant barriers.
