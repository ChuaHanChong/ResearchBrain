# SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes: A Comparative Study of Foundation Model Post-training

> [!summary] Summary
> A comparative study by researchers from Hong Kong University, UC Berkeley, NYU, and Google DeepMind empirically demonstrates that Reinforcement Learning (RL) promotes generalization to novel rules and visual inputs, while Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) tends to induce memorization, particularly in complex reasoning tasks for foundation models like Llama-3.2-Vision-11B. RL improved out-of-distribution performance by up to +61.1% on visual tasks and also enhanced underlying visual recognition capabilities.

## Problem

- A lack of clear understanding regarding the differential impact of Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) on generalization versus memorization in foundation models.
- Unclear how SFT and RL influence performance on complex, rule-based reasoning tasks, particularly in multimodal (vision-language) contexts.
- The need for specific experimental setups to clearly differentiate a model's capacity to memorize training data from its ability to acquire transferable principles.

## Method

- Conducted a controlled comparative study using `Llama-3.2-Vision-11B` as the backbone model for both SFT and RL post-training.
- Developed two custom environments: ==`GeneralPoints`== (a card game for arithmetic reasoning) and adapted ==`V-IRL`== (a navigation task for spatial reasoning), both with pure language and vision-language variants.
- Employed a ==multi-turn Reinforcement Learning framework== with an iterative verifier that provided outcome-based rewards and textual feedback, allowing for dynamic self-correction and OOD evaluation on textual (rule) and visual variations.

## Results

- RL consistently improved out-of-distribution (OOD) performance across all tasks (e.g., **+3.5%** on GP-L and **+61.1%** on V-IRL-VL visual OOD), while SFT consistently showed performance degradation (e.g., **-8.1%** on GP-L and **-79.5%** on V-IRL-L OOD).
- RL training enhanced the VLM's underlying visual recognition accuracy and achieved a new state-of-the-art performance on the V-IRL benchmark (**+33.8%** to **77.8%** success rate).
- Increasing verification steps in RL training led to a +**5.99%** OOD performance improvement on GP-L, highlighting the importance of iterative self-correction for generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning fosters generalizable knowledge, enabling models to adapt to novel rules and unseen visual contexts.
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning primarily leads to memorization of training data and struggles with out-of-distribution generalization.
> - SFT serves as a necessary prerequisite, acting as a "format teacher" to stabilize model outputs for effective subsequent RL training, while more verification steps in RL significantly enhance generalization.
