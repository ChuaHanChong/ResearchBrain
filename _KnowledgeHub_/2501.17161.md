# SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes: A Comparative Study of Foundation Model Post-training

> [!summary] Summary
> A comparative study by researchers from Hong Kong University, UC Berkeley, NYU, and Google DeepMind empirically demonstrates that Reinforcement Learning (RL) promotes generalization to novel rules and visual inputs, while Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) tends to induce memorization, particularly in complex reasoning tasks for foundation models like Llama-3.2-Vision-11B. RL improved out-of-distribution performance by up to +61.1% on visual tasks and also enhanced underlying visual recognition capabilities.

## Problem

- A lack of clear understanding regarding the differential impact of Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) on generalization versus memorization in foundation models.
- Unclear how SFT and RL influence performance on complex, rule-based reasoning tasks, particularly in multimodal (vision-language) contexts.
- The need for specific experimental setups to clearly differentiate a model's capacity to memorize training data from its ability to acquire transferable principles.

## Method

- Conducted a controlled comparative study using `Llama-3.2-Vision-11B` as the backbone model for both SFT and RL post-training.
- Developed two custom environments: ==`GeneralPoints`== (a card game for arithmetic reasoning) and adapted ==`V-IRL`== (a navigation task for spatial reasoning), both with pure language and vision-language variants.
- Employed a ==multi-turn Reinforcement Learning framework== with an iterative verifier that provided outcome-based rewards and textual feedback, allowing for dynamic self-correction and OOD evaluation on textual (rule) and visual variations.

## Results

- RL consistently improved out-of-distribution (OOD) performance across all tasks (e.g., **+3.5%** on GP-L and **+61.1%** on V-IRL-VL visual OOD), while SFT consistently showed performance degradation (e.g., **-8.1%** on GP-L and **-79.5%** on V-IRL-L OOD).
- RL training enhanced the VLM's underlying visual recognition accuracy and achieved a new state-of-the-art performance on the V-IRL benchmark (**+33.8%** to **77.8%** success rate).
- Increasing verification steps in RL training led to a +**5.99%** OOD performance improvement on GP-L, highlighting the importance of iterative self-correction for generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning fosters generalizable knowledge, enabling models to adapt to novel rules and unseen visual contexts.
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning primarily leads to memorization of training data and struggles with out-of-distribution generalization.
> - SFT serves as a necessary prerequisite, acting as a "format teacher" to stabilize model outputs for effective subsequent RL training, while more verification steps in RL significantly enhance generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the crucial intersection of foundation model post-training, generalization theory, and multimodal AI. Foundation models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have demonstrated impressive capabilities, but their exact mechanisms of learning and generalization remain areas of active investigation. Two primary post-training techniques, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or task-specific RL, are widely adopted. However, a clear understanding of their differential impact on a model's ability to generalize to novel, unseen situations versus merely memorizing training data has been lacking, especially in complex reasoning tasks and multimodal contexts.

The broader research landscape includes:
- **Foundation Model Training Paradigms:** SFT, as exemplified by models like FLAN and LIMA, is known for adapting pre-trained models to instruction-following and specific output formats. RL, as seen in RLHF, primarily aligns models with human preferences or optimizes for specific task objectives. This paper moves beyond simply applying these methods to *comparatively analyze* their fundamental learning characteristics.
- **Memorization vs. Generalization in Neural Networks:** This is a long-standing challenge in deep learning. Recent studies have explored how LLMs exhibit memorization (e.g., reproducing training data) and how generalization manifests, particularly in complex, reasoning-intensive tasks. This paper contributes by specifically examining how different *post-training methodologies* influence this trade-off, rather than just observing it in pre-trained or generally fine-tuned models.
- **Multi-modal Reasoning:** VLMs are increasingly capable but often face limitations in visual perception and spatial reasoning. Prior work on improving VLMs has largely focused on SFT data curation or architectural enhancements. This study uniquely investigates the role of RL in enhancing *underlying visual capabilities* and promoting generalization in multimodal reasoning tasks.
- **Inference-Time Computation:** Recent trends emphasize the importance of scaling up computation during inference, for instance, through chain-of-thought prompting or iterative verification. This work integrates a multi-turn RL framework that inherently leverages such iterative processes, building upon ideas of verifiers and self-correction, and explicitly studies how increasing verification steps impacts generalization.

This paper addresses a critical gap by providing a direct, controlled comparison of SFT and RL on generalization and memorization across both unimodal (LLM) and multimodal (VLM) settings. It introduces novel environments designed to isolate and evaluate rule-based and visual generalization. By showing that RL fosters generalization while SFT promotes memorization, the work provides crucial insights for designing more robust and adaptable AI systems, moving the field towards a better understanding of how different post-training strategies shape the capabilities of large foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a comprehensive comparative study of Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) as post-training techniques for foundation models, specifically focusing on their respective roles in enhancing model generalization versus promoting memorization.

The core motivation stems from the prevailing ambiguity surrounding the distinct effects of SFT and RL on generalization in complex, rule-based reasoning tasks, particularly within the context of both text-based and visual domains. Building reliable and robust AI systems necessitates a clear understanding of whether these post-training methods merely lead models to recall trained examples (memorization) or enable them to apply learned principles to novel, unseen situations (generalization).

To address this overarching question, the authors set forth several specific objectives:

- **Comparative Analysis of Generalization:** To systematically compare the generalization capabilities induced by SFT and RL to novel textual rules and visual domains. This involves training models on a specific rule or visual context and evaluating their performance on out-of-distribution (OOD) variants.
- **Distinguishing Memorization from Generalization:** To develop experimental setups that clearly differentiate between a model's capacity to memorize training data and its ability to acquire transferable principles.
- **Evaluation in Diverse Reasoning Tasks:** To assess these phenomena in two distinct types of reasoning:
 - **Arithmetic Reasoning:** Through the introduction of `GeneralPoints`, a novel card game designed to evaluate a model's ability to apply arithmetic rules to unseen variations.
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** By adapting `V-IRL`, a real-world navigation environment, to test a model's spatial understanding and generalization in both rule-based and visual contexts.
- **Investigating Multi-modal Impact:** To specifically study how SFT and RL affect generalization in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), examining both rule-based textual generalization and generalization to novel visual inputs (e.g., different card colors, new cities).
- **Understanding RL's Effect on Visual Capabilities:** To explore whether RL training, particularly with outcome-based rewards, can improve a VLM's underlying visual recognition capabilities.
- **Determining the Role of SFT for RL:** To ascertain if SFT is a necessary prerequisite or a beneficial step for effective RL training, especially in terms of stabilizing model output formats.
- **Assessing the Impact of Inference-Time Compute:** To examine how scaling up inference-time computation (via more verification steps) influences the generalization performance of RL.

In essence, the research aims to provide empirical evidence that clarifies the often-assumed but underexplored dichotomy between SFT and RL, arguing that RL is more conducive to learning generalizable knowledge for robust foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this study is grounded in a comparative experimental design, meticulously evaluating Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) on carefully constructed tasks that allow for explicit testing of generalization across both rule-based and visual variations. The core approach involves training foundation models (specifically, Llama-3.2-Vision-11B) using these two post-training paradigms and then assessing their performance on in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) test sets.

#### 3.1. Foundation Model and Post-training Setup

- **Backbone Model:** The study utilizes `Llama-3.2-Vision-11B`, a state-of-the-art vision-language model, as the base for all experiments.
- **Training Pipeline:** Following standard practices in RLHF and RL4VLM, models are first initialized with SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning) to enable basic instruction following and then subjected to further SFT or RL training. This allows for a controlled comparison of the *incremental* effects of each method.
- **Reinforcement Learning Framework:** A multi-turn RL setting is employed, similar to `RL4VLM` (Zhai et al., 2024a).
 - **State Space:** For LLMs, the state is defined by the input text sequence; for VLMs, it includes both input text and RGB images.
 - **Action Space:** The model's output text sequence.
 - **Verifier (VER):** A critical component, the verifier evaluates the model's output, provides an outcome-based reward (`r_t`), and generates textual feedback (`v_ver_t`). This allows for dynamic, iterative self-correction.
 - **Sequential Revision:** The input prompt for subsequent turns (`v_in_t`) is constructed by concatenating the initial system prompt with all previous model outputs and verifier feedback (`[v_out_k, v_ver_k]`). This enables the model to revise its response based on past interactions and verification results.
 - **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used as the backbone algorithm for updating the policy network.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Environments and Generalization Modes

The study introduces one novel environment and adapts another, both designed to test distinct forms of generalization:

- **GeneralPoints Environment (Arithmetic Reasoning):**
 - **Task:** A card game where the model receives four cards (text or image) and must formulate an equation using each card's value exactly once to reach a target number (default 24).
 - **Variants:**
 - `GP-L`: Pure language input.
 - `GP-VL`: Vision-language input, requiring visual recognition of cards.
 - **Rule Variations (Textual Generalization):** Training uses one rule for face cards ('J','Q','K' all count as '10'). OOD evaluation uses a different rule (J=11, Q=12, K=13), forcing the model to apply arithmetic reasoning to novel interpretations of symbols.
 - **Visual Variations (Visual Generalization - for GP-VL):** Training uses cards of one color (black suits ♠,♣); OOD evaluation uses cards of a different color (red suits ♥,♦), assessing generalization independent of visual attributes.
 - **Reward Design:** A structured reward system penalizes incorrect calculations, illegal number usage, or exceeding max steps, and rewards correct equations. `GP-VL` also penalizes visual recognition failures.

- **V-IRL Environment (Spatial Reasoning):**
 - **Task:** A real-world navigation task where the model follows instructions to navigate to a target location, processing visual observations (landmarks) and taking actions.
 - **Variants:**
 - `V-IRL-L`: Pure language description of the environment.
 - `V-IRL-VL`: Vision-language input, including street views.
 - **Rule Variations (Textual Generalization):** Training uses an 'absolute orientation' action space (e.g., 'north', 'east'). OOD evaluation uses a 'relative orientation' action space (e.g., 'left', 'right'), testing the model's ability to adapt spatial reasoning to different linguistic action paradigms.
 - **Visual Variations (Visual Generalization - for V-IRL-VL):** Training on routes from New York City; OOD evaluation on routes from diverse cities worldwide, assessing robustness to novel visual environments.
 - **Reward Design:** Rewards correct actions at each step, penalizes wrong actions, exceeding max steps, or failing to detect landmarks.

#### 3.3. Ablation Studies and Analysis

Beyond the main comparison, the study conducts specific ablations:
- **Visual Capabilities:** Analyzing how SFT and RL affect a VLM's visual recognition accuracy (e.g., identifying cards in `GP-VL`).
- **Necessity of SFT:** Directly applying RL to the base model without prior SFT to determine its critical role in instruction following.
- **Verification Iterations:** Varying the number of allowed verification steps during RL training to understand its impact on generalization.

#### 3.4. Metrics and Computation

- **Success Rate:** For `GeneralPoints` (at least one success during inference time verification) and overall `V-IRL` (correct action at each movable point).
- **Per-step Accuracy:** For `V-IRL-VL`.
- **Computation Estimation:** FLOPs are estimated for training and inference to normalize comparisons across experiments.

This rigorous methodology allows the authors to draw clear conclusions about the distinct characteristics of SFT and RL, particularly concerning their impact on generalization and memorization in complex multimodal reasoning tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents compelling evidence demonstrating distinct behaviors of SFT and RL concerning generalization and memorization across various tasks and modalities.

#### 4.1. RL Generalizes, SFT Memorizes Across Rule Variations

- **Consistent Generalization by RL:** RL consistently improved out-of-distribution (OOD) performance across all tested tasks, including both pure language (LLM) and vision-language (VLM) settings.
 - In `GP-L` (language-only arithmetic), RL achieved a +3.5% increase in OOD success rate (from 11.5% to 15.0%).
 - In `V-IRL-L` (language-only navigation), RL improved OOD per-step accuracy by +11.0% (from 80.8% to 91.8%).
 - Even with the added complexity of visual recognition, RL maintained OOD improvements in VLMs: +3.0% on `GP-VL` (11.2% to 14.2%) and +9.3% on `V-IRL-VL` (35.7% to 45.0%). This indicates RL's ability to learn generalizable rules that adapt to unseen textual variants.
- **SFT Tends to Memorize:** In stark contrast, SFT consistently showed performance degradation across all OOD evaluations.
 - On `GP-L`, SFT performance decreased by -8.1% (from 11.5% to 3.4%).
 - On `V-IRL-L`, the drop was significant, -79.5% (from 80.8% to 1.3%).
 - Similar degradation was observed in VLMs: -5.6% on `GP-VL` (11.2% to 5.6%) and -33.2% on `V-IRL-VL` (35.7% to 2.5%). These results strongly suggest that SFT primarily memorizes training rules and struggles to generalize out-of-distribution.

#### 4.2. RL Generalizes in Visual Out-of-Distribution Tasks

- The findings extended to visual OOD tasks, where the underlying rules remained consistent but the visual input varied.
- **RL's Visual Generalization:** RL continued to generalize effectively. For instance, in `GP-VL` (trained on black card suits, tested on red), RL showed a +17.6% improvement in OOD success (from 23.6% to 41.2%). On `V-IRL-VL` (trained on NYC routes, tested on worldwide cities), RL achieved a remarkable +61.1% increase (from 16.7% to 77.8%).
- **SFT's Visual Memorization:** SFT, conversely, suffered performance decreases: -9.9% on `GP-VL` (23.6% to 13.7%) and -5.6% on `V-IRL-VL` (16.7% to 11.1%).
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** As a byproduct, the multi-turn RL formulation achieved a new state-of-the-art performance on the `V-IRL` mini benchmark, improving it by +33.8% (from 44.0% to 77.8%). This was achieved with an open-source model and an end-to-end RL approach, unlike prior SOTA that relied on two-stage VLM-LLM collaboration and closed-source models.

#### 4.3. RL Improves Visual Capabilities

- Further analysis on `GP-VL` revealed that scaling RL compute not only improves overall generalization but also enhances the VLM's underlying visual recognition accuracy (e.g., correctly identifying card numbers from images).
- In contrast, scaling SFT compute was observed to *deteriorate* both visual recognition accuracy and overall performance, suggesting it might locally overfit to reasoning tokens while neglecting recognition tokens.

#### 4.4. The Role of SFT for RL Training

- The study found that SFT initialization is *necessary* for effective RL training, at least with the `Llama-3.2-Vision-11B` backbone. Without SFT, direct end-to-end RL attempts failed, as the base model exhibited poor instruction-following capabilities, generating long, unstructured, and tangential responses that prevented the retrieval of task-related information and rewards.
- This suggests SFT acts as a "format teacher," stabilizing the model's output format and enabling subsequent RL to effectively optimize for task performance and generalization. However, RL struggles if initialized from an *overly-tuned* (overfitted) SFT checkpoint, implying limits to its recovery capabilities.

#### 4.5. Role of Verification Iterations

- Increasing the number of verification steps during RL training significantly improves generalization. For `GP-L`, increasing verification steps from 1 to 10 led to a +5.99% OOD performance improvement, compared to a marginal +0.48% for a single verification step. This highlights the importance of iterative self-correction and external feedback for fostering generalizable knowledge.

In summary, the results strongly support the hypothesis that RL is superior to SFT for achieving generalization in rule-based reasoning across both textual and visual modalities, while SFT tends towards memorization. Furthermore, RL training can improve underlying visual perception, and SFT plays a crucial enabling role by establishing basic instruction following before RL can be effectively applied.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides crucial insights into the fundamental learning mechanisms of foundation models under different post-training paradigms, with significant implications for the development of more robust, generalizable, and intelligent AI systems.

#### 5.1. Clarifying the Role of SFT vs. RL

The most profound contribution is the clear empirical demonstration that **Reinforcement Learning fosters generalization, while Supervised Fine-tuning tends to induce memorization** in rule-based reasoning tasks, both text-based and multimodal. This finding challenges the assumption that extensive SFT alone is sufficient for broad generalization in complex tasks. It suggests that while SFT is excellent for teaching desired output formats and instruction-following, RL is superior for instilling transferable rules and knowledge.

#### 5.2. Implications for Foundation Model Training Strategy

- **Strategic Post-training:** For applications requiring robust generalization to novel rules or unseen visual contexts (e.g., in autonomous navigation, complex problem-solving, or scientific discovery), RL should be prioritized or integrated strategically in the post-training pipeline.
- **SFT as an Enabler:** The study clarifies that SFT is not redundant but serves a critical prerequisite role, acting as a "format teacher" to stabilize the model's output and instruction-following capabilities. This initial alignment is essential for subsequent RL to operate effectively and achieve its performance gains. This suggests a multi-stage training paradigm where SFT provides the initial scaffolding, and RL then refines and generalizes.
- **Importance of Iterative Verification:** The finding that more verification steps enhance RL's generalization capability underscores the value of iterative self-correction and external feedback mechanisms. This points towards the importance of active learning, human-in-the-loop systems, or sophisticated automated verifiers in training advanced AI.

#### 5.3. Advancing Multimodal AI Capabilities

- **Enhanced Visual Generalization:** The demonstration that RL can improve a VLM's underlying visual recognition capabilities (e.g., distinguishing card values regardless of color) is highly significant. This suggests that RL, driven by outcome-based rewards, can refine perception beyond what supervised pre-training or SFT alone achieves, leading to more perceptually robust VLMs. This is a critical step towards developing VLMs that are truly grounded in real-world visual understanding rather than just pattern matching.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving new state-of-the-art results on the `V-IRL` benchmark with an open-source model using an end-to-end RL approach further validates the practical efficacy of this paradigm for complex multimodal tasks like real-world navigation.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The paper openly discusses its limitations, which in themselves define critical avenues for future research:
- **SFT's Visual Shortcomings:** Further investigation is needed into why SFT struggles with `GP-VL` and appears to degrade visual recognition. Hypothesizing "local overfitting to reasoning tokens while neglecting recognition tokens" provides a concrete starting point.
- **Limits of RL's Recovery:** Understanding the precise conditions under which RL can effectively recover from underfit or overfit SFT initial checkpoints is crucial. This will help define the optimal hand-off point from SFT to RL and build more resilient training processes.

In conclusion, this work significantly deepens our understanding of how SFT and RL fundamentally shape foundation models. It provides compelling evidence for RL's advantage in fostering generalizable knowledge and improving multimodal perception, while also highlighting the necessary, albeit distinct, role of SFT. These insights are instrumental for developing the next generation of AI systems that are not only powerful but also truly robust and adaptable to the complexities of the real world.
