# LongE2V: Long-Horizon Event-based Video Reconstruction, Prediction, and Frame Interpolation with Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University developed LongE2V, a unified framework leveraging fine-tuned video diffusion models for high-fidelity event-based video reconstruction, prediction, and zero-shot frame interpolation. The model demonstrates superior perceptual quality with improved LPIPS scores across real-world datasets and maintains long-term temporal stability in generated sequences.

## Problem

- Traditional event-based video reconstruction methods often yield blurry textures and loss of fine details due to regression-to-the-mean effects.
- Existing generative approaches for event-based video prediction struggle with long-term temporal stability, leading to error accumulation and visual drift.
- Current event-based frame interpolation methods frequently produce artifacts like ghosting in complex, fast motion scenarios and lack zero-shot generalization.

## Method

- A pre-trained ==Video Diffusion Model (CogVideoX I2V)== is fine-tuned to create a unified framework, **LongE2V**, capable of conditional event-based video reconstruction, prediction, and interpolation.
- An **Autoregressive Unrolling** training strategy combined with **Adaptive Context Switching** is introduced to mitigate error propagation and maintain temporal coherence in long video sequences.
- Techniques like **Event Voxel Density Augmentation**, **Reencoding Alignment**, and **Cross Residual Correction** are implemented to enhance robustness across varying event densities and improve interpolation quality.

## Results

- LongE2V consistently achieved the best LPIPS scores across multiple datasets (ECD: **0.139**, MVSEC: **0.405**, HQF: **0.240**) for reconstruction, indicating superior perceptual quality over regression-based methods.
- The model demonstrated superior performance in video prediction, obtaining higher PSNR/SSIM and lower LPIPS (e.g., ECD **0.110** LPIPS) compared to diffusion-based baselines, validating its long-term robustness.
- LongE2V exhibited zero-shot generalization for **31-frame** interpolation, achieving lower LPIPS (BS-ERGB: **0.124**) compared to supervised baselines and maintaining significantly higher subject consistency (**0.7204** on HQF) in generated videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-tuning a foundational video diffusion model on event camera data provides a data-efficient way to achieve high perceptual quality in event-based video generation tasks.
> - Explicit context management via autoregressive unrolling and adaptive context switching is crucial for maintaining long-term temporal stability and preventing drift in event-driven video prediction.
> - Accurate temporal alignment through re-encoding and cross-residual correction in the latent space is essential for synthesizing artifact-free, high-fidelity intermediate frames during interpolation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Event cameras represent a distinct paradigm in visual sensing, offering advantages such as high temporal resolution (microsecond scale), high dynamic range (HDR), and absence of motion blur. These bio-inspired sensors asynchronously capture brightness changes, yielding sparse, intensity-free event streams rather than conventional image frames. This unique output format, while advantageous in high-speed or challenging lighting conditions, poses difficulties for standard computer vision algorithms that typically operate on dense, intensity-based frames. Consequently, reconstructing high-fidelity, human-interpretable video from event streams is an inherently ill-posed problem and an active area of research.

Early approaches to event-based video generation, such as E2VID and FireNet, primarily utilized Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to aggregate temporal information from event streams. While these methods established foundational baselines, they often suffered from limitations such as "regression-to-the-mean" effects, leading to blurry textures and a loss of fine details in reconstructed videos. Moreover, their ability to maintain temporal stability and coherence over extended video sequences was limited, resulting in error accumulation and visual drift.

The advent of generative artificial intelligence, particularly Video Diffusion Models (VDMs), has introduced new possibilities for video synthesis. Recent works, such as VDM-EVFI, have adapted these models for tasks like event-based video frame interpolation, demonstrating improvements in generation quality compared to regression-based methods. However, a naive application of diffusion models can still lead to instability in long-term predictions, manifesting as severe error accumulation and temporal drift. Additionally, existing interpolation methods may struggle with complex, fast dynamics, producing artifacts such as ghosting. A notable challenge in the field is that many prior solutions are specialized for individual tasks (reconstruction, prediction, or interpolation), often requiring distinct architectures and training pipelines, which limits their flexibility and generalizability.

LongE2V addresses these limitations by leveraging pre-trained video diffusion models within a unified framework. The work aims to integrate event-based video reconstruction, prediction, and frame interpolation into a single architecture. By fine-tuning a foundational video model, LongE2V seeks to achieve high data efficiency and superior perceptual quality, while explicitly tackling the issues of long-term stability and temporal consistency that have hampered previous generative approaches in event-based vision. The research positions itself as a bridge between neuromorphic sensing and human-interpretable video generation, striving for high-fidelity outputs that are temporally coherent and robust across various scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified and robust framework, named LongE2V, capable of simultaneously performing event-based video reconstruction, prediction, and frame interpolation. This is motivated by several identified shortcomings in existing methodologies for processing event camera data into conventional video formats.

Specifically, the key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **High-Fidelity Video Reconstruction:**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional regression-based methods (e.g., E2VID, E2VID+) often produce blurry textures and lose fine details due to their tendency to average out possible solutions, a phenomenon known as "regression-to-the-mean." This results in perceptually suboptimal video quality, hindering applications that require clear visual information.
 - **Objective:** To recover sharp, high-fidelity photometric details and textures from sparse event streams, surpassing the perceptual quality of existing regression-based techniques.

2. **Long-Term Temporal Stability in Video Prediction:**
 - **Motivation:** Directly applying existing generative models, including some video diffusion models (e.g., VDM-EVFI), to generate long video sequences from event data often leads to error accumulation and severe temporal drift. This causes generated frames to progressively deviate from realistic dynamics or lose scene coherence over time, making long-horizon prediction unreliable.
 - **Objective:** To mitigate error accumulation and maintain temporal coherence and minimal drift for extremely long video sequences in prediction tasks, ensuring structural and identity integrity over extended periods.

3. **Accurate Video Frame Interpolation with Zero-Shot Capability:**
 - **Motivation:** Current frame interpolation methods frequently struggle with fast and complex motion dynamics, leading to artifacts such as ghosting or structural collapse in intermediate frames. Furthermore, many dedicated interpolation methods require specific training, limiting their flexibility.
 - **Objective:** To synthesize accurate intermediate frames by leveraging event dynamics as temporal guidance, capable of handling complex motion without ghosting artifacts. A secondary objective is to achieve this with zero-shot adaptation, utilizing the same model weights trained for reconstruction and prediction, thus demonstrating broad generalization.

4. **Development of a Unified and Data-Efficient Architecture:**
 - **Motivation:** A common limitation in the field is the development of task-specific architectures for reconstruction, prediction, or interpolation. This necessitates separate training efforts and reduces the versatility of models.
 - **Objective:** To propose a single, integrated architecture that can address all three core event-based video generation tasks by adjusting input conditions. This approach aims to leverage the data efficiency offered by fine-tuning a pre-trained foundational video diffusion model, reducing the need for extensive task-specific datasets.

5. **Robustness Across Varying Sensor Resolutions:**
 - **Motivation:** Event camera data can originate from diverse sensors with varying resolutions and characteristics, leading to significant variations in event voxel density. This inconsistency can affect the robustness and generalization of models.
 - **Objective:** To ensure the model's robustness and consistent performance across different event sensor resolutions and scene depths by developing mechanisms that account for variations in event voxel density.

These objectives collectively aim to bridge the gap between the specialized capabilities of event cameras and the demands of standard vision applications, providing a more versatile, stable, and high-quality solution for event-based video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LongE2V's methodology is built upon a pre-trained Video Diffusion Model, specifically CogVideoX I2V, which it fine-tunes to handle the intricacies of event-based video generation. The approach formulates event-based video reconstruction, prediction, and frame interpolation as conditional generation tasks driven by event voxel representations.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

- **Video Diffusion Model (VDM) Backbone:** The chosen backbone, CogVideoX I2V, utilizes a 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for compressing video frames into latents ($Z_0$) with a 4x temporal and 8x spatial compression ratio. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) is then employed to denoise these latents, minimizing a denoising objective. Conditioning signals ($C$) guide the generation process.
- **Event Representation:** Raw asynchronous event streams are converted into a synchronized, frame-based format. Events are discretized into a 3-channel voxel grid ($V \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times H \times W}$, with $B=3$ temporal bins) by accumulating event polarities through linear interpolation. This voxel grid serves as a spatio-temporal input for the VDM.

#### 3.2. Event-based Video Generation Framework

The framework unifies the three tasks by adjusting input conditions:
- **Video Reconstruction:** Recovers photometric details from event streams. For the initial video segment (first chunk), both the start frame latent and context latents are zero-initialized. For subsequent chunks, the last generated frame of the preceding chunk serves as the start frame for the current chunk, transitioning into a prediction-like mode.
- **Video Prediction:** Generates future video frames given a start frame, the corresponding event stream, and historical context. For the first chunk, the context frames are populated by replicating the start frame, while context event voxels are initialized as empty.
- **Video Frame Interpolation:** Synthesizes intermediate frames between a given start and end frame. This task utilizes both forward and backward event streams as dense motion guides.

#### 3.3. Key Technical Contributions

1. **Autoregressive Unrolling:**
 - **Problem:** During standard autoregressive video generation, a domain gap exists because the model is trained with Ground Truth (GT) context frames but infers using its own generated predictions as context. This discrepancy can lead to error accumulation.
 - **Approach:** An iterative training strategy is introduced. Initially, the model is trained with GT context until convergence. Subsequently, an "unrolling mechanism" is activated, where predictions generated on the training set replace the GT context frames for further fine-tuning. This process is repeated for a set number of iterations ($T$), enabling the model to adapt to its own generation errors and reduce the training-inference gap.

2. **Adaptive Context Switching:**
 - **Problem:** Standard autoregressive updates after every chunk can lead to continuous error propagation, causing temporal drift in long sequences.
 - **Approach:** This mechanism dynamically decides when to update the context. After generating a chunk, the denoised latents are re-input into the DiT to extract attention maps. An average attention weight ($\mu_{attn}$) is calculated, representing how much current tokens attend to context tokens. If $\mu_{attn}$ falls below a threshold ($\tau=0.05$), indicating low relevance of the existing context, a context switch is triggered. The current generation is discarded, the context is updated to the immediate predecessor, and the chunk is regenerated, thereby mitigating drift.

3. **Event Voxel Density Augmentation:**
 - **Problem:** Event voxel density varies significantly due to diverse sensor resolutions and scene depths, potentially impacting model robustness and generalization.
 - **Approach:** During training, event voxels are randomly resized within a dynamic range (e.g., [S_min, S_max]), while preserving aspect ratio. Non-zero values are normalized to maintain sparsity. A random crop is then applied to match the network's input resolution. Crucially, identical geometric transformations are synchronously applied to the first frame, context frames, and current video frames to maintain spatial alignment.

4. **Reencoding Alignment (for Frame Interpolation):**
 - **Problem:** The 3D VAE's temporal compression means that temporal flipping in the latent space is not equivalent to flipping in the pixel space (i.e., `Flip_lat(Z_t) ≠ E(Flip_pix(D(Z_t)))`). This discrepancy causes misalignment when fusing bidirectionally generated latents for interpolation.
 - **Approach:** To ensure precise temporal alignment, the denoised latents ($\hat{Z}_0$) from both forward and backward predictions are first decoded into pixel space. These pixel-space videos are then temporally flipped (`Flip_pix`) and subsequently re-encoded back into latent space using the 3D VAE. This decode-flip-encode process yields temporally aligned latents ($\tilde{Z}_{fwd_0}$, $\tilde{Z}_{bwd_0}$).

5. **Cross Residual Correction (for Frame Interpolation):**
 - **Problem:** The additional decode-encode loop introduced by Reencoding Alignment can lead to a reconstruction loss and degradation of high-frequency details.
 - **Approach:** This method compensates for the information loss. It calculates the residual difference between the original denoised latents ($\hat{Z}_0$) and their re-encoded counterparts ($\bar{Z}_0 = E(D(\hat{Z}_0))$). These residuals are then cross-injected into the aligned latents from the opposing branch (e.g., the forward residual is added to the backward aligned latents). This symmetric cross-injection mechanism helps restore fine-grained details and promotes temporal consensus between the bidirectional branches.

#### 3.4. Finetuning with Event Voxels and Context

The training pipeline involves several steps:
- Spatial and temporal alignment of inputs is ensured. The first frame ($x_0$) is provided, and current event voxels are zero-padded at the initial timestamp.
- All inputs (current frames, context frames, first frame, and corresponding event voxels) are encoded into latents using a frozen 3D VAE. The 3-channel event voxel input is naturally processed by the VAE.
- Final input latents are constructed by concatenating three temporally-aligned sequences along the channel dimension: (1) context latents with the zero-padded first-frame latent, (2) context latents with the noisy latents ($Z_t$), and (3) context event latents with current event latents.
- The first projection layer within the patchify module of the DiT backbone is augmented to accommodate the additional event channels. This expanded layer is fully fine-tuned, while the rest of the DiT blocks are efficiently fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).
- The denoising loss is applied exclusively to the $Z_t$ component.
- A 5% dropout rate is applied to $Z_{x_0}$ to enhance reconstruction robustness.
- Text prompts are introduced with a 20% probability for text-guided generation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LongE2V was evaluated across reconstruction, prediction, and frame interpolation tasks on multiple real-world event datasets (ECD, MVSEC, HQF, BS-ERGB). The model was trained exclusively on the BS-ERGB training set.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Video Reconstruction (Table 1):** LongE2V consistently achieved the best LPIPS scores across all three datasets (ECD: 0.139, MVSEC: 0.405, HQF: 0.240), indicating superior perceptual quality compared to state-of-the-art regression-based methods like E2VID+, FireNet+, ET-Net, and HyperE2VID. While PSNR and SSIM were competitive, the LPIPS metric specifically highlights the generation of perceptually richer textures.
- **Video Prediction (Table 1):** LongE2V demonstrated superior performance over VDM-EVFI, which is a diffusion-based baseline. It obtained higher PSNR, SSIM, and lower LPIPS scores across all datasets (e.g., LPIPS: ECD 0.110, MVSEC 0.359, HQF 0.229), validating its robustness in long-term video generation.
- **Video Frame Interpolation (Zero-Shot) (Table 2):** Despite being trained for reconstruction and prediction, LongE2V exhibited zero-shot generalization capabilities for 31-frame interpolation. It achieved lower LPIPS scores (BS-ERGB: 0.124, HQF: 0.105) compared to supervised baselines like CBMNet-Large and TLXNet+, indicating better perceptual quality and generalization.
- **Subject Consistency (VBench, Table 6):** Evaluations on the VBench Subject Consistency metric for the HQF dataset showed that LongE2V significantly outperformed existing methods. For reconstruction, LongE2V achieved a score of 0.7204 compared to 0.5413 (E2VID+) and 0.4953 (HyperE2VID). For prediction, LongE2V scored 0.7187 against VDM-EVFI's 0.6279, demonstrating its ability to maintain identity and structural integrity over extended sequences.
- **Inference Speed (Table 7):** LongE2V's inference speed was measured at 3.5964 seconds per frame, which is slower than non-diffusion methods like E2VID (0.0024 s/frame) but significantly faster than VDM-EVFI (5.4483 s/frame), indicating improved efficiency among diffusion-based frameworks.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results (Figures 6, 7, 10, 11, 12)

- **Reconstruction:** LongE2V was observed to recover high-frequency textures effectively, avoiding the blurring artifacts prevalent in regression-based baselines such as E2VID+ and HyperE2VID.
- **Prediction:** The model demonstrated stable generation over long sequences, successfully avoiding noise accumulation and ghosting artifacts that were evident in VDM-EVFI's outputs. It maintained superior structural fidelity and temporal stability.
- **Frame Interpolation:** In zero-shot interpolation, LongE2V accurately captured complex dynamics and produced frames free from structural collapse or severe ghosting, particularly noticeable in scenarios with large motion or fine details (e.g., legible text), where baselines like CBMNet-Large and TLXNet+ struggled.
- **Text-Guided Colorization (Figure 10):** The model demonstrated the ability to decouple event-driven motion from text-defined appearance. When provided with event voxels and a text prompt, it successfully reconstructed scene geometry from events while applying coloration and stylization based on the textual description, with the XT-slice confirming temporal coherence of generated textures.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Tables 3, 4, 5 and Figures 8, 9)

- **Pretrained Prior:** The pretrained video diffusion prior was found to be critical for model convergence. Training the diffusion backbone from scratch on limited event data yielded pure noise.
- **Context Mechanisms:** The inclusion of context frames and event voxels, combined with Autoregressive Unrolling and Adaptive Context Switching, was crucial for mitigating long-term drift and error accumulation. Ablating these components individually or entirely resulted in significant degradation (e.g., point artifacts, grid artifacts, structural ambiguity), confirming their necessity for stable, high-fidelity generation.
- **Reencoding Alignment:** Directly flipping latents without re-encoding led to temporal misalignment and significantly blurred dynamic figures in interpolation, demonstrating the importance of the decode-flip-encode process.
- **Cross Residual Correction:** Removing this component resulted in ghosting and semi-transparent artifacts due to information loss from the 3D VAE's decode-encode cycle, highlighting its role in restoring high-frequency details and promoting temporal consensus.
- **Event Voxel Density Augmentation:** Omitting this augmentation strategy led to unstable generation, color deviations, and physical artifacts (e.g., black stripes on objects) when there was a density mismatch between training and inference (e.g., due to upsampling inputs during testing).
- **Backbone Generalizability (Table 5):** The efficacy of the proposed components was further demonstrated by their consistent performance gains when integrated with a different backbone (Wan 2.2 5B model), suggesting the general applicability of LongE2V's architectural enhancements.

#### 4.4. Limitations

The paper notes two main limitations: (a) LongE2V struggles to reconstruct high-quality frames when input event streams are highly sparse or of poor quality (Figure 13a). (b) The approach is sensitive to noise in the event condition, with "hot pixels" often being erroneously preserved or amplified in reconstructed frames (Figure 13b).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LongE2V represents a notable advancement in the field of event-based vision by providing a unified, robust, and perceptually high-quality solution for video reconstruction, prediction, and frame interpolation. The significance of this work can be understood through several key aspects:

1. **Addressing Core Challenges in Event-Based Video Generation:** The research directly tackles long-standing issues that have limited the practical application of event cameras. By mitigating blurry textures from regression-based methods, preventing long-term temporal drift in generative prediction, and resolving ghosting artifacts in interpolation, LongE2V makes event camera outputs more reliable and visually coherent for human interpretation and downstream machine vision tasks.

2. **Leveraging Foundational Models for Neuromorphic Data:** The effective fine-tuning of a pre-trained video diffusion model (CogVideoX I2V) for event-based data demonstrates a powerful paradigm. This approach capitalizes on the extensive prior knowledge embedded in large generative models, enabling high data efficiency and achieving superior perceptual quality that would be challenging to attain with models trained from scratch on limited event datasets. This strategy highlights the potential for broader adaptation of foundational AI models to specialized sensor modalities.

3. **Unified Architecture for Multiple Tasks:** The development of a single framework capable of handling three distinct, yet related, event-based video generation tasks offers significant practical advantages. It simplifies deployment, reduces computational overhead by eliminating the need for multiple specialized models, and demonstrates a more comprehensive understanding of event data processing within a consistent latent space. This versatility could accelerate research and application development in event-based vision.

4. **Enhanced Temporal Coherence and Zero-Shot Generalization:** The introduction of Autoregressive Unrolling and Adaptive Context Switching mechanisms is critical for achieving exceptional long-term temporal coherence, addressing a major weakness of previous autoregressive methods. Furthermore, the demonstrated zero-shot capability for frame interpolation highlights the model's robust generalization, suggesting it can adapt to new scenarios or tasks without requiring specific retraining, which is valuable in diverse real-world applications.

5. **Opening Avenues for Multimodal Applications:** The successful implementation of text-guided event video colorization showcases LongE2V's potential for multimodal conditioning. This ability to decouple motion (from events) from appearance (from text) offers creative control and opens doors for applications like content creation, scene stylization, or data augmentation by combining event streams with natural language prompts.

6. **Potential Impact on Real-World Applications:** Improved event-based video generation capabilities have implications for various fields. In robotics and autonomous driving, clearer and more stable videos from event cameras could enhance perception in challenging conditions (e.g., high speed, low light, high dynamic range). In surveillance, it could lead to more robust tracking and anomaly detection. In AR/VR, the ability to generate high-fidelity, temporally coherent frames from event data could improve user experience and immersion.

Future work aiming to accelerate inference and explore more efficient memory mechanisms will further enhance the practical utility of LongE2V, potentially broadening its applicability to real-time systems and even longer video sequences.
