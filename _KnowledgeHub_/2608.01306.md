# SPAE: Spectrally Guided Autoencoder for Pretrained Visual Latents

> [!summary] Summary
> SPAE adapts high-dimensional semantic latents from Vision Foundation Models for effective image generation while maintaining their utility for visual understanding. It introduces a spectrally guided autoencoder framework to reorganize the latent space, mitigating spectral mismatch and semantic-detail entanglement, leading to improved reconstruction and competitive generative performance.

## Problem

- High-dimensional semantic latents from Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) tend to preserve fine-grained details, which can be unstable and detrimental for robust image generation.
- Existing high-dimensional VFM latents exhibit a "training-inference spectral mismatch" with Diffusion Transformers (DiT), where DiT struggles to capture high-frequency components during inference.
- Semantic and detail information are coupled and broadly distributed across channels in VFM latents, leading to a disorganized space difficult for generative models to effectively model.

## Method

- A compact ==bottleneck compression== module (**SPAE**) maps high-dimensional VFM latents into a lower-dimensional space, reducing the high-frequency spectral mismatch for DiT modeling.
- Structured ==channel-wise masking== is applied during training in the bottleneck space to encourage the organization of low-frequency semantic information in early channels and high-frequency details in later channels.
- A ==three-stage training strategy== progressively adapts the modules, including decoder pretraining, bottleneck adaptation, and end-to-end finetuning with a ==semantic alignment loss== to maintain VFM latent fidelity.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art image reconstruction fidelity on ImageNet-1K (e.g., PSNR **30.28**, SSIM **0.896** with DINOv2 latents), outperforming various established latent autoencoder methods.
- Demonstrated competitive class-conditional image generation with a gFID of **1.12** (with guidance), outperforming RAE and other baselines, and producing visually coherent samples.
- Maintained or improved visual understanding performance on linear-probe tasks (CIFAR-100, Food101) and downstream multimodal understanding benchmarks (e.g., MME, MMB, POPE) when integrated with a Qwen3-8B language backbone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic "training-inference spectral mismatch" exists between VFM encoder latents and DiT-generated latents, particularly in high-frequency components, hindering robust generation.
> - Semantic and fine-grained detail information are entangled across all channels in high-dimensional VFM latents, indicating a lack of structured organization crucial for effective generative modeling.
> - Reorganizing the latent space through bottleneck compression and channel-wise masking can decouple semantic and detail information, creating a more stable and modelable space for diffusion models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of artificial intelligence for computer vision has historically developed distinct latent spaces for visual understanding and image generation. Visual understanding tasks commonly leverage semantically rich latents derived from Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), while generative models, particularly diffusion models, have often operated within latent spaces produced by Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). Recent research, exemplified by methods like Representation Autoencoders (RAE) and Semantic-VAE for Generation (SVG), has explored integrating VFM-derived semantic latents directly into generative models. This approach aims to capitalize on the rich semantic information provided by encoders such as DINOv2 and SigLIP2, which can potentially accelerate Diffusion Transformer (DiT) training and improve sample quality.

However, high-dimensional semantic latents from VFMs present specific challenges for direct generative modeling. When these latents are optimized for reconstruction, they tend to preserve fine-grained details, which can be unstable and detrimental for robust generation. Prior work has attempted to balance reconstruction fidelity and generative performance (e.g., FAE, RPiAE, PS-VAE, LV-RAE) by adapting pretrained features to lower-dimensional or augmented latent spaces.

A parallel line of research investigates the spectral organization and diffusability of latent spaces. Studies have indicated that poorly organized or excessive high-frequency components can impede diffusion model training and impact coarse-to-fine generation. Approaches like EQ-VAE focus on latent geometry, while UAE provides a spectral interpretation associating low-frequencies with semantic abstraction and high-frequencies with fine details. These works suggest that the spectral properties of a latent space are critical for its suitability for diffusion models.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two areas. It addresses the difficulties in directly modeling high-dimensional semantic latents for generation by analyzing their spectral properties and channel-wise organization. Specifically, the authors identify issues of spectral mismatch and semantic-detail entanglement within VFM latents. The proposed method, SPAE, contributes to the ongoing effort to adapt pretrained representations for generation by introducing mechanisms that reorganize the latent space to improve its modelability for diffusion processes, while aiming to preserve semantic information and reconstruction quality. This work seeks to advance the goal of creating visual encoders that can concurrently support both visual understanding and generation within unified multimodal frameworks.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a latent adaptation framework that enables high-dimensional semantic latents from Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) to be effectively used for image generation, while simultaneously preserving their utility for visual understanding and maintaining high reconstruction fidelity. The authors are motivated by the observation that while VFM latents are semantically rich and beneficial for visual understanding, their direct application in generative models, particularly Diffusion Transformers (DiT), presents specific challenges.

The motivation for SPAE stems from two identified obstacles to generative modeling with high-dimensional semantic latents:

1. **Training-Inference Spectral Mismatch:** The authors observed a systematic distribution shift between VFM encoder latents (used during autoencoder training) and DiT-generated latents (used during inference). This discrepancy is primarily concentrated in the high-frequency components of the latent space. For RAE (Representation Autoencoder) latents, a significant spectral gap emerges from the middle-frequency range onward, with DiT-generated latents exhibiting lower spectral power compared to encoder latents. In contrast, this mismatch is less pronounced in compact, low-dimensional VAE latent spaces. This indicates that DiT models struggle to faithfully capture high-frequency content in high-dimensional semantic latent spaces, leading to a lack of spectral consistency between training and inference data.

2. **Semantic-Detail Coupling:** A detailed channel-wise spectral analysis revealed that in high-dimensional VFM latents, high-frequency components are broadly dispersed across latent channels and are entangled with lower-frequency semantic information. The energy of both low and high-frequency components is distributed uniformly across channels, suggesting a lack of clear channel-wise hierarchy. This coupling means that stable semantic information and unstable fine-grained details are mixed, making the latent space structurally less organized and consequently more challenging for DiT to model effectively.

These observations motivate the central research question: what kind of latents can preserve semantic information and reconstruction fidelity while also providing a stable space for generation? To address this, the authors aim to develop a method that can distill stable semantic information, suppress problematic high-frequency components, and promote the decoupling of semantic and detailed information within the latent space, thereby improving its suitability for DiT modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Spectrally Guided Autoencoder (SPAE), is a latent adaptation framework designed to reorganize high-dimensional semantic latents for improved generative suitability while maintaining visual understanding and reconstruction fidelity. Its development is based on a preliminary analysis of the limitations of existing high-dimensional semantic latent spaces.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Analysis (Section 3.1)

The authors define the latent space as an input image `x` mapped by a representation encoder `E_rep` to a high-dimensional latent `Z_h`. A decoder reconstructs the image from `Z_h`, and a DiT is trained to model `Z_h` for generation. The analysis focuses on two aspects:

- **Training–Inference Spectral Mismatch (Section 3.1.1):** To quantify the discrepancy between encoder-extracted latents (used during autoencoder training) and DiT-generated latents (used during inference), the authors collected latents from ImageNet-1K. These latents were transformed into the frequency domain using a 2D Fourier transform, and their radial power spectra were computed. The comparison revealed that while VAE latents showed high spectral similarity between encoder and DiT-generated outputs, RAE latents exhibited a significant spectral mismatch, particularly from middle to high frequencies, where DiT-generated latents had lower spectral power. This suggests that DiT struggles with high-frequency components in high-dimensional spaces.
- **Semantic–Detail Coupling (Section 3.1.2):** To assess channel-wise organization, RAE encoder latents were divided into leading, intermediate, and trailing channel groups. Radial power spectra were computed for each group. The analysis showed that all channel groups had highly similar spectral trends and magnitudes across the full frequency range, indicating that both low-frequency (semantic) and high-frequency (detail) components are broadly distributed and entangled across channels, rather than being concentrated in specific subsets. This lack of a clear channel-wise hierarchy makes the latent space difficult to model.

#### 3.2. Generation-Oriented Latent Reorganization (Section 3.2)

Based on the preliminary analysis, SPAE introduces two main components:

- **Compact Bottleneck Compression (Section 3.2.1):** To mitigate the high-frequency mismatch and provide a more stable space for generation, a compact bottleneck is introduced. The architecture is asymmetric:
 - A lightweight one-layer compressor (`E_c`) maps the high-dimensional VFM latent (`Z_h`) into a compact bottleneck latent (`Z_b` where `C_b ≪ C_h`).
 - A deeper lifting module (`E_u`, composed of six transformer blocks) maps `Z_b` back to a high-dimensional lifted latent (`Z_u`) for the image decoder.
 - The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) operates directly on the compact `Z_b`, making the generation target more manageable.
- **Structured Channel-Wise Masking (Section 3.2.2):** To address the semantic-detail coupling, a channel-wise masking strategy is applied during training within the bottleneck space.
 - A random masking width `k` is sampled from a predefined set `S = {s_1, ..., s_M}`.
 - The masking preserves the first `C_b - k` channels and masks the last `k` consecutive channels of `Z_b`. This is implemented as an element-wise multiplication with a binary mask `m^(k)`.
 - This contiguous suffix masking encourages the model to organize the latent space such that low-frequency semantic information is concentrated in the earlier, unmasked channels, while high-frequency details are deferred to the later, masked channels. This is intended to create a more structured and modelable latent space for generation.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy (Section 3.3)

SPAE adopts a three-stage training strategy to ensure stability:

- **Stage I: Decoder Pretraining:** An image decoder (`D`, a ViT-XL architecture) is trained to reconstruct images directly from the frozen pretrained representation encoder (`E_rep`)'s high-dimensional latent `Z_h`. The objective includes reconstruction loss (`L_rec`), perceptual loss (`L_perc`), and adversarial loss (`L_adv`).
- **Stage II: Bottleneck Adaptation:** The bottleneck modules (`E_c` and `E_u`) are introduced and trained to reconstruct images from the bottleneck latent `Z_b`, while `E_rep` remains frozen. The objective is the same as in Stage I.
- **Stage III: End-to-End Finetuning:** All modules (`E_rep`, `E_c`, `E_u`, `D`) are jointly finetuned. The channel-wise masking strategy is applied in the bottleneck space. To prevent `E_rep` from drifting too far from its original pretrained state when unfrozen, a semantic alignment loss (`L_align`) is added. This loss measures the L2 distance between the output of the finetuned `E_rep` and a frozen copy of the pretrained `E_rep`. The overall objective combines all previous losses with `L_align`.

Training is performed on ImageNet-1K at 256x256 resolution. For generation, DiT_DH is used as the diffusion backbone. Pretrained DINOv2-Base and SigLIP2-Base are used as latent extractors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results across image reconstruction, class-conditional generation, and visual understanding tasks to evaluate the performance of SPAE.

#### 4.1. Image Reconstruction

SPAE demonstrates strong reconstruction performance.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 1):** On ImageNet-1K, SPAE (DINOv2) achieved PSNR of 30.28, SSIM of 0.896, LPIPS of 0.033, and rFID of 0.13, outperforming all compared methods (SD-VAE, VA-VAE, SVG, RPiAE, RAE, PS-VAE). SPAE (SigLIP2) also performed favorably, surpassing VA-VAE. Similar superior performance was observed on COCO 2017.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 3):** Visual comparisons showed that SPAE reconstructed structures and textures more faithfully than RAE, particularly in detailed regions.

#### 4.2. Class-Conditional Generation

SPAE exhibited competitive performance in class-conditional image generation.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 2):** In the unguided generation setting, SPAE achieved a gFID of 1.47, slightly better than RAE (1.51) and notably better than other latent diffusion methods. With guidance, SPAE obtained the best gFID of 1.12 and the highest recall of 0.70 among compared methods. While its Inception Score (IS) was competitive, it was not always the highest.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4):** Random class-conditional samples generated by SPAE on ImageNet-1K were observed to be visually coherent and diverse.

#### 4.3. Visual Understanding

SPAE demonstrated its ability to preserve semantic information for downstream understanding tasks.
- **Linear Probe Results (Table 3a):** Linear-probe accuracy on CIFAR-100, Food101, ImageNet-1K, and SUN397 remained comparable to that of the original pretrained DINOv2 and SigLIP2 encoders. This suggests that the latent adaptation largely retained the semantic information from the original VFMs.
- **Downstream Multimodal Understanding Tasks (Table 3b):** When integrated into a Qwen3-8B language backbone, SPAE consistently improved performance over the original SigLIP2 encoder across various benchmarks, including MME, MMB, POPE, TextVQA, and SQA. This indicates that the adapted latent space maintains strong semantic utility beyond generative tasks.

#### 4.4. Analysis and Ablation Studies

The paper included ablation studies to investigate the impact of the key components.

- **Effect of Bottleneck Compression (Section 4.5):**
 - **Spectral Analysis (Figure 5 vs. Figure 2b):** Compression into a 64-dimensional bottleneck substantially reduced the spectral mismatch between encoder and DiT-generated latents, especially in the high-frequency range. This indicates that compression suppresses latent components difficult for DiT to model.
 - **Quantitative Trade-off (Table 4a):** Varying bottleneck dimensions showed a trade-off: wider bottlenecks improved reconstruction (higher PSNR, lower rFID) but degraded generative quality (higher gFID, lower IS). A dimension of 64 was chosen as a favorable balance.
- **Effect of Channel-Wise Masking (Section 4.5):**
 - **Spectral Characteristics (Figure 6):** Analysis of 64-dimensional bottleneck latents revealed that channel-wise masking induced a consistent frequency separation. Front channels preserved more low-frequency energy, while back channels retained more high-frequency components. This structured organization became more pronounced with higher masking probabilities of trailing channels.
 - **Quantitative Impact (Table 4b):** Channel-wise masking had a marginal effect on reconstruction quality (slight PSNR drop, rFID stable) but significantly improved generation performance (gFID reduced from 8.30 to 5.00, IS increased from 97.5 to 151.2, compared to the unmasked setting). Token masking, an ablation baseline, yielded limited gains, suggesting the specific pattern of channel-wise masking is beneficial.
 - **Additional Spectral Analysis (Appendix Figure 7):** This reinforced that earlier channel groups exhibited smoother, more stable spectral behavior, easier for the generator to match, while later channel groups had stronger high-frequency components that were harder to model.

In summary, the main findings indicate that SPAE successfully addresses the identified challenges by creating a latent space that is more amenable to diffusion modeling, achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction and competitive generation quality, and preserves the semantic richness necessary for visual understanding tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "SPAE: Spectrally Guided Autoencoder for Pretrained Visual Latents" contributes to the field of multimodal AI by providing a framework that improves the utility of Vision Foundation Model (VFM) latents for generative tasks without compromising their capabilities for visual understanding or image reconstruction.

The significance of this work lies in its systematic analysis of the inherent difficulties in directly utilizing high-dimensional semantic latents for generative modeling. By identifying and empirically demonstrating the "training-inference spectral mismatch" and "semantic-detail coupling," the authors provide a principled understanding of why VFM latents, despite their semantic richness, pose challenges for Diffusion Transformers (DiT). This diagnostic approach provides a foundation for more effective latent space design.

The proposed SPAE framework, through its two core components—compact bottleneck compression and structured channel-wise masking—offers a practical solution to these identified problems. The bottleneck compression strategy effectively addresses the spectral mismatch by creating a more compact and stable latent space that is easier for DiT to model, particularly by suppressing difficult high-frequency components. The channel-wise masking strategy introduces a novel mechanism to induce an ordered organization within the latent space, decoupling semantic information from fine-grained details. This structured latent representation is shown to be particularly beneficial for generative performance.

The potential impact of SPAE is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced Unified Models:** By enabling a favorable balance among visual understanding, generation quality, and reconstruction fidelity within a single latent representation, SPAE moves closer to the goal of unified multimodal models. This could streamline research and development by allowing a common backbone to serve diverse vision tasks, reducing the need for separate or extensively adapted latent spaces.
- **Improved Generative Models:** The ability to leverage semantically richer VFM latents more effectively could lead to more robust and higher-quality image generation, with faster convergence and improved semantic control, as suggested by the performance metrics.
- **Deeper Understanding of Latent Spaces:** The spectral and channel-wise analyses conducted in this paper offer insights into the properties that make a latent space "generatively suitable." This understanding could guide future research in designing or adapting latent representations for various generative and discriminative tasks.
- **Foundational for Multimodal Research:** The demonstrated preservation of semantic utility for downstream multimodal understanding tasks (e.g., in Qwen3-8B) implies that SPAE-adapted latents can serve as effective visual inputs for large language models, potentially fostering advancements in vision-language models and other cross-modal applications.

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the fixed training resolution which might affect fine-grained detail modeling (e.g., in TextVQA) and the fixed channel-wise masking schedule, suggesting avenues for future research into adaptive strategies. Nevertheless, SPAE represents a step toward building more versatile and performant visual encoders that can support the demands of both visual understanding and generation in complex AI systems.
