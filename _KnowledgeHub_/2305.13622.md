# Continual Learning with Strong Experience Replay

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method called Strong Experience Replay (SER) improves continual learning by incorporating dual consistency losses, one for past experiences and a novel one leveraging current training data as "future experiences" for the old model. This approach effectively reduces catastrophic forgetting, demonstrating, for instance, a 20.5 percentage point accuracy improvement over DER++ on CIFAR100 Class-IL with a limited memory buffer.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks experience catastrophic forgetting when learning new tasks sequentially, struggling to balance the acquisition of new knowledge with the retention of old information.
- Conventional Experience Replay (ER) methods often overfit to the limited samples stored in their memory buffers, leading to continued forgetting, especially when memory capacity is low or the number of tasks is high.
- Existing knowledge distillation techniques within ER extensions are constrained by the small size of the memory buffer, offering insufficient signal to fully stabilize the model's performance across all prior tasks.

## Method

- **Strong Experience Replay (SER)** enhances the traditional ==Experience Replay== framework by integrating two distinct ==consistency losses== into the overall training objective.
- A ==backward consistency loss== distills knowledge from the previous model by enforcing consistency between current and old model predictions on samples replayed from the memory buffer.
- A novel ==forward consistency loss== leverages the complete current training data to enforce consistency with the previous model's predictions, using this data as "future experiences" to mitigate overfitting to the limited memory buffer.

## Results

- SER consistently achieved superior average accuracy and lower average forgetting rates across CIFAR10, CIFAR100, TinyImageNet, and MNIST datasets in Class-IL, Task-IL, and Domain-IL settings.
- On CIFAR100 Class-IL (5 tasks, 200-sample memory buffer), SER reached **47.96%** average accuracy, an improvement of **20.5 percentage points** over the DER++ baseline (**27.46%**).
- In challenging TinyImageNet Class-IL with a 200-sample memory, SER demonstrated a **17.54 percentage point** accuracy gain over DER++, achieving **28.50%** average accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Overfitting to a small memory buffer is a critical bottleneck for rehearsal-based continual learning methods, significantly hindering knowledge preservation.
> - Leveraging current training data as "future experiences" for the old model provides a rich, generalizable signal for consistency regularization, extending beyond the limited scope of the memory buffer.
> - Combining both backward (memory-based) and forward (current data-based) consistency losses creates a more comprehensive and robust mechanism for balancing stability and plasticity during sequential task learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep neural networks, conventionally trained offline with complete datasets, face a challenge in dynamic environments where data arrives sequentially in nonstationary tasks. This challenge is known as Continual Learning (CL), which aims to enable models to incrementally learn new tasks without experiencing catastrophic forgetting—a phenomenon where new learning drastically degrades performance on previously acquired knowledge. The core difficulty in CL lies in balancing the model's plasticity (ability to learn new information) with its stability (ability to retain old information).

Current approaches to CL generally fall into several categories:
1. **Rehearsal-based methods**: These store a subset of previous data in a memory buffer and replay these samples during new task training to mitigate forgetting. Experience Replay (ER) is a foundational and widely used strategy within this category, employing techniques like reservoir sampling to manage the memory buffer. Various extensions of ER, such as iCaRL, GEM, A-GEM, DER++, and CLS-ER, have been developed to enhance its effectiveness by improving sample selection, incorporating meta-learning, or utilizing knowledge distillation.
2. **Regularization-based methods**: These introduce penalty terms into the loss function to prevent significant changes to critical parameters or prediction spaces associated with old tasks. Examples include Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) and Synaptic Intelligence (SI), or methods using knowledge distillation like Learning without Forgetting (LwF). These methods often struggle in class-incremental scenarios without access to previous data.
3. **Other CL methods**: This category includes approaches that expand network architectures (e.g., Progressive Neural Networks, DER), use prompt learning (e.g., L2P, Dualprompt), or employ generative models to synthesize old data for replay (e.g., Deep Generative Replay).

Despite advancements, rehearsal-based methods, particularly ER and its extensions, continue to demonstrate strong performance due to their direct approach to revisiting past experiences. However, a limitation identified in recent research is the risk of overfitting to the memory buffer when only a limited number of previous samples are available. This overfitting can lead to continued forgetting, especially in scenarios with many tasks or limited memory capacity. This work positions itself within the rehearsal-based paradigm, specifically as an extension to Experience Replay, aiming to enhance its robustness against catastrophic forgetting by addressing the overfitting issue, particularly when memory resources are constrained.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Continual Learning is to enable deep learning models to sequentially acquire knowledge from a stream of tasks without suffering from catastrophic forgetting of previously learned information. This necessitates maintaining an optimal balance between the model's capacity to learn new tasks (plasticity) and its ability to retain knowledge from old tasks (stability).

The motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing Experience Replay (ER) methods, which, despite their simplicity and effectiveness, face challenges in preserving knowledge under specific conditions:
1. **Overfitting to limited memory buffer**: When the memory buffer, which stores a subset of previously seen data, is small, existing ER extensions can overfit to these limited samples. This diminishes their ability to generalize to the full distribution of previous tasks, thereby leading to continued forgetting. This problem is particularly pronounced in class-incremental scenarios where the number of classes per task is high relative to the memory capacity.
2. **Insufficient knowledge distillation**: While some ER extensions incorporate knowledge distillation to reduce drastic model changes by seeking consistency on memory buffer data, this approach is constrained by the small size of the buffer. The limited "past experiences" available for distillation may not be sufficient to fully stabilize the model's performance across all previous tasks.

To address these issues, the authors propose Strong Experience Replay (SER), motivated by the hypothesis that enhancing the consistency of model predictions—not only on past experiences but also on current learning data—can significantly improve generalization and reduce forgetting. The specific motivations are:
- To develop a method that effectively reduces catastrophic forgetting, particularly when only a few previous samples are available for replay.
- To move beyond solely relying on past experiences stored in a limited memory buffer for consistency regularization.
- To explore how "future experiences" (data from the current task, as seen by the old model) can contribute to preserving previously learned knowledge.
- To improve the model's generalization capabilities on previous tasks by leveraging the full current training data for consistency enforcement.
- To provide a simple, yet robust and computationally efficient, extension to the existing ER framework.

The authors postulate that introducing two distinct consistency losses—a **backward consistency loss** focusing on past experiences in the memory buffer and a novel **forward consistency loss** leveraging current training data as "future experiences" for the old model—will provide a more comprehensive mechanism for knowledge preservation. This dual consistency approach is intended to stabilize model predictions more broadly, thereby mitigating the overfitting problem and enhancing the overall stability-plasticity balance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Strong Experience Replay (SER) method extends the conventional Experience Replay (ER) framework by incorporating two consistency losses: a backward consistency loss and a forward consistency loss. This aims to improve model generalization and reduce catastrophic forgetting by distilling knowledge from both past experiences (memory buffer) and future experiences (current training data).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

In a continual learning setting, a model is sequentially trained on a series of datasets ${D_1..., D_T}$. At task $t$, the goal is to update the model parameters $\theta_t$ using data $D_t$ while preserving knowledge from previous tasks $D_1..., D_{t-1}$. Standard finetuning by minimizing the cross-entropy loss $L_t^{cls} = E_{(x,y) \sim D_t}[\ell_{ce}(f(x; \theta_t), y)]$ leads to catastrophic forgetting.

#### 3.2. Experience Replay (ER)

ER alleviates forgetting by maintaining a memory buffer $M$ containing a subset of samples from previous tasks. The model is trained jointly on current data $D_t$ and samples from $M$. The training loss for ER is $L = L_t^{cls} + L_m^{cls}$, where $L_m^{cls} = E_{(x,y) \sim M}[\ell_{ce}(f(x; \theta_t), y)]$ is the classification loss on memory samples. A reservoir sampling method is used to update $M$, ensuring each past sample has an equal probability of being stored. While effective, ER's performance can degrade significantly with a limited memory buffer due to overfitting.

#### 3.3. Strong Experience Replay (SER)

SER enhances ER by introducing two consistency losses that regulate the model's prediction outputs, guiding the new model $f(x; \theta_t)$ to approximate the outputs of the old model $f(x; \theta_{t-1})$.

- **Backward Consistency Loss ($L_m^{bc}$)**:
 - **Purpose**: To distill past experiences and prevent significant changes in the model's predictions for data from previous tasks.
 - **Mechanism**: It measures the consistency between the current model's output logits ($f(x; \theta_t)$) and the previous model's output logits ($f(x; \theta_{t-1})$) on samples drawn from the memory buffer $M$. An Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is used:
 $L_m^{bc} = E_{x \sim M}[\|f(x; \theta_t) - f(x; \theta_{t-1})\|^2]$.
 - **Implementation Note**: To reduce computational cost, the output logits $f(x; \theta_{t-1})$ for memory samples are pre-computed and stored in the memory buffer alongside the samples, making $L_m^{bc}$ computed as $E_{x \sim M}[\|f(x; \theta_t) - z\|^2]$, where $z$ are the stored logits. This approach is similar to DER++.

- **Forward Consistency Loss ($L_t^{fc}$)**:
 - **Purpose**: To mitigate overfitting to the limited memory buffer and improve model generalization on previous tasks by leveraging the complete current training data.
 - **Motivation**: Data from the current task $D_t$ is "future" data from the perspective of the old model $f(x; \theta_{t-1})$. By enforcing consistency on this data, the method encourages the new model to retain a broader overlap with the old model's representations, even for data it hasn't directly seen before in previous tasks.
 - **Mechanism**: It measures the consistency between the current model's output logits ($f(x; \theta_t)$) and the previous model's output logits ($f(x; \theta_{t-1})$) on samples drawn from the current training dataset $D_t$. An MSE loss is employed:
 $L_t^{fc} = E_{x \sim D_t}[\|f(x; \theta_t) - f(x; \theta_{t-1})\|^2]$.
 - **Benefits**: Unlike backward consistency, $L_t^{fc}$ utilizes all current training data, potentially offering a richer signal for consistency. It is designed to propagate consistent predictions to new tasks over time, enhancing the overall stability of the model without solely relying on limited past samples.

#### 3.4. Total Training Loss

The complete loss function for SER combines the classification losses for current and memory data with both consistency losses:
$L = L_t^{cls} + L_m^{cls} + \alpha L_m^{bc} + \beta L_t^{fc}$
where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are balancing parameters to weigh the contribution of each consistency loss.

#### 3.5. Algorithm Implementation

The SER algorithm involves:
1. Initializing model parameters $\theta_{t-1}$ from the previous task.
2. For each batch in the current training data $D_t$:
 - Sample a batch from $D_t$ and from the memory buffer $M$.
 - Apply data augmentation to both batches.
 - Compute $L_t^{cls}$ on the current data.
 - If $t > 1$:
 - Compute $L_m^{cls}$ on memory data.
 - Compute $L_m^{bc}$ on memory data (using stored logits).
 - Compute $L_t^{fc}$ on current data (by passing current data through $\theta_{t-1}$).
 - Combine all losses with $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
 - Optimize the model parameters $\theta_t$ using SGD.
 - Update the memory buffer $M$ using reservoir sampling, storing input samples along with their current model logits $f(x; \theta_t)$.
3. After training on $D_t$, update $\theta_{t-1} \leftarrow \theta_t$.
The method explicitly avoids using consistency losses for the first task, as there is no prior knowledge to preserve.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **CL Settings**: Evaluated across Class-Incremental Learning (Class-IL), Task-Incremental Learning (Task-IL), and Domain-Incremental Learning (Domain-IL). Task identity is not used for inference.
- **Datasets**: CIFAR10 (10 classes), CIFAR100 (100 classes), TinyImageNet (200 classes) for Class-IL/Task-IL; Permuted MNIST (P-MNIST) and Rotated MNIST (R-MNIST) for Domain-IL.
- **Baselines**: Compared against rehearsal-free methods (oEWC, SI, LwF) and various rehearsal-based methods (ER, GEM, A-GEM, iCaRL, DER++, CLS-ER, LVT, TAMiL), along with SGD (lower bound) and Joint training (upper bound).
- **Metrics**: Final average accuracy (average accuracy across all tasks after final task training) and average forgetting (average of the maximum accuracy drop for each task).
- **Architecture**: Modified ResNet18 for CIFAR/TinyImageNet, fully connected network for MNIST variants, following DER++.
- **Training Details**: SGD optimizer, specific epochs (e.g., CIFAR10: 20, CIFAR100: 50, TinyImageNet: 100), learning rate schedules, and dataset-specific balancing parameters $\alpha, \beta$ (e.g., CIFAR100: $\alpha=0.5, \beta=0.5$, TinyImageNet: $\alpha=0.2, \beta=1$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across multiple image classification datasets and continual learning settings demonstrate that Strong Experience Replay (SER) effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting and improves performance compared to previous state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on CIFAR100 (Class-IL and Task-IL)

- **Superiority over Baselines**: SER consistently outperformed both rehearsal-free methods (which largely failed to reduce forgetting) and many rehearsal-based methods.
- **Improvements over DER++**: SER showed substantial improvements over DER++, a closely related method that uses backward consistency. For a memory buffer of 200 samples in Class-IL:
 - On 5 tasks, SER achieved 47.96% accuracy, surpassing DER++ (27.46%) by 20.5 percentage points.
 - On 20 tasks, SER achieved 24.35% accuracy, outperforming DER++ (15.16%) by 9.19 percentage points.
 - In Task-IL with 10 tasks, SER achieved 79.85% accuracy, an improvement of 16.31 percentage points over DER++ (63.54%).
- **Improvements over LVT**: SER also surpassed the more recent LVT method, demonstrating an 8.3% accuracy improvement on 5 tasks Class-IL and 3.0% on 20 tasks Class-IL (buffer 200).
- **Consistent with Larger Buffer**: With a larger memory buffer of 500 samples, SER continued to show consistent accuracy improvements, reinforcing its effectiveness.
- **Accuracy Trends**: Plots of average accuracy across learning stages on CIFAR100 (10 and 20 tasks) indicated that SER maintained higher accuracy throughout the incremental learning process in both Class-IL and Task-IL scenarios.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on CIFAR10 (Class-IL and Task-IL)

- **Best Performance**: SER achieved the highest average accuracy among all compared algorithms on CIFAR10 (5 tasks).
- **Improvements over DER++**: SER outperformed DER++ by 5% in Class-IL (buffer 200) and 3.7% (buffer 500).
- **Improvements over CLS-ER and TAMiL**: SER improved accuracy by 3.7% over CLS-ER (buffer 200) and slightly outperformed TAMiL (by 1%), even without using task identities during training.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on TinyImageNet (Class-IL and Task-IL)

- **Challenge with Limited Samples**: TinyImageNet proved particularly challenging due to its larger number of classes and the limited memory buffer (e.g., 200 samples meant only 1 sample per class on average). Many baseline methods failed to show significant improvement over the lower bound (SGD).
- **Significant Gains**: SER demonstrated substantial performance gains in this challenging scenario:
 - In Class-IL (buffer 200), SER achieved 28.50% accuracy, a significant improvement of 17.54 percentage points over DER++ (10.96%).
 - In Task-IL (buffer 200), SER achieved 70.12% accuracy, an absolute improvement of 29.25 percentage points over DER++ (40.87%).
 - SER also surpassed Co2L, CLS-ER, and TAMiL by noticeable margins (e.g., 14.6% over Co2L in Class-IL, 5.03% over CLS-ER in Class-IL).
- These results suggest that SER is effective in preserving knowledge even when very few samples are available for experience replay.

#### 4.4. Evaluation on P-MNIST and R-MNIST (Domain-IL)

- In Domain-IL settings, SER, DER++, and CLS-ER were effective.
- The forward consistency was observed to be more impactful in Class-IL and Task-IL compared to Domain-IL. This is attributed to the fact that Class-IL and Task-IL involve disjoint category spaces, where the forward consistency's ability to propagate consistent predictions for new classes is more critical, whereas in Domain-IL, categories remain the same.

#### 4.5. Average Forgetting

- SER achieved the lowest average forgetting rates in all comparison experiments on CIFAR10 (5 tasks) and CIFAR100 (20 tasks) for both Class-IL and Task-IL settings. This quantitatively supports the claim that SER effectively reduces the forgetting issue.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study

- The ablation study on CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 confirmed the contribution of each loss component.
- Adding $L_t^{cls}$ (SGD baseline) resulted in significant forgetting.
- Adding $L_m^{cls}$ (ER baseline) substantially reduced forgetting.
- Adding $L_m^{bc}$ (DER++ baseline) further improved performance.
- Critically, adding only the forward consistency loss ($L_t^{fc}$) to ER ($L_t^{cls} + L_m^{cls} + L_t^{fc}$) already surpassed DER++, highlighting the independent effectiveness of $L_t^{fc}$. For example, on CIFAR10 Class-IL, this combination achieved 69.04% accuracy, exceeding DER++'s 64.88%.
- The full SER method ($L_t^{cls} + L_m^{cls} + L_m^{bc} + L_t^{fc}$) achieved the best overall performance, indicating the complementary roles of both consistency losses.

#### 4.7. Stability-Plasticity Dilemma

- Analysis of task-wise accuracy on CIFAR10 showed that SER maintained a better balance between stability (retaining old task performance) and plasticity (learning new tasks) compared to ER, DER++, and CLS-ER. SER preserved more knowledge from previous tasks while also achieving strong performance on the most recent tasks.

#### 4.8. Computational Cost Analysis

- The computational cost of SER was found to be comparable to DER++ and more efficient than CLS-ER. SER reuses data batches for multiple loss computations and does not introduce additional models beyond the current and a frozen old model, minimizing overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Strong Experience Replay (SER), a method designed to enhance continual learning by effectively mitigating catastrophic forgetting, particularly in scenarios where memory resources are limited. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Addressing a Critical Limitation in Rehearsal-Based CL**: SER directly addresses the problem of overfitting to small memory buffers, a known weakness of many rehearsal-based continual learning methods. By introducing a novel forward consistency loss, the method leverages the complete current training data to stabilize knowledge, rather than relying solely on a limited set of replayed samples. This represents a methodological advancement in how consistency is enforced in CL.

2. **Improved Generalization and Knowledge Preservation**: The empirical results demonstrate that SER consistently achieves higher average accuracy and lower average forgetting across a range of benchmark datasets (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, TinyImageNet, P-MNIST, R-MNIST) and continual learning settings (Class-IL, Task-IL, Domain-IL). The substantial performance gains, especially in challenging class-incremental scenarios with few samples per class (e.g., on TinyImageNet), indicate a more robust preservation of learned knowledge and improved model generalization on previous tasks.

3. **Enhanced Stability-Plasticity Balance**: The dual consistency mechanism, combining backward (past experiences) and forward (future experiences on current data) consistency, provides a more effective way to balance the stability-plasticity dilemma inherent in continual learning. It enables the model to learn new tasks without drastically altering the prediction spaces for old ones, as evidenced by the reduced forgetting and maintained performance across tasks.

4. **Practicality and Efficiency**: The proposed SER method is characterized by its simplicity of implementation and computational efficiency. It does not introduce complex architectural changes or significantly increase training overhead compared to baselines like DER++. This ease of integration makes SER a practical candidate for deployment in real-world applications where continual learning capabilities are required.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The concept of leveraging "future experiences" (current training data processed by the old model) for consistency regularization offers a new perspective for knowledge distillation in CL. This approach could inspire further research into alternative forms of consistency constraints or novel ways to utilize unlabeled or newly acquired data to protect existing knowledge.

In summary, SER offers a robust, efficient, and effective solution to a fundamental challenge in continual learning. Its demonstrated ability to significantly reduce catastrophic forgetting, particularly under memory constraints, positions it as a valuable contribution to the field, with potential for broader impact on the development of more adaptive and intelligent deep learning systems capable of lifelong learning.
