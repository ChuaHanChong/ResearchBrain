# OPD-V: Visual On-Policy Self-Distillation with Modality Balance

> [!summary] Summary
> OPD-V is a visual On-Policy Self-Distillation method that enhances Multimodal Large Language Models by addressing Modality Imbalance, achieving a 15.71 percentage point increase in average accuracy for the Qwen3.5-4B backbone while reducing training step latency by 31.8%. This approach enabled a compact 4B-parameter model to outperform several larger state-of-the-art MLLMs.

## Problem

- Existing On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) methods for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently overlook "Modality Imbalance."
- MLLMs often disproportionately rely on textual context, even in visually critical tasks, leading to underutilization of visual information and limiting OPSD effectiveness.
- Current OPSD techniques do not explicitly account for how effectively an MLLM integrates visual relative to textual inputs.

## Method

- **OPD-V** introduces a ==dual-teacher OPSD== framework using a "Positive Teacher" with a Zoom-In Image (enhanced visual context) and a "Negative Teacher" with a Mask Image (degraded visual context).
- It defines a ==Modality-Balance Logits Margin== and a ==Modality-Balance Trust Region== based on the differential support from these two teachers, selecting tokens that indicate a healthier balance of visual and textual attention.
- Distillation is applied from the Positive Teacher to the student only within this trust region, scaled by the logits margin, to guide the student towards more balanced multimodal reasoning.

## Results

- OPD-V consistently improved reasoning performance across MLLM backbones, boosting Qwen3.5-4B's average accuracy by **15.71 percentage points** to **80.01%**, surpassing significantly larger state-of-the-art MLLMs.
- The method significantly reduced computational costs, cutting mean step latency by **31.8%** for Qwen3.5-4B and **24.7%** for Qwen3.5-9B, partly due to generating **74.5%** shorter student responses.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Positive and Negative Teachers are crucial and complementary, with the Zoom-In and Mask image operations proving most effective for defining the Modality-Balance Trust Region.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modality Imbalance, where MLLMs disproportionately rely on text, is a prevalent issue that varies with visual input and hinders effective multimodal reasoning.
> - The degree of Modality Balance can be operationalized as valuable "privileged information" for OPSD, providing a direct signal for improving visual-textual integration.
> - A "Modality-Balance Logits Margin"—the difference in log probabilities from teachers with enhanced vs. degraded visual inputs—correlates positively with student correctness and acts as an effective directional supervisory signal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are foundational for a wide array of visual understanding and reasoning tasks. A post-training technique that has gained prominence for enhancing visual reasoning in MLLMs is On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD). OPSD operates by having a student model sample its own response trajectories, then using a detached copy of the same model (the "teacher") to provide dense supervision targets at the prefixes visited by the student. This approach removes the necessity of a separately trained external teacher by leveraging "privileged information" available only during the training phase.

Existing OPSD methods for MLLMs typically derive this privileged information from various sources. Some approaches use verified textual solutions or reference answers to guide distillation. More recent visual OPSD techniques leverage additional or transformed visual inputs, such as evidence-centered crops (Vision-OPD), privileged visual thoughts (Visual-OPSD), or by creating teacher contrasts through differential access to visual content (VA-OPD, VCSD).

However, the current work identifies a limitation in these existing visual OPSD methods: they frequently overlook "Modality Imbalance." Modality Imbalance describes a prevalent challenge in MLLM reasoning where models, despite being designed for multimodal input, tend to disproportionately rely on textual context, often due to strong textual priors, even when the task critically depends on visual information. This tendency can lead to the underutilization of carefully designed privileged visual information, thereby limiting the overall effectiveness of OPSD.

This research positions itself within this landscape by addressing the Modality Imbalance challenge directly. It proposes that Modality Balance itself—the degree to which a model integrates visual information relative to textual information—can serve as a valuable form of privileged information. By explicitly incorporating Modality Balance into the OPSD framework, the work aims to create a more effective self-distillation mechanism that encourages MLLMs to better integrate their visual and textual inputs for reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the effectiveness of On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by addressing the inherent challenge of Modality Imbalance. The authors are motivated by the observation that existing OPSD methods, while leveraging privileged information from various sources (e.g., textual solutions, transformed visual inputs), do not explicitly account for the MLLM's tendency to rely disproportionately on textual context over visual input. This disproportionate reliance, termed "Modality Imbalance," can diminish the utility of otherwise well-designed privileged information, as the model may not fully integrate the multimodal cues.

The core research question guiding this work is: "How can privileged information be designed to remain effective for MLLM OPSD under Modality Imbalance?"

To address this, the authors develop a specific motivation derived from empirical observations:
1. **Existence of Modality Imbalance:** They demonstrate that MLLMs exhibit varying degrees of Modality Imbalance depending on the visual input. Specifically, by constructing a "Positive Teacher" with a "Zoom-In Image" (magnifying the task-relevant region) and a "Negative Teacher" with a "Mask Image" (masking parts of the Zoom-In Image), they observe distinct Modality-Balance Attention Ratios. The Negative Teacher, with reduced visual information, shows the lowest visual-to-textual attention ratio, the student with the Original Image shows an intermediate ratio, and the Positive Teacher, with enhanced visual detail, shows the highest ratio. This confirms that different visual conditions expose varying levels of Modality Imbalance.
2. **Modality Balance as Privileged Information:** Further analysis reveals that a "Modality-Balance Logits Margin"—which quantifies the difference in how the Positive and Negative Teachers score a student's response token—correlates positively with student correctness and widens the gap between the teachers' Modality-Balance Attention Ratios. This indicates that the distinction provided by teachers with different levels of Modality Balance can provide a directional supervisory signal.
3. **Feasibility of Instantiation:** The authors argue that while Modality Balance is an internal model condition, it can be externally expressed and converted into token-level supervision for OPSD through carefully designed teacher inputs.

Motivated by these findings, the primary objective is to introduce OPD-V, a novel visual OPSD paradigm. OPD-V aims to operationalize Modality Balance as privileged information by:
- Instantiating it through a Positive Teacher (using a Zoom-In Image) and a Negative Teacher (using a Mask Image).
- Defining a "Modality-Balance Trust Region" based on positive Modality-Balance Logits Margins, which selects the on-policy tokens deemed to reflect a healthier balance of visual and textual attention.
- Applying Jensen–Shannon distillation from the Positive Teacher to the student *only within* this trust region, thereby encouraging the student to align its reasoning with a more modality-balanced perspective.

Ultimately, the objective is to demonstrate that OPD-V can consistently improve reasoning performance in MLLMs while simultaneously reducing training costs, thereby offering a more effective and efficient approach to post-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on developing OPD-V, a visual OPSD framework that leverages Modality Balance as privileged information.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation and OPSD Context

The work considers supervised post-training for multimodal reasoning, where the goal is to improve a trainable MLLM, denoted $p_\theta$. For each training example, the model receives an Original Image $I$ and a textual query $x$. During training, a verified target $a^*$ is available. The MLLM generates an on-policy trajectory $y = (y_1, \ldots, y_T)$ by sampling from $p_\theta(\cdot | I, x)$. Standard OPSD uses a detached copy of the MLLM, $p_{\bar{\theta}}$, as a teacher, which scores the student-generated prefixes $y_{<t}$ conditioned on $I$, $x$, and the verified target $a^*$. The distillation loss $L_{OPSD}$ minimizes the divergence between the privileged teacher distribution $q_{priv,t}$ and the student distribution $p_{\theta,t}$ along the on-policy trajectory.

#### 3.2. Measuring Modality Imbalance

To quantify Modality Imbalance, the authors define the Modality-Balance Attention Ratio, $\rho(c; y)$. This ratio is calculated as the sum of visual attention masses over the trajectory divided by the sum of textual attention masses, plus a small constant for numerical stability: $\rho(c; y) = \frac{\sum_{t \in T_y} \alpha_{V,t}(c)}{\sum_{t \in T_y} \alpha_{T,t}(c) + \epsilon}$. A higher $\rho$ indicates stronger visual attention relative to textual attention, signifying better Modality Balance.

#### 3.3. OPD-V Framework

OPD-V introduces a dual-teacher mechanism and a novel token selection strategy:
- **Dual Teacher Design:** Instead of a single teacher with a reference target, OPD-V uses two teacher configurations that manipulate visual input:
 - **Positive Teacher:** Receives a "Zoom-In Image" ($I_{zoom}$). This image is obtained by cropping and magnifying the task-relevant region from the Original Image, making the visual evidence more prominent. The Positive Teacher distribution is denoted $q^+_t(\cdot) = p_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot | I_{zoom}, x, y_{<t})$.
 - **Negative Teacher:** Receives a "Mask Image" ($I_{mask}$). This image is created by taking the $I_{zoom}$ and replacing a randomly selected rectangular region within it with black pixels, thus visually weakening a key part of the input while preserving most context. The Negative Teacher distribution is denoted $q^-_t(\cdot) = p_{\bar{\theta}}(\cdot | I_{mask}, x, y_{<t})$.
 Both teachers score the same student-generated tokens under the matched textual query and on-policy prefix. The teacher parameters $\bar{\theta}$ are maintained using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student weights.

- **Modality-Balance Logits Margin ($\delta_{MB}$):** For each on-policy token $y_t$, the margin is calculated as the difference between the log probabilities assigned by the Positive and Negative Teachers: $\delta_{MB,t} = \ell^+_t(y_t) - \ell^-_t(y_t)$, where $\ell^+_t(v) = \log q^+_t(v)$ and $\ell^-_t(v) = \log q^-_t(v)$. A positive margin implies that the token receives more support from the Positive Teacher (with enhanced visual information) than from the Negative Teacher (with degraded visual information).

- **Modality-Balance Trust Region ($R_{MB}$):** This region comprises all token positions $t$ for which $\delta_{MB,t} > 0$. These are the tokens where the Positive Teacher's support is stronger, suggesting a more visually grounded prediction.

- **OPD-V Objective:** The distillation loss $L_{OPD-V}$ is applied only within the Modality-Balance Trust Region. It uses Jensen–Shannon divergence ($D_{JS}$) between the Positive Teacher distribution ($q^+_t$) and the student distribution ($p_{\theta,t}$). Importantly, the loss for each selected token is scaled by its corresponding positive Modality-Balance Logits Margin:
 $L_{OPD-V}(\theta) = E\left[\frac{1}{\sum_{t \in T_y} r_t} \cdot \sum_{t \in R_{MB}(y)} r_t \delta_{MB,t} D_{JS}(q^+_t, p_{\theta,t})\right]$. Here, $r_t$ is an indicator for generated response tokens.

#### 3.4. Computational Efficiency

Compared to standard OPSD, OPD-V introduces an additional forward pass for the Negative Teacher. However, the authors argue that this overhead is offset by shorter student responses and more efficient construction of teacher inputs (image transformations instead of complex reference target insertion), which reduce overall processing time.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Top-K Distillation:** To mitigate memory overhead, a top-K distillation strategy (K=100) is used. Only the top K logits from the student model and corresponding teacher probabilities are evaluated, with a tail-probability adjustment for the remaining vocabulary mass.
- **Training:** Models are trained for 1 epoch using a synthetic dataset of 6.2K visual reasoning samples. Jensen-Shannon Divergence with $\beta = 0.5$ is used as the divergence objective.
- **Backbones:** Experiments are conducted on Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Six benchmarks are used: V* Bench, ZoomBench, HR-Bench (4K, 8K), and MME-RealWorld (English, Chinese subsets). These cover various aspects of visual reasoning, including localization, fine-grained VQA, high-resolution processing, and real-world scenarios.
- **Baselines:** Two groups of baselines are used:
 - State-of-the-art MLLMs (closed-source like Gemini, GPT, and open-source models of various scales).
 - Alternative optimization strategies (SFT, GRPO, OPSD, Vision-OPD, VA-OPD) applied to the same Qwen3.5-4B backbone for a controlled comparison. All models are evaluated in non-thinking mode.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across diverse settings yielded several findings regarding the effectiveness, efficiency, and mechanistic validity of OPD-V.

#### 4.1. Overall Effectiveness (RQ1)

- **Consistent Performance Improvements:** OPD-V consistently improved reasoning performance across all evaluated MLLM backbones and benchmarks. For the Qwen3.5-4B backbone, OPD-V raised the average accuracy from 64.30% to 80.01%, an absolute gain of 15.71 percentage points. This performance surpassed all other matched post-training baselines (e.g., Vision-OPD achieved 77.10%, leaving a 2.91-point gap). The improvements extended to other architectures, increasing Qwen3-VL-4B accuracy from 68.00% to 74.14%, Qwen3-VL-8B from 68.93% to 73.03%, and Qwen3.5-9B from 69.75% to 77.63%. These gains were observed across different mechanisms for visual feature integration and model architectures.
- **Outperformance of Larger Models:** The Qwen3.5-4B model enhanced with OPD-V achieved an average accuracy of 80.01%, which outperformed all listed closed-source models (e.g., Gemini-3.1-Pro at 79.25%) and substantially larger open-source systems (e.g., 397B Qwen3.5 at 77.44% and 1T Kimi-K2.6 at 72.81%). This suggests that OPD-V enables compact models to achieve high performance.
- **Concise Response Generation:** OPD-V maintained shorter and more stable response lengths throughout training compared to standard OPSD. Over the final ten training steps, OPD-V's mean response length was 140.9 tokens, a 74.5% reduction compared to OPSD's 553.8 tokens. This indicates more direct problem-solving trajectories.
- **Stable Policy Entropy:** After an initial warm-up phase, the policy entropy for both Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B models remained stable, suggesting that OPD-V maintained a non-degenerate and active learning signal throughout the training process.
- **Persistent Modality-Balance Trust Region:** The Modality-Balance Trust Region (tokens with positive Modality-Balance Logits Margin) persisted throughout training, averaging approximately 53.8% for Qwen3.5-4B and 49.6% for Qwen3.5-9B. This indicates that the dual-teacher mechanism consistently identified a selective subset of tokens for self-distillation across different model scales.

#### 4.2. Computational Efficiency (RQ2)

- **Reduced Step Latency:** OPD-V significantly reduced wall-clock step time. Mean step latency decreased by 31.8% (from 352s to 240s) for Qwen3.5-4B and by 24.7% (from 451s to 340s) for Qwen3.5-9B.
- **Contributing Factors:** This efficiency gain was attributed to two main factors:
 - Shorter student rollouts: The concise responses generated by OPD-V reduced the number of tokens processed during generation, forward passes for student and teachers, and backpropagation.
 - Efficient privileged-input construction: OPD-V's method of constructing teacher inputs via image operations (Zoom-In and Mask) was more efficient than standard OPSD's approach of inserting the reference target into an answer-hint prompt and reconstructing the multimodal teacher sequence. The combined forward time for the two OPD-V teachers (27.1s) was less than that for a single OPSD teacher (40.4s) in 9B timing logs, despite the additional teacher pass.

#### 4.3. Mechanistic Validity (RQ3)

- **Complementary Supervision from Dual Teachers:** Ablation studies demonstrated that both the Positive and Negative Teachers contributed to the overall performance, and their combination yielded superior results. The Negative Teacher alone improved the base model accuracy from 64.30% to 71.98%, while the Positive Teacher alone reached 74.62%. Combining both resulted in 80.01%, exceeding the stronger single-teacher variant by 5.39 percentage points. This supports the notion that the two teachers provide complementary signals: the Positive Teacher as the distillation target and the Negative Teacher for defining the Modality-Balance Trust Region.
- **Optimal Image Operation Pair:** The specific choice of image operations—Zoom-In Image for the Positive Teacher and Mask Image for the Negative Teacher—was found to be the most effective. Replacing Zoom-In Image with Repeat Image for the Positive Teacher, while holding the Negative Teacher's Mask Image fixed, lowered accuracy from 80.01% to 75.95%. For the Negative Teacher, with Zoom-In Image fixed, Mask Image (80.01%) outperformed Blur (74.31%), Prune (73.74%), and No Image (72.17%). This suggests that these particular transformations are effective in exposing and leveraging Modality Imbalance for defining the trust region.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents OPD-V, a novel visual On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) paradigm, which introduces several points of significance and potential impact for the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

**Novel Conceptual Framework:** The work's primary significance lies in identifying Modality Imbalance as a critical, yet previously overlooked, limitation in existing MLLM OPSD approaches. By re-conceptualizing Modality Balance itself as a form of "privileged information," the authors offer a new perspective on designing effective supervision signals for MLLMs. This moves beyond traditional privileged information that might simply enrich teacher input, towards an approach that actively guides the model to integrate visual and textual information more effectively.

**Enhanced Reasoning Performance:** OPD-V demonstrates consistent and substantial improvements in visual reasoning performance across a diverse suite of benchmarks and various MLLM architectures and scales. The reported absolute accuracy gains are notable, particularly the ability of a 4B-parameter model with OPD-V to outperform significantly larger state-of-the-art MLLMs (including closed-source and multi-trillion parameter open-source models). This indicates that OPD-V can lead to more robust and accurate MLLMs for visually demanding tasks.

**Improved Computational Efficiency:** A significant impact of this work is the simultaneous reduction in training cost alongside performance improvements. By promoting shorter, more concise response trajectories and streamlining the construction of privileged teacher inputs, OPD-V makes the self-distillation process more computationally efficient. This addresses a critical concern for training large models, potentially making advanced post-training techniques more accessible and sustainable.

**Enabling Compact and Powerful Models:** The finding that a compact 4B-parameter model, when enhanced with OPD-V, can achieve competitive or superior performance compared to much larger models, suggests a path towards more parameter-efficient MLLMs. This could lower the resource barrier for developing and deploying high-performing multimodal AI systems.

**Guidance for Future Research:**
- **Mitigating Modality Bias:** The research provides a concrete method to mitigate the inherent Modality Imbalance problem in MLLMs. Future work can explore how this concept of Modality Balance as privileged information can be extended or adapted to other forms of modality bias or cross-modal alignment challenges.
- **Teacher Design:** The ablation studies on different visual transformation strategies (Zoom-In, Mask, Blur, Prune, No Image) provide insights into how to design effective teacher inputs that can differentiate modality integration levels. This could inform the development of more sophisticated visual or multi-modal privileged information signals.
- **Interpretability:** By explicitly defining and leveraging Modality-Balance Attention Ratios and Logits Margins, the framework offers potential avenues for better understanding how MLLMs process and weigh information from different modalities during reasoning.

In summary, OPD-V represents a significant advancement in MLLM post-training, offering a principled, effective, and efficient method to improve visual reasoning by directly addressing Modality Imbalance. Its impact could extend to the development of more capable, robust, and resource-efficient multimodal AI systems.
