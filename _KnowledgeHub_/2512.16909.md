# MomaGraph: State-Aware Unified Scene Graphs with Vision-Language Model for Embodied Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley, UMD College Park, and the University of Toronto introduced MOMAGRAPH, a unified scene graph representation and an RL-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) for embodied task planning. This framework, coupled with a new dataset and benchmark, enables mobile manipulators to perform complex household tasks, achieving a 70% overall success rate in real-world, unseen environments.

## Problem

- Traditional scene graphs often fragment relationships (spatial vs. functional) and treat environments as static, leading to incomplete and less actionable representations for robots.
- Existing Vision-Language Models struggle with direct task planning from raw visual inputs due to sensitivity to noise, shallow semantic grounding, and a lack of structured object-relationship representations.
- Current scene representations often lack task relevance, failing to prioritize information essential for a robot's current objective in dynamic human environments.

## Method

- A novel, instruction-conditioned, state-aware unified ==scene graph== representation is proposed, integrating spatial and functional relationships, including fine-grained, part-level interactive elements.
- **MomaGraph**-R1, a 7B Vision-Language Model, is trained using the DAPO reinforcement learning algorithm with a custom graph-alignment reward function to predict accurate, task-oriented scene graphs.
- A "Graph-then-Plan" paradigm is employed, where the dynamically updated scene graph guides a zero-shot task planner, enabling robust and interpretable action sequencing.

## Results

- MomaGraph-R1 achieved an overall accuracy of **71.6%** on the MomaGraph-Bench, matching leading closed-source models and showing an **11.4%** relative improvement over its base model in graph-based reasoning.
- The model demonstrated state-of-the-art performance among open-source VLMs on visual correspondence tasks, leading by **3.8%** on BLINK and **4.8%** on MomaGraph-Bench.
- Deployed on a real RobotEra Q5 mobile manipulator, MomaGraph-R1 achieved a **70%** overall task success rate on complex, multi-step long-horizon tasks in unseen household environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified spatial-functional scene graphs are essential for effective embodied task planning, consistently outperforming fragmented or static representations.
> - Reinforcement learning with graph-alignment rewards successfully teaches VLMs to generate high-quality, task-oriented scene graphs, significantly improving planning capabilities.
> - The "Graph-then-Plan" paradigm enhances interpretability and robustness for embodied agents, providing a more reliable approach to task execution than opaque end-to-end VLM planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent embodied agents, particularly mobile manipulators operating in complex human environments like households, represents a grand challenge in AI and robotics. These robots must not only navigate but also interact with and manipulate objects, requiring a sophisticated understanding of their surroundings. This paper directly addresses the fundamental problem of how to represent an indoor scene in a way that is effective, compact, and semantically rich for such agents.

The current research landscape for embodied AI heavily relies on two main pillars: **scene graphs** for structured environment representation and **vision-language models (VLMs)** for multimodal understanding and planning. However, existing approaches suffer from significant limitations that MomaGraph aims to overcome:

- **Scene Graph Limitations:** Traditional scene graphs often compartmentalize relationships, focusing either on spatial layouts (e.g., objects being "left of" or "on top of" each other) or functional interactions (e.g., a "remote control" operating a "TV"). This separation leads to incomplete representations that are less actionable for robots needing to understand both where objects are and how they can be used. Furthermore, most existing scene graphs treat environments as static snapshots, failing to account for dynamic changes in object states or positions, which are commonplace in real-world human environments. They also tend to be generic, not explicitly highlighting information most relevant to a robot's current task.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM) Limitations for Planning:** While VLMs have shown remarkable abilities in processing multimodal inputs and generating language, directly using them for task planning often encounters issues like sensitivity to visual noise, shallow semantic grounding, and a lack of structured object-relationship representations. Prior "Graph-then-Plan" methods have existed, but they often assume the availability of a reliable 3D scene graph, which is not always practical, or treat graph construction and planning as separate, unoptimized modules.

MomaGraph positions itself as a critical bridge between these two areas. It proposes a **unified scene graph representation** that integrates both spatial and functional relationships, incorporates fine-grained, part-level interactive elements, and is explicitly **task-aware** and **dynamic**. By doing so, it directly addresses the shortcomings of prior scene graph approaches. Concurrently, it leverages the power of VLMs by proposing a novel **reinforcement learning (RL) based training paradigm** to teach these models to generate these richer scene graphs, thereby enabling more robust and interpretable "Graph-then-Plan" strategies. This approach enhances the VLM's ability to reason effectively and reduces reliance on direct, potentially unreliable, end-to-end planning from raw visual inputs.

This work aligns with broader trends in embodied AI striving for more interpretable, generalizable, and robust robotic systems that can operate intelligently in unstructured human environments. It contributes to the growing body of research on grounding language in physical actions, enhancing scene understanding for robot manipulation, and developing comprehensive benchmarks for evaluating complex reasoning abilities in AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MomaGraph stems from the observed limitations of current scene representation and planning methods for mobile manipulators in household environments. The authors identify a central question: **"What is the most effective, compact, and semantically rich representation of an indoor scene?"** Their analysis of existing approaches reveals three key deficiencies that drive their work:

1. **Fragmented Relationships:** Current scene graphs typically encode only a single type of relationship—either spatial (geometric layout) or functional (operability). This fragmentation leads to incomplete and less actionable representations for robots that need to both navigate *and* manipulate, requiring understanding of both 'where' and 'how' objects interact.
2. **Static Scene Snapshots:** Most methods treat scenes as static, failing to adapt to dynamic environments where object positions, states, or relationships change over time. Cognitive science research suggests that human perception is dynamic and task-oriented, flexibly adjusting attention to relevant information, a capability missing in static scene graphs.
3. **Lack of Task Relevance:** Existing representations often overlook information most pertinent to the current task, leading to inefficiencies. An ideal scene graph should emphasize relevant objects and relationships for a given task, much like a human "zooms in" on details on a map.

To overcome these limitations, the key objectives of the MomaGraph project are:

- **To propose MomaGraph:** A novel, unified scene graph representation for embodied agents that integrates spatial-functional relationships and part-level interactive elements. This representation is designed to be compact, adaptive to dynamic changes, and highly aligned with task instructions.
- **To construct MomaGraph-Scenes:** The first large-scale dataset specifically designed for task-driven scene graphs in household environments. This dataset provides rich annotations of spatial and functional relationships, part-level interactive elements, multi-view observations, executed actions, and task-aligned scene graphs to support the MomaGraph representation.
- **To develop MomaGraph-Bench:** A comprehensive and systematic evaluation suite to rigorously assess six key reasoning capabilities—from high-level task planning to fine-grained scene understanding—essential for embodied agents, thus addressing a lack of unified benchmarks.
- **To develop MomaGraph-R1:** A 7B vision-language model trained with reinforcement learning (using the DAPO algorithm) on MomaGraph-Scenes. This model is engineered to predict task-oriented scene graphs and function as a zero-shot task planner within a "Graph-then-Plan" framework, enhancing reasoning effectiveness and interpretability.
- **To demonstrate state-of-the-art performance:** Through extensive experiments, show that MomaGraph-R1 achieves superior results among open-source models, generalizes across public benchmarks, and effectively transfers to real-robot experiments.

Ultimately, the motivation is to provide embodied agents with a more concrete guidance for perception and task planning, moving beyond brittle, direct end-to-end approaches towards a more structured, interpretable, and human-like understanding of environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MomaGraph framework is built upon several interconnected components: a novel scene graph definition, a comprehensive dataset, an RL-trained vision-language model, a dynamic update mechanism, and a rigorous evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. MomaGraph Definition

MomaGraph is defined as an instruction-conditioned, task-oriented scene graph $G_T = (N_T, E_T^s, E_T^f)$.
- $N_T$: A set of nodes representing objects relevant to a given task $T$. Critically, these nodes can include fine-grained, part-level interactive elements (e.g., a "handle" of a "fridge"), providing a more granular representation than object-level graphs.
- $E_T^s$: Directed edges encoding spatial relationships among nodes (e.g., [LEFT OF], [CLOSE]).
- $E_T^f$: Directed edges capturing functional relationships, where one object can change the state of another (e.g., [CONTROL], [OPEN OR CLOSE]). Six functional types are defined, including [PAIR WITH] for assembly tasks.
This unified structure provides a minimal yet sufficient representation for grounding task instructions and facilitating embodied planning.

#### 3.2. MomaGraph-Scenes Dataset

To support the MomaGraph representation, the authors introduce MomaGraph-Scenes, the first large-scale dataset to jointly model spatial and functional relationships with part-level annotations.
- **Scale:** Approximately 1,050 task-oriented subgraphs, 6,278 multi-view RGB images, spanning over 350 diverse household scenes and 93 distinct task instructions.
- **Data Sources:** A combination of manually collected real-world data, re-annotated existing datasets (OpenFunGraph, SceneFun3D), and simulated environments built with AI2-THOR.
- **Annotation Details:**
 - **Multi-View Observation:** Flexible number and configuration of viewpoints, reflecting realistic perception conditions where agents must reason across partial observations.
 - **Task Instructions:** Natural language instructions (e.g., "Fill the bathtub") that require the model to infer relevant objects and relationships, promoting true understanding rather than reliance on explicit mentions.
 - **Nodes:** Include both task-relevant objects and specific interactive parts.
 - **Edges:** Cover 9 spatial relationship types (directional and distance-based) and 6 functional relationship types (Parameter Adjustment, Device Control, Open/Close, Water Flow Control, Power Supply, Assembly).

#### 3.3. MomaGraph-R1: VLM Training with Reinforcement Learning

MomaGraph-R1 is a 7B vision-language model built upon Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. The core innovation in its training is the application of the DAPO reinforcement learning algorithm to teach the VLM to generate accurate, task-oriented scene graphs.
- **Graph-Alignment Reward Function:** A carefully designed reward function $R(G_{pred_T}, G_{gt_T})$ guides the model:
 - **Action type prediction ($R_{action}$):** Binary reward for correctly predicting the required action type.
 - **Spatial-functional integration on edges ($R_{edges}$):** Measures semantic similarity between predicted and ground truth edges based on both spatial and functional relationship labels.
 - **Node completeness ($R_{nodes}$):** Intersection-over-union (IoU) similarity for task-relevant nodes.
 - **Auxiliary Rewards:** $R_{format}$ for valid JSON structure and $R_{length}$ to penalize verbose outputs.
This RL approach encourages the model to explore and refine its internal representations, discovering effective strategies for structured scene graph construction, rather than just memorizing patterns.

#### 3.4. State-Aware Dynamic Scene Graph Update

MomaGraph is designed to be dynamic. The scene graph $G_T^{(t)}$ is updated at each time step $t$ based on the agent's executed action $a_t$ and the observed new environment state $s_{t+1}$.
- **Update Function $U(·)$:** Refines the graph by removing inconsistent hypotheses and strengthening confirmed correspondences. For example, if rotating a specific knob affects a burner, that functional link is established, and ambiguous links from other knobs are pruned. This allows the graph to evolve into a compact, reliable representation. The paper clarifies that the focus is on capturing and incorporating observed state changes into the graph, not on the agent's interaction policy itself.

#### 3.5. MomaGraph-Bench Benchmark

A crucial contribution is MomaGraph-Bench, a comprehensive evaluation suite to systematically assess six key reasoning capabilities across four tiers of difficulty:
- **Reasoning Capabilities:** Action Sequence, Spatial, Object Affordance, Precondition & Effect, Goal Decomposition, and Visual Correspondence (across multiple views).
- **Format:** Formulated as a multi-choice VQA (Vision-Question Answering) task.
- **Difficulty Tiers:** Ranging from single-step manipulations (T1) to complex multi-step replanning and dynamic verification tasks (T4).
- **Generalization:** All scenarios are drawn from entirely unseen environments to ensure evaluation of true generalization rather than memorization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments presented in the paper demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of MomaGraph across various evaluations, from controlled benchmarks to real-world robot deployments.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Findings

Two motivating experiments confirmed the core hypotheses:
- **Graph-then-Plan Superiority:** Direct task planning by strong closed-source VLMs (like GPT-5) from raw visual inputs often failed, producing incorrect or incomplete plans. In contrast, the "Graph-then-Plan" approach, where MomaGraph first generates a task-specific scene graph before planning, consistently yielded correct and complete action sequences, highlighting the significant enhancement structured representations provide.
- **Unified Graph Necessity:** Models trained with unified spatial-functional scene graphs (MomaGraph-R1 and LLaVA-Onevision variants) consistently outperformed those trained with only spatial or only functional relationships, confirming that integrated representations are essential for effective embodied task planning.

#### 4.2. Benchmark Evaluation on MomaGraph-Bench

The MomaGraph-Bench, evaluating six reasoning capabilities across four tiers of difficulty, revealed several key insights:
- **Effectiveness of Graph-then-Plan:** Across all evaluated models (both open and closed source), enabling graph-based reasoning (`w/ Graph`) consistently improved performance compared to direct planning (`w/o Graph`). This validates the central hypothesis that structured, task-oriented scene graphs enhance downstream planning reliability.
- **MomaGraph-R1 Competitiveness:** MomaGraph-R1 achieved an overall accuracy of 71.6%, matching the performance of leading closed-source models like GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro. Crucially, it delivered a substantial +11.4% relative improvement over its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) when using graph-based reasoning, demonstrating the power of RL with graph-based rewards.
- **Scalability to Complex Tasks:** While most open-source baselines showed sharp performance drops on higher-tier (T3 and T4) complex, multi-step tasks, MomaGraph-R1 exhibited significantly less degradation. This indicates superior scalability and generalization to long-horizon planning scenarios.

#### 4.3. Benchmark Evaluation for Visual Correspondence

Beyond task planning, MomaGraph-R1 also demonstrated improved visual perception capabilities:
- Scene graph representations generally enhanced performance on visual correspondence tasks (both on public benchmark BLINK and MomaGraph-Bench) by reducing VLM hallucinations.
- MomaGraph-R1 achieved state-of-the-art performance among open-source VLMs, leading by 3.8% on BLINK and 4.8% on MomaGraph-Bench compared to the best competing open-source models. This confirms MomaGraph-R1's ability to enable more nuanced and reliable perception of complex indoor scenes by explicitly reasoning about spatial and functional relationships.

#### 4.4. Real Robot Demonstrations

To validate real-world applicability, MomaGraph-R1 was deployed on a RobotEra Q5 mobile manipulator for four diverse household tasks (e.g., "Open the cabinet," "Turn on the TV").
- **Robust Generalization:** The robot successfully performed these tasks in unseen environments, processing multi-view observations, generating task-specific subgraphs, and planning action sequences via the "Graph-then-Plan" paradigm.
- **Quantitative Real-Robot Evaluation:** A rigorous 10-trial evaluation on a complex, multi-step long-horizon task ("I need better lighting. Turn on the light closest to the remote so I can find it and turn on the monitor to watch.") yielded:
 - 80% success rate in graph generation.
 - 87.5% success rate in planning (conditioned on correct graphs).
 - **70% overall task success rate.**
- **Failure Analysis:** Main failure modes included spatial relation errors or missing nodes during graph generation, and action sequencing errors during planning, indicating areas for future refinement but confirming the system's overall robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MomaGraph paper makes several significant contributions that collectively advance the field of embodied AI and robotics, with substantial potential impact:

1. **Novel and Unified Scene Representation:** MomaGraph introduces a groundbreaking scene graph representation that transcends the limitations of prior work. By unifying spatial and functional relationships, incorporating fine-grained part-level interactive elements, and inherently supporting dynamic updates and task relevance, it provides a significantly more comprehensive and actionable understanding of environments for embodied agents. This representation is a crucial step towards equipping robots with a truly human-like cognitive map of their surroundings.

2. **Comprehensive Data and Benchmarking Resources:** The creation of **MomaGraph-Scenes** is a major contribution to the research community. As the first large-scale, richly annotated dataset of task-driven scene graphs that jointly models both spatial and functional relationships with part-level details, it addresses a critical data gap. Complementary to this, **MomaGraph-Bench** provides a much-needed, systematic, and comprehensive evaluation suite. By measuring six distinct reasoning capabilities across varying difficulty tiers, it offers a rigorous framework for assessing progress in embodied task planning and scene understanding, promoting fair comparison and targeted development. These resources are poised to become standard tools for future research in the field.

3. **Advancement in VLM for Embodied AI:** MomaGraph-R1 demonstrates a powerful methodology for training VLMs. The application of reinforcement learning with a custom graph-alignment reward function is highly effective in teaching a 7B VLM to generate high-quality, task-oriented scene graphs. This not only enhances the model's ability to understand complex scenes but also enables robust zero-shot task planning within an interpretable "Graph-then-Plan" framework. This approach offers a practical path to reducing VLM hallucinations and improving the reliability of VLM-based robotic systems.

4. **Bridging Interpretability and Performance:** The "Graph-then-Plan" paradigm is a key conceptual contribution, offering a more interpretable and robust alternative to opaque end-to-end VLM planning. By explicitly generating a structured intermediate representation (the scene graph), the system's reasoning process becomes more transparent and amenable to debugging, while simultaneously achieving superior performance. This work fosters stronger connections between traditional symbolic AI approaches (scene graphs) and modern deep learning (VLMs).

5. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment and quantitative evaluation of MomaGraph-R1 on a real mobile manipulator (RobotEra Q5) performing complex household tasks in unseen environments is a testament to its practical utility. This real-world validation, coupled with strong generalization capabilities observed on public benchmarks, indicates that MomaGraph is not merely a theoretical advance but a viable technology for developing more intelligent, adaptive, and deployable robotic assistants.

In conclusion, MomaGraph provides a foundational advancement in scene representation and task planning for embodied agents. It offers a new paradigm that promises to enhance robot autonomy, interpretability, and reliability in complex, dynamic environments, paving the way for a new generation of intelligent robots capable of seamless interaction within human habitats. The authors' hope that MomaGraph will "serve as a foundation for advancing scene representations, fostering stronger connections between the spatial VLM and robotics communities, and ultimately enabling more intelligent and adaptive embodied agents" is well-supported by their impactful contributions.
