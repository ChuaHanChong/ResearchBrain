# SAMPO:Scale-wise Autoregression with Motion PrOmpt for generative world models

> [!summary] Summary
> The SAMPO framework introduces a hybrid autoregressive architecture to improve generative world models for action-conditioned video prediction and model-based control, yielding higher fidelity and temporal consistency. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across various tasks while notably speeding up inference.

## Problem

- Existing autoregressive world models suffer from structural degradation in visual predictions due to 1D sequence flattening, leading to incoherent or implausible outputs.
- Sequential token prediction in these models results in slow inference speeds and accumulating errors over longer prediction horizons.
- Prior models inadequately capture salient motion and dynamic object interactions, producing videos that lack physical realism, especially in static-heavy scenes.

## Method

- A hybrid autoregressive architecture combines temporal causal decoding with ==scale-wise==, coarse-to-fine spatial generation, allowing parallel token generation within each spatial scale.
- An asymmetric multi-scale tokenizer densely encodes observed frames for detail while sparsely encoding future frames to focus on dynamic changes and reduce redundancy.
- A trajectory-aware motion prompt module extracts and filters dynamic 2D point trajectories, which are then explicitly integrated as spatiotemporal cues to guide the model's attention.

## Results

- SAMPO achieves state-of-the-art performance in action-conditioned video prediction, significantly outperforming prior methods like iVideoGPT across FVD, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics (e.g., FVD 55.5 vs 60.8 on BAIR).
- The framework shows improved success rates in visual planning benchmarks (VP2), averaging **2.1%** better than iVideoGPT, and accelerates policy convergence in model-based reinforcement learning (Meta-World) compared to iVideoGPT and DreamerV3.
- SAMPO demonstrates substantial inference speedups (e.g., **4.4x** faster than traditional autoregressive models) and exhibits strong zero-shot generalization capabilities when pretrained on large-scale datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining causal temporal autoregression with parallel scale-wise spatial generation effectively preserves both spatial locality and temporal consistency while significantly boosting inference efficiency.
> - Asymmetrically tokenizing observed and future frames optimizes memory and processing, allowing the model to efficiently disentangle static background from dynamic foreground changes.
> - Explicitly guiding the model with trajectory-aware motion prompts significantly improves its capacity to focus on and accurately model dynamic object-agent interactions and physical dependencies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" is a foundational challenge on the path to Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), aiming to enable agents to simulate, understand, and interact with dynamic environments. SAMPO addresses a critical subset of this challenge: creating generative world models that can accurately predict future visual states conditioned on an agent's actions, thereby facilitating planning, control, and long-horizon decision-making.

The broader research landscape for generative world models primarily encompasses three generative paradigms:
1. **Masked Modeling:** These models (e.g., MaskViT, MAGVIT) achieve efficient pretraining by reconstructing missing parts of an input. While effective for feature learning, they often struggle with preserving temporal consistency and causality, which are crucial for dynamic world modeling. Their localized objective can lead to predictions that lack a coherent narrative over time.
2. **Diffusion-based Models:** Models like MCVD and Video Diffusion Models produce high-fidelity video through an iterative denoising process. They excel at visual quality but are often computationally intensive, leading to slow inference speeds, and can be limited in their interactivity, which is essential for closed-loop world model applications.
3. **Autoregressive Models:** Approaches like iVideoGPT and VideoGPT generate tokens sequentially, naturally preserving causal structure and enabling in-context prediction. This makes them inherently well-suited for forecasting future states in response to actions. However, existing autoregressive models face significant limitations:
 - **Structural Degradation:** Flattening video frames into a 1D sequence (raster-scan order) disrupts spatial locality, leading to visually incoherent or physically implausible predictions.
 - **Inefficient Decoding:** Sequential next-token prediction can be slow and prone to error accumulation, hindering real-time applications and long-horizon rollouts.
 - **Inadequate Motion Modeling:** They often struggle to capture salient motion and dynamic interactions, particularly in scenes dominated by static backgrounds, leading to blurred or inconsistent object behaviors.

SAMPO strategically positions itself within the autoregressive paradigm but explicitly tackles these long-standing limitations. It seeks to overcome structural degradation by adopting a scale-wise (coarse-to-fine) spatial generation approach, similar to those used in high-resolution image synthesis, rather than a simple raster-scan. It improves efficiency by parallelizing token generation within each spatial scale. Most significantly, it enhances dynamic scene understanding by introducing an explicit "motion prompt" mechanism, a technique borrowed from the emerging field of prompt engineering for guiding generative models, but adapted for spatiotemporal cues in world models. By doing so, SAMPO aims to achieve the best of both worlds: the causal integrity and interactive potential of autoregressive models, combined with the visual coherence, fidelity, and efficiency often found in other generative paradigms. This work contributes to the growing effort to build generalizable and control-centric world models capable of operating in diverse robotic and human-interaction environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SAMPO is to address the inherent weaknesses of current autoregressive world models, which, despite their ability to preserve causal structure, fall short in terms of visual coherence, computational efficiency, and accurate dynamic scene understanding. The paper outlines three core challenges that SAMPO aims to overcome:

1. **Mitigate Structural Degradation in Visual Predictions:** Traditional autoregressive models often flatten 2D video frames into 1D sequences (e.g., raster-scan order) for token-by-token generation. This process disrupts the spatial locality of visual information, making it difficult for the model to capture long-range dependencies across space. The consequence is often physically implausible generations, such as objects appearing or disappearing abruptly, or blurred manipulators in robotic scenes. SAMPO's objective is to develop an autoregressive formulation that explicitly preserves spatial locality and structural coherence within each frame.
2. **Improve Rollout Efficiency and Reduce Error Accumulation:** The sequential nature of next-token prediction in existing autoregressive models leads to slow inference times. Furthermore, errors can accumulate over long generation sequences, diminishing the quality and reliability of future predictions. A key objective for SAMPO is to accelerate inference and enhance the robustness of predictions over longer horizons.
3. **Enhance Dynamic Scene Understanding and Physical Realism:** Many world models struggle to accurately capture salient motion, object interactions, and physical dependencies, especially in environments where most of the scene is static or quasi-static. This often results in generated videos that lack physical realism and smoothness, particularly when modeling robotic manipulation tasks. SAMPO aims to provide the model with explicit guidance to focus on dynamic regions, thereby improving its ability to simulate physically plausible interactions.

In essence, SAMPO is motivated by the need for a generative world model that is simultaneously high-fidelity, temporally consistent, interactively scalable, and capable of nuanced understanding of dynamic interactions, thereby advancing the capabilities of agents in planning and control for complex real-world tasks, particularly in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAMPO introduces a hybrid framework that synergistically combines advances in visual autoregressive modeling with targeted mechanisms for dynamic scene understanding. The core methodology revolves around three key components:

#### 3.1. Hybrid Autoregressive Architecture with Scale-wise Generation

SAMPO's foundational approach reimagines autoregressive generation by unifying temporal and spatial prediction. Instead of a linear, raster-scan order, it adopts a **scale-wise autoregressive scheme**. The model is autoregressive *across frames* (temporal causality), meaning it generates one frame after another. However, *within each frame*, it generates visual tokens in a **coarse-to-fine manner**, progressing from low-resolution token maps to high-resolution ones.

- **Temporal Causal Decoding:** Given an input sequence of observed frames and actions, SAMPO predicts future frames sequentially. Actions are integrated into the model by linear projection and added to the start token embedding of each frame, providing action-conditioned generation.
- **Bidirectional Spatial Attention:** Within each frame, after discretizing it into hierarchical multi-scale token maps, SAMPO generates tokens at each spatial scale. Critically, tokens within the *same* scale are generated in parallel, reducing the sequential dependency inherent in traditional autoregressive models. This parallel generation is guided by a block-wise causal mask during training to ensure each token only attends to already generated coarser scales and previously generated tokens in time. During inference, KV-caching is employed, and masks are not needed for intra-frame generation. This design preserves spatial locality, enhances structural coherence, and significantly improves decoding efficiency.

#### 3.2. Asymmetric Multi-scale Tokenizer

To optimize both memory usage and model performance, SAMPO employs an **asymmetric multi-scale tokenizer** based on vector quantization (VQ-VAE). This design leverages the observation that observed (context) frames and future (predicted) frames often have different information densities:
- **Observed Frames (t ≤ T₀):** These frames contain rich static background information, contextual cues, and sensor noise. Therefore, they are *densely tokenized* across all available spatial scales (L_full) to preserve fine-grained details.
- **Future Frames (t > T₀):** These frames primarily depict dynamic changes involving the robotic arm and manipulated objects, while most of the background remains static. To reduce token redundancy and focus the model's capacity on relevant dynamic regions, future frames are tokenized using only a *sparse subset* of coarser scales (L_sub < L_full). Cross-attention mechanisms are used to incorporate information from the densely tokenized observed frames into the sparsely tokenized future frames.

This asymmetric approach improves inference speed and disentangles static background priors from dynamic foreground variations, leading to more structured and efficient visual representations.

#### 3.3. Trajectory-aware Motion Prompt Module

Recognizing that visual fidelity alone is insufficient for effective interactive world models, SAMPO introduces a **trajectory-aware motion prompt module** to explicitly guide the model's attention towards dynamic interactions.
- **Motion Trajectory Extraction:** The CoTracker3 point-tracking model is used to extract 2D point trajectories across observed frames. A regular grid of query points is sampled on the first frame and tracked over time.
- **Dynamic Trajectory Filtering:** To focus on relevant motion and suppress noise or static elements, raw trajectories are filtered based on two criteria: low tracking confidence (c_i < τ_c) and minimal displacement over a short temporal window (d_i < 2% of image diagonal). Only trajectories exhibiting significant movement are retained.
- **Prompt Injection:** The filtered dynamic trajectories are overlaid onto the original observation frames, creating "motion prompts." These prompts are then concatenated with the raw visual input, typically separated by a special token, to form a dual-branch input. This explicit conditioning mechanism provides spatiotemporal cues that direct the model's attention to interaction-relevant regions (e.g., robotic arms, manipulated objects), enhancing its ability to model object-agent interactions, maintain temporal consistency, and capture underlying physical dependencies.

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

SAMPO is trained using a multi-scale cross-entropy loss over future frames (L_CE), where each spatial scale's contribution is weighted (λ_l). This encourages the model to predict future token maps autoregressively across time and space. For model-based reinforcement learning applications, an optional auxiliary linear head can be added to predict rewards, utilizing a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, further aligning the model with control-centric objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SAMPO demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across various benchmarks in action-conditioned video prediction, visual planning, and model-based reinforcement learning, while also achieving significant inference speedups.

#### 4.1. Video Prediction Performance

- **Quantitative Results:** SAMPO consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods across standard video prediction metrics: Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS).
 - On **BAIR Robot Pushing (64x64 resolution, action-conditioned)**, SAMPO achieves an FVD of 55.5, significantly better than iVideoGPT's 60.8. It also shows improvements in PSNR (26.7 vs 24.5), SSIM (94.7 vs 90.2), and LPIPS (3.7 vs 5.0).
 - On **RoboNet (64x64, action-conditioned)**, SAMPO yields an FVD of 57.1 compared to iVideoGPT's 63.2. Similar improvements are observed across other metrics.
 - For **high-resolution (256x256) action-conditioned prediction on RoboNet**, the larger SAMPO-L model achieves an FVD of 175.3, outperforming iVideoGPT's 197.9.
 - On the challenging **1X World Model dataset (256x256, action-conditioned)**, SAMPO achieves an FVD of 227.1, substantially better than iVideoGPT's 251.8.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons (e.g., Figure 4 and Appendix C) reveal that SAMPO produces videos with higher perceptual quality and more realistic motion, especially in complex backgrounds and dynamic object interactions. It avoids artifacts like object disappearances or blurred manipulators common in other autoregressive models.

#### 4.2. Planning and Reinforcement Learning

- **Visual Planning (VP² Benchmark):** SAMPO is evaluated on the VP² benchmark for visual planning, which involves noisy, scripted interaction trajectories.
 - SAMPO achieves the best or second-best success rates across 8 robotic manipulation tasks (Robosuite, RoboDesk), with an average performance improvement of 2.1% over iVideoGPT. This demonstrates SAMPO's ability to facilitate effective planning from visual predictions, indicating a strong correlation between its improved visual fidelity and control performance.
- **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) on Meta-World:** Integrated into an MBPO framework, SAMPO is tested on six Meta-World robotic manipulation tasks.
 - SAMPO significantly accelerates policy convergence and achieves higher upper bounds in success rate compared to iVideoGPT. This improvement is attributed to the temporal consistency and semantic coherence provided by SAMPO's hybrid framework and motion prompts.
 - Notably, SAMPO also substantially outperforms DreamerV3, a leading MBRL method, in both sample efficiency and overall success rate.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies & Model Analysis

Ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of SAMPO's components:
- **Hybrid Framework:** The scale-wise autoregressive generation with bidirectional spatial attention shows competitive FVD and SSIM, with parallel prediction within scales leading to significant inference speedups (e.g., 4.4x faster inference compared to AR models, and 9.05 s/vid for AR vs 2.06 s/vid for SAMPO-S on BAIR, Table 5).
- **Motion Prompt and Temporal Embedding:** Both modules contribute positively to temporal consistency and semantic coherence. Temporal position embedding, in particular, plays a crucial role, yielding substantial improvements in FVD and SSIM. Adding motion prompt alone improved FVD by 9.6 and SSIM by 2.4 points on RoboNet.
- **Spatial Scale Design:** Experimentation with different scale configurations reveals that smaller inter-scale strides (more gradual resolution refinement) lead to better performance, suggesting the coarse-to-fine residual-based generation benefits from smoother transitions.
- **Scaling Laws:** SAMPO exhibits clear scaling behaviors. Larger models (SAMPO-B and SAMPO-L) consistently show improved FVD and SSIM, indicating that the architecture benefits from increased parameter count by capturing more complex spatiotemporal dependencies.
- **Zero-shot Generalization:** A model pretrained on the large-scale Open X-Embodiment dataset demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization capabilities when tested on the 1X World Model dataset, generating high-quality, temporally consistent videos without fine-tuning. This highlights SAMPO's potential for broad applicability in diverse and unseen environments.

In summary, SAMPO's main findings confirm its effectiveness in generating high-fidelity, temporally consistent, and efficient video predictions crucial for robust world models. Its improvements translate directly into enhanced performance in downstream planning and reinforcement learning tasks, positioning it as a significant advancement in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SAMPO represents a significant stride in the development of generative world models, offering a robust and efficient framework that addresses critical limitations of existing autoregressive approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Generative World Models:** By introducing a novel hybrid autoregressive architecture that integrates scale-wise spatial generation with causal temporal modeling, SAMPO effectively overcomes the structural degradation and inefficiency issues prevalent in prior autoregressive models. This design allows for the preservation of both spatial locality within frames and temporal consistency across frames, leading to visually coherent and physically plausible predictions—a crucial requirement for truly intelligent agents.
- **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** SAMPO's ability to perform parallel decoding within each spatial scale results in a remarkable acceleration of inference (e.g., 4.4× faster than iVideoGPT), without compromising generation quality. This efficiency is paramount for real-time planning, control, and long-horizon decision-making in robotic systems, where rapid response is essential. Furthermore, its demonstrated scaling behavior suggests that SAMPO can benefit from larger model sizes and datasets, paving the way for even more capable future iterations.
- **Enhanced Dynamic Understanding for Robotics:** The trajectory-aware motion prompt module is a key innovation, providing explicit spatiotemporal cues that guide the model's attention to dynamic regions. This significantly improves SAMPO's capacity to model complex object-agent interactions and physical dependencies, a weakness in many previous models. This is particularly impactful for robotic manipulation tasks, where precise understanding and prediction of object movements are vital for successful execution.
- **Strong Performance Across Benchmarks:** SAMPO's consistent state-of-the-art performance in action-conditioned video prediction, visual planning (VP²), and model-based reinforcement learning (Meta-World) solidifies its position as a leading architecture in the domain. Its ability to accelerate policy learning and achieve higher success rates in MBRL tasks compared to both prior world models (iVideoGPT) and dedicated MBRL methods (DreamerV3) underscores its practical utility for developing more efficient and effective robotic policies.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks and datasets, following pretraining on large-scale robot datasets like Open X-Embodiment, is a strong indicator of SAMPO's potential for building truly generalizable world models. This capability is crucial for reducing the need for task-specific fine-tuning and enabling agents to operate robustly in novel environments.

**Potential Impact:**
The advancements brought by SAMPO hold profound implications for the field of embodied AI and robotics. It offers a more reliable and efficient internal simulator for agents, enabling them to:
- **Better Plan and Explore:** Agents can accurately imagine the consequences of their actions, exploring vast possibilities in a simulated environment before committing to real-world execution.
- **Learn More Efficiently:** The improved fidelity and consistency of world models accelerate policy learning in reinforcement learning settings, reducing the amount of real-world data required.
- **Tackle Complex Manipulation Tasks:** Enhanced understanding of object dynamics will enable robots to perform more dexterous and intricate manipulation tasks.
- **Bridge the Sim-to-Real Gap:** More realistic and interactive world models can help in creating better synthetic data for training, reducing the gap between simulated and real-world performance.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges that SAMPO still faces challenges, particularly with **long-term modeling and error accumulation** over extended prediction horizons. Future research could focus on techniques to improve long-term consistency and prevent error propagation, potentially through hierarchical temporal abstractions or more robust uncertainty modeling. The appendix further notes a **limitation in motion prompt generation from a single input frame**, which could be addressed by developing implicit dynamic priors or stochasticity control mechanisms. Integrating motion guidance more deeply, possibly through end-to-end learning or neural differential equations, also presents a promising avenue for future exploration.

**Broader Societal Impact:**
While primarily aimed at enhancing robotic intelligence, SAMPO's generative capabilities come with broader societal considerations. On the positive side, it can accelerate automation in various sectors, leading to increased productivity and the ability to perform dangerous or mundane tasks. However, as with any powerful generative AI, there's a risk of misuse, such as generating deepfakes for disinformation, enhancing surveillance, or creating manipulative content. The authors briefly touch upon mitigation strategies, emphasizing the need for responsible development, restricted access, robust detection systems, ethical guidelines, transparency, and continuous monitoring for biases. Ensuring these safeguards are prioritized alongside technological advancement will be crucial for maximizing the beneficial impact of models like SAMPO.
