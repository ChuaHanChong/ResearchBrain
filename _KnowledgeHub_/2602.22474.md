# When to Act, Ask, or Learn: Uncertainty-Aware Policy Steering

> [!summary] Summary
> Carnegie Mellon University researchers introduced Uncertainty-aware Policy Steering (UPS), a framework integrating VLM-in-the-loop steering with conformal prediction to robustly quantify and resolve multi-level uncertainties in robot tasks. UPS achieves statistically guaranteed performance by differentiating between high-level semantic ambiguity and low-level policy incapability, significantly reducing human intervention costs.

## Problem

- Existing VLM verifiers in robot policy steering often exhibit overconfidence and miscalibration, leading to confident selection of incorrect or unfeasible actions.
- Current robot learning methods typically decouple high-level semantic uncertainty (ambiguous task specifications) from low-level action uncertainty (policy's inability to execute a skill).
- Robots lack adaptive, cost-efficient strategies to resolve different modes of uncertainty, often requiring expensive, generic human feedback for all issues.

## Method

- Developed **Uncertainty-aware Policy Steering (UPS)** which uses a ==latent world model== for long-horizon outcome prediction and a VLM for narrating and verifying action samples, including a 'none of the above' option.
- Implemented ==Conformal Prediction (CP)== with a novel ==Bayesian intent factorization== score function for VLM verifier uncertainty quantification, providing statistical guarantees for prediction sets.
- Designed adaptive uncertainty resolution strategies: executing confident actions, requesting natural language clarification for semantic ambiguity, or triggering targeted ==residual policy retraining== for policy incapability.

## Results

- The proposed Bayesian Intent score function combined with Conformal Prediction maintained high coverage (**>= 85%**) while minimizing clarification rates across straightforward, ambiguous, and incapable scenarios.
- UPS with clarification significantly increased success rates in ambiguous hardware tasks (e.g., a **15%** increase in the PnP Cup task) by effectively triggering natural language clarifications.
- UPS with clarification and residual learning demonstrated the lowest human intervention rate (**0.05-0.06** across tasks) compared to DAgger variants, while achieving an **85%** success rate for the PnP Cup task after retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Calibrating VLM verifiers with Conformal Prediction and Bayesian intent factorization effectively reshapes the VLM's uncertainty distribution, leading to tighter and more informative prediction sets.
> - Jointly reasoning about multi-level uncertainty allows the system to differentiate between semantic task ambiguity and low-level policy incapability, enabling more precise and cost-efficient interventions.
> - Employing residual policy learning, specifically when policy incapability is detected, facilitates continual improvement without catastrophic forgetting, preserving the base policy's diverse capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research operates within the domain of robot learning, specifically focusing on the deployment-time adaptation of learned robot behaviors, a paradigm referred to as policy steering. Recent advancements in imitation-based generative robot policies, such as diffusion policies and vision-language-action (VLA) models, have enabled the generation of low-level action distributions conditioned on multimodal inputs. However, directly executing samples from these policies does not consistently lead to successful task completion in real-world scenarios.

Policy steering approaches aim to address this challenge by employing a verifier to analyze proposed action samples from a pre-trained policy, selecting those that align with the task instructions. Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as potential general-purpose verifiers due to their inherent reasoning capabilities over both visual and linguistic information.

The existing body of work in VLM-based policy steering often implicitly assumes that these verifiers are well-calibrated, meaning they can confidently and accurately select appropriate actions or correctly reject unsuitable candidates. However, this assumption frequently fails in practice, as VLMs can exhibit overconfidence, particularly when task instructions are ambiguous or the underlying low-level policy is incapable of performing the requested action.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing the calibration deficiencies of VLM verifiers and coupling uncertainty quantification with appropriate resolution strategies. Previous efforts in uncertainty quantification (UQ) for learned robot policies typically decouple semantic ambiguity from low-level action uncertainty. This paper integrates these two levels of uncertainty, grounding semantic intent reasoning in the practical capabilities of the low-level action policy. Furthermore, it differentiates itself from interactive imitation learning (IIL) methods like DAgger variants by proposing a mechanism for the robot to autonomously identify when human intervention is necessary, thus minimizing costly human feedback, and by utilizing residual learning for continuous policy improvement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Address VLM Overconfidence and Miscalibration:** Existing VLM verifiers often provide overconfident judgments even when task instructions are ambiguous or when the pre-trained policy is incapable. This can lead to the confident selection of incorrect or unfeasible actions, degrading overall steering performance. The work aims to develop a mechanism to calibrate these verifiers.
2. **Jointly Reason about Multi-level Uncertainty:** Robot tasks often involve both high-level semantic uncertainty (e.g., ambiguous task specifications) and low-level action uncertainty (e.g., the base policy's inability to execute a specific skill or generate appropriate samples). A key objective is to enable the system to jointly consider both types of uncertainty rather than treating them in isolation.
3. **Develop Adaptive Uncertainty Resolution Strategies:** When uncertainty or incapability is detected, the robot should not simply stop or request generic human help. The motivation is to equip the robot with distinct, actionable strategies for resolving different modes of uncertainty, ranging from low-cost natural language clarifications to more expensive human interventions for policy retraining.
4. **Minimize Costly Human Interventions:** Human feedback, especially for low-level action demonstrations, is expensive. The research aims to reduce the frequency and cost of such interventions by allowing the robot to first attempt to resolve uncertainty through cheaper methods (like clarification) and only request retraining when absolutely necessary, based on a calibrated assessment of policy incapability.
5. **Enable Continual Learning with Statistical Assurances:** The system is motivated to learn and improve continually from collected interventions. A critical objective is to provide statistical assurances that the chosen uncertainty resolution strategy will lead to successful task completion with a user-specified probability, thereby increasing system reliability.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond uncalibrated, "always-act" or "always-ask-for-help" VLM-based steering systems, towards a more sophisticated framework that understands *when to act, ask, or learn*, with principled uncertainty quantification and adaptive response mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Uncertainty-aware Policy Steering (UPS), integrates VLM-in-the-loop policy steering with conformal prediction for robust uncertainty quantification and dynamic resolution strategies.

#### 3.1. VLM-in-the-loop Policy Steering

The UPS framework builds upon existing VLM-in-the-loop steering by formulating the problem as an open-ended multiple-choice Question & Answer (Q&A) task. This process involves three stages:

- **Outcome Prediction:** A latent world model (Dreamer-v3) is used to predict the long-horizon outcomes of low-level action samples. Since base policies often generate short action chunks, the system interleaves action generation (from the base policy) and imagination (from the world model) to construct longer, T-length action sequences and corresponding imagined observation sequences. This provides more informative outcomes for the VLM verifier.
- **Narration:** The imagined observation sequences are translated into textual narrations using a VLM (Gemini-3-flash-preview). These narrations describe the predicted outcome of each action sequence (e.g., "The robot inserts the block into the back right hole."). A (K+1)-th option, "none of the above samples are correct," is added to the set of narrations to explicitly allow for the detection of policy incapability.
- **Verification:** A VLM verifier (Gemini-2.0-flash) selects the most likely option from the set of narrations by answering a multiple-choice Q&A problem, thus approximating P(y | {ℓ_k}, L).

#### 3.2. VLM Verifier Uncertainty Quantification

Conformal Prediction (CP) is employed to quantify the VLM verifier's uncertainty and construct prediction sets with statistical guarantees.

- **Sequence-level Calibration:** To address temporal dependencies in sequential decision-making, calibration is performed at the sequence level. A calibration dataset, D_calib, consists of sequences of observations, action narrations, task instructions, and ground-truth labels indicating correct action(s) for each step in a task.
- **Non-conformity Score:** A novel non-conformity score, κ(¯x, ¯y) = 1 − min_{x_i ∈ ¯x} min_{y ∈ Y*i} P_VLM(y | x_i), is defined. This score calculates the minimum VLM probability assigned to any true label across all steps in a sequence. This conservative design ensures a "completeness guarantee," meaning the prediction set will include *all* suitable options, rather than just one.
- **Prediction Set Construction:** An empirical quantile, ˆq, is derived from the non-conformity scores of the calibration set. At deployment, a prediction set C(x_test) is constructed by including all labels for which the VLM verifier's confidence (1 - P_VLM(y | x_test)) is below or equal to ˆq. This process provides a formal statistical assurance (Theorem 1) that the true set of correct actions will be included in C(x_test) with a probability of at least 1-ϵ.
- **Shaping VLM's Distribution (Bayesian Intent Factorization):** To improve the tightness of prediction sets, the VLM's raw probability distribution (P_VLM) is intrinsically recalibrated *before* CP. This is achieved by factoring the VLM's reasoning, inspired by Bayesian models of human intent: P_VLM(y | {ℓ_k}, L) = Σ_θ∈Θ P(y | {ℓ_k}, θ)P(θ | L). The VLM is prompted to hypothesize hidden human intents (θ), estimate their probabilities given the task instruction (P(θ | L)), and then infer the likelihood of each action option given a hypothesized intent (P(y | {ℓ_k}, θ)). Marginalizing over intents leads to a more balanced and less overconfident P_VLM, especially in ambiguous or incapable scenarios.

#### 3.3. Resolving Uncertainty: From High-level Clarifications to Low-Level Continual Learning

The prediction set C(x_test) generated by CP dictates the robot's resolution strategy:

- **Confident and Capable Policy (Execute):** If |C(x_test)| = 1 and K+1 (the "none of the above" option) is not in the set, the robot executes the single, high-confidence action sequence.
- **Resolving Task Uncertainty (Clarify):** If |C(x_test)| > 1, indicating high-level semantic ambiguity (multiple options seem plausible), the robot asks a natural language clarification question to the user, summarizing the options in C(x_test). The user's response is used to update the task instruction, and the verification loop repeats.
- **Resolving Policy Incapability (Re-train):** If C(x_test) = {K+1}, the VLM verifier determines that none of the generated action samples align with the task, signaling policy incapability. This triggers interactive imitation learning, where the robot requests human supervision to correct a failed trajectory.
 - **Residual Policy Learning:** To improve the policy, a lightweight residual model (π_residual) is trained to predict the difference (Δa) between human corrections and base policy outputs. A gating classifier determines when this correction is active.
 - **Deployment with Residuals:** During re-deployment, action samples are drawn from a mix of the frozen base policy and the combined (base + residual) policy to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. The VLM verifier is then recalibrated to account for the shifted action distribution. This selective intervention strategy is designed to minimize human effort by only requesting low-level interventions when the policy is truly incapable of performing the *semantically correct* action.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework was evaluated in both a simulated Robomimic nut-on-peg task and real-world hardware manipulation tasks (PnP Cup and Insert Block) using a Franka manipulator.

#### 4.1. Uncertainty-Aware Steering (VLM Verifier Calibration)

- **Score Function Performance:** The research compared three UQ methods (Simple Set, APS, Conformal Prediction (CP)) combined with three score functions (Vanilla, Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Bayesian Intent (Ours)).
 - The **Bayesian Intent score function with CP** achieved a balance between maintaining high coverage (≥ 85% as targeted 1-ϵ) and minimizing the clarification rate across straightforward, ambiguous, and incapable scenarios in both simulation and hardware.
 - CoT-based methods often overestimated ambiguity, leading to clarification rates near 100%, or suffered from significant coverage drops when prediction set sizes were reduced.
 - Vanilla methods exhibited poor calibration, resulting in overconfidence or under-coverage.
 - This indicates that the proposed Bayesian intent factorization effectively reshapes the VLM's uncertainty distribution, leading to tighter and more informative prediction sets.
- **Impact on Policy Steering Performance:**
 - **Forewarn** (an uncalibrated VLM steering baseline) improved success rates in straightforward and ambiguous scenarios compared to the Base Policy, but showed smaller gains in ambiguous cases due to VLM overconfidence.
 - **UPS w/ Clarification** significantly improved success rates in ambiguous scenarios (e.g., a 15% increase in the PnP Cup task) by effectively using calibrated uncertainty to trigger natural language clarifications, demonstrating the benefit of the calibrated VLM verifier.

#### 4.2. Continual Learning

- **Minimizing Human Feedback:**
 - **UPS w/ Clarification + Residual** demonstrated the lowest human intervention rate compared to **HG-DAgger** and **EnsembleDAgger** across all tasks.
 - For the PnP Cup task, UPS required interventions at a rate of 0.06, significantly lower than HG-DAgger (0.1) and EnsembleDAgger (0.16). Similar trends were observed in the Insert Block task (0.05 for UPS vs. 0.06 for HG-DAgger and 0.26 for EnsembleDAgger) and simulation (0.058 for UPS vs. 0.20 for HG-DAgger and 0.27 for EnsembleDAgger).
 - This finding suggests that semantic-level uncertainty quantification, which triggers interventions only when no action sample *semantically aligns* with the task, is more efficient than low-level action-uncertainty gating.
- **Improved Re-Deployment Performance:**
 - After residual policy training, **UPS w/ Clarification + Residual** maintained and improved performance, achieving a final success rate of 85% for the PnP Cup task, aligning with the theoretical 1-ϵ coverage guarantee. It specifically showed a 15% increase in success rate in ambiguous settings for both hardware tasks compared to UPS w/ Clarification only.
 - In contrast, both **EnsembleDAgger + Residual** and **HG-DAgger + Residual** struggled to learn new behaviors effectively while preserving existing capabilities, sometimes leading to mode collapse or new incapability modes, as illustrated in the Insert Block task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Principled Uncertainty Quantification for VLM Verifiers:** The introduction of conformal prediction for VLM verifiers, combined with a novel Bayesian intent factorization score function and sequence-level calibration, provides statistical guarantees for VLM-based policy steering. This moves beyond heuristic confidence measures, offering a more reliable foundation for robot decision-making under uncertainty.
2. **Adaptive and Cost-Efficient Uncertainty Resolution:** By explicitly disentangling high-level semantic task uncertainty from low-level policy incapability, the system can selectively employ appropriate resolution strategies. This capability allows the robot to first attempt cheaper natural language clarifications, only resorting to more expensive human interventions for policy retraining when truly necessary. This dynamic approach leads to a significant reduction in overall human effort.
3. **Enhanced Continual Learning Efficiency:** The method's ability to precisely identify scenarios requiring low-level intervention enables more targeted and efficient data collection for continual policy improvement. The use of residual learning allows the system to integrate human corrections without compromising the diverse capabilities of the base policy, leading to improved re-deployment performance compared to prior interactive imitation learning approaches.
4. **Increased Robot Autonomy and Trustworthiness:** By equipping robots with the ability to "know when they don't know" and to articulate their uncertainties, this framework can lead to more autonomous and trustworthy robotic systems. Robots can intelligently self-diagnose problems and request specific types of help, enhancing their utility in complex, open-world environments where task specifications may be vague or conditions unpredictable.

**Limitations** acknowledged by the authors include the assumption that world model imaginations accurately predict future outcomes and that behavior narrations are consistently correct and complete. Future work could address these limitations by incorporating uncertainty within the world model and narration processes for a more robust overall system.
