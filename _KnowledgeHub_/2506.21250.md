# ACTLLM: Action Consistency Tuned Large Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ACTLLM introduces a framework for robot manipulation that unifies visual information interpretation with policy learning through structured, human-interpretable scene descriptions. This approach achieved up to 93.4% success rates in compositional generalization tasks on VIMA-BENCH, outperforming baselines like VIMA, and improved success rates in CLIPORT tasks by up to 13 percentage points.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation systems often separate vision model learning from action policy learning, leading to sub-optimal integration and perception models not ideally suited for policy models.
- Many existing methods use opaque hidden representations for visual observations, hindering interpretability and direct optimization for policy learning.
- Generating effective action policies requires learning a broad spectrum of skills to interpret open-ended language instructions and leverage visual representations efficiently, especially for long-term tasks.

## Method

- **ACTLLM** utilizes ==Large Language Models (LLMs)== to generate structured scene descriptions (e.g., `{(object, color, coordinates)}`) as state representations, enforced by a predefined JSON schema during decoding.
- An action consistency loss is introduced to integrate visual perception with action generation, where actions are derived from an aggregated token embedding of the structured state representation.
- The Markov Decision Process (MDP) is reframed into a multi-turn visual dialogue framework, allowing the model to leverage historical context for improved long-term task execution and decision-making.

## Results

- ACTLLM demonstrated superior compositional generalization, achieving **64.0%** success on CLIPORT `put-shapes-in-bowls` (vs. PAFF's **51.0%**) and **85.8%** on `pack-unseen-objects` (vs. PAFF's **72.8%**).
- It consistently outperformed baselines across VIMA-BENCH L1, L2, and L3 tasks, reaching **90.5%** in L1, **90.9%** in L2, and **93.4%** in L3, surpassing VIMA's success rates.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of task tuning, future state prediction, and multi-turn tuning for in-context generalization, with the full ACTLLM achieving an overall **36.2%** success rate on novel VIMA L4 tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using structured, human-interpretable scene descriptions as state representations enhances model optimization through clear loss functions and provides stable ground-truth, improving transparency.
> - The action consistency loss effectively aligns visual perception with corresponding actions, ensuring that generated actions are contextually relevant and directly influenced by scene changes.
> - A multi-turn visual dialogue framework enables the model to incorporate historical context, facilitating more informed decision-making and better generalization over variable-length action sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation aims to enable robots to operate effectively in diverse and dynamic environments, executing tasks specified through flexible instructions. Language-based vision manipulation systems represent a significant area of research in this domain, leveraging rich visual information and linguistic input to interpret environmental conditions and task requirements. These systems typically comprise a vision component that grounds language concepts in visual data and a policy module that generates actions based on these visual outputs.

Recent advancements include end-to-end frameworks that integrate semantic meanings with visual information, often by learning affordances—the possible actions an agent can perform with objects in a given scene. Examples include approaches that merge linguistic context with visual cues to identify feasible actions and their locations. Conversely, other research streams, such as CALVIN, explore adaptable, task-agnostic manipulation policies that utilize general, unstructured language for task definition, promoting versatility in task execution.

A recurring challenge in existing research is the frequent separation between the learning processes of vision models and action policies. This division can lead to perception models generating visual representations that are not optimally suited for use by policy models. Furthermore, action policies face the complexity of learning a broad spectrum of skills required to interpret open-ended language instructions and effectively leverage visual representations. This necessitates more sophisticated and integrated learning approaches to enhance the alignment between vision and language concepts and optimize the combined information for action policy generation.

The advent of foundation models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), with their robust zero-shot performance, has led to their integration into robotic applications. These applications range from generating natural language scene descriptions and leveraging pretrained representations for reactive control, to grounding actions in symbolic representations for test-time adaptation and automating dense reward generation. Additionally, some methods adapt foundation model architectures to address policy generation, such as ReAct for generating conditional sequences for low-level policies or PaLM-E for integrating multimodal inputs to produce instructional texts. However, these methods often treat task specification and environment understanding as distinct problems, which can impede the seamless integration of visual concepts with their semantic meanings and create barriers to effective robot instruction. Moreover, visual observations are frequently processed into hidden representations, which can be difficult to interpret and optimize directly for policy learning.

ACTLLM contributes to this landscape by proposing a unified approach that integrates visual information interpretation with policy learning, aiming to overcome the limitations of separate learning processes and opaque hidden state representations. It seeks to bridge the gap by leveraging structured language for scene descriptions and introducing novel optimization techniques that enhance the alignment between perception and action.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel approach, ACTLLM (Action Consistency Tuned Large Language Model), for robot manipulation in dynamic environments. This approach aims to address the limitations of traditional vision-based systems and current language-based robotic systems, specifically:

- **Bridging the gap between vision models and action policies:** Existing systems often learn visual representations and action policies independently, leading to sub-optimal integration. ACTLLM aims to unify the interpretation of visual information with policy learning to produce actionable visual representations.
- **Enhancing interpretability and optimization of visual representations:** Traditional methods often process visual observations into opaque hidden representations, which are challenging for both human understanding and direct policy optimization. A key motivation is to replace these hidden representations with structured, human-interpretable scene descriptions that are also amenable to more stable and effective model optimization.
- **Improving alignment between visual perception and actions:** The research seeks to introduce a mechanism that explicitly aligns visual perception with corresponding actions, thereby enhancing the learning of visual representations that are directly relevant to generating effective actions.
- **Facilitating long-term task execution with contextual relevance:** Many robot manipulation tasks involve sequences of actions that require a comprehensive understanding of task history and evolving environmental states. The work aims to reformulate the Markov Decision Process (MDP) for manipulation into a multi-turn visual dialogue framework, enabling the model to leverage historical context for more informed decision-making over long horizons.
- **Achieving better generalization:** By addressing the above points, the authors intend for ACTLLM to demonstrate superior performance in diverse scenarios, particularly in tasks requiring compositional generalization (applying learned knowledge to new combinations of familiar elements) and in-context generalization (adapting to novel tasks or conditions not explicitly seen during training).

In summary, the core motivations stem from the need for robot manipulation systems that are more adaptable, interpretable, and capable of robustly integrating complex visual and linguistic information to execute tasks effectively in dynamic settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ACTLLM framework introduces a multi-modal approach that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to integrate structured scene descriptions with policy learning for robot manipulation.

**A. Preliminary Formulation**
The problem is defined as learning a policy model $\pi$ that generates an action $a_t$ given a multimodal instruction $I$ and the current execution history. Following imitation learning principles, the policy is designed as $\hat{a} = \pi_{\theta}(x_t, x_{t+1})$, where $x_t$ and $x_{t+1}$ are state representations extracted from observations $o_t$ and $o_{t+1}$ via an observation model $M$. During inference, a forward model $F_{\beta}(o_t, I_t)$ is used to forecast the future state $\tilde{x}_{t+1}$, allowing the policy to focus on the desired final state rather than strict adherence to ground-truth actions, as multiple action sequences can lead to the same outcome.

**B. Structured Scene Description**
A core innovation is the use of structured scene descriptions as the state representation for both the observation model ($M$) and the prediction model ($F$). This approach utilizes an LLM not only for its language generation capabilities but also for its ability to produce consistent and interpretable representations.
To ensure structured output, a predefined JSON schema is leveraged during the LLM's decoding phase. Fixed tokens of the schema are pre-filled, and the LLM is restricted to generating content tokens, thereby maintaining a consistent structure while allowing dynamic content generation.
The state representation is defined as a set of `{(object, color, coordinates)}` tuples. Object coordinates are normalized within a range and represented with two decimal places (e.g., [0.50, 0.50] for the center).
This structured representation offers several benefits:
- **Interpretability:** Facilitates understanding and validation of the model's decision-making.
- **Clarity of Loss Function:** Enables the formulation of a clear loss function directly based on the state representation, in contrast to ambiguous regression losses associated with hidden states.
- **Stable Ground-Truth:** Simulator access provides precise internal states for all objects, offering a stable ground-truth for learning.
The loss function for scene description, $L(x_t, x_{t+1}, \tilde{x}_{t+1}, \tilde{x}_t)$, adopts a contrastive loss approach inspired by GLIP. This involves:
1. Computing dot products between predicted object features and text description feature vectors to generate logits for each text token, followed by applying focal loss.
2. Using box regression and classification costs to perform bipartite matching between predictions and ground-truth data, accounting for the unordered nature of object sets.
3. Calculating loss values for matched predictions and their corresponding ground-truths, ensuring alignment of object properties and locations with language tokens.

**C. Action Consistency Loss**
To generate actions $a_t = \pi_{\theta}(x_t, x_{t+1})$ based on the state representation, the method avoids directly concatenating another language model for action prediction, as potential inaccuracies in early-stage scene descriptions could introduce noise. Instead, the policy is constructed by aggregating the sequence of token embeddings from the state representation using a learnable parameter $W$ and an attention module:
$Q = EW$
$x_{agg} = softmax(QQ^T)Q$
$\hat{a}_t = MLP(x_{agg})$
where $E$ represents the sequence of token embeddings of $x_t$ and $x_{t+1}$. This mechanism derives actions from an aggregated token that encapsulates a sentence-level representation of the state. Its lightweight design enhances adaptability, accommodating improvements in state representations over time.
The joint objective for training combines the scene description loss and an action loss:
$\min_{\theta, \beta} L(x_t, x_{t+1}, \tilde{x}_{t+1}, \tilde{x}_t) + L(a_t, \hat{a}_t)$,
subject to $\tilde{x}_t, \tilde{x}_{t+1} = F_{\beta}(o_t, I_t)$ and $\hat{a}_t = \pi_{\theta}(x_t, x_{t+1})$.
The action loss incorporates both the classification loss of the primitive skill and the scene description loss. This action consistency loss tightly integrates the action with the scene description, ensuring actions are contextually relevant and influenced by scene changes.

**D. Markov Decision Making as Visual Dialogue**
The one-step optimization is extended to a variable-length sequence of actions, framed as a multi-turn visual dialogue. In this framework, each step includes a current observation and instruction, leading to a unique state. The model selects the most suitable action based on the current state, the overall task goal, and the historical context of predicted states.
The joint loss is calculated at each time step and optimized alongside the action prediction loss across the entire trajectory. The final multi-step objective is:
$\min_{\theta, \beta} \sum_{t=1}^{T-1} [L(x_t, x_{t+1}, \tilde{x}_{t+1}, \tilde{x}_t) + L(a_t, \hat{a}_t)]$,
subject to $\tilde{x}_t, \tilde{x}_{t+1} = F_{\beta}(o_t, I_t)$ and $\hat{a}_t = \pi_{\theta}(x_t, x_{t+1})$.
This multi-turn tuning enables the model to learn and adapt over time, leveraging extended context for action generation and allowing for dynamic task switching or interventions.

**E. Implementation Details**
The model architecture consists of a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that serves as both the observation and forward model ($F$ and $M$), and a policy head ($\pi$).
- **MLLM:** Comprises a visual encoder ($\Phi_V$), a projection layer ($\Phi_P$), and a Language Model ($\Phi_L$). The visual encoder transforms images into visual tokens, which are then converted by the linear projection layer into the LLM's input space. The LLM processes these concatenated visual and query tokens to produce scene descriptions.
- **Policy Head:** Processes the scene description tokens to generate actions, consisting of an attention module and an MLP.
- **Training:** The visual encoder is kept frozen to prevent semantic loss, particularly when operating with simulated data. LLAMA3-8B is used as the LLM, instruction-tuned. An alignment layer bridges visual tokens with the static LLM, supplemented by an action projection module. AdamW is used as the optimizer. During testing, the model generates the structured scene description internally, not relying on external object detectors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ACTLLM was conducted across two benchmarks: VIMA-BENCH and CLIPORT, focusing on Compositional Generalization and In-context Generalization.

**A. Compositional Generalization**
This evaluation assesses the model's capacity to apply learned knowledge to novel combinations of familiar elements.

- **CLIPORT Environment:**
 - **Protocols:** Two protocols were used: `pack-unseen-objects` (training on packing various shapes into a brown box and testing on unseen objects) and `put-shapes-in-bowls` (training on placing different colored blocks into different colored bowls and testing on placing different shaped objects into different colored bowls).
 - **Baselines:** CLIPORT, MdetrORT, AugORT, and PAFF.
 - **Results:** ACTLLM demonstrated substantial improvements over all baselines. For `put-shapes-in-bowls`, ACTLLM achieved a success rate of 64.0%, compared to PAFF's 51.0%. In the `pack-unseen-objects` task, ACTLLM achieved 85.8% success, outperforming PAFF's 72.8%.
 - **Interpretation:** The results suggest that ACTLLM's ability to consider all objects in a scene, rather than just task-relevant ones, contributes to its superior compositional generalization. Its capacity to generate accurate scene descriptions involving various concepts (objects, containers) in compositional settings, and to learn object-aware representations without explicit bounding box requirements, were highlighted.

- **VIMA-BENCH:**
 - **Levels of Evaluation:**
 - **L1 (Placement):** Prompts are familiar during training, but object placement is randomized at testing.
 - **L2 (Combinatorial):** All textures and objects are familiar during training, but new combinations are introduced at testing.
 - **L3 (Novel Object):** Both test prompts and simulated workspaces feature textures and objects not encountered during training.
 - **L4 (In-context):** Novel tasks defined by new prompt templates, including novel actions and objects not in training data.
 - **Baselines:** Gato, Flamingo, GPT (results reported from the VIMA paper), and VIMA.
 - **Results:** ACTLLM consistently outperformed all baseline methods across L1, L2, and L3. For instance, in L1 (Placement), ACTLLM achieved an average success rate of 90.5%, surpassing VIMA's 87.2%. Similar improvements were observed in L2 (90.9% vs. VIMA's 87.0%) and L3 (93.4% vs. VIMA's 84.0%). Specific tasks within these levels also showed significant performance differences.
 - **Interpretation:** These findings indicate ACTLLM's effectiveness in learning and applying knowledge to new combinations of familiar elements and concepts.

**B. In-context Generalization**
This evaluation focused on Level 4 tasks in VIMA-BENCH, where new tasks are defined by novel prompt templates, including novel actions and objects not present in the training data. In-context demonstration videos were provided during testing to assess zero-shot adaptation.

- **Tasks:** T9 (Twist), T10 (Follow Motion), T11 (Follow Order) were identified as novel testing tasks.
- **Ablation Study:**
 - **Our Method (Full ACTLLM):** Achieved an overall success rate of 36.2% across the three novel tasks (20.1% for T9, 63.4% for T10, 10.0% for T11).
 - **`w/o task tuning`:** Performance dropped significantly to an overall 26.7% (14.5% for T9, 38.9% for T10, 6% for T11). This suggests that task tuning is critical for the model to interpret tasks from videos, given that training primarily involves multimodal prompts rather than video content.
 - **`w/o future state`:** This omission severely impacted performance, resulting in an overall 22.3% (12.4% for T9, 23.9% for T10, 4.7% for T11). The reduction, particularly noticeable in task T10, underscores the importance of the model's predictive capabilities for effective action planning.
 - **`w/o multi-turn`:** Removing multi-turn tuning also reduced effectiveness, yielding an overall 24.3% (16.1% for T9, 29.9% for T10, 3.5% for T11), though the impact was less severe than removing future state prediction. This suggests multi-turn tuning likely serves as a regularization mechanism.

These results collectively indicate that ACTLLM's structured scene descriptions, action consistency loss, and multi-turn visual dialogue framework contribute to its enhanced generalization capabilities across various robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ACTLLM represents an advancement in the field of robot manipulation, particularly in addressing the challenges associated with dynamic environments and flexible task specifications. Its significance lies in several key contributions:

- **Unified Vision and Policy Learning:** ACTLLM provides a unified framework that explicitly integrates the interpretation of visual information with policy learning. This contrasts with traditional approaches that often separate these processes, leading to more actionable visual representations that are directly optimized for policy generation.
- **Structured and Interpretable Scene Descriptors:** By harnessing language to create structured scene descriptors, ACTLLM offers a uniform interface for both spatial understanding and task performance. These descriptors are more accessible for human interpretation compared to opaque hidden vectors, which can aid in debugging and validation of robotic systems. They also provide a stable ground-truth for model optimization.
- **Novel Action Consistency Constraint:** The introduction of an action consistency constraint loss ensures that visual perception is aligned with corresponding actions. This mechanism enhances the learning of visual representations that are directly relevant to executing tasks, improving the reliability and accuracy of robot actions.
- **Multi-turn Visual Dialogue Framework:** Reformulating the Markov Decision Process (MDP) into a multi-turn visual dialogue framework allows for modeling long-term task execution with enhanced contextual relevance derived from the history of task execution. This enables more sophisticated decision-making, task switching, and adaptation to interventions.
- **Demonstrated Generalization Capabilities:** The experimental results indicate superior performance in compositional generalization, where the model applies learned knowledge to new combinations of familiar elements, and in-context generalization, where it adapts to novel tasks not explicitly seen during training. This suggests a higher level of adaptability and robustness compared to baseline methods.

The potential impact of ACTLLM is multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to intuitively understand and execute human language commands through structured scene descriptions, ACTLLM could lead to more natural and efficient human-robot collaboration in various settings, from industrial automation to service robotics.
- **Broader Deployment of Intelligent Robotic Systems:** The improved adaptability and generalization capabilities could facilitate the deployment of robotic systems in more complex and less structured real-world environments, where traditional systems often struggle.
- **Advancements in Foundation Models for Robotics:** ACTLLM demonstrates an effective method for enhancing LLM fine-tuning for manipulation control, leveraging contextual data to refine decision-making processes. This could inspire further research into integrating large language models with robotic perception and control.
- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations:** The approach addresses a fundamental limitation in vision-based systems regarding their struggle to learn visual representations that excel in both task execution and spatial reasoning, thereby paving the way for more capable autonomous agents.

While the paper acknowledges the limitation that 2D images may not fully capture the precise 3D locations and desired states essential for effective robot control, the advancements presented by ACTLLM lay a foundation for more robust, interpretable, and adaptable robot manipulation systems. Future research exploring real-world applications and refining in-context learning capabilities could further broaden its impact.
