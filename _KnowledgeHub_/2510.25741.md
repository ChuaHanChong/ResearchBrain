# Scaling Latent Reasoning via Looped Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Ouro, a family of Looped Language Models (LoopLMs), embeds iterative computation directly into the pre-training process through parameter reuse, leading to enhanced parameter efficiency and reasoning abilities. These models achieve the performance of much larger non-looped Transformers while demonstrating improved safety and a more causally faithful internal reasoning process.

## Problem

- Large Language Model scaling strategies primarily focused on increasing model size and data, leading to high deployment costs, latency, and diminishing returns from data scarcity.
- Existing inference-time reasoning techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) expand context length and often reflect post-hoc rationalization rather than intrinsic, efficient internal reasoning.
- A need for LLM architectures capable of adaptively allocating computational depth based on input complexity to optimize resource usage.

## Method

- Developed ==Ouro==, a family of ==LoopLMs== that recursively apply a ==shared block of Transformer layers== to integrate iterative computation into the model's latent space during pre-training.
- Implemented a two-stage training scheme with an ==entropy-regularized objective== and focused ==adaptive gate training==, enabling the model to learn adaptive computation and dynamic early exiting.
- Trained Ouro models (up to 2.6B parameters) on an extensive 7.7 trillion token dataset, demonstrating the efficacy of the LoopLM architecture at a frontier-level scale.

## Results

- Ouro models achieved significant parameter efficiency gains, with Ouro **1.4B** matching or exceeding **4B** parameter dense models, and Ouro **2.6B** outperforming **8B** parameter dense models on reasoning benchmarks like BBH, GSM8K, and MATH500.
- The adaptive computation mechanisms, particularly the two-stage gate training, enabled efficient and dynamic compute allocation, consistently yielding optimal accuracy across various computational budgets.
- LoopLMs showed improved safety, with harmfulness decreasing as recurrent steps increased, and demonstrated more causally faithful reasoning through observable evolution of intermediate latent state predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Loop depth serves as a distinct and powerful third scaling axis for LLMs, complementing model size and data volume to unlock enhanced capabilities.
> - LoopLMs improve model capabilities primarily by enhancing *knowledge manipulation* (reasoning, application of rules) rather than increasing raw *knowledge storage* capacity.
> - Adaptive computation, facilitated by learned gating mechanisms, allows LoopLMs to dynamically adjust their computational effort, optimizing performance-cost trade-offs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has primarily been driven by scaling up model size, training data, and computational resources. While effective, this "larger is better" paradigm faces increasing challenges related to deployment costs, latency, energy consumption, and the diminishing returns of data scarcity. In response, research has intensified on improving *parameter efficiency* – achieving more capability within a fixed parameter budget.

This paper positions itself within this crucial context by proposing architectural innovation as a third pathway to efficiency, distinct from merely expanding data corpora or relying on inference-time techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. The authors argue that modern LLMs primarily "think" through explicit text generation (like CoT), which defers reasoning to a post-training phase and under-leverages pre-training data for internal reasoning development.

The introduction of **Looped Language Models (LoopLM)**, particularly the Ouro family, is a direct response to these limitations. The core idea of reusing shared parameters iteratively is not entirely new; it builds upon prior concepts like the Universal Transformer (Dehghani et al., 2018), recursive Transformers, and earlier latent reasoning approaches. However, previous work in this area has largely been at modest scales, leaving the question of whether LoopLMs translate into "frontier-level gains" at practically meaningful scales largely unanswered.

Ouro's contribution to this landscape is demonstrating that LoopLMs *do* exhibit more favorable scaling behavior when trained on multi-trillion token datasets, typical of state-of-the-art foundation models. This work distinguishes itself by:
- **Deepening the internal computational graph:** Unlike CoT, which extends the output sequence and leads to context-length bloat, LoopLMs perform iterative computation within their latent space.
- **Adaptive computation:** Introducing mechanisms for models to dynamically allocate computational depth based on input complexity, rather than fixed-depth execution or simply generating more tokens.
- **Focus on parameter efficiency at scale:** By pre-training models up to 2.6B parameters on 7.7 trillion tokens, the authors show that LoopLMs can match or exceed the performance of much larger (4B to 12B) standard Transformers, yielding significant efficiency gains.
- **Mechanistic understanding:** Probing *why* LoopLMs are superior, showing it's about enhanced *knowledge manipulation* rather than increased *knowledge storage*.

By successfully demonstrating these capabilities at a scale comparable to leading foundation models, the paper firmly establishes **loop depth as a critical third scaling axis**, alongside model size and data quantity. This shifts the focus from merely "scaling up" to "scaling smarter," which is vital for the future accessibility and deployment of powerful AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and validate a novel LLM architecture, the Looped Language Model (LoopLM), that significantly enhances parameter efficiency and reasoning capabilities by embedding iterative computation directly into the pre-training process. The authors aim to achieve superior performance within a fixed parameter budget, making LLMs more accessible and cost-effective.

The primary motivations driving this work are:

1. **Addressing Parameter Inefficiency:** Traditional LLM scaling, while powerful, leads to models with hundreds of billions of parameters that are expensive, slow, and resource-intensive to deploy. This limits their accessibility and practical application, especially in resource-constrained environments. The authors seek architectures that achieve better capabilities with fewer trainable parameters.
2. **Overcoming Data Scarcity Limitations:** While expanding the training corpus is one way to improve efficiency, the increasing scarcity of high-quality data is becoming a bottleneck. Architectural innovations offer an alternative path to enhancing model capabilities without solely relying on ever-larger datasets.
3. **Improving Reasoning Beyond Explicit CoT:** Current explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning methods, while effective, occur at inference time by extending the output sequence. This leads to longer context lengths, increased latency, and often results in post-hoc rationalization rather than causally faithful internal reasoning. The authors aim to integrate reasoning directly into the model's pre-training via internal latent computation, making it more intrinsic and efficient.
4. **Enabling Adaptive Computation:** Not all inputs require the same amount of computational effort. A key motivation is to design models that can adaptively allocate compute – spending less on simple problems and more on complex ones – thereby optimizing resource usage without sacrificing peak performance.
5. **Unlocking a New Scaling Dimension:** The authors hypothesize that iterative computation, or "loop depth," can serve as a distinct and powerful scaling axis, analogous to model size and data volume, to unlock enhanced capabilities.

To validate these motivations, the research addresses two key questions:
- **Does LoopLM exhibit more favorable scaling behavior** (in capabilities, efficiency, and safety) compared to non-recursive transformer models at frontier-level training scales?
- **What are the underlying mechanisms** of LoopLM's improvements? Specifically, does parameter reuse primarily increase knowledge storage or enhance knowledge manipulation? Are these gains monotonic with the number of loops, and what factors influence this?

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for developing and evaluating the Ouro LoopLMs is comprehensive, encompassing architectural design, a multi-stage training pipeline, novel adaptive computation objectives, and a deep mechanistic analysis.

#### 3.1. LoopLM Architecture (Ouro)

The Ouro models are based on a standard decoder-only Transformer architecture, distinguishing themselves by recursively applying a shared stack of `N` layers for `t` recurrent steps.
- **Core Components:** Each Transformer block includes Multi-Head Attention (MHA) with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) and a Feed-Forward Network (FFN) with SwiGLU activation. Sandwich normalization is used for enhanced training stability, which is critical for deep recurrent computation.
- **Parameter-Efficiency via Reuse:** Instead of stacking `L` unique layers, a LoopLM reuses a block of `N` layers `t` times, resulting in `N` unique parameters but `N*t` computational depth. The Ouro 1.4B model has 24 layers and 4 recurrent steps, while the Ouro 2.6B model has 48 layers (achieved by layer duplication/upcycling) and 4 recurrent steps.
- **Vocabulary:** Both models use a 49,152-token vocabulary optimized for code and Latin-alphabet languages.

#### 3.2. Learning Adaptive Latent Reasoning with LoopLM

To enable dynamic computation, the authors introduce a novel gating mechanism and a two-stage training scheme:
- **Gating Mechanism:** An "exit gate" runs in parallel with the LM head at each recurrent step `t`, outputting an instantaneous exit probability `λ_t(x)`. These probabilities form a discrete distribution `p_φ(t|x)` over exit steps.
- **Inference with Early Exit:** During inference, the model uses a Q-exit criterion, terminating when the cumulative exit probability `CDF(m|x)` exceeds a threshold `q`. This `q` acts as a deployment-time knob to balance compute and accuracy.
- **Stage I: Entropy-Regularized Objective (Pre-training):** The gate parameters are learned jointly with the LM by optimizing a combined loss: `Expected Task Loss - β * H(p_φ(·|x))`. The expected task loss weights the standard cross-entropy loss `L^(t)` at each step `t` by `p_φ(t|x)`. The crucial `entropy regularization` term `H(p_φ(·|x))` prevents the gate from collapsing to always exiting at the maximum depth `T_max`, ensuring an unbiased exploration of depths. This is framed as an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective with a uniform prior over exit steps.
- **Stage II: Focused Adaptive Gate Training:** After pre-training, the LM parameters are frozen, and only the exit gate parameters `φ` are fine-tuned. This stage uses a specialized adaptive loss that directly teaches the gate to make termination decisions based on *realized performance gains*. It quantifies loss improvement `I^(t)_i` from step `t-1` to `t` and trains the gate to predict a continuation probability `w^(t)_i` that aligns with these improvements. This penalizes both "underthinking" (exiting too early) and "overthinking" (continuing unnecessarily).

#### 3.3. Training Looped Language Models (7.7T Tokens)

The training pipeline for the Ouro family is extensive, involving multiple stages and a vast dataset:
- **Data Corpus:** Total of 7.7T tokens from open-source datasets.
 - **Stage 1 (Pre-training I & II):** Primarily Web CommonCrawl data (Nemotron-CC, MAP-CC, Ultra-FineWeb-zh), augmented with Code (OpenCoder) and Math (MegaMath). 6T tokens. Recurrent steps were reduced from 8 to 4 in Stage 1b for stability.
 - **Stage 2 (Continual Training Annealing):** Higher-quality data, extended sequence length (16K tokens), and more specialized math/code data (Nemotron-CC-Math-v1, OpenCoder-Annealing). 1.4T tokens.
 - **Stage 3 (Long Context Training - LongCT):** Focus on long-context capabilities using ProLong-64K (64K length). 20B tokens.
 - **Stage 4 (Mid-training):** Diverse, high-quality SFT-style data (Question, Answer / Question, CoT, Answer) to develop advanced abilities. 300B effective tokens.
- **Training Stability & Adaptive Configuration:** Used `flame` framework on `torchtitan`. Key adjustments for stability included recurrent step reduction (8 to 4), progressive batch size scaling (4M to 8M tokens), and strategic reduction of the `β` coefficient for the KL divergence term. Conservative AdamW optimizer settings and learning rates were adopted.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The base Ouro models undergo SFT on 8.3M examples from high-quality public datasets (OpenThoughts3, AceReason-1.1-SFT, OpenCodeReasoning, OO1-Chat-747K, DeepWriting-20K) to create "Ouro-Thinking" variants, emphasizing math, code, and scientific reasoning.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Attempts:** Exploratory RLVR (DAPO, GRPO) attempts were made but were unsuccessful due to technical challenges related to LoopLM's dynamic early-exit mechanism conflicting with fixed execution paths in vLLM.

#### 3.4. Mechanistic Understanding via Controlled Experiments

To understand the source of LoopLM's superior performance, controlled synthetic experiments were conducted:
- **Knowledge Capacity (Capo Task):** Used synthetic biographies (from "Physics of Language Models") to measure the model's ability to memorize factual knowledge (bits per parameter).
- **Knowledge Manipulation (Mano & Multi-hop QA Tasks):**
 - **Mano Task:** Modular arithmetic problems with complex tree structures to assess the model's ability to apply arithmetic rules and parse structures without explicit intermediate thoughts.
 - **Multi-hop QA:** Natural language questions requiring composition of individual facts from a synthetic knowledge graph to assess multi-hop reasoning and sample efficiency.

#### 3.5. Safety, Faithfulness, and Consistency Analysis

- **Safety:** Evaluated using the HEx-PHI dataset (harmfulness scores via GPT-4o judge) and PCA on hidden representations to visualize the separation of benign and harmful prompts.
- **Faithfulness:** Explored on the Quora Question Pairs dataset. Linear probes on intermediate hidden states were used to predict step-wise answers, and an agreement matrix tracked consistency across recurrent steps. This aimed to differentiate genuine iterative refinement from post-hoc rationalization often seen in CoT.
- **Consistency-derived deployment advantages:** Discussed how LoopLM's structure naturally enables built-in speculative decoding, joint acceleration with pre-emptive safety checks, and anytime generation with monotone refinement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, showcasing the effectiveness of Looped Language Models in enhancing parameter efficiency, reasoning, and safety:

1. **Exceptional Parameter Efficiency at Scale:**
 - **Ouro 1.4B (R4):** Achieves performance comparable to or exceeding 4B parameter standard transformers (e.g., Qwen3-Base) across a wide range of benchmarks. Notably outperforms on challenging reasoning tasks like BBH (71.02 vs 70.95), GSM8K (78.92 vs 72.86), and MATH500 (82.40 vs 59.60).
 - **Ouro 2.6B (R4):** Outperforms dense models up to 8B parameters on reasoning-intensive benchmarks, scoring higher on MMLU-Pro (55.73 vs 53.72 for Qwen3-8B), BBH (80.46 vs 77.65), and MATH500 (90.85 vs 62.30).
 - This demonstrates a **2-3x parameter-efficiency gain**, which is critical for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

2. **Effective Adaptive Computation:**
 - The **entropy-regularized objective with a uniform prior** successfully guides the model to learn adaptive depth allocation during pre-training, preventing collapse to maximum depth and fostering exploration.
 - The **focused adaptive gate training (Stage II)**, which explicitly teaches the gate to base decisions on realized task loss improvements, further enhances efficiency. It achieves the best accuracy at every computational budget compared to standard pre-training or heuristic methods.

3. **Mechanistic Understanding: Knowledge Manipulation, Not Capacity:**
 - **Knowledge Capacity:** Controlled experiments on the Capo task reveal that LoopLMs *do not* increase raw knowledge storage capacity (maintaining ~2 bits per parameter, similar to non-looped models of the same size).
 - **Knowledge Manipulation:** LoopLMs *dramatically enhance knowledge manipulation capabilities*. On synthetic tasks like Mano (modular arithmetic) and multi-hop QA, looped models consistently outperform iso-parameter and even iso-FLOP non-looped counterparts. They learn these complex tasks faster and with fewer unique training samples.
 - **Theoretical Support:** A theoretical analysis shows LoopLM can solve graph reachability problems in O(log D) steps, highlighting its efficient parallel latent reasoning ability.
 - **Real-world Validation:** MMLU sub-category analysis corroborates this, showing the largest gains in reasoning-heavy tasks (e.g., Elementary Mathematics: +155.6%, Formal Logic: +143.3%) rather than knowledge-heavy, retrieval-based subjects (e.g., Moral Scenarios: +7.8%, Global Facts: +8.3%).

4. **Performance by Recurrent Depth and Extrapolation:**
 - For both base and SFT models, performance generally improves up to the trained depth (T=4).
 - Performance degrades moderately when extrapolating to unseen depths (T=5-8) for general benchmarks, but the model still demonstrates a capacity to generalize beyond its trained depth.
 - For SFT "Ouro-Thinking" models on complex reasoning tasks, performance at T=1 is very low, underscoring the necessity of iterative refinement. Peak performance often occurs at or near the trained depth.

5. **Efficient KV Cache Sharing:**
 - During the decoding phase, KV cache reuse strategies (specifically, last-step or averaged reuse) successfully reduce memory requirements by 4x with minimal performance loss (e.g., within 0.3 points on GSM8K). This makes LoopLMs practical for real-world deployment by mitigating their memory overhead.

6. **Improved Safety and Faithfulness:**
 - **Safety:** LoopLM significantly reduces harmfulness on the HEx-PHI dataset. Crucially, safety *improves* as the number of recurrent steps increases, even into extrapolated depths (T>4). PCA analysis shows that increased recurrent steps help the model better distinguish benign from harmful prompts.
 - **Faithfulness:** On ambiguous tasks like Quora Question Pairs, intermediate predictions from LoopLM's latent states genuinely evolve and change as recurrence deepens. This "systematic disagreement across steps" (where intermediate predictions are not frozen rationalizations of the final output) indicates a more *causally faithful* reasoning process compared to the post-hoc rationalization often observed in standard CoT.

7. **Scaling Laws for LoopLMs (Appendix):**
 - Small-scale experiments demonstrate that LoopLMs exhibit predictable and generalizable scaling laws for both total loss and step-wise loss, correlating strongly with model size, training data, and recurrent steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Ouro and the comprehensive analysis of Looped Language Models mark a significant advancement in the field of LLM research, with far-reaching implications and potential impact:

1. **A Novel Scaling Axis for LLMs:** By demonstrating that iterative latent computation (loop depth) is a distinct and powerful scaling axis, comparable to model size and data volume, this work opens up a new paradigm for designing and training more capable LLMs. This shifts the focus from purely "bigger is better" to "smarter and more efficient scaling."

2. **Enhanced Resource Efficiency for Deployment:** The demonstrated 2-3x parameter efficiency means that models can achieve comparable or superior performance with significantly fewer parameters. This directly translates to:
 - **Reduced computational cost:** Lower training and inference costs.
 - **Lower memory footprint:** Enables deployment on edge devices, mobile platforms, or within constrained data center environments, expanding accessibility.
 - **Reduced latency:** Faster inference times for real-time applications.
 This is especially critical as the LLM landscape moves towards broader industrial adoption and resource constraints become a bottleneck.

3. **Fundamental Improvement in Reasoning Capabilities:** By integrating iterative reasoning directly into the pre-training phase and the model's latent space, LoopLMs provide a more intrinsic and adaptive form of reasoning. This addresses key limitations of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, such as context-length bloat and the tendency for post-hoc rationalization. The findings on knowledge manipulation versus storage are a critical contribution to the mechanistic understanding of LLMs.

4. **Increased Safety and Trustworthiness:** The observation that LoopLMs' safety alignment *improves* with increased recurrent steps, even beyond the trained depth, is a highly significant finding. Furthermore, the evidence for causally faithful reasoning (where intermediate latent states genuinely influence the final decision) offers a promising path towards more interpretable and trustworthy AI systems, mitigating concerns about LLMs "thinking" without genuine internal process.

5. **Practical Deployment Advantages:** Innovations like the adaptive computation mechanism with a two-stage gate training allow for dynamic compute allocation, optimizing performance-cost trade-offs at inference time. The effective KV cache sharing strategies further ensure that the memory overhead of recurrent architectures is manageable for practical deployment. The conceptualization of LoopLM as a "built-in draft model" for speculative decoding and pre-emptive safety checks highlights its architectural primitives for robust, efficient, and safe generation.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** This work establishes a robust foundation for future exploration of recurrent architectures. It encourages research into:
 - Optimizing extrapolation to even greater recurrent depths.
 - Developing more sophisticated recurrent mechanisms and parameter-sharing strategies.
 - Further understanding the inductive biases and theoretical underpinnings of looped models.
 - Addressing the challenges of RL alignment with dynamic computation graphs.

By open-sourcing the Ouro model family, the authors facilitate broader research and adoption, accelerating innovation in parameter-efficient and reasoning-capable LLMs. This paper is a landmark contribution to the quest for more intelligent, efficient, and responsible AI.
