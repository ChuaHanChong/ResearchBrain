# VICoT-Agent: A Vision-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought Framework for Interpretable Multimodal Reasoning and Scalable Remote Sensing Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> The VICoT-Agent framework introduces a Vision-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approach for interpretable multimodal reasoning in remote sensing, embedding visual tool outputs directly into the reasoning process. This framework achieves superior performance over existing methods in remote sensing report quality and trajectory accuracy, while significantly reducing token consumption by 65% and latency by 48% through its stack-based architecture, and enables deployment on edge devices via distillation to lightweight models.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with fine-grained, multi-scale, and multi-round analysis required for complex remote sensing tasks, particularly with Ultra-High-Resolution (UHR) imagery.
- Traditional "plan-execute" agent frameworks suffer from high token consumption, latency, and a lack of interpretability due to their separation of planning and execution, making them inefficient for numerous visual interactions.
- Integrating diverse visual-language tools into a unified agent framework is challenging due to inconsistent formats and limited generalization capabilities for smaller models, hindering scalable deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.

## Method

- A Vision-Interleaved ==Chain-of-Thought== (VICoT) framework dynamically incorporates visual tool outputs into an explicit, multi-round reasoning stack, enhancing transparency and efficiency.
- The Model Context Protocol (MCP) standardizes tool invocation in XML format, enabling modular and flexible integration of 10 domain-specific remote sensing tools (e.g., object detection, super-resolution, cloud removal).
- Reasoning Stack Distillation, utilizing the new VICoT-HRSC dataset, fine-tunes lightweight models (like Qwen3-14B) from powerful teacher models (GPT-4o) to enable efficient edge deployment, complemented by Region-Aware Captioned Prompting for UHR image understanding.

## Results

- VICoT (GPT-4o teacher model) significantly outperformed existing frameworks in trajectory quality, achieving up to +**34.1%** accuracy and +**30.1%** BLEU score compared to baselines.
- The distilled VICoT (Qwen3-14B) model demonstrated excellent transferability, with less than a **5%** accuracy drop compared to its GPT-4o teacher, and even showed a +**3.2%** BLEU gain, validating the distillation method.
- VICoT consistently surpassed state-of-the-art remote sensing agents across various resolution datasets, outperforming the GPT-4o baseline by +**16.91%** on RSVQA-HR and maintaining robustness on UHR imagery where the baseline struggled (over **40%** accuracy drop).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly embedding multi-step execution within a Chain-of-Thought via a stack-based structure significantly enhances reasoning interpretability and reduces computational overhead (65% less tokens, 48% less latency).
> - A standardized Model Context Protocol (MCP) provides a versatile, model-size agnostic approach to integrate and manage diverse visual and text tools, overcoming limitations of traditional function calling.
> - Complex agent behaviors can be effectively distilled from large foundation models to lightweight models using a dedicated multimodal, multi-turn reasoning dataset, enabling robust performance on edge devices without compromising accuracy significantly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of remote sensing image analysis is rapidly evolving, moving beyond traditional object recognition to more sophisticated tasks like object interpretation, geo-localization, and intelligence extraction. This shift necessitates models capable of complex intelligence reasoning and flexible tool invocation.

**Current Challenges Addressed by VICoT-Agent:**

- **Limitations of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** While remote sensing-specific MLLMs have emerged, they often struggle with the fine-grained, multi-scale analysis, post-processing, and multi-round reasoning inherent in many remote sensing tasks. Solely relying on the massive parameter sizes of MLLMs for complex workflows is insufficient and impractical for deployment in resource-constrained environments like edge devices or satellites.
- **Inefficiencies of Existing Agent Frameworks:**
 - **Predefined Workflows:** Many current agent frameworks are limited to predefined workflows or single-turn interactions, functioning mainly as executors that blend outputs into LLM prompts for summarization, often leading to suboptimal results.
 - **"Plan-Execute" Paradigms:** More advanced agents incorporate planning and memory modules, engaging in multi-round interactions. However, these "plan-execute" or "plan-replan" strategies consume massive tokens and storage, resulting in significant redundancy. While suitable for short, low-interaction tasks, they falter in remote sensing, which often demands numerous visual interaction rounds and aims for edge deployment. This "split between brain and limbs" design also deviates from simulating human problem-solving, where reasoning and action are often intertwined.
 - **Lack of Interpretability and Transparency:** Approaches that separate planning and execution often sacrifice the interpretability of the reasoning chain, making it difficult to trace how decisions are made.
- **Tool Integration Difficulties:** Integrating diverse visual-language tools into a unified agent framework faces challenges. Traditional function calling (FC) methods can be complex, inconsistent across tools, and heavily dependent on pre-training data quality, especially for smaller models.
- **Ultra-High-Resolution (UHR) Imagery:** Remote sensing frequently deals with UHR imagery, which poses significant challenges for models due to its vast scale and fine-grained details.
- **Scalability and Edge Deployment:** The immense computational requirements of large LLMs hinder their deployment on edge devices, which are crucial for real-time remote sensing applications.

VICoT-Agent addresses these gaps by proposing a novel framework that directly embeds multi-step execution into the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process. It aims to create an interpretable, efficient, and scalable multimodal agent that can dynamically incorporate visual tools within a continuous reasoning flow, specifically tailored for the complexities of remote sensing analysis. This positions VICoT-Agent as a significant step forward in developing autonomous and intelligent systems for Earth observation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel multimodal agent framework, **Vision-Interleaved Chain-of-Thought (VICoT)**, designed to overcome the limitations of existing approaches in complex remote sensing image analysis. The motivations and specific objectives are:

1. **Enhance Interpretable Multimodal Reasoning:** To enable explicit, multi-round vision-language interleaved reasoning by dynamically incorporating visual tool outputs directly into the chain of thought. This aims to foster greater transparency and interpretability in the agent's decision-making process, mirroring human cognitive workflows.
2. **Achieve Scalable and Flexible Remote Sensing Analysis:** To provide a robust framework that efficiently performs complex multi-round tasks with strong generalization, even in challenging remote sensing scenarios. This includes adapting to varied image resolutions and environmental conditions.
3. **Optimize Efficiency and Reduce Resource Consumption:** To address the high token consumption and latency issues of traditional "plan-execute" agent architectures by unifying reasoning and tool invocation within a stack-based structure, making it more practical for real-world deployment.
4. **Facilitate Modular and Plug-and-Play Tool Integration:** To develop a standardized protocol for integrating diverse visual-language tools, ensuring modularity, easy adaptability to different task objectives, and isolation of tool environments from the agent's runtime.
5. **Enable Edge Deployment for Lightweight Models:** To propose a method for distilling complex agent behaviors from powerful teacher models (e.g., GPT-4o) to smaller, lightweight models, thereby reducing computational complexity and enabling deployment on resource-constrained edge devices (e.g., satellites, UAVs).
6. **Improve UHR Image Understanding:** To introduce specialized mechanisms for effectively processing and reasoning over ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing imagery, which is common in Earth observation.

Ultimately, the motivation is to build an intelligent agent that can autonomously conduct sophisticated, transparent, and efficient intelligence reasoning from remote sensing data, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and practical for operational use.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VICoT framework is a multimodal agent architecture that integrates reasoning and tool invocation through a dynamic, vision-interleaved chain-of-thought process. It comprises several key methodological innovations:

#### 3.1. Overview Framework

VICoT operates with two plug-and-play modules:
- **Think Module:** Built upon a Large Language Model (LLM), this module performs contextual reasoning, deciding whether to invoke a tool at each turn. It is enhanced through prompt engineering or instruction tuning to follow commands.
- **Vision Module (VLM):** Constrained to vision-to-text models, it acts as a bridge, converting visual inputs (raw images, cropped regions, enhanced outputs) into descriptive text understandable by the LLM.
These modules interact with a **Reasoning Stack** (memory) and an **MCP Tool Set** (tool component) to enable multi-round, multimodal reasoning with high transparency.

#### 3.2. Stack-based Reasoning Modeling

- **Motivation for a Stack:** Unlike traditional agents that re-feed entire histories, causing exponential token increase and interpretability loss, VICoT uses a stack to manage the reasoning trajectory. This structure compresses context, preserves causality, and exposes intermediate evidence, similar to how human analysts work.
- **Formal Definition:** At each round `t`, the agent maintains a stack `St = [s1..., st]`, where each stack frame `st = (φt, mt, et)` represents the LLM's decision (φt), the tool matching process (mt), and the returned evidence (et). The LLM's decisions `φt` for `t ≥ 2` are based on the previous stack state `St-1`, ensuring a continuous and automatic process of matching the historical reasoning stack with the tool set.
- **Complexity Analysis:** VICoT significantly reduces computational complexity compared to "Plan–Replan Loop" strategies. It changes context complexity from quadratic `O(T^2)` to linear `O(k)` (where `k` is a sliding window of frames), and eliminates full tool scans. This results in a 65% reduction in token consumption and a 48% reduction in latency.
- **Stack-Based State Search:** When multiple tools are plausible, the system duplicates the top stack frame to spawn parallel stacks, evaluating them with a lightweight heuristic from the LLM to retain correct paths.

#### 3.3. MCP Compatible Tool Suite

- **Motivation for MCP:** Traditional function calling (FC) methods often suffer from inconsistent formats, limited tool support (due to reliance on pre-training), and poor generalization for smaller models. VICoT addresses this by discarding varied FC formats in favor of standardizing tool parameters in XML format from the LLM.
- **Model Context Protocol (MCP):** VICoT encapsulates tools using the MCP protocol, allowing a single XML output to match and retrieve from the entire encapsulated toolset. Tools are categorized into Vision Tools and Text Tools for coarse-to-fine selection. This design is versatile, model-size agnostic, and facilitates remote calls and tool updates due to environment isolation.
- **RS Tool Set:** VICoT integrates 10 domain-specific tools, including:
 - **Vision Tools:** Open-Vocabulary Object Detector (fine-tuned for RS), Crop, Super-Resolution, Binarization, Cloud Removal, Rain Removal, Denoising, and Motion Deblurring.
 - **Text Tools:** Search and RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) for intelligence information.
Each tool call is a compact XML block with standardized tags, ensuring consistent invocation.

#### 3.4. Reasoning Stack Distillation

- **Motivation:** Smaller LLMs (e.g., 7B or 14B) often make logical errors in multi-turn reasoning and tool invocation, even with basic multimodal capabilities.
- **VICoT-HRSC Dataset:** To address this, the authors created VICoT-HRSC, a multimodal, multi-turn reasoning dataset. It comprises 364 representative HRSC images, each paired with a comprehensive "Reasoning Stack" generated by a prompt-driven GPT-4o within the VICoT framework. Each stack includes `<think>` reasoning, MCP-formatted `<tool>` invocations, tool return results (including intermediate image paths and VLM interpretations), and a final SOAP-style intelligence summary. This dataset, with ~4,400 interaction trajectories, supports full reconstruction and replay of the reasoning process.
- **Fine-tuning:** Qwen3-14B was fine-tuned on VICoT-HRSC. The distilled model, VICoT-qwen, is significantly smaller (12 GB with AWQ 4-bit quantization), enabling deployment on 16 GB VRAM devices with reduced inference latency (10-15 seconds per UHR image) and improved BLEU scores.

#### 3.5. Region-Aware Captioned Prompting

- **Purpose:** To effectively understand ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing images.
- **Mechanism:** UHR images are hierarchically decomposed into fixed-size tiles. Instruction-relevant regions within each tile are identified using GroundingDINO. Tiles without detected objects are discarded. For valid tiles, a complete VICoT reasoning cycle is invoked, generating a region-level language summary. These descriptions, annotated with spatial tags, are then concatenated to form a unified contextual narrative. Finally, this aggregated regional information is fed into a dedicated UHR Information Integration Prompt, guiding the LLM to synthesize high-resolution regional insights into a coherent, context-aware final response.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on multiple remote sensing benchmarks demonstrate VICoT-Agent's superior performance in reasoning transparency, execution efficiency, and generation quality.

#### 4.1. Trajectory Quality Evaluation

- **Datasets:** Evaluated on the self-built VICoT-HRSC validation set.
- **Metrics:** Accuracy (tool invocation correctness), GPT-4.1 Rating (rationality, completeness, coherence), Human Expert Assessment (rationality, completeness), and BLEU score (output quality and formatting).
- **Baselines:** VisionGPT, ViperGPT, HuggingGPT, VisualSketchpad, and a baseline GPT-4o without the VICoT framework.
- **Results:**
 - **VICoT (4o) Teacher Model:** Significantly outperformed all existing frameworks, with accuracy gains up to +34.1% over VisionGPT and +24.6% over HuggingGPT. Compared to the GPT-4o baseline, VICoT (4o) showed remarkable improvements: +34.1% accuracy, +29.8% GPT-4.1 score, +45.2% human score, and +30.1% BLEU. This highlights that the performance gains are due to the VICoT framework design, not solely the underlying LLM's capabilities.
 - **VICoT (Distilled Qwen3-14B) Model:** Achieved excellent transferability, with less than a 5% drop in accuracy compared to the GPT-4o teacher. Notably, it even showed a +3.2% BLEU gain, indicating better formatted output generation. This validates the effectiveness of the Reasoning Stack Distillation method for lightweight models.

#### 4.2. Remote Sensing Report Quality Evaluation

- **Datasets:** RSVQA-LR, RSVQA-HR (low- and high-resolution VQA), MME-RealWorld-RS, and LRS-VQA-FAIR (ultra-high-resolution imagery). These datasets cover rural/urban classification, presence detection, regional comparison, and fine-grained VQA.
- **Baselines:** A range of well-known multimodal LLMs and domain-specific remote sensing agents (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl2, MiniGPTv2, Qwen-VL-Chat, Qwen2.5-VL, RSGPT, GeoChat, SkyEyeGPT, LHRS-Bot, RS-Agent, LRS-VQA, EarthDial, and a GPT-4o baseline).
- **Results:**
 - **VICoT (4o) Teacher Model:** Consistently surpassed multiple domain-specific remote sensing agents across low-, high-, and ultra-high-resolution datasets. It outperformed the GPT-4o baseline by +4.85% on RSVQA-LR, +16.91% on RSVQA-HR, +3.76% on MME-RW-RS, and +2.41% on LRS-FAIR in average accuracy. The GPT-4o baseline showed a significant accuracy drop (over 13% from LR to HR, and exceeding 40% on UHR datasets), demonstrating its weakness in fine-grained RS tasks without specialized tools, whereas VICoT maintained enhanced robustness.
 - **VICoT (Distilled Qwen3-14B) Model:** Showed only a small accuracy drop on RSVQA-LR (–2.67%) and RSVQA-HR (–3.21%) compared to VICoT (4o). Impressively, it achieved notable gains of +14.72% on MME-RW-RS and +2.91% on LRS-FAIR, indicating that the fine-tuning on VICoT-HRSC (with UHR support) coupled with Qwen2.5-VL's inherent UHR capabilities resulted in strong performance, nearing SOTA accuracy even with a lightweight model.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Gains

- Compared to the Plan-Execute architecture, VICoT reduced token consumption by 65% and latency by 48% while maintaining similar accuracy. This confirms the efficiency benefits of the stack-based reasoning.

In summary, the results firmly establish VICoT-Agent as a highly effective and efficient framework for interpretable multimodal reasoning in remote sensing, capable of outperforming existing SOTA methods across diverse datasets and resolutions, and remarkably scalable through its distillation strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VICoT-Agent framework represents a significant advancement in the field of interpretable multimodal reasoning, particularly for complex remote sensing analysis. Its contributions and potential impacts are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Interpretability and Transparency:** By embedding visual tool outputs directly into a dynamic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and maintaining an explicit Reasoning Stack, VICoT-Agent offers unprecedented transparency. Each decision and action taken by the agent is grounded in specific visual regions and tool outputs, making the reasoning process easily traceable and understandable. This interpretability is crucial for high-stakes applications like intelligence analysis, disaster response, and environmental monitoring, where understanding *why* a decision was made is as important as the decision itself.
- **Improved Reasoning Efficiency and Scalability:** The stack-based reasoning mechanism and the reduction in token consumption (65% less) and latency (48% lower) compared to traditional "Plan-Execute" architectures make VICoT significantly more efficient. This addresses a critical bottleneck in deploying complex AI agents, enabling faster analysis workflows.
- **Robust and Flexible Tool Integration:** The adoption of the Model Context Protocol (MCP) for tool integration provides a modular, plug-and-play architecture. This standardized approach allows for seamless incorporation of diverse visual and language tools without reliance on specific model capabilities or extensive pre-training. This flexibility ensures that VICoT can easily adapt to new tasks, sensors, and environmental conditions by simply adding or updating tools in its suite, making it a future-proof solution.
- **Enabling Edge Deployment for Remote Sensing:** The Reasoning Stack Distillation method is a groundbreaking contribution for remote sensing. By successfully transferring complex agent behaviors from large teacher models (GPT-4o) to lightweight student models (Qwen3-14B), VICoT-Agent can run efficiently on resource-constrained edge devices (e.g., satellites, UAVs, ground sensors). This dramatically expands the potential for real-time, autonomous intelligence extraction directly at the source, offering benefits in terms of latency, bandwidth, security, and operational autonomy in remote or austere environments.
- **Superior Performance in Complex Remote Sensing Tasks:** Extensive experiments demonstrate that VICoT-Agent consistently outperforms existing SOTA frameworks and MLLMs across various remote sensing VQA and intelligence analysis benchmarks, including challenging ultra-high-resolution (UHR) imagery. Its specialized Region-Aware Captioned Prompting module further enhances its ability to derive structured reasoning from large-scale scenes, which is vital for detailed geospatial intelligence.
- **Creation of a Novel Dataset:** The VICoT-HRSC dataset, designed for multimodal, multi-turn reasoning, is a valuable resource for the research community. It facilitates the development and evaluation of agents capable of intricate vision-language interactions, particularly for fine-tuning smaller models for specialized tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
The VICoT-Agent framework has the potential to revolutionize how remote sensing data is analyzed and utilized. It can empower intelligence analysts with highly autonomous and interpretable systems, improve the speed and accuracy of disaster response by providing real-time insights from satellite imagery, enhance environmental monitoring capabilities, and enable new applications in defense and urban planning. The ability to deploy sophisticated reasoning agents on edge devices democratizes access to advanced AI for remote sensing, opening doors for applications in areas with limited connectivity or computational infrastructure.

**Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge that future work will focus on extending the VICoT framework to open-domain scenarios, moving beyond specialized remote sensing tasks, and further improving the generalization capabilities of lightweight models to tackle even broader ranges of visual and linguistic challenges. Addressing error cases related to LLM output uncertainties and parameter mismatches, as detailed in the supplementary material, will also be crucial for enhancing the robustness of the system.
