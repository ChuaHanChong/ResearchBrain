# Interactive Language: Talking to Robots in Real Time

> [!summary] Summary
> Robotics at Google presents a framework for real-time, interactive natural language control of physical robots, achieving a 93.5% success rate on over 87,000 unique short-horizon commands and enabling humans to guide robots to complex, long-horizon goals with 85.0% success. The approach also facilitates simultaneous control of multiple robots by a single operator using spoken language, supported by a large-scale language-annotated dataset and a transformer-based policy.

## Problem

- Real-world robots struggled to respond capably and interactively to a large, diverse set of natural language commands in real time.
- Existing language-interactive systems often exhibited slow response times, used blocking parameterized skills, or relied on simplifying assumptions, hindering fluid human-robot collaboration.
- The absence of large-scale, high-quality language-labeled robot datasets impeded the development of robust language-conditioned visuomotor policies.

## Method

- Developed an "Interactive Language" imitation learning framework encompassing high-throughput data collection and real-time interaction.
- Introduced "Event-Selectable Hindsight Relabeling" to efficiently create a large-scale dataset of language-annotated robot trajectories from raw teleoperation.
- Utilized LAVA, a transformer-based policy architecture, to fuse multi-scale visual features and language embeddings for continuous visuomotor control.

## Results

- A single robot policy achieved an estimated 93.5% average success rate on 87,588 unique short-horizon natural language commands in a real-world setting.
- The framework enabled humans to guide robots to 85.0% average success on over 100,000 distinct long-horizon compositional rearrangement goals using real-time language feedback.
- One human operator successfully controlled four robots simultaneously via spoken natural language to accomplish complex long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time natural language feedback is critical for successful completion of complex, long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks, increasing success rates from 25% to 85%.
> - Scaling data collection, particularly with event-selectable hindsight relabeling, enables a single robot policy to master a diverse repertoire of over 87,000 unique natural language-conditioned skills.
> - The development of robust short-horizon language-conditioned skills allows for advanced applications such as a single human operator simultaneously guiding multiple robots via spoken language.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robots capable of understanding and executing natural language instructions has been a long-standing objective in artificial intelligence and robotics, tracing back to early systems like SHRDLU. Recent advancements in deep learning and natural language processing have led to numerous studies on language-conditioned robot control. However, many of these efforts have been situated in simulated environments, utilized simplified or parameterized action spaces, or focused on fixed, open-loop instructions rather than real-time, interactive guidance.

Prior work in imitation learning has enabled robots to acquire behaviors from human demonstrations, initially for single tasks and later expanding to multi-task learning using various conditioning methods like discrete task identifiers or goal images. More recently, natural language has emerged as a preferred method for specifying diverse tasks due to its convenience and flexibility. Despite this, robots capable of performing continuous visuomotor manipulation in the real world based on a large vocabulary of natural language commands, particularly with real-time feedback, have remained a significant challenge. Existing language-interactive robots often exhibit slow response times, rely on blocking parameterized skills, or operate under simplifying assumptions that restrict live interaction.

This research addresses these limitations by focusing on real-time, continuous visuomotor control of physical robots conditioned by open-vocabulary natural language. It integrates large-scale data collection and a transformer-based policy architecture to enable a robot to respond capably to a wide range of diverse commands. The work also contributes significantly to the availability of large-scale, language-labeled robot datasets, which has historically been a bottleneck in the field. By demonstrating the benefits of interactive language feedback for complex, long-horizon tasks and exploring multi-robot control, this paper extends the capabilities and potential applications of language-instructable robots beyond what has been previously shown in real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for building interactive, real-time, natural language-instructable robots that operate in physical environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

- **Addressing Limitations of Prior Work:** The authors observe that despite abundant research, few efforts have yielded real-world robots that can capably respond to a large and diverse set of natural language commands in a real-time, interactive manner. Previous approaches often involved slow interaction, utilized blocking parameterized skills, or relied on self-resetting behaviors, which limited the fluidity and responsiveness necessary for live human-robot collaboration. The motivation is to overcome these practical limitations and enable seamless, in-the-moment guidance.
- **Scaling Robot Capabilities:** A key objective is to significantly expand the repertoire of skills a single robot policy can execute based on natural language. The authors aim to achieve an order of magnitude increase in the number of unique commands a robot can proficiently handle compared to existing methods.
- **Enabling Interactive Language Guidance:** The research explicitly seeks to explore and demonstrate the capacity for robots to follow interactive language commands, meaning the robot can react capably and immediately to new instructions provided during ongoing task execution. This includes addressing precise, long-horizon rearrangement goals that require continuous human input and correction.
- **Facilitating Further Research:** To accelerate progress in this domain, the authors aim to open-source related assets, including a large-scale dataset of language-annotated trajectories, a hardware environment description, a simulated analogue environment, and a research benchmark for language-conditioned manipulation. This objective is motivated by the recognition that data scarcity and lack of standardized evaluation environments often impede research in real-world robotics.
- **Studying Nonobvious Benefits of Real-Time Language:** Beyond basic instruction following, the work investigates advanced capabilities unlocked by real-time language competency. This includes allowing humans to guide robots through complex, long-horizon tasks requiring minutes of precise control via occasional natural language feedback, and exploring the potential for a single human operator to simultaneously guide multiple real-time robots.
- **Developing a Scalable Learning Framework:** The authors aim to establish a simple yet scalable imitation learning framework that combines efficient data collection methods with straightforward language-conditioned behavioral cloning. The focus is on demonstrating that the complexity of enabling such capabilities primarily lies in data effort rather than intricate learning algorithms.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between theoretical advancements in natural language processing and the practical deployment of highly capable, responsive, and easily instructable robots in real-world settings, while also providing the community with the tools to build upon this foundation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the "Interactive Language" framework, a large-scale robot imitation learning approach designed for real-time natural language interaction. This framework comprises three main stages: high-throughput data collection, language-conditioned behavioral cloning (LCBC), and real-time human-robot interaction.

**A. Data Collection**

The data collection strategy is designed for scalability and diversity, focusing on generating a large dataset of robot trajectories annotated with natural language.

- **High Throughput Raw Data Collection:** Operators teleoperate robots to perform long-horizon behaviors without explicit low-level task definitions, segmentation, or episodic resets. This "play" style collection is guided by high-level prompts (e.g., "make a square shape out of the blocks") that encourage temporally extended, low-entropy states. The collected data is optimality-agnostic and includes various edge cases. The real-world setup involved four xArm6 robots and ten teleoperators, accumulating 2.7k hours of collect time.
- **Event-Selectable Hindsight Relabeling:** The raw collected data (D_collect) is converted into language-conditioned demonstrations (D_training) using a novel "Event-Selectable Hindsight Relabeling" method. Unlike previous "random window" relabeling, crowdsourced annotators watch full teleoperation videos and identify K (24 in this work) coherent behaviors. Annotators precisely mark the start and end frames of each behavior and provide natural language commands describing the action. This approach is reported to yield data that is more aligned with test-time language, featuring fewer complex compound instructions and more compositionally directed instructions compared to random window relabeling. The process involved 64 crowdsourced annotators and resulted in 299k relabeled demonstrations with 87k unique instructions for the real-world dataset subset used for training. The total Language-Table dataset (real + sim) comprises nearly 600,000 language-labeled trajectories, an order of magnitude larger than prior datasets.
- **Throughput Analysis:** The authors identify crowdsourced language annotation as the main bottleneck in the data operation, with approximately 18% of raw data undergoing annotation before model training.

**B. Policy Learning**

A transformer-based neural network architecture, termed LAVA ("Language Attends to Vision to Act"), is employed for policy learning.

- **Transformer-based Agent Architecture (LAVA):**
 - **Input:** The policy takes a history of RGB observations ($s \in R^{seqlen \times 640 \times 320 \times 3}$) and a human-provided natural language instruction ($l \in L$) as input. The observation history length (seqlen) used is 4.
 - **Perception Module:** Each frame in the video history is processed by a ConvNet, which includes ImageNet-pretrained ResNet layers and additional convolutional layers, to extract multi-scale visual features.
 - **Language Module:** The natural language instruction is embedded using a pretrained CLIP text encoder. This encoder is finetuned on in-domain data but kept fixed during policy training. Text preprocessing includes stripping punctuation and extra spaces.
 - **Vision-Language Fusion Module:** This module uses a "Language-Attends-to-Vision" transformer block. It takes multi-scale pixel features and the sentence embedding as input. It maps each visual feature layer, applies 2D sinusoidal positional embeddings, and flattens them into a visual token list. The language embedding is projected, and then a pre-norm decoder-only transformer performs cross-attention where language acts as the query and visual tokens as keys and values. This process is applied to each image in the sequence.
 - **Temporal Fusion Module:** The output sequence of vision-language embeddings is fed into a 1D sinusoidally position-encoded pre-norm transformer that performs self-attention, capturing temporal dependencies. The output is then average-pooled over the time dimension.
 - **Policy Output:** The pooled embedding is passed to a deep residual multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that outputs the predicted 2D delta Cartesian action (setpoints for the end-effector).
- **Training:** Policies are trained using a standard supervised Language Conditioned Behavioral Cloning (LCBC) objective. A simple mean squared error (MSE) loss is minimized, aiming for deterministic policies: $\min_{\theta} \sum_{(s,a,l) \sim D_{training}} ||a-\pi_{\theta}(s, l)||_2^2$. Training is conducted on a TPUv3 pod for approximately 500,000 steps, using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-3 and a batch size of 4096.

**C. Language-Table: Datasets and Environment**

To support future research, the authors release Language-Table, comprising datasets and a simulated benchmark.

- **Datasets:** Language-Table provides both the human-relabeled D_training and the raw human-teleoperated D_collect datasets, available for both simulation and real-world scenarios. The combined real and simulation D_training dataset includes 594k trajectories with 198k unique instructions.
- **Environment and Benchmark:** The simulated environment mirrors the real-world tabletop manipulation setup, featuring an xArm6 robot constrained to move in a 2D plane with a cylindrical end-effector, interacting with 8 plastic blocks of various colors and shapes on a board. Visual input consists of third-person perspective 320x180 RGB images. Actions are 2D delta Cartesian setpoints. The real-world control operates at 5Hz, while the simulated benchmark policies operate at 10Hz. The Language-Table benchmark evaluates performance across five task families (e.g., block-to-block, block-to-absolute location, separate), encompassing 696 unique task variations, using metrics like success rate and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL). SPL is noted to correlate well with human-preferred real-world performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the capabilities of the Interactive Language framework across diverse short-horizon skills, long-horizon tasks, and multi-robot control in both real-world and simulated settings.

**A. Real World: Diverse Short-Horizon Language-Conditionable Skills**

- A single robot policy trained with the Interactive Language framework was evaluated on its ability to follow a wide variety of short-horizon natural language commands.
- On a test set derived from 87,588 unique crowdsourced language instructions, the policy achieved an estimated average success rate of 93.5%, with a 95% confidence interval of [90.08%, 96.92%].
- These instructions required the robot to ground object properties, compositional spatial concepts (e.g., "top right side of the yellow hexagon" versus "top right side of the board"), and resolve ambiguities (e.g., "nudge the cube left a bit"). This represents a substantial increase in the scale of language-conditioned behaviors a real-world policy has been demonstrated to capably address.

**B. Real World: Long-Horizon Real-Time Language Guidance**

- **Long-Horizon Goal Reaching:** The policy's ability to achieve complex, multi-step compositional rearrangements was tested by having humans guide it through over 100,000 distinct long-horizon goal states from 11 families (e.g., making shapes, sorting by color, arranging blocks into lines).
- The policy achieved an 85.0% expected average success rate on this diverse set of goals, with a 95% confidence interval of [69.35%, 100.00%], demonstrating that humans can effectively guide the robot to precise, extended goals using real-time language.
- **Open-Loop vs. Real-Time Language Feedback:** An ablation study quantified the benefit of real-time language feedback compared to an open-loop execution where the sequence of subgoals is predetermined.
- When real-time language feedback was removed (open-loop), the success rate significantly deteriorated from 85% to 25%. This indicates that for contact-rich manipulation tasks, continuous, iterative feedback from the human operator is crucial for successful task completion.
- **Multi-Robot Control via Spoken Language:** The research explored a new capability: simultaneous control of multiple robots by a single operator using spoken natural language.
- It was demonstrated that one operator could guide four robots equipped with Interactive Language policies concurrently through long-horizon tasks. This suggests that the short-horizon skill competency of the policies, combined with language guidance, can relax the requirement for undivided operator attention, which is common in many prior online robot correction methods.

**C. Simulation: Architecture and Data Ablation**

- **Architecture Comparison:** In simulation, the LAVA transformer-based architecture was compared against a baseline FiLM-conditioned ResNet architecture. LAVA demonstrated significant gains in Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), a metric that accounts for task efficiency and correlated well with real-world performance.
- **Data Scaling:** Ablations on the amount of training data revealed that policy performance continued to improve with increasing data, albeit with diminishing returns. This suggests that even with the large scale of collected data (nearly 600,000 trajectories), the complexity of open-vocabulary visuomotor learning and the environment still benefit from more data.
- **CLIP Finetuning and Temporal Fusion:** Ablation studies also showed that finetuning the CLIP language module on in-domain data and the use of the transformer-based temporal fusion module contributed to performance improvements, with a more pronounced qualitative difference observed in real-robot behavior for CLIP finetuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Interactive Language framework and its associated assets represent a notable contribution to the field of robotics and human-robot interaction, with several areas of potential significance and impact:

- **Advancement in Real-World Language-Conditioned Robotics:** This work demonstrates a real-world robot system capable of executing an estimated 93.5% of 87,000 unique natural language commands, significantly expanding the scale and diversity of behaviors achieved by prior real-world language-instructable robots. This achievement indicates progress towards more generally capable and versatile robotic systems.
- **Enabling Real-Time, Interactive Robot Guidance:** The framework emphasizes and validates the importance of real-time language interaction. The substantial performance drop observed when removing real-time feedback highlights its critical role in complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks. This suggests a paradigm for human-robot collaboration where robots handle low-level skills, and humans provide high-level, adaptive guidance and corrections.
- **Scalable Data Collection and Benchmarking:** The release of the "Language-Table" dataset, comprising nearly 600,000 language-labeled trajectories (an order of magnitude larger than previous comparable datasets), addresses a significant bottleneck in robot learning. The associated simulated environment and multi-task benchmark provide a standardized platform for future research, enabling reproducible evaluation and fostering advancements in open-vocabulary visuomotor learning. The event-selectable hindsight relabeling method offers a scalable technique for generating high-quality language-annotated data.
- **Novel Capabilities and Applications:** The demonstration of simultaneous multi-robot control by a single human operator using natural language is a novel capability. This implies that language interfaces can reduce the cognitive load on operators, potentially leading to more efficient and scalable deployment of robot fleets in various domains. Such a capability could impact logistics, manufacturing, and even personal assistance, where one person could oversee multiple robotic agents.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The simple yet effective behavioral cloning approach, combined with a robust data collection methodology and a transformer-based architecture, provides a strong baseline. It establishes a foundation for future investigations into more complex loss functions, policy architectures, and the integration of autonomous long-horizon planning with real-time interactive control. The work also opens avenues for exploring real-time assistive robots, which could greatly benefit from more intuitive natural language interfaces.

While the approach has limitations, such as not addressing broader human-robot collaboration challenges like intention detection or non-verbal communication, it makes substantial progress in real-time language-guided manipulation, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with current visuolinguomotor control systems.
