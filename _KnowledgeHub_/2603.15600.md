# From Passive Observer to Active Critic: Reinforcement Learning Elicits Process Reasoning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The PRIMO R1 framework transforms multimodal large language models (MLLMs) into active critics that explicitly reason about task progress, offering robust reward signals for robotic manipulation. This approach achieved an average Mean Relative Accuracy of 82.90 and a Mean Absolute Error of 15.52 across diverse environments, significantly improving upon existing MLLMs.

## Problem

- Developing effective, dense reward signals for complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks is a critical bottleneck for general-purpose robots.
- Current video MLLMs function as "passive observers," excelling at description but lacking rigorous quantitative reasoning for task progress and exhibiting poor generalization to novel objects or scenarios.
- Existing progress estimation methods often rely on direct regression or explicit reference demonstrations, limiting their capacity for causal failure analysis and generalization.

## Method

- Introduces PRIMO R1, a framework that reformulates progress estimation as a generative reasoning task, leveraging structured temporal input (initial, current states, and video sequence).
- Employs outcome-based Reinforcement Learning (specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization) to incentivize the MLLM to generate explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning for progress estimation, without requiring dense reasoning annotations.
- Developed the PRIMO Dataset and PRIMO Benchmark to support a two-stage post-training paradigm (SFT followed by RL) and to systematically evaluate in-domain and zero-shot out-of-domain generalization.

## Results

- PRIMO R1 achieved an average Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) of 82.90 and a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 15.52 across diverse environments, significantly outperforming larger MLLMs (e.g., +9.10 MRA over Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and specialized video reasoning models.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization in sim-to-real transfer to an unseen physical humanoid robot (72.32 MRA) and matched closed-source models in failure detection on the RoboFail benchmark (**67.0%** accuracy).
- Reinforcement Learning training significantly boosted out-of-domain generalization, improving cross-task and cross-environment performance, thereby preventing the overfitting and degradation seen in SFT-only models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-based Reinforcement Learning effectively elicits interpretable Chain-of-Thought reasoning from MLLMs, fundamentally transforming them into active critics capable of generalizing progress estimation.
> - Explicitly anchoring video sequences with initial and current state images provides crucial boundary conditions, maintaining spatial detail and temporal context necessary for robust long-horizon task progress tracking.
> - Optimizing for continuous task progress reasoning intrinsically constructs prerequisite temporal context representations, enabling strong zero-shot performance in discrete execution failure detection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robots capable of performing complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks is a central challenge in the field of embodied AI. A fundamental bottleneck in this endeavor is the derivation of effective reward signals for policy learning. While sparse rewards, such as binary success or failure indicators, are straightforward to define, they often prove insufficient for guiding efficient learning in intricate environments. Conversely, dense rewards, which offer granular feedback on task progress, typically necessitate extensive manual engineering or privileged access to internal ground-truth states, neither of which is readily available in real-world scenarios.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have introduced capabilities for understanding and generating human-like language and visual representations. These models have shown promise in learning universal reward functions directly from visual observations, potentially alleviating the burden of manual reward engineering. However, the current paradigm for most video MLLMs, particularly those trained via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), positions them as "passive Observers." These models excel at descriptive tasks like captioning and question answering about ongoing events but exhibit limitations in rigorous quantitative reasoning concerning task progress relative to a final goal. This limitation results in models that are brittle, struggle with generalization to novel objects, lack explainability for their predictions, and can misinterpret visual trajectories, potentially assigning high progress scores to failed attempts that superficially resemble successful motions.

This work addresses this limitation by proposing a shift from the "passive Observer" paradigm to an "active Critic" paradigm. It builds upon existing efforts in video MLLMs for understanding temporal dynamics and reasoning, as well as vision-based state estimation and reward modeling. Prior approaches in progress estimation often frame the problem as direct regression or classification, or rely on explicit reference demonstrations, which can restrict a model's capacity for causal failure analysis and generalization. The paper also integrates concepts from reinforcement learning (RL) for reasoning elicitation, specifically leveraging outcome-based RL to induce Chain-of-Thought (CoT) behaviors, a technique previously applied to enhance static visual reasoning and dynamic temporal grounding. By transforming video MLLMs into active critics that can explicitly reason about task progress, this research aims to provide more reliable and interpretable reward signals, thereby contributing to the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome a critical bottleneck in long-horizon robotic manipulation: the lack of accurate and effective process supervision. The motivation stems from several key challenges inherent in current approaches to robotic task learning and multimodal model capabilities.

Firstly, the pursuit of general-purpose robots necessitates robust reward signals that can guide complex, multi-step tasks over extended periods. Existing reward mechanisms are often either too sparse to facilitate efficient learning or too labor-intensive to engineer for dense feedback. The potential of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to learn universal reward functions from visual data is recognized, but their current limitations prevent their effective deployment as "process supervisors."

Secondly, a core limitation identified in current video MLLMs is their functioning as "passive Observers." These models, primarily developed through supervised fine-tuning (SFT), are adept at identifying and describing events unfolding in a video. However, they struggle with the quantitative reasoning required to accurately assess how much progress has been made towards a task's final goal. This manifests as several issues:
- **Lack of Generalization:** Models fail to generalize to unseen objects or variations in task execution.
- **Absence of Explainability:** They cannot provide justifications for their progress predictions.
- **Vulnerability to Hallucination:** They may assign high progress scores to visually similar, but fundamentally failed, task attempts.
- **Structural Deficit:** Without explicit temporal boundary anchoring and continuous reasoning pathways, these models are unable to align continuous visual trajectories with the discrete logical conditions necessary for successful task completion.

The key motivation is to bridge this gap between passive visual perception and active evaluative judgment. The authors argue that a reliable reward model for robotics must evolve into an "active Critic" capable of not just observing, but also evaluating the current state against the task goal.

To achieve this, the specific objectives are:
1. **Transform Video MLLMs into Active Critics:** Develop a framework (PRIMO R1) that elicits explicit process reasoning from video MLLMs, enabling them to evaluate task progress rather than merely describing events.
2. **Incentivize Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation:** Leverage outcome-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) to encourage the model to generate explicit reasoning chains, which are hypothesized to improve progress estimation and provide interpretability.
3. **Implement Structured Temporal Input:** Design an architecture that explicitly anchors the video sequence between initial and current state images. This aims to provide clear visual boundary conditions, transforming the reasoning task from generic temporal perception into a structured state-alignment verification.
4. **Establish Language-Guided Generalization:** Condition the reasoning process on diverse natural language task goals, thereby exploiting the inherent linguistic generalization capabilities of foundational MLLMs to process varying evaluation criteria.
5. **Create Supporting Resources:** Construct the PRIMO Dataset and PRIMO Benchmark to facilitate the training, post-training, and systematic evaluation of this new paradigm, particularly focusing on out-of-domain generalization.

These objectives are driven by the overarching goal of enabling more intelligent, autonomous, and robust robotic systems through improved process supervision and interpretable reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology outlined in the paper focuses on transforming video MLLMs from passive event observers into active critics capable of explicit process reasoning for robotic manipulation. This is achieved through a multi-modal input strategy, a generative reasoning formulation, and a reinforcement learning optimization approach, supported by a custom dataset and benchmark.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task is formalized as a multi-modal state estimation problem. The model receives four inputs: an initial state image ($I_{init}$), a process video sequence ($V_{seq}$), a current state image ($I_{curr}$), and a natural language instruction ($\mathcal{L}$) specifying the task goal. The objective is to learn a mapping function $F$ that outputs a scalar progress indicator $y \in$, where $y=0$ signifies the initial state and $y=100$ denotes successful task completion. The paper argues that explicitly modeling both boundary states ($I_{init}$ and $I_{curr}$) alongside the temporal transition ($V_{seq}$) is critical for accurate progress estimation.

In contrast to existing approaches that treat progress estimation as direct regression or classification via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), PRIMO R1 reformulates it as a multi-step generative reasoning task. A policy $\pi_\theta$ is defined to sequentially generate a latent reasoning chain (Chain-of-Thought, C), followed by the final progress estimate ($\hat{y}$).

#### 3.2. PRIMO R1 Framework

The core of PRIMO R1 involves:
- **Structured Temporal Input:** The architecture explicitly anchors the video sequence $V_{seq}$ between the initial state image $I_{init}$ and the current state image $I_{curr}$. This design provides clear visual boundary conditions, converting the reasoning task into a structured state-alignment verification.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation:** Instead of relying on dense annotations for the intermediate reasoning chain C, the policy $\pi_\theta$ is optimized using outcome-based Reinforcement Learning. The objective is to maximize a reward function $R(\hat{y}, y_{gt})$ based solely on the accuracy of the final prediction $\hat{y}$. This incentivizes the policy to self-organize the intermediate reasoning C to accurately align the temporal transition $V_{seq}$ between the boundary states.
- **Language-Guided Generalization:** The generative reasoning process is conditioned on diverse natural language task goals ($\mathcal{L}$). This establishes a direct structural mapping between the semantic objective and visual execution logic, leveraging the linguistic generalization capabilities of foundational MLLMs.

#### 3.3. PRIMO Dataset and Benchmark

To support the two-stage post-training paradigm (SFT and RL) and evaluate generalization, a dedicated dataset and benchmark were constructed.
- **PRIMO Dataset for Post-Training:** This dataset includes fine-grained progress indicators annotated with Chain-of-Thought reasoning paths. It aggregates multi-source trajectories from a real-world environment (AgiBot) and two high-fidelity simulations (BEHAVIOR-1k, RoboTwin). Additionally, general video reasoning datasets (e.g., EgoPlan, RoboVQA, Perception Test) are incorporated during the SFT phase for data diversity. The dataset is partitioned into a 116k-sample SFT dataset (PRIMO-R1-CoT-116k) and a 182k-sample RL dataset (PRIMO-R1-182k).
- **PRIMO Bench for Generalization Evaluation:** Designed to systematically assess robustness against distribution shifts, it comprises two splits:
 - **In-Domain (ID) - Same Task:** Evaluates estimation accuracy on task categories seen during training within familiar environments.
 - **Out-of-Domain (OOD) - Cross-Task & Cross-Environment:** Tests zero-shot generalization. Cross-Task involves entirely unseen tasks in familiar environments. Cross-Environment introduces a stringent domain transfer challenge, evaluating on real-world trajectories from an unseen physical humanoid robot (Leju KUAVO-MY) in unstructured physical environments.

#### 3.4. Process Reasoning RL with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO is employed for its ability to estimate baselines using group statistics of sampled outputs, thereby circumventing the computational expense of a separate value network required by methods like PPO.
- **Group Sampling and Advantage Estimation:** For a given task input, a group of G outputs are sampled from the policy. The advantage $A_i$ for each output $o_i$ is computed by normalizing its reward $r_i$ against the group's mean and standard deviation. This encourages generating reasoning paths that yield higher-than-average rewards.
- **Rule-Based Reward Design:** A composite reward function $R(o_i, y_{gt}) = r_{fmt} + r_{acc}$ is designed:
 - **Format Reward ($r_{fmt}$):** A positive reward (e.g., +1) is given if the output strictly adheres to the `<think>reasoning</think> <answer>prediction</answer>` format, promoting CoT generation.
 - **Accuracy Reward ($r_{acc}$):** A bounded linear decay function (max(0, 1 - |$\hat{y}_i$ - $y_{gt}$|$ / R_{max}$)) provides dense feedback for numerical accuracy, ranging from 1.0 (exact match) to 0.0 (error approaches $R_{max}$).
- **Optimization Objective:** The policy $\pi_\theta$ is updated to maximize the expected advantage, while a KL divergence penalty ensures it remains close to a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$, preventing reward hacking and language degradation. This optimization implicitly trains PRIMO R1 to generate detailed, causal reasoning to maximize accuracy.

The foundational model used for all training phases is Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Inference configurations involve 32 sampled frames at 256x28x28 pixels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluated PRIMO R1's capabilities in continuous task progress estimation and zero-shot discrete execution failure detection, both in-domain and out-of-domain environments. The evaluation utilized Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) as primary metrics.

#### 4.1. Generalization in Progress Estimation

PRIMO R1 consistently demonstrated superior performance across four diverse environments: AgiBot, Behavior, RoboTwin, and Real Humanoid.
- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** PRIMO R1 (7B parameter model) achieved the highest average MRA of 82.90 and the lowest average MAE of 15.52 across all domains. This represents a significant improvement over open-source general MLLMs; for instance, it surpassed the 72B-scale Qwen2.5-VL-72B model by 9.10 absolute MRA points. Compared to specialized reasoning and video MLLMs (e.g., Video R1 7B, Robobrain 7B), PRIMO R1 halved the absolute estimation error.
- **Fine-Grained Error Analysis (Figure 4):** PRIMO R1 maintained low error rates across all task completion intervals (0-100%). It effectively mitigated severe error spikes and hallucinations observed in baseline models, particularly during the final execution phase (80-100%), preventing premature task completion attribution based on superficial visual similarities.
- **Robustness in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** In the challenging, unstructured "Real Humanoid" environment, PRIMO R1 maintained a strong MRA of 72.32, while general MLLMs experienced substantial performance drops (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B dropped to 56.46 MRA). This underscores the effectiveness of explicit reasoning chains in bridging distribution gaps.
- **Impact of RL on Generalization (Table 3):** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) alone improved performance but showed overfitting, with a degradation in the Cross-Environment OOD setting (67.30 MRA). The integration of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) after SFT (PRIMO R1, SFT+RL) demonstrated a powerful synergy. The RL phase not only pushed in-domain (ID) performance closer to 90% MRA but also significantly enhanced OOD generalization, boosting Cross-Task performance and lifting Cross-Environment accuracy to 72.32%. This indicates that RL-driven process supervision fundamentally transforms the model into an active, generalizing critic.

#### 4.2. Generalization Enhancement in Failure Detection

PRIMO R1 also exhibited strong zero-shot generalization capabilities in execution anomaly verification, specifically on the RoboFail benchmark.
- **RoboFail Performance (Table 2):** PRIMO R1 achieved an accuracy of 67.0%, matching the closed-source Gemini 2.0 Flash and outperforming other models including GPT-4o (63.0%), OpenAI o1 (61.0%), and Cosmos-Reason1-56B (66.2%).
- **Role of RL:** SFT alone resulted in a performance regression (51.0%) on failure detection, suggesting a compromise between continuous progress estimation and discrete failure identification. The subsequent integration of Process Supervision RL with GRPO corrected this degradation, raising accuracy to 63.0%, with the full PRIMO R1 formulation achieving 67.0%. This finding suggests that optimizing for continuous progress reasoning intrinsically constructs the necessary temporal context representations for failure verification.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: The Necessity of Temporal Context

An ablation study on input modalities revealed the importance of the structured temporal input strategy.
- **Input Modality Impact (Table 4, Figure 7):** Relying solely on the current state image ($I_{curr}$) resulted in the highest MAE (59.50), indicating insufficient causal context. Pure temporal sequence ($V_{seq}$) alone was suboptimal (MAE 36.61). The combination of $V_{seq}$ with either $I_{init}$ or $I_{curr}$ improved performance. However, the complete triad of $I_{init}$, $V_{seq}$, and $I_{curr}$ was crucial for long-horizon tasks, achieving the lowest MAE of 22.73 on the Behavior dataset and increasing Acc@10 to 31.83. This confirms that explicit boundary state modeling is a necessary prerequisite for robust progress tracking over extended temporal sequences.
- **GRPO Training Dynamics (Figure 7):** The inclusion of static boundary images ($I_{init} + I_{curr}$) accelerated reward convergence during GRPO training and achieved higher optimal rewards compared to the $V_{seq}$-only baseline. It also reduced reward variance, leading to more stable policy optimization.

#### 4.4. Case Studies

Qualitative analysis of generated reasoning chains demonstrated PRIMO R1's ability to decompose tasks, extract fine-grained visual primitives, and map observations to a structured plan.
- **Continuous Progress Tracking (Figure 5):** For the "Clear the countertop waste" task, PRIMO R1 maintained a monotonically increasing trajectory that linearly correlated with ground truth, accurately mapping visual state changes to progress metrics, unlike baselines that exhibited decoupling or plateauing.
- **Explicit Reasoning (Figure 6, 9):** In "Fold shorts" and "Sequential Part Sorting" tasks, PRIMO R1 generated explicit "Planning," "Observation," and "Reasoning" modules. The "Planning" module decomposed high-level goals into step-by-step plans. The "Observation" module extracted specific dynamic variables and object state changes. The "Reasoning" module then performed state alignment against the plan, leading to grounded and interpretable numerical predictions.

#### 4.5. Inference Efficiency (Table 6)

PRIMO R1 achieved an optimal balance between accuracy and computational efficiency. With an average inference latency of 0.62 seconds and a reasoning chain length of 359.18 tokens, its computational overhead was competitive with faster baselines, while delivering significantly higher accuracy (82.90 MRA). This makes it a cost-effective solution for real-time robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces PRIMO R1, a framework designed to transform multimodal large language models from passive observers into active critics for robotic process supervision. The work presents several areas of significance and potential impact:

Firstly, PRIMO R1 addresses a fundamental limitation in embodied AI by providing a robust method for generating effective, dense reward signals for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. The transition from passive observation to active, interpretable criticism is critical for enabling robots to not just perform tasks, but also to understand their progress, identify errors, and potentially self-correct. This capability is essential for developing truly autonomous and intelligent robotic systems that can operate in complex and unstructured environments.

Secondly, the methodological advancements contribute to the broader field of multimodal learning. The use of outcome-based Reinforcement Learning (specifically GRPO) to incentivize explicit Chain-of-Thought generation offers a pathway for eliciting complex reasoning behaviors without requiring dense, hand-annotated reasoning labels. This approach leverages the inherent generative capabilities of MLLMs, allowing them to self-organize intermediate reasoning steps based on the accuracy of their final predictions. Furthermore, the structured temporal input strategy, which explicitly anchors video sequences between initial and current state images, provides a clear mechanism for maintaining spatial detail and temporal context, improving the precision of state-alignment and progress estimation.

Thirdly, the development of the PRIMO Dataset and PRIMO Benchmark is a significant contribution to the research community. These resources provide a standardized and comprehensive framework for training and systematically evaluating the out-of-domain generalization capabilities of video MLLMs in robotic contexts. The benchmark's distinction between in-domain, cross-task, and cross-environment evaluations is crucial for assessing true generalization, particularly in sim-to-real transfer scenarios, which remain a major challenge in robotics.

Finally, the findings that optimizing for continuous task progress reasoning intrinsically constructs the prerequisite representations for zero-shot discrete failure detection have substantial implications. This suggests a deep functional relationship between these two aspects of temporal reasoning, indicating that a model capable of accurately tracking progress is inherently better equipped to identify when a task has gone awry. This integrated capability is vital for error recovery and robust task execution in robotics, offering a foundation for future policy learning paradigms that can dynamically adapt and respond to unforeseen challenges. The superior performance of PRIMO R1, even when compared to larger and closed-source models, highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of its design, suggesting its potential for practical application in real-world robotic deployments.
