# Learn to Reason Efficiently with Adaptive Length-based Reward Shaping

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents LASER methods (LASER, LASER-D, LASER-DE), which utilize adaptive length-based reward shaping within a reinforcement learning framework to enhance the efficiency of Large Reasoning Models. These methods improve the balance between reasoning accuracy and token usage, achieving substantial reductions in token consumption while maintaining or increasing accuracy on challenging mathematical benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) employing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) often 'overthink', generating verbose and redundant reasoning steps.
- This overthinking leads to inefficiency, increasing computational costs and hindering the practical deployment of these Large Reasoning Models (LRMs).
- Existing methods for improving reasoning efficiency often face a steep trade-off, where token reduction comes at the cost of reasoning accuracy.

## Method

- Introduces a unified reinforcement learning framework that formulates efficient reasoning as an optimization problem incorporating correctness and ==length-based rewards==.
- Proposes ==LASER (Length-bAsed StEp Reward)==, a method that applies a step function reward to penalize reasoning chains exceeding a target length.
- Develops ==LASER-D (Dynamic and Difficulty-aware)== which adaptively adjusts the target reasoning length throughout training based on problem difficulty and current model accuracy.
- Introduces ==LASER-DE (with additional Exploration)==, a variant of LASER-D that encourages further exploration on incorrect responses to discover better reasoning paths.

## Results

- The LASER methods achieve an improved Pareto-optimal balance between reasoning accuracy and token efficiency compared to prior approaches.
- On the AIME2024 benchmark, LASER-D and LASER-DE improved accuracy by **6.1 percentage points** while reducing token usage by **63%**.
- The proposed methods demonstrate robust generalization across out-of-domain benchmarks, including MATH500, AMC2023, and OlympiadBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework for length-based reward shaping provides a systematic approach to mitigating overthinking in large reasoning models.
> - Reinforcement learning with length-based reward shaping leads to more concise reasoning chains, eliminating redundant steps and reducing unnecessary self-reflection.
> - Dynamic adjustment of target reasoning length based on problem difficulty allows for adaptive 'fast' and 'slow' thinking, optimizing efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in complex reasoning tasks when combined with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting techniques. However, these Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often suffer from inefficiency due to excessive token usage and redundant reasoning steps—a phenomenon known as "overthinking." This inefficiency not only increases computational costs but also impacts the practical deployment of these models.

This paper introduces a novel approach to improve the efficiency of LRMs through adaptive length-based reward shaping in reinforcement learning. The proposed methods—LASER (Length-bAsed StEp Reward), LASER-D (Dynamic and Difficulty-aware), and LASER-DE (with additional Exploration)—achieve a better Pareto-optimal balance between reasoning accuracy and token efficiency compared to existing approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Background and Problem

LLMs can be transformed into powerful reasoning systems through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which encourages models to generate step-by-step reasoning processes. While this approach improves reasoning performance, it often leads to verbose and redundant reasoning chains. Previous attempts to address this issue include:

1. Test-time prompt engineering techniques
2. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT)
3. Reinforcement learning (RL) approaches

Among these, RL-based approaches have shown the most promise for achieving a balance between reasoning accuracy and efficiency. However, existing methods often face a steep trade-off, where improvements in token efficiency come at the expense of reasoning accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Unified Framework for Length-based Reward Shaping

The paper first introduces a unified framework that formulates various RL strategies for mitigating overthinking through the lens of length-based reward shaping. This framework allows for a systematic analysis and comparison of different approaches.

The framework defines the efficient reasoning optimization problem as:

```
max_θ E_{x∼D, y∼π_θ(·|x)}[r_c(x,y) + r_l(l(y))]
```

Where:
- `r_c(x,y)` is the correctness reward
- `r_l(l(y))` is the length-based reward shaping term
- `l(y)` is the length of the generated response

Different forms of `r_l` correspond to different approaches to efficient reasoning. The framework encompasses various existing methods like truncation-based approaches and more sophisticated reward shaping techniques.

#### 3.2. LASER Methods

Building on this framework, the paper introduces three novel methods:

1. **LASER (Length-bAsed StEp Reward)**: A reward shaping method that uses a step function guided by a desired target length. LASER balances between encouraging shorter responses and maintaining reasoning accuracy by providing a neutral reward until the target length is reached, after which a penalty is applied.

2. **LASER-D (Dynamic and Difficulty-aware)**: Extends LASER by dynamically adapting the target length throughout training based on problem difficulty. This approach recognizes that different problems require different reasoning depths, allowing for a combination of fast and slow thinking.

3. **LASER-DE (with additional Exploration)**: A variant of LASER-D that explicitly encourages additional exploration on incorrect responses. This enables models to discover potentially correct reasoning patterns through extended deliberation.

The LASER-D method introduces difficulty-awareness through a dynamic target length adjustment mechanism:

```
D_LT = max(D_LT - δ, D_min) if acc > acc_thresh_high
      D_LT                  if acc_thresh_low ≤ acc ≤ acc_thresh_high
      min(D_LT + δ, D_max) if acc < acc_thresh_low
```

Where `D_LT` is the target length, `acc` is the recent accuracy, and `δ` is the adjustment step size.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The experiments use three reasoning models of varying sizes:
- DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B
- DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B
- DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B

These models are trained using the DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset, a high-quality mathematics dataset, and evaluated on four challenging benchmarks:
- MATH500
- AIME2024
- AMC2023
- OlympiadBench

The training uses Reinforcement Learning with rule-based correctness rewards, and the optimization is performed with Generalized Proximal Policy Optimization (GRPO).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Results and Analysis

The experiments reveal several key findings:

1. **Surprising Effectiveness of Truncation**: The truncation baseline, which restricts the context window size during RL training, proves to be surprisingly effective. It achieves substantial improvements in token efficiency with only a modest reduction in accuracy.

2. **Superior Performance of LASER Methods**: The LASER, LASER-D, and LASER-DE methods achieve an improved Pareto-optimality compared to previous methods. They deliver significant improvements in both accuracy and token efficiency across various benchmarks.

3. **Impressive Gains on Challenging Benchmarks**: On the challenging AIME24 benchmark, LASER-D/LASER-DE improves accuracy by +6.1 percentage points while reducing token usage by 63%.

4. **Robust Generalization**: The proposed methods demonstrate robust generalization capabilities on out-of-domain benchmarks, suggesting their applicability to a wide range of tasks.

#### 4.2. Cognitive Behavior Analysis

Beyond quantitative improvements, the paper analyzes changes in reasoning patterns using keyword counts and cognitive behavior analysis. The results indicate that RL-based CoT compression leads to more concise and improved reasoning behaviors with fewer redundant steps and unhelpful "self-reflection."

The analysis tracks specific cognitive behaviors:
- **Backtracking**: The frequency of correction and revision in reasoning
- **Verification**: Double-checking or verifying intermediate steps
- **Enumeration**: Listing multiple options or cases
- **Subgoal Setting**: Breaking problems into smaller, manageable parts

The results show that as token usage decreases, the model becomes more selective about which cognitive behaviors to employ, prioritizing those most relevant to the problem at hand.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Conclusion

The paper introduces a unified framework for efficient reasoning through length-based reward shaping and proposes three novel methods—LASER, LASER-D, and LASER-DE—that achieve a better Pareto-optimal balance between reasoning accuracy and token efficiency.

The key contributions include:
1. A unified perspective on efficient reasoning through length-based reward shaping
2. Novel reward shaping methods that adapt to problem difficulty
3. Empirical evidence showing significant improvements in both accuracy and efficiency
4. Analysis of changes in reasoning patterns and cognitive behaviors

These methods address the critical challenge of overthinking in LRMs, making them more efficient and practical for real-world applications. The approach not only reduces computational costs but also improves reasoning quality by encouraging more concise and focused reasoning chains.

The research opens several directions for future work, including extending the approach to other types of reasoning tasks, developing more sophisticated difficulty assessment mechanisms, and exploring the transfer of efficient reasoning patterns across domains.
