# CLAMP: Crowdsourcing a LArge-scale in-the-wild haptic dataset with an open-source device for Multimodal robot Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Cornell University developed the open-source CLAMP device and crowdsourced a large-scale multimodal haptic dataset, encompassing 12.3 million samples from over 5,000 household objects. This data facilitated the training of a visuo-haptic perception model, which achieved 0.87 accuracy for material recognition and enabled improved performance in real-world robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing haptic datasets lack multimodality, diversity, realism (often collected in lab settings with limited objects), and focus on non-prehensile interaction rather than grasping actions.
- Acquiring large-scale and diverse haptic data is resource-intensive, necessitating easy-to-build, user-friendly, and standardized sensing hardware for broad deployment and crowdsourcing.
- Robots require a comprehensive understanding of object material and compliance for robust manipulation in unstructured environments, properties that vision-only perception systems struggle to infer reliably.

## Method

- Designed and built the **CLAMP** device, a low-cost (under $200), open-source, sensorized reacher-grabber capable of capturing five ==haptic modalities==, vision, and speech annotations.
- Leveraged the **CLAMP** device for crowdsourced data collection from 41 non-expert users, creating a large-scale (12.3 million samples, 5,357 diverse household objects) and in-the-wild dataset of grasping interactions.
- Developed the **CLAMP** model, a multimodal perception system that fuses features from an ==InceptionTime== haptic encoder and a ==GPT-4o== visual encoder to robustly infer object material and compliance.

## Results

- The CLAMP dataset, comprising **12.3 million** data samples from **25,100** object touches across **5,357** unique household objects, is reported as the largest open-source multimodal haptic dataset to date.
- The CLAMP visuo-haptic fusion model achieved a material recognition accuracy of **0.87** and an nMCC of **0.93**, outperforming the best vision-only (**0.65** accuracy) and haptic-only (**0.59** accuracy) baselines.
- The model demonstrated generalization to novel objects (**0.73** accuracy) and various robot embodiments, enabling successful performance in real-world robotic tasks like sorting recyclable waste, retrieving occluded objects, and differentiating object compliance (e.g., ripe vs. overripe bananas).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, in-the-wild, and multimodal haptic data is crucial for developing generalizable robot perception models that can understand object properties beyond geometry, such as material and compliance.
> - Low-cost, open-source hardware combined with a crowdsourcing strategy can overcome scalability challenges in haptic data collection, enabling the creation of diverse and realistic datasets from non-expert users.
> - The fusion of haptic and visual modalities offers complementary cues, leading to substantially improved accuracy in object property recognition and better generalization to novel objects and robot embodiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robust robot manipulation in unstructured environments necessitates an understanding of object properties that extend beyond geometric characteristics, such as material composition and compliance. While vision-based systems excel at geometry and pose estimation, inferring properties like material or compliance reliably through vision alone presents challenges. Haptic sensing offers a complementary pathway for perceiving these properties, both independently and when integrated with visual information. However, the utilization of haptics in robotics has been comparatively limited when contrasted with vision or language modalities.

Prior research in haptic sensing and robot manipulation has been constrained by several factors:
1. **Limited Multimodality:** Many existing haptic datasets primarily focus on a single modality, such often contact force. Haptic perception, however, is inherently multimodal, involving temperature, force, vibration, and proprioception. Research suggests that incorporating multiple haptic modalities can enhance the recognition of object properties.
2. **Scalability Challenges:** Haptic data acquisition requires physical interaction, making it a resource-intensive process. Generating large-scale datasets from multiple users requires standardized, easy-to-build, and user-friendly sensing hardware, which has been an area of development.
3. **Lack of Diversity and Realism:** The majority of haptic datasets are collected in controlled laboratory settings with a pre-defined set of objects. These conditions do not fully capture the diversity and variability of real-world haptic interactions or the characteristics of heterogeneous household objects (e.g., objects composed of multiple materials).
4. **Limited Grasping Interaction Data:** Haptic sensing is action-conditioned, meaning the sensor readings depend on the interaction dynamics. Many datasets emphasize non-prehensile interaction with single-finger contacts, which differs from the data generated during real-world grasping actions typical in robot manipulation.

This work addresses these limitations by developing a novel data acquisition device and subsequently collecting a large-scale, multimodal, in-the-wild haptic dataset through crowdsourcing. The objective is to facilitate the development of generalizable perception models that can enable diverse robot manipulation capabilities in realistic environments, thereby expanding the utility of haptic perception in robotics beyond controlled laboratory settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized need for robots to possess a comprehensive understanding of object properties, such as material and compliance, for effective manipulation in complex, unstructured environments. Existing perception systems, often reliant solely on vision, face inherent difficulties in reliably inferring these properties. While multimodal haptic sensing holds significant promise for addressing this gap, its full potential has been constrained by a lack of suitable datasets—specifically, datasets that are large-scale, diverse, and representative of real-world interactions.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work were:

1. **Device Development:** To design and create the CLAMP device, a low-cost (under $200), open-source, sensorized reacher-grabber. This device was specifically engineered for ease of assembly, portability, and user-friendliness to enable non-expert users to collect multimodal haptic data in their everyday household settings. The aim was to capture five distinct haptic modalities (active thermal, passive thermal, force, vibration, and proprioception) alongside visual and speech annotations.
2. **Dataset Construction:** To leverage the deployed CLAMP devices to crowdsource and compile the CLAMP dataset. This objective aimed to create the largest open-source multimodal haptic dataset to date, characterized by its scale (millions of data points), diversity (thousands of in-situ household objects), and realism (collected in-the-wild by non-expert users performing grasping actions).
3. **Model Development for Object Property Recognition:** To utilize the CLAMP dataset to train a haptic encoder and subsequently develop the CLAMP model, a visuo-haptic perception model. A central goal was to enable robust inference of object material and compliance, with a focus on generalization to novel objects.
4. **Demonstration of Robot Generalizability and Manipulation Capabilities:** To evaluate the CLAMP model's ability to generalize across different robot embodiments and gripper types with minimal finetuning. Furthermore, a critical objective was to demonstrate the practical utility of this multimodal perception capability in real-world robot manipulation tasks, such as waste sorting, object retrieval under occlusion, and distinguishing between visually similar objects based on compliance.

Ultimately, the research sought to demonstrate that large-scale, multimodal, in-the-wild haptic data collection can unlock new capabilities for generalizable robot manipulation, thereby advancing the field towards more capable and adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach involving the development of a novel sensing device, a crowdsourced data collection strategy, advanced data processing techniques, and a multimodal deep learning model.

#### 3.1. The CLAMP Device

The core of the data collection methodology is the CLAMP device, a low-cost, sensorized reacher-grabber.
- **Design Principles:** The device was engineered to be "Easy to Build" (assemble within 5 hours using readily available sensors), "Easy to Carry" (lightweight at 0.59 kg, portable), "Easy to Use" (features a GUI with on-screen prompts and LED feedback for contact), and "Easy to Scale" (costs less than $200 per unit, allowing for broad deployment).
- **Sensing Modalities:** It captures five haptic modalities:
 - **Active Thermal:** A 10 kΩ NTC thermistor heated to 55 °C, measuring heat capacity upon contact.
 - **Passive Thermal:** A 10 kΩ NTC thermistor, recording ambient object temperature.
 - **Force:** An Interlink UX 402 force-sensing resistor (FSR) measuring normal force up to 150 N.
 - **Vibration:** A 20 mm piezo disc (contact microphone) with a MAX4466 amplifier, detecting audio signals from contact.
 - **Proprioception:** Two 6-axis MPU6050 IMUs; one on the end-effector and one on the device body frame, enabling isolation of end-effector motion and calculation of relative angular velocity.
 - The device also includes a camera for visual data and a microphone for speech annotations.
- **Compute and Storage:** A Raspberry Pi Pico microcontroller (Pico) interfaces with the sensors at 50 Hz (100 Hz for microphone), transferring data to a Raspberry Pi Zero 2W (SBC) via UART for on-board storage.
- **User Interface:** An Adafruit 2.2” PiTFT Display with four buttons and a PyGame-powered GUI guides non-expert users through the data collection process, providing real-time contact feedback via LEDs.

#### 3.2. The CLAMP Dataset

The CLAMP dataset was built by crowdsourcing data from 16 CLAMP devices deployed to 41 non-expert users.
- **Data Collection Process:** Users followed a GUI-guided protocol for each object: capturing an image, recording a spoken annotation, and performing five haptic grasping trials, each up to 10 seconds.
- **Data Post-processing and Featurization:**
 1. All sensor data streams were synchronized based on timestamps (within 2 ms).
 2. Raw voltage readings from thermal sensors were converted to °C.
 3. Data from thermal sensors were passed through a Butterworth filter; IMU data through a causal moving average filter.
 4. Nine features were extracted: smoothed readings from active/passive thermal sensors, force sensor, contact microphone, relative angular velocity from IMUs, differences in active thermal, passive thermal, and force readings between consecutive timesteps, and an impedance feature (force difference divided by angular velocity).
 5. Contact onset and release for each suction cup were detected using rule-based thresholds on active thermal differences and force readings.
 6. All features were padded to a uniform length of 491 timesteps.
- **Object Property Annotations:** Audio transcriptions (via Whisper) and object images were fed into GPT-4o, along with in-context examples, to generate three types of labels:
 1. One of 16 material labels (e.g., aluminium, brass, cardboard, hard plastic, wood), derived from CES Edupack subcategories and covering 93% of objects.
 2. A binary label indicating whether the object had heterogeneous surfaces.
 3. An adjective label for compliance (soft, hard). Expert annotators verified these labels.
- **Dataset Filtering:** For model training, specific data points were excluded, including contact instances with the smallest material classes (granite, dry wall), those with non-zero initial force, low initial active thermal temperature, erroneous sensor readings, very slow grasps, or objects with heterogeneous surfaces.
- **Dataset Statistics:** The processed CLAMP dataset comprises 12.3 million data samples across 25,100 object touches from 5,357 unique household objects, exhibiting diversity in material, grasping forces, and speeds.

#### 3.3. The CLAMP Model

The CLAMP model is a multimodal perception model designed for object material recognition, fusing visual and haptic information.
- **Haptic Encoder:** This component consists of a six-block InceptionTime model followed by a global average pooling layer and a three-layer MLP for classification. It was trained on the CLAMP dataset's haptic features for material classification.
- **Visual Encoder:** GPT-4o serves as a low-dimensional visual encoder. It processes input images, audio transcriptions, and in-context examples to output a filtered and normalized categorical distribution over material classes. A two-step prompting approach was used.
- **Visuo-Haptic Fusion:** The CLAMP model combines the outputs of the haptic and visual encoders using a 2-layer MLP. During training, the pretrained haptic encoder was frozen, and a loss function combining a weighted cross-entropy loss and a KL-divergence loss (penalizing deviations from vision probabilities) was minimized.
- **Uncertainty Quantification:** The model was designed to quantify uncertainty in its predictions, flagging instances where softmax values were below a threshold or the difference between the top two softmax values was small.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the device, dataset, and model performance, as well as its application to robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. CLAMP Dataset Contributions

The CLAMP dataset is reported as the largest open-source multimodal haptic dataset to date. It contains 12.3 million data samples, derived from 25,100 object touches across 5,357 distinct household objects. The dataset captures 7 modalities in total: 5 haptic modalities (active thermal, passive thermal, force, vibration, proprioception), alongside vision and structured language annotations. This scale and diversity address limitations present in previous haptic datasets.

#### 4.2. CLAMP Device Efficacy

The design principles of the CLAMP device (easy to build, carry, use, and scale) facilitated its deployment to 41 non-expert users. This crowdsourcing approach enabled the collection of a large volume of in-the-wild haptic data from diverse household objects, validating the device's utility for large-scale data acquisition.

#### 4.3. Haptic Encoder Performance for Material Recognition

- An InceptionTime-based haptic encoder achieved an accuracy of 0.59 (± 0.01) and a normalized Matthews correlation coefficient (nMCC) of 0.76 (± 0.006) for material recognition. This performance surpassed an alternative Random Forest encoder, which scored 0.52 accuracy and 0.71 nMCC.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the complementary nature of the haptic modalities. Removing any single modality resulted in a decrease in performance, with the removal of 'Force' leading to the most significant drop (accuracy decreasing to 0.22, nMCC to 0.57), indicating its importance.

#### 4.4. Visual Encoder Performance

Among the vision-only models tested, GPT-4o with raw image input achieved the highest material recognition performance (0.65 accuracy, 0.80 nMCC). CLIP and PG-VLM models generally exhibited lower performance.

#### 4.5. CLAMP Model (Visuo-Haptic Fusion) Performance

The CLAMP model, which fuses haptic and visual information, attained a material recognition accuracy of 0.87 and an nMCC of 0.93. This represents a substantial improvement over both the best vision-only baseline (GPT-4o raw: 0.65 accuracy) and the haptic-only encoder (InceptionTime: 0.59 accuracy), suggesting that vision and haptics provide complementary cues. The CLAMP model also outperformed a CLIP-based visuo-haptic fusion strategy.

#### 4.6. Generalization to Novel Objects

When tested on a held-out set of novel objects, the CLAMP model maintained its advantage, achieving an accuracy of 0.73 and an nMCC of 0.85, compared to 0.70 accuracy and 0.83 nMCC for the vision-only baseline. This indicates the model's ability to generalize to previously unencountered object instances.

#### 4.7. Transferability to Compliance Recognition

The haptic encoder, initially trained for material classification, demonstrated transferability to object compliance recognition (soft/hard). With a new classification head, it achieved an accuracy of 0.88 and an nMCC of 0.869 on the test set, indicating that the learned haptic representations are applicable across different object properties.

#### 4.8. Uncertainty Quantification

The CLAMP model's ability to quantify uncertainty was evaluated. By filtering out predictions below certain confidence thresholds (p1=0.45 for softmax value, p2=0.25 for difference between top two softmax values), the test accuracy improved from 0.73 to 0.82 and nMCC from 0.85 to 0.90, while retaining 75% of the predictions.

#### 4.9. Generalization to Robot Embodiments

The pretrained CLAMP model demonstrated generalization across three distinct robot embodiments: a Franka Panda with a CLAMP device, a Franka Panda with parallel jaw grippers, and a WidowX 250s with parallel jaw grippers. With 15% finetuning data from the specific robot embodiment, the models for all three robots surpassed vision-only performance for material recognition. For example, the Franka with Parallel Jaw embodiment achieved 0.93 accuracy and 0.96 nMCC with 15% finetuning data, compared to 0.77 accuracy for vision-only. Performance generally improved with increased finetuning data.

#### 4.10. Real-world Robot Manipulation Tasks

The finetuned CLAMP model was deployed in three real-world robotic manipulation scenarios using a Franka Panda with parallel jaw grippers:
- **Sorting Recyclable Waste:** The robot successfully sorted 9 out of 10 unseen objects into "Trash" or "Recycle" bins per trial over three trials, while making uncertain predictions in 0.66 out of 10 instances. This demonstrated accurate material recognition in cluttered environments.
- **Retrieving Objects from a Cluttered Bag:** In a visually challenging scenario with occluded objects inside a cloth bag, the finetuned CLAMP model retrieved a metallic object in 6 out of 13 trials, whereas a vision-only model failed to identify any metallic objects in the same number of trials. This highlighted the role of haptics under visual ambiguity.
- **Sorting Ripe from Overripe Bananas:** The haptic encoder, transferred to compliance prediction and finetuned on robot data, successfully differentiated ripe (hard) from overripe (soft) bananas with an accuracy of 0.833 and an nMCC of 0.86. This demonstrated fine-grained object property discrimination for visually similar items.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions to the field of robotics and multimodal perception, with considerable potential impact.

**Addressing Data Scarcity in Haptics:** The development and release of the CLAMP dataset represent a substantial advancement in addressing the historical lack of large, diverse, and realistic haptic datasets. With 12.3 million data samples from over 5,000 household objects, it is positioned as the largest open-source multimodal haptic dataset available. This resource can serve as a foundational tool for researchers, enabling the training and evaluation of more robust haptic and visuo-haptic perception models.

**Enabling Robust and Generalizable Robot Perception:** The CLAMP model demonstrates that integrating large-scale, in-the-wild haptic data with visual information significantly enhances the accuracy of material and compliance recognition. The model's ability to generalize to novel objects and transfer across different robot embodiments with minimal finetuning is a key achievement. This indicates a move towards more adaptable and less domain-specific robot perception systems, which is critical for deploying robots in varied, unstructured environments.

**Unlocking New Robot Manipulation Capabilities:** The successful demonstration of the CLAMP model in real-world robot manipulation tasks highlights its practical utility. Tasks such as sorting recyclable waste, retrieving objects from cluttered and occluded bags, and distinguishing between ripe and overripe produce showcase how multimodal haptic perception can empower robots with capabilities previously challenging to achieve with vision alone. These applications have direct relevance for automation in households, logistics, and quality control.

**Advancing Multimodal Learning in Robotics:** The work underscores the complementary nature of vision and haptics for comprehensive object understanding. By providing a framework for collecting and leveraging multimodal data, it encourages further research into fusion strategies, representation learning, and the development of integrated perception systems that mimic human sensory capabilities more closely.

**Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing the CLAMP device's design, the dataset, and the code lowers barriers to entry for researchers, fostering collaboration and accelerating progress in haptic robotics. The low-cost, easy-to-build nature of the device promotes wider adoption and diverse data collection efforts.

**Future Directions:** The study also outlines future research avenues, including exploring more powerful microprocessors for richer data collection (e.g., video, higher-frequency haptic data), refining sensor design for consistent contact with varied object geometries, scaling the dataset further to train "visuo-haptic foundation models," integrating data from additional sensor types (e.g., vision-based tactile sensors), and developing end-to-end models that process raw visuo-haptic inputs without explicit featurization. These directions suggest a pathway toward even more sophisticated and autonomous robotic manipulation in real-world settings.
