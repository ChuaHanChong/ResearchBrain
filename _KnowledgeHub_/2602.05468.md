# TaSA: Two-Phased Deep Predictive Learning of Tactile Sensory Attenuation for Improving In-Grasp Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Waseda University developed TaSA, a two-phased deep predictive learning framework that enables robots to perform tactile sensory attenuation for in-grasp manipulation. The system learns to distinguish between self-contact and external object contact, leading to significantly higher success rates in contact-rich precision insertion tasks, with absolute improvements ranging from 24% to 32% over a baseline method.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to accurately interpret tactile feedback during multi-contact in-hand manipulation, often conflating self-contact (fingers touching each other) with external object contact.
- Current robotic manipulation systems typically ignore self-contact or treat all tactile input uniformly, limiting their generalization and robustness in complex environments.
- The absence of a mechanism akin to human sensory attenuation leads to misinterpretations, unstable grasps, and potential damage during delicate tasks.

## Method

- A two-phase deep learning framework (TaSA) is introduced, starting with a 'Self-Touch Learning Phase' where a Fully Connected Network (FCN) predicts self-generated tactile signals based on robot joint positions.
- In the subsequent 'Motion Learning Phase,' a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network integrates the frozen self-touch FCN's predictions, allowing it to attenuate predictable self-touch signals from raw tactile input.
- The framework is applied to an Allegro Hand equipped with uSkin tactile sensors and evaluated on three precision insertion tasks: paper clip fixing, coin insertion, and pencil lead insertion.

## Results

- The self-touch FCN accurately predicted self-generated tactile signals during contact, with high correlation coefficients (r 

0.96 for thumb, r 

0.98 for index), demonstrating effective self-touch cancellation.
- TaSA (RT+Self) substantially improved task success rates over a baseline (RT only): 95% vs. 70% for paper clip fixing, 92% vs. 68% for coin insertion, and 58% vs. 26% for pencil lead insertion.
- Principal Component Analysis showed that attenuating self-touch reduced nuisance variance in the feature space, leading to clearer separation and increased inter-centroid distance between different object states and placements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mimicking human sensory attenuation, where predictable self-generated sensory input is filtered out, is critical for robots to robustly perceive and interact with external objects during contact-rich manipulation.
> - Explicitly modeling and learning self-touch dynamics allows a deep learning system to effectively disambiguate internal robot-robot contact from external object-robot contact.
> - Integrating a predictive self-touch model into a recurrent neural network for motion control enhances the clarity and discriminability of external tactile features, improving manipulation performance.
