# DynamicRAG: Leveraging Outputs of Large Language Model as Feedback for Dynamic Reranking in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign introduce DynamicRAG, a framework that enhances Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) by dynamically reranking and selecting documents based on feedback from the Large Language Model's generation quality. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse knowledge-intensive tasks while significantly improving computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems struggle with irrelevant retrieved documents misleading the LLM and the 'lost-in-the-middle' phenomenon where crucial information is buried.
- Determining the optimal number of documents ('k') for rerankers is challenging, as fixed 'k' values either miss vital information or introduce noise and increase computational cost.
- Existing LLM-based rerankers often overlook the actual quality of the LLM's generated response as a direct feedback signal for optimizing reranking decisions.

## Method

- **DynamicRAG** models the reranker as a reinforcement learning (RL) agent that dynamically adjusts both the order and the number of documents passed to the generator.
- A two-stage training process is employed, starting with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) via behavioral cloning for initial reranker training, followed by refinement using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
- A comprehensive, multi-dimensional reward function is designed to evaluate the LLM's generated response quality, integrating Exact Match, Semantic Similarity, Textual Fluency, Length Penalty, and LLM-Based Evaluation.

## Results

- DynamicRAG achieves state-of-the-art performance across seven knowledge-intensive datasets, with a LLaMA3-**8B** backbone surpassing powerful non-retrieval LLMs like GPT-4o (e.g., 48.4 EM on NQ).
- The framework demonstrates superior efficiency, requiring only two LLM calls for reranking and generation, and offering approximately **17x** higher throughput compared to sequential scoring methods.
- Ablation studies confirm that retrieval, reranking, and particularly the reinforcement learning optimization guided by generation quality feedback, are crucial for the system's performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality of the LLM's generated output serves as a direct and potent supervisory signal for optimizing reranking decisions in an end-to-end manner.
> - Treating the reranker as an RL agent allows for dynamic adjustment of document selection ('k') and ordering, mitigating fixed 'k' limitations and improving adaptability.
> - A combined training approach using behavioral cloning for initial policy acquisition and DPO for fine-tuning with generation quality feedback effectively guides the reranker to optimal strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems have revolutionized how Large Language Models (LLMs) handle knowledge-intensive tasks, providing them with access to external, up-to-date knowledge and mitigating issues like hallucination. By integrating a retriever (to find relevant documents) and a generator (the LLM to synthesize information), RAG systems have become a cornerstone of practical LLM applications. However, RAG systems face inherent challenges. Chief among these are the potential for **irrelevant retrieved documents to mislead the LLM** and the "**lost-in-the-middle**" phenomenon, where crucial information is buried within an excessively long list of retrieved passages, diminishing the LLM's ability to utilize it effectively.

The **reranker** is a critical, yet often underexplored, component designed to address these issues. Its role is to refine the initial set of documents retrieved by selecting the most relevant `k` documents for the generator. A major challenge for rerankers has been determining the **optimal number of documents (`k`)** to pass to the LLM. A `k` that is too small risks omitting vital information, leading to incomplete or incorrect answers, while a `k` that is too large introduces noise, increases computational cost, and exacerbates the 'lost-in-the-middle' problem. Existing reranking approaches often rely on fixed `k` values, which fail to adapt to the varying complexity and information needs of different queries.

Recent advancements have seen the emergence of **LLM-based rerankers**, which leverage the sophisticated understanding capabilities of LLMs to assess document relevance. While these models have shown promise, they primarily depend on the LLM's internal knowledge to score documents independently or use predefined selection thresholds. A significant limitation of these existing LLM-based rerankers is their oversight of a crucial supervisory signal: **the actual quality of the LLM's generated response**. This direct feedback on generation quality, given a particular set of reranked documents, provides a powerful and natural reward signal that can guide the optimization of reranking decisions.

DynamicRAG addresses this gap by positioning itself at the forefront of adaptive RAG systems. It moves beyond static reranking and fixed `k` thresholds by introducing a framework where the reranker dynamically adjusts both the order and number of documents based on direct feedback from the LLM's generation quality. By modeling the reranker as an agent optimized through reinforcement learning, DynamicRAG integrates the reranking process directly into the end-to-end performance of the RAG system, making it a more intelligent, adaptive, and efficient component. This work thus represents a significant step towards more robust and context-aware RAG architectures that can dynamically tailor their retrieval strategies to optimize downstream generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DynamicRAG is to develop a novel RAG framework that features a **dynamic reranker capable of adaptively adjusting both the order and the number (`k`) of retrieved documents** based on the specific query and the feedback from the Large Language Model's generation. This dynamic adjustment aims to optimize the input provided to the LLM, thereby maximizing the quality and efficiency of its generated responses.

The key motivations driving this research are:

- **Addressing the "Optimal `k` Problem"**: A central challenge in RAG systems is determining the appropriate number of documents (`k`) for the reranker to select. The authors highlight that a `k` that is too small can lead to critical information being missed, while a `k` that is too large introduces irrelevant noise, increases computational load, and can degrade generation quality due to the 'lost-in-the-middle' phenomenon. DynamicRAG seeks to overcome this by allowing `k` to be contextually determined.
- **Improving Generation Quality and Explainability**: Irrelevant documents can mislead the generator, leading to incorrect or unhelpful outputs. By dynamically selecting and ordering only the most relevant documents, the system aims to improve the factual accuracy, coherence, and overall quality of the generated text, while also making the generation process more explainable by providing a refined set of supporting evidence.
- **Leveraging LLM Output Quality as Direct Feedback**: Existing LLM-based rerankers often rely on internal model knowledge or static relevance scores. The authors identify a crucial oversight: the actual quality of the LLM's generated response provides a direct and natural supervisory signal for optimizing reranking decisions. This insight motivates the use of reinforcement learning (RL) where generation quality serves as a reward for the reranker's actions.
- **Enhancing Efficiency**: By intelligently pruning irrelevant documents and selecting an optimal `k`, DynamicRAG aims to reduce the context length provided to the generator, thereby improving inference speed and reducing computational costs without sacrificing accuracy.

In essence, DynamicRAG is motivated by the need for a more intelligent and adaptive reranking mechanism within RAG systems that can dynamically optimize document selection by directly learning from the success (or failure) of the downstream generation task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DynamicRAG proposes a novel RAG framework that models the reranker as an agent, optimized through reinforcement learning (RL), using rewards derived from the quality of the LLM's output. The framework consists of three main components: a frozen retriever, a trainable dynamic reranker, and a trainable generator.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Reranking Mechanism

Unlike traditional rerankers that output a reordered fixed-size list, DynamicRAG's reranker dynamically adjusts both the order and the *number* of documents (`k`) passed to the generator. Given a query `q` and an initial set of retrieved documents `D`, the reranker `Reranker_θr(q, D)` produces a reordered subset `ˆD ⊂ D` with a dynamically determined size. This formulation allows for greater flexibility in accommodating diverse queries and the specific needs of the generation task. The reranking process is treated as a list-wise ranking problem.

#### 3.2. Training Paradigm

The training of DynamicRAG is a two-stage process (illustrated in Figure 3 and Algorithm 1):

- **Stage 1: Behavioral Cloning (SFT) for Initial Reranker Training**
 - The reranker is initially trained using **supervised fine-tuning (SFT)** via behavioral cloning. This stage provides the reranker with a foundational understanding of dynamic document selection and ordering.
 - The reranker is trained on a dataset of expert demonstrations (`De`) to mimic optimal document selection decisions. The objective `J_BC(θ)` maximizes the likelihood of the expert's choice of `k` documents given a query and retrieved documents.
 - For this, MonoT5 is employed as the expert model to filter a subset of data. The number of documents `k` is constrained to a maximum of 15 during this phase, aiming for a fair comparison with existing studies that often use Top-10 documents.
 - Concurrently, the generator (`π_θg`) is trained via SFT on a normal dataset (`D_train`), maximizing the likelihood of generating correct responses given a query and *reranked* documents.

- **Stage 2: Optimizing the Reranker via Exploration and Feedback (Reinforcement Learning)**
 - After behavioral cloning, the reranker is further refined through interactive learning, where the **generator acts as an environment** providing feedback.
 - The reranker (`π_θr`) explores the action space by sampling different trajectories (sets of `k` documents). For each trajectory, the generator produces a response.
 - The quality of the generator's response serves as a **reward signal** for the reranker's actions.
 - To optimize the reranker's policy, the **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** framework is employed. DPO simplifies reward optimization by using pairwise comparisons of trajectories.
 - From the sampled trajectories and their associated rewards, the system identifies a "preferred" trajectory (`τ+`, with the highest reward) and a "dispreferred" trajectory (`τ−`, with the lowest reward).
 - The DPO objective `J_DPO(θ)` then encourages the reranker to assign higher probabilities to trajectories that lead to higher rewards. This iteratively refines the reranker's policy to maximize the expected reward.

#### 3.3. Reward Function Design

To comprehensively evaluate the quality of the LLM's generated response (`ˆy`) against the ground-truth answer (`y_gt`), a multi-dimensional reward function `r(G, i, τ)` is designed. This function integrates five key aspects:
- **Exact Match (EM)**: Binary score for exact match with `y_gt`.
- **Semantic Similarity (SS)**: Uses BERTScore to measure semantic alignment.
- **Textual Fluency (TF)**: Evaluated using ROUGE metrics.
- **Length Penalty (LP)**: Penalizes longer responses to encourage conciseness.
- **LLM-Based Evaluation (LLM-Eval)**: Uses an LLM to score alignment with task requirements (details in Appendix C.3).
These components are weighted by coefficients (α, β, γ, λ, δ, initially set to 0.2 each) to provide a holistic assessment.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Base LLMs**: LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA2-13B, and LLaMA3-8B are used as backbone models for both the reranker and generator.
- **Retriever**: A frozen, off-the-shelf Contriever-MS MARCO model is used to retrieve an initial Top-N documents (up to 45 for LLaMA3, 20 for LLaMA2).
- **Training Data**: 150k diverse instruction-output pairs from Alpaca, KILT (NQ, FEVER, HotpotQA, ELI5, TriviaQA), ASQA, and OpenBookQA. 20k for reranker cold-start, 100k for generator SFT, and 30k for DPO training.
- **Hardware & Settings**: Trained on 8 Nvidia A100 GPUs, using DeepSpeed Stage 3 and Bfloat16 precision. FlashAttention is used for long context efficiency.
- **Inference**: Temperature 0.2 for reranker (diversity), 0 for generator (stability). Uses vLLMs for acceleration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DynamicRAG demonstrates superior performance and efficiency across a wide range of knowledge-intensive tasks, establishing new state-of-the-art results for models of similar parameter sizes.

#### 4.1. Comparison Against Baselines (with and without Retrieval)

- **Outperformance of Non-Retrieval LLMs**: DynamicRAG significantly outperforms powerful non-retrieval LLMs like GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4, and even GPT-4o across multiple datasets. For instance, with LLaMA3-8B, DynamicRAG achieves an EM score of 48.4 on NQ, surpassing GPT-4o. This highlights the substantial benefits of the retrieval-augmented approach when coupled with dynamic reranking.
- **State-of-the-Art Among RAG Baselines**: DynamicRAG consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance against various agent-based (e.g., IRCoT, ReAct, FLARE, Reward-RAG) and other retrieval-based baselines (e.g., Self-RAG, DRAGIN, Smart-RAG, ChatQA-1.5, RankRAG).
- **Data Efficiency**: Notably, DynamicRAG often achieves superior performance with significantly less training data (e.g., ~150k examples for DynamicRAG vs. ~470k for RankRAG), demonstrating its efficiency in learning from data.
- **Scalability and Robustness**: The effectiveness of DynamicRAG is consistent across different backbone LLM sizes (LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA2-13B, LLaMA3-8B), indicating its scalability and robust design.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Reranking Baselines

- DynamicRAG (LLaMA3-8B) shows highly competitive reranking performance, achieving an average R@10 of 73.7 across NQ and HotpotQA, which is comparable to or surpasses leading models like RankRAG (72.5) while using only 20k training samples for the reranker, compared to RankRAG's 50k.
- The approach significantly outperforms other open-source rerankers (e.g., monoT5, RankLLaMA) and generative rerankers (e.g., Re3eval), particularly due to its list-wise reranking strategy and the use of generated response quality as a direct feedback signal.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Reranker and Generator Size**: Performance generally improves with larger model sizes. A configuration where the Reranker and Generator share parameters (`LLaMA2-13B* LLaMA2-13B*`) shows particular benefit, suggesting mutual enhancement from shared learning.
- **Effect of Key Components**:
 - **Retrieval**: Removing retrieval causes a drastic performance drop (e.g., average EM to 22.1), emphasizing its critical role.
 - **Reranking**: Excluding reranking also degrades performance, confirming its beneficial effect.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Removing RL leads to a noticeable performance decrease (average EM to 39.9), highlighting that the RL optimization, guided by generation quality feedback, is crucial for maximizing performance.
- **Performance with Different Retrievers**: DynamicRAG's performance scales positively with the quality of the underlying retriever (DPR, Contriever, MonoT5), demonstrating the robustness and compatibility of the framework with various retrieval methods.

#### 4.4. Model Analysis (Efficiency)

- **LLM Calls**: DynamicRAG requires only **two LLM calls** (one for reranking, one for generation) when the retriever returns 20 documents, placing it in the top-left quadrant of an efficiency-effectiveness plot. This is significantly more efficient than many agent-based RAG models that require multiple LLM interactions.
- **Token Perspective**: The contextual integration approach (processing Top-N documents once to determine Top-K) offers **~17x superior throughput** compared to sequential scoring methods (like RankRAG's assumed implementation). Furthermore, DynamicRAG introduces minimal computational overhead (2.3x latency increase) compared to vanilla RAG without reranking, while delivering substantial performance gains (e.g., 9.6 percentage point EM improvement over LLaMA2-7B vanilla RAG on NQ).
- **Reranked Document Distribution**: Analysis shows that before RL training, the reranker tended to select a higher `k` (e.g., 14 or 15). After RL training, especially with the length penalty, the distribution shifts towards fewer documents (peaks at 12, 13, 14), demonstrating that RL effectively optimizes `k` for efficiency without sacrificing quality.
- **Closed-Source Model Enhancement (Appendix B.3)**: Experiments in the appendix reveal that the Dynamic Reranker module consistently improves the performance of closed-source models like GPT-4o across NQ, HotpotQA, and ASQA, confirming the broad applicability and benefit of the dynamic reranking mechanism even for powerful proprietary LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DynamicRAG represents a significant advancement in the field of Retrieval-Augmented Generation, offering a novel and effective solution to long-standing challenges in RAG system design.

**6.1 Novelty and Methodological Innovation**: The core novelty lies in modeling the reranker as a reinforcement learning agent that leverages the *quality of the LLM's generated output* as a direct reward signal. This moves beyond static reranking heuristics or independent relevance scoring, introducing a truly adaptive mechanism that dynamically tunes both the order and number of retrieved documents (`k`) to optimize end-to-end performance. The two-stage training process, combining behavioral cloning with DPO, is a robust and effective way to teach the reranker complex, context-dependent selection strategies.

**6.2 Enhanced Performance and Reliability**: By dynamically adjusting the input documents, DynamicRAG effectively mitigates issues like the "optimal `k` problem" and the "lost-in-the-middle" phenomenon. The reported state-of-the-art performance across seven diverse knowledge-intensive datasets (including QA, multi-hop reasoning, long-form generation, and fact verification) demonstrates a substantial improvement in the factual accuracy, relevance, and overall quality of LLM-generated responses. This directly translates to more reliable and trustworthy RAG applications.

**6.3 Significant Efficiency Gains**: Beyond improved accuracy, DynamicRAG also brings notable efficiency benefits. By intelligently pruning irrelevant documents and selecting a minimal yet sufficient set, it reduces the context window size for the generator. This leads to fewer LLM calls (only two for reranking and generation) and significantly faster processing times (e.g., 17x faster than sequential scoring methods), reducing computational costs and enabling faster inference. This is crucial for deploying RAG systems in real-world, latency-sensitive applications.

**6.4 Broad Applicability and Robustness**: The framework's consistent performance across different open-source LLM backbones (LLaMA2/3) and its proven ability to enhance even powerful closed-source models like GPT-4o (as shown in the appendix) highlight its broad applicability. This suggests that DynamicRAG is a generalizable solution that can benefit various RAG implementations, regardless of the specific LLM or retriever used. The robustness to different retriever choices further underscores its versatility.

**6.5 Future Research Directions**: This work opens several promising avenues for future research. The RL-based approach could be extended to explore more sophisticated agent behaviors, such as iterative retrieval or multi-step reasoning, where the reranker's decisions influence subsequent retrieval actions. The multi-dimensional reward function could be further refined with more nuanced metrics or learned reward models. The paper explicitly mentions leaving "observation-based optimization for future work" in Section 2.2, implying potential for more granular, step-wise reward signals within the reranking process. Ultimately, DynamicRAG provides a strong foundation for developing more intelligent, autonomous, and efficient RAG systems that can adapt dynamically to complex information needs.
