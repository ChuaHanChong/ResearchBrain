# DIM-WAM: World-Action Modeling with Diverse Historical Event Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CASIA, Shenzhen Yinwang, and FiveAges developed DiM-WAM, a World Action Model (WAM) featuring a Diverse Historical Event Memory (DHEM) to enhance robot manipulation in long-horizon tasks. This approach improved the average full-task success rate on RMBench simulation tasks to 69.8% from 34.8% for a training-matched baseline, and raised the average success rate on real-world Franka Panda tasks to 90.0% from 52.5%, by effectively leveraging historical context to resolve temporal ambiguities.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) primarily rely on current observations or short local contexts, limiting their effectiveness in long-horizon robot manipulation tasks.
- Complex tasks involve temporal ambiguities where visually similar local observations require different actions based on extensive historical context.
- Current WAMs lack effective mechanisms to retain and leverage diverse categories of task-relevant historical information (e.g., interaction occurrence, subtask completion, object states) over extended periods.

## Method

- **DiM-WAM** integrates a ==Diverse Historical Event Memory (DHEM)== with K parallel memory banks, each with N bounded slots, to store and manage historical event tokens.
- A ==novelty-aware, redundancy-based compression== mechanism with ==mass-weighted fusion== is used to maintain each bank's bounded capacity while preserving accumulated evidence and chronological order.
- An ==auxiliary task-progress-aware training objective==, along with a ==diversity loss==, guides DHEM to encode coarse task progress cues and encourages banks to specialize in complementary event patterns.

## Results

- DiM-WAM improved the average full-task success rate on RMBench simulation tasks from **34.8%** (LingBot-VA baseline) to **69.8%**, achieving the highest success rate on **8 out of 9** tasks.
- On real-world Franka Panda tasks, DiM-WAM increased the average full-task success rate from **52.5%** to **90.0%**, with significant gains (e.g., **80** percentage points on Find Blue Block).
- Ablation studies showed that increasing the number of memory banks (while fixing or increasing capacity) significantly improved success rates (**75.5%→96.5%**), and the auxiliary progress supervision further enhanced performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Organizing historical information into multiple, implicitly specialized memory banks, inspired by human cognition, can prevent destructive interference and facilitate retention of complementary event patterns in WAMs.
> - Coupling action learning with future visual prediction (WAMs' dense supervision) provides rich signals that facilitate learning diverse historical event representations for effective memory.
> - Actively managing memory through novelty-aware compression and mass-weighted fusion allows for efficient retention of critical, diverse historical events in a bounded capacity without simply enlarging context windows.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control and manipulation is increasingly leveraging Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which learn robot policies by predicting actions based on language instructions and visual observations. While VLAs enable scalable policy learning, they often face limitations due to sparse action labels, providing insufficient supervision for fine-grained manipulation dynamics.

World Action Models (WAMs) address this limitation by augmenting action prediction with future visual state prediction. This joint formulation provides dense temporal supervision, enabling models to learn not only executable actions but also the anticipated visual consequences of those actions. Existing WAMs have improved future prediction, visual-action coupling, and inference efficiency. However, they typically rely on current observations or short local contexts. This reliance restricts their applicability to long-horizon tasks, where correct actions often depend on understanding the sequence of past events, subtask completion, or object states that may not be immediately visible or inferable from recent sensory input.

This paper positions DiM-WAM within this landscape by identifying a critical gap: the lack of effective mechanisms for WAMs to retain and leverage diverse historical information over extended temporal horizons. While memory-augmented VLAs exist, they primarily focus on action inference and may not provide sufficient signals for discovering and organizing heterogeneous historical events within the richer visual-action coupling of WAMs. Similarly, long-video generation models have explored memory for visual consistency, but WAM memory requires encoding task progress, object states, and action consequences for closed-loop control. DiM-WAM aims to integrate a sophisticated memory mechanism directly into the WAM framework, leveraging WAMs' dense visual-action supervision to facilitate the learning of diverse, task-relevant historical event representations, thereby extending their capabilities to long-horizon, memory-dependent manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of existing World Action Models (WAMs) in handling long-horizon robot manipulation tasks. Current WAMs, despite their advantages in providing dense temporal supervision through joint visual and action prediction, primarily rely on short local contexts. This reliance leads to significant challenges when task execution requires recalling information from earlier stages or understanding the overall task progress.

The specific problems and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Temporal Ambiguity in Long-Horizon Tasks:** Many complex manipulation tasks involve stages where visually similar local observations can require fundamentally different actions depending on the historical context. For example, the paper illustrates a water-dispenser task where interacting with a switch to turn it "on" or "off" might appear visually similar at different stages. A WAM operating solely on immediate visual input or a short temporal window would struggle to disambiguate these situations, potentially leading to incorrect actions or premature task termination. Resolving such ambiguities necessitates access to key historical events and cues about overall task progress.

- **Need for Diverse Event Retention:** Long-horizon tasks require retaining different categories of task-relevant events. These can include:
 - Indicators of interaction occurrence (e.g., whether a switch has been pressed).
 - Completion status of subtasks (e.g., whether a specific object has been picked up).
 - Preservation of information about initial layouts, target identities, or object-state changes that become relevant much later in the task.
 A single, undifferentiated memory mechanism might not effectively capture and distinguish these diverse types of information, leading to competition for memory slots or destructive mixing of important cues.

- **Inefficiency of Simple Context Enlargement:** A straightforward approach to incorporate more history would be to increase the size of the local context window. However, this method introduces several inefficiencies:
 - **Increased Computational Cost:** Larger context windows demand greater computational resources.
 - **Dilution of Attention:** Critical but sparse events might be "diluted" or overlooked within a vast local context, as the model struggles to focus attention on the most relevant information.

- **Leveraging WAMs' Rich Supervision:** While other memory-augmented Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exist, their action-centric supervision provides limited signals for discovering and organizing heterogeneous historical events. WAMs, conversely, couple action learning with future visual prediction, offering a richer supervisory signal over both robot actions and environmental changes. This visual-action coupling is hypothesized to facilitate the learning of diverse historical event representations and enable a memory module to implicitly preserve complementary event patterns more effectively for prediction and control.

- **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** The research draws inspiration from the "multiple memory subsystems (MMSS)" view of human cognition, which posits that functionally distinct memory systems cooperate or compete. This inspires the design choice of organizing historical information into multiple parallel memory banks, allowing them to implicitly specialize in complementary event patterns while operating within a bounded capacity.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives for DiM-WAM are:
1. To develop a memory mechanism, Diverse Historical Event Memory (DHEM), that can effectively discover and preserve complementary event patterns over long horizons.
2. To integrate DHEM into a WAM framework, conditioning both video and action denoising on these historical event tokens.
3. To enable task-progress-aware training through auxiliary supervision, encouraging memory representations to encode coarse task progress cues.
4. To demonstrate significant improvements in robot manipulation performance on long-horizon, memory-dependent tasks in both simulation and real-world environments compared to existing WAM baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiM-WAM (World Action Modeling with Diverse Historical Event Memory) is designed to augment a standard WAM with a sophisticated memory mechanism to address limitations in long-horizon tasks. The methodology is structured around a multi-scale world action modeling framework, comprising Diverse Historical Event Memory (DHEM) banks, specific writing and maintenance procedures for these banks, a cooperative reading mechanism, and a task-progress-aware training objective.

**A. Overall DiM-WAM Framework:**
At each decision segment `i`, DiM-WAM takes three primary inputs: a language instruction `c`, a local key-value (KV) context `C_i` from a sliding-window WAM cache, and a persistent long-term memory state `M_i` provided by DHEM. The system predicts future visual latents and actions over a specified prediction horizon `H`. The DHEM-derived memory-token sequence (`R_i`) conditions both the video and action denoising branches of the model. An auxiliary progress objective is incorporated during training to supervise the memory readout and encourage the encoding of coarse task-progress cues. The overall memory cost is maintained at O(KN), where K is the number of memory banks and N is the number of slots per bank, independent of the trajectory length.

**B. Diverse Historical Event Memory Banks (DHEM):**
To prevent destructive interference and facilitate specialization, DHEM organizes historical information into K parallel memory banks. Each bank `M_k` contains N bounded slots, `e_i,k,n`. Each memory event `e_i,k,n` is a tuple comprising:
- `m_i,k,n`: An event token (vector of dimension `d`).
- `t_i,k,n`: Its timestamp.
- `α_i,k,n`: Its accumulated mass, indicating the amount of evidence it represents.
- `v_i,k,n`: A binary validity indicator.
The roles of these banks are not predefined but are learned implicitly. Learnable, bank-specific summary queries induce different observational views during memory writing, and bank-local maintenance policies lead to distinct retention trajectories. A diversity loss further encourages these banks to specialize in complementary event patterns.

**C. Writing and Maintenance of Historical Events:**
The process of updating DHEM involves extracting candidate event features and compressing redundant information within each bank.

1. **Visual Event Feature Extraction and Writing:**
 - For the current observation at segment `i`, visual features `X_i` are encoded.
 - Each bank `k` uses its unique learnable summary query `q_k` to attend over `X_i` and extract a candidate event feature `˜u_i,k`. This ensures that each bank processes the same observation but derives a bank-specific representation.
 - The persistent memory stores these event tokens, which are derived from the observation features, not the model's generated predictions.

2. **Novelty-Aware Bounded Event Compression:**
 - Within each bank `k`, memory slots are structured: slot 1 is a fixed initial-state anchor, slot N holds the latest event, and slots 2 to N-1 contain compressed historical events.
 - **Redundancy Priority:** A priority score `ρ_k(p, q)` is calculated for any two valid events `p` and `q`. This score combines the cosine similarity of their event tokens with an exponential temporal decay based on the difference between their timestamps. Higher priority indicates greater semantic redundancy and temporal proximity.
 - **Discard Condition:** When a bank is full, the incoming candidate event `˜u_i,k` is compared for redundancy against the latest event in the bank (`e_N`). This `ρ_new_i,k` is then compared to the maximum redundancy `ρ_k(p*, q*)` found among eligible adjacent historical pairs (slots 2 to N-2). If `ρ_new_i,k` is greater than or equal to `ρ_k(p*, q*)`, the incoming candidate is discarded, implying that it is not sufficiently novel compared to existing history that could be merged.
 - **Redundancy-Based Merging:** If the incoming candidate is sufficiently novel, the most redundant adjacent pair `(p*, q*)` in the middle history slots (2 to N-2) is selected for merging. The merged event (`e_pq`) combines their tokens (`m_pq`), timestamps (`t_pq`), and accumulated masses (`α_pq`) using a *mass-weighted fusion* strategy. This mechanism ensures that events representing more accumulated evidence (higher mass) contribute proportionally more to the merged representation. The merged event replaces the two constituent events, and the new candidate event is placed in slot N.
 - **Chronological Order:** The merging process preserves chronological order among the historical events. Initial slots are handled with specific rules to fill uninitialized or duplicate slots before the general similarity-based compression.

**D. Cooperative Memory Reading:**
After bank-local updates, valid event tokens from all banks are flattened into a single sequence `R_i`. This sequence is ordered slot-major, then bank-minor. Each token is augmented with a bank-identity embedding `b_k` to indicate its origin bank. Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) are applied to inject slot-relative temporal order based on their assigned coordinates, without modifying the persistent content of the memory banks. This readable memory token sequence `R_i` then conditions the denoising operations for both future video latents and actions. Crucially, these memory tokens condition the current prediction but are not directly added to the WAM's persistent KV cache; DHEM is updated only from subsequent observations.

**E. Task-Progress-Aware Training:**
To encourage memory representations to encode task progress, an auxiliary training objective is introduced:
- **Progress Head:** A feed-forward network, `h_prog`, predicts a coarse *progress bin* `p_i` from the mean-pooled valid event tokens in `R_i`. The number of bins `B` discretizes the task completion ratio.
- **Target Progress:** For each demonstration segment `i` in a trajectory of length `T`, a normalized completion ratio `r_i = i / (T-1)` is computed. This ratio is then mapped to a target progress bin `y_i`.
- **Progress Loss (`L_prog`):** A cross-entropy loss is applied between the predicted progress `p_i` and the target bin `y_i`. This head is only active during training and does not participate in inference.

**F. Joint Objective:**
The complete training objective is a combination of several loss terms:
- **`L_video`**: Mean-squared error for diffusion denoising on future visual latents.
- **`L_action`**: Masked mean-squared error for diffusion denoising on action prediction.
- **`L_div` (Diversity Loss):** To prevent all memory banks from converging to similar representations (bank collapse), a diversity loss is introduced. This loss minimizes the sum of squared cosine similarities between the normalized mean features of each pair of distinct memory banks.
- **`L_prog`**: The auxiliary task-progress loss.
The final objective function `L` is a weighted sum of these components: `L = L_video + L_action + λ_div * L_div + λ_prog * L_prog`, where `λ_div` and `λ_prog` are hyperparameters for balancing the auxiliary objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DiM-WAM was conducted across simulation (RMBench) and real-world Franka Panda tasks, along with ablation studies and an analysis of cross-bank behavior.

**A. Simulation Experiments on RMBench:**
DiM-WAM was integrated into LingBot-VA and compared against it as a training-matched controlled comparison, and also against Fast-WAM as another protocol-matched in-house WAM baseline. Benchmark-reported results from other methods (DP, ACT, π0.5, X-VLA, Mem-0) were included for contextual reference.

- **Protocol Audit:** An initial audit on the `put_back_block` task revealed that specific camera views and large local context windows could inadvertently expose initial object locations, potentially reducing the need for explicit memory. Based on this, a standardized protocol was established for in-house WAM evaluations: head + front views, 30-frame window, and strides 1 (for M(1) tasks) or 2 (for M(n) tasks).
- **Performance:** DiM-WAM achieved the highest reported success rate on 8 out of 9 RMBench tasks.
 - In the training-matched comparison with LingBot-VA, DiM-WAM improved the average full-task success rate across all tasks from 34.8% to 69.8%.
 - Specific gains were observed for both M(1) (memory-dependent on a few key observations) tasks (34.2% to 80.6%) and M(n) (history accumulated across interaction steps) tasks (35.5% to 56.3%).
 - DiM-WAM also exceeded the benchmark-reported Mem-0 model by 27.8 percentage points in overall average success.

**B. Real-World Experiments on Franka Panda:**
Four multi-stage Franka Panda tasks were used: Find Blue Block, Line Swap, Triangle Swap, and Press Twice, which require retaining prior spatial states, event order, or target identities. DiM-WAM was compared against π0.5, Fast-WAM, and LingBot-VA using 15–25 demonstrations and 10 full-task trials per task. Metrics included Stage Success Ratio (SSR) and full-task Success Rate (SR).

- **Performance:**
 - DiM-WAM raised the average SSR from 70.6% to 93.5% and the average SR from 52.5% to 90.0%.
 - The most significant SR gains were on Find Blue Block (from 10.0% to 90.0%, an 80.0 percentage point increase) and Triangle Swap (from 40.0% to 80.0%, a 40.0 percentage point increase), both tasks requiring cross-stage identity or spatial memory.
 - The π0.5 and Fast-WAM baselines achieved 0% full-task success on all evaluated real-world tasks.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Baselines (π0.5 and Fast-WAM) frequently failed due to confusion regarding task progress when local observations were visually similar but required different actions based on historical context.
- **Ceiling Effect:** For the Press Twice task, both LingBot-VA and DiM-WAM achieved 100% full-task success, indicating that this specific task's memory requirements might have been largely covered by the local window in these models, limiting its diagnostic value for memory-specific advantages.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
Ablations focused on the memory structure and the impact of progress supervision, conducted on `Swap T` and `Swap Blocks` tasks.

- **Multi-Bank Structure:** Increasing the number of memory banks while keeping total slot capacity fixed (e.g., from 1 bank with 32 slots to 4 banks with 8 slots each) improved the average success rate from 75.5% to 90.0%.
- **Memory Capacity:** Further increasing the capacity to 8 banks with 12 slots each (total 96 slots) raised the average success rate to 96.5%.
- **Progress Supervision:** Removing the auxiliary progress head from the 8x12 memory configuration reduced the average success rate from 96.5% to 90.5%.

**D. Cross-Bank Behavior Analysis:**
A qualitative analysis was performed on one successful `put_back_block` episode to understand how different memory banks retained events. The visualization showed:
- Banks retaining events from different positions within the task progress.
- Accumulated mass often concentrating near task transitions.
- Principal Component Analysis (PCA) of event tokens indicating distinct, yet overlapping, distributions across banks.
These observations provide anecdotal evidence consistent with complementary cross-bank specialization but do not establish a generalized trend across tasks or episodes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DiM-WAM represents a contribution to the field of robot manipulation by addressing the challenge of long-horizon temporal reasoning in World Action Models (WAMs). Its significance and potential impact stem from its methodological novelty and the demonstrated improvements in performance.

**A. Addressing a Key Limitation in WAMs:**
The work directly tackles a known limitation of existing WAMs, which often struggle with tasks requiring extensive temporal context or memory of past events. By enabling WAMs to effectively utilize diverse historical information, DiM-WAM extends their capability beyond short-horizon reactive behaviors to more complex, multi-stage tasks.

**B. Enhanced Performance on Complex Tasks:**
The substantial improvements demonstrated across both simulation benchmarks (RMBench) and real-world Franka Panda tasks highlight the practical effectiveness of DiM-WAM. The notable gains on tasks requiring cross-stage memory, such as "Find Blue Block" and "Triangle Swap," indicate its ability to resolve ambiguities arising from visually similar local observations by leveraging historical context. This suggests a step towards more robust and intelligent robotic systems.

**C. Novel Memory Architecture (DHEM):**
The proposed Diverse Historical Event Memory (DHEM) introduces several novel architectural and algorithmic components:
- **Multi-Bank Organization:** The use of multiple parallel memory banks, inspired by human cognition, allows for implicit specialization and retention of complementary event patterns, preventing catastrophic interference or dilution of information.
- **Novelty-Aware Selection and Mass-Weighted Fusion:** The mechanism for deciding whether to retain new events or merge redundant historical ones, using both semantic similarity and accumulated mass, provides a principled way to manage bounded memory while preserving accumulated evidence.
- **Progress-Aware Training and Diversity Loss:** The auxiliary supervision for task progress and the diversity loss function are designed to guide the learning of meaningful and distinct memory representations, respectively.

**D. Implications for Future Embodied AI:**
The success of DiM-WAM in integrating sophisticated memory into a WAM framework opens avenues for future research in embodied AI. Effective memory is crucial for agents operating in dynamic, open-ended environments. The principles underlying DHEM – particularly the concept of diverse, specialized memory banks and context-aware retention – could inform the design of memory systems for other long-horizon AI tasks beyond manipulation.

**E. Robustness and Autonomy:**
By enabling robots to draw on historical context, DiM-WAM improves their ability to navigate situations where current observations are insufficient for decision-making. This enhanced contextual understanding contributes to greater task success rates, which is critical for increasing the autonomy and reliability of robotic systems in real-world applications.

**F. Limitations and Future Directions (as stated by authors):**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that provide directions for future work:
- **Generalizability:** The current evaluation is primarily on memory-dependent manipulation tasks, suggesting a need to establish effectiveness across broader task distributions, robot embodiments, and observation settings.
- **Component Isolation:** While ablations support multi-bank organization and progress supervision, they do not independently isolate every component of the memory-maintenance strategy. Further analysis could clarify the specific contributions of individual elements.
- **Progress Representation:** The reliance on a coarse trajectory-position proxy for progress supervision may be less reliable when task stage durations vary significantly. Richer, semantic task state representations could offer improvements.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** The cross-bank behavior analysis is qualitative and episode-specific, requiring more rigorous quantitative methods to establish stable semantic specialization across banks, tasks, and episodes.

Despite these limitations, DiM-WAM provides a robust framework and demonstrated improvements, positioning it as a foundational step towards more intelligent and capable robotic agents that can operate effectively over extended temporal horizons.
