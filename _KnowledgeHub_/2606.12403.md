# World Pilot: Steering Vision-Language-Action Models with World-Action Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> World Pilot introduces a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that integrates a frozen World-Action Model (WAM) to enhance robotic manipulation. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks and real-world tasks by providing the VLA with anticipated scene dynamics and trajectory-level motion guidance, leading to improved robustness and out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks due to their limited understanding of continuous, action-conditioned scene dynamics.
- VLA policies, primarily relying on semantic grounding from image-text pretraining, lack an internal representation of how scenes physically evolve under robot actions, making them fragile to visual and physical variations.
- Effectively integrating the rich dynamic knowledge from World-Action Models (WAMs) into VLAs without introducing noise or diluting control-relevant information has been a significant challenge.

## Method

- **World Pilot** integrates a frozen ==World-Action Model== (WAM) into the VLA's decision chain through two complementary pathways: Latent Steering and Action Steering.
- Latent Steering injects a compact scene-evolution latent, derived from the WAM, into the VLM's hidden states via a lightweight encoder and residual cross-attention, enriching the VLM with anticipated spatiotemporal dynamics.
- Action Steering provides trajectory-level motion context to the VLA's action generator by compressing the WAM's coarse action-trajectory hypothesis into a single prior token, which conditions the action generator's denoising process.

## Results

- World Pilot achieved an **84.7%** total success rate on the LIBERO-Plus zero-shot OOD benchmark, outperforming the strongest baseline (ABot-M0) by 4.2 percentage points.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization, showing up to a **13.2%** improvement over baselines in specific OOD categories like camera perturbations, and reduced performance drops under real-world OOD conditions (within 20 absolute points).
- On real-robot manipulation tasks, World Pilot achieved the highest success rates across all in-distribution and OOD settings, notably succeeding in **70%** of trials for a geometrically sensitive 'Container-Lid Alignment' OOD task where baselines failed to exceed **30%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting VLA policies with abstract, dynamics-focused priors from a world model, specifically via latent scene-evolution cues and trajectory-level motion hints, significantly improves OOD generalization.
> - Injecting compact scene-evolution latents directly into the VLM's perception layers is more effective than using pixel-decoded future images, as latents avoid noise and artifacts while preserving control-relevant dynamics.
> - A single encoded trajectory-level token efficiently summarizes motion guidance, providing a flexible prior for the action generator without over-constraining it with noisy, per-step predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models leverage large-scale image-text pretraining from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to acquire semantic grounding, enabling them to competently perform in-distribution manipulation tasks. However, a prevalent limitation of current VLA approaches is their fragility when confronted with tasks that deviate from their training distribution, particularly under shifts in viewpoint, object geometry, or contact conditions. This fragility arises because the pretraining data, consisting of static image-text pairs, does not inherently capture the continuous, dynamic process of scene evolution under robot actions. Consequently, the action generators in these VLAs operate primarily on semantic hidden states, lacking an internal representation or anticipation of the physical dynamics required for successful manipulation.

To address this gap, World-Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a complementary paradigm. WAMs are typically pretrained on large-scale video sequences, allowing them to learn action-conditioned scene evolution and contact dynamics that image-text pretraining alone cannot capture. These models can generate two key types of outputs that are highly relevant to VLA shortcomings: a scene-evolution latent describing future visual states and a coarse action-trajectory hypothesis. The challenge lies in effectively integrating these dynamics-rich WAM outputs with the semantic understanding of VLAs.

Previous attempts to combine VLAs and WAMs have explored several avenues:
- Some methods, like Motus and DreamVLA, jointly generate future images and actions within a unified framework. However, the visual reconstruction loss incurred during this process can lead the action representation to absorb appearance details that are irrelevant to control, potentially diluting the control-relevant information.
- Other approaches, such as π0.7 and VISTA, guide policy learning using predicted future images or subgoal images. The use of pixel-space outputs, however, often introduces appearance details (e.g., texture, lighting, background, generation artifacts) that are extraneous to action selection and can obscure the underlying dynamics structure encoded in the world model's latent space.
- More recent works, including Being-H0.7 and WoG, have attempted to pass world-model knowledge through latents or implicit features, thereby reducing the loss of information from pixel-level reconstruction. Nonetheless, these methods often still rely on static future snapshots rather than continuous spatiotemporal evolution.

World Pilot positions itself within this landscape by proposing a specific and strategic integration framework. It aims to realize the complementarity between VLA semantic grounding and WAM dynamics knowledge by carefully routing relevant WAM outputs (scene-evolution latents and anticipated action trajectories) into the VLA decision chain at appropriate architectural layers. This approach seeks to avoid the pitfalls of prior methods by utilizing compact, dynamics-focused priors without explicit pixel-level reconstruction or coarse, static predictions, thereby equipping VLAs with an anticipated view of the scene and a trajectory-level motion hint.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, by augmenting their policy learning with priors derived from a World-Action Model (WAM).

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent limitations of existing VLA models:
- **Deficiency in Dynamics Understanding:** Current VLA policies primarily rely on semantic grounding acquired from large-scale image-text pretraining. While this provides strong understanding for in-distribution tasks, it fundamentally lacks a model of how a scene evolves over time under the influence of actions. Image-text pairs are static representations, and the action generator within a VLA typically consumes only semantic hidden states without an internal representation of the dynamics it needs to produce.
- **Fragility in Out-of-Distribution Conditions:** This lack of dynamics understanding renders VLAs fragile when faced with variations in viewpoint, object geometry, contact interactions, or other environmental changes that fall outside their training distribution. For instance, a VLA trained on specific object poses might struggle when the object is presented in a novel orientation.
- **Underutilized Potential of World Models:** World-Action Models (WAMs), pretrained on diverse video data, are specifically designed to capture action-conditioned scene evolution and physical contact dynamics. These models are capable of generating two key signals: a scene-evolution latent that compacts information about future visual states and a coarse action-trajectory hypothesis that outlines the anticipated motion. These signals directly address the dynamic understanding gap in VLAs.
- **Challenges in Effective Integration:** While the complementarity of VLAs and WAMs is apparent, effectively combining them in practice presents a significant challenge. The research identifies critical design questions:
 - Which specific signals should be extracted from the WAM?
 - In what form should these signals be represented and carried into the VLA?
 - At which layers within the VLA's architecture should these signals be injected to maximize their impact without being diluted or introducing noise?
 - Previous attempts at integration have often involved generating future images, which can introduce action-irrelevant visual details and artifacts that dilute the control-relevant dynamics. Others have relied on static future snapshots, missing the continuous temporal evolution.
- **Strategic Prior Injection:** World Pilot is motivated to overcome these integration challenges by developing a principled framework. The goal is to route WAM outputs into the VLA through two distinct, complementary pathways—one for scene dynamics and one for motion guidance—ensuring that the VLA gains both an "anticipated view of the scene" and a "trajectory-level motion hint" alongside its semantic conditioning. The emphasis is on injecting abstract, control-relevant dynamics information rather than raw pixel data, and providing a flexible, guiding motion prior rather than a rigid, per-step trajectory. This approach seeks to unlock the full potential of integrating world models with VLAs for more robust and generalizable robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

World Pilot proposes a VLA framework that integrates priors from a frozen World-Action Model (WAM) into the VLA's decision chain via two complementary pathways: Latent Steering and Action Steering. The underlying VLA policy is built upon ABot-M0, utilizing Qwen3-VL as the Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based flow-matching action head. Cosmos Policy serves as the WAM, which provides the dynamics priors.

**World-Action Model (WAM) as a Prior Source:**
The WAM, denoted $W_\phi$, takes the current visual observation ($O_t$), language instruction ($\ell$), and optional proprioceptive state ($q_t$) as inputs. It jointly predicts two outputs:
1. A **scene-evolution latent** ($Z_w$), which compacts information about the predicted future visual state.
2. A **coarse action-trajectory hypothesis** ($eA_w$), which outlines the anticipated sequence of actions.
Crucially, the WAM is kept *frozen* throughout the VLA fine-tuning process. This modular design treats the WAM as an external prior model, preventing VLA-specific gradients from altering its pretrained world knowledge and allowing its forward pass to be precomputed or cached for efficiency.

**Two Pathways for Prior Integration:**

1. **Latent Steering (Perception Layer):**
 - **Purpose:** To enrich the VLM hidden states with cues about anticipated spatiotemporal scene evolution.
 - **Rationale for using Latent:** Unlike decoded future images, which contain action-irrelevant details (texture, lighting, generation artifacts) that can dilute control-relevant dynamics, the scene-evolution latent ($Z_w$) directly encodes compact, control-relevant information about object motion, contact outcomes, and local state changes.
 - **Mechanism:**
 - The WAM generates $Z_w$ as a per-view latent representation of the future visual state. In Cosmos Policy, this involves VAE encoding and DiT-based denoising.
 - World Pilot projects this latent through a lightweight `dynamics encoder` ($f_{dyn}$).
 - A `temporal embedding` ($\rho_{fut}$) is added to the projected latent tokens, explicitly marking them as future-scene tokens, which is empirically shown to be important. This combined representation forms $D_w = f_{dyn}(Z_w) + \rho_{fut}$.
 - A `cross-attention` mechanism is applied, where the VLM hidden states ($H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$) query $D_w$.
 - The output of this cross-attention is added to the original VLM hidden states as a `residual connection`: $\bar{H}_t = H_t + \text{CrossAttn}(H_t, D_w)$.
 - This residual, cross-attention-based fusion allows each VLM token to selectively attend to relevant parts of the dynamics prior, preserving the VLM's original token order and hidden-state structure, which facilitates direct integration into the downstream action generation path.

2. **Action Steering (Action-Generation Layer):**
 - **Purpose:** To supply the action generator with a soft, trajectory-level motion context.
 - **Rationale for Single Token:** A single token summarizes the overall shape and intent of the anticipated trajectory ($eA_w$) rather than forcing the generator to match noisy per-step targets. This provides guidance while allowing the VLA action generator the flexibility to commit to a specific, continuous action chunk that also accounts for the dynamics-enhanced VLM hidden states.
 - **Mechanism:**
 - The WAM provides $eA_w$. This trajectory is first aligned to the VLA's action prediction horizon ($K$) through resampling.
 - An `action encoder` ($f_{act}$) compresses the aligned trajectory into a `single prior token` ($s_w = f_{act}(\text{Align}_K(eA_w))$).
 - The flow-matching action generator, which denoises a noisy trajectory $X_{\tau,t}$ towards a clean action chunk, receives an augmented input. This input includes state tokens ($u_t$), learned future-query tokens ($Q_t$), the noisy trajectory ($X_{\tau,t}$), and the `single prior token` ($s_w$).
 - The prior token $s_w$ is incorporated as a prefix, conditioning the denoising recurrence through self-attention without itself being subjected to denoising.
 - The dynamics-enhanced VLM hidden states ($\bar{H}_t$) from Latent Steering serve as the cross-attention condition for the action generator.

**Policy Training:**
- **Objective Function:** World Pilot adopts the clean-action parameterization for the flow-matching action generator, using a reweighted velocity-space objective. The training objective minimizes the $L_2$ error between the predicted clean action chunk ($\hat{A}_{\theta,t}$) and the expert action chunk ($A^{\star}_t$): $L_{\text{World Pilot}} = E_{\tau,\epsilon} [w(\tau) ||\hat{A}_{\theta,t} - A^{\star}_t||_2^2]$, where $w(\tau) = \frac{1}{(1-\tau)^2}$.
- **Parameter Updates:** Only the VLA-side parameters ($\theta$), including the VLM backbone, dynamics encoder ($f_{dyn}$), Latent Steering cross-attention, action encoder ($f_{act}$), and the flow-matching action generator, are updated with gradients. The WAM parameters ($W_\phi$) remain frozen.
- **Regularization:** Dropout with a rate of 0.3 is applied to the WAM conditions ($D_w$ and $s_w$) to prevent the policy from overly relying on the priors and to promote robustness.
- **Hardware and Evaluation:** The model is fine-tuned on 8 RTX PRO 6000 GPUs. Evaluation is conducted on the LIBERO-Plus and RoboCasa simulation benchmarks, and on four real-robot manipulation tasks, with success rate as the primary metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of World Pilot was conducted across both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, with a focus on out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.

**Simulation Benchmarks:**
- **LIBERO-Plus (Zero-Shot OOD):** This benchmark comprises 10,030 perturbed tasks across seven perturbation axes.
 - World Pilot achieved a Total success rate of 84.7% (averaged over three random seeds), representing a 4.2 percentage point improvement over the strongest reported baseline, ABot-M0 (80.5%).
 - World Pilot demonstrated notable gains in specific OOD categories: it led on Camera (82.8%, +13.2 over the next baseline), Light, Background, and Noise perturbations. This indicates enhanced robustness to variations in visual appearance and viewpoint.
 - It remained competitive with baselines on Language, Robot, and Layout perturbations.
 - On the in-distribution LIBERO benchmark, where strong baselines already achieve over 98% success, World Pilot also performed strongly (98.5%), indicating that its gains are primarily concentrated on OOD scenarios.
- **RoboCasa (Long-Horizon Tasks):** This benchmark focuses on long-horizon manipulation in everyday kitchen scenes.
 - World Pilot achieved a success rate of 65.5%, which is competitive with the strongest reported baseline, Cosmos Policy (67.1%), and significantly higher than ABot-M0 (54.0%). This suggests that the conditioning design of World Pilot effectively transfers to more complex, long-horizon tasks.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- **Setup:** World Pilot was evaluated on a physical robot arm using RGB inputs across four manipulation tasks: stacking blocks, folding towels, placing fruit on a plate, and container-lid alignment. Each task included one in-distribution (ID) setting and two out-of-distribution (OOD) variants, perturbing geometry, deformable state, or pose.
- **Performance and Robustness:**
 - World Pilot attained the highest success rate on every task setting (both ID and OOD) compared to baselines (π0.5, ABot-M0, Cosmos Policy).
 - A key finding was World Pilot's reduced performance drop under OOD perturbations. While other baselines experienced success rate drops of 25 to 50 absolute percentage points from their ID performance, World Pilot's drop remained within 20 absolute points.
 - For the highly sensitive "Container-Lid Alignment" task, which requires tight geometric tolerance, World Pilot achieved 70% and 65% success on OOD pose and object changes respectively (14 and 13 out of 20 trials), whereas no baseline exceeded 30% success (6 out of 20 trials).
 - These results suggest that both Latent Steering and Action Steering priors provide critical trajectory-level conditioning and anticipated scene-state cues that are not inherently present in VLM hidden states, leading to greater resilience in varying real-world conditions.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Contribution of Each Pathway:**
 - Latent Steering alone improved performance over the ABot-M0 baseline on LIBERO-Plus by +3.2% (83.7%).
 - Action Steering alone improved performance by +2.6% (83.1%).
 - Combining both pathways yielded the highest result, 84.7% (+4.2%), indicating that both priors contribute complementary and independent signals to the policy.
- **Latent Steering: World Prior Source:**
 - Using Cosmos-Predict (a video-pretrained world model without action-specific post-training) for Latent Steering still resulted in improvements over ABot-M0 across LIBERO-Plus (+2.1%), RoboCasa (+8.7%), and RoboTwin2.0 (+4.1%).
 - This demonstrates that the scene-evolution prior is effective even when sourced from a world model that has not been action-post-trained, indicating broad transferability of its encoded dynamics structure. Action post-training further refines this signal.
- **Latent Steering: Future-Scene Representation Form:**
 - Latent injection, regardless of denoising depth (1-step, 3-step, or 5-step from Cosmos denoising), yielded stable high performance (84.5% to 84.7%).
 - Replacing the latent with a fully *decoded future image* resulted in a performance drop to 83.5% (-1.2%), confirming that pixel-level decoding introduces visual artifacts and dilutes the dynamics structure relevant for control.
- **Action Steering: Trajectory Prior Form:**
 - The chosen method of a *single encoded trajectory-level token* yielded the highest success rate (84.7%).
 - Alternative forms, such as per-step encoded tokens (83.6%), flow-matching initialization from the WAM trajectory (84.1%), or using the raw WAM trajectory (83.0%), all resulted in lower performance. This suggests that the single-token representation effectively summarizes the overall motion guidance without propagating noise from approximate step-level predictions or overly constraining the VLA's action generator.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

World Pilot represents a notable advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by systematically addressing a fundamental limitation: the lack of intrinsic understanding of action-conditioned scene dynamics.

The significance of this work can be understood through several key aspects:
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The most prominent contribution is the substantial improvement in robustness and generalization capabilities of VLA policies, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) settings. By integrating dynamics priors, World Pilot consistently achieved higher success rates under various perturbations (viewpoint, geometry, deformable state, pose) in both simulations and real-world tasks. This improved resilience is crucial for the deployment of robotic systems in unpredictable and unstructured environments, moving beyond meticulously controlled in-distribution scenarios.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like LIBERO-Plus and demonstrating superior performance across diverse real-robot manipulation tasks validates the efficacy of the proposed framework. These results position World Pilot as a leading approach for building more capable and reliable generalist robot policies.
- **Principled Integration of World Models:** The paper provides a principled and empirically supported methodology for integrating World-Action Models (WAMs) with VLAs. The careful design of two complementary pathways—Latent Steering for scene dynamics and Action Steering for motion guidance—addresses the nuances of how different types of dynamic information should be routed and represented. The findings from ablation studies (e.g., preference for latent representations over decoded images, single-token trajectory summarization over per-step conditioning) offer valuable architectural insights for future research in this domain.
- **Modular and Efficient Framework:** By treating the WAM as a frozen, external prior, World Pilot maintains a modular architecture. This modularity allows for the independent development and improvement of both the VLA backbone and the WAM, fostering interchangeability and easier upgrades. The strategy of precomputing WAM outputs during training also contributes to computational efficiency, although the online WAM forward pass at inference introduces a per-step overhead.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** This research contributes to the broader goal of developing more intelligent embodied AI agents that possess a deeper understanding of the physical world and the consequences of their actions. By bridging the gap between semantic understanding and dynamic reasoning, World Pilot moves towards agents that can not only interpret instructions but also anticipate and plan their physical interactions more effectively.

The potential impact of World Pilot extends to various applications where robots need to operate reliably in dynamic and varied conditions:
- **Industrial and Logistics Automation:** Robots could perform tasks on production lines or in warehouses with greater adaptability to variations in object placement, lighting, or minor deformations.
- **Service and Domestic Robotics:** Robots could more reliably assist in everyday tasks in homes or public spaces, where environmental conditions are rarely static or perfectly controlled.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Robots operating in unknown or hazardous environments could benefit from enhanced scene anticipation and robust action execution, even when encountering novel situations.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as WAM coverage, uneven improvements across OOD axes, the modular coupling, and per-step inference overhead, it also outlines clear directions for future research. These include developing uncertainty-aware prior gating, exploring tighter prior-policy co-adaptation through joint training, and implementing prior distillation or adaptive querying to reduce computational costs. Addressing these limitations could further amplify the impact of World Pilot, fostering the development of even more robust and generalizable robotic systems.
