# Simultaneous Scene Reconstruction and Whole-Body Motion Planning for Safe Operation in Dynamic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> An integrated system pairing GPU-Voxels real-time distance-field reconstruction with GPMP2 factor-graph trajectory optimisation for receding-horizon whole-body planning on a mobile manipulator. A study across 15,300 problems shows unsigned distance fields match signed ones on articulated systems while costing half the compute, so the pipeline maps live depth data and re-plans around moving obstacles, validated in simulation and on a real Toyota HSR.

## Problem

- Prior motion planners assume static, pre-computed signed distance fields, so environment representations cannot be refreshed fast enough to react to moving obstacles.
- Incremental distance-field builders were only demonstrated for aerial path planning, never for whole-body or articulated mobile-manipulator motion planning.
- No published justification existed for whether distance fields should be signed or unsigned, leaving a costly default choice unexamined in trajectory optimisation.

## Method

- Hybrid pipeline threading **GPU-Voxels** ==Parallel Banding Algorithm== ==Euclidean distance transform== directly into **GPMP2** obstacle factors via shared memory pointers, mapping running concurrently with planning.
- ==Receding-horizon== re-planning loop that validates the current trajectory against the live field, rebuilds the ==factor graph== with re-estimated horizon, and refits the previous trajectory as warm start.
- Signed-versus-unsigned comparison protocol, extending GPU-Voxels and **FIESTA** to emit signed fields by subtracting distance transforms of the occupancy and inverse-occupancy maps.

## Results

- Unsigned fields raised 2D navigation failure **18.3x** versus signed, but on 7-DoF arm and 8-DoF whole-body tasks failure stayed near **0.0003%** with iteration counts and trajectory costs essentially identical.
- GPU-Voxels computed unsigned fields in **25.3 ms** at 0.025 m resolution against FIESTA's **176.4 ms** and Voxblox's **1232.5 ms** signed field, fastest at every resolution tested.
- Warm-starting from the refitted previous trajectory gave roughly **30%** optimisation speed-up; validity checks ran at **250 Hz** and full re-planning at **10 Hz** on hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Signed distance fields buy little on high-DoF articulated robots because non-colliding links supply gradients that pull colliding states out; cheaper unsigned fields suffice.
> - Compute saved on the environment representation converts directly into re-planning frequency, which matters more than per-plan optimality in dynamic scenes.
> - Perception, not planning, becomes the bottleneck: unseen obstacles never enter the field, making camera view selection the limiting design problem.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of robotic systems into human-centric environments necessitates a foundational capability: the real-time recognition and safe interaction with dynamic surroundings. This research addresses a critical challenge in robotics: the ability for robots to operate safely and robustly in unknown, dynamic environments, particularly in the presence of moving obstacles, through simultaneous scene reconstruction and whole-body motion planning.

Prior research in motion planning has largely focused on static environments, an assumption that frequently breaks down in real-world applications. While significant advancements have been made in mapping and scene reconstruction, enabling detailed, real-time environment models using various representations (e.g., voxel grids, TSDFs, surfels, octrees), their integration with motion planning for real-time re-planning in dynamic settings has remained an open challenge. Similarly, motion planning algorithms have evolved to generate collision-free trajectories quickly, leveraging methods like sampling-based algorithms, graph search, and trajectory optimization. Trajectory optimization methods, such as CHOMP, STOMP, TrajOpt, and GPMP2, are particularly relevant due to their potential for fast planning and smooth re-planning. However, these often require a continuously varying environment representation, typically Euclidean Signed Distance Fields (ESDFs), which can be computationally intensive to update in real-time.

Various methods have been proposed to accelerate ESDF computation, including incremental updates (e.g., Voxblox, FIESTA, ewok) and GPU-accelerated approaches (e.g., GPU-Voxels, Parallel Banding Algorithm). While some of these have been demonstrated for path planning in Micro Aerial Vehicles (MAVs), their application to whole-body motion planning for articulated systems or mobile manipulation platforms in dynamic environments has been less explored.

Integrated systems for dynamic obstacle avoidance have predominantly focused on mobile aerial robots or employed restricted 2.5D environment models, or reported planning times that are significantly longer than optimization-based methods. Few studies have demonstrated real-time reactive collision avoidance using whole-body trajectory optimization with live sensor data for complex, articulated mobile manipulators in unknown 3D environments. Furthermore, a comparative analysis of the impact of Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) versus Unsigned Distance Fields (USDFs) on trajectory optimization convergence and optimality, especially for multi-degree-of-freedom robotic systems, has been largely absent in the literature.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these fields, aiming to bridge the gap between advanced real-time 3D environment reconstruction and robust whole-body motion planning, specifically for mobile manipulators operating in highly dynamic, unknown environments. It seeks to provide a practical, integrated framework that addresses the computational demands and theoretical considerations necessary for safe human-robot co-existence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are multifaceted:

1. **Address the Safety Challenge in Dynamic Robotic Operations:** The overarching motivation stems from the need for robots to operate safely and reliably in complex, human-populated environments. This requires robots to perceive, reason about, and avoid both static and dynamic obstacles in real-time to prevent collisions with people, the environment, and themselves. Existing motion planning often assumes static environments, which is insufficient for real-world scenarios.

2. **Develop a Fully Integrated Real-time Re-planning System:** A key objective is to demonstrate a robust, integrated system that can safely re-plan robot motions in unknown environments, accommodating both static and dynamic obstacles using live sensor data. While advancements exist in real-time mapping and fast motion planning, their seamless combination into a practical framework for whole-body mobile manipulators has remained an engineering and research challenge.

3. **Investigate the Impact of Signed vs. Unsigned Distance Fields:** The authors identify a gap in the literature regarding the empirical comparison of SDFs and USDFs on trajectory optimization convergence and error costs. Despite the widespread use of distance fields, there has been no clear justification for preferring one over the other, particularly for different types of planning problems (e.g., 2D path planning vs. high-DoF arm/whole-body manipulation). The motivation is to provide empirical evidence to guide the selection of distance field representations.

4. **Evaluate State-of-the-Art Mapping and Reconstruction Approaches:** A practical system requires an efficient real-time scene reconstruction module. The authors aim to analyze the performance of prominent distance field generation approaches (Voxblox, FIESTA, GPU-Voxels) to identify the most suitable method for integration into a real-time motion planning system, considering factors like update rate and computational efficiency.

5. **Construct a Hybrid Mapping and Motion Planning System for Mobile Manipulators:** The ultimate objective is to leverage the findings from the distance field analysis and mapping package comparison to build a practical, integrated system. This system specifically targets receding-horizon whole-body motion planning for mobile manipulators, enabling smooth avoidance of moving obstacles in 3D space using live sensor data. This capability is crucial for advanced applications such as domestic robots or industrial co-bots.

6. **Demonstrate Real-time Reactive Collision Avoidance on a Physical Robot:** A critical goal is to validate the integrated system not only in simulation but also on a real-world mobile manipulator (Toyota HSR) to demonstrate its efficacy in practical, live scenarios with unknown and dynamic obstacles, thereby verifying its readiness for real-world deployment.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge theoretical advancements in mapping and planning with practical, real-world robotic applications, focusing on safety and efficiency in dynamic, unstructured environments, while also addressing foundational questions about the choice of environment representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-stage approach, starting with fundamental investigations into distance field properties, followed by a comparative analysis of real-time mapping frameworks, and culminating in the integration and validation of a complete system.

**A. Study on Signed vs. Unsigned Distance Fields (SDFs vs. USDFs)**

1. **Experimental Design:** To assess the impact of SDFs and USDFs on trajectory optimization, a series of simulation experiments were conducted across three planning domains:
 - 2D path planning.
 - 7-Degrees of Freedom (DoF) arm manipulation (Franka Panda).
 - Whole-body loco-manipulation with an 8-DoF mobile manipulator (Toyota HSR, 5-DoF arm on a holonomic base).
2. **Problem Generation:** For each domain, 18 robot states were generated, yielding 153 unique pairs of start and goal configurations. An obstacle set consisting of 100 randomly sized cuboids (0.0 to 1.0 m per dimension) was also generated and placed randomly within the workspace for each problem instance. This resulted in 15,300 motion planning problems.
3. **Motion Planner:** The GPMP2 (Gaussian Process Motion Planning 2) framework was utilized as the motion planner for all experiments. GPMP2 frames motion planning as a probabilistic inference problem on a factor graph, employing Gaussian Process Interpolation for continuous trajectory representation.
4. **Optimization Settings:** The Levenberg-Marquardt optimization method was used, with an initial damping parameter of 0.01. Optimization termination criteria were a relative decrease in error smaller than 10⁻⁵ or a maximum of 100 iterations.
5. **Metrics:** The primary metrics for comparison included failure rate (failure to find a collision-free trajectory), number of iterations to convergence, and the cost of valid, collision-free trajectories.

**B. Real-Time Scene Reconstruction Comparison**

1. **Mapping Frameworks Evaluated:** Three state-of-the-art mapping packages were selected:
 - Voxblox (CPU-based, generates SDFs).
 - FIESTA (CPU-based, primarily USDFs).
 - GPU-Voxels (GPU-based, primarily USDFs).
2. **SDF Adaptation:** For a comprehensive comparison, GPU-Voxels and FIESTA were adapted to optionally produce SDFs. This was achieved by calculating distance fields for both the occupancy map and its inverse, then subtracting the values. For GPU-Voxels, this was parallelized where possible, while for FIESTA, inverse distance transform was implemented by reversing roles of occupied/unoccupied cells in ray-casting.
3. **Dataset and Hardware:** The "Cow and Lady" dataset, featuring real RGB-D point cloud data from a Kinect v1 camera with ground-truth Vicon poses, was used. The dataset duration was 142 seconds, simulating live operation. Experiments were performed on an 8-core Intel Core i7-9700 CPU and 2133 MHz DDR4 RAM. GPU computations for GPU-Voxels were performed on an Nvidia RTX 2060 GPU.
4. **Evaluation Parameters:** Each package was evaluated across a range of voxel resolutions, maintaining full spatial coverage. For Voxblox, a propagation distance of 0.8 m was chosen to ensure coverage for collision models (0.3 m max sphere radius) and penalty distances (0.5 m max).
5. **Metric:** The update rate (in milliseconds) for computing distance fields from point cloud data was measured.

**C. Integration of Mapping and Motion Planning**

1. **Component Selection:** Based on the comparison, GPU-Voxels was selected as the mapping framework due to its superior speed. GPMP2 was confirmed as the motion planner.
2. **System Architecture:** The mapping and motion planning processes were run concurrently on separate threads. GPU-Voxels was integrated within the same ROS node as GPMP2, providing fast, direct query access to the updated Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT) memory via pointers for the obstacle factors in GPMP2.
3. **Receding-Horizon Re-planning Algorithm:**
 - **Initial Planning:** Given a goal pose (base x, y, θ, and arm joint configuration), parameters like planning time are estimated. A factor graph is built with prior factors on current and goal states. A straight-line trajectory initializes the optimization, which runs until convergence. The resultant trajectory is interpolated and executed.
 - **Re-planning Loop:** During execution, a timer callback regularly checks if the current trajectory remains collision-free and within an acceptable error tolerance using the real-time updated EDT. If not, the robot's current pose is acquired, parameters re-estimated, and a new factor graph is built. Instead of a straight-line initialization, the previous trajectory is refitted to the new graph discretization, which accelerates optimization by approximately 30%. The graph is re-built from scratch each re-planning iteration, simplifying factor management and allowing dynamic re-parametrization of the planning horizon.
 - **Collision Avoidance:** GPMP2's obstacle factors query the real-time updated distance field, assigning a hinge-loss cost and associated gradients for optimization.
4. **Environment Handling:** To avoid classifying the floor as a collision object, points within 3 cm of the ground were used for ray-casting to clear space but were not inserted into the voxelmap as occupied points.

**D. Experimental Validation**

1. **Simulation Experiments:** The integrated system was tested on an 8-core Intel Core i7 CPU and Nvidia GeForce RTX 2060 GPU. Various whole-body motion tasks were simulated, including general navigation, reaching into shelves, and picking objects from the floor, all in the presence of dynamic obstacles (e.g., a moving red cylinder).
2. **Hardware Experiments:** The system was deployed on a physical Toyota Human Support Robot (HSR). The HSR's onboard i7 processor handled joint control and sensing, while an external laptop (Intel Core i7-10875H CPU, Nvidia GeForce RTX 2070 SUPER GPU) performed motion planning and mapping. Depth images from an Xtion PRO LIVE sensor were streamed to the laptop. A voxel resolution of 2.5 cm with a grid size of 320 × 320 × 128 was used.
3. **Tasks:** Three specific real-world tasks were conducted:
 - **Shelf Pickup:** Robot reaches into a shelf, a human demonstrator walks into the path, and later a wooden plank obstructs the end-effector.
 - **Floor Pickup:** Robot travels to pick up a bottle, a human demonstrator walks into the path.
 - **Table Pickup:** Robot picks an object from a table, a human demonstrator moves an object across the path.
4. **Performance Metrics:** The trajectory validity check operated at 250 Hz, and the re-planning loop ran at 10 Hz.

This systematic approach allowed for both fundamental insights into distance field properties and the development and rigorous validation of a practical, real-time whole-body motion planning system for dynamic environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings across the comparative studies and the integrated system's performance:

**A. Signed vs. Unsigned Distance Fields (SDFs vs. USDFs) Impact**

- **2D Navigation:** In 2D path planning, USDFs resulted in a failure rate 18.3 times greater than SDFs. This supported the intuition that SDFs provide beneficial gradients to "push" a trajectory out of collision, especially when the initial state is entirely within an obstacle region where USDFs would offer no gradient information.
- **Higher-Dimensional Systems (Arm and Whole-Body):** For 7-DoF arm manipulation and 8-DoF whole-body loco-manipulation, SDFs showed no significant practical advantage over USDFs. Both types of distance fields successfully solved nearly all planning problems. The minor differences in failure rates (e.g., USDF failure rate 0.75 times that of SDF for arm, and 5.0 times for whole-body, but all very low absolute values) were not considered practically significant. The number of iterations to convergence and valid trajectory costs were comparable across both types of fields for these higher-dimensional tasks.
- **Conclusion:** Given that SDF calculation typically takes approximately twice as long as USDF computation, the authors concluded that USDFs are preferable for articulated and whole-body systems. Their faster computation speed supports more rapid re-planning in dynamic environments without a practical detriment to convergence or optimality in high-DoF tasks, as gradients from non-colliding parts of the robot can still "pull" other parts out of collision.

**B. Real-Time Scene Reconstruction Performance**

- **Speed Comparison:** GPU-Voxels (GPU-based) consistently demonstrated the fastest performance across all tested resolutions, outperforming both Voxblox (CPU-based) and FIESTA (CPU-based).
 - At 0.025m resolution: GPU-Voxels (unsigned) averaged 25.3 ms, FIESTA (unsigned) 176.4 ms, and Voxblox (signed) 1232.5 ms.
 - GPU-Voxels' advantage persisted even when configured to produce SDFs (36.2 ms at 0.025m resolution), which was still faster than CPU-based USDF calculations.
- **Bottleneck Analysis:** Analysis of GPU-Voxels' update loop revealed that while PCL processing and synchronization times were relatively constant, the time spent transferring the full EDT from the GPU to the host CPU became a more significant factor as voxel resolution and grid size increased. However, even with this transfer cost, GPU-Voxels remained the fastest option.

**C. Integrated System Performance (Simulation and Hardware)**

- **Simulation Robustness:** The integrated system, combining GPU-Voxels for mapping and GPMP2 for motion planning, robustly avoided moving obstacles in simulated whole-body motion tasks (navigation, reaching, picking). The robot successfully re-planned smoothly around obstacles that moved into its planned path. A limitation noted was the dependency on the robot's head camera perception; obstacles not observed were not registered in the distance field.
- **Real-world Hardware Validation (HSR):** The system was successfully implemented and validated on a physical Toyota Human Support Robot (HSR).
 - In all three complex tasks (Shelf Pickup, Floor Pickup, Table Pickup) involving dynamic obstacles (human demonstrators moving into or across the planned trajectory, or objects being moved), the HSR successfully re-planned and achieved its desired goal state without collisions.
 - The system demonstrated real-time re-planning, adapting the robot's trajectory in response to newly observed dynamic obstacles.
 - The real-time distance field reconstruction generated by GPU-Voxels provided clear and detailed environmental perception for the motion planner.
- **Re-planning Rate:** The system could evaluate trajectory validity at 250 Hz, and the re-planning loop, which involved rebuilding and re-optimizing the factor graph, operated at 10 Hz.

These results indicate that USDFs are computationally advantageous for high-DoF systems, GPU-Voxels offers superior real-time scene reconstruction performance, and their integration facilitates effective and safe whole-body motion planning in dynamic, unknown environments on both simulated and physical mobile manipulators.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics by addressing a fundamental challenge in enabling autonomous systems to operate safely and effectively in human-centric, dynamic environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Real-time Whole-Body Motion Planning:** The paper presents a fully integrated system capable of real-time receding-horizon whole-body motion planning with live sensor data in unknown 3D environments for mobile manipulators. This is a notable achievement, as previous work has largely focused on aerial robots or lacked the integration of real-time scene reconstruction with trajectory optimization for complex articulated systems. This capability is critical for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic settings, such as homes, hospitals, or service industries.

2. **Empirical Guidance on Distance Field Selection:** The comparative study on Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) and Unsigned Distance Fields (USDFs) provides empirical evidence that, for high-degree-of-freedom articulated systems, USDFs offer computational advantages (faster calculation) without sacrificing planning performance (convergence, optimality). This finding offers practical guidance for researchers and practitioners in designing and implementing motion planning systems, potentially leading to more efficient and responsive robotic behaviors.

3. **Identification of Optimal Real-time Mapping Framework:** The comprehensive analysis of state-of-the-art mapping libraries (Voxblox, FIESTA, GPU-Voxels) clearly identifies GPU-Voxels as the superior choice for real-time distance field generation. This selection, based on empirical performance, provides a robust foundation for building high-performance integrated systems, leveraging the parallel processing capabilities of GPUs.

4. **Enhanced Robot Safety and Reliability:** By enabling robots to reactively and smoothly avoid dynamic obstacles using live sensor data, this work directly enhances the safety and reliability of robotic operations. This is a prerequisite for widespread robot integration into daily life, fostering trust and acceptance in human-robot collaboration scenarios. The validated performance on a physical HSR robot in challenging dynamic tasks underscores this impact.

5. **Foundation for Future Research and Applications:** This integrated framework opens several avenues for future research:
 - **Advanced Obstacle Avoidance:** It lays the groundwork for incorporating predictive models of moving obstacles (e.g., using predicted composite signed distance fields), which can lead to more optimal and less reactive trajectories than those based solely on instantaneous perception.
 - **Simultaneous Mapping, Localization, and Planning (SLAM/STEAP):** The efficient distance field computation method presented here addresses a key computational bottleneck cited in prior work on simultaneous trajectory estimation and planning (STEAP). This could enable the extension of STEAP to dynamic, unknown environments, allowing robots to concurrently localize, map, and plan in complex settings.
 - **Active Perception and Next Best View Planning:** The observation regarding the head camera's limitation highlights the need for active perception strategies and "Next Best View" planning specifically tailored for receding-horizon trajectory optimization, which could further enhance system robustness.
 - **GPU-native Motion Planning:** The increased significance of GPU-to-host data transfer for high-resolution maps motivates future work into implementing optimization-based motion planning directly on the GPU, potentially eliminating this bottleneck and further boosting performance.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the state-of-the-art in integrated robotic systems for dynamic environments. Its practical contributions to real-time safe operation, coupled with fundamental insights into distance field utilization, provide a valuable foundation for the development of more autonomous, reliable, and socially integrated robots.
