# Large Language Models as End-to-end Combinatorial Optimization Solvers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Eindhoven University of Technology and Singapore Management University developed a two-stage fine-tuning framework that enables Large Language Models to act as end-to-end combinatorial optimization solvers, generating high-quality solutions directly from natural language problem descriptions. This approach achieves near-perfect feasibility and substantially lower optimality gaps across seven NP-hard problems compared to general-purpose LLMs and reasoning models.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) historically exhibit poor feasibility rates and high optimality gaps when attempting to solve complex NP-hard combinatorial optimization (CO) problems.
- Existing LLM approaches for CO often depend on intermediate steps like code generation or external solver invocation, which still require significant domain-specific knowledge from users.
- Traditional CO solver design is highly problem-specific, lacking a unified and generalizable framework for diverse optimization tasks.

## Method

- A two-stage fine-tuning framework is introduced, combining Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) with ==Feasibility==-and-Optimality-Aware Reinforcement Learning (FOARL), to train LLMs for direct solution generation.
- High-quality training datasets are created by generating numerical instances, solving them with state-of-the-art CO solvers, and converting both problem and solution into Text-Attributed Instances (TAI) incorporating simple heuristic features.
- Best-of-N (BoN) sampling is applied during inference to generate multiple candidate solutions and select the highest-quality feasible one, improving solution robustness.

## Results

- The fine-tuned **7B**-parameter LLM achieved a **100%** feasibility rate across six out of seven NP-hard problems and **94%** on the remaining, with average optimality gaps ranging from **1.03%** to **8.20%**.
- This approach consistently outperformed much larger general-purpose LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Llama3.3-**70B**) and advanced reasoning models, which exhibited significantly lower feasibility rates (e.g., 3-**8%** for GPT-4o on CVRP) and much higher optimality gaps.
- The framework demonstrated versatility by enabling a single LLM to effectively solve seven diverse NP-hard problems, showing robust generalization to out-of-distribution instances and competitive performance against domain-specific heuristics and high-performance solvers under time constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervised fine-tuning alone, while teaching solution patterns, can lead to "over-greedy" behavior where LLMs marginally violate constraints in pursuit of better objective values.
> - Feasibility-and-Optimality-Aware Reinforcement Learning (FOARL) is crucial for explicitly enhancing an LLM's understanding of constraints and improving solution quality by balancing feasibility and optimality with specific reward functions.
> - The inclusion of computationally inexpensive heuristic features within Text-Attributed Instances (TAI) effectively guides the LLM's latent solution-space exploration and improves performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Combinatorial Optimization (CO) problems are foundational to countless real-world decision-making processes across logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, and beyond. Historically, solving these NP-hard problems has relied on specialized algorithms, heuristics, and metaheuristics, demanding significant domain expertise for modeling and implementation. The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has opened new avenues, promising to automate and simplify these complex tasks.

The current research landscape for LLMs in CO can be broadly categorized into several approaches:
1. **Code Generation/Solver Invocation:** Many existing methods leverage LLMs to generate executable code (e.g., Python programs) that either discover novel heuristics or interface with established optimization solvers like Gurobi or OR-Tools. While powerful, these approaches still require users to understand algorithmic templates, debug code, or be proficient in using external solvers, thus not fully eliminating the need for domain-specific expertise.
2. **Reasoning Models:** LLMs equipped with advanced prompting techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or Tree-of-Thought have shown capabilities in mathematical reasoning. However, their application to NP-hard CO problems, especially large-scale and constrained ones, remains limited. These models often struggle with feasibility and optimality, exhibiting high optimality gaps and low feasibility rates, as step-by-step reasoning becomes computationally intractable for complex combinatorial spaces.
3. **End-to-End Solutions:** A nascent but highly appealing direction is to enable LLMs to act as direct, end-to-end CO solvers, mapping natural language problem descriptions directly to solutions. This approach promises to drastically reduce the reliance on coding or formal modeling, making CO accessible to non-experts. However, significant challenges persist, particularly in achieving both high feasibility and strong optimality on constrained and large-scale problems.

This paper explicitly positions itself within this third category, aiming to advance the "end-to-end" paradigm. It directly addresses the shortcomings of current LLM-based CO solutions, particularly their poor feasibility rates and substantial optimality gaps on non-trivial problems. By proposing a unified, language-based pipeline that bypasses intermediate code execution or complex prompt engineering, this work seeks to establish LLMs as truly general-purpose solvers that can interpret user requests and generate high-quality solutions solely through natural language. This represents a significant step towards democratizing access to powerful optimization tools, contrasting sharply with traditional, labor-intensive solver design and more recent LLM-as-programmer or LLM-as-reasoner approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **empower Large Language Models to serve as effective end-to-end combinatorial optimization solvers by directly mapping natural language problem descriptions to high-quality solutions.** The authors aim to achieve this by developing a unified, language-based pipeline that significantly reduces dependence on domain-specific knowledge, code generation, or solver invocation.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:
1. **Limitations of Existing LLM-based CO Approaches:** Current methods often rely on intermediate steps like generating executable code or interacting with external optimization solvers. These methods, while innovative, still require users to possess substantial domain-specific expertise in algorithmic design or solver usage. This creates a barrier to entry, hindering the broader accessibility and generality of LLMs for CO.
2. **Poor Performance of End-to-End LLMs:** While the vision of end-to-end LLM solvers is attractive, their current capabilities for NP-hard CO problems are severely limited. State-of-the-art models like GPT-4o often yield low feasibility rates (around 50% for problems with <30 nodes) and large optimality gaps (e.g., 133% for TSP-50). This performance gap, particularly in maintaining constraints and generating optimal solutions, raises significant concerns about their practical utility.
3. **Need for Generalizability and Accessibility:** Traditional CO solver design is highly problem-specific, requiring customized heuristics or neural network architectures for each problem type. The authors are motivated to move towards a generalizable, language-driven alternative that can handle diverse CO problems without extensive manual architectural adjustments, thereby simplifying the problem-solving pipeline and making it more accessible to non-experts.
4. **Addressing Over-greedy Behavior:** Initial attempts at supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often lead LLMs to generate solutions that marginally violate constraints in pursuit of better objective values. This "over-greedy" behavior necessitates a targeted approach to explicitly foster feasibility awareness.

In essence, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between LLMs' powerful language understanding and generation capabilities and the rigorous requirements of combinatorial optimization, aiming for a reliable, user-friendly, and generalizable solution that can stand as a new paradigm for CO.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a novel, two-stage fine-tuning framework that transforms a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM) into an end-to-end combinatorial optimization (CO) solver. This framework emphasizes direct solution generation from natural language problem descriptions, bypassing traditional code generation or solver invocation.

#### 3.1. Data Generation

The first crucial step involves creating a high-quality dataset of problem-solution pairs. This is achieved by:
- **Instance Generation:** Numerical instances for seven NP-hard CO problems (TSP, OP, CVRP, MIS, MVC, PFSP, JSSP) are generated. These instances vary in size and distribution (e.g., uniform, Gaussian mixture for routing problems; Erdős–Rényi or Barabási–Albert graphs for graph problems).
- **Solver-based Solutions:** Domain-specific, state-of-the-art solvers (e.g., LKH-3 for TSP/CVRP, Q-learning Iterated Greedy for PFSP, Gurobi for MIS/MVC, OR-Tools CP-SAT for JSSP, genetic algorithm for OP) are used to compute (near)optimal solutions for these instances. These solver outputs serve as the "ground truth" labels for LLM training.
- **Textual Instance Representation (TAI):** Numerical instances and their solutions are converted into a natural language format called Text-Attributed Instances (TAI). Each TAI comprises:
 - **Instruction:** A natural language description of the CO problem, its objective, and constraints.
 - **Input:** Instance-specific details (e.g., node coordinates, demands, processing times) along with **heuristic features**. These features (e.g., top-k nearest neighbors for routing, lowest processing time jobs for scheduling) are computationally inexpensive, representative of well-known heuristics, and easily expressible in natural language. They serve to guide the LLM's latent solution-space exploration.
 - **Output:** The natural language representation of the solver-generated solution (e.g., "Route: [0, 3, 5...], Objective: 5797.33").

#### 3.2. Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)

In the first stage, the LLM is fine-tuned using the generated TAI dataset.
- **Next-Token Prediction:** CO problem-solving is framed as a language generation task. The LLM learns to map the textual problem description (ϕ(p)) to its textual solution representation (ψ⁻¹(x\*)).
- **Instruction Tuning:** An Alpaca-style instruction tuning paradigm is adopted, where the LLM learns to follow problem instructions and generate solutions in a specified format.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT):** To ensure computational efficiency and preserve the LLM's general capabilities, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is employed. LoRA injects small, trainable low-rank matrices into selected layers (query, key, value, output, gate, up, down projections) of the pre-trained Qwen2.5-7B model, keeping the vast majority of original weights frozen. This updates only ~2.08% of the parameters.
- **Observed Limitation:** Despite SFT's effectiveness in teaching solution patterns, it often leads to "over-greedy" behavior, where the LLM generates solutions that slightly violate constraints in pursuit of better objective values (e.g., marginally exceeding a distance limit or vehicle capacity). This highlights the LLM's lack of explicit constraint understanding, motivating the second stage.

#### 3.3. Feasibility-and-Optimality-Aware Reinforcement Learning (FOARL)

The second stage addresses the SFT's limitation by explicitly enhancing the LLM's understanding of feasibility and optimizing solution quality through Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- **Reward Functions:** Two types of rewards are defined:
 - **Feasibility-aware Reward (R_f):** This reward encourages adherence to all problem-specific constraints (e.g., node visitation, capacity limits, subtour elimination). It includes terms for output format adherence and weighted constraint satisfaction.
 - **Optimality-aware Reward (R_o):** This reward promotes better objective values, incorporating the optimality gap (1 / (1 + M_o(x̂_p))) for minimization problems or direct ratio (x̂_p / x\*_p) for maximization problems.
 - **Combined Reward:** R_f and R_o are linearly combined (R_P = R_f + R_o) to balance feasibility and optimality.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** FOARL is built upon GRPO, an efficient RL algorithm that estimates baselines directly from group-level rewards, avoiding the need for a separate critic model and significantly reducing computational overhead. The LLM policy (specifically, its LoRA modules) is updated by maximizing an objective function that considers the ratio of current to old policy probabilities and the advantage (R_P minus a group reward mean).

#### 3.4. Best-of-N Inference (BoN)

During inference, to overcome the autoregressive nature's limitation of committing to early decisions and to explore the solution space more effectively, BoN sampling is employed:
- **Multiple Candidates:** N distinct solution candidates are generated from the fine-tuned LLM using top-p sampling.
- **Selection:** The highest-quality *feasible* solution among these N candidates is selected based on the objective function. This post-processing step ensures that the final output is both valid and optimized.

The methodology utilizes Qwen2.5-7B as the base LLM, generates extensive datasets (500,000 instances for SFT, up to 3,200 for FOARL), and fine-tunes with efficient techniques like LoRA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of the proposed end-to-end LLM-based combinatorial optimization solver, demonstrating superior performance across a diverse set of NP-hard problems.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Against General-Purpose and Reasoning LLMs (Table 1)

- **Dominant Performance:** The fine-tuned 7B-parameter LLM (SFT+RL+BoN) significantly outperforms much larger general-purpose LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet, DeepSeek-V3-671B, Llama3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-72B) and advanced reasoning models (e.g., GPT-o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1).
- **High Feasibility and Low Optimality Gaps:** The proposed method achieves a **100% feasibility rate** across TSP, OP, CVRP, MVC, PFSP, and JSSP, and 94% for MIS, compared to often very low feasibility rates (e.g., 3-8% for GPT-4o on CVRP, MIS, MVC) from general LLMs. The average optimality gaps range from **1.03% to 8.20%**, drastically lower than the tens or hundreds of percent exhibited by baselines (e.g., GPT-4o's 76.62% on CVRP or GPT-o1's 276% on TSP).
- **Efficiency:** The fine-tuned 7B model maintains competitive average solving times (around 9.8s with BoN) compared to general-purpose LLMs, and is substantially faster than reasoning models (which can take minutes, e.g., DeepSeek-R1's 6.5 minutes average), which struggle to scale with instance size due to verbose CoT.
- **Superiority Over Prompt Strategies:** The method also outperforms advanced prompt-based strategies (OPRO, LMEA, PHP) and code-generation approaches (SGE), which, despite their iterative reasoning or code execution, fail to achieve comparable feasibility and optimality, often taking much longer to solve (e.g., LMEA's 5.3 minutes average).

#### 4.2. Comparison with Domain-Specific Methods and Scalability (Table 2)

- **Competitive with Heuristics:** The LLM solver (SFT+RL+BoN) demonstrates strong performance against simpler domain-specific algorithms and metaheuristics (e.g., NN, FI for TSP; Greedy, GI, Tsili for OP; Sweep, PS for CVRP; Degree, Greedy for MIS/MVC; Palmer’s, NEH for PFSP; SPT, FIFO, ATC for JSSP). For small graphs, it often surpasses them, achieving lower optimality gaps and higher Gap@K percentages (e.g., 0.14% gap vs. FI's 2.27% on small TSP).
- **Performance Degradation with Scale:** While maintaining excellent performance on small and medium graphs, the optimality gap tends to increase for large graphs, similar to many heuristics, but generally remains competitive (e.g., 1.34% for large TSP, 7.24% for large CVRP).
- **Comparison with Classical Solvers (Appendix E.5):** Against high-performance heuristic solvers (LKH, HGS, EA4OP, QIG), the LLM solver is competitive, often achieving comparable or better performance on problems like TSP and OP. Crucially, against the exact solver Gurobi, the LLM solver significantly outperforms it on complex routing problems (CVRP: 4.53% vs. 35.09%; OP: 1.85% vs. 21.96%) under similar time budgets, highlighting its strength in scenarios where exact solvers struggle with time constraints.

#### 4.3. Role of Reinforcement Learning (FOARL) (Figure 3)

- **Feasibility Improvement:** FOARL demonstrably improves solution feasibility, especially for constrained problems like OP and CVRP. It mitigates the "over-greedy" behavior from SFT by increasing constraint margins, leading to fewer constraint violations.
- **Sampling Efficiency:** FOARL enhances sampling efficiency during inference. RL-trained policies achieve comparable performance levels with fewer Best-of-N samples (smaller N) than SFT-only policies, reducing test-time computational costs.

#### 4.4. Versatility and Generalizability (Appendices E.8, E.9, E.10, E.11)

- **Unified Solver:** A single LLM trained jointly on all seven CO problems can effectively solve diverse tasks, maintaining strong feasibility and optimality (e.g., 100% feasibility for most, gaps ranging from 0.86% to 8.25%). This demonstrates the potential for LLMs as universal CO foundation models.
- **Backbone Versatility:** The framework's effectiveness extends to other LLM backbones like Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B, consistently improving feasibility and optimality from SFT to the full SFT+RL+BoN pipeline.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Performance:** The trained LLM solvers generalize well to OOD routing problem instances (clustered and mixed distributions), showing only a slight performance drop compared to in-distribution data, confirming robustness.
- **Benchmark Performance:** On real-world benchmarks like Taillard (JSSP) and TSPLIB (TSP), the method outperforms existing heuristic, learning-based, and other LLM-based approaches, validating its generalizability to unseen, complex instances.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study: Heuristic Features (Table 9, Figure 4)

- Including simple, computationally inexpensive heuristic features (like top-k nearest neighbors) in the TAI consistently leads to better performance and faster training convergence. These features effectively guide the LLM's latent solution-space exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the application of Large Language Models to Combinatorial Optimization, proposing a novel paradigm that shifts away from intermediate code generation or solver invocation towards truly end-to-end, language-based problem-solving.

**Key Significance:**
1. **End-to-End Language-Based CO Solving:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating that LLMs can function as direct, end-to-end CO solvers, interpreting natural language descriptions and generating solutions without requiring complex programming interfaces or external solver calls. This simplifies the problem-solving pipeline and democratizes access to optimization tools.
2. **Addressing Core LLM Limitations in CO:** The proposed two-stage fine-tuning (SFT + FOARL) effectively tackles the persistent issues of low feasibility rates and high optimality gaps that plague previous LLM-based CO attempts. The FOARL mechanism, specifically designed to instill feasibility awareness and improve optimality, is a crucial innovation for reliable LLM deployment in constrained environments.
3. **Unified and Generalizable Approach:** Unlike traditional problem-specific heuristics or Neural Combinatorial Optimization (NCO) models that often require tailored architectures, this method provides a unified framework. A single fine-tuned LLM can address a wide array of NP-hard CO problems without extensive architectural customization, showcasing the potential for LLMs as foundation models for optimization.
4. **Competitive Performance:** Despite using a relatively smaller 7B-parameter LLM, the method achieves remarkably high feasibility and low optimality gaps, consistently outperforming much larger general-purpose LLMs, reasoning models, and even some specialized heuristics. This underscores the power of targeted fine-tuning for specific domains.
5. **Reduced Domain Expertise:** By translating complex CO problems into a language generation task, the reliance on domain experts for modeling, algorithm design, or solver usage is substantially reduced. This could empower a broader range of users to leverage advanced optimization techniques.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Democratization of Optimization:** This work could make powerful CO tools accessible to non-experts, enabling better decision-making in various industries (e.g., logistics, supply chain, manufacturing, resource allocation) where specialized optimization expertise is scarce.
- **Accelerated Prototyping and Deployment:** The ability to rapidly generate feasible and near-optimal solutions from natural language could significantly accelerate the prototyping and deployment of optimization solutions in dynamic or evolving scenarios.
- **New Research Avenues:** The success of this end-to-end approach opens up new research directions in LLM fine-tuning strategies for complex problem-solving, efficient textual representation for numerical data, and novel reward designs for constrained tasks.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge a major limitation: the solving efficiency of their LLM-based method is not yet on par with traditional lightweight heuristics for certain tasks. Future research will focus on:
1. **Efficiency Improvements:** Developing more efficient fine-tuning and inference strategies to tackle larger-scale CO problems.
2. **Enhanced Textual Representations:** Exploring more effective textual input representations (TAIs) to further boost learning performance.
3. **Hybrid Approaches:** Integrating the LLM solver with existing heuristics to leverage their computational speed and further enhance feasibility guarantees and optimality.

In conclusion, this paper presents a robust and effective method for empowering LLMs to act as end-to-end CO solvers. It not only addresses critical performance gaps in existing LLM-based approaches but also lays the groundwork for a more accessible, generalizable, and language-driven future for combinatorial optimization.
