# Where to Touch, How to Contact: Hierarchical RL-MPC Framework for Geometry-Aware Long-Horizon Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) and model predictive control (MPC) framework addresses contact-rich robotic manipulation by decoupling geometric reasoning from contact dynamics through a "contact intention" interface. This approach enables robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and demonstrates significant data efficiency improvements, achieving 100% success on seen pushing tasks and 95% on unseen ones in real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation systems struggle to jointly reason about global geometry and non-smooth contact dynamics, particularly in contact-rich tasks.
- End-to-end learning policies are data-inefficient and face challenges in zero-shot sim-to-real transfer due to implicitly learning complex contact dynamics.
- Model-based optimization methods, like MPC, often suffer from short planning horizons, leading to local optima and poor generalization for long-horizon or geometrically diverse tasks.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical framework== integrates a high-level ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== policy with a low-level ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== operating at different frequencies.
- The high-level RL policy, trained in an ==abstract simulation==, predicts an object-centric ==contact intention== comprising desired contact locations and a post-contact object subgoal pose.
- A low-level ==contact-implicit MPC (ComFree-MPC)== then executes this contact intention by planning precise robot actions to realize the specified contacts and achieve the subgoal.

## Results

- The framework achieved **100%** success for seen letter shapes and **99.69%** for unseen letter shapes in abstract simulation after approximately **40,000** RL steps, indicating high data efficiency.
- Policies trained in abstract simulation transferred zero-shot to real-world hardware, achieving **100%** success for seen pushing tasks, **95%** for unseen pushing, and **100%** for 3D reorientation on a Franka robot arm.
- The hierarchical approach demonstrated a **40x** reduction in RL decision steps and higher success rates compared to end-to-end RL baselines, while MPC-only baselines consistently failed in long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling geometric planning from physical contact dynamics into a hierarchical structure simplifies the overall manipulation problem, making each sub-problem more tractable.
> - An object-centric "contact intention" interface serves as an effective abstraction, enabling high data efficiency for the RL policy and robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer by focusing on geometry and kinematics.
> - Explicitly guiding the low-level MPC with high-level object subgoals helps overcome the short-sightedness of purely model-based planners and prevents them from getting stuck in local optima.
