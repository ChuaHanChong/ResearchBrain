# Real-Time Robot Execution with Masked Action Chunking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois Chicago and Cisco Research developed REMAC, a method that improves real-time robotic manipulation by learning a delay-aware policy for asynchronous action chunking in Vision-Language-Action models. It consistently outperforms existing baselines in both simulation and real-world Franka robot tasks, achieving higher success rates and smoother execution without introducing additional inference latency.

## Problem

- Deploying large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in real-time robotic tasks faces challenges due to critical responsiveness requirements, where even small latencies can lead to instability or failure.
- Asynchronous inference with action chunking exacerbates two problems: inter-chunk discontinuity (jerky movements between action chunks) and intra-chunk inconsistency (actions within a chunk being executed based on outdated perceptions due to inference delay).
- Prior solutions often focus on inter-chunk discontinuity and rely on test-time corrections, which typically introduce additional computational latency, limiting real-time applicability.

## Method

- **REMAC** (Real-time Execution with Masked Action Chunking) learns a ==delay-aware policy== by finetuning a pretrained ==action-chunking== policy using ==LoRA==, explicitly accounting for inference delays during training.
- During training, **Masked Action Chunking** employs ==prefix masking== to focus the loss on actions that will actually be newly executed given a delay, and a ==self-conditioned curriculum== to improve robustness to distribution shifts.
- During inference, ==Prefix-Preserved Sampling== initializes new action chunks with previously committed actions and only updates the unexecuted segment, enhancing inter-chunk continuity and aligning with the training procedure.

## Results

- REMAC consistently outperformed Naive Async, Bidirectional Decoding (BID), and Real-time Trajectory Correction (RTC) across **12 dynamic Kinetix simulation tasks**, showing pronounced gains under larger inference delays and achieving shorter average execution times.
- In real-world Franka robot grasp-and-place tasks, REMAC achieved **higher average completion rates** and produced the most stable robot dynamics with fewer abrupt changes in velocity and acceleration compared to synchronous and other asynchronous baselines.
- The method demonstrated strong robustness to noisy and injected latency, maintained performance close to models trained with **20 times more data**, and was successfully integrated into Transformer-based ACT policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intra-chunk inconsistency, the misalignment between current robot perception and actions being executed within a chunk due to inference delay, is a critical and previously overlooked failure mode in asynchronous robotic control.
> - Learning delay-aware adjustments directly into the policy during training, rather than relying solely on test-time corrections, allows for significant performance gains without incurring additional inference latency.
> - A training regimen incorporating prefix masking, a self-conditioned curriculum, and residual alignment enables the policy to become inherently resilient to varied real-world execution delays and improve the refinement of existing policy proposals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large-scale AI models, reinforcement learning, and real-time robotic control. The broader landscape has been significantly influenced by the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which represent a paradigm shift in enabling robots to interpret high-level human instructions and sensory observations into low-level motor commands. Models like RT-1, RT-2, and π0 (mentioned in the paper) exemplify this trend, showcasing impressive capabilities in dexterous manipulation and generalist policies.

A critical challenge for deploying these advanced models in real-world robotics, particularly cyber-physical systems, is ensuring **real-time responsiveness**. Unlike language or image generation models where delays primarily impact user experience, in robotics, even small latencies can lead to mission failure, instability, or unsafe operations. The paper highlights that traditional approaches to real-time control, such as merely accelerating model prediction, are increasingly insufficient given the escalating size of VLA models, higher control frequencies demanded by complex tasks, and the variability of real-world deployment conditions.

The concept of **action chunking**, where a policy predicts a sequence of actions rather than a single immediate action, has emerged as a partial solution for managing temporal coherence in long-horizon tasks. However, action chunking introduces its own set of problems: reduced system reactivity to sudden state changes and discontinuities at chunk boundaries.

To overcome the fundamental latency limitations of synchronous inference (where the robot waits for the next chunk to be predicted), **asynchronous inference** has gained traction. This paradigm allows the VLA model to predict the next action chunk concurrently while the robot executes the current one, ensuring a continuous supply of actions. However, the paper argues that the naive integration of asynchronous inference with action chunking can exacerbate existing limitations, leading to substantial performance degradation.

Prior research has primarily focused on mitigating **inter-chunk discontinuity**—the large jumps or incoherence at the boundaries between consecutive action chunks. Methods like Temporal Ensembling (TE), Bidirectional Decoding (BID), and Real-time Trajectory Correction (RTC) attempt to smooth these transitions, often by using test-time refinements. While effective to varying degrees, these methods often introduce additional computational latency (as with RTC) or are heuristic in nature.

This paper makes a crucial contribution by identifying and rigorously addressing another critical yet overlooked failure mode: **intra-chunk inconsistency**. This refers to the misalignment between the robot's current perception and the actions *being executed within* a single chunk, which arises because the initial actions of a chunk might have been predicted based on an older observation (due to inference delay). By tackling this alongside inter-chunk discontinuity, the paper offers a more comprehensive solution to the challenges of real-time asynchronous robotic execution. It moves beyond purely test-time corrections by proposing a training-time adaptation, which offers the significant advantage of improved performance without incurring additional inference latency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust and reliable real-time robot manipulation using Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly when operating under an asynchronous inference paradigm combined with action chunking. The authors identify two core challenges that impede the effective deployment of such systems:

1. **Exacerbated Inter-chunk Discontinuity:** While action chunking helps manage long-horizon tasks, switching between consecutive chunks can lead to abrupt, incoherent, or "jerky" movements, especially when inference is asynchronous. Different chunks, even if both valid continuations of the past, might originate from different "latent expert modes," causing large jumps at boundaries. Previous methods have primarily focused on smoothing these transitions.
2. **Intra-chunk Inconsistency (Novel Contribution):** This is a critical factor identified by the authors that has been largely overlooked. It stems from the fundamental mismatch between the robot's perception and the actions being executed *within* a single action chunk. Due to unavoidable inference delays (d), the robot might execute the first $d$ actions of a chunk that were actually predicted based on an *older observation* ($o_{t-h}$), rather than the current observation ($o_t$). This creates a perception-action mismatch within the currently executing chunk, leading to suboptimal or incorrect actions.

The key motivation is to develop a robust and efficient solution that addresses *both* inter-chunk discontinuity and intra-chunk inconsistency without introducing any additional inference latency, which is a significant limitation of several existing test-time approaches. The authors aim to learn corrective adjustments directly into the policy during training, making the policy inherently resilient to the real-world complexities of delayed execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **REMAC (Real-time Execution with Masked Action Chunking)**, is designed to learn a delay-aware policy $\hat{v}_\pi(A_t|o_t, d)$ that builds upon a pretrained action-chunking policy $v_\pi(A_t|o_t)$, specifically targeting flow-matching policies. REMAC comprises several interconnected strategies applied during training and inference.

#### 3.1. Masked Action Chunking (Training Strategies)

This set of strategies aims to mitigate intra-chunk inconsistency by exposing the policy to conditions resembling delayed execution during training:

- **Prefix Masking (Equation 2 & 3):**
 - **Concept:** To address the perception-action mismatch within a chunk, REMAC shifts the learning focus toward the "to-be-executed" segment of the action chunk.
 - **Mechanism:** During training, a delay-conditioned prefix mask $m_d$ is introduced. This mask zeroes out (excludes from supervision) the initial $d$ actions of a predicted chunk, focusing the loss calculation only on actions that would *actually be newly executed* given an inference delay $d$.
 - **Adaptability:** The delay $d$ is randomly sampled uniformly from a range during training. This forces the policy to learn to adapt to various degrees of partial misalignment between observation and execution, making it resilient to uncertainty in unexecuted prefix actions. This strategy enables a single policy to handle multiple delay settings.
- **Self-conditioned Curriculum (Equation 4):**
 - **Concept:** To bridge the "train-test mismatch" (where training uses ground truth but testing relies on the model's own predictions), REMAC incorporates a curriculum learning approach.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of always providing ground-truth action chunks ($A_t$) as input targets during flow-matching training, the input is a mixture of ground-truth and the *pretrained policy's own predictions* ($\tilde{A}_t$). A mixing parameter $\sigma$ (controlled by a Bernoulli distribution) anneals from 1 (pure ground-truth) to 0 (pure self-conditioned input) over the course of training, typically using a piecewise linear decay schedule.
 - **Benefits:** This gradually transitions the policy from relying on perfect ground truth to refining its own proposals, stabilizing training, mitigating exposure bias, and improving robustness to distribution shifts and compounding errors in rollouts.
- **Residual Alignment (Equation 5 & 6):**
 - **Concept:** This objective explicitly encourages the policy's learned corrections to align with the difference between the pretrained policy's output and the true target.
 - **Mechanism:** A $\Delta$-matching term is added to the loss, which minimizes the difference between the actual residual $(u_\tau - \tilde{u}_\tau)$ and the policy's predicted residual $(\hat{u}_\tau - \tilde{u}_\tau)$ on the unmasked action chunk segment.
 - **Benefits:** While related to the direct alignment with ground truth (Prefix Masking), Residual Alignment focuses on learning *how to adjust* from the pretrained policy, leading to substantial performance improvements by refining the existing policy rather than completely re-learning it. The overall loss is a weighted sum of Prefix Masking loss ($L_m$) and Residual Alignment loss ($L_\Delta$).

#### 3.2. Prefix-Preserved Sampling (Inference Strategy)

To enhance inter-chunk continuity and align with the training procedure, REMAC refines the sampling process during inference:

- **Initialized Action State:** Unlike conventional flow matching that samples an initial latent action sequence from a Gaussian prior, REMAC initializes the action state $A_0^t$ using "can-be-executed actions" from the previous chunk. Specifically, the first $P-h$ entries (where $P$ is prediction horizon, $h$ is execution horizon) are filled with the last $P-h$ actions from the *previous predicted chunk*, and the rest are zero-initialized.
- **Masked Integration (Equation 7):** During the flow-matching integration steps, the portion of the action chunk that has already been committed to (i.e., the *overlapping segment* with previously executed actions) is preserved. Only the remaining portion of the chunk is updated by integrating the learned velocity field $\hat{v}_\pi$. This ensures that actions already in the control queue are not altered while the new chunk is being generated, reinforcing continuity.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Model Adaptation:** REMAC utilizes **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)** for parameter-efficient finetuning. This allows for adapting the pretrained policy by introducing a small number of additional parameters (e.g., 1.5% of original model), which can then be merged back into the backbone model layers for zero additional inference time overhead. LoRA is particularly suitable for making distributional adjustments without retraining the entire model.
- **Training and Sampling:** During training, the prefix mask coverage is controlled by uniformly sampling the delay $d$ from a gradually shrinking interval $[q_{min}, q_{max}]$. During inference, the model takes the estimated inference delay and the previous action chunk as explicit inputs for predicting the next chunk.
- **Deployment Framework:** For simulations, predicted actions are segmented and concatenated based on predefined delays. For real-world deployment, a common setup is used where the VLA model runs on a remote server, transmitting actions via gRPC to the robot client. The robot client estimates the inference delay based on recent measurements and maintains an action queue.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

REMAC was extensively evaluated in both simulation and real-world settings, consistently demonstrating superior performance over existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Kinetix Environment (Simulation)

Experiments were conducted on 12 highly dynamic and stochastic Kinetix tasks, designed to test asynchronous execution. Policies were configured with a prediction horizon of $P=8$, and inference delays $d$ ranged from 0 to 4.

- **Overall Performance:** REMAC consistently outperformed Naive Async, Bidirectional Decoding (BID), and RTC (Real-time Trajectory Correction) across all delay settings, showing particularly pronounced gains under larger inference delays.
- **Efficiency:** REMAC achieved shorter average execution times, indicating faster task completion.
- **Robustness to Delay:** The method exhibited a smaller performance drop and a lower increase in execution time as inference delay increased, highlighting its robustness. Even under $d=0$ (no nominal delay), REMAC showed performance improvements, attributed to its masked action chunking strategy enforcing stronger coherence.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Component Contributions:** Each proposed component (Prefix Masking, Self-conditioned Curriculum, Residual Alignment) progressively contributed to the overall performance improvement, while LoRA alone (without REMAC's training paradigm) yielded no significant gains.
 - **Curriculum Scheduling:** The self-conditioned curriculum (gradually blending ground-truth with self-conditioned inputs) proved superior to training exclusively with ground-truth or purely self-conditioned inputs, stabilizing learning and mitigating exposure bias.
 - **Integration with Test-Time Methods:** REMAC showed compatibility with and offered modest additional performance gains when combined with test-time methods like BID and RTC, especially under higher delays, demonstrating its utility as a foundational backbone policy.
 - **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The method proved robust to the specific choices of $q_{max}$ and $q_{min}$ for mask coverage.
 - **Generalizability:** REMAC was successfully integrated into Transformer-based ACT policies, outperforming naive asynchronous and LoRA-only baselines, indicating its applicability beyond flow-matching models.
 - **Data Efficiency:** REMAC maintained performance very close to models trained with 20 times more data (10 vs 200 trajectories), suggesting it's not data-hungry.
 - **Robustness to Noisy Delays:** Under simulated noisy, fluctuating, and spiky delay patterns, REMAC degraded gracefully and still largely outperformed RTC even when RTC received accurate delay inputs.

#### 4.2. Real-World Environment (Franka Robot)

Real-world experiments used a Franka Research 3 robot on three single-arm grasp-and-place tasks (Grasp-Easy, Medium, Hard), with a control frequency of 15Hz and an execution horizon $h=8$. The VLA model ($\pi_0$) was hosted remotely, leading to an effective inference delay of $d=2$ or $3$ (122-140ms).

- **Completion Progress:** REMAC achieved higher average completion rates across all grasp-and-place tasks, including challenging ones requiring fine manipulation.
- **Robustness to Injected Latency:** Even with additional injected delays (75ms and 150ms, simulating slower hardware/networks), REMAC consistently outperformed all baselines, demonstrating strong robustness to varying real-world delay levels.
- **Robot Kinematics:** Qualitative and quantitative analysis showed that REMAC produced the most stable robot dynamics with fewer abrupt changes in velocity and acceleration compared to synchronous inference (which caused jerky motions and pauses) and other asynchronous methods.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Synchronous inference struggled with frequent pauses and errors. Naive Async and Temporal Ensembling were prone to grasping/placement inaccuracies. RTC, while strong, suffered from the additional latency it introduced, particularly under larger overall delays.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, with its novel REMAC method, holds significant implications for the field of robotics and the broader application of AI in cyber-physical systems.

- **Addressing a Critical Gap in Real-Time Robotics:** The paper tackles a fundamental challenge that currently limits the widespread deployment of advanced VLA models in dynamic, real-world robotic tasks: ensuring robust real-time performance. By effectively mitigating both inter-chunk discontinuity and the newly identified intra-chunk inconsistency, REMAC provides a more comprehensive and practical solution than prior art.
- **Novel Conceptual Contribution (Intra-Chunk Inconsistency):** The explicit identification and systematic addressing of intra-chunk inconsistency represents a crucial conceptual advancement. Recognizing that the executed actions within a chunk can misalign with current perception due to inference delays provides a deeper understanding of real-time control failures and opens new avenues for research.
- **Zero-Latency Performance Enhancement:** A major strength of REMAC is that it achieves substantial performance improvements (higher success rates, faster completion, smoother motion) through a training-time adaptation without introducing any additional inference latency during deployment. This is a critical advantage over many test-time correction methods like RTC, which add computational overhead and can degrade performance under tighter real-time constraints.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** The extensive experiments demonstrate REMAC's exceptional robustness to varying and even noisy inference delays, a common characteristic of real-world environments. This resilience makes robot policies more reliable and adaptable to fluctuating network conditions or hardware limitations, paving the way for safer and more consistent robot operation.
- **Versatility and Compatibility:** REMAC's design, particularly its use of LoRA for parameter-efficient finetuning, makes it a flexible and plug-and-play solution. It is shown to be compatible with different policy architectures (flow-matching and Transformer-based ACT) and can complement existing test-time strategies, suggesting broad applicability across the evolving landscape of VLA models.
- **Practical Impact and Generalizability:** The method's effectiveness in both high-fidelity simulations and challenging real-world tasks (grasp-and-place) underscores its practical utility. Its data efficiency, demonstrated by maintaining performance with significantly less training data, further enhances its real-world viability where data collection can be costly.
- **Future of Cyber-Physical Systems:** By enabling VLA models to operate reliably in real-time under practical constraints, REMAC contributes to accelerating the adoption of intelligent robots in diverse domains such as manufacturing, logistics, exploration, and service robotics, where responsiveness and autonomy are paramount.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the need to specify a maximum inference delay in advance and potential demands for finetuning data (though ablations suggest good data efficiency), the overall contribution of REMAC is a significant step forward in making advanced robotic manipulation policies truly real-time and robust in complex, dynamic environments.
