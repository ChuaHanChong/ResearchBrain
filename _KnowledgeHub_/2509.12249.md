# Why and How Auxiliary Tasks Improve JEPA Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Johns Hopkins and Brown Universities provides a theoretical explanation for how auxiliary tasks improve Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), proving they prevent representation collapse and guide the learning of task-relevant abstractions. The work demonstrates that the choice of auxiliary function determines the information the representation must preserve, offering principled guidance for designing JEPA encoders in areas like model-based reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) frequently experience "brittleness and representation collapse" without careful, often heuristic, tuning.
- Existing theoretical frameworks for self-supervised learning often operate under idealized infinite-data conditions, offering limited practical guidance for finite-data scenarios where collapse is common.
- The precise role and mechanism of auxiliary tasks in shaping JEPA's learned representations remained poorly understood, treated more as a heuristic than a principled component.

## Method

- The authors define **P-JEPA**, a simplified JEPA variant trained jointly with a ==latent transition== dynamics loss and an auxiliary regression loss.
- They adapt the concept of bisimulation to define "non-bisimilar" observations based on both transition dynamics and the chosen auxiliary function.
- A central theoretical result, the "No Unhealthy Representation Collapse" theorem, proves that a well-trained P-JEPA maps non-bisimilar observations to distinct latent representations.

## Results

- In a controlled counting environment, P-JEPA with a reward-based auxiliary accurately produced nine distinct latent clusters, each corresponding to a unique object count, confirming the theoretical prediction.
- The model successfully abstracted away irrelevant visual details (e.g., object shape, color, position), preserving only the object count when guided by the reward auxiliary.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that both the latent dynamics and auxiliary losses are crucial for learning robust, well-separated, and abstract representations, as neither component alone achieved comparable results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Auxiliary tasks are not merely heuristics but fundamental components that actively define which information JEPA representations must preserve, thereby preventing unhealthy representation collapse.
> - JEPA, through the combined objective of dynamics and auxiliary tasks, implicitly performs state abstraction, discarding irrelevant variations while retaining distinctions relevant to the auxiliary function.
> - The specific choice of the auxiliary function directly dictates the equivalence relations formed in the latent space, providing a principled method to tailor JEPA representations for specific downstream tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper makes significant contributions to several interconnected areas within artificial intelligence:

- **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) and Representation Learning:** Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) have emerged as a powerful family of SSL methods for learning rich visual representations from unlabeled data, particularly for images and videos. However, a persistent challenge in SSL, including JEPA, is representation collapse, where the learned embeddings fail to distinguish between semantically different inputs. Existing SSL theories often operate in idealized infinite-data or nonparametric regimes, offering limited guidance for practical, finite-data scenarios where collapse is a real concern. This paper directly addresses this gap by providing a *finite-data regime* theoretical characterization for a practical JEPA variant, explaining how auxiliary tasks can prevent "unhealthy" representation collapse. It clarifies the role of auxiliary tasks as not just heuristics but as crucial anchors for distinguishing relevant information.
- **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL):** JEPA models are increasingly integrated into MBRL systems to learn compact, predictive latent dynamics models. In MBRL, the quality of learned representations profoundly impacts the agent's ability to plan and generalize. This paper's insights into how auxiliary tasks guide JEPA to learn *meaningful* representations are therefore directly applicable and highly significant for improving the efficacy and robustness of MBRL agents. The focus on deterministic MDPs and the use of bisimulation directly tie into the theoretical foundations of state abstraction in RL.
- **Bisimulation and State Abstraction:** The concept of bisimulation is foundational in RL for identifying observation states that are behaviorally equivalent, allowing for state abstraction and more efficient learning. This paper re-conceptualizes bisimulation by replacing the reward function with a more general "auxiliary function." By proving that a well-trained JEPA with an auxiliary task cannot collapse non-bisimilar observations (defined by the auxiliary and transition dynamics), the work provides a mechanism for learning task-relevant state abstractions implicitly through the JEPA training objective, rather than explicitly optimizing for bisimulation distances.
- **Addressing Known JEPA Challenges:** The paper explicitly confronts the "brittleness and representation collapse unless carefully tuned" issue reported by JEPA practitioners. It offers a theoretical explanation for *why* certain tunings (specifically, the choice of auxiliary tasks) are effective, thus transforming empirical observations into principled design choices. This positions the work as a foundational step towards building more robust and interpretable JEPA models.

In essence, this research bridges theoretical computer science (formal guarantees, bisimulation), core machine learning (representation learning, SSL), and reinforcement learning (model-based control), offering a unified perspective on how auxiliary tasks empower JEPA to learn useful, non-collapsing, and abstract representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **provide a theoretical characterization of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) when trained with an auxiliary regression head, and to explain how this auxiliary task contributes to learning robust and meaningful representations.**

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Understanding JEPA's Behavior:** Despite its growing popularity in visual representation learning and model-based Reinforcement Learning, the underlying behavior and mechanisms of JEPA remain "poorly understood." Practitioners often encounter "brittleness and representation collapse unless carefully tuned", highlighting a critical need for theoretical insights to guide its application.
- **Filling Theoretical Gaps in SSL:** Existing theories for self-supervised learning often focus on connections between methods or offer guarantees under idealized conditions like infinite or nonparametric regimes. There is a lack of theoretical statements that hold in realistic finite-data scenarios, which are crucial for practical development and debugging of SSL models like JEPA.
- **Formalizing the Role of Auxiliary Tasks:** Auxiliary tasks are frequently used in complex deep learning architectures as heuristics to improve performance or stability. The authors aim to elevate the auxiliary task from a heuristic to a principled component of JEPA training, demonstrating its fundamental role in shaping the learned representation. They seek to answer: "the auxiliary task is not a heuristic—it determines the information the representation must preserve."
- **Guiding JEPA Encoder Improvement:** The ultimate motivation is to offer actionable guidance for improving JEPA encoders. By understanding *what* information the auxiliary task forces the representation to preserve, researchers and practitioners can select auxiliary tasks that lead to representations beneficial for specific downstream applications, rather than relying on trial-and-error. This promises to make JEPA more reliable and effective.

In summary, the paper aims to move beyond empirical observations of JEPA's behavior by providing a rigorous theoretical framework that explains *why* auxiliary tasks are effective and *how* they prevent representation collapse, thereby offering a principled approach to designing and improving JEPA models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a dual approach, combining rigorous theoretical analysis with controlled empirical validation.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework

1. **Model Definition (P-JEPA):** The authors consider a simplified yet practical variant of JEPA, termed P-JEPA (Fig. 1). This model consists of:
 - An **encoder** ($E_\phi$) that maps observations ($o_t$) to latent representations ($z_t$).
 - A **latent transition model** ($T_\psi$) that predicts the next latent state ($z_{t+1}$) given the current latent state ($z_t$) and action ($a_t$).
 - An **auxiliary regression head** ($P_\theta$) that predicts an auxiliary function ($p(o)$) from the latent representation ($z_t$).
 Crucially, the P-JEPA variant is defined without a stop gradient in JEPA’s latent dynamics loss and without a target/EMA encoder, simplifying the setup for theoretical analysis while retaining core JEPA characteristics.

2. **Training Objective:** The model is trained jointly by minimizing a combined loss function: $L(\theta, \phi, \psi) = L_{dyn} + c_p L_p$.
 - $L_{dyn}$: Measures the consistency between the predicted next latent state from $T_\psi(E_\phi(o_t), a_t)$ and the actual next latent state $E_\phi(o_{t+1})$.
 - $L_p$: Measures the difference between the auxiliary head's output $P_\theta(E_\phi(o))$ and the ground truth auxiliary function value $p(o)$.
 The hyperparameter $c_p$ controls the weight of the auxiliary loss.

3. **Deterministic MDP Assumption:** The theoretical analysis is conducted within the context of a deterministic Markov Decision Process (MDP), which simplifies the dynamics and allows for clearer formal definitions of equivalence.

4. **Redefining Bisimulation:** To formally define "non-equivalent" observations in the context of an auxiliary task, the authors adapt the classical concept of bisimulation (traditionally based on rewards and transitions). They introduce **Definition 1 (Largest bisimulation)**, which defines pairs of observations as non-bisimilar if they differ in their auxiliary function values *or* if they lead to non-bisimilar states after applying the same action. This iterative definition collects all pairs distinguishable either immediately or after a sequence of actions, with respect to the auxiliary function $p$.

5. **Main Theoretical Result (Theorem 1: No Unhealthy Representation Collapse):** This central theorem proves that if the P-JEPA model is "well-trained" (i.e., both the latent-transition consistency loss and the auxiliary regression loss are driven to zero), then any two observations that are *not* in the largest bisimulation (as defined by the MDP and the auxiliary function $p$) *must* map to distinct latent representations. This means the encoder *cannot* collapse representations for observations that are deemed distinct by the combined dynamics and auxiliary task. An empirical, finite-data version of this theorem (Theorem 3) is also provided, which is more directly applicable to real-world training scenarios.

#### 3.2. Empirical Validation

1. **Counting Environment:** To validate the theory, the authors design a controlled "counting environment."
 - **Observations:** 64x64 RGB images containing a variable number ($k \in \{0, \dots, 8\}$) of objects. Object shape, color, and position are randomized to introduce irrelevant variations.
 - **Actions:** Increase or decrease the object count by one.
 - **Auxiliary Function:** The primary auxiliary task is to regress to a simple **reward function** which is 1 if the object count equals a fixed number ($n=4$) and 0 otherwise.
 - This setup allows for precise control over bisimulation properties. The theory predicts 9 distinct non-bisimilar sets (one for each object count), even though the reward function itself only has two values (0 or 1).

2. **Experiment Scenarios:**
 - **P-JEPA with Reward Auxiliary:** Training the full P-JEPA model with the reward as the auxiliary task. This is the main experiment testing Theorem 1's prediction about count-based separation.
 - **P-JEPA with Random Auxiliary:** The auxiliary function is replaced with a fixed random 256-D linear mapping. This creates a scenario where almost all observation pairs are non-bisimilar, testing how much information the encoder is forced to preserve.
 - **Ablation Studies:**
 - Encoder receiving gradients *only* from the reward loss (no dynamics loss).
 - Encoder receiving gradients *only* from the latent transition loss (no auxiliary loss), which is known to cause complete collapse.

3. **Analysis Methods:**
 - **PCA Visualization:** Used to visualize the 256-D latent embeddings and identify clustering patterns.
 - **Pairwise $L_2$ Distances:** Heatmaps of distances between embeddings are generated to quantitatively assess separation and compactness of clusters.
 - **Decoder Reconstructions:** A separate decoder is trained in parallel, *without backpropagating gradients to the encoder*, to reconstruct observations from latent states. This tests whether the encoder has discarded "irrelevant" information (like shape, color, position) while preserving task-relevant distinctions.
 - **Nearest-Centroid Classification Accuracy:** Used to quantify compactness of clusters based on object counts.

This comprehensive methodology allows the authors to both formally prove a key characteristic of auxiliary-task-enhanced JEPA and empirically demonstrate its implications in a controlled environment, providing strong evidence for their claims.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, strongly corroborating the theoretical predictions and demonstrating the practical implications of auxiliary tasks in JEPA.

1. **Confirmation of "No Unhealthy Representation Collapse" (Theorem 1):**
 - **Reward Auxiliary:** In the counting environment, with the reward function as the auxiliary task, the theory predicted that observations corresponding to different object counts ($k \in \{0, \dots, 8\}$) would form 9 distinct non-collapsible classes. The empirical results perfectly matched this prediction. PCA visualization (top row, left, Fig. 2) clearly showed **nine distinct clusters** in the latent space, with each cluster corresponding to a unique object count. The pairwise $L_2$ distance heatmap (top row, middle, Fig. 2) exhibited dark diagonal blocks and brighter off-diagonal blocks, indicating that distances *within* an object count were significantly smaller than distances *across* different counts, signifying strong separation.
 - **Abstraction of Irrelevant Information:** The decoder, trained without backpropagating into the encoder, was unable to reconstruct details such as shape, color, or specific object positions (top row, right, Fig. 2). This crucial result demonstrates that the encoder successfully abstracted away task-irrelevant variations, focusing solely on the object count, as guided by the auxiliary task and transition dynamics.

2. **Impact of Auxiliary Task Specificity:**
 - **Random Auxiliary:** When the auxiliary task was a fixed random 256-D linear mapping, the theory suggested that almost all pairs of observations would become non-bisimilar, thus preventing most representation collapse. Empirically, the embeddings were indeed separated, but without the organized structure by object count (middle row, left, Fig. 2). The corresponding heatmap (middle row, middle, Fig. 2) showed diagonal blocks that were as bright as off-diagonal blocks, indicating similar distances within and across different object counts. This implies that the encoder was forced to preserve more distinctions than just the object count. Furthermore, the decoder in this setting *was* able to recover position information and some aspects of color and shape (middle row, right, Fig. 2), indicating that the encoder preserved these "irrelevant" factors when they became part of the non-bisimilar definition. This highlights that the choice of auxiliary task directly controls which distinctions the representation must preserve.

3. **Synergy of Dynamics and Auxiliary Losses (Ablation Studies):**
 - **Reward Loss Alone:** Training the encoder solely with the reward loss yielded only "coarse separation" (bottom row, left, Fig. 2). The heatmap showed a rough grouping of counts into three sets (e.g., 0-2, 3-5, 6-8), but not the fine-grained 9 distinct clusters achieved by P-JEPA. The decoder failed to recover any meaningful visual information (bottom row, right, Fig. 2).
 - **Latent Transition Loss Alone:** Consistent with previous research, training with only the latent transition loss led to "complete collapse into a single compact cluster" (not explicitly shown in Fig. 2, but stated in text).
 - **Key Conclusion:** The combined training of P-JEPA, incorporating both latent transition dynamics and an auxiliary head, was essential for generating the rich, well-separated, and abstract representations observed. The auxiliary task alone was insufficient for fine-grained distinction, and dynamics alone led to collapse. This demonstrates a crucial synergy.

In essence, the empirical results consistently validated the theoretical prediction that the auxiliary task, in conjunction with transition dynamics, anchors which distinctions the JEPA encoder *must* preserve. This mechanism allows the model to learn abstract representations by collapsing irrelevant variations while maintaining task-relevant information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant theoretical and practical contributions, with a broad potential impact across self-supervised learning, representation learning, and reinforcement learning.

1. **Grounding JEPA in Theory:** The most significant impact is providing a **first-principles theoretical characterization** for a practical variant of JEPA. By proving the "No Unhealthy Representation Collapse" theorem, the authors move JEPA research beyond empirical observation and into a realm of formal guarantees. This theoretical grounding addresses a critical pain point—the brittleness and collapse often reported by practitioners—by explaining *why* and *how* auxiliary tasks mitigate these issues. It elevates auxiliary tasks from ad-hoc heuristics to indispensable components whose choice fundamentally dictates the learned representation.

2. **Clear Path for JEPA Encoder Improvement:** The paper offers **actionable guidance for designing better JEPA encoders**. Instead of relying on architectural tweaks or hyperparameter sweeps, researchers can now strategically choose auxiliary functions that, when combined with environmental transition dynamics, define the "right equivalence relations" for their target problem. This means designing auxiliaries that encapsulate the "phenomenon that the representation should explain," allowing the encoder to discard irrelevant variations while preserving essential distinctions. This principled approach promises to accelerate the development of more robust, efficient, and task-specific JEPA models.

3. **Theoretical Foundation for Model-Based RL (MBRL):** The work provides a strong **theoretical underpinning for the success of JEPA-based MBRL architectures**. Specifically, it validates approaches like TD-MPC2 and PLDM which incorporate reward or Q-functions as auxiliary tasks. The paper explains *why* these choices are effective: they guide the JEPA encoder to learn representations that are relevant for decision-making and control, as they prevent the collapse of states that are non-bisimilar with respect to rewards and dynamics. This understanding can lead to more principled designs of world models in MBRL.

4. **Enabling Robust State Abstraction:** By leveraging an extended bisimulation concept, the research demonstrates how JEPA, through the interplay of dynamics and auxiliary tasks, can implicitly achieve **effective state abstraction**. The encoder learns to collapse observations within equivalence classes (e.g., all images of 4 objects, regardless of their color or position) while preserving distinctions across non-equivalent ones (e.g., 4 objects vs. 5 objects). This capability is vital for building scalable and generalizable RL agents that can abstract away irrelevant environmental details.

5. **A "Knowledge Discovery" Paradigm:** The paper frames the training of JEPA with auxiliary tasks as a form of "knowledge discovery." The learned triple of encoder, latent dynamics, and auxiliary predictor ($E_\phi, T_\psi(z,a), P_\theta(z)$) represents the discovered knowledge, explaining a user-specified phenomenon. This paradigm separates the process of learning general environmental regularities from task-specific control, suggesting a powerful workflow where foundational knowledge is acquired first and then leveraged for various downstream tasks. This perspective could foster new research directions in how models build comprehensive "world models" for subsequent reasoning and planning.

6. **Addressing a Core Challenge in SSL:** Representation collapse is a pervasive issue in SSL. This work provides a concrete mechanism and theoretical explanation for how to mitigate unhealthy collapse, which could have implications beyond JEPA to other self-supervised architectures struggling with similar issues.

In conclusion, "Why and How Auxiliary Tasks Improve JEPA Representations" is a landmark paper that transforms the understanding of a crucial component of modern self-supervised learning. By providing theoretical guarantees and empirical validation, it offers clear guidance for building more effective, robust, and interpretable JEPA models, with profound implications for visual representation learning and model-based reinforcement learning.
