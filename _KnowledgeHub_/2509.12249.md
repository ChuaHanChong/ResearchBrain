# Why and How Auxiliary Tasks Improve JEPA Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Johns Hopkins and Brown Universities provides a theoretical explanation for how auxiliary tasks improve Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), proving they prevent representation collapse and guide the learning of task-relevant abstractions. The work demonstrates that the choice of auxiliary function determines the information the representation must preserve, offering principled guidance for designing JEPA encoders in areas like model-based reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) frequently experience "brittleness and representation collapse" without careful, often heuristic, tuning.
- Existing theoretical frameworks for self-supervised learning often operate under idealized infinite-data conditions, offering limited practical guidance for finite-data scenarios where collapse is common.
- The precise role and mechanism of auxiliary tasks in shaping JEPA's learned representations remained poorly understood, treated more as a heuristic than a principled component.

## Method

- The authors define **P-JEPA**, a simplified JEPA variant trained jointly with a ==latent transition== dynamics loss and an auxiliary regression loss.
- They adapt the concept of bisimulation to define "non-bisimilar" observations based on both transition dynamics and the chosen auxiliary function.
- A central theoretical result, the "No Unhealthy Representation Collapse" theorem, proves that a well-trained P-JEPA maps non-bisimilar observations to distinct latent representations.

## Results

- In a controlled counting environment, P-JEPA with a reward-based auxiliary accurately produced nine distinct latent clusters, each corresponding to a unique object count, confirming the theoretical prediction.
- The model successfully abstracted away irrelevant visual details (e.g., object shape, color, position), preserving only the object count when guided by the reward auxiliary.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that both the latent dynamics and auxiliary losses are crucial for learning robust, well-separated, and abstract representations, as neither component alone achieved comparable results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Auxiliary tasks are not merely heuristics but fundamental components that actively define which information JEPA representations must preserve, thereby preventing unhealthy representation collapse.
> - JEPA, through the combined objective of dynamics and auxiliary tasks, implicitly performs state abstraction, discarding irrelevant variations while retaining distinctions relevant to the auxiliary function.
> - The specific choice of the auxiliary function directly dictates the equivalence relations formed in the latent space, providing a principled method to tailor JEPA representations for specific downstream tasks.
