# SAIL: Faster-than-Demonstration Execution of Imitation Learning Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The Georgia Institute of Technology researchers developed SAIL, a full-stack framework enabling visuomotor policies to execute tasks faster than human demonstrations. The framework achieves up to a 4x speedup in simulation and 3.2x in real-world scenarios while maintaining high success rates by addressing challenges like altered robot dynamics and system latencies.

## Problem

- Offline imitation learning policies typically execute tasks at the same slow speed as human demonstrations, limiting task throughput.
- Increasing execution speed fundamentally alters robot dynamics, leading to out-of-distribution states and compounding errors for policies trained on slower data.
- Real-world system latencies and the need for precision during critical task phases complicate continuous, high-speed, and accurate robot control.

## Method

- **SAIL** incorporates ==Error-Adaptive Guidance (EAG)== to ensure consistent action prediction by adaptively adjusting guidance based on end-effector tracking error.
- Policies are trained to predict controller-invariant 'reached poses' and executed by a high-fidelity tracking controller to mitigate distribution shifts caused by varying dynamics.
- Adaptive speed modulation dynamically adjusts the execution speed based on motion complexity and gripper events, allowing for slowing down in critical phases and speeding up during simpler motions.
- Action scheduling addresses system latencies by executing actions from previous policy inferences during delays, ensuring continuous real-time control at high speeds.

## Results

- SAIL achieved up to a **4x** speedup over demonstration speed in simulation and up to **3.2x** in real-world scenarios across diverse manipulation tasks, maintaining high success rates.
- Error-Adaptive Guidance (EAG) significantly enhanced motion consistency and improved task success rates during accelerated execution.
- Using controller-invariant action targets and a high-fidelity tracking controller reduced controller-induced distribution shifts, improving success rates by up to **55%** in ablation studies.
- Adaptive speed modulation proved vital for precision tasks, preventing up to a **31%** drop in success rates compared to uniform speedup, and SAIL consistently improved throughput-with-regret on 6 out of 7 real-world tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving faster-than-demonstration execution requires a full-stack approach that integrates policy-level adaptations with system-level engineering solutions.
> - Decoupling policy learning from the dynamics of the demonstration controller, by training on reached poses, is crucial for robust performance at accelerated speeds.
> - Dynamically adjusting execution speed based on task demands and motion complexity allows for balancing high throughput with precision in complex manipulation tasks.
> - Mitigating the impact of tracking errors and system latencies through adaptive guidance and intelligent action scheduling is essential for stable and continuous high-speed robot operation.
