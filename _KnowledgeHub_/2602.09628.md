# TeleGate: Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation via Gated Expert Selection with Motion Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> The TeleGate framework enables high-precision, real-time whole-body teleoperation of humanoid robots for diverse and dynamic motions. It integrates a gated expert selection mechanism with a VAE-based motion prediction prior, achieving a 97.3% success rate and 17.22mm mean per-joint position error in simulation, and demonstrating successful deployment on a Unitree G1 robot with 97.2% success using 2.5 hours of motion data.

## Problem

- Real-time teleoperation systems lack future motion information, hindering smooth execution of sudden action switches or complex dynamic motions requiring anticipation.
- Unified control policies for humanoid robots experience performance degradation on diverse motions due to catastrophic forgetting or lossy information compression during knowledge distillation.
- Training generalist policies for diverse human motions typically requires extremely large datasets (e.g., hundreds of hours), leading to high computational costs and limited data efficiency.

## Method

- A gated expert selection mechanism dynamically chooses the most appropriate specialized policy from a set of experts, preserving their full capabilities across different motion categories.
- A VAE-based motion prediction prior infers future motion intent from historical observations, providing anticipatory control to compensate for the absence of explicit future reference trajectories.
- Expert policies and the VAE module are jointly trained using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, utilizing a failure-rate based curriculum and domain randomization for robustness.

## Results

- TeleGate achieved an overall **97.3%** success rate and 17.22mm mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe) in simulation, outperforming baselines like TWIST, Any2track, and GMT in overall tracking accuracy and success rate.
- The gated expert selection alone (without VAE) achieved a **96.7%** success rate and 18.03mm E_mpjpe, matching or slightly surpassing oracle expert selection; adding the VAE prior further reduced E_mpjpe by **13%** for jumping and **8%** for fall recovery, raising success rate to **97.3%**.
- Real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot achieved a **97.2%** success rate and 18.57mm E_mpjpe for diverse dynamic motions, demonstrating effective sim-to-real transfer and generalization with a comparatively small **2.5**-hour motion dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining distinct, specialized expert policies with dynamic selection effectively prevents performance degradation inherent in consolidating diverse skills into a single, generalist policy.
> - Incorporating anticipatory control through a motion prediction prior significantly improves tracking accuracy and stability, particularly for dynamic and complex actions requiring foresight like jumping and fall recovery.
> - Jointly training expert policies with a VAE-based motion prediction prior ensures the latent space representation is optimized for effective control decisions, enhancing the utility of anticipatory information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Real-time whole-body teleoperation of humanoid robots is a research area focused on enabling robots to execute complex tasks in dynamic, unstructured environments by mapping human motion control to the robot. This capability is relevant for applications such as disaster rescue and industrial inspection, while also generating valuable motion data for embodied intelligence development.

Existing approaches to whole-body control for humanoid robots primarily utilize Reinforcement Learning (RL). Early works such as DeepMimic and AMP demonstrated high-quality tracking of reference motions in simulation for single tasks. However, extending these methods to encompass multiple, diverse human motion categories presents challenges, notably "catastrophic forgetting," where a single policy struggles to learn skills with significantly different state-action distributions (e.g., walking versus jumping). Attempts to mitigate this often involve scaling training data, with methods like SONIC requiring extensive datasets (e.g., 700 hours), leading to high computational costs and limited generalizability.

Another category of approaches involves a two-stage pipeline: training multiple domain-specific expert policies and then integrating them into a unified controller, often through knowledge distillation or behavioral cloning (e.g., Any2Track, BumbleBee). The paper notes that distillation is inherently a lossy compression process, which can lead to performance degradation in the generalized policy, particularly for highly dynamic movements, as it attempts to reconcile conflicting decision boundaries from multiple experts. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, as seen in GMT, have also been explored for joint training, but their effectiveness has not been consistently validated across diverse motion types.

In the context of teleoperation systems, various input modalities exist, including VR keypoints, exoskeletons, motion capture suits, and visual pose estimation, each with trade-offs in accuracy, cost, and comfort. Many early teleoperation systems primarily focused on upper-body control, with limited or pre-programmed lower-body movements, restricting the robot's operational workspace and dynamic capabilities. More advanced "leg joint tracking" methods, such as HumanPlus, OmniH2O, ExBody, and TWIST, aim to directly control whole-body joint commands for arbitrary motions. However, these often face limitations in supporting highly dynamic actions, require large datasets, or suffer from insufficient accuracy (e.g., TWIST's real-world deployment struggles with dynamic motions despite 42 hours of training data; SONIC scales data further but at extreme cost).

TeleGate positions itself within this landscape by addressing the performance degradation issues of distillation-based methods and the high data requirements of large-scale unified policy training. It aims to achieve high-precision, real-time whole-body teleoperation for diverse and dynamic motions with a comparatively smaller dataset by employing a gated expert selection mechanism and an anticipatory motion prior, thereby providing a more efficient and scalable solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified whole-body teleoperation framework for humanoid robots that enables high-precision tracking of diverse and dynamic human motions in real-time. This objective is motivated by two significant challenges inherent in existing teleoperation systems:

1. **Absence of Future Motion Information in Real-time:** In real-time teleoperation scenarios, the control policy cannot access future segments of the human operator's reference trajectory. This lack of anticipatory information makes it difficult for the robot to prepare for and smoothly execute sudden action switches or complex dynamic motions that require forethought, such as jumping, landing, or standing up from a prone position. Existing methods typically rely on current or historical data, leading to reactive control that can struggle with motions requiring prediction.

2. **Performance Degradation of Unified Policies on Diverse Motions:** Human motions exhibit significant diversity in their dynamic characteristics, ranging from subtle gestures to highly agile and dynamic movements like running, jumping, and fall recovery. Developing a single, unified controller that can robustly and accurately track this wide spectrum of motions simultaneously has proven challenging.
 - **Catastrophic Forgetting:** When a single reinforcement learning policy is trained on multiple motion types, the distinct state-action distributions of different motions can lead to "catastrophic forgetting," where optimizing for one skill degrades performance on others.
 - **Knowledge Distillation Limitations:** Approaches that train multiple specialized "expert" policies and then distill their knowledge into a single general policy (e.g., behavioral cloning) often suffer from performance degradation. This is because the distillation process is a form of lossy compression; the single network struggles to perfectly fit the complex and potentially conflicting decision boundaries of multiple experts, leading to reduced capability, especially for highly dynamic motions.
 - **Data Inefficiency:** Many existing methods attempting to generalize across motions require extremely large datasets (e.g., hundreds of hours of motion capture data), making training computationally intensive and resource-demanding.

To address these motivations, TeleGate proposes a framework designed to:
- Preserve the full capabilities of specialized expert policies rather than compressing them into a single, potentially degraded policy.
- Enable dynamic, real-time selection of the most appropriate expert for the current motion context.
- Compensate for the absence of future motion data by inferring motion intent from historical observations, thereby providing anticipatory control.
- Achieve high-precision tracking with a comparatively smaller and more targeted motion capture dataset, enhancing data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TeleGate framework operates in three distinct stages: data collection and preprocessing, expert policy training with a motion prediction prior, and gating network training for dynamic expert selection.

#### 3.1. A. Whole-Body Motion Data Collection and Preprocessing

The initial stage involves gathering human motion data.
- **Data Source:** Inertial motion capture equipment is used to collect whole-body motion data from human operators. This choice prioritizes portability and insensitivity to environmental factors like lighting, while offering refined joint information compared to VR devices.
- **Retargeting:** The collected human motion data is converted in real-time to the robot's joint space using online retargeting algorithms, generating reference trajectories for the robot.
- **Data Format:** Reference trajectories include the robot's global base pose (position and orientation) and joint angles. During training, link position and velocity information are derived via forward kinematics and numerical differentiation.
- **Categorization:** The collected data (approximately 2.5 hours, covering walking, running, dancing, martial arts, fall recovery, and jumping) is partitioned into distinct categories based on motion dynamic characteristics. This categorization serves as clear task boundaries for subsequent expert policy training. The use of a self-collected dataset ensures consistency between training and deployment, mitigating domain shift issues prevalent with public datasets.

#### 3.2. B. Expert Policy Training with Motion Prediction Prior

This stage focuses on training specialized policies for different motion types, augmented with a module for anticipating future movements.
- **MDP Formulation:** The problem is modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) (S, A, P, r, γ).
 - **State Space (s_t):** Comprises proprioceptive state (o_t), historical reference trajectory information (m_t), and a motion prediction prior (z_t).
 - `o_t` includes gravity projection, linear/angular velocities, joint positions/velocities, and previous target motor positions.
 - `m_t` consists of a sparse window of recent reference trajectories (t-20, t-10, t-5, t-1, t) to capture both instantaneous dynamics and long-term trends.
 - **Action Space (a_t):** Defined as residual corrections to all robot joint angles. Target joint angles for a low-level PD controller are computed as `q_target_t = ˆq_t + a_t * α`, where `ˆq_t` is the reference joint angle and `α` is a scaling coefficient. Joint torques are then applied using a PD control law. The policy outputs actions at 50Hz.
 - **Reward Function (r_t):** Composed of tracking rewards (for upper/lower body position, foot position, rigid body rotation/velocity, joint position/velocity, root height/velocity, roll-pitch angle), a survival reward, and regularization penalties (for action rate, torque, joint limits, self-collision, foot slip, foot contact angular velocity, and joint smoothness).
- **Reinforcement Learning:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed for RL training within the MuJoCo physics simulator, utilizing an asymmetric actor-critic architecture. The actor receives deployable observations, while the critic accesses privileged simulation information (e.g., ground-truth states, future trajectories) for more accurate value estimation.
- **Motion Prediction Prior (VAE-based):**
 - **Purpose:** To compensate for the absence of future reference trajectories in real-time.
 - **Design:** A small Transformer-based Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is used.
 - **Encoder (E_ϕ):** Takes the historical reference trajectory window (M_t^-) as input and outputs the mean (μ_t) and standard deviation (σ_t) of a low-dimensional latent variable (z_t).
 - **Sampling:** `z_t` is sampled from N(μ_t, σ_t^2) using the reparameterization trick.
 - **Decoder (D_ψ):** Predicts the future reference trajectory window (M_t^+) conditioned on `z_t`.
 - **Rationale:** VAE is chosen because KL divergence regularization encourages `z_t` to capture predictive information, and the asymmetric design (encoder on historical, decoder on future) makes `z_t` inherently represent future motion intent.
 - **Integration:** The latent variable `z_t` is concatenated to the policy network's observation input, enabling anticipatory control.
- **Joint Training Loss:** The VAE and expert policy are trained jointly, not sequentially. This ensures the VAE's latent space representation is optimized to be useful for the policy's control decisions. The total loss function is `L = L_PPO + λ_recon L_recon + λ_KL L_KL`, incorporating the PPO policy gradient loss, a mean squared error for trajectory reconstruction (L_recon), and a KL divergence regularization term (L_KL) for the latent space.
- **Failure-Rate Based Curriculum Sampling:** To address varying difficulty across motion clips, an adaptive sampling strategy is used. Each motion clip's sampling weight is positively correlated with its duration and dynamically tracked failure rate (updated via exponential moving average). This directs training resources towards more challenging samples.
- **Domain Randomization:** To improve sim-to-real transferability and robustness, external push disturbances, state observation noise, PD gain coefficients, contact friction, joint friction loss, joint armature, torso COM position, torso mass, and initial joint positions are randomized during training (detailed in Appendix C).

#### 3.3. C. Expert Policy Selection via Gating Network

This stage introduces a mechanism for dynamically selecting the most appropriate expert policy during runtime.
- **Expert Partitioning:** The six collected motion categories are grouped into four based on dynamic similarity: (1) walking and running, (2) dancing and martial arts, (3) fall and recovery, and (4) jumping. Each expert policy `π_i` is trained exclusively on its corresponding motion subset.
- **Gating Network Design:** A lightweight gating network `G_θ` is introduced. It receives the current proprioceptive state `o_t` and reference trajectory information `m_t` as input, outputting selection scores for each of the K (K=4) expert policies.
- **Dynamic Selection:** During inference, the expert `i*` with the highest score from the gating network is dynamically selected to execute the action: `a_t = π_i*(o_t, m_t, z_i*t)`, where `i* = arg max_i G_θ(o_t, m_t)_i`. All expert policy parameters are frozen during this stage.
- **Gating Network Training:** Only the parameters of the gating network are optimized. Training settings (simulation environment, reward, domain randomization, data sampling) mirror the expert training phase, with the loss function consisting solely of PPO's policy gradient loss.
- **Expert Switching Mechanism:** The system is designed to maintain control continuity during expert switching. This is achieved by including the previous timestep's target motor position in each expert policy's input, allowing the newly activated expert to account for the system's instantaneous dynamics. Additionally, action regularization rewards in both training stages encourage smooth action outputs.

#### 3.4. D. Implementation of Physical Whole-Body Teleoperation

- **Deployment Workflow:** On the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, the operator wears inertial motion capture equipment. The motion data is wirelessly transmitted, converted to a robot reference trajectory via skeleton retargeting, and then fed into the TeleGate framework for policy inference. The policy's output actions are low-pass filtered before being sent to the robot's low-level PD controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical evaluations in simulation and real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to address four specific experimental questions.

#### 4.1. E1: Performance Comparison with Existing Methods

TeleGate was compared against TWIST, Any2track, and GMT using a 2.5-hour self-collected motion capture dataset. Evaluation metrics included Success Rate (SR), pelvis linear velocity error (E_plve), mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe), and mean per-joint angle error (E_mpjae).
- **Overall Performance:** TeleGate achieved the highest overall performance with a 97.3% success rate, 8.37 E_plve, 17.22mm E_mpjpe, and 0.085rad E_mpjae. This represented the best overall performance in both tracking accuracy and success rate.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - TWIST showed the lowest E_plve (3.88) but a low success rate (68.9%), indicating early failures on difficult motions.
 - Any2track achieved a 91.2% success rate, but its tracking accuracy was lower than TeleGate due to information compression during distillation.
 - GMT demonstrated worse tracking accuracy (29.14mm E_mpjpe, 0.181rad E_mpjae) compared to expert distillation methods.
- **Robustness to Error Thresholds:** TeleGate consistently exhibited the highest success rates across a range of error thresholds (0.2m to 0.8m), indicating its robustness.
- **Expert Switching Smoothness:** Analysis of continuous motion sequences indicated that joint commands remained continuous during expert switching, with an average target joint position change of 3.49 degrees/frame compared to 1.38 degrees/frame without switching. This suggests that the switching mechanism, aided by the policy's input of the previous target motor position, manages transitions without significant control instability.

#### 4.2. E2: Advantages of Gated Expert Selection

To evaluate the gated expert selection mechanism, various expert integration approaches were compared without the VAE module. Expert (Oracle) provided an upper bound, using ground-truth labels for expert selection.
- **Single Policy:** A single generalist policy achieved a 94.7% success rate but the worst tracking accuracy (24.25mm E_mpjpe), indicating its difficulty in mastering diverse motion dynamics.
- **DAgger Distillation:** Resulted in a significantly lower success rate (91.2%) compared to the Oracle (96.3%), supporting the hypothesis that distillation leads to performance degradation through lossy compression.
- **Top-2 Gating:** Employing weighted fusion of the top two experts led to a lower success rate (93.7%), suggesting that incorporating suboptimal expert outputs can interfere with overall performance.
- **Random Partition:** Randomly grouping motion data for expert training resulted in worse tracking accuracy (19.89mm E_mpjpe), emphasizing the importance of partitioning experts based on dynamic similarity.
- **Top-1 Gating (TeleGate without VAE):** Achieved a 96.7% success rate and 18.03mm E_mpjpe, which was comparable to or slightly surpassed the Expert (Oracle) performance (96.3% SR, 18.03mm E_mpjpe). This was attributed to the gating network's ability to select the optimal expert at a frame-by-frame level, potentially outperforming segment-level ground-truth labels.

#### 4.3. E3: Impact of Motion Prediction Prior

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the effectiveness of the VAE-based motion prediction prior.
- **Gains for Anticipatory Motions:** The VAE module provided the most substantial performance improvements for motions requiring anticipation. Jumping E_mpjpe was reduced by 13% (from 22.27mm to 19.40mm), and Fall Recovery E_mpjpe was reduced by 8% (from 28.23mm to 25.94mm). Success rates for these categories also improved by 1.5–2.5 percentage points.
- **Marginal Gains for Other Motions:** For periodic and quasi-static motions (e.g., walking, running, dancing, martial arts), where future intent is more predictable from the current state, the VAE provided only marginal changes.
- **Overall Impact:** The inclusion of the VAE-based motion prediction prior contributed to an overall increase in success rate from 96.7% to 97.3% and a decrease in E_mpjpe from 18.03mm to 17.22mm.
- **VAE Trajectory Prediction:** Visualizations confirmed that the VAE accurately captures motion trends from historical trajectories and predicts reasonable future poses for dynamic actions (e.g., standing up, lying down, running, walking), effectively providing anticipatory information to the policy.

#### 4.4. E4: Real Robot Teleoperation

TeleGate was deployed on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot for real-world validation.
- **Demonstrated Skills:** The system successfully achieved real-time teleoperation for a variety of highly dynamic motions, including grasping and placing objects, standing long jumps, prone position stand-ups, ball kicking, sitting, walking, running, lateral slides, and single-leg hops.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Quantitative evaluation on 36 real-robot motion clips showed an overall success rate of 97.2% and an E_mpjpe of 18.57mm, indicating a small sim-to-real gap compared to simulation results (100% SR, 17.14mm E_mpjpe). The slight decrease in success rate for Fall Recovery (83.3%) was attributed to sim-to-real discrepancies in contact dynamics during whole-body landings.
- **Generalization:** The real-world experiments validated the effectiveness of TeleGate in sim-to-real transfer and its generalization capability to diverse, out-of-distribution motions, ranging from fine manipulation to highly dynamic movements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TeleGate presents a framework for humanoid robot teleoperation that addresses limitations in existing methods by combining a gated expert selection mechanism with a VAE-based motion prediction prior.

The **gated expert selection** mechanism is significant as it provides a strategy to maintain the full capabilities of domain-specific expert policies. By avoiding the inherent performance degradation associated with knowledge distillation, the framework allows for high-precision tracking across a diverse range of motion patterns. This approach potentially offers a more scalable and robust solution for handling the heterogeneity of human movements without compromising accuracy on dynamic tasks.

The **VAE-based motion prediction prior** module is a contribution towards enabling anticipatory control in real-time teleoperation. By extracting implicit future motion intent from historical observations, it compensates for the information bottleneck of lacking explicit future reference trajectories. This capability is particularly impactful for dynamic actions that require foresight, such as jumping, landing, and fall recovery, where reactive control alone is insufficient for stable and accurate execution.

The successful **sim-to-real transfer and real-world deployment** on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, using only 2.5 hours of self-collected motion capture data, demonstrates the practical feasibility and data efficiency of the TeleGate framework. This contrasts with other state-of-the-art methods that often require substantially larger datasets or struggle with highly dynamic motions in real-world scenarios.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Enhanced Humanoid Robot Capabilities:** Enabling humanoid robots to perform complex and agile tasks in unstructured environments with improved precision and robustness, which could accelerate their adoption in fields such as disaster response, industrial assistance, and exploration.
- **Improved Data Efficiency:** The method's ability to achieve high performance with a relatively small, focused dataset offers a more accessible and resource-efficient training paradigm for teleoperation systems.
- **Foundation for Autonomous Control:** The framework's ability to collect high-quality, diverse motion data through teleoperation lays a groundwork for future research into transferring these teleoperated skills to train autonomous policies, bridging the gap between human guidance and autonomous robot operation.

Future work outlined includes investigating methods to leverage teleoperation-collected data for training autonomous policies, indicating a direction towards integrating human-guided learning with autonomous robotic intelligence.
