# TeleGate: Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation via Gated Expert Selection with Motion Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> The TeleGate framework enables high-precision, real-time whole-body teleoperation of humanoid robots for diverse and dynamic motions. It integrates a gated expert selection mechanism with a VAE-based motion prediction prior, achieving a 97.3% success rate and 17.22mm mean per-joint position error in simulation, and demonstrating successful deployment on a Unitree G1 robot with 97.2% success using 2.5 hours of motion data.

## Problem

- Real-time teleoperation systems lack future motion information, hindering smooth execution of sudden action switches or complex dynamic motions requiring anticipation.
- Unified control policies for humanoid robots experience performance degradation on diverse motions due to catastrophic forgetting or lossy information compression during knowledge distillation.
- Training generalist policies for diverse human motions typically requires extremely large datasets (e.g., hundreds of hours), leading to high computational costs and limited data efficiency.

## Method

- A gated expert selection mechanism dynamically chooses the most appropriate specialized policy from a set of experts, preserving their full capabilities across different motion categories.
- A VAE-based motion prediction prior infers future motion intent from historical observations, providing anticipatory control to compensate for the absence of explicit future reference trajectories.
- Expert policies and the VAE module are jointly trained using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, utilizing a failure-rate based curriculum and domain randomization for robustness.

## Results

- TeleGate achieved an overall **97.3%** success rate and 17.22mm mean per-joint position error (E_mpjpe) in simulation, outperforming baselines like TWIST, Any2track, and GMT in overall tracking accuracy and success rate.
- The gated expert selection alone (without VAE) achieved a **96.7%** success rate and 18.03mm E_mpjpe, demonstrating its effectiveness in matching or slightly surpassing oracle expert selection by choosing optimal experts frame-by-frame.
- The VAE-based motion prediction prior reduced E_mpjpe by **13%** for jumping and **8%** for fall recovery, improving overall success rate from **96.7%** to **97.3%**, validating its benefit for anticipatory motions.
- Real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot achieved a **97.2%** success rate and 18.57mm E_mpjpe for diverse dynamic motions, demonstrating effective sim-to-real transfer and generalization with a comparatively small **2.5**-hour motion dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining distinct, specialized expert policies with dynamic selection effectively prevents performance degradation inherent in consolidating diverse skills into a single, generalist policy.
> - Incorporating anticipatory control through a motion prediction prior significantly improves tracking accuracy and stability, particularly for dynamic and complex actions requiring foresight like jumping and fall recovery.
> - Jointly training expert policies with a VAE-based motion prediction prior ensures the latent space representation is optimized for effective control decisions, enhancing the utility of anticipatory information.
