# SP-VLA: A Joint Model Scheduling and Token Pruning Approach for VLA Model Acceleration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University developed SP-VLA, a unified framework to accelerate Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by jointly optimizing model scheduling and token pruning based on temporal and spatial redundancies. The approach achieves up to 2.4x speedup and an average performance gain of 6% in simulation environments, enabling VLA models for real-time applications.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models incur immense computational costs, with billions of parameters, leading to slow inference times.
- The slow inference rates of VLA models make them impractical for real-time, latency-sensitive applications in robotics and autonomous systems.
- Existing VLM acceleration techniques often overlook VLA-specific temporal redundancy in sequential action generation and spatial redundancy in visual inputs.

## Method

- An action type-aware ==model scheduling== mechanism dynamically switches between a full VLA model for 'deliberative' actions and a lightweight Ridge Regression generator for 'intuitive' actions.
- A spatio-semantic dual-aware token pruning method reduces spatial redundancy by selecting tokens based on cumulative attention (semantic importance) and Canny edge features (spatial contours), while preserving token order.
- The framework jointly optimizes both scheduling and pruning, adapting computational load to the varying demands of different task phases and visual complexities.

## Results

- SP-VLA achieved **1.35x** lossless acceleration and up to **1.5x** speedup with less than a **3%** accuracy drop on LIBERO, and **2.4x** speedup with an average **6%** performance gain on SimplerEnv.
- Ablation studies showed model scheduling was the most effective for acceleration, yielding a **1.27x** speedup with only a **1%** accuracy drop, demonstrating considerable temporal redundancy.
- Removing Canny edge information from the token pruning process led to a dramatic **50%** performance degradation, highlighting the indispensable role of spatial contour cues for VLA models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models exhibit distinct 'deliberative' and 'intuitive' action types, allowing for dynamic model scheduling based on task complexity, inspired by human motor control.
> - VLA models are highly sensitive to object relative positions and contour information, making the preservation of Canny edge features during token pruning critical for maintaining performance.
> - Temporal redundancy, addressed by the action type-aware model scheduling, is the most effective component for VLA acceleration, contributing significantly to speedup with minimal accuracy loss.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a rapidly evolving frontier in artificial intelligence, bridging visual perception, language understanding, and actionable outputs for embodied agents, particularly in robotic control. This field has witnessed significant advancements with models like Google’s RT-X series (Brohan et al., 2023; Belkhale et al., 2024), OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024), and diffusion-based models like π0 (Black et al., 2024), demonstrating remarkable capabilities in complex tasks. These models integrate large language models (LLMs) and vision transformers to enable robots to understand high-level commands, perceive their environment, and execute multi-step actions.

However, the defining challenge for VLA models, and the central focus of this paper, is their immense computational cost. Models like RT-X can exceed 55 billion parameters, and even "lightweight" counterparts like OpenVLA can reach over 7 billion. This scale translates into slow inference times, making them largely unsuitable for real-time applications such as industrial control, autonomous navigation, or medical robotics, where immediate and continuous decision-making is paramount.

The existing research landscape for VLA acceleration primarily borrows techniques from Vision-Language Model (VLM) compression. These methods include:
- **Structural Optimization:** Pruning (Li et al., 2024c), quantization (Tang et al., 2024; Park et al., 2024), and caching (Li et al., 2024d; Xu et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2024) reduce single-step computational redundancy by compressing the model architecture or reusing computation. Examples include DeeR-VLA (Yue et al., 2024) with early-exit mechanisms and QAIL (Park et al., 2024) using quantization.
- **Alternative Action Generation:** Methods like Fast (Pertsch et al., 2025) transform actions into the frequency domain for compression, while PD-VLA (Song et al., 2025) and VLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025) introduce parallel decoding for action generation.

The critical gap addressed by SP-VLA is that these existing methods largely overlook the unique characteristics of VLA models operating in sequential decision-making environments. Specifically, they fail to systematically tackle:
1. **Temporal Redundancy:** The sequential, step-by-step nature of action generation often involves repetitive or less critical actions where the full computational power of a large VLA model might not be necessary.
2. **Spatial Redundancy:** Visual inputs, while rich, often contain significant spatial redundancy, with much of the information being less critical for a given action step.

SP-VLA distinguishes itself by being the "first work to accelerate VLA models via reducing temporal and spatial redundancy." By explicitly considering VLA models as operating over both a temporal sequence of actions and a spatial visual input, the authors introduce a unified framework that is more tailored to the intricacies of embodied AI tasks. This approach shifts the focus beyond mere architectural compression to a deeper understanding of information flow and processing needs within the VLA paradigm, promising more effective and application-relevant acceleration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly accelerate Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** to enable their deployment in real-time, high-stakes scenarios such as robotic manipulation, autonomous navigation, industrial control, and medical robotics.

The motivation for this objective stems directly from the inherent limitations of current VLA models:
- **High Computational Cost:** VLA models are inherently large-scale, with parameter counts often in the billions (e.g., RT-X series at 55B+, OpenVLA at 7B+). This leads to substantial computational overhead and slow inference times.
- **Unsuitability for Real-Time Applications:** The slow inference rates make these powerful models impractical for tasks demanding immediate and continuous decision-making, where latency is a critical factor.
- **Limitations of Existing Acceleration Methods:** While VLM acceleration techniques (pruning, quantization, caching) have been applied, they primarily focus on single-step computation reduction. These methods fall short because they neglect two fundamental characteristics unique to VLA models in embodied tasks:
 1. **Temporal Dependencies:** VLA models operate in sequential decision-making environments, generating actions step-by-step. This sequential nature introduces temporal redundancy, where not every action requires the same level of complex reasoning.
 2. **Spatial Redundancy in Visual Input:** Camera inputs, though crucial, often contain superfluous visual information that is not equally salient or necessary for every decision, leading to spatial redundancy.

To address these shortcomings, the paper sets two specific, interlinked objectives:
- **Reduce Temporal Redundancy:** Develop a mechanism to efficiently generate action sequences by leveraging historical information and adapting the computational load based on the action's complexity.
- **Mitigate Spatial Redundancy:** Create a method to selectively retain the most informative visual content, focusing on task-relevant elements while discarding less critical information.

A key motivation for the temporal redundancy approach is drawn from **human motor behavior**, specifically the observation that humans employ "deliberate thinking" only for critical, complex actions (like grasping or turning), while executing simpler, transitional movements "intuitively." The authors hypothesize and then observe that VLA models exhibit a similar behavioral pattern, categorizing actions into "deliberative" and "intuitive." This human-inspired insight forms the bedrock of their model scheduling strategy.

For spatial redundancy, the motivation comes from empirical observations that VLA models are **highly sensitive to the relative positions of objects and object contour information**. Simply pruning tokens based on semantic importance or reordering them can severely degrade performance, indicating that the spatial context and boundary information are crucial for the robot's understanding of its environment.

By jointly tackling both temporal and spatial redundancies, the authors aim to guide the VLA model to focus its computational resources on critical actions and salient visual information, thereby achieving significant acceleration without compromising, and in some cases even improving, task accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (SP-VLA)

SP-VLA (Scheduling Models and Pruning Tokens for VLA acceleration) is a unified framework designed to jointly optimize VLA model inference by addressing both temporal and spatial redundancies. The framework operates in two main stages: action type-aware model scheduling and spatio-semantic dual-aware token pruning.

#### 3.1. Action Type-Aware Model Scheduling (Addressing Temporal Redundancy)

Inspired by human motor control, which distinguishes between deliberative and intuitive actions, SP-VLA dynamically switches between a full-scale VLA model and a lightweight action generator.

- **Action Type Indicator:** The core idea is to classify actions based on their nature. "Deliberative" actions (e.g., precise grasping, complex turns) require the full VLA model for accuracy, while "intuitive" actions (e.g., fast, linear movements between key points) can be handled by a simpler, lightweight generator.
 - The paper observes that low-speed movements often correspond to deliberative actions requiring precision, while high-speed movements are typically intuitive.
 - An action $a_t = \{a_x, a_y, a_z\}$ (translational velocity components) is classified as intuitive if all its components exceed a predefined minimum velocity threshold ($v_{\min}$).
 - **Model Scheduler Trigger Conditions:** A lightweight model (LWM) is triggered if:
 1. The previous action $a_{t-1}$'s velocity components fall within an intuitive range $[v_{\min}, v_{\max}]$.
 2. The ratio of VLA-generated actions in a historical action buffer ($N_G/N_A$) exceeds a threshold $\tau$. This ensures the lightweight generator is not overused, and the VLA model periodically provides directional guidance.
 - This approach enables frequency-adaptive inference, where high-frequency, small-step actions can be generated faster.

- **Lightweight Action Generator:** For intuitive actions, SP-VLA employs a simple yet effective generator based on Ridge Regression and an action buffer.
 - **Assumption:** Short segments of intuitive actions can be approximated as linear movements.
 - **Mechanism:** It uses the most recent $n$ actions stored in an action buffer $S_A = \{a_{t-n}, \ldots, a_{t-1}\}$ to train a Ridge Regression model. This model learns the relationship between time and velocity.
 - **Prediction:** The trained model then predicts the current action $a_t$ using the current time step.
 - **Gripper State:** For binary end-effector states (e.g., gripper open/closed), the value from the previous time step is reused, with the VLA model handling actual state transitions.
 - This lightweight generator provides fast and reliable action approximations, significantly reducing the need to invoke the computationally intensive VLA model for every step.

#### 3.2. Spatio-Semantic Dual-Aware Token Pruning (Addressing Spatial Redundancy)

When the full VLA model is invoked (for deliberative actions), SP-VLA further reduces computational overhead by dynamically pruning less important input tokens before they enter the LLM backbone. This is crucial because the LLM typically accounts for the majority of VLA model computation.

- **Motivation:** VLA models are highly sensitive to both the relative positions of objects and their contours. Random pruning or purely semantic-based pruning (which might reorder tokens or remove background edges) can severely degrade performance.
- **Semantic-aware Token Importance:**
 - The vision encoder transforms the input image $X$ into a sequence of tokens.
 - Cumulative attention scores (AccuAttn) from the final layer of the vision encoder are used to gauge the semantic importance of each token.
 - Tokens with AccuAttn scores exceeding a threshold $t_k^s$ are identified as semantically relevant ($T_{se}$).
- **Spatial-aware Token Importance:**
 - The Canny edge detector is applied to the input image $X$ to extract an edge-only image $X_s$, preserving object contour information.
 - Tokens corresponding to these edge features are extracted to form a set of spatially informative tokens ($T_{sp}$). This explicitly captures the crucial contour information.
- **Combined Token Selection:** The final set of selected tokens ($T_{select}$) is obtained by taking the **order-preserving union** of $T_{se}$ and $T_{sp}$. Preserving the original token order is emphasized as critical for VLA's spatial understanding.
- **Speed-Aware Pruning Rate:** The pruning ratio is dynamically adjusted based on the current motion speed (velocity $v$).
 - No pruning occurs at very low speeds ($v < v_{p_{\min}}$) to ensure precise manipulation.
 - The pruning ratio increases linearly with speed for $v \ge v_{p_{\min}}$, meaning more tokens are pruned during faster, more intuitive movements. This aligns with the model scheduling strategy.

#### 3.3. Joint Optimization

The two mechanisms work synergistically: the model scheduler decides *when* to use the VLA model, and the token pruning method optimizes *what* information the VLA model processes when it is active. This joint approach allows SP-VLA to adapt to the varying demands of different task phases and visual complexities, leading to efficient and accurate inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SP-VLA demonstrates significant advancements in accelerating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, as evidenced by extensive experiments on two challenging simulation environments: LIBERO (Liu et al., 2023) and SimplerEnv (Li et al., 2024b), using OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) and CogACT (Li et al., 2024a) as VLA backbones.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Speedup

- **LIBERO Environment:** SP-VLA achieves a **1.35× lossless acceleration** (i.e., no drop in success rate) and up to **1.5× faster inference with less than a 3% accuracy drop**. Inference frequency improves by **1.4×**, and latency is similarly reduced.
- **SimplerEnv Environment:** SP-VLA delivers an even more substantial **2.4× speedup**, often accompanied by an **average performance gain of up to 6%**. Inference frequency improves by **2.2×**, and latency is reduced accordingly. Notably, on the Visual Aggregation Drawer task, SP-VLA boosts performance by approximately 27% while achieving a 1.8× speedup, showcasing error-correction capabilities.
- These results demonstrate that SP-VLA effectively exploits both temporal and spatial redundancies in VLA models, leading to substantial real-world performance benefits in terms of speed and efficiency.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study: Contribution of Individual Modules

A detailed ablation study highlights the effectiveness of each component:

- **Model Scheduling (Temporal Redundancy):** This component is identified as the **most effective for acceleration**, achieving a **1.27× speedup with only a 1% drop in accuracy**. This strongly indicates that VLA models possess considerable temporal redundancy, meaning much of their operation involves intuitive, repetitive actions that do not require the full model's reasoning capabilities.
- **Token Pruning (Spatial Redundancy):** While contributing to acceleration, token pruning alone yields only **moderate speedup** and results in a **more substantial accuracy drop**. This suggests that VLA models are highly sensitive to visual token reduction, implying limited inherent redundancy in raw visual tokens that can be aggressively compressed without impacting performance.
- **Importance of Canny Edge Information:** Critically, eliminating the Canny edge information from the token pruning process leads to a **dramatic 50% degradation in performance, effectively collapsing the model's functionality**. This finding underscores the indispensable role of relative token positions and object contour information for the VLA model's spatial perception and understanding. Without these spatial cues, the model struggles to accurately interact with its environment.
- **Synergistic Effect:** The joint application of model scheduling and spatio-semantic dual-aware token pruning yields the best overall acceleration, often with no compromise in model accuracy, validating the unified framework approach.

#### 4.3. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Baselines

SP-VLA significantly outperforms existing VLM acceleration methods when adapted for VLA tasks. Most baseline methods, designed for VLMs, struggled with VLA-specific challenges:

- **Spatial Information Challenges:** Methods like FoPru and PruMerge, despite some spatial awareness, often fail because they don't adequately preserve relative positioning or critical object contours necessary for VLA tasks. VisionZip, which merges tokens, disrupts spatial information, leading to severe performance collapse.
- **Temporal Redundancy Neglect:** Methods like FastVLM and FastV, which apply fixed token pruning, fail to adapt to the varying complexity of VLA tasks, resulting in degraded performance. SP-VLA's adaptive pruning, maintaining a constant cumulative attention threshold and adjusting token retention based on speed, preserves performance better.
- **Orthogonality with VLA-specific Methods:** While VLA-Cache and EfficientVLA are VLA-specific, SP-VLA is largely orthogonal, as it doesn't modify the model architecture itself but rather optimizes its invocation and input. This suggests potential for combining SP-VLA with such methods for even greater acceleration.

#### 4.4. Sensitivity Analysis

SP-VLA demonstrates robustness to changes in execution speed ($V_{\max}, V_{\min}$), with minor impact on accuracy or acceleration. However, parameters like buffer size ($n$) and the proportion of deliberative actions ($\tau$) have a stronger influence on accuracy, highlighting the need for careful tuning in complex tasks. This also reinforces the finding that the VLA model's directional guidance is crucial even for "intuitive" phases.

#### 4.5. Visualizations

Visualizations confirm that SP-VLA effectively identifies redundant image regions and adaptively prunes tokens, focusing on critical objects while preserving essential contour information. This visual evidence supports the efficacy of the spatio-semantic dual-aware token pruning in enhancing inference efficiency without losing task-relevant visual understanding.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that SP-VLA successfully addresses the unique temporal and spatial redundancies in VLA models, achieving substantial, often lossless, acceleration and improved real-time performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SP-VLA marks a significant step forward in making Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models practical for real-world applications. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Enabling Real-Time VLA Deployment:** The most immediate and profound impact is the ability to deploy large-scale VLA models in real-time scenarios. Current VLA models, despite their powerful control capabilities, are often too computationally expensive and slow for latency-sensitive applications like industrial robots, autonomous navigation systems, surgical robots, or even consumer robotics. SP-VLA's ability to achieve 1.5× to 2.4× acceleration (with sustained or improved accuracy) directly addresses this bottleneck, opening doors for VLA technology in areas previously inaccessible due to computational constraints.
- **Novel Perspective on VLA Acceleration:** This work is the first to explicitly identify and systematically address both temporal redundancy (in sequential action generation) and spatial redundancy (in visual input) as distinct challenges for VLA model acceleration. By shifting the focus from general model compression to VLA-specific optimization strategies, SP-VLA provides a novel and more effective paradigm for improving efficiency.
- **Human-Inspired Robotics:** The "action type-aware model scheduling" mechanism, inspired by human cognitive-motor patterns of deliberative versus intuitive actions, offers a compelling new direction for designing more efficient and human-like robotic control systems. This conceptual framework could inspire future research into hybrid AI architectures that dynamically allocate computational resources based on task complexity, mirroring biological intelligence.
- **Improved Accuracy and Robustness:** Beyond acceleration, SP-VLA often achieves a performance gain (up to 6% in SimplerEnv) or "lossless" acceleration in LIBERO. This suggests that judiciously pruning redundancy and focusing the model's attention on critical information can not only speed up inference but also enhance the model's understanding and decision-making, potentially correcting errors and leading to more robust policies.
- **Generalizability and Compatibility:** The modular design of SP-VLA, separating model scheduling and token pruning, suggests its compatibility with diverse VLA architectures. The methods are applied *during* inference without requiring extensive retraining or fundamental changes to the backbone VLA model, making them plug-and-play solutions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies its current exploration of intuitive action generation as preliminary, suggesting that further research into explicitly distinguishing and optimizing these action types could lead to even greater accuracy, speed, and energy efficiency. This opens up a significant avenue for future work in creating more sophisticated and biologically plausible VLA systems.
- **Economic and Environmental Benefits:** Faster and more efficient VLA models require less computational power per inference, which translates into lower operational costs (less energy consumption, fewer specialized hardware requirements) and reduced environmental impact, a crucial consideration for large-scale AI deployment.

In conclusion, SP-VLA's innovative joint approach to tackling temporal and spatial redundancies fundamentally advances the field of VLA model acceleration. It transforms powerful yet sluggish VLA models into agile, real-time decision-makers, thereby pushing the boundaries of what is possible in embodied AI and robotic control.
