# Efficient Deep Learning of Robust Policies from MPC using Imitation and Tube-Guided Data Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new Imitation Learning strategy, Sampling Augmentation (SA), efficiently trains robust deep neural network policies by distilling knowledge from Model Predictive Control (MPC) experts. SA enables zero-shot sim-to-real and lab-to-real transfer with 100% success rates on complex tasks using as few as one demonstration, significantly outperforming baselines in data and training efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing Imitation Learning (IL) methods for Model Predictive Control (MPC) often require a large number of demonstrations, leading to high data collection costs and prolonged training.
- Policies trained with current IL techniques exhibit limited robustness to unseen disturbances, model errors, and sim2real/lab2real gaps due to covariate shifts.
- Updating or adapting learned policies to changes in the MPC expert or new tasks is challenging due to the need for extensive retraining.

## Method

- Developed Sampling Augmentation (SA), an IL strategy that leverages Robust Tube **==MPC==** (RTMPC) to generate additional training samples within the expert's disturbance-invariant set (tube).
- For linear RTMPC, SA samples states within an approximated tube and efficiently computes corresponding actions using the ancillary controller's linear feedback law.
- For nonlinear RTMPC, SA utilizes parametric sensitivities of the NMPC ancillary controller to efficiently approximate and generate augmented actions, avoiding repeated expensive NMPC solves.

## Results

- SA methods achieved **100%** success rates in disturbed environments with as few as one demonstration (**3x** more sample-efficient) for both linear trajectory tracking and nonlinear acrobatic maneuvers.
- Policies trained with SA demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real and lab-to-real transfer on physical multirotors under unseen disturbances, such as wind, slung loads, and drag boards.
- SA significantly reduced wall-clock training time (e.g., **64.8%** reduction over DAgger+DR for trajectory tracking), and the learned policies achieved 180-280 times faster inference on onboard hardware compared to the MPC expert.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent robustness properties of RTMPC, particularly its disturbance-invariant sets (tubes), can be effectively exploited to guide data augmentation for learning robust policies.
> - Sampling within the RTMPC tube allows for efficient generation of diverse training data that covers states likely to be encountered under bounded uncertainties, significantly improving robustness and sample efficiency.
> - Parametric sensitivity analysis of the ancillary Nonlinear MPC (NMPC) in nonlinear RTMPC enables computationally efficient data augmentation without solving the full NMPC for each augmented sample.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a control methodology that enables high-performance capabilities for complex and agile robotic systems. However, its online computational demands frequently restrict its real-time deployment on platforms with limited processing power. A common strategy to address this limitation involves training computationally efficient deep neural network (DNN) policies through Imitation Learning (IL), where the MPC acts as an expert providing demonstrations.

Existing IL methods, such as Behavior Cloning (BC) and Dataset-Aggregation (DAgger), face several challenges:
1. **Data Inefficiency:** They often require a substantial volume of demonstrations from the MPC expert, leading to extensive data collection in simulations or prolonged real-world robot operation, which can be resource-intensive and impractical.
2. **Limited Robustness:** Policies trained via these methods frequently exhibit limited robustness when confronted with previously unseen disturbances, model errors, or discrepancies between the training and deployment environments (known as the sim2real or lab2real gap), stemming from covariate shifts.
3. **Adaptability:** Updating policies to accommodate changes in the MPC expert (e.g., tuning, model updates) or new tasks is challenging due to the need for large-scale retraining.

Approaches to enhance robustness, such as Domain Randomization (DR), modify the training environment by introducing random disturbances. While effective, DR typically necessitates a large number of demonstrations and the ability to apply these disturbances during training, which can be impractical in lab-to-real-world scenarios. Data augmentation (DA) strategies, aimed at generating additional training samples from existing demonstrations, have been explored to improve sample efficiency in IL for MPC. However, many existing DA methods do not explicitly account for environmental uncertainties or the propagation of errors in the learned policy, resulting in limited improvements in robustness. Specifically, prior work in this area, while improving sample efficiency, generally does not integrate uncertainty awareness into the expert controller or the data augmentation process itself. The presented work aims to address these identified gaps by developing a method that is simultaneously data-efficient, computationally efficient, and robust to uncertainties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an Imitation Learning strategy that can efficiently compress a computationally expensive Model Predictive Control (MPC) expert into a robust deep neural network (DNN) policy. The specific goals are to generate policies that are:

1. **Demonstration-Efficient:** Significantly reduce the number of demonstrations required from the MPC expert. This is motivated by the high computational cost of running MPC, particularly for complex systems, and the practical difficulties of collecting extensive real-world robot data.
2. **Computationally Efficient during Training:** Minimize the overall training time, including data collection and policy optimization. This addresses the bottleneck of generating large datasets for training, especially when interacting with complex simulation environments or physical robots.
3. **Robust to Unseen Disturbances:** Enable the learned policies to generalize effectively to deployment environments characterized by previously unencountered bounded model errors or external perturbations. This aims to bridge the "sim2real" and "lab2real" gaps, which are common challenges in robotics.
4. **Capable of Zero-Shot Transfer:** Facilitate the transfer of policies trained from a single nominal demonstration (e.g., in a simulation or controlled laboratory setting) directly to a new domain with unseen disturbances, without further retraining.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the limitations of current IL methods for MPC. These methods often demand extensive data, which is costly to acquire, and struggle to produce policies that maintain performance and safety under real-world uncertainties. By addressing these issues, the authors aim to make high-performance MPC policies more practical and deployable on resource-constrained robotic platforms performing agile and complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Sampling Augmentation (SA)**, an Imitation Learning (IL) strategy that leverages properties of a robust variant of MPC, specifically **Robust Tube MPC (RTMPC)**, to efficiently generate robust DNN policies. The approach is detailed for both linear and nonlinear system models.

**A. Foundation: Robust Tube MPC (RTMPC)**
The methodology uses RTMPC, which intrinsically accounts for bounded disturbances by maintaining the system state within a "tube" around a nominal trajectory. This tube, or disturbance invariant set, provides an estimate of the states the system might visit under uncertainty. RTMPC also incorporates an ancillary controller that actively keeps the system within this tube.

**B. Sampling Augmentation (SA) for Linear RTMPC (Trajectory Tracking)**
For systems well-approximated by linear dynamics (e.g., hover-linearized multirotor models for trajectory tracking), the linear RTMPC formulation (based on) is employed.
1. **Expert Formulation:** The linear RTMPC solves a Quadratic Program (QP) at each time step to compute a sequence of safe reference states and actions. These nominal states and actions are constrained to lie within tightened state and input bounds, which are derived by subtracting a disturbance invariant set (the tube, `Z`) from the original constraints.
2. **Ancillary Controller:** A linear feedback law, derived from an LQR problem, acts as an ancillary controller (Equation 11). This controller ensures the real system, subject to disturbances, stays within the tube `Z` centered around the nominal trajectory.
3. **Data Augmentation Strategy:**
 - The tube `Z` is approximated by an axis-aligned bounding box `^Z`.
 - Extra states (`x+`) are sampled from within this `^Z` (either densely from vertices or sparsely from facet centers, as illustrated in Fig. 4).
 - Corresponding actions (`u+`) are computed efficiently using the linear ancillary controller (Equation 13), which involves a simple matrix-vector multiplication.
 - These augmented state-action pairs are added to the training dataset, alongside the original demonstrations collected using BC or DAgger. This effectively expands the training data to cover states likely to be encountered under disturbances in the target domain (Algorithm 1).

**C. Sampling Augmentation (SA) for Nonlinear RTMPC (Acrobatic Maneuvers)**
For systems requiring the exploitation of full nonlinear dynamics (e.g., multirotor acrobatic maneuvers like flips), a nonlinear RTMPC variant (based on) is utilized. This introduces a significant challenge: the ancillary controller in nonlinear RTMPC is typically a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC), which is computationally expensive to solve repeatedly for data augmentation.
1. **Expert Formulation:**
 - **Nominal Safe Planner:** An outer-loop OCP (Equation 15) generates a nominal, open-loop safe plan. This planner uses further tightened state and actuation constraints, derived from estimated state and action "tubes" that contain the uncertain system's trajectories. The cost function is adapted for near-minimum time objectives (Equation 31).
 - **Ancillary NMPC:** An inner-loop NMPC (Equation 16) acts as the ancillary controller, tracking the nominal plan. This NMPC is solved using a Sequential Quadratic Program (SQP) solver (e.g., ACADOS).
2. **Computationally-Efficient Data Augmentation:**
 - To avoid repeatedly solving the full NMPC for each augmented sample, the authors propose using **parametric sensitivities**.
 - The NMPC's implicit feedback law is locally approximated by a time-varying linear feedback law (Equation 18).
 - The sensitivity matrix (`K_chi_t`) is computed by solving a simplified QP (Equation 21) that leverages the existing linearizations and Hessian information from the primary NMPC solution. This process is significantly faster than solving the full NMPC for each sample.
 - Extra actions (`u+`) are then generated efficiently using this sensitivity-based approximation (Equation 19).
3. **Addressing Approximation Errors:**
 - **Robustness:** The inherent constraint tightening in RTMPC can be further adjusted, potentially through iterative Monte-Carlo simulations, to account for errors introduced by the sensitivity-based DA.
 - **Performance Improvements (Fine-tuning):** An optional fine-tuning step (Algorithm 2) is introduced. After initial training with augmented data, additional demonstrations are collected *without* DA, and the policy is further refined using this new data. This helps mitigate any performance degradation due to approximation errors in the augmented samples.
 - **Quaternion Handling:** For attitude representation, Modified Rodriguez Parameters (MRPs) are used to map quaternions to a vector space for tube definition and sampling (Equation 32).

**D. Training and Evaluation:**
DNN policies, typically 2-hidden layer fully connected networks, are trained using the collected and augmented datasets. The methods are evaluated against baselines (BC, DAgger, DR, and other DA strategies) using metrics such as success rate (robustness), MPC stage cost/expert gap (performance), number of demonstrations, and wall-clock training time (efficiency) in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive numerical and experimental evaluations that support the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed Sampling Augmentation (SA) approach.

**A. Efficiency, Robustness, and Performance (Linear RTMPC for Trajectory Tracking):**
- **Demonstration Efficiency:** SA-based methods (SA-sparse, SA-dense) achieve 100% success rate in the target domain (with disturbances) after only **one demonstration** (Fig. 5, Table II). This represents a significant improvement over DAgger+DR, which required approximately 15-20 demonstrations for comparable robustness. SA was found to be three times more sample-efficient than the best baseline.
- **Robustness:** SA consistently enabled policies to maintain high success rates (near 100%) in environments with previously unseen wind-like disturbances (T1) and model errors (T2). Other baselines (BC, DAgger, DA with linear interpolation or expert neighborhood sampling) showed lower or inconsistent robustness in disturbed environments.
- **Performance:** SA-trained policies exhibited performance closest to the RTMPC expert, indicated by the lowest "expert gap" (Table II), demonstrating fidelity to the expert's behavior while handling perturbations.
- **Training Time:** SA-sparse significantly reduced the wall-clock training time compared to robust baselines. For a single trajectory, DAgger+SA-sparse reduced training time by 64.8% compared to DAgger+DR. SA-dense, while robust with one demonstration, incurred higher training time due to the larger number of generated samples.
- **Scalability:** The method demonstrated improved scalability with increasing task complexity (trajectory length) compared to DAgger+DR (Fig. 12).
- **Hardware Validation (Sim2Real and Lab2Real):**
 - A policy trained from a single simulation demonstration using SA-sparse successfully performed zero-shot sim2real transfer, robustly tracking a figure-8 trajectory on a real multirotor under significant wind disturbances from leaf blowers (Fig. 6).
 - A policy trained from a single real-robot demonstration in a controlled lab environment (Lab2Real) with SA-sparse successfully generalized to unseen disturbances like a slung load or a drag board (Fig. 7).
 - In direct real-world comparisons (Table III), SA-sparse (1 demonstration) achieved comparable or superior performance and robustness to DAgger+DR (10-20 demonstrations) under various real-world disturbances.
- **Computational Performance:** The learned DNN policy executed significantly faster (280 times) on the onboard CPU (Nvidia Jetson TX2) compared to the linear RTMPC expert (Table IV), with an average inference time of 15 µs.

**B. Efficiency, Robustness, and Performance (Nonlinear RTMPC for Acrobatic Maneuvers):**
- **Demonstration Efficiency:** For performing a 360-degree flip, SA-based methods achieved 100% success rate in both source and target (disturbed) environments after only **one demonstration** (Fig. 13). This contrasted sharply with DAgger+DR, which required over 100 demonstrations to achieve similar robustness in the target domain.
- **Training Time:** SA-based approaches drastically reduced training time. DAgger+DR required more than three times the training duration compared to SA methods (Fig. 14).
- **Performance:** SA-based methods achieved low tracking errors and close-to-expert performance with minimal demonstrations (Fig. 15, Table VII). The optional fine-tuning step proved beneficial in further reducing performance gaps.
- **Importance of Tube-Guided Sampling:** Ablation studies showed that sampling within the entire RTMPC tube was critical for robustness; "expert neighborhood" sampling (even with many samples) failed to achieve robustness in the target domain (Table VII).
- **Computational Efficiency of Nonlinear DA:** The computation of the sensitivity matrix for efficient data augmentation required only approximately 32% of the time needed to solve the full ancillary NMPC (Fig. 16), making the DA process practical for nonlinear systems.
- **Hardware Validation:** A policy trained with only two demonstrations (one with DA, one for fine-tuning) using DAgger+SA-25 successfully executed repeated 360-degree flips on a real multirotor. This policy demonstrated robustness to significant real-world uncertainties, including a slung load and a drag surface (Fig. 17, Fig. 18). The onboard inference time was 15 µs, an 180-fold improvement over state-of-the-art NMPC on the same hardware (Table VIII).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in the field of learning control policies for robotic systems by addressing critical limitations of existing Imitation Learning (IL) strategies when applied to Model Predictive Control (MPC). The primary significance lies in demonstrating a method that simultaneously achieves high **demonstration efficiency, training efficiency, and robustness to unseen disturbances**.

**Key Impacts:**

- **Enabling Practical Robot Autonomy:** By generating robust and computationally lightweight policies from a single demonstration, the method significantly lowers the barrier for deploying sophisticated MPC-derived behaviors on resource-constrained robotic platforms, such as drones, in real-world, uncertain environments. This includes complex agile maneuvers and precise trajectory tracking.
- **Accelerated Policy Development:** The substantial reduction in the number of required demonstrations and overall training time facilitates a faster iteration cycle for developing, updating, and adapting policies. This is crucial for rapid prototyping and deployment in dynamic applications or scenarios where expert tuning and model updates are frequent.
- **Bridging the Sim2Real/Lab2Real Gap:** The demonstrated ability of zero-shot transfer from simulation or controlled lab environments to the real world with robust performance under unseen disturbances is a substantial contribution. This reduces the need for extensive, often hazardous, real-world data collection and fine-tuning.
- **Foundational for Hybrid Learning Approaches:** The efficient policy pre-training procedure, leveraging model and uncertainty priors from RTMPC, offers a promising foundation for subsequent fine-tuning via model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL). This could potentially mitigate the challenges of inefficient random exploration and complex reward design typically associated with pure RL methods.

**Future Directions and Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**

- **Robustness Certificates:** Future work aims to integrate DNN reachability tools to provide formal robustness guarantees, enabling deployment in safety-critical applications.
- **Adaptive Tubes:** Exploring tubes with varying cross-sections is proposed to facilitate more aggressive expert demonstrations and potentially enhance robustness further.
- **Integration with Adaptive Control:** Combining the method with adaptive cascaded attitude controllers could address larger uncertainties in rotational dynamics, reducing manual tuning.
- **Dynamic Adaptation:** The training efficiency of the approach could be leveraged to design online adaptation strategies, generating new policies rapidly as new model or environmental estimates become available.
- **Hardware Expansion:** Applying the learned policies to aerial platforms with even more extreme payload and compute constraints is a future objective.

In conclusion, this research provides a practical and efficient framework for transferring the benefits of robust model predictive control to deep neural network policies, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with learning-based control in complex, uncertain, and resource-limited robotic systems.
