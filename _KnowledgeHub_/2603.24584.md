# TAG: Target-Agnostic Guidance for Stable Object-Centric Inference in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Target-Agnostic Guidance (TAG) is an inference-time mechanism for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models designed to enhance instance-level grounding robustness in cluttered scenes. It significantly improves success rates by explicitly distinguishing target objects from background and distractors, for example, more than doubling the average success rate for 	extpi^{0.5} on VLABench from 29.40% to 55.41%.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle with reliable instance-level grounding in cluttered environments, leading to "near-miss" or "wrong-object" executions.
- Existing VLA policies implicitly learn foreground-background interactions, causing performance degradation under visual perturbations and distraction.
- Failures stem from misidentifying specific object instances rather than a fundamental inability to generate feasible motions.

## Method

- Introduced ==Target-Agnostic Guidance== (**TAG**), a lightweight, inference-time residual guidance mechanism inspired by ==Classifier-Free Guidance== (**CFG**).
- **TAG** contrasts policy predictions from the original observation with those from a target-agnostic visual baseline to amplify target-driven action components and suppress nuisance priors.
- Developed an automated pipeline to generate ==target-erased images== for training and static background-only images for inference, ensuring high-fidelity counterfactuals.

## Results

- TAG-bg improved the 	extpi^{0.5} baseline's success rate on Libero-Long tasks from **89.6%** to **97.0%** and more than doubled the average success rate on VLABench from **29.40%** to **55.41%**.
- The method consistently enhanced robustness across diverse out-of-distribution visual and dynamic perturbations on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated TAG's superior cross-view robustness under camera pose perturbations, allowing stable manipulation where baseline policies failed due to spatial misalignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instance-level target grounding under visual distraction is a critical robustness bottleneck for VLA policies, manifesting as systematic near-miss and wrong-object executions.
> - Explicitly disentangling foreground object evidence from background and distractor cues via an inference-time guidance mechanism substantially improves action grounding.
> - Utilizing static, consistent target-agnostic baselines at inference time is crucial for stable guidance, as dynamic, real-time visual modifications can introduce detrimental temporal inconsistencies and artifacts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant area of research in robotics, aiming to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and visual observations to perform complex actions. Recent advancements have led to the development of powerful VLA architectures, including large-scale imitators like RT-1 and RT-2, autoregressive frameworks such as OpenVLA, models incorporating 3D spatial cues (SpatialVLA), and generative approaches like Diffusion Policy and flow-matching foundation models (e.g., $\pi^0$, $\pi^{0.5}$). These models have also been augmented with reasoning capabilities through textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT-VLA) and visual generative rollouts (GR-1, GR-2) to handle long-horizon tasks.

Despite these developments, a persistent challenge for VLA models lies in their reliability in cluttered environments. Existing models often struggle with accurately grounding actions to specific object instances when distractors are present. This can lead to "near-miss" or "wrong-object" executions, rather than fundamental failures in generating feasible motions. Current VLA policies typically rely on implicit learning from data to balance object evidence, contextual structure, and background appearance within unified representations. However, robustness analyses indicate performance degradation under visual perturbations like changes in lighting or background texture. This suggests a gap in explicitly modeling and regulating foreground-background interactions.

This paper addresses this specific limitation by introducing an inference-time guidance mechanism, drawing inspiration from Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG). While CFG has been applied in VLA contexts primarily to the language modality (e.g., randomly dropping instructions during training to strengthen semantic alignment), its application to disentangle static backgrounds from manipulation targets in the visual domain remains largely unexplored. This work extends the CFG paradigm to visual-spatial disentanglement, aiming to enhance instance-level grounding robustness in VLA models without altering their core architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the reliability and robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, particularly in cluttered scenes with distractors. The authors identify a specific and common failure mode: VLA policies often produce plausible grasp trajectories but execute them slightly off-target or on an incorrect object instance. This suggests that the failures stem not from an inability to generate physically feasible motions, but from instance-level grounding errors in complex visual environments.

The motivation for this work is rooted in an analysis of these failure cases. The authors observe that visual observations contain multiple types of evidence:
1. **Object-centric cues:** These specify the target for action.
2. **Contextual cues:** These come from other manipulable items and define relative spatial layout.
3. **Background appearance statistics:** These include textures, reflections, and lighting.

Ideally, instance-level grounding should prioritize foreground object evidence. However, in cluttered scenes, VLA policies can be unduly influenced by competing distractor objects and by background appearance correlations learned from the training data. While contextual objects offer valuable spatial reference, they also introduce competition. The core challenge is therefore to suppress bias induced by distractors and background appearance, while retaining the structural cues necessary for robustly associating the target object with the intended action.

Existing VLA policies typically attempt to learn these foreground-background interactions implicitly from data. This implicit learning can be effective in nominal settings but has shown substantial performance degradation under controlled perturbations and challenging visual conditions.

To address this, the authors propose an explicit mechanism to stabilize instance grounding. Their key objectives include:
- **Identifying the root cause:** To formally characterize instance-level target grounding under visual distraction as a dominant robustness bottleneck, manifesting as systematic near-miss and wrong-object executions.
- **Developing a novel solution:** To design a lightweight, inference-time visual guidance mechanism that explicitly reduces background- and distractor-induced bias.
- **Ensuring architectural compatibility:** To achieve this without requiring modifications to the underlying VLA policy architecture, allowing for broad applicability.
- **Demonstrating effectiveness:** To validate the proposed method's ability to consistently improve success rates and visual robustness across diverse manipulation tasks and VLA architectures.

This motivation highlights a shift from focusing solely on motion generation capabilities to enhancing the perceptive grounding of VLA models in visually complex scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Target-Agnostic Guidance (TAG), is an inference-time residual guidance mechanism designed to improve instance-level grounding. It is inspired by Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and operates by contrasting policy predictions under two different visual conditions.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Continuous-Time Flow Matching

The underlying generative modeling framework adopted by TAG is continuous-time flow matching, which models the learning of a continuous-time velocity field. The model is trained to predict the analytical ground-truth velocity field $u_{\tau} = \epsilon - x_0$, where $x_0$ is a clean data sample, $\epsilon$ is noise, and $\tau$ is a continuous denoising time step. During inference, actions are generated by solving a corresponding Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE), integrating backward from a noisy state to a clean data sample.

#### 3.2. TAG Formulation During Inference

TAG contrasts the velocity field predictions of the policy $v_{\theta}(x_t, I)$ under two visual conditions:
- $I_{cond}$: The original observation, containing full target evidence, manipulation, and scene context.
- $I_{uncond}$: A target-agnostic visual baseline, designed to suppress target-specific cues while preserving (or controlling) scene context.

The guided prediction $v_{TAG}(x_t)$ is formed by a CFG-style residual extrapolation:
$v_{TAG}(x_t) = v_{\theta}(x_t, I_{uncond}) + w \cdot (v_{\theta}(x_t, I_{cond}) - v_{\theta}(x_t, I_{uncond}))$
Here, $w$ is the guidance scale. The residual term $(v_{\theta}(x_t, I_{cond}) - v_{\theta}(x_t, I_{uncond}))$ estimates the marginal contribution of target evidence to the policy prediction. Scaling this residual is intended to suppress clutter-induced nuisance priors and strengthen target-driven action components. This mechanism aims to shift the model’s attention from distractors to the correct target.

#### 3.3. Constructing $I_{uncond}$

The authors investigate three practical instantiations for the target-agnostic baseline $I_{uncond}$:
- **Variant A: Background-only image ($I_{bg}$):** This is constructed by taking a reference frame from the episode and erasing/inpainting the entire foreground (manipulated target, manipulator, other movable objects), leaving only the static environment (table, background). $I_{bg}$ is fixed for the entire episode to ensure temporal stability, providing an estimate of environment-induced nuisance priors. This is the primary baseline for inference.
- **Variant B: Target-erased image ($I_{erase}$):** This variant removes only the manipulated target object while preserving the manipulator and other scene elements. This is closer to the counterfactual observations used during training but may retain stronger distractor competition.
- **Variant C: Black-only image ($I_{black}$):** A constant black image serves as $I_{uncond}$, intentionally suppressing visual features to provide a simple, temporally stable nuisance baseline.

A deliberate train-inference asymmetry is employed: $I_{erase}$ is used during training to learn a grounded target-agnostic prior, while $I_{bg}$ is primarily used for inference. This asymmetry is strategic, aiming to stabilize learning under plausible observations during training and maximize robustness by providing a cleaner nuisance estimate for guidance during deployment.

#### 3.4. Training-Time Calibration

To ensure the mathematical validity and stability of the two-branch contrast in TAG, a training-time calibration procedure is incorporated, analogous to standard CFG implementation. During training, with a probability $p_{cf} = 0.1$, the original conditional observation $I_{cond}$ is replaced with the target-erased image $I_{erase}$. This process encourages the policy to produce a stable nuisance prediction when target evidence is absent, enabling the residual term in the TAG formulation to more accurately capture the marginal contribution of the target evidence at inference time.

#### 3.5. Counterfactual Synthesis Pipeline

An automated pipeline is developed to generate counterfactual video sequences where target objects are removed and the background is reconstructed. This pipeline produces both $I_{erase}$ for training and $I_{bg}$ for inference. It consists of:
- **Instruction-Guided Target Semantic Parsing:** Qwen3-VL is used to extract precise textual descriptions of target objects from video frames, specifically sampled from the latter half of the video to capture definitive interactions. A caching mechanism is employed to avoid redundant VLM inference for recurring tasks.
- **Dynamic Masking and Inpainting:** Grounding DINO localizes target objects using the semantic prompts, and SAM 2 generates continuous spatio-temporal masks. An area-constrained rejection mechanism filters out oversized bounding boxes to prevent poor inpainting. The masks undergo morphological dilation. The MiniMaxRemover model then performs object erasure and background inpainting.
- **Closed-Loop Quality Assessment:** Qwen3-VL acts as an automated quality inspector, verifying inpainted frames for residual target fragments or structural artifacts. Failed samples trigger a retry loop (max 3 attempts) with stricter detection thresholds; otherwise, they are discarded. This ensures high-fidelity, target-agnostic environmental priors.
- **Background Reference Synthesis for Inference ($I_{bg}$):** For inference, the pipeline is adapted to process only the initial frame (t=0) and identify *all* movable objects, including the robotic arm, to create a completely static, clean background.

#### 3.6. Policy Architecture

The baseline models adopted are $\pi^0$ and $\pi^{0.5}$, which rely on a Gemma vision-language backbone. These are fine-tuned for specific tasks, integrating the TAG enhancements within their architecture as a dual-branch network that processes both the original conditional input and the erased unconditional input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of TAG was evaluated across three manipulation benchmarks: LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and VLABench. The baseline models were $\pi^0$ and $\pi^{0.5}$, which use a Gemma vision-language backbone pretrained on diverse robotic datasets.

#### 4.1. Analysis on LIBERO

On the standard LIBERO benchmark, TAG-bg (using background-only image as $I_{uncond}$) achieved the highest average success rate (SR), outperforming the $\pi^{0.5}$ baseline and other state-of-the-art models like InternVLA-M1 and GR00T-N1.6.
- TAG consistently improved performance even on tasks where baselines approached saturation (Spatial, Object, Goal tasks).
- The most significant improvements were observed in the challenging Libero-Long category, which involves complex, long-horizon manipulation sequences. TAG-bg increased the SR for $\pi^{0.5}$ from 89.6% to 97.0%. This supports the hypothesis that explicitly reducing background distractors helps the model maintain focused attention on manipulated objects and end-effector actions, which is critical for mitigating compounding errors in long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.2. Analysis on LIBERO-Plus

To assess robustness against out-of-distribution visual and dynamic perturbations, experiments were conducted on LIBERO-Plus, which includes seven distinct perturbation settings (Camera, Robot, Language, Light, Background, Noise, Layout).
- TAG-bg and TAG-black (using black-only image as $I_{uncond}$) achieved the second-highest and highest average success rates, respectively, among all compared methods.
- Compared to the $\pi^{0.5}$ baseline, TAG yielded notable improvements across nearly all perturbation categories.
- Significant robustness gains were observed under severe visual domain shifts, such as "Camera" (viewpoint changes) and "Noise" (sensor artifacts). This suggests that explicitly teaching the model to differentiate active foreground from static background enhances its resilience to environmental variations and visual distractors.

#### 4.3. Analysis on VLABench

Performance on VLABench Track 1, which features fine-grained and complex manipulation tasks in visually dense scenes (e.g., selecting specific items from clutter), was also evaluated using both success rate (SR) and process score (PS) for intermediate action precision.
- TAG substantially improved baseline performance. For $\pi^{0.5}$, the average SR more than doubled from 29.40% to 55.41% with TAG-bg. Specific tasks like "Select Poker" and "Select Mahjong" saw significant increases (e.g., to 53.06% and 58.16% for $\pi^{0.5}$ with TAG-bg).
- The average PS also increased significantly, indicating improved intermediate trajectory generation in addition to task completion.
- Qualitative comparisons and attention map visualizations showed that while the baseline policy's attention diffused across similar distractors, TAG successfully concentrated focus on the language-instructed target, leading to accurate manipulation in cluttered environments.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Guidance in Training:** Training with the target-erased ($I_{erase}$) frames yielded the most robust performance, particularly when evaluated with the background-only ($I_{bg}$) method at inference. This indicates that explicit object erasure during training fosters generalized and resilient representations, effectively preparing the model for TAG.
- **Erase Method at Inference:** Static extraction methods ($I_{bg}$ or $I_{black}$) significantly outperformed real-time dynamic masking/erasure strategies during inference. Dynamic modifications (e.g., frame-wise masking) caused substantial performance drops, especially in long-horizon tasks (SR collapsing from 97.0% to approximately 13%). This is attributed to temporal inconsistencies and visual artifacts introduced by real-time dynamic erasure, which disrupt the model's coherent understanding of spatial layout. A static, clean reference for the background was found to be crucial for stable residual computation.
- **Guidance Scale (w):** An optimal guidance scale was observed around $w=1.25$. Moderate guidance effectively amplified the model's attention towards the foreground manipulation. However, excessively large guidance scales (e.g., $w=15$) led to performance degradation, suggesting that overly aggressive guidance can distort the learned action distribution manifolds, resulting in less smooth or feasible trajectories.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments (Appendix S3)

Real-world pick-block tasks were conducted on a Franka Research 3 platform using the Gello teleoperation framework. The policy was trained with a fixed overhead camera viewpoint.
- Under camera pose perturbations (out-of-distribution viewpoint changes), TAG demonstrated superior cross-view robustness. It accurately localized the target and completed the pick task.
- In contrast, the $\pi^{0.5}$ baseline exhibited high sensitivity to viewpoint variations, frequently suffering from spatial misalignment and action stagnation, failing to execute the final grasp. This provides real-world evidence that TAG maintains stable manipulation performance even when camera viewpoints deviate from the training configuration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research identifies instance-level target grounding under visual distraction as a critical and previously underexplored robustness bottleneck in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. By diagnosing systematic near-miss and wrong-object executions as a prevalent failure mode, the paper highlights a specific area where VLA models require improvement beyond fundamental motion generation capabilities.

The introduction of TAG (Target-Agnostic Guidance) offers a novel and effective solution to this problem. As a lightweight, inference-time guidance mechanism inspired by Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), TAG extends the CFG paradigm from the linguistic domain to explicit visual disentanglement. Its ability to contrast policy predictions under original and target-erased observations generates a residual steering signal that effectively suppresses distractor- and appearance-induced bias while preserving essential contextual structure.

The significance of TAG lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Robustness:** It consistently and substantially improves the success rates and visual robustness of VLA models across diverse and challenging manipulation benchmarks, including LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus (stressing out-of-distribution conditions), and VLABench (involving fine-grained selection in cluttered scenes).
- **Architectural Agnosticism:** A key advantage is that TAG integrates with existing VLA policies with minimal changes to their architecture and training/inference procedures. This makes it broadly applicable to a wide range of current and future VLA models, enhancing their deployability without requiring significant re-design or re-training.
- **Improved Instance Grounding:** By explicitly guiding the model's attention away from distractors and towards the intended target, TAG directly addresses the instance-level grounding failures that previously limited VLA performance in complex environments.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The demonstrated improvements in robustness, including under camera viewpoint perturbations in real-world settings, suggest that TAG can contribute to the development of more reliable and generalizable robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured and dynamic human environments.

The potential impact of this work is in fostering more stable and dependable VLA deployments in practical applications. By improving a robot's ability to precisely identify and interact with target objects amidst clutter, TAG contributes to safer, more efficient, and more versatile robotic manipulation. Future research could explore extending this residual guidance to other controllable factors and enhancing its robustness to imperfect visual observations in even more challenging real-world scenarios.
