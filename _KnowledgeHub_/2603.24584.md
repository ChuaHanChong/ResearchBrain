# TAG: Target-Agnostic Guidance for Stable Object-Centric Inference in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Target-Agnostic Guidance (TAG) is an inference-time mechanism for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models designed to enhance instance-level grounding robustness in cluttered scenes. It significantly improves success rates by explicitly distinguishing target objects from background and distractors, for example, more than doubling the average success rate for 	extpi^{0.5} on VLABench from 29.40% to 55.41%.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle with reliable instance-level grounding in cluttered environments, leading to "near-miss" or "wrong-object" executions.
- Existing VLA policies implicitly learn foreground-background interactions, causing performance degradation under visual perturbations and distraction.
- Failures stem from misidentifying specific object instances rather than a fundamental inability to generate feasible motions.

## Method

- Introduced ==Target-Agnostic Guidance== (**TAG**), a lightweight, inference-time residual guidance mechanism inspired by ==Classifier-Free Guidance== (**CFG**).
- **TAG** contrasts policy predictions from the original observation with those from a target-agnostic visual baseline to amplify target-driven action components and suppress nuisance priors.
- Developed an automated pipeline to generate ==target-erased images== for training and static background-only images for inference, ensuring high-fidelity counterfactuals.

## Results

- TAG-bg improved the 	extpi^{0.5} baseline's success rate on Libero-Long tasks from **89.6%** to **97.0%** and more than doubled the average success rate on VLABench from **29.40%** to **55.41%**.
- The method consistently enhanced robustness across diverse out-of-distribution visual and dynamic perturbations on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated TAG's superior cross-view robustness under camera pose perturbations, allowing stable manipulation where baseline policies failed due to spatial misalignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instance-level target grounding under visual distraction is a critical robustness bottleneck for VLA policies, manifesting as systematic near-miss and wrong-object executions.
> - Explicitly disentangling foreground object evidence from background and distractor cues via an inference-time guidance mechanism substantially improves action grounding.
> - Utilizing static, consistent target-agnostic baselines at inference time is crucial for stable guidance, as dynamic, real-time visual modifications can introduce detrimental temporal inconsistencies and artifacts.
