# Spatial Traces: Enhancing VLA Models with Spatial-Temporal Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> ST-VLA augments a VLA policy with Spatial Traces: keypoint trajectories from Co-Tracker are overlaid onto ZoeDepth depth maps, fusing spatial and temporal cues into one visual prompt fed to a PaliGemma2-based SpatialVLA backbone. Fine-tuned on only 52 Bridge trajectories, it beats SpatialVLA by 4% and TraceVLA by 19% mean success rate in SimplerEnv.

## Problem

- Prior VLA models like OpenVLA predict actions from single static observations, lacking spatial understanding of depth and 3D geometry needed for precise manipulation.
- Trace-based prompting methods such as TraceVLA add temporal history via keypoint traces on RGB images but provide no explicit spatial or depth context.
- Depth-aware methods such as SpatialVLA encode spatial geometry from single observations but ignore temporal dynamics of agent-environment interaction across steps.

## Method

- **Spatial Traces**: projects ==Co-Tracker== keypoint trajectories from a buffer of prior observations directly onto ==ZoeDepth==-predicted depth maps, unifying spatial and temporal signals in one image.
- Depth-trace embedding fusion: passes the traced depth map through SpatialVLA's ==Ego3D Positional Encoder== and sums resulting embeddings with ==SigLIP== observation embeddings before the ==VLA policy==.
- Trace-application variant selection: assigns each trace pixel the depth of the nearest object in the current frame (Variant C) rather than the traced point's own or prior-frame depth, found empirically superior.

## Results

- ST-VLA achieves **38.1%** mean success rate in SimplerEnv versus **34.1%** for SpatialVLA and **19.9%** for TraceVLA, using only **52** fine-tuning trajectories.
- Mean GCS reaches **64.8%**, exceeding SpatialVLA by **14.8** points and TraceVLA by **13.7** points across Put Spoon, Put Carrot, Stack Blocks, Put Eggplant tasks.
- Ablation shows longer trace buffers (**30** images) outperform shorter ones (**7** or **15**) on spatially demanding tasks, and naive Bridge fine-tuning alone drops SR by **10%** while trace prompting recovers gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing depth and temporal trace information into a single visual prompt lets a VLA generalize with minimal fine-tuning data, valuable when real-world demonstrations are scarce.
> - Gains come from the trace-prompting mechanism itself, not from Bridge dataset fine-tuning, since naive fine-tuning alone degrades baseline performance.
> - Trace usefulness degrades when consecutive observations are near-identical, such as slow rotations, since keypoint tracking yields short or absent trajectories, a caution for motion-dependent visual prompting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, robotics, and embodied artificial intelligence, specifically addressing a critical challenge in enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks effectively. The broader research landscape for VLA models has been rapidly evolving, largely driven by the advancements in large language models (LLMs) and large vision models (LVMs), which are increasingly being adapted for robotic control and task planning.

**Current Trends and Gaps:**
- **LLMs in Robotics:** The emergence of LLMs has opened new avenues for generating sequences of manipulator movements based on environmental observations and task specifications. This has led to VLA models that integrate visual and textual information to predict agent actions.
- **Generalist Robot Policies:** A significant trend is the development of "generalist" robot policies, like RT-1-X and Octo, trained on vast datasets to achieve strong zero-shot generalization across diverse tasks and embodiments. While impressive, these models often rely on static observations and may lack explicit mechanisms for incorporating temporal dynamics.
- **Spatial Grounding:** Recognizing the importance of depth and 3D information, recent works like SpatialVLA and SEM have incorporated spatial context through RGB-D images, voxel grids, or 3D affordances. These approaches enhance spatial understanding but typically focus on single, static observations, overlooking the temporal dimension.
- **Temporal Understanding and Visual Prompting:** Another growing area involves using visual prompting to provide temporal context. Approaches like TraceVLA, RoboTAP, RT-Trajectories, and RT-Affordance use visual traces of manipulator movements, key points, or interaction regions to enhance the model's understanding of historical dynamics. However, these methods often lack explicit spatial encoding, treating temporal information separately or without strong depth awareness.

**The Niche Addressed by "Spatial Traces":**
The core contribution of "Spatial Traces" is its novel approach to bridge the gap between these two distinct lines of research: spatial grounding and temporal understanding. Existing VLA models typically excel in one aspect but fall short in the other. The paper explicitly identifies that "existing VLA models focus either on spatial understanding or temporal context, but do not combine both modalities in a unified framework." By proposing a technique that integrates both spatial (depth maps) and temporal (visual traces of key points) information into a *single visual input*, this work aims to provide a more holistic understanding of the environment and agent-environment interactions. This fusion allows the VLA model to simultaneously leverage where objects are in 3D space and how they (or the robot's end-effector) have moved over time, which is critical for complex, dynamic manipulation tasks. This positions "Spatial Traces" as a significant step towards more robust, context-aware, and data-efficient embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for "Spatial Traces" stems from a critical limitation observed in contemporary Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their inability to comprehensively understand both the spatial layout of an environment and the temporal dynamics of agent-environment interactions simultaneously. While VLA models have shown remarkable capabilities in predicting robot movements based on visual observations and textual instructions, they often falter in complex scenarios requiring nuanced spatial-temporal reasoning.

**Specific limitations and motivations identified by the authors:**

1. **Lack of Comprehensive Spatial Understanding:** Current VLA models, even those incorporating visual information, often lack a thorough grasp of the 3D spatial properties of the environment. While methods like SpatialVLA have introduced RGB-D images to improve spatial awareness, many models still treat the scene as a 2D projection, hindering precise manipulation.
2. **Insufficient Temporal Context:** A significant drawback is the treatment of manipulation tasks as sequences of independent action predictions. Most models do not effectively incorporate the history of agent-environment interactions. Although approaches like CogACT attempt multi-action prediction, they still do not fully leverage temporal context, which is crucial for tasks involving sequential operations, dynamic changes, or long-horizon planning.
3. **Disjoint Spatial and Temporal Information:** Existing solutions tend to address spatial understanding (e.g., using depth maps) or temporal context (e.g., using visual traces) in isolation. There is a lack of unified frameworks that seamlessly integrate both modalities into a single, cohesive representation that the VLA model can process. Visual prompting has demonstrated its efficacy for both spatial, and temporal information, but a combined visual prompt encoding both aspects has been missing.

**The key objective of this work is to address these limitations by proposing a novel approach called "Spatial Traces" (ST-VLA).** This method is designed to enhance VLA models with a simultaneous spatial-temporal understanding of the environment through an innovative visual prompting technique.

**The specific objectives are:**

- **To introduce a novel visual prompting technique** that integrates both spatial and temporal information by projecting visual traces of key points from observations onto predicted depth maps. This creates a unified visual input that provides the model with rich context about "where" objects are and "how" the agent or objects have moved over time.
- **To empirically demonstrate the effectiveness** of the Spatial Traces approach in improving robot manipulation performance in challenging virtual environments (SimplerEnv).
- **To prove that this enhancement can be achieved with minimal training data**, making the approach particularly valuable for real-world applications where data collection is often challenging and resource-intensive. This is directly stated as a contribution: "Experiments in SimplerEnv show that with only 52 training trajectories, our approach improves task success by 4% over SpatialVLA and 19% over TraceVLA."

In essence, the motivation is to overcome the fragmented understanding of existing VLA models by providing them with a "spatial-temporal glance" of the environment, thereby enabling more intelligent and robust robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Spatial Traces" method introduces a novel visual prompting technique to imbue Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with both spatial and temporal understanding through a single, enriched visual input. The approach, termed ST-VLA, addresses the limitations of current VLA models that either focus on spatial information from static observations or temporal information via visual traces without explicit spatial grounding.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The authors formulate robotic manipulation as predicting a sequence of actions `[a_0, ..., a_N]` based on an instruction `I` to transition an environment from an initial state `S_I` to a goal state `S_G`. Traditional VLA models predict each action `a_t` independently based on the current observation `o_t` and instruction `I` (`a_t = VLA(o_t, I)`). The Spatial Traces approach extends this by incorporating a buffer of `b` previous observations, `[o_{t-b+1}, ..., o_t]`, into the model input to capture historical context (`a_t = VLA([o_{t-b+1}, o_t], I)`). The training objective is to minimize the mean squared error (MSE) between the predicted and ground truth actions, optimizing the VLA policy to maximize task success.

**B. Spatial Traces Mechanism (ST-VLA Architecture):**
The core methodology involves integrating two complementary types of visual information: Depth Information and Visual Traces, into a unified representation. The overall processing pipeline is described in Algorithm 1 and illustrated in Figure 1.

1. **Observation Embeddings (`E_O_t`):** The current RGB observation `o_t` is processed by an image processor (`P_I`, specifically Siglip) to generate visual embeddings `E_O_t`.
2. **Depth Map Generation (`D_t`):** A depth estimation model (`M_D`, specifically ZoeDepth) predicts a depth map `D_t` from the current observation `o_t`.
3. **Visual Trace Prediction (`T_t`):** A trace predictor (`M_T`, specifically Co-Tracker) analyzes a buffer of `b` previous observations (`[o_{t-b+1}, ..., o_t]`) to identify and track key points, generating visual traces `T_t`. Each trace `t_i` is a sequence of 2D coordinates `[(x_0, y_0), ..., (x_b, y_b)]`, representing the trajectory of a specific point over time.
4. **Traces-on-Depth Application (`D_T_t`):** The predicted visual traces `T_t` are then overlaid onto the current depth map `D_t`. This step, `D_T_t = D_t ← T_t`, involves mapping the 2D trace coordinates to the depth map, effectively embedding the temporal movement history directly into the spatial context.
5. **Depth Map Embeddings with Traces (`E_D_T_t`):** The combined depth map with traces (`D_T_t`) is then processed by a depth processor (`P_D`, specifically the Ego3D Positional Encoder from SpatialVLA) to predict depth map embeddings `E_D_T_t`.
6. **Unified Visual Embeddings (`E_V`):** The observation embeddings `E_O_t` and the depth map embeddings with traces `E_D_T_t` are summed (`E_V = E_O_t + E_D_T_t`) to form a comprehensive visual embedding that encapsulates both original RGB features, spatial depth, and temporal traces.
7. **Action Prediction (`a_t`):** Finally, this unified visual embedding `E_V` is fed into the VLA model (based on PaliGemma2, initialized from SpatialVLA checkpoint) along with language embeddings `E_I` (derived from the natural language instruction `I`) to predict the next action `a_t`.

**C. Implementation Details:**
- **Model Components:** The choice of specific models for each component (Siglip for `P_I`, Co-Tracker for `M_T`, ZoeDepth for `M_D`, Ego3D Positional Encoder for `P_D`, and PaliGemma2-based VLA) ensures a fair comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods, particularly SpatialVLA and TraceVLA.
- **Trace Generation Nuance:** Co-Tracker's effectiveness relies on sufficient differences between consecutive images. To prevent issues during initial slow movements, traces are not provided for the first five steps of an episode.
- **Training Setup:** Fine-tuning was performed on a minimal subset of 52 trajectories (1,969 steps) from the Bridge dataset. LoRA adapters were used with specific hyperparameters (learning rate 5e-5, batch size 1, 2 epochs, AdamW optimizer, LoRA rank 32). The computational efficiency is highlighted by a total training time of two hours on a single NVIDIA TITAN RTX.

**D. Experimental Setup and Research Questions:**
The evaluation was conducted in the SimplerEnv virtual environment, which features challenging robot manipulation tasks based on the Bridge dataset (e.g., Put Spoon, Put Carrot, Stack Blocks, Put Eggplant).
- **Metrics:** Goal Condition Success (GCS, basic task completion) and Success Rate (SR, full task completion) were used.
- **Research Questions:**
 - **RQ1:** Does integrating keypoint traces into depth maps improve manipulation performance with minimal training data?
 - **RQ2:** How does the length of the agent’s interaction history (buffer size) affect predictions?
 - **RQ3:** Which strategies for embedding trajectories into depth observations are most effective?
 - **RQ4:** Is the performance gain due to fine-tuning on Bridge data or the trace-based visual prompting?
- **Baselines:** OpenVLA, RT-1-X, Octo, RoboVLM, SpatialVLA, and TraceVLA were used for comparison.

This systematic methodology ensures that the proposed Spatial Traces approach is rigorously evaluated, addressing key questions about its effectiveness, efficiency, and robustness in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in SimplerEnv provide strong evidence for the efficacy of the Spatial Traces (ST-VLA) method, particularly its ability to enhance VLA models with combined spatial and temporal understanding using minimal training data.

**RQ1: To what extent does integrating keypoint traces into depth maps improve manipulation performance, and can this improvement be achieved with minimal training data?**
- **Significant Performance Improvement:** ST-VLA demonstrated notable improvements over baseline methods. The average Goal Condition Success (GCS) for ST-VLA was 64.8%, outperforming SpatialVLA (50.0%) by 14.8% and TraceVLA (51.1%) by 13.7%. More critically, the average Success Rate (SR) for ST-VLA reached 38.1%, an increase of 4% over SpatialVLA (34.1%) and a substantial 18.7% over TraceVLA (19.9%).
- **Minimal Data Efficiency:** These performance gains were achieved with exceptionally limited fine-tuning data: only 52 training trajectories from the Bridge dataset. This finding is crucial, highlighting the method's data efficiency and potential for real-world applications where data collection is costly.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** ST-VLA showed strong performance across various tasks. For instance, in the challenging "Stack blocks" task, ST-VLA achieved 90.9% GCS and 36.4% SR, which, while slightly lower in SR than SpatialVLA (45.5%), signifies a higher rate of initial success in object manipulation (grasping) before precise placement. In "Put spoon," ST-VLA's SR of 36.4% was double that of SpatialVLA and quadruple that of TraceVLA.
- **Illustrative Example:** A qualitative example ("Put spoon on towel") showed ST-VLA successfully completing a task where SpatialVLA and TraceVLA failed, even after initial difficulties. This demonstrates ST-VLA's ability to recover and adapt using the combined spatial-temporal cues.
- **Limitations in Specific Scenarios:** The paper noted a limitation in tasks involving slow gripper rotation. In such cases (e.g., a "put carrot on plate" failed sequence), consecutive observations might be too similar, leading Co-Tracker to generate short or absent traces. This reduces the informational value of Spatial Traces, impacting performance in scenarios requiring precise rotational control without significant translation.

**RQ2: How does the length of the agent’s interaction history (i.e., the number of traced steps) affect the quality of model predictions?**
- **Optimal Buffer Size:** Experiments with different buffer sizes (7, 15, 30 images) indicated that a longer interaction history generally leads to more stable and higher performance. A buffer size of 30 images yielded the best overall average GCS and SR across tasks, achieving the highest SR in "Put spoon" (36.4%) and "Put eggplant" (54.5%), and the highest GCS in "Stack blocks" (90.9%).
- **Temporal Context Importance:** These results suggest that accumulating temporal information over a longer window significantly enhances the model's ability to reason about dynamic scene changes and improves the accuracy of action planning, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding and sequential interactions.

**RQ3: What strategies for embedding trajectories into depth observations are most effective?**
- **Superiority of Nearest Object Depth:** The paper compared two strategies for applying traces to depth maps. Strategy C, which assigns each trace pixel the depth of the *nearest object* in the current frame, consistently outperformed Strategy A, which assigns each trace pixel its *own depth* from the corresponding observation.
- **Reasoning:** Strategy C makes the traces more distinct and visually salient, separating them from the manipulator itself. This helps the model to effectively interpret traces as meaningful historical paths rather than mere extensions of the robot's end-effector, thus capturing the model's attention more effectively and improving performance.

**RQ4: Is the observed performance gain attributable solely to fine-tuning on Bridge data, or does it stem from the trace-based visual prompting?**
- **Visual Prompting as Primary Driver:** This crucial experiment clarified that the performance gain is predominantly due to the trace-based visual prompting, not merely the fine-tuning process.
- **Evidence:** Direct fine-tuning of the base model on the Bridge dataset *without* traces actually led to a performance decrease (mean SR decrease of 10%). In contrast, running SpatialVLA with traces in a zero-shot mode (without fine-tuning) showed better performance than the fine-tuned baseline, demonstrating that the visual prompting mechanism itself effectively provides valuable information even without task-specific training. ST-VLA's superior performance is attributed to its ability to leverage the temporal information from traces effectively.

In summary, the main findings confirm that integrating spatial depth with temporal keypoint traces through visual prompting provides a significant boost to VLA model performance, is data-efficient, and that the method of trace application plays a critical role in its success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Spatial Traces" method represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts.

**A. Unified Spatial-Temporal Understanding:**
The primary significance lies in the paper's successful integration of spatial and temporal information into a single, cohesive visual input. Previously, researchers often tackled these challenges separately. By overlaying visual traces onto depth maps, "Spatial Traces" provides VLA models with a richer, more contextually aware understanding of the environment—knowing both "where" things are in 3D space and "how" the agent or objects have moved over a recent history. This holistic perception is crucial for truly intelligent and adaptable robotic agents.

**B. Enhanced Performance and Robustness:**
The empirical results demonstrate clear and substantial performance gains (up to 19% increase in Success Rate compared to TraceVLA and 4% compared to SpatialVLA). This improvement translates directly to more reliable and robust robotic manipulation policies. In complex, multi-stage tasks, a robot that understands both the current 3D geometry and the trajectory of past movements is far more likely to succeed, especially in dynamic or cluttered environments. The qualitative examples, showing ST-VLA's ability to solve tasks where baselines fail, underscore this enhanced robustness.

**C. Remarkable Data Efficiency:**
Perhaps one of the most impactful findings is the ability to achieve these significant performance improvements with *minimal training data* (only 52 trajectories). Data collection for real-world robotics is notoriously expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive. By demonstrating that effective spatial-temporal understanding can be imparted with such a small fine-tuning dataset, "Spatial Traces" dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for developing capable robotic systems. This efficiency makes the approach highly practical for real-world deployment, where access to massive, perfectly curated datasets is often infeasible.

**D. Advancing Generalist Robot Policies:**
The data efficiency and robust performance contribute directly to the goal of creating "generalist robot policies." If a model can effectively generalize from limited demonstrations by understanding fundamental spatial-temporal principles, it can be more readily adapted to new tasks and environments without extensive retraining. This moves the field closer to robots that can operate flexibly across a wide range of tasks and scenarios, akin to human versatility.

**E. Guiding Future Research Directions:**
The identified limitations and proposed future work also point to important avenues for further research:
- **Improving Trace Generation:** The dependency on accurate trace generation (e.g., Co-Tracker's performance with slow movements) suggests a need for more robust and adaptive trace generation methods, perhaps even those that are task-aware or incorporate uncertainty.
- **Richer Temporal Prompts and 3D Reconstruction:** Exploring richer temporal visual prompts beyond simple keypoint traces, or integrating with full 3D reconstructed observations, could unlock even deeper spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities. This moves towards a more comprehensive environmental model rather than just 2D projections.
- **Real-world Applications:** The clear next step is to test and validate this approach in more complex real-world robotics tasks, moving beyond virtual environments. Success in these scenarios would solidify its practical utility.

In conclusion, "Spatial Traces" is a pivotal contribution that addresses a fundamental challenge in embodied AI. By intelligently combining spatial and temporal information through visual prompting and demonstrating remarkable data efficiency, it paves the way for more capable, robust, and deployable robotic systems, ultimately accelerating the development of truly intelligent and autonomous agents.
