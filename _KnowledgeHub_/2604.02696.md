# VBGS-SLAM: Variational Bayesian Gaussian Splatting Simultaneous Localization and Mapping

> [!summary] Summary
> VBGS-SLAM, developed by researchers at the University of California, Riverside, introduces a fully probabilistic RGB-D SLAM framework that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting with Variational Bayesian Inference, jointly inferring camera poses and map uncertainties through closed-form updates. The system achieved state-of-the-art or competitive performance in localization (e.g., 0.33 cm ATE on Replica dataset) and 3D reconstruction quality, demonstrating improved robustness and efficiency across synthetic and real-world datasets.

## Problem

- Traditional SLAM methods relying on sparse point clouds or dense voxels are memory-intensive and can result in incomplete 3D reconstructions.
- Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) based SLAM systems are sensitive to initialization, prone to catastrophic forgetting, and lack principled uncertainty modeling due to deterministic, gradient-based optimization.
- Variational Bayesian Gaussian Splatting (VBGS) offers efficient updates and uncertainty quantification for 3D scene representation but traditionally assumes known camera poses, limiting its application to real-time SLAM.

## Method

- **VBGS-SLAM** formulates a unified generative probabilistic model that tightly couples ==3D Gaussian Splatting== map refinement and camera pose tracking, treating camera pose as a latent variable.
- It derives ==closed-form variational update rules== for both splat map parameters and camera poses by linearizing the ==spatial likelihood== around the current pose estimate, enabling efficient concurrent updates.
- The framework incorporates ==keyframe selection== based on co-visibility and temporal constraints, along with ==adaptive Gaussian management== to maintain a compact, high-resolution map without excessive redundancy.

## Results

- Achieved an average Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of **0.33 cm** on the synthetic Replica Dataset, outperforming **SplaTAM** (0.36 cm) by **8.3%** and **Point-SLAM** (0.53 cm) by **37.7%**.
- Demonstrated superior robustness on the long-sequence AR-TABLE dataset, achieving the best average ATE of **5.94 cm** and stable tracking where other leading methods experienced failures.
- Produced high-fidelity 3D reconstructions with competitive PSNR (e.g., **37.94 dB** on Replica) and LPIPS, operating at approximately **5x** faster rendering speed than **SplaTAM** on AR-TABLE (**1.374 FPS** vs. 0.115 FPS).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Variational Bayesian Inference with 3D Gaussian Splatting enables explicit modeling and propagation of uncertainty in both camera poses and 3D scene parameters, enhancing robustness.
> - Treating camera pose as a latent variable within a generative probabilistic model allows for joint, online inference of pose and map uncertainties, improving the coherence between localization and mapping.
> - Closed-form variational updates derived from conjugate priors and spatial likelihood linearization significantly reduce computational overhead compared to differential rendering and separate gradient-based optimization in other 3DGS pipelines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a foundational technology in computer vision, supporting applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and 3D reconstruction. Traditionally, SLAM systems have relied on environment representations such as sparse point clouds or dense voxels. While these methods are effective for localization, they can be memory-intensive and may result in incomplete reconstructions due to difficulties in filling unobserved regions.

More recently, implicit geometry representations using neural networks, such as Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), have emerged. These offer benefits including compact representation, robustness to noise, and flexible resolution. However, a limitation of single global MLPs is the necessity for retraining with new observations, which can lead to prohibitively long training and inference times, particularly in large-scale environments. To address scalability, some approaches have integrated neural implicit representations with voxel-based data structures, achieving faster training. Nevertheless, these methods still typically involve computationally expensive per-pixel raycasting for rendering and are susceptible to catastrophic forgetting when map updates overwrite prior content.

A recent development in 3D scene representation is 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). This technique models scenes as a collection of 3D Gaussian ellipsoids, offering an explicit yet differentiable representation that supports real-time rendering. The local editability of Gaussian splats without global retraining makes 3DGS adaptable for streaming updates in SLAM contexts. Consequently, 3DGS has been adopted in several dense visual SLAM systems, such as SplaTAM and MonoGS, demonstrating improved performance over earlier neural-implicit solutions. Despite these advancements, existing 3DGS-based SLAM systems often exhibit sensitivity to initialization and depend on tightly coupled, computationally intensive optimization processes, which can limit their scalability to long sequences. Furthermore, their reliance on deterministic, gradient-based optimization typically precludes a principled mechanism for uncertainty modeling, contributing to fragility and susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting.

An alternative approach, Variational Bayesian Gaussian Splatting (VBGS), frames 3D scene representation as a variational inference problem over model parameters. This framework enables closed-form updates for efficient incorporation of sequential observations and explicitly quantifies uncertainty. However, VBGS assumes known camera poses and data statistics, which restricts its application to offline reconstruction scenarios where poses are pre-determined.

The presented work, VBGS-SLAM, is positioned to address the limitations of existing 3DGS SLAM systems by integrating the uncertainty-aware, closed-form update capabilities of Variational Bayesian Inference with 3D Gaussian Splatting. It aims to develop a framework that can jointly infer camera poses and map uncertainties online, thereby providing a more robust, efficient, and scalable solution for real-time dense RGB-D SLAM.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop Variational Bayesian Gaussian Splatting Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (VBGS-SLAM), a fully probabilistic RGB-D SLAM framework. This framework aims to couple 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) map refinement and camera pose tracking through a generative probabilistic model, explicitly maintaining posterior uncertainty over both poses and scene parameters.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations observed in current SLAM approaches:

- **Sensitivity to Initialization and Catastrophic Forgetting in Existing 3DGS SLAM:** Current 3DGS-based SLAM systems (e.g., SplaTAM, MonoGS) typically employ direct, deterministic optimization for camera poses against the splat map. This approach makes them vulnerable to poor initialization and prone to "catastrophic forgetting," where new map updates can overwrite or degrade previously learned scene representations, particularly as the map evolves over long sequences.
- **Lack of Principled Uncertainty Modeling:** Deterministic gradient-based optimization, common in existing 3DGS SLAM, does not inherently provide a mechanism for quantifying uncertainty in either the camera poses or the map parameters. This absence of uncertainty awareness can reduce robustness in challenging conditions.
- **Computational Intensity and Scalability Issues:** Tightly coupled, computation-heavy optimization processes in existing 3DGS SLAM systems can limit their scalability, particularly when dealing with long sequences or large-scale environments.
- **Limitations of Variational Bayesian Gaussian Splatting (VBGS):** While VBGS offers efficient closed-form updates and uncertainty quantification for 3D scene representation, it traditionally assumes known camera poses. This constraint restricts its application to offline reconstruction rather than real-time SLAM, where pose and map uncertainties must be inferred jointly and online.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of VBGS-SLAM are:

- To formulate a generative mixture model that incorporates both 3D Gaussians for scene representation and SE(3) poses expressed in Lie groups.
- To derive closed-form variational update rules for both the splat map parameters and the camera poses. This is intended to leverage the conjugate properties of multivariate Gaussians and variational inference for efficient computation.
- To achieve a tighter probabilistic coupling between the Gaussian map and SE(3) pose variables. This design aims to enable pose uncertainty to propagate through the mapping process, rather than being handled as a separate or post-hoc step. This coupling is hypothesized to produce more accurate maps and stabilize pose tracking.
- To develop a system that updates map parameters and poses more efficiently than gradient-based Gaussian Splatting pipelines due to its closed-form variational updates.
- To demonstrate superior tracking performance and robustness in long sequence prediction, alongside efficient, high-quality novel view synthesis across diverse synthetic and real-world scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VBGS-SLAM is a fully probabilistic RGB-D SLAM framework that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with Variational Bayesian Inference. The system operates by coupling splat map refinement and camera pose tracking within a generative probabilistic model.

**System Initialization:**
The process begins with an initial RGB-D image pair, camera intrinsic matrix, and an initial camera pose. A dense 3D point cloud is generated by back-projecting image pixels using their depth values, then transformed into the world frame. This raw point cloud is subsequently downsampled using a voxel grid filter to mitigate sensor noise and redundancy. The filtered points are used to construct the initial 3D Gaussian map, where each point seeds a Gaussian primitive. The mean of each Gaussian is set to the point’s 3D coordinates, and an anisotropic covariance matrix is derived from the spatial distribution of neighboring points.

**Generative Prior Model:**
To enable real-time SLAM, the authors formulate a unified probabilistic framework that jointly performs pose tracking and dense mapping. The camera pose ($T_t$) is modeled as a latent variable, allowing the system to capture uncertainty in both scene geometry and camera motion. The generative model defines the spatial position ($s_n$) of each 3D point, obtained from observed RGB-D data, as being conditioned on the camera pose ($T_t$) and its Gaussian component ($z_n=k$). Specifically, the spatial term for a Gaussian mixture model is reformulated as $p(s_n | z_n=k, T_t) = \mathcal{N}(T_t \odot \mu_{k,s}, R_t \Sigma_{k,s} R_t^\top)$, where $T_t \odot \mu$ denotes the frame transformation $R_t \mu + t_t$. This formulation explicitly links the spatial distribution of the Gaussian ellipsoids to the current camera pose, thereby integrating mapping and tracking. The camera pose $T_t$ itself is modeled as a multivariate Normal (MVN) distribution $p(T_t) \sim \mathcal{N}(\xi|\mu_{\xi,t}, \Sigma_{\xi,t})$ in the $se(3)$ Lie algebra space, with $T_t$ obtained via the exponential map.

**Variational Posterior and Inference:**
By treating the camera pose as a latent variable, its distribution is included in the variational posterior, allowing joint inference of pose and map parameters. A mean-field approximation is employed for tractability, factorizing the joint posterior $q(z, \mu_s, \Sigma_s, \mu_c, \Sigma_c, \pi, T_t)$ into independent factors. The pose factor $q(T_t)$ is parameterized on the Lie group as $\xi_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\xi,t}, \Sigma_{\xi,t})$. Variational inference minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the approximate posterior $q(\cdot)$ and the true posterior $p(\cdot | s, c)$, which is equivalent to maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO). This approach optimizes the variational parameters that define each latent variable.

**Closed-Form Updates:**
The system leverages Coordinate Ascent Variational Inference (CAVI) and conjugate priors to derive closed-form update rules. Unlike previous VBGS methods that assume known poses, VBGS-SLAM promotes the camera pose to a latent variable. Pose uncertainty is propagated into the map updates by taking expectations over the pose distribution $q(T_t)$ during the point assignment update.
The soft assignment $\gamma_{nk}$ for a point $n$ to component $k$ is now explicitly coupled with the probabilistic estimate of the camera pose through an expected log-likelihood term for the spatial position. The sufficient statistics for the spatial posterior $T(s_n)$ become dependent on the pose posterior.
To derive a closed-form solution for the approximate pose posterior $q(T_t)$, the non-linear relationship between pose $T_t$ and the Gaussian map is addressed by linearizing the spatial likelihood. This linearization is performed around the current pose estimate using a first-order Taylor expansion on the tangent space. This allows the derivation of closed-form update rules for both the mean ($\mu_{\xi,t+1}$) and covariance ($\Sigma_{\xi,t+1}$) of the camera pose posterior. Crucially, these updates for both the map and the pose are computed concurrently in a single pass, which is intended to reduce computational time compared to differential rendering and separate pose optimization, thus enabling real-time SLAM.

**Keyframe Management:**
To maintain a compact and high-resolution map, VBGS-SLAM incorporates two strategies:
1. **Keyframe Selection:** A dual-criteria strategy, inspired by MonoGS, is used. It combines Gaussian co-visibility, triggering a new keyframe only when a sufficient number of previously unobserved Gaussians are seen, with a temporal constraint that enforces a maximum interval between keyframes. Selected keyframes are stored in a fixed-size buffer and optimized using a sliding-window variational inference scheme. This scheme jointly optimizes the approximate posterior distributions of keyframe poses and associated Gaussian map parameters, while marginalizing older keyframes. When a new keyframe is selected, a new set of Gaussian components is generated from the current RGB-D image pair, but an additional filter is applied to prevent redundant Gaussian insertion in already well-represented regions.
2. **Adaptive Gaussian Management:** This mechanism refines the Gaussian map by addressing insufficient coverage and excessive redundancy. In regions with sparse Gaussian coverage, new Gaussians are introduced based on a soft Manhattan distance-based analysis derived from the current RGB-D image pair. For redundancy, components with unchanged prior $\pi$ are re-spawned, ensuring the Gaussian set remains representative and captures fine-grained detail without excessive cloning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VBGS-SLAM involved comparisons with five state-of-the-art dense visual SLAM approaches: NICE-SLAM, Vox-Fusion, Point-SLAM, SplaTAM, and MonoGS. The experiments were conducted on diverse datasets, including the synthetic Replica Dataset, and the real-world TUM-RGBD and AR-TABLE datasets, using an Intel Core i9-12900K processor and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. Performance was assessed using Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) for localization, and PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FPS for 3D reconstruction quality and efficiency.

**Localization Performance:**
- **Replica Dataset (Synthetic):** VBGS-SLAM achieved an average ATE of 0.33 cm, outperforming SplaTAM (0.36 cm) by 8.3% and Point-SLAM (0.53 cm) by 37.7%. It delivered competitive or superior results across all individual sequences.
- **TUM-RGBD Dataset (Real-world):** The method recorded an average ATE of 7.69 cm, which was second to SplaTAM's 5.70 cm, but still surpassed other baseline methods. The largest deviation was noted on the `fr1/room` sequence, attributed to tracking instability caused by a moving pedestrian.
- **AR-TABLE Dataset (Real-world, Long Sequences):** VBGS-SLAM achieved the best average ATE of 5.94 cm, outperforming SplaTAM (7.75 cm) and MonoGS. The method demonstrated stability across all sequences, in contrast to SplaTAM, which experienced failures in some runs due to tracking jumps, and MonoGS, which exhibited tracking failures with long sequences. This indicated improved robustness for long-duration tasks.

**3D Reconstruction Performance:**
- **Replica Dataset:** VBGS-SLAM achieved a PSNR of 37.94 dB, SSIM of 0.95, and LPIPS of 0.097, which were competitive with leading rendering methods such as MonoGS, and surpassed earlier systems like NICE-SLAM. The rendering speed was 0.465 FPS, slightly exceeding MonoGS (0.445 FPS) and significantly faster than SplaTAM (0.115 FPS).
- **TUM-RGBD Dataset:** The method yielded the highest PSNR of 23.46 dB, the second-best LPIPS of 0.31, and a second-best SSIM of 0.74. The renderer achieved 1.89 FPS, outperforming all methods except Vox-Fusion.
- **AR-TABLE Dataset:** VBGS-SLAM showed competitive performance with MonoGS (37.79 dB PSNR, 0.077 LPIPS) with a PSNR of 35.24 dB and LPIPS of 0.079, while substantially outperforming SplaTAM (18.15 dB PSNR, 0.348 LPIPS). It operated at 1.374 FPS, approximately twice as fast as MonoGS and five times faster than SplaTAM, with the lowest GPU usage.
- **Qualitative Comparison:** Renderings from VBGS-SLAM exhibited fewer artifacts at depth edges, higher texture contrast, and improved perceptual coherence compared to MonoGS (which showed halos, floaters, and texture stretching) and SplaTAM (which tended to oversmooth, inflate splats, and introduce blotchy artifacts).

**Ablation Study on IMU Propagation:**
An ablation study on the AR-TABLE dataset investigated the effect of integrating IMU data.
- **Tracking Accuracy:** The average ATE improved modestly from 5.94 cm (without IMU) to 5.71 cm (with IMU), a gain of 0.23 cm.
- **Rendering Quality:** Minor differences were observed, with the IMU-enabled variant achieving a PSNR of 36.75 dB (vs. 35.24 dB without IMU) and LPIPS of 0.075 (vs. 0.079), while SSIM remained identical.
- **Efficiency:** Training throughput and GPU utilization remained largely unchanged.
The study indicated that the RGB-D variational pipeline of VBGS-SLAM provides stable and accurate localization and reconstruction independently, with IMU propagation offering a slight enhancement in accuracy and perceptual quality without significant additional computational cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of VBGS-SLAM represents an advancement in real-time dense Simultaneous Localization and Mapping by integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting with Variational Bayesian Inference. This framework addresses several limitations present in existing 3DGS SLAM systems and neural implicit representations.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Principled Uncertainty Modeling:** By formulating SLAM as a variational inference problem, VBGS-SLAM explicitly models and propagates uncertainty in both camera poses and 3D scene parameters. This contrasts with deterministic, gradient-based optimization methods that typically lack such a mechanism, potentially leading to increased robustness in complex or challenging environments.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Accuracy:** The uncertainty-aware nature of VBGS-SLAM, coupled with its tighter probabilistic coupling between pose and map, contributes to mitigating drift and improving stability. This is demonstrated by superior or competitive localization accuracy across various datasets, particularly in long sequences where other systems may exhibit tracking failures or increased error.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The derivation of closed-form update rules for both map parameters and camera poses, leveraging conjugate properties of multivariate Gaussians, allows for efficient updates without the computational overhead of differential rendering or separate gradient-based optimization common in other 3DGS pipelines. This efficiency is critical for real-time SLAM applications.
- **High-Fidelity Reconstruction:** The method maintains the rendering quality benefits of 3DGS, producing high-fidelity novel view synthesis with competitive PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics. Qualitative results also suggest improved handling of texture, edges, and fewer artifacts compared to baseline methods.
- **Adaptive Map Management:** The keyframe selection and adaptive Gaussian management strategies contribute to maintaining a compact yet accurate 3D map, avoiding excessive redundancy and addressing insufficient scene coverage, which is crucial for scalability in large-scale environments.

**Potential Impact:**
The proposed VBGS-SLAM framework has the potential to influence several areas:
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** The enhanced robustness and accuracy in localization, especially for long sequences, can improve the reliability of robotic navigation, manipulation, and interaction in diverse environments.
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** The combination of real-time pose tracking, high-fidelity 3D reconstruction, and efficient rendering capabilities could support more immersive and stable AR/VR experiences, particularly for live scene capture and interactive applications.
- **3D Reconstruction and Scene Understanding:** The method provides a robust and efficient tool for dense 3D scene modeling, which could be valuable for applications requiring accurate and dynamic representations of environments, such as digital twins or urban mapping.
- **Further Research in Probabilistic SLAM:** VBGS-SLAM offers a foundation for future research in probabilistic SLAM, particularly in extending the variational Bayesian framework to address more complex scenarios such as dynamic scenes with motion priors, or multi-sensor integration (e.g., combining visual data with LiDAR or event cameras) to further enhance robustness and accuracy.
