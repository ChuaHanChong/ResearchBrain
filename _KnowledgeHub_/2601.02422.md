# Watch Wider and Think Deeper: Collaborative Cross-modal Chain-of-Thought for Complex Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and Fujian Normal University introduced CoCoT, a framework that enhances Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for complex visual reasoning through dynamic multi-region grounding and relation-aware reasoning. The approach achieved an average accuracy gain of 15.4% for LLaVA-1.5 and 4.0% for Qwen2-VL across diverse visual question answering benchmarks, supported by a new high-quality dataset, CoCoT-70K.

## Problem

- Existing multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods often rely on single, coarse-grained image regions, leading to diluted or missed critical visual information.
- Previous approaches suffer from semantic fragmentation, where successive reasoning steps lack coherent and logical connections, resulting in disjointed thought processes.
- A lack of high-quality, large-scale datasets specifically designed for complex, multi-region visual reasoning with structured reasoning chains.

## Method

- CoCoT developed a ==Dynamic Multi-Region Grounding mechanism== that collaboratively uses MLLMs (Qwen2-VL) and OCR (PaddleOCR) to identify and correct multiple precise bounding boxes for relevant visual elements.
- A ==Relation-Aware Reasoning module== was implemented, enabling MLLMs to iteratively select the most relevant regions and infer their relationships (parallel or sequential) to construct a coherent reasoning chain.
- The ==CoCoT-70K dataset== was constructed with 74,691 samples from six benchmarks, featuring multi-region annotations and structured reasoning chains specifically for complex visual questions, and utilized in a two-stage progressive training paradigm.

## Results

- CoCoT enhanced LLaVA-1.5's performance with an average accuracy gain of **15.4%** and Qwen2-VL's performance by **4.0%** across six diverse visual reasoning benchmarks compared to direct inference.
- The CoCoT-70K dataset, comprising **74,691 samples** with multi-region annotations and structured reasoning chains, demonstrated its efficacy by enabling a fine-tuned LLaVA-1.5 model (on **20%** of the dataset) to achieve comparable performance to a VisCoT model trained on **363,000** samples.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the Relation-Aware Reasoning module; removing it or integrating its function into the grounding stage severely degraded performance, particularly for stronger MLLMs like Qwen2-VL (up to **-22.4%** accuracy).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling dynamic multi-region grounding from relation-aware reasoning is crucial for robust performance, as attempting to generate both simultaneously significantly degrades model accuracy.
> - Weaker MLLMs (like LLaVA-1.5) benefit substantially from explicit, structured reasoning chains, especially when those chains are generated by more capable models (like Qwen2-VL).
> - High-quality, meticulously annotated datasets like CoCoT-70K can efficiently impart advanced reasoning capabilities, rivaling the performance of models trained on much larger, less structured datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned at the forefront of advancing reasoning capabilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically within the context of the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm. The CoT approach, initially popularized for Large Language Models (LLMs) by Wei et al. (2022), enables models to generate intermediate reasoning steps, thereby improving their ability to tackle complex problems. Multi-modal CoT extends this concept to MLLMs, allowing them to bridge visual perception with high-level linguistic reasoning, as highlighted by Shao et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2024b). This has become a cornerstone technique for MLLMs across diverse applications like visual question answering, document analysis, and video reasoning.

However, the authors identify critical limitations in existing multi-modal CoT methods, such as Visual CoT (Shao et al. 2024) and SPHINX (Lin et al. 2024). These prior works often suffer from two key issues: (1) an over-reliance on single, coarse-grained image regions for generating reasoning steps, which can either miss crucial details or include irrelevant context; and (2) semantic fragmentation between successive reasoning steps, leading to disjointed or illogical thought processes.

The work by Lu et al. directly addresses these shortcomings by proposing a more sophisticated and human-like approach to visual reasoning. Humans naturally employ "collaborative perception," dynamically shifting attention across multiple relevant regions, binding them into semantic concepts, and inferring intricate relationships to form a holistic understanding. CoCoT (Collaborative Cross-modal Thought) aims to imbue MLLMs with similar capabilities, thereby pushing the field towards more robust, interpretable, and context-aware multi-modal reasoning. This aligns with a broader trend in AI research to move beyond superficial pattern recognition towards deeper cognitive processes and more transparent, step-by-step problem-solving. Furthermore, the creation of a new, high-quality dataset, CoCoT-70K, complements this methodological advancement by providing a much-needed resource for training and evaluating MLLMs on truly complex visual reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the complex visual reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by overcoming inherent limitations in existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methodologies. The authors are motivated by two critical problems observed in current cross-modal CoT approaches:

1. **Over-reliance on single coarse-grained image regions**: Existing methods often delineate a single, large bounding box, which, while indicating general attention, frequently encompasses too much irrelevant visual context or fails to capture fine-grained details necessary for complex queries. This "dilutes critical information and harms performance," making it difficult for models to pinpoint specific visual cues.
2. **Semantic fragmentation between successive reasoning steps**: When models generate reasoning steps based on isolated visual cues, they often fail to establish coherent and logical connections between these steps. This results in a fragmented "chain of thought" that lacks the natural flow and relational understanding characteristic of human cognition.

To address these issues, the authors propose the CoCoT (Collaborative Cross-modal Thought) framework with the following key objectives:
- To develop a **Dynamic Multi-Region Grounding** mechanism that can adaptively detect *multiple, precise image regions* most relevant to a given question, thus moving beyond the single, coarse-grained region limitation.
- To implement a **Relation-Aware Reasoning** process that enables *multi-region collaboration* by iteratively aligning visual cues and inferring relationships (parallel or sequential) to form a coherent and logical chain of thought, thereby resolving semantic fragmentation.
- To construct a **high-quality, large-scale dataset, CoCoT-70K**, specifically designed to support and evaluate this collaborative and relation-aware reasoning paradigm, complete with multi-region annotations and structured reasoning chains for complex visual questions.

Ultimately, the motivation is to bridge the gap between current MLLM reasoning and human cognitive processes, where understanding involves dynamically shifting attention, integrating information from various sources, and inferring complex relationships to build a holistic comprehension.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CoCoT (Collaborative Cross-modal Thought) framework is built upon two core innovations designed to tackle the limitations of prior multi-modal CoT methods. These innovations are supported by a novel dataset construction pipeline and a two-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Multi-Region Grounding

This component addresses the problem of over-reliance on single, coarse-grained image regions. It employs a collaborative approach involving a powerful Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM), Qwen2-VL (Wang et al., 2024b), and an Optical Character Recognition (OCR) system, PaddleOCR (Du et al., 2020):
1. **Initial Region Generation**: Qwen2-VL is prompted to generate multiple bounding boxes and corresponding textual descriptions for elements relevant to the input question. While Qwen2-VL excels at producing semantically accurate descriptions, its initial bounding box localizations may be imprecise.
2. **Bounding Box Correction**: To rectify the inaccuracies in localization, PaddleOCR extracts content from the generated bounding boxes. This extracted content is then compared with Qwen2-VL’s descriptions. If a mismatch occurs, the system actively searches for a better-fitting region whose OCR-extracted content closely aligns with the description.
3. **Fallback Mechanism**: In cases where Qwen2-VL fails to provide usable regions, PaddleOCR steps in to provide bounding boxes for keywords directly extracted from the question.
This dynamic collaboration leverages Qwen2-VL’s robust comprehension abilities with PaddleOCR’s precision in localization, yielding high-quality, grounded visual representations where textual semantics are accurately aligned with spatial features.

#### 3.2. Relation-Aware Reasoning

This component is designed to overcome semantic fragmentation by fostering coherent, multi-region collaboration in the reasoning process, mimicking human cognitive habits:
1. **Question Parsing and Entry Points**: The process begins by parsing the question to identify key information. Qwen2-VL then determines which of the bounding boxes generated in the grounding stage serves as the most appropriate starting point (entry point) for the reasoning chain.
2. **Iterative Region Selection and Relationship Inference**: The selected region, along with the unselected candidate regions, is fed back into Qwen2-VL. The MLLM is then prompted to select the *next most relevant region* and, crucially, to determine the *relationship* between this new region and the evolving reasoning chain.
3. **Relationship Categorization**: Relationships are categorized into two types:
 - **Parallel**: For independent but related pieces of information (e.g., A→B, C→D).
 - **Sequential**: For steps that logically follow one another (e.g., A→B→C).
4. **Chain Construction**: This iterative process continues until the current reasoning chain sufficiently answers the question or all relevant regions have been explored. The system dynamically updates the chain and candidate regions in each step.
This structured approach ensures that reasoning steps are not isolated but are built upon logical connections, forming a coherent and "relation-aware" chain of thought.

#### 3.3. CoCoT-70K Dataset Construction

A new high-quality dataset, CoCoT-70K, is constructed using the Dynamic Multi-Region Grounding and Relation-Aware Reasoning pipeline. This dataset comprises 74,691 samples and integrates six authoritative benchmarks across four critical domains:
- **Basic Visual Reasoning**: Fundamental "seeing" and description (e.g., VQAv2, Visual7W, GQA).
- **Document & Chart Analysis**: Structural understanding and information extraction (e.g., InfographicsVQA, DocVQA).
- **Commonsense & Logic Reasoning**: Deep visual commonsense and contextual inference (e.g., GQA, Visual7W, VQAv2).
- **Scene Text Understanding**: Robust comprehension of embedded text (e.g., TextVQA).
To ensure complexity, the dataset creation involved filtering questions with high keyword counts and those requiring multi-step or parallel-answer reasoning. Each sample is augmented with multi-region annotations (bounding boxes), detailed region descriptions, and structured reasoning chains.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy and Implementation

The CoCoT framework employs a **two-stage progressive training paradigm** for MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5:
1. **Stage 1: Reasoning Chain Generation**: The model is fine-tuned to generate detailed reasoning chains. Inputs include the original image, cropped regions from bounding boxes, the question, and region descriptions. This stage uses a small batch size and a specific crop-and-pad strategy for image preprocessing.
2. **Stage 2: Answer Synthesis**: After reasoning chains are generated using the Stage 1 model, the model is further fine-tuned to produce the final answer, taking the original image, question, and the *generated reasoning chain* as input.
A small subset (20%, ~14k samples) of the CoCoT-70K dataset is used for fine-tuning. The training utilizes Adam optimizer, cosine learning rate scheduler, mixed-precision training (FP16), and DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 for memory efficiency on 4 NVIDIA V100 GPUs. The base model uses CLIP ViT-L14336 as the vision encoder and Vicuna7Bv1.5 as the LLM, following LLaVA's practice.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate CoCoT's effectiveness in enhancing complex visual reasoning across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. CoCoT-70K Dataset

The developed CoCoT-70K dataset, comprising 74,691 high-quality samples, is a significant contribution. It spans six established visual question answering benchmarks (InfographicsVQA, DocVQA, TextVQA, Visual-7W, GQA, VQA-v2) and covers four distinct reasoning domains. By filtering for questions with multiple keywords and those requiring multi-step or parallel answers, the dataset is specifically tailored for challenging, complex reasoning scenarios, making it an ideal resource for developing advanced MLLMs.

#### 4.2. Inference Performance

The study evaluated CoCoT's performance in an inference-only setting, comparing it against direct inference and a VisCoT-style approach on two MLLM baselines: LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2-VL.
- **LLaVA-1.5**: Direct inference from LLaVA-1.5 performed poorly. The CoCoT chain-based reasoning, however, yielded substantial improvements, achieving an average accuracy gain of **15.4%** across all tasks. This highlights CoCoT's ability to guide weaker MLLMs through complex reasoning by providing structured thought processes. LLaVA-1.5 struggled to utilize the VisCoT method effectively without prior training, often failing to generate valid bounding boxes.
- **Qwen2-VL**: For the more powerful Qwen2-VL model, CoCoT generally delivered the strongest overall performance, with an average accuracy improvement of **4.0%**. While VisCoT sometimes slightly outperformed CoCoT on specific tasks (e.g., InfoVQA), the overall trend indicated CoCoT's benefit. The authors hypothesize that for extremely strong, generalist models like Qwen2-VL, overly detailed reasoning chains might occasionally introduce constraints, though the overall gain is still positive.

#### 4.3. Training Performance

The research also explored the impact of fine-tuning LLaVA-1.5 using a portion of the CoCoT-70K dataset.
- Fine-tuning LLaVA-1.5 on just **14,000 samples** (20% of CoCoT-70K) achieved performance comparable to VisCoT, which was trained on a much larger **363,000 samples** based on LLaVA-1.5. This underscores the high quality and efficiency of the CoCoT-70K dataset in imparting reasoning capabilities.
- However, the fine-tuned CoCoT model showed some underperformance in tasks specifically requiring *multi-box reasoning* compared to the fully fine-tuned VisCoT. This suggests that while CoCoT-70K is effective, achieving peak performance on the most intricate multi-region tasks might still necessitate larger-scale training data or more advanced training strategies.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on Relation-Aware Reasoning (RAR)

A detailed ablation study was conducted to understand the contribution of the Relation-Aware Reasoning (RAR) module:
- **-RAR (without explicit RAR)**: Simply providing region descriptions and bounding boxes without explicit relational reasoning improved LLaVA-1.5's accuracy but severely *degraded* Qwen2-VL's performance by 19.5%. This finding is critical: weaker models can benefit from even basic visual grounding, but stronger models, which implicitly possess more reasoning capacity, are impaired without structured, explicit relational guidance.
- **Replaced RAR (relations generated with grounding)**: Attempting to generate bounding boxes and relational rankings simultaneously during the Dynamic Multi-Region Grounding stage (instead of a separate RAR module) resulted in the worst performance across both models (e.g., -22.4% for Qwen2-VL). This strongly validates the necessity of decoupling grounding from relational reasoning into distinct stages.
- **Qwen RAR (Qwen2-VL generated chains for LLaVA-1.5)**: Using reasoning chains directly generated by Qwen2-VL for LLaVA-1.5 inference yielded the most substantial improvement for LLaVA-1.5 (a 23% accuracy gain). This indicates that Qwen2-VL is adept at generating high-quality reasoning chains that can effectively guide and boost the performance of less capable MLLMs.
- **CoCoT (full method)**: The complete CoCoT framework delivered optimal results for Qwen2-VL itself, achieving a 4% accuracy improvement.

These ablation studies conclusively demonstrate that the proposed two-stage reasoning paradigm, with its dedicated Dynamic Multi-Region Grounding and Relation-Aware Reasoning modules, is essential for robust and effective multi-modal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "CoCoT" framework and the accompanying CoCoT-70K dataset represent a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Human-AI Reasoning Gap**: By explicitly addressing the limitations of single-region focus and fragmented reasoning, CoCoT moves MLLMs closer to human-like cognitive processes in visual reasoning. The dynamic multi-region attention and relation-aware step-by-step thinking enable MLLMs to process visual information in a more holistic, contextual, and interpretable manner. This enhances not just accuracy but also the transparency of MLLM decision-making.

2. **Advancing MLLM Capabilities for Complex Tasks**: The consistent and substantial performance improvements across six diverse benchmarks—especially for challenging questions requiring multi-step or parallel reasoning—demonstrate CoCoT's ability to unlock higher-order visual understanding. This is crucial for real-world applications where visual queries are rarely simple and often demand deep contextual and relational inference (e.g., interpreting complex infographics, analyzing medical images, or understanding intricate social scenes).

3. **A Novel and High-Quality Dataset**: CoCoT-70K is a valuable contribution to the research community. Its multi-domain coverage, rigorous filtering for complexity, and explicit annotations (multi-region bounding boxes, descriptions, structured reasoning chains) provide a much-needed resource. This dataset will facilitate the development and evaluation of future MLLMs, enabling researchers to push the boundaries of complex visual reasoning and allowing for fair comparisons across models.

4. **Insights into Model Design and Training**: The ablation studies offer critical insights into the architectural needs of MLLMs. They highlight that explicit, structured relational reasoning is not merely a beneficial add-on but can be essential, particularly for stronger generalist models like Qwen2-VL, which might otherwise struggle with implicit reasoning over fragmented inputs. The finding that a smaller, high-quality dataset can rival much larger, less structured ones for fine-tuning underscores the importance of data quality and annotation detail.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: CoCoT opens several promising avenues for future work. This includes:
 - Developing vision models with inherently stronger localization capabilities to integrate bounding box generation into end-to-end training.
 - Exploring more sophisticated relationship types beyond sequential and parallel.
 - Investigating how to adaptively adjust the level of reasoning chain detail based on MLLM strength to optimize performance.
 - Applying CoCoT's principles to other multi-modal tasks, such as video understanding, embodied AI, and scientific data analysis.

In summary, CoCoT represents a significant step forward in making MLLMs not just "see" but also "think deeper" and "watch wider," fostering more robust, intelligent, and human-aligned visual reasoning systems.
