# STAR-R1: Spatial TrAnsformation Reasoning by Reinforcing Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> STAR-R1 enhances multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) spatial reasoning by achieving state-of-the-art performance on the Transformation-Driven Visual Reasoning (TVR) task, surpassing models like GPT-4o by 37.9% and Gemini-1.5-Pro by 45.5% in Total Accuracy using a single-stage Reinforcement Learning approach with a dense, fine-grained reward function.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex spatial reasoning, particularly the Transformation-Driven Visual Reasoning (TVR) task which requires identifying and describing object transformations across images, often under varying viewpoints.
- Traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods fail to adequately capture the multi-step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning required for TVR, especially in cross-view scenarios where object correspondence is ambiguous.
- Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) applications for multimodal reasoning often rely on two-stage training paradigms and sparse reward signals, leading to inefficient exploration and slow convergence for multi-component answers like those in TVR.

## Method

- The proposed framework, STAR-R1, employs a novel ==single-stage, pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigm== based on Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- It features a sophisticated, ==dense reward function== comprising a binary format reward and a multi-component accuracy reward. The accuracy reward provides positive feedback for partial correctness (matching object index, attribute, and value) and specific punishments for mistaken predictions and under-predictions.
- The model is trained end-to-end on a cleaned version of the ==TRANCE dataset==, without any supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as a prerequisite, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the base MLLM.

## Results

- STAR-R1 achieves state-of-the-art performance across all eleven TVR evaluation metrics, surpassing GPT-4o by **37.9%** and Gemini-1.5-Pro by **45.5%** in Total Accuracy.
- The pure RL approach of STAR-R1 yields approximately a **13%** gain in Total Accuracy compared to SFT methods under the same training settings.
- STAR-R1 demonstrates robust generalization in challenging Out-of-Domain (cross-view) scenarios, performing full object comparisons in **81%** of OOD cases and showing a substantial performance advantage over SFT-based approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - STAR-R1's pure Reinforcement Learning approach fosters an "anthropomorphic behavior" for systematic object comparison across views, leading to a 23% Total Accuracy advantage over Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in Out-of-Domain (OOD) scenarios.
> - The model's response length exhibits a non-linear dynamic during RL training, initially decreasing for conciseness, then gradually increasing as the model learns to systematically explore all objects for comprehensive analysis.
> - Ablation studies confirm that all components of the dense reward function (object, attribute, under-prediction, and wrong-answer punishments) are essential for efficient exploration and robust performance, significantly outperforming naive GRPO.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have witnessed an explosion of progress in recent years, demonstrating impressive capabilities across a wide spectrum of tasks, including visual question answering, text-to-image generation, and video understanding. Models like the LLaVA series, QwenVL, and InternVL have significantly advanced open-source MLLMs, narrowing the performance gap with commercial giants such as GPT-4o and Gemini. However, despite these advancements, a critical gap persists: MLLMs still largely struggle with complex spatial reasoning. This fundamental aspect of human intelligence, involving the comprehension and manipulation of objects and their relationships in space, remains a significant challenge for current models. Prior research has consistently highlighted this deficiency, underscoring the imperative for targeted frameworks to assess and enhance MLLMs' spatial understanding.

To address this gap, the paper focuses on the challenging task of Transformation-Driven Visual Reasoning (TVR). TVR requires an MLLM to compare two images, identify which objects have undergone transformations (e.g., changes in color, size, shape, material), and precisely describe these transformations, often under varying viewpoints. This task demands multi-step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods often fail to adequately capture, especially in cross-view scenarios where object correspondence becomes ambiguous due to perspective shifts.

In parallel, recent breakthroughs in training LLMs using Reinforcement Learning (RL), exemplified by the DeepSeek-R1-Zero paradigm, have shown remarkable promise in generating coherent CoT reasoning paths. This approach offers a compelling alternative to SFT, particularly for tasks requiring complex, multi-step deduction. However, existing RL applications in multimodal reasoning, such as MM-Eureka, LMM-R1, and Video-R1, typically rely on naive GRPO (Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization) strategies and often employ two-stage training paradigms (SFT followed by RL). These methods frequently suffer from issues like sparse rewards, inefficient exploration, and slow convergence when adapted to tasks with complex, multi-component answers like TVR. The current work aims to overcome these limitations by proposing a novel, single-stage, pure RL training paradigm with a custom, fine-grained reward mechanism specifically designed for the intricacies of TVR.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically targeting the challenging Transformation-Driven Visual Reasoning (TVR) task. The authors aim to close the pronounced gap between MLLMs and human performance in spatial understanding, particularly when dealing with object transformations across varying viewpoints.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations and limitations of existing approaches:
- **Persistent Spatial Reasoning Deficit:** Despite the remarkable advancements of MLLMs in general vision-language tasks, they demonstrably lag behind humans in spatial reasoning. Even powerful commercial models like GPT-4o achieve a mere 23.5% accuracy on the TVR task, highlighting the inherent difficulty and the need for specialized solutions.
- **Inadequacy of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** While SFT can offer some performance improvements, it falls short for TVR. This is because TVR demands intricate, step-by-step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, and merely training on final object and attribute changes fails to impart the necessary robust reasoning pathways, especially in complex cross-view settings.
- **Limitations of Sparse-Reward Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While RL has shown promise in generating CoT for LLMs, direct application of sparse-reward methods (e.g., binary correct/incorrect signals) to TVR leads to inefficient exploration and slow convergence. The multi-component nature of TVR answers (object index, attribute, value) means that partially correct responses rarely receive positive feedback under a binary scheme, hindering learning.
- **Need for Tailored RL:** Existing rule-based RL methods for reasoning often employ two-stage training and lack task-specific customization for dense reward signals. There is a clear need for a single-stage, pure RL paradigm with a fine-grained reward mechanism that can effectively guide the model's exploration and reasoning process for the unique demands of TVR.

In summary, the authors are motivated to develop a novel, reinforcement learning-based framework that can foster precise, anthropomorphic spatial reasoning in MLLMs by overcoming the shortcomings of current training paradigms, especially regarding reward sparsity and generalizability to out-of-domain scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named STAR-R1 (Spatial TrAnsformation Reasoning with R1), is a novel approach that integrates a single-stage Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigm with a finely-tuned, dense reward mechanism specifically designed for the Transformation-Driven Visual Reasoning (TVR) task.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The TVR task involves analyzing an initial image ($I_{\text{initial}}$) and a final image ($I_{\text{final}}$) to identify specific transformation operations performed on objects, based on a given question ($Q$). The model ($\phi$) is required to generate a sequence of transformations, $T = \{t_1, t_2, \dots, t_n\}$, where each atomic transformation $t_i$ is defined as a triplet: $(index_i, attribute_i, value_i)$. This triplet specifies the object by its index, the attribute that was changed (e.g., color, shape, size, material), and the new value of that attribute. The multi-step nature of these transformations necessitates meticulous analysis of fine-grained differences and step-by-step reasoning.

#### 3.2. The Design of Reward Function

The core innovation of STAR-R1 lies in its sophisticated reward function, which provides dense feedback to guide efficient exploration and accurate prediction. It comprises two main components:

- **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** This binary reward ensures structural adherence of the model's output. A reward of 1 is given if the reasoning process is enclosed within `<think></think>` tags and the final answer within `<answer></answer>` tags; otherwise, the reward is 0. This enforces a consistent and parseable output structure.

- **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):** This is the most crucial component, incorporating both dense positive rewards for partial correctness and targeted punishment mechanisms.
 - **Positive Rewards ($R_{pos}$):** For each predicted transformation $t_i = (index_i, attribute_i, value_i)$ compared against ground-truth transformations $\hat{t}_i$:
 - **Highest Reward (5.0):** If all three components ($index_i$, $attribute_i$, and $value_i$) perfectly match a ground truth transformation. This signifies a complete and accurate inference.
 - **Medium Reward (1.5):** If only the $index_i$ and $attribute_i$ match. This acknowledges correct identification of the changed object and attribute, even if the final value is incorrect, promoting the formation of accurate reasoning chains.
 - **Minimal Reward (0.5):** If only the $index_i$ matches. This encourages preliminary object identification, fostering iterative refinement.
 - **Punishment Mechanisms ($R_{pun}$):** These are designed to prevent reward hacking and encourage comprehensive exploration:
 - **Mistaken Prediction Penalty (-1.0):** For each predicted transformation that is inconsistent with the final image state (i.e., incorrect predictions), a penalty is applied. This prevents the model from exhaustively enumerating all possible triplets to maximize positive rewards.
 - **Under-prediction Penalty ($-(\hat{n} - n)$):** If the number of predicted transformations ($n$) is less than the number of ground truth transformations ($\hat{n}$), a penalty proportional to the deficit is applied. This encourages the model to be thorough and "attempt potentially correct answers over overlooking possible ones," promoting active and comprehensive analysis.
 The total accuracy reward is the sum of positive rewards and punishment rewards: $R_{acc} = R_{pos} + R_{pun}$.

#### 3.3. The Learning Algorithm

STAR-R1 employs **Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO)** as its underlying RL algorithm. GRPO is chosen for its efficiency, as it eliminates the need for a separate critic model by estimating the baseline from group scores, thereby significantly improving training efficiency compared to traditional PPO.
The process involves:
1. For a given input ($I_{\text{initial}}$, $I_{\text{final}}$, $Q$), the model generates a group of $G$ responses, $\{T_1, \dots, T_G\}$.
2. The combined reward $R_g = R_{format_g} + R_{acc_g}$ is calculated for each response.
3. The advantage $\hat{A}_g$ for each response is computed by normalizing its reward against the mean ($\mu_G$) and standard deviation ($\sigma_G$) of rewards within the group.
4. The policy model is optimized by maximizing a training objective that combines these advantages with a KL penalty term to constrain divergence from a reference policy.

#### 3.4. Dataset and Implementation

The study utilizes a cleaned and filtered version of the TRANCE dataset. The dataset is split into:
- **Training Set:** 9,000 samples where both initial and final images are from the same central viewpoint (In-Domain).
- **Test Set:** 4,500 samples comprising initial images from the central view, paired with final images from left, center, and right views. This includes both In-Domain (center-to-center) and more challenging Out-of-Domain (OOD, center-to-left/right) scenarios.
The base model for experiments is **Qwen2.5-VL-7B**. The training is conducted in a single-stage pure RL paradigm for 2 epochs on 8x H20 GPUs, without any supervised fine-tuning as a prerequisite.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of STAR-R1 reveals significant advancements in spatial reasoning capabilities for MLLMs, particularly on the challenging TVR task.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

STAR-R1 achieves state-of-the-art performance across all eleven evaluation metrics used for TVR, demonstrating its exceptional ability in visual reasoning tasks.
- **Overall Superiority:** STAR-R1 significantly outperforms the base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and other powerful MLLMs, including larger-scale open-source models like InternVL3 and even closed-source commercial models such as GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro. In terms of Total Accuracy (TAcc), STAR-R1 surpasses GPT-4o by 37.9% and Gemini-1.5-Pro by 45.5%.
- **RL vs. SFT:** The pure RL approach of STAR-R1, without any cold-start SFT data, yields approximately a 13% gain in TAcc compared to SFT methods under the same training settings. This provides strong evidence for RL's efficacy in complex visual reasoning.
- **Attribute Consistency:** Performance is comparable across all four attribute accuracy metrics (color, shape, size, material), indicating robust learning without catastrophic forgetting or attribute dominance.
- **Complexity Impact:** As the number of objects increases in the scene (population-level metrics), model performance declines, highlighting the compounding challenges of object identification, association, and change perception in more complex scenarios.

#### 4.2. Anthropomorphic Behavior: RL vs SFT in Cross-View Reasoning

A key insight from the study is STAR-R1's remarkable superiority in **Out-of-Domain (OOD)** scenarios (cross-view images), where it achieves a substantial 23% performance advantage in TAcc over SFT-based approaches. This generalization capability is attributed to STAR-R1's "anthropomorphic behavior."
- **Systematic Comparison:** Unlike STAR-SFT, which performs "cursory comparisons" (often failing to account for viewpoint changes and misidentifying objects based on position), STAR-R1 systematically compares *all* objects between the initial and final images, including untransformed ones. This meticulous approach allows it to accurately establish object correspondences even when viewpoints shift.
- **Adaptation to Complexity:** Quantitatively, STAR-R1 performs full object comparisons in 81% of OOD cases, compared to 67% in In-Domain (ID) cases. This suggests that the model, much like humans, adapts its verification intensity based on the perceived complexity, performing more exhaustive comparisons when viewpoint changes demand higher confidence in object correspondences.
- **SFT's Limitation:** SFT models, trained to imitate annotations, fail to perceive viewpoint changes and mistakenly assume objects at the same visual position are identical, leading to incorrect object matching and significant performance degradation in OOD settings. The ablation study in Appendix C further supports this, showing that while RL training can slightly improve SFT's OOD performance, pure RL (STAR-R1) still achieves superior results, suggesting that SFT might "lock" the model's reasoning patterns.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics and Response Length

Analysis of the model's response length during training reveals an intriguing non-linear dynamic:
- **Initial Decline:** The response length rapidly decreases as the model moves from verbose, multi-object descriptions (e.g., "The object with index X... changed...") to more concise, single-object reasoning (e.g., "The small brown sphere changed its color..."). This indicates an initial phase of refining language and directness.
- **Gradual Increase and Stabilization:** Following the minimum length, the model's response length gradually increases. This is because the model realizes that focusing on only a few objects might lead to errors or omissions. While maintaining the refined, concise style for individual objects, it systematically explores and adopts a strategy of comparing *all* objects to ensure comprehensive analysis, leading to higher accuracy and a stable, appropriate reasoning length. This specific dynamic highlights the unique learning process for TVR compared to other reasoning tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies robustly validate the design choices of STAR-R1:
- **Reward Design:** Removing any component of the dense reward function (object reward, attribute reward, under-prediction punishment, or wrong-answer punishment) significantly degrades performance, confirming that all components are crucial for efficient exploration and accurate answers. Notably, the under-prediction punishment is vital for preventing model inaction, and the fine-grained punishment for wrong answers prevents "reward hacking" through exhaustive enumeration. Naive GRPO performs poorly, underscoring the necessity of the customized reward system.
- **Training Data Volume:** Model performance consistently improves with increasing training data volume, although the rate of improvement varies, suggesting an initial rapid learning phase followed by diminishing returns.
- **Model Size:** Larger base models (7B vs. 3B) achieve substantially higher performance gains from RL training, indicating that the foundational capacity of the MLLM is a critical factor in determining the upper bound of reasoning capabilities unlocked by the RL process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The STAR-R1 research represents a significant stride in advancing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in the domain of spatial reasoning, a known weakness of current models. Its implications are multifaceted:

- **Validation of the R1-Zero Paradigm for Multimodality:** This work conclusively validates the effectiveness and potential of the R1-Zero paradigm—a single-stage, pure Reinforcement Learning approach without initial supervised fine-tuning—for complex multimodal reasoning tasks. This challenges the conventional multi-stage training pipelines often seen in RL for LLMs, suggesting a more direct and potentially more efficient path to highly capable models.
- **Breakthrough in Spatial Reasoning:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance on the challenging Transformation-Driven Visual Reasoning (TVR) task, especially in cross-view scenarios, STAR-R1 demonstrates a tangible improvement in MLLMs' ability to understand and reason about spatial transformations. This is crucial for applications requiring a deep understanding of object interactions and changes in dynamic environments.
- **Inducing Anthropomorphic Reasoning:** The discovery that STAR-R1's superior performance, particularly in out-of-domain settings, stems from its systematic and comprehensive comparison of all objects across scenes—mirroring human problem-solving strategies—is a profound insight. This suggests that carefully designed RL frameworks can encourage more human-like, robust, and generalizable reasoning patterns in AI models, rather than just superficial pattern matching.
- **Novel Insights into RL Training Dynamics:** The analysis of the non-linear response length dynamics during training offers unique theoretical insights into how MLLMs evolve their reasoning strategies under RL. This understanding can inform future reward function designs and training methodologies, guiding models to adopt more efficient and effective problem-solving approaches.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a robust foundation for future research in several directions:
 - **Beyond Concurrent Transformations:** The current work focuses on transformations at a single timestamp. Extending this to temporally extended sequences of transformations with latent dependencies and memory (e.g., in video understanding) is a natural next step, which would significantly broaden the applicability.
 - **Generalizing Spatial Cognition:** The success of STAR-R1 hints at the potential for designing RL frameworks to unlock other forms of human-like spatial cognition, such as navigation, manipulation, and intricate geometric reasoning.
 - **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to publicly release codes, model weights, and data will empower the broader research community to build upon these findings, accelerate advancements, and foster reproducibility in multimodal AI.

In conclusion, STAR-R1 not only delivers a highly capable model for spatial reasoning but also provides critical mechanistic insights into how RL can foster robust, generalizable, and anthropomorphic reasoning in MLLMs. This work paves the way for a new generation of multimodal AI systems with enhanced cognitive abilities.
