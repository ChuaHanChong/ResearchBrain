# Riemann-1.0: An Embodied World Action Model for Physical AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Riemann-1.0 is a fully causal autoregressive World Action Model that serializes multi-view observations, robot states, and embodiment-specific actions into one causal sequence, predicting each action before the visual latent it causes. Flow-matching heads plus a three-stage pretraining curriculum over 200K+ hours of embodied data make a single model both an executable policy and an action-conditioned visual simulator, reaching 85.0% real-world success.

## Problem

- Existing pretraining recipes are tightly coupled to robot trajectories, so vast weakly supervised egocentric human video cannot be exploited, limiting knowledge transfer across heterogeneous embodiments.
- Joint-denoising, video-first, and decoupled world-action formulations never model observations, states, actions, and world evolution as one causal process, forcing modality coupling or extra inference latency.
- Data sources differ in observation modalities, action spaces, coordinate systems, and control rates, so naively mixing them yields inconsistent supervision and unstable optimization.

## Method

- ==Fully causal action-video factorization==: each step predicts the action from past latents, states, and actions, then generates the next visual latent conditioned on that action, under a structured ==teacher-forcing attention mask==.
- ==Progressive Embodied Pretraining==, a three-stage curriculum: frozen ==latent-action-model== pseudo actions, then trajectory-grounded alignment on handheld-gripper, robot, and 3D-hand data, then robot-only enhancement, raising action loss weight from 0.1 to 0.5 to 0.9.
- Unified embodied data engine converts human video, handheld-gripper, and robot data into common video-state-action trajectories; embodiment-specific projections and action heads share one backbone trained with masked ==flow-matching== velocity losses.

## Results

- Simulation state of the art: **94.3%** average on RoboTwin 2.0, **99.0%** on LIBERO, and **62.6%** on RoboCasa365, beating the strongest baseline's **54.2%** by **8.4 points**.
- Real-world four-task averages of **85.0%** SR and **94.43%** PSR versus **70.0%** and **80.18%** for the best open-source baseline; kitchen organization rises from **35.0%** to **90.0%** SR.
- Held-out tests give **65.0%** compositional and **85.0%** out-of-domain average success over **10 trials** per task, against **45.0%** and **50.0%** for the strongest compared policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ordering actions before their visual consequences lets one model serve as both policy and simulator; the training attention mask must match inference visibility or deployment mismatches appear.
> - Supervision is a continuum rather than labeled versus unlabeled: pseudo actions, then hand poses, then real trajectories, with loss weight annealed toward action fidelity.
> - Cross-embodiment training needs validity masking of padded action channels in both loss and noise injection, otherwise unused dimensions create train-test mismatch.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on Riemann-1.0 operates within the field of Physical AI, which aims to extend artificial intelligence capabilities from digital understanding to perception, reasoning, and action within physical environments. This domain necessitates the development of Embodied Brain Models capable of integrating environmental perception, world dynamics modeling, executable action generation, and long-horizon reasoning.

A significant area of recent development in this landscape is World Action Models (WAMs), which represent an approach to jointly model robot actions and their visual consequences, thereby providing a unified framework for robot policy learning, predictive world modeling, and long-horizon planning. Existing WAMs typically fall into categories such as joint generation (e.g., DreamZero-style methods), video-first prediction (e.g., LingBot-VA), or decoupled action-video modeling (e.g., FastWAM-style methods). These approaches have demonstrated capabilities in various aspects of robot interaction but have shown limitations in simultaneously supporting efficient robot policy learning and causally consistent action-conditioned world simulation within a single model.

A further challenge in scaling embodied AI models stems from the heterogeneity, multimodality, and embodiment-dependence of available embodied experience data. This includes diverse data sources like egocentric human videos, handheld-gripper demonstrations, and varied robot trajectories, each presenting distinct observation modalities, action spaces, and supervision signals. Effectively leveraging these complementary, yet disparate, data sources for large-scale learning and knowledge transfer remains an open problem.

Riemann-1.0 positions itself as an effort to address these challenges by introducing a fully causal autoregressive World Action Model that unifies online robot policy execution and action-conditioned world simulation. It also proposes a progressive embodied pretraining framework designed to integrate heterogeneous data sources, from weakly supervised human interactions to precise robot control, into a shared World Action Modeling objective. This approach aims to bridge the gap between large-scale embodied experience and generalizable robot manipulation capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop Riemann-1.0, a unified embodied intelligence model capable of simultaneously functioning as both an executable robot policy and a multi-embodiment visual world simulator. This objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from identified limitations in the current state of embodied AI research:

1. **Unified Causal Modeling of Robot Interaction**: Existing World Action Model (WAM) formulations—including those based on joint generation, video-first prediction, or decoupled action-video modeling—do not adequately capture observations, robot states, actions, and world evolution within a single, unified causal process. This leads to models that may excel in one aspect (e.g., visual prediction) but struggle with efficient policy learning or causally consistent action-conditioned simulation. Riemann-1.0 aims to overcome this by proposing a "fully causal autoregressive World Action Model" where robot actions and world evolution are explicitly represented as causal state transitions. This formulation is intended to align with the real-world interaction process of robots, enabling a single model to serve dual functions without significant architectural or operational discrepancies.

2. **Effective Leverage of Heterogeneous Embodied Experience**: Embodied experience, critical for developing intelligent agents, is inherently diverse. It encompasses egocentric human videos (rich in manipulation skills and long-horizon tasks but weakly supervised), handheld-gripper demonstrations (bridging human and robot action spaces), and robot trajectories (providing precise, embodiment-specific control). Current pretraining strategies often struggle to effectively integrate these heterogeneous data sources, frequently relying heavily on robot trajectories, which limits the transfer of knowledge from large-scale, weakly supervised human data. The motivation for Riemann-1.0 is to develop a robust methodology to progressively unify learning from these complementary data sources, enabling continuous capability transfer from broad interaction knowledge to executable robot manipulation. This led to the development of a "progressive embodied pretraining framework."

3. **Scaling Embodied Learning Beyond Robot Trajectories**: The development of Large Language Models has demonstrated the power of scaling textual knowledge. Similarly, the authors posit that Embodied Brain Models should continuously evolve by scaling embodied experience. However, simply collecting more robot trajectories is often insufficient due to cost, diversity limitations, and the inherent differences across robot embodiments. The motivation is to create a data infrastructure and training paradigm that can process and integrate a vast corpus of embodied experience, including diverse, weakly supervised data, thereby allowing the model to improve by scaling the breadth of embodied interaction rather than being restricted to directly executable robot data alone.

In summary, the key objectives are to introduce a novel WAM architecture and a scalable training framework that can effectively learn from and generalize across a wide spectrum of embodied data, thereby advancing generalizable robot manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in Riemann-1.0 involves three primary components: a unified embodied data infrastructure, a fully causal autoregressive World Action Model (WAM) architecture, and a progressive embodied pretraining framework.

#### 3.1. Data Infrastructure

The foundation of Riemann-1.0 is a unified embodied data infrastructure designed to process and integrate diverse embodied experiences.
- **Multi-Source Embodied Experience**: The corpus comprises over 230,000 hours of data, consisting of approximately 200,000 hours of egocentric human videos, 12,000 hours of handheld-gripper and wearable demonstrations, and 20,000 hours of heterogeneous robot trajectories. These sources are considered complementary: human videos provide scalable interaction knowledge, handheld-gripper demonstrations bridge the human-robot embodiment gap, and robot trajectories ground embodiment-specific executable control.
- **Unified Embodied Data Engine**: This engine converts heterogeneous raw data into a standardized action-level video-state-action representation, including language instructions, embodiment identities, visual observations, states, actions, and semantic metadata.
 - **Egocentric Human Videos**: Undergo visual normalization, followed by a hierarchical VLM-based annotation pipeline to decompose recordings into task-level and action-level clips with semantic labels. To bridge visual interactions with actions, continuous 3D hand trajectories are reconstructed using hand detection, tracking, MANO-based 3D hand reconstruction, and VGGT-Ω for camera pose estimation. Temporal refinement is applied to reduce jitter. Quality filtering based on semantic consistency, interaction completeness, hand motion, visibility, and camera motion retains high-quality clips.
 - **Handheld-Gripper and Robot Trajectories**: These data sources, already containing structured control supervision, are processed for temporal alignment, action normalization, and trajectory quality filtering. Gripper-state transitions are used for action boundary refinement, while filters address camera shake, stationary end-effector periods, and abnormal control.
 - **Standardization**: All data streams are temporally aligned, normalized, and organized under embodiment-specific canonical definitions. Validity masks accommodate variable-dimensional actions across different embodiments within a shared data format.
- **Progressive Supervision and Data Balancing**: The processed corpus is treated as a continuum of supervision. Weak supervision from pseudo actions (derived from a frozen Latent Action Model, LAM) on human videos precedes real-action supervision from 3D hand-annotated human videos, handheld-gripper demonstrations, and robot trajectories. High-quality robot trajectories provide the final, specialized executable control supervision. A semantic-aware sampling strategy balances data across various dimensions (scenes, tasks, skills, objects, robot embodiments) to counteract long-tail distributions.

#### 3.2. Riemann-1.0 Design

Riemann-1.0 is built upon a fully causal autoregressive World Action Model architecture.
- **Fully Causal Autoregressive Action-Video World Model**: The model factorizes robot interaction as a unified causal autoregressive sequence over multi-view visual observations, robot states, and embodiment-specific actions. The core principle is that at each step, the model predicts the current action from preceding visual observations, robot states, and action histories. This action is then used as a condition to predict the corresponding visual latent. This is expressed as $p(a_{1:T}, z_{1:T} | z_0, s_0, c) = \prod_{t=1}^{T} p(a_t | z_{<t}, s_{<t}, a_{<t}, c) p(z_t | z_{<t}, s_{<t}, a_{\leq t}, c)$. Robot states ($s_t$) are observed from the environment or replayed, rather than generated by the model.
- **Inputs and Conditioning**: Language instructions, embodiment IDs, and multi-view visual observations are primary inputs. Language conditions are encoded by a T5 encoder. Visual observations are packed into a multi-view canvas, resized, encoded into latents by a Wan VAE, and converted into transformer tokens. Actions and robot states are transformed into embodiment-specific canonical representations and normalized.
- **Embodiment-Specific Interfaces**: A shared transformer backbone is used, but input and output interfaces are specialized for each embodiment. Raw action and state vectors are converted to a canonical order, padded to fixed model dimensions, and mapped to category-specific linear projections and prediction heads.
- **Token Sequence and Temporal Alignment**: All modalities are serialized into a single transformer sequence `[context latent, clean latent, noisy latent, state, clean action, noisy action]`. The action stream is temporally denser than the visual latent stream, with action chunks aligned with visual latents.
- **Fully Causal Training Objective**: A structured attention mask is employed to ensure that predictions do not access future information. Clean tokens attend to preceding clean tokens, while noisy target tokens attend to preceding clean tokens and other tokens within the same noisy block but not to future clean tokens.
- **Latent and Action Flow-Matching Losses**: The model uses a flow-matching objective for both a visual-latent head ($\mathcal{L}_z$) and an action head ($\mathcal{L}_a$), predicting velocities of noisy latent and action tokens, respectively. The final objective is a weighted sum: $\mathcal{L} = (1 - \lambda)\mathcal{L}_z + \lambda\mathcal{L}_a$. Loss masking is applied to exclude padded channels and invalid data points.
- **Autoregressive Online Inference**: During inference, the model denoises a Gaussian noisy action tensor, which is executed. The environment then returns new observations, encoded into a latent, and appended to a growing KV cache along with the updated state. This process continues autoregressively, allowing the model to function as a policy (using real observations) or a simulator (generating visual latents).

#### 3.3. Training Recipe

Riemann-1.0's training involves a three-stage progressive pretraining curriculum, followed by post-training:
- **Stage I: LAM-Action Bootstrap ($\lambda=0.1$)**: Trains on large-scale unlabeled human manipulation videos. A frozen Latent Action Model (LAM) provides pseudo-action supervision. This stage primarily initializes the visual dynamics backbone and teaches the correlation between action-like motion tokens and future visual changes.
- **Stage II: Trajectory-Grounded Alignment ($\lambda=0.5$)**: Uses a mixed multi-embodiment corpus (UMI, robot trajectories, human videos with 3D hand annotations). This stage grounds the autoregressive action-video interface in real trajectory supervision, aligning the shared backbone across different camera layouts, action spaces, and robot bodies. Action prediction becomes a more central training signal.
- **Stage III: Robot Policy Enhancement ($\lambda=0.9$)**: Exclusively uses high-quality robot demonstrations with real action-state supervision. This stage refines the model's executable action distribution, focusing primarily on state-conditioned action prediction and trajectory consistency. The latent objective continues to regularize learned dynamics.
- **Post-training**: After pretraining, Riemann-1.0 is fine-tuned on task-specific teleoperation data collected from real robots (e.g., ordered cube stacking, clothes folding). All collected data for various tasks are aggregated to jointly fine-tune a single generalist model. The action loss weight ($\lambda$) is increased to 0.95 during this phase to enhance action execution performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Riemann-1.0 was evaluated across both simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating performance across various metrics.

#### 4.1. Real-World Experiments

Evaluation on the Tianji Marvin dual-arm robot involved four manipulation scenarios: ordered cube stacking, clothes folding, desk organization, and kitchen organization. Each task had 15 teleoperated demonstrations for post-training. Performance was measured by Success Rate (SR) and Progress Success Rate (PSR).

- **Overall Performance**: Riemann-1.0 achieved an average SR of 85.00% and an average PSR of 94.43% across the four tasks. This performance exceeded that of several open-source VLA and WAM baselines, including DreamZero*, $\tau_0$-WM, LingBot-VLA, $\pi_{0.5}$, LingBot-VA, and G0.5.
 - On ordered cube stacking, Riemann-1.0 achieved 85.0% SR and 91.6% PSR, outperforming the next best baseline (G0.5) by 5.0 SR points.
 - For kitchen organization, it obtained 90.0% SR and 98.4% PSR, substantially higher than G0.5's 35.0% SR.
 - In clothes folding, Riemann-1.0 achieved 85.0% SR (tying with G0.5) and 92.5% PSR (the highest).
 - For desk organization, it achieved 80.0% SR (tying with G0.5) and 95.2% PSR (the highest).
- **Compositional Generalization**: The policy was tested on held-out instructions involving seen object categories recombined into unseen task goals.
 - In "Cube-to-bowl/plate placement," Riemann-1.0 succeeded in 8 out of 10 trials (80.0%).
 - With added color-order constraints and multi-step sequencing, it achieved 50.0% SR.
 - Across these compositional tasks, Riemann-1.0 achieved an average SR of 65.0%, outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ (45.0%) and LingBot-VA (15.0%).
- **Out-of-Domain (OOD) Evaluation**: The model was evaluated on tasks with unseen scenes, objects, and task goals without additional training.
 - For placing a Rubik's cube into a storage box, it achieved 100.0% SR.
 - For placing a towel into a basin, it achieved 70.0% SR.
 - Across these OOD tasks, Riemann-1.0 achieved an average SR of 85.0%, surpassing $\pi_{0.5}$ (50.0%) and LingBot-VA (30.0%).
 - The overall average SR across all four held-out tasks (compositional and OOD) was 75.0%.

#### 4.2. Simulation Evaluation

Riemann-1.0 was also assessed on three simulation benchmarks.
- **RoboCasa365**: On the Target-50 split, Riemann-1.0 achieved an average success rate of 62.6%, improving upon the previous best method, ABot-M0.5, by 8.4 percentage points. It demonstrated improvements across Atomic-Seen, Composite-Seen, and Composite-Unseen categories, with notable gains in compositional tasks (11.7 and 10.7 percentage points, respectively).
- **RoboTwin 2.0**: The model achieved an average success rate of 94.3% across 50 bimanual tasks, including both clean and randomized scene settings, positioning it as the top performer among compared baselines.
- **LIBERO**: Across the Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites, Riemann-1.0 reached an overall average success rate of 99.0%. Specifically, it achieved 99.6% on Spatial, 100.0% on Object, 97.6% on Goal, and 98.6% on Long.

#### 4.3. Action-Conditioned Visual Rollout as a Multi-Embodiment Simulator

Qualitative results showed Riemann-1.0's capability to function as an action-conditioned visual simulator. The model generated multi-embodiment rollouts (e.g., humanoid, dual-arm, single-arm, dexterous-hand robots, and simulation benchmarks). These generated videos maintained scene structure and exhibited action-consistent object and robot motion across diverse viewpoints, embodiment geometries, and task types. This suggests the model learned a shared action-conditioned visual dynamics prior that generalizes beyond a single robot body.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Riemann-1.0 presents several points of significance and potential impact within the field of embodied AI:

1. **Unified Model for Policy and Simulation**: Riemann-1.0's fully causal autoregressive World Action Model unifies the functions of an executable robot policy and an action-conditioned visual world simulator within a single architecture. This integration could streamline the development and deployment of robot intelligence systems by reducing the need for separate models for control and environmental prediction. This dual capability facilitates model-based planning, policy evaluation, and future-state imagination, potentially leading to more robust and adaptable robot behaviors.

2. **Effective Integration of Heterogeneous Embodied Data**: The progressive embodied pretraining framework demonstrates a method for integrating and leveraging a large and diverse corpus of embodied experience. By progressively transferring knowledge from weakly supervised egocentric human videos, through handheld-gripper demonstrations, to precise robot trajectories, Riemann-1.0 addresses a fundamental challenge in scaling robot learning. This approach provides a scalable pathway for converting broad interaction knowledge into specialized, executable robot control, which is crucial for advancing generalist robot capabilities.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: The experimental results, particularly in compositional generalization and out-of-domain evaluation on real-world tasks, indicate that Riemann-1.0 develops robust and transferable manipulation skills. Its ability to perform well on tasks involving unseen combinations of objects and instructions, as well as entirely new objects and scenes, suggests that the learned manipulation prior is not merely memorizing training tasks but generalizing to novel scenarios. This capability is essential for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments.

4. **State-of-the-Art Performance Across Benchmarks**: Consistent superior performance on both real-world manipulation tasks and established simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa365, RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO) establishes Riemann-1.0 as a competitive model in the current landscape of embodied AI. These quantitative results validate the effectiveness of the proposed architecture and training methodology.

5. **Advancement of Foundation Models for Physical AI**: By demonstrating how large-scale embodied experience can be effectively transformed into generalizable robot manipulation, Riemann-1.0 contributes to the ongoing effort to develop foundation models for physical AI. Similar to how large language models have transformed natural language processing, this work suggests a pathway for creating foundational robot models that can continuously improve by scaling diverse embodied data, paving the way for more capable and autonomous intelligent agents in the physical world.
