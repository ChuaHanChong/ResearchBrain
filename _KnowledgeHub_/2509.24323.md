# MAS$^2$: Self-Generative, Self-Configuring, Self-Rectifying Multi-Agent Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> MAS² is a recursive paradigm where a multi-agent system builds other multi-agent systems. A trained generator-implementor-rectifier meta-team drafts a workflow template, assigns LLM backbones per role, and repairs configurations online when failures or budget overruns occur. Collaborative Tree Optimization specializes each meta-agent from value-annotated decision trees, yielding reported gains up to 19.6% over prior systems while staying on the cost-performance Pareto frontier.

## Problem

- External search modules such as MCTS, Bayesian optimization and graph networks confine multi-agent design to a fixed space of atomic operators, capping architectural innovation.
- Agent-driven generators trained with SFT, DPO or GRPO still instantiate one system per task and execute it unchanged regardless of success or failure.
- Such generate-once-and-deploy systems collapse on a single disruption like a tool crash, search failure or file loss, lacking capacity to adapt after instantiation.

## Method

- **Generator** agent samples an uninstantiated ==workflow template== of agent roles, communication protocol and tools from the query; **implementor** then maps each role to a concrete ==LLM backbone==.
- **Rectifier** agent monitors execution and fires when cumulative cost exceeds a budget threshold or an explicit failure occurs, rewriting tools, prompts or the whole workflow mid-run.
- **Collaborative Tree Optimization** expands a ==decision tree== of configurations, scores leaves by cost-normalized success reward, propagates path credit, then trains each agent with a value-margin-scaled ==preference loss==.

## Results

- Across seven benchmarks it leads all 13 baselines: **89.3%** HotpotQA, **97.0%** HumanEval, **71.3%** MATH, **19.7%** BrowseComp+, beating self-consistency on Bamboogle by **12.8 points** at **25x** lower cost.
- Adding unseen backbones at inference without retraining lifts MATH from **71.3%** to **90.6%** and Bamboogle to **84.2%**, against **32.8%** for vanilla Qwen3-Coder alone.
- Ablations confirm each role: removing the trained generator drops MBPP from **85.2%** to **79.0%**, and removing the rectifier drops MATH from **71.3%** to **64.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Runtime rectification, not only better upfront design, is what lets automatically generated agent systems survive tool crashes and other unpredictable environment failures.
> - Splitting architecture, resource allocation and repair into separately trained agents lets each specialize; ablations show none of the three roles is redundant.
> - Heterogeneous backbone assignment, cheap models for throughput and strong models for judgment, buys accuracy and cost efficiency together rather than trading one against the other.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM)-powered multi-agent systems (MAS) has experienced explosive growth in recent years, driven by the ambition to harness collective intelligence akin to human societies. This research paper positions itself at the forefront of this evolution, addressing a critical limitation in current automated MAS design.

Historically, MAS design has progressed through distinct stages:
1. **Manually Configured Systems:** Early frameworks like AutoGen and MetaGPT relied heavily on human-crafted specifications for agent roles, prompts, tools, and communication protocols. While powerful, these systems lacked flexibility and required significant engineering effort for each new task.
2. **Partially Automated Systems:** Subsequent efforts introduced automation to specific aspects, such as inter-agent communication topology (e.g., GPTSwarm, G-Designer) or LLM routing (e.g., MasRouter). These marked a step towards reducing manual overhead but still left significant portions of the system design to human expertise.
3. **Fully Automated "Generate-Once-and-Deploy" Systems:** More recently, the community has seen the emergence of fully automated MAS, where configurations are synthesized end-to-end and adapted online to domain and instance demands (ee.g., ADAS, MaAS, AFlow). These systems often leverage external modules like Bayesian optimization, Monte Carlo tree search, or graph neural networks, or enable agents themselves to construct MAS through techniques like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL).

Despite the advancements in full automation, a fundamental limitation persists: most current systems adhere to a "generate-once-and-deploy" paradigm. This means a system is instantiated once per task and executed without further modification, regardless of success or failure. This rigidity renders them brittle and ill-equipped for the inherent dynamism, uncertainty, and error-prone nature of real-world environments (e.g., API failures, unexpected data formats, tool crashes). This brittleness is a major bottleneck preventing MAS from reaching their full potential in complex, long-horizon tasks.

MAS^2 introduces a **third, novel paradigm** that transcends this "generate-once-and-deploy" limitation. It proposes a system predicated on **recursive self-generation**, where a multi-agent system itself autonomously architects, configures, and, crucially, *rectifies* other multi-agent systems. This represents a significant conceptual leap towards truly adaptive and robust agentic AI. By internalizing the entire lifecycle of MAS creation and adaptation within a meta-MAS, MAS^2 moves beyond external orchestrators or static agent-driven generation. It aligns with the broader push in AI research towards self-improving, self-healing, and increasingly autonomous intelligent systems, offering a path towards more resilient and capable AI agents that can operate effectively in unpredictable real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind MAS^2 stems from the observed shortcomings of existing automated multi-agent systems, particularly their inability to adapt to the inherent uncertainties and failures in real-world applications.

**Motivation:**
1. **Brittleness of "Generate-Once-and-Deploy" Paradigm:** Current automated MAS, whether generated by external modules (e.g., GNNs, search algorithms) or by a single LLM agent (e.g., SFT-based, DPO-based), suffer from a fundamental flaw: they are configured once and then deployed without mechanisms for dynamic adaptation or self-correction during execution.
2. **Vulnerability to Real-World Dynamism:** Real-world environments are inherently dynamic, uncertain, and prone to errors. Factors like network failures, tool crashes, API rate limits, or unexpected data formats can easily disrupt a static MAS. Existing systems lack the capacity to detect these disruptions and adapt beyond their initial instantiation, leading to system collapse or suboptimal performance.
3. **Limited Architectural Innovation:** While some automated systems can configure agent parameters or communication topologies, their generative capacity is often confined to predefined search spaces of atomic operators (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, Reflexion). This limits their ability to innovate and truly customize MAS architectures for diverse problems.
4. **Lack of Self-Rectification:** The absence of a mechanism for real-time monitoring and correction makes current MAS fragile, hindering their application in complex, long-horizon tasks that require sustained robustness.

**Key Objectives:**
To address these motivations, MAS^2 sets out to achieve the following objectives:
1. **Introduce a Novel Paradigm of Recursive Self-Generation:** The primary objective is to formulate and implement MAS^2, a framework where a multi-agent system (meta-MAS) autonomously constructs *other* multi-agent systems, moving beyond external module-based or single-agent-based generation.
2. **Develop a Self-Configuring Architecture:** Design a meta-MAS that can dynamically architect bespoke multi-agent systems tailored to diverse problems and real-time task demands, encompassing roles, communication, tools, and LLM backbone assignments.
3. **Enable Real-Time Self-Rectification:** Integrate a mechanism within the meta-MAS that actively monitors the execution state of a generated MAS and issues timely corrections and modifications to ensure adaptiveness to dynamic conditions and to recover from failures.
4. **Achieve Superior Performance and Robustness:** Develop a system that consistently outperforms state-of-the-art MAS across a range of complex tasks, demonstrating enhanced competence, adaptability, and resilience.
5. **Ensure Cost-Effectiveness and Generalization:** Design the system to operate efficiently in terms of computational cost and to exhibit strong generalization capabilities, particularly in leveraging previously unseen LLM backbones without requiring retraining.
6. **Devise an Efficient Training Methodology:** Propose a novel offline reinforcement learning strategy, Collaborative Tree Optimization (CTO), to efficiently train and specialize the distinct meta-generative functions of the meta-agents.

In essence, MAS^2 aims to create a truly autonomous and self-adaptive multi-agent system pipeline, providing a crucial step towards more intelligent and reliable AI agents capable of navigating the complexities of the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MAS^2 introduces a sophisticated methodology centered around a meta-multi-agent system (meta-MAS) comprising three specialized meta-agents: a Generator, an Implementor, and a Rectifier. This tri-agent architecture enables recursive self-generation, dynamic configuration, and real-time self-rectification of target multi-agent systems (MAS).

#### 3.1. Meta-Scheduling of Multi-Agent Systems

The core of MAS^2's inference-time operation involves these three meta-agents working in sequence and adaptively:

- **Formalizing a Target MAS (M):** A target MAS is defined by four core operational components:
 - **R:** A set of allocated agent roles (e.g., Manager, Coder, Web Surfer).
 - **P:** Communication protocols specifying permissible message structures between agents.
 - **T:** A set of available tools (e.g., Python interpreter, browser).
 - **B:** Assignment of concrete LLM backbones to each agent role.

- **Generator Agent (A_gen):** As the system architect, the Generator takes a task query (Q) and produces a high-level, abstract workflow template (M_temp = ⟨R, P, T⟩). This template outlines the sequence of agentic operations without specifying the underlying LLM models, allowing for architectural innovation. Its policy is denoted as π_gen(·|Q).

- **Implementor Agent (A_imp):** This agent instantiates the abstract template (M_temp) into a fully executable system. It defines an assignment policy (ϕ) that maps each role in R to a concrete LLM backbone from a candidate pool (L = {b_1..., b_|L|}). The Implementor's policy is π_imp(·|M_temp, L, Q). The final, executable MAS (M) is then composed by integrating these backbone assignments into the workflow template.

- **Rectifier Agent (A_rec):** Operating as an online monitor, the Rectifier ensures system resilience during execution. It is triggered when its function A_R(s_t) evaluates to 1, indicating either:
 - Cumulative resource consumption C(s_t) (e.g., token count, execution steps) exceeds a predefined budget θ_C.
 - The operational outcome O(s_t) results in an explicit failure (e.g., search engine failure, code execution error).
 Upon activation, the Rectifier intervenes by generating a modification to the current system configuration (M_t). These modifications can range from local adjustments (e.g., re-assigning tools, modifying agent prompts) to global architectural changes (e.g., revising workflow code). The system then resumes execution with the updated configuration (M_t+1) based on the Rectifier's learned policy π_rec(·|M_t, s_t). This continuous feedback loop ensures real-time self-correction.

#### 3.2. Training Trajectory Curation: Collaborative Tree Optimization (CTO)

To train and specialize these meta-agents, MAS^2 employs an offline reinforcement learning strategy called Collaborative Tree Optimization (CTO).

- **Collaborative Decision Tree (G_Q):** For each task query Q, a rooted, directed decision tree G_Q = (V, E) is constructed.
 - The process starts from the query root node (v_Q).
 - The Generator agent expands this with K candidate MAS templates (v_G nodes).
 - The Implementor agent further expands each v_G with N executable instantiations (v_I nodes).
 - Rectifier nodes (v_R) are adaptively introduced during simulated execution if issues arise, adding further branches.
 - The tree terminates at leaf nodes (v_F), representing final execution states.
- **Trajectory (τ):** A unique path from the root v_Q to a terminal node v_F defines a trajectory.
- **Cost-Sensitive Reward Function R(τ):** Trajectories are evaluated not just by performance but also by resource efficiency.
 - Failed trajectories receive zero reward.
 - Successful trajectories are rewarded inversely proportional to their normalized resource consumption (LLM API cost). Normalization is done against the empirical mean cost across all trajectories for that task.
- **Path Credit Propagation:** After assigning terminal rewards, a Monte Carlo estimate propagates credit backward to intermediate decision nodes. The value V(v) of any node v is the expected reward of all trajectories passing through it. This provides a principled value signal for each decision made by the meta-agents.

#### 3.3. Training and Specializing Meta Agents

The value-annotated decision tree G_Q forms a structured dataset used to train the meta-agent policies:

- **Value-Guided Preference Construction:** From G_Q, preference tuples are extracted for each non-terminal decision node (v). Each tuple is (c_v, a_win, a_lose, ΔV), where c_v is the decision context, a_win and a_lose are winning and losing actions, and ΔV = V(v') - V(v'') is the "preference strength" – the quantitative difference in expected rewards between the two choices. This goes beyond simple binary preferences by capturing the magnitude of difference.
- **Policy Specialization via Value-Scaled Optimization:** The training refines a reference policy (π_ref, typically a vanilla LLM) into specialized policies (π*gen, π*imp, π*rec). A value-scaled loss function L_CTO is minimized:
 - L_CTO incorporates ΔV as a weight, prioritizing learning from high-confidence preference pairs where the value difference is significant. This ensures the model learns most effectively from clear signals of good vs. bad decisions.
 - The loss function leverages a logistic function and a temperature parameter (β) to modulate the relative probability of chosen actions, similar to Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) but with the added value-scaling.
This optimization procedure is applied independently to each of the Generator, Implementor, and Rectifier agents, resulting in three specialized policies that collectively enable the dynamic, self-adaptive MAS^2 pipeline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical evaluation across eight benchmarks in four distinct domains (multi-hop search, deep research, code generation, and mathematical reasoning) demonstrates MAS^2's significant advancements over existing methods.

#### 4.1. Superior Competence Across Diverse Domains

MAS^2 consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance across all tested benchmarks, surpassing both handcrafted and automated multi-agent systems.
- **Outperformance over Handcrafted MAS:** MAS^2 showed substantial gains, for example, achieving 89.3% on HotpotQA, outperforming the best manual setting (DyLAN) by 8.5%, and MedPrompt by 16.9%. In MATH, MAS^2 scored 71.3%, slightly better than LLM-Debate's 67.3%.
- **Outperformance over Automated MAS:** Against leading automated systems like ScoreFlow and MaAS, MAS^2 maintained an advantage. In multi-hop search, it averaged 78.5%, exceeding ScoreFlow by 2.8% and MaAS by 4.7%. For code generation, MAS^2 scored 97.0% on HumanEval, a 4.1% improvement over AFlow. In MATH, it improved by 6.9% over ScoreFlow and 2.8% over AFlow.
- **Consistent Generalization (Obs. ❶ & ❷):** Unlike many existing automated MAS that show inconsistent performance across domains, MAS^2 demonstrates robust multi-task adaptability, excelling in tasks ranging from factual QA and deep research to complex code generation and mathematical reasoning.

#### 4.2. Cross-Backbone Generalization (Obs. ❸)

A crucial finding is MAS^2's ability to effectively integrate and leverage previously *unseen* LLM backbones at inference time without requiring additional fine-tuning.
- When augmented with stronger, unseen models like Qwen3-Coder, GPT-5-Mini, and Gemini-2.5-Pro, MAS^2 significantly improved performance. For instance, combining Qwen3-Coder and GPT-5-Mini boosted MATH performance from 69.7% (vanilla Qwen3-Coder) to 88.6% and Bamboogle from 32.8% to 82.5%.
- This demonstrates MAS^2's flexibility and practical deployability, as it can dynamically harness the strengths of new, powerful models as they become available, enhancing its accuracy while maintaining reasonable cost increases.

#### 4.3. Pareto-Optimal Cost-Performance (Obs. ❹)

MAS^2 established a new Pareto frontier in the cost-performance trade-off, achieving superior results without incurring excessive token costs.
- On the Bamboogle benchmark, MAS^2 achieved a 12.8% higher pass rate than the expensive SC (GPT-4o) baseline while being over 25 times cheaper.
- For NQ, it surpassed ScoreFlow by 2.7% in accuracy at nearly identical cost, and outperformed SC (GPT-4o) by 5.5 points while cutting costs by over 20 times.
- This efficiency stems from MAS^2's dynamic operation: assigning simpler tasks to smaller, cost-effective LLMs and reserving more capable LLMs for complex reasoning, as well as configuring lightweight MAS for easy problems and more sophisticated architectures for challenging ones.

#### 4.4. Framework Analysis (Ablation Study)

An ablation study confirmed the indispensable contribution of each of the three meta-agent components: Generator, Implementor, and Rectifier.
- **W/o Generator:** Substantial performance drops (e.g., 85.2% to 79.0% on MBPP) indicate the generator's critical role in architecting effective MAS templates.
- **W/o Implementor:** Notable degradation (e.g., 80.4% on MBPP) highlights the implementor's learned ability to optimally allocate LLM backbones to roles.
- **W/o Rectifier:** Intermediate but significant drops (e.g., 3.5% on MBPP, 6.6% on MATH) prove the rectifier's importance for real-time self-adjustment and recovery from runtime failures.

#### 4.5. Case Studies of Dynamic MAS Generation and Rectification

Visualizations and detailed case studies showcased MAS^2's ability to flexibly design heterogeneous MAS tailored to domain-specific demands (e.g., allocating different LLMs for different stages of QA, code generation, or deep research). Furthermore, the rectification case studies demonstrated the agent's practical utility in diagnosing and repairing execution failures:
- **Case 1 (Coding Task):** Rectified a `KeyError` arising from an ensemble selection failure by adding a validation step and a fallback mechanism to ensure continued operation.
- **Case 2 (Malformed Output):** Diagnosed a `pydantic.ValidationError` due to an operator returning malformed structured output. The rectifier intelligently bypassed the faulty review operators, directly passing initial solutions to the ensemble stage, thereby allowing the workflow to complete successfully.

These findings collectively validate MAS^2 as a robust, adaptive, and efficient paradigm for automating multi-agent system design and operation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MAS^2 paradigm represents a significant leap forward in the design and deployment of Large Language Model (LLM)-powered multi-agent systems, carrying profound implications for the future of agentic AI.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift Towards Recursive Self-Generation

The most significant contribution of MAS^2 is its introduction of a novel paradigm: a multi-agent system that can autonomously generate, configure, and rectify *other* multi-agent systems. This moves beyond the prevalent "generate-once-and-deploy" approach, addressing the fundamental brittleness that limits current automated MAS. This recursive self-generation capability pushes the boundaries of autonomous system design, enabling a truly self-evolving intelligence.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability in Dynamic Environments

By integrating a dedicated Rectifier agent, MAS^2 directly tackles the challenge of real-world dynamism and unpredictability. The ability to monitor execution, detect failures (e.g., exceeding cost budgets, tool errors, malformed outputs), and dynamically correct the system configuration in real-time makes MAS^2 far more robust than its predecessors. This is crucial for deploying MAS in complex, long-horizon tasks where unexpected events are inevitable, ensuring greater reliability and resilience.

#### 5.3. Cost-Efficiency and Resource Optimization

MAS^2's ability to operate on the Pareto frontier of cost-performance is a major practical advantage. By intelligently allocating LLM backbones based on task complexity (e.g., using smaller models for simpler tasks and reserving larger ones for critical reasoning) and dynamically configuring system architectures, MAS^2 achieves high performance without incurring prohibitive costs. This economic efficiency can significantly lower the barrier to entry for deploying sophisticated MAS, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and sustainable for a wider range of applications and industries.

#### 5.4. Superior Generalization and Future-Proofing

The demonstrated cross-backbone generalization, allowing MAS^2 to effectively leverage unseen LLM models at inference time, is highly impactful. This means the system is not tied to a fixed set of models but can adapt and integrate with new, more powerful LLMs as they emerge, extending its lifespan and relevance without requiring extensive retraining. This flexibility makes MAS^2 a more future-proof and adaptable solution for evolving AI landscapes.

#### 5.5. Advancement in Meta-Learning and Agentic AI

The Collaborative Tree Optimization (CTO) framework, with its value-guided preference construction and optimization, offers a powerful methodology for specializing meta-agents. This approach contributes to the broader field of meta-learning for agent systems, providing a structured way to learn optimal strategies for agent design and adaptation. MAS^2's success further validates the potential of reinforcement learning and preference-based alignment in building sophisticated, autonomous agents.

#### 5.6. Broadening Applicability to Complex Real-World Problems

The demonstrated superior performance across diverse and complex benchmarks, including deep research and code generation, suggests that MAS^2 can unlock new possibilities for automating tasks that currently require significant human oversight or are too dynamic for static AI systems. This could accelerate scientific discovery, improve software development processes, and enhance intelligent assistants capable of handling multifaceted queries.

In summary, MAS^2 represents a pivotal step towards building genuinely intelligent and autonomous agents that can not only solve problems but also understand, adapt, and self-rectify in the face of uncertainty. Its principles are likely to inspire future research in self-organizing AI, dynamic resource management, and robust agent architectures, ultimately pushing the capabilities of AI closer to human-like adaptability and resilience.
