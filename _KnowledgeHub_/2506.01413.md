# Incentivizing Reasoning for Advanced Instruction-Following of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RAIF, a framework from Tencent Youtu Lab and collaborators, cultivates deep reasoning in large language models to significantly enhance their ability to follow complex instructions, overcoming superficial Chain-of-Thought. The approach includes a scalable LLM-based data generation method and a tailored reinforcement learning strategy, enabling a 1.5B LLM to achieve an 11.74% average gain across seven benchmarks, reaching performance comparable to an 8B model.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex instructions involving multiple, intricately structured constraints, often ignoring parts or misinterpreting relationships.
- Vanilla Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting often leads to 'superficial reasoning' for instruction following, merely paraphrasing instructions without deep structural analysis, leading to minimal or negative performance gains.
- Existing solutions like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and template-guided inference are task-specific, prone to overfitting, lack generalization to unseen constraints, and require extensive manual data curation.

## Method

- A scalable LLM-based ==instruction evolving== method generates diverse, high-quality complex instructions with verifiable constraints, leveraging both code-execution and LLM-as-a-Judge verification.
- Reinforcement Learning using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed with a rule-centric reward model to explicitly incentivize deep, effective reasoning within LLMs.
- The method incorporates 'superior CoT enforcement' to filter the replay buffer for genuinely effective reasoning examples and uses behavior cloning as a regularizer to stabilize training and maintain semantic quality.

## Results

- RAIF consistently improved instruction-following across various LLMs and seven benchmarks, with a **1.5B** LLM achieving an **11.74%** average gain, reaching performance comparable to an **8B** model.
- Optimized models exhibited deeper, more structured reasoning, evidenced by an increased frequency of step-by-step keywords in their Chains-of-Thought, particularly on challenging benchmarks.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization to unseen, out-of-domain constraints and improved performance across six diverse multi-purpose benchmarks, confirming the cultivation of fundamental reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vanilla Chain-of-Thought's effectiveness in mathematical reasoning does not directly translate to complex instruction following, where it often yields 'superficial reasoning' without cultivating true adherence.
> - Explicitly incentivizing reasoning through a tailored RL approach, guided by rule-centric rewards and a mechanism to prioritize truly effective reasoning, can overcome the superficiality issue in instruction following.
> - A reproducible LLM-based data generation pipeline, combining diverse constraint types and verification methods, is crucial for addressing data scarcity and fostering strong generalization across various tasks and out-of-domain constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models has seen remarkable progress in generalizability across diverse tasks, largely attributed to their instruction-following capabilities. However, a significant challenge persists: LLMs often struggle with *complex instructions*, particularly those involving multiple constraints structured in parallel, chaining, or branching patterns. Existing studies predominantly benchmark these capabilities, but many LLMs still fall short of expectations when faced with intricate, compositional rules.

Prior attempts to enhance LLMs in this domain largely fall into two categories: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and template-guided inference. While effective for specific tasks, these methods are prone to overfitting to training data and exhibit limited generalization to unseen constraints, often requiring extensive manual effort for data curation or template enumeration. This points to a critical need for more scalable, constructive, and generalizable solutions.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting emerged as an intuitive solution, demonstrating success in tasks requiring step-by-step reasoning, such as mathematics. The expectation was that CoT would universally improve LLM capabilities by encouraging structured analysis. However, this paper critically identifies a "superficial reasoning" phenomenon when vanilla CoT is applied to complex instruction following. Instead of deep structural analysis, LLMs often merely paraphrase instructions, overlooking crucial constraint compositions—a stark contrast to its efficacy in mathematical problem-solving where formalizing intermediate steps is indispensable. This limitation highlights a gap: existing CoT methods, while encouraging "thinking," do not inherently cultivate *effective* reasoning specifically for complex instruction adherence.

This work directly addresses this gap by positioning itself within the emergent trend of "test-time compute scaling" and "slow-thinking LLMs," exemplified by models like OpenAI's o1, DeepSeek R1, and QwQ. These models leverage reinforcement learning (RL) to incentivize deeper reasoning and exploration. RAIF extends this paradigm from primarily mathematical and logical problem-solving to the broader and more nuanced domain of complex instruction following. It differentiates itself from contemporaries like Tülu 3 by focusing on a broader problem definition, encompassing a wider array of constraint types and compositions, utilizing a richer reward modeling strategy (combining code-execution and LLM-as-a-Judge), and employing an RL algorithm specifically designed to overcome the superficial reasoning challenge in this context. RAIF thus aims to pioneer a systematic approach to cultivate genuine, effective reasoning for advanced instruction following, moving beyond task-specific solutions towards a more generalizable capability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **incentivize deep reasoning within large language models to significantly enhance their ability to follow complex instructions.** This goal is pursued by developing a systematic and generalizable method named RAIF.

The core motivations stem from observed shortcomings in current LLM capabilities and existing solutions:

1. **Challenges with Complex Instructions:** Current LLMs frequently fail to adhere to complex instructions, especially those involving multiple, intricately structured constraints (e.g., parallel, chained, branched requirements). They often ignore certain constraints or misinterpret their relationships.
2. **Failure of Vanilla Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Contrary to its success in mathematical tasks, direct application of CoT prompting to complex instruction following often yields minimal or even negative performance gains. This is attributed to "superficial reasoning," where LLMs merely paraphrase instructions without engaging in the deep, structured analysis required to understand and satisfy compositional constraints. There's no inherent tendency for LLMs to perform deep reasoning for instruction following as they are often aligned to produce direct responses.
3. **Limitations of Existing Task-Specific Solutions:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and template-guided inference, while somewhat effective, are task-specific. They tend to overfit to training constraints, struggle to generalize to unseen scenarios, and demand considerable manual effort for data generation or template design, rendering them unscalable.
4. **Data Scarcity:** There is a lack of diverse, high-quality datasets of complex instructions that adequately capture various constraint types and compositions, complete with verifiable rule sets for evaluation.
5. **Lack of Effective CoT Cultivation for Instruction Following:** The "secret recipe" for formulating effective CoT specifically for complex instruction following, as opposed to mathematical problem-solving, remains elusive. Existing RL-based approaches for reasoning primarily focus on math problems with easily verifiable rule-based rewards, which are not directly applicable to the diverse and often semantic nature of instruction-following constraints.

To address these motivations, RAIF sets out to tackle two main challenges:
- Overcoming the data scarcity issue by proposing a reproducible data acquisition method based on LLM-based evolving of instructions under existing taxonomies.
- Formulating an RL-based strategy with verifiable, rule-centric reward signals to cultivate reasoning specifically for instruction following, combating superficial reasoning through sample-wise contrast for superior CoT enforcement and behavior cloning to manage policy distribution shift.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RAIF (Reasoning for Advancing Instruction-Following) introduces a systematic two-pronged approach to enhance LLMs' ability to follow complex instructions: **LLM-based instruction evolving** for data generation and **reinforcement learning for reasoning** tailored to instruction following.

**Problem Definition:** The goal is for a language model (parameterized by $\theta$) to generate an output `y` that satisfies all constraints in a given complex instruction `x`. The output `y` comprises a Chain-of-Thought (`CoT(y)`) and a final answer (`y \ CoT(y)`). For fast-thinking models, explicit CoT prompts (`x_CoT`) might be necessary, while "slow-thinking" reasoning models might not require them.

#### 3.1. LLM-based Instruction Evolving with Complex Rules and Constraints

To address data scarcity and ensure diversity, RAIF proposes a scalable data acquisition method:
- **Seed Instruction Selection:** A diverse set of seed instructions ($D_{seed}$) is chosen from general-purpose datasets like WildChat and Alpaca. These are pre-tagged by topics and tasks (using methods like InsTag) to ensure a broad range of abilities is covered.
- **Instruction Evolving with Rules and Constraints:** The self-instruct paradigm is adapted to generate more complex instructions:
 - **Code-Execution Verification:** For constraints verifiable by code (e.g., lexical, numerical, format), a pool of 25 atomic constraints (from IFEval) and their executable verification codes are maintained. Compatible combinations are randomly instantiated, with LLMs filling semantic placeholders (e.g., keywords) to ensure contextual coherence.
 - **LLM-as-a-Judge Verification:** For semantic, stylistic, or linguistic constraints, LLMs are used to generate sub-instructions and corresponding boolean "scoring questions" that evaluate adherence. These sub-instructions, representing various constraint types (And, Chain, Selection, Nested), are then assembled and fused by an LLM into a coherent, integrated complex instruction.
- **Response Generation and Quality Check:** LLMs (specifically, DeepSeek R1) generate responses to these evolved instructions. A multi-stage filtering process discards low-quality instruction-response pairs:
 - Initial filtering removes responses that fail either the code-execution or LLM-as-a-Judge verifications.
 - Further filtering identifies and removes issues like language inconsistency, answer leakage, under/over-length responses, hallucination, poorly-defined/problematic instructions, and NSFW content, using curated judgment prompts with an LLM (DeepSeek R1). This rigorous filtering ensures a high-quality, verifiable dataset of 13K instances.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning Under Complex Instructions

RAIF employs Reinforcement Learning to cultivate deep, effective reasoning, specifically designed to overcome the "superficial reasoning" issue observed in vanilla CoT:
- **GRPO Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is adopted to optimize the policy model ($\pi_\theta$). GRPO updates the policy by maximizing an objective function that considers group-level rewards, normalizing advantages across multiple generated outputs (`G`) per prompt. This differs from PPO's reliance on an explicit critic model.
- **Rule-Centric Reward Modeling:** To guide reasoning effectively, a composite reward function ($R_i$) is defined:
 - **Format Reward ($R_{i}^{format}$):** Encourages the structured output format of `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`. It yields +1 for adherence and -1 otherwise, ensuring explicit separation of reasoning and answer.
 - **Accuracy Reward ($R_{i}^{accuracy}$):** Evaluates compliance with the instruction's constraints. It grants +2 if all active constraints are satisfied, a proportional score based on the number of satisfied constraints, and -2 if none are satisfied. Verification relies on Python heuristics for lexical, numerical, and format constraints, and a reward model ($r_\phi$, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) for boolean validation of semantic, stylistic, and linguistic constraints.
- **Experience Replay Buffer with Superior CoT Enforcement:** Crucially, RAIF addresses the non-compulsory nature of reasoning for instruction following. It filters the replay buffer to retain only "superior CoT" examples. If all generated responses *with reasoning* (`y_i`) for a given prompt perform worse than a "reasoning-free" counterpart (`ˆy_i`) (i.e., `max(R_i_accuracy) < min(ˆR_i_accuracy)`), the sample is discarded. This ensures that only reasoning trajectories that genuinely lead to improved instruction-following performance are used for training, preventing the model from generating verbose but ultimately unhelpful "reasoning."
- **Policy Distribution Drift Control with Behavior Cloning (SFT):** To prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain semantic quality, explicit behavior cloning (SFT) is performed on expert responses (`˜y`) containing both reasoning and answers. This SFT loss (`J_SFT`) acts as a regularizer, ensuring: 1) expedited adherence to expected output formats; 2) imitation and traceability of deep reasoning patterns; and 3) mitigation of "reward hacking" (where models satisfy constraints but produce semantically poor text) without relying solely on the reward model's scalar scores. This explicitly constrains the policy to remain semantically aligned with high-quality responses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings across extensive evaluations, demonstrating the efficacy and generalizability of RAIF.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Baselines and Overall Performance

- **Significant Performance Gains:** RAIF consistently boosts the instruction-following capabilities of various LLMs across seven comprehensive benchmarks (IFEval, CELLO, CFBench, ComplexBench, FB-Bench, FollowBench, InfoBench).
 - Notably, a 1.5B LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) achieved an **11.74% average gain**, reaching performance levels comparable to an 8B LLM, highlighting the potential of test-time scaling for smaller models.
 - Even 7B-scale models showed consistent, albeit smaller, improvements across both cold-start and warm-start reasoners.
- **Critique of Vanilla CoT:** Preliminary experiments confirmed that vanilla CoT prompting often leads to *negative or minimal* performance gains for complex instruction following due to "superficial reasoning." This validates the core motivation for RAIF.
- **Superiority over Other Methods:**
 - **SDC (Self-DeepClaude):** While better than vanilla CoT, SDC (which decouples reasoning generation from answering) still struggled with the intrinsic superficial reasoning, failing to significantly improve reasoning quality.
 - **SFT:** Supervised fine-tuning proved to be a good starting point but exhibited limited generalization, suggesting that SFT teaches memorization of reasoning patterns rather than genuine "how to think" abilities. RAIF's RL paradigm, in contrast, cultivates varied reasoning.
 - **SOTAs on ComplexBench:** RAIF demonstrated superiority over several state-of-the-art methods (Oracle Decomposition, Self-Consistency, Conifer, FollowComplex, Self-Refine), particularly in handling the most complicated "Chain" and "Selection" categories of instructions. Training-free methods, even with increased test-time compute, did not consistently benefit due to LLMs' shallow reasoning.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Reasoning Patterns

- Detailed analysis of step-by-step keywords (e.g., "first," "second," "next," "finally") revealed an **increased frequency of such tokens** in RAIF-optimized models, especially on challenging benchmarks like CFBench and ComplexBench. This directly supports the claim that RAIF successfully cultivates deeper, more structured reasoning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Maths Problems:** Incorporating the DeepScaleR mathematical dataset positively contributed to reasoning development. An increase in maths problems was positively correlated with both CoT token count and overall performance, suggesting that mathematical reasoning is a crucial, supplementary component to general-purpose reasoning.
- **Effect of Superior CoT Enforcement:** The mechanism of filtering the experience replay buffer based on "superior CoT" (reasoning leading to better outcomes than no-reasoning) proved critical. It ensured that training signals were derived from truly effective reasoning, preventing the policy model from learning verbose but non-compliant chains, and mitigating reward hacking. The ratio of samples kept by superior CoT initially dropped then steadily improved, indicating a successful transition from shallow to deep reasoning.
- **Effect of Behavior Cloning:** Behavior cloning of expert responses played a vital role in encouraging adherence to expected output formats, stabilizing training, filling gaps in rule-centric rewards, and mitigating reward hacking. It facilitated the imitation of deep reasoning from experts, especially for models initially lacking such capabilities.

#### 4.4. Generalization Studies

- **Generalization on OOD Constraints (IFBench):** RAIF exhibited **consistent improvement on unseen, out-of-domain (OOD) constraints** from the IFBench dataset across various model sizes and families. This directly contrasts with other RLVR methods (e.g., Tülu 3), which reported overfitting to training constraints. This strong generalization is attributed to the diversity of the evolved complex instructions and the universal applicability of the cultivated deep reasoning.
- **Generalization on Multi-Purpose Benchmarks:** Optimized models generally showed **improved performance on six diverse multi-purpose benchmarks** (ARC-C/E, BBH, CMMLU, MMLU, StrategyQA), indicating that the reasoning capacity developed for complex instructions is broadly generalizable across different tasks and domains.
- **Generalization on Maths Benchmarks:** Fast-thinking LLMs optimized with RAIF achieved **consistent performance gains on six popular Maths benchmarks** (GSM8K, MATH500, MINERVA-MATH, OlympiadBench, AIME24, AMC23). While some reasoning-first models saw slight drops (attributed to distributional shifts and token generation limits), the overall trend confirms enhanced mathematical reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RAIF framework represents a significant step forward in enhancing the advanced instruction-following capabilities of large language models, addressing several critical limitations of current approaches.

**Core Contributions and Scientific Significance:**
- **Systematic Reasoning Cultivation:** RAIF offers a systematic and pioneering method to explicitly incentivize and cultivate deep, effective reasoning in LLMs for complex instruction following, moving beyond the "superficial reasoning" often observed with vanilla CoT. This de-mystifies the recipe behind reasoning cultivation in a context beyond traditional mathematical problems.
- **Scalable Data Generation:** The proposed LLM-based evolving pipeline for complex instructions, complete with verifiable rule sets, provides a reproducible and scalable solution to address the persistent problem of data scarcity in this domain. This method integrates both code-execution and LLM-as-a-Judge verifications, offering comprehensive feedback.
- **Novel RL Paradigm:** The integration of GRPO with rule-centric reward modeling, superior CoT enforcement, and behavior cloning introduces a robust RL paradigm specifically tailored to the unique challenges of instruction following. The superior CoT enforcement mechanism is particularly innovative in ensuring that generated reasoning is genuinely beneficial, rather than merely verbose.

**Practical Implications and Potential Impact:**
- **More Capable LLMs:** By enabling LLMs to better handle instructions with multiple, structured constraints, RAIF can lead to the development of more reliable and robust AI assistants, agents, and automation tools capable of executing complex, multi-step tasks in real-world applications. This can significantly reduce instances of models "hallucinating" or ignoring critical user requirements.
- **Enhanced Small Models:** The demonstrated ability of smaller models (e.g., 1.5B) to achieve performance comparable to much larger models through RAIF’s test-time compute scaling is highly impactful. This opens avenues for deploying highly capable LLMs in resource-constrained environments, making advanced AI more accessible and efficient.
- **Generalizable AI:** The strong generalization observed across OOD constraints and diverse multi-purpose benchmarks suggests that RAIF fosters a fundamental reasoning capability rather than merely task-specific memorization. This is crucial for developing truly intelligent and adaptable AI systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides valuable insights and practical guidelines for researchers and practitioners aiming to build "slow-thinkers" for various complex tasks. The detailed analysis of training dynamics, ablation studies, and generalization to maths problems enriches the understanding of LLM cognitive behaviors, which can inform the development of even stronger base models and more sophisticated reasoning architectures.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The public availability of code and data ensures reproducibility and encourages further research and development within the community, accelerating progress in advanced LLM capabilities.

In conclusion, RAIF makes a substantial contribution by systematically tackling the complex problem of instruction following in LLMs. Its innovative methodology for data generation and reinforcement learning not only pushes the boundaries of LLM capabilities but also offers a pragmatic pathway for developing more intelligent, reliable, and resource-efficient AI systems.
