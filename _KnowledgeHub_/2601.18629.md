# ExoGS: A 4D Real-to-Sim-to-Real Framework for Scalable Manipulation Data Collection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed ExoGS, a 4D Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework that uses a low-cost AirExo-3 exoskeleton to capture human manipulation demonstrations and reconstruct them into editable 3D Gaussian Splatting assets. This system enables scalable, geometry-consistent data augmentation and employs a Mask Adapter for robust policy learning, demonstrating improved data collection efficiency and enhanced generalization across various robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation research suffers from a data scarcity bottleneck for imitation learning, as traditional data collection methods are costly and time-consuming.
- Existing Real-to-Sim-to-Real (R2S2R) pipelines often struggle to bridge the sim-to-real gap for dynamic, contact-rich interactions, primarily focusing on static visual fidelity.
- Policies trained on limited or purely synthetic data lack robustness and struggle to generalize to real-world visual variations and dynamic interaction complexities.

## Method

- Introduces **AirExo-3**, a low-cost, open-source passive exoskeleton for efficient and kinematically consistent human manipulation demonstration capture.
- Leverages a 4D ==Real-to-Sim-to-Real== pipeline based on ==3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)== to reconstruct real-world scenes and dynamic interactions into editable digital assets, enabling scalable, geometry-consistent data augmentation via rendering diverse viewpoints, lighting, backgrounds, and object substitutions.
- Proposes a lightweight **Mask Adapter** module to enhance visuomotor policies with instance-level semantic awareness, improving robustness to visual domain shifts.

## Results

- AirExo-3 demonstrated faster data collection and higher success ratios (e.g., **100%** for Pick and Place vs. **92.3%** for teleoperation) across tasks, especially for complex ones.
- Policies trained with 3DGS-based data augmentation exhibited substantial generalization improvements, outperforming non-augmented synthetic data and real-world teleoperation data under varied visual conditions.
- The Mask Adapter significantly mitigated the sim-to-real gap, enabling policies trained on non-augmented ExoGS data to surpass teleoperation baselines in standard and color-varied scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-cost, robot-free exoskeletons like AirExo-3 can efficiently capture high-quality, kinematically consistent human manipulation demonstrations, especially for complex tasks.
> - 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) can effectively reconstruct both static environments and dynamic interactions, providing editable 4D digital assets for scalable and geometry-consistent data augmentation.
> - Incorporating instance-level semantic awareness via modules like the Mask Adapter into visuomotor policies helps mitigate visual domain shifts and improves policy robustness to real-world variations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is heavily reliant on effective data, yet acquiring this data remains a significant bottleneck. ExoGS addresses several critical challenges at the intersection of imitation learning, sim-to-real transfer, and advanced neural scene representations.

**Imitation Learning (IL) and Data Scarcity:** IL enables robots to learn complex skills from human demonstrations, but its performance is directly tied to the scale and quality of the training data. Traditional methods for collecting such data, like teleoperation using actual robots, are expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, particularly for complex, contact-rich tasks. This creates a data bottleneck for developing generalizable robotic policies.

**The Sim-to-Real Gap:** Training policies purely in simulation offers scalability but introduces the "sim-to-real gap," where policies fail to generalize to the real world due to discrepancies in geometry, visual appearance, and physical interactions. This gap is particularly pronounced for vision-based policies relying on RGB observations.

**Real-to-Sim-to-Real (R2S2R) Techniques:** Recent advances in neural scene representations, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have opened new avenues for bridging the sim-to-real gap. R2S2R pipelines leverage these representations to reconstruct photorealistic "digital twins" of real-world scenes. Data generation and policy learning can then occur in these reconstructed, visually high-fidelity simulated environments. However, previous R2S2R methods often focused on static scene reconstruction, overlooking the complex dynamics of physical interactions. Acquiring high-fidelity interaction data, especially for contact-rich tasks, remains challenging and inefficient if solely relying on simulation or expensive robot hardware.

**The Rise of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** 3DGS has emerged as a particularly promising neural scene representation due to its explicit, point-based nature, fast optimization, and real-time rendering capabilities. This makes it more suitable for interactive robotics applications than NeRF, which can be computationally intensive. Recent extensions of 3DGS to incorporate dynamics and physical priors further enhance its utility as an editable, geometry-aware world model for manipulation and navigation. ExoGS capitalizes on 3DGS's advantages for both photorealistic rendering and editable asset generation.

**Robot-Free Data Collection:** To circumvent the cost and complexity of robot teleoperation, an alternative trend involves "in-the-wild" demonstration collection using human-operated devices like handheld grippers, VR/AR systems, motion-capture gloves, or exoskeletons. While these systems reduce deployment costs, they often struggle with limited kinematic fidelity, trajectory accuracy, or environment diversity, preventing effective transfer to specific robot kinematics.

**ExoGS's Unique Contribution:** ExoGS positions itself by addressing the limitations of existing approaches. It provides a novel solution that combines:
1. **Low-cost, robot-free, kinematically consistent data acquisition:** Using the custom AirExo-3 exoskeleton, it captures precise human manipulation trajectories that are directly transferable to a target robot's kinematics. This fills a gap where previous robot-free methods lacked sufficient fidelity.
2. **4D (spatial-temporal) R2S2R:** It reconstructs both static environments and dynamic interactions as editable 3DGS assets, enabling not just visual fidelity but also the replay and augmentation of *interactions* in simulation. This goes beyond static scene R2S2R.
3. **Scalable and geometry-consistent data augmentation:** The editable 3DGS assets allow for massive, diverse data augmentation (viewpoints, lighting, backgrounds, object poses) that is consistent with the scene's geometry, which is crucial for robust policy learning.
4. **Robust sim-to-real transfer:** By introducing the lightweight Mask Adapter, it further mitigates the residual visual domain shift by injecting instance-level semantic awareness into visuomotor policies, guiding attention to task-relevant features.

In essence, ExoGS integrates best practices from hardware design for human-robot interaction, cutting-edge neural scene representations, and robust policy learning techniques to offer a comprehensive, scalable, and effective framework for advancing robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the ExoGS framework are multifaceted, aiming to resolve significant bottlenecks in current robotic manipulation research:

1. **Overcome Data Scarcity and Collection Costs:** The efficacy of imitation learning is profoundly dependent on large, high-quality datasets. Traditional robot-based data collection methods (e.g., teleoperation) are prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and complex to deploy, leading to limited dataset sizes and hindering generalization. ExoGS seeks to provide a **low-cost, robot-free, and efficient solution** for acquiring manipulation demonstrations.

2. **Bridge the Sim-to-Real Gap for Interactions:** While existing Real-to-Sim-to-Real (R2S2R) pipelines, particularly those leveraging NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have shown promise in bridging the *visual* domain gap for static scenes, they often fall short in capturing and transferring dynamic *interactions*, especially contact-rich ones. A key objective is to enable the seamless transfer of **both static environments and dynamic, kinematically consistent interactions** from the real world into a high-fidelity simulated environment.

3. **Enable Scalable and Geometry-Consistent Data Augmentation:** The goal is to move beyond simple 2D image augmentations and leverage the rich 3D information from neural scene representations. ExoGS aims to reconstruct real-world assets (robots, objects, environments) as *editable* 3DGS representations. This enables **massive, geometry-consistent data augmentation** across various aspects like viewpoints, lighting conditions, backgrounds, and object poses, significantly enhancing the diversity and scale of training data.

4. **Improve Robustness and Generalization of Learned Policies:** Policies trained on synthetic or even limited real-world data often struggle with robustness to visual domain shifts in real-world deployment. ExoGS aims to improve the **generalization capabilities and robustness** of visuomotor policies. This is achieved not only through extensive 3D-aware data augmentation but also by injecting semantic understanding directly into the policy architecture using a lightweight module.

5. **Develop an Open-Source, Accessible Platform:** Underlying these technical objectives is a commitment to accessibility. By developing and open-sourcing the hardware (AirExo-3) and software, the authors aim to **democratize access to high-quality manipulation data collection**, allowing a wider research community to benefit and build upon their work.

In summary, ExoGS is motivated by the need for a scalable, efficient, and robust solution to the data problem in robotic manipulation, particularly for complex, contact-rich tasks, by synergistically combining innovative hardware, advanced neural scene representations, and intelligent policy design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ExoGS is structured as a comprehensive 4D Real-to-Sim-to-Real framework, integrating custom hardware with advanced software pipelines for data generation, augmentation, and policy learning.

#### 3.1. A. Hardware Design: AirExo-3

The foundation of data collection is **AirExo-3**, a custom-designed, open-source, low-cost (approximately $400) passive exoskeleton. It builds upon previous AirExo iterations, focusing on:
- **Ease of Deployment:** Simplified mechanical design for accurate zero-position calibration and straightforward installation.
- **High Accuracy:** Achieves millimeter-level end-effector positioning accuracy through high-precision 12-bit rotary encoders and forward kinematics. It provides robust motion tracking insensitive to visual conditions and enforces robot-consistent kinematic constraints.
- **Ease of Use:** Lightweight design (glass-fiber-reinforced nylon) for prolonged use and intuitive, contact-rich manipulation via a replaceable gripper, sharing identical kinematic parameters and joint limits with the target robot.

AirExo-3's structure consists of seven articulated joints and a parallel gripper, geometrically matched to the target robotic arm (Flexiv Rizon 4s in experiments). Its joint modules house rotary encoders and incorporate tunable passive damping for stable operation.

#### 3.2. B. 4D Data Generation and Augmentation

The framework digitizes real-world demonstrations into editable 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assets, enabling scalable data augmentation.

1. **Manipulation Demonstration Collection:** Human users operate AirExo-3 to perform demonstrations. During this process, the exoskeleton records kinematically consistent joint-angle sequences (`q_t`) and gripper openings (`g_t`). Simultaneously, multiple calibrated Intel RealSense D415 cameras capture synchronized multi-view RGB-D observations (`I_t^(k)`). These provide geometric constraints crucial for 3D reconstruction and object pose estimation.

2. **Digital Asset Generation via Multi-View Reconstruction:**
 - **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** The core neural representation used. The scene (robot, objects, environment) is represented as a collection of 3D Gaussians.
 - **"Capture-Reconstruct-Assetize" Pipeline:** Multi-view images are processed by COLMAP to recover camera poses. These poses initialize the optimization of Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics), driven by a photometric loss between rendered and captured views. This yields high-fidelity, *decoupled* 3D assets for the robotic arm and manipulated objects, making them independently editable.

3. **Object Pose Estimation and Trajectory Processing:**
 - **Object Pose Tracking:** Multi-view RGB-D sequences are processed using FoundationPose to estimate object poses (`T_o,t`). These estimates are then fused for robustness and consistency, resulting in a unified pose sequence in the robot base frame.
 - **PoseProcess Module:** A lightweight module for normalizing and recomposing object pose sequences. A "fix" operation attaches the object rigidly to the robot end-effector frame during manipulation. Crucially, this module allows for **object substitution**, where the same pose sequence can be applied to different object models, generating diverse task instances without new real-world data collection.
 - **Robot Kinematics:** The URDF model of the target robot is used with the recorded joint-angle sequences (`q_t`) to compute poses for each robot link (`T_ℓ,t`). These robot link poses are combined with object poses for subsequent 3DGS rendering.

4. **Data Augmentation under Gaussian Rendering:** The editable 3DGS representation is leveraged for massive data augmentation to improve policy robustness:
 - **Camera Viewpoint Augmentation:** Renders demonstrations from perturbed camera extrinsics.
 - **Color and Illumination Augmentation:** Applies random scaling to Gaussian color attributes and global/local brightness.
 - **Background Augmentation:** Composites diverse real-world images as backgrounds behind the geometry-consistent Gaussian foregrounds.
 - **Object Pose Augmentation:** Perturbs object poses/scales or substitutes objects with affordance-compatible alternatives, enabling trajectory reuse.

#### 3.3. C. Policy Module: Mask Adapter

To address residual visual domain shifts and improve policy generalization, ExoGS introduces the Mask Adapter, a lightweight plug-in module for visuomotor policies. It builds on an enhanced ACT backbone with a DINOv3 ViT encoder and LoRA fine-tuning.

1. **Motivation:** Standard 2D visuomotor policies often lack explicit interaction-centric inductive biases and are susceptible to spurious background correlations. The Mask Adapter aims to inject instance-level semantic constraints.

2. **Two-Stage Training:**
 - **Stage 1 (Semantic Segmentation Pretraining):** The visual encoder and a lightweight multi-scale segmentation head (`H_mask`) are fine-tuned using pixel-level semantic supervision generated by the 3DGS pipeline (which provides ground-truth masks for robot, objects, background). This step learns stable, transferable patch-level semantic labels (`ℓ`).
 - **Stage 2 (Mask-guided Token Modeling):** The learned patch-level labels (`ℓ`) are used to:
 - **Enhance Positional Encodings:** A learnable label embedding is added to the base positional encoding (`˜p = p + E_label(ℓ)`), providing semantic context.
 - **Impose Mask-guided Attention:** An additive attention mask (`A`) is constructed based on a predefined label relation set (`R`). This mask encourages or discourages token communication between patches based on their semantic labels (e.g., preventing background patches from excessively influencing robot/object patches), guiding attention towards interaction-relevant regions.

3. **Training Objective:** Stage 1 optimizes a cross-entropy loss for segmentation (`L_seg`). Stage 2 optimizes the original policy action loss (`L_act`) jointly with `L_seg` to stabilize semantic alignment. The Mask Adapter can still provide positional enhancement and attention constraints even if ground-truth masks are unavailable in the deployment environment, using its own Stage-1 predictions.

This comprehensive methodology, spanning hardware for precise data capture, 3DGS for scalable and editable digital twins, and a semantic-aware policy module, forms a robust Real-to-Sim-to-Real pipeline for advanced robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate the effectiveness of ExoGS in improving data collection efficiency and policy generalization across various manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. A. Evaluation of Demonstration Data Collection Efficiency

- **Faster Collection:** AirExo-3 consistently enables faster data collection compared to teleoperation, with the advantage increasing significantly for more complex tasks (e.g., Unscrew Bottle Cap). For instance, the average time per successful demonstration was notably lower with AirExo-3.
- **Higher Success Ratios:** AirExo-3 achieved higher success ratios across all tasks (100% for Pick and Place/Pick Place Close, 87% for Unscrew Bottle Cap) compared to teleoperation (92.3%, 83%, 17% respectively). This indicates more reliable and consistent demonstrations, especially for contact-rich tasks where teleoperation often resulted in failures and collisions.
- **Reduced Variance:** AirExo-3 showed reduced inter-subject variance in collection times, indicating improved consistency and ease of use across different operators.

#### 4.2. B. Policy Performance without Data Augmentation

- **Initial Performance Gap:** Policies trained on non-augmented ExoGS-generated data generally underperformed those trained on teleoperation data for simpler tasks (Pick and Place, Pick Place Close). This is attributed to the residual visual gap between rendered synthetic observations and real-world ones.
- **Advantage in Complex Tasks:** For the Unscrew Bottle Cap task, ExoGS-trained policies (24% success) significantly outperformed teleoperation-trained policies (8% success). This highlights that for inherently difficult tasks, the higher quality and consistency of AirExo-3 demonstrations outweigh the visual fidelity benefits of real teleoperation data, which often contains noisy and inconsistent trajectories.
- **Benefit of Synthetic Data Expansion:** For the "Pick and Place (New Object)" scenario, where objects were replaced by entirely new digital assets, ExoGS-trained policies achieved 76% success, while teleoperation-trained policies failed entirely (0%). This demonstrates the powerful scalability and generalization potential of the 3DGS-based data generation pipeline for creating diverse datasets at low cost.

#### 4.3. C. Policy Performance with Data Augmentation

- **Substantial Generalization Improvement:** Data augmentation using the editable 3DGS representation substantially improved policy generalization. Policies trained with augmented data consistently outperformed policies trained on non-augmented synthetic data and even real-world teleoperation data, especially under variations in object color, background, and lighting.
- **Effectiveness in Challenging Settings:** In scenarios with varied object colors, backgrounds, and lighting, policies trained solely on real-world data often failed completely, whereas augmented data significantly boosted performance.
- **Limitations in Physical Interaction-Dominated Tasks:** For the Unscrew Bottle Cap task, data augmentation offered limited improvement. Failures in this task were primarily due to strong physical constraints (e.g., slippage, jamming) inherent in the threaded coupling, rather than visual perception issues. This suggests that the quality of the underlying captured motion trajectories remains critical for such tasks.

#### 4.4. D. Ablation Study of Data Augmentation Methods

- **Viewpoint and Color Jitter as Key Contributors:** An ablation study on the Pick Place Close task showed that viewpoint variation and color jitter provided the largest performance gains. Color jitter, which included randomized background textures and lighting, yielded the strongest overall improvement, effectively addressing appearance and illumination discrepancies.
- **Marginal Benefit of Pose Augmentation:** Object pose augmentation offered only marginal benefits, implying that the inherent diversity in collected object poses was sufficient, and pose perturbations alone did not address the primary visual domain gap as effectively as appearance or viewpoint changes.

#### 4.5. E. Effect of Mask Adapter on Policy Performance

- **Effective Sim-to-Real Gap Mitigation:** The Mask Adapter proved highly effective at mitigating the sim-to-real gap. Policies equipped with the Mask Adapter, trained on non-augmented ExoGS data, even outperformed teleoperation baselines in standard and color-varied scenarios.
- **Robust Segmentation:** The Mask Adapter achieved accurate and automatic segmentation of target objects, background, and the robot, demonstrating strong generalization to novel objects and backgrounds not seen during training.
- **Robustness to Object Color:** Policies with the Mask Adapter showed a notable degree of robustness to object color variations.
- **Sensitivity to Severe Visual Shifts:** While effective, the Mask Adapter's performance still degraded under more severe visual conditions, such as drastic background changes and colored lighting that adversely affected the segmentation model. However, considering its lightweight design and role as a complementary module, its performance was deemed satisfactory.

In conclusion, ExoGS demonstrates that a combined hardware-software approach can significantly enhance data collection efficiency and policy robustness. The open-source AirExo-3 hardware, coupled with 3DGS-based 4D data generation and augmentation, along with the semantic guidance from the Mask Adapter, provides a powerful framework for advancing robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ExoGS framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Democratization and Scalability of Data Collection:** By introducing AirExo-3, a low-cost, open-source, and highly accurate passive exoskeleton, ExoGS drastically lowers the barrier to entry for collecting high-quality robot manipulation data. This "robot-free" approach liberates researchers and practitioners from the high cost and complexity of deploying real robots for data collection, enabling significantly larger datasets to be gathered more efficiently and consistently by a wider user base. This scalability is critical for the progression of data-hungry imitation learning algorithms.

2. **Bridging the "Interaction Gap" in R2S2R:** While existing R2S2R methods focused primarily on visual fidelity for static scenes, ExoGS uniquely captures and reconstructs *dynamic interactions* in 4D (spatiotemporal) sequences. This capability is crucial for contact-rich manipulation tasks where accurate interaction data is paramount. By transferring both visual environments and physical interactions to simulation, ExoGS provides a more complete digital twin, reducing a previously overlooked dimension of the sim-to-real gap.

3. **Harnessing 3DGS for Geometry-Consistent Augmentation:** The innovative use of editable 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assets for generating diverse data is a key contribution. This allows for geometry-consistent augmentation across viewpoints, lighting, backgrounds, and object poses, which is far more powerful and realistic than traditional 2D augmentations. The ability to swap objects and reuse trajectories further boosts data diversity at minimal cost, leading to policies with enhanced generalization capabilities.

4. **Enhanced Policy Robustness through Semantic Awareness:** The proposed Mask Adapter is a clever, lightweight addition that injects instance-level semantic understanding into visuomotor policies. By guiding attention to task-relevant regions, it effectively mitigates visual domain shifts and improves policy robustness. This approach offers a valuable alternative or complement to extensive data augmentation, making policies more resilient to real-world variations.

5. **Accelerated Research and Development:** ExoGS provides a holistic pipeline that simplifies and accelerates the entire robot learning workflow—from data acquisition to robust policy deployment. Its open-source nature (both hardware and code) encourages community adoption and further innovation, potentially leading to faster development cycles for new manipulation skills and applications.

6. **Broader Applications:** The principles demonstrated by ExoGS could extend beyond manipulation to other areas of robotics requiring scalable interaction data, such as assembly, dexterous manipulation, or even human-robot collaboration. The ability to accurately capture human intent and interaction patterns could fuel advances in areas like learning from demonstration for complex industrial tasks or assistive robotics.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges that the current framework relies on the rigid-body assumption of 3DGS, limiting its ability to model deformable objects with variable geometry. Future work will likely focus on incorporating deformable object representations within the 3DGS framework to expand the range of manipulable objects. Additionally, improving the automation level of the entire data generation pipeline, perhaps through more advanced capture or reconstruction techniques, will be a natural progression.

In conclusion, ExoGS offers a pragmatic yet scientifically rigorous solution to long-standing challenges in robotic manipulation. By making high-quality, scalable, and robust data collection accessible, it has the potential to significantly impact the development of more capable and generalizable robotic systems.
