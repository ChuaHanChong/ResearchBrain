# TempoFit: Plug-and-Play Layer-Wise Temporal KV Memory for Long-Horizon Vision-Language-Action Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TempoFit is a training-free retrofit that gives frozen single-frame VLA policies temporal memory by caching prefix attention K/V at select intermediate layers, retrieving history via K-to-K matching with a recency bias, and injecting it through norm-preserving residual loading, boosting LIBERO-LONG success rate by up to +4.0 points with near-zero latency cost.

## Problem

- Frame-stacking history injection scales visual tokens and attention compute, adding near-duplicate pixels and raising inference latency substantially.
- Learned temporal interfaces (retrieval-and-fusion memory modules) require additional training or fine-tuning and are not compatible with frozen pretrained backbones.
- Most VLA inference is memoryless and Markovian, causing repeated actions, missed steps, and cross-stage discontinuity under occlusion or state aliasing.

## Method

- **Layer-Wise FIFO KV Cache** stores prefix-time key/value tensors at a small subset of intermediate transformer layers, avoiding input token expansion.
- **K-to-K Retrieval** performs parameter-free ==address-space matching== using current prefix keys as queries against cached historical keys within the same key space.
- **Frame-Gap Temporal Bias** adds a fixed, ==ALiBi==-inspired recency penalty to retrieval logits, and **Norm-Preserving Residual Loading** injects retrieved context while rescaling to original norm.

## Results

- On LIBERO-LONG, TempoFit raises pi-0.5 from **92.6%** to **96.6%** and QwenGR00T from **90.8%** to **94.4%** average success rate across 500 trials.
- On CALVIN, average completed task length improves from **3.78** to **3.84** (D-D) and **3.83** to **3.87** (ABC-D), with gains concentrated on later instructions.
- Ablations show KV memory alone gives **+1.2** points but FGTB adds **+2.8** more, while concatenation injection collapses performance to **0.8%** versus **96.6%** for norm-preserving residual loading.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prefix attention K/V already function as a model-native, content-addressable memory, so temporal context can be added without new tokens or parameters.
> - Restricting memory to intermediate layers matters: enabling memory at all layers or only shallow/deep layers substantially degrades performance versus intermediate-only.
> - A fixed, interpretable recency bias can substitute for a learned gating mechanism to keep training-free retrieval present-dominant and suppress stale-context interference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have gained prominence in robotic manipulation by leveraging large pre-trained vision-language backbones to map visual-linguistic inputs to robotic actions. Despite progress in single-step manipulation, a significant challenge remains: most VLA models operate in a largely memoryless fashion, making decisions based solely on the current observation and instruction. This approach implicitly assumes a Markovian environment, where the current state is sufficient to predict the next action. However, real-world robotic tasks are frequently non-Markovian, characterized by partial observability, occlusion, state aliasing (visually similar but semantically different states), and subtle post-action visual changes. Such conditions lead to failure modes like repeated actions, skipped steps, and lack of continuity across task stages.

Previous efforts to incorporate temporal awareness into VLAs generally fall into two categories. One approach involves expanding the observation context, typically by stacking a short history of raw frames or using video-style encoders. While straightforward, this method is often inefficient. It increases the number of visual tokens, leading to higher computational costs and inference latency. Furthermore, much of the added information comprises near-duplicate pixels, introducing redundancy that can obscure critical task-relevant dynamics. Examples include Octo and GR-2, which often necessitate retraining with temporally structured inputs, thus limiting their plug-and-play adaptability for established single-frame backbones.

A second category of approaches introduces explicit temporal interfaces. These methods encode historical information into compact representations, injecting them through retrieval and fusion mechanisms. Examples include ContextVLA, MemoryVLA, and HAMLET. While these methods mitigate raw-frame redundancy, they often require learning new interfaces or modules that are not part of the original single-frame inference graph. Consequently, they typically demand additional training or fine-tuning, which can be computationally expensive, time-consuming, and may disrupt the carefully learned representations of powerful pre-trained backbones. This limits their direct "plug-and-play" applicability to strong, frozen VLA policies.

TempoFit aims to address these limitations by offering a training-free, inference-time temporal retrofit. It seeks to inject history awareness into frozen VLA backbones without expanding the input context, introducing new trainable modules, or requiring additional training. This positions TempoFit as a solution that leverages the existing computational structures of pre-trained models to enhance their temporal reasoning capabilities efficiently and adaptably, thus bridging a gap in the current landscape of VLA research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the temporal consistency and long-horizon manipulation capabilities of pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. The authors aim to achieve this through a training-free, inference-time mechanism that allows VLA models to leverage historical context without modifying their existing parameters or input structures.

The motivation for TempoFit arises from several critical observations regarding current VLA models:
- **Memoryless Inference in Non-Markovian Settings:** Most VLA models operate in a "memoryless" fashion, processing only the current observation and instruction. This design is problematic in real-world robotic tasks, which are frequently non-Markovian. Scenarios involving occlusion, state aliasing (e.g., visually identical objects requiring different actions based on prior interactions), or subtle visual changes post-action often render the current frame insufficient for correct action determination. This leads to common failure modes such as repeating actions, skipping necessary steps, or exhibiting discontinuity between task stages.
- **Inefficiencies of Existing Temporal Modeling Approaches:**
 - **Frame Stacking:** While simple, stacking raw frames to provide history introduces significant inefficiencies. It increases the number of visual tokens, thereby escalating computational load and inference latency. Much of this additional visual data can be redundant, comprising near-duplicate pixels that potentially obscure crucial task-relevant dynamics rather than providing meaningful temporal context.
 - **Learned Temporal Interfaces:** Approaches that learn new modules for encoding and injecting history (e.g., retrieval-and-fusion mechanisms) require additional training or fine-tuning. This process can be costly, time-consuming, and may compromise the pre-trained weights of powerful VLA backbones, potentially breaking their original single-frame inference graph or leading to catastrophic forgetting. These methods are generally not directly plug-and-play for frozen, pre-trained models.
- **Gap in Current Solutions:** A clear need exists for a temporal enhancement that can retrofit strong, pre-trained VLAs with history awareness without incurring the costs of increased input context length, new trainable modules, or additional training. Such a solution would enable more scalable deployment of advanced VLA policies.

To address these motivations, TempoFit's key objectives include:
- **Training-Free Operation:** The module must integrate with frozen VLA policies without requiring any parameter updates or fine-tuning.
- **Preservation of Original Inference Graph:** It should avoid altering the input tokenization, tensor shapes, or masks, ensuring the original single-frame inference graph and action head can function transparently.
- **Efficient Memory Utilization:** Leverage existing model-native internal attention states (specifically, keys and values, K/V) as a compact carrier of past context, analogous to KV-caches in large language models, to minimize new computational overhead.
- **Controlled Context Injection:** Introduce historical context in a manner that remains present-dominant, preventing stale or irrelevant information from unduly influencing current decisions. This requires a mechanism to prioritize recent information.
- **Scalability and Real-time Performance:** Maintain near-real-time inference latency and minimal memory footprint, essential for practical robotic control.

By achieving these objectives, TempoFit aims to unlock the full potential of powerful pre-trained VLAs for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in real-world non-Markovian environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TempoFit introduces a training-free, inference-time module designed to inject temporal consistency into frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies by reusing the backbone's internal attention state. The approach operates on the principle that prefix attention Keys (K) and Values (V) within a Transformer-based VLA backbone already form a model-native, content-addressable runtime state.

The methodology can be broken down into three main stages:
1. **Memory Write: Layer-Wise FIFO KV Cache:**
 - **Concept:** Instead of storing raw frames or learning external memory interfaces, TempoFit caches the K/V pairs generated during the vision-language encoding phase at selected intermediate layers of the Transformer backbone. This approach is motivated by the observation that Transformer representations are hierarchically organized, with intermediate layers capturing transferable features.
 - **Mechanism:** For a chosen subset of intermediate layers, denoted as L_mem, a First-In, First-Out (FIFO) buffer of fixed capacity `C` is maintained. At each timestep `t`, after the K and V projections for the prefix tokens (visual observations and language instructions) are computed (before Rotary Positional Embeddings, RoPE), they are stored in the buffer along with their timestep `t`. If the buffer exceeds capacity `C`, the oldest entry is evicted. Only K/V for prefix tokens are cached, avoiding expansion of the input sequence with new tokens.

2. **K-to-K Retrieval: Memory Update via Address-Space Matching:**
 - **Concept:** Leveraging the established understanding of attention mechanisms as content-addressable memory, TempoFit retrieves relevant historical context by matching the current step's prefix keys against the cached historical keys.
 - **Mechanism:** For each memory-enabled layer `l`, the historical prefix keys and values are concatenated to form `K_hist_l` and `V_hist_l`. The current prefix keys `K^(t)_l` act as queries. Per-head logits are computed through a key-to-key similarity dot product: `A_kk_l,h = (K^(t)_l,h * (K_hist_l,h)^T) / sqrt(d_k)`. This operation reuses the Transformer's existing addressing metric, `W_K`, ensuring compatibility with the frozen backbone's attention geometry. A softmax function is applied to these logits to obtain weights (`W_l`), which are then used to read out the context keys (`K_ctx_l = W_l K_hist_l`) and context values (`V_ctx_l = W_l V_hist_l`). The retrieval is performed on pre-RoPE projections, making it primarily content-driven. This approach is parameter-free, layer-local, and model-native, interpreting retrieval as an associative memory read within the key space.

3. **Time-Biased Retrieval: Frame-Gap Temporal Bias (FGTB):**
 - **Concept:** To prevent stale or irrelevant historical context from dominating current decisions in a training-free setting, TempoFit introduces an explicit recency prior.
 - **Mechanism:** A Frame-Gap Temporal Bias (FGTB) is added to the K-to-K retrieval logits. This additive linear bias is defined as `Bias_l,h(t, τ) = -β * m_h * |t - τ| * α_S`, where `t` is the current timestep, `τ` is a historical timestep, `β` controls the decay strength, `m_h` is a head-wise slope schedule (inspired by ALiBi), and `α_S` scales frame gaps to token scale. This bias enforces a monotonic decay of retrieval scores with increasing temporal distance, ensuring that more recent historical frames are prioritized while older, potentially less relevant, information is down-weighted.

4. **KV Injection: Norm-Preserving Residual Loading:**
 - **Concept:** After retrieving the historical context (`K_ctx_l`, `V_ctx_l`), it must be seamlessly integrated into the current step's K/V state for processing by the frozen self-attention mechanism.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of concatenating the retrieved features as "virtual tokens" (which would alter attended length, softmax normalization, and computational costs), TempoFit uses a residual loading approach: `K_tilde^(t)_l = K^(t)_l + K_ctx_l` and `V_tilde^(t)_l = V^(t)_l + V_ctx_l`. This additive update preserves tensor shapes, masks, and tokenization. To prevent distribution shifts that could destabilize the frozen backbone's softmax, a norm-preserving rescaling is applied. The fused K/V tensors are rescaled to match the `ℓ2` norm of the original current-step K/V: `K_tilde^(t)_l ← K_tilde^(t)_l * (||K^(t)_l||_max / (||K_tilde^(t)_l|| + ε))`. This ensures that the injection acts as a directional update, steering the effective K/V associations without inflating or deflating their scale.

This integrated methodology allows TempoFit to enhance VLA policies with temporal awareness by operating entirely within the frozen backbone's internal attention mechanisms, offering a computationally efficient and architecturally consistent solution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides quantitative and qualitative evidence for TempoFit's effectiveness across various benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks.

**A. Overall Performance (RQ1)**

- **LIBERO-LONG Benchmark:** TempoFit demonstrates notable improvements on this benchmark, which involves ten multi-subgoal manipulation tasks.
 - For the π0.5 backbone, TempoFit increased the average success rate from 92.6% to 96.6% (an absolute improvement of 4.0%).
 - For the QwenGR00T backbone, the average success rate improved from 90.8% to 94.4% (an absolute improvement of 3.6%).
 - These results place TempoFit's performance competitively with or even surpassing representative training-based temporal models such as MemoryVLA (93.4%) and HiF-VLA (96.4%). The improvements were particularly pronounced in subgoals requiring strict cross-stage temporal association, such as "Put both pots on stove," where π0.5's success rate surged from 58.0% to 84.0%.
- **CALVIN Benchmark:** TempoFit also improved performance on long-horizon sequential execution tasks in the CALVIN D-D and ABC-D settings.
 - In the in-domain D-D setting, TempoFit improved the average task length for QwenGR00T from 3.78 to 3.84.
 - In the cross-domain ABC-D setting, TempoFit improved the average task length for RLinf π0.5 from 3.83 to 3.87.
 - The gains were observed to concentrate on later instructions (tasks 3-5), indicating improved long-horizon retention and better handling of temporal credit assignment and partial observability, rather than merely enhancing short-horizon action selection.

**B. Inference Efficiency and Horizon Scalability (RQ2)**

TempoFit exhibits minimal overhead, which is crucial for real-time robotic control.
- **Latency:** For a history capacity of 8 frames, TempoFit increased latency from the 1-frame baseline of 71.2 ms to 74.4 ms (1.04x). Even with 32 frames, latency was 86.8 ms (1.21x).
- **Peak Memory:** For 8 frames, TempoFit increased peak memory from 6,396 MB to 6,600 MB (1.03x). For 32 frames, it was 7,030 MB (1.10x).
- **Comparison to Frame Stacking:** In contrast, naive frame stacking for 8 frames resulted in significantly higher latency (176.3 ms, 2.48x) and peak memory (45,980 MB, 7.19x), demonstrating TempoFit's superior efficiency.

**C. Ablation Studies (RQ3)**

A detailed ablation study using the π0.5 backbone on LIBERO-LONG confirmed the contribution of TempoFit's core components:
- **Component Contribution:** Adding KV memory alone provided a modest improvement (+1.2% over baseline), but the inclusion of Frame-Gap Temporal Bias (FGTB) significantly amplified this gain, boosting performance to 96.6% (+2.8% over KV-only). This supports FGTB's role in suppressing stale context and maintaining present-dominance.
- **Retrieval Strategy:** K-to-K retrieval (the proposed method) achieved 96.6%, while a Q-to-K alternative yielded a lower 93.3%, indicating the importance of matching within the backbone's native key-address space for compatibility with frozen weights.
- **Injection Strategy:** Naive concatenation of retrieved features resulted in performance collapse (0.8% success rate). Residual loading was more stable, but removing norm-preserving rescaling caused a significant drop (from 96.6% to 90.2%). Norm-preserving residual loading proved crucial for maintaining inference stability under frozen weights.
- **Layer Selection:** Activating memory across all layers substantially degraded performance (74.2%). Restricting memory to only bottom (59.8%) or top (89.4%) layers was also suboptimal. The proposed strategy of enabling memory only at selected intermediate layers achieved the best performance (96.6%), balancing temporal continuity with minimal interference.
- **History Capacity (C):** A moderate history length (C=8) achieved the peak performance of 96.6%. Both smaller (C=4, 95.2%) and larger (C=16, 96.2%; C=32, 95.2%) capacities showed slight decreases, suggesting diminishing returns and potential for increased redundancy or staleness with excessively large caches.

**D. Real-World Robotic Platforms (RQ4)**

TempoFit was evaluated on a Realman RM-65B robot across three long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- The baseline π0.5 policy showed performance degradation as the number of subtasks increased, often exhibiting stalls or repeated actions due to state aliasing or subtle visual changes.
- TempoFit improved full-task success rates: +9.5% on Task 1 (57.1% to 66.7%), +4.8% on Task 2 (81.0% to 85.7%), and +14.3% on Task 3 (66.7% to 81.0%), leading to an average improvement of +9.5% across all real-world tasks. This indicates TempoFit's ability to maintain action consistency and robustly track temporal states in practical robotic settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TempoFit contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by providing a practical and efficient solution for enhancing the temporal reasoning capabilities of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Robust Long-Horizon Manipulation:** By retrofitting VLA policies with history awareness, TempoFit directly addresses the limitations of memoryless inference in non-Markovian environments. This allows robots to perform long-horizon tasks more reliably, reducing failures due to occlusion, state aliasing, and subtle visual changes that are common in real-world scenarios. This capability is critical for deploying robots in complex, multi-stage tasks.
- **Training-Free and Plug-and-Play Integration:** A key innovation is its training-free nature. TempoFit does not require re-training or fine-tuning of the base VLA backbone, preserving the extensively learned representations and generalization capabilities of powerful pre-trained models. This "plug-and-play" characteristic significantly reduces the computational cost and development time associated with integrating temporal awareness, making it highly scalable and accessible for researchers and practitioners.
- **High Inference Efficiency:** TempoFit achieves its temporal enhancements with minimal overhead in terms of inference latency and memory consumption. By leveraging model-native KV-cache mechanisms and selective layer activation, it avoids the inefficiencies of frame stacking, which typically leads to substantial increases in computational resources. This efficiency is crucial for maintaining real-time control in closed-loop robotic systems.
- **Broad Applicability and Generalizability:** The method has demonstrated consistent improvements across different VLA backbones (π0.5, QwenGR00T) and diverse benchmarks (LIBERO-LONG, CALVIN), as well as on real-world robotic platforms. This suggests a generalizable approach that can benefit a wide range of VLA models and robotic applications without task-specific modifications.
- **Unlocking Pre-trained Model Potential:** TempoFit allows the full potential of powerful pre-trained vision-language models to be leveraged in dynamic, sequential robotic tasks. It extends the utility of these models beyond single-step decisions, making them more adaptable to complex, real-world manipulation challenges.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While the current implementation uses fixed parameters for memory management, the work lays a foundation for future research into more adaptive memory selection, automatic layer discovery, and integration with higher-level planning or recovery mechanisms.

In conclusion, TempoFit offers a substantial step forward in making VLA-based robotic systems more intelligent, robust, and autonomous in challenging long-horizon tasks by efficiently and effectively incorporating temporal memory into their decision-making processes.
