# RiskPO: Risk-based Policy Optimization via Verifiable Reward for LLM Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> RISKPO presents a risk-based policy optimization method for large language model (LLM) post-training, employing a Mixed Value-at-Risk (MVaR) objective and a reward bundling scheme. This approach consistently improves performance across diverse reasoning tasks, achieving an average +2.78 absolute improvement on hard mathematical benchmarks compared to strong baselines and effectively mitigating policy entropy collapse.

## Problem

- Mean-based RL with verifiable reward (RLVR) methods, such as GRPO, frequently suffer from rapid policy entropy collapse, leading to premature convergence and limiting LLMs' ability to acquire new knowledge or expand genuine reasoning capacity.
- The mean-based objective is misaligned with improving reasoning, as it tends to overemphasize common solutions and neglects rare but informative reasoning trajectories, often yielding zero gradients on consistently incorrect problems.
- Feedback in RLVR is typically a single binary success/failure signal per question, offering limited distributional information and making it hard to construct rich, informative gradient signals.

## Method

- Introduces a Mixed ==Value-at-Risk== (MVaR) objective that emphasizes lower-tail samples of the reward distribution, encouraging learning from challenging instances and preventing overconfident convergence.
- Proposes a bundling scheme that groups multiple questions into 'bundles' to transform sparse binary rewards into richer, more informative 'bundle scores', providing stable gradient signals even for difficult problems.
- Develops a clipped MVaR objective for stable policy updates and provides theoretical justification demonstrating that risk-averse updates mitigate entropy collapse and promote exploration.

## Results

- RiskPO achieved an average Pass@1 score of **46.65** on hard-level mathematical reasoning benchmarks, representing a +**2.78** absolute improvement over DAPO (**43.87**) and +**6.24** over GRPO (**40.41**).
- Analysis of Pass@k metrics demonstrated that RiskPO expands the LLM's reasoning boundary by enabling the discovery of genuinely new solution strategies, not just improved sampling efficiency.
- Empirical training dynamics showed RiskPO maintained substantially higher policy entropy throughout training compared to GRPO, confirming its ability to mitigate entropy collapse and foster sustained exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing for distributional risk, specifically focusing on low-reward outcomes, significantly enhances an LLM's ability to tackle difficult problems and genuinely expand its reasoning capabilities.
> - Risk-averse policy updates inherently maintain higher policy entropy, which is crucial for sustained exploration and preventing premature convergence often observed in mean-based RL methods.
> - Aggregating sparse binary rewards into 'bundle scores' provides a more continuous and informative feedback signal, enabling effective gradient-based learning even when individual questions consistently yield zero reward.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become a significant paradigm for the post-training of large language models (LLMs), enabling them to acquire complex skills by leveraging diverse training targets and feedback mechanisms. Within this field, RL with verifiable reward (RLVR) is recognized as an effective approach for enhancing the reasoning abilities of LLMs. Unlike RL from human feedback, RLVR utilizes objective and often binary reward signals, providing clear and direct optimization feedback.

A prominent method in RLVR is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which has been established as a baseline due to its efficiency in discarding structures redundant for standard RL tasks. Several variants of GRPO have since been proposed to further improve its performance and stability. However, a known limitation of these mean-based RLVR methods, including GRPO, is the phenomenon of "entropy collapse." This issue involves a rapid decrease in policy entropy during early training stages, leading to premature convergence and a plateau in performance. This is attributed to the models becoming overconfident, reducing exploration prematurely, and consequently failing to acquire new knowledge or genuinely expand their intrinsic reasoning capacity. Improvements observed often reflect more efficient sampling of known answers rather than fundamental advancements in reasoning skills.

This paper introduces a novel perspective by integrating risk measures into the RLVR objective, a concept drawn from the field of risk-sensitive RL, which aims to shape the entire reward distribution rather than solely optimizing its mean. While risk-sensitive RL has been explored in various contexts, its application to address the specific challenges of entropy collapse and limited reasoning gains in LLM post-training, particularly within the RLVR framework, represents a distinct contribution. By proposing a risk-based approach, the authors aim to mitigate the identified limitations of prevailing mean-based methods and foster more robust and exploratory training dynamics for LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from observed limitations in current mean-based RL with verifiable reward (RLVR) methods for post-training large language models (LLMs), particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and its variants. The authors identify two key issues:

1. **Entropy Collapse and Limited Reasoning Gains:** Prevailing mean-based methods, such as GRPO, frequently suffer from rapid entropy collapse early in the training process. This leads to premature convergence and a performance plateau, limiting subsequent improvements. The authors argue that this phenomenon curtails exploration capacity, causing the model to become overconfident and hindering its ability to acquire new knowledge or discover novel reasoning paths. Consequently, LLMs trained with these methods may not genuinely expand their intrinsic reasoning abilities but rather become more efficient at sampling known correct answers, without pushing the boundaries of their problem-solving capacity.

2. **Misalignment of Mean-Based Objectives with Reasoning Improvement:** The authors contend that the mean-based objective used in GRPO is inherently misaligned with the goal of improving reasoning ability. This objective tends to overemphasize common, high-probability output sequences, while neglecting rare but potentially informative reasoning trajectories. This can lead to premature convergence on easier problems. Furthermore, in scenarios where the model consistently generates incorrect answers for a particular question, the estimated GRPO advantage can collapse to zero, providing no learning signal on the model's weakest areas. This over-exploitation of gradients on simpler problems results in marginal performance gains, as optimization largely reinforces existing knowledge rather than guiding the model toward solving more challenging problems.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Introduce Risk Measures into the Training Objective:** To move beyond mean-based optimization, the authors propose substituting classical mean-based objectives with principled risk measures. This is intended to encourage the model to explore difficult problems and enhance its reasoning abilities by amplifying gradient signals from low-reward answers.

2. **Design a Mixed Value-at-Risk (MVaR) Objective:** Specifically, the paper aims to develop MVaR, an objective that integrates weighted attention over multiple regions of the reward distribution. The goal of MVaR is to amplify gradient signals on challenging instances, thereby preventing overconfident convergence and encouraging more robust learning.

3. **Propose a Bundling Scheme for Feedback Signal Enrichment:** Recognizing that the binary reward for single questions offers limited distributional information, a goal is to group multiple questions into "bundles." This aggregation aims to transform sparse binary feedback into a richer, more informative distribution over bundle scores, facilitating finer distinctions between performance levels and avoiding zero gradients on difficult questions.

4. **Provide Theoretical Justification for Entropy Preservation:** A crucial objective is to theoretically demonstrate that risk-averse updates can alleviate entropy collapse and promote exploration, contrasting with the behavior of mean-based methods.

5. **Achieve Consistent and Significant Empirical Improvements:** The ultimate objective is to empirically validate that the proposed RiskPO method consistently outperforms GRPO and its variants across various benchmarks, including mathematical reasoning, multi-modal reasoning, and code generation, on both Pass@1 and Pass@k metrics. This aims to demonstrate that risk-based optimization provides a rigorous and effective paradigm for enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors formalize the post-training problem in RLVR as maximizing the expected reward, $J(\theta) = E_{x \sim D, y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}[R(y)]$, where $R(y)$ is a binary (correct/incorrect) reward signal. GRPO, a prominent baseline, uses sequence-level standardized rewards computed within a group of responses to reduce variance. However, this method remains mean-based.

The core of the proposed methodology, Risk-based Policy Optimization (RiskPO), involves two main components: a novel risk-sensitive objective and a bundling scheme for reward signal enrichment.

1. **Risk-sensitive Policy Optimization with Mixed Value-at-Risk (MVaR):**
- **RVaR Foundation:** RiskPO builds upon the concept of Range Value-at-Risk (RVaR), which captures the average performance within a specified quantile interval $[F^{-1}_\theta(\alpha), F^{-1}_\theta(\beta)]$ of the reward distribution $F_\theta(\cdot)$. The gradient of RVaR is derived, indicating how policy parameters should be updated to influence the reward distribution within this range.
- **MVaR Objective:** To shape the overall reward distribution more effectively, the authors introduce Mixed Value-at-Risk (MVaR). This objective integrates metrics over multiple distributional segments, specifically emphasizing lower-tail samples. The MVaR objective is defined as $J^{MVaR}_{\omega, \alpha:\beta}(\theta) = (1+\omega)\int^{F^{-1}_\theta(\alpha)}_{F^{-1}_\theta(0)} rdF_\theta(r) + \int^{F^{-1}_\theta(\beta)}_{F^{-1}_\theta(\alpha)} rdF_\theta(r)$, where $\omega \ge 0$ controls the emphasis on tail samples. This formulation allows RiskPO to prioritize learning from challenging instances that yield low rewards.

2. **Reward Signal Enrichment via Bundling Scheme:**
- **Addressing Sparse Feedback:** The binary nature of rewards for single questions provides limited distributional information, often leading to zero gradients when all responses are incorrect. To overcome this, RiskPO proposes grouping several questions into a "bundle" $X = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^B$.
- **Bundle Score:** For each bundle, a "bundle score" $R_B = \sum_{i=1}^B R(y_i)$ is calculated, which transforms the sparse binary feedback into a more informative, continuous-like distribution. This enables finer distinctions in performance and ensures a meaningful gradient signal even for difficult problems.
- **Gradient Estimation with Bundles:** To calculate gradients, for each of the $B$ questions in a bundle, $G$ responses are sampled. These $G \times B$ responses are then permuted to form $G$ non-overlapping bundles, ensuring each response is used exactly once across bundles. The MVaR-based advantage is then calculated at the bundle level.

3. **Clipped MVaR Objective and Policy Update:**
- **Trust-Region Style Update:** To ensure stable improvement, a trust-region style update with clipping and sequence-level importance sampling is adopted, similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The final loss for backpropagation is a clipped MVaR objective: $J^{clip}_{MVaR}(\theta) = E_{X,Y,\{\xi_i\}} \left[ \frac{1}{G} \sum_{j=1}^G \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B \min \left( s^j_i(\theta) A^{(j)}, \text{clip}(s^j_i(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) A^{(j)} \right) \right]$, where $s^j_i(\theta)$ is the sequence-wise importance sampling ratio and $A^{(j)}$ is the bundle-wise advantage under the MVaR objective.
- **Quantile Tracking:** The quantiles $F^{-1}_\theta(\alpha)$ and $F^{-1}_\theta(\beta)$ are tracked online using a two-timescale stochastic approximation algorithm. The model parameters are updated using standard gradient descent based on the derived gradient of the clipped MVaR objective.

4. **Theoretical Analysis of Entropy Mechanism:**
- **Entropy Dynamics:** The authors analyze the change in policy entropy per step, linking it to the covariance between the advantage value and the log-probability of the output. Proposition 1 states that a positive correlation leads to an entropy decrease.
- **Assumption 1:** A key assumption is made that the conditional log-probability of output ($\psi(r)$) is non-decreasing for high rewards and non-increasing for low rewards, suggesting that the model is confident but potentially wrong on hard problems and well-calibrated on easy ones. This assumption is empirically validated.
- **Entropy Preservation:** Theorem 2 demonstrates that the covariance between MVaR-based advantages and output log-probabilities is smaller than that of mean-based methods. This theoretical result suggests that RiskPO, by focusing on the lower tail of the reward distribution and clipping gradients for well-learned problems, constitutes a relatively high-entropy update scheme, thereby mitigating entropy collapse and promoting exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate RiskPO covered a range of tasks, including mathematical reasoning (hard and easy levels), multi-modal reasoning, and code generation, across more than ten datasets.

1. **Consistent Performance Improvement on Hard-Level Mathematical Reasoning:**
- RiskPO consistently outperformed all baseline methods on six hard-level mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME25, AIME24, AMC, MATH500, Minerva, Olympiad).
- It achieved an average Pass@1 score of 46.65, representing a +2.78 absolute improvement over DAPO (43.87), the strongest baseline, and a +6.24 improvement over vanilla GRPO (40.41).
- The gains were particularly evident on the challenging AIME datasets, where RiskPO surpassed DAPO by approximately 6.7 percentage points (33.3 vs. 26.6). This suggests that emphasizing distributional risk leads to substantial improvements, especially on more difficult problems.

2. **Broad Applicability and Improvements on Diverse Tasks:**
- On easier mathematical reasoning tasks (MATH, GSM8K), multi-modal reasoning (Geometry3K), and code generation (LiveCodeBench), RiskPO maintained a measurable advantage over GRPO and DAPO.
- While the performance gaps on GSM8K were smaller due to its simplicity, RiskPO still yielded consistent improvements across the other benchmarks, indicating the broad applicability of the risk-sensitive objective.

3. **Expansion of Reasoning Boundary (Pass@k Metrics):**
- Analysis of Pass@k (k=1, 8, 16) learning curves on AMC and MATH500 showed a widening gap between RiskPO and GRPO as `k` increased.
- This pattern suggests that RiskPO is not merely improving sampling efficiency for problems the model could already solve (e.g., converting a low-probability success into a single-shot success) but is acquiring genuinely new solution strategies. RiskPO solved instances that GRPO could not, even after multiple attempts, implying a genuine expansion of the base model's reasoning capabilities.

4. **Mitigation of Entropy Collapse and Enhanced Exploration:**
- Training dynamics on the DAPOMATH-17K dataset revealed that while the mean reward curves for GRPO and RiskPO were almost indistinguishable, the lower-tail RVaR and MVaR curves demonstrated a pronounced advantage for RiskPO. This indicates superior performance on more challenging problems.
- Crucially, RiskPO maintained substantially higher entropy throughout training, whereas GRPO's entropy collapsed early. This empirical observation supports the theoretical claim that RiskPO mitigates entropy collapse, thereby fostering sustained exploration and improving the ability to tackle difficult instances.

5. **Ablation Studies Validating Design Choices:**
- **Risk Profile:** A comparison between risk-averse and risk-seeking versions showed that the risk-seeking objective led to sharp entropy collapse and limited long-term improvement, whereas the risk-averse version maintained stable entropy and continuous performance gains. This validates the design choice of a risk-averse objective.
- **Quantile Levels:** Deviations from the chosen quantile configuration (0.2, 0.8) consistently led to performance deterioration, especially when reducing emphasis on the lower tail or intensifying emphasis on the upper tail. This highlights the importance of carefully tuning these parameters to maintain a proper risk-averse stance.
- **Bundle Size:** Optimal performance was observed at a bundle size of B=5. Both excessively large bundles (diluting gradient signals) and overly small bundles (unstable quantile tracking) resulted in performance drops, with no bundling (B=1) showing the most significant degradation. This confirms the necessity and proper scaling of the bundling scheme for RiskPO's effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RiskPO represents a notable shift from mean-based to risk-based objectives in the post-training of large language models (LLMs) via verifiable rewards. This approach has several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By explicitly focusing on the lower tail of the reward distribution, RiskPO aims to improve the LLM's performance on challenging problems. The empirical results demonstrate consistent and significant gains across mathematical reasoning, multi-modal reasoning, and code generation tasks, particularly on highly difficult benchmarks. This suggests that RiskPO can genuinely expand the reasoning frontier of LLMs, enabling them to acquire new problem-solving strategies rather than merely refining existing ones or improving sampling efficiency.

2. **Mitigation of Entropy Collapse and Promotion of Exploration:** One of the most critical contributions is the theoretical and empirical demonstration that risk-averse updates alleviate entropy collapse. This is a prevalent issue in current RLVR methods that prematurely limits exploration. By maintaining higher policy entropy, RiskPO encourages sustained exploration of alternative reasoning paths, which is crucial for discovering novel solutions and robustly improving performance on complex tasks.

3. **Richer Feedback Signals via Bundling:** The bundling scheme addresses the limitation of sparse binary rewards for individual questions. By aggregating rewards across multiple questions, it creates a more informative, continuous-like signal. This enrichment of feedback is crucial for providing stable and meaningful gradient signals, especially for challenging problems where individual responses might consistently yield zero reward. This technique could be adopted in other RLVR settings where individual rewards are sparse or binary.

4. **Principled Framework for LLM Optimization:** RiskPO provides a rigorous theoretical foundation, deriving the gradients for its Mixed Value-at-Risk (MVaR) objective and analyzing its impact on entropy dynamics. This offers a principled alternative to purely engineering-driven optimizations in RLVR, providing a deeper understanding of why certain approaches might lead to more robust and capable LLMs.

5. **Broad Applicability Across Domains:** The consistent performance gains observed across diverse domains (mathematics, multi-modal, coding) indicate that the risk-based optimization paradigm is broadly applicable. This suggests that the principles behind RiskPO could be generalized to enhance LLM capabilities in various other complex reasoning and generation tasks where verifiable rewards are available.

6. **Future Research Directions:** This work opens avenues for further research into integrating more sophisticated risk measures, adapting risk profiles dynamically during training, and exploring the interplay between risk aversion and other RL regularization techniques. It also highlights the importance of distributional perspectives in LLM training, moving beyond simple mean optimization.

In summary, RiskPO offers a theoretically grounded and empirically validated method that addresses key limitations of existing LLM post-training techniques. Its ability to enhance genuine reasoning capacity, mitigate entropy collapse, and leverage enriched feedback signals positions it as a significant advancement in the field, with the potential to influence the development of more capable and robust large language models.
