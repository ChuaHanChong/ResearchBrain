# Learning Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation with Multifingered Hands via Visuomotor Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KTH Royal Institute of Technology developed a framework for learning dexterous in-hand manipulation, demonstrating an 85% success rate in unscrewing a bottle lid with a four-fingered robot hand using visuomotor diffusion policies and a wrist-mounted camera. Their work includes an AR-based teleoperation system for data collection and a data filtering strategy to enhance learning robustness.

## Problem

- Achieving human-like, fine-grained dexterity for in-hand manipulation with multifingered robot hands remains a significant challenge.
- Collecting high-quality expert demonstration data for complex dexterous tasks is difficult due to limitations in existing teleoperation systems, such as calibration drift or inadequate feedback.
- Suboptimal or inconsistent demonstrations within training datasets can negatively impact the performance of learned manipulation policies.

## Method

- A custom AR-based teleoperation system was developed, utilizing a ==Meta Quest== 3 for human hand tracking and sophisticated motion retargeting to the robotic Allegro Hand, for efficient collection of expert demonstrations.
- Visuomotor diffusion policies were employed, framing robot control as a conditional denoising diffusion process that learns from offline datasets of expert demonstrations.
- A two-stage outlier removal paradigm, based on HDBSCAN clustering and GLOSH scores, was introduced to filter out low-quality or anomalous demonstrations from the collected data.

## Results

- The most effective policy configuration, utilizing a wrist-mounted camera along with joint position and joint effort data, achieved an **85%** success rate in unscrewing a bottle lid.
- Removing joint effort information from the input modalities significantly reduced the policy's success rate to **30%**, underscoring its importance for maintaining a stable grasp.
- Ablation studies indicated that using only a wrist camera (**85%** success) or only a top camera (**70%** success) performed better than using both cameras simultaneously (**55%** success), suggesting potential redundancy or noise from combined visual inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprioceptive feedback, specifically joint effort information, is crucial for maintaining stable grasps and ensuring successful task completion in dexterous in-hand manipulation.
> - A single, well-placed wrist-mounted camera, combined with proprioceptive data (joint position and effort), can provide sufficient visual information for complex in-hand tasks, outperforming configurations that use multiple cameras.
> - Judicious removal of extreme outliers from demonstration datasets can help maintain or slightly improve policy performance by eliminating highly suboptimal data, but aggressive filtering risks removing valuable learning information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly dexterous in-hand manipulation, has been a longstanding challenge. Traditional approaches often relied on analytical methods or precomputed grasping strategies, which presented limitations in adaptability and generalization. More recent advancements have leveraged deep learning and reinforcement learning to enable robots to perform more complex tasks. However, achieving fine-grained, human-like dexterity in manipulating objects with multifingered hands, such as unscrewing a bottle lid, remains an area of active research.

This work integrates several contemporary advancements in robotics and artificial intelligence to address this challenge:

- **In-Hand Manipulation:** Building upon prior work that demonstrated success in object reorientation, this research extends the capabilities to more intricate tasks like unscrewing. It aims to bridge the gap between human and robotic dexterity, a crucial step for wider adoption of robots in everyday and industrial settings.
- **Visuomotor Diffusion Policies:** The paper utilizes and extends visuomotor diffusion policies, a relatively recent paradigm that formulates robot control as a conditional denoising diffusion process. This approach has shown promise in various complex manipulation tasks, distinguishing itself from reinforcement learning by relying on offline datasets of expert demonstrations rather than extensive environment interaction. This work investigates their application to high-dexterity in-hand tasks.
- **Teleoperation for Data Collection:** High-quality expert demonstrations are fundamental for training effective visuomotor policies. Previous teleoperation systems often faced issues such as calibration drift, occlusion, or inadequate operator feedback. This research contributes to the development of intuitive and responsive teleoperation by employing an Augmented Reality (AR) headset, which provides real-time visual feedback and hand tracking for precise control. This method addresses the need for efficient and accurate data collection in dexterous manipulation contexts.
- **Outlier Detection in Demonstration Data:** The quality of training data directly impacts policy performance. The paper addresses the issue of suboptimal or inconsistent demonstrations by integrating robust outlier detection techniques. This aligns with broader efforts in machine learning to improve model robustness and training efficiency through data curation, specifically by adapting density-based clustering and outlier scoring algorithms to robotic demonstration datasets.

By combining these elements, the research advances the state of the art by demonstrating a comprehensive framework for learning complex in-hand manipulation skills. It particularly focuses on multimodal sensory input and robust data filtering to enhance policy performance and feasibility for deployment on mobile manipulation platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to present a framework for learning dexterous in-hand manipulation using multifingered hands via visuomotor diffusion policies. The work specifically targets complex in-hand manipulation tasks, exemplified by the task of unscrewing a bottle lid with a single four-fingered Allegro Hand.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Bridging Human-Robot Dexterity Gap:** In-hand manipulation is identified as a fundamental challenge in robotics. Achieving human-like dexterity is considered crucial for enabling robots to perform a wide range of everyday tasks with ease and adaptability. This research seeks to contribute to closing this gap.
- **Enabling Complex Manipulation Skills:** The ability to perform fine-grained manipulation, such as unscrewing a lid, is essential for numerous real-world applications. These include:
 - **Household Robotics:** Robots capable of handling household objects with dexterity would significantly enhance their utility in domestic environments.
 - **Industrial Automation:** Advanced in-hand manipulation can improve flexibility and efficiency in manufacturing and assembly tasks.
 - **Assistive Technologies:** Dexterous robots could provide valuable assistance to individuals with limited mobility, performing tasks that require fine motor skills.
- **Leveraging Visuomotor Diffusion Policies:** The authors aim to extend the capabilities of visuomotor diffusion policies, which have previously shown promise in various manipulation tasks, to the domain of complex in-hand manipulation. This involves investigating how these policies can effectively learn and execute intricate sequences of finger movements.
- **Developing High-Quality Data Collection Methods:** A significant motivation is the need for high-quality expert demonstrations, which are critical for training data-driven policies. The authors sought to develop an intuitive, fast, and responsive teleoperation setup that allows for precise control of the robotic hand. This addresses limitations in existing data collection methods, such as issues with calibration or lack of real-time feedback.
- **Improving Policy Robustness through Data Curation:** Recognizing that not all collected demonstrations are optimal, the research is motivated by the need to filter out low-quality or outlier data. This aims to prevent degradation of policy performance and enable more robust learning, even when demonstrations may contain inconsistencies.
- **Investigating Multimodal Sensory Input:** The research aims to understand how different combinations of sensory inputs (e.g., wrist-mounted camera, top-down camera, joint positions, joint efforts) affect the performance of visuomotor policies in dexterous tasks. This is motivated by the desire to identify effective and practical sensor configurations for real-world robotic deployments, particularly on mobile manipulation platforms.

In summary, the key objectives are to demonstrate the feasibility of learning complex in-hand manipulation using visuomotor diffusion policies, to develop an efficient AR-based teleoperation system for data collection, to implement an effective outlier removal strategy, and to determine optimal sensory modalities for robust policy performance in real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research integrates a custom teleoperation system for data collection, a data filtering strategy, and visuomotor diffusion policies for learning the manipulation task.

**A. Allegro Hand Teleoperation System**

The system facilitates expert demonstration collection for the Allegro Hand, a four-fingered robotic hand. It comprises:

1. **Hand Tracking via Meta Quest 3:** An Augmented Reality (AR) headset (Meta Quest 3) is used for real-time human hand tracking. A custom Unity application accesses the Meta Quest's XR Hands package to obtain vertex poses of tracked hand points. These 26-dimensional vertex pose arrays are transmitted to a ROS-based processing node via Unity’s ROS-TCP connector. The AR headset operates in passthrough mode, overlaying tracked hand movements onto the operator's real-world view for visual feedback.
2. **Hand Retargeting:** Due to significant kinematic differences between the human and Allegro hands, a multi-step retargeting process is implemented:
 - **Initial Alignment:** The human pinky finger is disregarded. Two planes are defined (using index/ring finger knuckles and wrist points) on both human and Allegro hands to align their orientations. The middle finger roots are also aligned for initial translational correction.
 - **Finger Joint Length Scaling:** Root positions of human fingers are mapped to corresponding Allegro Hand joints. A scaling factor `k` is computed as the ratio of summed human finger joint lengths to summed robot finger joint lengths to adjust for size discrepancies.
 - **Specific Adjustments:**
 - The thumb fingertip is shifted 2.3 cm towards the wrist to maximize the Allegro Hand's operational range, compensating for the human thumb's typically shorter relative reach.
 - Fingertips of the index, middle, and ring fingers are shifted 3.4 cm towards the hand plane to facilitate full finger closure on the robot without requiring excessive flexion from the human operator.
3. **Individual Finger Control with Inverse Kinematics (IK):** Each of the four fingers (index, middle, ring, thumb) is controlled independently. For the index, middle, and ring fingers, their root joints are fixed at 0 degrees, and IK is used to reach the retargeted fingertip positions. Separate Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) parameters are provided for these three fingers and for the thumb to define their kinematic structures for IK calculations. This approach prioritizes responsiveness and control over inter-finger collision avoidance.

**B. Expert Demonstration Collection**

A single expert operator collected 300 demonstrations of unscrewing a bottle lid. To ensure diversity, the bottle was placed in different positions within the Allegro Hand's palm, with 100 demonstrations involving randomized target positions within the workspace. Demonstration recording was initiated and stopped using a left-hand fist gesture. The collected data for each demonstration included:
- Joint positions (`q`)
- Joint efforts (`τ`)
- Top camera image (`I_t`)
- Wrist camera image (`I_w`)

The total dataset accumulated to 2.37 hours of demonstrations, equating to approximately 5 hours of real-time execution.

**C. Visuomotor Diffusion Policies for In-Hand Manipulation**

The core learning algorithm is based on the Visuomotor Diffusion Policy. This method frames robot control as a conditional denoising diffusion process, where actions are iteratively refined rather than directly predicted. A 1D temporal Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) models action sequences, starting from Gaussian noise and progressively denoising through a learned noise prediction network. Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) is used to condition the CNN on visual inputs, enabling responsive and temporally consistent actions. The policy utilizes closed-loop action prediction, continuously updating action sequences based on new observations to enhance smoothness and support longer-horizon planning.

**D. Outlier Removal from Demonstrations**

To enhance policy learning by filtering out low-quality demonstrations, a two-stage outlier removal paradigm is introduced:

1. **Feature Extraction:** Raw images from both the top and wrist cameras for each demonstration are encoded using a pre-trained ConvNeXt-Tiny model. This extracts meaningful feature representations from the visual data.
2. **Clustering and Outlier Scoring:** The extracted feature embeddings are then clustered using HDBSCAN (Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise), with a minimum cluster size of 2. HDBSCAN inherently handles clusters of varying densities and identifies points that do not belong to any cluster as noise or outliers. For each demonstration, a Global-Local Outlier Score from Hierarchies (GLOSH) is computed. GLOSH quantifies how well a data point fits into its local density structure, effectively identifying anomalous demonstrations. The final outlier score for a demonstration is the average of its GLOSH scores derived from the top and wrist camera features. **E. Experimental Evaluation Setup**

Experiments were conducted in real-world settings to answer three key questions:
1. Impact of different input modalities (cameras, joint effort) on task performance.
2. Effectiveness of outlier removal on task performance.
3. Overall success rate of visuomotor diffusion policies for dexterous in-hand manipulation.

Seven different policies were trained and evaluated:
- **Input Modality Ablation:**
 - `π_all`: Trained with all 300 demonstrations and all inputs (top camera `I_t`, wrist camera `I_w`, joint effort `τ`, joint positions `q`).
 - `π_nt`: Excludes the top camera.
 - `π_nw`: Excludes the wrist camera.
 - `π_ne`: Excludes joint effort.
- **Outlier Removal Ablation:**
 - `π_90_all`: Removes demonstrations above the 90th percentile of outlier scores.
 - `π_70_all`: Removes demonstrations above the 70th percentile of outlier scores.
 - `π_50_all`: Removes demonstrations above the 50th percentile of outlier scores.

All policies were trained with a prediction horizon of 16 steps, an action horizon of 8 steps, and 3 past observations, for 600 epochs (with early stopping and a 10% validation split). Each policy was evaluated in 20 real-world trials, with a 2-minute time limit per trial. To ensure comparability, 20 novel randomized rectangular placements were used as starting positions for the bottle.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation yielded several findings regarding the performance of visuomotor diffusion policies for dexterous in-hand manipulation, the impact of various input modalities, and the effect of outlier removal.

**A. Baseline Performance**
The baseline policy, `π_all`, which utilized all 300 demonstrations and all available inputs (top camera, wrist camera, joint positions, and joint effort), achieved a 55% success rate in unscrewing the bottle lid.

**B. Impact of Input Modalities**
Ablation studies on input modalities revealed distinct effects on policy performance:
- **Exclusion of Joint Effort (`π_ne`):** Removing joint effort information significantly degraded performance, resulting in only a 30% success rate. This was attributed to an increased number of failure cases where the policy struggled to maintain a successful grasp, leading to the bottle slipping or being mispositioned. This highlights the importance of proprioceptive feedback regarding applied forces for dexterous tasks.
- **Exclusion of Camera Inputs:** Counter-intuitively, removing *either* camera input improved performance compared to using both simultaneously.
 - **Exclusion of Wrist Camera (`π_nw`):** Relying solely on the top camera, along with joint position and effort, resulted in a 70% success rate. However, the authors noted that a top-down view is often impractical for real-world mobile manipulation deployments.
 - **Exclusion of Top Camera (`π_nt`):** Utilizing only the wrist camera, combined with joint position and effort, achieved the highest success rate of 85%. This configuration was deemed more feasible for mobile manipulation platforms (e.g., humanoid robots) where wrist cameras are readily deployable. This finding suggests that a single, well-placed camera view can provide sufficient visual information for certain dexterous tasks, and that incorporating redundant or less informative views might sometimes introduce noise or complexity that hinders learning.

**C. Impact of Outlier Removal**
The evaluation of policies trained on filtered datasets provided insights into the effectiveness of the outlier removal strategy:
- **Moderate Outlier Removal (`π_90_all`):** Removing the top 10% of demonstrations with the highest outlier scores (reducing the dataset from 300 to 270 demonstrations) maintained the policy performance. This suggests that filtering out the most significant low-quality or anomalous demonstrations does not degrade performance and can potentially improve data efficiency without losing crucial information.
- **Aggressive Outlier Removal (`π_70_all`, `π_50_all`):** Removing a larger proportion of demonstrations, such as the top 30% (`π_70_all`) or top 50% (`π_50_all`), led to a substantial decrease in success rates (25% and 10%, respectively). This indicates that while filtering extreme outliers is beneficial or neutral, removing too many demonstrations, even if they have higher outlier scores, removes valuable data points that contribute to the policy's learning capacity.

**D. Overall Success and Failure Analysis**
The most successful policy (`π_nt`, with wrist camera, joint position, and joint effort) achieved an 85% success rate. Analysis of success and failure cases provided qualitative understanding:
- **Failure Cases:** Common failures included losing control of the bottle (pushing it out of a stable grasp, resulting in unrecoverable positions) and early misalignment that prevented a stable grip, leading to repeated failed repositioning attempts.
- **Success Cases:** Successful trials demonstrated the policy's ability to recover from initial errors and adjust its manipulation strategy to complete the task, even if it took longer. The fastest successful trials showed efficient object positioning and stable unscrewing motions with minimal adjustments.

In summary, the research demonstrates the viability of visuomotor diffusion policies for complex in-hand manipulation. The wrist-mounted camera, combined with proprioceptive joint position and effort data, proved to be the most effective sensory configuration, achieving an 85% success rate. Furthermore, a judicious application of outlier removal can help maintain policy performance by eliminating highly suboptimal data without excessively reducing the training dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing several critical challenges in learning dexterous in-hand manipulation tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancing Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation:** The work demonstrates a framework for enabling complex in-hand manipulation tasks, specifically unscrewing a bottle lid, using visuomotor diffusion policies. This extends the applicability of diffusion models to finer-grained, high-dexterity tasks that are challenging for traditional robotic control methods. Achieving an 85% success rate on a real-world task with a multifingered hand highlights a substantial step towards more versatile and capable robotic manipulators.
- **Innovation in Data Collection:** The development of an intuitive and responsive AR-based teleoperation system for collecting expert demonstrations is a key contribution. This system, utilizing Meta Quest 3 for hand tracking and sophisticated motion retargeting, provides a high-quality, efficient, and accessible method for generating training data. This addresses a bottleneck in data-driven robot learning, where high-fidelity, large-scale datasets are often difficult to acquire. The system’s design offers a blueprint for future teleoperation setups, potentially lowering the barrier for data collection in complex robotic tasks.
- **Robustness through Data Curation:** The introduction and evaluation of an outlier removal strategy based on HDBSCAN clustering and the GLOSH algorithm enhance the robustness of policy learning. By effectively identifying and filtering out low-quality or anomalous demonstrations, the framework ensures that the learning process is not negatively impacted by suboptimal human input. This method of data curation is generalizable and can be applied to other imitation learning contexts, improving the efficiency and reliability of training data.
- **Guidance for Sensor Modality Selection:** The extensive ablation studies on different input modalities provide empirical evidence for optimal sensor configurations. The finding that a wrist-mounted camera combined with joint position and effort readings yields the best performance (85% success rate) is particularly significant. This suggests that a streamlined sensor setup can be highly effective, reducing complexity and computational overhead compared to multi-camera systems. This insight is crucial for the practical deployment of dexterous manipulation systems.
- **Enabling Mobile Manipulation:** The demonstrated effectiveness of a wrist-mounted camera as the primary visual input is highly impactful for mobile manipulation platforms, such as humanoid robots. These platforms often have limited space for external cameras or require self-contained sensing capabilities. The findings suggest that this framework is well-suited for integration into mobile robots, expanding their ability to perform dexterous tasks in unstructured or dynamic environments.
- **Broader Applications in Robotics:** The developed framework has potential applications beyond the specific task of unscrewing a bottle lid. It can serve as a foundation for learning a variety of other complex in-hand manipulation skills, opening opportunities in areas such as:
 - **Household Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform tasks like opening containers, preparing food, or handling delicate objects.
 - **Industrial Assembly:** Enhancing robot capabilities for intricate assembly operations that require fine motor control.
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Providing more capable robotic assistance for individuals needing help with daily tasks involving object manipulation.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the state of the art in learning dexterous in-hand manipulation by combining an innovative data collection approach, robust data filtering, and effective visuomotor diffusion policies. Its practical insights into sensor modalities and its implications for mobile manipulation platforms underscore its potential to impact future developments in autonomous and semi-autonomous robotic systems.
