# RL from Physical Feedback: Aligning Large Motion Models with Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and BeingBeyond introduced Reinforcement Learning from Physical Feedback (RLPF), a framework that fine-tunes large text-to-motion models with physical feedback from a motion tracking policy and semantic alignment verification. This approach generates physically feasible and semantically accurate motions for humanoid robots, achieving up to 0.97 success rate in IsaacGym and 0.75 in MuJoCo on the CMU dataset, outperforming baselines.

## Problem

- Labor-intensive, motion-specific parameter tuning limits the scalability and diversity of humanoid robot behaviors.
- Text-to-motion (T2M) models generate motions often lacking physical feasibility (e.g., foot sliding, dynamic instability) for real-world humanoid robots, despite semantic accuracy.
- Adapting human motions to robots requires overcoming morphological differences and ensuring kinematic and dynamic consistency.

## Method

- Developed a ==Reinforcement Learning== from Physical Feedback (RLPF) framework to fine-tune pre-trained Text-to-Motion (T2M) generators.
- Incorporated a motion tracking reward, derived from a robust teacher-student tracking policy and motion retargeting, to provide physical feedback for RL optimization.
- Integrated an alignment verification module using contrastive encoders to ensure semantic fidelity to text instructions during physical fine-tuning.

## Results

- RLPF significantly improved physical feasibility, achieving tracking success rates up to **0.97** in IsaacGym and **0.75** in MuJoCo on the CMU dataset, outperforming baselines.
- The motion tracking reward was shown to be critical for physical feasibility, with its removal causing success rates to drop from **0.97** to **0.32** in IsaacGym for some configurations.
- An alignment verification module was essential for preserving semantic fidelity to text, as its absence led to a severe degradation of high-level generation metrics and a loss of semantic correspondence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct physical feedback, obtained from a robust motion tracking policy, can effectively transform semantically accurate motions into physically feasible actions for robots.
> - Semantic fidelity to text instructions during physical fine-tuning is preserved by integrating an alignment verification module.
> - The motion tracking policy's architecture, such as the Exbody2-based approach, plays a key role in reducing the sim-to-real gap and enhancing real-world deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of human-like motion for virtual and physical agents has been a longstanding objective in robotics and computer graphics. Recent advancements in humanoid robot hardware, exemplified by platforms such as Unitree G1 and Tesla Optimus, have led to increased degrees of freedom and reliability, enabling the execution of complex and fluid motions. However, the development and deployment of diverse robot behaviors remain constrained by the need for labor-intensive, motion-specific parameter tuning.

This limitation has catalyzed interest in automated motion generation techniques, particularly text-to-motion (T2M) models, which translate natural language descriptions into temporal sequences of joint positions and rotations. The integration of large language models (LLMs) into T2M frameworks has further evolved these systems into "large motion models," enhancing their generalizability and capacity to interpret user intent. Existing T2M research, predominantly originating from computer graphics, has prioritized semantic text-motion alignment and visual quality. Consequently, the motions generated often lack physical feasibility, failing to adhere to fundamental kinematic and dynamic constraints necessary for deployment on real-world humanoid robots. Issues such as foot sliding, ground penetration, and dynamic instability are common. This gap between semantically aligned, visually appealing motions and physically executable robot actions presents a significant challenge for the widespread adoption of T2M in robotics.

Previous work in human motion generation has leveraged large-scale datasets like AMASS, HumanML3D, and MotionX, alongside diverse model architectures including VQ-VAEs, generative transformers (e.g., T2M-GPT, MotionGPT), and diffusion models (e.g., MotionDiffuse, MDM). These models have demonstrated capabilities in synthesizing expressive and varied human motions. Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has been utilized for fine-tuning large pre-trained models across various domains, including natural language processing (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback - RLHF) and human motion (e.g., InstructMotion, physics-based tracking policies like PHC). This research integrates these advancements by employing RL to refine pre-trained motion generation models, specifically to imbue them with physical plausibility while preserving semantic fidelity. It aims to bridge the current disconnect by aligning motion generation models with whole-body control systems, thereby enabling humanoid robots to execute a broader spectrum of human-like motions with natural fluidity and physical integrity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address a critical challenge in humanoid robotics: aligning motion generation models with whole-body control systems to ensure that generated motions maintain both semantic accuracy relative to textual descriptions and physical feasibility for robotic execution.

This objective is motivated by several factors:
- **Scaling Robot Behaviors:** Current methods for developing new humanoid robot behaviors require extensive manual parameter tuning, severely limiting the scalability and diversity of motions that can be deployed. Automated solutions, particularly text-to-motion generation, hold promise but face significant hurdles.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** While text-to-motion models have advanced significantly in generating semantically aligned human motions, these motions often originate from computer graphics applications where physical realism is secondary. This results in kinematically or physically infeasible motions unsuitable for real-world robotic deployment, creating a "critical disconnect" between generated motions and executable actions.
- **Ensuring Physical Feasibility:** For humanoid robots, physical feasibility necessitates two key requirements:
 - **Morphological Adaptation:** Direct transfer of human motions to robots is often impractical due to inherent morphological differences. Advanced motion retargeting is required to adapt motions while respecting robotic kinematic constraints and preserving functional equivalence.
 - **Physical Consistency:** Generated motions frequently contain non-physical artifacts such as foot sliding, ground penetration, or dynamically unstable body movements. These violations of physical laws lead to catastrophic failures in real-world robot execution, highlighting the need for dynamic stability and adherence to physical constraints.
- **Enabling Cost-Effective Learning:** The ability to translate text-driven human motions into executable actions for robots efficiently and cost-effectively would enable robots to learn new behaviors rapidly, advancing the vision of versatile, lifelike robotic partners.

Therefore, the research aims to develop a framework that can enhance motion models to generate outputs that are not only interpretable by humans via text but also physically plausible and directly deployable on humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Reinforcement Learning from Physical Feedback (RLPF), addresses the challenge of generating physically feasible and semantically aligned motions for humanoid robots by integrating physics-aware evaluation into a text-conditioned motion generation process. RLPF consists of three main components: a pre-trained Text-to-Motion (T2M) generator, a motion tracking policy, an alignment verification module, and an RL optimization framework.

#### 3.1. Pre-Training of Text-to-motion (T2M) Generator

The foundation of RLPF is a pre-trained T2M generator. This generator utilizes a motion tokenizer, typically based on vector quantization (VQ), to discretize motion sequences into tokens, analogous to how language models process text. The tokenizer comprises an encoder for mapping motion sequences to latent codes, a decoder for reconstructing motions from tokens, and a codebook storing distinct body movement patterns. The core of the T2M generator is an LLM backbone, implemented as a decoder-only causal transformer (e.g., LLaMA2-7B). This LLM’s vocabulary is extended with motion codes from the tokenizer. The model is pre-trained on large-scale text-motion pairs (e.g., MotionX dataset) by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of predicted motion tokens given a text instruction and previous tokens.

#### 3.2. Fine-Tuning of Motion Generator via RL

Following pre-training, the T2M generator is fine-tuned using reinforcement learning to enhance both physical plausibility and text-motion alignment. The motion generator is formulated as an actor policy (πθ) and optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO, an alternative to PPO, estimates baselines from relative group scores, eliminating the need for a critic model. For each text instruction, GRPO samples a group of motion token sequences from the current policy, then optimizes the policy by maximizing an objective function that considers the advantage (Ai) derived from a group of rewards and a KL-divergence penalty against a reference policy.

#### 3.3. Motion Tracking Reward

A critical innovation of RLPF is the generation of physical feedback as a motion tracking reward. This involves a three-stage pipeline:
- **Motion Retargeting:** Due to morphological differences between humans and humanoid robots, human motions from datasets must be adapted. An optimization-based method (inspired by H2O) is used. This process first optimizes SMPL model shape parameters to align with the robot’s geometry via gradient descent. Then, iterative adjustment of pose parameters combines inverse kinematics with gradient optimization, ensuring joint rotations match the robot’s target posture, with full-body alignment achieved through keypoint constraints.
- **Motion Tracking Policy:** A robust motion tracking policy is trained using a two-stage teacher-student framework (inspired by Exbody2) on the AMASS dataset, leveraging the IsaacGym simulator for high-performance parallel training.
 - **Teacher Policy:** An oracle teacher policy (ˆπ) is trained with PPO in simulation, utilizing privileged information (e.g., ground-truth root velocity, global joint positions, environmental properties) in addition to proprioceptive states and motion tracking targets. It outputs target joint positions for Proportional Derivative (PD) controllers to maximize accurate tracking and robust behavior.
 - **Student Policy:** The teacher’s knowledge is distilled into a student policy (π) that operates solely on real-world observable inputs (history of observations and motion target). A DAgger-like approach collects states by rolling out the student, then uses the teacher to compute oracle actions as supervision, minimizing an MSE loss.
- **Offline Evaluation:** The generalizable student policy is evaluated in a simulator (IsaacGym). The tracking performance is quantified by a binary success flag (Succ(π, m_i)), which serves as the motion tracking reward (R_tracking). Success criteria include maintaining a positional deviation below 0.5m and preserving body balance.

#### 3.4. Motion Alignment Verification

To ensure that RL fine-tuning for physical feasibility does not compromise semantic alignment with textual instructions, an alignment verification module is introduced. This module regularizes the motion generator using contrastive-based pre-trained encoders (E_m for motion and E_t for text). These encoders are trained with a contrastive loss to project both modalities into a shared embedding space where semantic similarity corresponds to smaller Euclidean distances. Two verification mechanisms are implemented:
- **Motion Alignment Verification (R_MA):** Measures the squared Euclidean distance between the generated motion's embedding (E_m(m_pred)) and the ground truth motion's embedding (E_m(m)), indicating deviation from the original motion.
- **Text Alignment Verification (R_TA):** Measures the squared Euclidean distance between the generated motion's embedding (E_m(m_pred)) and the input text's embedding (E_t(t)), indicating fidelity to the text description.
These verification rewards are integrated into the RL objective to guide the fine-tuning process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RLPF involved comparisons against baseline methods across high-level motion generation metrics and low-level motion tracking metrics in both simulation (IsaacGym, MuJoCo) and real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

#### 4.1. High-level Generation Evaluation

High-level metrics (R@1, R@2, R@3 for retrieval; MMDist; FID) assess text-to-motion alignment and fidelity to ground truth. As shown in Table 1, RLPF-MA (motion alignment verification) and RLPF-TA (text alignment verification) demonstrated competitive performance compared to the Base Model and SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning). While SFT sometimes achieved lower FID scores (e.g., 1.37 on AMASS) and higher retrieval rates, RLPF-MA achieved strong MMDist values (3.63 CMU, 3.75 AMASS) and respectable FID scores (3.61 CMU, 3.34 AMASS), indicating that the framework generally maintained high-level generation accuracy and semantic correspondence. RLPF-TA showed slightly higher FID scores and lower retrieval rates compared to RLPF-MA.

#### 4.2. Low-level Tracking Evaluation (Q1: Feasibility Improvement)

To evaluate physical feasibility, low-level tracking metrics were used: Success Rate (Succ), Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE), and Mean Per Keybody Position Error (MPKPE). RLPF was compared against a Base Model, SFT, and SFT-Filter (SFT on tracking-optimized data).
- **IsaacGym and MuJoCo Simulators (Tables 2 & 3):** RLPF consistently and significantly outperformed all baseline methods across both CMU and AMASS datasets. For instance, on the CMU dataset in IsaacGym, the Base Model achieved a Success Rate of 0.43, while RLPF-MA reached 0.95 and RLPF-TA achieved 0.97. Similarly, in the more challenging MuJoCo simulator, the Base Model had a Success Rate of 0.43 on CMU, which RLPF-MA improved to 0.75 and RLPF-TA to 0.61. This pattern of superior performance extended to MPJPE and MPKPE, with RLPF variants demonstrating lower errors, indicating more accurate and stable tracking. This clearly establishes RLPF's effectiveness in generating physically feasible motions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis (Q2: Reward Analysis)

Ablation studies investigated the contribution of the motion tracking reward:
- **RLPF-w/o track:** Removing the motion tracking reward resulted in a substantial decrease in Success Rate and an increase in tracking errors across both simulators and datasets (e.g., CMU IsaacGym Succ dropped from 0.95/0.97 to 0.32 for RLPF-MA/TA respectively). This demonstrates that the motion tracking reward is critical for enhancing physical feasibility in real-world environments.
- **RLPF-PHC:** Replacing the Exbody2-based motion tracking policy with PHC resulted in lower success rates compared to RLPF-Full (e.g., CMU IsaacGym Succ 0.91 vs. 0.95/0.97). This indicates that the choice of the motion tracking policy significantly impacts performance, and Exbody2-based tracking is more effective for real-world deployment due to a reduced sim-to-real transfer gap.

#### 4.4. Ablation Analysis (Q3: Alignment Verification)

The contribution of the alignment verification module was examined:
- **RLPF-w/o align:** When the alignment verification module was removed, while low-level tracking metrics (Succ, MPJPE, MPKPE) remained high, or even slightly improved in some cases (e.g., AMASS MuJoCo Succ 0.99), the high-level generation metrics drastically deteriorated (Table 6). FID scores increased dramatically (e.g., CMU FID 32.53 vs. RLPF-MA 3.61), and retrieval rates dropped significantly (e.g., CMU R@1 0.09 vs. RLPF-MA 0.26). Visualizations confirmed a near-complete loss of semantic correspondence, with the model predominantly generating generic standing motions irrespective of the text input (Figure 3). This indicates that the alignment verification module is crucial for maintaining semantic fidelity to textual instructions during the RL fine-tuning process.

In summary, RLPF effectively generates physically feasible motions for humanoid robots, significantly outperforming baselines in tracking success and accuracy. This capability is attributed to the integration of a motion tracking reward derived from a robust policy and the regularization provided by the alignment verification module, which ensures semantic fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Reinforcement Learning from Physical Feedback (RLPF), a framework that directly addresses a fundamental challenge in humanoid robotics: translating text-driven human motion descriptions into physically feasible and semantically aligned actions for robots. The significance and potential impact of this work are substantial:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Motion Generation:** RLPF effectively bridges the critical gap between motions generated by large motion models, often optimized for visual quality, and the physical constraints of real-world humanoid robots. By integrating physics-aware evaluation, the framework ensures that generated motions are not only semantically accurate but also kinematically and dynamically plausible, enabling successful deployment on hardware platforms like the Unitree G1.
- **Enhancing Scalability and Diversity of Robot Behaviors:** By automating the process of generating physically feasible motions from textual descriptions, RLPF mitigates the labor-intensive parameter tuning typically required for each new robot behavior. This capability can significantly accelerate the development and deployment of a wider array of complex and fluid motions for humanoid robots, fostering greater versatility and adaptability.
- **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction and Collaboration:** The ability for humanoid robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute corresponding, physically sound actions can profoundly impact human-robot interaction. It could lead to more intuitive programming, more natural collaborative tasks, and the creation of more capable and adaptable robotic partners in various environments, from industrial settings to assistive roles.
- **Foundation for Future Research in Embodied AI:** RLPF establishes a novel methodology for fine-tuning large motion models using physical feedback. This approach could serve as a foundational step for future research in embodied AI, particularly in areas where intelligent agents need to generate complex physical actions grounded in textual commands.
- **Generalizable Framework:** While demonstrated with specific models and robot platforms, the RLPF framework's principles—integrating physics-based rewards and semantic alignment regularization—are generalizable. This suggests potential applicability to other robotic systems or even to the development of more robust physics-informed generative models in related domains.

A stated limitation of the current framework is its reliance on a frozen, pre-trained motion tracking policy, which may restrict its generalization to out-of-distribution motions. Future work aimed at jointly training the large motion generation model with an adaptable tracking policy could further enhance robustness and adaptability, unlocking even broader potential for creating agile and versatile humanoid robots.
