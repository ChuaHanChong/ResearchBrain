# AnyDexRT: Calibration-Free Dexterous Hand Retargeting with Few-Shot Human Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> AnyDexRT offers a calibration-free approach for dexterous robot hand retargeting by learning fingertip-level correspondence with minimal human guidance and refining pinch poses via a contact classifier. It achieved an average local motion consistency of 90.2% across diverse hands and a 62.0% pinch success rate in real-world teleoperation.

## Problem

- Existing kinematic retargeting methods for dexterous hands often require extensive manual calibration, parameter tuning, and lack generality across various robot morphologies.
- Prior approaches can produce unintuitive or unnatural robot motions, hindering effective teleoperation and the collection of high-quality demonstrations for imitation learning.
- Accurate retargeting of precise, contact-rich motions, such as pinching, is challenging due to inherent sensor limitations and geometric complexities.

## Method

- A ==self-supervised fingertip mapping== component learns robust shape correspondence using ==partial Chamfer==, ==distance preservation==, and ==local motion preservation losses==.
- ==Few-shot human guidance== provides sparse anchor points for geometric alignment, resolving mapping ambiguity and stabilizing training without extensive paired data.
- A ==binary contact classifier== refines pinch-related poses to enhance the accuracy and stability of critical fine manipulation tasks.

## Results

- AnyDexRT achieved an average local motion consistency (LMC) of **90.2%** across seven diverse dexterous hands in simulation, outperforming GeoRT (**59.8%**) and optimization baselines (**52.2%**).
- It demonstrated calibration-free robustness by maintaining stable retargeting quality under ±90∘± 90^\circ±90∘ input data rotations and required only **3** hyperparameters, significantly less than baselines.
- In real-world teleoperation, AnyDexRT achieved the highest pinch success rate of **62.0%** for small-ball picking and reduced task completion times across various manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymmetric partial Chamfer loss effectively maps human fingertip space to feasible robot fingertip space, preventing distortions from redundant robot motion regions.
> - Combining self-supervised learning with minimal, task-relevant human guidance via anchor points can resolve mapping ambiguities and improve stability in high-dimensional spaces.
> - Specialized refinement for contact-critical motions using a dedicated classifier significantly improves performance in precise manipulation tasks beyond general kinematic mapping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation by robotic hands is a capability that offers adaptability in physical interactions, contrasting with the more limited expressiveness of simple grippers. However, the high-dimensional action spaces, joint couplings, and complex contact dynamics of dexterous hands present challenges for direct control. Teleoperation serves as an interface to leverage human dexterity, mapping operator hand motions to robot hand motions in real time. This process, known as kinematic retargeting, is fundamental not only for real-time control but also for generating high-quality demonstrations for imitation learning, a data-driven approach to robot skill acquisition.

Existing kinematic retargeting methods typically fall into several categories, each with limitations. Traditional optimization-based approaches often rely on inverse kinematics with hand-crafted task vectors or keypoints, requiring meticulous calibration of coordinate frames, scale factors, and objective weights. These methods are sensitive to specific hand characteristics and can lack generality. Neural variants aim to reduce runtime costs by learning to imitate these pipelines but inherit the underlying assumptions and tuning requirements. More recent work, such as GeoRT, attempts to learn correspondence by globally aligning reachable fingertip spaces. However, global shape matching can be problematic when robot hands possess redundant motion regions that have no direct human counterpart, potentially leading to distorted task-relevant mappings and geometrically plausible but unintuitive teleoperation.

This research introduces AnyDexRT to address these limitations. The work is positioned as an advancement that provides a calibration-free, intuitive, and generalizable retargeting solution for human-like dexterous hands. By focusing on learning fingertip-level correspondence with minimal manual guidance and incorporating mechanisms for handling specific contact-rich motions like pinching, AnyDexRT aims to improve retargeting quality, reduce manual tuning effort, and enhance the intuitiveness of teleoperation across diverse robotic hand morphologies. This contributes to making dexterous robot control more accessible and robust for both real-time operation and the generation of training data for learning-based manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AnyDexRT is to develop a calibration-free retargeting method that facilitates intuitive dexterous teleoperation across various human-like robotic hands with reduced manual tuning. This objective is driven by the identified shortcomings of current retargeting techniques and the need for a more practical and robust solution for controlling high-dimensional robot hands.

The motivation for AnyDexRT is articulated through three core requirements for an effective retargeting algorithm, which existing methods are stated to incompletely satisfy:

- **R1: Intuitiveness:** The retargeting process should accurately preserve the operator's motion intent, translating human movements into feasible and predictable robot motions that are easy to control. The motivation here stems from observations that existing methods can produce unnatural or difficult-to-control robot motions, detracting from the teleoperation experience and the quality of collected demonstrations. An intuitive mapping is crucial for effective human-robot interaction and for generating useful data for imitation learning.
- **R2: Calibration Efficiency:** A practical retargeting system should minimize its dependence on precise calibration or extensive manual tuning of hyperparameters such as scale factors, offsets, and task weights. Current methods often require significant calibration efforts, which can be time-consuming, error-prone, and require expertise. Reducing this dependence enhances the system's ease of use and deployability across different operational setups.
- **R3: Generality:** The retargeting system should be capable of controlling diverse human-like dexterous hands using the same operator hand input space without requiring hand-specific redesign or extensive handcrafted tuning. Many existing approaches are tailored to specific robot hand morphologies or require considerable adaptation for new hands. The motivation is to achieve a more universal solution that can generalize across variations in kinematic structures, scales, and degrees of freedom (DoFs) of human-like robotic hands.

AnyDexRT is designed to address these motivations by incorporating specific methodological components. Self-supervised fingertip correspondence learning aims to provide a generalizable mapping without explicit paired data. Few-shot human guidance is introduced to anchor the mapping in task-relevant regions, thereby reducing ambiguity and the need for precise calibration. Furthermore, a contact classifier refines pinch-related poses to enhance the accuracy of fine manipulation, which is often challenging for general kinematic mapping due to sensor limitations and geometric precision requirements. By targeting these areas, AnyDexRT endeavors to improve retargeting quality, streamline deployment, and offer a more intuitive and efficient teleoperation experience.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AnyDexRT proposes a calibration-efficient pipeline for mapping operator fingertip motions to robot hand commands. The approach assumes a robotic hand structurally similar to the human hand, where fingertip positions and reference joint angles are sufficient for inverse kinematics, and that the human fingertip motion space can be covered by the robot's, but not necessarily vice versa. The method primarily focuses on learning a fingertip mapper ($f_m$) from human fingertip positions ($C_H$) to robot fingertip positions ($C_R$), with a subsequent inverse kinematics step ($f_s$) to convert $C_R$ to robot joint commands ($J_R$).

The methodology is composed of three main components:

#### 3.1. Fingertip Mapping by Self-Supervised Shape Correspondence

The core of AnyDexRT's fingertip mapper relies on learning correspondence through self-supervised shape matching, utilizing several objectives:

- **Partial Correspondence:** Unlike bidirectional Chamfer loss used in some prior works (e.g., GeoRT), AnyDexRT employs a partial Chamfer loss ($L_{P-Chamfer}$). This asymmetric objective maps the human fingertip space into the feasible robot fingertip space without forcing full coverage of the robot space. This design prevents distortions in the learned mapping that could arise from forcing human motions to fill redundant or unnatural regions within a highly dexterous robot's motion space, thereby preserving the structure of natural operator motions.
- **Distance Preservation:** To prevent geometric distortion during mapping, a pairwise distance preservation objective ($L_{dist}$) is introduced. This loss minimizes the difference between the distances between pairs of human fingertips and the distances between their corresponding retargeted robot fingertips. This regularization helps maintain the geometric integrity and scale of the retargeted motions, ensuring a more natural appearance and feel.
- **Local Motion Preservation:** To ensure that local motion directions are maintained and to mitigate issues arising from calibration errors (e.g., misalignment of global coordinate frames), AnyDexRT introduces a local motion loss ($L_{motion}$). This loss encourages consistency between the relative movement directions in the human and robot local coordinate frames. By focusing on local consistency, the method becomes less sensitive to global translational or rotational discrepancies that might occur due to sensor calibration inaccuracies.

#### 3.2. Geometric Alignment with Few-Shot Human Guidance

Self-supervised learning, while scalable, can result in ambiguous mappings where multiple plausible robot configurations correspond to a single human hand pose. To resolve this non-uniqueness and provide stability, AnyDexRT integrates few-shot human guidance. Operators perform a small set of reference gestures, providing paired human-robot fingertip anchors. An alignment loss ($L_{align}$) is then used to minimize the distance between the mapped human anchor positions and their corresponding robot anchor positions. This sparse supervision effectively anchors the mapping in task-relevant regions, reducing ambiguity, stabilizing training, and enabling personalized retargeting without requiring extensive paired data. The collection process for anchors involves sampling human joint configurations for lateral rotation and bending, interpolating to increase data points, and then generating corresponding robot anchor data in simulation.

#### 3.3. Pinch Pose Refinement using Contact Classifier

Precise fingertip contact is crucial for tasks involving tiny objects, particularly pinch motions. Due to sensor limitations, measured human hand poses may not accurately reflect the fine contact patterns of a pinch. To address this, AnyDexRT trains a binary contact classifier ($f_c$). This classifier takes human fingertip positions (specifically the thumb and another finger) as input and predicts whether a pinch contact is occurring. It is trained using a binary cross-entropy loss ($L_{contact}$) on labeled pinch/non-pinch poses, which are relatively easy to collect. During inference, if the contact classifier detects a human fingertip contact, the system searches the neighborhood of the corresponding mapped robotic position for a predefined robotic pinch pose template, rather than solely relying on the general fingertip position mapping. This refinement enhances the accuracy and stability of contact-critical pinch motions.

**Implementation Details:** Both the fingertip mapper ($f_m$) and the contact classifier ($f_c$) are implemented using Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) and trained in parallel. For $f_m$, separate sub-networks are used for each finger, mapping 3D human fingertip positions to 3D robot fingertip positions. For $f_c$, separate sub-networks predict pinch status for the thumb and each other finger. Data is preprocessed by centralization and normalization. During inference, a nearest neighbor search (NNS) is used to convert retargeted fingertip positions to robot joint configurations, and a threshold is applied to the contact classifier's output.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of AnyDexRT was evaluated through both simulated experiments on diverse dexterous hands and real-world teleoperation tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

Seven human-like dexterous hands with varying DoFs (6 to 20 DoFs), including Inspire Hand, Ability Hand, XHand, Wuji Hand, Allegro Hand, Leap Hand, and Shadow Hand, were used for evaluation. The primary metrics were global motion consistency (GMC) and local motion consistency (LMC), reflecting how well the robot hand motion aligns with the operator's intent. AnyDexRT was compared against an optimization-based method and GeoRT.

- **Retargeting Quality (Table 1):** AnyDexRT consistently demonstrated improved local motion consistency across all tested hands, achieving an average LMC of 90.2% compared to 59.8% for GeoRT and 52.2% for the optimization baseline. This indicates a better preservation of operator motion intent. While optimized for local consistency, AnyDexRT also maintained competitive global motion consistency. The results showed consistently high scores with small standard deviations, indicating robust and stable performance across different hand embodiments and training initializations.
- **Manual Tuning Effort and Retargeting Efficiency (Table 2):** AnyDexRT required only 3 hyperparameters, fewer than GeoRT (4) and significantly less than the optimization method (≥10). It achieved a runtime speed of approximately 293 Hz, comparable to GeoRT (281.7 Hz) and substantially faster than the optimization baseline (93.4 Hz). This suggests a reduction in manual tuning effort while maintaining real-time performance.
- **Calibration Sensitivity (Figure 5):** Using the Wuji Hand, AnyDexRT's calibration sensitivity was tested by rotating input human data around the y-axis. AnyDexRT maintained stable retargeting quality under rotations of up to $\pm 90^\circ$, while the optimization baseline and GeoRT showed substantial degradation. This demonstrates AnyDexRT's calibration-free capability and adaptability to misaligned coordinate systems.
- **Training Stability (Figure 6):** AnyDexRT exhibited significantly better training stability than GeoRT, with orders-of-magnitude lower standard deviations in retargeted positions across different random seeds and all hands. This suggests higher reproducibility and reliability of the learned mappings.
- **Ablation Study (Table 3):** An ablation study on Wuji Hand showed the contribution of each objective. The partial Chamfer loss alone yielded low LMC (4.1%). Adding the distance preservation loss ($L_{dist}$) significantly improved LMC to 83.8%. Including the local motion preservation loss ($L_{motion}$) further increased LMC to 89.1%. Finally, incorporating the few-shot anchor alignment loss ($L_{align}$) resulted in the highest LMC of 92.3%, confirming the contribution of each component to the overall retargeting quality.

#### 4.2. Real-World Teleoperation Performance

Real-world experiments utilized a Flexiv Rizon 4 arm with a Wuji Hand, controlled by an operator wearing a Manus glove and HTC Vive Tracker. Four tasks were evaluated: spray-bottle triggering (Sprink), light-bulb screwing (Screw), steak shoveling (Shovel), and small-ball picking (Pick-10).

- **Intuitiveness and Efficiency (Table 4):** AnyDexRT achieved the shortest task completion times across all four tasks, demonstrating more efficient and predictable teleoperation compared to the baselines. The improvements were particularly noticeable in tasks requiring precise finger response and stable grasp adjustment like Sprink and Shovel.
- **Pinch Performance (Table 4):** For the Pick-10 task, which requires reliable thumb-finger contacts, AnyDexRT achieved the highest pinch success rate of 62.0%, significantly outperforming the optimization method (39.6%) and GeoRT (29.2%). This indicates that the contact classifier refinement effectively improves performance in contact-critical pinch motions.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 7 & 8):** Qualitative observations and visualizations supported the quantitative results, showing that AnyDexRT provided precise and intuitive hand retargeting for various gestures, and generated more accurate pinch poses and stable power grasps compared to GeoRT, which struggled with the widely distributed redundant fingertip space of the Wuji Hand.

In summary, the experimental results indicate that AnyDexRT provides enhanced retargeting quality, reduced manual tuning, improved calibration robustness, and greater stability compared to existing methods. These benefits translate to more intuitive and efficient real-world teleoperation, particularly for tasks requiring precise contact.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AnyDexRT addresses several significant challenges in the field of dexterous robotic hand control, offering advancements that could impact both research and practical applications.

**Improved Teleoperation and Operator Experience:** By providing a calibration-free, intuitive, and efficient retargeting system, AnyDexRT has the potential to significantly enhance the teleoperation experience for human operators. The demonstrated improvements in motion consistency, task completion times, and pinch success rates suggest that operators can control dexterous robots with greater ease, precision, and predictability. This reduces operator burden and allows for more nuanced and complex manipulations.

**Enhanced Data Collection for Imitation Learning:** The ability to generate high-quality, intuitive robot motions directly from human demonstrations is crucial for data-driven robot learning approaches, particularly imitation learning. AnyDexRT's focus on preserving operator intent and refining critical contact motions means that the collected teleoperated data is more representative of natural and effective human dexterity. This could lead to more robust and generalizable learned manipulation policies for complex tasks, including those requiring delicate object interaction or precise grasping.

**Increased Generality and Accessibility of Dexterous Robots:** The method's calibration-free nature and ability to generalize across diverse human-like dexterous hands (ranging from 6 to 20 DoFs) diminish the need for hand-specific engineering and extensive parameter tuning. This makes dexterous robotic hands more accessible and deployable in various research and industrial settings, as the same retargeting framework can be applied to different hardware platforms with minimal adaptation. The few-shot human guidance approach also offers a path towards personalized retargeting without requiring large, paired datasets.

**Robustness to Calibration Errors:** The demonstrated stability of AnyDexRT under significant frame misalignments (calibration sensitivity) is a practical advantage, as perfect calibration is often difficult to achieve in real-world scenarios. This robustness enhances the reliability of teleoperation systems and makes them more tolerant to variations in sensor placement or environmental conditions.

**Advancements in Retargeting Methodology:** AnyDexRT introduces novel combinations of techniques, including partial Chamfer loss for robust shape correspondence, local motion preservation to mitigate calibration issues, and a contact classifier for refining specific task-critical poses. These methodological contributions provide insights into designing more effective and resilient retargeting algorithms.

While the current work primarily validates teleoperation performance, its future impact could extend to training downstream manipulation policies, further assessing its value for data collection. Limitations, such as the requirement for few human-guided anchors and the focus on pinch-related contact, suggest avenues for future research, including automating anchor selection and developing richer contact models for broader contact-rich behaviors. Overall, AnyDexRT represents a step towards making dexterous robot manipulation more practical and widely applicable through a more intuitive and robust teleoperation interface.
