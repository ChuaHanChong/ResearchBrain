# MAL: Motion-Aware Loss with Temporal and Distillation Hints for Self-Supervised Depth Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Motion-Aware Loss (MAL) module improves self-supervised monocular depth estimation in dynamic environments by addressing motion-induced errors during training with temporal and distillation hints. This plug-and-play module reduced Absolute Relative Error by up to 10.8% for DualRefine on the CityScapes benchmark, without increasing inference time.

## Problem

- Self-supervised depth estimation methods degrade significantly in dynamic scenes due to the violation of the static scene assumption, leading to inaccurate depth predictions.
- Moving objects introduce errors in two key stages: the image reprojection loss calculation and the feature matching process within multi-frame student networks.
- Existing solutions for dynamic scenes often increase inference complexity by modifying the network's forward pass or utilize incomplete distillation strategies that do not fully leverage teacher knowledge.

## Method

- A plug-and-play Motion-Aware Loss (**MAL**) module is introduced, integrating two components: Temporal Hints and Distillation Hints, operating solely during the training phase.
- Temporal Hints utilize instance segmentation and motion tracking to rectify moving objects in reconstructed images, reducing motion-induced errors in the image reprojection loss.
- Distillation Hints expand the distillation domain to the entire image and generate a more accurate target depth map by fusing teacher and student predictions based on lower reprojection loss, enhancing knowledge transfer and correcting feature matching errors.

## Results

- On the CityScapes benchmark, MAL improved the Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel) for ManyDepth by **10.1%** (from **0.114** to **0.103**) and for DualRefine by **10.8%** (from **0.111** to **0.099**).
- Integrating MAL into DynamicDepth, a method already optimized for dynamic scenes, still yielded noticeable improvements, reducing Squared Relative Error (SqRel) by **5.34%** (from **1.011** to **0.957**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Temporal Hints and Distillation Hints contribute to the overall performance, with the full MAL module achieving the best results (AbsRel **0.103**, SqRel **1.073** for ManyDepth on CityScapes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust self-supervised depth estimation in dynamic scenes requires mitigating errors stemming from both image reprojection inconsistencies and unreliable feature matching processes.
> - Motion-induced errors can be effectively addressed at the loss computation level during training, allowing for significant performance gains without incurring additional computational overhead during inference.
> - The Temporal and Distillation Hints are complementary, with Temporal Hints refining the photometric loss and Distillation Hints correcting student network errors in feature matching, both synergistically contributing to improved accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of depth perception is critical for various robotic and autonomous applications. Recent advancements in deep learning have significantly progressed self-supervised depth estimation, particularly by leveraging large-scale, unlabeled monocular video data. This approach typically frames depth estimation as a cross-view consistency problem, where a network is trained to predict depth and camera ego-motion by minimizing the photometric difference between observed and reprojected frames.

Initial self-supervised methods largely relied on a foundational assumption of a static scene. However, real-world environments, especially those encountered by autonomous vehicles, are often dynamic, containing moving objects like other vehicles and pedestrians. This dynamic nature violates the static scene assumption, leading to degradation in depth estimation performance.

The broader research landscape has seen several approaches to address this challenge:
- **Multi-frame methods:** Recent state-of-the-art techniques leverage multiple input frames during inference, often incorporating feature-level reprojection and matching to improve scene geometry understanding (e.g., ManyDepth, DualRefine).
- **Teacher-student distillation:** Some methods employ a single-frame depth network as a teacher to guide a multi-frame student network, aiming to mitigate errors arising from feature matching. However, these often confine distillation operations to regions where teacher and student outputs diverge significantly, and errors in the image reprojection loss itself may persist.
- **Explicit motion modeling:** Approaches that model pixel-wise motion using optical flow or 3D motion fields, or utilize semantic segmentation to differentiate between moving foreground and static background objects, have been explored. These methods often introduce additional computational complexity into the network's forward pass, which can pose challenges for real-time integration and inference.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by seeking to improve multi-frame self-supervised depth estimation in dynamic scenes. It aims to address the limitations of motion-induced errors in both image reprojection loss and feature matching, specifically for existing teacher-student multi-frame frameworks. A key differentiator is its focus on a "plug-and-play" module that operates at the loss computation level, rather than modifying the network's forward pass, thereby aiming to minimize integration challenges and preserve real-time inference efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy and robustness of self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods in dynamic scenes. This is achieved by introducing a novel, plug-and-play loss module named Motion-Aware Loss (MAL).

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current self-supervised depth estimation methodologies, particularly in real-world dynamic environments:

1. **Degradation in Dynamic Scenes:** A fundamental problem is the performance degradation of self-supervised depth estimation methods when the static scene assumption, upon which many are built, is violated by moving objects. This leads to inaccuracies in depth prediction, which is critical for applications like autonomous navigation.

2. **Sources of Motion-Induced Errors:** Moving objects introduce errors at two primary stages of the self-supervised learning pipeline for multi-frame methods:
 - **Image Reprojection Loss:** When objects move, the photometric consistency between the current frame and its reprojected neighbors is disturbed. This results in inaccurate loss signals, hindering effective network training.
 - **Feature Matching Process:** In multi-frame student networks, the process of matching features across adjacent frames (often involving cost volume construction) is disrupted by object motion. This leads to unreliable feature correspondences and, consequently, suboptimal depth estimates.

3. **Limitations of Existing Solutions:**
 - **Incomplete Distillation:** While teacher-student distillation schemes are employed in multi-frame methods to mitigate feature matching errors, they often restrict distillation to regions of high uncertainty (where teacher and student predictions diverge significantly). This limited application may not fully exploit the knowledge available from the teacher network to correct errors across the entire image or address the fundamental errors in the reprojection loss.
 - **Increased Inference Cost and Complexity:** Alternative approaches that explicitly model motion (e.g., optical flow, 3D motion fields) or use semantic segmentation to distinguish dynamic objects typically integrate complex algorithms into the network's forward pass. This can lead to increased computational overhead during inference, making them less suitable for real-time applications, and can complicate integration with existing self-supervised depth estimation architectures.

To address these motivations, the authors set out to develop a solution that:
- **Leverages Temporal Information:** Capitalizes on the temporal relationships between consecutive frames to identify and correct for motion-induced errors directly within the image reprojection loss calculation.
- **Optimizes Distillation:** Enhances the knowledge transfer from the teacher network to the student network by expanding the scope of distillation and refining the selection of distillation targets to better account for object motion.
- **Maintains Efficiency:** Operates exclusively during the training phase, ensuring that the module does not add any computational burden or latency during the inference phase, which is crucial for real-time applications.
- **Ensures Compatibility:** Is designed as a "plug-and-play" module, allowing for seamless and swift integration into existing multi-frame self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods without requiring modifications to their underlying network architectures.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a practical and effective method for improving depth perception in dynamic scenes, thereby enhancing the capabilities of autonomous systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Motion-Aware Loss (MAL)** is presented as a plug-and-play module designed to augment multi-frame self-supervised depth estimation by addressing motion-induced errors during the training phase. The module operates at the loss computation level, ensuring that it does not incur additional computational overhead during inference. MAL integrates two main components: Temporal Hints and Distillation Hints, combined with a loss balancing mechanism.

**A. Foundation: Multi-Frame Self-Supervised Depth Estimation Framework**
The work is built upon a conventional multi-frame self-supervised depth estimation architecture, typically comprising:
- A **Teacher Depth Network** that predicts depth from a single image.
- A **Student Depth Network** that predicts depth from two consecutive frames, employing a cost volume for feature matching.
- A **Shared Pose Network** that estimates camera ego-motion.

The training process in these frameworks usually involves:
- **Image Reprojection Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{reproj}$):** Based on the assumption of a static scene, this loss minimizes the photometric difference between a target frame and a reconstructed version generated by projecting pixels from neighboring frames using predicted depth and pose. Occlusions are handled by selecting pixels with lower photometric error.
- **Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{consis}$):** An asymmetric distillation mechanism used to transfer knowledge from the teacher network to the student. An uncertainty mask ($M$) identifies unreliable regions where the student's depth (derived from feature matching) significantly deviates from the teacher's. In these regions, the student is supervised by an L1 loss against the teacher's depth.
- **Smoothness Loss ($\mathcal{L}_s$):** An edge-aware term promoting smooth depth predictions.
The student's original loss is formulated as: $\mathcal{L}_{ori} = \neg M \cdot \mathcal{L}_{reproj} + M \cdot \mathcal{L}_{consis} + \lambda_s \cdot \mathcal{L}_s$.

**B. Temporal Hints**
This component specifically targets and mitigates errors in the image reprojection loss that arise from moving objects.
1. **Dynamic Object Identification:** A pre-trained instance segmentation model (with frozen parameters) is used to identify moving objects (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians) in the input frames.
2. **Motion Tracking and Displacement Estimation:** The Hungarian algorithm tracks instances across three consecutive frames ($I_{t-1}, I_t, I_{t+1}$). Assuming uniform linear motion due to short inter-frame intervals (e.g., 0.1 seconds), object displacement between $I_{t-1}$ and $I_{t+1}$ is approximated. To handle truncated objects robustly, bounding-box-level horizontal and vertical displacements are calculated as the maximum displacement of their respective boundaries.
3. **Motion Rectification and Occlusion Restoration:**
 - The calculated displacements are used to translate dynamic objects in the reconstructed images ($I_{t-1 \to t}$ and $I_{t+1 \to t}$) to align their positions with their estimated state at time $t$. This results in motion-rectified images, $I^{rec}_{t-1 \to t}$ and $I^{rec}_{t+1 \to t}$.
 - Areas newly exposed due to object translation are restored by leveraging the temporal symmetry between $t-1$ and $t+1$. For instance, an area occluded by an object at $t+1$ but exposed at $t$ cannot be occluded by the same object at $t-1$. Therefore, pixels from $I_{t-1 \to t}$ are used to fill newly uncovered regions in $I^{rec}_{t+1 \to t}$, and vice versa.
4. **Enhanced Image Reconstruction:** The final reconstructed image, $I'_t$, for calculating the photometric reprojection loss is generated by selecting the pixel with the lowest photometric error from an expanded set of candidates: $I_{t-1 \to t}$, $I_{t+1 \to t}$, $I^{rec}_{t-1 \to t}$, and $I^{rec}_{t+1 \to t}$. This process directly reduces motion-induced errors in the reprojection loss.

**C. Distillation Hints**
This component addresses errors in the student network's feature matching process, which are particularly susceptible to dynamic object motion, by enhancing the teacher-student distillation scheme.
1. **Expanded Distillation Domain:** Unlike prior methods that confine distillation to the uncertainty mask $M$, MAL extends the distillation process to cover the *entire image domain*.
2. **Loss-Based Distillation Target Selection:** Instead of directly using the teacher's depth prediction ($D_t$) as the distillation target in uncertain regions, MAL proposes to generate a more accurate target depth map ($D_{td}$). This is achieved by fusing the depth predictions from both the teacher ($D_t$) and the student ($D_s$) on a pixel-wise basis. For each pixel, the depth value that yields the *lower image reprojection loss* is chosen as the target $D_{td}$.
3. **Distillation Loss Calculation:** The new distillation loss ($\mathcal{L}_{distil}$) is calculated as the L1 difference between the student's predicted depth ($D_s$) and this selected target depth ($D_{td}$) specifically in the *reliable* area ($\neg M$): $\mathcal{L}_{distil} = \neg M \cdot ||D_s - D_{td}||_1$. Critically, the teacher network's gradients are not updated during the back-propagation of $\mathcal{L}_{distil}$, maintaining its role as a stable knowledge source.

**D. Loss Balancing**
The overall training objective combines the original student network loss (now incorporating Temporal Hints) and the new distillation loss: $\mathcal{L} = w_1 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{ori} + w_2 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{distil}$. To dynamically balance the contributions of these two loss terms, the Multi-Loss Rebalancing Algorithm (MLRA) is employed. MLRA iteratively adjusts the weights ($w_1, w_2$) throughout training based on the observed descending rate of each individual loss term, with a hyperparameter $\lambda$ controlling the prioritization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the performance of MAL by integrating it into existing multi-frame self-supervised depth estimation methods: ManyDepth, DynamicDepth, and DualRefine. Experiments were conducted on the KITTI and CityScapes benchmarks, using standard depth evaluation metrics including Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel), Squared Relative Error (SqRel), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Root Mean Squared Log Error (RMSElog), and accuracy within specified thresholds ($\delta < 1.25, \delta < 1.25^2, \delta < 1.25^3$).

**A. Evaluation Results on KITTI Benchmark (Table I)**
On the KITTI dataset, which contains a relatively lower proportion of dynamic pixels (0.34%), MAL demonstrated performance improvements when integrated into baseline methods:
- **ManyDepth + MAL:** Achieved an AbsRel of 0.094 compared to ManyDepth's 0.098, and a SqRel of 0.732 (down from 0.770). Accuracy metrics also showed gains, with $\delta < 1.25$ increasing from 0.900 to 0.906. The paper notes an improvement of up to 4.2% on specific metrics.
- **DualRefine + MAL:** Showed modest improvements. AbsRel remained 0.087, while SqRel decreased slightly from 0.698 to 0.690. $\delta < 1.25$ accuracy improved from 0.914 to 0.915. The paper indicates that the smaller impact on KITTI is consistent with the lower prevalence of dynamic objects in this dataset.

**B. Evaluation Results on CityScapes Benchmark (Tables II and III)**
The CityScapes dataset, known for its numerous dynamic scenes, revealed more pronounced performance enhancements:
- **ManyDepth + MAL:** All seven evaluation metrics showed consistent improvement. AbsRel decreased from 0.114 to 0.103, SqRel from 1.193 to 1.073, RMSE from 6.223 to 5.952, and RMSElog from 0.170 to 0.157. Accuracy $\delta < 1.25$ increased from 0.875 to 0.896. This represents an improvement of up to 10.1%.
- **DynamicDepth + MAL:** Even when applied to DynamicDepth, a method already optimized for dynamic scenes, MAL provided a noticeable improvement. SqRel decreased by 5.34% (from 1.011 to 0.957 for the officially provided model), and $\delta < 1.25$ accuracy increased from 0.890% to 0.895%.
- **DualRefine + MAL:** Demonstrated the most substantial improvements on CityScapes. AbsRel decreased from 0.111 to 0.099, SqRel from 1.248 to 0.973, RMSE from 6.035 to 5.530, and RMSElog from 0.164 to 0.149. Accuracy $\delta < 1.25$ increased from 0.896 to 0.905. This represents an improvement of up to 10.8%.

The consistent enhancements across multiple baselines on CityScapes underscore MAL's effectiveness in highly dynamic environments. The performance gains enable these methods to achieve results comparable to or in some cases surpass state-of-the-art dynamic-scene-oriented methods, while maintaining greater portability due to its loss-level optimization.

**C. Ablation Study (Table IV - ManyDepth on CityScapes)**
An ablation study on ManyDepth using the CityScapes dataset was conducted to dissect the contribution of each MAL component:
- **ManyDepth (Baseline):** AbsRel 0.114, SqRel 1.193.
- **+ Temporal Hints Only:** Improved AbsRel to 0.111 and SqRel to 1.182, demonstrating the positive impact of correcting motion errors in the image reprojection loss.
- **+ Distillation Hints Only (MLRA):** When only Distillation Hints were added (with MLRA), AbsRel improved to 0.111 and SqRel to 1.179, indicating the effectiveness of the refined distillation in knowledge transfer.
- **+ MAL (Temporal Hints + Distillation Hints + MLRA):** The full MAL module, combining both Temporal and Distillation Hints with MLRA for loss balancing, achieved the best results: AbsRel 0.103 and SqRel 1.073. This confirms that both components contribute to the overall performance and that MLRA further optimizes their combined effect.

**D. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4)**
Qualitative analysis revealed the distinct and complementary roles of Temporal and Distillation Hints:
- **Temporal Hints Only:** While effective at refining the image reprojection loss, this alone was insufficient to fully correct student network errors for moving objects (e.g., a car), as issues originating from the feature matching process in the student's encoder persisted. The teacher network, which does not use feature matching, still provided a more accurate depth for such objects.
- **Distillation Hints Only:** This component significantly improved depth estimation for dynamic objects by facilitating more effective knowledge transfer from the teacher, compensating for student network errors in feature matching. However, relying solely on distillation hints led to less accurate depth for certain surrounding dynamic elements compared to the full MAL.
- **Full MAL (Temporal + Distillation Hints):** The combination of both hints yielded the most accurate depth predictions across various dynamic objects, illustrating that each component addresses distinct sources of error (reprojection loss and feature matching) and their synergistic contribution is necessary for robust performance in dynamic scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Motion-Aware Loss (MAL) presents a significant advancement in the field of self-supervised monocular depth estimation, particularly for applications in dynamic environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Dynamic Scenes:** MAL directly addresses a critical limitation of self-supervised depth estimation methods: their susceptibility to performance degradation in the presence of moving objects. By strategically mitigating errors stemming from object motion in both image reprojection loss and feature matching, MAL substantially improves depth accuracy in complex, dynamic scenarios, as evidenced by the considerable gains on the CityScapes benchmark. This enhanced robustness is crucial for real-world autonomous systems.

2. **Plug-and-Play Design and Inference-Time Efficiency:** A key strength of MAL is its design as a "plug-and-play" module that exclusively operates during the training phase. This ensures that it adds no computational overhead during inference, a vital characteristic for real-time applications such as autonomous vehicles and robotics that require immediate depth perception without latency. Its seamless integration capability allows existing state-of-the-art multi-frame self-supervised depth networks to benefit from MAL without requiring fundamental architectural changes or increased inference complexity, unlike many other dynamic-scene solutions.

3. **Comprehensive Error Mitigation Strategy:** The dual-pronged approach of Temporal Hints and Distillation Hints provides a holistic solution to motion-induced errors. Temporal Hints rectify inconsistencies in the photometric loss by geometrically aligning moving objects, while Distillation Hints enhance the knowledge transfer from a more stable teacher network to the student, specifically targeting feature matching errors. The ablation and qualitative studies confirm that these two components are complementary and indispensable for achieving optimal performance, each addressing distinct aspects of the dynamic scene problem.

4. **Broad Applicability and Portability:** MAL's generic applicability to existing multi-frame self-supervised depth estimation frameworks (demonstrated across ManyDepth, DynamicDepth, and DualRefine) highlights its portability. By optimizing at the loss computation level rather than modifying network architectures, MAL can potentially be integrated into future advancements in the field, making them more robust to dynamic elements. This broadens the utility of self-supervised learning for depth estimation in a wider range of practical scenarios.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Autonomous Driving and Robotics:** More accurate and reliable depth information in dynamic urban environments directly translates to improved safety and operational efficiency for autonomous vehicles, drones, and mobile robots. This includes better obstacle detection, more precise path planning, and enhanced navigation capabilities.
- **Computer Vision Research:** MAL offers a template for how sophisticated loss function design and refined distillation strategies can lead to significant performance improvements without altering the core inference model. This could inspire further research into optimizing training objectives for specific challenges in various self-supervised computer vision tasks.
- **Industrial Applications:** Industries relying on 3D scene understanding, such as automated inspection, virtual/augmented reality, and logistics (e.g., warehouse robots), could benefit from more robust depth sensing, especially in environments involving moving machinery or personnel.

In conclusion, MAL represents a practical and effective step forward in making self-supervised monocular depth estimation more robust to the complexities of real-world dynamic scenes, providing a valuable tool for current and future vision systems.
