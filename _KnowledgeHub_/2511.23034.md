# LatBot: Distilling Universal Latent Actions for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LatBot learns universal latent actions from robot and human-hand videos by disentangling scene and motion tokens and decoding them jointly into future frames and physical action sequences, then distills this knowledge into a VLA via alignment plus reasoning-preservation losses. Achieves state-of-the-art SIMPLER, LIBERO, and real-world Franka few-shot results.

## Problem

- Latent action models like Genie lack task-instruction guidance, so learned latents fail to capture task-relevant scene changes.
- Methods such as UniVLA underuse multi-frame context, yielding imprecise latent actions that cannot accurately capture motion dynamics.
- Prior latent action representations optimize only visual reconstruction and entangle robot motion with environment change, causing a semantic gap from executable actions.

## Method

- ==Decoupled Latent Action Representation==: a VLM encoder with two learnable tokens extracts separate ==scene tokens== (environment change) and ==motion tokens== (robot movement) under task-instruction guidance.
- ==Unified Latent Action Decoder==, initialized from SANA, fuses scene and motion tokens via layer-wise bidirectional interaction to jointly reconstruct future frames and generate inter-frame actions.
- Knowledge distillation into VLA backbones via a ==Latent Action Alignment Loss== (MSE plus KL divergence against the LAM teacher) and a ==Reasoning Preservation Loss== (next-token subtask prediction) to inject physical priors without harming reasoning.

## Results

- On SIMPLER WidowX robot, LatBot reaches **87.5%** average success rate, beating the pi-0.5 baseline by **32.3** points and UniVLA by **39.6** points.
- On LIBERO across four suites, LatBot attains **98.0%** average success, a **3.0**-point gain over pi-0.5 on the long-horizon LIBERO-Long suite.
- Ablation shows decoupled representation and unified decoder are complementary: baseline **51.0%** to **59.4%** (decoupling only) to **61.5%** (decoder only) to **87.5%** combined on SIMPLER WidowX.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adding an action-generation objective alongside visual reconstruction closes the semantic gap between latent actions and physically executable robot motions.
> - Explicitly disentangling robot-induced motion from passive scene change filters task-irrelevant noise and yields more transferable representations.
> - More demonstrations are not always better: redundant full-dataset action patterns can dilute latent action quality compared to a smaller, cleaner subset.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and latent action learning, two rapidly evolving domains in robotic control. The broader research landscape is striving to develop generalist robot policies that can perform diverse tasks across various environments and embodiments, moving beyond task-specific solutions.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models (Section 2.1)** represent a significant leap by extending traditional Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate robot actions conditioned on visual observations and natural language instructions. Early pioneers like RT-1 and Octo demonstrated the power of transformer-based policies trained on vast datasets of robotic trajectories. Subsequent works, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0, further integrated pre-trained VLMs, often by discretizing actions into text-like tokens or appending specialized policy heads. While these models have shown impressive generalization, their primary limitation lies in their heavy reliance on large-scale datasets with explicitly annotated ground-truth action labels. This dependency restricts their scalability and diversity, as collecting such high-quality, large-scale robot demonstration data is inherently expensive and time-consuming.

To overcome these scalability challenges, **Latent Action Models (LAMs) (Section 2.2)** have emerged as a promising direction. The core idea is to extract compact, transferable motion semantics from videos, often without explicit action supervision, making them agnostic to specific robot embodiments. This allows LAMs to leverage a much broader range of data, including large-scale human videos, significantly expanding training sources for robotic policies. Early work on latent actions, such as Genie and LAPO, explored unsupervised learning in virtual environments, with DynaMo extending this to structured state representations using inverse and forward dynamics. In robotics, methods like LAPA, ViLLA-X, Moto-GPT, and UniVLA have integrated latent actions into VLA pretraining to facilitate cross-domain transfer (e.g., from human to robot demonstrations) and reduce reliance on explicit action labels. Moto-GPT, for instance, focuses on motion-centric representations by converting videos into discrete motion tokens. UniVLA uses a two-stage pipeline for task-centric latent actions.

However, existing LAM approaches, as identified by the authors, suffer from several critical shortcomings:
1. **Lack of task instruction guidance:** Some LAMs fail to incorporate high-level task instructions, leading to latent actions that do not fully capture task-relevant dynamics.
2. **Insufficient utilization of multi-frame inputs:** Relying on limited temporal context can result in imprecise latent action representations that struggle to capture complex motion dynamics accurately.
3. **Absence of physical priors:** A crucial weakness is that these models often focus predominantly on visual appearance changes, neglecting the underlying physical principles of real-world interactions. This creates a "semantic gap" between the learned latent actions and the actual executable actions required by a robot, hindering effective transfer.
4. **Entanglement of motion and scene:** Many methods entangle all visual variations—both robot-induced movements and environmental changes—within a single latent representation. This entanglement can introduce irrelevant noise and obscure the direct correspondence between robot motion and latent actions.

LatBot directly addresses these limitations. It seeks to bridge the semantic gap by explicitly incorporating physical priors into latent action learning, disentangling robot motion from environmental changes, and then effectively transferring this enhanced understanding to VLA models through a novel knowledge distillation strategy. By focusing on learning "universal" latent actions that are both visually comprehensive and physically grounded, LatBot aims to significantly improve generalization and few-shot transferability in diverse robotic manipulation tasks, thereby pushing the boundaries of what generalist robot policies can achieve.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the LatBot research is to significantly enhance the generalization capabilities of robotic policies in downstream manipulation tasks by learning highly transferable "universal" latent actions from a diverse range of large-scale object manipulation videos. The authors are motivated by several critical shortcomings observed in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and latent action learning (LAM) approaches.

The primary motivations and corresponding objectives are:

1. **Addressing the Neglect of Physical Priors in Latent Actions:** Existing LAMs predominantly rely on visual reconstruction objectives. While effective for capturing image-space features, this bias often neglects crucial physical priors (e.g., real-world distances, orientations, forces) that underpin executable robot actions.
 - **Objective:** To propose a Universal Latent Action Learning framework that incorporates action sequence prediction alongside future frame reconstruction, thereby enabling the model to capture richer physical priors and bridge the semantic gap between visual representations and executable robot actions.

2. **Improving Task-Relevant Information Capture:** Some prior latent action models lack task instruction guidance, leading to representations that may not effectively capture task-relevant changes and dynamics.
 - **Objective:** To learn latent actions under the explicit guidance of task instructions and utilize multiple frames as input, ensuring that the representations are comprehensive and contextually aware of the task at hand.

3. **Disentangling Robot Motion from Environmental Changes:** Current methods often entangle robot-induced movements with passive environmental changes within a single latent action representation. This introduces irrelevant noise and weakens the correspondence between latent actions and actual robot dynamics.
 - **Objective:** To decompose latent actions into distinct "motion tokens" (encoding active robot movements like end-effector translation, rotation, and gripper actions) and "scene tokens" (capturing passive environmental changes like object position, pose, and background dynamics). This disentanglement aims to filter out irrelevant dynamics and provide more structured, cleaner latent actions.

4. **Enabling Seamless Transferability to Downstream Robotic Tasks:** The ultimate goal of learning universal latent actions is to facilitate their effective transfer to real-world robot control, especially in few-shot settings where demonstration data is scarce. The semantic gap and entangled representations in prior works hinder this transfer.
 - **Objective:** To develop an effective knowledge distillation strategy that transfers the physically-grounded, disentangled latent action knowledge from the pre-trained Latent Action Model (LAM) into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This strategy must preserve the VLA model's reasoning and instruction-understanding capabilities while equipping it with robust action awareness and motion planning abilities, leading to strong few-shot transferability in robotic manipulation.

In essence, LatBot aims to create a new paradigm for learning latent actions that are not only visually descriptive but also deeply aware of physical interactions, structured, and readily transferable to practical robot control scenarios with minimal task-specific demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LatBot framework is designed to learn universal latent actions and effectively distill this knowledge into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. It comprises three main stages: Latent Action Disentanglement, Unified Latent Action Decoding, and Knowledge Distillation for VLA models.

#### 3.1. Decoupled Latent Action Representation

LatBot addresses the problem of entangled visual variations by proposing a decoupled latent action representation. Instead of a single latent action, it separates it into two components:
- **Motion Representation (Z_mot):** This component is designed to capture the active changes driven by the robot's own movements, such as end-effector translation, rotation, and gripper actions.
- **Scene Representation (Z_sce):** This component captures passive scene changes induced by environmental dynamics, like object positions, poses, and background fluctuations.

This decomposition aims to reduce task-irrelevant noise and establish a clearer correspondence between specific robot movements, environmental variations, and the latent action representations. To extract these representations, a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), such as InternVL3.5-2B, is employed as the latent action encoder (`f_vlm`). The VLM takes a sequence of visual frames (`V_t:t+k`) and a task instruction (`ℓ`) as input. The key innovation here is the introduction of two learnable latent action tokens, `[CP_SCE]` and `[CP_MOT]`, into the VLM's vocabulary. These tokens guide the VLM to encode contextual information specifically into structured scene and motion representations, respectively. An instruction-tuning template is used to guide the VLM in summarizing these latent actions from multi-frame sequences, ensuring task-relevant encoding.

#### 3.2. Unified Latent Action Decoding

To ensure that the disentangled latent actions (Z_mot and Z_sce) are physically grounded and capture rich dynamics, they are used as conditional inputs to a unified decoder. This decoder is tasked with jointly guiding two objectives:
- **Future frame reconstruction (V_t+k):** This objective encourages the latent actions to capture observable visual scene variations.
- **Inter-frame action generation (A_t:t+k):** This objective provides physical-level guidance, enabling the model to establish a direct connection between the latent actions and real physical motions (e.g., gripper or hand trajectories and orientations).

The unified decoder is initialized from a powerful pre-trained image generation model like SANA-1.6B. A crucial design feature is the progressive fusion of scene and motion information through layer-wise bidirectional interactions within the decoder. This mechanism allows scene dynamics to inform and guide action generation, while motion tokens refine and improve visual reconstruction, leading to mutual reinforcement between the two modalities. This joint optimization ensures that the learned universal latent actions encode both visual dynamics and physical priors, enhancing prediction across diverse manipulation scenarios and closely aligning with real robot actions.

#### 3.3. Knowledge Distillation for VLA Models

Since the pre-trained Latent Action Model (LAM) is primarily focused on scene reconstruction and action generation, its learned knowledge needs to be effectively transferred to a full Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for downstream robot control. LatBot proposes a novel knowledge distillation strategy to achieve this, allowing the VLA (student model, typically a VLM like π0.5) to inherit motion understanding and physical priors from the LAM (teacher model) while preserving its inherent vision-language reasoning abilities.

The distillation process involves two main loss functions:
- **Latent Action Alignment Loss (L_a):** This loss function aims to align the latent action representation (`Z_a`) extracted by the teacher LAM with the action representation (`ˆZ_a`) generated by the student VLA. The LAM's representation `Z_a` is conditioned on multi-frame inputs and language instructions, while the VLA's `ˆZ_a` is conditioned on the first frame and instruction, as it's meant to anticipate future motion. `L_a` combines an MSE term for feature consistency (`∥ˆZ_a - Z_a∥₂²`) and a KL divergence term for distributional alignment (`KL(p(ˆZ_a) ∥ p(Z_a))`). This helps the student VLA gain future frame forecasting and physics-aware capabilities.
- **Reasoning Preservation Loss (L_r):** To prevent the distillation from compromising the VLM's crucial language understanding and reasoning abilities, `L_r` is introduced. This is an autoregressive next-token prediction objective (`-Σ log p(w_i+1 | w_≤i, ℓ, V_1)`) that encourages the student VLM to generate coherent sub-task descriptions based on the current frame and task instruction. This maintains its capacity for sub-task planning and instruction-following.

The total objective for knowledge transfer is `L = L_a + λ_r · L_r`, where `λ_r` (defaulted to 0.5) balances the two loss components.

**Action Expert Finetuning:** After knowledge distillation, the VLA model possesses enhanced motion planning and action awareness but still generates latent representations. To make these directly executable by a robot, a final fine-tuning stage is performed in both real-world and simulated robotic environments. An "action expert" module is incorporated, and the overall action loss is decomposed into `L_ee` (Mean Squared Error for end-effector translation and rotation) and `L_gripper` (Binary Cross-Entropy for gripper state), enabling precise generation of continuous, executable robot actions.

**Training Data:** The entire framework is pre-trained on a massive, diverse dataset comprising one million video episodes from robot manipulation datasets (OXE, DROID, AgiBoT) and human hand manipulation datasets (EgoDex). EgoDex is particularly rich in its detailed action annotations (3D positions and 6D orientations of hands and fingertips), which provides fine-grained supervision for learning high-quality latent actions. The authors define unified action and state spaces to handle the heterogeneity of robot and human demonstration data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LatBot's performance was rigorously evaluated across simulated and real-world robotic environments, demonstrating significant advancements over existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

#### 4.1. Manipulation Benchmarks on SIMPLER

The SIMPLER benchmark, designed to bridge the real-to-sim gap, was used to evaluate LatBot on both Google and WidowX robots.
- **Google Robot:** LatBot achieved state-of-the-art results in both Visual Matching and Variant Aggregation settings.
 - In **Visual Matching**, LatBot scored an average success rate of **78.0%**, a substantial **25.3% improvement** over the strong baseline π0. It also surpassed the closed-source RT-2-X.
 - In **Variant Aggregation**, it achieved **70.1%**, outperforming π0 by **24.1%** and RT-2-X by **15.7%**. These results highlight LatBot's robustness and ability to generalize across visually varied scenes.
- **WidowX Robot:** LatBot showed an even more pronounced advantage, achieving an average success rate of **87.5%**. This represents a remarkable **32.3% improvement** over π0.5 and gains of **39.6%** over UniVLA and **46.7%** over villa-X, both of which are latent-action-based methods. This strongly validates LatBot's effectiveness in transferring latent action knowledge to the VLA domain, enhancing robustness and generalization.

#### 4.2. Manipulation Benchmarks on LIBERO

The LIBERO benchmark, which assesses lifelong learning in robotic manipulation across four task suites, also saw LatBot achieve top performance.
- LatBot achieved the highest overall success rate of **98.0%** across LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Spatial, and LIBERO-Long environments.
- Notably, on **LIBERO-Long**, which involves complex long-horizon tasks, LatBot demonstrated a **3.0% improvement** over π0.5. This indicates that the knowledge distillation process equipped the VLA model with superior motion planning capabilities and future-state awareness, crucial for handling extended task sequences.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation with Franka Robot

To assess performance in the high-uncertainty, high-generalization demand of real-world robotics, experiments were conducted on a Franka Research 3 robot across five challenging manipulation tasks: Pick up the cup, Put the building block into the corresponding slot, Close the oven, Dip the brush in the sauce, and Put the pot in the oven. The evaluation specifically focused on few-shot transferability, using 10, 50, and all available demonstrations (100) per task.
- LatBot consistently **outperformed π0 and π0.5** across almost all tasks and training settings.
- **Strong Few-Shot Learning:**
 - For "Pick up the cup" (Task 1), LatBot achieved **60% success with only 10 demonstrations** and **80% with 50-shot**, while baselines entirely failed in few-shot and reached at most 20% with the full dataset. The higher 50-shot performance over the full dataset was attributed to a more concise and cleaner subset, leading to better-focused latent actions.
 - For "Put the building block into the corresponding slot" (Task 2), requiring multi-stage control and precise alignment, LatBot achieved **80% success with 50 demonstrations**, whereas baselines showed no success until trained on the full dataset.
 - For fine-grained manipulation tasks like "Dip the brush in the sauce" (Task 4), LatBot achieved **50% success with 50 demonstrations**, a task where π0.5 entirely failed. With the full dataset, LatBot surpassed π0.5 by 60%.
- These results robustly confirm that LatBot's physically-grounded latent actions enable the VLA model to effectively plan and execute complex, multi-step, and fine-grained manipulations with significantly less real-world training data.

#### 4.4. Components Analysis (Ablation Study)

An ablation study on the SIMPLER benchmark revealed the individual and synergistic contributions of LatBot's key components:
- The **Decoupled Latent Action Representations (DLA)** alone improved the UniVLA-style baseline from 51.0% to 59.4%, demonstrating the benefit of isolating motion-critical cues.
- The **Unified Action Decoder (UAD)** alone further improved the average to 61.5%, highlighting its role in strengthening the mapping between latent actions and executable robot actions.
- When **both DLA and UAD were combined (full LatBot)**, the average success rate dramatically jumped to **87.5%**. This strong synergy suggests that DLA provides structured, manipulation-relevant latent actions, while UAD injects physical priors, leading to highly accurate and physically consistent action predictions.

#### 4.5. Additional Analysis: Visual Grounding

Analysis of attention maps showed that latent action distillation significantly enhanced the VLM's spatial grounding capabilities. After distillation, the VLM's attention maps were more accurately concentrated on task-relevant targets, even in the presence of distractors, indicating improved visual understanding aligned with the physical task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LatBot represents a substantial step forward in the field of generalist robotic learning, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Semantic Gap in Latent Actions:** The most critical significance lies in LatBot's novel approach to explicitly incorporate physical priors and action sequence prediction into latent action learning. By moving beyond pure visual reconstruction, it imbues latent actions with a deeper, physically-grounded understanding of robot interactions, effectively bridging the "semantic gap" between abstract visual changes and concrete executable robot actions. This makes the learned representations far more meaningful and directly transferable for control.

2. **Enhanced Transferability and Generalization:** The core achievement of LatBot is the creation of "universal" latent actions that are highly transferable across diverse robotic platforms and tasks. The results on SIMPLER, LIBERO, and real-world Franka robot experiments unequivocally demonstrate superior generalization and robustness compared to state-of-the-art VLA models. This is particularly crucial for real-world robotics, where environments are dynamic and tasks vary.

3. **Few-Shot Learning Capabilities:** LatBot's ability to achieve high success rates with an extremely limited number of real-world demonstrations (e.g., 10 or 50 trajectories per task) is a game-changer. This drastically reduces the prohibitively high cost and time associated with collecting large, task-specific robot demonstration datasets, which is a major bottleneck in robot learning. It paves the way for rapid deployment of new skills in novel scenarios.

4. **Disentangled and Structured Representations:** The decomposition of latent actions into motion and scene tokens is a powerful design choice. By disentangling active robot movements from passive environmental changes, LatBot learns cleaner, more structured representations. This filtering of irrelevant dynamics leads to more precise motion understanding and better action prediction, contributing to the overall robustness and accuracy.

5. **Effective Knowledge Distillation for VLA Models:** The proposed knowledge distillation strategy is highly impactful. It successfully transfers the rich, physically-grounded latent action knowledge from the specialized LAM to a generalist VLA model without compromising the VLA's inherent language understanding and reasoning abilities. This means that VLA models can leverage the best of both worlds: robust perception and reasoning, combined with precise, physics-aware action generation.

6. **Scalability to Diverse Data Sources:** LatBot's pre-training on a massive dataset combining robot and human hand manipulation videos (including detailed EgoDex data) underscores its potential for scalability. The unified action and state spaces facilitate learning from heterogeneous data, which is essential for developing truly generalist robot policies that can learn from various sources, including human demonstrations.

7. **Future Impact on Robotics Development:** The advancements brought by LatBot could accelerate the development of more autonomous and adaptable robots. By enabling robots to learn complex manipulation skills with fewer demonstrations and generalize across varied contexts, it moves closer to the vision of general-purpose robots that can operate effectively in unstructured human environments. Future work on extracting additional latent tokens from even larger and more diverse datasets, and applying them to long-horizon, multi-embodiment tasks, could unlock further capabilities for highly intelligent and versatile robotic systems.
