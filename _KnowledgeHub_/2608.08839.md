# SG-WAM: Text-Grounded and Spatial-aware Semantic Guidance for World-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SG-WAM guides a world-action model with semantic foresight predicted by a VLM planner instead of observation-independent text embeddings. Learnable query tokens decode text-grounded features aligned to SigLIP2 and spatial-aware features aligned to Depth Anything 3 for future keyframes, then gated fusion injects them into a video expert whose joint attention feeds an action expert. It reaches 98.7% average on LIBERO and 81.3% on LIBERO-Plus.

## Problem

- Existing world-action models embed instructions with frozen encoders like CLIP or T5 computed independently of the observation, so generation leans on visual cues.
- Predicted future videos drift semantically away from the commanded task, and the action expert inherits dynamic features poorly aligned with the instruction, degrading manipulation accuracy.
- Prior guidance signals fall short: pixel subgoals entangle intent with appearance, masks and object slots carry little instruction semantics, and subgoal text stays too coarse.

## Method

- A ==Qwen3.5-2B== ==VLM planner== appends base plus two modality-specific ==query token groups== to the image-instruction sequence, and lightweight ==resamplers== decode dense foresight maps for K=4 future keyframes.
- ==Alignment loss== supervises the two branches against frozen teachers: MSE to ==SigLIP2== penultimate patch tokens for text grounding, smooth-L1 to ==Depth Anything 3== features for scene geometry.
- A learnable ==sigmoid gate== fuses both maps into one guidance representation injected via ==parallel cross-attention== in every video-expert block, trained in three stages with ==flow matching==.

## Results

- LIBERO average success reaches **98.7%**, above LingBot-VA at **98.5%**, and beats the real-world baselines FastWAM and GE-Act by **1.1** and **2.2** points.
- LIBERO-Plus average hits **81.3%**, **+3.5** points over the best baseline, with language-perturbation success at **81.7%** (**+2.2**) and sensor-noise at **90.7%**.
- Ablations on LIBERO: dropping guidance costs **1.5** points (**97.2%**), removing text-grounded semantics **1.0**, spatial-aware **0.4**, and single-stage training **0.8**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning generation on foresight computed jointly from observation and instruction, rather than a precomputed text embedding, is what makes generated futures obey commands.
> - Aligning to dense features of language-aligned and geometry foundation models beats regressing raw pixels or depth, staying reliable under lighting and height shifts.
> - Staged curricula matter: an action expert trained before its guidance signal is reliable learns from noise, costing accuracy versus phased attachment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation research has increasingly explored various paradigms for developing intelligent agents. World-Action Models (WAMs) represent a class of approaches that aim to learn physical dynamics and task-relevant temporal structures by jointly predicting future observations (videos) and actions. This predictive modeling allows WAMs to leverage large-scale video data for pretraining, in addition to robot demonstrations, leading to performance and generalization capabilities across diverse manipulation tasks. This contrasts with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, which directly map observations and instructions to actions without explicit future prediction.

Despite the advancements in WAMs, a common limitation in many existing video-based WAMs is their primary reliance on visual cues for generating future frames and actions. Language instructions are typically incorporated via text embeddings from off-the-shelf encoders (e.g., CLIP, T5) that process text independently of visual observations. This independent processing can lead to a semantic misalignment between the predicted videos and actions and the actual language instructions, impacting the accuracy and reliability of robotic manipulation. The action expert, therefore, often operates on dynamic features that are poorly aligned with the given instruction.

Previous efforts to address this limitation have included incorporating explicit spatial cues or predicting visual subgoals (e.g., future masks, object slots, subgoal images) for world-action models. Other approaches have focused on language planning, such as interleaving language subgoals with video generation or decomposing high-level commands into subtask text. However, these methods often present their own challenges: pixel-level subgoals can conflate task intent with appearance details, explicit masks or slots may lack rich instruction-relevant semantics beyond object layout, and subgoal text might remain too coarse to specify precise interaction timing and location.

The presented work introduces SG-WAM to address the semantic misalignment and instruction-grounding limitations of existing WAMs. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level language instructions and low-level action execution by providing fine-grained, instruction-level semantic guidance derived from a Vision-Language Model (VLM). This approach positions SG-WAM as an evolution within the WAM paradigm, seeking to enhance their instruction-following capabilities through a more integrated and semantically aware guidance mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the instruction-grounding capacity of World-Action Models (WAMs) for robotic manipulation. The motivation stems from a critical limitation observed in most existing video-based WAMs. These models typically generate future videos and corresponding actions by predominantly leveraging visual cues, with language instructions integrated through off-the-shelf text encoders. These encoders embed instructions independently of the visual observations.

This independent processing leads to several issues:
1. **Semantic Misalignment:** The videos predicted by WAMs frequently exhibit semantic misalignment with the corresponding language instructions. For instance, an instruction to "put the red mug in the blue bowl" might lead to the model attempting to manipulate an incorrect object or place it in an unintended location, even if the visual dynamics appear plausible.
2. **Degraded Action Accuracy:** The semantic misalignment directly impacts the accuracy of predicted actions. If the model's internal representation of the future state does not accurately reflect the instruction, the resulting actions will likely fail to achieve the desired task. The action expert, relying on dynamic features that are poorly aligned with the language instruction, cannot effectively execute the commanded task.
3. **Lack of Fine-grained Guidance:** Existing approaches that introduce spatial cues or visual subgoals often fall short of providing fine-grained, instruction-level semantic guidance. Pixel-level subgoals can entangle appearance details with task intent, while explicit masks or object slots may not convey sufficient instruction-relevant semantics beyond basic object localization. Text-based subgoals, while helpful, can still be too coarse to specify the precise "where" and "when" of interactions.

To overcome these limitations, the authors propose SG-WAM. The key motivation is to introduce a semantic guidance mechanism that leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to act as a semantic planner. This planner is designed to predict "text-grounded" and "spatial-aware" semantic foresight.
- **Text-grounded semantic foresight:** This component aims to ensure the instruction is properly understood and applied by identifying the correct target objects within the scene. It grounds the instruction in the visual context.
- **Spatial-aware semantic foresight:** This component provides information about the scene's geometry, which is crucial for executing precise manipulation tasks. It informs the model about object layout, surface geometry, and relative distances.

By injecting this combined foresight into the world-action model as high-level semantic guidance, the research aims to ensure that both the generation of future videos and the prediction of actions faithfully adhere to the given language instruction, thereby improving manipulation precision and instruction-following capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SG-WAM framework consists of two primary components: a Vision-Language Model (VLM) planner responsible for predicting semantic guidance, and a world-action model that generates future video frames and actions under this guidance. The overall training proceeds in three distinct stages.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The system takes a current observation ($o_t$) and a language instruction ($\ell$) as input. The VLM planner processes these inputs to produce semantic foresight, which then conditions the world-action model. The world-action model, composed of a video expert and an action expert, predicts a sequence of future observations ($o_{t+1:t+T}$) and a chunk of actions ($a_{t:t+H-1}$).

#### 3.2. Semantic Foresight Learning

The semantic planner is instantiated using Qwen3.5. It is designed to predict dense feature maps representing future semantic foresight in a single forward pass.
- **Input Processing:** Learnable query tokens are appended to the combined image-instruction sequence. The hidden states of these query tokens are then used to decode the foresight.
- **Query Token Structure:** The query tokens are divided into three groups: a base group that learns shared semantic knowledge and two modality-specific groups. One specific group learns text-grounded semantics, and the other learns spatial-aware semantics. Each branch (text-grounded and spatial-aware) processes its corresponding specific group concatenated with the base group's states.
- **Multi-View Processing:** The planner is applied independently to each camera view, with shared weights, ensuring that the instruction is grounded separately within every view.
- **Resampler:** A lightweight resampler ($R_m$) decodes the concatenated query states ($H_m$) into a dense feature map ($\hat{F}_m$). This resampler utilizes a bank of learnable grid queries ($U_m$) that cross-attend to both the image states (E) and $H_m$. This mechanism allows the resampler to incorporate fine spatial details from the observation while leveraging the query states to specify anticipated changes.
 - The output feature maps, $\hat{F}_{tg}$ (text-grounded) and $\hat{F}_{spa}$ (spatial-aware), each consist of P=256 tokens. The text-grounded map has a feature dimension $d_{tg}=1024$, and the spatial-aware map has $d_{spa}=2048$.
- **Training Supervision:** The predicted foresight maps are supervised by features extracted from actual future keyframes using frozen teacher encoders.
 - **Text-Grounded Target ($F_{tg}$):** Derived from the penultimate-layer patch tokens of SigLIP2, a language-aligned visual feature space with dense spatial structure.
 - **Spatial-Aware Target ($F_{spa}$):** Obtained from the dense features of Depth Anything 3, which encodes object layout, surface geometry, and relative distances. The alignment is performed in feature space rather than directly regressing raw depth.
- **Loss Function:** The planner is optimized to match these teacher features using a combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Smooth L1 (SL1) losses:
 $L_{plan} = MSE(\hat{F}_{tg}, F_{tg}) + \lambda_{spa} SL_1(\hat{F}_{spa}, F_{spa})$
 This loss is averaged over tokens, feature channels, and K predicted keyframes. The foresight covers K=4 future keyframes at evenly spaced offsets.

#### 3.3. Semantic-Guided World-Action Model

The world-action model is constructed upon a pretrained video diffusion model, with its video expert coupled to an action expert via joint attention.
- **Guidance Representation (Z):** The predicted text-grounded ($\hat{F}_{tg}$) and spatial-aware ($\hat{F}_{spa}$) feature maps are fused into a single guidance representation (Z) using learned linear projections and a scalar gate ($g$):
 $Z = Proj_{tg}(\hat{F}_{tg}) + \sigma(g) Proj_{spa}(\hat{F}_{spa})$
- **Guidance Injection:** This guidance representation (Z) is injected into every block of the video expert. Crucially, it operates through an *additional* parallel cross-attention branch, maintaining the original text conditioning. Positional encodings for keyframe time and spatial location are added to each guidance token to direct its influence spatially and temporally.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance:** To enable classifier-free guidance at inference, the semantic guidance (Z) is randomly dropped with a probability of 0.15 during training.
- **Action Expert:** The action expert does not receive direct guidance injection but benefits from the semantic foresight indirectly through its joint attention mechanism with the video expert, which is conditioned by Z.
- **Training Objective:** Both the video and action experts are trained using a flow-matching objective. For a clean target ($y$) and Gaussian noise ($\epsilon$), a noisy sample $x_\tau = (1-\tau)y + \tau\epsilon$ is formed, and the model regresses the velocity $u = \epsilon - y$:
 $L_{gen} = E||\hat{v}_{vid} - u_{vid}||^2_2 + \lambda_{act} E||\hat{v}_{act} - u_{act}||^2_2$
 where $\hat{v}$ denotes the predicted velocity, and subscripts refer to video and action experts.

#### 3.4. Three-Stage Training Paradigm

The model is trained in a progressive manner:
1. **Stage 1: Semantic Planner Pre-training:** The planner, including query-token embeddings and resampler heads, is trained independently to predict the future keyframe features. Only the alignment loss ($L_{plan}$) is used, and teacher encoders are frozen. This stage ensures the planner produces consistent foresight.
2. **Stage 2: Semantic-Guided World Model Co-Training:** The world model (initialized from a pretrained LTX-Video diffusion model) is connected to the semantic planner. The planner and world model are jointly optimized with: $L_{stage2} = L_{vid} + \lambda_{plan} L_{plan}$. This allows the world model to learn to utilize the foresight before action-specific gradients are introduced.
3. **Stage 3: Semantic-Guided World-Action Co-Training:** The action expert is added. All components (planner, video expert, action expert) are jointly optimized with the combined loss: $L = L_{gen} + \lambda_{plan} L_{plan}$. In this final stage, all components are conditioned on the planner's own predictions, matching the inference setup.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **VLM Planner:** Qwen3.5 2B.
- **Teacher Models:** SigLIP2 for text-grounded (penultimate-layer patch tokens) and Depth Anything 3 for spatial-aware (last-layer features).
- **Keyframes:** K=4 future keyframes, 32 shared base tokens, 32 query tokens per semantic branch.
- **World Model Initialization:** From a pretrained LTX-Video diffusion model.
- **Prediction Horizon:** T=9 future frames, H=36 action steps.
- **Training Resources:** Eight H100 GPUs.
- **Hyperparameters:** Specific learning rates, weight decay, warmup steps, and loss weighting coefficients ($\lambda_{spa}=4 \times 10^{-3}$, $\lambda_{plan}=0.25$, $\lambda_{act}=1.0$) are specified for each stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SG-WAM was conducted across simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus) and a real-world robotic platform, addressing four key questions regarding performance, robustness, instruction-following capabilities, and component contributions.

#### 4.1. Performance and Robustness

**LIBERO Benchmark (Q1):**
- SG-WAM achieved an average success rate of 98.7% on the LIBERO benchmark, which covers Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites. This performance surpassed existing state-of-the-art methods, including LingBot-VA (98.5%).
- Compared to the baselines used in real-world experiments, SG-WAM exhibited an improvement of 1.1 points over FastWAM and 2.2 points over GE-Act.

**LIBERO-Plus Benchmark (Q2 - Robustness under Perturbations):**
- This benchmark evaluates performance under seven perturbation factors (object layout, camera viewpoint, robot initial state, language rephrasing, lighting, background texture, and sensor noise).
- SG-WAM achieved the highest average success rate of 81.3%, exceeding the next best baseline by 3.5 points.
- A notable improvement was observed under language perturbation, with an 81.7% success rate (+2.2 points), suggesting that the method's semantic foresight, grounded in the current observation, contributes to robustness when instructions or visual appearances are altered.

**Real-world Multi-task Experiments (Q1 & Q2):**
- Experiments were conducted on an AgileX Cobot dual-arm platform for four manipulation tasks: "Put kiwi into basket," "Stack bowls in order," "Fill pan in order," and "Lift pot."
- SG-WAM consistently showed higher success rates compared to GE-Act and FastWAM across all four standard tasks. The gains were particularly pronounced in tasks requiring object selection among distractors and adherence to a commanded placement order.
- **Generalization:** SG-WAM maintained its effectiveness in generalization tests. It performed accurately when the basket height was unseen during training ("Put kiwi into basket (Height)") and under unseen lighting conditions for the pan task ("Fill pan in order (Light)"). This indicates that the semantic foresight, represented in a feature space, remains reliable despite shifts in scene geometry or appearance.

#### 4.2. Instruction-Following Analysis (Q3)

Two qualitative studies were performed to investigate how the predicted foresight contributes to instruction-following.

**Out-of-Distribution Instruction Following:**
- When trained on DROID and presented with instructions unseen during training, an RGB-based WAM frequently failed to follow the instruction (e.g., placing a bottle on a stove instead of a microwave, grasping the wrong item).
- In contrast, SG-WAM accurately performed the commanded actions, such as placing the bottle on the microwave and grasping the specified carrot from the sink to place it in the black pot. This illustrates SG-WAM's ability to ground instructions effectively, even for novel scenarios.

**Instruction-Grounded Attention Analysis:**
- Comparison of action-to-video attention heatmaps on LIBERO revealed that the RGB-based WAM diffused its attention across multiple objects and the robot arm itself.
- SG-WAM, however, concentrated its attention on the specific object mentioned in the instruction, suggesting that the semantic guidance directs the model's focus to task-relevant regions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Q4)

Ablation studies on LIBERO evaluated the contribution of key SG-WAM components:

- **Semantic Guidance:** Removing semantic guidance (relying only on text conditioning) resulted in the largest performance drop, from 98.7% to 97.2%. This demonstrates the positive impact of instruction-grounded guidance for joint video-action generation.
- **Dual-Semantics Design:**
 - Removing text-grounded semantics led to a drop to 98.3%, indicating its role in correct object identification and selection.
 - Removing spatial-aware semantics resulted in a drop to 97.7%, highlighting its importance for precise manipulation by providing geometric cues.
 - Replacing shared semantic query tokens with specific query tokens caused a minor but consistent drop to 98.4%, suggesting the benefit of learning shared semantic knowledge.
- **Three-Stage Training:** A single-stage training approach, where all components are trained jointly from scratch, degraded performance to 97.9%. This supports the staged training paradigm, which allows the planner to develop reliable guidance before the action expert begins extensive learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SG-WAM, a semantic guidance method for world-action models, addressing a core limitation in robotic manipulation: the semantic misalignment between language instructions and generated robotic actions. The work introduces a novel paradigm by guiding WAMs with explicit text-grounded and spatial-aware semantic foresight, rather than relying solely on observation-independent text embeddings.

The significance and potential impact of SG-WAM include:
- **Enhanced Instruction Following:** By leveraging a VLM planner to translate instructions into fine-grained semantic foresight, SG-WAM enables WAMs to more faithfully interpret and execute language commands. This is crucial for developing robots that can understand and perform complex tasks described in natural language.
- **Improved Manipulation Precision:** The incorporation of spatial-aware semantic foresight provides critical geometric information, allowing for more accurate and precise manipulation. This capability is vital for tasks requiring delicate object interaction and placement.
- **Increased Robustness:** The experimental results on LIBERO-Plus demonstrate that SG-WAM improves robustness against various environmental and instructional perturbations, including changes in object layout, camera views, lighting, and language rephrasing. This suggests a greater adaptability of robots deployed in real-world, dynamic environments.
- **Strong Generalization to Unseen Conditions:** The real-world experiments showed that SG-WAM retains its performance under generalization tests, such as tasks involving unseen container heights or novel lighting conditions. This indicates a potential for deploying SG-WAM-equipped robots in scenarios beyond their immediate training data.
- **Integration of VLM Reasoning with WAM Generative Capabilities:** The approach effectively combines the high-level reasoning and understanding capabilities of Vision-Language Models with the predictive and generative power of World-Action Models. This integration represents a step towards more intelligent and versatile robotic systems that can reason about tasks at a semantic level while physically interacting with the environment.
- **Setting a New Benchmark:** SG-WAM achieves higher performance on standard benchmarks like LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, establishing a new baseline for instruction-conditioned robotic manipulation. This can stimulate further research in developing more semantically aware and robust robotic control policies.

In summary, SG-WAM offers a foundational advancement in robot manipulation by explicitly guiding WAMs with contextually relevant, instruction-grounded, and spatially informed semantic blueprints. This could lead to a new generation of robots capable of more reliably and precisely executing human-given instructions in diverse and challenging environments.
