# PhyGDPO: Physics-Aware Groupwise Direct Preference Optimization for Physically Consistent Text-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyGDPO introduces a physics-aware groupwise direct preference optimization framework for text-to-video generation, creating videos that adhere more faithfully to physical laws. This method achieved superior physical consistency compared to state-of-the-art models on various benchmarks, supported by a novel data construction pipeline and efficient DPO training.

## Problem

- Current text-to-video (T2V) models often lack implicit physics reasoning, failing to consistently model complex physical interactions and human motions despite high visual fidelity.
- Existing physics modeling approaches (graphics-based for specific scenarios, or LLM-based prompt extension) are impractical for complex real-world scenes or delegate reasoning, leading to errors and limited implicit physics understanding within T2V models.
- The scarcity of physics-rich training data and the limitations of vanilla Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), such as suboptimal 'winning' cases and computational inefficiency, hinder effective physics alignment for T2V models.

## Method

- A novel data construction pipeline, ==PhyAugPipe==, was developed to filter and sample physics-rich text-video pairs (==PhyVidGen-135K==) using a Vision-Language Model with ==Chain-of-Thought== reasoning and difficulty-aware sampling.
- The **PhyGDPO** framework was formulated, employing a ==Plackett-Luce probabilistic model== for holistic preference learning across a group of generated videos against a real-world 'winning' video.
- ==Physics-Guided Rewarding (PGR)== adaptively modulates DPO parameters to amplify the influence of physics-violating samples, and ==LoRA-Switch Reference (LoRA-SR)== efficiently shares the model backbone to reduce GPU memory and stabilize training.

## Results

- PhyGDPO achieved a **4.5x higher score** on 'hard actions' compared to the base model (Wan2.1-14B) and outperformed closed-source models like OpenAI Sora2 and Google Veo3.1 on VideoPhy2 and PhyGenBench datasets.
- User studies involving **104 participants** showed a significant preference for PhyGDPO-generated videos, favored by **94.2%** over Vcrafter2, **67.3%** over Sora2, and **64.4%** over Veo3.1, indicating superior physical plausibility.
- The LoRA-Switch Reference (LoRA-SR) scheme reduced GPU memory consumption by **44%** and storage size by over **60x**, while concurrently improving the 'hard action' score by **14%**, demonstrating efficiency gains without sacrificing performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Curating specialized, physics-rich datasets through VLM-guided filtering and difficulty-aware sampling significantly improves a text-to-video model's ability to learn and apply physical principles.
> - A groupwise preference model, especially when using real-world videos as definitive 'winning' cases, provides a more robust and holistic signal for aligning generative models with complex, nuanced attributes like physical consistency than traditional pairwise DPO.
> - Adaptive rewarding based on physical plausibility can effectively steer DPO towards correcting significant errors, while an efficient LoRA-based reference scheme can make DPO scalable and stable for training large generative models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Text-to-Video (T2V) generation has advanced significantly, achieving notable visual quality in synthesized videos. However, a persistent challenge in this domain is the accurate and consistent modeling of physical laws within the generated content. Current T2V models, despite their progress in visual fidelity, frequently exhibit deficiencies in depicting realistic physical interactions and phenomena.

Existing approaches to improve physics modeling in T2V can be broadly categorized. Graphics-based methods, such as those relying on simulation engines, are limited to specifying physical parameters for simple scenarios like basic rigid body dynamics. Their applicability to complex, real-world scenes is constrained by the impracticality of comprehensive parameterization. Another technical route involves prompt extension with large language models (LLMs). These methods leverage LLMs to enrich text prompts with explicit physics laws, subsequently using these extended prompts for iterative video generation or model finetuning. However, such approaches have been noted to lack implicit physics reasoning, essentially outsourcing the reasoning process to the LLM. This can lead to inaccuracies, as current LLM physics reasoning capabilities may be weak or erroneous, and the prompt-following ability of T2V models remains limited.

Most contemporary T2V foundation models are trained on extensive text-video datasets. While this leads to high visual quality, their inherent physics reasoning capabilities are often limited. Models like OpenAI Sora2 and Google DeepMind Veo3.1, while impressive, can falter in complex human motions or physical phenomena. A primary reason identified for this limitation is the scarcity of negative training data, which would provide contrastive signals to discourage physically inconsistent generations.

The emergence of direct preference optimization (DPO) offers a potential solution by aligning models with human preferences. However, directly applying vanilla DPO to physics-aware T2V generation presents several issues: (i) a lack of training data specifically designed to capture diverse physical activities and interactions; (ii) the inability to guarantee correct physics learning when using generated videos as "winning" cases, as their physical realism can be limited, and the pairwise comparison nature of the Bradley-Terry probabilistic model struggles to capture holistic preference signals for physical plausibility; and (iii) inefficiencies arising from the full model duplication required for the reference model in vanilla DPO, leading to high GPU memory consumption and reduced efficiency.

This research aims to address these identified limitations by introducing a novel data construction pipeline and a modified DPO framework, positioning itself to advance physically consistent T2V generation beyond the current state-of-the-art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to synthesize videos that adhere faithfully to physical laws, thereby moving beyond mere visual quality to achieve physical consistency in text-to-video generation. This goal is motivated by the potential for T2V models to evolve into more sophisticated real-world simulators, benefiting a range of applications.

The key motivations and specific objectives are:

- **Improve Physics Reasoning in T2V Models:** The primary motivation is to enhance the implicit physics reasoning capability of video generation models. This would make them more akin to real-world simulators, which could significantly impact fields such as video gaming, autonomous driving, robotics, and film making, where realistic physical interactions are crucial.
- **Address Data Scarcity:** A significant challenge is the lack of comprehensive training data pairs that adequately capture rich physical activities, interactions, and phenomena. An objective is to develop a method for constructing such a dataset.
- **Ensure Physically Correct Learning in Preference Optimization:** Traditional DPO struggles when using generated videos as winning cases due to their potential physical inconsistencies. A core objective is to formulate a DPO framework where correct physics learning is guaranteed by leveraging real-world videos as physically consistent winning examples.
- **Capture Holistic Preference Signals:** Vanilla DPO's reliance on pairwise comparisons limits its ability to capture inherently holistic and global preference signals relevant to physical plausibility. The research aims to move towards a groupwise preference model to better reflect complex physical interactions.
- **Focus Optimization on Challenging Physics Cases:** It is crucial for the model to learn from and improve upon difficult physics scenarios. An objective is to design a mechanism within the DPO framework to guide the optimization process to focus on such challenging cases, allowing physics-violating samples to exert a stronger influence.
- **Enhance DPO Training Efficiency and Stability:** The computational demands and memory requirements of vanilla DPO, particularly the full model duplication for the reference model, pose barriers to scalability and stability. An objective is to devise a more memory-efficient and stable DPO training scheme.

By addressing these specific problems, the research seeks to enable T2V models to generate videos that are not only visually appealing but also physically sound, contributing to more realistic and useful generative AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research comprises two main components: a Physics-Augmented video data construction Pipeline (PhyAugPipe) and a Physics-Aware Groupwise Direct Preference Optimization (PhyGDPO) framework.

#### 3.1. Physics-Augmented Video Data Construction (PhyAugPipe)

The scarcity of text-video data rich in physics interactions necessitates a specialized data construction approach. PhyAugPipe addresses this through a multi-stage process:

1. **Data Filtering with Chain-of-Thought (CoT):**
 - A Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, is utilized with a designed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning rule.
 - The CoT process begins with **Element Parsing**, extracting physical objects, materials, actions, and forces from given prompts and video frames.
 - This is followed by **Vision Checking**, where parsed elements are compared with video frames to remove hallucinations and identify missing visible entities or interactions.
 - **Physics Reasoning** then generates an explanation of how parsed entities interact through physical forces.
 - **Data Scoring** computes a "physics richness" score (0 to 1), considering factors like entity interactions, explicit forces, causal clarity, and penalties for irrelevant elements (e.g., camera motion, stylization). Data with a score ≥ 0.60 are deemed physics-rich.
 - Finally, **Prompt Extending** augments the original prompt with causal physical details from the reasoning (limited to 100 words). While these extended prompts can support other research, the core DPO training focuses on the original prompts to promote implicit physics learning.
 - This process yields PhyVidGen-135K, a dataset of 135K physics-rich text-video pairs.

2. **Action Clustering via Semantics Matching:**
 - To address potential imbalances in action distribution within the filtered data, a clustering step is performed.
 - A predefined list of challenging action categories is established.
 - A sentence Transformer processes the original text prompts of the filtered samples to perform fuzzy semantics matching, assigning each sample to an action category. This results in a distribution histogram (H_f) of samples across categories.

3. **Data Sampling with Physics Rewarding:**
 - Acknowledging that different action categories vary in physical complexity and generation difficulty, a balanced sampling strategy is implemented.
 - Representative samples from each category (top-n_c based on semantic matching) are evaluated by a physics-aware VLM, VideoCon-Physics. This VLM provides semantics adherence (s_sa) and physics commonsense (s_pc) scores.
 - An average of these scores determines the overall category score, from which a category difficulty (d_k = 1 - S_f(k)) is derived.
 - A sampling weight (r_k = exp(τ d_k)) is assigned, prioritizing data from more difficult action categories. This strategy ensures that the sampled training data (H_r(k)) allocates more examples to challenging actions, fostering more robust physics learning. It is noted that the VLM rewarding influences physics richness and difficulty, not correctness, as real-world videos (which inherently follow physical laws) serve as the winning cases.

#### 3.2. Physics-Aware Groupwise Direct Preference Optimization (PhyGDPO)

PhyGDPO is a novel DPO framework designed to overcome the limitations of existing DPO methods for physically consistent T2V generation:

1. **Groupwise Probability (Plackett–Luce Probabilistic Model):**
 - Unlike vanilla DPO, which uses a pairwise Bradley–Terry model, PhyGDPO is based on the groupwise Plackett–Luce (PL) probabilistic model.
 - The framework uses a real-world video (x_w0) as the definitive "winning case," ensuring physical correctness. A group of generated videos (G_l(c)) from different random seeds serve as "losing cases."
 - The preference probability of the PL model is formulated (Eq. 1), leading to a groupwise DPO (GDPO) loss (Eq. 4). This loss incorporates the log-likelihood ratio (f_θ(x_0, c)) between the trained model (θ) and a reference model (ψ).
 - To improve computational efficiency, the GDPO loss is further approximated and bounded (Eq. 6 and 8), allowing for efficient training with single data pair sampling within each group per iteration.

2. **Physics-Guided Rewarding (PGR):**
 - To direct DPO training towards challenging physics cases, PGR modulates the parameters (γ_j and α_j) within the GDPO loss (Eq. 8).
 - These parameters are controlled by a physics difficulty measure (v_j), derived from the semantics adherence and physics commonsense scores (s_sa_j, s_pc_j) provided by VideoCon-Physics.
 - γ_j amplifies rewards for samples with lower physical plausibility, while α_j adaptively adjusts the sharpness of the preference comparison. This ensures that physics-violating samples exert a stronger influence during optimization, promoting focused learning on areas requiring improvement. The VLM reward affects sample importance, while the real-world winning case guarantees physical correctness.

3. **Flow Matching Probabilistic Modeling:**
 - The method builds upon rectified flow matching (x_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1).
 - The log-ratio of conditional probabilities (p_θ(x_t-h | x_t, c) and p_ψ(x_t-h | x_t, c)) is approximated using Gaussian distributions with vanishing noise (Eq. 11, 12).
 - This approximation reformulates the upper bound of the GDPO loss into a final training objective (Eq. 13), which minimizes the difference in L2 norms between the predicted velocities and the oracle velocity.

4. **LoRA-Switch Reference (LoRA-SR) Scheme:**
 - To address the memory and efficiency issues of vanilla DPO, LoRA-SR is introduced.
 - The backbone of the T2V model is frozen as the reference model (ψ). Trainable LoRA modules are attached to specific layers (query, key, value, output linear projections) of the Transformer backbone, forming the action model (θ).
 - An environment manager acts as a switch, enabling flexible toggling between reference (LoRA inactive) and action (LoRA active) modes.
 - This scheme allows the reference and action models to share the same heavy backbone while only switching lightweight LoRA parameters. This significantly reduces GPU memory and storage requirements, improves computational efficiency, enhances scalability, and stabilizes training by preventing the action model from diverging too rapidly from the reference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate PhyGDPO utilized a dataset collected via PhyAugPipe, named PhyVidGen-135K (derived from 1 million initial text-video pairs, with 17K selected for training). Evaluation was performed on two benchmarks: VideoPhy2 and PhyGenBench.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Methods (Table 1a, 1b):**
 - PhyGDPO consistently outperformed SOTA methods, including closed-source models such as OpenAI Sora2 and Google DeepMind Veo3.1.
 - On the VideoPhy2 dataset, PhyGDPO achieved an overall score of 0.1627, surpassing Veo3.1 (0.1525) and Sora2 (0.1508). Notably, on the "hard actions" track, PhyGDPO scored 0.0500, a 4.5-fold improvement over the base model Wan2.1-14B (0.0111) and significantly higher than Sora2 (0.0389) and Veo3.1 (0.0444).
 - On PhyGenBench, PhyGDPO achieved an overall score of 0.55, outperforming VideoDPO (0.54) and PhyT2V (0.50). It demonstrated particular strength in the mechanics (0.55) and thermal (0.58) physics domains.
- **User Study (Table 1c):**
 - A user study involving 104 participants indicated a strong human preference for videos generated by PhyGDPO over all competing entries. Preference rates for PhyGDPO ranged from 64.4% (vs. Veo3.1) to 94.2% (vs. Vcrafter2), with 95% confidence intervals no wider than ±2.4%. This suggests that PhyGDPO generates genuinely more physically plausible content, extending beyond VLM-based evaluation agreement.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- Visual comparisons (Figures 1, 4, 5, 6) demonstrated PhyGDPO's ability to generate videos with more realistic physical dynamics, deformation-free body motion, and accurate object interactions across challenging scenarios such as gymnastics, soccer, basketball, glass smashing, polo, squash, handsprings, water refraction, and paper combustion. The method exhibited superior generalization in modeling various physical events.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 2)

- **PhyAugPipe Ablation (Table 2a):** Each component of PhyAugPipe (Chain-of-Thought, action clustering, and physics rewarding) incrementally contributed to improved performance, particularly enhancing scores on hard actions.
- **PhyGDPO Ablation (Table 2b):** Progressively applying LoRA-SR, the groupwise probabilistic model, and physics-guided rewarding led to steady and substantial performance gains. A 4.5-fold improvement was observed in the "hard action" categories. Visual analysis (Figure 7) supported these findings, illustrating improved physical realism in scenarios like a tennis ball floating on water.
- **Comparison with SOTA DPO Methods (Table 2c):** When fairly compared on the Wan2.1-1.3B model, PhyGDPO surpassed Flow-DPO and VideoDPO across all metrics, achieving a 50% improvement on hard actions. Visual comparisons (Figure 8) confirmed PhyGDPO's ability to generate more coherent body motion and stable shapes in complex actions like weightlifting.
- **LoRA-SR Analysis (Table 2d):** The LoRA-SR mechanism significantly reduced GPU memory consumption by 44% and storage space by over 60 times, while simultaneously improving hard action scores by 14% compared to training without it. It also substantially outperformed LoRA-SFT with a similar memory footprint. Visual analysis (Figure 9) showed that LoRA-SR enabled more accurate physical interactions, such as those between hands and a snow shovel.
- **Cross Evaluation (Table 2e, 2f):**
 - **Cross-VLM Evaluation:** Using Gemini-2.5-pro as an alternative physics evaluator confirmed PhyGDPO's superior performance, suggesting genuine learning of physical consistency.
 - **Cross-T2V-model Evaluation:** Applying PhyGDPO to Vcrafter2 as a different base T2V model demonstrated its consistent superiority over SOTA DPO methods, indicating strong generalization capabilities.

#### 4.4. Parameter Analysis (Table 3)

- Analysis of hyperparameters in the physics rewarding function (Eq. 9) demonstrated that optimal performance was achieved within specific ranges for α_min, k_α, b_α, λ, k_γ, and b_γ, and confirmed that physics-guided rewarding consistently provided performance gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of text-to-video generation by directly addressing the challenge of physical consistency. By enabling T2V models to implicitly reason about and adhere to physical laws, the work moves beyond purely aesthetic quality and introduces a crucial dimension of realism and functional accuracy.

The methodological contributions are substantial:
- **Data Generation:** The proposed PhyAugPipe offers a scalable and VLM-driven approach to construct physics-rich video datasets, effectively mitigating the pervasive issue of data scarcity for physics-aware generative models. This pipeline's ability to filter and re-sample data based on physics richness and difficulty is a notable innovation.
- **Enhanced Direct Preference Optimization:** PhyGDPO introduces a suite of enhancements to the DPO framework. Utilizing real-world video as the definitive "winning case" provides an objective ground truth for physics learning. The shift to a groupwise Plackett–Luce probabilistic model from traditional pairwise comparisons allows for a more holistic capture of preference signals related to physical plausibility. The Physics-Guided Rewarding (PGR) scheme directs the model's focus toward challenging physics scenarios, optimizing learning efficiency.
- **Computational Efficiency and Stability:** The LoRA-Switch Reference (LoRA-SR) scheme represents a practical solution to the computational and memory demands of DPO training. By enabling a lightweight, shared backbone for reference and action models, it significantly improves training efficiency and scalability while contributing to greater training stability by modulating the divergence of the action model.

The potential impact of PhyGDPO extends to various practical applications:
- **Foundation Models:** The techniques developed in this paper can be integrated into the development of future T2V foundation models, leading to inherently more physically accurate and reliable video generation capabilities.
- **Simulation and Design:** Improved physics reasoning can directly benefit applications requiring high-fidelity simulations, such as advanced video game environments, more realistic autonomous driving simulators for training and validation, and enhanced robotics training environments where physical interactions are paramount.
- **Content Creation:** For industries like film, animation, and visual effects, the ability to generate physically plausible video content with greater consistency could streamline production workflows and open new creative possibilities.
- **Fundamental Research:** This work provides a robust framework and empirical evidence for the feasibility of implicit physics reasoning in generative models, without relying on explicit physics rules in prompts during inference. This may stimulate further research into more sophisticated neural physics engines and advanced data-driven simulation methods within artificial intelligence.
