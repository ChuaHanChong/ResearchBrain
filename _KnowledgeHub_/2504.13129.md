# Science-T2I: Addressing Scientific Illusions in Image Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NYU, UW, UPenn, and UCSD develop a framework to improve scientific accuracy in text-to-image synthesis through a 20K expert-annotated dataset of scientific phenomena, a specialized reward model (SCISCORE), and a two-stage fine-tuning approach that achieves 50% improvement in scientific realism while matching human-level evaluation capabilities.

## Problem

- Text-to-image models generate visually appealing but scientifically inaccurate images
- Lack of datasets and metrics specifically focused on evaluating scientific realism in generated images
- Existing evaluation methods rely heavily on complex prompt engineering

## Method

- Created **SCIENCE-T2I** dataset with 20K image pairs and 9K prompts covering physics, chemistry, and biology
- Developed **SCISCORE** ==reward model== extending **CLIP** architecture for scientific accuracy evaluation
- Implemented two-stage fine-tuning process combining supervised and online fine-tuning with masking

## Results

- SCISCORE achieves 5% improvement comparable to experienced human evaluators, outperforming large multimodal models like GPT-4o in capturing fine-grained visual details
- Fine-tuned FLUX model shows over 50% improvement in scientific accuracy
- Demonstrates strong generalization across both simple and complex scientific scenes

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scientific realism can be improved through targeted dataset creation and specialized reward models
> - Implicit prompt alignment and image encoder enhancement improve understanding of scientific concepts
> - Performance gaps exist between subject-oriented and condition-oriented generation tasks

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in generative modeling have significantly improved the visual fidelity and aesthetic quality of images synthesized from text prompts. However, this work identifies a persistent gap: these models frequently produce images that are visually compelling but scientifically or physically implausible. This phenomenon suggests that current models often create "superficial imitations" rather than "authentic representations" of the real visual world, indicating an inadequate understanding of underlying scientific principles.

Previous research, such as PhyBench and Commonsense-T2I, has established and benchmarked the lack of physical and commonsense reasoning in text-to-image (T2I) models. These efforts have primarily focused on evaluation, using LMM-based discrete scoring, but have not provided dedicated training data or continuous evaluation mechanisms to enhance model capabilities in this specific area. Moreover, general-purpose vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP, while effective for broad text-image alignment, have been shown to struggle with fine-grained scientific cues, often prioritizing surface-level associations over scientific implications.

The current work differentiates itself by addressing these limitations through a multi-faceted approach. First, it introduces a large-scale, expert-annotated training dataset designed to specifically target scientific realism, a feature absent in prior benchmarks. Second, it develops a specialized reward model, SciScore, which is explicitly trained to assess fine-grained scientific phenomena, thereby offering a more calibrated and continuous evaluation than discrete LMM-based methods. Third, it proposes a two-stage alignment framework that aims to inject scientific knowledge directly into generative models, moving beyond the assumption that desired scientific outputs are already within the pre-trained model's distribution. This approach aims to equip T2I models with the ability to reason from scientific principles to correct visual outcomes, thereby bridging the identified gap between visual imagination and scientific reality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the observation that despite significant progress in image generation, current text-to-image models consistently produce scientifically inaccurate or implausible images. These "scientific illusions" manifest as outputs that are aesthetically pleasing but fail to adhere to fundamental physical, chemical, or biological principles. This represents a fundamental gap between a model's capacity for visual fidelity and its ability to reflect physical realism.

The authors identify two primary root causes for this limitation:
1. **Inadequate Training Data:** Existing large-scale training datasets for image generation models rarely contain explicit pairings of scientific concepts with their correct visual manifestations. This absence means that models lack the foundational knowledge required to learn and reproduce scientifically accurate visual phenomena.
2. **Insufficient Evaluation Protocols:** Standard evaluation metrics and benchmarks for image generation models do not adequately assess a model's understanding of the scientific principles embedded in a prompt. Instead, they often focus on surface-level textual descriptions, allowing models to achieve high scores by matching superficial cues rather than demonstrating deep scientific reasoning.

To address these motivations, the research paper outlines several key objectives:
- **Dataset Development:** To create Science-T2I, a novel, expert-annotated dataset specifically designed to challenge and improve models' scientific reasoning capabilities. This dataset includes a training set of over 20,000 adversarial image pairs and 9,000 prompts across 16 scientific domains, along with an isolated test set of 454 challenging prompts. The training set aims to facilitate preference modeling by contrasting scientifically accurate images with flawed counterparts.
- **Benchmarking Current Capabilities:** To conduct a systematic evaluation of 18 recent image generation models using the Science-T2I test set. This objective aims to quantify the extent of the "scientific illusion" problem and highlight the performance gap between models' ability to render explicit instructions versus their capacity for implicit scientific reasoning.
- **Reward Model Development:** To develop SciScore, a specialized reward model fine-tuned from CLIP-H. The objective for SciScore is to accurately capture fine-grained scientific phenomena and assess whether generated images faithfully reflect the physical principles implied by a prompt, without relying solely on language-guided inference. This model is intended to surpass the performance of existing large multimodal models (LMMs) and human evaluators in this specific task.
- **Generative Model Alignment:** To propose and implement a two-stage alignment framework. This framework's objective is to inject scientific knowledge directly into generative models, combining supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to establish a scientific knowledge base with masked online fine-tuning (OFT) using SciScore as a reward signal to refine implicit scientific reasoning. The ultimate goal is to demonstrate that scientific reasoning in image generation can be substantially improved through targeted data and alignment strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-stage methodology encompassing dataset creation, model benchmarking, reward model development, and a novel alignment framework for generative models.

**Science-T2I Dataset Construction:**
The dataset consists of 16 tasks covering physics, chemistry, and biology, requiring models to infer visual outcomes based on scientific principles not explicitly stated in the prompt. Tasks were designed with two objectives: "Rewriting Capability," allowing prompts to be rephrased while retaining visual meaning, and "Scientific Knowledge Integration," ensuring grounding in unambiguous scientific principles. Tasks were categorized into "Subject-oriented Tasks (ST)" and "Condition-oriented Tasks (CT)" based on whether reasoning originates from intrinsic subject properties or applied conditions, respectively.

A three-tier prompt structure was developed for each task:
- **Implicit Prompt (IP):** Contains terms implying visual characteristics requiring scientific interpretation (e.g., "an unripe apple").
- **Explicit Prompt (EP):** Reformulates the IP into a direct, descriptive statement of the intended visual outcome (e.g., "a green apple").
- **Superficial Prompt (SP):** Offers a plausible but scientifically incorrect interpretation, focusing on surface-level associations (e.g., "a red apple").

The data curation pipeline involved:
1. **Prompt Generation:** GPT-4o generated structured templates for implicit prompts, which were then expanded into corresponding explicit and superficial prompts.
2. **Image Synthesis:** FLUX.1[dev] was utilized to generate image pairs for explicit and superficial prompts due to its text-image alignment and photorealistic output.
3. **Quality Control:** All generated data underwent manual verification by domain experts, ensuring scientific accuracy and focusing on background simplicity and region prominence of the scientific phenomenon.

The dataset was partitioned into a training set (>20k adversarial image pairs, 9k prompts) and a test set (454 challenging prompts), with the latter split into "Science-T2I-S (Simple)" (227 tuples with minimalist compositions) and "Science-T2I-C (Complex)" (227 tuples with scientific tasks embedded in diverse real-world scenes).

**Benchmarking Scientific Image Synthesis:**
Eighteen recent image generation models were evaluated using the Science-T2I test set. A two-tiered grading framework, implemented by Qwen3.5-27B, assessed generated images:
- **Scene Score (SS):** Measures the faithful presence of descriptive visual content (max 2 points).
- **Reality Score (RS):** Assesses the accuracy of implicit scientific elements (max 3 points). RS was set to zero if SS was not full. Scores were normalized to a range.

**SciScore: A Reward Model Grounded in Reality:**
To address CLIP's limitations with implicit scientific prompts, SciScore was developed by fine-tuning CLIP-H, with both text and image encoders learnable.
The reward formulation (Eq. 1) used the cosine similarity between text and image representations, scaled by a learnable temperature.
The training objective (Eq. 8) combined two complementary losses:
- **Implicit Prompt Alignment (IPA) (Eq. 4):** Minimizes KL divergence to align the implicit prompt embedding with the explicit image.
- **Image Encoder Enhancement (IEE) (Eqs. 5-7):** Strengthens the image encoder's sensitivity to fine-grained visual details, crucial for scientific phenomena.
SciScore was compared against VLMs (CLIP-H, BLIPScore, SigLIP), LMMs (LLaVA-OV, Qwen2-VL, InternVL, GPT-4o-mini with/without Chain-of-Thought), and human evaluators on a two-choice selection task.

**Aligning Generative Models with Scientific Principles:**
A two-stage alignment framework was proposed using FLUX.1[dev] as the base model:
1. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) (Eq. 9):** This stage used the Science-T2I training set (explicit image pairs) to provide the generative model with a foundational knowledge base of scientific visual phenomena, as pre-trained models often lack exposure to such data. LoRA was applied for SFT.
2. **Online Fine-tuning (OFT):** This stage optimized for implicit reasoning using SciScore as the reward signal. The denoising process was formulated as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP) for flow matching models (Eqs. 10-16). The DPO objective (Eq. 29) was employed. A key component was **Subject-based Masking**, where GroundingDINO extracted bounding-box masks for subjects, ensuring that only gradients from scientifically relevant regions contributed to backpropagation (Eq. 19), thereby stabilizing training.

Evaluation for the alignment framework involved generating five images per implicit prompt from Science-T2I-S and Science-T2I-C and reporting the average SciScore. The "Relative Improvement (RI)" metric (Eq. 19) was introduced to quantify progress toward the model's performance ceiling with explicit prompts. Ablation studies were conducted to assess the necessity of SFT and masking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the current state of scientific realism in image generation and the effectiveness of the proposed solutions.

**Current Models Lack Scientific Image Synthesis Capabilities:**
An evaluation of 18 recent image generation models on the Science-T2I benchmark under implicit prompts revealed consistently low scores. The highest-performing model, FLUX.2[dev], achieved only 47.80 out of 100, while most models scored between 20 and 35 (Table 1). Biology-related tasks posed the greatest challenge, with no model exceeding 33%. The performance of models known for photorealistic quality (e.g., Z-Image at 26.73) suggests that advancements in visual fidelity do not automatically translate to improved scientific reasoning.

**Explicit Prompts Reveal a Composition–Reasoning Disconnect:**
When re-evaluated with explicit prompts, all models showed a significant performance increase, averaging approximately 35 points higher than with implicit prompts (Figure 4). This finding indicates that current models possess the capability to render scientifically correct scenes when provided with direct instructions but fail to infer the same visual outcomes from implicit scientific cues. This suggests the primary bottleneck is in scientific reasoning rather than visual rendering.

**SciScore Outperforms VLMs, LMMs, and Human Evaluators:**
SciScore achieved an accuracy of 93.14% on Science-T2I-S and 91.19% on Science-T2I-C in a two-choice selection task (Table 2). This performance surpassed that of human evaluators (87.01% and 86.02%) by approximately 6 and 5 points, respectively. In contrast, general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP-H, BLIPScore, and SigLIP exhibited near-random accuracy (54-61%), and large Multimodal Models (LMMs) such as GPT-4o-mini (even with Chain-of-Thought prompting) significantly underperformed compared to SciScore and human evaluators. SciScore's robust performance on complex scenes (Science-T2I-C) indicates its ability to focus on scientifically relevant image regions despite environmental distractions. Analysis showed that SciScore's failure cases primarily concentrated in Subject-oriented Tasks, where subject-specific knowledge is more critical than generalizable visual patterns (Figure 5). The optimal weight for Image Encoder Enhancement (λ=0.25) was found to balance fine-grained visual detection with prompt alignment (Table 3).

**Two-Stage Training Yields Over 50% Improvement:**
The proposed two-stage alignment framework (Supervised Fine-tuning + Online Fine-tuning) applied to FLUX.1[dev] resulted in a substantial improvement in SciScore. The combined approach increased the SciScore from a baseline of 23.56 to 28.52 on Science-T2I-S, yielding a Relative Improvement (RI) of 53.39%. On Science-T2I-C, the score improved from 27.26 to 30.11, with an RI of 38.31% (Table 4). Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) contributed a larger share of this improvement, suggesting its role in providing foundational scientific visual knowledge. The fine-tuned model's improved performance on complex scenes (Science-T2I-C) indicates knowledge generalization rather than mere memorization of training examples.

**Both Stages and Masking Are Necessary:**
Ablation studies confirmed the importance of both SFT and subject-based masking. Online Fine-tuning (OFT) without prior SFT failed to improve performance, indicating that a pre-existing scientific knowledge base is essential (Figure 7). Additionally, OFT without subject-based masking resulted in model instability or stalled performance, demonstrating the critical role of restricting gradients to scientifically relevant regions for effective optimization. Further experiments demonstrated that the OFT pipeline also improved rewards with alternative reward models like LAION aesthetic predictor and ImageReward, suggesting generalizability beyond SciScore (Table 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of generative AI by identifying and addressing a limitation in text-to-image models: the consistent generation of scientifically implausible images despite high visual fidelity. The work provides resources and methodologies that could influence future development in several ways.

**Quantifying the "Scientific Illusion":** The study systematically quantifies the performance gap in scientific reasoning across a range of contemporary image generation models. By demonstrating that models perform significantly better with explicit scientific instructions than with implicit ones, it clarifies that the limitation lies in reasoning and inference, rather than solely in visual rendering capability. This diagnostic insight is significant for guiding future model development efforts.

**Novel Dataset and Benchmark:** The introduction of Science-T2I represents a novel resource for the research community. As a large-scale, expert-annotated dataset featuring adversarial image pairs and a comprehensive test set across 16 scientific domains, it provides both training data for model alignment and a robust benchmark for evaluating scientific realism. Its dual-purpose design, allowing for the evaluation of both generative models and LMMs, offers a unified framework for assessing progress in this area, which distinguishes it from prior evaluation-only benchmarks.

**Specialized Reward Model:** SciScore, a reward model specifically fine-tuned for scientific correctness, demonstrates a capability to capture subtle scientific phenomena more accurately than general-purpose VLMs, LMMs, and even experienced human evaluators. This specialized tool can serve as a more reliable and continuous feedback signal for developing models that prioritize scientific accuracy, moving beyond subjective aesthetic preferences or broad semantic alignment.

**Actionable Alignment Framework:** The proposed two-stage alignment framework, combining supervised fine-tuning with masked online fine-tuning, offers a practical methodology for injecting scientific knowledge into generative models. The demonstrated relative improvement exceeding 50% indicates that scientific reasoning in image generation is amenable to improvement through targeted data and alignment strategies. The success of the subject-based masking strategy also highlights the importance of focusing optimization on relevant visual features, a technique that could be transferable to other fine-grained image generation tasks.

**Potential Impact on Applications:** The advancements presented in this paper could have implications for various applications where scientific accuracy in visual content is critical. This includes:
- **Scientific Education:** Generating accurate illustrations for textbooks, educational materials, and interactive learning tools.
- **Research and Development:** Creating realistic simulations or visual aids for scientific experiments and conceptualization in fields like materials science, chemistry, and biology.
- **Engineering and Design:** Producing visuals for product design, prototyping, and analysis where physical principles must be faithfully represented.
By facilitating the generation of images that are not only visually appealing but also scientifically sound, this work contributes to building more reliable and trustworthy AI systems for creative and scientific endeavors. It establishes that scientific realism is not an inherent limitation of current architectures but can be addressed through deliberate data and training interventions.
