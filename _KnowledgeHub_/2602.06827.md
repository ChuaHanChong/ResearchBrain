# DynaRetarget: Dynamically-Feasible Retargeting using Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> DynaRetarget is a pipeline that employs a novel Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization (SBTO) framework to refine imperfect kinematic human motions into dynamically feasible, long-horizon robot trajectories. It generates high-quality demonstration data for reinforcement learning (RL), leading to faster policy convergence and robust zero-shot transfer to physical humanoid robots, with downstream RL policies achieving a 97.09% success rate.

## Problem

- Existing kinematic retargeting methods often produce physically inconsistent robot motions, featuring issues such as missing contacts, penetrations, and discontinuities, particularly for complex loco-manipulation tasks.
- Current sampling-based model predictive control (SBMPC) methods for retargeting are myopic due to short optimization horizons, making them ineffective for long-horizon tasks and sensitive to initial errors.
- Optimizing high-dimensional control variables simultaneously over long horizons for humanoid loco-manipulation is challenging for sampling-based optimizers, often leading to convergence failures due to local minima and computational cost.

## Method

- The **DynaRetarget** pipeline consists of initial ==Inverse Kinematics (IK)-based retargeting==, followed by dynamic refinement using the novel ==Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization (SBTO)== framework, and finally, training and deployment of an RL tracking policy.
- **SBTO** addresses long-horizon optimization by ==incrementally increasing the optimization horizon==, ==warm-starting== each larger-horizon problem with solutions from the previous shorter-horizon one, and iteratively refining control knots.
- An improved variant, SBTOskip_{skip}skip, enhances computational efficiency by identifying and caching converged initial trajectory segments, skipping their recomputation in subsequent roll-outs for long-horizon problems.

## Results

- **SBTO** variants achieved a **76.8%** success rate in generating dynamically feasible trajectories, nearly doubling the **37.9%** success rate of the SPIDER baseline and producing smoother trajectories (normalized smoothness of **1.41** vs **3.4**).
- Reinforcement learning policies trained with **DynaRetarget** trajectories achieved a **97.09%** success rate in downstream tracking tasks, outperforming policies trained with raw kinematic data (**79.41%**) and exhibiting lower object position error (**8.81 cm** vs. **12.50 cm**).
- The SBTOskip_{skip}skip variant reduced computational cost by a factor of three compared to standard SBTO, making it slightly more efficient than the SPIDER baseline while maintaining superior performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incrementally increasing the optimization horizon with warm-starting allows sampling-based trajectory optimization to successfully converge on complex, contact-rich, long-horizon tasks where full-horizon methods fail.
> - Dynamically consistent demonstrations generated by SBTO lead to significantly more stable, efficient, and robust training for downstream RL policies compared to kinematically retargeted data, enabling faster convergence and better performance.
> - The SBTO framework can generalize motion refinement across varying object properties (mass, size, geometry) using the same tracking objective, effectively augmenting existing demonstrations and reducing the need for new data collection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generating dynamically feasible loco-manipulation behaviors for humanoid robots presents a complex challenge, primarily due to the underactuation of both the robot and manipulated objects, as well as the intricate nature of contact interactions. Traditional approaches to this problem have largely fallen into two categories: gradient-based trajectory optimization (TO) and deep reinforcement learning (RL).

Gradient-based TO methods are effective at finding locally optimal trajectories efficiently. However, they often require augmentation with search-based techniques to achieve sufficient exploration of the solution space. Deep RL, conversely, has demonstrated efficacy in generating robust behaviors, but it commonly faces significant challenges with exploration, frequently necessitating extensive reward shaping tailored to individual motions.

The use of demonstrations, particularly human motions, has emerged as a promising avenue to address the exploration problem in RL for humanoid robots, benefiting from the morphological similarities between humans and humanoids. This approach involves retargeting human motions to humanoid robots to generate reference trajectories, which are then used as inputs for RL policies, often combined with domain randomization to facilitate sim-to-real transfer. This methodology can reduce the complexity of the RL component, allowing for simpler, shared reward terms across various motions.

Within this demonstration-based paradigm, the retargeting module itself varies significantly among existing methods. Many approaches rely on kinematic optimization, which can result in physically and geometrically inconsistent robot motions, particularly for complex loco-manipulation tasks involving contacts. Common artifacts include foot skating and unintended penetrations. While some works, such as (SPIDER), have employed sampling-based model predictive control (SBMPC) to enhance retargeting quality, these methods typically solve short-horizon optimization problems in a receding horizon fashion. This short-sighted nature makes them less suitable for long-horizon behaviors and sensitive to imperfections in the initial kinematic reference, often failing to establish correct contacts or recover from early errors.

Prior research in computer graphics has explored data-driven, particularly RL-based, motion tracking for various morphologies, extending to loco-manipulation by modeling object motion and contact, primarily validated in simulation. For humanoid robots, RL-based frameworks like have successfully deployed dynamic locomotion policies on real hardware using retargeted human demonstrations. Further extensions to loco-manipulation have leveraged proprioceptive observations and data augmentation. However, these RL methods generally require substantial training times and high-quality demonstrations, with accurate contact information being particularly challenging to obtain from kinematic retargeting alone.

This work aims to bridge the gap by developing a robust retargeting method that can generate dynamically feasible, long-horizon trajectories, thereby improving the quality of demonstrations for downstream RL tasks and enabling more complex robot behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce DynaRetarget, a comprehensive pipeline designed for retargeting human motions to generate control policies for humanoid robots. The core component of this pipeline is a novel Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization (SBTO) framework.

The motivation for developing SBTO stems from identified limitations in existing retargeting approaches:
- **Addressing kinematic imperfections**: Kinematic retargeting methods often produce trajectories that are physically inconsistent, featuring issues such as missing contacts, penetrations, and discontinuities. SBTO aims to refine these imperfect kinematic trajectories into dynamically feasible, whole-body humanoid motions.
- **Overcoming SBMPC limitations**: Current SBMPC-based retargeting methods suffer from several drawbacks. They exhibit myopic behavior due to their short optimization horizon, making them ineffective for long-horizon tasks. Their greedy nature prevents re-optimization of past actions, leading to difficulties in recovery from early errors (e.g., dropping an object). Additionally, SBMPC-generated trajectories can be jerky, which negatively impacts the training and performance of downstream RL policies. SBTO is designed to mitigate these issues by considering the full problem horizon.
- **Improving efficiency for long-horizon optimization**: While optimizing over the full horizon could resolve the issues of SBMPC, applying single-shooting sampling-based optimizers to high-dimensional humanoid loco-manipulation problems over long horizons is challenging. The increased number of variables and local minima often leads to convergence failures. SBTO addresses this by incrementally increasing the optimization horizon, allowing for efficient warm-starting and continued refinement of early control variables.
- **Facilitating large-scale data generation**: A significant bottleneck in developing advanced humanoid loco-manipulation skills is the scarcity of high-quality, real-world data. By robustly retargeting diverse human demonstrations into dynamically feasible robot trajectories, DynaRetarget aims to enable the generation of large-scale synthetic datasets.
- **Enhancing downstream RL performance**: The ultimate goal is to provide higher quality demonstration data for RL tracking policies. It is hypothesized that dynamically consistent trajectories will lead to faster convergence, higher success rates, and more robust policies that transfer effectively to real hardware, without requiring extensive reward shaping or specific curricula for challenging tasks.
- **Generalization across object properties**: The research also aims to demonstrate that the SBTO framework can generalize across varying object properties, such as mass, size, and geometry, using the same tracking objective. This would further reduce the need for collecting new demonstrations for each object variation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DynaRetarget is presented as a complete pipeline for transferring human motions to real-world humanoid control policies. The pipeline consists of initial kinematic retargeting, followed by dynamic refinement using the novel Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization (SBTO) framework, and finally, training and deployment of an RL tracking policy.

**Pipeline Overview:**
1. **Inverse Kinematics (IK)-based Retargeting**: Given a human-object demonstration, an initial IK-based retargeting step is performed to generate a kinematically feasible robot-object trajectory. This step is acknowledged to produce imperfections, such as missing contacts or geometric inconsistencies, due to morphological differences between human and robot.
2. **Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization (SBTO)**: The imperfect kinematic trajectory serves as a reference for SBTO, which refines it to ensure physical consistency and dynamic feasibility. This is the core technical contribution.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Tracking Policy Training**: The refined, dynamically feasible motion is used to train an RL tracking policy in simulation. Domain randomization is applied during training to enhance robustness and facilitate sim-to-real transfer.
4. **Zero-Shot Transfer to Real Robot**: The learned RL policy is deployed directly on a physical humanoid robot.

**Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization (SBTO):**
SBTO is designed to address the generic optimal control problem where the objective is to find a control sequence that minimizes a cost function while satisfying the system's dynamics, with the dynamics treated as a black box (simulator).
- **Addressing SBMPC Limitations**: SBTO directly tackles the issues of existing SBMPC methods (myopia, greedy behavior, jerky trajectories) by optimizing over the full problem horizon.
- **Incremental Optimization Horizon**: To overcome the challenge of optimizing high-dimensional control variables simultaneously over long horizons, SBTO adopts an incremental approach. It optimizes control knots iteratively:
 - It begins by optimizing a small set of control knots over a short horizon.
 - The optimization horizon is then incrementally increased, adding more knots.
 - Crucially, each larger-horizon optimization is warm-started with the solution from the previous, shorter-horizon problem.
- **Algorithm Structure (Algorithm 2)**: SBTO employs nested loops. The outer loop increments the number of decision variables (knots) currently being optimized (from $k=1$ to $K-1$). The inner loop repeatedly refines all currently active variables using a Fixed-Horizon Trajectory Optimization (FHTO) process (Algorithm 1).
- **Convergence Criterion for Increment**: An increment to a longer horizon occurs when the maximum diagonal value of the covariance matrix ($\Sigma$) for the currently active variables falls below a predefined threshold ($\sigma_{min}$). This criterion indicates that the optimization for the current window has sufficiently converged.
- **SBTO$_{skip}$ Optimization**: An improved implementation, SBTO$_{skip}$, is introduced for computational efficiency. It identifies and caches initial trajectory segments where the control variables have already converged (their variances fall below a threshold $\sigma_{skip}$). In subsequent roll-outs, the recomputation of these fixed initial segments is skipped, significantly reducing computational cost for long-horizon problems.
- **Implementation Details**:
 - Implemented in the MuJoCo simulator, leveraging its parallel roll-out functionality.
 - Simulation timestep: $\Delta t = 0.01$ s.
 - Control knots are spaced at 0.25 s intervals.
 - Control commands are PD target trajectories.
 - Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is used for updating the sampling distribution, incorporating elite sample retention and Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) for mean and covariance updates to prevent premature distribution shrinking.
 - The initial mean of the distribution is set to the joint positions from the reference at knot times, and initial covariance is $\sigma_0^2 I$. Full covariance matrix is used for better convergence.
- **Cost Function (Table II)**: The cost function is comprehensive, penalizing deviations from the reference trajectory for:
 - **Motion Tracking**: Joint positions and velocities, base position and orientation, object position, orientation, and linear velocity.
 - **Task-Space Tracking**: Torso position, orientation, linear and angular velocities, foot positions, and hand positions.
 - **Regularization**: Penalties for robot-object collisions and self-collisions. Quaternions are handled by subtraction in the tangent space.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) Tracking Controller Training:**
- **Controller Architecture**: PPO algorithm is used to train RL tracking controllers, employing a residual action space.
- **Observations**: The policy observes the one-step desired robot trajectory, the one-step object pose error relative to the desired pose, and the object pose expressed in the robot frame.
- **Reward Function (Table IV)**: A multi-component reward function is used:
 - **Motion Tracking**: Rewards for tracking root position/orientation, body position/orientation/linear/angular velocities, and joint position.
 - **Object Tracking**: Contact matching reward (incentivizing desired contacts and penalizing excessive forces), object position/orientation/linear/angular velocity tracking.
 - **Regularization**: Penalties for action rate, joint limits, and self-collisions.
- **Domain Randomization**: To achieve robust transfer to the real robot, extensive domain randomization techniques are employed, building on existing methods:
 - Adaptive sampling, which prioritizes more challenging portions of the trajectory.
 - Randomization of object pose (initial frames) or velocities (later frames) around desired values at episode reset.
 - Random external pushes applied to the object.
 - Randomization of object friction parameters and mass.
 - Crucially, no additional termination conditions related to object tracking are used, as these were found to degrade performance.
- **Training Environment**: Training is conducted using mjlab, a GPU-optimized MuJoCo framework, with an IsaacLab-style API, utilizing 8192 environments over 10000 iterations on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DynaRetarget and its core component, SBTO, was conducted on the OmniRetarget dataset, which comprises hundreds of humanoid-object loco-manipulation motions.

**Performance Evaluation (Table III):**
- **Success Rate**: SBTO variants demonstrated significantly higher success rates compared to the SPIDER baseline. SBTO$_{skip}$ achieved a 76.8% success rate, and SBTO achieved 74.6%, nearly twice the success rate of SPIDER (37.9%). Even SBTO$_{pos}$, a variant with a simplified cost function similar to SPIDER, showed a clear improvement at 62.1%.
- **Trajectory Smoothness**: SBTO produced smoother refined trajectories. SBTO$_{skip}$ had a normalized smoothness of 1.41, and SBTO had 1.7, both considerably lower (smoother) than SPIDER's 3.4.
- **Computational Efficiency**: Standard SBTO was found to be computationally more expensive ($\eta_{eff}$ of 4.06e7) than SPIDER (1.23e7). However, the SBTO$_{skip}$ variant substantially reduced computational cost by a factor of three (1.18e7), making it slightly more efficient than SPIDER while yielding superior performance. In practical terms, the refinement process takes approximately 20 seconds per second of motion on a high-core CPU.
- **Error Metrics**: SBTO and SBTO$_{skip}$ resulted in lower average object position errors ($E_{pos}$) and object rotation errors ($E_{rot}$) compared to SPIDER, indicating more accurate tracking of the reference object trajectory.
- **Failure Analysis**: Failures typically occurred when the initial kinematic references were of particularly poor quality, especially with sudden changes in hand-object contact or abrupt object orientation flips.

**Algorithm Analysis (Figures 3, 4, 5):**
- **Incremental Warm-Starting**: SBTO successfully converged on complex, contact-rich tasks (e.g., kicking a box) where full-horizon Fixed-Horizon Trajectory Optimization (FHTO) consistently failed due to the robot falling or incorrect task execution. This demonstrates SBTO's ability to efficiently warm-start long-horizon problems and mitigate convergence instability.
- **Effective Horizon**: Analysis showed that early control variables in SBTO continue to be actively refined over a significantly extended period. For a 4.6-second motion, the object position error at a fixed early time point (t0 = 1s) steadily decreased over approximately 200 iterations, corresponding to an "effective horizon" of about 3.4 seconds. This is substantially longer than both the per-increment look-ahead (0.25s) and the fixed horizon used by SBMPC (1.2s), suggesting that SBTO's longer-term planning leads to more optimal solutions, especially for cumulative costs like object position.
- **Hyperparameter Influence**: A parameter sweep indicated that the effective horizon is primarily governed by $\sigma_{min}$ (the threshold for incrementing the horizon), rather than $\alpha_{\Sigma}$ (the covariance shrinking rate), highlighting the importance of this adaptive incrementation criterion.

**Demonstration Augmentation (Figure 6, Table IV-D):**
- SBTO demonstrated the ability to refine motions for objects with properties significantly different from the original demonstration. Using a single motion reference, it successfully handled boxes with masses ranging from 0.1 to 8 kg and sizes from 0.2 m to 0.4 m.
- The dynamic refinement process also generalized across various object geometries, successfully adapting the motion for a cylinder, a chair, and a shelf, without changes to the cost terms or optimization settings. This indicates that SBTO can augment existing demonstrations, reducing the need for new data collection for varied object characteristics.

**Motion Tracking using Reinforcement Learning (Table V, Figure 7):**
- **Downstream RL Policy Performance**: Policies trained with DynaRetarget trajectories achieved a success rate of 97.09% (with low variance), significantly outperforming policies trained with raw OmniRetarget kinematic trajectories, which achieved 79.41% (with higher variance).
- **Tracking Accuracy**: DynaRetarget-trained policies exhibited lower object position error (8.81 cm vs. 12.50 cm) and object orientation error (0.11 rad vs. 0.18 rad), while maintaining similar Mean Per-Keypoint Position Error (MPKPE).
- **Sample Efficiency**: The RL tracking policies converged considerably faster and more reliably when trained on the dynamically consistent trajectories generated by SBTO. Policies trained on kinematically retargeted data either took much longer to converge or failed to track the object effectively, necessitating larger joint deviations.
- **Robustness and Transfer**: The use of physically consistent trajectories from SBTO, combined with domain randomization, enabled the RL tracking controller to learn challenging behaviors such as object sliding and manipulation with robot legs without requiring additional curriculum shaping. The learned policies were successfully transferred zero-shot to a real humanoid robot, as qualitatively shown in Figure 1.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DynaRetarget framework and its core component, Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization (SBTO), present several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotics:

- **Addresses a Critical Data Bottleneck**: The work directly tackles the challenge of generating high-quality, dynamically feasible demonstration data for humanoid loco-manipulation tasks. By refining imperfect kinematic human motions, DynaRetarget provides a robust method to create the rich, physically consistent datasets necessary for advanced robot learning, significantly alleviating the real-world data collection bottleneck.
- **Improved Quality of Robot Motions**: SBTO generates trajectories that are dynamically feasible, smoother, and globally consistent over long horizons. This improvement over existing kinematic retargeting and short-horizon SBMPC methods is crucial for realistic and stable robot operation, especially in contact-rich and complex interaction tasks.
- **Enhanced Reinforcement Learning for Humanoids**: The higher quality of SBTO-generated demonstrations directly translates to more effective and efficient training of downstream RL policies. This leads to:
 - **Faster Convergence**: RL agents learn desired behaviors more quickly.
 - **Higher Success Rates**: Policies achieve better performance in terms of task completion and tracking accuracy.
 - **Increased Robustness**: The consistency of the training data allows policies to learn robust behaviors, reducing the need for intricate reward shaping or specialized curricula for challenging motions.
 - **Reliable Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Dynamically consistent training data, combined with comprehensive domain randomization, facilitates more reliable zero-shot transfer of learned policies to real humanoid hardware.
- **Generalization and Data Augmentation**: The demonstrated ability of SBTO to adapt a single motion reference to objects with varying masses, sizes, and geometries is a substantial advancement. This capability enables effective data augmentation, making existing human demonstration datasets more versatile and reducing the overall cost and effort associated with creating diverse robot behaviors.
- **Advancement in Trajectory Optimization**: SBTO's incremental horizon optimization strategy offers a novel solution to the inherent difficulties of applying sampling-based optimization to complex, high-dimensional, and long-horizon problems in robotics. By warm-starting and iteratively refining control variables, it manages to achieve global consistency without encountering the convergence issues typically associated with full-horizon approaches.
- **Enabling Complex Human-Robot Interaction**: By providing a robust pipeline for generating dynamically feasible loco-manipulation, DynaRetarget facilitates the development of humanoid robots capable of executing a wider range of sophisticated, contact-rich tasks, such as precise object handling, kicking, lifting, and pushing, which are critical for future applications in human environments.

Future work directions include exploring multi-modal sampling distributions to further improve scalability and computational efficiency, and investigating the possibility of using SBTO to directly track human keypoints, potentially bypassing the explicit kinematic retargeting step entirely.
