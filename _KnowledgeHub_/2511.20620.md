# Wanderland: Geometrically Grounded Simulation for Open-World Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from New York University developed WANDERLAND, a real-to-sim framework and dataset for open-world embodied AI, which establishes geometrically grounded, photorealistic urban simulation environments. This work demonstrates that accurate metric geometry is crucial for reliable novel view synthesis, semantic consistency, and effective training/evaluation of navigation policies, addressing the limitations of existing vision-only and video-based simulation methods.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI simulation platforms are primarily confined to indoor environments, lacking the scale and diversity needed for complex open-world urban settings.
- Traditional RGB-D based data collection struggles outdoors due to sensor limitations and inefficient large-scale capture, leading to low-quality, non-watertight, or fragmented 3D reconstructions.
- Emerging video-based 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods for outdoor scenes lack accurate metric 3D reconstruction, yielding unreliable geometry for physical interaction and degraded novel-view extrapolation.

## Method

- The **WANDERLAND** framework utilizes a multi-sensor (LiDAR-IMU-GNSS-Camera) handheld scanner with a proprietary ==LIV-SLAM== pipeline to generate dense, metric-scale point clouds and globally consistent camera trajectories.
- It employs a two-stage ==3DGS== training approach, initializing Gaussians from accurate point clouds, incorporating ground truth depth regularization, and using a curriculum-based view augmentation for improved photorealism and extrapolated view synthesis.
- A lightweight, occupancy-accurate ==collision mesh== is extracted from the global point cloud and integrated with the trained 3DGS model into a unified USD format, enabling geometrically grounded physical interaction within simulators.

## Results

- Vision-only 3D reconstruction methods showed significant camera pose errors (e.g., **meter-level T-ATE_R**) and produced fragmented, unreliable meshes compared to the LIV-SLAM ground truth.
- WANDERLAND achieved superior novel view synthesis quality (higher **PSNR**, **SSIM**, lower **LPIPS**) and maintained better semantic consistency in its renderings compared to video-3DGS baselines like Vid2Sim and GaussGym.
- Reinforcement learning models fine-tuned in WANDERLAND's geometrically grounded environments consistently improved performance, whereas models in video-3DGS environments often deteriorated, and a benchmark on the WANDERLAND dataset showed existing navigation models achieved less than **50%** success rate in open-world tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-only 3D reconstruction methods are fundamentally insufficient for providing the metric accuracy and reliable geometry required for robust open-world embodied AI simulations.
> - Geometric grounding is essential for achieving high-fidelity photorealistic sensor simulation, significantly improving novel view synthesis quality, especially for extrapolated views, and ensuring semantic consistency for perception models.
> - Reliable training and evaluation of embodied navigation policies are critically dependent on metrically accurate and collision-consistent simulation environments, as ungrounded simulations lead to unreliable policy learning and assessment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied AI, particularly visual navigation, has experienced substantial advancements, largely supported by high-performance simulation platforms and datasets designed for standardized tasks and closed-loop evaluation. Historically, these platforms have primarily focused on indoor environments, exemplified by datasets such as Matterport3D, ScanNet, and HM3D. However, with the development of foundation models for perception and decision-making, there is an increasing demand to extend embodied AI applications to complex, open-world urban settings. These applications include last-mile delivery, campus-scale wayfinding, and service robots operating across diverse public spaces.

Developing simulations for these open-world scenarios presents significant challenges that traditional RGB-D based datasets and curation pipelines are not equipped to handle. Conventional RGB-D sensors often fail in outdoor conditions due to sunlight interference and limited range. Furthermore, tripod-based capture methods are inefficient for large-scale urban areas, and the subsequent reconstruction processes are prone to issues like pose estimation drift in low-texture or unstructured environments. The resulting low-quality textured meshes often exhibit artifacts such as non-watertight or fragmented geometry, compromising simulation fidelity.

To address the limitations of indoor-only datasets, recent research has explored leveraging online videos for constructing open-world environments. Methods based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have demonstrated potential in generating visually compelling simulations from casually captured videos and enabling navigation policy training through reinforcement learning, as seen in projects like Vid2Sim and GaussGym. However, these video-based approaches introduce their own set of fundamental limitations that hinder their suitability as standardized benchmarks. These include inaccurate 3D reconstruction due to reliance on pure RGB-based Structure-from-Motion (SfM) or deep reconstruction models, leading to non-metric camera poses and depth estimations. The geometry extracted for physical interaction is often unreliable, fragmented, and metrically ungrounded. Additionally, these methods commonly exhibit degraded novel-view extrapolation, as uniform video trajectories result in poor rendering quality for viewpoints significantly different from the captured paths.

The WANDERLAND project positions itself within this landscape by identifying and aiming to bridge the sim-to-real gap inherent in current embodied AI simulations. It seeks to develop a real-to-sim framework that combines photorealistic sensor rendering with geometrically grounded interaction in large-scale, open-world urban environments. This approach aims to overcome the shortcomings of both traditional indoor-focused datasets and emerging video-based 3DGS methods, thereby establishing a more robust and reliable foundation for reproducible research and benchmarking in open-world embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a high-fidelity real-to-sim testbed for the reproducible evaluation and benchmarking of open-world embodied navigation systems. This overarching goal is motivated by a recognized bottleneck in Embodied AI: the difficulty of performing consistent closed-loop evaluation, particularly for visual navigation tasks in complex, expansive urban environments.

The motivation for developing a high-fidelity simulation stems from the increasing need for embodied AI agents to operate in diverse real-world settings beyond controlled indoor spaces. Applications such as last-mile delivery, campus wayfinding, and general service robotics in urban areas require simulation environments that possess large spatial extents, cover both indoor and outdoor areas, provide photorealistic sensor simulation, and support reliable physics for agent interaction. Existing simulation platforms and datasets, while foundational for indoor scenarios, demonstrably fall short in these open-world requirements.

Specifically, the authors identify several critical limitations in current approaches that WANDERLAND aims to address:
1. **Limitations of Classic RGB-D Datasets**: These datasets are primarily restricted to indoor settings. Their reliance on RGB-D sensors makes them unsuitable for outdoor environments due to susceptibility to sunlight interference and limited sensing range. The associated data collection methods, often tripod-based, are inefficient for covering the vast scales of urban environments. Furthermore, reconstruction from RGB-D data can suffer from pose estimation drift in large, low-texture, or unstructured areas, leading to meshes with insufficient resolution, non-watertight geometry, or fragmentation.
2. **Limitations of Video-Based 3DGS Methods**: While promising for generating visually rich environments from casual videos, these methods fundamentally lack geometric grounding. They often yield inaccurate 3D reconstructions due to dependence on pure RGB-based Structure-from-Motion (SfM) or deep learning models, resulting in non-metric camera poses and depth estimations. The collision meshes derived from 3DGS opacity fields are frequently fragmented and lack metric accuracy, rendering them unreliable for physical interaction simulations. Additionally, these methods often exhibit severe degradation in rendering quality for extrapolated views (viewpoints significantly off the capture path) because the original video trajectories typically offer limited view diversity.

Against this background, the key objectives of the WANDERLAND project are:
- To develop and introduce a novel real-to-sim framework that integrates multi-sensor diverse-view capture, reliable reconstruction, accurate metric-scale geometry, and robust view synthesis.
- To curate a diverse dataset of indoor-outdoor urban scenes using this framework, designed to facilitate rigorous testing.
- To systematically investigate and demonstrate the deficiencies of image-only reconstruction pipelines, specifically how their geometric inaccuracies negatively impact novel view synthesis quality and subsequently undermine the reliability of navigation policy learning and evaluation.
- To provide a trusted testbed for benchmarking embodied navigation tasks, including image-goal and vision-language navigation, ensuring evaluations are conducted in environments that are both visually realistic and geometrically consistent.
- To release rich raw sensor data from the curated scenes, enabling the benchmarking and advancement of fundamental 3D vision tasks such as 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis.
- Ultimately, the work aims to establish a new foundation for reproducible research in open-world embodied AI by providing simulation environments that overcome the visual and geometric sim-to-real gaps.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WANDERLAND framework is a real-to-sim pipeline designed to create geometrically grounded and photorealistic simulation environments for open-world embodied AI. Its methodology encompasses data collection, multi-stage data processing, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training, and integration into a simulation platform.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

The data is collected using a commercial handheld 3D scanner named MetaCam. This device integrates a Livox Mid-360 non-repetitive LiDAR with a built-in IMU, an RTK-GNSS antenna, and two synchronized 4K fisheye cameras offering over 180° field of view. The LiDAR is mounted to optimize ground-level detail capture and maximize FOV overlap with the cameras. A mobile application provides real-time previews of the colorized point cloud, allowing operators to identify and fill coverage gaps during capture.

The data collection protocol is structured for specific scene sizes of 5,000–10,000 square meters. RGB images are captured based on displacement or angular rotation thresholds rather than a fixed frame rate, ensuring a uniform distribution of viewpoints. Trajectories are systematically planned: "training trajectories" involve closed-loop paths with dense, multi-view coverage of navigable areas, while "extrapolation trajectories" simulate natural navigation paths with minimal overlap. This strategy aims to mitigate the view-diversity limitations of casual video capture. Quality control measures include minimizing dynamic obstacles, ensuring consistent lighting, and real-time point cloud monitoring. Data was collected in diverse indoor and outdoor urban settings across New York City and Jersey City, encompassing various scene types and captured under different times of day and weather conditions to ensure appearance robustness.

#### 3.2. Data Processing

Raw sensor data is processed using MetaCam Studio, a proprietary pipeline implementing LiDAR-inertial-visual-GNSS (LIV-SLAM) sensor fusion. This fusion system generates dense, metric-scale point clouds with high accuracy and globally consistent camera trajectories, forming the geometric foundation for subsequent steps.

A two-stage masking strategy is applied to the raw fisheye images: Egoblur is used for privacy protection (masking faces and car plates), and an object detector is employed to mask dynamic objects (people, animals, vehicles). These masked pixels are then filtered during 3DGS training. Fisheye images are undistorted and cropped to 120° perspective views for 3DGS training, a practice chosen to address limitations in low-order camera distortion approximations and to align with models primarily trained on pinhole images.

#### 3.3. Training 3D Gaussians

3D Gaussians are initialized directly from the dense, colorized global point cloud generated by MetaCam Studio. This millimeter-level accurate point cloud, downsampled to approximately 5 million points per scene, provides a high-quality geometric starting point. Gaussian opacity is parameterized inversely proportional to volumetric density using a K-nearest-neighbor heuristic to manage initial rendering behavior.

Depth regularization is incorporated into the 3DGS training. Instead of relying on monocular depth estimation, ground truth depth maps are generated by projecting the initialized Gaussians to each camera pose. These depth maps are then used as a geometric prior, combined with photometric losses during optimization. This approach stabilizes 3DGS training and ensures the learned Gaussians align with the scene geometry, balancing per-view visual quality with multi-view consistency.

To enhance extrapolated view synthesis, a pretrained generative model, Difix3D+, is utilized for training view augmentation. A self-training strategy is employed where novel views are synthesized for sampled extrapolated camera poses using the current 3DGS model and neighboring training views as references. These synthesized images are then added to the training set with a lower loss weight. A curriculum-based approach is used, gradually increasing the sampling radius for extrapolated viewpoints during training to improve generalization to unseen views.

#### 3.4. Geometrically Grounded Simulation

For geometric grounding, a collision mesh is extracted from the dense global point cloud. Points far from the sensor trajectories are filtered, and the remaining points are voxelized into a 3D occupancy grid. The Marching Cubes algorithm is then applied to generate a triangle mesh. Post-processing involves removing small isolated fragments to improve rendering performance and ensure a lightweight, occupancy-accurate mesh suitable for collision detection.

Both the extracted mesh and the trained 3DGS model, sharing the same metric coordinate system from the global point cloud, are integrated into the Universal Scene Description (USD) format. In this unified representation, the mesh functions as a lightweight physics and collision layer, while the 3DGS model serves as the primary renderer. This USD scene can be directly loaded into simulators like Isaac Sim for embodied AI research.

#### 3.5. Defining Navigation Tasks

Navigation tasks are defined based on the geometrically grounded environment. Expert trajectories are derived by importing the collision mesh into Unity and using its NavMesh baking API to extract a triangulated navigable surface. Unity’s pathfinding module then generates collision-free optimal paths between sampled start and goal locations, which are chosen near captured camera poses to ensure visual interpretability.

For Vision-Language Navigation (VLN), egocentric videos are rendered along these expert trajectories within the 3DGS-based simulator. The Gemini 2.5 Flash Visual Language Model (VLM) is then employed to automatically generate natural language instructions based on these videos and trajectory summaries. A two-stage prompting scheme—first generating a structured JSON description, then condensing it into fluent instructions—is used to enhance consistency and scalability, with human verification for quality control.

#### 3.6. Data Statistics

The WANDERLAND dataset currently comprises 530 distinct scenes, encompassing over 420,000 frames captured over 100 hours, covering a total area exceeding 3.8 million square meters. Each scene provides a comprehensive set of raw and processed data, including synchronized RGB fisheye images, intrinsic calibrations, globally consistent camera poses, colorized metric point clouds, optimized 3DGS models, extracted collision meshes, and ready-to-simulate USD scenes. The project commits to continuous expansion, targeting over 1,000 scenes for future releases.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted within the WANDERLAND framework aimed to address three core questions regarding the efficacy of vision-only reconstruction, the benefits of geometric grounding for photorealistic simulation, and the reliability of video-3DGS frameworks for embodied AI training and evaluation.

**Q1: Is the best vision-only 3D reconstruction method as good as LIV-SLAM?**
**A1: Vision-only 3D reconstruction methods exhibit substantial deficiencies compared to LiDAR-inertial-visual (LIV) SLAM, failing to provide reliable geometric correctness.**

- **Inaccurate Camera Pose Estimation**: Evaluation of camera pose accuracy against LIV-SLAM ground truth (Table 2) reveals that even advanced vision-only methods like DUSt3R, MUSt3R, VGGT, π³, MapAnything, DA3, and COLMAP (with or without intrinsic calibration) yield significant errors. For scenes under 100 meters, these methods show meter-level raw translational ATE (T-ATE_R) and an average error of approximately 30 cm and 5 degrees after scale alignment (T-ATE_S, R-ATE). This indicates that fundamental modality limitations and current foundation models have not yet closed the gap with multi-sensor fusion systems, rendering casual videos unsuitable for reliable metric reconstruction.
- **Unreliable Mesh Extraction**: Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5) demonstrate that meshes generated from video-based frameworks such as Vid2Sim and GaussGym are characterized by noise, fragmentation, and incompleteness. These issues originate from inaccuracies in the underlying neural representations. In contrast, WANDERLAND's method, which directly extracts meshes from globally consistent LiDAR point clouds, produces clean, geometrically grounded meshes that are metrically accurate and complete, which is crucial for robust physical interaction simulation.

**Q2: Does geometric grounding lead to a better photorealistic simulation quality?**
**A2: Photorealistic sensor simulation benefits demonstrably from geometric grounding.**

- **Superior Novel View Synthesis (NVS)**: Quantitative metrics for NVS (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) presented in Table 3 show that all baseline methods, including 3DGS, 2DGS, 3DGUT (using COLMAP reconstruction), Vid2Sim, and GaussGym, achieve subpar results compared to WANDERLAND. GaussGym exhibited the lowest metrics, attributed to the inaccuracies from VGGT reconstruction. Vid2Sim's performance was affected by its reliance on inaccurate monocular depth estimation. WANDERLAND’s approach, leveraging LIV-SLAM’s accurate poses and geometric initialization, yielded superior NVS, particularly for extrapolated views. This indicates that robust NVS is contingent upon a strong foundation of both visual and geometric accuracy.
- **Semantically Consistent Sensor Simulation**: Beyond standard NVS metrics, the research investigated semantic consistency for sensor simulation (Figure 6). GaussGym’s fragmented renderings were found to impede reliable semantic segmentation, with Grounded SAM 2 failing to detect environmental elements. While Vid2Sim produced more coherent images, its DINOv3 features diverged significantly from ground truth. WANDERLAND’s renderings, however, maintained both structural integrity and semantic consistency, enabling accurate segmentation and producing DINOv3 features that closely aligned with real imagery. This demonstrates that geometric accuracy directly influences the quality and reliability of perception models, which is critical for embodied AI.

**Q3: Does the video-3DGS framework provide a reliable environment for training and evaluation?**
**A3: WANDERLAND's framework constructs a more reliable environment for both training and evaluating embodied navigation policies compared to video-3DGS approaches.**

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Geometric Grounding**: Experiments on RL training (Table 4) revealed that navigation models (NoMaD, CityWalker, MBRA) generally experienced performance deterioration (e.g., increased Navigation Error, decreased Success Rate) after fine-tuning in environments built by Vid2Sim. This suggests that inaccurate geometry in these environments leads to the learning of locally optimized but globally unreliable behaviors. Conversely, all models showed significant improvement when trained within WANDERLAND’s geometrically grounded environments. This highlights the sensitivity of RL fine-tuning to simulation fidelity and the necessity of metrically accurate, collision-consistent geometry for developing effective navigation policies.
- **Evaluation with Geometric Grounding**: Evaluation results (Figure 7) further demonstrated the unreliability of model assessment in the absence of grounded geometry. Models evaluated in Vid2Sim-built environments consistently exhibited lower success rates and higher intervention rates compared to evaluations in WANDERLAND environments. This indicates that geometrically ungrounded simulations cannot provide a faithful or reproducible measure of navigation policy performance, underscoring that geometric grounding is a prerequisite for robust benchmarking.
- **Benchmarking Pretrained Navigation Policies**: A benchmark of various pretrained navigation models on the entire WANDERLAND dataset (Table 5) revealed that Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) models generally outperformed point-goal and image-goal models. It was also observed that outdoor navigation tasks posed a greater challenge than indoor scenes, likely due to longer trajectories, more complex topologies, and elevation changes. Overall, the benchmark indicated a substantial research gap in open-world embodied navigation, as none of the evaluated models achieved a success rate exceeding 50%.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WANDERLAND framework and dataset represent a contribution to the fields of embodied AI and 3D computer vision. The work primarily addresses the critical need for geometrically grounded simulation environments in open-world embodied AI, a requirement largely unmet by existing video-based 3DGS pipelines. By demonstrating that vision-only reconstruction methods fail to deliver the necessary metric accuracy, reliable geometry, and consistent view synthesis for reproducible benchmarking, WANDERLAND establishes a new foundation for research.

The significance of WANDERLAND extends beyond closing the real-to-sim gap for embodied navigation:
- **Foundational Resource for 3D Vision Geometry**: The provision of metric-scale camera poses and dense LiDAR point clouds offers ground-truth data essential for developing and evaluating foundational vision geometry models. This addresses a scarcity of large-scale metric benchmarks for outdoor 3D vision, facilitating new research directions in areas like long-range depth estimation and geometric learning.
- **Rigorous Benchmarking for Novel View Synthesis**: The carefully designed extrapolated views within the dataset enable more rigorous and realistic benchmarking of novel view synthesis methods, particularly for off-trajectory conditions, which are critical for agent exploration and robustness.
- **Advancing Embodied AI Research**: By providing accurate and scalable simulations, WANDERLAND allows for perception, planning, and evaluation components of embodied AI systems to be built and tested on reliable foundations. This shift is argued to be essential for the next generation of embodied systems that must operate effectively in real-world urban environments.

Despite its contributions, the framework currently has two primary limitations. First, the data capture system operates at 1 FPS due to hardware constraints, leading to sparser viewpoint sampling than ideal and potentially affecting rendering quality in highly complex scenes. The authors plan to address this through future hardware upgrades. Second, while the current focus is on geometric reconstruction and static environment simulation, real urban environments involve complex dynamics such as moving pedestrians, vehicles, and traffic patterns. Modeling these dynamic elements remains a significant challenge for future work, requiring integration with behavior prediction and interactive simulation, which is beyond the scope of the current paper.

In conclusion, WANDERLAND highlights that geometric grounding is not an optional feature but a fundamental requirement for reliable simulation in open-world embodied AI. The framework and dataset aim to serve as a trusted testbed, fostering advancements in embodied AI research by ensuring that simulated environments faithfully represent the geometric and visual complexities of the real world.
