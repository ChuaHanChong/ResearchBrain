# Quantifying and Visualizing Sim-to-Real Gaps: Physics-Guided Regularization for Reproducibility

> [!summary] Summary
> DENSO IT Laboratory researchers developed a physics-guided regularization method combined with parameter-conditioned domain randomization to improve sim-to-real transfer for robotic control, particularly on systems with large sim-to-real gaps. The approach demonstrated enhanced transfer fidelity and reduced anomalous oscillations on a high-gear-ratio balancing robot, achieving closer alignment between simulated and real-world angular velocity recovery compared to traditional domain randomization.

## Problem

- Standard sim-to-real transfer methods using domain randomization (DR) often fail for robots with wide sim-to-real gaps, such as those with high-gear-ratio mechanisms or requiring precise maneuvers.
- Naïve DR can lead to "overly conservative policies" and reduced success rates when randomization ranges are expanded to cover larger discrepancies, compromising performance at nominal parameters.
- Existing alternatives like system identification are data-intensive and costly, while classical controllers are linear and cannot achieve the nonlinear performance needed for complex robotic tasks, often lacking reproducibility.

## Method

- A ==physics-guided gain regularization loss== was formulated, constraining the neural network controller's ==input-output partial derivatives (equivalent gains)== to match ==empirically measured hardware proportional gains==.
- ==Parameter-conditioned domain randomization== was implemented by appending current plant parameters to the controller's input, allowing the network to specialize its output for different plant configurations.
- A single-layer **Elman Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)** was used as the controller, trained with a ==continuous curriculum learning== strategy to progressively increase simulation complexity and noise.

## Results

- The proposed **GC-DR-RNN** maintained equivalent gains below **2.0**, consistent with empirical hardware constraints, unlike the DR-RNN which showed unconstrained gains fluctuating up to **4.0**.
- On a physical balancing robot (**110:1** gearbox), the DR-RNN exhibited persistent **5 Hz** oscillations, while the GC-DR-RNN achieved smoother corrections and remained within tighter angular velocity bounds.
- The GC-DR-RNN demonstrated nearly identical decay envelopes and peak timings between simulated and real-world angular velocity recovery responses, confirming effective sim-to-real transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unconstrained neural network policies trained solely with domain randomization in simulation tend to develop unrealistically high equivalent gains, which manifest as unstable oscillations on physical hardware.
> - Naïve domain randomization can bias the training process towards 'easy' low-loss regions in the parameter space that may not correspond to the real hardware, leading to degraded performance at nominal plant parameters.
> - Integrating lightweight empirical physics guidance (measured proportional gains) into the learning process is effective for explicitly embedding real-world actuation characteristics and closing sim-to-real gaps.
