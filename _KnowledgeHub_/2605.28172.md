# Provably Guaranteed Polytopic Uncertainty Quantification for SLAM

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a framework for provably guaranteed uncertainty quantification in 3D-3D landmark-based SLAM, integrating set-membership estimation with conformal prediction to output polytopic uncertainty sets that consistently contain the true robot poses and map landmarks. The approach achieved 100% containment of ground truth in simulations and real-world experiments, outperforming probabilistic baselines in reliability.

## Problem

- Existing probabilistic uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods for SLAM often lack formal guarantees that estimated confidence regions contain the true state, and frequently assume Gaussian distributions which may not hold.
- Deterministic UQ (set-membership estimation) methods are typically limited to single-pose estimation due to computational complexity with non-convex SE(3) constraints, making them impractical for full SLAM pipelines.
- A unified and tractable approach for representing and propagating both rotation and translation uncertainties in rigid body transformations within SLAM has been challenging.

## Method

- The framework integrates ==Conformal Prediction (CP)== to calibrate data-driven, distribution-free measurement uncertainty bounds, feeding these into a deterministic ==set-membership estimation (SME)== pipeline.
- **Polytopic sets** are used to represent all 3D point and robot pose uncertainties, enabling a unified and computationally tractable representation for both translation and rotation.
- Three fundamental UQ primitives are developed: forward UQ for mapping, backward UQ for pose tracking, and rigid transformation uncertainty compounding, all relying on ==SDP-relaxed optimization== to ensure provable over-approximations.

## Results

- The proposed framework consistently achieved **100%** containment of ground-truth poses and map landmarks within the estimated polytopic uncertainty sets across all simulations and a Replica dataset experiment.
- A global SLAM framework was shown to yield tighter *joint* pose uncertainty compared to a relative framework, and loop closure smoothing significantly reduced accumulated uncertainties in both trajectory and map.
- In open-loop comparisons, a probabilistic linear-Gaussian baseline failed to cover ground truth in a significant number of estimates, while the proposed method maintained full containment, highlighting its reliability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Polytopic representation, combined with Semidefinite Programming (SDP) relaxations, allows for the propagation of uncertainty with formal containment guarantees across the entire SLAM pipeline, addressing the challenges of SE(3) non-convexity.
> - The integration of Conformal Prediction provides a robust, statistically valid mechanism for generating initial uncertainty bounds from data without strong distributional assumptions, enhancing the practical applicability of deterministic set-membership methods.
> - Decomposing the complex SLAM UQ problem into modular forward, backward, and compounding primitives, each with provable over-approximation, facilitates a structured and certifiable uncertainty analysis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental capability in robotics, enabling a robot to construct a map of its environment while simultaneously determining its own location within that map. While significant progress has been made in improving SLAM accuracy, robustness, and efficiency, many algorithms primarily yield point estimates for robot poses and map landmarks. For safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving and unmanned aerial vehicle navigation, point estimates are insufficient; reliable uncertainty quantification (UQ) is required to enable informed, safety-aware decision-making in downstream planning and control.

Existing UQ approaches can be broadly categorized into probabilistic and deterministic methods. Probabilistic UQ typically assumes that measurement uncertainty follows a known distribution, often Gaussian. Methods in this category often compute a point estimate (e.g., via maximum likelihood or maximum a posteriori inference) and then approximate uncertainty using covariance matrices derived from the inverse Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) or Hessian. However, this approach has limitations: real-world measurement uncertainties often deviate from Gaussian distributions, particularly in dynamic environments; FIM/Hessian approximations inherently assume Gaussianity and may underestimate uncertainty; and the resulting covariance ellipsoids do not provide formal guarantees of containing the true parameters.

Deterministic UQ, or set-membership estimation (SME), offers an alternative. SME models measurement uncertainty using hard bounds rather than probability distributions, computing the set of all parameters consistent with these bounds. Advantages of SME include its requirement for only the support of the uncertainty (without assuming a specific distribution) and its ability to provide provable guarantees that the resulting parameter uncertainty set contains the true parameter. SME has been explored in control theory and signal processing, and more recently in robotics perception. Some recent works integrate conformal prediction (CP) to construct statistically valid measurement uncertainty bounds from data, which are then used for pose estimation. While such approaches can define feasible pose sets, their direct computation, analysis, or manipulation is often difficult due to the non-convex nature of rigid body transformations (SE(3) constraints). Tractable approximations, such as outer or inner approximations, have been adopted, but these are generally limited to single pose estimation problems and have not been extended to a complete SLAM pipeline.

This paper addresses the gap in providing provably guaranteed, tractable uncertainty quantification for the full 3D-3D landmark-based SLAM pipeline, moving beyond single-pose estimation and integrating distribution-free calibration with set-membership methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a novel framework for provably guaranteed uncertainty quantification (UQ) in 3D-3D landmark-based SLAM. The motivation stems from several key challenges and requirements in safety-critical robotics applications:

1. **Ensuring Formal Containment Guarantees**: Traditional probabilistic UQ methods often lack formal guarantees that their estimated confidence regions contain the true state. For safety-critical systems, it is essential to have certifiable bounds on perception uncertainty to avoid unsafe actions. The paper aims to provide UQ algorithms where, given deterministic input uncertainty bounds, the output sets are guaranteed to contain the true poses and landmark positions.
2. **Tractability for Complete SLAM Pipelines**: Existing deterministic UQ methods are often limited to individual pose estimation problems due to the computational complexity of handling non-convex constraints in SE(3). A key motivation is to extend these guaranteed UQ capabilities to a full SLAM pipeline, encompassing localization, mapping, and smoothing, while maintaining computational tractability.
3. **Unified Treatment of Pose Uncertainty**: Rigid body transformations involve both rotation and translation, which can be challenging to represent and propagate uncertainty for in a unified manner. The paper aims to use polytopic sets to represent uncertainty, enabling a unified and computationally tractable representation for both rotation and translation uncertainties.
4. **Practical Usability through Data-Driven Calibration**: While SME provides theoretical guarantees, the selection of hard uncertainty bounds for measurements can be challenging in practice. The work is motivated to enhance practical usability by incorporating conformal prediction (CP), which allows for the calibration of measurement uncertainty from data with a prescribed statistical probability, thus bridging distribution-free calibration with deterministic set-membership estimation.
5. **Modularity and Duality**: The framework aims to be modular, breaking down the complex SLAM UQ problem into fundamental primitives (forward UQ, backward UQ, pose compound). It also seeks to demonstrate duality, transitioning seamlessly between probabilistic confidence sets (via CP) and strict deterministic enclosures.

In essence, the paper seeks to provide a comprehensive, theoretically sound, and practically usable framework for uncertainty quantification in SLAM that provides strong containment guarantees, which is critical for the deployment of autonomous systems in safety-critical domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for guaranteed polytopic SLAM uncertainty quantification (UQ) integrates set-membership estimation (SME) with conformal prediction (CP) to achieve tractable and provably guaranteed UQ for 3D-3D landmark-based SLAM. The approach is built upon three fundamental UQ primitives: forward UQ for mapping, backward UQ for pose tracking, and pose compound for accumulating pose uncertainties. These primitives operate on polytopic uncertainty sets and are integrated into a complete SLAM pipeline.

**A. Conformal Prediction (CP) for Measurement Uncertainty Calibration**
Conformal prediction is employed as a statistical tool to calibrate measurement uncertainty from data. For a given calibration dataset, CP constructs a prediction set that is guaranteed to contain the true value with a user-specified probability (1-$\delta$). This involves computing a nonconformity score for each data point, which quantifies the deviation between a predictor's output and the true value (e.g., using Euclidean norms of prediction errors, potentially scaled by a covariance matrix for normalization). The (1-$\delta$)-quantile of these scores then defines the uncertainty bound. This approach allows for statistically valid uncertainty bounds without restrictive distributional assumptions, feeding into the deterministic UQ modules.

**B. Polytopic Uncertainty Representation**
The framework utilizes polytopes to represent uncertainty sets for 3D points and robot poses. A polytope $P(A, b)$ is defined by a set of linear inequalities $Ax \le b$. Key operations and definitions for polytopes are used:
- **Affine Map**: $HP + q = \{Hx + q | x \in P\}$.
- **Minkowski Sum**: $P_1 + P_2 = \{x + y | x \in P_1, y \in P_2\}$.
- **Intersection**: $P_1 \cap P_2 = \{x | x \in P_1 \text{ and } x \in P_2\}$.
- **Set Projection**: $\pi_{R^d}(P)$ projects a polytope onto a lower-dimensional subspace.
- **Polytope Diameter**: Maximum distance between any two points in the polytope.
- **Chebyshev Ball and Center**: The unique ball of minimal radius enclosing the polytope, and its center. These are computed via convex optimization.
These operations are closed for polytopes, meaning the result of an operation on polytopes is also a polytope, contributing to tractability.

**C. Basic Uncertainty Quantification Primitives**

1. **Forward Uncertainty Quantification (Mapping)**
 - **Problem**: Given an uncertainty set for a robot pose $T \subset SE(3)$ and an uncertainty set for a local point $S \subset R^3$, determine the uncertainty set $Q$ of the point in the global frame, $q = \phi(T, p) = Rp + t$, where $p \in S, T \in T$.
 - **Challenge**: The transformation $\phi(T, p)$ is bilinear, and $SE(3)$ has non-convex constraints, making $Q$ difficult to characterize exactly.
 - **Approach**: The method computes a tight polytopic over-approximation $P(A_2, \hat{b}_2)$ of $Q$. This is achieved by fixing the polytope normal vectors $A_2$ a priori and then solving $M$ optimization problems (one for each facet) to find the smallest bounds $\hat{b}_2$. Each optimization problem seeks to maximize $a_m^T q$ subject to $T \in T$ and $p \in S$.
 - **Simplification**: Lemma 2 shows that the maximum value is attained at one of the vertices of the input point polytope $S$.
 - **SDP Relaxation**: The inner optimization problem (for a fixed vertex $v_i$) is still non-convex due to the $SE(3)$ constraint. It is relaxed to a Semidefinite Program (SDP) to obtain a certifiable upper bound for each facet. The resulting SDP uses a lifted variable $X = yy^T$ where $y$ represents the vectorized pose and a homogeneous coordinate.
 - **Guarantee**: Theorem 1 proves that the resulting polytopic set $P(A_2, \hat{b}_2)$ is a guaranteed over-approximation of $Q$.

2. **Backward Uncertainty Quantification (Pose Tracking)**
 - **Problem**: Given an uncertainty set for a local point $S \subset R^3$ and an uncertainty set for a global point $Q \subset R^3$, infer the uncertainty set $T$ for the robot pose $T \in SE(3)$ such that $q = \phi(T, p)$ for some $p \in S, q \in Q$.
 - **Approach**: Theorem 2 provides an upper bound for the preimage $\phi_S^{-1}(Q)$ by relating it to $\phi_s^{-1}(Q^+)$ for a single point $s \in S$ and an inflated set $Q^+ \supseteq Q + B(0, d(S))$ (or $Q^+ \supseteq Q + B(0, r(S))$ if $s$ is the Chebyshev center of $S$). Lemma 3 shows that inflating a polytope $P(A, b)$ by a ball $B(0, \epsilon)$ can be conservatively bounded by $P(A, b + \epsilon \mathbf{1})$.
 - **Polytopic Enclosure**: By choosing $s$ as the Chebyshev center of $S$ (prioritizing tightness) or an arbitrary point (prioritizing efficiency), and inflating $Q$ by the Chebyshev radius or diameter of $S$, the problem becomes finding $T$ such that $A_2 \phi(T, s) \le b_2 + r(S) \mathbf{1}$. This leads to a polytopic representation of the pose uncertainty $T_r$ or $T_d$.
 - **Guarantee**: Theorem 3 establishes that both $T_r$ and $T_d$ are guaranteed over-approximations of $T$. $T_r$ is proven to be the unique minimum set of a certain form that encloses $T$.

3. **Rigid Transformation Uncertainty Compound**
 - **Problem**: Given two uncertainty sets for poses $T_1, T_2 \subset SE(3)$, find the uncertainty set $T_3$ for the compound pose $T_3 = T_1 T_2$. Lemma 4 confirms that this is equivalent to computing the Minkowski product $T_1 \cdot T_2$.
 - **Direct Computation Method**:
 - **Approach**: Similar to forward UQ, it aims to find a tight polytopic over-approximation $P(A_3, \hat{b}_3)$ for $x(T_3)$. This involves solving $M$ optimization problems, each maximizing $a_m^T x(T_3)$ subject to $T_3 = T_1 T_2$, $T_1 \in T_1$, and $T_2 \in T_2$.
 - **SDP Relaxation**: This bilinear optimization problem is also relaxed to an SDP for a certifiable upper bound, using a lifted variable $X = yy^T$ where $y$ includes $x(T_1)$, $x(T_2)$, and a homogeneous coordinate.
 - **Guarantee**: Theorem 4 states that the resulting set is a guaranteed approximation.
 - **Indirect Computation Method**:
 - **Motivation**: The direct method can be computationally expensive due to the large dimension of the SDP for $x(T_3) \in R^{12}$. This method decomposes the pose uncertainty into rotation and translation.
 - **Rotation Uncertainty**: The rotation component $R_i$ of each pose $T_i$ is over-approximated by a set of rotations $ \bar{R}_i X_i$, where $X_i = \{Exp(\theta w) | w \in S^2, \theta \in [0, \theta'_i]\}$ represents a geodesic ball about identity. Proposition 1 details how to find $ \bar{R}_i$ and $\theta'_i$ via SDP relaxation. Lemma 5 (supported by Lemmas 6 and 7) shows that $ \bar{R}_3 X_3 = ( \bar{R}_1 X_1 \bar{R}_2) \cdot X_2$ where $\theta'_3 = \theta'_1 + \theta'_2$ and $ \bar{R}_3 = \bar{R}_1 \bar{R}_2$.
 - **Translation Uncertainty**: For $t_3 = R_1 t_2 + t_1$, Lemma 8 shows how to compute a polytopic over-approximation for $t_3$. This involves a maximization problem with a closed-form solution (Eq. 35) that accounts for the rotation uncertainty $X_1$ and the translation uncertainty $P(A_{t_2}, b_{t_2})$ of $t_2$, then summing with $P(A_{t_1}, b_{t_1})$.
 - **Guarantee**: Theorem 5 proves that this decomposed set is a guaranteed approximation.

**D. SLAM Pipeline Integration**
The UQ primitives are integrated into a 3D-3D landmark-based SLAM pipeline (Algorithm 5):
1. **Local Point Cloud Generation**: For each new frame, 3D points are extracted. Their uncertainties are quantified using CP to generate polytopic uncertainty sets $P(A_i, b_i)$.
2. **Localization**:
 - **Relative Framework**: Estimates relative pose $T^{k-1}_k$ between current and previous frames using backward UQ on point correspondences. The global pose $T_k$ is then obtained by compounding $T^{k-1}_k$ with the previous global pose $T_{k-1}$ using either the direct or indirect pose compound method.
 - **Global Framework**: Directly estimates the global pose $T_k$ by tracking local points against existing global map landmarks. Backward UQ is applied.
3. **Mapping**: Newly observed local points are transformed into the global frame. Forward UQ is applied using the estimated global pose $T_k$ and the local point uncertainty sets to obtain global map point uncertainty sets.
4. **Smoothing**: Upon loop closure detection, an alternating optimization scheme is used. Map points are refined using forward UQ based on associated frames, and frame poses are refined using backward UQ based on visible points. This iterative process is guaranteed to converge as uncertainty sets monotonically decrease and are bounded.

The framework ensures that if input uncertainty sets are valid, the outputs maintain containment guarantees.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper evaluates its proposed polytopic uncertainty quantification (UQ) framework for SLAM through simulations of basic UQ primitives, a numerical SLAM simulation, and an experiment on the Replica dataset.

**A. Basic Uncertainty Quantification Simulation**

1. **Forward Uncertainty Quantification (UQ)**:
 - Six random trials were conducted, generating random poses and point uncertainties.
 - The results indicated 100% containment: all 1000 samples drawn from the original true uncertainty set (Q) consistently fell within the estimated polytopic set $P(A_2, \hat{b}_2)$ produced by Algorithm 1. This empirically validated the guaranteed nature of the forward UQ.
 - Visualizations (Figure 3) corroborated this, showing the estimated polytope enclosing the sampled true points.

2. **Backward Uncertainty Quantification (UQ)**:
 - The proposed method was compared against CLOSURE (an inner approximation method) and GRCC (an outer approximation method) for pose UQ.
 - **Containment**: Both the proposed method and GRCC consistently achieved 100% containment of true pose samples (1000 samples from set T). In contrast, CLOSURE exhibited highly variable and often low containment rates (ranging from 10.4% to 87.1%), as it provides an inner approximation.
 - **Tightness (Marginal)**: When projecting the estimated polytopic sets onto separate translation and rotation uncertainties for direct comparison, the proposed method showed more conservative bounds than CLOSURE for both translation and rotation, and more conservative than GRCC for rotation (Figure 4). This conservatism in marginal projections was attributed to the adaptation required for direct comparison with baseline methods.
 - **Tightness (Joint Pose)**: A sampling-based visualization method, transforming a small ball using 1000 sampled poses, revealed that the proposed method's joint pose uncertainty was comparable to CLOSURE and tighter than GRCC (Figure 5). This suggests that while marginal projections might appear more conservative, the overall joint uncertainty might be tighter due to the polytopic representation capturing the coupling between rotation and translation more effectively.

3. **Pose Compound**:
 - Six trials for pose compounding also demonstrated 100% containment for both the direct and indirect computation methods.
 - **Tightness (Marginal)**: The direct method generally yielded a more conservative translation uncertainty but a tighter rotation uncertainty compared to the indirect method (Figure 8). The indirect method's higher rotation conservatism was partly attributed to its geodesic-ball approximation.
 - **Tightness (Joint Pose)**: Sampling-based visualization confirmed that the direct algorithm achieved a tighter overall pose uncertainty than the indirect method (Figure 6).

**B. Numerical SLAM Simulation**

- **Uncertainty Accumulation**: In the relative framework, uncertainties for both robot poses and landmark positions accumulated over time. The indirect pose compound algorithm exhibited a more rapid growth in pose uncertainty compared to the direct method, consistent with the basic UQ findings.
- **Comparison of Frameworks**: The global framework resulted in significantly smaller map and trajectory uncertainties than the relative framework. An ablation study confirmed that this reduction was due to the robot observing previously registered landmarks with low uncertainty, which helped mitigate current pose uncertainties. When each frame registered all visible landmarks (instead of just newly observed ones), uncertainty increased significantly, particularly when the trajectory returned to the starting point.
- **Loop Closure Smoothing**: When a loop closure was detected and smoothing was applied, the uncertainties in both the trajectory and the map were substantially reduced, demonstrating the effectiveness of the smoothing module.
- **Containment**: All ground-truth poses and landmarks consistently remained within their corresponding estimated uncertainty sets across all simulations.
- **Computational Performance**: The MATLAB implementation showed that forward UQ (9.3s) and direct pose compound (12s) were the primary computational bottlenecks due to repeated SDP optimizations. Backward UQ (0.0036s) and indirect pose compound (0.5s) were significantly faster. Loop-closure smoothing took 29.55s per frame (591s for 20 frames involving a loop). The current implementation is suitable for offline applications.
- **Probabilistic Baseline Comparison**: An open-loop comparison with a probabilistic linear-Gaussian baseline (99% confidence ellipsoids) showed that the baseline failed to cover the ground truth in a significant number of pose (9/20) and map point (26/120) estimates by frame 20, whereas the proposed method achieved full containment. In the relative framework with few points per frame, the probabilistic baseline's uncertainty grew approximately three times faster (translation ellipsoid volume 3.12 vs. polytope volume 1.2 by frame 30).

**C. SLAM Experiment (Replica Dataset)**

- **Setup**: The method was evaluated on the Replica dataset using ORB features, RANSAC for outlier rejection, and CP for calibrating local point uncertainties (target miscoverage $\delta=0.01$).
- **Containment**: Both the relative (using direct pose compound) and global frameworks successfully enclosed their ground truth trajectories and maps (Figure 9).
- **Framework Comparison**: The global framework yielded tighter map uncertainty but more conservative *marginally projected* translation uncertainty compared to the relative framework. However, a sampling-based pose uncertainty test (Figure 10) indicated that the global framework had smaller *joint* pose uncertainty. This suggests that the global framework's stronger coupling between translation and rotation, captured by the polytopic representation, leads to a smaller overall pose uncertainty despite potentially larger marginal translation bounds.
- **Uncertainty Growth**: In the absence of loop-closure smoothing, uncertainty sets for both frameworks expanded relatively rapidly, highlighting the importance of smoothing for long trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for provably guaranteed uncertainty quantification (UQ) in 3D-3D landmark-based SLAM, representing a notable advancement in addressing a critical need for safety-critical robotics applications.

**Significance:**

- **Formal Containment Guarantees**: A key contribution is the development of UQ algorithms that provide certified uncertainty sets, which are mathematically guaranteed to contain the true robot poses and landmark positions. This moves beyond approximate probabilistic methods that often lack such formal guarantees.
- **Tractable and Unified Representation**: By employing polytopic sets for uncertainty representation, the framework enables computationally tractable propagation of uncertainty through the SLAM pipeline. Furthermore, this approach offers a unified method for representing and manipulating uncertainty for both rotation and translation components of rigid transformations, which are often treated separately or approximately in other methods.
- **Modular Design**: The decomposition of the UQ problem into three fundamental primitives—forward UQ (for mapping), backward UQ (for pose tracking), and rigid transformation uncertainty compound (for pose accumulation)—provides a modular and flexible architecture. This modularity allows for the integration of these components into various SLAM formulations.
- **Practical Usability with Conformal Prediction**: The integration of conformal prediction (CP) for calibrating measurement uncertainty directly from data, with prescribed statistical probability, enhances the practical applicability of the deterministic set-membership framework. This addresses a common challenge in set-membership estimation regarding the specification of initial measurement bounds.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability**: By providing guaranteed uncertainty bounds, the framework enables downstream planning and control modules in autonomous systems to explicitly account for perception risk, potentially leading to more robust and safer decision-making in complex and safety-critical environments.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Autonomous System Reliability**: The provision of guaranteed uncertainty bounds can significantly improve the reliability of autonomous systems in applications like self-driving cars, unmanned aerial vehicles, and industrial robotics, where perception errors can have severe consequences.
- **New Benchmarking Standards**: The framework introduces a new paradigm for evaluating SLAM performance, moving beyond point estimate accuracy to focus on the reliability and tightness of guaranteed uncertainty sets. This could spur the development of new benchmarks and evaluation metrics in the SLAM community.
- **Foundation for Safe Planning and Control**: The output of this UQ framework can directly inform safety-critical planning and control algorithms, allowing them to optimize trajectories or actions while formally respecting the perceptual uncertainties.
- **Extension to Other Robotics Problems**: The developed UQ primitives and polytopic representation techniques could be extended to other robotics perception and estimation problems beyond SLAM, such as object tracking, multi-robot coordination, and manipulation, where guaranteed uncertainty information is valuable.

**Future Work:**
The authors identify several directions for future research:
1. **Tightness**: Incorporating techniques like sliding-window bundle adjustment and developing tighter SDP formulations to reduce the expansion rate of uncertainty sets. A rigorous theoretical analysis of uncertainty growth rates is also planned.
2. **Real-time Performance**: Optimizing the current research prototype for real-time applications through C++ implementation, parallel SDP computation, keyframe mechanisms, and customized solver designs.
3. **Practical Utility**: Extending the approach to support more sensor modalities (e.g., IMUs, range sensors) and validating its performance in complex, large-scale environments with frequent loop closures.
