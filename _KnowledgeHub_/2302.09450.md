# Robust and Versatile Bipedal Jumping Control through Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of California, Berkeley, Simon Fraser University, Université de Montréal, and Mila developed a reinforcement learning framework that enables the torque-controlled bipedal robot Cassie to execute versatile, goal-conditioned jumps in the real world. The approach achieved robust jumps with controlled landing locations, elevations, and turns, demonstrating successful sim-to-real transfer and emergent recovery behaviors from perturbations.

## Problem

- Developing robust and versatile control for dynamic bipedal maneuvers like jumping is challenging due to complex hybrid dynamics and high-impact landings.
- Traditional model-based optimal control methods often rely on simplified models, pre-defined contact sequences, or separate controllers, limiting their versatility and real-world applicability for torque-controlled bipedal robots.
- Existing reinforcement learning applications to bipedal robots have primarily achieved periodic hopping rather than versatile, aperiodic jumps with precise control over landing locations.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning framework was developed, training a goal-conditioned policy in simulation for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Cassie bipedal robot.
- A multi-stage training scheme was implemented, progressively increasing task complexity from single-goal jumping to multi-goal diverse jumps with extensive dynamics randomization.
- A novel policy architecture was designed, incorporating both short-term (previous 4 timesteps) and long-term (past 2 seconds) input/output history using a 1D CNN encoder to improve learning performance and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The Cassie robot successfully performed diverse real-world jumps, including 1m forward, 0.3m backward, and 60-degree turns, as well as jumping onto 0.44m high platforms and across 1.4m distances.
- Policies demonstrated robustness by recovering from significant external perturbations (e.g., backward force at jump apex) and adapting to a substantial sim-to-real gap (over **0.35** rad joint errors) without fine-tuning.
- Ablation studies in simulation showed the proposed policy architecture yielded the best learning performance compared to baselines, including residual policies and RMA-based approaches, without requiring additional training stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-goal training across diverse jumping tasks significantly enhances policy robustness, enabling emergent recovery behaviors from external perturbations or unstable landings by leveraging a variety of learned maneuvers.
> - The combined short-term and long-term I/O history in the policy architecture provides a more effective representation for dynamic bipedal control, outperforming policies with only one type of history or more complex teacher-student methods.
> - Model-free reinforcement learning can achieve online, contact-implicit control for bipedal robots, allowing dynamic adjustment of contact plans and utilizing emergent hops for stabilization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of agile and robust control for bipedal robots in complex environments is a longstanding challenge in robotics. Jumping, a highly dynamic maneuver, presents particular difficulties due to the hybrid system dynamics involved, switching between contact, underactuated flight, and high-impact landing phases, all occurring within a short timeframe. Prior work in bipedal jumping has largely relied on model-based optimal control (OC) frameworks. These methods often require carefully crafted robot models, simplified dynamics, pre-defined contact sequences, or separate landing controllers, which limit their versatility and real-world applicability, particularly for torque-controlled bipedal platforms like Cassie. For instance, previous OC approaches on Cassie have been restricted to fixed hops, without precise control over landing pose or location.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated success in dynamic locomotion for quadrupedal robots but has faced challenges in achieving aggressive, dynamic maneuvers with bipedal robots in real-world settings. Existing RL applications to bipedal robots, such as Cassie, have primarily achieved periodic hopping rather than versatile, aperiodic jumps with controlled landing. The question of how to enable bipedal robots to perform dynamic skills like standing long jumps and high jumps, which are more demanding than periodic motions, remains a significant open area of research.

This work addresses these gaps by exploring an RL-based framework to enable versatile, goal-conditioned jumping for a life-sized torque-controlled bipedal robot in the real world. It aims to overcome limitations of model-based methods by using model-free RL and to advance RL-based bipedal control beyond periodic motions and uncontrolled landings. The paper positions itself as a step toward enabling more diverse, dynamic, and robust legged locomotion skills, specifically highlighting its capacity to control landing locations and adapt to various jump commands, a capability largely absent in prior bipedal jumping research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a torque-controlled bipedal robot to perform robust and versatile dynamic jumps in the real world, with precise control over landing locations and directions. This aims to push the boundaries of agility for bipedal robots, ultimately enhancing their mobility in unstructured or complex environments where obstacles may require jumping rather than walking or running.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in bipedal jumping:
1. **Complex Hybrid Dynamics:** Jumping involves transitions between ground contact, an underactuated flight phase, and high-impact landing, creating a hybrid system with discontinuous dynamics. Planning and controlling such a high-dimensional, nonlinear, and underactuated system is a complex task.
2. **Limitations of Model-Based Control:** Traditional model-based optimal control (OC) methods, while making progress, often depend on simplified models, require manually designed task-specific control structures, and frequently rely on pre-defined contact sequences. These limitations restrict their scalability and adaptability to diverse jumping tasks and make real-world deployment challenging due to model inaccuracies.
3. **Challenges for RL in Bipedal Dynamics:** While model-free reinforcement learning (RL) has shown promise in quadrupedal locomotion by leveraging full-order dynamics, its application to dynamic and aggressive maneuvers on inherently less stable bipedal robots in the real world has been limited. Achieving aperiodic jumps with controlled landing locations using RL remains an open problem.

To address these, the authors hypothesize that a versatile jumping controller, trained by exploring a diverse range of jumping tasks, can lead to increased robustness. This robustness is expected to allow the robot to leverage different learned maneuvers to maintain stability, such as switching to a different hop to recover from large landing impacts or external perturbations.

The key contributions developed to achieve these objectives are:
- The creation of a system that enables a life-sized torque-controlled bipedal robot (Cassie) to perform versatile jumping maneuvers with controlled landing locations in the real world.
- The development of a new policy architecture that incorporates both long-term and short-term input/output (I/O) history of the robot, intended to improve learning performance and sim-to-real transfer for dynamic tasks.
- The implementation of a multi-stage training scheme to effectively train a versatile jumping policy by progressively increasing task complexity and introducing dynamics randomization.
- A demonstration that training with diverse goals enhances controller robustness, enabling recovery from unstable states or external perturbations by exploiting a variety of learned maneuvers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a reinforcement learning framework trained in simulation and deployed on a real Cassie bipedal robot.

**A. Robot Platform and RL Formulation:**
The experimental platform is Cassie, a 1.1-meter tall, 31 kg bipedal robot with 10 actuated motors and 4 passive joints. The robot has 20 degrees of freedom (DoFs). The observable states, including joint positions, base orientation, and estimated linear velocity, are used as inputs.
The locomotion control problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). A goal-conditioned policy `π(a_t|s_t, c)` is trained to output actions `a_t` (desired motor positions) based on the observed state `s_t` and a goal `c`.
The goal `c` specifies target commands for a desired jump, defined by `[c_x, c_y, c_z, c_ϕ]`, representing target horizontal position, vertical elevation change, and turning direction relative to the robot's starting pose.

**B. Multi-Stage Training Scheme:**
A three-stage training process is employed, starting with simpler tasks and progressively increasing complexity to achieve versatility and robustness, all performed in a MuJoCo simulation of Cassie.
1. **Single-Goal Training (Stage 1):** The policy is trained from scratch to imitate a single jumping-in-place animation. This stage establishes a fundamental jumping behavior. The reward function heavily penalizes deviations from the reference motion and encourages stable standing post-jump. Early termination conditions are designed to push the robot to lift its feet and attempt the jump.
2. **Multi-Goal Training (Stage 2):** The goal `c` is randomized every episode, training the robot to jump to different targets (locations, elevations, turns). The reward function shifts focus towards task completion, with reduced weight on reference motion tracking to encourage exploration of diverse maneuvers. The episode design incorporates a "jump ↔ stand" switch, where the robot performs repeated jumps to random targets after random standing intervals. Early termination conditions prioritize reaching the target with tolerance, allowing for deviation from the reference foot trajectory.
3. **Dynamics Randomization (Stage 3):** This stage introduces extensive domain randomization to the simulation environment while continuing multi-goal training. This is critical for improving robustness and generalization to facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. Randomized parameters include floor friction, joint damping, link mass and inertia, Center of Mass (CoM) positions, motor PD gains, spring stiffness, sensor noise (joint encoders, IMU, base linear velocity estimation), and communication delay.

**C. Reference Motion:**
A human-authored animation of Cassie jumping in place serves as a kinematic reference motion. It has a specific apex foot height, pelvis height, and timespan (`T_J`). After `T_J`, the reference motion reverts to a fixed standing pose. This animation is kinematically feasible but not dynamically optimized.

**D. Reward Function Design:**
The reward `r_t` is a weighted sum of components, `r(u, v) = exp(-α||u - v||^2_2)`, structured across two phases (before and after `T_J`) and adapted for each training stage.
- **Reference Motion Tracking:** Rewards for tracking reference motor positions, pelvis height, and foot height. Weights are higher in Stage 1 and reduced for multi-goal training to allow for diverse maneuvers.
- **Task Completion:** Rewards for reaching desired pelvis `(x, y)` position and `(ψ, θ, ϕ)` orientation (with `θ, ψ` minimized to zero), and for tracking desired linear and angular velocities. This term dominates in multi-goal training.
- **Smoothing Terms:** Rewards for minimizing ground impact force, torque consumption, motor velocity, joint acceleration, and changes in action. These terms contribute to stable and energy-efficient behavior, particularly after landing.

**E. Policy Architecture:**
The policy `π_θ` is a deep neural network operating at 33 Hz, outputting desired motor positions to joint-level PD controllers (2 kHz). It comprises a multilayer perceptron (MLP) as the base network and a 1D convolutional neural network (CNN) for encoding long-term history.
The policy inputs include:
- The current goal `c`.
- A preview of the reference trajectory `q_r_t` (current pelvis height and future motor positions).
- A short-term I/O history (previous 4 timesteps of observations `q_o` and actions `a`), directly fed to the base MLP.
- A long-term I/O history (past 2 seconds, 66 timesteps of `q_o` and `a`), compressed into a latent representation by the 1D CNN before being fed to the base MLP.

**F. Training Details:**
Two specialized goal-conditioned policies are trained: a flat-ground policy (for `c_z = 0`) and a discrete-terrain policy (for `c_ϕ = 0`). Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for training. Each stage is trained with a specific number of iterations (6k, 12k, 20k) and batch sizes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both simulation validation and real-world hardware experiment results to support its claims.

**A. Simulation Validation:**
1. **Policy Structure Comparison:** An ablation study compared the proposed policy architecture (combined short-term and long-term I/O history with direct action output) against several baselines:
 - **Residual Policy:** Outputting a residual term to a reference motion resulted in the worst performance, attributed to the dynamically infeasible nature of the reference animation.
 - **Long History Only / Short History Only:** Policies using only one type of I/O history showed similar learning performance but were inferior to the proposed combined approach. This indicates that direct access to recent feedback (short-term history) is crucial and not fully captured by the compressed long-term history.
 - **RMA/Teacher-Student (TS):** While RMA (Rapid Motor Adaptation) and its finetuned variant A-RMA showed improvement over single-history policies, they had lower returns than the proposed method and required additional training stages (expert, student, finetuning), incurring significantly more training time and data.
 - **Conclusion:** The proposed architecture, which outputs desired motion positions directly and provides both direct short-term I/O history access and a long-term I/O history encoder, demonstrated the best learning performance, comparable to or exceeding the expert policy (with privileged information) without requiring complex multi-stage teacher-student training.
2. **Advantages of Versatile Policy:** The goal-conditioned policy, trained with multiple jumping tasks, was compared against single-goal policies (jumping in place, with or without external perturbations).
 - **Test Scenarios:** The policies were tested with unseen perturbations, including a consistent lateral force and an 8 cm CoM offset in all dimensions.
 - **Result:** Both single-goal policies failed to stabilize the robot, even the one trained with randomized perturbations. In contrast, the goal-conditioned policy successfully stabilized the robot by dynamically utilizing learned diverse maneuvers. For instance, in the presence of a lateral force, it performed a lateral jump despite being commanded to jump in place. With CoM errors, it executed emergent forward jumps to adapt.
 - **Conclusion:** Multi-goal training for diverse tasks significantly enhances policy robustness and generalization, allowing the robot to select and execute appropriate recovery maneuvers, a capability not easily achieved by single-goal training or extensive dynamics randomization alone.

**B. Hardware Experiments (Sim-to-Real Transfer):**
The policies, trained purely in simulation, were directly deployed on a real Cassie robot without any fine-tuning.

1. **Task Completion:**
 - **Flat-ground policy:** Successfully performed diverse jumps, including jumping in place with a 60-degree turn, 0.3m backward jumps, and 1m forward jumps. The robot demonstrated adapting its maneuver, for example, executing a small forward hop after landing to reach a 1m target. It achieved turning angles of up to 55 degrees.
 - **Discrete-terrain policy:** Enabled Cassie to jump onto elevated platforms and across varying distances, such as 0.88m ahead and 0.44m high, or a 1.4m long jump. The policy adapted its contact plan and flight phase duration (e.g., shorter flight for high jumps, longer for long jumps) to achieve target locations and elevations accurately, without global position feedback.
2. **Sim-to-Real Gap Analysis:** Comparison of joint position profiles between simulation and the real robot revealed significant deviations (e.g., over 0.35 rad error in passive tarsus joints) during jumping maneuvers. Despite this substantial sim-to-real gap, the policies remained robust and successfully controlled the robot.
3. **Diverse and Robust Maneuvers:**
 - The flat-ground policy performed a repertoire of multi-axis jumps, blending forward, lateral, and turning movements.
 - **Recovery from Perturbation:** When subjected to a backward perturbation force at the apex of a jump (despite being commanded an in-place jump), the robot recovered stability by executing an emergent backward hop, a maneuver learned during multi-goal training. This demonstrated the policy's ability to deviate from the commanded task for recovery.
 - **Emergent Hops:** The robot frequently utilized small, emergent hops after initial landing to adjust its body pose and stabilize from high impact forces, highlighting its dynamic adaptability.
 - **Conclusion:** The policies exhibit online, contact-implicit control, capable of breaking and re-establishing contact dynamically to stabilize, a capability previously achieved mainly through offline optimal control methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents an advancement in the control of bipedal robots, particularly in enabling dynamic and versatile jumping maneuvers.

1. **Enhanced Bipedal Agility:** The development of a robust and versatile jumping controller for a torque-controlled bipedal robot like Cassie, capable of precise landing control across various locations, elevations, and directions, significantly pushes the limits of bipedal agility. This capability is crucial for robots operating in complex, unstructured environments that necessitate navigating obstacles that cannot be traversed by walking or running alone.
2. **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for Legged Robotics:**
 - **Novel Policy Architecture:** The proposed policy structure, which combines direct access to short-term I/O history with a long-term I/O history encoder, is shown to improve learning performance and sim-to-real transfer over previous architectures. This design choice provides insights for future RL-based locomotion controller development.
 - **Robustness from Versatility:** The research empirically demonstrates that multi-goal training, by exposing the policy to diverse tasks, is a source of robustness, enabling emergent recovery behaviors from unexpected perturbations or unstable landings. This goes beyond the commonly used dynamics randomization techniques and offers a new strategy for developing more robust controllers.
 - **Online Contact-Implicit Control:** The learned policies can dynamically adjust contact sequences and utilize emergent hops for stabilization, similar to contact-implicit trajectory optimization, but achieved online through a learned policy.
3. **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful direct deployment of simulation-trained policies onto hardware without further tuning, despite a notable sim-to-real gap, validates the effectiveness of the multi-stage training framework and dynamics randomization strategy.
4. **Broader Impact on Robot Mobility:** By enabling bipedal robots to perform a wider array of dynamic movements, this work contributes to the development of more capable and autonomous robots that can traverse challenging terrains, potentially impacting applications in exploration, disaster response, and assistance.

A noted limitation is occasional oscillation after a jump, suggesting potential challenges in having a single policy manage both highly dynamic jumps and stationary standing. Future work may involve integrating this jumping capability with advanced perception systems to enable navigation in more complex and dynamic environments.
