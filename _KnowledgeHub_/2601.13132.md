# GaussExplorer: 3D Gaussian Splatting for Embodied Exploration and Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> GaussExplorer introduces a unified framework integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enable embodied exploration and reasoning in 3D scenes. The system achieves state-of-the-art performance in embodied question answering by dynamically adjusting viewpoints for complex natural language queries and significantly improves 3D referring segmentation.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based embodied reasoning systems are limited by pre-fixed viewpoints and object-level abstractions, preventing dynamic exploration or fine-grained spatial reasoning.
- Current language-driven 3D Gaussian Splatting methods struggle with complex, compositional natural language queries, primarily relying on static embedding similarity for simple category-level matching.
- Intelligent embodied agents require both high-level understanding of natural language instructions and precise 3D localization capabilities to reason and interact effectively within complex 3D environments.

## Method

- Constructs ==semantic 3D Gaussian representations== by integrating object categories or open-set CLIP embeddings into individual Gaussians, enabling rich scene understanding.
- Implements an ==initial view selection process== that uses LLMs to extract relevant 'evidence categories' from queries, followed by a Gaussian search and instance-level clustering to identify candidate objects.
- Introduces a ==novel-view adjustment mechanism== where VLMs dynamically synthesize and evaluate perturbed camera poses, acting as a 'judge' to select optimal viewpoints that provide the strongest visual evidence for a given query.

## Results

- Achieved an LLM-Match score of **57.8** on the EM-EQA benchmark for Embodied Question Answering, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art 3D-Mem (**54.6**) while maintaining comparable VLM processing efficiency (**2.6** vs. **2.7** frames).
- Demonstrated superior performance in Question-based 3D Referring Segmentation with a 3D mIoU of **12.87** and Acc@10 of **45.71**, significantly surpassing Dr. Splat (**10.56** 3D mIoU) and ReferSplat (**2.34** 3D mIoU).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the proposed visibility score, novel-view adjustment, and VLM-based verification steps are crucial for the observed performance gains in both embodied reasoning and 3D localization tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic, VLM-guided novel-view synthesis from 3D Gaussian Splatting can overcome the fixed-view limitations of traditional embodied AI systems, leading to more accurate and reliable reasoning.
> - Integrating semantic embeddings directly into 3D Gaussians enables robust open-vocabulary understanding and fine-grained 3D localization in response to complex, compositional natural language queries.
> - A 'visibility score' for initial view selection, combined with a 'VLM-as-Judge' mechanism for novel-view adjustment, effectively identifies and leverages informative viewpoints to enhance embodied reasoning accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "GaussExplorer" positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: embodied artificial intelligence (AI), 3D scene understanding, and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The overarching goal in this landscape is to develop intelligent agents that can interpret natural language instructions and navigate or reason within complex 3D environments, moving beyond static image analysis to dynamic, interactive intelligence.

Previous research efforts in embodied AI have largely focused on two main directions, each with inherent limitations:

- **VLM-based Embodied Reasoning with Structured Memory:** Approaches like 3D-Mem (Yang et al., 2025) integrate powerful VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, GPT-4V) with structured memories built from RGB-D observations. While these systems demonstrate strong reasoning capabilities across multi-view images, they are typically constrained by **pre-fixed viewpoints** and **object-level abstractions**. This means they operate on existing, pre-captured image sequences and object representations, fundamentally limiting their ability to synthesize novel views or conduct fine-grained spatial reasoning beyond what was initially recorded. They cannot actively "explore" new perspectives to gather more evidence, a critical component for true embodied intelligence. Furthermore, methods relying on high-level textual abstractions (e.g., scene graphs from RGB-D data) discard low-level geometric and visual details, hindering fine-grained understanding necessary for complex tasks.

- **Language-driven 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** The advent of 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) has revolutionized real-time 3D reconstruction and rendering. This has spurred research into augmenting 3DGS with language features for open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding (e.g., Dr. Splat by Kim Jun-Seong et al., 2025; ReferSplat by He et al., 2025). These methods embed features (like CLIP embeddings) into individual Gaussians, enabling tasks such as open-vocabulary object retrieval and segmentation. However, a significant limitation here is their reliance on **static embedding similarity**, primarily optimized for **simple, category-level text queries** (e.g., "chair"). They struggle to interpret **complex, compositional language queries** (e.g., "What color are the pillows in the kitchen?") because their visual reasoning operates at the level of individual Gaussians without explicit mechanisms for iterative grounding or contextual aggregation. ReferSplat, while aiming for fine-grained descriptions, employs a rendering-based optimization that makes it less suitable for direct 3D search.

GaussExplorer directly addresses the shortcomings of both these paradigms. It proposes a novel integration that combines the sophisticated **reasoning capabilities of modern VLMs** with the high-fidelity **novel-view synthesis power of 3DGS**. By doing so, GaussExplorer aims to:
1. Overcome the **fixed-view limitations** of memory-based VLM systems by enabling dynamic, question-driven exploration and viewpoint adjustment.
2. Enhance the linguistic understanding of 3DGS, moving beyond simple category matching to handle **complex, compositional natural language queries**.
3. Provide precise **3D localization and visual grounding**, which is essential for detailed interaction within 3D environments.

This work contributes to the broader goal of making embodied agents more intelligent, adaptable, and capable of truly "seeing" and reasoning about their environment in response to nuanced human instructions. It pushes the boundaries of how 3D representations can serve as active memory structures for advanced AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of GaussExplorer is to develop a unified framework that enables **embodied exploration and reasoning** within 3D scenes by seamlessly integrating **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** with **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**.

The motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

- **Bridging the Gap in Embodied AI:** A long-standing goal in computer vision and robotics is to create embodied agents that can interpret natural language and act intelligently within 3D environments. While recent VLMs have demonstrated remarkable reasoning on static images, equipping them with an understanding of 3D space and the ability to dynamically interact with it remains a significant challenge.
- **Overcoming Fixed-View Limitations of Existing Embodied Systems:** Prior approaches to embodied reasoning (e.g., 3D-Mem) often rely on pre-captured image sequences and object-level abstractions as structured memories. This inherently limits exploration to fixed viewpoints, preventing the system from synthesizing novel views to gather more precise visual evidence. The authors are motivated to move beyond these passive memory systems to enable active, question-driven exploration.
- **Addressing the Insufficiencies of Language-driven 3DGS:** Existing methods that augment 3DGS with language embeddings (e.g., CLIP features) are generally optimized for simple, category-level text queries. They struggle to interpret complex, compositional language (e.g., "What color are the pillows in the kitchen?"). The motivation is to enhance 3DGS with VLM-level reasoning to understand and ground such intricate queries more effectively.
- **Leveraging Novel-View Synthesis for Enhanced Reasoning:** The inherent capability of 3DGS to render high-fidelity novel views in real-time is a powerful but underexplored asset for embodied reasoning. The authors are motivated to exploit this capability to allow agents to actively adjust their viewpoints, overcome occlusions, and resolve visual ambiguities, thereby providing richer visual evidence to VLMs.
- **Enabling Fine-Grained 3D Localization and Grounding:** For truly intelligent embodied agents, not only high-level reasoning but also precise 3D localization of objects based on complex language queries is crucial. The paper aims to support this fine-grained visual grounding, going beyond merely identifying object categories to pinpoint specific instances and their attributes or spatial relationships.
- **Improving VLM-based Reasoning Accuracy and Reliability:** By allowing VLMs to dynamically choose the most informative viewpoints, the framework aims to significantly improve the accuracy and reliability of their answers to embodied questions, especially in scenarios where initial or fixed views might be suboptimal.

In essence, GaussExplorer seeks to create a more capable and versatile embodied agent by empowering VLMs with dynamic 3D exploration via 3DGS, enabling them to interpret complex language and ground their understanding precisely within the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GaussExplorer introduces a structured pipeline for embodied exploration and reasoning built upon semantic 3D Gaussians and VLM interaction. The methodology is divided into three core stages: Construction of Semantic 3D Gaussians, Initial View Selection, and Novel-view Adjustment using VLMs.

#### 3.1. Construction of Semantic 3D Gaussians

The first step involves creating a 3D representation of the scene enriched with semantic information.
- **3DGS Reconstruction:** A 3D scene is reconstructed from a set of N input multi-view images and their associated camera parameters using the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) technique. Each 3D Gaussian is parameterized by its 3D position, scale, rotation, opacity, and spherical harmonics coefficients for color.
- **Semantic Information Lifting:** Semantic information is then integrated into these 3D Gaussians, with two distinct strategies depending on the task:
 - **Closed-set categories:** For embodied reasoning tasks (e.g., EM-EQA), a closed-set object detector (like YOLOv8) extracts categorical labels (e.g., "chair", "table") from the input images. These categories are then lifted and associated with individual 3D Gaussians.
 - **Open-set semantic embeddings:** For visual grounding and 3D localization tasks, an open-set segmentation pipeline (combining SAM and CLIP) extracts CLIP embeddings from image segments. These embeddings are then lifted and associated with 3D Gaussians, allowing for open-vocabulary understanding.
- **3D Direct Registration:** Semantic object category information is infused into the 3D Gaussians, leveraging a 3D direct registration approach (following Dr. Splat).

#### 3.2. Initial View Selection in Semantic Gaussians

This stage focuses on identifying pre-recorded camera views that are most relevant to a given natural language query.
- **Gaussian Search from Query:**
 - **LLM-based Evidence Category Extraction:** A Large Language Model (LLM) (e.g., GPT-4) analyzes the input natural language query (e.g., "Where can I take a nap?") and extracts "evidence categories" (e.g., "pillow," "cushion") relevant to answering the question.
 - **Activated Gaussians Retrieval:** Based on these evidence categories:
 - For closed-set categories, 3D Gaussians whose associated category matches one of the evidence categories are directly retrieved.
 - For open-set embeddings, Gaussians are retrieved based on the similarity between their embedded CLIP features and the text embeddings of the evidence categories, exceeding a predefined threshold.
- **Clustering 3D Gaussians for Instance Assignment:** The retrieved "activated" Gaussians are then grouped into distinct object instances. A two-step clustering process, using HDBSCAN followed by a merge operation for physically close clusters, is applied to form instance-level Gaussian clusters. This handles the fact that a single Gaussian is not an object and ensures robust instance segmentation.
- **Initial View Selection:** For each identified instance cluster, the goal is to select the most informative initial camera view from the original set of input images.
 - **Visibility Score:** To determine view informativeness, a novel "visibility score" is computed for each instance cluster and each available training camera view. This score assesses how well the Gaussians belonging to a target instance cluster are visible from a particular viewpoint. It achieves this by rendering an activation map and identifying Gaussians with the highest rendering weight that belong to the target instance. The score is defined as the ratio of visible Gaussians to the total Gaussians in the cluster. This metric is robust to occlusion and effectively serves as a proxy for object surface coverage.
 - The training camera view that maximizes this visibility score for a given instance cluster is chosen as the initial view for that instance.

#### 3.3. Novel-view Adjustment using VLMs

This is the most innovative part of the methodology, where the system actively explores new viewpoints to enhance reasoning.
- **Novel-view Synthesis:** From each initial camera view selected in the previous stage, a small set of K perturbed "novel views" are generated. This is achieved by subtly shifting the camera pose (e.g., left, right, zoom in, zoom out) around the initial camera center. Each of these perturbed poses is then used to render an RGB image via the differentiable 3DGS renderer.
- **VLM-as-Judge for View Informativeness:**
 - **Initial Answer Prediction:** Each rendered novel-view image (along with the original user question) is independently fed into a powerful VLM (e.g., GPT-4o). The VLM generates an "initial answer prediction" for each candidate view. This strategy avoids overwhelming the VLM with long sequences of visual data.
 - **LLM-guided Final View Selection:** An LLM (e.g., GPT-5-mini) then evaluates all these per-view answer predictions (from both initial and novel views) in the context of the original question. It acts as a "judge" to select the single best answer, which implicitly determines the most informative "final view" (or set of views) that provides the strongest visual evidence for the query.
- **Verification Stage:** An additional VLM-based verification step is incorporated. This stage compares the answer derived from the initially selected views (without novel-view adjustment) against the answer from the final, VLM-adjusted novel views. This verifies whether the novel-view adjustment process indeed led to a more reliable and accurate answer, confirming the necessity of active exploration.
- **Application to 3D Referring Segmentation:** For fine-grained 3D localization tasks, the final selected camera view (which is most aligned with the question) is used to prune the activated 3D Gaussians. Only those Gaussians that fall within the frustum of this "answer view" are retained, providing precise 3D object localization.

This comprehensive approach allows GaussExplorer to dynamically adapt its visual input, moving beyond static observations to actively seek out and synthesize optimal viewpoints for accurate embodied reasoning and fine-grained 3D grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GaussExplorer's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on two challenging tasks: Embodied Question Answering (EQA) and Question-based 3D Referring Segmentation, supported by detailed ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Embodied Question Answering (EQA)

- **Dataset:** OpenEQA's Episodic-Memory Embodied Question Answering (EM-EQA) benchmark, comprising over 1,600 questions across 180 environments from Habitat-Matterport 3D and ScanNet.
- **Metrics:** Performance is evaluated using the LLM-Match metric, which uses a strong LLM (GPT-4) to assess the semantic equivalence between predicted and ground-truth answers. Efficiency is measured by the average number of frames (initial or final views) fed to VLMs.
- **Results:** GaussExplorer achieved the highest LLM-Match score of **57.8**, outperforming all compared baselines, including:
 - BlindLLM (34.8) and Frame Captions (24.1), which rely solely on textual input or static captions, confirming the necessity of visual context.
 - Structured memory methods like CG Captions (36.5), SVM Captions (38.9), and Multi-Frame (49.1), highlighting the benefits of a more dynamic 3D representation.
 - The state-of-the-art 3D-Mem (54.6), which uses object-centric multi-view Memory Snapshots. GaussExplorer's superior performance (57.8 vs. 54.6) demonstrates the effectiveness of its semantic 3DGS representation and novel-view adjustment.
- **Efficiency:** GaussExplorer maintained comparable efficiency with an average of **2.6 frames** fed to VLMs, similar to 3D-Mem's 2.7 frames, indicating that performance gains were not at the cost of significantly increased computational burden on the VLMs.

#### 4.2. Question-based 3D Referring Segmentation

- **Dataset:** A manually curated benchmark derived from ScanNet scenes, featuring 24 distinct objects with 49 human-written, complex compositional questions that require distinguishing among multiple instances based on spatial relationships or attributes (e.g., "Which bed is closest to the window?").
- **Metrics:** 3D mean Intersection over Union (3D mIoU) between activated and ground-truth Gaussians, and Acc@k (percentage of predictions with IoU > k).
- **Results:**
 - GaussExplorer achieved the highest 3D mIoU of **12.46**, significantly outperforming Dr. Splat (category-level: 10.03, question-level: 10.56) and ReferSplat (2.34).
 - The framework also demonstrated superior Acc@k scores, with Acc@10 at **45.14**.
 - Crucially, the addition of the **novel-view adjustment** further boosted performance to **12.87 3D mIoU** and **45.71 Acc@10**, underscoring its value for fine-grained 3D localization.
 - Qualitative results (Fig. 6) visually confirmed that GaussExplorer more accurately aligns with ground truth, effectively distinguishing target instances based on complex language queries, a task where competing methods struggled.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Initial View Selection Strategy:** Comparing the proposed "visibility score" with a "volume score" (based on Gaussian volumetric properties), the visibility score (48.2 LLM-Match) proved superior to the volume score (47.8). This indicates that the visibility score, acting as a proxy for object surface coverage, is more robust to outlier Gaussians and provides more stable and reliable view estimates.
- **View Selection Pipeline:** The ablation confirmed the necessity of each stage:
 - Initial view selection alone improved over 3D-Mem (48.2 vs. 45.4 LLM-Match).
 - Integrating the final view selection with novel views further boosted performance (50.5 LLM-Match), demonstrating that 3DGS-based exploration uncovers more informative viewpoints.
- **Final VLM Reasoning:** Using a "visual prompt only" strategy for the final VLM reasoning stage achieved the highest accuracy (57.8 LLM-Match) compared to a "visual + textual prompt" (56.2). This suggests that relying directly on visual evidence, without auxiliary textual cues at this late stage, is more effective for VLMs.
- **Verification Process:** The "verification" step, which compares answers from initial vs. novel views, significantly improved LLM-Match accuracy (57.8) compared to using only final views without verification (54.5). This crucial step ensures that the selected novel views genuinely enhance the evidence, without increasing rendering costs.

Overall, the findings robustly support GaussExplorer's design, demonstrating its ability to significantly enhance embodied reasoning and 3D localization by intelligently integrating VLM capabilities with 3DGS's novel-view synthesis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GaussExplorer represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and 3D scene understanding, with the potential to profoundly impact several related domains. Its core significance lies in its innovative integration of two powerful, yet previously disparate, technologies: the robust 3D representation and novel-view synthesis capabilities of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and the sophisticated language understanding and reasoning abilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

The key impacts are as follows:

- **Elevating Embodied AI Capabilities:** GaussExplorer pushes the frontier of embodied agents beyond passive perception and memory-based reasoning. By enabling question-driven, active exploration and dynamic viewpoint adjustment in 3D environments, it brings agents closer to human-like situational awareness and interaction. This is crucial for developing agents that can genuinely understand complex instructions and navigate real-world scenarios.
- **Unlocking Complex Language Understanding in 3D:** Prior language-driven 3DGS methods were largely limited to simple, category-level queries. GaussExplorer demonstrates a robust solution for interpreting and grounding highly compositional and nuanced natural language queries directly within a 3D representation. This capability is vital for intuitive human-agent interaction, where instructions are rarely simplistic.
- **Pioneering VLM-Guided 3D Exploration:** The "VLM-as-Judge" mechanism for novel-view adjustment is a groundbreaking approach. It leverages the VLM's inherent visual reasoning to actively seek out optimal viewpoints, overcoming challenges like occlusion and ambiguity. This paradigm shift from passive observation to active, intelligent data gathering significantly enhances the reliability and accuracy of VLM-based reasoning in 3D.
- **Enabling Fine-Grained 3D Localization:** The framework’s ability to perform precise 3D referring segmentation based on complex questions is a major contribution. This is critical for applications requiring exact object identification, manipulation, or interaction (e.g., "pick up the blue cup on the left of the monitor," rather than just "find a cup").
- **Practical Applications Across Industries:**
 - **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** GaussExplorer could power more intelligent AR glasses or VR environments, allowing users to ask natural language questions about their surroundings or virtual objects, with the system dynamically providing precise visual answers or highlighting relevant elements.
 - **Robotics:** This work is directly applicable to robotics, enabling robots to better understand and execute complex instructions in unstructured environments. Robots could use GaussExplorer to actively explore a scene to locate specific items, verify conditions, or plan actions with greater precision.
 - **Digital Twins and Smart Environments:** In digital twin applications, users could interact with virtual replicas of physical spaces using natural language, asking complex queries about objects, layouts, or attributes, and receiving highly accurate, visually grounded responses.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies clear avenues for future work, notably extending GaussExplorer from episodic-memory settings (like smart glasses) to fully active embodied exploration and navigation for robotic agents, which would involve integrating action policies and sequential decision-making. This framework provides a solid foundation for researchers to build upon, exploring more dynamic scenes, real-time interactive capabilities, and more complex long-horizon reasoning tasks.

In conclusion, GaussExplorer pushes the boundaries of how AI agents perceive and interact with the 3D world, offering a novel and effective solution to long-standing challenges in embodied AI by harnessing the synergistic power of 3D Gaussian Splatting and Vision-Language Models.
