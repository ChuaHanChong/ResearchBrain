# WSM: Decay-Free Learning Rate Schedule via Checkpoint Merging for LLM Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Warmup-Stable and Merge (WSM) introduces a decay-free learning rate schedule for LLM pre-training that achieves the benefits of learning rate decay through principled checkpoint merging. This approach consistently outperforms the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) baseline, demonstrating average improvements of +1.3 points across benchmarks and significant gains such as +3.5% on MATH, +2.9% on HumanEval, and +5.5% on MMLU-Pro.

## Problem

- Conventional learning rate (LR) schedules (e.g., cosine decay) require pre-specifying the total training duration, making training extensions or data changes computationally prohibitive due to mandatory restarts.
- The Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) strategy, while more flexible, still demands manual decisions for decay initiation, duration, and function, hindering autonomous and continuous training.
- A lack of a robust, general, and decay-free LR scheduling mechanism that simplifies LLM pre-training while maintaining or improving performance.

## Method

- WSM eliminates explicit LR decay during live training by employing a ==Warmup-Stable phase== where the learning rate remains constant at its peak value.
- It strategically merges periodically saved ==model checkpoints== (an asynchronous, post-hoc process) using mathematically derived weights to emulate the effects of various LR decay curves.
- A formal theoretical connection (==Theorem 3.1==) is established, mapping desired ==gradient decay coefficients== to unique, non-negative checkpoint merge weights, enabling WSM to approximate traditional decay strategies.

## Results

- WSM consistently outperforms the WSD baseline across diverse tasks, achieving an average improvement of **+1.3 points**, with specific gains of **+3.5%** on MATH, **+2.9%** on HumanEval, and **+5.5%** on MMLU-Pro.
- Models initialized from WSM checkpoints maintain their performance advantage after supervised fine-tuning, demonstrating sustained benefits for downstream tasks.
- Merging algorithms derived from the theoretical framework (e.g., 1-sqrt or mean averaging) perform significantly better than Exponential Moving Average (EMA) for checkpoint merging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning rate decay can be formally linked to and emulated by a principled weighted merging of model checkpoints, transforming an empirical heuristic into a systematic approach.
> - A decay-free, constant LR during live training combined with post-hoc checkpoint merging provides greater flexibility, enabling continuous training and exploration of annealing strategies without costly restarts.
> - The duration of the merging window is the most critical hyperparameter for WSM's performance, analogous to the total annealing data used in traditional LR decay schedules.
