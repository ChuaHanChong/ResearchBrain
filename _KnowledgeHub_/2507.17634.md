# WSM: Decay-Free Learning Rate Schedule via Checkpoint Merging for LLM Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Warmup-Stable and Merge (WSM) introduces a decay-free learning rate schedule for LLM pre-training that achieves the benefits of learning rate decay through principled checkpoint merging. This approach consistently outperforms the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) baseline, demonstrating average improvements of +1.3 points across benchmarks and significant gains such as +3.5% on MATH, +2.9% on HumanEval, and +5.5% on MMLU-Pro.

## Problem

- Conventional learning rate (LR) schedules (e.g., cosine decay) require pre-specifying the total training duration, making training extensions or data changes computationally prohibitive due to mandatory restarts.
- The Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) strategy, while more flexible, still demands manual decisions for decay initiation, duration, and function, hindering autonomous and continuous training.
- A lack of a robust, general, and decay-free LR scheduling mechanism that simplifies LLM pre-training while maintaining or improving performance.

## Method

- WSM eliminates explicit LR decay during live training by employing a ==Warmup-Stable phase== where the learning rate remains constant at its peak value.
- It strategically merges periodically saved ==model checkpoints== (an asynchronous, post-hoc process) using mathematically derived weights to emulate the effects of various LR decay curves.
- A formal theoretical connection (==Theorem 3.1==) is established, mapping desired ==gradient decay coefficients== to unique, non-negative checkpoint merge weights, enabling WSM to approximate traditional decay strategies.

## Results

- WSM consistently outperforms the WSD baseline across diverse tasks, achieving an average improvement of **+1.3 points**, with specific gains of **+3.5%** on MATH, **+2.9%** on HumanEval, and **+5.5%** on MMLU-Pro.
- Models initialized from WSM checkpoints maintain their performance advantage after supervised fine-tuning, demonstrating sustained benefits for downstream tasks.
- Merging algorithms derived from the theoretical framework (e.g., 1-sqrt or mean averaging) perform significantly better than Exponential Moving Average (EMA) for checkpoint merging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning rate decay can be formally linked to and emulated by a principled weighted merging of model checkpoints, transforming an empirical heuristic into a systematic approach.
> - A decay-free, constant LR during live training combined with post-hoc checkpoint merging provides greater flexibility, enabling continuous training and exploration of annealing strategies without costly restarts.
> - The duration of the merging window is the most critical hyperparameter for WSM's performance, analogous to the total annealing data used in traditional LR decay schedules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Learning Rate (LR) scheduling is a cornerstone of deep learning, critically influencing the stability, convergence, and ultimate performance of models, especially Large Language Models (LLMs) during their resource-intensive pre-training phase. Historically, LR schedules like cosine decay have been dominant, characterized by an initial warm-up phase followed by a gradual reduction of the LR. While effective, these schedules suffer from a significant drawback: they require a predetermined total training duration. This inflexibility means that any extension of training, such as incorporating new data or continuing pre-training, necessitates a complete restart to recalculate the entire decay curve, which is computationally prohibitive for trillion-token-scale LLMs.

To address these limitations, the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) strategy emerged as a more flexible alternative. WSD introduces a stable training phase with a constant LR between the warm-up and the decay, decoupling the decay initiation from the total training duration. This approach has gained traction, being adopted in notable LLMs like DeepSeek-V3 and ERNIE 4.5. However, WSD still demands manual decisions regarding the decay phase—when to start, how long it should last, and which decay function (e.g., cosine, linear) to use. Furthermore, extending training *after* decay has begun still requires rolling back and redesigning the decay strategy, impeding the goal of truly autonomous and continuous training.

Against this backdrop, recent research has explored "decay-free" approaches that aim to eliminate the explicit decay phase entirely. A prominent direction within this space leverages weight averaging, also known as model merging techniques. Empirical studies have shown that maintaining a constant LR combined with standard weight averaging (e.g., Exponentially Weighted Averaging, EWA) can achieve performance comparable to WSD-based schedules (Li et al., 2025).

The present work, WSM, fits squarely into this cutting-edge "decay-free" paradigm. It significantly advances the field by:
1. **Formalizing the Connection:** Unlike prior empirical explorations, WSM establishes a robust theoretical connection between LR decay and checkpoint merging, transforming an intuitive heuristic into a principled approach.
2. **General Framework:** It provides a general framework to emulate various traditional LR decay strategies (cosine, linear, inverse square root) as principled model averaging schemes. This means the benefits of sophisticated decay strategies can be obtained without explicit LR manipulation during live training.
3. **Surpassing Baselines:** While other model merging techniques have aimed to *match* the performance of decay-based schedules, WSM consistently *outperforms* the widely-adopted WSD approach, pushing the state-of-the-art for LR scheduling in LLM pre-training.
4. **Practicality for Continuous Training:** By eliminating the decay phase from the live training process and achieving its benefits through post-hoc merging, WSM enables a truly autonomous, continuous training pipeline, removing the need for pre-specified durations or costly restarts.

In essence, WSM represents a pivotal step towards more flexible, efficient, and higher-performing LLM pre-training, bridging theoretical insights with practical demands for large-scale model development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, decay-free learning rate schedule, called Warmup-Stable and Merge (WSM), that addresses the inherent inflexibility and operational complexities of traditional and existing advanced LR schedules in LLM pre-training. The ultimate goal is to simplify the training process, enable continuous model refinement, and achieve superior performance compared to established baselines.

The motivation for proposing WSM stems from several critical pain points observed in current LLM pre-training practices:

- **Inflexibility of Conventional LR Schedules:** Traditional schedules like cosine decay necessitate pre-specification of the total training duration (`T_max`). This fixed duration makes it extremely challenging and resource-intensive to extend training, incorporate new data, or adapt to evolving training needs. Any such modification requires a complete restart of the training process to recalibrate the entire LR decay curve.
- **Operational Burden of Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD):** While WSD introduced a stable LR phase to offer more flexibility by decoupling decay initiation from total steps, it still places a significant burden on researchers. They must manually determine the decay start step (`T_decay_start`), the duration of the decay, and the specific decay function (e.g., cosine, linear). Furthermore, if training needs to be extended post-decay, a rollback to the state before decay is required, followed by a re-design of the decay strategy. This manual intervention fundamentally opposes the objective of a fully autonomous and continuous training process.
- **Desire for Autonomous and Continuous Training:** The authors aim for a scheduling mechanism that removes these manual decision points and pre-specifications, allowing for seamless and indefinite continuation of training without performance degradation or complex reconfigurations.
- **Leveraging Emerging "Decay-Free" Concepts:** Recent research has hinted at the potential of completely eliminating the decay phase through techniques like weight averaging. This motivates a deeper exploration into how such methods can effectively capture the benefits traditionally associated with LR decay.

To address these motivations, WSM sets out to achieve three specific objectives:

1. **Formalize the Connection between LR Decay and Checkpoint Merging:** To move beyond empirical observations, the authors aim to establish a rigorous theoretical foundation demonstrating how LR decay can be mathematically linked to and emulated by checkpoint merging. This involves deriving checkpoint merging weights from desired gradient decay coefficients.
2. **Develop a General and Optimizer-Agnostic Framework:** The framework should be versatile enough to instantiate and approximate various traditional decay strategies (e.g., cosine, linear, inverse square root decay) through principled model averaging schemes, without requiring modifications to the underlying optimization algorithm (e.g., SGD, Adam).
3. **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Practical Benefits:** Through extensive experimentation, WSM aims to show consistent and significant performance improvements over the strong WSD baseline across a diverse set of benchmarks. This includes demonstrating its benefits not only in the pre-training phase but also in subsequent supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stages, and identifying key hyperparameters that drive its effectiveness.

By achieving these objectives, WSM seeks to offer a more elegant, efficient, and high-performing solution for LR scheduling in large-scale LLM training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of WSM is to completely eliminate the explicit learning rate decay phase from the live training process and instead achieve its benefits through the strategic merging of model checkpoints. This transforms the complex, pre-defined LR decay into a flexible, post-hoc operation.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Connection between LR Decay and Checkpoint Merging

The foundational aspect of WSM is establishing a formal link between LR decay and checkpoint merging.
- **Weighted Averaging:** The general form of checkpoint merging is defined as a weighted average of `k+1` ordered checkpoints, `[θ_n, θ_{n+1}..., θ_{n+k}]`, to produce a merged model `ˆθ_{n+k}`: `ˆθ_{n+k} = Σ c_j θ_{n+j}`, where `c_j` are non-negative weights summing to one.
- **Gradient Updates:** Each subsequent checkpoint `θ_{n+j}` can be expressed as an initial state `θ_n` minus the accumulated gradient updates `g` up to that point: `θ_{n+j} = θ_n - Σ g_{n+l-1}`.
- **Equivalence Derivation:** By substituting the gradient-based expression for `θ_{n+j}` into the weighted averaging formula and rearranging the summations, the authors demonstrate that `ˆθ_{n+k}` can be equivalently expressed as `θ_n` minus a weighted sum of the gradient updates: `ˆθ_{n+k} = θ_n - Σ w_i · g_{n+i-1}`. Here, `w_i = Σ_{j=i}^k c_j` acts as an effective learning rate coefficient applied to past gradients.
- **Theorem 3.1:** This theorem formalizes the unique mapping from a desired sequence of gradient decay coefficients `{w_i}` (which are monotonically non-increasing and bounded `1 ≥ w_1 ≥ ... ≥ w_k ≥ 0`) to the corresponding non-negative checkpoint merge weights `{c_j}`. This enables WSM to theoretically derive `c_j` to emulate specific decay curves (e.g., cosine, linear, inverse square root decay).

#### 3.2. The Warmup-Stable and Merge (WSM) Schedule

The WSM schedule operates in two primary phases (Algorithm 1):
1. **Warmup Phase:** The learning rate linearly increases to its peak value (`lr_peak`) over `T_warmup` steps, identical to conventional schedules.
2. **Stable Training Phase:** After warmup, the learning rate is held constant at `lr_peak` indefinitely. Crucially, *no LR decay* occurs during this live training phase.
 - **Dataset Annealing (Optional):** After a specified `T_switch` step, the model can transition from the general pre-training data (`D`) to a smaller, high-quality annealing dataset (`D_anneal`). This allows for focused refinement on high-quality data, akin to later stages of traditional pre-training.
 - **Checkpointing:** Throughout the stable phase, checkpoints (`W_t`) are periodically saved to storage at a fixed interval (`T_cpt`).
 - **Asynchronous Merging:** Concurrently, an asynchronous process fetches the last `n` checkpoints from storage. Using the `Merge` operation, these checkpoints are combined into `W_merged`. The `Merge` operation employs the `c_j` weights derived from Theorem 3.1 to approximate the desired LR decay behavior. The merged model `W_merged` is used for evaluation, while the live training continues with `W_t` and the constant `lr_peak`.

#### 3.3. Experiment Setup

- **Model Architecture:** The experiments utilize "Ling-mini," a 16.3 billion parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with 1.4 billion active parameters. It features a fine-grained expert configuration (1 dense, 19 MoE layers, 256 experts per MoE layer, 8 activated per token + 1 shared). It uses GQA attention and Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), supporting 8K token sequence lengths.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with `β1=0.9`, `β2=0.95`, and `0.1` weight decay. Gradient clipping norm is `1.0`.
- **Hyperparameters:** Peak LR of `4.78e-4` and a batch size of `2048` (16M tokens per batch), determined through preliminary scaling law experiments.
- **Pre-training Baseline:** Experiments begin with a checkpoint pre-trained on 10.2 trillion tokens (10T standard + 200B long-context) using a stable, constant LR.
- **Comparison Branches:** From this baseline, training branches for an *additional 400 billion tokens* on a specialized high-quality annealing dataset:
 1. **WSD Baseline:** Applies a conventional LR decay schedule (e.g., 1-sqrt decay, based on preliminary experiments showing its superiority, detailed in Appendix C).
 2. **WSM (Proposed):** Continues training with the same constant LR, saving checkpoints every 25 billion tokens. The final model is produced by merging the most recent checkpoints (mean averaging by default, unless otherwise specified).
- **Evaluation:** A comprehensive suite of downstream tasks covering General Knowledge/Reasoning, Language Understanding, Professional Knowledge, Math, and Code benchmarks is used for evaluation. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is also conducted on WSM and WSD checkpoints to assess long-term impact.
- **Exploration Strategy:** The initial use of *offline, checkpoint-based merging* allows for extensive exploration of various merging algorithms and durations without affecting the main training run, preserving all discrete checkpoints. Once an optimal strategy is identified, it can be efficiently operationalized as an *online, sliding-window merging process*.

This methodical approach, combining theoretical rigor with extensive empirical validation on a large-scale LLM, underpins the claims of WSM's effectiveness and superiority.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that WSM is a highly effective and robust decay-free learning rate scheduling approach, consistently outperforming conventional methods and offering significant practical advantages.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance of WSM Schedule (Section 4.2)

- **Superiority over WSD:** WSM consistently outperforms the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) approach across the majority of evaluated tasks and benchmark categories. When comparing the best-performing checkpoints, WSM achieves an average improvement of +1.3 points over WSD.
- **Significant Gains in Key Areas:** WSM shows remarkable improvements in critical LLM capabilities:
 - +3.5% on MATH
 - +2.9% on HumanEval (Code)
 - +5.5% on MMLU-Pro (Professional Knowledge)
 These improvements are clearly illustrated in Figure 3 and Table 1, providing compelling evidence that checkpoint merging is a more effective strategy than LR decay for enhancing diverse model capabilities.
- **Long-term Benefits (Post-training):** The performance advantages of WSM extend beyond pre-training. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) experiments show that models initialized from WSM checkpoints continue to outperform those from WSD checkpoints under identical SFT settings, indicating sustained improvements for long-term model refinement (Table 2).

#### 4.2. Empirical Analysis of WSM Schedule (Section 4.3)

- **Robustness Across Pre-training Process (Section 4.3.1):** WSM proves to be a reliable proxy for evaluating a model's potential. Models generated by WSM merging at various intermediate stages of a long pre-training run consistently mirror the performance of computationally intensive full LR anneals. This allows for effective assessment throughout pre-training without launching expensive decay runs (Figure 5a).
- **Impact of Merging Algorithm (Section 4.3.2):** The choice of merging algorithm significantly affects performance, reinforcing the theoretical connection between merging and decay.
 - Algorithms designed to approximate effective decay curves (like the 1-sqrt derived from Theorem 3.1) or simple mean averaging perform best.
 - 1-sqrt merging shows slight advantages over mean averaging, and both are markedly superior to Exponential Moving Average (EMA) (Table 3). This suggests that optimal merging weights effectively capture the benefits of known superior decay schedules.
- **Impact of Merging Duration and Granularity (Section 4.3.3):**
 - **Merge Duration:** Identified as the *most critical factor* influencing model performance, surpassing checkpoint interval and merge quantity. Larger merging windows generally lead to better results, but with diminishing returns, analogous to increasing annealing data in LR decay (Figure 4).
 - **Merging Granularity:** Finer-grained merging (saving checkpoints more frequently for merging) tends to achieve better performance, though it incurs higher storage overhead, highlighting a practical trade-off (Table 4).
- **Compatibility of Merge and Decay (Section 4.3.4):** Hybrid approaches, such as "Decay-then-Merge" or "Merge-then-Decay," did not yield synergistic performance gains. This suggests that checkpoint merging and LR decay are alternative mechanisms to achieve similar optimization objectives rather than complementary strategies (Figures 5b, 5c).
- **Impact on MoE Load Balancing (Section 4.3.5):** WSM leads to more balanced MoE routing, indicated by lower `mean_global_max_violation` and `mean_global_min_violation` scores, despite a slightly higher language modeling loss. This trade-off is interpreted as enhanced generalization rather than overfitting, as improved routing stability can contribute to better overall model behavior (Table 5).

#### 4.3. Parameter Trajectories and Model Performance (Section 4.4)

- **Convergence to Optimal Space:** t-SNE visualizations (Figure 6) reveal that during the LR decay phase, model parameters gradually converge toward the solution space occupied by the merged model. This merged model solution space consistently achieves superior performance compared to models trained with a constant LR.
- **Precise Refinement:** The LR reduction in the decay phase enables more precise parameter refinement, akin to finding a "river" at the "canyon floor," allowing controlled convergence towards optimal solutions, whereas constant LR training exhibits more expansive exploration.

In summary, WSM not only provides a theoretically grounded alternative to LR decay but also empirically demonstrates superior performance, flexibility, and robustness across various training and evaluation scenarios for large language models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WSM framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) pre-training, offering profound implications for both research and industrial applications.

- **Simplification and Automation of Training:** The most immediate impact of WSM is the elimination of the complex, manually configured learning rate decay phase. This removes the need for researchers to pre-specify total training steps, decide on decay functions, or manually intervene for training extensions. This simplification fosters a more autonomous and continuous training process, crucial for the long and evolving lifecycle of state-of-the-art LLMs. The ability to seamlessly extend training without costly restarts or re-designing decay schedules is a major practical benefit.

- **Unified Theoretical Foundation:** By establishing a formal, mathematical connection between learning rate decay and checkpoint merging, WSM provides a robust theoretical underpinning that transforms an empirical heuristic into a principled approach. This connection not only explains *why* model merging works but also offers a systematic way to emulate various decay strategies (cosine, linear, inverse square root) through calculated merge weights. This theoretical clarity can inspire further research into the interplay between optimization dynamics and model ensembling.

- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** WSM consistently outperforms the widely adopted Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) baseline across a diverse range of benchmarks, including significant gains on tasks like MATH (+3.5%), HumanEval (+2.9%), and MMLU-Pro (+5.5%). This establishes WSM as a new state-of-the-art for LR scheduling in LLM pre-training. The benefits also extend to supervised fine-tuning, demonstrating the method's potential for long-term model refinement and robustness. Furthermore, improved MoE load balancing and higher SVD entropy suggest enhanced generalization capabilities and model plasticity, which are critical for adaptability to diverse downstream tasks.

- **Computational Efficiency for Exploration:** The offline nature of checkpoint merging in WSM is a key advantage. It allows practitioners to explore and optimize various annealing strategies (e.g., merge duration, algorithms) *after* the primary training run, without the prohibitive cost of launching multiple, full training runs for each strategy. Once an optimal strategy is identified, it can be efficiently implemented online. This greatly reduces the computational resources and time required for hyperparameter tuning related to the decay phase.

- **Broad Applicability:** The optimizer-agnostic design of WSM ensures its compatibility with diverse optimization algorithms (e.g., SGD, Adam) and existing LLM training pipelines without requiring extensive modifications. This broad applicability facilitates its adoption across different research groups and industrial settings.

- **New Avenues for Research:** WSM opens up several promising future research directions. It can be expanded to incorporate more sophisticated decay strategies by leveraging Theorem 3.1 to derive appropriate merge weights. Its flexibility also makes it suitable for adapting to complex tuning scenarios, such as optimizing dataset mixtures during continuous pre-training, where dynamic "decay-like" effects can be achieved through merging without disrupting the live training. The observed impact on MoE load balancing also warrants further investigation into how merging influences the internal dynamics and robustness of complex model architectures.

In conclusion, WSM offers an elegant and powerful solution to a long-standing challenge in LLM training. By effectively decoupling the benefits of LR decay from the live optimization process, it delivers superior performance, simplifies operational complexities, and paves the way for more flexible and efficient large-scale language model development.
