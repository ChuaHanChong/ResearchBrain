# Learned Perceptive Forward Dynamics Model for Safe and Platform-aware Robotic Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich, NVIDIA, and the Max Planck Institute developed a learned perceptive Forward Dynamics Model for quadrupedal robots, enabling safe and adaptable navigation in complex, unstructured terrains. The model, trained with a hybrid simulation-real-world data approach, predicts future robot states and failure risks, allowing an integrated Model Predictive Path Integral planner to achieve up to 88.33% navigation success rates in diverse environments with reduced manual tuning.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic navigation methods often struggle with accurately assessing real-time traversability and understanding intricate robot-environment interactions due to reliance on simplified dynamics models and labor-intensive, environment-specific tuning of cost functions.
- Physics-based dynamics models fail to capture complex, nonlinear robot-terrain interactions in contact-rich scenarios, while purely data-driven models face a 'reality gap' and data scarcity, especially for high-risk maneuvers.
- Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) and similar classical motion planners typically require extensive and tedious manual tuning of reward functions for different environments, hindering generalizability and 'zero-shot' adaptation.

## Method

- A novel perceptive ==Forward Dynamics Model (FDM)== is developed to predict the robot's future pose and probability of failure, conditioned on surrounding geometry (height scans) and a history of proprioceptive measurements, implicitly learning terrain traversability.
- A hybrid data collection and training strategy is employed, combining large-scale synthetic data (generated in NVIDIA IsaacLab, including high-risk maneuvers) with targeted real-world interaction data to bridge the simulation-reality gap.
- The learned FDM is integrated into a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) planning framework, enabling a simplified reward function that uses only a goal-oriented pose reward and a penalty for learned failure risk, thus eliminating the need for complex, handcrafted cost maps and extensive tuning.

## Results

- The proposed FDM achieved an average of **41.28%** lower position error compared to a perceptive FDM baseline and **70.57%** lower error compared to a constant velocity model at the final prediction step, across various environments.
- The FDM demonstrated high accuracy in predicting collisions, with an F1 score of **0.9** in 2D environments and **0.85** in 3D environments, indicating precise and reliable failure estimation.
- When integrated into an MPPI planner, the method achieved navigation success rates of **88.33%** in 2D and **73.75%** in 3D environments, outperforming baselines, and generated the shortest mean path length and mean path time for successful trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned perceptive forward dynamics model can implicitly capture complex robot-terrain interactions, including failure probabilities, crucial for safe navigation in unstructured environments.
> - A hybrid training approach, leveraging both vast simulated risky data and real-world subtle dynamics, effectively bridges the simulation-reality gap for robust deployment.
> - Integrating such a dynamics model into a planning framework like MPPI significantly simplifies the planning objective, enabling "zero-shot" adaptation to new environments without extensive manual tuning.
> - The learned FDM can inherently adapt to different robot embodiments and their specific locomotion policies, demonstrating platform-awareness.
