# Scalable Policy-Based RL Algorithms for POMDPs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign developed a framework for solving Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) by approximating them as finite-state "Superstate MDPs" using truncated histories, applying standard policy optimization with linear function approximation. The work provides tighter theoretical guarantees, demonstrating an exponential decay of approximation error with history length and establishing finite-time performance bounds for standard Temporal Difference learning without requiring computationally intensive m-step methods.

## Problem

- Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) are computationally intractable due to their continuous belief state space, making exact solutions infeasible for most real-world applications.
- Heuristic methods for solving POMDPs, while empirically successful, often lack robust theoretical performance guarantees and convergence proofs.
- Existing approximations of POMDPs as finite-state MDPs struggled with providing convergence guarantees for standard Temporal Difference (TD) learning, required computationally expensive m-step TD, or lacked finite-time bounds for linear function approximation.

## Method

- Approximating POMDPs as finite-state "==Superstate MDPs==" by representing states as `l`-length histories of action-observation pairs, thereby discretizing the state space.
- Applying a policy optimization algorithm that utilizes standard Temporal Difference (TD) learning with linear function approximation to evaluate policies within the constructed Superstate MDP.
- Establishing theoretical guarantees, including a uniform filter stability condition and a novel algebraic identity, to justify the convergence of standard TD learning and bound approximation errors.

## Results

- Tighter theoretical bounds were derived, quantifying the exponential decay of the optimal value function difference between the original POMDP and its Superstate MDP approximation with increasing history length `l`.
- Finite-time performance bounds were established for the policy optimization algorithm using linear function approximation, enhancing scalability and providing stronger guarantees compared to asymptotic methods.
- Convergence guarantees for standard Temporal Difference (TD) learning in the policy evaluation step were proven, enabling efficient policy optimization that shows improved performance with longer histories on a FrozenLake environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The uniform filter stability condition is crucial for demonstrating that belief states corresponding to identical finite histories remain close, enabling the Superstate MDP approximation.
> - A newly introduced algebraic identity provides a more refined method for bounding sums of product differences, leading to tighter theoretical guarantees for approximation errors.
> - Standard Temporal Difference (TD) learning can effectively evaluate policies within Superstate MDPs despite a mismatch between the true POMDP sampling and the approximate MDP updates, without requiring computationally expensive modified TD methods.
