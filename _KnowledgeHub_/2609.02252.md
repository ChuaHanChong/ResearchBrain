# DiffuSearch: How Hybrid Trajectory Planning Benefits from Aligned Objectives in Diffusion and Action Space

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffuSearch is a hybrid autonomous-driving trajectory planner that shares one set of driving objectives (collision, drivable area, progress, comfort) across two stages: a guided diffusion transformer producing a scene-consistent joint prediction, then a Monte Carlo Tree Search over discretized acceleration/steering actions that refines the ego plan using those same objectives as reward. It reaches state-of-the-art reactive closed-loop scores on nuPlan and interPlan, cutting collisions and raising comfort.

## Problem

- Hybrid planners bolt a refinement stage onto a proposal generator, with each module optimizing different objectives and no unifying principle across them.
- Mismatched objectives let refinement rewrite the initial plan into a completely different, suboptimal behavior instead of continuously improving it.
- Classifier-free diffusion guidance gives no formal constraint compliance, and prior work reported only qualitative gains, never quantitative closed-loop improvements at scale.

## Method

- ==Guided diffusion transformer== (==DiT== backbone) jointly predicts ego and agent futures, steered at each ==denoising== step by four differentiable cost terms for collision, drivable area, progress and comfort.
- ==Action-space MCTS== refines the ego plan with ==PUCT selection==, a ==kinematic bicycle model== for ego rollout and diffusion joint predictions as the ==world model== for other agents.
- Objective alignment: the MCTS reward is the negated version of the same four weighted guidance costs, and the diffusion ego plan sets a tight ==Gaussian action prior== confining the search locally.

## Results

- Achieves **79.98** R and **77.07** SR on nuPlan Test14-hard, beating DiffusionPlanner by up to **1.82%** and the challenge winner PDM-Hybrid by up to **4.92%**.
- Ablation shows guidance alone hurts (up to **-2.1%** on Test14-SR), MCTS alone always helps, and the combination is best on every split; comfort rises up to **+18%**.
- Refinement stays local: L2 deviation from the initial plan is **0.754 m** versus PLUTO's **1.469 m** under IDM agents, with performance saturating around **256** search steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sharing one objective set across a generative proposer and an explicit refiner keeps refinement a local repair rather than a plan replacement, improving consistency.
> - Differentiable guidance on a diffusion planner can degrade closed-loop scores alone yet still help once an explicit search smooths the out-of-distribution artifacts it introduces.
> - Explicit search is most valuable as focused local improvement around a strong learned prior, not as broad global exploration, and it saturates quickly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Trajectory planning is a critical component of autonomous driving systems, bridging the gap between perception and actuation. Historically, various approaches have been explored, ranging from monolithic deep learning models that directly regress trajectories to more modular, end-to-end architectures. While deep learning-based methods offer flexibility in diverse traffic scenarios, they often lack the explicit safety checks and explainability deemed essential for real-world deployment.

To address these limitations, a significant trend in the field has been the adoption of hybrid planning architectures. These systems typically involve a proposal generation stage, often powered by learned models, followed by a refinement stage that applies explicit rules or optimization techniques to ensure safety and adherence to specific driving objectives. However, a prevalent challenge in these hybrid approaches is the disconnect between the objectives used in the initial proposal generation and those employed during refinement. Different modules optimizing for distinct goals can lead to inconsistencies, where the refinement stage might drastically alter the initial plan rather than incrementally improving it, potentially resulting in suboptimal or even unsafe behaviors.

This work directly addresses this inconsistency by proposing a hybrid planner that employs a *unified set of objectives* across both the generation and refinement stages. It integrates two prominent machine learning and search paradigms:
- **Diffusion Models:** These generative models have demonstrated capabilities in capturing complex, multi-modal data distributions and are increasingly applied in motion prediction and planning for autonomous driving. Their strength lies in generating diverse, scene-consistent trajectories, which can be implicitly guided by differentiable objective functions during the denoising process.
- **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS):** This heuristic search algorithm is well-suited for sequential decision-making and refinement, particularly when a strong prior guides the search. MCTS-based planners are valued for their explicit constraint enforcement and interpretability when searching in a discretized action space.

DiffuSearch thus positions itself at the intersection of generative AI and classical search-based planning, aiming to leverage the strengths of both while mitigating the inconsistencies often found in multi-module hybrid systems. By unifying objectives, the paper contributes to the ongoing effort to develop more robust, reliable, and explainable planning systems for autonomous vehicles that can handle complex, interactive scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DiffuSearch stems from an identified weakness in current hybrid trajectory planning architectures for autonomous driving. Specifically, the authors observe that:

1. **Objective Discrepancy in Hybrid Planners:** Many state-of-the-art hybrid planners divide the planning task into distinct modules (e.g., initial proposal generation and subsequent refinement). These modules frequently operate with their own, often disparate, sets of objectives. This lack of a unifying principle can lead to inconsistencies where the refinement stage deviates significantly from the intent of the initial proposal, rather than performing a continuous and coherent improvement. Such radical changes can result in suboptimal or undesirable driving behaviors.

2. **Need for Joint Prediction and Planning:** An ideal planner would model bidirectional interactions between agents by repeatedly interleaving prediction and planning. However, this approach is computationally prohibitive. A strong practical alternative is a joint prediction and planning model, which implicitly captures these interactions within a single, unified step. The authors aim to extend this concept to a two-stage hybrid architecture, ensuring that the initial generation captures scene consistency before refinement.

3. **Limitations of Implicit Guidance in Diffusion Models:** While diffusion models can generate plausible trajectories and be implicitly steered by differentiable guidance functions (e.g., for safety or comfort), this implicit guidance does not provide formal guarantees of constraint compliance. For safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, explicit constraint checks and guarantees are essential. This necessitates a subsequent refinement stage.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the DiffuSearch work are:

1. **Introduce a Unified Set of Driving Objectives:** The central goal is to design a planning framework where a shared set of driving objectives guides *all* components. These objectives are:
 - **Collision Avoidance:** Preventing contact with other agents or static obstacles.
 - **Drivable Area Compliance:** Ensuring the ego vehicle remains within designated drivable regions.
 - **Comfort:** Minimizing uncomfortable maneuvers (e.g., excessive jerk).
 - **Progress:** Encouraging efficient movement towards the destination by maintaining a target speed.
 This unification is intended to foster a consistent and continuous refinement process, aligning the "intent" of the initial proposal with the "optimization" of the refinement.

2. **Combine Guided Diffusion with MCTS Refinement:** Develop a two-stage architecture where:
 - A guided diffusion model generates a scene-consistent, joint trajectory prediction for the ego vehicle and surrounding agents. This model uses the unified driving objectives as differentiable guidance functions to implicitly steer the denoising process, generating an initial proposal that is already biased towards desired behaviors.
 - A Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) performs an explicit, local refinement of the ego trajectory proposal in a discretized action space. Crucially, the MCTS leverages the *exact same* driving objectives as its reward function, ensuring that the refinement process optimizes for the same goals that guided the initial generation.

3. **Leverage Synergistic Strengths:** The design aims to combine the diffusion model's capacity for finding multi-modal, scene-consistent solutions (providing a strong, high-quality prior) with the MCTS's ability to perform focused, explainable, and constraint-aware optimization. The strong prior from diffusion is intended to make the MCTS computationally efficient by confining its search to a relevant region of the action space.

4. **Demonstrate Competitive and State-of-the-Art Performance:** Evaluate the proposed DiffuSearch model on challenging reactive closed-loop benchmarks (nuPlan and interPlan) to demonstrate its efficacy in reducing collisions, improving comfort, and generally achieving superior planning performance, particularly in complex, interactive scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffuSearch employs a two-stage hybrid architecture for trajectory planning, built on a unified set of driving objectives that govern both stages: joint prediction and explicit refinement.

#### 3.1. Joint Prediction via Guided Diffusion

The first stage generates a scene-consistent joint prediction of future trajectories for the ego vehicle and surrounding agents, serving as a prior for subsequent refinement.
- **Model Architecture:** A Transformer-based Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM), specifically a DiT-architecture, is used. This model is trained via imitation learning on a large-scale trajectory dataset to learn the distribution of expert driving behavior.
- **Conditional Generation:** The model generates future trajectories conditioned on the current scene context, which includes map information, route data, static objects, ego state, and agents' history.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance:** During inference (denoising), the trajectory generation is implicitly steered by differentiable driving objectives. The score function is augmented with a weighted corrective term derived from these objectives:
 - $O(x_t) = O_{collision}(x_t) + O_{drivable}(x_t) + O_{progress}(x_t) + O_{comfort}(x_t)$
 - **Collision Avoidance ($O_{collision}$):** Penalizes proximity and overlap with other agents using an exponential penalty based on signed distance to bounding boxes.
 - **Drivable Area Compliance ($O_{drivable}$):** Penalizes ego poses outside the drivable area, calculated using a Euclidean Signed Distance Field.
 - **Progress ($O_{progress}$):** Penalizes squared deviations from a target speed, but only outside a defined tolerance range.
 - **Comfort ($O_{comfort}$):** Penalizes excessive longitudinal and lateral jerk (rate of change of acceleration) beyond specified thresholds.
- **Weighting:** Objectives are weighted to prioritize safety (collision, drivable area) over comfort and progress ($w_{collision} > w_{drivable} >> w_{progress} \approx w_{comfort}$).
- **Output:** The diffusion model generates an initial, scene-consistent joint trajectory proposal, with the ego trajectory biased towards satisfying the defined driving principles.

#### 3.2. MCTS in Action Space

The second stage is an MCTS planner that performs an explicit, interpretable refinement of the ego trajectory generated by the diffusion model. This search operates in a discretized action space of longitudinal (acceleration $\alpha$) and lateral (steering angle $\phi$) commands.
- **Search Space:** The MCTS explores a tree of future vehicle states and driving actions, represented by tuples $(\alpha, \phi)$. This action-space approach provides explainability and allows for explicit constraint enforcement.
- **Strong Prior:** The diffusion-generated ego trajectory serves as a strong prior for the MCTS. The prior probability $P(s, a)$ for selecting an action is modeled as a tight Gaussian distribution centered on the discrete action closest to the one prescribed by the diffusion proposal at that timestep. This focuses the MCTS search locally.
- **MCTS Steps:**
 - **Selection:** Starting from the current vehicle state (root node), the algorithm selects child nodes by maximizing the PUCT (Polynomial Upper Confidence bound for Trees) criterion. This balances exploration of new paths with exploitation of promising ones.
 - **Expansion:** When a leaf node is reached, new child nodes are created for all feasible actions from the discretized set. Feasibility is constrained by continuity, promoting comfort and physical plausibility.
 - **Simulation:** For each expanded leaf node, the scene state is updated.
 - **Ego Vehicle:** A kinematic bicycle model translates ego actions into new poses, ensuring physically plausible motion.
 - **Other Agents:** Other traffic agents' future positions are updated using the joint predictions from the diffusion model (at 10 Hz), providing a realistic and consistent world model for MCTS planning.
 - **Reward Function:** The value of a simulated trajectory is explicitly calculated as a reward $R(x_{ego})$ by summing the *same four driving objectives* used for diffusion guidance. Since the guidance functions are costs ($O \in [0, +\infty)$), the rewards are inverse ($R \propto -O \in (-\infty, 0]$). The same weights are applied.
 - **Backpropagation:** The computed reward is propagated back up the search path to the root node, updating visit counts $N(s, a)$ and mean action values $Q(s, a)$.
- **Final Trajectory Planning:** After a fixed computational budget (e.g., 256 search steps), the optimal ego trajectory is extracted by greedily following the child node with the highest visit count from the root. A kinematic bicycle model then transforms this optimal action sequence into the final ego trajectory. An emergency braking mechanism is included for safety, although infrequently activated due to frequent replanning.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets & Benchmarks:** nuPlan framework (Val14, Test14, Test14-hard splits) and interPlan lane-change benchmark (low, medium, high traffic densities).
- **Simulation:** Reactive closed-loop simulation (15s scenarios at 10 Hz) with both rule-based Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) agents and learned, reactive SMART agents.
- **Metrics:** R-score (IDM agents) and SR-score (SMART agents), normalized to, derived from collision count (NC), time-to-collision (TTC), drivable area compliance (DAC), progress, and comfort.
- **Baselines:** PDM-Hybrid, GameFormer, DTPP, PLUTO, DiffusionPlanner.
- **Shared Backbone:** DiffuSearch utilizes the same pretrained diffusion backbone as DiffusionPlanner for a fair comparison of the MCTS refinement and aligned objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DiffuSearch was conducted on the nuPlan and interPlan benchmarks, focusing on reactive closed-loop simulation performance and ablations of the proposed components.

#### 4.1. DiffuSearch Excels in Hard, Interactive Scenarios

- DiffuSearch achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) results on all SMART-reactive (SR) benchmarks and two out of three reactive (R) benchmarks on nuPlan.
- Performance gains were most pronounced in challenging, interactive traffic scenarios, particularly on the Test14-hard-SR split, where DiffuSearch improved performance by up to 1.82% over DiffusionPlanner and up to 4.92% over PDM-Hybrid.
- Qualitative analysis (Figure 3) demonstrated that DiffuSearch could correct collision-prone diffusion proposals, maintain larger safety margins, handle pedestrians, and proactively change lanes in critical situations, indicating robust local repair capabilities.
- On the interPlan lane-change benchmark, DiffuSearch consistently outperformed DiffusionPlanner across all traffic densities and agent types (IDM and SMART), with larger gains in medium and high-density traffic. This suggests its effectiveness in tightly coupled maneuvers requiring repeated local corrections.

#### 4.2. MCTS Refinement Consistently Improves Results, Aligned Objectives Provide Additional Gains

- **Ablation Study (Table 3):**
 - Classifier-free diffusion guidance *alone* did not improve planning performance and sometimes led to degradation (up to -2.1% on Test14-SR).
 - Adding MCTS refinement *alone* consistently improved performance (up to +1.11% on Test14-hard-SR) and never degraded it.
 - The combination of diffusion guidance and MCTS refinement (DiffuSearch) achieved the *best performance* across all data splits and benchmarks. This indicates a synergistic effect where MCTS leverages the "local hints" from guidance, smoothing inconsistencies.
- **Trajectory Consistency (Table 4):** DiffuSearch yielded substantially smaller deviations between initial and refined trajectories (in L2 position and speed) compared to PLUTO. This implies that the MCTS acts as a local repair mechanism, maintaining consistency with the initial diffusion proposal rather than replacing it.
- **Runtime Analysis (Figure 4):** The diffusion backbone dominated runtime for small MCTS budgets. The MCTS contribution grew linearly with search steps, and planning performance improved steadily, saturating around 256 search steps. This supports the design choice of using MCTS for focused local improvement around a strong prior.

#### 4.3. Unified Objectives Provide Additional Gains

- **Ablation Study (Table 5):**
 - Each individual objective component (collision, drivable, progress, comfort) contributed to overall performance.
 - Switching off MCTS reward components generally led to larger performance decreases than switching off diffusion guidance objectives. This is consistent with MCTS directly optimizing these rewards for the final plan.
 - The comfort reward had a minimal impact on overall performance, attributed to the kinematic bicycle model used in MCTS, which inherently promotes smooth and feasible trajectories.
 - The results confirm that using the same set of objectives for both diffusion guidance and MCTS reward yields the best overall planned trajectory.

#### 4.4. DiffuSearch Reduces Collisions and Boosts Comfort

- **Metric Breakdown (Table 6):**
 - **Collision Avoidance (NC, TTC):** DiffuSearch significantly improved safety-critical metrics, reducing the number of collisions (NC) and increasing time-to-collision (TTC) – implying larger safety margins.
 - **Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) & Progress:** These metrics remained competitive or slightly decreased. This outcome aligns with the defined objective prioritization, where collision avoidance is prioritized over strict lane adherence or maximizing progress in critical situations. The planner may swerve into shoulders or parking bays to avoid collisions.
 - **Comfort:** DiffuSearch considerably improved ride comfort (up to +18%), attributed to the kinematic model used during MCTS refinement, which promotes smoother maneuvers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DiffuSearch makes several significant contributions to the field of autonomous driving, with notable potential impacts:

1. **Addresses a Core Problem in Hybrid Planners:** The work directly tackles the prevalent issue of inconsistency arising from disparate objectives in multi-stage hybrid planning architectures. By proposing a unified set of driving objectives across both generative proposal and explicit refinement stages, DiffuSearch offers a principled solution for achieving more coherent and continuous trajectory planning.

2. **Demonstrates Synergistic Benefits of Combined Architectures:** It provides empirical evidence that combining a powerful generative model (guided diffusion) with a robust search-based refiner (MCTS) can lead to superior performance. The diffusion model provides a high-quality, scene-consistent prior, while MCTS performs targeted, constraint-aware local optimization, creating a mutually beneficial relationship.

3. **Enhances Safety and Comfort in Complex Scenarios:** The model achieves competitive and often state-of-the-art results on challenging reactive closed-loop benchmarks, particularly in hard and interactive traffic scenarios. This is critical for autonomous driving systems, which must reliably navigate unpredictable environments. The observed improvements in collision avoidance (reduced NC, increased TTC) and ride comfort are directly impactful for the safety and user acceptance of autonomous vehicles.

4. **Improves Explainability and Trustworthiness:** By using MCTS in a discretized action space and explicitly evaluating driving objectives as rewards, DiffuSearch retains a degree of interpretability often lacking in purely end-to-end deep learning models. This explicit reasoning about actions and constraints contributes to the trustworthiness of the planning system, which is crucial for regulatory approval and public acceptance.

5. **Provides a Foundation for Future Research:** The findings, particularly the complex interplay between implicit guidance and explicit search, offer insights for future hybrid planner designs. The paper itself outlines future directions such as incorporating error feedback mechanisms for temporal consistency and handling multi-modal proposals, suggesting avenues for developing even more robust and intelligent autonomous driving systems.

In summary, DiffuSearch represents a step towards more reliable and performant autonomous driving systems by offering a method that harmonizes the strengths of modern generative models with established search techniques through a unified objective framework. Its demonstrated ability to navigate complex, interactive scenarios with improved safety and comfort metrics indicates its relevance for the advancement of autonomous vehicle technology.
