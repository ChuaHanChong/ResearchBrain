# No Prompt Left Behind: Exploiting Zero-Variance Prompts in LLM Reinforcement Learning via Entropy-Guided Advantage Shaping

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST and EPFL developed Reinforcement Learning with Zero-Variance Prompts (RL-ZVP), an algorithm that extracts meaningful learning signals from prompts where all generated responses yield identical rewards, often disregarded in existing LLM reinforcement learning frameworks. RL-ZVP consistently outperformed prior methods, achieving average gains of 2.84 to 5.15 points in Acc@8 and 3.95 to 4.62 points in Pass@8 on math reasoning benchmarks, while promoting more sophisticated reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs, such as GRPO, typically disregard 'zero-variance prompts' where all sampled responses yield identical rewards (either all correct or all incorrect).
- This leads to 'advantage vanishing,' where normalized reward values collapse to zero, eliminating learning signals and wasting up to 50% of computational resources spent on generating uninformative rollouts.
- Prior prompt-filtering approaches acknowledge the inefficiency but still discard these prompts, failing to extract and utilize any potential learning signals from them.

## Method

- Introduces Reinforcement Learning with ==Zero-Variance Prompts== (**RL-ZVP**), an algorithm that generalizes the GRPO framework by providing a specialized update mechanism for zero-variance prompts.
- Categorizes zero-variance prompts into 'positive' (all correct responses) and 'negative' (all incorrect responses).
- Applies an entropy-guided, token-level advantage shaping: positive advantage for correct responses (scaled proportionally to token entropy) and negative advantage for incorrect responses (scaled inversely to token entropy) to preserve exploration flexibility.

## Results

- RL-ZVP consistently outperformed GRPO and prompt-filtering baselines across six math reasoning benchmarks, achieving average gains of 2.84-5.15 Acc@8 and 3.95-4.62 Pass@8 for **1.7B** and **8B** Qwen3 models, even with fewer rollouts than filtering methods.
- Demonstrated superior training stability and consistency compared to GRPO, maintaining smooth performance curves and avoiding performance drops, attributed to denser and more consistent feedback across training stages.
- Fostered more sophisticated reasoning behaviors, including longer, more confident, and systematically planned solutions, and showed improved cross-domain generalization on out-of-domain benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Zero-variance prompts, previously assumed to be useless and discarded, contain valuable learning signals that can be effectively exploited for LLM policy optimization.
> - Token-level entropy can be used to modulate the magnitude of advantage, providing nuanced feedback that goes beyond simple sample-level correctness for zero-variance prompts.
> - An asymmetric advantage formulation, where high-entropy tokens are rewarded more for correct responses and penalized less for incorrect responses, is crucial for stable and effective learning from zero-variance data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has demonstrated enhanced reasoning capabilities, often through methods like Chain of Thought prompting. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a prominent framework for further strengthening these reasoning abilities during LLM post-training. Existing RLVR techniques, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO), typically operate by sampling multiple responses (rollouts) to a given prompt and then reinforcing correct answers while penalizing incorrect ones.

However, a known limitation in current RLVR methods is their handling of "zero-variance prompts." These are prompts for which all sampled responses in a group yield identical rewards—meaning either all responses are correct or all are incorrect. In such scenarios, the normalized reward values, often referred to as advantage values, collapse to zero. This collapse results in no learning signal being generated for these prompts, rendering the associated rollouts uninformative for policy optimization.

Previous research has acknowledged the inefficiency posed by zero-variance prompts. Some approaches attempt to filter out these prompts, either before rollout generation (e.g., GRESO) or after rollouts have been generated (e.g., GRPO with Dynamic Sampling, DAPO). These filtering methods aim to reduce the computational overhead associated with generating uninformative rollouts.

This paper challenges the prevailing assumption that zero-variance prompts are entirely useless. It positions itself as the first work to investigate and propose a method for actively extracting valuable learning signals from these prompts, rather than simply discarding them. By doing so, the work aims to improve the data efficiency and overall effectiveness of RLVR training for LLMs, particularly given that rollout generation can constitute a significant portion (approximately 50%) of the total training time per step. The proposed method generalizes existing RLVR approaches by providing a non-trivial update mechanism for zero-variance prompts, thereby expanding the utility of generated data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to demonstrate that zero-variance prompts, typically disregarded in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks, can, in fact, provide meaningful learning signals for Large Language Model (LLM) policy optimization.

This objective is motivated by several key observations and challenges within the current RLVR landscape:

- **Prevalence of Zero-Variance Prompts:** The authors observed that zero-variance prompts are not uncommon; they can account for a substantial portion, ranging from approximately 30% to 99%, of each training batch. This high frequency implies that a significant amount of generated data is potentially wasted if these prompts are ignored. The paper notes that these prompts often arise in two distinct scenarios: at the beginning of training when the model struggles to solve problems (leading to all-incorrect responses) and near convergence when the model consistently solves many problems (leading to all-correct responses).
- **Computational Inefficiency of Discarding Rollouts:** Rollout generation, the process of sampling multiple responses for a prompt, is identified as a major computational bottleneck in RL training for LLMs, consuming up to 50% of the total time per training step. Discarding rollouts generated for zero-variance prompts represents a substantial waste of this expensive computation. Current filtering methods, while addressing the *selection* of prompts, still imply that the effort of generating these rollouts is unproductive if they are ultimately ignored.
- **Advantage Vanishing in GRPO:** The standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective calculates advantage values by normalizing rewards within a group of responses. If all responses for a given prompt receive identical rewards (i.e., zero variance), the standard deviation becomes zero, causing the advantage values to collapse to zero. This "advantage vanishing" problem means that zero-variance prompts contribute no gradient signal to the policy optimization, effectively rendering them uninformative within the GRPO framework.
- **Limitations of Existing Filtering Methods:** While previous work acknowledges the inefficiency of zero-variance prompts and attempts to filter them out (either before or after rollout generation), no prior research has explored the possibility of extracting *useful learning signals* directly from these prompts. The existing paradigm assumes these prompts are inherently uninformative for policy optimization.
- **Intuition for Policy Optimization:** The authors propose that even without contrasting responses within a group, a policy model should still receive feedback. Specifically, it should be rewarded for correct answers (even if all sampled answers are correct) and penalized for incorrect answers (even if all sampled answers are incorrect). This intuition suggests that the direction of the reward/penalty can be determined by the correctness, and the magnitude can be modulated by token-level characteristics.

By addressing these points, the research aims to unlock a previously untapped source of learning signals, leading to more effective and stable RL training for LLMs while maximizing the utility of generated rollouts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce Reinforcement Learning with Zero-Variance Prompts (RL-ZVP), a novel algorithm designed to exploit learning signals from prompts where all sampled responses yield identical rewards. RL-ZVP generalizes the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework by modifying its behavior for these specific "zero-variance prompts."

**Core Idea:**
RL-ZVP operates identically to GRPO for non-zero-variance prompts. However, when encountering a zero-variance prompt (where all responses are either correct or all are incorrect), instead of discarding the learning signal, RL-ZVP applies a specialized advantage formulation.

**Classification of Zero-Variance Prompts:**
Zero-variance prompts are categorized into two types:
1. **Positive Prompts:** All sampled responses are correct.
2. **Negative Prompts:** All sampled responses are incorrect.

**Entropy-Guided Advantage Shaping for Zero-Variance Prompts:**
The core of RL-ZVP lies in its novel token-level advantage formulation, which focuses on two properties: direction and magnitude.

- **Advantage Direction:**
 - For **positive prompts** (all correct responses), the advantage is positive (+). The intuition is to reinforce the policy's certainty in correct answers and encourage exploitation of successful paths.
 - For **negative prompts** (all incorrect responses), the advantage is negative (−). The intuition is to reduce the probabilities of these incorrect responses, discouraging repetition of mistakes and promoting exploration of alternative paths.

- **Advantage Magnitude (Entropy Scaling):**
 The magnitude of the advantage is determined by the entropy of individual tokens within a response, leveraging token-level characteristics to provide nuanced feedback.
 - For **positive prompts** (all correct responses), the advantage magnitude is proportional to the token's entropy (H\_i,t). High-entropy tokens, which are often crucial for complex reasoning (e.g., connectors, branching points), receive larger positive updates. This encourages the model to refine its reasoning processes and avoid over-exploitation of trivial text completion. The entropy (H\_i,t) is defined as:
 H\_i,t = - Σ\_(j=1)^|V| π\_θ(v\_j | q, o\_i,<t) log(π\_θ(v\_j | q, o\_i,<t))
 where `π_θ(v_j | q, o_i,<t)` is the probability of vocabulary token `v_j` at token position `t` given the query `q` and preceding tokens `o_i,<t`.
 - For **negative prompts** (all incorrect responses), the advantage magnitude is inversely proportional to the token's entropy, using `max(|o_i|)_k=1 (H_i,k) - H_i,t`. This means high-entropy tokens are penalized *less severely*. The rationale is to preserve flexibility for the model to re-explore reasoning paths that might have led to high-entropy tokens, even if the current attempt was incorrect. Penalizing high-entropy tokens less preserves potential avenues for future successful exploration, rather than aggressively pruning them.

The full token-level advantage formula for zero-variance prompts is:
`Â_ZVP(o_i,t) = αH_i,t` if all `R_i > 0` (positive prompt)
`Â_ZVP(o_i,t) = -α (max(|o_i|)_k=1 (H_i,k) - H_i,t)` if all `R_i <= 0` (negative prompt)
where `α` is a scaling factor hyperparameter, and `H_i,t` is detached from the computational graph to act as a scalar.

**Final Objective:**
The RL-ZVP objective is defined as a conditional branching formulation. If a prompt is a zero-variance prompt (q ∈ D\_ZVP), the advantage term uses `Â_ZVP(o_i,t)`. Otherwise, it reverts to the standard GRPO objective using `Â_i,t`. This structure ensures that RL-ZVP is a direct generalization of GRPO, providing non-trivial updates where GRPO would yield zero.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** Qwen3-1.7B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base were used to assess performance across model scales. Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct was used to test generalization across model families.
- **Datasets:** Training was conducted on the MATH dataset (small scale, 7.5k problems) and DAPO-Math-17k (large scale, more challenging). An additional 20k-prompt subset of NuminaMath-CoT was used for further validation.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Six standard math reasoning benchmarks were used for in-domain evaluation: Minerva, AMC23, MATH, AIME24, AIME25, and OlympiadBench. Models generated eight responses per question, and results were reported as Acc@8 (accuracy) and Pass@8 (pass rate). Out-of-domain evaluation included LiveCodeBench (coding), MMLU-Pro (general knowledge), GPQA-Diamond (scientific reasoning), and BIG-Bench Hard (logical reasoning).
- **Baselines:** RL-ZVP was compared against GRPO and two prompt-filtering methods: GRPO with Dynamic Sampling (GRPO-DS) and GRESO. For filtering baselines, results were reported under two settings: (r) same number of rollouts as RL-ZVP, and (g) same number of gradient steps (which typically allows filtering baselines 3-5 times more rollouts).
- **Implementation Details:** All experiments used the `verl` framework. Standard hyperparameters were adopted, including a batch size of 512, mini-batch size of 32, 8 or 16 samples per prompt, and specific clip ratios. The `α` parameter for RL-ZVP was empirically set to 0.10.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies were conducted on the small-scale setting (Qwen3-1.7B-Base trained with MATH) to assess:
- The individual contributions of negative prompts, positive prompts, and the entropy scaling factor in the advantage formulation.
- The impact of the `α` scaling factor across a range of values.
- The effectiveness of the asymmetric advantage formulation by comparing it to a symmetric variant.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness of RL-ZVP:

- **Superior Performance over GRPO:** RL-ZVP consistently achieved significant improvements over GRPO across all six math reasoning benchmarks and both model scales. On Qwen3-1.7B-Base (small scale), average gains were 2.84 points in Acc@8 and 4.62 points in Pass@8. For Qwen3-8B-Base (large scale), average gains were 5.15 points in Acc@8 and 3.95 points in Pass@8. Individual improvements reached up to 8.61 points in Acc@8 (OlympiadBench) and 7.77 points in Pass@8 (AIME25). These results indicate that utilizing zero-variance prompts provides valuable learning signals that enhance reasoning performance.

- **Outperformance of Prompt-Filtering Baselines:** RL-ZVP consistently surpassed prompt-filtering baselines (GRPO-DS and GRESO).
 - Under a fair comparison (same rollout budget, 'r' setting), RL-ZVP significantly outperformed GRPO-DS-r and GRESO-r across all benchmarks, with average gains of 3.28 Acc@8 / 4.03 Pass@8 (small scale) and 19.20 Acc@8 / 13.56 Pass@8 (large scale).
 - Even under disadvantageous settings where filtering baselines were allocated 3-5 times more rollouts ('g' setting), RL-ZVP still outperformed them on the majority of benchmarks, achieving average improvements of 0.99 Acc@8 / 1.19 Pass@8 (small scale) and 5.51 Acc@8 / 5.32 Pass@8 (large scale) over their best performance. This finding suggests that actively exploiting zero-variance prompts is more beneficial than simply discarding them.

- **Enhanced Training Stability and Consistency:** RL-ZVP demonstrated more stable and consistent learning dynamics compared to GRPO. On the small scale, RL-ZVP showed a clear performance boost after approximately 500 gradient steps. On the large scale, GRPO experienced a sharp performance drop early in training, while RL-ZVP maintained a smooth and steadily improving curve. This robustness is attributed to RL-ZVP's ability to leverage abundant negative prompts (all-incorrect) in early training and more positive prompts (all-correct) later, providing denser and more consistent feedback.

- **Promotion of Sophisticated Reasoning Behaviors:**
 - **Longer and More Confident Reasoning:** RL-ZVP-trained models exhibited a steady decrease in entropy and an increase in response length during training, correlating with validation gains. This suggests the emergence of more extensive and confident long-form reasoning.
 - **Qualitative Improvements:** Models trained with RL-ZVP displayed more sophisticated and systematic reasoning strategies. They demonstrated behaviors such as restating hypotheses, interpreting clues, step-by-step planning, organizing solutions, and principled exploration of alternative strategies. These advanced behaviors were observed even in smaller models (1.7B) trained with RL-ZVP, whereas GRPO-trained models at the same scale often produced simpler, less structured solutions or shallow trial-and-error attempts. On the 8B scale, RL-ZVP outputs were more flexible and exploratory than GRPO's rigid and repetitive tone.

- **Ablation Study Results:**
 - **Importance of Entropy Scaling:** Removing the entropy scaling factor (i.e., using only sample-level +1/-1 advantages) resulted in the most severe performance degradation, indicating that the magnitude shaping with entropy is crucial for effective learning from zero-variance prompts. Simply rewarding correctness and penalizing errors without nuanced weighting is insufficient.
 - **Contribution of Positive and Negative Prompts:** Ignoring either positive or negative zero-variance prompts individually led to comparable performance drops, suggesting both types contribute significantly to the learning signal.
 - **Optimal Alpha (α) Scaling Factor:** The scaling factor `α` was found to be critical. Optimal performance was achieved with `α = 0.10` or `α = 0.20`. Values that were too small (`α = 0.05` or less) attenuated the learning signal, while excessively large values (`α = 0.30` or more) led to unstable training and performance degradation.
 - **Asymmetric Advantage Formulation:** A symmetric variant of RL-ZVP, where negative prompt advantage was simply the inverse of positive prompt advantage, performed worse than both the full RL-ZVP and even the GRPO baseline. This supports the design choice of an asymmetric advantage formulation, where high-entropy tokens are penalized less severely in negative prompts.

- **Generalization Across Datasets and Model Families:** RL-ZVP's effectiveness was validated across different training datasets (NuminaMath-CoT) and extended to a different model family (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct), consistently outperforming GRPO and baselines. Furthermore, models trained with RL-ZVP showed noticeable cross-domain gains on out-of-domain benchmarks, including coding, general knowledge, scientific, and logical reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant shift in the approach to handling zero-variance prompts in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs). By demonstrating that these prompts can be a valuable source of learning signals rather than computational waste, the work challenges a prevailing assumption in the field.

The potential impacts and significance include:

- **Improved Data Efficiency:** The primary impact is the enhanced utilization of generated rollouts. Since rollout generation is a significant bottleneck in RL training for LLMs, leveraging all generated data, including zero-variance prompts, leads to more efficient use of computational resources and training time. This mitigates the "useless rollout costs" associated with discarding such prompts.
- **Enhanced LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across multiple math reasoning benchmarks and model scales indicate that RL-ZVP effectively improves the reasoning abilities of LLMs. The qualitative analysis further supports this, showing that RL-ZVP fosters more sophisticated, systematic, and confident long-form reasoning behaviors, even in smaller models.
- **Greater Training Stability:** RL-ZVP's ability to provide a more consistent learning signal across different training stages, particularly by leveraging all-incorrect responses early on and all-correct responses later, contributes to a more stable and robust training process. This reduces the risk of performance collapses observed in baselines.
- **New Direction for RLVR Research:** By establishing the utility of zero-variance prompts, this work opens a new avenue for research in RLVR. Future efforts can explore more advanced formulations for extracting signals from these prompts, potentially leading to further performance improvements.
- **Advancement in Fine-grained Reward Shaping:** The success of entropy-guided advantage shaping highlights the importance of token-level characteristics in modulating learning signals. This approach can be integrated with or inspire further developments in fine-grained reward shaping methods, moving beyond coarse-grained, sample-level advantages.
- **Generalizability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across different model scales, training datasets, model families, and even on out-of-domain reasoning tasks suggests broad applicability of the RL-ZVP approach, indicating its potential to benefit a wide range of LLM applications.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including experiments being constrained to models up to 8B parameters, suggesting future work on larger models (e.g., 14B or 32B). The current work focuses on verifiable tasks with binary rewards, and extending RL-ZVP to settings with graded or ambiguous feedback remains an open challenge. Finally, combining the proposed advantage formulation with existing token-level reward shaping methods or generalizing it beyond zero-variance prompts could be promising avenues for future research.
