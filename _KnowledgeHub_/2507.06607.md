# Decoder-Hybrid-Decoder Architecture for Efficient Reasoning with Long Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The SambaY architecture, integrating a novel Gated Memory Unit (GMU), improves Large Language Model (LLM) decoding efficiency for long generation tasks by sharing State Space Model (SSM) representations. This design significantly reduces attention memory I/O costs, leading to up to 10x higher decoding throughput for 32K-length generations and demonstrating superior performance scalability on complex reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing hybrid LLM architectures suffer from high attention memory I/O costs during the generation stage, particularly for long Chain-of-Thought responses.
- Traditional Transformer architectures struggle with efficiency and context extrapolation for increasingly long sequences, while pure SSMs lack the same theoretical in-context retrieval expressiveness.
- Developing an architecture that combines the efficiency of SSMs with the expressiveness of Transformers for effective long-context reasoning remains challenging.

## Method

- Introduces the **Gated Memory Unit** (GMU), a lightweight mechanism for inter-layer memory sharing that adaptively reweights previous token mixing operations.
- Proposes SambaY, a decoder-hybrid-decoder architecture that replaces half of the cross-attention layers in YOCO with GMUs, sharing internal State Space Model (SSM) representations from the self-decoder.
- Utilizes a μP++ hyperparameter transfer scheme and iso-parametric equations to enable fair scaling comparisons and maintain linear pre-filling complexity without explicit positional encodings.

## Results

- SambaY achieved the lowest irreducible loss (**0.58**) in FLOPs scaling experiments, indicating better long-term performance scalability compared to Transformer++.
- The Phi4-mini-Flash-Reasoning model (**3.8B** parameters) demonstrated up to **10x** higher decoding throughput for **32K**-length generations and a **4.9x** speedup in long-context processing.
- SambaY variants consistently outperformed pure Transformer architectures in long-context retrieval tasks and showed robust performance on reasoning benchmarks like Math500 and GPQA Diamond.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Gated Memory Unit (GMU) reduces decoding memory I/O complexity from O(d_kvN) to a constant O(d_h) by efficiently reusing SSM kernel output states.
> - Proper normalization (RMSNorm after output gating) and the use of normalized GMU (nGMU) are critical for training stability and robust long-context retrieval performance in linear-attention-based models.
> - Hybrid architectures like SambaY exhibit superior long-term performance scalability with lower irreducible loss and allow for effective zero-shot context extrapolation up to 2× without explicit positional encodings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the domain of efficient sequence modeling, particularly focusing on large language models (LLMs) and their performance on long-context tasks. Recent advancements have highlighted State Space Models (SSMs) as a computationally efficient alternative or complement to the Transformer architecture, which has dominated the field. While pure SSMs/RNNs offer linear computational complexity, they have been observed to possess a theoretical expressiveness gap in in-context retrieval compared to Transformers.

To bridge this gap, hybrid architectures have emerged, combining the strengths of both SSMs/RNNs and Transformers. Examples include Samba, which interleaves Mamba with Sliding Window Attention (SWA), and YOCO (You Only Cache Once), a decoder-decoder architecture that achieves linear complexity for long context processing by caching Key-Value (KV) pairs from a single full-attention layer in the self-decoder for reuse across subsequent cross-attention layers in the cross-decoder.

However, existing hybrid models, including YOCO, still face challenges. Specifically, YOCO does not fully mitigate the attention memory I/O cost for its cross-attentions during the generation stage of model responses, a limitation that becomes more pronounced with the increasingly long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generations required for complex reasoning tasks in modern LLMs. This paper aims to address this specific inefficiency by exploring novel mechanisms for representation sharing across SSM layers within hybrid architectures, seeking to further enhance decoding efficiency without compromising performance, especially for long-context and reasoning applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in existing hybrid language model architectures, specifically concerning decoding efficiency during long generation tasks. While architectures like YOCO have made strides in achieving linear pre-filling complexity for long contexts, the attention memory I/O cost associated with its cross-attention layers during the *generation* stage remains a significant bottleneck. This inefficiency is particularly critical for modern LLMs that produce extensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT) responses for complex reasoning problems, where long generation lengths lead to substantial computational overheads. The paper identifies an untapped potential in representation sharing between SSM layers to mitigate this issue.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Investigate and enhance decoding efficiency through representation sharing:** The central objective is to explore mechanisms that allow SSM layers to share internal representations, thereby reducing redundant computations and memory accesses during the decoding phase.
2. **Introduce a novel memory sharing mechanism:** To achieve the first objective, the authors aim to propose a simple yet effective mechanism, the Gated Memory Unit (GMU), designed for efficient memory sharing across different layers of a neural network.
3. **Develop a new decoder-hybrid-decoder architecture (SambaY):** The paper intends to integrate the proposed GMU into a hybrid architecture, specifically by applying it to the cross-decoder of YOCO, using Samba as the self-decoder. This new architecture, named SambaY, is designed to reduce decoding complexity by replacing computationally expensive cross-attention layers with GMUs.
4. **Preserve linear pre-filling time complexity and boost long-context performance:** SambaY is engineered to maintain the linear pre-filling time complexity benefits of YOCO while simultaneously improving performance on long-context tasks and eliminating the need for explicit positional encoding.
5. **Demonstrate superior scalability and practical efficiency for reasoning tasks:** The research seeks to empirically validate that SambaY exhibits a lower irreducible loss in scaling experiments, indicating better performance scalability under large compute regimes. Furthermore, a large-scale prototype model based on this architecture (Phi4-mini-Flash-Reasoning) is developed to demonstrate significant practical gains in decoding throughput for long-generation reasoning tasks, outperforming strong baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the introduction and application of the Gated Memory Unit (GMU) within a novel hybrid architecture called SambaY, alongside a rigorous experimental evaluation framework.

**Gated Memory Unit (GMU)**
The GMU is defined as a mechanism for inter-layer memory sharing. At a given layer `l`, it takes the current layer's input hidden state, $X_l \in R^{n \times d_m}$, and a mixed representation $M^{(l')} \in R^{n \times d_h}$ from a preceding layer $l'$ (where $l' < l$). The output $Y_l \in R^{n \times d_m}$ is produced via a gating mechanism:
$Y_l = (M^{(l')} \odot \sigma(X_l W_1^T))W_2$
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the SiLU activation, $\odot$ is element-wise multiplication, and $W_1, W_2 \in R^{d_h \times d_m}$ are learnable weight matrices. An optional RMSNorm layer after the element-wise multiplication yields the normalized GMU (nGMU), which is shown to be crucial for memory originating from linear attention mechanisms to maintain training stability. The GMU effectively reweights the token mixing operation of a previous layer, conditioned on the current layer's input, creating a channel-specific adaptation of the original token-mixing operator. This approach reduces both parameter count and computational cost compared to standard attention or SSM layers.

**SambaY Architecture**
SambaY adopts a decoder-hybrid-decoder architecture, building upon the YOCO framework. It utilizes Samba for its self-decoder (the first half of layers), which comprises SSM layers (Mamba) interleaved with Sliding Window Attention (SWA), and a final full-attention layer. The key innovation lies in the cross-decoder (the second half of layers), where half of the cross-attention layers from the original YOCO design are replaced with GMUs. These GMUs are configured to share the inner representations (specifically, the final SSM kernel output state $m \in R^{d_h}$) from the last Mamba layer in the self-decoder. This design allows SambaY to maintain linear pre-filling time complexity by only caching the KV pairs from the full-attention layer and a negligible additional SSM kernel output state. During decoding, GMUs reduce the memory I/O complexity for half of the cross-decoder layers from $O(d_{kv}N)$ to a constant $O(d_h)$, where N is sequence length and $d_{kv}$ is key/value dimension, leading to efficiency gains for $N \gg d_h/d_{kv}$. SambaY also operates without explicit positional encodings.

**Scaling Experiments Setup**
To ensure fair architectural comparisons, the authors develop a $\mu$P++ hyperparameter transfer scheme. This scheme extends existing $\mu$P and Depth-$\mu$P laws by accounting for both depth and width scaling, and applying zero weight decay to vector-like or scalar-like parameters for training stability. The learning rate $\eta$ is scaled as $\eta \propto \sqrt{B/d}$, where B is batch size and d is model depth. The Chinchilla scaling law is adopted for training tokens, scaling $T$ linearly with the number of model parameters $N(d)$. Iso-parametric equations are used to align the total number of non-embedding parameters across different hybrid architectures and the Transformer++ baseline, allowing for a principled comparison of scaling behaviors. Models are trained on the SlimPajama dataset for up to 3.4B parameters and 600B tokens, using a 4K training sequence length. Performance is assessed by fitting validation loss to a power law $L(D_{FLOPs}) = A \cdot D^{-b}_{FLOPs} + C$ to estimate irreducible loss $C$ and scaling exponent $b$.

**Baselines and Variants**
The study compares SambaY against several baselines:
- **Transformer++:** A standard Transformer with SwiGLU and RoPE.
- **Samba+YOCO:** A naive combination of Samba as a self-decoder with the original YOCO cross-decoder.
- **SWA+YOCO:** Original YOCO with SWA layers in the self-decoder.
- **TransformerLS:** Transformer interleaving SWA with full attention (3:1 ratio).
- **SambaY+DA:** SambaY augmented with Differential Attention for all attention layers.

**Long-Context Retrieval Experiments**
Models (1.0B parameters) are pre-trained on the ProLong-64k dataset (32K sequence length, 40B tokens) with variable-length training. Long-context retrieval capabilities are evaluated using the Phonebook benchmark (32K context length, 1,850 name-number pairs) and the RULER Needle-In-A-Haystack tasks. Sliding Window Attention (SWA) sizes are varied from 64 to 2048 to determine optimal configurations.

**Large-Scale Pre-training for Reasoning**
The SambaY+DA architecture is used to pre-train a 3.8B-parameter prototype model named Phi4-mini-Flash. This model is trained on 5T tokens from the Phi4-mini data corpus. Stability issues during training are addressed by using label smoothing (0.1) and attention dropout (0.05) with FP32 up-casting for the fused linear cross-entropy loss kernel.
A reasoning model, Phi4-mini-Flash-Reasoning, is then continually trained from Phi4-mini-Flash through multi-stage distillation with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), without reinforcement learning. Its performance is evaluated on reasoning benchmarks such as Math500, AIME24/25, and GPQA Diamond, along with decoding throughput measurements using the vLLM inference framework.

**Ablation Studies**
Comprehensive ablations are conducted on 1.0B-parameter models trained on ProLong-64K. These studies investigate:
- **Alternative self-decoder architectures:** Replacing Mamba with Mamba-2 or Gated DeltaNet (GDN/GDN-A) in SambaY and pure SSM self-decoders (MambaY, MambaY-2, GDNY).
- **GMU effectiveness with other memory types:** SambaY-A (gating attention inner representations), SambaY-AA (removing cross-attention entirely and gating attention memory), and SambaY-MLP (gating MLP intermediate representations).
- **Impact of normalization placement and nGMU:** Examining the effect of normalization before versus after output gating and using nGMU versus GMU in linear-attention based models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings across scaling, long-context retrieval, and large-scale reasoning, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed SambaY architecture and the Gated Memory Unit (GMU).

**Scaling Experiments on Open-Source Data:**
- **Irreducible Loss:** SambaY exhibits the lowest irreducible loss (C = 0.58) in FLOPs scaling comparisons, indicating superior performance scalability under increasingly large computational resources. This suggests a better long-term scaling potential compared to Samba+YOCO and Transformer++.
- **Compute Efficiency Exponent:** Under the $\mu$P++ scaling scheme, all tested architectures (SambaY, Samba+YOCO, Transformer++) shared a similar compute efficiency exponent ($b = 0.07$), implying that the hybrid architectures did not yield improvements in learning efficiency *with respect to compute* within the evaluated range.
- **$\mu$P++ Benefits:** The $\mu$P++ scaling laws generally resulted in a lower irreducible loss compared to Standard Parametrization (SP) under both data and compute scaling, suggesting more favorable scaling potentials for training stability and performance.
- **Data Scaling vs. FLOPs Scaling:** While Transformer++ with $\mu$P++ showed a large validation loss gap compared to hybrid models in data scaling within the measured range, its fitted irreducible loss was comparable to hybrid models. However, when both model size and data scaled proportionally (FLOPs scaling), Transformer++ exhibited the highest irreducible loss (C=0.64), indicating that hybrid architectures could offer superior scalability under limited data regimes.

**Efficient Long Context Retrieval:**
- **Optimal SWA Size:** Counter-intuitively, larger Sliding Window Attention (SWA) sizes did not consistently yield better results on the Phonebook benchmark. SambaY and SambaY+DA demonstrated strong performance with modest SWA sizes (e.g., 256 for SambaY, 512 for SambaY+DA), suggesting a focus on local patterns can be beneficial.
- **Hybrid vs. Transformer:** Hybrid models incorporating SSMs consistently outperformed pure Transformer architectures in long-context retrieval tasks. SambaY variants showed notable advantages, even with smaller SWA sizes compared to Samba+YOCO. The addition of Differential Attention (DA) further enhanced multi-key and single-needle retrieval capabilities for SambaY+DA.
- **NoPE Extrapolation:** SambaY and its variants utilizing NoPE (No Positional Encoding) demonstrated a capability to extrapolate their retrieval ability by $2\times$ in a zero-shot setting (e.g., from 32K to 64K context length on Phonebook), whereas RoPE-based models (Transformer++ and TransformerLS) experienced substantial performance drops beyond their trained context length.
- **Short-Context Performance:** SambaY variants maintained strong performance on traditional short-context language modeling and common-sense reasoning tasks (e.g., Wikitext, HellaSwag), indicating that long-context benefits do not compromise short-context abilities.

**Large-Scale Pre-training on High-quality Data:**
- **Phi4-mini-Flash Performance:** The 3.8B-parameter Phi4-mini-Flash model, based on SambaY+DA, outperformed the Phi4-mini baseline on 7 out of 8 diverse downstream benchmarks, including knowledge-intensive tasks like MMLU and coding tasks like MBPP, while maintaining significantly higher computational efficiency.
- **Training Stability:** Techniques such as label smoothing and attention dropout, coupled with FP32 up-casting for the fused linear cross-entropy loss kernel, were critical in mitigating loss divergence during large-scale pre-training.

**Efficient Reasoning with Long Generation:**
- **Reasoning Benchmark Performance:** Phi4-mini-Flash-Reasoning, despite being trained without a Reinforcement Learning (RL) stage (unlike Phi4-mini-Reasoning), achieved better performance on challenging reasoning benchmarks such as Math500, AIME24/25, and GPQA Diamond.
- **Decoding Throughput:** Critically, Phi4-mini-Flash-Reasoning delivered up to 10x higher decoding throughput on 2K-length prompts with 32K generation length, and a 4.9x speedup in long-context processing, under the vLLM inference framework. This highlights substantial practical efficiency gains for LLM reasoning requiring long Chain-of-Thought generations.
- **Case Studies:** The model exhibited advanced reasoning techniques in mathematical and daily-life reasoning case studies, including double-checking, reflection, and backtracking, even guessing user intent in some instances.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Self-Decoder Architecture:** Replacing Mamba with Mamba-2 in SambaY led to a significant drop in Phonebook accuracy, potentially due to Mamba-2's coarse forget gates reducing fine-grained positional information encoding. The importance of local retrieval from SWA was underscored by the poorer performance of pure MambaY on long-context tasks. GDN-based models showed impressive retrieval capabilities, with S-GDNY (interleaving GDN with SWA) offering a good balance of accuracy and training speed.
- **GMU Memory Source:** While GMUs were effective with attention and MLP intermediate representations, their performance on long-context retrieval tasks was worse than when gating SSM memories. This difference is attributed to the inherent recency bias provided by SSMs, which is beneficial for encoding contiguous local information. SambaY-AA, which entirely removed cross-attention, showed significant performance degradation, emphasizing the necessity of a sufficient number of cross-attention layers.
- **Normalization and nGMU:** The study confirmed that applying RMSNorm *after* the output gating in linear-attention layers and using the normalized GMU (nGMU) are critical for robust long-context retrieval performance. Shifting normalization before the gate or using simpler GMU without normalization led to severe degradation in Phonebook accuracy, while largely preserving short-context performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces the Gated Memory Unit (GMU) and the SambaY architecture, contributing to the field of efficient sequence modeling with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Decoding Efficiency:** The GMU offers a novel, computationally lightweight mechanism for memory sharing across layers, effectively reducing the memory I/O costs associated with attention layers during the critical generation phase. This directly addresses a bottleneck in current hybrid architectures like YOCO, making LLMs more efficient, especially for tasks requiring extensive outputs.
2. **Superior Scalability:** The empirical evidence from scaling experiments, showing SambaY's lower irreducible loss, suggests that this architecture possesses better long-term performance potential as computational resources increase. This insight is valuable for guiding the design of future large-scale language models.
3. **Improved Long-Context and Reasoning Capabilities:** SambaY, particularly its variant with Differential Attention (Phi4-mini-Flash-Reasoning), demonstrates competitive or superior performance on challenging long-context retrieval and reasoning benchmarks. This achievement, combined with significantly higher decoding throughput, points to the potential for more effective and practical LLMs in complex problem-solving scenarios that rely on extensive Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
4. **Architectural Flexibility and NoPE Benefits:** The work highlights the versatility of GMUs, showing their applicability beyond SSM memories, although with varying performance characteristics. The demonstration of zero-shot context extrapolation with NoPE for SambaY variants suggests a potential avenue for developing models that generalize better to unseen longer contexts without explicit positional encodings.
5. **Principled Scaling Methodology:** The introduction of the $\mu$P++ hyperparameter transfer scheme and the use of iso-parametric equations provide a more robust and fair framework for comparing the scaling behaviors of diverse neural architectures. This methodology can serve as a foundation for future architectural research, ensuring more accurate assessments of intrinsic model capabilities.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. By enabling more efficient reasoning with long generations, SambaY and similar architectures could:
- **Reduce operational costs** for deploying and running LLMs, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible.
- **Accelerate research and development** in LLMs by providing more performant and scalable foundational architectures.
- **Facilitate the creation of more capable AI agents** that can engage in longer, more complex reasoning chains, leading to breakthroughs in areas like scientific discovery, creative writing, and advanced problem-solving.

However, the authors also acknowledge limitations, such as the unexplored potential of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in hybrid architectures and the use of a generic optimization setup. Future work aims to explore dynamic sparse attention mechanisms to achieve constant decoding complexity for extremely long sequence generation, further pushing the boundaries of efficiency in agentic applications.
