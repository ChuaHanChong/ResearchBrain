# From None to All: Self-Supervised 3D Reconstruction via Novel View Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Imperial College London introduce NAS3R, a fully self-supervised feed-forward framework that jointly reconstructs explicit 3D Gaussians and predicts all camera parameters from multi-view images. The framework operates without ground-truth annotations or pretrained priors, demonstrating competitive performance in novel view synthesis, relative pose estimation, and depth estimation compared to existing self-supervised methods.

## Problem

- Traditional and deep learning 3D reconstruction methods often rely on extensive and costly 3D labeled datasets or explicit geometric correspondences, limiting scalability.
- Existing self-supervised novel view synthesis (NVS) methods face stability issues, frequently requiring pretrained priors or ground-truth camera intrinsics, which constrains their true self-supervision and applicability to uncalibrated data.
- Many self-supervised NVS approaches operate entirely in latent space, failing to produce explicit 3D geometry crucial for various downstream applications.

## Method

- **NAS3R**, a ==self-supervised feed-forward transformer framework==, is developed to jointly infer explicit ==3D Gaussians== and all camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) from multi-view images.
- The architecture employs a ==masked attention decoder== for controlled cross-view information exchange and uses a "local-to-global" approach where ==DPT-predicted depth maps== are lifted to 3D to establish Gaussian centers.
- The model is trained end-to-end using solely a ==photometric consistency loss== between rendered novel views and actual target images, eliminating the need for any ground-truth annotations or pretrained priors.

## Results

- NAS3R consistently outperforms other prior-free self-supervised methods across novel view synthesis, relative pose estimation, and depth estimation benchmarks, often achieving performance comparable to or surpassing supervised approaches.
- The framework demonstrates strong scalability, with performance improvements observed when trained on larger datasets and provided with more input views.
- Qualitative analyses show that NAS3R produces more coherent 3D Gaussian distributions, cleaner spatial organization, and sharper depth renderings than other methods, preserving fine scene details.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate explicit 3D geometry (Gaussians and depth) and all camera parameters can be learned in a fully self-supervised manner, driven solely by 2D photometric consistency.
> - Integrating an intermediate depth prediction step to determine Gaussian centers provides strong, physically grounded geometric constraints, ensuring stable and robust self-supervised convergence.
> - Self-supervised pretraining with NAS3R serves as an effective initialization strategy, significantly enhancing the performance of supervised models on downstream tasks through fine-tuning.
