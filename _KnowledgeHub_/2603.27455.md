# From None to All: Self-Supervised 3D Reconstruction via Novel View Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Imperial College London introduce NAS3R, a fully self-supervised feed-forward framework that jointly reconstructs explicit 3D Gaussians and predicts all camera parameters from multi-view images. The framework operates without ground-truth annotations or pretrained priors, demonstrating competitive performance in novel view synthesis, relative pose estimation, and depth estimation compared to existing self-supervised methods.

## Problem

- Traditional and deep learning 3D reconstruction methods often rely on extensive and costly 3D labeled datasets or explicit geometric correspondences, limiting scalability.
- Existing self-supervised novel view synthesis (NVS) methods face stability issues, frequently requiring pretrained priors or ground-truth camera intrinsics, which constrains their true self-supervision and applicability to uncalibrated data.
- Many self-supervised NVS approaches operate entirely in latent space, failing to produce explicit 3D geometry crucial for various downstream applications.

## Method

- **NAS3R**, a ==self-supervised feed-forward transformer framework==, is developed to jointly infer explicit ==3D Gaussians== and all camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) from multi-view images.
- The architecture employs a ==masked attention decoder== for controlled cross-view information exchange and uses a "local-to-global" approach where ==DPT-predicted depth maps== are lifted to 3D to establish Gaussian centers.
- The model is trained end-to-end using solely a ==photometric consistency loss== between rendered novel views and actual target images, eliminating the need for any ground-truth annotations or pretrained priors.

## Results

- NAS3R consistently outperforms other prior-free self-supervised methods across novel view synthesis, relative pose estimation, and depth estimation benchmarks, often achieving performance comparable to or surpassing supervised approaches.
- The framework demonstrates strong scalability, with performance improvements observed when trained on larger datasets and provided with more input views.
- Qualitative analyses show that NAS3R produces more coherent 3D Gaussian distributions, cleaner spatial organization, and sharper depth renderings than other methods, preserving fine scene details.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate explicit 3D geometry (Gaussians and depth) and all camera parameters can be learned in a fully self-supervised manner, driven solely by 2D photometric consistency.
> - Integrating an intermediate depth prediction step to determine Gaussian centers provides strong, physically grounded geometric constraints, ensuring stable and robust self-supervised convergence.
> - Self-supervised pretraining with NAS3R serves as an effective initialization strategy, significantly enhancing the performance of supervised models on downstream tasks through fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The simultaneous recovery of 3D structure from 2D images and the estimation of corresponding camera parameters constitutes a longstanding and fundamental challenge in computer vision. This problem is often characterized as a "chicken-and-egg" optimization dilemma: accurate 3D reconstruction typically requires reliable camera parameters, while precise camera estimation relies on consistent geometric correspondences derived from the 3D structure.

Historically, classical approaches such as Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Bundle Adjustment (BA) have addressed this through sequential and complex iterative optimization pipelines. More recently, feed-forward deep learning models, particularly those leveraging transformer architectures, have demonstrated the ability to directly learn dense 3D reconstruction from large-scale labeled 3D datasets. These data-driven methods aim to bypass much of the traditional geometric post-processing. However, a significant limitation of such supervised approaches is the high cost and labor intensity associated with collecting vast amounts of high-quality 3D ground-truth data, which constrains their scalability and generalizability.

To overcome the data dependency of supervised methods, self-supervised novel view synthesis (NVS) has emerged as an alternative. These methods learn 3D scene representations and camera poses without explicit 3D or pose annotations, primarily by enforcing photometric consistency between rendered novel views and target images. Despite this advancement, the inherent chicken-and-egg problem becomes more pronounced in the absence of ground-truth supervision, often leading to instability or convergence to degenerate solutions.

Existing self-supervised NVS methods have employed various strategies to address these challenges. Some approaches perform scene representation and rendering entirely in latent space, thereby circumventing explicit 3D optimization, though this may not infer transferable camera poses and can limit applicability to geometry-dependent downstream tasks. Other methods, building upon explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), frequently rely on pretrained geometric priors derived from supervised tasks. These priors are utilized either for initialization or for generating pseudo-labels, which helps stabilize optimization and ensure convergence. A further limitation in many existing pose-free self-supervised NVS methods is their continued reliance on ground-truth camera intrinsics during training. This dependency restricts their application to uncalibrated, "in-the-wild" imagery, hindering true scalability.

This work, NAS3R, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical gap of fully self-supervised 3D reconstruction. It aims to develop a system that can jointly learn explicit 3D geometry and camera poses entirely from multi-view images, without requiring any ground-truth annotations, pretrained priors, or ground-truth camera intrinsics during training. This represents a "From None to All" approach, seeking to eliminate all forms of explicit 3D supervision or strong prior information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce NAS3R, a self-supervised feed-forward framework designed to jointly learn explicit 3D geometry and camera parameters. This framework aims to achieve comprehensive 3D reconstruction from uncalibrated and unposed multi-view images, without requiring any ground-truth annotations or pretrained priors during its training phase.

The key motivations and detailed objectives underpinning the development of NAS3R are:

- **Eliminating Annotation and Prior Dependencies**: A central goal is to overcome the scalability limitations of existing 3D reconstruction methods. Supervised approaches are constrained by the scarcity and cost of high-quality 3D labeled data, while many self-supervised methods still depend on pretrained priors or ground-truth camera intrinsics. NAS3R seeks to remove all such dependencies, making it applicable to vast amounts of unconstrained, unlabeled image data.
- **Achieving Stable Convergence in Self-Supervised Settings**: The inherent "chicken-and-egg" problem in joint 3D geometry and camera estimation often leads to instability and degenerate solutions without ground-truth supervision. NAS3R addresses this by integrating 3D reconstruction and camera prediction within a shared transformer backbone, which is regulated by masked attention. Furthermore, it adopts a depth-based Gaussian formulation designed to facilitate a more physically grounded and well-conditioned optimization landscape, promoting stable and consistent convergence.
- **Joint Inference of All 3D Quantities**: The framework is designed to jointly infer multiple critical 3D quantities. This includes 3D Gaussian parameters (centers, rotations, scales, opacities, spherical harmonics coefficients), camera intrinsics, camera extrinsics (poses), and depth maps. Predicting these quantities from a shared backbone is intended to encourage consistent geometric reasoning across these interrelated tasks.
- **Enabling Novel View Synthesis (NVS)**: A core function of NAS3R is to facilitate high-quality novel view synthesis. The self-supervised training process is driven by photometric consistency between target views rendered with the model's self-predicted camera parameters and the actual target images.
- **Compatibility and Flexibility**: While aiming for a "None to All" approach, the framework is also designed to be compatible with existing state-of-the-art supervised transformer-based 3D reconstruction architectures. This ensures that if pretrained priors or intrinsic information become available, they can be seamlessly incorporated to potentially achieve further performance improvements, offering a practical pathway to scale supervised models to large-scale unlabeled datasets.
- **Generalizability to Uncalibrated Data**: By removing the requirement for ground-truth camera intrinsics, NAS3R is motivated to be more applicable to "in-the-wild" imagery where camera calibration information is often unavailable.

In essence, NAS3R's objectives are to establish a scalable, robust, and geometrically aware paradigm for 3D reconstruction that operates entirely from image-level self-supervision, thereby expanding the potential for 3D vision in unconstrained environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NAS3R operates as a self-supervised feed-forward framework that simultaneously reconstructs 3D Gaussian primitives and predicts corresponding camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) from unconstrained multi-view images. The entire system is trained end-to-end using photometric consistency supervision on novel target views rendered with the self-predicted camera parameters.

**Problem Formulation:**
The framework processes a set of input images, divided into context views ($I_C$) for 3D reconstruction and target views ($I_T$) for self-supervision.
1. **3D Gaussian Reconstruction**: A model $f_\theta$ reconstructs pixel-aligned Gaussians ($G_v$) from the context images. These Gaussians are expressed in the camera coordinate frame of a designated reference view (e.g., the first context view).
2. **Camera Prediction**: A camera predictor $f_\phi$ estimates both the intrinsic parameters ($K_v \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$) and the relative extrinsic parameters ($P_v = [R_v|T_v]$) for all input views (context and target) relative to the reference view. Camera poses are normalized, and intrinsics assume a shared field of view (FOV) along x and y axes with the principal point at the image center.
3. **Self-Supervised Novel View Synthesis (NVS)**: A differentiable rendering function $R$ utilizes the reconstructed Gaussians and the *self-predicted* camera parameters of the target views to generate rendered novel target views ($\hat{I}_T$).
4. **Training Objective**: The model is optimized by minimizing a photometric consistency loss ($L_{render}$) between the rendered target views ($\hat{I}_T$) and the ground-truth target images ($I_T$). This loss is a combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) and LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity), balancing pixel-level fidelity and perceptual quality.

**Architecture:**
The core of NAS3R is a standard transformer architecture comprising an encoder, a masked decoder, and multiple parallel prediction heads.

1. **Encoder**:
 - For each input image, it is patchified and projected into visual tokens.
 - These per-view tokens are processed by a shared-weight Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder to generate feature embeddings.
 - A learnable camera token is concatenated with these feature embeddings to form the input to the decoder, enabling camera-specific information processing.

2. **Masked Decoder**:
 - A ViT decoder with cross-attention mechanisms facilitates information exchange and aggregation across views.
 - **Masked Attention**: A key design choice to prevent information leakage and ensure stable training. Context tokens are restricted to only attend to other context tokens, preserving the independence of Gaussian reconstruction from target view information. Conversely, target tokens can attend to both context and target tokens, allowing the model to leverage global scene cues for more accurate pose estimation.
 - The decoder outputs refined image tokens and a refined camera token for each view.

3. **Camera Predictor**:
 - The camera head processes the refined camera tokens to predict the per-view camera extrinsics ($P_v$) and intrinsics ($K_v$).
 - Extrinsics are normalized such that the first input view serves as the canonical reference frame. Rotation is represented using a 6D representation, and translation using four homogeneous coordinates (for the MASt3R variant). This head can be implemented as a lightweight multi-layer perceptron (MLP).

4. **Gaussian Predictor**:
 - **Depth-Based Gaussian Formulation**: Unlike methods that directly regress unconstrained 3D points, NAS3R adopts a local-to-global paradigm to derive 3D Gaussian centers. This formulation is designed to be physically grounded and well-conditioned, promoting stable convergence without needing pretrained priors. Gaussian centers are constrained to lie along their corresponding viewing rays.
 - **Depth Head**: A DPT-based architecture predicts per-pixel depths from the refined context image tokens. These predicted depths are passed through a sigmoid function, linearly interpolated between near and far planes, and then lifted into 3D space using the *predicted* camera poses and intrinsics to obtain the Gaussian centers.
 - **Gaussian Parameter Head**: A DPT-style architecture takes both refined context image tokens and original context images as input to predict the remaining Gaussian parameters: rotation quaternion, scale, opacity, and spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients.

**Model Compatibility:**
NAS3R is designed to be compatible with existing state-of-the-art transformer-based 3D models like 3R-based frameworks (e.g., MASt3R, DUSt3R) and VGGT. This involves adapting their decoders with masked attention and adding the necessary depth, Gaussian parameter, and camera prediction heads. The framework can also incorporate available pretrained priors (e.g., initializing from MASt3R or VGGT weights) or calibrated intrinsics (by embedding them with camera/encoder tokens) to enhance performance.

**Training and Inference:**
- **Training**: The entire pipeline is optimized end-to-end using the photometric consistency loss. A CUDA-based 3D Gaussian Splatting renderer supports gradient computation with respect to camera poses, intrinsics, and Gaussian parameters. This process conceptually functions as a form of photometric bundle adjustment, where rendering errors backpropagate to refine both scene representation and camera parameters.
- **Inference**: At inference time, only the context images are required as input to predict their camera parameters and reconstruct the 3D scene.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducts extensive experiments to evaluate NAS3R's performance in novel view synthesis, relative pose estimation, depth estimation, and geometry reconstruction. Evaluations are performed on various datasets, including RealEstate10K (RE10K), DL3DV, DTU, ACID, ScanNet++, and BlendedMVS.

#### 4.1. Performance without Supervised 3D Priors

- **Novel View Synthesis (NVS)**: NAS3R demonstrates superior performance compared to other self-supervised methods that do not rely on supervised 3D priors (e.g., SelfSplat, SPFSplat*). Quantitatively (Table 2), NAS3R achieved higher PSNR, SSIM, and lower LPIPS on in-domain (RE10K) and out-of-domain (ACID, DL3DV, DTU) datasets. In several instances, its performance was competitive with, or even surpassed, supervised methods like pixelSplat and MVSplat, despite not using any ground-truth poses or intrinsics during training. Qualitative comparisons also indicated higher quality output under wide viewpoint changes (Figure 3).
- **Relative Pose Estimation**: On two-view relative pose estimation across RE10K, ACID, and DL3DV datasets, NAS3R consistently outperformed other self-supervised NVS-based approaches (Table 3). Its pose estimation capabilities, particularly on the more challenging DL3DV dataset, approached or surpassed those of SuperPoint + SuperGlue, a fully supervised feature matching method. This suggests NAS3R's ability to learn robust feature correspondences in a self-supervised manner. Qualitative results also showed more accurate pose trajectories (Figure 4).
- **Depth Estimation**: For two-view depth estimation on the BlendedMVS dataset (using models trained on RE10K), NAS3R achieved the best performance in terms of relative error and inlier ratio (Table 4). This result exceeded the performance of the supervised NVS method MVSplat, which leverages ground-truth camera parameters and cost volumes. This finding indicates the ability of the transformer backbone in NAS3R to learn accurate geometry from image-level supervision alone, without explicit 3D inductive biases.
- **Geometry Reconstruction**: Qualitative analysis (Figure 5) revealed that NAS3R produced more coherent Gaussian distributions and cleaner spatial organization compared to MVSplat, which often yielded irregular and noisy geometry. This improved structure translated into sharper, more accurate depth renderings that better preserved planar surfaces and fine details.

#### 4.2. Leveraging Pretrained Priors

The study also investigated NAS3R's compatibility with existing pretrained priors.
- **Enhanced NVS and Pose Estimation**: Incorporating pretrained priors, specifically by initializing the encoder and decoder with weights from MASt3R or VGGT, further boosted both NVS and pose estimation performance (Table 5 and Table 6). NAS3R, when initialized with priors, outperformed other competing NVS-based methods that also used priors. In pose estimation, NAS3R consistently matched or exceeded the performance of the MASt3R and VGGT models themselves.
- **Impact of Ground-Truth Intrinsics**: When ground-truth intrinsics were additionally provided (NAS3R-I variants), performance in pose estimation improved further (Table 6), suggesting that explicit calibration information aids in reducing scale ambiguity and facilitating optimization.
- **Conclusion on Priors**: These results indicate that pretrained priors serve as effective geometric initializations. Moreover, the improvements observed over the initial pretrained models highlight the potential of self-supervised photometric optimization to adapt and enhance supervised 3D priors on large-scale unlabeled data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis

- **Scaling with Larger Training Sets**: Training NAS3R on a combined RE10K+DL3DV dataset consistently improved NVS, pose estimation (on ScanNet++), and depth estimation (on BlendedMVS) performance (Table 7). This demonstrates the framework's scalability and its capacity to leverage larger unlabeled datasets effectively.
- **More Input Views**: Increasing the number of context views for reconstruction led to consistent improvements in both NVS and pose estimation performance (Table 8), indicating that the method effectively utilizes additional multi-view information.
- **Self-supervised Pretraining for Downstream Finetuning**: NAS3R trained solely on RE10K+DL3DV performed comparably or better than supervised training from scratch on BlendedMVS for depth, and significantly better for pose estimation (Table 9). Furthermore, initializing supervised finetuning with self-supervised pretrained NAS3R weights substantially outperformed random initialization. This highlights the model's ability to learn meaningful geometry from self-supervision and provide a strong, generalizable initialization for downstream supervised tasks.
- **Progressive Interval Curriculum**: While a progressive frame-interval curriculum enhanced performance, NAS3R still converged stably without it, indicating robustness (Table 10).
- **Ground-truth Intrinsics on NVS**: Ground-truth intrinsics had a minimal impact on NVS quality (Table 11), suggesting that photometric supervision can effectively adjust other 3D attributes to be consistent with self-learned intrinsics for rendering.
- **Comparison with AnySplat**: NAS3R outperformed AnySplat in the 2-view setting and achieved comparable performance with more views, despite using less training data and no pretrained priors (Table 12). This was attributed to NAS3R's bounded depth formulation and supervision on novel target views.
- **Inference Efficiency**: The paper provides metrics for model parameters, FLOPs, and inference time for both MASt3R-based and VGGT-based NAS3R variants (Table 13).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of NAS3R represents an advancement in the field of 3D reconstruction by demonstrating a fully self-supervised framework that requires no ground-truth annotations and no pretrained priors during training. Its ability to jointly infer 3D Gaussian parameters, camera intrinsics, camera extrinsics, and depth maps from uncalibrated and unposed multi-view images addresses several critical limitations of current approaches.

The primary significance lies in its **scalability**. By removing the reliance on expensive and laborious 3D labeled datasets or strong geometric priors, NAS3R unlocks the potential to leverage vast quantities of readily available unlabeled, in-the-wild image data. This capability could democratize 3D reconstruction, making it accessible for applications where structured 3D data collection is impractical.

The framework's architectural design, particularly the shared transformer backbone with masked attention and the depth-based Gaussian formulation, contributes to **stable convergence and robust geometric reasoning** in a challenging self-supervised setting. This is crucial for avoiding degenerate solutions often encountered when ground truth is absent. The demonstrated performance, which in some instances approaches or surpasses supervised methods and robustly generalizes across diverse datasets, suggests an improved level of reliability for self-supervised 3D reconstruction.

Furthermore, NAS3R offers a **strong pretraining strategy** for downstream supervised tasks. Its capacity to learn meaningful geometry and accurate camera parameters from self-supervision can provide a robust and broadly generalizable initialization, which has been shown to significantly outperform random initialization when fine-tuning with ground-truth supervision. This could accelerate the development and performance of supervised 3D applications.

The **compatibility** of NAS3R with existing state-of-the-art transformer-based 3D models means it can be readily integrated into current research and development pipelines, providing a practical route to adapt powerful supervised architectures to unlabeled data.

The potential impact of this work extends to various applications, including **augmented reality, virtual reality, robotics, and 3D content creation**, particularly in scenarios where acquiring calibrated multi-view images or ground-truth 3D models is difficult. By offering a method for high-quality novel view synthesis, accurate pose estimation, and coherent depth and geometry reconstruction from unconstrained inputs, NAS3R contributes to bridging the gap between theoretical advancements in 3D vision and practical real-world deployments. It establishes a new baseline and research direction for self-supervised 3D reconstruction, challenging the perceived necessity of extensive supervision or strong priors for achieving competitive results.
