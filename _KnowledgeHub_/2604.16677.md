# ReconVLA: An Uncertainty-Guided and Failure-Aware Vision-Language-Action Framework for Robotic Control

> [!summary] Summary
> ReconVLA introduces an uncertainty-guided and failure-aware framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, employing conformal prediction for action-level reliability and Mahalanobis distance for state-level anomaly detection. This framework enhances robotic control by proactively anticipating failures and improving task success rates in both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, without modifying the base VLA policy.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack calibrated confidence measures for their action predictions, hindering reliability in real-world, out-of-distribution (OOD) environments.
- Current VLA-equipped robots cannot proactively anticipate or mitigate impending failures, leading to reactive responses or safety risks only after errors occur.
- Uncertainty quantification (UQ) efforts in VLA have been fragmented, with no unified framework addressing both action-level and state-level uncertainties holistically across the control process.

## Method

- **ReconVLA** proposes an external, lightweight framework that augments pretrained VLA policies without requiring model retraining or architectural modifications.
- An action-level uncertainty-aware module utilizes Conformal Quantile Regression (CQR) to quantify the uncertainty of VLA-generated action candidates and select the most reliable action for execution.
- A state-level runtime failure detection module employs State Mahalanobis Distance (SMD) to monitor for deviations from nominal operating states, providing early warnings of impending failures.

## Results

- CQR-based action selection improved average task success rates by **+17%** over the default π₀ policy in simulation (from 0.56 to 0.73), with notable gains up to **+40%** on specific tasks.
- The SMD-based failure detector consistently achieved the highest predictive power (Spearman ρ = -0.784, AUC = 0.922 for π₀) for identifying failures in simulation and provided early warning signals before actual failure events.
- In real-world experiments, ReconVLA-CQR maintained higher success rates under distribution shifts (e.g., improving success from **60%** to **80%** under lighting shifts), and ReconVLA-SMD proactively halted execution in 16/20 trials before physical hardware limits were reached, demonstrating enhanced safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA unreliability can be systematically decomposed into input uncertainty (state-level) and noise uncertainty (action-level), allowing for targeted quantification and mitigation strategies.
> - Combining CQR for action-level uncertainty and SMD for state-level anomaly detection provides a robust and comprehensive approach to managing multifaceted uncertainties in VLA pipelines.
> - Proactive uncertainty awareness enables robots to 'know when they don't know' or when they are likely to fail, facilitating safer and more reliable operation through timely intervention or assistance requests.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has seen the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as generalist robotic controllers. These models integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation into unified policies, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and generate continuous action sequences based on visual observations. Examples include models like RT-2, OpenVLA, and π₀, which leverage large-scale multimodal data to achieve generalization to novel tasks and environments. This represents a shift from conventional task-specific policies towards more versatile, foundation-model-level robotics.

However, a significant challenge with contemporary VLAs is their lack of calibrated confidence measures in their action predictions. This limitation hinders their reliability in real-world environments where robots encounter out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions (e.g., unexpected lighting, occluded visuals, ambiguous instructions) and potential failures must be anticipated and mitigated. Existing VLA evaluations typically focus on binary success rates, offering limited insight into the model's confidence or reasons for failure. This absence of introspection means VLA-equipped robots may execute confidently even in failure-prone situations without warning signals, potentially leading to errors or safety risks.

Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) has been recognized as a crucial aspect for trustworthy robotics, providing signals to anticipate errors, guide human-robot interaction, and improve decision-making under ambiguity. While UQ techniques such as Bayesian deep learning, deep ensembles, and conformal prediction (CP) have been applied in computer vision, natural language processing, and general robot perception and planning, their specific application to VLA models remains largely underexplored. Previous efforts have tended to address isolated facets of uncertainty within VLA pipelines, such as calibrating high-level planners or using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for post-hoc failure detection, but a unified framework for holistic uncertainty management across the entire VLA process has been lacking. This fragmented approach leaves robots vulnerable to errors stemming from misinterpretations or OOD states without mechanisms for proactive intervention.

ReconVLA addresses this gap by introducing a unified framework that endows VLAs with calibrated uncertainty estimation and proactive failure detection capabilities. It aims to integrate uncertainty awareness throughout the VLA control process, specifically by leveraging conformal prediction for action-level uncertainty and statistical methods for state-level anomaly detection. This work builds upon the growing consensus that UQ is essential for reliable robotic systems, bridging the gap between powerful function approximators and the need for predictable and safe operation in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ReconVLA is to enhance the reliability and safety of generalist VLA robotic controllers, particularly in unstructured real-world settings. Current VLA models, despite their advanced generalization capabilities, lack intrinsic mechanisms to assess their confidence in predictions or detect impending failures. This deficiency leads to several critical issues:
- **Lack of calibrated confidence:** VLAs do not provide a quantifiable measure of how certain they are about their generated actions. This makes it difficult to trust their decisions, especially when operating in conditions that differ from their training data.
- **Inability to anticipate failures:** Without self-assessment, robots cannot foresee or avoid situations that are likely to lead to execution failures, such as encountering OOD inputs, ambiguous instructions, or unstable environmental conditions. This can result in costly errors, task performance degradation, or even safety hazards.
- **Opaque decision-making:** The complex, opaque internal structures of large VLA models make it challenging for practitioners to understand when and why a robot might fail, limiting their trustworthy deployment.
- **Reactionary vs. Proactive control:** Existing systems often detect failures only after they occur, rather than proactively identifying high-risk situations to enable mitigation.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of ReconVLA are:
1. **Systematic Uncertainty Decomposition:** To analyze and decompose the sources of unreliability in VLA policies into two primary types:
 - **Input Uncertainty:** Arising from imperfect or OOD observations, reflected in the agent’s internal state representation.
 - **Noise Uncertainty:** Stemming from stochastic variations inherent in generative policies (e.g., diffusion models that produce multiple action candidates).
 This decomposition provides a foundation for targeted uncertainty estimation and mitigation strategies.
2. **Action-Level Uncertainty Quantification:** To develop a method that quantifies uncertainty in action predictions. This involves capturing the variability induced by the policy's sampling noise and producing calibrated confidence intervals over candidate actions. The aim is to enable uncertainty-guided action selection, favoring actions with higher predicted reliability.
3. **State-Level Failure Detection:** To develop a runtime mechanism that assesses whether the robot’s current state deviates from the distribution of safe states observed during training. This aims to provide early warnings of emerging risks, identifying outlier or unsafe states before failures materialize, thereby complementing action-level uncertainty with continuous state-consistency checks.
4. **Unified Uncertainty-Aware Control Framework:** To integrate the action-level uncertainty quantification and state-level failure detection modules into a cohesive framework. This framework aims to provide end-to-end reliability enhancement for VLA deployment, from action generation to execution monitoring, without requiring retraining or modification of the underlying VLA policy.

The overarching motivation is to enable robots to "know when they don't know" or when they are likely to fail, thereby allowing them to adjust actions, request assistance, or halt operations proactively, moving towards truly trustworthy and safe robotic autonomy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReconVLA proposes an uncertainty-guided control framework that operates in conjunction with a pretrained VLA base model without altering its parameters. The framework is composed of two main, complementary modules: an action-level uncertainty-aware action selection module and a state-level runtime failure detection module.

**A. Action-Level Uncertainty-Aware Action Selection Module**
This module addresses noise uncertainty inherent in generative VLA policies, which produce multiple candidate action sequences for the same observation due to stochastic sampling. The objective is to evaluate the uncertainty of each candidate and select the most reliable action. This involves two stages:

1. **Uncertainty Quantification with Conformal Quantile Regression (CQR):**
 - **Concept:** CQR combines traditional Quantile Regression (QR) with Conformal Prediction (CP) to produce statistically rigorous prediction intervals. The goal is to ensure that the true expert action lies within the predicted interval with a specified probability (e.g., 90% confidence).
 - **Action Error Definition:** The true action error ($d_a$) is defined as the Euclidean distance between a VLA-generated action ($\hat{a}$) and the ground-truth expert action ($a_{gt}$), i.e., $d_a = \|\hat{a} - a_{gt}\|_2$.
 - **Step 1: Quantile Regression Model Training:** A QR model ($f_\tau$) is trained to map the VLA's final action latent embedding ($z_a$) and the generated action ($\hat{a}$) to an estimated $\tau$-quantile of the action error distribution. This model outputs $\hat{d}^{(\tau)}_a(x) = f_\tau(z_a, \hat{a})$, approximating the $\tau$-quantile of $d_a$. The model is optimized using the pinball loss. A high quantile level (e.g., $\tau = 0.9$) is chosen to focus on the upper tail of the error distribution, indicating risk.
 - **Step 2: Conformal Calibration:** To ensure valid uncertainty bounds, a holdout calibration dataset is used. For each calibration sample, a conformity score $S_j = d^{(j)}_a - \hat{d}^{(\tau)}_a(x_j)$ is computed, measuring how well the predicted quantile covers the actual error. A conformal offset $q_\tau$ is then determined as the $\alpha$-quantile of these conformity scores (where $\alpha$ is the desired miscoverage rate, e.g., $\alpha = 0.1$). This offset corrects the QR model's bias and ensures the desired statistical coverage. The calibrated upper bound is $\hat{d}^{(\tau)}_{a,cal}(x) = \hat{d}^{(\tau)}_a(x) + q_\tau$.
 - **Step 3: Uncertainty Estimation at Runtime:** During execution, for any VLA-generated action, its latent embedding and action are fed into the trained QR model to get $\hat{d}^{(\tau)}_a$. The conformal offset $q_\tau$ is then added to obtain the calibrated uncertainty score $\tilde{d}^{(\tau)}_a$.

2. **Uncertainty-Aware Action Selection:**
 - Given an observation $o_t$, the VLA policy generates $K$ candidate actions $\{\hat{a}^{(1)}_t, \dots, \hat{a}^{(K)}_t\}$ by sampling different latent noise.
 - For each candidate $\hat{a}^{(i)}_t$ and its corresponding latent embedding $z_{a,i}$, a calibrated uncertainty score $\tilde{d}^{(\tau)}_{a,i}$ is computed using the CQR model.
 - The action with the lowest calibrated uncertainty score is selected for execution: $\hat{a}^* = \arg \min_i (\tilde{d}^{(\tau)}_{a,i})$. This process prevents the execution of actions with high predicted discrepancy from expert behavior.

**B. State-Level Runtime Failure Detection Module**
This module addresses input uncertainty by monitoring the robot's internal state for deviations from the distribution of safe states observed during training. It employs a distance-based anomaly detector:

1. **Nominal State Distribution Characterization:**
 - An expert state dataset $D_{expert} = \{s_i\}_{i=1}^N$ is collected from all successful expert trajectories used for VLA training.
 - This distribution is approximated by a multivariate Gaussian, computing the sample mean $\mu$ and covariance $\Sigma$ of $D_{expert}$. This $(\mu, \Sigma)$ model represents the "normal" operating envelope of the robot.
2. **State Mahalanobis Distance (SMD) Calculation:**
 - For any given state $s$, the Mahalanobis distance is computed as $D_M(s) = \sqrt{(s - \mu)^T \Sigma^{-1}(s - \mu)}$. A larger $D_M(s)$ indicates a greater deviation from the nominal state distribution.
3. **Failure Detection Threshold Calibration:**
 - A separate calibration dataset of robot states, including both successful and failure scenarios, is used to set a risk threshold.
 - The Mahalanobis distance is computed for each state in this calibration set. The threshold $t^{calib}_s$ is then defined as a high percentile (e.g., 99th percentile) of these distances. This ensures that approximately 99% of normal operating states fall below this threshold.
4. **Runtime Monitoring and Intervention:**
 - During execution, at each time step $t$, $D_M(s_t)$ for the current robot state $s_t$ is computed.
 - If $D_M(s_t) > t^{calib}_s$, the state is flagged as unsafe or OOD, and an intervention is triggered (e.g., immediate halt, switch to safe mode, or human assistance request). Otherwise, execution proceeds normally.

By integrating these two modules, ReconVLA creates a runtime layer that augments a frozen VLA policy, enabling proactive anticipation of high-risk situations and mitigation of failures without modifying the underlying model architecture or parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ReconVLA was conducted in both simulation (LIBERO-Object benchmark) and real-robot experiments (Universal Robots UR5 manipulator) using two representative VLA policies: the generative π₀ and the deterministic OpenVLA-OFT. Metrics included Spearman rank correlation (ρ) for uncertainty-performance, Vargha-Delaney’s A12 and Cohen’s d for inter-group separability, and Area Under the Curve (AUC) for failure detection reliability.

**A. Action-Level Uncertainty Quantification**
- **Uncertainty Score Design:** Three candidate CQR-based uncertainty scores were compared: Prediction Interval Width (PIW-CQR), Cosine Similarity Score (COS-CQR), and Euclidean Distance (DIS-CQR). DIS-CQR, which directly quantified execution error as the Euclidean distance between predicted and expert actions, demonstrated the strongest negative correlation with task success (Spearman ρ = -0.781) and the largest effect size (Cohen's d = 0.91). This indicated DIS-CQR as the most reliable predictor of execution error, leading to its adoption as the default action uncertainty measure.
- **Uncertainty-Guided Action Selection (Simulation):** On the LIBERO-Object benchmark (10 tasks, 30 trials each), ReconVLA's CQR method, by selecting the least-uncertain action among 10 sampled candidates, achieved an average success rate of 0.73. This significantly outperformed the default π₀ policy (0.56) and a "mean action" baseline (0.66). The CQR method improved success rates in 9 out of 10 tasks, with an overall average increase of +17% over the default policy. Notable improvements included the tomato sauce task (from 40% to 80% success, +40%) and BBQ sauce (from 70% to 93%, +23%). One task (milk) showed a performance drop, suggesting a trade-off in specific scenarios.

**B. State-Level Failure Detection**
- **Performance Comparison (Simulation):** The SMD-based failure detector was compared against seven baseline uncertainty metrics from the EU-VLA benchmark (token-based and action instability-based). On test sets of 600 trajectories for π₀ and 600 for OpenVLA-OFT, SMD consistently achieved the highest Spearman ρ, Cohen's d, and AUC scores across both VLA models. For π₀, SMD achieved Spearman ρ = -0.784, Cohen's d = 0.914, and AUC = 0.922, outperforming all baselines. CQR was the second-best performer. Token-based metrics showed limited effectiveness. The results qualitatively demonstrated that CQR and SMD produced the clearest separation between uncertainty score distributions for successful and failed executions.
- **Early Warning Capability:** Qualitative analysis showed that successful task executions maintained low SMD values, while failed runs often exhibited a sharp increase in SMD *prior* to the actual failure event (e.g., robot entering an unstable configuration). This confirmed SMD's capability to provide early warning signals for impending failures.
- **Failure Detection Threshold Sensitivity:** Youden’s J statistic was used to identify an optimal threshold for SMD. The maximum J score was achieved at a confidence level of τ = 0.85, corresponding to an SMD threshold of $t_s = 8.16$. This threshold was found to effectively identify failure segments with minimal disruption (reducing overall task success by only 0.006).

**C. Real-World Deployment Results (UR5 Robotic Arm)**
- **Success-Failure Separation:** On a UR5 robotic arm, across four "move-near" manipulation tasks (80 real-robot rollouts), ReconVLA's CQR and SMD scores achieved the highest AUC values for discriminating between successful and failed executions (SMD AUC = 0.912, CQR AUC = 0.759). This demonstrated the transferability and strong predictive power of the proposed uncertainty estimates to real hardware.
- **Action-Level Reliability Under Distribution Shifts:** CQR-based action selection was tested under various real-world perturbations (lighting shifts, camera perturbations, cluttered scenes) on two tasks. ReconVLA-CQR consistently maintained higher success rates compared to the default π₀ and the Mean-action baseline across all conditions. For instance, on Task 1 with lighting shift, success improved from 60% (default) to 80% (CQR). This indicates that calibrated uncertainty estimation provides tangible robustness benefits against realistic scene perturbations.
- **State-Level Failure Detection at the Workspace Edge:** The SMD module was evaluated in a controlled failure setting where the robot attempted to reach an object beyond its workspace, intentionally pushing it towards joint limits. In 20 trials, the default π₀ policy led to 20/20 protective-stop events (hardware safety halts). ReconVLA-SMD proactively halted execution in 16/20 trials before the robot reached its hardware limits, triggering a safety stop without entering protect mode. This demonstrated SMD's ability to reliably signal emerging risks and enable proactive, graceful intervention, contrasting with the hardware-triggered shutdowns of the default policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReconVLA framework addresses a critical gap in the reliability and trustworthiness of generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control. By integrating uncertainty-guided decision-making and proactive failure detection, ReconVLA offers several significant contributions:

1. **Enhanced Reliability and Safety:** The framework significantly improves the reliability and safety of VLA-enabled robots. By quantifying uncertainty at both the action and state levels, it enables robots to anticipate and mitigate failures before they occur, reducing catastrophic errors and preventing unsafe situations. This is particularly crucial for real-world deployments where unforeseen conditions are common.
2. **Proactive Failure Anticipation:** Unlike many existing methods that detect failures post-hoc, ReconVLA provides mechanisms for *proactive* intervention. The CQR-based action selection allows the robot to choose the most reliable action among stochastic candidates, while the SMD-based state monitoring flags anomalous states *before* they lead to a complete breakdown or hardware safety trip. This shifts control from reactive to preventive.
3. **No Retraining of Base Models:** A key advantage is that ReconVLA operates as an external, lightweight layer that augments pretrained VLA policies without requiring architectural modifications or expensive retraining of the underlying models. This makes it applicable to existing and future VLA architectures, offering a practical path to enhance their trustworthiness.
4. **Unified Uncertainty Management:** The systematic decomposition of uncertainty into input (state-level) and noise (action-level) and the integration of complementary modules (SMD and CQR) provide a holistic framework for managing multifaceted uncertainties in the VLA pipeline. This unified approach is more robust than fragmented solutions that address individual uncertainty sources in isolation.
5. **Improved Task Success and Robustness:** Experimental results in both simulation and real-world settings demonstrate that ReconVLA consistently leads to higher task success rates and increased robustness under challenging conditions, such as lighting shifts, camera perturbations, and cluttered scenes. This validates its practical utility in diverse operational environments.
6. **Contribution to Trustworthy AI and Robotics:** By endowing VLA models with introspection and calibrated self-assessment capabilities, ReconVLA contributes to the broader goal of trustworthy AI in robotics. It helps bridge the gap between powerful but opaque foundation models and the need for predictable, safe, and interpretable robotic behavior in human-centric environments.

The potential impact of ReconVLA extends to any application relying on VLA models, from industrial automation and logistics to assistive robotics and exploration. By making VLA-controlled robots more reliable and safer, this work facilitates their broader adoption and integration into complex, unstructured real-world scenarios.
