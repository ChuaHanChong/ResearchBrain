# Directly Fine-Tuning Diffusion Models on Differentiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> A new direct gradient-based fine-tuning method, DRaFT, optimizes diffusion models with differentiable rewards, offering significantly enhanced sample efficiency compared to reinforcement learning methods. It enables models to align with diverse objectives, including aesthetic quality and object presence, while providing novel control mechanisms for blending learned styles.

## Problem

- Pre-trained diffusion models often fail to align with specific human preferences, aesthetic standards, or complex user-defined objectives, as they are primarily trained to imitate data distribution.
- Existing alignment methods for diffusion models, particularly those based on reinforcement learning, are highly sample-inefficient and computationally expensive, hindering scalability and broad application.
- Prior gradient-based fine-tuning approaches either optimize only initial noise samples for each generation or rely on limited one-step gradient estimation, which are less efficient or effective than direct model parameter optimization.

## Method

- **DRaFT** directly backpropagates the ==gradient of a differentiable reward function== through the entire multi-step diffusion sampling process to fine-tune the model's parameters.
- It leverages ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== and ==gradient checkpointing== to manage the computational and memory demands, making full backpropagation feasible for large diffusion models.
- The method introduces **DRaFT-K** for ==truncated backpropagation== and **DRaFT-LV** for ==low-variance gradient estimation==, further improving training efficiency and stability.

## Results

- DRaFT variants demonstrated superior sample efficiency, achieving strong performance (e.g., maximizing LAION Aesthetics scores) over **200x** faster than reinforcement learning baselines like DDPO.
- Fine-tuned models showed substantial improvements in aesthetic quality and human preference alignment, achieving **state-of-the-art** performance on benchmarks like **HPSv2**, and generalized well to unseen prompts.
- The approach successfully applied to diverse objectives, including controlling image compressibility, enabling or disabling specific object presence using object detectors, and generating diffusion adversarial examples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct gradient-based optimization using differentiable rewards achieves substantially higher sample efficiency and faster convergence compared to reinforcement learning approaches for diffusion model alignment.
> - Truncating backpropagation to the last few sampling steps (DRaFT-K) can lead to better performance and training stability than full backpropagation through the entire diffusion chain, suggesting issues with exploding gradients for very long sequences.
> - LoRA parameters offer unique benefits for fine-tuning diffusion models, including the ability to scale the effect of fine-tuning and linearly combine multiple learned styles, enhancing model control and customization.
