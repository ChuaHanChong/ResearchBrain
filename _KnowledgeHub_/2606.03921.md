# GARDEN: Gravity-Aligned Reconstruction of Disentangled ENvironments from RGB images

> [!summary] Summary
> GARDEN is an RGB-only pipeline that turns multi-view images into simulation-ready 3D scenes via physically-grounded factorization: a learned Gravity-View alignment resolves global orientation ambiguity, box-prompted amodal meshes get refined 6-DoF poses, and a conditional point classifier strips duplicate object geometry from the background. The hybrid mesh-plus-point-cloud/3DGS output simulates directly in MuJoCo, cutting runtime from 4333s to 560s.

## Problem

- Feed-forward multi-view reconstruction yields monolithic scenes defined up to arbitrary global rotation, entangling rigid objects with background and preventing stable rigid-body anchoring or independent manipulation.
- CAD-retrieval pipelines restore interactivity by swapping real objects for artist assets, sacrificing instance-level geometric fidelity and scaling poorly to long-tailed in-the-wild objects.
- Those multi-stage systems chain layout estimation, retrieval and alignment, accumulating errors and costing thousands of seconds per scene, while naive box cropping deletes nearby background.

## Method

- **Gravity-View Align Module**: a context transformer decoder over frozen DepthAnything-3 camera tokens regresses a ==6D rotation== from the reference view into a ==gravity-aligned frame==, trained with an L1 rotation loss.
- ==Target-driven object generation==: a user or VLM box feeds SAM-3 masks into ==SAM-3D amodal meshes==, then ==FoundationPose== refines only translation and yaw since gravity fixes the upright axis.
- ==Point-conditional background disentanglement==: a ==cross-attention point classifier== labels cropped scene points against mesh-sampled condition points with BCE loss, trained on InternScenes with ghosting, thin-part and virtual-scanning augmentations.

## Results

- Under a stricter post-MuJoCo protocol, GARDEN cuts object-centric RMSE from LiteReality's **0.2163** to **0.1880** and LPIPS from **0.5854** to **0.4444**; holistic scene RMSE drops **0.2664** to **0.1616**.
- Gravity prediction reaches **1.40** degree mean error on Hypersim and **1.56** on TartanAir with **0%** failures above 10 degrees, beating GeoCalib+RANSAC; runtime falls **4332.8s** to **560.2s**, **7.7x** faster.
- Ablations: removing Gravity-View alignment degrades every NVS metric, completeness improves **0.0516** to **0.0387** over the DepthAnything-3 baseline, and the point classifier holds **0.976** F1 under artifact augmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gravity is a cheap universal prior: fixing the vertical axis shrinks pose search to yaw plus translation and makes floor-object separation nearly linear.
> - Scene-specific reconstruction can replace asset retrieval entirely; structure and fidelity are not a trade-off once a physical frame anchors the factorization.
> - Evaluating renders after 10 seconds of simulation is a stricter protocol that penalizes floating or tilted placements which static metrics hide.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D scene reconstruction from multi-view RGB images has advanced significantly, with systems capable of generating visually impressive "3D photographs" suitable for novel view synthesis. However, a limitation of many contemporary methods, such as those based on Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), is their tendency to produce monolithic scene representations. These representations often entangle rigid foreground objects with background geometry and are defined up to an arbitrary global rotation, lacking an explicit gravity direction. This inherent lack of physical structure and global frame ambiguity renders the reconstructed environments unsuitable for physically grounded applications, such as stable object interaction in embodied AI or realistic physics simulations.

To introduce the necessary structure for interactivity, existing approaches frequently employ CAD retrieval pipelines. These systems typically identify object bounding boxes and replace reconstructed objects with pre-designed 3D CAD assets. While this strategy provides structural disentanglement, it often compromises geometric fidelity, as retrieved CAD models rarely match the precise shapes and fine details of real-world objects. Additionally, CAD-based pipelines can be complex, brittle, prone to accumulating errors across multiple stages (layout estimation, retrieval, alignment), and challenging to scale to diverse, long-tailed object categories.

GARDEN addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that reformulates 3D reconstruction as physically-grounded scene factorization. It aims to overcome the trade-off between structural disentanglement and geometric fidelity by producing a structured hybrid scene representation directly from RGB input, without relying on external CAD models. This work contributes to the broader goal of generating interactive, simulation-ready digital twins that preserve the visual realism of the original scenes, moving beyond purely visual rendering towards functional, physically consistent environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to convert multi-view RGB observations into structured, simulation-ready 3D environments that support stable physical interaction and maintain high geometric fidelity. This objective is driven by several motivations arising from the limitations of current RGB-only reconstruction pipelines:

1. **Addressing Monolithic Scene Representations:** Existing reconstruction systems typically generate "3D photographs" that are largely monolithic, meaning foreground rigid objects are intrinsically coupled with background geometry. This entanglement hinders independent manipulation of individual objects, which is crucial for interactive applications and physics simulations. The motivation is to decouple these components, allowing for object-level manipulation and interaction.

2. **Resolving Global Orientation Ambiguity:** Current reconstruction outputs are often defined only up to an arbitrary global rotation, lacking an explicit gravity direction or a physically grounded global frame. This "gauge ambiguity" makes rigid-body anchoring and physics-aware interactions fundamentally unstable, as objects lack a consistent "upright" orientation. A key motivation is to establish a physically grounded coordinate system to ensure stable object placement and behavior under gravity.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of CAD Retrieval:** While existing solutions attempt to inject structure through CAD retrieval (replacing reconstructed objects with retrieved assets), this approach introduces significant drawbacks. It compromises instance-level geometric fidelity, as pre-modeled assets rarely match the exact shape and fine details of real-world objects. Furthermore, these pipelines are complex, prone to error accumulation across stages, and difficult to scale to a wide variety of real-world objects. The motivation is to develop a CAD-free pipeline that preserves scene-specific geometric fidelity while achieving structural disentanglement.

4. **Enabling Direct Physics Simulation:** The ultimate goal is to generate scene representations that can be directly used in physics engines (e.g., for embodied AI or robotics simulations) without extensive post-processing or manual refinement. This requires a representation that explicitly delineates rigid bodies with accurate poses and provides a clean, decoupled background.

To achieve these objectives, GARDEN proposes a core insight: utilizing gravity as a universal physical prior to resolve global orientation ambiguity and consistently anchor objects. The framework aims to output a structured hybrid representation consisting of explicit standalone meshes for foreground rigid objects, complete with accurate 6-DoF poses, and a clean, high-fidelity background environment represented by point clouds or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). This combination is designed to facilitate both physical independence for manipulation and geometric authenticity for visual realism, thereby enabling direct and stable downstream physics simulations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GARDEN formulates multi-view scene reconstruction as physically-grounded scene factorization, leveraging gravity to align and disentangle scene elements. The pipeline operates in four main stages:

#### 3.1. Multi-View Reconstruction with Gravity-View Alignment

The first stage addresses the global orientation ambiguity present in conventional multi-view reconstructions, which typically lack an explicit gravity direction. GARDEN introduces a **Gravity-View (GV) Align Module** to align the reconstructed coordinate system with the physical direction of gravity, creating a unified Gravity-View frame.

1. **GV Coordinate System Construction:** The GV frame is established by aligning its y-axis with the predicted gravity direction ($g$) from the camera coordinate system. The x-axis is derived from the cross product of the y-axis and the camera's view direction ($v =^T$), and the z-axis is determined by the right-hand rule. This process yields a rotation matrix $R_{c2gv}$ to transform camera coordinates to the GV frame.

2. **Gravity Direction Prediction:** The module utilizes the global camera tokens from a multi-view foundation model, Depth-Anything-3 (DA3), for its robust scene representation capabilities. The global camera token ($f_{ref}$) from a dynamically identified reference view ($c_{ref}$) acts as a query. A **Context Transformer Decoder** processes $f_{ref}$ against the complete token sequence from all views, aggregating gravity-aware geometric clues. The output feature is then passed to an MLP head to regress a continuous 6D rotation representation, which is converted into a $3 \times 3$ rotation matrix, $\hat{R}_{c_{ref} \to gv}$.

3. **Supervision:** Ground-truth alignment matrices are derived from artist-designed simulation datasets (e.g., Hypersim, TartanAir, vKitti) where the world coordinate systems are inherently gravity-aligned. The world gravity vector ($g_w = [0, 0, -1]^T$) is projected into the camera coordinate system using ground-truth camera extrinsics $R_{w \to c}$ to obtain the true GV y-axis. The $L_1$ loss is applied between the predicted and ground-truth rotation matrices.

This alignment ensures that the entire reconstructed scene is physically grounded, which is critical for stable downstream physical simulations and improves geometric separability between support structures and objects.

#### 3.2. Target-Driven Object Generation and Layout Estimation

This stage focuses on reconstructing specific rigid objects as standalone meshes with accurate 6-DoF poses.

1. **Target Selection:** Users can specify interactive objects via a 2D bounding box on a reference view, or this box can be automatically generated by a vision-language model (VLM) or open-vocabulary grounding module.

2. **2D to 3D Lift:** The 2D mask from the bounding box is extracted using SAM-3 and then lifted into 3D space by SAM-3D, an amodal reconstruction model that recovers complete object geometry even under partial occlusion.

3. **6-DoF Pose Refinement:** While SAM-3D provides an initial spatial layout, its accuracy is often insufficient for strict physical simulations. **FoundationPose** is integrated as a dedicated module to refine the 6-DoF pose of the reconstructed mesh. The gravity direction from the GV Alignment Module transforms the problem into a physically grounded one: SAM-3D meshes are axis-aligned, so their vertical axis is deterministically aligned with gravity, reducing FoundationPose's task to estimating translation and yaw angle. The locally aligned mesh is then transformed into the global Gravity-View space using $R_{c_{ref} \to gv}$.

#### 3.3. Point-Conditional Background Disentanglement

To prevent foreground-background entanglement and ensure a clean static environment, duplicate object geometry must be removed from the original monolithic scene representation.

1. **Conditional 3D Point Classification:** This task is formulated as a conditional 3D point classification problem. A Transformer-based network processes two inputs: scene points cropped from a relaxed bounding box around the object and condition points sampled from the generated object mesh.

2. **Network Architecture:** Both point sets are mapped into a shared feature space using coordinate and type-specific embeddings. A multi-layer Transformer, employing self- and cross-attention, models fine-grained correspondences. The network predicts a binary probability for each scene point, classifying it as belonging to either the target object or the static background, optimized with a Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss.

3. **Training Data and Augmentations:** The network is trained on InternScenes, a large-scale simulation dataset providing precise object-scene labels. To bridge the domain gap between clean simulated data and artifact-prone real reconstructions, **artifact-oriented 3D augmentations** are introduced during training. These include multi-layer ghosting, thin-part deformation, virtual multi-view scanning, and jittering of mesh pose and scale. These techniques simulate systematic reconstruction errors, enhancing robustness and enabling reliable disentanglement in real scenes while preserving adjacent background geometry.

#### 3.4. Unified Physics Simulation

The final stage integrates the decoupled representation into a physics engine (e.g., MuJoCo) for interactive simulation.

1. **Environment Setup:** The static environment is established by identifying the supporting floor plane (Top-K coordinate peaks along the gravity vector) from the gravity-aligned point cloud and instantiating it as a static collision body in the simulator. The reconstructed objects, with their 6-DoF poses, are imported as independent, dynamic rigid bodies.

2. **Physical Consistency:** All scene components share the unified Gravity-View coordinate system, allowing the simulator's gravity vector to be directly aligned with the canonical vertical axis (e.g., +y-axis) for consistent physical behavior.

3. **Dual Representation:** Object meshes and the floor govern rigid-body collision dynamics, while the cleaned background (colored point clouds or 3DGS) serves as a high-fidelity visual backdrop. This design ensures that the environment adheres to physical laws for embodied AI interactions while maintaining the photorealistic visual context of the original scene.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GARDEN was evaluated against state-of-the-art scene generation and retrieval pipelines across object-centric perceptual quality, holistic scene quality, computational efficiency, and gravity estimation accuracy, using both simulated (Hypersim, TartanAir, InternScenes, vKitti) and real-world (LiteReality, Mip-NeRF360, ETH3D, custom tabletop) multi-view scenes.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Object-Centric Material Recovery (Table 1):** Under a stricter post-simulation protocol (objects simulated for 10 seconds under gravity), GARDEN consistently improved over retrieval-based baselines like LiteReality. Ours (3DGS, post-sim) reduced RMSE from 0.2163 to 0.1880, and Ours (point cloud, post-sim) reduced LPIPS from 0.5854 to 0.4444. While LiteReality reported strong SSIM, the gap with GARDEN was small, attributed to GARDEN's lack of a dedicated texture prediction stage and the minor physically induced pose perturbations in the post-simulation evaluation. Static GARDEN results (without simulation) showed even higher scores, indicating robust gains under the challenging protocol.

- **Holistic Graphics-Ready Scene Comparison (Table 2):** GARDEN demonstrated consistent scene-level improvements. Compared with LiteReality, Ours (point cloud, post-sim) improved RMSE from 0.2664 to 0.1616 and LPIPS from 0.6522 to 0.4081, while maintaining comparable SSIM (0.5735 vs. 0.5818). These results were also measured after a 10-second simulation, penalizing gravity-inconsistent placement or instability.

- **Gravity Estimation Accuracy (Table 3):** The proposed GV predictor achieved the lowest mean and 90th percentile angular errors on both Hypersim and TartanAir datasets, with a 0.0% failure rate above 10 degrees. It outperformed classical methods like Plane-RANSAC and normal clustering, as well as learned methods like COLMAP's Manhattan alignment and GeoCalib.

- **NVS Ablation on Hypersim (Table 4):** Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of GARDEN's factorization and gravity alignment. Compared to directly using DepthAnything3 (DA3) output for point clouds, Ours (point cloud) achieved better completeness (0.0387 vs. 0.0516) and improved RMSE/SSIM in rendering. The inclusion of the GV alignment module was crucial; removing it from the full FoundationPose setting caused substantial degradation in rendering metrics and point-cloud geometry metrics (completeness, accuracy, normal consistency), both without and after simulation, confirming its role in improving pose initialization, background disentanglement, and physical stability.

- **Computational Efficiency (Table 5):** GARDEN exhibited substantially lower inference latency compared to LiteReality, achieving an average runtime reduction from 4332.8s to 560.2s (approximately 7.7x speedup) across five evaluation scenes.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Scene Fidelity and Disentanglement (Figure 5):** Qualitative comparisons with LiteReality showed that GARDEN preserved scene-faithful background geometry and better maintained original object structure and placement. LiteReality, which relies on CAD retrieval, tended to replace scene content with generic assets, altering native object structure and original background context. In contrast, GARDEN's physically-grounded factorization produced coherent, interaction-ready reconstructions with pristine, scene-faithful backgrounds.

- **Object Reconstruction and Layout Consistency (Figure 6, Figure 10):** Across diverse datasets, GARDEN consistently recovered more complete geometry and higher-quality objects compared to DepthAnything3 (which often failed to fully reconstruct key objects) and SAM-3D (which struggled with occluded structures, generated incorrect geometries, and produced unreliable layout estimates from single-view perspectives). GARDEN also restored physically plausible object layouts while preserving background fidelity.

- **Physics Simulation (Figure 11):** The reconstructed environments demonstrated physical stability, as evidenced by simulations where rigid-body spheres interacted stably within the gravity-aligned scenes. The accompanying supplementary video further illustrated these simulation capabilities.

#### 4.3. Failure Cases

The supplementary material discusses representative failure cases primarily stemming from limitations of upstream foundation models or insufficient visual evidence. These include degraded mesh quality for severely occluded or tiny objects, SAM-3 reconstructing only dominant semantic subjects instead of fine-grained parts, and motion blur affecting SAM-3 mask quality. Pose estimation can fail with limited pixel observations, particularly when the target occupies a very small image region, leading to unreliable 6-DoF placements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GARDEN introduces a significant shift in RGB-only 3D reconstruction by offering a paradigm of physically-grounded scene factorization. Its primary significance lies in reconciling the often-conflicting requirements of structural interactability and geometric fidelity within a unified framework. By explicitly leveraging gravity as a universal physical prior, the method effectively resolves gauge ambiguity, a long-standing challenge in global scene orientation, thereby enabling consistent rigid-body anchoring.

The potential impact of GARDEN is substantial across several domains:

- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** The framework provides a practical and scalable solution for generating interactive digital twins. Robots and embodied AI agents require physically plausible environments for training and deployment. GARDEN's output—explicit rigid bodies with accurate 6-DoF poses within a gravity-aligned, disentangled scene—directly facilitates stable physical interaction, making it suitable for tasks involving manipulation, navigation, and interaction in simulated or real-world environments.

- **Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** The generation of high-fidelity, interactive digital content from real-world captures is a core challenge in VR/AR. GARDEN's ability to produce simulation-ready environments that preserve visual realism and allow object-level manipulation can enhance the realism and interactivity of virtual experiences.

- **3D Content Creation and Asset Generation:** The CAD-free pipeline offers an efficient alternative to traditional methods that rely on manual modeling or brittle CAD retrieval. It enables the creation of scene-specific 3D assets and environments directly from multi-view images, reducing processing time and improving geometric accuracy compared to retrieval-based baselines. This could streamline workflows for game development, film production, and architectural visualization.

- **General 3D Scene Understanding:** The method contributes to a deeper understanding of 3D scenes by providing a structured, semantically factorized representation. This structured output can serve as a robust foundation for various downstream 3D vision tasks beyond simulation, such as scene editing, rearrangement, and analysis.

By offering a comprehensive, RGB-only, and efficient pipeline that addresses fundamental limitations of prior methods, GARDEN moves beyond purely visual 3D reconstruction towards generating functional, interactive, and physically consistent digital representations of the world. Its modular design also implies that future advancements in 2D segmentation, 3D generation, or pose estimation can be readily integrated, further enhancing its capabilities.
