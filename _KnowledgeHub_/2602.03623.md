# Self-supervised Physics-Informed Manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects with Non-negligible Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed SPiD, a self-supervised physics-informed framework for dynamic manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) like ropes. It integrates a differentiable extended mass-spring model with a neural controller and a markerless perception system, achieving robust stabilization and significantly lower tracking errors compared to baselines in both simulation and real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Dynamic manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) is challenging due to their infinite-dimensional degrees of freedom, highly nonlinear dynamics, and coupled physical effects.
- Existing physics-based models are often computationally expensive or oversimplified, while data-driven methods require extensive, costly expert demonstrations and struggle with generalization.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap and ensuring robustness against variations in object properties for DLOs in real-world scenarios remains a significant challenge, often requiring expensive perception systems.

## Method

- A differentiable extended mass-spring model, incorporating a novel analytical formulation for bending damping, was developed to accurately capture DLO dynamics.
- A self-supervised neural controller was trained using task-oriented loss functions and the differentiable physics model, leveraging initial state diversity, data augmentation, domain randomization, and a self-supervised DAgger variant for online refinement.
- An affordable markerless perception system, comprising a fine-tuned YOLOv11-seg model and a custom rope splitter algorithm, was integrated to provide real-time 2D rope state feedback.

## Results

- The extended mass-spring model demonstrated improved rope tip prediction accuracy, reducing RMSE (e.g., from **0.1510** to **0.0704** m in simulation) compared to previous baselines.
- SPiD achieved superior rope stabilization and trajectory tracking, with significantly lower mean tracking errors and standard deviations compared to PID and double-pendulum baselines across diverse rope properties, initial states, and external disturbances.
- The framework enabled robust DLO manipulation using the affordable markerless perception system (F1-score **0.8980** at **39.80** FPS) despite lower frequency and noisier 2D state updates compared to marker-based alternatives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a detailed, differentiable physics model with neural controllers allows for accurate capture of DLO dynamics and efficient end-to-end optimization for control.
> - Self-supervised learning strategies, including domain randomization and an online Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection mechanism via DAgger, are effective for overcoming data scarcity and enhancing sim-to-real generalization for deformable object manipulation.
> - A curriculum learning approach during system identification (variable prediction horizon and two-stage parameter strategy) is crucial for accurately learning complex physical parameters from noisy data and navigating non-convex loss landscapes.
