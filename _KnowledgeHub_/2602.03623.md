# Self-supervised Physics-Informed Manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects with Non-negligible Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed SPiD, a self-supervised physics-informed framework for dynamic manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) like ropes. It integrates a differentiable extended mass-spring model with a neural controller and a markerless perception system, achieving robust stabilization and significantly lower tracking errors compared to baselines in both simulation and real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Dynamic manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) is challenging due to their infinite-dimensional degrees of freedom, highly nonlinear dynamics, and coupled physical effects.
- Existing physics-based models are often computationally expensive or oversimplified, while data-driven methods require extensive, costly expert demonstrations and struggle with generalization.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap and ensuring robustness against variations in object properties for DLOs in real-world scenarios remains a significant challenge, often requiring expensive perception systems.

## Method

- A differentiable extended mass-spring model, incorporating a novel analytical formulation for bending damping, was developed to accurately capture DLO dynamics.
- A self-supervised neural controller was trained using task-oriented loss functions and the differentiable physics model, leveraging initial state diversity, data augmentation, domain randomization, and a self-supervised DAgger variant for online refinement.
- An affordable markerless perception system, comprising a fine-tuned YOLOv11-seg model and a custom rope splitter algorithm, was integrated to provide real-time 2D rope state feedback.

## Results

- The extended mass-spring model demonstrated improved rope tip prediction accuracy, reducing RMSE (e.g., from **0.1510** to **0.0704** m in simulation) compared to previous baselines.
- SPiD achieved superior rope stabilization and trajectory tracking, with significantly lower mean tracking errors and standard deviations compared to PID and double-pendulum baselines across diverse rope properties, initial states, and external disturbances.
- The framework enabled robust DLO manipulation using the affordable markerless perception system (F1-score **0.8980** at **39.80** FPS) despite lower frequency and noisier 2D state updates compared to marker-based alternatives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a detailed, differentiable physics model with neural controllers allows for accurate capture of DLO dynamics and efficient end-to-end optimization for control.
> - Self-supervised learning strategies, including domain randomization and an online Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection mechanism via DAgger, are effective for overcoming data scarcity and enhancing sim-to-real generalization for deformable object manipulation.
> - A curriculum learning approach during system identification (variable prediction horizon and two-stage parameter strategy) is crucial for accurately learning complex physical parameters from noisy data and navigating non-convex loss landscapes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects is a long-standing grand challenge in robotics, significantly more complex than rigid-body manipulation. This complexity stems from the infinite-dimensional degrees of freedom of deformable objects, their highly nonlinear deformation behaviors, and coupled physical effects like tension, bending, and twisting. The research presented here specifically targets *dynamic manipulation*, a particularly challenging subset where inertial and time-varying effects dominate the object's motion, demanding fast and pronounced deformations from the robot.

Traditionally, approaches to deformable object manipulation fall into several categories, each with limitations:
- **Physics-based Modeling**: Methods like Mass–Spring Systems (MSSs), Position-Based Dynamics (PBD), Finite Element Methods (FEM), and elastic rod models offer varying degrees of accuracy and computational efficiency. MSSs are fast but often too simplified for precise dynamics, while FEM and elastic rod models are accurate but computationally expensive, hindering real-time applications.
- **Data-driven Approaches**: Learning models or controllers directly from visual data or human demonstrations can handle complex nonlinearities. However, they typically require vast amounts of high-quality data, struggle with generalization to novel objects or scenarios, and often lack physical interpretability.
- **Quasi-static Manipulation**: Many successful methods simplify the problem by assuming slow interactions where inertial effects are negligible. While effective for tasks like folding clothes or tying knots slowly, these approaches are inherently inefficient for agile, high-speed tasks.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these fields, aiming to overcome their respective limitations, especially in the context of dynamic manipulation. It addresses the gap by proposing a framework that leverages an accurate yet computationally efficient *physics-informed model* and combines it with a *self-supervised learning strategy*. Most existing dynamic manipulation research either relies on large expert demonstrations (limiting scalability) or requires frequent re-identification of physical parameters (hindering robustness across objects). By focusing on ropes—a representative Deformable Linear Object (DLO)—the authors tackle a problem often simplified to pendulum models in prior work, instead embracing its full 3D dynamic complexity. Furthermore, it directly confronts the practical challenge of perception by developing an affordable markerless system, moving beyond the idealized marker-based setups common in research labs. Thus, this work contributes to making dynamic DLO manipulation more practical, robust, and data-efficient for real-world robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a novel, robust, and data-efficient framework, termed SPiD (Self-supervised Physics-informed learning for Deformable object manipulation), for the dynamic manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs) like ropes. The framework aims to demonstrate strong sim-to-real generalization and operate without relying on extensive expert demonstrations.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the inherent challenges and limitations of existing approaches:

- **Ubiquity and Practical Importance**: Deformable objects are pervasive in human-centered environments (homes, hospitals, industry). Equipping robots with the ability to manipulate them is crucial for expanding robot autonomy and utility in service and domestic applications.
- **Complexity of Deformable Dynamics**: The high-dimensional, nonlinear, and coupled dynamics of deformable objects make accurate modeling and real-time control exceedingly difficult, especially in dynamic regimes where inertial effects are significant.
- **Limitations of Traditional Control**: Purely model-based approaches (e.g., MPC) often struggle with real-time performance and local optima for complex deformable dynamics.
- **Drawbacks of Data-Driven Learning**: While powerful, data-driven methods typically demand massive amounts of high-quality, expert-labeled data, which is time-consuming and expensive to collect, and often leads to limited generalization.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap**: Bridging the gap between simulation and the real world remains a major hurdle for learning-based control, especially for deformable objects whose physical properties are difficult to model perfectly.
- **Need for Robustness**: Controllers must be robust to variations in object properties, initial conditions, and external disturbances to be practical in unstructured environments.
- **Affordable Perception**: High-precision, marker-based perception systems are often impractical for real-world applications, necessitating the development of robust and affordable markerless alternatives.

In response to these motivations, SPiD aims to:
- Develop an *accurate yet computationally efficient physics model* for DLOs that can capture non-negligible dynamic characteristics.
- Propose a *self-supervised learning strategy* for neural controllers that minimizes reliance on expert demonstrations and heavy data collection.
- Incorporate mechanisms for *robustness and generalization* against physical parameter variations and the sim-to-real gap, including a novel self-supervised DAgger variant for online adaptation.
- Demonstrate the framework's versatility across *different dynamic manipulation tasks*, specifically rope stabilization and trajectory tracking.
- Develop and integrate an *affordable markerless perception system* to enable practical deployment in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SPiD framework comprises an offline phase for modeling, system identification, and control learning, and an online phase for real-time control, out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, and self-correction.

**A. Physics-informed Modeling of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs)**
The core of SPiD is an extended mass–spring model, representing a DLO as `N+1` mass points connected by `N` links. Each mass point has a position and velocity, forming a 6(N+1)-dimensional state space.
- **Force Components**: The model incorporates linear spring-damper forces (resisting distance/velocity changes), bending spring forces (resisting curvature changes), and crucial **bending damping forces** (resisting bending deformation rates). The paper notably derives the analytical formulation for bending damping, which is a key contribution to capturing dynamic effects. Additionally, torsion spring forces (geometric torsion) and external forces (gravity, air drag) are included.
- **Forward Dynamics**: Newton's second law is applied to calculate accelerations, which are then integrated forward in time using the symplectic Euler scheme.
- **Control Integration**: The robot end-effector's commanded velocity `u` directly determines the velocity of the first mass point, making the DLO dynamics a controlled, differentiable system `X_{t+1} = f_dlo(X_t, u_t)`.

**B. Differentiable Physics-based System Identification**
Given the differentiable nature of the DLO model, its parameters `θ` (masses, lengths, stiffnesses, damping coefficients, air drag) are identified using a gradient-based optimization approach (Adam).
- **Data Collection**: Real-world (or simulated MuJoCo) trajectories of the rope's position under controlled inputs are collected.
- **Loss Function**: The objective is to minimize the squared error between the predicted and observed rope point positions over time.
- **Curriculum Learning**: To overcome the challenge of a highly non-convex loss landscape for a large number of parameters and long time series:
 - **Variable Horizon**: Identification starts with a short prediction horizon, gradually increasing it as the model's accuracy improves.
 - **Two-stage Parameter Strategy**: Initially, all springs and dampers of the same type share a common parameter (homogeneous model). Once this converges, the constraint is relaxed, and each parameter is identified independently (heterogeneous model) for refined accuracy.

**C. Augmented Self-supervised Neural Controller Learning**
A neural network (`π_φ`) is trained to output 3D end-effector velocity commands given the current rope state `X` and a task specification `G`. The training is entirely self-supervised, leveraging the identified differentiable physics model.
- **Task-Oriented Loss**: The controller is trained to minimize a task-specific loss function `L_task`. For rope stabilization, this is the rope's total energy at the terminal state; for trajectory tracking, it's the squared Euclidean distance between the rope tip and target trajectory.
- **End-to-End Optimization**: The differentiable model allows for backpropagation of the `L_task` through the simulated rollouts to update the neural controller's parameters `φ`.
- **Robustness and Generalization Strategies**:
 - **Initial State Diversity**: A diverse set of initial rope states `X_0` is collected and augmented to ensure broad coverage of possible configurations.
 - **Data Augmentation**: Recorded states are rotated and translated in 3D space to generate a massive, comprehensive training dataset.
 - **Self-supervised DAgger**: During deployment, the system monitors the task loss. If it deviates abnormally (e.g., increases, oscillates, diverges), the current state is identified as an Out-of-Distribution (OOD) sample. These OOD samples are then added to the training set with higher sampling weights, enabling offline policy refinement without expert intervention.
 - **Domain Randomization**: During training, Gaussian noise is injected into the identified model parameters `θ` to simulate ropes with varying physical properties, thereby enhancing the controller's robustness to real-world parameter mismatches (sim-to-real gap).

**D. Rope Perception**
To provide real-time rope state feedback:
- **Marker-based Perception (OptiTrack)**: Used as the high-fidelity default. It involves reflective markers on the rope and multiple infrared cameras, providing accurate 3D positions at 100 Hz.
- **Markerless Perception (Single RGB Camera)**: Developed for affordability and portability.
 - **Detection & Segmentation**: A custom dataset of rope images is collected and annotated to fine-tune a YOLOv11-seg model. This unified model performs both detection and instance segmentation, chosen for its balance of high accuracy (F1-score of 0.8980) and real-time inference speed (39.80 FPS) compared to foundation models like Grounded-SAM.
 - **Rope Splitter Algorithm**: An efficient algorithm, utilizing a KD-tree, is developed to extract equidistant 2D points from the segmentation mask, representing the rope's state. While acknowledging limitations (e.g., 2D tracking, curvature drift), it aims for sufficient precision for control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world, validating the SPiD framework across its modeling, control, robustness, and generalization capabilities.

**A. Model Validation:**
- **Improved Accuracy**: The extended mass–spring model, incorporating the newly derived damping terms, significantly outperforms a previous mass–spring baseline in accurately predicting long-horizon rope motion. In simulation, RMSE for rope tip prediction was reduced (e.g., from 0.1510 to 0.0704 m in one case), and similar improvements were observed in real-world experiments (from 0.0810 to 0.0591 m). Visualizations (Fig. 4, 5) clearly show the proposed model maintaining accurate rope configuration and dynamics over longer durations compared to the baseline, which quickly degrades.

**B. Rope Stabilization Task (Primary Testbed):**
- **Superior Control Performance**: In both MuJoCo simulation and real-world experiments, SPiD consistently achieves faster and smoother rope stabilization compared to a passive case (rope left to rest) and a double-pendulum baseline controller. The baseline often exhibits significant oscillations or takes much longer to stabilize.
- **Strong Generalization**:
 - **Initial States**: SPiD performs robustly across various unseen initial rope positions (Fig. 7, 8).
 - **Object Properties**: The controller generalizes effectively to ropes with different physical properties, including varying lengths (short-rope), masses (thick-rope), and non-uniform mass distributions (knotted-rope). The baseline controller often struggles to generalize, sometimes performing worse than the passive case for different rope types (Table II).
 - **External Disturbances**: SPiD demonstrates high robustness to manual external disturbances, rapidly dissipating injected energy and re-stabilizing the rope (Fig. 9).

**C. Markerless Rope Stabilization:**
- **Feasibility with Low-Fidelity Perception**: Despite the markerless perception system providing noisier and lower-frequency (22 Hz average) 2D state updates compared to the marker-based system (100 Hz), SPiD successfully maintains robust and swift rope stabilization. The controller's inherent robustness, enhanced by domain randomization, allows it to perform well even with these perception limitations (Fig. 10, 12).
- **Effective Markerless Perception**: The fine-tuned YOLOv11-seg model achieved high segmentation accuracy (F1-score of 0.8980) and a real-time frame rate of 39.80 FPS, demonstrating its suitability for practical deployment (Table III).

**D. Generalization to Rope Trajectory Tracking:**
- **Task Adaptability**: SPiD successfully adapts to the dynamic rope trajectory tracking task with only minor modifications to the loss function and controller input, highlighting its inherent flexibility.
- **High-Speed and Accurate Tracking**: SPiD significantly outperforms a finely tuned PID baseline controller across various complex target trajectories (Sinusoid, Egg, Lemniscate) and different motion speeds (2.5 s vs. 5.0 s duration). SPiD achieves substantially lower mean tracking errors and standard deviations (Table IV, Fig. 13, 14), demonstrating more agile and stable tracking performance, especially for faster and more complex trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SPiD framework represents a notable advancement in the field of deformable object manipulation, particularly for dynamic scenarios. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Bridging Key Gaps in Robotics**: SPiD effectively combines the interpretability and physical grounding of an accurate dynamics model with the flexibility and learning capabilities of neural networks. This hybrid approach addresses critical limitations of purely model-based (computational cost, local optima) and purely data-driven (data hungry, poor generalization, sim-to-real gap) methods.
- **Data Efficiency and Reduced Supervision**: The self-supervised learning paradigm, coupled with domain randomization and the self-supervised DAgger mechanism, drastically reduces the need for large, expert-labeled datasets and human supervision. This makes the development and deployment of DLO manipulation skills significantly more practical and scalable in real-world settings.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization**: The comprehensive training strategies, including diverse initial states, data augmentation, and domain randomization, lead to a highly robust controller. Its ability to generalize across varying object properties (length, mass, non-uniformity) and initial conditions, as well as its resilience to external disturbances, is a critical step towards reliable robotic systems operating in unstructured, dynamic environments.
- **Adaptive Learning through Self-Correction**: The novel self-supervised DAgger variant provides an online mechanism for detecting out-of-distribution scenarios and automatically refining the policy. This continuous self-correction capability is crucial for long-term autonomous operation and adaptation to unforeseen real-world complexities without requiring human intervention.
- **Enabling Affordable Robotic Systems**: The development and integration of an effective markerless perception system, based on a fine-tuned deep learning model, significantly lowers the cost and complexity of deploying DLO manipulation robots. This promotes wider adoption of such capabilities beyond highly controlled laboratory environments.
- **Broadening Robotic Applications**: The demonstrated effectiveness across distinct tasks—rope stabilization (bringing to rest) and trajectory tracking (following a moving path)—highlights the framework's versatility. This suggests a strong potential for application to other complex DLO tasks such as knotting, folding, untangling, or assembly, which are currently challenging for robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: While currently focused on one-dimensional DLOs (ropes), the authors acknowledge the straightforward scalability of the approach. Its extension to two- and three-dimensional deformable objects (e.g., cloth, sponges, soft tissues) is a logical and impactful next step. This could revolutionize applications in domestic robotics (laundry, cooking), industrial automation (handling cables, fabrics), and even medical robotics (surgical manipulation of soft tissues), paving the way for more dexterous and intelligent robots.

In conclusion, SPiD offers a robust, data-efficient, and physically grounded framework that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in dynamic deformable linear object manipulation. Its innovative combination of modeling and learning strategies, coupled with practical perception, lays a strong foundation for future developments in versatile and autonomous robotic systems.
