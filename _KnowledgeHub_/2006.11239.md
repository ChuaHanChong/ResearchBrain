# Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Jonathan Ho, Ajay Jain, and Pieter Abbeel demonstrated that Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) can generate high-fidelity images, achieving state-of-the-art FID scores on CIFAR10. This was accomplished by re-parameterizing the model to predict noise and optimizing a simplified objective, which enabled the models to produce samples competitive with leading Generative Adversarial Networks.

## Problem

- Prior diffusion probabilistic models were theoretically sound but struggled to generate high-fidelity samples competitive with leading generative models like GANs.
- Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), while achieving high sample quality, were notoriously difficult to train, suffered from mode collapse, and lacked direct likelihood estimation.
- Likelihood-based models (VAEs, Autoregressive Models, Normalizing Flows) often traded off sample quality (e.g., blurriness in VAEs) or generation speed (autoregressive models) for tractable likelihoods.

## Method

- Defines a fixed ==forward Markov chain== to gradually add Gaussian noise to data and learns a ==reverse Markov chain== parameterized by a ==U-Net== to progressively denoise it.
- Reparameterizes the neural network to ==predict the noise component== added at each diffusion step, connecting the model to ==denoising score matching==.
- Utilizes a simplified training objective which is an unweighted ==mean squared error== between the actual noise and the predicted noise, found to be critical for maximizing sample quality.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art sample quality on CIFAR10 with an FID score of **3.17** and Inception Score of **9.46**, surpassing or competing with leading GANs.
- Produced high-resolution **256x256** images on LSUN datasets (Church, Bedroom, Cat) with quality comparable to or better than ProgressiveGAN and competitive with StyleGAN.
- Demonstrated smooth and meaningful latent space interpolations between images, indicating a well-structured representation, and illustrated a progressive coarse-to-fine generation process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A direct theoretical connection exists between diffusion probabilistic models and denoising score matching with annealed Langevin dynamics, unifying these generative approaches.
> - Optimizing a simplified mean squared error objective, focused on predicting the noise component, is crucial for achieving high sample quality in diffusion models, outperforming the full variational bound for this purpose.
> - Diffusion models, when trained for high sample quality, exhibit an inductive bias towards excellent lossy compression, prioritizing visually perceptible details over exact pixel-wise fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Before "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models," the landscape of deep generative models was primarily dominated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), autoregressive models, Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), and Normalizing Flows.

- **GANs** (e.g., BigGAN, StyleGAN) were the undisputed champions of high-fidelity image synthesis, producing incredibly realistic samples. However, they were notoriously difficult to train, suffered from mode collapse, and lacked a direct mechanism for likelihood estimation.
- **Autoregressive Models** (e.g., PixelCNN, WaveNet) excelled at modeling exact likelihoods and generating diverse samples, but their sequential generation process was slow and their samples, while high-quality, often lagged behind GANs in perceptual realism for complex images.
- **VAEs** offered tractable likelihoods and stable training but often produced blurrier samples compared to GANs.
- **Normalizing Flows** provided exact likelihoods and invertible transformations, allowing for efficient inference and generation, but their architectural constraints could limit expressivity.
- **Energy-Based Models (EBMs)** and **Score-Based Generative Models**, though conceptually powerful (especially for likelihoods and density estimation), had struggled to achieve the same level of sample quality as GANs on high-resolution images. Jascha Sohl-Dickstein's 2015 paper, which introduced "Diffusion Probabilistic Models" (the conceptual ancestor), laid the theoretical groundwork inspired by non-equilibrium thermodynamics, but did not demonstrate high-quality sample generation. Similarly, Song and Ermon's "Generative Modeling by Estimating Gradients of the Data Distribution" (NCSN, 2019) showed promise for score-based models but still had FID scores significantly higher than leading GANs.

This paper by Ho, Jain, and Abbeel represents a **critical turning point** for diffusion models. It demonstrates, for the first time, that diffusion probabilistic models are not only theoretically sound but are **capable of generating samples of unprecedented quality, directly challenging and often surpassing the state-of-the-art GANs** in metrics like FID score. It revitalized interest in this class of models, showing their practical viability and unlocking their full potential. Furthermore, the paper establishes a novel and crucial theoretical connection between diffusion models and denoising score matching with Langevin dynamics, unifying previously disparate lines of research and providing a deeper understanding of the underlying principles. This theoretical link was key to their empirical success, enabling a simplified and more effective training objective.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research were:

1. **Demonstrate High-Quality Sample Generation with Diffusion Models:** The most pressing motivation was to prove that diffusion probabilistic models, despite their elegant theoretical foundation, could actually produce visually compelling, high-fidelity samples. Previous work had not shown diffusion models to be competitive with other leading generative models in terms of sample quality.
2. **Establish a Deeper Theoretical Understanding and Connection:** The authors aimed to forge a novel and explicit connection between diffusion probabilistic models and denoising score matching with annealed Langevin dynamics. This was motivated by the desire to leverage the insights from score-based models to improve the training and parameterization of diffusion models, and to provide a unified theoretical framework.
3. **Develop an Improved Training Methodology:** Building on the theoretical insights, a key objective was to design a simplified, yet highly effective, training objective that would unlock the high sample quality. The standard variational bound, while theoretically sound, proved suboptimal for practical high-fidelity generation.
4. **Explore Practical Implications beyond Sample Generation:** Beyond just generating images, the authors were motivated to investigate other properties of diffusion models, such as their performance on log-likelihoods, their progressive generation capabilities, and their potential for lossy compression and latent space interpolation. They also aimed to draw connections to autoregressive decoding, suggesting a more generalized form of data ordering.

In essence, the paper sought to transform diffusion models from a theoretically interesting but empirically limited concept into a leading paradigm for high-quality generative modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around a carefully constructed forward diffusion process and a learned reverse (denoising) process, trained via a novel objective derived from the variational lower bound.

#### 3.1. Background: Diffusion Probabilistic Models

Diffusion models are latent variable models that define a Markov chain in two directions:
- **Forward Process (Diffusion Process):** This is a fixed Markov chain that gradually adds Gaussian noise to the data $x_0$ over $T$ timesteps, creating a sequence of noisy latent variables $x_1, \dots, x_T$. As $t \to T$, $x_T$ approaches a simple prior distribution (e.g., standard Gaussian). This process is tractable and allows for closed-form sampling of $x_t$ conditioned on $x_0$.
 $q(x_{1:T}|x_0) = \prod_{t=1}^T q(x_t|x_{t-1})$, where $q(x_t|x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}x_{t-1}, \beta_t I)$.
- **Reverse Process (Sampling Process):** This is a learned Markov chain that attempts to reverse the forward process, gradually denoising $x_T$ back to $x_0$. The transitions are parameterized by a neural network $\mu_\theta(x_t, t)$ and variance $\Sigma_\theta(x_t, t)$.
 $p_\theta(x_{0:T}) = p(x_T) \prod_{t=1}^T p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)$, where $p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t) = \mathcal{N}(x_{t-1};\mu_\theta(x_t,t), \Sigma_\theta(x_t,t))$.

Training is typically performed by optimizing the variational lower bound (ELBO) on the negative log-likelihood, $L = E_q[-log \frac{p_\theta(x_{0:T})}{q(x_{1:T}|x_0)}]$. This ELBO can be decomposed into a sum of KL divergences between the forward process posterior and the learned reverse process transitions, plus a reconstruction term.

#### 3.2. Novel Contributions in Methodology

The authors introduce several key innovations that unlock the model's performance:

1. **Fixed Variance Schedule for Forward Process:** Instead of learning the variances $\beta_t$, the authors fix them to constants, increasing linearly from $\beta_1 = 10^{-4}$ to $\beta_T = 0.02$ over $T=1000$ steps. This simplifies the model and ensures the forward process eventually converts to Gaussian noise.
2. **Specific Parameterization of the Reverse Process Mean Function ($\mu_\theta$):** This is a crucial innovation. Instead of directly predicting the mean $\tilde{\mu}_t(x_t, x_0)$ of the true posterior $q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)$, the neural network $\epsilon_\theta$ is parameterized to predict the **noise component** $\epsilon$ that was added at step $t$ in the forward pass.
 The mean $\mu_\theta(x_t, t)$ is then derived from this predicted noise:
 $\mu_\theta(x_t,t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} \left(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\right)$.
 This reparameterization reveals a direct connection to denoising score matching, where the goal is to predict the noise to estimate the score function (gradient of the log data density).
3. **Fixed Variances for Reverse Process ($\Sigma_\theta$):** The authors set $\Sigma_\theta(x_t,t) = \sigma_t^2 I$ to untrained, time-dependent constants. They found that fixing $\sigma_t^2$ to either $\beta_t$ or $\tilde{\beta}_t$ yielded similar and better results than learning them, contributing to training stability.
4. **Simplified Training Objective ($L_{simple}$):** While the full variational bound $L$ is used for likelihood evaluation, the authors found that optimizing a **simplified objective** during training significantly improves sample quality. This objective is an unweighted mean squared error between the actual noise $\epsilon$ (used to generate $x_t$ from $x_0$) and the model's predicted noise $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$:
 $L_{simple}(\theta) = E_{t,x_0,\epsilon} \left[ \| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon, t) \|^2 \right]$.
 This simplified objective effectively down-weights the loss terms corresponding to small $t$ (where noise is minimal), allowing the network to focus on more substantial denoising tasks. This reweighting is shown empirically to be critical for high sample quality.
5. **Neural Network Architecture:** The reverse process is modeled using a U-Net backbone, similar to an unmasked PixelCNN++ and Wide ResNet, widely used in image synthesis and processing. Time $t$ is incorporated into the network using Transformer-style sinusoidal position embeddings, allowing parameters to be shared across timesteps. Group normalization and self-attention layers are also employed for better performance.
6. **Data Scaling and Discrete Decoder:** Image data is scaled to $[-1, 1]$. For the final step from $x_1$ to $x_0$, a discrete decoder (based on the Gaussian distribution and integrating over pixel bins) is used to handle discrete pixel values, ensuring a lossless codelength for log-likelihood estimation. During sampling, the mean $\mu_\theta(x_1,1)$ is displayed directly without adding noise.

#### 3.3. Sampling Approach

Sampling (Algorithm 2) involves starting from random noise $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and iteratively applying the learned reverse diffusion steps for $t = T, \dots, 1$. Each step denoises the image slightly, guided by the $\epsilon_\theta$ prediction and a carefully derived noise term. The process resembles Langevin dynamics, where $\epsilon_\theta$ acts as a learned gradient of the data density.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's findings demonstrated the groundbreaking potential of diffusion models for generative tasks:

1. **State-of-the-Art Sample Quality:**
 - On the unconditional **CIFAR10** dataset, their model achieved an **Inception Score (IS) of 9.46** and a **state-of-the-art FID score of 3.17**. This FID score surpassed most existing models, including class-conditional GANs (e.g., BigGAN, SNGAN), and was highly competitive with or even better than StyleGAN2+ADA (v1)'s reported 3.26.
 - On **256x256 LSUN** datasets (Church, Bedroom, Cat), the model produced sample quality similar to or better than ProgressiveGAN and competitive with StyleGAN, with FID scores of 7.89 (Church), 4.90 (Bedroom, large model), and 19.75 (Cat). This marked a significant leap for diffusion models in high-resolution image synthesis.
 - Qualitative samples (Fig. 1, 3, 4, 11, 13, 16-19) showcase highly realistic and diverse images across different datasets.

2. **Ablation Studies on Parameterization and Objective:**
 - The **$\epsilon$-prediction parameterization** (predicting the noise) combined with the **simplified training objective ($L_{simple}$)** was found to be crucial for achieving the best sample quality (FID of 3.17 vs. 13.51 with full L).
 - Predicting the mean ($\tilde{\mu}$-prediction) or learning the reverse process variances resulted in significantly poorer sample quality or unstable training. This strongly validates their specific choices for model parameterization and loss function.

3. **Log-Likelihoods vs. Sample Quality:**
 - While sample quality was excellent, the model's lossless log-likelihoods (e.g., $\le$ 3.75 bits/dim on CIFAR10) were not competitive with other likelihood-based models like Sparse Transformers (2.80 bits/dim).
 - The authors interpret this as an **inductive bias towards excellent lossy compression**. They found that the majority of the lossless codelength was consumed to describe "imperceptible image details," suggesting the model prioritizes perceptual quality over exact pixel-wise fidelity.

4. **Progressive Lossy Compression and Generation:**
 - The paper introduces a **progressive lossy compression scheme** based on the diffusion process, mirroring the variational bound. By transmitting data (or generating samples) across different timesteps, they show a rate-distortion curve (Fig. 5).
 - The distortion (RMSE) decreases steeply in the low-rate region, confirming that core image features are encoded with very few bits, while higher rates contribute to fine, imperceptible details.
 - **Progressive generation** (Fig. 6, 10) vividly illustrates the coarse-to-fine nature of the sampling process: large-scale image features appear first, and finer details are progressively filled in over time.

5. **Latent Space Interpolation:** The model demonstrated the ability to perform smooth and meaningful interpolations between source images in the diffusion latent space (Fig. 8, 9). This indicates a well-structured and semantically rich latent representation, allowing for realistic transitions between different image attributes.

6. **Connection to Autoregressive Decoding:** The authors draw an insightful analogy between the Gaussian diffusion model and a generalized autoregressive model. By setting the diffusion process length to data dimensionality and defining specific forward/reverse processes, diffusion models can be seen as training an autoregressive model with a novel, generalized "bit ordering" that is not limited to simple pixel reordering. This suggests that the Gaussian noise schedule provides a beneficial inductive bias for image generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models" is a **landmark publication** that had a profound and transformative impact on the field of generative AI:

1. **Rebirth of Diffusion Models:** This work single-handedly revitalized interest in diffusion models, demonstrating their capability for state-of-the-art image synthesis. Prior to this, diffusion models were largely considered a niche area with theoretical appeal but limited practical generative power. This paper changed that perception entirely, making them a primary focus of research in subsequent years.
2. **Unified Theoretical Framework:** The explicit connection drawn between diffusion models, denoising score matching, and annealed Langevin dynamics was a significant theoretical contribution. It unified previously distinct lines of research, providing a deeper understanding of how these mechanisms relate and can be leveraged for generative modeling. This insight has guided subsequent advancements.
3. **New Paradigm for High-Fidelity Generation:** The paper established a new competitive baseline for high-quality image generation, offering an alternative to GANs that is more stable to train and provides direct likelihood estimation (albeit not always competitive for lossless compression). This opened up new avenues for tackling complex generative tasks.
4. **Practical Tool for Researchers and Practitioners:** The demonstrated strong empirical results, coupled with the open-source implementation, made diffusion models a readily accessible and powerful tool for the broader machine learning community. This accessibility accelerated further research and application.
5. **Potential for Broader Applications:**
 - **Data Compression:** While the progressive compression scheme presented is theoretical, it highlights the inherent lossy compression capabilities of diffusion models, which could be explored for practical data compression solutions, especially as data resolutions increase.
 - **Representation Learning:** The structured latent space and the coarse-to-fine generation process suggest that diffusion models are excellent at learning meaningful hierarchical representations of data, which could be beneficial for various downstream tasks beyond just generation, such as image classification or reinforcement learning.
 - **Creative Applications:** The high quality and controllability of generation (e.g., through interpolation) pave the way for numerous creative applications in art, photography, and potentially music and video synthesis.

The authors also responsibly address the **broader societal impact**, acknowledging the dual nature of generative AI. They highlight concerns such as the generation of malicious deepfakes and the reinforcement of dataset biases, while also pointing to positive applications like data compression, improved representation learning, and creative tools. The paper's contribution has indeed amplified these broader discussions, given the subsequent explosion of diffusion model applications.
