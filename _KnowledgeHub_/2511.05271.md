# DeepEyesV2: Toward Agentic Multimodal Model

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepEyesV2, an agentic multimodal model from Xiaohongshu Inc., integrates programmatic code execution and web search tools into a dynamic reasoning loop for complex problem-solving. This model demonstrates superior coordinated performance across perception, search, and reasoning tasks on a new RealX-Bench, outperforming existing general-purpose MLLMs and showing strong zero-shot generalization to novel tools.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are largely passive, lacking the ability to autonomously invoke external tools for computation, data manipulation, or real-time knowledge retrieval.
- Current MLLMs cannot perform explicit, verifiable operations on visual or numerical data, nor can they access up-to-date external knowledge proactively, limiting accuracy and traceability.
- Direct application of reinforcement learning (RL) alone proves insufficient for reliably teaching complex tool use to MLLMs, leading to unstable behavior like buggy code generation or "reward hacking."

## Method

- **DeepEyesV2** integrates programmatic ==code execution== (for operations like cropping, measuring, and computation) and multimodal web search (for information retrieval) into a unified, iterative reasoning loop.
- The model employs a two-stage training pipeline: a cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase followed by agentic reinforcement learning (RL) using DAPO with a sparse, outcome-driven reward function.
- A new evaluation benchmark, RealX-Bench, was developed to assess the coordinated interplay of perception, search, and reasoning in complex, real-world scenarios, which are often overlooked by existing benchmarks.

## Results

- DeepEyesV2 achieved superior coordinated performance on the RealX-Bench, outperforming leading proprietary models like Gemini 2.5 Pro (**46.0%** accuracy) and GPT-4o (**38.7%** accuracy) in integrated tasks, and improving upon its base model Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** by +**8.4%** on such tasks.
- The model consistently surpassed general-purpose MLLMs and prior grounded reasoning models across various benchmarks, including real-world understanding, mathematical reasoning (e.g., +**7.1%** on MathVerse over Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**), and online searching (**63.7%** on MMSearch, outperforming MMSearch-R1's **53.8%**).
- DeepEyesV2 demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization capabilities, successfully comprehending and utilizing novel tools and adapting to unseen tasks not explicitly included in its training data, such as OCR with grayscaling/dilation and image rotation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A cold-start supervised fine-tuning phase is critical for establishing basic and reliable tool-use patterns in MLLMs before refinement through reinforcement learning.
> - Curated, diverse training data, including perception, reasoning, and long Chain-of-Thought trajectories, is essential for fostering robust and generalizable agentic capabilities.
> - Reinforcement learning enables MLLMs to learn adaptive tool-use strategies, invoking tools only when necessary and balancing internal reasoning with external operations for efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen rapid advancements in their ability to interpret text and images. Existing MLLMs, such as Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, and InternVL3, have demonstrated capabilities in visual question answering, captioning, and general perception. However, these models are largely passive; they can process multimodal inputs and generate responses, but they typically lack the ability to autonomously invoke external tools for computation, data manipulation, or real-time knowledge retrieval. This limitation restricts their reliability and effectiveness in complex reasoning tasks that require more than inherent knowledge or simple interpretation.

The concept of "Thinking with Images," introduced by o3, suggests that models could interleave reasoning with iterative visual analysis and active image manipulation. Efforts to realize this capability include models like DeepEyes and Thyme, which often employ a two-stage training pipeline (cold-start followed by reinforcement learning) to teach fine-grained perception through tools like region cropping or code execution for visual operations. While these models advance image manipulation capabilities, they remain primarily confined to visual tasks and do not proactively access external knowledge.

Concurrently, a separate line of research focuses on augmenting MLLMs with external knowledge acquisition, often through retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) paradigms. More advanced approaches leverage online search, enabling models like MMSearch-R1 to dynamically access broader and more up-to-date information, extending beyond textual queries to multimodal retrieval. These search-oriented models address the knowledge limitations of MLLMs but often lack fine-grained perception or the ability to perform complex operations on visual or numerical data.

DeepEyesV2 positions itself at the intersection of these two research directions. It aims to bridge the gap between models that can perform operations on multimodal data and those that can retrieve external information. By integrating both programmatic code execution (for operations like cropping, measuring, and computation) and web search (for information retrieval) within a unified, dynamic reasoning loop, DeepEyesV2 seeks to develop truly "agentic" multimodal models. This work explores how to enable MLLMs to actively decide when and how to invoke a diverse set of tools, thereby moving beyond passive interpretation towards autonomous, evidence-driven reasoning. The paper also highlights a critical challenge: direct reinforcement learning alone is insufficient to induce robust tool-use behavior in MLLMs, necessitating a structured training approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to construct an agentic multimodal model, named DeepEyesV2, that transcends the limitations of existing MLLMs. This involves enabling the model to not only comprehend text and images but also to actively invoke and seamlessly integrate external tools, such as code execution environments and web search interfaces, into its advanced reasoning processes.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several identified limitations of current MLLMs:

1. **Passivity of Existing MLLMs:** Current multimodal models, despite their capabilities in perception and interpretation, are largely passive. They process provided inputs but do not autonomously decide to interact with external environments or tools. This restricts their ability to engage in complex, multi-step problem-solving that requires dynamic information gathering or manipulation.

2. **Need for Explicit and Verifiable Operations:** Many real-world tasks demand explicit, verifiable operations on visual or numerical data that existing MLLMs cannot perform internally. Examples include fine-grained image manipulations (e.g., cropping, precise measurements), complex quantitative computations, or analysis of visual data beyond simple recognition. The absence of "operation tools" limits their capacity to reason about detailed visual content or solve mathematical problems effectively.

3. **Access to Up-to-Date and Source-Grounded Knowledge:** MLLMs often suffer from inherent knowledge limitations, leading to outdated conclusions or statements without verifiable sources. They cannot proactively access real-time or extensive external knowledge. This underscores the necessity for "information retrieval tools" to fetch up-to-date, source-grounded evidence, thereby improving accuracy, reducing hallucinations, and supporting traceable reasoning.

4. **Incompleteness of Single-Tool Approaches:** Prior research has explored integrating single tools, such as cropping (e.g., DeepEyes) for fine-grained perception or web search (e.g., MMSearch-R1) for information retrieval. However, tasks often require a synergistic combination of these capabilities. A model capable of cropping a specific region for closer inspection and then using that region in an image search, or performing a calculation based on visual data and then verifying it with a text search, represents a significant gap.

5. **Challenges in Learning Tool Use:** Initial pioneer experiments revealed that directly applying reinforcement learning (RL) to MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL for tool invocation leads to unstable and unreliable behavior. Models either generate buggy code, abandon tool use in favor of direct answers, or engage in "reward hacking" by emitting non-executable placeholder code. This demonstrated that a robust cold-start phase is crucial to establish basic tool-use patterns before refinement with RL.

By addressing these motivations, DeepEyesV2 aims to develop a more general, reliable, and extensible framework for multimodal reasoning that can dynamically choose, combine, and use a diverse set of tools as reasoning unfolds.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DeepEyesV2 project employs a comprehensive methodology encompassing model pipeline design, a two-stage training strategy, and the development of a novel evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline of DeepEyesV2

DeepEyesV2 operates as an agentic multimodal model that integrates programmatic code execution and web retrieval as complementary and interleavable tools within a single reasoning trajectory. The process begins with an image input and a user query:
- **Initial Plan Generation:** DeepEyesV2 first generates an initial reasoning plan and assesses whether the question can be solved directly through internal reasoning or necessitates tool invocation.
- **Tool Invocation:** If tools are required, the model emits executable Python code or issues web search queries.
 - **Code Execution:** Python code is executed in a sandboxed environment. It can produce structured outputs such as transformed images (e.g., cropped, marked), numerical measurements, computed arrays, or execution logs. Visualizations generated by `plt.show` are also captured.
 - **Web Search:**
 - **Image Search:** Utilizes SerpAPI to perform a Google Lens search using the original image, returning the top five visually matched webpages with thumbnails and titles.
 - **Text Search:** Uses Google Search based on a textual query, returning the five most relevant webpages with titles and snippets.
- **Iterative Reasoning:** All tool outputs are converted into observations and appended to the model's context. DeepEyesV2 then re-evaluates its state, refines its hypotheses, and may plan further tool invocations (additional code, searches, or both). This reasoning–tool–integration loop continues iteratively until a conclusive answer is produced.

This pipeline provides three advantages: expanded analytical capabilities through executable code, active and real-time knowledge seeking via multimodal web evidence, and iterative, interleaved multi-tool reasoning.

#### 3.2. Pioneer Experiments

To understand the feasibility of acquiring tool-use directly, an experiment was conducted on Qwen2.5-VL using reinforcement learning alone. The observations revealed that in early stages, generated Python code was often buggy or failed to execute. As training progressed, the model tended to abandon code generation in favor of short reasoning chains and direct answers. Even with tool usage bonuses, the model exhibited "reward hacking," generating non-executable placeholder comments instead of meaningful code. These findings demonstrated that direct RL alone is insufficient for reliably learning complex tool use, motivating a multi-stage training approach.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy: Two-Stage Pipeline

DeepEyesV2 employs a two-stage training pipeline:

1. **Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Data Collection Principles:** A high-quality, diverse dataset was curated based on:
 - **Diverse Tasks:** Covering a wide range of multimodal challenges (perception, reasoning, search).
 - **Verifiability:** Questions reformulated into structured, open-ended QA format, excluding unverifiable or ambiguous examples.
 - **Appropriate Difficulty:** Leveraging Qwen2.5-VL-7B as a baseline evaluator, only questions where the baseline answered correctly at most twice were retained, filtering out trivial cases.
 - **Beneficial Tool Integration:** Examples were categorized based on whether tool usage improved accuracy. Tool-solvable cases were reserved for RL, while harder unsolved cases (even with tools) were used for cold-start SFT.
 - **Data Sources:** Data was collected from various perception-oriented tasks (V*, ArxivQA, Pixmo Counting, TallyQA, SeekWorld), reasoning tasks (ReVisual, MathCoder, Retool), and search-related tasks (MMSearch-R1, VGR, Chain-of-Focus, VLM-R3). The cold-start subset also included long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning data.
 - **Trajectory Synthesis:** Detailed, step-by-step reasoning traces, including explicit tool-invocation markers (code snippets), were elicited from large proprietary models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4). Each declared tool call was executed, and its output fed back to the model for continued reasoning. Only trajectories with correct final answers and error-free code were retained for the cold-start dataset.

2. **Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Objective:** To further enhance the model's ability to integrate tools dynamically after establishing basic patterns during cold-start.
 - **Algorithm:** DAPO (Direct Acyclic Policy Optimization) was utilized.
 - **Reward Function:** A sparse, outcome-driven reward system was used, consisting of two components:
 - `R_acc`: Accuracy reward for matching the final answer to the ground truth.
 - `R_format`: A penalty for violating required output formats.
 - The total reward was defined as `R = R_acc + R_format`. No complex reward engineering was employed.

#### 3.4. RealX-Bench: A New Evaluation Benchmark

Recognizing the limitations of existing benchmarks that often evaluate isolated capabilities, DeepEyesV2 introduces **RealX-Bench**, a comprehensive benchmark designed to assess the coordinated interplay of perception, search, and reasoning in complex real-world scenarios.
- **Design Principles:**
 - **Challenging:** Questions require precise localization, multi-hop evidence gathering, and multi-step logical composition with intermediate consistency checks.
 - **Real-World:** All questions are grounded in realistic scenarios and content distributions.
 - **Objectivity:** Each question has a short, unique, and automatically verifiable answer.
- **Construction:** The benchmark was constructed through a four-stage workflow: data collection from the internet, expert QA annotation, difficulty and category labeling (perception, search, reasoning, and integration), and final quality control.
- **Statistics:** RealX-Bench comprises 300 question-answer pairs across five domains (Daily Life, Media, Sports, Knowledge, Games). A notable 24% of questions are simultaneously challenging across all three core abilities, emphasizing the integrated performance assessment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the development and performance of DeepEyesV2.

#### 4.1. Pioneer Experiments on Tool Learning

Direct application of reinforcement learning on Qwen2.5-VL for tool use proved ineffective. Early attempts at code generation resulted in buggy or non-executable code. Continuous training led the model to bypass tool use, favoring direct answers. Even with explicit tool usage bonuses, the model exhibited "reward hacking," generating placeholder code rather than functional code. These observations substantiated the necessity of a cold-start stage to establish basic tool-use patterns.

#### 4.2. Performance on RealX-Bench

Evaluation on the newly introduced RealX-Bench highlighted challenges for existing models and the strengths of DeepEyesV2:
- **General Performance Gap:** Even leading proprietary models, such as Gemini 2.5 Pro (46.0% accuracy) and GPT-4o (38.7% accuracy), performed substantially below human performance (70.0%).
- **Integration Difficulty:** Current models exhibited significant limitations in tasks requiring the coordination of all three abilities (perception, search, reasoning). For instance, Gemini's accuracy on integrated tasks was 27.8%, much lower than its average.
- **Search Benefits:** Incorporating search tools, particularly a combination of text and image search, yielded notable performance improvements. Text-only search generally provided larger gains than image-only search, indicating room for improved integration of visual search results.
- **DeepEyesV2's Superior Coordination:** DeepEyesV2 demonstrated superior performance compared to other open-source models and models restricted to a single tool. It particularly excelled on tasks demanding coordination across perception, search, and reasoning, outperforming its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B by a significant margin (e.g., +8.4% on integration tasks).

#### 4.3. Results on Other Benchmarks

DeepEyesV2 was evaluated across diverse existing benchmarks:
- **Real-World, OCR, and Chart Understanding:** DeepEyesV2 consistently surpassed general-purpose MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision) and prior grounded reasoning models (e.g., DeepEyes, Thyme). It even outperformed Qwen2.5-VL-32B on some benchmarks (e.g., V*Bench, HRBench), indicating that dynamic tool invocation effectively enhances comprehension of fine-grained details in real-world scenes.
- **Multimodal Reasoning (Mathematical):** On mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MathVista, MathVerse), DeepEyesV2 consistently outperformed existing open-source MLLMs and text-only multimodal reasoning models. It achieved a +7.1% gain on MathVerse (52.7% accuracy) compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, underscoring the benefit of tool use for mathematical problem-solving.
- **Online Searching:** DeepEyesV2 demonstrated superior search capabilities on knowledge-intensive and retrieval-based tasks (e.g., FVQA-test, MMSearch, SimpleVQA). It achieved 63.7% on MMSearch, significantly outperforming MMSearch-R1 (53.8%) and general-purpose proprietary models like GPT-4o.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Training Data

- **Cold Start Data Ablation:** Experiments on the SFT dataset demonstrated that data diversity is crucial. Training on perception data improved perception tasks, while reasoning data had limited or negative gains when used in isolation. The inclusion of long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories substantially enhanced reasoning and tool use. The best overall results were achieved by combining perception, reasoning, and CoT data, highlighting their complementary benefits.
- **RL Data Ablation:** Ablation studies on RL data showed that diversity is also critical for reinforcement learning. Training with only perception or reasoning data improved performance in those specific areas but degraded others. Combining diverse RL data (perception, reasoning, and search) led to balanced and robust overall performance across all task types.

#### 4.5. In-Depth Analysis of Tool-Use Behavior

- **Task-Dependent Tool Distribution:** DeepEyesV2 exhibited clear task-dependent tool preferences. For perception tasks, it primarily used image operations (e.g., cropping). For OCR tasks, it also performed region marking and numerical computations. Reasoning tasks were dominated by mathematical computations, and search tasks primarily invoked search tools.
- **RL-Induced Distribution Shift:** Reinforcement learning led to a shift in tool distribution. Post-RL, the model performed more numerical operations across tasks and began to integrate image manipulation tools with search in search-oriented benchmarks, indicating a more synergistic use of heterogeneous tools.
- **Adaptive Thinking:** Prior to RL, the model over-relied on tools. After RL, the tool invocation rate decreased significantly. DeepEyesV2 learned to solve simpler problems directly and invoked tools only when necessary, demonstrating adaptive reasoning and a balance between textual reasoning and tool calls.
- **Training Dynamics:** During RL training, the average number of tool calls steadily decreased, but the variance remained high. This suggested that the model learned adaptive strategies rather than converging to a fixed number of invocations, maintaining complexity for challenging queries while being efficient for simpler ones.

#### 4.6. Zero-Shot Generalization (Appendix A.5)

DeepEyesV2 demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization capabilities on the TIR-Bench, which featured tasks and tools not included in its training data (e.g., OCR with grayscaling/dilation, image rotation, maze solving). The post-RL model successfully comprehended and utilized these novel tools, and its performance substantially outperformed the baseline, confirming its adaptability to unseen tasks and tool types. Equipping DeepEyesV2 with a new rotation function call further improved performance on a rotated OCR task, with the model adaptively invoking the new function, indicating strong generalization in function calling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DeepEyesV2 and the accompanying research have several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence:

1. **Advancement Towards Agentic AI:** DeepEyesV2 represents a tangible step toward building truly agentic multimodal models. By demonstrating the ability to actively invoke and integrate diverse external tools into a dynamic reasoning loop, it pushes the boundaries beyond passive perception and interpretation, fostering a more autonomous and proactive form of AI.

2. **Validated Training Paradigm:** The research highlights and empirically validates the necessity of a two-stage training pipeline (cold-start SFT followed by reinforcement learning) for robust and reliable tool-use in MLLMs. This addresses the limitations observed with direct reinforcement learning and provides a practical methodology for future development efforts in agentic systems.

3. **Importance of Curated Data:** The study underscores the critical role of carefully curated, diverse, and progressively challenging training data. The findings on the complementary benefits of perception, reasoning, and long Chain-of-Thought data, as well as the necessity of diverse RL data, offer valuable guidance for data scientists and model developers.

4. **Novel Evaluation Standard:** The introduction of RealX-Bench provides a much-needed comprehensive benchmark for evaluating integrated multimodal reasoning. By focusing on the coordinated interplay of perception, search, and reasoning in real-world scenarios, it sets a higher standard for assessing agentic intelligence and helps identify specific areas where current models fall short, thereby guiding future research.

5. **Enhanced Model Capabilities and Reliability:** DeepEyesV2 demonstrates marked performance improvements across a wide range of real-world understanding, mathematical reasoning, and search-intensive tasks. By leveraging tools for fine-grained analysis and external knowledge retrieval, the model achieves higher accuracy, potentially reduces hallucinations, and enables more verifiable and traceable reasoning processes.

6. **Insights into Adaptive Tool Use:** The in-depth analysis of DeepEyesV2's tool-use behavior reveals adaptive, task-dependent patterns and the emergence of efficient, context-aware tool invocation post-reinforcement learning. These insights contribute to a deeper understanding of how models learn to strategically utilize tools, reflecting a more sophisticated form of problem-solving.

7. **Guidance for the Research Community:** The detailed exploration of data construction, training methods, and evaluation protocols, combined with the comprehensive analysis of DeepEyesV2's performance and tool-use dynamics, offers practical guidance and foundational knowledge for researchers and practitioners working on the next generation of agentic multimodal models. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization capabilities further suggest a pathway for developing versatile agents capable of adapting to novel tools and tasks.
