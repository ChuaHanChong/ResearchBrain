# What-If World: A Causal Benchmark for General World Models in Embodied Scenarios

> [!summary] Summary
> The "What-If World" benchmark introduces a contrastive intervention methodology to evaluate the causal sensitivity of video generation models when used as world simulators for embodied scenarios. It found that current state-of-the-art models do not reliably simulate distinct physical outcomes in response to subtle input variations, with no model exceeding 52% on paired causal evaluation metrics.

## Problem

- Existing video generation benchmarks primarily assess visual realism or isolated physical plausibility of single video clips, rather than evaluating how model outputs change appropriately in response to varied inputs.
- Current models can generate individually plausible videos for different inputs (e.g., "gentle braking" vs. "hard braking") but often produce nearly identical outcomes, failing to capture critical causal differences.
- Embodied AI applications like autonomous driving and robotic manipulation require world models to accurately simulate causal relationships between actions or conditions and outcomes for reliable training and planning, a capability not addressed by prior evaluations.

## Method

- Defined a ==taxonomy of six physical primitives== (e.g., Surface Friction, Force/Degree) across Environment and Interaction domains, relevant for embodied scenarios, to systematically test causal mechanisms.
- Constructed a benchmark dataset of ==319 contrastive prompt pairs==, anchored on real-world initial frames, where each pair differs in only one semantic variable to create ==controlled causal probes==.
- Developed the **APEO** (==Adherence, Physics, Environment, Outcome==) evaluation framework, employing a ==VLM judge== to score generated video pairs on both individual video plausibility and comparative causal correctness across four dimensions.

## Results

- No state-of-the-art video generation model achieved an APEO score above **52%** on paired causal metrics, with open-source models clustering around **28%**, revealing substantial room for improvement in causal sensitivity.
- Single-video evaluation scores consistently overestimated model capabilities, showing a significant average performance drop (e.g., **52.2 percentage points** for Physics) when transitioning to paired causal evaluation.
- Model rankings shifted considerably between single-video and paired evaluations, demonstrating that high visual fidelity does not reliably predict strong causal reasoning ability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Identified a "contrastive bottleneck" where models produce individually plausible videos but often fail to reflect distinct causal outcomes in paired comparisons, a limitation invisible to prior single-video evaluation paradigms.
> - Model performance in causal sensitivity correlates with the visual prominence of an intervention's consequences rather than its underlying physical complexity, suggesting reliance on superficial cues.
> - Temporal sequencing of actions proved to be a consistent challenge across all models, indicating a universal difficulty in simulating precise timing and relational coupling, regardless of model scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Current advancements in video generation models have positioned them as potential general-purpose world simulators for embodied applications, such as autonomous driving and robotic manipulation. Existing benchmarks for these models primarily evaluate visual realism, 3D consistency, spatial-temporal coherence, or the physical plausibility of individual generated clips. Examples include VBench, EvalCrafter, VideoPhy, and PhyGenBench. However, these benchmarks typically assess each video in isolation. A model might generate two visually convincing videos for "gentle braking" and "hard braking," yet produce nearly identical outcomes, a failure that current single-video evaluation metrics cannot detect.

Concurrently, a separate line of research focuses on causal benchmarks for visual understanding, such as CLEVRER and NExT-QA. These benchmarks test a model's ability to perceive causal structures within *provided* videos, rather than its capacity to *generate* physically correct causal dynamics from scratch in response to varied inputs. Image editing benchmarks, like InstructPix2Pix, similarly focus on spatial changes in still images without requiring temporal dynamics or causal inference in generation.

The "What-If World" benchmark addresses a notable gap between these two research streams. It focuses on evaluating the causal sensitivity of video generation models by assessing whether a controlled change in a model's input prompt leads to a correspondingly controlled, physically predicted change in its video output. This work introduces a comparative, "contrastive intervention" methodology that distinguishes it from prior evaluations, which largely overlook the generative model's ability to simulate differential physical consequences. The paper argues that this capability is critical for applications like action-conditioned simulation, model-based planning, and policy training, where the reliability of a simulated world depends on its accurate response to variations in controlled variables.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a benchmark for evaluating the causal sensitivity of video generation models when operating as world simulators in embodied scenarios. The authors identify a fundamental limitation in current models: their outputs may appear plausible individually, but they often fail to reflect distinct physical outcomes when their inputs are subtly varied to represent different causal interventions. This failure mode is critical for embodied AI applications where models are expected to simulate how different actions or environmental conditions lead to different future states.

The motivation for developing What-If World stems from several key observations and challenges:

1. **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Current video generation benchmarks primarily focus on visual quality or isolated physical plausibility of single videos. They do not assess whether a model's output *changes appropriately* when its input prompt is modified to reflect a different physical condition or action. This means a model could generate two visually convincing videos that are causally identical, rendering it unreliable for comparative tasks.
2. **Importance for Embodied AI:** In applications like autonomous driving and robotic manipulation, video generation models are increasingly used as "world simulators" to train agents or plan actions. For these uses, it is crucial that the simulator accurately reflects the causal relationships between actions/conditions and outcomes. If a simulator produces similar rollouts for "braking gently" versus "braking hard," it cannot be used to compare the effectiveness of different braking actions.
3. **Complex Nature of Causal Sensitivity Evaluation:** Probing causal sensitivity in generative models presents multiple difficulties:
 - **Defining Intervention Variables:** A clear taxonomy of valid physical intervention variables is lacking in existing work, making it challenging to systematically test causal mechanisms.
 - **Controlled Comparison:** Ensuring *ceteris paribus* (all else being equal) in a generative setting is difficult, as models are text-driven and the shared initial state and single-variable change must be carefully engineered in the input.
 - **Diagnosing Failure Modes:** Causal sensitivity is not a monolithic property. Failures can occur if the intervention is not executed, if videos execute but do not diverge, or if divergence occurs due to scene drift rather than the intended intervention. Existing metrics cannot differentiate these specific failure types.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are structured around three core questions:
- **What to test:** To define a taxonomy of physical variables suitable for intervention in embodied video generation.
- **How to test:** To establish a pipeline for constructing controlled contrastive prompt pairs anchored on real-world initial frames.
- **How to evaluate:** To develop a multi-dimensional evaluation framework that jointly scores video pairs, separating per-video plausibility from causal correctness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The What-If World benchmark employs a three-stage methodology to evaluate the causal sensitivity of video generation models: Taxonomy Definition, Dataset Construction, and APEO Causal Evaluation.

**Stage 1: Taxonomy Definition (What to Test)**
The first stage involved identifying physical variables that govern real-world outcomes. Two domain experts (one in autonomous driving, one in robotic manipulation) independently reviewed 1,000 real-world videos from nuScenes (autonomous driving) and DROID (robotic manipulation) datasets. Candidate variables were selected based on three criteria:
1. **Physically fundamental:** Driven by a real physical mechanism, not just stylistic or perceptual changes.
2. **Embodiment-shared:** Applicable to both driving and robotics, to ensure general physical sensitivity testing.
3. **Operationally isolable:** Capable of being changed while holding all other variables fixed, enabling controlled causal evaluation.
Through a process of joint adjudication and merging based on shared underlying causal mechanisms, the experts established a taxonomy of six physical primitives, organized into two domains:
- **Environment Domain (D_env):** External conditions that influence action outcomes. This includes Surface Friction (e.g., dry asphalt vs. icy road), Material & Medium (e.g., clear weather vs. rain, rigid block vs. deformable sponge), and Obstacle Configuration (e.g., clear path vs. static obstacle).
- **Interaction Domain (D_int):** Parameters of the agent's action itself. This includes Spatial Alignment (e.g., lateral lane offset, gripper closure position), Force/Degree (e.g., light vs. hard braking, gentle vs. forceful push), and Temporal Sequencing (e.g., accelerating before vs. after a car speeds up, gripper closing before vs. after reaching a target).

**Stage 2: Benchmark Construction (How to Test)**
This stage focuses on creating the test instances as controlled probes to ensure that any observed divergence in generated videos is attributable to the intended intervention. Each test instance is built following two requirements:
1. **State fixation:** Both generated videos (`V+` and `V-`) must originate from the same initial state. This is achieved by anchoring each test on a real image frame `x0` extracted from a dataset, combined with textual descriptions of the camera perspective and initial scene state. For environmental primitives where `x0` might contradict a prompt (e.g., sunny road for an "icy surface" prompt), `x0` is minimally edited using a VLM (Nano Banana) to align with both prompts, ensuring only the target environmental condition is altered.
2. **Single-variable change:** The two prompts (`p+` and `p-`) in a pair differ in exactly one semantic variable, specifically within the agent action description. For example, `p+` might describe "accelerates aggressively," while `p-` describes "brakes sharply." Critically, both prompts *withhold* the physical consequence, forcing the model to simulate the outcome from `x0` and the varied action, rather than retrieving it from text priors.
The dataset comprises 319 such prompt pairs built on real frames from nuScenes and DROID, spanning the defined taxonomy across 18 sub-categories (11 for autonomous driving, 7 for robotic arm manipulation).

**Stage 3: APEO Causal Evaluation (How to Evaluate)**
The APEO framework is introduced for scoring the generated video pairs. It comprises four dimensions, each scored as a binary success (1) or failure (0), designed to diagnose specific failure modes:
1. **Adherence (A):** Checks if the intervention action prescribed by the prompt is executed by the model.
2. **Physics (P):** Checks if the motion and dynamics within the video are physically consistent (e.g., no teleportation, morphing).
3. **Environment (E):** Checks if the background, camera viewpoint, and non-target objects remain stable and consistent.
4. **Outcome (O):** Checks if the resulting states of `V+` and `V-` terminate in measurably distinct final states, with the divergence aligned to the prediction of the intervened variable.

Dimensions A, P, and E are evaluated in two modes:
- **Single-video (A_s, P_s, E_s):** Assesses whether each video is individually plausible and correctly executes its prompt.
- **Paired-video (A_p, P_p, E_p):** Enforces *ceteris paribus* by comparing `V+` and `V-`. For instance, `A_p` checks if actions are visually distinguishable and aligned; `P_p` checks if trajectories align before intervention and diverge plausibly after; `E_p` checks if backgrounds match.
- **Outcome (O_p)** is evaluated only in paired mode, as it inherently requires a comparative assessment of divergence.

The evaluation is performed at scale by a VLM judge (Gemini 3.1 Pro), which receives the video pair and prompt pair and answers primitive-conditioned binary questions. This VLM judge was validated against 421 human-annotated samples, demonstrating an average agreement of 82.30%, comparable to inter-human agreement.

Nine state-of-the-art video generation models (four open-source, five closed-source) were benchmarked using this protocol, producing 5,742 generated videos for analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of nine state-of-the-art video generation models using the What-If World benchmark yielded several findings regarding their causal reasoning capabilities:

1. **Limited Causal Sensitivity:** No model achieved an APEO score above 52% on the paired metric (`pAvg`), indicating that even the best-performing model (Grok Imagine, 51.7%) failed on nearly half of the causal tasks. Open-source models clustered around 28% `pAvg`. This suggests substantial room for improvement before these models can reliably support action-conditioned simulation or model-based planning.

2. **The Contrastive Bottleneck:** A significant discrepancy was observed between single-video evaluation scores (`sAvg`) and paired evaluation scores (`pAvg`). Every model achieved higher scores on single-video metrics for Adherence (A) and Physics (P) than on their paired counterparts. For example, HunyuanVideo-1.5 scored 64.4% for single-video Physics (`P_s`) but only 12.2% for paired Physics (`P_p`), a difference of 52.2 percentage points. This phenomenon, termed the "contrastive bottleneck," indicates that models frequently generate individually plausible videos that nevertheless fail to differ in the manner predicted by the input intervention. This limitation is often invisible to existing per-video evaluation paradigms. Specifically, among instances where all single-video checks passed, paired physics still failed in 13.1% of cases, and the overall outcome failed in 8.3%.

3. **Shifting Leaderboards:** The ranking of models shifted when comparing single-video aggregate scores (`sAvg`) to paired aggregate scores (`pAvg`). The model leading in `sAvg` often dropped in `pAvg`, and vice-versa, demonstrating that single-video metrics do not reliably predict causal reasoning ability. For instance, Seedance 2.0 ranked highest in `sAvg` but fell to third in `pAvg`, while Grok Imagine became the top performer in `pAvg`.

4. **Correlation with Visual Prominence:** Performance across the six physical primitives varied, appearing to correlate with the visual prominence of the intervention's consequences rather than the underlying physical complexity.
 - **Force/Degree** (e.g., hard vs. gentle braking, producing dramatic motion differences) achieved the highest `O_p` score at 40.4%.
 - **Surface Friction** (e.g., dry vs. icy road, producing subtle trajectory differences and manipulating latent properties invisible in `x0`) yielded a low `O_p` score of 14.2%.
 - Other primitives like **Spatial Alignment** (30.9%), **Material/Medium** (23.9%), **Obstacle Configuration** (15.5%), and **Temporal Sequencing** (15.3%) fell in between. This suggests models are better at mimicking visually familiar patterns of change than at simulating physics based on abstract or latent properties.

5. **Universal Challenge in Temporal Sequencing:** Temporal Sequencing, which involves coordinating the timing of sub-actions relative to each other, proved to be a consistent challenge across all models. Even the best model (Veo 3.1) only achieved an `O_p` of 24.4% for this primitive, and the performance gap between closed-source and open-source models was significantly smaller (5.9 pp) compared to other primitives. This indicates that current model architectures and training strategies may not effectively address the relational coupling and precise timing required for temporal reasoning.

6. **Domain Differences and Balance:** Most models performed better on Autonomous Driving tasks than on Robotic Manipulation tasks, likely due to greater representation of driving data in training datasets. However, the top-performing models (Grok Imagine and Veo 3.1) exhibited relatively balanced performance across both domains (difference of less than 2.2 percentage points), suggesting some degree of domain-general physical reasoning.

7. **Limited Benefit of Outcome Hints:** An ablation study where outcome hints were explicitly included in the generation prompts showed only modest performance gains (an average increase of 4.7 percentage points in `pAvg`). This suggests that the models' failures are not primarily due to an inability to *predict* the outcome, but rather an inability to *render* the correct physical transitions accurately, even when the desired outcome is specified.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "What-If World" benchmark addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of video generation models, particularly those intended as world simulators for embodied AI. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **First Contrastive-Intervention Benchmark:** This work introduces the first benchmark specifically designed to assess the causal sensitivity of video world models through contrastive interventions. By requiring models to generate two videos that diverge precisely as predicted by a single variable change, it directly probes a capability essential for intelligent agents operating in dynamic environments.
2. **Reveals a Fundamental Capability Gap:** The identification and quantification of the "contrastive bottleneck"—where models produce individually plausible videos but fail to correctly differentiate outcomes based on varied inputs—highlights a core limitation overlooked by previous evaluation methods. This indicates that current state-of-the-art generative video systems, despite their visual fidelity, are not yet reliable as world models for tasks requiring comparative or counterfactual reasoning.
3. **Diagnostic Tool for Model Development:** The APEO evaluation framework, with its distinct dimensions (Adherence, Physics, Environment, Outcome) and single-video vs. paired modes, provides a granular diagnostic tool. It can pinpoint specific failure modes, helping researchers understand *where* a model's causal pipeline is breaking down (e.g., failure to execute the action, physical inconsistencies, environmental drift, or incorrect outcome divergence). The six-primitive taxonomy further enables analysis of strengths and weaknesses across different types of physical phenomena.
4. **Guides Future Research Directions:** The findings provide clear directions for future research in generative AI. The observed correlation between performance and visual prominence suggests that models may be relying on superficial visual cues rather than deeper physical understanding. The consistent struggle with temporal sequencing across all models, regardless of scale, indicates a need for architectural innovations or new training paradigms beyond current scaling and data strategies.
5. **Impact on Embodied AI Applications:** The benchmark directly informs the development and deployment of video generation models in high-stakes embodied applications such as:
 - **Action-conditioned Simulation:** Models that cannot reliably differentiate outcomes based on varied actions are unsuitable for training robust policies.
 - **Model-based Planning:** Effective planning relies on accurate simulation of future states under different actions and conditions.
 - **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Policy Training:** Policies learned from causally unreliable simulators would likely exhibit poor generalization and safety.
 What-If World provides a framework for ensuring that these critical systems are built upon world models that possess a foundational understanding of cause and effect.

In summary, What-If World serves as a standardized and reproducible framework for diagnosing current video generation systems' limitations as world models, highlighting a significant capability gap that needs to be addressed for the advancement of embodied artificial intelligence.
