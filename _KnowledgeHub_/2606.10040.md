# Efficient-WAM: A 1B-Parameter World-Action Model with Low-Cost Future Imagination

> [!summary] Summary
> Efficient-WAM, a 1-billion-parameter World-Action Model, achieves up to a 32x speedup in inference latency for robot control compared to prior models, enabling real-time deployment while maintaining competitive performance on both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks. The approach demonstrates that photorealistic future visual prediction is not necessary for effective action generation, prioritizing task-relevant dynamic cues instead.

## Problem

- Existing World-Action Models (WAMs) rely on large, computationally intensive video generators for photorealistic future predictions, leading to high inference latency.
- The high computational cost and hardware requirements of current WAMs hinder their real-time deployment on physical robotic systems.
- There is a need to integrate efficient future visual prediction with action generation without compromising control performance, challenging the assumption that visual fidelity directly correlates with action quality.

## Method

- **Efficient-WAM** employs a compact, 1-billion-parameter architecture with a ==Mixture-of-Transformers== framework, using structured pruning and knowledge distillation from a larger foundation model to create a lightweight video expert.
- It utilizes a multiscale video-latent layout for coarse future prediction, encoding current observations at high resolution and target future frames at lower resolution to reduce visual token count.
- The model implements asymmetric video-action denoising during inference, allocating more steps to action generation while refreshing video predictions with significantly fewer steps for early structural cues.

## Results

- Efficient-WAM-RT achieved an average inference latency of **98 ms** per action chunk on an RTX 4090 GPU, representing a **32x** speedup over the Motus baseline (**3215 ms**).
- In simulation, Efficient-WAM (**1B** parameters) performed comparably or superior to several larger baselines, achieving **86.7%** clean success, demonstrating strong control capabilities with reduced parameter count.
- Efficient-WAM-RT achieved a **66.25%** average success rate across four real-world manipulation tasks on the Astribot S1 robot, slightly outperforming the heavyweight Motus (**63.75%**) and significantly better than π₀.₅ (**53.75%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective robot control does not necessitate photorealistic future visual predictions; task-relevant geometric information, motion tendencies, and contact cues are sufficient.
> - Knowledge distillation and structural pruning from large foundation models can effectively transfer spatiotemporal priors into compact, action-oriented video experts, reducing model size without significant performance degradation.
> - Decoupling denoising schedules for video and action generation allows for significant latency reductions, as essential visual dynamics emerge early, while precise actions require more iterative refinement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot control necessitates an understanding of how physical scenes evolve during interaction. World-Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a paradigm to address this by integrating future visual prediction with action generation. By modeling the temporal evolution of observations, WAMs aim to embed physical dynamics and world priors into control policies, thereby enhancing robot learning capabilities.

The broader landscape of WAMs, including approaches like UWM, GigaWorld-Policy, and Motus, has largely relied on extensive video generators, often with large backbones and dense visual tokens, to achieve photorealistic future predictions. This approach is predicated on the assumption that higher visual fidelity in future predictions correlates with improved action generation. However, this reliance on photorealistic rendering incurs significant computational costs, high inference latency, and substantial hardware requirements, which hinder real-time deployment in physical robotic systems.

Recent research has begun to question the necessity of pixel-level fidelity for effective control. For instance, studies such as VPP have indicated that action generation can remain effective even with reduced denoising steps, and Fast-WAM has explored competitive WAM performance even when explicit future generation is omitted during inference. Other approaches like Being-H0.7 avoid raw-pixel prediction, and WAM variants explore action-centered or asynchronous video-action designs. These works suggest that task-relevant geometric information, motion tendencies, and contact cues might be more critical for policy decisions than photorealistic details.

This research contributes to this evolving understanding by proposing an efficient WAM design that maintains the control benefits of future visual prediction while significantly reducing its inference cost. It positions itself as a refinement of WAM methodology, explicitly challenging the notion that photorealism is a prerequisite for effective action generation. The work also relates to broader efficiency efforts in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and generative robot policies, which involve compact architectures, quantization, early-exit strategies, token compression, and action-sampling acceleration. Unlike these methods that primarily optimize the policy backbone or action sampler, Efficient-WAM specifically targets the video-imagination bottleneck within WAMs. Furthermore, it leverages knowledge distillation and structural pruning techniques, distinct from generic model compression, to transfer spatiotemporal knowledge from a large foundation model into a compact, action-oriented video expert.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the computational burden and latency associated with existing World-Action Models (WAMs). Traditional WAMs, which couple future visual prediction with action generation, often rely on large, computationally intensive video generators to produce photorealistic future scene predictions. This approach leads to high inference latency and substantial hardware demands, making real-time deployment on physical robots challenging.

The key objectives of this research are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the Deployment Bottleneck of WAMs:** The most critical objective is to mitigate the high inference latency inherent in current WAMs. This latency is identified as a major impediment to their practical application in real-world, closed-loop robot control scenarios.
2. **Introduce an "Action-Centric Future Imagination" Design Principle:** The authors propose a fundamental shift in the design philosophy of WAMs. Instead of pursuing photorealistic video generation, the objective is to demonstrate that WAMs can be effectively decoupled from this pursuit. The core hypothesis is that control policies do not require pixel-perfect visual fidelity but rather task-relevant information such as geometry, motion tendencies, and contact cues. The motivation is to capture these essential dynamic cues at a lower computational cost.
3. **Propose Efficient-WAM as a Novel Architecture for Holistic Inference Cost Reduction:** The research aims to develop a new WAM architecture that systematically reduces inference costs by optimizing three key factors:
 - **Model Size:** Reducing the parameter count of the video generation component.
 - **Token Count:** Decreasing the number of visual tokens used for future prediction.
 - **Denoising Steps:** Optimizing the iterative denoising schedule for both video and action generation.
 The motivation here is to integrate these optimizations into a unified system that enables low-latency, real-world deployment while preserving strong world priors and control performance.
4. **Decompose WAM Inference Cost and Demonstrate the Efficacy of Pruning for Efficiency Gains:** A specific objective is to analyze the components contributing to WAM inference cost and demonstrate how structural pruning, guided by knowledge transfer from a large foundation model, allows for:
 - The use of lower-resolution future latents.
 - Shorter sampling schedules for the video prediction branch.
 The motivation is to show that intelligent architectural design and knowledge distillation can concentrate representational capacity on control-critical content, making subsequent efficiency optimizations safer and more effective.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful generative world modeling capabilities and the stringent real-time requirements of embodied robotic control, by challenging and re-evaluating the role of visual fidelity in future imagination for action generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Efficient-WAM is designed to reduce the computational cost of future imagination while preserving its benefits for action generation. The methodology centers on an "action-centric" design principle, where future video prediction is treated as a compact guidance signal rather than a pursuit of photorealistic rendering. The approach factorizes the joint distribution of future visual latents (z_v) and action chunks (a_1:H) as p(z_v, a_1:H | o, l, s) = p_phi(z_v | o, l) * p_theta(a_1:H | o, l, s, z_v), where p_phi predicts future context and p_theta generates actions conditioned on this imagination. The video-side computational cost is formalized as C_video = F_video(M_v, N_v_tok(r_v), K_v), focusing on model size (M_v), number of video tokens (N_v_tok), and video denoising budget (K_v).

The methodology incorporates three main components to compress these factors:

1. **Compact Architecture with World-Knowledge Transfer:**
 - **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT):** The architecture employs a MoT framework, integrating a lightweight video expert and a dedicated action expert.
 - **Structured Pruning and Knowledge Transfer:** To create the compact video expert, the authors prune the large WAN-2.2-5B foundation model. This is achieved by reducing transformer depth and layer width. Instead of random initialization, weights are copied from specific teacher layers (e.g., layers from WAN-2.2-5B's 30 layers) via layer slicing. The pruned student architecture uses 2048 hidden dimensions, 8192 FFN dimensions, and 16 attention heads, with corresponding components extracted from the teacher.
 - **Teacher-Guided Distillation:** To stabilize knowledge transfer and ensure the student inherits essential physical priors (geometry, motion tendencies, contact cues), the standard video flow-matching objective is supplemented with a distillation loss. This loss aligns intermediate hidden states (L_hid via cosine similarity) and temporal changes (L_mot via cosine similarity of frame-to-frame deltas) between the student and the frozen teacher model.
 - **Expert Interaction:** The compact video expert is layer-wise coupled with the action expert. Action tokens attend to video tokens to extract future context. During the main action training, the compact video expert is frozen to maintain stable world priors while optimizing the lightweight action expert for control.

2. **Coarse Future Prediction with Multiscale Video-Latent Layout:**
 - **Multiscale Latent Encoding:** The approach utilizes a multiscale video-latent layout to avoid wasting computational capacity on action-irrelevant visual details. The current observation is encoded by a VAE into high-resolution condition tokens (e.g., 384 × 320). In contrast, target future frames are spatially downsampled (e.g., to 192 × 160) before VAE encoding, resulting in token-sparse, low-resolution future latents.
 - **Unified Visual Context:** Both sets of latents (high-resolution current, low-resolution future) are patchified and concatenated to form a unified visual context.
 - **Action Expert Interaction:** The action expert performs joint video-action attention over this multiscale token sequence, allowing it to leverage high-fidelity details of the current state while using the low-resolution future latents as a coarse dynamic guide.

3. **Asymmetric Video-Action Denoising:**
 - **Decoupled Denoising Schedules:** Recognizing that visual structure and precise control coordinates converge at different rates, the method introduces training-free asymmetric denoising during inference.
 - **Differential Budget Allocation:** A larger denoising budget is allocated to the action branch (e.g., 5 to 10 steps) to generate precise, executable trajectories. The video branch is refreshed with significantly fewer steps (e.g., only the initial 2 steps), as global structural cues emerge early in denoising.
 - **Feature Reuse:** Between video refresh steps, the model reuses cached video features to condition the ongoing action refinement, reducing computational overhead by halting video generation once actionable dynamics are sufficiently clear.

**Training Objectives:**
The system is trained using conditional flow matching. Let x_1 be the target data (clean future video latents x_v_1 or action chunks x_a_1) and x_0 ∼ N(0, I). The objective is L_FM = E_t,x_0,x_1[||f(x_t, t; c) - u_t||^2]. Training proceeds in three phases:
- **Stage 1:** Adapting the compact video expert using L_stage-1 = L_video-FM + λ_distill(L_hid + L_mot). The distillation weight λ_distill decays progressively (0.2 → 0.1 → 0).
- **Stage 2:** Attaching and training the action expert with the video branch frozen, using L_stage-2 = L_action-FM + λ_v L_video-FM.
- **Stage 3:** Co-training both experts end-to-end for unified refinement, using the same joint objective.
A decoupled noise scheduling strategy is applied during the video-action forward pass, where action chunks and future video latents are corrupted using independently sampled flow-matching timesteps, ensuring distinct supervision intensities while maintaining cross-modal interactions via the shared attention backbone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Efficient-WAM in simulation on RoboTwin 2.0 and in real-world manipulation tasks on the Astribot S1 robot, focusing on control performance and inference latency. Two main configurations were tested: Efficient-WAM, a structural baseline demonstrating the compact video expert's capability, and Efficient-WAM-RT, the fully optimized version for real-time deployment. Both variants predict closed-loop action chunks with a horizon of H=16.

#### 4.1. Evaluation in Simulation Environment (RoboTwin 2.0)

- **Compact Architecture Performance (Efficient-WAM):**
 - Efficient-WAM, with 1 billion parameters, achieved 86.7% clean success and 85.7% randomized success.
 - This performance is competitive with, or superior to, several heavier baselines: it outperformed LingBot-VLA (4B parameters, 86.5% clean) and GigaWorld-Policy (5B parameters, 86.4% clean).
 - It trailed the largest baseline, Motus (8B parameters), by a small margin of 2.0% in the clean setting. This indicates that the structurally pruned video branch retains a robust control capability without requiring a massive parameter count.
- **Deployment-Optimized Performance (Efficient-WAM-RT):**
 - Efficient-WAM-RT, which integrates low-resolution future latents and asymmetric denoising, achieved 83.1% clean success and 82.0% randomized success.
 - While showing a slight reduction in success rates compared to Efficient-WAM (a deliberate trade-off for speed), it still outperformed several heavyweight baselines like π₀ (3.3B) and StarVLA-α (2B).

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments (Astribot S1 Robot)

- **Task Success Rate:**
 - Efficient-WAM-RT achieved an average success rate of 66.25% across four diverse real-world tasks (pipette-tray grasping, reagent-bottle transfer, LEGO color sorting, pen uncapping).
 - This performance was slightly higher than the heavyweight WAM baseline Motus (63.75%) and significantly better than π₀.₅ (53.75%), particularly on complex, long-horizon, or fine-grained tasks.
- **Inference Latency:**
 - Efficient-WAM-RT demonstrated an average latency of 98 ms per action chunk on an RTX 4090 GPU.
 - This represents a 32x speedup compared to Motus (3215 ms per chunk) and is also faster than π₀.₅ (113 ms per chunk).
 - The average latency per step was 6.1 ms for Efficient-WAM-RT, compared to 200.9 ms for Motus and 7.1 ms for π₀.₅.
- The results indicate that Efficient-WAM-RT's strategy of prioritizing essential dynamic cues over photorealistic rendering enables highly reactive, real-time manipulation comparable to or better than much larger models, with significantly reduced latency.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (RoboTwin 2.0)

- **Compact Video Expert:**
 - Scaling down the video expert without proper knowledge transfer resulted in significant performance degradation (e.g., Compact-random achieved 69% clean success compared to 86.4% for Full WAN).
 - Inheriting structural priors via layer slicing was critical for maintaining performance (Compact-sliced: 82% clean).
 - Teacher-guided distillation further improved performance and reduced latency (Efficient-WAM with distillation: 87% clean, 430 ms latency vs. Full WAN: 86.4% clean, 2013 ms latency).
- **Future Resolution:**
 - Predicting low-resolution latents (reducing token count from 240 to 60) decreased latency from 430 ms to 377 ms while largely preserving task success (e.g., 87% clean for Efficient-WAM vs. 83% clean with low-resolution latents). This suggests that coarse structural cues are sufficient for the action expert.
- **Asymmetric Video-Action Denoising:**
 - Reducing the video denoising budget from [10 video steps, 10 action steps] to [2 video steps, 10 action steps] decreased latency from 430 ms to 139 ms (a 3.1x speedup).
 - The success rate only marginally dropped from 87.1% to 86.3%, supporting the hypothesis that early-stage video generation captures actionable dynamics sufficiently.

#### 4.4. System-Level Efficiency Analysis and Qualitative Future Prediction

- The combined effect of the three efficiency components (compact expert, low-resolution future latents, asymmetric denoising) led to compounding accelerations. The fully optimized Efficient-WAM-RT, deployed on an RTX 4090, achieved a physical task latency of 98 ms per action chunk, a 30x speedup over standard WAMs.
- Qualitative analysis showed that Efficient-WAM-RT produces visibly coarse, blurred, and ghosted future frames compared to the Full WAN model, yet this degradation in visual fidelity did not result in a proportional drop in control performance. This observation empirically validates the central premise that action-centric structural and dynamic cues are more important than photorealistic appearance for effective control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotic control by addressing a critical deployment bottleneck in World-Action Models (WAMs): the high inference latency associated with photorealistic future visual prediction. The proposed Efficient-WAM framework, particularly its real-time optimized variant (Efficient-WAM-RT), offers several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enabling Real-Time Robot Deployment:** By achieving an average inference latency of approximately 100 ms per action chunk on consumer-grade GPUs (RTX 4090), Efficient-WAM-RT overcomes a major barrier for deploying complex generative world models in real-time, closed-loop robotic systems. This speedup, demonstrated at 30x to 32x over prior heavyweight WAMs, facilitates more reactive and fluid robot behaviors, moving beyond sluggish, open-loop-like execution.
2. **Re-evaluating the Role of Visual Fidelity in Robot Control:** The work empirically demonstrates that photorealistic future prediction is not a prerequisite for effective robot control. By introducing the "action-centric future imagination" principle, it establishes that preserving task-relevant geometry, motion tendencies, and contact cues, even with visibly coarse future predictions, is sufficient for maintaining strong action performance. This challenges a prevalent assumption in generative robot learning and may influence future model design towards more computationally efficient representations.
3. **Holistic Optimization for Efficiency:** The methodology integrates model size reduction through structured knowledge transfer, token-sparse future latent representation, and asymmetric video-action denoising. This holistic approach to efficiency, rather than optimizing a single component, provides a robust blueprint for developing more resource-efficient robot learning models. It shows how distilling world knowledge from large foundation models can be strategically used to create compact, high-performing experts.
4. **Broader Accessibility of Generative Models:** By substantially reducing the computational and hardware demands, Efficient-WAM can make powerful generative world models more accessible for a wider range of robotic applications and research labs that may not have access to high-end, specialized computing infrastructure. This could accelerate innovation in robot learning.
5. **Setting New Benchmarks for Efficiency-Performance Trade-offs:** The comprehensive evaluations in both simulation and real-world scenarios, alongside detailed ablation studies, provide clear evidence of the efficiency-performance trade-offs. The specific configurations (e.g., [2 video steps, 10 action steps] for denoising) and their associated latency and success rates offer valuable insights for practitioners aiming to balance control accuracy with computational budget.
6. **Guiding Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as potential trade-offs in micro-manipulation tasks requiring extreme pixel-level precision and the use of static inference schedules, suggest clear avenues for future research. This includes exploring dynamic compute allocation (adaptive denoising budgets) based on task uncertainty or complexity, and investigating more granular representations for highly precise tasks.

In summary, Efficient-WAM represents a step towards making advanced generative world models a practical tool for real-world robotics by demonstrating that intelligent design for efficiency does not necessitate a significant compromise in control performance.
