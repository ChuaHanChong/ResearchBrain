# 3D Flow Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Generating Flow in 3D Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at GIST developed the 3D Flow Diffusion Policy (3D FDP), a visuomotor learning framework that models scene-level 3D flow as an intermediate representation for action generation. This approach significantly improved robot manipulation success rates, outperforming baselines by up to 2x in real-world tasks and achieving higher success rates in complex simulated environments.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policies often overlook fine-grained, localized motion cues crucial for precise and contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- Many current approaches, including object-centric representations, struggle to capture comprehensive scene-level dynamics and how actions propagate through an entire environment.
- Prior flow-based methods for robotics primarily operate in 2D image space, lacking the 3D spatial precision necessary for effective physical manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces **3D ==Flow Diffusion Policy==** (3D FDP), a framework that learns visuomotor policies by explicitly modeling scene-level 3D flow, captured by sampled query points, as a structured intermediate representation.
- Decomposes the policy into two jointly trained conditional diffusion models: a flow predictor for future 3D flow and an action generator conditioned on observations and the predicted flow sequence.
- Employs a scalable method for generating ground-truth 3D flow in real-world settings by leveraging 2D point trackers (CoTracker) and depth information to lift 2D trajectories into 3D space.

## Results

- In simulation (MetaWorld benchmark), 3D FDP achieved the highest success rates across all difficulty levels, outperforming DP3 by **4.2** percentage points on hard tasks (**55.6%** vs. **52.4%**) and by **4.0** percentage points on medium tasks (**55.3%** vs. **51.1%**).
- On a challenging real-world benchmark of 8 tasks, 3D FDP demonstrated an average success rate of **56.9%**, more than double the **27.5%** achieved by the DP3 baseline.
- 3D FDP uniquely succeeded in complex real-world tasks involving long-horizon or non-prehensile interactions (e.g., **30%** for Hang, **20%** for Shelve, **35%** for Non-prehensile Manipulation), where DP3 consistently failed with **0%** success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling scene-level 3D flow provides superior interaction-aware spatial grounding compared to direct observation-to-action mappings or object-centric pose predictions, especially for precise and contact-rich tasks.
> - Integrating 3D flow prediction directly into a unified diffusion architecture ensures action generation is consistent with and explicitly conditioned on predicted scene dynamics, enhancing policy robustness and generalizability.
> - Dense, scene-level 3D flow, captured by query points across the entire environment, is more informative for complex manipulation than sparse or object-only flow, improving understanding of broad action consequences.
