# Reproducible scaling laws for contrastive language-image learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers systematically investigated scaling laws for contrastive language-image models using public LAION datasets, revealing how performance scales with model size, data quantity, and training samples, and crucially, how pre-training data distribution influences task-specific scaling behaviors. This open-source study provides a reproducible framework for understanding large-scale multimodal AI, indicating that gains continue with scale, but the optimal scaling strategy depends on the underlying dataset.

## Problem

- Prior research on scaling laws primarily focused on uni-modal models (language or vision) and often relied on private datasets and proprietary models.
- The influential CLIP model demonstrated strong multimodal capabilities, but its training dataset (WIT-400M) was private, hindering systematic, reproducible investigations of scaling trends in this domain.
- A lack of open and reproducible large-scale studies made it difficult for the broader research community to understand and verify fundamental scaling properties of multimodal AI models.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic empirical study of ==scaling laws for contrastive language-image learning== using OpenCLIP models (ViT-B/32 to ViT-g/14) and publicly available LAION datasets (LAION-80M, LAION-400M, LAION-2B).
- Models were trained using an open-source framework on supercomputing infrastructure, varying ==model size, data size, and total samples seen== (up to 34 billion) to explore scaling dimensions.
- Performance was evaluated comprehensively across diverse downstream tasks, including ==zero-shot classification, image/text retrieval, linear probing==, and full fine-tuning, to identify generalizable scaling trends.

## Results

- Performance across zero-shot classification, retrieval, linear probing, and fine-tuning consistently follows **power-law relationships** with increased scale, confirming scaling laws for multimodal models.
- OpenCLIP models (LAION-trained) demonstrate stronger scaling for zero-shot image retrieval tasks compared to OpenAI's CLIP, while OpenAI's CLIP shows stronger scaling for zero-shot classification (e.g., OpenCLIP α=**−0.11** vs. CLIP α=**−0.16** on ImageNet).
- Derived power laws enable predictions for larger scales, estimating that a ViT-G/14 CLIP model trained on **68 billion samples** could achieve **81.9%** zero-shot ImageNet accuracy and **7**6.99%** Recall@5** on MS-COCO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling laws observed in unimodal learning extend to contrastive language-image models, with consistent power-law improvements across diverse downstream tasks as model size, data size, and samples seen increase.
> - The specific pre-training data distribution critically influences scaling behavior and task-specific performance; for instance, models trained on LAION datasets show stronger scaling for retrieval tasks, while OpenAI's CLIP (trained on private WIT-400M) exhibits stronger scaling for zero-shot classification.
> - Performance improvements plateau if one scaling dimension (model size, data quantity, or training steps) is fixed while others are increased, highlighting the necessity of balanced scaling across all dimensions.
