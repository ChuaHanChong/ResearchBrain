# GLoRI: Closed-Loop Whole-Body Tracking with Global-Local Reference Interaction for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GLoRI is a closed-loop whole-body controller for humanoid loco-manipulation that splits the motion reference into a placement-invariant local prior and a world-frame global reference with pose-error feedback. Its GLoRI-Net fuses them via Global-Local Cross Attention, using local keypoint tokens as queries. Tracking only head, hands, and root, it reaches 100% completion and 6.44 cm global MPJPE on held-out HuMoTo motions.

## Problem

- Most reference-conditioned whole-body controllers express targets in local frames, gaining invariance but never constraining world placement, so root and foot errors accumulate into global drift.
- Globally aware controllers like CLONE, CLOT, and ScaleBFM concatenate global observations without a dedicated mechanism modeling how global correction should reshape the local motion prior.
- Existing global-feedback systems center on teleoperation, where a human closes the loop; autonomous references from visuomotor or vision-language-action models have no such human correction.

## Method

- Reference observation split by control role: a placement-invariant local keypoint prior plus a global motion reference (world-frame and current-root-frame targets, pose differences) and a global root reference with trajectory feedback.
- **GLoRI-Net**'s ==Global-Local Cross Attention== uses local keypoint tokens as queries and global motion tokens as keys and values, with residual connection and layer norm preserving motion structure while injecting correction.
- Root feature encoded separately (it defines the local frame, so has no local counterpart), fused with refined keypoint tokens, then temporally encoded; ==PPO== trains the policy with tracking plus regularization rewards.

## Results

- On unseen HuMoTo motions GLoRI hits **100%** completion and **6.44 cm** g-MPJPE versus **11.84 cm** for CLOT and **9.00 cm** for the whole-body ScaleBFM variant, with root error **1.52 cm** against **10.37 cm**.
- Ablation: dropping global observations raises g-MPJPE to **9.19 cm** and drops completion to **97%**; replacing direct concatenation fusion with cross attention cuts g-MPJPE from **5.39** to **4.47 cm**.
- Direct Isaac Gym to MuJoCo transfer without fine-tuning retains **97.0%** completion at **4.19 cm** on seen and **93.9%** at **7.53 cm** on unseen motions; real Unitree G1 succeeds in **7 of 9** trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supplying global observations is insufficient on its own; how they interact with the local prior matters, and a structured query-from-local attention beats undifferentiated concatenation.
> - Tracking reward supervision alone cannot suppress inference-time drift because correction requires state-dependent feedback actually present in the observation.
> - Sparse task-space references for head, hands, and root can outperform dense full-body tracking inputs on world-frame accuracy, easing interfaces to upstream motion generators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is progressing towards enabling robots to operate autonomously in complex, human-centric environments such as factories and homes. A critical capability for this goal is loco-manipulation, which involves simultaneous movement through an environment and interaction with objects. Early approaches to humanoid control often decoupled locomotion from manipulation, prioritizing stability. More recent developments have leveraged learning-based motion imitation from human demonstrations or sparse pose cues to develop unified whole-body control policies.

Despite these advancements, existing whole-body controllers face limitations, particularly concerning spatial accuracy in autonomous loco-manipulation. Many reference-conditioned controllers predominantly express motion targets in local coordinate frames. While this approach enhances invariance and tracking robustness, it lacks explicit constraints on absolute spatial placement within the world frame, which can lead to the accumulation of global errors in the robot's root and end effectors. Such global displacement can cause interaction failures, especially in tasks requiring high precision, like grasping an object.

A few recent methods have begun to incorporate global references and localization feedback, but these are often centered on teleoperation scenarios. In teleoperation, a human operator continuously adjusts commands, effectively compensating for tracking errors. However, for fully autonomous loco-manipulation, where references are generated by upstream visuomotor or vision-language-action models without human intervention, precise execution in the global workspace becomes paramount. This necessitates a control framework that can explicitly integrate global correction with local motion guidance to maintain both coordinated motion structure and accurate world-frame placement. GLoRI positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenge of achieving precise global whole-body tracking in autonomous settings, aiming to bridge the gap between local motion guidance and world-frame spatial accuracy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a closed-loop whole-body controller for humanoid robots that achieves accurate world-frame motion tracking, a critical requirement for precise loco-manipulation tasks. This objective is motivated by two principal limitations identified in current whole-body control systems:

1. **Accumulation of Global Errors from Local Reference Tracking:** Many existing reference-conditioned controllers define motion targets in local coordinate frames (e.g., relative to the robot's root). While this approach offers benefits like invariance to absolute position and robustness in tracking the *structure* of motion, it fundamentally lacks explicit constraints on the robot's absolute spatial placement in the world. As a result, even small errors in root or foot placement can accumulate over time, leading to significant global displacement. This global drift can cause the robot's end effectors (e.g., hands) to miss their intended interaction targets, directly leading to task failure in loco-manipulation. The motivation here is to develop a mechanism that can preserve the coordinated local motion while explicitly constraining and correcting the robot's global position and orientation.

2. **Inadequate Global Integration for Autonomous Loco-Manipulation:** While some recent approaches have incorporated global references and feedback, they often remain reliant on teleoperation, where human operators can provide continuous, real-time adjustments to compensate for tracking inaccuracies. In contrast, autonomous loco-manipulation systems generate references offline or via high-level AI models (e.g., visuomotor or vision-language-action models). These autonomous systems lack the human-in-the-loop correction mechanism. Therefore, there is a strong motivation to integrate precise global whole-body tracking directly into the autonomous control loop, ensuring high accuracy without external human intervention. This would enable robust execution of tasks generated by upstream intelligent systems, extending the capabilities of humanoid robots beyond teleoperated scenarios.

To address these motivations, GLoRI aims to provide a compact, task-space interface that can accept sparse future pose references (root, head, hands) from high-level models, and critically, to robustly integrate structured global reference and feedback with local motion guidance to achieve high accuracy and generalization for real-world autonomous loco-manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GLoRI proposes a closed-loop whole-body tracking framework for humanoid loco-manipulation, implemented as a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (RL) task.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The control problem is framed as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP). At each control step, the policy receives an observation (`o_t`) consisting of a reference observation (`o_ref_t`) and a proprioceptive observation (`o_prop_t`). The policy outputs a 29-dimensional action (`a_t`) representing residual joint position targets relative to a nominal standing configuration, which are executed by a low-level PD controller for a Unitree G1 robot. A sparse reference comprising three keypoint poses (head, left hand, right hand) is used as the goal condition, along with the desired root state.

#### 3.2. Observation Design

The policy observation `o_t` is meticulously designed to address the global drift problem. It separates the reference into local and global components, each serving distinct control roles. An external localization device (e.g., VIVE tracker) provides global root pose feedback during deployment.

- **Reference Observation (`o_ref_t`):** This consists of `H = 20` future frames. Each frame (`o_ref_t,h`) is composed of:
 - **Local Reference (`o_l_t,h`):** Contains placement-invariant motion guidance for the tracked keypoints (head, left hand, right hand). It provides the poses of these keypoints in the local reference-root frame, serving as a motion prior for *how* the action should be performed, independent of absolute placement.
 - **Global Reference (`o_g_t,h`):** Further decomposed into:
 - **Global Motion Reference (`o_g,m_t,h`):** Includes keypoint poses in the world frame (`¯p_W_i,t+h`), keypoint poses in the current robot-root frame (`¯p_C_i,t+h`), and world-frame keypoint pose differences (`∆p_W_i,t,h`). These provide absolute interaction poses, robot-centric cues for direction/distance, and direct tracking error for closed-loop correction.
 - **Global Root Reference (`o_g,root_t,h`):** Contains the target root pose in the world frame (`¯p_W_root,t+h`), the world-frame root pose difference (`∆p_W_root,t,h`), and reference linear and angular velocities (`¯v_W_root,t+h`, `¯ω_W_root,t+h`). This anchors the motion's absolute position and heading, describes its future evolution, and provides closed-loop correction for the executed root trajectory.

- **Proprioceptive Observation (`o_prop_t`):** Includes the robot’s current executed root state (position, 6D orientation, linear/angular velocities), current joint positions and velocities, and the previous action. This provides the policy with the robot's actual state for closed-loop control.

#### 3.3. GLoRI-Net Architecture

The core of GLoRI is GLoRI-Net, a policy network designed to explicitly model the interaction between global and local references. It comprises three main components:

1. **Global-Local Reference Interaction Encoder:**
 - **Feature Encoding:** Separate encoders process the local motion reference (`o_l_t,h`), global motion reference (`o_g,m_t,h`), and global root reference (`o_g,root_t,h`) for each future frame `h`, projecting them into token matrices (`F_m,l_t,h`, `F_m,g_t,h`) and a root feature (`f_g,root_t,h`).
 - **Global-Local Cross Attention Module (GLCA):** This module is central to GLoRI-Net. It uses the local motion tokens (`F_m,l_t,h`) as queries and the global motion tokens (`F_m,g_t,h`) as keys and values. This directional attention mechanism allows the policy to refine the local motion prior with keypoint-specific global target and feedback information, while a residual connection preserves the original local motion structure. The output, `eF_m,l_t,h`, represents a globally corrected motion guidance that combines *how* the action should be performed with *where* its keypoints should be placed and *how* current errors should be corrected.
 - **Root Fusion and Temporal Encoding:** The separately encoded global root feature (`f_g,root_t,h`) is fused with the vectorized globally corrected motion guidance (`vec(eF_m,l_t,h)`). A temporal encoder then processes this sequence of fused frame features to produce a final reference representation (`f_ref_t`), combining global root guidance with closed-loop keypoint correction over the prediction horizon.

2. **Proprioception Encoder:** A two-layer MLP processes the current proprioceptive observation (`o_prop_t`) to generate a proprioceptive feature (`f_prop_t`).

3. **Action Decoder:** The concatenated reference (`f_ref_t`) and proprioceptive (`f_prop_t`) features are fed into an MLP-based action decoder. This decoder predicts the mean (`μ_t`) of the action distribution, specifying the residual joint position targets.

#### 3.4. Reward Design

The reward function combines motion tracking rewards with physical regularization terms, similar to prior work. Tracking terms (e.g., root position/rotation/velocity, joint position/velocity, global root translation/rotation/velocity, key-body position) use an exponential kernel to encourage accurate motion. Regularization penalties (e.g., action rate, joint/position/torque limits, undesired contact, foot slip/stumble, ankle/DoF velocity/acceleration, base angular velocity) promote smooth, feasible, and stable robot behavior. Dense whole-body references are used for auxiliary supervision during training, even though the actor only observes sparse keypoint poses.

#### 3.5. PPO Training

The actor and critic networks are trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. Both networks share the GLoRI-Net architecture and process identical observations. Training is conducted in Isaac Gym with 4,096 parallel environments, with a simulation step of 0.002 s and policy running at 50 Hz. Robustness for sim-to-real transfer is enhanced through extensive randomization of physical parameters (gravity, friction, base mass/COM, actuator strength, external pushes, observation noise) and the use of a noise curriculum and adaptive sampling of difficult motions.

#### 3.6. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics

- **Whole-body Motion Datasets:** Training data combines AMASS, OMOMO, and HuMoTo datasets, including diverse locomotion, daily activities, and human-object interactions. Human motions are retargeted to the Unitree G1. Robot-validated teleoperation trajectories from TWIST2 are also incorporated. HuMoTo sequences are held out for unseen-motion evaluation.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Six metrics assess whole-body tracking:
 - **Success rate (Succ.):** Percentage of reference motion completed.
 - **Global MPJPE (g-MPJPE):** Mean position error over nine whole-body keypoints in the world frame (global accuracy).
 - **Root-aligned MPJPE (RA-MPJPE):** Mean position error after removing root translation (local pose tracking).
 - **Head MPJPE, Hand MPJPE, Root MPJPE:** World-frame errors for specific body parts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Tracking Performance on Seen Motions:**
 GLoRI demonstrated a 100% motion completion rate on HuMoTo motions included during training. It achieved an overall g-MPJPE of 4.47 cm and an RA-MPJPE of 3.60 cm. The performance remained consistent across diverse categories such as Transport, Housework, Pick & Place, Inspection, Organizing, and Locomotion, with root errors consistently below 1.9 cm. This indicates the effectiveness of GLoRI's global-local reference interaction in constraining world-frame motion while maintaining local coordination.

- **Comparison with Prior Methods on Unseen HuMoTo Motions:**
 GLoRI was compared against TWIST2, CLOT, and ScaleBFM (Whole-Body and Root-Hand variants) on held-out HuMoTo sequences not seen during training.
 - TWIST2 achieved 48.5% success with a g-MPJPE of 48.82 cm, indicating its struggle with global placement despite competitive root-aligned accuracy.
 - CLOT (100% success, 11.84 cm g-MPJPE) and ScaleBFM-WB (100% success, 9.00 cm g-MPJPE) showed improved global accuracy with feedback.
 - GLoRI achieved a 100% completion rate and the lowest g-MPJPE of 6.44 cm, a relative reduction of 45.6% compared to CLOT. It also recorded the lowest Head (3.91 cm), Hand (3.89 cm), and Root (1.52 cm) MPJPEs. Notably, GLoRI achieved this superior world-frame tracking using sparse pose references for only three keypoints (head, two hands) and root-trajectory guidance, outperforming methods that utilized full-body tracking inputs.

- **Ablation Study:**
 An ablation study was conducted to quantify the contributions of global information and the GLCA module:
 - **"No Global Obs.":** Removing global references from the policy input (while retaining global tracking rewards) resulted in a 97.0% success rate and a g-MPJPE of 9.19 cm. This indicates that reward supervision alone is insufficient for closed-loop drift suppression at inference time.
 - **"Direct Fusion":** Restoring global observations but concatenating them directly with local references and processing them with a single MLP encoder resulted in 100% success and a g-MPJPE of 5.39 cm. This confirmed the importance of explicit global feedback.
 - **GLoRI (with GLCA):** Replacing direct fusion with the GLCA module further reduced g-MPJPE to 4.47 cm and RA-MPJPE to 3.60 cm, while maintaining 100% completion. This simultaneous improvement in both global and local accuracy validates that GLCA effectively corrects global placement while preserving coordinated local motion structures.

- **Qualitative Simulation Results:**
 Visualizations of representative motions (e.g., walking, chair carrying, picking) showed that executed keypoints remained closely aligned with global targets while maintaining coherent local motion structure, despite the policy receiving only sparse keypoint inputs.

#### 4.2. Sim-to-Sim Result

To evaluate generalization and robustness to dynamics mismatch, the GLoRI policy trained in Isaac Gym was directly deployed to MuJoCo without fine-tuning.
- On seen motions, the transferred policy achieved a 97.0% completion rate and 4.19 cm g-MPJPE.
- On unseen HuMoTo motions, it maintained a 93.9% completion rate with 7.53 cm g-MPJPE.
The moderate degradation across simulators highlights motion generalization under mismatch, while the high completion rates demonstrate robust direct transferability. Qualitative results confirmed consistent alignment with world-frame targets across diverse loco-manipulation tasks in MuJoCo.

#### 4.3. Real-World Deployment

- **Portable Global Localization Setup:**
 For real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 robot, a lightweight VIVE localization system (base stations and VIVE Tracker 3.0) was used to provide 6-DoF root pose feedback. The tracker was rigidly mounted on the robot with a custom 3D-printed adapter, and its pose was calibrated to the robot's root frame. This portable setup offered a less specialized alternative to traditional motion capture systems.

- **Real-World Task Evaluation:**
 GLoRI's autonomous loco-manipulation capability was evaluated on three tasks: "carry box," "pick bag," and "pick pillow," with three repetitions per task. Motion demonstrations were collected offline via teleoperation to provide reference motions.
 GLoRI achieved 7 successes out of 9 trials (Carry Box: 2/3, Pick Bag: 3/3, Pick Pillow: 2/3). This outcome provides preliminary verification of the real-world transferability and effectiveness of GLoRI's closed-loop global tracking for autonomous loco-manipulation tasks using offline references and portable localization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GLoRI introduces a closed-loop whole-body tracking framework that addresses a critical challenge in humanoid robotics: achieving precise world-frame motion execution in autonomous loco-manipulation. The key significance of this work lies in several areas:

1. **Enhanced Global Tracking Accuracy:** By explicitly integrating structured global reference and feedback with local motion guidance, GLoRI substantially improves global tracking accuracy. The reported g-MPJPE of 6.44 cm on unseen HuMoTo motions represents a notable reduction compared to prior methods, enabling the precision required for physical interaction tasks.

2. **Effective Global-Local Reference Interaction:** The introduction of GLoRI-Net with Global-Local Cross Attention (GLCA) is a novel contribution. It demonstrates that explicitly modeling the interaction between local motion priors and global correction cues allows the policy to preserve coordinated whole-body motion while simultaneously improving world-frame placement. This contrasts with previous approaches that simply concatenate global and local observations, highlighting the benefit of a dedicated interaction mechanism.

3. **Robustness and Generalization:** GLoRI exhibits strong generalization capabilities. Its direct transfer from Isaac Gym to MuJoCo without fine-tuning, maintaining high completion rates and relatively low g-MPJPE, indicates robustness to simulator dynamics mismatches. This property is crucial for developing policies that can reliably operate in real-world environments.

4. **Enabling Autonomous Loco-Manipulation with Sparse Inputs:** GLoRI's ability to achieve high accuracy from sparse keypoint pose references (head, hands, and root trajectory) is significant. This compact task-space interface simplifies reference generation from upstream systems like visuomotor or vision-language-action models, reducing the burden of dense full-body motion input. The successful real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 with a portable localization system, completing autonomous loco-manipulation tasks with offline references, demonstrates a step towards operator-independent humanoid capabilities. This extends beyond prior systems that often rely on continuous human teleoperation or focus on single-object interactions.

The potential impact of GLoRI lies in advancing the capabilities of humanoid robots for real-world applications. By providing a reliable foundation for precise, autonomous loco-manipulation, GLoRI contributes to realizing the potential of humanoids in factories, homes, and other human-centered environments. Future work aims to integrate GLoRI with high-level perception and reasoning models, paving the way for fully autonomous systems that can interpret tasks, generate references, and execute them with closed-loop precision.
