# RoboVAST: Automated Scenario-Based Validation of Robots at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> ROBOVAST introduces an open-source framework for automated, scenario-based validation of robotic systems at scale, enabling systematic exploration of environmental, task, and system parameter variations. It facilitates large-scale testing with over 100,000 runs to statistically assess the performance, robustness, and safety of mobile robot navigation.

## Problem

- Current scenario-based testing for robots is often manual, experience-driven, and difficult to scale, leading to limited reproducibility and generalizability of validation conclusions.
- Scenarios are frequently treated as monolithic test cases, preventing systematic analysis of how individual environmental, task, or system parameter variations influence behavior.
- Defining meaningful test coverage and determining when sufficient testing has occurred remains challenging due to the unbounded nature of the total scenario space.

## Method

- A methodology grounded in ==compositional scenario specifications== that explicitly models variability across environmental, task, and system dimensions to enable structured test space exploration.
- The **ROBOVAST** framework provides a ==declarative== mechanism for configuring and varying scenarios, facilitating systematic generation and large-scale, parallel execution using ==Kubernetes==.
- A ==plugin-based== system supports diverse variability dimensions, including discrete value enumeration, stochastic sampling, and navigation-specific producers for maps, tasks, and obstacles.

## Results

- The ROBOVAST framework generated a dataset from over **100,000** execution runs across **5,480** distinct scenario variants for ROS2 Nav2, totaling over **1,800** simulated hours.
- Evaluation showed high performance success rates (**98.12%**), but lower robustness (**82.80%** success under sensor degradation) and safety (**61.24%** success with obstacles) for the mobile robot.
- Analysis of failures revealed that safety violations were predominantly systematic (**94.02%** of failing runs belonged to variants where all **20** repetitions failed), and failures were spatially concentrated in specific map features like corners and doorways.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formalizing scenario compositionality and explicitly defining variability dimensions allows for systematic exploration of the test space, improving validation rigor and reproducibility.
> - Decoupling scenario specification, instantiation, parallel execution via containerized Kubernetes jobs, and post-processing enables efficient and scalable validation campaigns.
> - Collecting statistical evidence from multiple execution runs per scenario configuration is crucial for distinguishing systematic failures from stochastic anomalies and providing reliable confidence in validation conclusions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The responsible deployment of robotic systems, particularly mobile robots, necessitates rigorous validation to ensure they meet requirements such as safety and functional correctness. Scenario-based testing has emerged as a fundamental practice for this purpose, where system behavior is observed under specific operating conditions. This approach is well-established in domains like autonomous driving, with domain-specific languages (DSL) such as Scenic and OpenSCENARIO supporting probabilistic and parameterized scenario specifications. In robotics, similar scenario-based methods have been applied to mobile robots and drones, with recent work addressing compositional scenario specification and environment generation DSLs.

However, empirical studies indicate that testing robotic systems still encounters significant challenges. Current practices for scenario selection and variation are often manual, experience-driven, and challenging to scale, which can impede reproducibility and limit the generalizability of validation conclusions. Scenarios are frequently treated as monolithic test cases, rather than compositions of environmental structures, task specifications, system configurations, and contextual factors. This monolithic treatment can lead to validation results that are locally valid but may not generalize across the broader operational design domain.

Simulation-based testing is recognized as critical for modern robotics development, enabling controlled, repeatable exploration of conditions that would otherwise be costly or hazardous. Despite its importance, simulation-based testing faces challenges including the simulation-to-reality gap, reproducibility issues, and substantial resource costs. While some efforts focus on test generation for individual pipeline stages or benchmarking, recent advancements in robot learning leverage GPU-accelerated environments for large-scale data collection. Concurrently, cloud infrastructure is being explored to enable scalable robotics testing, but many existing solutions are commercial and proprietary.

This research positions itself within this landscape by seeking to bridge the gap between established parameterized scenario concepts in autonomous driving and the current challenges in robotic system testing. It extends existing efforts by proposing a comprehensive framework that supports the entire testing process—from scenario specification and configuration to scalable execution and evaluation—while explicitly introducing variation points to systematically explore the test space. The work aims to provide an open-source solution that integrates expressive scenario specifications with large-scale execution capabilities, contributing to more robust and generalized validation conclusions for robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the necessity for rigorous validation of robotic systems before their deployment. While scenario-based testing is a common practice, its effectiveness is often hampered by limitations in how scenarios are designed, varied, and executed. The authors identify several critical issues:

- **Manual and Experience-Driven Scenario Selection:** Current practices often rely on manual, experience-based scenario selection, which is difficult to reproduce and scale. This can lead to an insufficient diversity of tested conditions, undermining the reliability of validation decisions.
- **Limited Reproducibility:** The lack of systematic variation and documentation of operating conditions makes it difficult to reproduce test results, hindering verification and the identification of root causes for failures.
- **Scalability Challenges:** Manual approaches do not scale to the combinatorial complexity of possible system configurations and environmental conditions. This limits the ability to explore the vast "test space" and uncover corner cases.
- **Monolithic Scenario Treatment:** Scenarios are frequently treated as indivisible units, preventing a systematic understanding of how individual environmental, task, or system parameter variations influence system behavior. This limits the generalizability of validation conclusions.
- **Undefined Test Coverage and Sufficiency:** The effectively unbounded nature of the total scenario space makes it difficult to define meaningful test coverage and determine when sufficient testing has been performed to draw reliable conclusions.

To address these motivations, the central research question guiding this work is: "How can scenarios be specified to account for these dimensions, their variations, and their systematic execution?"

Based on this, the paper articulates three key objectives (contributions):

1. **Develop a Scenario-Based Validation Methodology:** Propose a methodology grounded in compositional scenario specifications. This approach aims to enable explicit modeling of variability across environmental, task, and system dimensions, allowing for a more structured and systematic exploration of the test space.
2. **Introduce the ROBOVAST Testing Framework:** Create a framework that implements the proposed methodology. ROBOVAST is designed to offer a declarative mechanism for configuring and varying scenarios, facilitating systematic generation and large-scale, parallel execution of scenario variations. It also aims to support quantifiable metrics for test coverage and completion criteria.
3. **Provide a Large-Scale Validation Dataset:** Furnish a publicly available, large-scale dataset to demonstrate the scalability and utility of the ROBOVAST approach. This dataset is intended to enable systematic analysis of variation coverage and provide statistical confidence in results through a sufficient number of execution runs.

These objectives collectively aim to improve the rigor, reproducibility, and scalability of robotic system validation, leading to more reliable conclusions about system performance, robustness, and safety.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper centers on a scenario-based approach that models scenario compositionality and formalizes the processes of varying, instantiating, executing, and interpreting these scenarios at scale. The authors define scenarios not as monolithic entities, but as compositions of scenario elements such as environments, tasks, and system configurations that can be systematically varied. The overarching process is referred to as a "test campaign," encompassing planning, monitoring, and completion activities.

**Formalization of Scenario Concepts:**
The approach begins by defining typed variability dimensions `D = {d1:τ1, ..., dn:τn}`. Key dimensions include:
- `dS:ΘS`: The robot or System Under Test (SUT) with its configuration space. This accounts for variations in sensor configurations, controller parameters, and software variants.
- `dE:E`: The set of environment representations `e` (models of the physical world `w`). These representations can include structured descriptions of geometry, static and dynamic entities, contextual parameters, and semantic regions.
- `dT:T`: Variations on the set of tasks, such as start/goal poses, paths, or repetitions.

A composable scenario specification is defined as a tuple `F = (a, K, Π, β, φ, γ, ω)`:
- `a`: An abstract scenario describing elements using abstract variables.
- `K`: Parameter ranges.
- `Π`: An ordered sequence of "producers" responsible for generating scenario instances.
- `β`: A set of random seeds for reproducible stochastic processes within producers.
- `φ`: A constraint predicate that restricts admissible combinations of variability choices, ensuring the validity of the scenario space `V(D)`.
- `γ`: Execution parameters, including the number of runs `ϕ`.
- `ω`: A set of evaluation functions.
Test coverage is then formally defined based on the ratio of concrete scenarios `C` generated from `F` to the total valid scenario space `V(D)`.

**Instantiation Through Ordered Producers:**
Concrete executable scenario instances `c ∈ C` are generated from `F` via a structured instantiation process managed by the ordered sequence of producers `Π = ⟨p1, ..., pn⟩`. Each producer `pi:(K, β) → C` is a (potentially stochastic) function that consumes a set of partial configurations and applies its generation or variation logic, accumulating configuration fragments combinatorially. This ensures that dependencies between variability dimensions are respected and transformations are deterministic given explicit seeds. The process `Inst(K, β) = Cn` results in all required test data for the scenarios in `F`.

**Scenario Execution and Evaluation:**
Once a scenario `cn` is instantiated, execution is modeled by `Exec: S(θn) × En × Tn → R`, where `R` is the space of raw execution outputs. This involves instantiating the environment, configuring the SUT, establishing task preconditions, and producing outputs.
Evaluation is a two-step process to decouple the execution backend from interpretation:
1. **Observation (`Obs: R → T`):** Transforms raw execution outputs into structured execution traces `T`.
2. **Oracle Function (`O: T → O`):** Evaluates the traces, mapping them to pass/fail criteria or quantitative metrics.
Statistical evidence is derived by aggregating the oracle evaluations over multiple runs `ϕ` for each scenario configuration.

**ROBOVAST Framework Implementation:**
ROBOVAST is presented as a framework that implements this methodology through a workflow combining declarative configuration and Kubernetes-based parallel execution.

- **Conceptual Architecture:** It separates concerns into three roles: (1) defining test assets and expectations, (2) declaratively configuring and instantiating scenario families, and (3) executing scenarios in parallel.
- **Declarative Specifications:** Uses a YAML-based configuration file (`.vast` file) to specify metadata, scenario families, variability dimensions, execution parameters (e.g., containerization, resource allocation), results processing pipelines, and evaluation settings. Variations are composed via a Cartesian product to generate all admissible parameter combinations.
- **Plugin-Based Variation System:** Implements variability dimensions through an extensible plugin architecture.
 - **General-purpose plugins:** Support discrete value enumeration and stochastic sampling (uniform, Gaussian) with explicit random seeds for reproducibility.
 - **Navigation-specific plugins:** Realize producers for maps (`FloorplanVariation`, `FloorplanGeneration` from DSLs), tasks (`PathVariation` for reachable paths, including rasterized and random sampling), and obstacles (`ObstacleVariation` for static and dynamic obstacle placement). These plugins generate artifacts like 2D occupancy grids, 3D meshes, and start-to-goal paths.
- **Scenario Execution:**
 - **Configuration Generation:** Producer plugins generate fully resolved OpenSCENARIO parameter values. Results are cached.
 - **Runtime Execution:** Integrated with the `Scenario Execution` framework, which parses OpenSCENARIO definitions into behavior trees to manage preconditions, monitoring, and post-execution activities.
 - **Containerized Architecture:** Utilizes a multi-container approach, allowing the SUT, simulation environment, and custom tools to run in separate containers. Resource monitoring is integrated.
 - **Scalable Distributed Execution:** Achieved through Kubernetes with Kueue for admission control and resource management. The workflow involves uploading configurations to S3, creating Kubernetes Jobs for each configuration-run pair, queueing jobs, and collecting results via S3.
- **Scenario Results:**
 - **Results Processing Stage:** Transforms raw execution artifacts (e.g., ROS bag files, logs) into inspectable formats (e.g., CSV tables), generates combined metadata, and facilitates publication packages. This stage bridges raw runs `R` and traces `T`, and artifacts are cached for iterative analysis.
 - **Result Interpretation:** Supported by a GUI tool and custom Jupyter notebooks for visualization and implementation of observation (`Obs`) and oracle (`O`) functions. Additionally, a Model Context Protocol server enables large language models to query campaign and run data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ROBOVAST framework was evaluated by creating an objective-oriented dataset for mobile robot navigation using a TurtleBot 4 with ROS2 Nav2. The validation campaign consisted of three distinct sub-datasets, each targeting a specific software quality: Performance, Robustness, and Safety. These datasets utilized five different indoor maps (one derived from a real-world building, four synthetic) and varied Nav2 parameters, sensor perturbations, and obstacle settings. Each scenario instance was executed 20 times to obtain statistically meaningful success rate estimates.

**Campaign Overview:**
- **SUT:** TurtleBot 4 with ROS2 Nav2, using `NavToPose` and `FollowWaypoint` actions.
- **Environments:** Five distinct maps (one real-world, four synthetic: hospital-like lobby, office, artificial corridor, nested-rooms).
- **Validation Objectives:** Performance, Robustness, Safety.
- **Scenario Configurations:** 5,480 distinct scenario variants.
- **Execution Runs:** Over 100,000 total runs (20 repetitions per configuration).
- **Simulated Time:** Over 1,800 hours.
- **Traveled Distance:** 1,873 km.
- **Infrastructure:** Auto-scaling Google Kubernetes Engine cluster (max 20 C4 worker nodes, 1920 CPUs) in approximately 7 hours.
- **Data Size:** Approximately 737 GB.

**Dataset-Specific Results:**

1. **Performance Dataset (Path Optimality):**
 - **Variations:** 100 rasterized paths plus additional random paths of varying lengths; two global costmap robot-radius values (0.22m, 0.175m).
 - **Criterion:** RMSE between executed trajectory and A\* reference path. Failure if RMSE exceeds a path-length–scaled threshold (A\* path length * Nav2 local costmap robot radius).
 - **Outcomes:** 31,714 successful runs, 609 failed runs (98.12% success rate). Of the failures, 214 runs (35.14%) originated from systematically failing variants, indicating that the majority of performance issues were stochastic.

2. **Robustness Dataset (Sensor Degradation):**
 - **Variations:** Laserscan message drop (0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.15); Gaussian noise (0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.15 standard deviations); task repetitions (loops=0, 1).
 - **Criterion:** Summed localization error (accumulated difference between estimated and ground-truth pose). Failure if accumulated error exceeds 0.3m (robot diameter).
 - **Outcomes:** 26,041 successful runs, 5,409 failed runs (82.80% success rate). Of the failures, 32.80% came from systematically failing variants. This suggests that robustness was more frequently affected by random factors (sensor dropout, noise, long task durations) than systematic issues under the tested conditions.

3. **Safety Dataset (Collision and Safety Response):**
 - **Variations:** Static obstacles (density 0, 1, 3 per 5m; max distance from A\* path 0m, 0.3m); dynamic obstacles (single 0.5m cube, 0m distance to path, spawned 1m or 2m ahead of robot; randomization factor of 3 for obstacle positions).
 - **Criterion:** Minimum safe separation. Failure if the robot comes closer to any obstacle than a calculated minimum distance (obstacle radius + robot radius + stopping distance of 0.05m).
 - **Outcomes:** 15,901 successful runs, 10,065 failed runs (61.24% success rate). A notable finding was that 9,463 of the failing runs (94.02%) belonged to scenario variants where all 20 repetitions failed. This indicates that most safety violations were systematic, occurring consistently under specific high obstacle counts or with dynamic obstacles, rather than being random occurrences.

**Spatial Distribution of Failures (Heatmaps):**
Analysis of failure heatmaps showed that regions with high failure density were not simply the most frequently traversed. Instead, failures were observed to be tied to specific map features such as corners, doorways, and areas near walls. Open areas and straight hallways rarely exhibited failures. Dense failure spots inside rooms predominantly corresponded to startup failures where the robot terminated or crashed before significant movement.

The complete postprocessed dataset is publicly available at [https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19042250](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19042250).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ROBOVAST framework and the accompanying methodology address several critical challenges in the validation of robotic systems, presenting implications for both research and practical development.

**Improved Validation Rigor and Reproducibility:** By proposing a methodology with compositional scenario specifications and explicit variation dimensions, ROBOVAST aims to enhance the rigor of validation campaigns. The use of declarative configurations, explicit random seeds, and structured output ensures that test campaigns are reproducible, allowing for consistent comparisons and a clearer understanding of how variations impact system behavior.

**Scalable and Systematic Test Space Exploration:** ROBOVAST enables the systematic generation and execution of a large number of scenario variations. This capability is significant because it allows developers and researchers to explore high-dimensional parameter spaces and uncover corner cases that might be missed with manual or limited testing. The Kubernetes-based orchestration facilitates parallel execution, making large-scale campaigns involving thousands of scenarios and runs feasible within reasonable timeframes and without prohibitive upfront hardware investment.

**Statistical Confidence in Validation Conclusions:** The framework supports executing multiple repetitions per scenario configuration. This allows for the collection of statistical evidence, enabling the distinction between systematic failures (consistently occurring under specific conditions) and stochastic anomalies. Such statistical confidence can lead to more reliable conclusions regarding the safety, robustness, and performance of robotic systems.

**Open-Source Contribution to the Robotics Community:** ROBOVAST is presented as an open-source framework and its associated dataset is publicly available. This contributes a practical toolchain and a large-scale benchmark for mobile robot navigation. This availability can accelerate research in large-scale robotic system validation, facilitate comparative studies, and provide a foundation for developing more robust and reliable robotic software.

**Extensibility and Adaptability:** The plugin-based architecture of ROBOVAST allows for extensibility, meaning developers can create new producers for different variation dimensions or integrate the framework with other test processes and robotic use cases. This flexibility suggests that ROBOVAST could be adapted beyond mobile navigation to areas like robotic manipulation or multi-robot fleet coordination.

**Foundation for Future Research:** The work identifies several avenues for future research, including addressing the sim-to-real gap, supporting additional simulators (e.g., Isaac Sim, O3DE), and exploring more systematic combinatorial strategies for covering high-dimensional variation spaces. The provided dataset and framework serve as a foundational platform for these future investigations.

In summary, ROBOVAST offers a comprehensive, scalable, and reproducible approach to scenario-based validation of robotic systems. Its impact lies in providing a systematic method and an open-source tool that can lead to more reliable assessment of robotic system qualities, contributing to safer and more robust robot deployments.
