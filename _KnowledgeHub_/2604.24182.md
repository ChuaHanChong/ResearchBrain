# $M^2$-VLA: Boosting Vision-Language Models for Generalizable Manipulation via Layer Mixture and Meta-Skills

> [!summary] Summary
> M2^22-VLA, an architectural paradigm, enables generalized Vision-Language Models to serve as robust backbones for robotic manipulation by freezing their core parameters, thereby preserving semantic understanding and avoiding catastrophic forgetting. It introduces Mixture of Layers (MoL) for feature extraction and a Meta Skill Module (MSM) for efficient trajectory learning, achieving an average 95.3% success rate in simulation and superior generalization to novel instructions and objects in real-world tasks with significantly fewer parameters (0.3B) than fine-tuned alternatives.

## Problem

- Traditional robot policies struggled with generalization to unseen objects or scenes, relying on task-specific engineering or domain-specific datasets.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models fine-tune pre-trained VLM backbones, leading to catastrophic forgetting of the VLM's inherent semantic understanding and limited generalization to novel instructions or objects.
- There is a fundamental gap in translating the high-level semantic features learned by VLMs into the precise, fine-grained control signals required for robot manipulation.

## Method

- M2^22-VLA proposes an architecture that adapts a generalized VLM as a ==frozen perceptual backbone==, preventing the degradation of its core visual-language reasoning capabilities.
- The ==Mixture of Layers== (**MoL**) strategy selectively extracts task-critical, fine-grained spatial information from various VLM layers, using decoupled attention branches to integrate knowledge for the action head.
- A ==Meta Skill Module== (**MSM**) enhances model capacity by maintaining a memory of key-value pairs representing perceptual contexts and successful action sequences, allowing for skill retrieval and action refinement via cross-attention.

## Results

- M2^22-VLA achieved an average success rate of **95.3%** across all four Libero suites in simulation, outperforming all other compared methods, including state-of-the-art approaches that fine-tune VLMs.
- In real-world experiments, the model demonstrated superior generalization, achieving **80%** success with rephrased instructions and **75%** with novel objects, significantly outperforming baselines which often failed (e.g., VLA-Adapter (Frozen) **5%** with novel objects).
- Both MoL and MSM modules were shown to contribute independently to performance improvements in ablation studies (e.g., MoL alone increased success from **46.6%** to **67.4%** on Libero spatial suite, MSM alone to **60.2%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Freezing the VLM backbone effectively preserves its robust semantic understanding and Visual Question Answering (VQA) capabilities, which is crucial for achieving superior generalization in robotic manipulation.
> - Explicitly selecting and integrating multi-scale features from different VLM layers (MoL) helps bridge the disparity between high-level semantic representations and the fine-grained requirements of robotic control.
> - Leveraging a memory of meta-skills (MSM) significantly enhances the learning of complex trajectories and overall model capacity, even under the constraint of a lightweight action head with limited trainable parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the fundamental challenge of achieving generalizable robot manipulation within the field of Embodied AI. Historically, robot policies relied on task-specific engineering, Behavior Cloning (BC), or Reinforcement Learning (RL) on domain-specific datasets, which exhibited limited generalization to unseen objects or scenes.

The recent success of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in general-purpose scene understanding has led to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to leverage the extensive pre-training of VLMs to transfer robust generalization capabilities to physical robot control. Current VLA frameworks typically adopt a paradigm of fine-tuning pre-trained VLM backbones on large-scale robotic datasets. This approach re-purposes the VLM's capabilities from language token generation to action token generation.

However, the paper identifies significant limitations with this prevalent fine-tuning paradigm:
1. **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Fine-tuning can degrade the VLM's inherent semantic understanding and Visual Question Answering (VQA) capabilities.
2. **Limited Generalizability:** The degradation of semantic understanding consequently restricts the VLA model's ability to generalize to novel instructions or objects beyond the training domain.
3. **Data Intensity:** The fine-tuning process is often data-intensive.

The research also situates itself in relation to:
- **Generalist Robot Policies:** Advances in Transformer-based architectures (e.g., Gato, RT-2, Octo, Open X-Embodiment) unify multi-modal inputs for diverse tasks and cross-embodiment training. The presented work, M$^2$-VLA, adheres to the VLA paradigm, leveraging a VLM backbone for uniform multi-modal encoding and action generation.
- **Vision-Language-Action Models:** Existing VLA models integrate visual, proprioceptive, and natural language inputs to generate low-level control signals, often by fine-tuning VLM backbones. While some report strong performance on benchmarks, the paper challenges the convention that fine-tuning is necessary, arguing it induces catastrophic forgetting.
- **Efficient Adaptation of VLMs:** Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) and adapter-based architectures have emerged to adapt VLMs without full fine-tuning, addressing cost and catastrophic forgetting. However, directly applying these to robotic manipulation still faces challenges:
 - Difficulty in extracting fine-grained, manipulation-relevant features from high-level VLM representations.
 - Insufficient trainable parameters in efficient adaptation methods, limiting model capacity for complex trajectories.

M$^2$-VLA proposes an alternative paradigm that posits a generalized VLM can be adapted as a robust backbone for robotic manipulation without fine-tuning its core parameters, thereby preserving its generalization capabilities. It directly addresses the identified gaps by introducing specific architectural components designed to bridge semantic understanding with precise control and enhance model capacity under parameter constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel architectural paradigm, M$^2$-VLA, that enables generalized Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to serve as powerful, high-performance backbones for robotic manipulation while preserving their inherent generalization capabilities and semantic understanding.

The motivation for this objective stems from critical limitations observed in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:
1. **Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting and Generalization Degradation:** Current VLA models predominantly rely on end-to-end fine-tuning of VLM backbones on robotic action datasets. This fine-tuning process frequently leads to catastrophic forgetting, where the VLM's original visual-language reasoning and Visual Question Answering (VQA) capabilities degrade. As a consequence, these models exhibit limited generalizability to novel instructions, unseen objects, or variations in task execution, even if they perform well on in-domain tasks. The authors argue that preserving the VLM's robust semantic understanding is a fundamental prerequisite for generalizable and robust manipulation. M$^2$-VLA aims to circumvent this issue by freezing the VLM backbone.
2. **Bridging the Semantic-Control Gap:** A key challenge arises from the inherent difference between the representations learned by VLMs and the requirements of robotic control. VLMs are primarily pre-trained on internet data for high-level semantic tasks (e.g., VQA), generating rich but often dense and high-level semantic features. Robotic control, however, demands precise, fine-grained spatial and contextual information. There is a fundamental gap in translating these high-level VLM semantics into the low-level, continuous action signals required for robot manipulation.
3. **Enhancing Trajectory Learning under Constrained Capacity:** By preserving the VLM backbone in a frozen state, the number of trainable parameters in the robotic action generation component (the "action head") is inherently restricted. This limitation in model capacity can hinder the ability to learn complex and diverse manipulation trajectories, potentially compromising overall performance and generalizability. The research aims to enhance the action head's capability despite these parameter constraints.

To achieve its primary objective and overcome these motivations, the paper sets forth two specific technical objectives:
- To introduce the **Mixture of Layers (MoL)** strategy to selectively extract task-critical, fine-grained spatial information from the VLM's dense, hierarchical semantic features, thereby bridging the high-level semantic understanding of VLMs with the precise requirements of robotic control.
- To propose the **Meta Skill Module (MSM)** to facilitate efficient trajectory learning and enhance the model's capacity under constrained trainable parameters. This module aims to introduce strong inductive biases and promote knowledge reuse through the retrieval and refinement of meta-skills from historical data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

M$^2$-VLA is an architectural paradigm designed to adapt a generalized VLM as a robust perceptual backbone for robotic manipulation without fine-tuning its internal parameters. The model comprises two main streams: Feature Extraction and Action Generation, incorporating two key strategies: Mixture of Layers (MoL) and Meta Skill Module (MSM).

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The robot manipulation task is formulated as a conditional decision-making problem. The robot receives a visual observation (O, composed of a global view $I_{global}$ and a wrist-mounted view $I_{wrist}$), a natural language instruction (L), and its proprioceptive state (P). The policy $\pi_\theta$ generates a sequence of actions $a_r$. The VLM backbone $E_{vlm}$ processes multimodal inputs.

**B. Main Structure:**
The VLM parameters are kept frozen to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The action generation is decoupled from the perceptual backbone.

1. **Feature Extraction:**
 - **Visual Embeddings:** Visual observations ($I_{global}$, $I_{wrist}$) are processed by DINOv2 and SigLIP to yield visual embeddings (V).
 - **Language Embeddings:** The language instruction (L) is tokenized into text embeddings ($L_{emb}$).
 - **Learnable Queries:** A set of learnable queries (S) is introduced and appended to the input sequence to capture cross-modal interactions.
 - **VLM Backbone Processing:** The combined input ($[V, L_{emb}, S]$) is fed into the pre-trained, frozen VLM backbone ($E_{vlm}$).
 - **Multi-scale Hidden States:** The VLM backbone produces multi-scale hidden states $H = \{(V'_l, L'_l, S'_l)\}_{l=1}^L$ from its L layers, representing processed visual, text, and query features. These, along with the proprioceptive state P, serve as the conditioning context for action generation.

2. **Action Generation:**
 - **Action Head:** A multi-layer transformer serves as the action head, designed for layer-wise iterative reconstruction.
 - **Denoising Process:** During training, Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$ is sampled to initialize a noisy action $a_n$. The action head, $D_\phi$, predicts this injected noise to reconstruct the target action $a_r = a_n - D_\phi(a_n, H, P)$.
 - **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a supervised learning paradigm with an L1 loss function: $L = E_H[||a_g - (a_n - D_\phi(a_n, H, P))||_1]$, where $a_g$ is the ground-truth action.

**C. Feature Selection via Mixture-of-Layers (MoL):**
MoL addresses the challenge of extracting manipulation-relevant features from the VLM's high-dimensional representations. It is applied at every layer of the action head to selectively integrate knowledge from different VLM layers, drawing inspiration from Mixture of Experts (MoE) frameworks. This mechanism aggregates information across network layers to extract task-critical features.
- **Decoupled Attention Branches:** To avoid cross-modal interference, MoL integrates information via three parallel attention branches for a given action head layer $l$, with $Q_A$ being the attention query from the previous layer's action latent $A_{l-1}$:
 1. **VLM Query Features Branch:** Uses processed VLM queries $S'_l$, concatenated with proprioceptive state P ($S''_l = [S'_l, P]$), as key ($K_S$) and value ($V_S$). A head-wise factor $\alpha_S = \text{sigmoid}(W_S S''_l)$ dynamically modulates the attention mechanism.
 2. **VLM Visual Features Branch:** Incorporates layer-wise visual information $V'_l$. A learnable static factor $\alpha_v$ is used to regulate attention scores due to the high dimensionality.
 3. **Action Latents Self-Attention Branch:** Performs self-attention on the action latents ($Q_A$) to suppress noise.
- **Aggregation:** The outputs from these three branches ($A_s$, $A_v$, $A_a$) are averaged: $A'_l = \text{Mean}(A_s, A_v, A_a)$ to obtain the final representation for that action head layer.

**D. Model Capacity Enhancement via Meta Skill Module (MSM):**
MSM addresses the limited capacity of the lightweight action head by leveraging knowledge reuse. It operates through memory construction during training and skill retrieval/action refinement during both training and inference.
- **Memory Construction:** An external queue M is maintained, storing key-value pairs.
 - **Key (k):** Represents the perceptual context, formed by concatenating hierarchical features (shallow, intermediate, deep layers) from the VLM: $k = [V'_{low} \oplus V'_{mid} \oplus V'_{high}]$.
 - **Value (v):** The action sequence that successfully accomplishes the task over a future chunk horizon. This queue functions as a repository of meta-skills.
- **Skill Retrieval and Action Refinement:** During action latent denoising, MSM refines the action latent.
 - **Query:** The multi-level features of the current observation serve as a query ($q_{obs}$).
 - **Retrieval:** The top-R nearest neighbors are retrieved from M using L1 distance (e.g., R=4).
 - **Cross-Attention:** The retrieved meta-skills are projected into a latent space (serving as $V^M, K^M$). A standard cross-attention mechanism uses the current action latent ($Q_A$) as the query to obtain a reference item ($A_{ref}$).
 - **Residual Integration:** The retrieved reference item $A_{ref}$ is integrated into the primary action flow via a residual learning paradigm: $A_l = A_{l-1} - (A'_l + A_{ref})$, where $A'_l$ is the output from MoL. This mechanism guides action reconstruction by referring to proven meta-skills, thereby reducing the policy's search space and improving the learning of complex trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of M$^2$-VLA was conducted in both simulated and real-world environments, alongside generalization and ablation studies.

**A. Performance on Generalization Tasks:**
M$^2$-VLA was assessed for its generalization capabilities to novel instructions and objects, comparing against OpenVLA and VLA-Adapter.
- **Instruction Following:** When tested with synonymically rephrased instructions on the Libero spatial suite, M$^2$-VLA exhibited a performance drop of 29.4%, achieving a success rate of 66.2%. This performance indicated greater robustness to language variations compared to OpenVLA (64.7% drop, 20.0% success) and VLA-Adapter (45.4% drop, 52.8% success).
- **Novel Object Tasks:** Trained on the Libero object suite and tested on a novel pick-and-place task with unseen objects, M$^2$-VLA showed a performance drop of 30.4%, with a 34.4% success rate. This was notably higher than OpenVLA (80.2% drop, 8.2% success) and VLA-Adapter (91.6% drop, 8.0% success), demonstrating M$^2$-VLA's preserved zero-shot ability.

**B. Performance in Simulation Environments:**
M$^2$-VLA was compared against baselines (vla-adapter (frozen), smolVLA) and state-of-the-art methods (OpenVLA, MolmoAct, ThinkAct, CoT-VLA, FlowVLA) across all four Libero suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long).
- M$^2$-VLA achieved an average success rate of 95.3%, outperforming all other methods.
- Specifically, it achieved 97.8% on Spatial, 99.0% on Object, 97.2% on Goal, and 87.0% on Long.
- It significantly outperformed other methods that do not fine-tune the VLM backbone (SmolVLA: 88.8% avg.; VLA-Adapter (Frozen): 89.4% avg.).
- M$^2$-VLA also showed comparable or superior performance to state-of-the-art approaches that *do* fine-tune VLMs (e.g., FlowVLA: 88.1% avg., MolmoAct: 86.6% avg.).
- The model also achieved this with a smaller parameter count (0.3B) compared to most baselines (e.g., 7.0B for OpenVLA, MolmoAct).

**C. Performance in Real-world:**
Experiments were conducted on an AgileX PiPer 6-DoF robotic manipulator across four task categories, with 20 real-world rollouts per task.
- **Fundamental Tasks:** M$^2$-VLA achieved an 85% success rate for "pick the apple and place it in the basket" and 90% for "pour water into the bowl," outperforming smolVLA (25%, 30%), VLA-Adapter (Frozen) (10%, 0%), and VLA-Adapter (60%, 50%).
- **Generalization Tasks:**
 - **Instruction Following:** M$^2$-VLA achieved an 80% success rate with synonymously rephrased instructions, demonstrating superior robustness.
 - **Novel Objects:** M$^2$-VLA achieved a 75% success rate when picking novel objects (e.g., water bottle, toy duck), while VLA-Adapter (Frozen) achieved 5%, and smolVLA and VLA-Adapter showed 20% and 0% respectively.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Fine-tuning methods exhibited overfitting and erratic end-gripper flailing with novel targets, attributed to distribution shift. M$^2$-VLA's generalized VLM backbone extracted invariant manipulation features, leading to robust execution.

**D. Visualization Analysis:**
- **MoL Analysis:** Visualization of $\alpha_S$ values (head-wise factors for VLM query features) in MoL showed higher attention paid to the middle layers of the VLM. Attention heatmaps on observed images for shallow, middle, and deep layers indicated that the middle layer's attention was concentrated on the gripper and the target. This suggests MoL successfully identifies operation-relevant visual information for manipulation from the VLM's features.
- **MSM Analysis:** Visualization of L1 and cosine distances between Keys and Values in the Meta Skill Module (MSM) revealed a significant positive correlation (correlation coefficients of 0.624 for Key L1, 0.618 for Value L1). This indicates that the Key construction strategy effectively retrieves useful skills. L1 distance was also observed to be more effective than cosine distance for distinguishing differences among meta-skills.

**E. Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies on the Libero spatial suite investigated the contribution of MoL and MSM:
- M$^2$-VLA without MoL and MSM achieved a 46.6% success rate.
- M$^2$-VLA with MoL but without MSM achieved a 67.4% success rate.
- M$^2$-VLA with MSM but without MoL achieved a 60.2% success rate.
- The full M$^2$-VLA achieved a 75.0% success rate.
These results demonstrate that both MoL and MSM independently contribute to performance improvements, with MoL aiding in beneficial information filtering and MSM enhancing model capacity for diverse scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The M$^2$-VLA framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by offering an alternative to the prevailing end-to-end fine-tuning paradigm for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Preservation of VLM Generalization:** By proposing that a generalized VLM can serve as a powerful backbone for robotic manipulation without fine-tuning its core parameters, M$^2$-VLA directly addresses the issue of catastrophic forgetting. This approach preserves the VLM's inherent semantic understanding and Visual Question Answering (VQA) capabilities, which are crucial for robust and adaptable robot behavior.
2. **Enhanced Generalization to Novelty:** The empirical results demonstrate M$^2$-VLA's superior performance in instruction following and handling novel objects, both in simulation and real-world scenarios. This enhanced generalization ability is critical for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic environments where encountering unseen objects or varied instructions is common.
3. **Architectural Innovations for Semantic-to-Control Bridging:** The introduction of the Mixture of Layers (MoL) mechanism provides a method for selectively extracting manipulation-critical, fine-grained spatial information from the VLM's high-level semantic features. This is a crucial step in bridging the information gap between general-purpose vision-language understanding and the precise requirements of robotic control.
4. **Efficient Trajectory Learning under Constraints:** The Meta Skill Module (MSM) offers an effective strategy for enhancing trajectory learning and model capacity within the constraint of limited trainable parameters (due to a frozen VLM backbone). By leveraging knowledge reuse and integrating meta-skills, MSM improves the policy's ability to learn complex behaviors without requiring extensive parameter updates.
5. **Resource Efficiency:** M$^2$-VLA demonstrates high performance with a significantly smaller parameter count (0.3 billion) compared to many state-of-the-art VLA models. This efficiency, coupled with faster training times (e.g., 8 hours on a single RTX 3090 GPU), makes advanced VLA capabilities more accessible and reduces computational resource requirements for development and deployment.
6. **Towards Universal Robot Policies:** The work lays groundwork for future developments in universal VLA models. The authors propose extending the framework with multiple action heads tailored to different tasks and robot embodiments, combining a shared perceptual "brain" with specialized controllers. This direction could lead to more versatile and adaptable robotic systems capable of operating across diverse tasks and physical platforms.
7. **Practical Deployment Implications:** The demonstrated robustness in real-world tasks, particularly concerning instruction following and novel object manipulation, suggests a path toward more reliable and autonomous robotic systems for practical applications in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and household assistance.

In summary, M$^2$-VLA's contribution lies in its principled approach to adapting powerful VLMs for robot manipulation while mitigating key limitations of current methods, thereby pushing the boundaries of generalizable robot intelligence.
