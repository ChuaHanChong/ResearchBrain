# Trust the Critic More

> [!summary] Summary
> AC2 (Actor-Critic with Action Chunking) is an LLM reinforcement-learning algorithm that replaces terminal-reward advantages with a learned critic's scores at the end of 10k-token action chunks continued from replayed trajectory prefixes. Trust in the critic is gated per problem by a readiness test, and the critic is conditioned on reference solutions. Trained on olympiad proofs, AC2 beats GRPO's peak IMO-ProofBench score with 2.5x fewer decoding FLOPs.

## Problem

- GRPO-style RL with verifiable rewards gives every token of a long rollout one terminal-reward advantage, so a minor late error penalizes an otherwise valuable reasoning trajectory.
- Recent critic-based LLM RL methods use learned value models only as baselines and still roll every trajectory to completion to observe terminal rewards, which is effectively GAE with lambda near 1.
- Learned critics are seen as too inaccurate to trust alone, while Monte-Carlo segment-value methods like VinePPO and SPO need many extra rollout tokens for each advantage estimate.

## Method

- ==Action-chunked lambda=0 advantages==: from replay-buffer prefixes, sample 16 continuations of up to 10k tokens, score each endpoint with the ==critic==, and subtract the group-mean value.
- ==Local readiness==: a per-problem switch that allows critic-based updates only when global and problem-specific critic errors fall below thresholds and a correct solution exists. Otherwise it uses full rollouts with terminal rewards.
- ==Generative shared-weight critic==: the policy model is prompted to output a discretized value, conditioned on a known-correct reference solution, and fit to group-mean targets. One quarter of ready problems are audited with full rollouts.

## Results

- AC2 passes GRPO's **18.50%** peak IMO-ProofBench score at **0.79e20** versus **1.99e20** decoding FLOPs (**2.5x** less), in **90** versus **120** steps, and peaks at **20.57%**.
- Prefix GRPO, which uses the same replayed-prefix states but full rollouts, lags well behind. 2k-token chunks peak at **16.62%** and then drop to **13.57%**, and removing local readiness stalls learning.
- Group-mean critic values match mean terminal rewards with MAE **0.065**, versus **0.211** for prefix predictions. Reference solutions lower critic error at every checkpoint, and about **70%** of problems become ready by step 200.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned critic can fully replace terminal rewards in LLM RL updates when trust is gated per problem, which opens off-policy, partial-rollout, and tree-search algorithm designs.
> - Credit-assignment granularity should match what the critic can judge reliably: coarse multi-thousand-token chunks beat finer ones because longer reasoning segments are easier to evaluate.
> - Caution: local readiness needs repeated epochs over a fixed problem set to measure critic error, so the gains may not carry over to single-pass, data-unconstrained training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

AC2 targets a specific bottleneck in reinforcement learning for long mathematical proofs. In the common GRPO setup, every token in a proof gets the same credit or blame from the proof's final score. Long proof rollouts also consume decoding work before a terminal reward can be used. AC2 instead resumes saved partial proofs and generates a shorter continuation, or "action chunk." A learned critic then estimates how much credit the unfinished proof would earn. AC2 does this only when its safeguards indicate the critic is ready for that particular problem. In one proof-training setup, AC2 clears GRPO's best mean validation score with much less *decoding* work.

Left: GRPO continuations end in terminal rewards and receive their reward minus the group mean. AC2 starts from a replayed prefix, branches into short chunks, and uses critic values at chunk endpoints. The diagram is schematic. Right: IMO-ProofBench score against decoding FLOPs, which is not elapsed time or all training compute. AC2 reaches GRPO's peak-score region at lower plotted decoding compute and rises above it. Prefix GRPO remains below AC2.

### 2. From whole proofs to chunks

GRPO samples complete responses to a problem. It uses the group's terminal rewards to form a baseline and assigns a shared advantage to every token of each response. The authors illustrate the cost of this approach: a small calculation mistake at the end of a proof can make a terminal-reward method penalize an otherwise valuable reasoning trajectory.

AC2 takes a prefix from a saved trajectory and samples continuations. If the problem is ready, it stops them after a chunk of up to 10,000 new tokens; the full response budget is 50,000 tokens. The critic estimates the expected eventual rubric score at each unfinished endpoint. AC2 compares those endpoint estimates within the group to assign one advantage to each newly generated chunk. The replayed prefix gets no actor loss. The critic is trained to predict the group's average endpoint value from the starting prefix. The same model parameters serve as actor and critic. The critic is prompted with the problem and partial proof, and with a previously successful reference proof when one is available.

### 3. When the critic earns trust

Readiness works as a per-problem gate based on observed critic error. A problem is ready only if it meets three conditions:

- It meets the global error threshold over the preceding five steps.
- It passes a tighter local error threshold based on its prior sample.
- It has at least one correct policy-generated trajectory.

For unready problems, continuations run to terminal rewards, which supervise the policy and critic. Auditing also sends one-quarter of sampled ready problems to full rollouts, so terminal outcomes remain available to monitor and train the critic. In the main run, the global threshold was first crossed at step 7. The fraction of sampled problems marked ready then rises to about 70% by step 200.

Sampled ready problems rise toward about 70% near step 200. Critic error on sampled prefixes declines over training, and audited critic error is shown against full terminal outcomes. These are measurements for this training run, not proof that the critic is accurate on all problems or tasks.

### 4. What the comparisons establish

The authors trained Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 on FineProofs-RL and evaluated it on IMO-ProofBench, which has 60 Olympiad proof problems with rubric grading. The score is the mean judge score, with proofs graded from 0 to 7 and the mean shown as a percentage. It is not an all-or-nothing pass rate. Evaluation draws 16 responses per problem every ten training steps.

AC2 first exceeds GRPO's peak mean score of 18.50% at 0.79 × 10²⁰ decoding FLOPs, compared with 1.99 × 10²⁰ for GRPO. The authors report this as 2.5× fewer decoding FLOPs. AC2's peak mean score is 20.57%, versus 18.50%.

A skeptic might credit the replayed starting states rather than the critic. The Prefix GRPO control addresses this. It uses replayed prefixes like AC2, but continues them to completion and still uses terminal-reward GRPO advantages. It falls behind AC2 in the score-versus-decoding-FLOPs comparison.

### 5. The new stopping point

In this repeated-data proof-training setup, a guarded critic can sometimes replace waiting for a complete outcome with learning from an unfinished segment. The readiness strategy depends on epoching the training data to obtain complete rollouts as ground truth. Adapting it to a single-epoch setting remains future work. The 2.5× figure concerns decoding FLOPs only. It excludes prefill, validation, value-function and judge calls, training forward and backward computation, and other costs.
