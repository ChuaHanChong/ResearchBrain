# Phys4D: Fine-Grained Physics-Consistent 4D Modeling from Video Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Phys4D develops a three-stage training paradigm for video diffusion models, integrating physics-based simulation data and explicit depth/motion supervision to generate fine-grained physics-consistent 4D world representations. The approach improved Physics-IQ scores by up to 11.4 percentage points on various backbones while maintaining high video quality and demonstrating superior 4D geometry and motion consistency.

## Problem

- Existing video diffusion models frequently produce fine-grained physical inconsistencies (e.g., implausible geometry, unstable motion) because their training prioritizes visual appearance over physical laws.
- There is a scarcity of scalable, high-fidelity supervision data with dense, temporally aligned ground-truth geometry and motion for training physics-aware 4D models.
- Many current physics-aware video generation methods are limited in scalability and generalization due to operating at the image level, focusing on short-horizon dynamics, or relying on specialized 3D representations.

## Method

- A three-stage training paradigm is introduced, progressively injecting physical structure into pretrained video diffusion models: pseudo-supervised pretraining, physics-grounded supervised fine-tuning, and simulation-grounded reinforcement learning.
- A scalable physics-based simulation pipeline, built on Isaac Sim, generates diverse and richly annotated 4D datasets for dense ground-truth geometry and motion supervision.
- Lightweight auxiliary heads for depth and motion prediction are attached to the ==diffusion model==, trained with physics-aware losses like warp-based geometry-motion consistency, and reinforcement learning is used for long-horizon physical consistency.

## Results

- Phys4D consistently improved overall Physics-IQ scores across tested video diffusion backbones, with the CogVideoX-5b backbone showing an 11.4 percentage point increase (from 18.8% to 30.2%).
- The model achieved superior per-frame 3D geometry accuracy (e.g., AbsRel 0.2711 vs. 0.3929 for WAN + DAV2) and enhanced temporal geometry-motion consistency, evidenced by reduced depth warp errors and improved optical flow accuracy.
- Demonstrated better world-level 4D geometry consistency (lowest 4D Chamfer Distance of 0.4626) and improved long-horizon trajectory dynamics, including lower Worldline L2 Error and reduced failure rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale physics-based simulation provides a scalable and accurate source for fine-grained 4D ground-truth data, essential for training physics-aware generative models.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training approach can effectively integrate explicit depth and motion supervision and reinforcement learning into pretrained video diffusion models to instill physical consistency.
> - Enforcing warp-based geometry-motion consistency during training is critical for coupling 3D structure and its temporal evolution, leading to more coherent 4D representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced rapid advancements, with models demonstrating increasingly high visual realism and fidelity. However, despite generating visually plausible content, many of these models implicitly lack a foundational understanding of physical laws. Videos are 2D observations of dynamic 3D scenes, meaning that inherent physical states like geometry, depth, object motion, contact, and deformation are often not explicitly represented or consistently maintained in generated sequences. This gap between visual plausibility and physical understanding presents a significant challenge for developing true "world models" capable of anticipating and interacting with environments according to physical principles.

Previous research efforts to introduce physics into video generation typically operate at the video-level, employing motion priors, reinforcement learning, or action-conditioned simulation interfaces. While these methods improve specific aspects of physical plausibility, they often rely on RGB-observable cues or task-specific controls, potentially overlooking the underlying 3D structure and its temporal evolution. Simultaneously, the domain of 4D scene modeling has explored rich representations like neural radiance fields and Gaussian splatting for reconstruction and controlled 3D generation. However, integrating these explicit 4D representations directly into large-scale video diffusion models for open-ended generation remains a complex problem, often requiring specialized architectures or explicit 4D optimization.

Phys4D addresses this landscape by proposing a structure-focused, appearance-constrained physics injection framework. It positions itself by leveraging the strong real-video generative prior of existing video diffusion models while simultaneously transferring simulation-derived physical structure. This approach contrasts with direct fine-tuning on simulated videos, which often introduces simulator-specific visual biases and degrades the visual diversity of models trained on internet-scale real video data. Instead, Phys4D introduces a "4D" (geometry and motion evolving over time) interface, compatible with video diffusion models, to expose and supervise physical variables, thereby aiming to bridge the gap between visually realistic 2D projections and physically coherent 3D scene evolution without abandoning the established strengths of current video generators.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of Phys4D is to enable the generation of videos that are not only visually realistic but also adhere to physically plausible scene evolution. This is motivated by the observation that while recent video generation models produce increasingly realistic visual outputs, their internal representations do not necessarily encode physical understanding. Physical laws govern the evolution of 3D world states, yet videos merely capture their 2D projections. The authors define this goal as a "structure-transfer problem," where physical knowledge from simulation should explicitly supervise the underlying structure, rather than just the RGB appearance.

The motivations for this approach are multifaceted:
1. **Addressing the Realism-Plausibility Gap:** Current video generation models often exhibit physical inconsistencies, such as objects deforming unnaturally, disappearing, or interacting implausibly. This highlights a fundamental limitation in their implicit understanding of the 3D world dynamics.
2. **Scalability of Physics Injection:** Fine-grained dynamics involve diverse physical regimes (e.g., rigid body, deformable, fluid, contact). Manually translating each physical law into generator-specific losses or constraints is not scalable for open-ended interactions.
3. **Leveraging Physics-Based Simulation:** Physics-based simulation offers a powerful, executable form of human physical knowledge. It inherently encodes physical principles through solvers and constraints, providing dense, temporally aligned structural signals such as depth, motion, visibility, object masks, and trajectories, which are rarely available in real-world video data.
4. **Mitigating Appearance Mismatch:** A critical challenge in using simulation is the visual domain gap. Simulated videos, generated from procedural assets and specific renderers, often possess visual statistics that differ significantly from internet-scale real videos. Directly training a generator on simulated RGB data risks causing "appearance drift" and degrading the model's pretrained real-video generative prior.
5. **Prioritizing Structure over Appearance:** To overcome the appearance mismatch, the core motivation behind Phys4D is the principle that "simulation teaches physics, not appearance." This means simulation should primarily provide structural supervision, while the model's appearance generation capability remains anchored to its real-video prior.

To achieve these objectives, Phys4D introduces an RGB-D-motion 4D interface, which allows for compatibility with existing video diffusion models while exposing explicit physical variables for geometry-level and motion-level supervision and evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Phys4D employs a structure-focused, appearance-constrained framework that integrates physical knowledge into pretrained video diffusion models through a three-stage training pipeline, supported by a custom-built scalable coupled-physics simulation system.

**A. Scalable Physics Simulation for 4D Supervision**
The foundation of Phys4D is a sophisticated simulation system built upon Isaac Sim and GarmentLab. This system is designed to generate diverse physical interactions and provide dense structural annotations, acting as an "executable physical teacher."
- **Unified Coupled-Physics Framework:** Integrates rigid-body dynamics, Position-Based Dynamics (PBD), Finite Element Method (FEM), and task-specific solvers. It supports a wide array of isolated regimes (rigid objects, garments, fluids, deformables, ropes, granular materials, thermodynamics) and cross-regime coupling (e.g., rigid-fluid, rigid-garment).
- **Asynchronous Rollout Generation:** To scale supervision, the system uses asynchronous rollouts, running independent scenes in parallel. This efficiently generates a large dataset: 200 base scenes expanded to approximately 250,000 environments, yielding 1.25 million annotated 10-second videos (about 3,500 hours and over 15TB of multimodal annotations).
- **Domain Randomization:** Physical parameters (mass, friction, restitution, stiffness, gravity), interaction layouts, geometry, and visual factors are randomized to encourage learning invariant geometry-motion regularities, preventing reliance on renderer-specific shortcuts.
- **4D Supervision Interface:** All rollouts export unified annotations including RGB frames, depth, scene flow, dynamic masks, camera poses, and spatiotemporal point trajectories. These signals convert solver-specific states into a common geometry-motion-trajectory supervision format.

**B. Three-Stage Training Pipeline**

1. **Stage I: Prior-Preserving 4D Interface Construction**
 This initial stage establishes an RGB-D-motion interface without altering the pretrained video diffusion model's RGB generative prior or using simulation data.
 - **Model Architecture:** Two lightweight auxiliary heads (a motion head for inter-frame optical flow $\hat{F}_{t \to t+1}$ and a depth head for per-frame depth $\hat{D}_t$) are attached to the *frozen* pretrained DiT-style video diffusion backbone. The depth head incorporates temporal attention and multi-scale feature fusion for coherence.
 - **Data Sources:** Training uses pseudo-labels from strong off-the-shelf monocular depth (Video Depth Anything) and optical flow (SEA-RAFT) estimators. These are applied to both *curated internet videos* (for real-world diversity) and *self-generated videos* from the pretrained model (for output distribution alignment). Confidence masks and per-clip depth normalization are used.
 - **Training Objective:** Supervised regression ($L_{pseudo\_D}$, $L_{pseudo\_F}$) matches predicted depth and flow to pseudo-labels. Gradients are applied *only* to the auxiliary heads, leaving the backbone unchanged.

2. **Stage II: Mask-Gated Local Physics Injection**
 This stage injects local physical structure derived from simulation into the model, again with the pretrained video backbone remaining *frozen*. Simulation supervision is carefully controlled to prevent appearance drift.
 - **Mask-Gated Physics Adapter:** A LoRA-style residual adapter $A_l$ is introduced as the *only* trainable pathway for simulation updates. The adapted features are $h'_l = h_l + \alpha(\sigma) \hat{G}_l \odot A_l(h_l)$.
 - **Noise Gate $\alpha(\sigma)$:** Activates the adapter only at *high-noise* denoising steps, which determine coarse structure and motion. Low-noise, appearance-refinement steps remain dominated by the real-video prior.
 - **Dynamic-Region Gate $\hat{G}_l$:** A learned gate supervised by simulator object and dynamic masks. This concentrates updates on physics-relevant dynamic regions, but it is not required at inference.
 - **Structural Supervision:** Simulator-provided depth, optical flow, visibility, and dynamic-region annotations are used for supervision. Dynamic masks $M^{dyn}_t$ act as soft weights (e.g., $w_t(u) = 1 + \beta M^{dyn}_t(u)$) to emphasize dynamic regions while preserving global scene geometry.
 - **Masked Depth & Motion Supervision ($L_{mask\_depth}$, $L_{mask\_motion}$):** The depth and motion heads are supervised by simulator depth and flow, respectively, with visibility and dynamic masks.
 - **Simulation-Flow Guided Geometry Warp ($L_{mask\_geom\_warp}$):** Ensures temporal coherence by enforcing that predicted depth at time $t$, warped by simulator-provided motion $F^{sim}_{t \to t+1}$, matches predicted depth at $t+1$. This teaches local geometry-motion coupling under physically valid correspondences.
 - **Generated-Frame RGB Warp Self-Consistency ($L_{self\_rgb\_warp}$):** A weak term encouraging adjacent *generated* RGB frames to be consistent under *predicted* motion. Crucially, it does not use simulator-rendered RGB as a photometric target.
 - **High-Noise Flow Matching ($L_{high\_FM}$):** Uses latents encoded from simulator-rendered RGB videos (only through the adapter pathway) to keep the adapted high-noise denoising trajectory compatible with the video manifold. This is a restricted simulator-latent adaptation, not direct RGB fine-tuning.
 - **Overall Objective ($L_{S2}$):** Combines $L_{high\_FM}$, $L_{self\_rgb\_warp}$, $L_{mask\_depth}$, $L_{mask\_motion}$, $L_{mask\_geom\_warp}$, and $L_{gate}$. Gradients flow only to the physics adapters, learned gates, and auxiliary heads.

3. **Stage III: Simulation-Grounded 4D Alignment by Direct Policy Optimization**
 This final stage aligns the global 4D evolution of generated scenes with simulation, addressing longer-range consistency not fully captured by local, adjacent-frame losses.
 - **Simulation-Paired Rollouts:** This stage operates on *simulation-paired training scenes*, where ground-truth 4D trajectories ($P_{sim}$) are available from the simulator under identical initial states, cameras, physical parameters, and prompts. The generated video is conditioned on the simulator-rendered initial frame and prompt.
 - **4D Trajectory Construction:** The frozen Stage I interface lifts generated RGB-D-motion sequences into a spatiotemporal point set ($P_{gen}$). Dynamic and visibility masks filter active regions.
 - **Simulation-Grounded 4D Alignment Reward ($R_{4D}$):** A unified, category-agnostic metric is used: the negative symmetric 4D Chamfer Distance ($CD_{4D}$) between $P_{gen}$ and $P_{sim}$. The 4D distance $d_{4D}(p, q) = ||x_p - x_q||^2_2 + \lambda_t |\tau_p - \tau_q|^2$ penalizes both spatial and temporal misalignment. This reward relies on simulator-paired trajectories generated by category- or solver-specific physical simulators.
 - **Direct Policy Optimization (PPO-style):** The stochastic reverse denoising process is formulated as a policy $\pi_{\theta+\phi}$. Only the Stage II LoRA adapters ($\phi$) are optimized. The depth head, motion head, and learned gate are frozen. A KL regularization term is included to prevent drift from the Stage II policy.
 - **Inference:** After Stage III training, only the learned adapters are used. No simulator-specific information (trajectories, depth, masks) is required at inference time for open-domain generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated Phys4D across three video generation backbones (CogVideoX-5B, WAN2.2-5B, Open-Sora V1.2) from three perspectives: observable physical plausibility, 4D geometry-motion consistency, and visual prior preservation.

**A. RQ1: Improved Observable Physical Plausibility**
Phys4D consistently enhanced the observable physical plausibility of generated videos across all tested backbones and three independent external diagnostics:
- **Physics-IQ:** Phys4D achieved substantial improvements, with CogVideoX's score increasing from 18.8 to 32.8, WAN2.2's from 16.8 to 30.9, and Open-Sora V1.2's from 14.5 to 24.7. This included relative gains in Spatiotemporal IoU (e.g., 56.9% for CogVideoX) and significant reductions in MSE.
- **VBench-2.0 Physics:** The overall Physics Score improved across all backbones (e.g., WAN2.2 from 0.4928 to 0.6247, a 26.8% relative gain). Improvements were observed across all sub-dimensions, including Mechanics, Thermotics, Material, and Multi-View Consistency.
- **PhyGenBench:** Average scores increased across all backbones (e.g., WAN2.2 from 0.45 to 0.57, a 26.7% relative gain). Consistent improvements were noted in categories such as Mechanics, Optics, Thermal phenomena, and Material properties.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons demonstrated that Phys4D generated more coherent fluid flow consistent with gravity, preserved object geometry during manipulation, maintained correct object counts and trajectories during rolling, and produced realistic soft-body deformations and slicing dynamics.

**B. RQ2: Improved 4D Geometry–Motion–Trajectory Consistency**
Evaluations on held-out simulated scenes with ground-truth 4D data showed that Phys4D improved the underlying RGB-D-motion interface:
- **Per-Frame 3D Geometry:** Phys4D significantly improved depth accuracy metrics. For example, on WAN2.2, AbsRel was reduced from 0.3929 (baseline with off-the-shelf estimators) to 0.2386, and RMSE from 0.9012 to 0.5712.
- **Temporal Geometry and Motion Consistency:** Marked improvements were observed in depth warp consistency (WAN2.2 L1 error reduced from 0.7990 to 0.4558) and optical flow accuracy (WAN2.2 Flow EPE reduced from 1.2516 to 0.4478). This indicated more stable geometry and motion across adjacent frames.
- **Sequence-Level 4D World Evolution:** Phys4D achieved lower 4D Chamfer distances (WAN2.2 from 0.5058 to 0.4159), reduced trajectory mean drift and final drift, and decreased the failure rate of object tracks (WAN2.2 from 12.38% to 8.36%), while increasing the length of valid trajectories.
- **Novel-Time Continuity:** The framework showed better interpolation behavior at unseen timestamps, reducing both Novel-Time Depth Error and Novel-Time Warp Error.
- **Comparison with other 4D methods:** Phys4D outperformed methods like PhysGen3D and TesserAct on most 4D diagnostics, including 4D Chamfer and trajectory stability.
- **Rigid-Body Consistency:** On rigid-body scenes, Phys4D reduced momentum error by 29.0% and energy error by 29.2% compared to the baseline.

**C. RQ3: Preservation of Real-Video Visual Prior**
VBench evaluations confirmed that Phys4D preserved the visual quality and diversity of the pretrained real-video generative prior:
- **Visual Quality:** The "Visual Avg." score (average of Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality, and Color) remained nearly unchanged across all backbones after Phys4D training (e.g., WAN2.2 0.7521 vs. 0.7519), indicating no significant degradation in appearance-level quality.
- **Style and Action:** Phys4D consistently maintained or improved Appearance Style and Human Action scores, suggesting compatibility with open-domain generation behaviors without trading off style or motion realism for physical structure.
- **FVD:** Frechet Video Distance (FVD) also improved for Phys4D, indicating better visual quality and domain fidelity after training.

**D. RQ4: Effectiveness of Design Choices (Ablations)**
Ablation studies on WAN2.2-5B using Physics-IQ elucidated the contribution of individual Phys4D components:
- **Stage-Wise Contribution:** Stage II (local physics injection) provided the primary gain (Physics-IQ 16.8 to 27.8), and Stage III (global trajectory alignment) further enhanced it (27.8 to 30.9). Stage III alone yielded minimal improvement, highlighting the importance of local initialization.
- **Auxiliary Signals:** Even without depth and flow supervision, Stage II improved Physics-IQ, indicating that gains were not solely due to improved auxiliary predictions but also modifications to the RGB generator's physical behavior.
- **Noise Interval for Injection:** Injecting physics supervision at *high-noise* denoising steps (coarse structure formation) was most effective (Physics-IQ 27.8), outperforming full-interval or low-noise-only adaptation, which supports the "structure-stage" physics injection principle.
- **Loss Components:** All main structural losses ($L_{mask\_depth}$, $L_{mask\_motion}$, $L_{mask\_geom\_warp}$) were critical, with removing any of them leading to significant performance degradation. This confirmed the necessity of coupled geometry-motion learning. The RGB warp self-consistency ($L_{self\_rgb\_warp}$) was useful but less impactful.
- **Simulator RGB Fine-Tuning Control:** Direct fine-tuning with simulator RGB (e.g., full simulator RGB LoRA or full-interval flow matching) improved physics scores but substantially degraded the real-video visual prior (lower VBench scores, worse FVD). Phys4D, with its high-noise RGB matching and structural losses, achieved the best balance of physics improvements and prior preservation.
- **Temporal Weight in 4D Chamfer:** A moderate temporal penalty ($\lambda_t = 0.03$) in the 4D Chamfer reward provided the best balance of Physics-IQ and trajectory stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Phys4D represents a significant advancement in the field of generative video models by explicitly addressing the critical gap between visual realism and physical understanding. Its primary significance lies in formulating physical knowledge injection as a structure-transfer problem, where rich, executable physical information from simulation is leveraged to supervise geometric and motion consistency, rather than superficial visual appearance.

The development of a scalable coupled-physics simulation system that produces dense, multimodal 4D annotations is a notable contribution, offering a robust data source for training physics-aware generative models. The three-stage training framework—comprising prior-preserving interface construction, mask-gated local physics injection, and simulation-grounded 4D trajectory alignment—provides a principled and effective method for integrating this structural knowledge. By freezing the pretrained backbone and using lightweight adapters and gate mechanisms, Phys4D successfully transfers physical intelligence without compromising the high visual fidelity and open-domain generative capacity derived from large-scale real-video datasets.

The consistent empirical improvements across diverse video generation backbones and multiple external physics diagnostics, coupled with the detailed 4D world-level evaluations, demonstrate the effectiveness of Phys4D in enhancing observable physical behavior, geometry-motion consistency, and trajectory-level evolution. Furthermore, the careful design that preserves the real-video generative prior is crucial, ensuring that the enhanced models remain suitable for practical applications requiring high-quality, diverse visual output.

The potential impact of Phys4D extends to several domains:
- **Generative World Models:** It offers a pathway towards creating more intelligent video models that can reason about and anticipate physical interactions, moving beyond mere visual synthesis.
- **Simulation and Robotics:** Generating physically consistent videos can benefit synthetic data generation for robotics training, virtual environment development, and realistic simulation scenarios.
- **Scientific Content Creation:** Artists and creators could leverage these models to produce visually stunning animations and simulations that also adhere to fundamental physical principles, opening new avenues for educational and entertainment content.
- **Video Editing and Manipulation:** Improved physical consistency can lead to more robust video editing tools capable of physically plausible object manipulation and scene modification.

While the current work focuses on short-horizon videos, and future efforts are needed to scale to longer durations and more complex real-world dynamics, Phys4D's success in explicitly training and evaluating a 4D interface highlights a crucial direction for advancing generative AI. The authors have committed to releasing code and datasets, which will facilitate reproducibility and further research in physics-aware video generation. However, they also acknowledge the potential for misuse in generating deceptive media, emphasizing the importance of responsible release with appropriate documentation and usage guidelines.
