# Towards Human-level Dexterous Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new hand-object co-tracking controller, TELEDEXTER, empowers dexterous robotic teleoperation by translating operator intent into learned, low-level contact execution for complex in-hand manipulations and long-horizon tool use. This system achieves an average success rate of 75.2% on diverse real-world tasks with a SharpaWave hand, outperforming existing baselines, and further enables the creation of training data for autonomous policies.

## Problem

- Current robotic teleoperation systems lack the precision and robustness for dynamic hand-object contact control required for human-level in-hand manipulation.
- Kinematic retargeting, a dominant teleoperation approach, disregards hand-object contact forces and object inertia, leading to instability and frequent task failures.
- Existing reinforcement learning methods for dexterous manipulation struggle with generalizing across diverse in-hand skills without extensive task-specific reward engineering or suffer from restrictive dense frame-wise tracking.

## Method

- Introduces **TELEDEXTER**, a ==hand-object co-tracking== controller that translates operator-specified geometric targets (fingertip positions, object poses) into physical realization via a learned, low-level contact execution.
- Employs a single-stage ==Reinforcement Learning== framework with a novel ==consecutive subgoal tracking== formulation and a ==hybrid reward== design to learn diverse contact-switching strategies in simulation.
- Integrates a ==geometry-aware retargeting== pipeline for human demonstrations and applies ==domain randomization== with a new ==random action masking== technique to achieve robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- Achieved an average **75.2%** success rate and **87.1%** task progress on **seven** real-world dexterous tasks with a SharpaWave robot hand, significantly surpassing baseline teleoperation methods which yielded near-zero success.
- Demonstrated generalization across robot hand embodiments, maintaining **60.0%** to **73.3%** success rates on reorientation tasks with a LeapHand, and successfully handled arbitrary in-hand object tracking goal transitions with up to **97.6%** per-target success.
- The teleoperation data collected with TELEDEXTER enabled training autonomous Diffusion Policies to perform contact-intensive tasks with **40.0%** to **73.3%** success, highlighting its efficacy as a data collection method for autonomous learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a co-tracking controller for both hand and object in a single-stage RL framework, guided by human reference motions through consecutive subgoals, allows the robot to discover robust dynamic contact strategies for complex in-hand manipulation.
> - The consecutive subgoal tracking formulation is crucial for learning, outperforming dense frame-wise tracking by enabling the policy to explore and find physically feasible contact strategies.
> - Random action masking acts as an effective action-space regularizer, preventing overfitting to simulation dynamics and enabling robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for dexterous tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic systems capable of human-level dexterity in manipulation tasks remains a significant challenge in robotics. Human operators routinely perform complex in-hand manipulations such as wielding tools, swapping grasps, and repositioning objects, requiring seamless orchestration of contact transitions involving translation, re-orientation, and finger gaiting. Endowing robots with this level of agility through teleoperation necessitates precise control of object motion via dynamic hand-object contact, a capability that current teleoperation systems generally lack.

Existing dexterous teleoperation approaches can be broadly categorized into two paradigms:
- **Kinematic Retargeting:** This dominant approach maps human hand motion directly onto robotic topologies, often leveraging vision-based tracking or wearable exoskeleton gloves. While offering an intuitive and low-latency interface that captures the operator's kinematic intent, these systems typically disregard hand-object contact forces and object inertia. This limitation results in instability during high-acceleration maneuvers, non-prehensile interactions, and continuous finger-gaiting, frequently leading to object slippage or task failures.
- **Learned Action Priors:** An alternative approach involves learning a dexterous action prior in simulation to translate coarse teleoperation commands into contact-rich hand actions. While this paradigm can improve local contact robustness, it often relies on synthetically generated grasp transitions as training targets, which may lack physical feasibility. Additionally, encoding the action prior into a generative model can introduce cascading trajectory drift and covariate shift during closed-loop execution, degrading real-world performance in long-horizon deployments.

Separately, Reinforcement Learning (RL) in simulation has driven progress in dexterous manipulation, including grasping, in-hand reorientation, and dynamic skills. However, these methods typically involve learning task-specific policies with bespoke reward engineering for each skill. Recent efforts have explored using human hand-object interaction data as reference motions to guide RL, aiming to enable diverse manipulation skills without per-task reward design. Nonetheless, these methods frequently learn one policy per trajectory and have not consistently achieved advanced in-hand dexterous skills like reorientation or finger gaiting. This is often attributed to overly restrictive dense frame-wise tracking formulations that limit the RL agent's ability to explore dynamic contact strategies beyond basic grasping and wrist motion.

The presented work, TELEDEXTER, aims to advance the field by addressing these limitations. It introduces a hand-object co-tracking controller that maps operator intent for fingertip positions and object poses to learned, low-level contact execution. This approach departs from pure kinematic mapping and generative models, integrating a dynamic prior through RL guided by human reference motions. It also moves beyond dense frame-wise imitation by employing a consecutive subgoal tracking formulation, intending to enable the discovery of more robust contact-switching strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to take a step towards achieving human-level dexterous teleoperation for robotic hands. The motivation stems from the observation that while humans effortlessly perform complex in-hand manipulation, current robotic teleoperation systems lack the precision and robustness required for dynamic hand-object contact control across diverse interaction modalities.

The specific objectives of the TELEDEXTER project are detailed as follows:
1. **Develop a Robust Hand-Object Co-tracking Controller:** Design a system that can accurately translate operator-specified geometric targets for both fingertip positions and object poses into physical realization through complex multi-contact dynamics. This aims to overcome the limitations of kinematic retargeting, which ignores dynamics, and learned action priors, which can suffer from physical infeasibility and compounding errors.
2. **Enable Learning of Diverse In-Hand Manipulation Skills within a Single-Stage RL Framework:** Train the low-level controller in simulation using Reinforcement Learning. The goal is to allow the policy to learn a variety of contact-switching strategies, including translation, re-orientation, and finger gaiting, without requiring separate policies or extensive task-specific reward engineering. This is to be achieved through a novel consecutive subgoal tracking formulation with a hybrid reward structure.
3. **Construct Physically Grounded Reference Motions:** Develop a geometry-aware retargeting pipeline that converts unscripted human hand-object demonstrations into robot-specific reference motions. This process is designed to enforce contact-surface attraction and penalize mesh interpenetration, ensuring the generated subgoals are geometrically and physically feasible for the robotic hand.
4. **Ensure Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Implement mechanisms to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap, allowing the trained controller to deploy directly onto real robots without further fine-tuning. This objective specifically involves the integration of domain randomization and a novel random action masking technique.
5. **Evaluate Performance on Challenging Dexterous Tasks:** Assess the capabilities of TELEDEXTER on a range of complex real-world tasks that demand continuous in-hand reorientation and long-horizon tool use across different robotic hand embodiments. This evaluation is intended to demonstrate the system's superiority over existing teleoperation baselines.
6. **Demonstrate Utility for Autonomous Policy Training:** Investigate whether the high-quality, contact-rich teleoperation demonstrations collected using TELEDEXTER can serve as effective training data for autonomous robot manipulation policies via behavioral cloning. This objective aims to establish TELEDEXTER as a scalable data collection pipeline for advancing autonomous robotic dexterity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TELEDEXTER formulates dexterous teleoperation as a hand-object co-tracking problem. The operator specifies explicit geometric targets for both the fingertip positions and the object pose, and a learned low-level controller executes the multi-contact dynamics necessary to reach these goals.

The methodology comprises three key technical designs:

#### 3.1. Learning a Co-tracking Controller

The co-tracking controller, a policy `a_t = π_θ(o_t, g_t)`, is trained in simulation using Reinforcement Learning. The policy receives an observation `o_t` (current hand joint positions, object pose, gravity direction in wrist frame, previous action) and a co-tracking goal `g_t` (target fingertip positions `ˆp_tip_t` and target object pose `ˆT_o_t`).

- **Consecutive Subgoal Co-tracking:** Human reference motions are converted into a sequence of co-tracking subgoals, sampled at varying intervals. The policy is required to reach each hand-object subgoal sequentially, while being allowed to discover its own contact strategy between subgoals. A subgoal `g_k` is considered reached when fingertip position errors, object position errors, and object rotation errors are below predefined thresholds (`ϵ_tip`, `ϵ_pos`, `ϵ_rot`) for `N_stay` consecutive frames.
- **Hybrid Reward Design:** The reward function combines a sparse consecutive goal-reaching term with a dense tracking reward and a time penalty.
 - **Sparse Subgoal Reward:** `1_reach(t) * w_step(t) * r_score(t)`. `1_reach(t)` is an indicator that fires when the active subgoal is reached. `w_step(t)` weights the reward by the inter-subgoal step size, incentivizing larger jumps. `r_score(t)` measures the match to the active subgoal using exponential kernels for per-finger, object position, and object rotation tracking errors.
 - **Dense Tracking Reward:** `α_dense * r_dense(t)`. This term provides a continuous signal based on fingertip and object tracking, assisting early exploration before subgoals are reliably reached.
 - **Time Penalty:** `-c_time`. A small constant penalty per step encourages efficient subgoal completion.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Training progressively increases difficulty across three dimensions:
 1. **Gravity:** Gradually annealed from reduced to full gravity.
 2. **Subgoal Tracking Tolerances:** `ϵ_tip`, `ϵ_pos`, `ϵ_rot` are tightened over training.
 3. **Inter-subgoal Step Size:** The maximum number of frames skipped between subgoals increases, progressing from fine-grained local tracking to longer-horizon goal jumps.
 Episodes are initialized at random frames, and successful traversal of a trajectory may trigger a cross-trajectory reset to promote generalization.
- **Single-Stage RL Training:** The controller is trained using the SAPG algorithm in Isaac Gym for approximately 10^10 environment steps across 62,400 parallel environments, leveraging 4 NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs.

#### 3.2. Hand-Object Reference Motion Construction

Human hand-object interaction trajectories, recorded by a motion-capture system, are converted into robot hand-object reference motions through a geometry-aware retargeting pipeline. The recorded interactions (150 trajectories, 20s each, per object) encompass in-hand translation, rotation, and free-play including tool-use sequences.

- **Geometry-aware Retargeting:** This process occurs in two stages:
 1. **Vector-based Retargeting:** An initial optimization matches human hand geometry to robot joint angles using directional and inter-finger vector alignment, following established methods.
 2. **Geometry-aware Optimization:** The result is refined by incorporating the object mesh. The optimization minimizes a composite loss function including:
 - `L_vec`: The vector-retargeting loss.
 - `L_surf`: A surface attraction term that pulls near-contact hand points onto the object surface.
 - `L_pen`: A penetration term that penalizes interpenetration of the hand and object meshes.
 - `L_col`: A self-collision term that prevents inter-finger overlap.
 - `L_smooth`: A temporal smoothness energy to suppress capture jitter.
 The output is a set of robot hand-object reference trajectories (`(q*_t, T_o_t)`) from which the co-tracking goals (`ˆp_tip_t`, `ˆT_o_t`) are constructed.

#### 3.3. Sim-to-Real Robustness

To facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, two primary techniques are employed:

- **Domain Randomization:** Randomization of hand and object dynamics properties (mass, friction), external forces applied to the object, and observation noise/latency is used to enhance the policy's robustness to discrepancies between simulation and reality.
- **Random Action Masking:** This technique serves as an action-space regularizer. During training, a binary mask `m_t` is applied to policy actions `a_t`, randomly freezing a subset of joint commands at their previous values for a sampled duration. This forces the policy to operate effectively even with partially stale commands, mimicking real-world actuation discrepancies and preventing overfitting to perfectly synchronized simulated actuation.

#### 3.4. Real-world Teleoperation Deployment

The learned co-tracking controller is deployed zero-shot as the teleoperation controller. A real-time system captures the operator's wrist, fingertip, and object poses. The robot arm tracks the operator's wrist via inverse kinematics, while the operator's fingertip and object poses define the co-tracking goal for the trained policy. For initial contact, kinematic retargeting handles pre-grasp positioning, and once stable contact is established, the operator switches to the co-tracking controller for dexterous manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several findings derived from experimental evaluations.

#### 4.1. Dexterous Teleoperation Evaluation

TELEDEXTER was evaluated on seven real-world dexterous tasks using two different robot hand embodiments: LeapHand (4-finger, 16-DoF) and SharpaWave (5-finger, 22-DoF). The tasks included three reorientation tasks (CylinderReorient, CuboidReorient, BunnyReorient) and four long-horizon tool-use tasks (HammerUse, BrushSweep, ScrewdriverUse, BulbReplace). Performance was measured by Success Rate (SR) and Task Progress (TP).

- **Performance on SharpaWave:** TELEDEXTER achieved an average SR of 75.2% and an average TP of 87.1% across all seven tasks. In contrast, baseline methods, including kinematic retargeting approaches (DexRT, GeoRT) and a learned generative action prior (DexGen), consistently exhibited near-zero SR and low TP, typically failing at or shortly after the initial stages requiring in-hand reorientation or functional grasp transitions. SimToolReal, an object-centric sim-to-real tool manipulation method (not a teleoperation approach), achieved at most 26.7% SR on a single task, indicating challenges in generalization across diverse contact transitions. The narrow gap between SR and TP for TELEDEXTER suggests that most failures occurred at later task stages, while baselines often collapsed early.
- **Generalization Across Embodiments (LeapHand):** When trained with the same human reference motions and applied to the LeapHand, TELEDEXTER achieved 60.0% to 73.3% SR on reorientation tasks. This indicates the framework's ability to generalize across robotic hand morphologies with minimal embodiment-specific tuning, primarily adapting the geometry-aware retargeting stage.
- **Any-to-Any Reposition Evaluation:** An "any-to-any" test on LeapHand demonstrated the controller's ability to handle arbitrary in-hand hand-object tracking goal transitions. The controller reached an average of 41.1 consecutive targets (97.6% per-target SR) for the Cylinder and 12.1 consecutive targets (92.3% per-target SR) for the Cuboid. The performance difference was attributed to the object's geometry, with the cylinder's smooth surface facilitating transitions more readily than the cuboid's edges and corners.

#### 4.2. From Teleoperation to Autonomy

The study demonstrated that the teleoperation data collected using TELEDEXTER could be leveraged to train autonomous policies. Diffusion Policies were trained on 50 expert demonstrations for three dexterous tasks: BulbInstall (46.7% SR), HammerDriver (73.3% SR), and BrushForward (40.0% SR). These tasks represented contact-intensive subsets of their teleoperation counterparts. The results indicate that TELEDEXTER captured sufficiently rich contact-mode coverage for behavioral cloning across diverse skills. Analysis of stage-wise success revealed task-specific bottlenecks, such as sustaining contact force for hammering, precision alignment for bulb installation, or grasping thin handles from visual observations for brushing.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of key methodological components:

- **Consecutive Subgoal Tracking vs. Dense Tracking:** Comparing the consecutive subgoal tracking formulation against a standard dense frame-wise tracking method in simulation showed that consecutive subgoal tracking resulted in significantly longer episode lengths and reached orders of magnitude more subgoals. Dense frame-wise tracking was found to be overly restrictive, hindering the policy's ability to discover physically feasible contact strategies.
- **Random Action Masking:** Removing random action masking from the training pipeline led to substantial degradation in real-world teleoperation performance across all evaluated tasks (e.g., HammerUse SR dropped from 66.7% to 33.3%, ScrewdriverUse SR from 73.3% to 0%). This confirmed that random action masking serves as a critical action-space regularization technique, preventing the policy from overfitting to simulation dynamics and enabling robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents TELEDEXTER, a hand-object co-tracking controller that contributes to the field of dexterous robotic teleoperation. The work addresses limitations in current teleoperation systems by enabling precise control of object motion through dynamic hand-object contact, a capability that existing kinematic retargeting and generative action prior methods have not fully achieved.

The significance of TELEDEXTER is multifaceted:
- **Advancement in Teleoperation Dexterity:** By mapping operator intent into learned, low-level contact execution via a single-stage RL pipeline, TELEDEXTER enables robots to perform complex in-hand manipulations and long-horizon tool use that were not consistently feasible with previous teleoperation systems. This represents progress towards human-level dexterity in robotic manipulation.
- **Scalable Data Collection for Autonomous Learning:** TELEDEXTER facilitates the collection of high-quality, contact-rich demonstration data that captures diverse in-hand manipulation skills. This data can then be utilized to train autonomous policies through behavioral cloning, offering a viable pathway to endowing robots with autonomous dexterous capabilities without the need for human intervention in the loop during execution. This contributes to bridging the data gap for complex robotic skills.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The integration of domain randomization and the novel random action masking technique addresses a critical challenge in robotics by enabling zero-shot transfer of policies from simulation to real-world robots. This reduces the need for extensive real-world training and calibration.
- **Generalization Across Embodiments and Tasks:** The framework demonstrated effectiveness across different robotic hand morphologies (LeapHand and SharpaWave) and various challenging tasks, including object reorientation and diverse tool use. The consecutive subgoal tracking and hybrid reward design contributed to learning diverse in-hand skills without task-specific reward engineering, suggesting a more generalizable learning approach.

Despite its contributions, the paper identifies limitations that suggest avenues for future research. TELEDEXTER currently learns an object-specific controller, requiring new data and training for each novel object. Developing a unified, object-conditioned controller that generalizes across object categories would enhance its practical applicability. Additionally, the reliance on a motion-capture system for real-time hand and object pose estimation could be replaced by a markerless, vision-based tracking system to lower deployment barriers and broaden the framework's accessibility. Addressing these limitations could further amplify the impact of this work on practical robotic applications.
