# QDepth-VLA: Quantized Depth Prediction as Auxiliary Supervision for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The QDepth-VLA framework enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating quantized depth prediction as an auxiliary task, providing robust 3D geometric understanding. This approach improves robotic manipulation performance on fine-grained and long-horizon tasks in both simulated and real-world settings.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with fine-grained manipulation and long-horizon planning due to inadequate 3D geometric reasoning, despite strong semantic understanding.
- Existing methods for incorporating 3D information into VLAs face issues like increased complexity, information loss, or problematic supervision signals.
- Prior auxiliary depth prediction techniques suffer from noisy depth maps, redundant pixel-wise learning, and interference with the Vision-Language Model's (VLM) pre-trained semantic alignment.

## Method

- **QDepth-VLA** introduces an ==auxiliary quantized depth prediction== task, using a pre-trained ==VQ-VAE== to represent depth maps as discrete latent tokens (less noisy than pixel-wise regression).
- A dedicated ==depth expert module== receives visual embeddings directly from the VLM's vision encoder to predict depth without interfering with VLM semantic processing.
- A ==hybrid attention mechanism== selectively integrates depth information with text, image, proprioception, and action tokens.

## Results

- Improved LIBERO performance: **+8.8%** gain over `open_pi_0` on Spatial tasks; competitive with multi-view baselines using only single-view input.
- Simpler benchmark: **+29.7%** success rate over `open_pi_0` for long-horizon tasks (e.g., 'open top drawer and put apple in').
- Real-robot: **10–20%** performance gains over `open_pi_0` on pick-and-place under challenging conditions.
- Ablation: removing any core component (depth loss, depth expert, latent token prediction, hybrid attention) degrades performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing depth as discrete, quantized latent tokens is more robust for learning salient geometric cues than pixel-wise regression, as it is less susceptible to noise and redundancy.
> - A dedicated, decoupled module for depth prediction is crucial for effectively incorporating 3D information without disrupting the VLM backbone's pre-trained semantic alignment and multimodal reasoning capabilities.
> - Strategic control over information flow through a hybrid attention mechanism allows depth cues to enhance spatial reasoning for action generation without negatively impacting the core visual-language understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a significant area of research in robotic learning, aiming to imbue robots with the ability to understand diverse instructions and visual contexts to perform tasks. These models often leverage pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and extend them with action-generation capabilities. While VLAs demonstrate strong generalization in various scenarios, their performance frequently degrades in tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation or long-horizon planning. This degradation is attributed to a persistent gap between the models' semantic understanding (derived from VLMs) and their ability to reason about the 3D geometric structures of the physical environment. Accurate 3D understanding is considered essential for precise object localization, gripper-object interaction, and avoiding cascading errors during complex manipulation sequences.

Current approaches to integrate geometric information into VLA models generally fall into three categories:
1. **Direct 3D feature injection:** This involves feeding encoded 3D representations, such as point clouds or depth maps, directly into the VLM backbone or the action head. While providing explicit geometric cues, this often requires additional encoders, increasing model complexity, and can interfere with the powerful 2D priors established during large-scale VLM pretraining.
2. **2D-projected 3D feature integration:** This method projects 3D features into 2D representations before feeding them into the VLM. This approach preserves 2D priors but may incur information loss during the projection process, potentially hindering fine-grained manipulation.
3. **Auxiliary 3D information prediction:** This paradigm enhances geometric understanding by training the VLA model to predict 3D information, such as future depth maps, as an auxiliary task. This approach aims to learn 3D-consistent spatial reasoning while preserving 2D VLM priors and avoiding additional sensory inputs during inference.

Despite the potential of auxiliary 3D prediction, existing works in this category have encountered challenges, including inconsistent performance improvements and, in some cases, detrimental effects on policy learning. These issues are typically linked to noisy depth map supervision, redundant pixel-wise learning signals, and potential interference with the VLM's pre-trained semantic alignment. QDepth-VLA is positioned within this third category, aiming to address these identified limitations through a refined approach to depth prediction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatial perception and reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly for fine-grained manipulation tasks that necessitate a robust understanding of 3D environmental structures. The authors identify specific limitations in existing VLA approaches that motivate their proposed framework:

1. **Lack of Robust 3D Understanding:** Current VLA models, despite their semantic comprehension, frequently struggle with precise geometric reasoning. This leads to inaccuracies in object localization and gripper-object interactions, which are critical for executing complex manipulation tasks.
2. **Limitations of Existing 3D Integration Methods:**
 - **Direct 3D Feature Injection:** This method can increase model complexity and computational cost due to the need for separate 3D encoders. More importantly, it can disrupt the powerful 2D priors learned by VLMs during large-scale pretraining, potentially degrading visual-language reasoning.
 - **2D-Projected 3D Features:** While preserving 2D priors, projecting 3D features into 2D inevitably leads to information loss, which can be detrimental for tasks requiring high precision.
 - **Auxiliary 3D Information Prediction:** While a promising alternative that avoids direct fusion and preserves 2D priors, prior attempts at using pixel-wise depth map prediction as an auxiliary task have not yielded consistent performance gains. In some instances, it has been observed to negatively impact policy learning.

The key challenges with existing depth-map-based auxiliary prediction methods are detailed as:
- **Noisy Supervision:** Depth maps often suffer from insufficient spatial-temporal consistency across frames, introducing noise that weakens geometric grounding.
- **Redundant Learning Signals:** Pixel-wise depth regression compels the model to reconstruct every pixel, which can be a redundant learning signal, distracting from salient structural cues crucial for manipulation.
- **Interference with VLM Pre-trained Semantics:** Using a shared vision-language backbone for depth prediction can interfere with its pre-trained semantic alignment, potentially degrading multimodal reasoning.

To address these motivations and challenges, the key objectives of QDepth-VLA are:
- To integrate a robust geometric understanding into VLA models by introducing an auxiliary depth prediction task.
- To represent depth information in a compact and optimization-friendly manner by predicting quantized latent tokens of depth maps, thereby mitigating the impact of noise and focusing on salient structural cues.
- To prevent interference with the VLA model's vision-language backbone by employing a dedicated "depth expert" for depth token prediction.
- To design a hybrid attention mechanism that effectively regulates cross-modal information flow, enhancing spatial reasoning while preserving pretrained semantic grounding.
- To demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed framework in both simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, particularly for long-horizon and fine-grained operations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QDepth-VLA is designed as a general framework that augments VLA models with an auxiliary depth prediction task, leveraging quantized depth representations and a specialized architecture.

#### 3.1. Depth Annotation

To acquire high-quality and temporally consistent depth maps, the authors utilize Video-Depth-Anything (ViDA), a state-of-the-art monocular video depth estimation framework based on a ViT-Large backbone. ViDA is applied to the main-view RGB frames from subsets of the OXE and LIBERO datasets to generate relative depth annotations, which serve as ground truth for depth tokenization and subsequent training.

#### 3.2. VQ-VAE Reconstruction

To represent depth information compactly and discretely, a Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) is pretrained. Given an input depth frame, the VQ-VAE encoder $f_\theta(\cdot)$ produces a latent representation, which is then quantized to the nearest code vector from a learnable codebook $C = \{c_1, \ldots, c_K\}$. The codebook contains $K=256$ entries, each with a dimension of $d=160$. The VQ-VAE is trained with a standard objective function comprising a reconstruction loss, a codebook update term, and a commitment loss. The VQ-VAE is pretrained independently on each dataset using AdamW, ensuring stable convergence and reconstruction quality. This process yields discretized depth code indices, which become the supervisory targets for the depth expert. The authors found a latent grid resolution of $16 \times 16$ to be effective and computationally efficient.

#### 3.3. QDepth-VLA Architecture

QDepth-VLA builds upon the Open $\pi_0$ architecture, extending its VLA pipeline with an additional depth supervision branch. The model consists of three main modules:
- **Pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM):** PaliGemma-3B is used as the backbone, integrating a SigLIP-based vision encoder with Gemma's language modeling capabilities. Input instructions are tokenized, and the main-view RGB image is processed by the SigLIP encoder to obtain visual tokens. These visual tokens are concatenated with text prefix tokens and fed into the Gemma decoder under full block attention to generate multimodal embeddings. This VLM remains trainable during the fine-tuning stage to allow for geometric adaptation.
- **Action Expert:** This is a transformer-based module, adapted from Open $\pi_0$'s action head. It processes multimodal embeddings from the VLM and proprioceptive states to produce executable robot actions. It integrates visual-language context with proprioceptive features via stacked transformer layers and MLP-based encoders/decoders.
- **Depth Expert:** A newly introduced, dedicated transformer-based module responsible for geometric reasoning. Architecturally aligned with the action expert, it takes visual embeddings from the SigLIP encoder (before language fusion) as input. These embeddings are projected via a lightweight MLP, processed by a transformer backbone, and then fed into a shallow CNN decoder to predict 256 depth tokens. Each predicted token corresponds to a latent vector, which is then aligned with the quantized tokens from the pretrained VQ-VAE's codebook. The VQ-VAE decoder can reconstruct the spatial depth map from these latent tokens. This discrete formulation aims to capture compact, structured geometric representations while maintaining optimization stability and avoiding semantic interference with the VLM.

#### 3.4. Hybrid Attention Mechanism

Traditional causal attention mechanisms can introduce noise when directly fusing depth modalities. QDepth-VLA redesigns the hybrid attention mechanism to regulate cross-modal information flow among text, image, depth, proprioception, and action tokens:
- Text and image tokens attend only within their respective modalities to preserve pretrained semantic grounding.
- Depth tokens attend to both image and text tokens, contextualizing geometric features with visual semantics.
- Action tokens attend to all preceding modalities (text, image, depth, and proprioception) to integrate fused perceptual and geometric cues for policy generation.
This hierarchical design allows depth information to enhance spatial understanding without over-interfering with the pretrained VLM and maintains computational efficiency.

#### 3.5. Co-Training Procedures

The model undergoes a co-training process with two primary objectives:
- **Quantized Depth Supervision:** The depth expert predicts latent depth tokens. These tokens are used to compute logits with the encoded image features against the VQ-VAE codebook. A cross-entropy loss is applied using ground-truth code indices obtained from the pretrained VQ-VAE, encouraging the visual encoder to learn geometry-aware embeddings aligned with the quantized depth representation.
- **Action Modeling:** The action prediction objective uses the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) action loss, as in $\pi_0$. This loss enables the model to approximate a continuous-time flow field, guiding noisy action samples towards their ground-truth counterparts. The action chunk is conditioned on observations including the RGB image, language instruction, and end-effector state.

The total loss is a weighted sum of the action loss and the depth loss: $L_{total} = L_{action} + \lambda_t \cdot L_{depth}$. The depth loss weight $\lambda_t$ exponentially decays over training steps, allowing the model to first establish stable geometric alignment before gradually emphasizing action refinement.

#### 3.6. Optimization Setup

QDepth-VLA is trained using the AdamW optimizer with a decoupled weight decay. Learning rates for both the action expert and the VLM backbone are set to $5 \times 10^{-5}$, with a cosine learning rate scheduler, 200 warm-up steps, and a cycle length of $10^7$ steps. The model is trained using a Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP) strategy on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs, with a global batch size of 1024 and an action chunk size of 4 (16 for real-robot experiments).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of QDepth-VLA was evaluated through comprehensive experiments in both simulation and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

**LIBERO Benchmark:**
QDepth-VLA, operating in a single-view setting (using only one RGB image), demonstrated improved performance across the LIBERO benchmark suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long).
- It consistently outperformed single-view baselines like CoT-VLA and Open $\pi_0$. For example, it achieved 94.0% on Goal tasks (6.4% higher than CoT-VLA) and 72.6% on Long tasks (3.6% higher than CoT-VLA).
- Compared to Open $\pi_0$, QDepth-VLA showed improvements across all four subsets, with an 8.8% gain on Spatial tasks.
- Despite being single-view, QDepth-VLA achieved an average success rate competitive with some multi-view VLAs, being only 0.1% lower than $\pi_0$-FAST and exceeding 4D-VLA by 3.1% and DreamVLA by 4.5% on Goal tasks. It also surpassed $\pi_0$-FAST by 12.4% on the Long tasks.
- A multi-view variant of QDepth-VLA, which predicts latent depth tokens for the main-view image, further improved performance, achieving an average success rate of 94.9%. This variant outperformed DreamVLA by 0.1% and $\pi_0$ by 0.8% on Spatial tasks, and reached 90.0% success on Long tasks. The authors note that while some leading multi-view models achieve higher overall results, they often require explicit point cloud or depth map inputs during inference, which QDepth-VLA does not.

**Simpler Benchmark:**
- **Google Robot tasks:** QDepth-VLA achieved a 98.3% success rate on "pick coke can" (0.8% higher than Open $\pi_0$ and 12.3% higher than SpatialVLA). On the "open top drawer and put apple in" task, it reached 62.6%, a 29.7% improvement over Open $\pi_0$. These gains are attributed to enhanced spatial perception and object localization for long-horizon tasks.
- **WidowX250 Robot tasks:** QDepth-VLA demonstrated high success rates, particularly on tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning. On the "stack block" task, it achieved 39.6% (10.4% higher than SpatialVLA). For "Put Eggplant in Basket" and "Put Spoon on Towel," it reached 95.0% and 82.0% respectively, outperforming Open $\pi_0$ by 5.4% and 8.3%. These results underscore the effectiveness of quantized depth supervision for manipulation precision in cluttered 3D environments.

**Depth Reconstruction Visualization:**
Visualizations of depth reconstructions from the predicted quantized features (decoded by VQ-VAE) showed that QDepth-VLA preserves structural details and aligns well with object boundaries. This indicated that the learned depth representations successfully capture spatial geometry.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiments

Experiments were conducted using a 6-DoF Piper robotic arm with a RealSense D455 camera on four pick-and-place tasks. The environment presented challenges such as complex lighting and low visual contrast between the gripper and the dark wooden tabletop.
- QDepth-VLA consistently outperformed ACT across all tasks, achieving robust success rates where ACT struggled.
- Compared to the Open $\pi_0$ baseline, QDepth-VLA showed a 20.0% improvement on "pick banana," and 10.0% gains on Task 3 ("put green block into bowl") and Task 4 ("stack green block on top of yellow block"). These results validate its effectiveness and generalization in challenging real-world scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

A controlled ablation study on the Simpler benchmark isolated the contribution of QDepth-VLA's components:
- **w/o Depth Loss:** Removing depth supervision led to an average performance decrease of 2.9% (from 68.5% to 65.6%). The most notable drops were in "Carrot" (-9.6%) and "Eggplant" (-12.5%) tasks, which require spatial grounding. This suggests that depth supervision provides informative cues beyond the primary control objective.
- **w/o Depth Expert:** Eliminating the dedicated depth branch resulted in the largest performance degradation (-8.5% average), with a significant drop in "Stack Block" (-23.8%) and declines in "Eggplant" (-5.4%) and "Spoon" (-8.3%). This indicates that an explicit, specialized depth pathway is critical for fine-grained spatial reasoning.
- **w/o Latent Prediction:** Replacing latent depth token prediction with direct pixel-wise regression reduced average performance to 64.6% (-3.9%). The "Eggplant" task saw the largest impact (-14.6%). This validates the benefit of quantized latent tokens in abstracting geometric cues and avoiding redundant low-level details.
- **w/o Hybrid Attention:** Replacing the proposed hybrid attention mask with a DreamVLA-style configuration led to a 5.5% average performance decline, with the "Carrot" task dropping by 15.8%. This result suggests that enforcing proprioception-to-depth attention without the careful regulation of the hybrid mask can introduce noise, as relative depth lacks the absolute positional encoding for stable control.

#### 4.4. Computational Cost Analysis

QDepth-VLA incurred a modest increase in parameters (from 2.606B to 2.924B, +12.2%) compared to Open $\pi_0$, primarily due to the depth expert. Inference time overhead in real-robot experiments was minimal, with comparable wall-clock control latency. Depth supervision data was generated offline using Video-Depth-Anything-Large in approximately 4 hours for the Bridge and Fractal datasets. The authors concluded that this computational overhead represents a favorable trade-off for the observed gains.

In summary, the experiments demonstrated that quantized depth supervision, channeled through a dedicated depth expert and regulated by a hybrid attention mechanism, effectively enhances VLA performance by improving spatial reasoning, particularly for complex and fine-grained manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The QDepth-VLA framework introduces a method for enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models through auxiliary quantized depth prediction, which addresses identified limitations in current approaches to integrating 3D spatial information.

The significance of this work lies in several key aspects:

1. **Improved Spatial Reasoning for Robotic Manipulation:** The research demonstrates that incorporating quantized depth information as an auxiliary supervision signal can substantially improve VLA models' spatial perception and reasoning capabilities. This is particularly relevant for fine-grained and long-horizon manipulation tasks, where precise understanding of 3D object positions and interactions is critical. The consistent performance gains observed across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, Simpler) and real-world tasks highlight the practical benefits of this approach.
2. **Robust and Efficient Depth Integration:** By utilizing quantized latent tokens for depth prediction rather than raw pixel-wise regression, QDepth-VLA provides a more compact, structured, and optimization-friendly supervision signal. This approach mitigates the issues of noisy depth supervision and redundant learning signals that have hindered previous attempts at auxiliary depth prediction. The design of a dedicated "depth expert" also ensures that geometric understanding is enhanced without interfering with the VLM's pre-trained semantic alignment, preserving multimodal reasoning capabilities.
3. **Modular and Compatible Architecture:** Building upon established VLA architectures like Open $\pi_0$, QDepth-VLA's modular design allows for integration with existing VLM backbones. The proposed hybrid attention mechanism strategically routes depth cues, enhancing performance without introducing detrimental noise or excessive computational overhead. This modularity suggests that similar auxiliary tasks could potentially be integrated into other VLA frameworks.
4. **Advancement in Single-View VLA Performance:** The results indicate that depth-augmented supervision can significantly compensate for the lack of multi-view observations in single-view VLA models, bringing their performance closer to that of multi-view systems on certain tasks. This is a notable achievement, as multi-view setups often entail higher hardware and computational costs.

**Potential Impact:**

The findings from QDepth-VLA have several potential impacts on the field of robotic learning and embodied AI:

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Robots equipped with QDepth-VLA-like capabilities could perform a wider range of complex manipulation tasks with greater accuracy and robustness, leading to more autonomous agents in manufacturing, logistics, domestic assistance, and hazardous environments.
- **Reduced Data Requirements for 3D Information:** By learning robust depth-aware representations from existing datasets through auxiliary supervision, QDepth-VLA potentially reduces the need for explicitly engineered 3D feature inputs or dense 3D annotations during inference, simplifying system deployment.
- **Inspiration for Future VLA Model Designs:** The success of quantized depth prediction and the specialized expert could inspire the integration of other compact, structured representations of perceptual information (e.g., object affordances, material properties) as auxiliary tasks in future VLA models, further broadening their reasoning capabilities.
- **Foundation for More Generalist Robots:** By bridging the semantic-geometric gap more effectively, QDepth-VLA contributes to the development of more generalist robot policies that can adapt to novel objects and environments based on language instructions and visual cues.

The authors also suggest future research directions, including extending the approach to predict future depth tokens for improved temporal reasoning and exploring more efficient VAE-based depth representations. These directions indicate a continuing effort to refine and expand upon the foundational insights provided by QDepth-VLA, further solidifying its potential impact on the development of advanced robotic systems.
