# ExGRPO: Learning to Reason from Experience

> [!summary] Summary
> EXGRPO is a framework that improves large language model reasoning by intelligently managing and replaying high-quality past experiences within reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR). It yielded average performance gains of +3.5 points on in-distribution math benchmarks and +7.6 points on out-of-distribution general reasoning benchmarks, while also substantially enhancing training stability for weaker models.

## Problem

- Traditional on-policy Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs) is computationally inefficient, discarding valuable reasoning trajectories after a single update.
- RLVR training suffers from instability, particularly for weaker LLMs, often leading to performance collapse or slow convergence due to the constant generation and discarding of new experiences.
- Prior RLVR methods lacked a systematic approach to identify and leverage "valuable" self-generated reasoning experiences within a replay buffer.

## Method

- EXGRPO introduces an ==experience management layer== that intelligently handles a replay buffer, partitioning it dynamically based on question correctness rates.
- The framework prioritizes ==questions of medium difficulty (25-75% correctness rate)== and selects the ==lowest-entropy successful reasoning trajectories== for replay, aiming for higher CoT quality.
- It integrates a ==mixed-policy optimization objective== that combines on-policy samples with replayed experiential samples, utilizing importance reweighting and a non-linear policy shaping function for stable training.

## Results

- EXGRPO achieved average performance gains of **+3.5** points on in-distribution mathematical reasoning benchmarks and **+7.6** points on out-of-distribution general reasoning benchmarks across five diverse LLM backbones.
- It dramatically enhanced training stability for weaker models (e.g., Llama-3.1 8B Base), improving average out-of-distribution performance from **1.3** to **30.8**, where on-policy RLVR failed.
- Ablation studies confirmed that selecting medium-difficulty questions, lowest-entropy trajectories, and utilizing policy shaping are crucial components, preventing performance degradation observed without them.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Medium-difficulty questions and low-entropy reasoning trajectories represent the most valuable learning signals for enhancing LLM reasoning in RLVR.
> - Principled experience management, which strategically identifies and reuses high-quality experiences, significantly boosts both sample efficiency and training stability in RLVR.
> - Prioritizing low-entropy trajectories effectively counters the "snowball effect," where repeatedly sampling misguided, high-entropy experiences can degrade reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) through reinforcement learning (RL). A prominent paradigm for this is Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), where chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is framed as action sequences optimized using rule-based or model-based verifiers for reward generation.

A known challenge in most current RLVR algorithms is their reliance on on-policy training. This approach discards generated "rollout" experiences after a single gradient update, leading to significant computational inefficiency and neglecting opportunities for the model to learn from past successful explorations. While experience replay is a foundational technique in general RL for improving sample efficiency and stabilizing training, its systematic application and optimization within the context of RLVR for large reasoning models (LRMs) remain underexplored. Previous work on off-policy techniques in RLVR has focused on mixing expert demonstrations or developing direct off-policy update rules but has largely overlooked the nuanced impact of data quality within the experience replay buffer itself.

This paper addresses this gap by investigating the characteristics that define a "valuable" reasoning experience and proposing a structured framework for managing and replaying such experiences. It contributes to the growing body of research on improving the efficiency and stability of RL-based fine-tuning for LLMs, particularly those designed for complex reasoning tasks. By focusing on principled experience management, the work aims to overcome the bottlenecks associated with scaling RL computation for LRMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning ability of large language models by improving the sample efficiency and training stability of Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks.

The motivation stems from several identified limitations in current RLVR practices:
1. **Computational Inefficiency of On-Policy Training:** Standard on-policy RLVR algorithms typically discard valuable experience generated during the rollout phase after only one gradient update. This practice is computationally wasteful and hinders the scalability of RL for large reasoning models (LRMs).
2. **Lack of Experience Exploitation:** Discarding past experiences means forfeiting critical opportunities for the model to learn from prior successful explorations. This limits the model's ability to stabilize training and accelerate convergence, a benefit widely recognized in general RL with experience replay.
3. **Underexplored Role of Experience Characteristics in RLVR:** While the benefits of reusing past experience are established in broader RL, the specific characteristics that make a *reasoning* experience valuable for LLM-based RLVR optimization had not been systematically investigated. There was a knowledge gap regarding how to effectively exploit past experiences based on their differing "values" in the context of generating complex reasoning chains.

To address this, the authors pursued a detailed preliminary study motivated by two specific questions:
1. **Question Utility:** Are all questions equally useful for training, or do certain difficulty levels provide stronger learning signals for RLVR? The hypothesis was that experience utility varies with measurable properties related to question difficulty.
2. **Trajectory Quality:** Does low entropy correlate with higher-quality reasoning trajectories that the model should prioritize? The hypothesis was that trajectory characteristics, specifically entropy, could serve as an online proxy for reasoning chain quality beyond just the final answer correctness.

The insights gained from this preliminary analysis—that tasks of intermediate difficulty and their associated low-entropy trajectories tend to be most beneficial—directly motivated the development of ExGRPO. The overarching goal of ExGRPO is to strategically identify, manage, and replay these valuable experiences, utilizing a mixed-policy objective to balance exploration with the efficient exploitation of past learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Experiential Group Relative Policy Optimization (ExGRPO), is a novel framework designed to strategically manage, select, and replay valuable reasoning experiences to improve RLVR. It builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) baseline and integrates structured experience replay.

The ExGRPO framework operates in two main phases: Experience Management and Experiential Policy Optimization.

#### 3.1. Experience Management

This phase focuses on how reasoning experiences (state-action-reward trajectories) are collected, organized, and retrieved.

1. **Experience Collection:**
 - During training, for each question `q` sampled from the dataset, the current rollout policy `π_θ_old` generates `K` trajectories (`G_q* = {o_i*}_K_i=1`).
 - A correctness rate `Acc(q*)` is computed for each question, defined as the number of successful trajectories (`k`) out of `K` rollouts.
 - All successful rollouts `o_j*` for a given `q*` are stored in a replay buffer `E`, mapping `q*` to its set of successful trajectories. The associated `Acc(q*)` is also stored.

2. **Experience Partition:**
 - The replay buffer `E` is partitioned into distinct buckets based on each question's latest correctness rate `Acc(q*)`.
 - To prevent overfitting on already mastered problems, a "Retired Set" (`S`) is maintained. Questions for which all `K` rollouts result in success (`Acc(q*) = 100%`) are moved from the replay buffer to this retired set. This ensures the optimization process focuses on partially solved or unsolved questions.

3. **Experience Selection:**
 - When creating a mini-batch of experiential samples, a two-step selection process is applied:
 - **Question Sampling:** Buckets are sampled probabilistically. The sampling probability `p` for a bucket is proportional to a Gaussian distribution centered at `Acc(q*) = 0.5` (i.e., `p ∝ N(Acc(q*); μ=0.5, σ=1)`). This prioritizes questions of intermediate difficulty, as identified in the preliminary study.
 - **Trajectory Selection:** For each question sampled from a bucket, the trajectory `o*` with the *lowest average token-level entropy* under the *current policy* (`π_θ`) is selected. This heuristic is based on the preliminary finding that lower entropy correlates with higher logical reasoning chain quality.

#### 3.2. Experiential Policy Optimization

This phase describes how the selected experiences are integrated into the policy update.

1. **Mini-Batch Construction:**
 - A training mini-batch `B` is constructed by combining two types of samples: on-policy samples (`B_on`) from the dataset `D` and experiential samples (`B_exp`) retrieved from the replay buffer `E`.
 - A hyperparameter `ρ ∈ [0, 1)` controls the proportion of experiential samples in the mini-batch, with `|B_exp| = min(⌊ρB⌋, |E|)` and `|B_on| = B - |B_exp|`.

2. **Unified Optimization Objective:**
 - The overall ExGRPO objective (`J_ExGRPO(θ)`) is a weighted combination of two components:
 - **On-Policy Objective:** For questions `q` from `B_on`, it follows the standard GRPO objective (`J_GRPO`) where `K` trajectories are sampled from `π_θ_old` to form an advantage group.
 - **Experiential Off-Policy Objective:** For questions `q*` from `B_exp`, a mixed advantage estimation group is formed: `{o*} ∪ {o_i}_K-1_i=1`, where `o*` is the replayed trajectory (from `π_θ_past`) and the other `K-1` trajectories are fresh rollouts from `π_θ_old`.
 - To account for the distribution shift when replaying `o*` (generated by `π_θ_past`), it is reweighted using per-token importance sampling ratio `w*t(θ)`. All trajectories in the mixed group use GRPO advantage estimates.

3. **Mechanisms for Stable Learning:**
 - **Policy Shaping for Entropy Preservation:** Instead of a hard clipping mechanism for importance weights, ExGRPO uses a non-linear transformation `f(w) = w / (w + β)` (with `β = 0.1`) for the importance weights of replayed trajectories. This aims to amplify low-probability signals and dampen excessively high-probability ones, encouraging the model to learn from novel aspects of experience while mitigating variance.
 - **Delayed Start Mechanism:** To prevent the accumulation and use of low-quality trajectories during early training when the model is still weak, ExGRPO is only activated after the `Pass@1` metric for a training batch exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., 35%).

#### 3.3. Extension to Continuous Rewards

The paper outlines a conceptual extension of ExGRPO to standard RLHF with continuous preference-model rewards. In this setting, the difficulty signal (replacing correctness rate) is derived from the *within-query reward variance* computed over multiple rollouts. Queries with moderate reward variance are preferentially selected. Trajectory entropy remains applicable as a measure of reasoning validity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across preliminary analyses, main experiments, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Study on Experience Data

- **Question Difficulty:** Training models exclusively on "Medium" difficulty questions (rollout correctness rate between 25% and 75%) yielded the largest performance gains on benchmarks, outperforming training on "Easy" or "Hard" questions, as well as the full dataset baseline. This suggests an optimal learning zone where questions are challenging but not impossible.
- **Trajectory Entropy and Quality:** Trajectories exhibiting lower average token-level entropy under the current policy correlated with higher logical correctness of the Chain-of-Thought (CoT), as assessed by an external LLM judge. Specifically, correct reasoning trajectories showed lower entropy than incorrect ones.
- **Mitigation of Misguided Learning (Snowball Effect):** High-entropy trajectories were associated with problematic reasoning patterns, such as the generation of unnecessary and often invalid code blocks for mathematical problems. Repeatedly sampling such "misguided" experiences from a replay buffer could lead to systematic reasoning errors. The entropy-based selection helps mitigate this.

#### 4.2. Main Performance Results

- **Overall Performance Gains:** ExGRPO consistently improved reasoning performance over on-policy RLVR baselines.
 - On the Qwen2.5-Math 7B model, ExGRPO achieved an average gain of +3.5 points on in-distribution mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AIME24/25, AMC) and +7.6 points on out-of-distribution reasoning benchmarks (ARC-c, GPQA*, MMLU-Pro) compared to on-policy RLVR.
 - It also surpassed other zero RLVR methods (PRIME-Zero, Oat-Zero, GPG-Zero, RePO-Zero) and off-policy learning strategies (SFT, SFT+RL, Continual LUFFY).
- **Robustness Across Models:** ExGRPO demonstrated consistent improvements across a range of backbone models, including Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B Instruct, Llama-3.1 8B Base, and Llama-3.1 8B Instruct.
 - For the weaker Llama-3.1 8B Base model, ExGRPO stabilized training and enabled successful learning, where the on-policy RLVR method experienced catastrophic collapse.
 - In a continual learning setting with the LUFFY model (which typically leverages external off-policy data), applying ExGRPO with the model's own generated experience led to greater performance gains compared to continued reliance on external data or direct on-policy training.

#### 4.3. Extension to RL with Continuous Rewards

- Preliminary experiments extending ExGRPO to RL with continuous preference-model rewards (using reward variance as a difficulty signal) showed performance improvements on benchmarks like ArenaHardV2, IFEval, and IFBench.

#### 4.4. Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Training Dynamics:** For weaker models (e.g., Llama-3.1 8B Base), ExGRPO stabilized training by providing meaningful reward signals from replayed successful experiences, preventing entropy explosion and collapse observed in on-policy training. For stronger models, experience replay improved data utilization by accumulating successful trajectories and retiring mastered problems.
- **Data Efficiency and Replay Ratio:** The efficiency of experience utilization was found to be more critical than sheer volume. An optimal experiential proportion `ρ = 50%` was identified, balancing exploration with efficient reuse. Higher (`ρ=75%`) or lower (`ρ=25%`) ratios led to suboptimal performance.
- **Contributions of ExGRPO Components:**
 - **Experience Selection Strategies:** Both question sampling (prioritizing medium difficulty) and trajectory selection (lowest entropy) empirically contributed to performance gains. Random selection strategies offered only marginal improvements.
 - **Policy Shaping:** Policy shaping was identified as essential for stable optimization, ensuring exploitation of past experiences did not hinder exploration. Without it, models could suffer premature entropy drops and performance degradation.
 - **Importance Sampling Correction:** Empirical results indicated its importance for preventing performance drops.
 - **Difficulty Sampling Strategies:** The Gaussian sampling scheme centered at `μ=0.5` (medium difficulty) consistently outperformed wider, narrower, or hard-biased sampling distributions.
 - **Retired Queries:** Reintroducing previously solved (retired) queries into training batches negatively impacted performance, suggesting that focusing on partially solved problems is more effective.
- **Computational Overhead:** ExGRPO introduced acceptable computational overhead (e.g., +2% to +19% average step time) and modest memory usage (e.g., 1.77 GB for over 200,000 trajectories) on modern hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in its systematic approach to addressing critical challenges in Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for large language models (LLMs).

1. **Addressing Core Inefficiencies in RLVR:** By being the first to systematically investigate the characteristics of valuable reasoning experiences, the paper directly tackles the computational inefficiency and instability inherent in traditional on-policy RLVR training. This is crucial for scaling RL to increasingly larger and more complex reasoning models (LRMs).
2. **Principled Experience Management:** The proposed ExGRPO framework provides a principled methodology for identifying, managing, and replaying high-quality experiences. The findings from the preliminary study—that medium-difficulty questions and low-entropy trajectories are most beneficial—offer concrete guidance for future experience replay designs in RLVR.
3. **Enhanced Performance and Stability:** ExGRPO consistently improves reasoning performance across diverse LLM architectures and reasoning benchmarks. Critically, it stabilizes RLVR training for weaker models where on-policy methods typically fail, thereby broadening the applicability of RL fine-tuning to a wider range of base models.
4. **Improved Data Efficiency:** The method demonstrates how effective experience utilization, rather than simply accumulating large volumes of data, can lead to substantial performance gains, reducing the need for extensive fresh rollouts.
5. **Generalizability:** The conceptual extension and preliminary results for RL with continuous rewards suggest that the core principles of ExGRPO's experience management (using variance for difficulty, entropy for trajectory quality) may be broadly applicable beyond binary RLVR settings, potentially impacting various RLHF applications.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for future research in several directions:
 - Exploring experience management in more open-ended tasks where rewards are subjective or dense.
 - Investigating the learning potential of "valuable failures" in addition to successful experiences.
 - Integrating ExGRPO's principles with other RL algorithms and advanced exploration strategies.
 - Extending the framework to multi-modal reasoning and agentic reinforcement learning.

Overall, ExGRPO represents a step towards more efficient, stable, and scalable reinforcement learning for improving the reasoning capabilities of large language models, contributing to the development of more robust and capable AI systems.
