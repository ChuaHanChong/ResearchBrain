# Kimi-VL Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> Kimi-VL is an efficient open-source Mixture-of-Experts vision-language model that achieves competitive performance across diverse multimodal tasks, often matching or exceeding larger proprietary and open-source models with only 2.8 billion activated parameters. Its "Thinking" variants enhance reasoning, while the architecture supports 128K extended context and native-resolution visual inputs.

## Problem

- Open-source multimodal models trail proprietary ones in scalability, computational efficiency, and advanced reasoning capabilities.
- Existing open-source vision-language models often rely on computationally intensive dense architectures.
- Current open-source solutions lack comprehensive support for long-context understanding and sophisticated multi-step chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Method

- Employ an efficient ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== architecture for the language model, ==Moonlight==, with only **2.8B** activated parameters.
- Integrate a native-resolution vision encoder, ==MoonViT==, capable of processing diverse image resolutions up to **3.2 million** pixels.
- Utilize a multi-stage pre-training and post-training pipeline, including specialized long-context activation and reinforcement learning for enhanced reasoning.

## Results

- Kimi-VL-A3B achieves **83.1%** on MMBench-EN-v1.1 and **68.7%** on MathVista, often matching or surpassing larger proprietary models like GPT-4o with only **2.8 billion** activated parameters.
- The model demonstrates strong performance in specific domains, scoring **86.7%** on OCRBench, **92.0%** on ScreenSpot-V2 for agent grounding, and competitive results on long-context benchmarks like MMLongBench-Doc.
- Kimi-VL-Thinking variants exhibit significant reasoning improvements, with gains such as **+15.4%** on MathVision, and achieve a **65.2%** score on VideoMMMU, setting a new open-source state-of-the-art.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High performance in vision-language models is achievable with an efficient Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture, significantly reducing activated parameters.
> - Combining native-resolution vision encoding with an extended context window (128K) is crucial for comprehensive understanding of high-detail and long-form multimodal content.
> - Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning effectively imbue models with robust multi-step reasoning and agent capabilities in multimodal settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly large language models (LLMs) and large multimodal models (LMMs/VLMs), is experiencing rapid advancements. The introduction highlights a critical gap: while proprietary multimodal models like OpenAI's GPT-4o and Google's Gemini have demonstrated impressive capabilities in seamless visual and language understanding, the open-source community has significantly lagged behind, especially concerning scalability, computational efficiency, and advanced reasoning.

Existing open-source VLMs, such as Qwen2.5-VL and Gemma-3, primarily rely on dense architectures, which can be computationally intensive and less scalable for very large models. Furthermore, many of these models lack support for sophisticated long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, which is crucial for tackling complex problems requiring multi-step deliberation. Early explorations into Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) based VLMs, like DeepSeek-VL2 and Aria, have also shown limitations; DeepSeek-VL2 has a limited context length (4K), and Aria struggles with fine-grained visual tasks, with neither fully supporting "long-thinking" capabilities. Architecturally, many still use fixed-size vision encoders, hindering adaptability to diverse visual inputs.

Kimi-VL directly addresses these deficiencies. By adopting an efficient MoE architecture for its language decoder (Moonlight) and a native-resolution vision encoder (MoonViT), it aims to bridge the performance and efficiency gap between proprietary and open-source models. Its emphasis on a 128K extended context window, strong agent capabilities, and the development of a "long-thinking" variant via CoT and reinforcement learning positions Kimi-VL as a pioneering effort within the open-source community. It seeks to set a new standard for efficient yet capable multimodal thinking models, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with publicly accessible research. This work contributes to democratizing advanced AI capabilities by making powerful VLMs more accessible and reproducible.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Kimi Team is to introduce **Kimi-VL**, an efficient, open-source Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) vision-language model. This overarching goal is driven by a clear motivation to fill the aforementioned gap in the open-source VLM landscape, providing a model that can compete with or even surpass leading proprietary models in key areas, despite its significantly smaller activated parameter count.

Specific objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

- **Efficiency and Scalability:** To develop a VLM that is computationally efficient and scalable, utilizing an MoE architecture with a low activated parameter count (2.8B in the language decoder) while maintaining high performance. This addresses the bottleneck of large, dense models in terms of training and inference costs.
- **Advanced Multimodal Reasoning:** To enable the model to perform advanced reasoning across diverse challenging vision-language tasks, including college-level image and video comprehension, mathematical reasoning, OCR, and multi-image understanding. This is crucial for real-world problem-solving.
- **Long-Context Understanding:** To equip Kimi-VL with the ability to process and understand long contexts, specifically a 128K extended context window, for various multimodal inputs (e.g., long videos, long documents). This is vital for complex, real-world scenarios that often involve extensive information.
- **High-Resolution Visual Perception:** To ensure the model can "perceive clear" by supporting native-resolution visual inputs through its MoonViT encoder, enabling fine-grained understanding of ultra-high-resolution images and screenshots (e.g., OS interfaces).
- **Strong Agent Capabilities:** To imbue Kimi-VL with robust agent capabilities, allowing it to perform well in multi-turn agent tasks like OSWorld and ScreenSpot-Pro, indicating practical utility in UI interaction and automation.
- **Long-Thinking via Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** To develop an advanced variant, Kimi-VL-Thinking, that leverages long chain-of-thought (CoT) supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning (RL) to significantly enhance long-horizon reasoning abilities, crucial for more complex, multi-step problems.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** To make the code and models publicly accessible, fostering collaborative research and development within the AI community.

In essence, the Kimi Team is motivated to create a powerful, versatile, and accessible multimodal AI assistant that not only understands and reasons across different modalities and contexts but also does so with remarkable efficiency, pushing the boundaries of open-source VLM capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Kimi-VL's development involves a sophisticated integration of architectural innovations, multi-stage training pipelines, and optimized infrastructure.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The architecture comprises three main components:
- **MoonViT (Native-resolution Vision Encoder):** This component is designed to handle images of varying resolutions natively, avoiding sub-image splitting. It employs a packing method similar to NaViT, dividing images into patches, flattening them, and concatenating them into 1D sequences. This allows it to share core computational operators with language models, like variable-length sequence attention (FlashAttention). MoonViT is initialized from SigLIP-SO-400M and further enhanced with 2D Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) for improved fine-grained positional information in high-resolution images. In Kimi-VL-Thinking-2506, it's continuously trained to encode up to 3.2 million pixels per image.
- **MLP Projector:** A two-layer MLP bridges MoonViT and the language model. It includes a pixel shuffle operation to compress the spatial dimension of image features while expanding the channel dimension before projection.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Language Model:** Kimi-VL uses the Moonlight model, an MoE LLM with 2.8B activated parameters (out of a total of 16B), similar in architecture to DeepSeek-V3. This model serves as the backbone for language understanding and generation.

#### 3.2. Muon Optimizer

An enhanced version of the Muon optimizer is used for training. This version incorporates weight decay, per-parameter update scale adjustments, and a distributed implementation based on the ZeRO-1 optimization strategy. This ensures optimal memory efficiency and reduced communication overhead, maintaining mathematical properties while scaling training.

#### 3.3. Pre-Training Stages

The pre-training is a multi-stage process, totaling 4.4T tokens after initial text-only pre-training of the LLM:
1. **Text Pre-training:** The Moonlight LLM is initialized from a checkpoint that has processed 5.2T tokens of pure text data, with an 8K context length.
2. **ViT Training Stages (2.1T tokens):** MoonViT is trained on image-text pairs using a combined SigLIP loss (contrastive) and cross-entropy loss for caption generation (CoCa-alike approach). It uses progressive resolution sampling and a subsequent 0.1T token alignment stage to reduce MoonViT embedding perplexity in the language model, preparing for joint training.
3. **Joint Pre-training Stage (1.4T tokens):** The full model (ViT, projector, LLM) is trained jointly on pure text and diverse multimodal data. Multimodal data proportion gradually increases to integrate visual comprehension while preserving language abilities.
4. **Joint Cooldown Stage (0.6T tokens):** High-quality language (including synthetic QA for math, knowledge, code) and multimodal (academic, vision-centric QA) datasets are used to refine performance and activate specific abilities, maintaining low ratios to prevent overfitting.
5. **Joint Long-context Activation Stage (0.3T tokens):** The context length is extended from 8K to 128K by resetting RoPE inverse frequency. Long text, long videos, and long documents (with QA augmentation) are used, with 25% long data and 75% shorter data replay, to develop robust long-context understanding in both text and multimodal domains.

#### 3.4. Post-Training Stages

Post-training refines the model for instruction following and advanced reasoning:
1. **Joint Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The base Kimi-VL model is fine-tuned using instruction-based ChatML format, applying supervision only to answers. It uses a mix of pure-text and vision-language SFT data, first at 32K token length for 1 epoch, then at 128K for another epoch, ensuring comprehensive dialogue proficiency.
2. **Long-CoT Supervised Fine-Tuning:** A high-quality, small long-CoT warmup dataset is constructed via prompt engineering (similar to rejection sampling), focusing on human-like reasoning processes like planning, evaluation, reflection, and exploration. This primes the model for detailed and logically coherent responses.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** An online policy mirror descent algorithm (similar to Kimi k1.5) is used to enable autonomous generation of structured CoT rationales. It optimizes a reward function based on answer correctness, with relative entropy regularization for stability. Length-based penalties and sampling strategies (curriculum, prioritized) enhance efficiency and mitigate "overthinking." This stage allows the model to encode planned search procedures into its parametric knowledge.

#### 3.5. Infrastructure

The team leverages robust infrastructure for training:
- **Storage:** S3-compatible object storage with an efficient data loading system supporting on-the-fly shuffling, mixing, tokenization, loss masking, packing, random augmentation, reproducibility, and high-performance caching. A centralized platform ensures data quality control.
- **Parallelism:** A 4D parallelism strategy (Data, Expert, Pipeline, Context Parallelism) is employed, alongside ZeRO1 and Selective Checkpointing Activation, to optimize training throughput and memory usage, achieving 60% higher throughput than comparable dense VLMs.

#### 3.6. Data Construction

The paper details the construction of pre-training and instruction data:
- **Pre-training Data:** Curated high-quality data from six categories: Caption, Image-text Interleaving, OCR (multilingual, dense layouts, web, handwritten), Knowledge (geometry, academic), Agent (screenshots, actions, trajectories, icons), and Video (long-form, short-clip, spatio-temporal). Strict quality control, filtering, synthesis, and deduplication are applied.
- **Instruction Data:** For non-reasoning tasks, a human-annotated seed dataset is used, followed by model-generated responses ranked and refined by annotators. For reasoning tasks, rejection sampling and rule-based/model-based verification are used to expand the SFT dataset.
- **Reasoning Data:** High-quality long-CoT data is collected and synthesized through a generation pipeline resembling rejection sampling and prompt engineering, focusing on multi-step reasoning QA.

This comprehensive methodology, from architectural design to training data curation and infrastructure optimization, underscores the rigorous approach taken to develop Kimi-VL as a powerful and efficient multimodal model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Kimi-VL demonstrates remarkable capabilities, setting new benchmarks for efficient, open-source multimodal models.

#### 4.1. Kimi-VL-A3B Performance (Base Model)

With only 2.8B activated parameters in its language decoder (and 0.4B for the vision encoder, total 16B parameters), Kimi-VL-A3B proves highly competitive:
- **General Performance:** Achieves 83.1% on MMBench-EN-v1.1, matching GPT-4o, and surpasses all other efficient VLMs. It scores 84.9% on AI2D, outperforming even GPT-4o.
- **College-level Academic Problems:** Shows competitive results on MMMU (57.0%), VideoMMMU (52.6%), and MMVU (52.2%), often surpassing DeepSeek-VL2 and competing with larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Gemma-3-12B-IT.
- **Multi-image Reasoning:** Scores 57.3% on BLINK, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B and other efficient VLMs.
- **Mathematical Reasoning:** Excels on MathVista (68.7%), notably outperforming GPT-4o (63.8%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (68.2%). While lower on MathVision, it still outperforms DeepSeek-VL2.
- **Document Understanding and OCR:** Demonstrates exceptional performance, achieving 83.2% on InfoVQA (surpassing GPT-4o) and 86.7% on OCRBench (outperforming all compared models). This highlights its strong text recognition and document interpretation.
- **Agent Grounding and Multi-turn Interaction:** Shows impressive single-step grounding (92.0% on ScreenSpot-V2, 34.5% on ScreenSpot-Pro for 4K screens) and strong multi-step agent interaction, achieving 8.22% on OSWorld and 10.4% on WindowsAgentArena, both surpassing GPT-4o.
- **Long Document and Long Video Understanding:** Exhibits strong capabilities in long-form content, scoring 35.1% on MMLongBench-Doc (outperforming GPT-4o-mini and Qwen2.5-VL-7B), 67.8% (w/o sub.) and 72.6% (w/ sub.) on Video-MME, 74.2% on MLVU MCQ (state-of-the-art for open-source), and 64.5% on LongVideoBench.
- **Egocentric and Fine-grained Video Perception:** Shows strong performance on EgoSchema full set (78.5%, surpassing GPT-4o), VSI-Bench (37.4%, surpassing GPT-4o), and TOMATO (31.7%), demonstrating advanced temporal visual understanding.

#### 4.2. Kimi-VL-A3B-Thinking Performance (Reasoning Extension)

Through long-CoT SFT and RL, the "thinking" variant significantly enhances reasoning abilities:
- **Reasoning Gains:** Kimi-VL-Thinking shows substantial improvements over the base model, with +2.6% on MathVista, +4.7% on MMMU, and +15.4% on MathVision, demonstrating effective use of test-time computation for complex queries.
- **Competitiveness:** It achieves 71.3% on MathVista (outperforming GPT-4o), 61.7% on MMMU (surpassing GPT-4o-mini and Qwen2.5-VL-7B), and 36.8% on MathVision (exceeding GPT-4o and even QVQ-72B Preview in some cases). This confirms its efficiency and effectiveness in multimodal reasoning despite its small activated parameter count.
- **Test-time Scaling:** The model generally benefits from increased max thinking token length (e.g., MathVision accuracy increases from 18.7% at 1k tokens to 36.8% at 16k tokens), although some benchmarks like MathVista show saturation at shorter lengths (4k tokens).

#### 4.3. Kimi-VL-A3B-Thinking-2506 Performance (Integrated Thinking Model)

This updated reasoning variant integrates the strengths of the instruct model while further improving reasoning efficiency:
- **Enhanced Reasoning & Efficiency:** Achieves 56.9% on MathVision (+20.1% over original Thinking), 80.1% on MathVista (+8.4%), 46.3% on MMMU-Pro (+3.2%), and 64.0% on MMMU (+2.1%), demonstrating non-trivial gains. Crucially, it reduces average output token length by about 20% (e.g., 2.9K to 2.4K on MMMU-val), making it more efficient for deployment.
- **Stronger Visual Perception & General Understanding:** Shows competitive or superior results on general multimodal benchmarks compared to the non-thinking variant, with 84.4% on MMBench-EN-v1.1, 70.4% on MMStar, 70.0% on RealWorldQA, and 78.4% on MMVet. It achieves these with significantly fewer output tokens (e.g., 180 tokens/answer on MMBench, 1/3 of previous thinking model).
- **Video and Long-context Integration:** Sets new open-source state-of-the-art on VideoMMMU (65.2%, 4% better than GPT-4o) and maintains robust Video-MME performance (71.9%). Scores 42.1% on MMLongBench-Doc (matching GPT-4o), a 10% improvement over the previous thinking model and 7% over the instruct model.
- **High-Resolution Perception:** Continual training on MoonViT significantly improves high-resolution perception and OS grounding benchmarks, achieving 83.2% on V* Benchmark, 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro, and 52.5% on OSWorld-G.

In summary, Kimi-VL, especially its Thinking variants, consistently delivers strong performance across a wide array of challenging multimodal tasks. Its most notable achievements lie in its remarkable efficiency (low activated parameters) combined with leading performance in specialized domains like OCR, agent interaction, long-context understanding, and complex mathematical/multimodal reasoning, often surpassing larger and proprietary models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Kimi-VL technical report represents a significant contribution to the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of large multimodal models. Its significance and potential impact can be articulated through several key points:

- **Advancing Open-Source Multimodal AI:** Kimi-VL directly addresses the glaring gap between proprietary and open-source multimodal models. By releasing an efficient, high-performing VLM with a competitive MoE architecture, the Kimi Team democratizes access to advanced multimodal AI capabilities. This fosters greater innovation, transparency, and reproducibility in research and development, allowing smaller teams and individual researchers to build upon state-of-the-art foundations.
- **Setting a New Standard for Efficiency:** The core innovation of achieving robust performance with only 2.8B activated parameters in its language decoder is a game-changer. This demonstrates that highly capable multimodal models do not necessarily require multi-hundred billion or trillion parameters, challenging conventional wisdom and pushing the frontier of efficient AI. This efficiency translates directly into lower computational costs for training and inference, making advanced VLMs more accessible and sustainable for a wider range of applications and deployments.
- **Pioneering Long-Context and High-Resolution Capabilities:** Kimi-VL's strong performance on long-context benchmarks (128K context window for long videos and documents) and its native-resolution vision encoder (MoonViT for ultra-high-resolution images) set new benchmarks for open-source models. These capabilities are crucial for real-world scenarios where information is often extensive and detailed, opening doors for applications in comprehensive document analysis, long-form video understanding, and precise interaction with high-fidelity visual interfaces.
- **Enhancing Multimodal Reasoning and Agency:** The introduction of Kimi-VL-Thinking, with its emphasis on long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and reinforcement learning, marks a significant step towards more human-like problem-solving in multimodal contexts. Its ability to excel in complex reasoning tasks (e.g., college-level problems, mathematical reasoning) and agent-based interactions (OSWorld, ScreenSpot-Pro) makes it a powerful tool for developing intelligent assistants that can not only understand but also act within complex digital environments.
- **Broadening Real-World Applications:** The combination of general multimodal understanding, specialized capabilities in OCR, mathematical reasoning, agent grounding, and long-context comprehension means Kimi-VL has immediate potential for a diverse range of real-world applications. These include:
 - **Intelligent Assistants:** Capable of processing complex queries involving visual and textual information, navigating UIs, and performing multi-step tasks.
 - **Document Intelligence:** Advanced extraction, analysis, and reasoning from dense and complex documents, including academic papers and financial reports.
 - **Video Analytics:** Detailed scene understanding, event detection, and content summarization from long video streams.
 - **Automated UI Interaction:** Enabling more sophisticated automation and testing for software interfaces.
 - **Educational Tools:** Supporting complex problem-solving in STEM fields with visual components.

The Kimi Team's stated future work, focusing on scaling up the model size, expanding pre-training data, and refining post-training algorithms, indicates a clear commitment to further advancing Kimi-VL's capabilities. This iterative development promises to yield even more robust and versatile multimodal AI systems, further solidifying its impact on both fundamental research and industrial applications.
