# SimWorlds: A Multi-Agent System for Dynamic 3D Scene Creation

> [!summary] Summary
> SimWorlds is a multi-agent framework that turns text prompts into editable, dynamic 4D Blender scenes. A planner, coder, and reviewer run a fixed sequence of construction stages, each closed by a deterministic scene-protocol verifier plus engine-state inspection tools that read modifier stacks, physics caches, and motion phases. On the new 4DBuildBench it reaches 0.87 mechanism pass rate against 0.67 for the visual-only baseline.

## Problem

- Prior LLM-agent Blender pipelines synthesize static scenes or object-level assets; dynamic 4D output where solvers, cloth, fluids and particles are first-class was never demonstrated end-to-end from text.
- Render-and-critique loops let a vision model judge sampled frames, which cannot distinguish a real physics solver from keyframed or shape-key motion that renders identically.
- Blender's native scene graph cannot express assembly correctness, so floating contacts, interpenetrations, and missing parent chains accumulate unchecked across long multi-stage builds.

## Method

- ==Staged construction pipeline==: a strategic planner emits one immutable scene plan, then per-stage tactical plans drive coder, deterministic verifier, and reviewer through eight ordered stages with bounded retries and replans.
- ==Scene protocol== and ==orchestrator-side verifier==: three-level collection hierarchy with root Empties, declared surface-contact and co-movement relations, a bipartite plan graph, checked by structural, ==BVH-geometric==, soft, and plan-versus-state rules.
- ==Engine-level tool suite== plus ==auto-derived knowledge base==: scene-state readouts, multi-granularity previews, and motion-sheet strips expose caches and fcurves; a docs tool serves per-version bpy schemas and manual pages.

## Results

- On 4DBuildBench's **50** scenes SimWorlds scores **0.87** mechanism and **0.89** structural pass rate versus the baseline's **0.67** and **0.70**, with the widest gap on fluid (**0.95** vs **0.56**).
- Edit mode runs unchanged on BlenderBench, cutting pixel loss from **6.02** to **4.63** and lifting the VLM score from **2.76** to **3.41** across all three difficulty levels.
- Ablations on **15** scenes show a double dissociation: dropping the verifier costs structural pass rate (**0.97** to **0.87**), dropping staging costs VLM score (**0.87** to **0.76**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rendered frames underdetermine mechanism: identical video can come from a solver or from keyframes, so agent verification should read engine state, not pixels.
> - Deterministic checks and perceptual review guard different axes, structural correctness versus visual polish, and ablations show neither one substitutes for the other.
> - Caution: prompt alignment, composition, and aesthetics still rest on LLM judges, and few-frame VLM scoring inflates results for physically faked motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "SimWorlds" is situated within the evolving field of artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), 3D graphics, and simulation. Modern 3D generative systems are increasingly expected to produce not just visually plausible renders, but editable scene artifacts that can be integrated into downstream graphics, simulation, and content-creation workflows. Recent advancements in text-to-3D methods have enabled the creation of object-level assets and static scenes using LLM agents. However, a significant gap remains in the ability to generate dynamic 4D scenes, where elements like fluids, particles, rigid bodies, and articulated mechanisms move and interact realistically according to physical laws.

Existing LLM-agent pipelines for 3D content creation, such as SceneCraft, 3D-GPT, BlenderAlchemy, and LL3M, typically focus on static outputs or single-room layouts. While some systems, like VIGA, have introduced dynamic modes, their verification processes often rely on visual inspection of rendered images using vision-language models (VLMs). This approach is noted as insufficient for dynamic scenes because a visually correct render does not guarantee a physically correct underlying mechanism. For instance, a draped tablecloth could be achieved via a cloth solver, hand-authored shape keys, or keyframed mesh edits, all rendering identically but only the solver-based version remaining editable, composable, and physically meaningful. This distinction highlights the need for *mechanism correctness* in addition to visual plausibility for dynamic content.

A separate paradigm of neural text-to-3D and text-to-4D approaches, including methods based on NeRFs or 3D Gaussians, produces visually compelling content but generates neural representations rather than editable graphics-engine code. These outputs cannot be directly opened, resimulated, or composed within standard tools like Blender, nor is their motion typically physics-solved.

"SimWorlds" positions itself by explicitly targeting the generation of *editable, physics-driven* 4D Blender scenes from text. It integrates principles from the field of LLM agents and long-horizon execution, such as alternating reasoning with environment feedback, iterative refinement, tool use, and plan-grounded execution, applying them specifically to the complex domain of dynamic 3D scene synthesis within a professional 3D software environment. The work directly addresses the limitations of prior systems by introducing mechanisms for detailed state inspection and programmatic verification of physical properties and structural integrity, aiming to ensure the generated scenes are not merely visually convincing but also functionally robust as 4D assets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a multi-agent framework, SimWorlds, capable of generating dynamic, editable 4D scenes within Blender directly from natural language text prompts. This involves translating high-level textual descriptions into `.blend` files where geometry, materials, lighting, camera, and especially motion and physics, are explicitly defined, controllable, and available for downstream editing and reuse.

The motivation for this work arises from several challenges and limitations observed in the current landscape of text-to-3D scene generation:

1. **Demand for Dynamic Content**: While LLM agents have shown increasing capability in creating static 3D scenes, the generation of dynamic 4D scenes remains largely unexplored. Dynamic scenes, which involve liquids flowing, particles emitting, rigid bodies cascading, and articulated mechanisms moving, are valuable as editable content for content creation, and as physics-grounded training data for applications like video generation and embodied AI.
2. **Complexity of Dynamic Scene Coordination**: Generating dynamic scenes is substantially more complex than static ones. It requires an agent to jointly coordinate multiple interacting elements, including spatial layout, the configuration of various physics solvers (e.g., rigid body, cloth, fluid, particle, soft body), temporal sequencing of events, camera placement, and lighting, all within a single coherent scene.
3. **Limitations of Visual-Only Verification**: A significant challenge in dynamic scene generation is the verification of motion correctness. Existing systems often rely on rendering the scene and asking a VLM to critique the image. However, a rendered video that appears visually correct can correspond to vastly different underlying mechanisms. For example, a motion achieved through hand-authored animation might look identical to one generated by a physics solver, but only the latter provides an editable, composable, and physically meaningful asset. This "mechanism correctness" is paramount for practical applications, but visual inspection alone cannot reliably distinguish between correct and "faked" dynamics.
4. **Compounding Failures in Current Pipelines**: The inability of current render-inspect loops to detect mechanism failures means that issues like missing fluid domains, improperly configured flow objects, or unbaked physics caches go unnoticed. As scenes become more complex with multiple objects, interactions, and temporal phases, these unchecked failures accumulate, resulting in scenes that visually approximate the prompt but are fundamentally unusable as 4D assets due to a lack of underlying physical integrity.
5. **Need for Grounded Program Synthesis**: The paper posits that dynamic 3D scene generation requires a new approach: plan-grounded, mechanism-aware program synthesis. This involves converting a text prompt into an explicit scene plan that details objects, spatial composition, physical roles, and motion phases. The generation process then needs to proceed through structured subtasks, with verification steps that inspect the live Blender state rather than just rendered images, checking for expected modifiers, baked physics caches, and correct temporal movement.

In addressing these motivations, SimWorlds aims to bridge the gap between visually plausible generative models and functionally robust, editable 4D content, thereby enabling more sophisticated applications in graphics, simulation, and AI training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimWorlds formulates dynamic 3D scene generation as plan-grounded, mechanism-aware program synthesis implemented through a multi-agent framework. The core principle guiding its methodology is that a visually correct render does not guarantee a correctly built scene, necessitating validation against Blender's engine state rather than solely its rendered image.

**Overall Architecture:**
The system comprises a Planner, Coder, and Reviewer agents, operating within a fixed, ordered sequence of construction stages. An orchestrator manages the workflow, incorporating a deterministic verifier and a tool suite for runtime state inspection.

**Staged Construction Pipeline (§3.1):**
SimWorlds employs a fixed sequence of construction stages: modeling, UV, texture, deformation setup, motion, camera, light, and render. This order reflects the intrinsic dependencies in Blender scene construction (e.g., materials depend on UVs, motion on rigging).
1. **Scene Plan**: The Planner, a persistent session across the entire run, first translates the natural language prompt into a comprehensive global scene plan. This plan specifies objects (dimensions, PBR materials, position, physics role), their spatial layout as a typed relation graph, physical properties, motion phases, and rendering intent. It also lists stages that can be optionally skipped for a given scene.
2. **Per-Stage Execution Loop**: For each active stage, a fresh Coder and Reviewer session is initiated. The Planner emits a tactical plan specific to the current stage, detailing assets to realize, relations to satisfy, and acceptance criteria.
 - **Coder**: Generates a `bpy` Python script to incrementally extend the running Blender scene.
 - **Orchestrator-side Verifier**: Mechanically checks the resulting Blender state against a fixed scene protocol (§3.2). This is a deterministic check for structural and mechanism correctness.
 - **Reviewer**: Judges per-stage acceptance criteria, utilizing rendered previews and structured runtime readouts from Blender. For motion stages, it also consumes per-phase motion evidence.
 - **Feedback and Retry**: Based on feedback from the verifier and reviewer, the Planner decides whether to advance to the next stage, retry the current stage (allowing the Coder to fix errors), replan the tactical approach, or abort. Hard verifier failures or blocking review issues force retries, ensuring early errors are addressed locally. Each stage has bounded retries and replans.
3. **Final Render and Review**: After all construction stages are completed, the scene is rendered (as a single frame for static scenes or a frame-sequence video for dynamic ones). A separate final reviewer assesses the rendered output against the global scene plan. Failures at this stage can trigger a rollback to the last checkpoint, invoking the Coder for a final round of consolidated fixes, followed by a re-render.
4. **Edit Mode**: The system can adapt to editing tasks. Given an existing `.blend` file and a text instruction, SimWorlds loads the file, populates its initial state, and the Planner decomposes the request into edit-specific tasks (additions, modifications, removals), leveraging the same staged pipeline.

**Scene Protocol and Verifier (§3.2):**
To ensure structural and mechanism correctness, SimWorlds introduces a declarative scene protocol layered over Blender's native scene graph primitives.
1. **Protocol Structure**: It imposes a three-level containment hierarchy using Blender collections: L1 (scene root), L2 (system groupings, e.g., rooms, lights, cameras), and L3 (individual logical objects, each rooted by an Empty object to which its meshes are parented). Custom properties (`protocol_role`, `is_object_root`, `contact_with`) tag collections and objects, defining their roles and relationships. It explicitly defines `surface contact` (e.g., a chair leg on the floor) and `co-movement` (via parent-child constraints).
2. **Bipartite Plan Graph**: The Planner establishes a bipartite graph upfront, specifying objects and their intended relations (support, containment, orientation, regular arrangement) within the scene. The Coder's task is to realize this graph.
3. **Rule Families**: The orchestrator-side Verifier runs four families of deterministic rules against the live Blender state after every Coder turn:
 - **Structural Rules (C, G)**: Check collection layout (e.g., L3s in L2s, meshes in L3s, naming conventions) and parent-child hierarchies within objects.
 - **Geometric Rules (V)**: Employ BVH distance tests on evaluated meshes to confirm declared contacts meet within tolerance (V1) and to detect undeclared interpenetrations (V2). These catch crucial geometric errors missed by purely structural checks.
 - **Soft Rules (W)**: Raise warnings for conditions like ungrounded objects, disconnected meshes within an L3, or missing cameras/lights.
 - **Plan-vs-State Rules (R, T)**: Re-check the Planner's specified relation graph and motion phases against the realized geometry and animation data (e.g., verifying a circular arrangement or a settle phase coming to rest).

**Tool Suite (§3.3):**
SimWorlds provides agents with a suite of tools for direct inspection and modification of Blender's runtime state, moving beyond visual critique. Key tools include:
- `blender_scene_state`: Provides a structured readout of the live scene, including collections, modifier stacks, physics caches, and f-curve channels.
- Multi-granularity preview family: Renders targeted multi-angle previews at mesh, object, system, or whole-scene levels.
- `blender_motion_sheet_preview`: Samples a per-actor frame strip, annotated with phase boundaries and bake state, to reveal motion-mechanism failures.
- `blender_execute`: Runs `bpy` code in the live Blender process.
- `blender_tag_object`: Writes custom properties for the scene protocol.

**Knowledge Base (§3.4):**
A `blender_docs(query)` tool grants agents access to an auto-derived knowledge base, compiled per Blender version from upstream sources (bl_rna schemas, bpy.ops signatures, API guides, Blender manual). This provides context-specific procedural knowledge on demand, without reliance on hand-curated LLM prompts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SimWorlds was evaluated against a newly introduced benchmark, 4DBuildBench, and compared with the prior dynamic scene generation system VIGA.

**4DBuildBench Benchmark:**
This benchmark comprises 50 self-contained prompts for text-to-4D Blender scene generation, categorized by the required physics solver (cloth, fluid, rigid body, particle, soft body, and a static category) and three difficulty levels (D1: single actor, D2: within-category complexity, D3: cross-category interaction).

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Two complementary evaluation tracks were used to assess both mechanism correctness and visual fidelity:
1. **Engine-state audit (Deterministic)**: Runs in headless Blender, inspecting runtime state (solver modifiers, baked caches, collision partners, keyframe density). It compares this against a hand-authored ground-truth mechanism specification (42 typed predicates).
 - **MPR (Mechanism Pass Rate)**: Mean fraction of satisfied mechanism predicates per actor per scene.
 - **SPR (Structural Pass Rate)**: Fraction of declared spatial relations that hold geometrically (BVH surface-distance test).
2. **Itemized VLM judge (GPT-5.5)**: Receives the prompt and five uniformly sampled frames from the rendered video. It provides binary verdicts for atomic items across five dimensions: objects present, spatial relations, actions visible, visual quality, and aesthetics. This track specifically excludes mechanism realism.

**Quantitative Results (Table 1):**
SimWorlds demonstrated superior performance over VIGA, particularly in mechanism correctness and structural integrity.
- **MPR**: SimWorlds achieved an overall MPR of 0.87, significantly higher than VIGA's 0.67. This indicates that SimWorlds consistently produces scenes with correct underlying physics mechanisms, whereas VIGA often generates visually plausible but mechanically incorrect dynamics (e.g., faked animation). The gap was particularly wide in Fluid (0.95 vs 0.56), Rigid body (0.83 vs 0.61), and Particle (0.79 vs 0.62) categories.
- **SPR**: SimWorlds achieved an overall SPR of 0.89, compared to VIGA's 0.70. This suggests better adherence to declared spatial relationships and structural integrity, crucial for functional 3D assets.
- **VLM Score**: The VLM scores were comparable, with SimWorlds at 0.82 and VIGA at 0.78. This result supports the paper's core hypothesis: VLMs, by relying on sampled frames, cannot reliably discern underlying mechanism correctness, only visual plausibility.

**Qualitative Results (Figure 3):**
Qualitative comparisons across various dynamic sequences (e.g., domino cascade, paper airplane landing, wind-blown curtain, hourglass) visually illustrated that SimWorlds successfully realizes the correct physics mechanisms, while VIGA frequently produces visually similar but mechanically incorrect effects (e.g., using keyframes instead of a cloth solver).

**Scene Editing (BlenderBench, Table 2):**
SimWorlds was also evaluated on BlenderBench in its edit mode, demonstrating improved performance over VIGA across all difficulty levels. SimWorlds achieved lower photometric loss (PL↓, 4.63 vs 6.02), lower N-CLIP distance (N-CLIP↓, 4.80 vs 5.28), and higher VLM judge scores (VLM↑, 3.41 vs 2.76). This is attributed to SimWorlds's precise localization of edit requests within its staged pipeline and robust planning-coding-reviewing loop.

**Ablation Studies (Table 3):**
Ablations on a 15-scene subset investigated the contributions of the scene protocol/verifier and the staged construction pipeline:
- **Without Verifier (Ab1)**: A noticeable drop in SPR (0.97 to 0.87) occurred, particularly on complex D3 scenes, indicating that structural breaks frequently reached the final scene without the deterministic checks. MPR saw a smaller drop (0.97 to 0.93).
- **Without Staged Construction (Ab2)**: The VLM score experienced a substantial drop (0.87 to 0.76), suggesting that the per-stage review process is critical for maintaining visual polish, material, lighting, and composition. MPR was largely robust (0.97 to 0.94).
These ablations confirmed that the verifier primarily safeguards structural correctness, while staged construction contributes to visual polish, with each component addressing a distinct aspect of scene generation quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SimWorlds represents a notable step in the field of generative AI for 3D content, particularly concerning dynamic and physically consistent scene creation. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Generation of Editable, Physics-Driven 4D Assets**: SimWorlds directly addresses the limitations of prior text-to-3D systems by producing `.blend` files where dynamic effects are realized through actual physics solvers rather than imitated by hand-authored animation. This yields editable, composable, and physically meaningful assets, which is crucial for professional content creation workflows in animation, game development, and architectural visualization.
2. **Advancing Mechanism Correctness**: The research shifts the paradigm from merely achieving visual plausibility to ensuring mechanism correctness. By integrating a deterministic verifier that inspects Blender's runtime state (e.g., modifier stacks, physics caches, f-curves), SimWorlds enables the reliable detection and correction of underlying physical inaccuracies that would otherwise be missed by purely visual evaluation. This contributes to the development of more robust and trustworthy generative AI systems.
3. **Enhanced Reliability through Staged Construction and Localized Retries**: The fixed staged construction pipeline and the per-stage execute-verify-review loop represent a structured approach to long-horizon generative tasks. This methodology ensures that errors are identified and repaired locally and early in the generation process, preventing them from compounding and contaminating later stages. This systematic error handling makes the generation of complex dynamic scenes more reliable and predictable.
4. **Creation of Physics-Grounded Training Data**: The ability to generate complex, physically accurate 4D scenes from text has significant implications for training other AI models. These scenes can serve as valuable, physics-grounded datasets for video generation, embodied AI agents (e.g., for robotic manipulation, navigation), and simulation environments, where realistic physical interactions are essential.
5. **Introduction of 4DBuildBench**: The accompanying 4DBuildBench benchmark is a contribution in itself. By providing a standardized set of 50 dynamic and static scenes, coupled with a two-track evaluation (engine-state audit for mechanism correctness and itemized VLM judge for visual fidelity), it establishes a rigorous framework for assessing future advancements in text-to-4D scene generation. This benchmark will facilitate comparative studies and drive innovation in the field.
6. **Extensible Multi-Agent Architecture**: The multi-agent framework (Planner, Coder, Reviewer) combined with a flexible tool suite and auto-derived knowledge base provides a robust and extensible architecture. This design can potentially be adapted to other complex generative tasks in 3D environments or integrated with diverse tool ecosystems.

While the current system has limitations, such as reliance on LLM/VLM for perceptual judgments and its text-only input, SimWorlds represents a foundational step towards procedural agents that generate functional, interactive, and physically grounded digital assets rather than solely static media. This transition has the potential to unlock new capabilities in automated content creation and simulation-driven AI research.
