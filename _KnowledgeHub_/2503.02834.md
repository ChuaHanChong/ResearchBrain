# MuBlE: MuJoCo and Blender simulation Environment and Benchmark for Task Planning in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Noah’s Ark London, Czech Technical University in Prague, and Imperial College London introduced MuBlE, a simulation environment integrating MuJoCo physics with Blender photorealistic rendering, alongside the SHOP-VRB2 benchmark for embodied robot manipulation tasks. This setup enabled a baseline reasoning model to achieve successful sim-to-real transfer on YCB objects, showing a performance drop from 76.7% in simulation to 64.4% on real hardware.

## Problem

- Embodied reasoning agents struggle with long-horizon tasks requiring active physical interaction to infer non-visual object properties (e.g., weight, stiffness).
- Existing simulation environments often present a trade-off between physics accuracy and photorealistic rendering, limiting their utility for effective sim-to-real transfer.
- Current benchmarks for language-conditioned manipulation typically do not focus on tasks that necessitate active physical interaction for information acquisition to complete the task.

## Method

- Developed **MuBlE** (MuJoCo and ==Blender Simulation Environment==), a modular platform integrating the MuJoCo physics engine for accurate physical interactions with the Blender renderer for high-quality, photorealistic visual observations.
- Introduced SHOP-VRB2, a new benchmark comprising 10 classes of multi-step reasoning scenarios (12,000 scenes) that explicitly require embodied, closed-loop reasoning over both visual and non-visual object attributes.
- Implemented procedural scene and instruction generation tools, along with a set of extendable primitive actions, to facilitate multimodal data generation and support diverse manipulation tasks including property measurement.

## Results

- The baseline CLIER method achieved an overall success rate of **43.9%** on the simulated SHOP-VRB2 dataset, highlighting the benchmark's complexity, with notably lower performance (**0.0%****-31.0%**) on multi-object and complex reasoning tasks.
- For sim-to-real transfer experiments with YCB objects, a model trained exclusively in MuBlE achieved a **76.7%** success rate in simulation and a **64.4%** success rate on a real Franka Emika Panda robot.
- Training the model with MuBlE's high-quality Blender rendering was critical for transfer, as using a lower-fidelity renderer resulted in near-zero accuracy on real-world datasets due to failures in object detection and property inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, photorealistic rendering in simulation is essential for effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap, significantly impacting the performance of models transferred to real hardware.
> - Integrating accurate physics simulation with photorealistic rendering in a single environment is crucial for developing and evaluating agents capable of active physical interaction to acquire task-relevant information.
> - The SHOP-VRB2 benchmark demonstrates that current reasoning methods face substantial challenges in complex, long-horizon tasks requiring combined visual, linguistic, and physical interaction-based reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied reasoning agents capable of performing complex, long-horizon tasks that require physical interaction with the world and information acquisition (e.g., sorting objects by weight) is a significant area of research in robotics and artificial intelligence. The advancement of such agents is substantially dependent on the availability of relevant and high-fidelity training and evaluation environments.

Existing simulation environments for robot manipulation, such as ManipulaTHOR, CoppeliaSim, and iGibson, often present a trade-off between the accuracy of physics simulations and the photorealism of visual observations. Many provide non-photorealistic rendering, which may lack accurate shades and reflections, thereby limiting their utility for sim-to-real transfer – the ability to train a model in simulation and deploy it effectively in the real world. While some simulators like ISAACSim offer high fidelity, they can be resource-intensive, closed-source, or have limited customizability. Others, like ThreeDWorld, may not provide the necessary infrastructure for robot manipulation tasks, such as integrated motion planners.

In terms of physics engines, MuJoCo is recognized for its speed and accuracy, particularly in simulating articulated systems and for reinforcement learning applications, often outperforming alternatives like Unity, PyBullet, and PhysX in robot manipulation contexts. Many existing environments also tend to offer either purely discrete or purely continuous action spaces, which can restrict the flexibility needed for diverse manipulation tasks. Furthermore, the capacity to account for non-visual object properties (e.g., weight, stiffness, temperature) and their direct impact on action planning in a closed-loop system is frequently limited or absent in current simulators.

Benchmarks for language-conditioned manipulation, such as ALFRED, VLMbench, IKEA Furniture Assembly, and CALVIN, address various aspects of robot learning from natural language instructions or complex assembly tasks. However, these often do not specifically focus on tasks requiring active physical interaction to infer non-visual object properties for task completion.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a novel simulation environment, MuBlE, that integrates high-fidelity physics simulation (MuJoCo) with photorealistic rendering (Blender). This combination aims to provide a platform that overcomes the sim-to-real gap, supports multimodal data generation, and facilitates the development of closed-loop reasoning methods that can leverage both visual and physical measurements. Coupled with the SHOP-VRB2 benchmark, it targets long-horizon tasks that specifically necessitate embodied reasoning over both visual and non-visual object attributes, thereby offering a more challenging and comprehensive evaluation framework for next-generation robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that current embodied reasoning agents face significant challenges in planning for long-horizon tasks that require active physical interaction with the environment to acquire necessary information. Examples include tasks that involve sorting objects based on properties that are not immediately visually apparent, such as their weight or stiffness. The authors contend that the advancement of these agents is directly contingent upon the availability of sophisticated and relevant training environments.

Against this background, the key objectives and contributions of this work are detailed as follows:

- **Development of MuBlE (MuJoCo and Blender Simulation Environment):**
 - **Objective:** To create a modular simulation environment built upon the `robosuite` framework that integrates the MuJoCo physics engine for realistic physical interactions with the Blender renderer for high-quality, photorealistic visual observations.
 - **Motivation:** The need for synthetic visual data that is both realistic and physically accurate is crucial for enhancing the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of robotics systems. Existing simulators often compromise on either visual fidelity or physics accuracy. MuBlE aims to be the first simulator to provide both high-quality rendering and precise physics modeling for long-horizon robot manipulation tasks.
 - **Functionality:** MuBlE is designed to enable multimodal data generation, including the synthesis of diverse scenes and natural language instructions. It facilitates the generation of ground truth scene graphs with detailed object attributes and offers tools for robust task completion assessment. The environment also includes a set of primitive action controllers that support the observation of physical object properties like weight and stiffness, which are critical for embodied reasoning. Its architecture, featuring high-speed physics and keyframe action-render loops, is motivated by the need to facilitate the integration and testing of closed-loop reasoning methods.

- **Introduction of SHOP-VRB2 Benchmark:**
 - **Objective:** To propose a new benchmark specifically designed for evaluating embodied, closed-loop reasoning capabilities in robot manipulation tasks.
 - **Motivation:** Existing benchmarks often do not fully address the need for systems that can perform complex, multi-step tasks requiring simultaneous reasoning over both visual attributes (e.g., object properties, spatial relations) and continuous physical measurements (e.g., weight, stiffness). SHOP-VRB2 aims to extend previous work (SHOP-VRB) by introducing a more challenging multimodal setup that deeply integrates vision, language, and manipulation.
 - **Scope:** The benchmark comprises 10 classes of multi-step reasoning scenarios, totaling 12,000 scenes with natural language instructions. These tasks explicitly require interaction with the environment to gather information about non-visual properties. Additionally, it includes 30 scenes featuring YCB objects to specifically enable and validate sim-to-real transfer experiments.

- **Demonstration of Environment and Benchmark Effectiveness:**
 - **Objective:** To validate the utility and capabilities of the MuBlE environment and the SHOP-VRB2 benchmark by integrating them with a state-of-the-art baseline reasoning method (CLIER) and conducting comparative experiments between simulation and the real world.
 - **Motivation:** This demonstration is crucial for empirically showing MuBlE's capacity to generate training data that enables effective transfer learning from simulation to real-world robot manipulation, thereby addressing the significant sim-to-real gap.

Collectively, these objectives aim to provide a comprehensive, open-source platform that pushes the boundaries of robot learning, enabling the development of more intelligent and adaptable manipulation agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MuBlE environment is conceptualized as an interactive platform for simulating manipulation tasks, built upon `robosuite` and integrating the MuJoCo physics engine with the Blender renderer. This architecture is designed for a generic tabletop scenario, utilizing a single robotic manipulator equipped with a gripper.

**Core Architecture and Data Flow:**
MuBlE initializes scenes based on provided specifications and maintains a scene graph that is exposed to both MuJoCo and Blender. Updates from the robot's control signals are processed by MuJoCo, which then updates the scene graph, ensuring consistency with the Blender-rendered scene. An interface is provided for rendering accurate scene images at any point.

**Data Representation:**
- **Scene Graph:** This serves as the foundational data structure, encoding positions, orientations, and various measurements for all objects within the environment. It is crucial for maintaining synchronization between the MuJoCo physics simulation and the Blender rendering engine. The initial scene graph can be generated using a dedicated Scene Generator tool.

**Scene Capture/Render:**
- **Blender-based Rendering:** High-quality, procedural rendering is achieved using Blender. Rendering is performed on a keyframe basis, specifically after a primitive action (e.g., moving between objects, closing a gripper) is completed. This keyframe approach balances rendering quality, essential for sim-to-real transfer, with computational speed, allowing for closed-loop recovery from failures. The procedural nature of Blender rendering allows for the realistic visual representation of object properties (color, material, texture) under varied lighting and shading conditions.

**Action Loop and Connection to Action Planner:**
MuBlE operates on two levels of interaction:
- **Action Loop:** This higher-level loop facilitates interaction with the environment in response to visual cues. It awaits a primitive action (`A`) and the predicted scene state (object poses and orientations) from an external Action Planning module. Upon receiving an action, MuBlE generates a desired trajectory for the robot's end effector. A new visual observation is rendered automatically once the trajectory for the given primitive action is achieved. The environment includes a trajectory planning module with obstacle avoidance and an interface for custom motion planners, enabling easy integration with external action planners.

**Physics Loop:**
- **Physics Calculation:** The lower-level physics loop calculates interactions using the MuJoCo engine. At a user-defined timestep, a control signal (`C`) is applied to the manipulator, and corresponding forces are calculated and applied to all scene objects. Similar to `robosuite`, the control signal typically specifies end-effector displacement (position, orientation change) and gripper state (open/close). An Operational Space Controller translates these signals into joint forces, which can be customized.
- **Observations (`P`):** Each step in the physics loop generates a set of observations, `P`, which include the end effector's position and orientation, joint states, gripper status, and critically, measurements of non-visual properties such as stiffness, weight, or elasticity. Object-specific data like positions, orientations, bounding boxes, and grasp status are also collected. The modular design permits the addition of custom sensors.

**Primitive Actions:**
A set of extendable primitive actions (`A`) is defined to enable diverse manipulation tasks:
- `move`: Moves the end effector to a specified target.
- `approach`: Positions the end effector for grasping a target object.
- `close gripper`, `open gripper`: For grasping or releasing objects.
- `lift`, `lower`: Alters the end effector's vertical position.
- `weigh`: Measures the weight of the object held in the gripper.
- `squeeze`: Squeezes an object to measure its stiffness.
These controllers execute actions in the MuJoCo physics engine or on a real robot, generating control signals until the planned path is completed. MuBlE supports both discrete (selecting from a finite set of options) and continuous (parameterized with real-valued inputs) action spaces. Grasping is simplified: an object is considered grasped if gripper pads contact it, fixing its pose relative to the end effector; it detaches with zero initial velocity upon gripper opening.

**Data Generation Tools:**
- **Scene Generator:** This tool procedurally generates data for training. It takes a set of customizable object models and randomly places them on a tabletop, randomizing properties like color, material, and size. It includes collision checking between meshes (not just bounding boxes) and ensures a desired level of occlusion. It then renders a scene image, generating comprehensive ground truth data including segmentation masks, depth maps, and object properties.
- **Instruction Generator:** This tool generates natural language instructions or tasks for a given scene, designed to require reasoning and interaction. It employs a template-based algorithm, which is an improvement over previous methods like CLEVR and SHOP-VRB in terms of efficiency and scalability. It generates short, validated descriptions of objects and randomly selects targets for instructions. Ground truth symbolic programs (sequences of actions) are automatically produced by backward reasoning from the task goal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the MuBlE environment and the SHOP-VRB2 benchmark was demonstrated through experiments using a baseline reasoning method called CLIER (Closed-Loop Interactive Embodied Reasoning). This method utilizes a transformer-based action planner, a ResNet for visual processing, and a Seq2Seq network for textual input, integrated within MuBlE. Experiments were conducted on the simulated SHOP-VRB2 dataset and a set of YCB objects, both in simulation and real-world settings.

**SHOP-VRB2 Simulation Experiments:**
The baseline method was evaluated on the full SHOP-VRB2 dataset, which includes 10 task classes.
- **Task Performance:** The results indicated a varied performance across task types. For tasks involving single-object manipulation, such as weighing or moving one object, the success rates were generally higher. For instance, "Weight single" achieved 74.0% success, and "Move single" reached 76.0%.
- **Multi-step/Embodied Reasoning Tasks:** A notable decrease in accuracy was observed for tasks requiring multi-object manipulation and complex embodied reasoning. "Stack weight" had a success rate of 31.0%, "Stack three" recorded 0.0%, and "Order weight" achieved 18.0%.
- **Overall Accuracy:** The overall success rate on the SHOP-VRB2 simulated dataset was 43.9%.
- **Failure Analysis:** The most common reasons for task failures were identified as:
 - **Execution error (14.4%):** Failures in executing primitive actions, often due to inaccurate scene descriptions (e.g., object pose).
 - **Scene inconsistency (12.6%):** Mistakes in tracking object IDs across different frames.
 - **Loop detection (10.8%):** Repetitive primitive action chains, such as continuously approaching an object due to misaligned positions.
- **Conclusion:** The relatively low overall accuracy on SHOP-VRB2 demonstrates the benchmark's complexity and highlights that current reasoning methods struggle with long-horizon, embodied reasoning tasks. This positions the dataset as a challenging and valuable tool for future research in this domain.

**Real-world Experiments (YCB Scenes):**
Comparative experiments were performed using 30 simulated and corresponding real-world YCB scenes.
- **Experimental Setup:** A Franka Emika Panda arm with a 2-finger parallel gripper was used. An Intel Realsense D455 camera captured front-view RGB images. A ZMQ-based communication layer was developed to emulate the simulated robot interface for controlling the real robot. Specialized skills for measuring stiffness (via squeezing and deformation) and weight (via lifting and joint torque differences) were implemented. Weight measurements had a relative error of less than ±10% within the 0-200g range, and stiffness measurements showed high repeatability with a coefficient of variation between 1.6-3.4%.
- **Sim2Real Transfer:** The baseline reasoning model was trained exclusively in the MuBlE simulator and remained unchanged for both simulated and real-world YCB experiments. For real-world input, raw RGB images from the Realsense camera were used, object poses were extracted by CosyPose, and visual properties (material, texture, color) were inferred by the same ResNet model used in simulation.
- **Success Rates:**
 - On the simulated YCB dataset, the overall success rate was 76.7%.
 - On the real-world YCB dataset, the overall success rate was 64.4%.
 - **Impact of Renderer Quality:** The study found that the high-quality rendering provided by Blender was crucial for sim-to-real transfer. When the model was trained using the default `robosuite` renderer (which has lower visual fidelity), its accuracy on real-world datasets dropped to near zero, as it failed to correctly detect objects and infer their visual properties.
- **Conclusion:** The results from the real-world experiments demonstrate MuBlE's capability to bridge the sim-to-real gap effectively. The relatively small drop in performance between simulation and reality (76.7% to 64.4%) validates the environment's utility for generating training data that enables successful transfer learning for robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MuBlE and SHOP-VRB2, contributing to the field of robot manipulation by addressing critical gaps in existing simulation environments and benchmarks. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The primary impact lies in demonstrating a viable method for overcoming the notorious sim-to-real gap. By integrating the accurate physics of MuJoCo with the photorealistic rendering capabilities of Blender, MuBlE can generate synthetic data that closely mirrors real-world visual observations. The experimental results, showing a relatively small performance drop when transferring a simulator-trained model to a real robot (76.7% to 64.4% on YCB objects), empirically validate that high-quality rendering is instrumental for effective transfer learning. This reduces the dependence on expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection.

- **Enabling Advanced Embodied Reasoning:** MuBlE is specifically designed to support the development of embodied reasoning agents for long-horizon tasks. Its capacity to handle both visual and non-visual object properties (e.g., weight, stiffness) and to integrate these into a closed-loop action planning framework is crucial. This facilitates research into how robots can actively interact with their environment to acquire information necessary for task completion, moving beyond purely visual cues.

- **Comprehensive and Challenging Benchmark:** The SHOP-VRB2 benchmark provides a standardized and challenging evaluation framework for future systems. Its 10 classes of multi-step tasks, requiring reasoning over both visual and physical properties, push the boundaries of current methods. The relatively low baseline performance on some complex tasks (e.g., "Stack three," "Order weight") indicates that the benchmark effectively identifies areas where existing reasoning capabilities are limited, thereby guiding future research efforts.

- **Multimodal Data Generation:** The procedural scene and instruction generation tools within MuBlE allow for the creation of diverse, multimodal training data (visual observations, ground truth scene graphs, linguistic instructions, physical measurements). This diverse dataset is vital for training robust machine learning models for visual question answering (VQA), embodied question answering (EQA), and visual-language-action (VLA) tasks in robotics.

- **Modular and Extensible Open-Source Platform:** MuBlE's modular architecture, built on `robosuite` and open-source, encourages community engagement and further development. Researchers can easily extend the environment with new objects, robots, primitive actions, sensors, and reasoning modules, fostering collaborative progress in robot learning.

- **Cost-Effective Development and Training:** By providing a realistic and high-fidelity simulation environment, MuBlE offers an efficient and low-cost alternative for extensive training and testing of robotic manipulation policies. This allows researchers to iterate rapidly on new algorithms and task designs without the logistical and financial constraints of continuous real-world experimentation.

In conclusion, MuBlE and SHOP-VRB2 represent a significant contribution by offering a robust, open-source platform that combines state-of-the-art physics simulation with photorealistic rendering. This platform is poised to accelerate the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and real-world-applicable robotic manipulation systems by providing the necessary tools for generating high-quality training data and rigorously benchmarking advanced embodied reasoning capabilities.
