# MuBlE: MuJoCo and Blender simulation Environment and Benchmark for Task Planning in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Noah’s Ark London, Czech Technical University in Prague, and Imperial College London introduced MuBlE, a simulation environment integrating MuJoCo physics with Blender photorealistic rendering, alongside the SHOP-VRB2 benchmark for embodied robot manipulation tasks. This setup enabled a baseline reasoning model to achieve successful sim-to-real transfer on YCB objects, showing a performance drop from 76.7% in simulation to 64.4% on real hardware.

## Problem

- Embodied reasoning agents struggle with long-horizon tasks requiring active physical interaction to infer non-visual object properties (e.g., weight, stiffness).
- Existing simulation environments often present a trade-off between physics accuracy and photorealistic rendering, limiting their utility for effective sim-to-real transfer.
- Current benchmarks for language-conditioned manipulation typically do not focus on tasks that necessitate active physical interaction for information acquisition to complete the task.

## Method

- Developed **MuBlE** (MuJoCo and ==Blender Simulation Environment==), a modular platform integrating the MuJoCo physics engine for accurate physical interactions with the Blender renderer for high-quality, photorealistic visual observations.
- Introduced SHOP-VRB2, a new benchmark comprising 10 classes of multi-step reasoning scenarios (12,000 scenes) that explicitly require embodied, closed-loop reasoning over both visual and non-visual object attributes.
- Implemented procedural scene and instruction generation tools, along with a set of extendable primitive actions, to facilitate multimodal data generation and support diverse manipulation tasks including property measurement.

## Results

- The baseline CLIER method achieved an overall success rate of **43.9%** on the simulated SHOP-VRB2 dataset, highlighting the benchmark's complexity, with notably lower performance (**0.0%****-31.0%**) on multi-object and complex reasoning tasks.
- For sim-to-real transfer experiments with YCB objects, a model trained exclusively in MuBlE achieved a **76.7%** success rate in simulation and a **64.4%** success rate on a real Franka Emika Panda robot.
- Training the model with MuBlE's high-quality Blender rendering was critical for transfer, as using a lower-fidelity renderer resulted in near-zero accuracy on real-world datasets due to failures in object detection and property inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, photorealistic rendering in simulation is essential for effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap, significantly impacting the performance of models transferred to real hardware.
> - Integrating accurate physics simulation with photorealistic rendering in a single environment is crucial for developing and evaluating agents capable of active physical interaction to acquire task-relevant information.
> - The SHOP-VRB2 benchmark demonstrates that current reasoning methods face substantial challenges in complex, long-horizon tasks requiring combined visual, linguistic, and physical interaction-based reasoning.
