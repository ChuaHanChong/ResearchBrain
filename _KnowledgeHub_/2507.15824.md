# Can Your Model Separate Yolks with a Water Bottle? Benchmarking Physical Commonsense Understanding in Video Generation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark, PhysVidBench, evaluates text-to-video (T2V) models' physical commonsense understanding, particularly concerning tool use and object affordances, using a caption-based LLM evaluation pipeline to mitigate VLM hallucination. The benchmark reveals that current T2V models struggle with spatial reasoning and temporal dynamics, while performance often improves with physically enriched prompts.

## Problem

- Text-to-video (T2V) models, despite visual advancements, often generate videos that lack basic physical commonsense, violating causality, realistic object behavior, and tool use.
- Existing physical reasoning benchmarks often focus on isolated principles, overlook crucial aspects like tool-mediated actions and object affordances, or rely on unreliable direct VLM scoring methods prone to hallucinations.
- Many current evaluation pipelines lack strong visual grounding, compromising the reliability and interpretability of assessments for complex, everyday physical interactions.

## Method

- Introduces **PhysVidBench**, a comprehensive benchmark dataset derived from PIQA, filtered to emphasize secondary tool use and non-obvious object affordances, and enhanced with upsampled prompts detailing physical attributes.
- Develops a novel three-stage evaluation pipeline: physics-grounded question generation, dense multi-perspective video captioning using AuroraCap, and an LLM-as-judge (Gemini-2.5-Flash) that answers questions solely based on these captions.
- Establishes a data-driven ontology of seven physical commonsense dimensions (e.g., Spatial Reasoning, Temporal Dynamics) to systematically structure the benchmark and provide interpretable evaluation categories.

## Results

- T2V models achieved modest absolute scores on physical commonsense understanding, even for top-performing systems like Sora and Veo-2, indicating a general deficiency in robust physical reasoning.
- Prompt upsampling led to performance gains for most models (e.g., 5-8 percentage points for MAGI-1 and Cosmos) in dimensions like material interaction and object properties, though Sora showed decreased performance with upsampled prompts.
- A human user study validated the caption-based LLM evaluation pipeline, showing strong alignment with collective human judgments (Pearson correlation coefficients reached up to **0.69** for some models).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enriching input prompts with grounded physical attributes generally enhances T2V models' physical plausibility, with larger models often showing more significant benefits from this enrichment.
> - Spatial Reasoning (SR) and Temporal Dynamics (TD) consistently emerge as the most challenging dimensions for T2V models, highlighting persistent limitations in understanding complex relational and sequential physical events.
> - Object Properties & Affordances (OP) appears to be a relatively easier dimension for models, suggesting they may rely on visual heuristics rather than deep causal understanding for these aspects.
