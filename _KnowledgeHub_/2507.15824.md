# Can Your Model Separate Yolks with a Water Bottle? Benchmarking Physical Commonsense Understanding in Video Generation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark, PhysVidBench, evaluates text-to-video (T2V) models' physical commonsense understanding, particularly concerning tool use and object affordances, using a caption-based LLM evaluation pipeline to mitigate VLM hallucination. The benchmark reveals that current T2V models struggle with spatial reasoning and temporal dynamics, while performance often improves with physically enriched prompts.

## Problem

- Text-to-video (T2V) models, despite visual advancements, often generate videos that lack basic physical commonsense, violating causality, realistic object behavior, and tool use.
- Existing physical reasoning benchmarks often focus on isolated principles, overlook crucial aspects like tool-mediated actions and object affordances, or rely on unreliable direct VLM scoring methods prone to hallucinations.
- Many current evaluation pipelines lack strong visual grounding, compromising the reliability and interpretability of assessments for complex, everyday physical interactions.

## Method

- Introduces **PhysVidBench**, a comprehensive benchmark dataset derived from PIQA, filtered to emphasize secondary tool use and non-obvious object affordances, and enhanced with upsampled prompts detailing physical attributes.
- Develops a novel three-stage evaluation pipeline: physics-grounded question generation, dense multi-perspective video captioning using AuroraCap, and an LLM-as-judge (Gemini-2.5-Flash) that answers questions solely based on these captions.
- Establishes a data-driven ontology of seven physical commonsense dimensions (e.g., Spatial Reasoning, Temporal Dynamics) to systematically structure the benchmark and provide interpretable evaluation categories.

## Results

- T2V models achieved modest absolute scores on physical commonsense understanding, even for top-performing systems like Sora and Veo-2, indicating a general deficiency in robust physical reasoning.
- Prompt upsampling led to performance gains for most models (e.g., 5-8 percentage points for MAGI-1 and Cosmos) in dimensions like material interaction and object properties, though Sora showed decreased performance with upsampled prompts.
- A human user study validated the caption-based LLM evaluation pipeline, showing strong alignment with collective human judgments (Pearson correlation coefficients reached up to **0.69** for some models).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enriching input prompts with grounded physical attributes generally enhances T2V models' physical plausibility, with larger models often showing more significant benefits from this enrichment.
> - Spatial Reasoning (SR) and Temporal Dynamics (TD) consistently emerge as the most challenging dimensions for T2V models, highlighting persistent limitations in understanding complex relational and sequential physical events.
> - Object Properties & Affordances (OP) appears to be a relatively easier dimension for models, suggesting they may rely on visual heuristics rather than deep causal understanding for these aspects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in text-to-video (T2V) generation have led to significant improvements in the visual quality, temporal coherence, and prompt alignment of synthetic videos. These models are increasingly being conceptualized and applied as "world video models" in domains such as robotics, embodied artificial intelligence, and simulation-based learning. In these applications, a foundational understanding of physical interactions is critical for the models to generate plausible and functional outputs.

Despite progress in visual realism, current T2V models frequently produce videos that exhibit a lack of basic physical commonsense. These outputs often violate intuitive expectations regarding causality, object behavior, and the appropriate use of tools. This discrepancy between visual fidelity and physical plausibility represents a notable limitation in the current generation of T2V systems.

Existing benchmarks designed to evaluate physical reasoning in video generation models have addressed various aspects of this challenge. VBench-2.0, for instance, focuses on state changes and temporal consistency, while VideoPhy 2 targets violations of physical laws. PhyGenBench tests understanding of specific physics-based scenarios, such as buoyancy and friction. Other related efforts include Physics-IQ and Cosmos-Reason1, which benchmark models on physical reasoning across different domains, and Impossible Videos, which challenges models with intentionally physically implausible scenarios to expose fundamental failures. The PIQA dataset and PhysBench evaluate physical commonsense in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), respectively.

However, these prior efforts possess certain limitations that this research aims to address. PhyGenBench is restricted to isolated physical principles, thereby limiting its applicability to more complex, everyday interactions. VideoPhy 2 omits crucial aspects like tool use and object affordance, and its reliance on vision-language model (VLM) scoring methods has been noted as prone to hallucinations, potentially yielding unreliable evaluations. VBench-2.0, although broader, uses predefined reasoning categories and a multi-question VLM-based evaluation that may lack strong visual grounding. More generally, many existing benchmarks often overlook scenarios involving tool use and object affordance—which are ubiquitous in real-world contexts—and rely on predefined physical categories that may not fully capture the multi-faceted nature of everyday physical interactions.

This work, through the introduction of PhysVidBench, aims to fill these identified gaps by centering on intuitive physical commonsense, with a particular emphasis on tool use and object affordance. It proposes a novel, grounded, caption-based question-answering pipeline designed to provide a more interpretable and reliable evaluation, specifically structured to circumvent the common hallucination issues observed in direct video-based or VLM-based evaluations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive benchmark, PhysVidBench, to rigorously evaluate the physical reasoning capabilities of text-to-video (T2V) generation models. This objective is motivated by the observation that despite significant advances in generating visually compelling and temporally coherent videos, current T2V models frequently produce outputs that violate fundamental physical commonsense. These violations manifest in aspects such as causality, realistic object behavior, and appropriate tool use, which are critical for deploying T2V models in applications requiring a robust understanding of the physical world, such as robotics, embodied AI, and advanced simulations.

A key motivation stems from the limitations identified in existing evaluation methodologies. Prior benchmarks for physical reasoning in video generation often either focus on isolated physical principles, thereby failing to capture the complexity of real-world interactions, or neglect crucial aspects like tool-mediated actions and object affordances. Furthermore, many current evaluation pipelines rely on direct vision-language model (VLM) scoring, a method known to be susceptible to hallucinations and lacking strong visual grounding, which can compromise the reliability and interpretability of the assessments.

To address these challenges, the research sets forth several detailed objectives:
- **Develop a Physically Grounded Benchmark Dataset**: To curate a benchmark dataset of prompts that explicitly emphasize tool use, material properties, and procedural interactions within domains where physical plausibility is paramount. This involves drawing from real-world, everyday scenarios rather than purely synthetic or abstract physics setups.
- **Establish a Comprehensive Ontology of Physical Commonsense**: To structure the benchmark around a data-driven ontology comprising seven core dimensions of physical reasoning, derived from analyzing real-world tasks. This framework aims to provide a systematic and interpretable decomposition of physical understanding, covering aspects like force and motion, object affordance, and temporal dynamics.
- **Design a Robust and Interpretable Evaluation Pipeline**: To introduce a multi-stage evaluation pipeline that mitigates the limitations of direct VLM scoring. This involves generating grounded physics questions from prompts, employing a vision-language model for dense, multi-perspective captioning of generated videos, and subsequently tasking a language model to answer physics-involved questions solely based on these captions, thereby improving reliability and interpretability.
- **Empirically Analyze State-of-the-Art Models**: To conduct an empirical analysis of a diverse range of open and closed-source T2V models using the proposed benchmark. This aims to reveal persistent gaps in their physical reasoning capabilities and to understand how different architectural choices and input prompt characteristics influence performance.
- **Highlight Overlooked Affordances and Tool-Mediated Actions**: To specifically focus on evaluating models' understanding of object affordances and tool-mediated actions, areas that the authors identify as largely overlooked in existing T2V evaluations, but which are essential for realistic interaction within a physical environment.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a structured, interpretable framework for assessing physical commonsense in generative video models, thereby guiding future architectural and training advancements towards more physically intelligent AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology encompasses a multi-stage approach for both the construction of the PhysVidBench benchmark and its subsequent evaluation of text-to-video (T2V) models.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Construction: PhysVidBench Pipeline

The creation of PhysVidBench involved a four-stage pipeline designed to generate physically grounded video prompts:

1. **Stage 1: PIQA Dataset & Initial Filtering**: The process initiated with the PIQA dataset, which contains human-authored physical commonsense questions. Only the correct solutions from the PIQA training and validation splits were extracted, totaling over 17,000 goal-solution pairs. To focus on tasks requiring deeper physical reasoning, these pairs were filtered using Gemini 2.5 Pro. The filtering criterion specifically retained scenarios involving *secondary tool use* or *non-obvious object affordances*, such as using a credit card to scrape ice or a water bottle to separate an egg yolk. This step distinguished the benchmark by emphasizing repurposed tool usage beyond canonical actions.
2. **Stage 2: Video Prompt Generation**: The filtered goal-solution pairs were then transformed into concise, visually explicit video generation prompts. Gemini 2.5 Pro was employed with specific instructions to generate brief scene descriptions that depict the correct solution as a physically realizable action. Prompts were constrained to describe realistic, self-contained short video clips focusing on observable physical interactions (e.g., placing, cutting, lifting) while avoiding non-visual elements. This stage yielded 383 "base video prompts."
3. **Stage 3: Prompt Upsampling**: Each base prompt underwent refinement through "upsampling" using Gemini 2.5 Pro. This stage was inspired by prior work (e.g., PhyT2V) demonstrating that enriching prompts with object-level physical attributes improves plausibility. The upsampling process elaborated on the physical structure and affordance logic (e.g., material composition, rigidity, weight, force application, spatial constraints) present in the scene, without introducing new objects, events, or outcomes. The goal was to enhance physical specificity and causal structure while preserving the original scene scope. This resulted in 383 "upsampled prompts," creating a total of 766 unique prompts (383 base + 383 upsampled).
4. **Stage 4: Video Generation**: Videos were generated for all 766 prompts (both base and upsampled) using a diverse set of state-of-the-art open-source and proprietary T2V models. This allowed for an assessment of how models respond to varying levels of physical detail in the input.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Pipeline

A three-stage evaluation pipeline was designed to assess the physical commonsense in the generated videos, aiming to overcome limitations of direct VLM scoring:

1. **Stage 1: Physics-Grounded Question Generation**: For each *upsampled prompt*, Gemini 2.5 Pro was used to generate targeted yes/no questions. These questions were aligned with the seven physical commonsense dimensions defined in the benchmark's ontology (Fundamental Physics, Object Properties & Affordances, Spatial Reasoning, Temporal Dynamics, Action & Procedural Understanding, Material Interaction & Transformation, Force and Motion). Each question was designed to be answerable solely by inspecting the prompt, with a ground truth answer always being "Yes" to avoid trivial "No" answers. A total of 4123 yes/no questions were generated, averaging 11 questions per video.
2. **Stage 2: Dense Video Captioning**: Each *generated video* (from Stage 4 of benchmark construction) was subjected to dense video captioning using the AuroraCap model. This process yielded eight distinct captions per video: one general-purpose caption and seven dimension-specific captions. Each dimension-specific caption was guided by a particular physical reasoning category from the ontology, designed to highlight relevant information such as object properties, material behavior, or spatial layout. This multi-perspective captioning aimed to ensure comprehensive coverage of visual evidence.
3. **Stage 3: LLM as a Judge**: Gemini-2.5-Flash-Preview-04-17 was used as a "judge" to answer the physics-grounded questions. Critically, the LLM was provided *only* with the eight generated captions from Stage 2, and *not* the original video or prompt, to circumvent VLM hallucination issues. A question was marked correct if the LLM answered "Yes" for at least one of the eight captions. The final model accuracy was calculated as the proportion of correctly answered questions.

#### 3.3. Difficulty Stratification

The benchmark dataset was partitioned into three difficulty subsets (medium, hard, and extra hard) based on the average question-answering accuracy of the top four performing open-source models. This allowed for a fine-grained analysis of model behavior across different complexity levels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of T2V models on PhysVidBench yielded several key findings regarding their physical commonsense understanding. The study included a diverse set of open-source and closed-source models, such as VideoCrafter2, CogVideoX (2B/5B), Wan-1.3B/14B, Cosmos-7B/14B, HunyuanVideo, LTX-Video, MAGI-1, Sora, and Veo-2.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Prompt Enrichment

Across the tested models, the absolute scores for physical commonsense understanding remained modest, even for the best-performing systems. This indicates a general shortfall in robust, generalizable physical reasoning capabilities. A consistent trend observed was the improvement in performance for most models when evaluated on upsampled prompts compared to base prompts. This supports the hypothesis that enriching input prompts with grounded physical attributes can enhance alignment with real-world physical expectations. Notable gains (5-8 percentage points on average) from prompt upsampling were observed for models like MAGI-1 and Cosmos (7B, 14B), particularly in dimensions involving material interaction (MT), object properties (OP), and force dynamics (FM). However, Sora, a proprietary model, exhibited a decrease in performance with upsampled prompts, which the authors hypothesize might be due to an internal prompt upsampling mechanism already present in its architecture.

#### 4.2. Challenging and Easier Dimensions

Two dimensions consistently emerged as the most challenging for T2V models: Spatial Reasoning (SR) and Temporal Dynamics (TD). Models struggled with tasks requiring precise reasoning over object placement, geometric relationships, occlusion, and the accurate sequencing of events over time. Even large open-source models like Wan 2.1-14B and Cosmos-7B, along with closed-source models such as Sora and Veo-2, demonstrated considerably lower scores in SR and TD compared to other categories. This highlights persistent limitations in current T2V architectures regarding relational and temporal understanding. In contrast, Object Properties & Affordances (OP) appeared to be the least challenging dimension, with many models achieving relatively higher scores. This suggests that models might rely on visual heuristics to identify basic object attributes (e.g., softness, containment) more readily than complex relational or causal patterns.

#### 4.3. Model Scaling Effects

The relationship between model size and physical commonsense performance showed a partial but not universal trend. Larger models generally exhibited improved performance; for instance, Wan2.1-14B consistently outperformed its 1.3B variant across all dimensions, and Cosmos-7B showed gains over smaller models. However, this scaling benefit was not linear or universal, as evidenced by CogVideoX-2B outperforming CogVideoX-5B in every category. This indicates that a larger parameter count alone does not guarantee superior physical reasoning capabilities, especially in challenging dimensions like SR and TD where even the largest models struggled. Interestingly, larger models tended to benefit more significantly from prompt upsampling. Cosmos-14B, for example, showed an average gain of over 8 percentage points from prompt enrichment, compared to more modest improvements in smaller models. This suggests that larger models are better equipped to leverage fine-grained physical detail when it is explicitly provided in the input.

#### 4.4. Human Evaluation Alignment

A user study conducted via Qualtrics involving human annotators validated the alignment between the automatic, caption-based evaluation pipeline and human judgments. Pearson correlation coefficients increased as more annotators responded, with questions answered by at least three participants achieving correlations as high as 0.69 for some models (e.g., Cosmos). This confirmed that the proposed caption-based LLM evaluation closely tracks collective human judgment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of PhysVidBench represents a significant contribution to the field of text-to-video (T2V) generation research, particularly in the critical area of physical commonsense understanding.

**6.1 Advancing Evaluation Methodologies**: The primary significance lies in providing a novel, structured, and interpretable benchmark specifically designed to evaluate a crucial, yet often overlooked, aspect of T2V model capabilities: understanding physical interactions, particularly tool use and object affordances. By emphasizing these real-world, goal-driven tasks derived from the PIQA dataset, PhysVidBench moves beyond benchmarks that focus solely on isolated physical principles or rely on predefined categories.

**6.2 Mitigating Evaluation Challenges**: The proposed three-stage evaluation pipeline—comprising physics-grounded question generation, dense video captioning (general-purpose and dimension-specific), and an LLM-as-judge operating solely on captions—offers a robust solution to common challenges in evaluating generative video models. This indirect approach effectively mitigates the hallucination issues frequently encountered with direct vision-language model (VLM) scoring, leading to more reliable and grounded assessments of physical plausense. The demonstrated alignment with human judgments further strengthens the validity of this methodology.

**6.3 Uncovering Persistent Limitations**: The empirical analysis across a wide range of state-of-the-art open-source and proprietary T2V models provides critical insights into their current limitations. The consistent struggle with Spatial Reasoning and Temporal Dynamics, even in the largest models, highlights fundamental architectural and training deficiencies in capturing complex relational and sequential physical events. Conversely, the relative performance in Object Properties & Affordances suggests models might rely on visual heuristics rather than deep causal understanding. These findings offer concrete directions for future research.

**6.4 Guiding Future Model Development**: The results indicate that while prompt enrichment can significantly activate latent physical reasoning capabilities, especially in larger models, scaling alone is insufficient to achieve robust physical understanding. This suggests that future progress in T2V models will require not just more data or larger architectures, but fundamental shifts towards incorporating architectural changes that emphasize temporal abstraction, object-centric modeling, and training strategies explicitly grounded in causal, physical, and procedural supervision. Integrating real-world physics priors or interactive environments could also be instrumental.

**6.5 Broader Impact on AI Systems**: By providing a foundation for tracking and accelerating developments in physically grounded T2V generation, PhysVidBench has the potential to significantly impact fields like robotics, embodied AI, and simulation. Models with a more robust understanding of the physical world could lead to the development of more reliable, safer, and intelligent autonomous systems capable of predicting and interacting with their environment in physically coherent ways. The release of the dataset and evaluation code further fosters community research and promotes reproducibility and transparency in this critical area.
