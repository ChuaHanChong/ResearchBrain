# NMPC-based Unified Posture Manipulation and Thrust Vectoring for Agile and Fault-Tolerant Flight of a Morphing Aerial Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework for the M4 morphing aerial robot implicitly adapts to actuator failures by integrating posture manipulation with thrust vectoring. High-fidelity simulations show the controller successfully recovers from thruster degradation and executes agile maneuvers, achieving yaw rates over 200 degrees/s at speeds exceeding 10 m/s.

## Problem

- Traditional Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) often lack agility and resilience to actuator failures due to fixed-thrust configurations.
- Existing control architectures for advanced aerial platforms, including morphing robots, frequently rely on simplified methods that are inadequate for highly nonlinear dynamics, actuator faults, or significant disturbances.
- Effectively leveraging the additional mechanical degrees of freedom, such as articulated limbs, for real-time, robust, and agile flight control presents significant computational and optimization challenges.

## Method

- Developed a computationally efficient, reduced-order dynamic model of the M4 robot to serve as the prediction model for NMPC.
- Formulated a unified Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (NMPC) framework that seamlessly integrates posture manipulation via articulated limbs with thrust vectoring from rotors.
- Designed the NMPC to implicitly handle actuator degradation and failures, dynamically reallocating thrust and exploiting limb articulation without requiring explicit fault detection or mode-switching.

## Results

- The NMPC's reduced-order prediction model closely tracked the high-fidelity Simscape model's states within a **0.3**-second horizon during simulated actuator failure.
- The NMPC achieved rapid fault recovery, stabilizing roll and pitch within approximately 1 second after complete thruster failure, and successfully maintained trajectory tracking under progressive thruster degradation.
- The M4 robot executed agile trajectory tracking, performing sharp turns (up to 120 degrees) at speeds exceeding 10 m/s with peak yaw rates over 200 degrees/s, while maintaining lateral tracking errors generally under 1 meter.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A reduced-order model can accurately capture essential morphing robot dynamics, enabling real-time NMPC for complex control tasks despite simplifications.
> - Unified NMPC offers an intrinsic mechanism for fault tolerance by continuously optimizing redundant control inputs (thrust and joint angles), simplifying control architecture compared to explicit fault detection systems.
> - Leveraging full limb articulation (2-DoF joints) significantly enhances a morphing robot's ability to maintain yaw stability and overall robustness during actuator failures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) has expanded significantly, finding applications from surveillance to planetary exploration. However, many current aerial platforms face limitations in terms of agility, resilience to actuator failures, and reliance on simplified control architectures. Traditional quadrotors, while mechanically simple, are often vulnerable to actuator degradation due to their fixed-thrust configurations, limiting their performance and maneuverability in constrained or unpredictable environments.

This research addresses these limitations by contributing to the emerging domain of multimodal robotic systems, specifically focusing on morphing aerial robots. Multimodal robots, such as the Multi-Modal Mobility Morphobot (M4) discussed in this paper, aim to overcome environmental challenges by seamlessly transitioning between different locomotion types (e.g., wheeled, legged, aerial). The M4 robot, for instance, dynamically adapts its locomotion strategy through appendage repurposing, enabling various modes like flying, rolling, crawling, and balancing. Previous work on the M4 platform has explored autonomous 3D path planning, efficient path tracking, and self-supervised cost of transport estimation, with a particular emphasis on extraterrestrial exploration contexts. Other relevant multimodal systems include Husky Carbon, a legged-aerial platform combining wheeled and thruster-assisted legged mobility, and the bipedal robot Harpy, which uses thrusters and posture manipulation for dynamic maneuvers like wall-jumping and steep slope walking. Further hybrid systems like FSTAR, HyFDR, ANYmal, DRAGON, and SPIDAR demonstrate diverse combinations of terrestrial and aerial capabilities.

Beyond multimodal ground-aerial systems, the landscape includes specialized aerial robot designs that aim to enhance maneuverability and robustness. These include tilt-rotor UAVs, fully actuated tri-rotors, and flying humanoids like iRonCub, which introduce mechanical degrees of freedom and over-actuated configurations. Morphing-wing platforms such as Aerobat also contribute to agility through dynamic structural reconfiguration. However, many control solutions for these advanced aerial platforms still utilize simplified frameworks, such as PID controllers, which often prove inadequate for scenarios involving strong nonlinear dynamics, actuator faults, or significant external disturbances. The challenge lies in developing control strategies that can leverage additional mechanical degrees of freedom, such as articulated limbs, for real-time flight control, agile maneuvering, and robust fault tolerance.

This thesis specifically builds upon previous work, such as that by Mandralis et al., which investigated M4’s morphing capabilities through combined posture control and thrust vectoring for minimum-time trajectories under spatial constraints. That study demonstrated the benefits of posture manipulation for navigating narrow geometries and enhancing maneuverability using collocation-based optimization. This current work extends that foundation by proposing a unified Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework. NMPC is particularly suitable due to its explicit handling of nonlinear system dynamics, actuator constraints, and dynamic objectives, offering an implicit mechanism for managing actuator faults by dynamically reallocating thrust and exploiting limb articulation. This distinguishes it from many existing fault-tolerant strategies, which often rely on explicit fault detection, separate estimation and compensation modules, or sacrifices in control authority.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of conventional UAVs in terms of agility, robustness to actuator failures, and adaptability in complex or unpredictable environments. Traditional aerial robots, especially fixed-thrust quadrotors, exhibit vulnerability to actuator degradation or complete rotor failures, significantly compromising their operational reliability. Moreover, their maneuverability is often insufficient for navigating cluttered or highly dynamic spaces, such as dense forests, urban canyons, or extraterrestrial terrains. These challenges are particularly pronounced in mission-critical applications like planetary exploration, search-and-rescue operations, and high-speed reconnaissance where system reliability, agility, and reconfigurability are paramount.

The M4 robot, with its unique morphing capabilities and articulated legs, offers a potential solution by introducing additional degrees of freedom for dynamic mid-air reconfiguration. Unlike standard quadrotors, M4 can actively modify its center of mass, aerodynamic profile, and thrust vector orientation through posture manipulation, thereby expanding its maneuverability envelope. However, effectively harnessing these articulated limbs for real-time, robust, and agile flight control presents significant challenges related to increased computational demands and complex state optimization.

This thesis aims to address these issues by developing a unified control framework that exploits the M4 robot's mechanical redundancy. The key objectives are:

1. **Reduced-Order Model Development:** To create a computationally efficient, reduced-order dynamic model of the M4 robot in its aerial configuration. This model must accurately capture the essential interactions between joint articulation and body dynamics while simplifying complex aspects for real-time control.
2. **NMPC Framework Formulation:** To formulate a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework that integrates posture manipulation with thrust vectoring. This framework is designed to facilitate both fault-tolerant recovery from actuator failures and agile trajectory tracking.
3. **High-Fidelity Simulation Validation:** To implement and rigorously validate the proposed NMPC control strategy through high-fidelity Simscape simulations. These simulations are intended to encompass varied flight conditions, including nominal flight, actuator degradation, and aggressive maneuvers.
4. **Demonstration of Performance:** To provide simulation-based evidence of the NMPC controller's ability to achieve successful fault recovery and execute agile maneuvers, specifically utilizing combined posture manipulation and thrust vectoring without requiring explicit fault detection or mode-switching.

The broader motivation of this work is to bridge the gap between mechanical redundancy (provided by articulated limbs) and algorithmic fault tolerance. By developing control strategies that fully exploit all available degrees of freedom, the research aims to transform aerial robots from limited-capability platforms into resilient, reconfigurable systems capable of performing complex missions in uncertain and dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodological approach for this research involved developing a multi-layered simulation and control architecture. This included a high-fidelity Simscape Multibody model of the M4 robot, a simplified reduced-order model (ROM) for NMPC prediction, and a unified NMPC framework parameterized for both fault-tolerant and agile flight.

#### 3.1. Flight Dynamics Model

The full-fidelity system was modeled as a collection of rigid bodies, comprising the main body and three lumped masses per leg. The generalized coordinates were defined as the main body's position ($p_b$), orientation ($\theta_b$, e.g., Euler angles), and the joint angles of the appendages ($q_a$). The state vector included these generalized coordinates and their rates. The dynamics were derived using the Euler-Lagrange formulation, incorporating total kinetic and gravitational potential energy. Generalized forces included joint torques ($\tau_j$) and thruster forces/moments ($T_k$, $M_k$). To match the Simscape model, a generalized drag term ($\tau_d$) was added, representing body-frame rotational damping moments proportional to angular velocities. The final equations of motion were expressed in a standard second-order form.

#### 3.2. Prediction Model (Reduced-Order Model - ROM)

To ensure computational efficiency for the NMPC, a reduced-order model (ROM) was developed. This ROM introduced several simplifications:
- Intermediate limb segments were assumed to have negligible mass and inertia, modeling appendages as point masses at leg ends.
- The main body was simplified to a lumped mass rectangular cuboid, omitting detailed aerodynamic effects and distributed mass properties.
- Structural deformations and their coupling effects were neglected.

The ROM state vector comprised the main body's position ($p_b$), orientation ($\theta_b$), joint angles ($q_a$), linear velocity ($\dot{p}_b$), angular velocity ($\omega_b$), and joint rates ($\dot{q}_a$). Translational dynamics were governed by Newton's second law, considering the total system mass and net thruster forces, adjusted by appendage configuration and gravity. Rotational dynamics followed the Newton-Euler formulation, including the main body's inertia matrix, external moments influenced by joint configuration and thrusters, and rotational damping. The kinematic relationship between Euler angle rates and angular velocity was also included.

#### 3.3. Simscape Multibody Model

A high-fidelity Simscape Multibody model was developed in MATLAB Simulink to serve as the ground truth and validation platform. This model precisely captured the M4 robot's full-body dynamics:
- **Architecture:** A central body connected to a 6-DOF joint for unconstrained motion. Four articulated appendages, each with two revolute joints (sagittal and frontal), constrained within [0°, 90°]. Custom STL files represented physical geometry.
- **Parameters:** Accurate mass, inertia, and center of mass locations for all components.
- **Actuation:** Thrust forces were applied along the z-direction at each wheel's base using external force blocks. Thrust-induced moments were modeled proportionally to thrust. Joint acceleration signals from the NMPC were converted to motion actuation inputs for the revolute joints.
- **State Extraction:** Body position, orientation, linear/angular velocities, and joint angles/rates were extracted for feedback to the NMPC.
- **Contact Forces:** Spatial Contact Force blocks were used, defining convex hull geometries for adjacent wheels and an infinite planar ground. Normal forces were computed using a smooth spring-damper model, and frictional forces using a smooth stick-slip friction model, ensuring non-collision constraints and realistic ground interaction.
- **Aerodynamic Effects:** Drag was modeled as damping torques proportional to the body's angular velocity, applied using an External Force and Torque block.
- **Control Interface:** An S-function block bridged MATLAB's CasADi-based NMPC (running at 0.1 s update rate) with the Simulink environment. NMPC outputs (thrust forces, joint accelerations) were applied directly to the multibody model.

#### 3.4. Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC)

A unified NMPC framework was designed, utilizing the ROM as its prediction model and solving a constrained optimization problem at each time step. The core NMPC structure remained consistent, but its parameters were adjusted for different operational modes:

- **NMPC for Fault-Tolerant Control:**
 - **Objective:** Prioritize recovery and stabilization after actuator degradation.
 - **Optimization Problem:** Minimizes a cost function penalizing deviations from a reference state (hover or recovery configuration) and control input magnitudes over a finite horizon.
 - **Dynamics:** The discretized ROM ($\Phi(x_j, u_j)$) was integrated using a fourth-order Runge-Kutta scheme.
 - **Key Characteristics:**
 - Thrust Limits: Constrained within N.
 - Cost Function: Weighting matrices ($Q_{fault}$, $R_{fault}$) prioritized roll and pitch stability.
 - Yaw Handling: Unconstrained or lightly penalized (in 1-DoF config), or more heavily penalized (in 2-DoF config) to encourage re-stabilization.
 - Control Response: Dynamic reallocation of thrust and joint torques without explicit fault detection or switching logic.

- **NMPC for Agile Trajectory Tracking:**
 - **Objective:** Enable fast and precise tracking of aggressive trajectories.
 - **Optimization Problem:** Similar structure to fault-tolerant mode, but with parameters tuned for agility.
 - **Key Differences:**
 - Thrust Limits: Expanded to N for aggressive maneuvers.
 - Cost Function: Weighting matrices ($Q_{agile}$, $R_{agile}$) emphasized tracking precision and velocity control, allowing greater joint movement and thrust variation.
 - Reference Handling: A collocation method interpolated intermediate reference states from the current state towards the goal, ensuring smoother transitions for high-speed flight.
 - Yaw Behavior: Yaw error was penalized to maintain a steady heading during aggressive turns.

All NMPC configurations imposed state constraints on body orientation (roll and pitch within [−90°, 90°]), joint angles (hip-sagittal [0°, 90°], hip-frontal [0°, 90°]), and joint accelerations ([−50, 50] rad/s²).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed NMPC framework was evaluated through extensive simulations using the high-fidelity Simscape Multibody model. The results focused on validating the prediction model's accuracy, demonstrating fault-tolerant flight under various conditions, and assessing agile trajectory tracking capabilities.

#### 4.1. Prediction Model Performance

The initial validation focused on the reduced-order prediction model used by the NMPC. During a simulated actuator failure (rotor 4 switching off at 0.2 seconds), the predicted states from the NMPC's ROM were compared against the actual states from the full-fidelity Simscape model. The results (Figure 4.1) indicated a close resemblance between the predicted and actual trajectories within a 0.3-second horizon, confirming that the ROM accurately captured the essential dynamics required for effective fault recovery. This agreement validated the reliability of the prediction model for the control framework.

#### 4.2. Fault-Tolerant Flight

Fault-tolerant performance was assessed across two different robot actuation models (1-DoF and 2-DoF articulation per arm) and multiple failure scenarios. The NMPC operated without prior knowledge of fault conditions, relying on its inherent adaptability.

#### 4.2.1. Simulation Results for Sagittal-Actuated Joint Model (1-DoF)

In this configuration, only the hip-sagittal joints were actuated, with hip-frontal joints fixed in a supinated position. An additional constraint limited combined sagittal joint angles to 110° to prevent collisions. Yaw was unconstrained or minimally penalized, allowing free rotation.

- **Scenario 1: Fault Recovery During Forward Flight to Hover:** The robot was commanded to fly forward at over 4 m/s. Thruster 4 failed randomly between 3.825 s and 3.925 s, with the controller reacting at 4 s.
 - **Observation:** Following the fault, the robot rapidly regained stability in roll and pitch within approximately 1 second, but its yaw began to rotate, progressively increasing its yaw rate (Figures 4.2, 4.3, 4.5). This behavior was consistent with the unconstrained yaw in this configuration, as the NMPC prioritized roll and pitch stabilization.
 - **Control Actions:** Figure 4.4 showed the complete loss of thrust from rotor 4, compensated by increased thrust from other rotors and dynamic adjustments of sagittal joint angles.
- **Scenario 2: Trajectory Tracking with Progressive Failure:** The robot tracked a waypoint trajectory, experiencing progressive Loss of Effectiveness (LoE) in rotor 4 (33% from 7-14 s, 66% from 14-21 s, complete failure after 21 s).
 - **Observation:** Despite increasing LoE and eventual complete failure, the robot successfully maintained accurate trajectory tracking (Figure 4.6). The yaw rate progressively increased and saturated after complete failure, reflecting the limited yaw control authority. The robot still performed a controlled descent and landing.
 - **Control Actions:** Figure 4.7 illustrated the NMPC's adaptive adjustment of thrust forces and sagittal joint angles to compensate for the varying levels of thruster degradation.

#### 4.2.2. Simulation Results for Sagittal and Frontal-Actuated Joint Model (2-DoF)

This configuration enabled actuation of both sagittal and frontal hip joints, providing enhanced maneuverability. Yaw deviations were penalized in the NMPC objective function, encouraging yaw stability.

- **Scenario 1: Fault Recovery During Hover:** The robot maintained a stationary hover while rotor 4 experienced progressive LoE (33% at 1 s, 66% at 3 s, complete failure after 5 s).
 - **Observation:** Each failure event caused transient deviations in roll and yaw, but the system re-stabilized effectively. Critically, unlike the 1-DoF model, the robot did not exhibit a continuously increasing yaw rate after complete failure (Figure 4.8), indicating that the additional frontal joint actuation enabled effective yaw stability.
 - **Control Actions:** Figure 4.9 showed the NMPC's adaptive use of both sagittal and frontal joint angles, alongside thrust re-allocation, to maintain orientation and compensate for the failed rotor.
- **Scenario 2: Trajectory Tracking with Progressive Failure:** Similar to the 1-DoF model, the robot tracked waypoints under progressive LoE in rotor 4 (33% from 12-16 s, 66% from 16-18 s, complete failure after 18 s).
 - **Observation:** The robot accurately tracked the reference trajectory and successfully reached the goal position without continuous spinning (Figure 4.10). The center of mass velocity and orientation rates were maintained within acceptable bounds (Figure 4.11).
 - **Control Actions:** The NMPC rapidly adjusted both thrust forces and sagittal/frontal joint angles to accommodate the thruster failures, maintaining stable tracking (Figure 4.12).

#### 4.3. Agile Trajectory Tracking

The NMPC's ability to perform agile maneuvers was tested by commanding the M4 robot to execute sharp turns (30°, 60°, 90°, and 120°) at speeds exceeding 10 m/s.

- **Observation:**
 - **Position Tracking:** The robot consistently tracked the reference trajectories with minimal lateral deviation, even for aggressive turns like 90° and 120° (Figure 4.13, left panel).
 - **Speed Profile:** The robot maintained a cruising speed of around 14.5 m/s before each turn, exhibiting controlled deceleration proportional to the turn sharpness (e.g., dropping below 8 m/s for a 120° turn) and then re-accelerating smoothly (Figure 4.13, top-right).
 - **Turning Rate:** Rapid reorientation was achieved, with peak yaw rates increasing with turn severity (exceeding 200°/s for the 120° turn) without overshoot or instability (Figure 4.13, middle-right).
 - **Tracking Error:** Tracking error remained tightly bounded, generally under 1 m for smaller turns, with transient peaks of approximately 2-2.5 m during the most challenging 90° and 120° turns, which were quickly corrected (Figure 4.13, bottom-right).

These results demonstrated the NMPC framework's effectiveness in leveraging combined thrust vectoring and posture manipulation for high-speed agile flight, enabling dynamic negotiation of sharp turns with stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a unified control framework that addresses critical challenges in aerial robotics, specifically enhancing agility and fault tolerance for morphing aerial robots like the M4. The development of a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) architecture that integrates posture manipulation with thrust vectoring represents a significant advancement.

The primary significance lies in the controller's capacity for **implicit fault recovery**. Unlike many conventional fault-tolerant control (FTC) systems that require explicit fault detection, isolation, and re-configuration modules, the proposed NMPC intrinsically adapts to actuator degradation. By continuously optimizing across the available control inputs—both thruster forces and joint angles—the system dynamically reallocates resources to maintain stability and performance even during partial or complete actuator failures. This approach simplifies the control architecture and potentially improves response time by eliminating the latency associated with fault diagnosis. The demonstration that a fully articulated system (with both sagittal and frontal joint actuation) can maintain yaw stability even after complete thruster failure, unlike a partially articulated system, highlights the benefits of leveraging mechanical redundancy through advanced control.

Furthermore, the framework enables **agile trajectory tracking** at high speeds. The NMPC's ability to command complex maneuvers, such as sharp turns exceeding 10 m/s and achieving yaw rates over 200°/s, demonstrates its potential for navigation in cluttered or dynamic environments. This agility, combined with fault tolerance, positions the M4 robot as a versatile platform capable of operating in demanding scenarios where both rapid maneuverability and operational reliability are crucial.

The research's use of a high-fidelity Simscape Multibody model for validation strengthens the credibility of the simulation results, providing a realistic digital twin of the physical system and accounting for intricate dynamics like aerodynamic effects and contact forces. The close agreement between the NMPC's reduced-order prediction model and the high-fidelity plant further supports the feasibility of implementing such a control strategy in real-time.

The potential impact of this work extends to several high-stakes applications:
- **Planetary Exploration:** Morphing aerial robots capable of agile, fault-tolerant flight can overcome obstacles (e.g., craters, steep slopes) and access terrain unreachable by conventional rovers or drones, enhancing exploration capabilities.
- **Search-and-Rescue Operations:** Resilient aerial platforms that can navigate dense, unstructured environments and recover from unforeseen damage are vital for locating survivors in disaster zones.
- **High-Speed Navigation in GPS-Denied Environments:** The demonstrated agility and robustness are beneficial for operating in indoor or urban settings without reliance on GPS, where precise, adaptable control is essential for obstacle avoidance.

In the long term, this research contributes to the broader goal of developing autonomous systems that are not only capable but also highly resilient and adaptable to unpredictable conditions. Future work involving hardware implementation, integration of fault detection, perception-aware planning, and learning-based model adaptation will further advance the field towards deploying truly robust and versatile multi-modal robotic platforms.
