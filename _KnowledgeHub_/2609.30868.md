# VLaRL: Augmenting Vision-Language-Action Models with Simulation-Trained Latent-Conditioned Residual RL

> [!summary] Summary
> VLaRL augments a frozen vision-language-action model with a residual RL policy trained entirely in simulation, conditioned on the VLA's internal vision-language latent. A lightweight optimal-transport-trained mapper aligns simulated latents to the real latent distribution, so the residual transfers without real-world RL or online adaptation. Across four contact-rich tasks and two VLA backbones, real-robot success improved in all eight combinations.

## Problem

- VLA models produce appropriate nominal behavior but execute imprecisely during contact-rich interaction, where small errors in contact location, alignment, or sustained contact cause task failure.
- Residual RL can correct frozen VLAs without retraining, but approaches like ResFiT or RL Token train on physical robots, incurring high interaction cost and safety risk.
- Sim-to-real interfaces force a tradeoff: image-based policies suffer the visual domain gap, while object-pose states need separate perception pipelines and task-specific state specification.

## Method

- Latent-conditioned residual policy: mean-pooled VLM tokens from the frozen VLA, plus nominal action, proprioception, and wrist force, feed a ==TD3== actor whose correction is added with fixed scale 0.1.
- Sim-to-real latent mapper: a ==four-layer Transformer== with zero-initialized residual connection, trained via ==Sinkhorn optimal transport== with a trajectory-progress cost on digital-twin sim-real pairs.
- Asymmetric actor-critic training in ==MuJoCo== digital twins reconstructed with ==FoundationPose==; critics use privileged state, demonstration-derived residual targets regularize the actor, and the mapper is dropped at real deployment.

## Results

- Real-robot success improved in all eight task-backbone pairs, e.g., Flower button pressing **67.5% to 100%**, Flower block pushing **22.5% to 50%**, GR00T cup stacking **17.5% to 45%**.
- Removing the mapper collapsed Flower block pushing and cup stacking to **0/40** real trials versus **50% and 70%**; removing the VLM latent dropped button pressing from **100% to 70%**.
- Integrated gradients attribute **72-91%** of residual-action norm to the VLM latent; on unseen objects success rose from **40% to 57.5%** for pushing and **55% to 62.5%** for stacking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A frozen VLA's internal vision-language latent can serve as a compact sim-to-real interface for residual control, replacing explicit object-pose pipelines or raw-pixel policy inputs.
> - Pretrained VLM features are not domain-invariant; explicit distribution alignment of latents is necessary, since unaligned simulated latents can fail completely when deployed on real observations.
> - Latent alignment only closes the visual gap; contact dynamics and friction mismatches remain, so simulation and real-world gains can diverge, as Flower cup stacking showed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot told to press a colored button can get close to the target and still fail to make reliable contact. More generally, a robot may understand an instruction and produce a sensible action, yet still miss during contact, alignment, or sustained interaction. Improving those physical corrections with reinforcement learning directly on a robot is costly and raises safety concerns.

VLaRL takes a different route. It keeps the vision-language-action model (VLA), a robot controller that interprets images and language to choose actions, fixed after task fine-tuning. It then trains a small corrective "nudge" in simulation and uses it to adjust the VLA's action on the real robot.

The method was evaluated on real-robot success rate: the percentage of trials that meet a task's success criterion, with 40 trials per condition. On the Flower backbone, button-pressing success rose from **67.5% with the frozen base VLA to 100.0% with VLaRL**. For button pressing, a trial succeeds when it produces a beep and light. This is one task and backbone result, not an overall average.

Real-robot success rates over 40 trials. Gray bars show the base VLA and blue bars show VLaRL, for button pressing, block pushing, cup stacking, and drawer closing on the Flower and GR00T backbones.

### 2. How the correction crosses the gap

The correction is a residual policy. It leaves the base controller in place and learns a bounded corrective action that is added to the VLA's nominal action. VLaRL builds this policy in four stages:

1. Fine-tune the VLA on task demonstrations and freeze it. The same demonstrations are used to reconstruct approximate digital twins and corresponding real and simulated trajectories.
1. Train a mapper that shifts simulated VLA token representations toward the real latent distribution.
1. Keep the VLA and mapper fixed while the residual policy trains through simulation interaction.
1. Deploy the real VLA representation and the residual policy, with the mapper omitted and all models fixed.

The residual policy receives four inputs: a pooled VLA vision-language latent, the VLA's nominal action, robot proprioception, and measured wrist force. At deployment there is no real-world RL or online adaptation. Real data is still required. Training begins with 32 real demonstrations per task.

VLaRL's four-stage workflow. Training uses mapped simulated latents. At deployment, real latents are supplied directly and the mapper is not used.

### 3. What the real-robot tests establish

VLaRL improves real-world success in all eight combinations of four tasks and two VLA backbones, without real-world RL or online adaptation. The size of the gain varies, and it is small for Flower cup stacking. Simulation and real-world trends also do not always match.

Controlled Flower ablations test the two parts of the interface:

- **Without the mapper**, block pushing and cup stacking each had zero successes in 40 real-robot trials. Full VLaRL reached 50.0% and 70.0% on those tasks.
- **Without the latent**, the policy kept the nominal action, proprioception, and force inputs. Button-pressing success fell from 100.0% to 70.0%, and block pushing fell from 50.0% to 20.0%.

These findings support the importance of the mapper and the latent input in the tested setup. They do not show that latent alignment is the only factor in transfer.

Flower ablations comparing full VLaRL, no VLM latent, and no mapper. Hatched bars show simulation over three seeds; solid bars show the real robot over 40 trials per task.

### 4. Keep the VLA, add local correction

In VLaRL, the VLA remains responsible for instruction-conditioned behavior, and a simulation-trained residual adds local physical corrections. The ablations show that both latent conditioning and sim-to-real latent mapping matter. The mapper has a clear limit: it addresses representation shift, not every difference between simulated and real contact physics.
