# ROPA: Synthetic Robot Pose Generation for RGB-D Bimanual Data Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Southern California developed ROPA, an offline data augmentation method that generates synthetic robot poses and corresponding action labels for RGB-D bimanual imitation learning. This approach consistently improved policy success rates and robustness across various simulated and real-world tasks, outperforming baseline methods by enabling the synthesis of diverse training data.

## Problem

- Acquiring diverse, action-labeled real-world demonstration data for bimanual robotic manipulation is time-consuming and costly.
- Existing data augmentation methods often lack the ability to generate both novel robot poses and corresponding accurate action labels, especially for bimanual systems.
- Challenges include maintaining visual consistency across two robot arms, ensuring physically plausible action labels, and integrating depth information for bimanual RGB-D setups.

## Method

- A three-stage method, **ROPA**, utilizes a ==conditional diffusion model (Stable Diffusion with ControlNet)== adapted for ==robot pose-guided image synthesis==.
- A dedicated ==skeleton pose generator== renders explicit skeletal representations with visual cues for joint orientations, enabling precise conditioning for the diffusion model and deriving target joint positions via ==inverse kinematics==.
- An ==action labeling framework== generates physically consistent joint-space actions for novel poses, using ==constrained optimization== for contact-rich scenarios to ensure feasibility and prevent collisions.

## Results

- **ROPA** consistently outperformed baseline methods in simulation, improving success rates across **five** bimanual tasks in both RGB and RGB-D, with an **80%** success rate on Coordinated Lift Ball using a four-camera setup.
- In real-world experiments, the method enhanced robustness and success rates on tasks such as Lift Ball, Push Block, and Lift Drawer, showing benefits from RGB-D data.
- Ablation studies confirmed that detailed visual skeleton pose representations significantly improve the performance of the diffusion model compared to raw joint positions or less informative visual cues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adapting pose-guided image synthesis with diffusion models can effectively generate diverse robot poses and corresponding action labels for bimanual manipulation.
> - Visually rich skeleton representations, rather than raw numerical joint data, serve as more effective conditioning inputs for diffusion models in robot image synthesis.
> - Incorporating constrained optimization during action label generation is crucial for ensuring the physical feasibility and consistency of augmented bimanual robot poses, especially in contact scenarios.
