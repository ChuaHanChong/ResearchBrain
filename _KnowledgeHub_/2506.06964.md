# Offline RL by Reward-Weighted Fine-Tuning for Conversation Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Adobe Research developed Refit and Swift, two reward-weighted fine-tuning algorithms for offline reinforcement learning (RL) with Large Language Models (LLMs) tailored for multi-turn conversational optimization. These methods directly leverage explicit reward signals from pre-collected datasets, demonstrating improved accuracy and conversation quality over existing supervised fine-tuning and direct preference optimization baselines in multi-turn question answering tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional online RL for LLMs often requires extensive, costly, or unsafe online interactions, making offline learning from pre-collected datasets essential.
- Existing offline RL methods for LLMs, such as those based on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), often indirectly optimize the reward signal, leading to potential information loss and instability from complex components like token-level propensity scores.
- A need existed for practical, stable, and statistically efficient offline RL algorithms that could directly optimize LLM policies using explicit reward functions for conversational tasks without high variance or numerical instability.

## Method

- The authors recast offline RL as '==reward-weighted fine-tuning==,' developing **Refit**, an algorithm that directly optimizes a lower bound of the online RL objective by weighting the log-probabilities of actions by the trajectory's overall reward, thus avoiding unstable token-level propensity score ratios.
- They introduced Swift, an extension of Refit, which incorporates context-specific standardized rewards to mitigate high empirical variance from unscaled reward signals, promoting more stable learning and improved policies.
- Both Refit and Swift are designed to be practical by implementing them as minor modifications to standard SFT training primitives, facilitating integration with existing LLM fine-tuning pipelines.

## Results

- Swift achieved the highest accuracy in 7 out of **12** multi-turn reasoning experiments and ranked among the top two in **10** out of **12**, consistently outperforming all SFT and DPO-based baselines.
- Both Refit and Swift demonstrated competitive or superior performance across a range of conversation quality metrics (e.g., Accuracy, Reasoning Ability, Pedagogical Value) in multi-turn QA tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that increasing training data improves performance and that the core findings are robust across different AI evaluators (GPT-4o and Claude 4 Opus), supporting the generalizability of the methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly weighting policy updates based on full trajectory rewards is more effective for LLM optimization than transforming rewards into binary preferences or selecting only top-performing trajectories for SFT.
> - Eliminating token-level propensity score ratios from the offline RL objective significantly improves numerical stability and practicality when fine-tuning LLMs, which are prone to high variance issues.
> - Standardizing rewards (using context-specific means and standard deviations) effectively reduces gradient variance during policy optimization, leading to empirically superior learned policies.
