# ORV: 4D Occupancy-centric Robot Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> ORV is a framework for 4D occupancy-centric robot video generation that produces high-fidelity, action-aligned, and multiview-consistent videos by integrating 4D semantic occupancy as a visual prior into a video foundation model. This approach demonstrates superior performance in conditional video generation, visual planning, and policy learning, enhancing data scarcity solutions for embodied AI.

## Problem

- Existing action-conditioned video generation models for robotics struggle with visual fidelity, temporal consistency, and accurate action alignment with robot control inputs.
- A "representational gap" between sparse robot actions (e.g., 7-DoF poses) and dense pixel outputs limits the reliability of generated visual predictions.
- The data scarcity in embodied AI presents a significant bottleneck for training robust robot policies, requiring scalable and realistic data generation methods.

## Method

- ORV integrates a pre-trained video foundation model (CogVideoX) with 4D semantic occupancy as a geometrically grounded visual prior, using adaptive Gaussian splatting to render occupancy maps for conditioning.
- A chunk-level action conditioning mechanism enhances temporal alignment by encoding 7-DoF robot actions into the Diffusion Transformer backbone.
- Extensions include ORV-MV for multiview consistent video generation via a view attention module and ORV-S2R for robust sim-to-real transfer by utilizing simulator-derived occupancy maps.

## Results

- ORV achieved an 18.8% lower FVD than the previous state-of-the-art in conditional video generation, demonstrating enhanced visual quality and temporal consistency.
- The framework attained a +3.5% average success rate over the best baseline in visual planning on Robodesk tasks, indicating improved capability to predict controllable future observations.
- Augmenting policy learning datasets with ORV-synthesized videos boosted the performance of RoboVLM by ~13.7% and SpatialVLA by ~6.5%, demonstrating its utility in alleviating data scarcity for policy learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 4D semantic occupancy acts as an effective, domain-invariant visual prior, bridging the representational gap between sparse robot controls and dense visual outputs for more accurate generation.
> - Combining a powerful pre-trained video foundation model with occupancy-derived visual conditions and chunk-level action conditioning significantly improves visual fidelity and action alignment.
> - Models trained with coarse occupancy priors exhibit greater robustness and generalize better to varying granularity of conditioning maps in zero-shot settings than those trained with fine pixel-level conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence and robotics is rapidly advancing, but it faces a critical bottleneck: data scarcity. Training robust robot policies often requires vast amounts of interaction data, which is expensive and time-consuming to collect in the real world. While conventional simulators offer a safe and efficient environment for data generation, they typically struggle to provide the visual realism necessary for effective sim-to-real transfer.

In response, generative world models, particularly action-conditioned video generation, have emerged as a promising alternative to traditional simulators. These models aim to predict future visual states based on an agent's actions, offering the potential to synthesize realistic RGB observations that respond to control inputs. However, existing action-conditioned methods suffer from several limitations:
1. **Limited visual fidelity and temporal consistency:** Generated videos often lack the realism and coherence needed for reliable simulations.
2. **Poor action alignment:** Future predictions can drift or fail to faithfully reflect the manipulation controls, creating inconsistencies with desired robot behaviors.
3. **Single-view constraint:** Most methods are limited to generating single-view observations, lacking the multiview coherence essential for a comprehensive understanding of a 4D world.

"ORV: 4D Occupancy-centric Robot Video Generation" directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel conditioning paradigm. The paper posits that the core issues (particularly poor action alignment and single-view constraints) stem from a "representational gap" between sparse robot control inputs (e.g., 7-DoF end-effector poses) and dense pixel outputs. Prior works have attempted to bridge this gap with fine-grained cues like optical flow or masks, but ORV takes a more comprehensive approach by leveraging 4D semantic occupancy.

This work builds upon advancements in video generation, especially diffusion-based models (e.g., CogVideoX), and expands the application of 3D occupancy representations, which have seen success in autonomous driving, to the more intricate domain of robot manipulation. By introducing 4D occupancy as a visual prior, ORV aims to provide a more geometrically grounded and domain-invariant representation, thereby advancing the state of the art in generative world models for robotics, fostering more effective data engines, and ultimately enhancing sim-to-real transfer capabilities. The creation of a dedicated dataset, ORV-Data, further contributes to the research landscape by providing a much-needed resource for occupancy-centric robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of ORV is to develop a robust and versatile framework for robot video generation that produces high-fidelity, action-aligned, and controllable visual simulations, thereby overcoming the critical limitations of existing methods in embodied intelligence. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Enhance Visual Fidelity and Temporal Consistency (Addressing p1):** Current action-conditioned video generation models often struggle with generating visually realistic and temporally coherent sequences. ORV aims to leverage the power of modern video foundation models while injecting more meaningful priors to improve the overall quality of generated videos.
2. **Improve Action Alignment and Controllability (Addressing p2):** A significant challenge is ensuring that generated visual dynamics faithfully reflect sparse robot control inputs. The authors hypothesize that the "representational gap" between 7-DoF actions and dense pixel outputs leads to drifts and misalignments. By introducing 4D semantic occupancy as a visual prior, ORV seeks to provide a geometric bridge that more precisely couples control inputs with visual outcomes.
3. **Enable Multiview Consistent Synthesis (Addressing p3):** Many prior models are confined to single-view observations, which limits their utility for comprehensive 4D world modeling and downstream tasks requiring spatial understanding. A key motivation for ORV is to natively support multiview-consistent video generation, allowing for a richer and more accurate representation of the robot's environment.
4. **Facilitate Simulation-to-Real Transfer:** The domain gap between visually simplistic simulators and complex real-world environments hinders the direct application of policies learned in simulation. ORV aims to alleviate this by leveraging the domain-invariant nature of occupancy-derived representations, enabling the generation of realistic videos from simulated geometric priors, thus bridging the sim-to-real gap.
5. **Address Data Scarcity in Embodied AI:** Fundamentally, ORV is motivated by the need for a scalable "data engine" for embodied learning. By generating high-quality, controllable, and diverse robot manipulation videos, the framework can augment scarce real-world datasets, thereby improving visual planning and policy learning for various robotic tasks.
6. **Curate a Dedicated Dataset (ORV-Data):** A practical motivation was the observed lack of a large-scale, high-quality 4D semantic occupancy dataset specifically for robot manipulation. To support the training of their occupancy-centric model, the authors were motivated to curate ORV-Data, which itself becomes a significant contribution to the community.

In essence, ORV seeks to build a more robust, versatile, and realistic generative world model for robotics that not only generates compelling videos but also directly contributes to overcoming practical challenges in data-driven robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ORV proposes a novel 4D occupancy-centric framework for robot video generation, fundamentally designed to bridge the representational gap between sparse control inputs and dense visual outputs. The methodology is built upon a pre-trained video foundation model, augmented with a two-stage supervised finetuning process, and includes a dedicated data curation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The model aims to predict future visual observations (RGB videos) conditioned on states, agent actions (`a`), and, critically, occupancy fields (`c`). This `(c, a)` conditioning (`ρ3`) is a key differentiator from conventional text-to-video (`ρ1`) or action-conditioned (`ρ2`) models. The `a` and `c` priors are collected non-interactively from real-world or simulated environments. Occupancy fields are chosen for their robustness to geometric noise and seamless integration with online occupancy generation.

#### 3.2. Occupancy-centric Robot Video Generation Core Framework

ORV leverages a pre-trained open-source video foundation model, specifically CogVideoX-2B, which incorporates a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, as its backbone. This choice aims to reduce costly pre-training and improve visual realism. A two-stage Supervised Finetuning (SFT) process is then employed to inject both action and visual cues.

- **Chunk-level Action Conditioning:**
 - Robot actions are 7-DoF end-effector poses (`A ∈ R^T × Da`).
 - These actions are integrated into the DiT block using an "Action-Expert AdaLN (Adaptive Layer Normalization)" modulation, similar to existing Vision Expert AdaLN designs.
 - A chunk-level scheme is introduced for temporal alignment, where consecutive `r` actions are mapped to a single token via a shallow MLP, then upsampled and repeated to match video latent dimensions. This improves efficiency and alignment.
 - Parameter reuse from the pretrained Vision Expert AdaLN further minimizes computational overhead.

- **Occupancy-derived Visual Conditioning:**
 - To address the challenge of translating abstract 3D actions to dense 2D pixels, ORV incorporates soft, pixel-level visual conditioning from occupancy fields.
 - Direct voxel projection causes instability; thus, the method assigns non-learnable Gaussian splatting to each grid cell and renders these Gaussians from specific camera views.
 - An **adaptive scaling mechanism** (`σ = k2 ⋅ ẑ^k1`) is proposed for Gaussians to counteract perspective distortion, where `ẑ` is normalized depth, and `k1`, `k2` control scaling behavior for far and near planes, respectively.
 - These rendered occupancy maps are injected into the generation process as soft guidance. An additional encoder MLP (`ε_visual`) and a zero-initialized projector add these visual conditions to the input noise (`z_in`), avoiding corruption from soft conditions when injected into deep layers (unlike ControlNet-like approaches).

#### 3.3. Extensions of ORV

- **ORV-MV: Multiview Robot Video Generation:**
 - To achieve cross-view consistency, ORV-MV introduces an additional "view attention" module (inspired by 3D attention layers) prior to the temporal attention within the DiT blocks.
 - Single-view modules are conditioned on text, actions, and occupancy maps, while multiview modules primarily focus on view correspondences and exclude action priors.
 - Special attention is paid to collecting multiview occupancy map data by aligning camera poses from VGGT with 4D points reconstructed by MonST3R using linear least-squares fitting on depth maps.

- **ORV-S2R: Bridge Simulation-to-Real Transfer:**
 - Leverages the appearance-agnostic and geometrically robust nature of occupancy-derived visual priors (e.g., depth maps) to bridge the visual realism gap.
 - Physical simulators (ManiSkill, MuJoCo) can readily provide these priors.
 - For synthesis, ORV-S2R combines simulator-derived occupancy maps with an additional image generator (e.g., ControlNet) to produce diverse initial frames, which are then extended into high-quality, realistic manipulation videos.

#### 3.4. 4D Occupancy Dataset of Robot Manipulation (ORV-Data)

To facilitate training, ORV-Data is curated from existing robot datasets (BridgeData V2, DROID, RT-1) through a structured pipeline:

1. **Semantics Space Construction:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs, e.g., Qwen-VL-Chat) caption scenarios, and K-means clustering on word embeddings creates a compact semantic label set (~50 categories). Grounding DINO and SAM2 are used for instance tracking and mapping to coherent semantics.
2. **Occupancy Construction:** Sparse 4D points are reconstructed (e.g., with MonST3R) or directly used from RGB-D videos. These are then densified by Neural Kernel Surface Reconstruction (NKSR) and voxelized into 4D occupancy in a canonical space.
3. **Semantic Enrichment:** Semantics are assigned to voxels via majority voting from projected semantic labels.
4. **Bullet-time Renderings:** Occupancy data is rendered into 2D condition maps for training, with filtering based on inter-frame consistency (RAFT).

This comprehensive methodology, combining advanced generative models with geometric priors and a dedicated dataset, forms the backbone of ORV's capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various benchmarks and tasks demonstrate the superior performance and versatility of the ORV framework.

#### 4.1. Conditional Video Generation

ORV was evaluated on three real-world robot datasets: BridgeV2, DROID, and RT-1.

- **Quantitative Results:** ORV significantly outperforms state-of-the-art text-conditioned (CogVideoX) and action-conditioned methods (AVID, HMA, IRASim) across all datasets.
 - It achieves an **18.8% lower FVD** (Fréchet Video Distance, indicating better video quality and temporal consistency) than the previous state-of-the-art.
 - Consistently higher PSNR and SSIM, and lower FID, across all benchmarks, signifying improved visual fidelity and realism. For instance, on BridgeV2, ORV achieves an FVD of 16.525, significantly better than IRASim's 20.910 (action-only) and 22.503 (augmented with occupancy/action).
- **Qualitative Results:** Generated videos exhibit superior fidelity, temporal consistency, and faithful alignment with robot actions. Baselines often fail to accurately infer object dynamics or show artifacts during gripper manipulation, while ORV produces realistic and coherent interactions.
- **Multiview Synthesis:** ORV-MV successfully generates multiview robot manipulation videos (e.g., a cloth-folding task across three views) with exceptional cross-view consistency, addressing a key limitation of prior works.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** ORV-S2R effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap. By conditioning on simulator-derived occupancy maps and using an image generator for diverse initial frames, ORV synthesizes high-quality, realistic manipulation videos from appearance-agnostic simulated information. This demonstrates the robustness of occupancy representations to domain gaps.

#### 4.2. Visual Planning

Evaluated on the VP2 benchmark, which assesses visual planning capabilities via action controls.

- **Success Rate:** ORV achieves superior visual planning performance, outperforming all baselines (MCVD, FitVid, MaskViT, iVideoGPT) in four Robodesk tasks and securing second-best results in the other four.
- It achieves a **+3.5% average success rate** compared to the best baseline (iVideoGPT), indicating its capability to predict high-fidelity and controllable future observations crucial for planning.

#### 4.3. Policy Learning

ORV was used as a data engine to augment existing datasets for policy learning, focusing on appearance randomization.

- **Performance Improvement:** Augmenting training data with ORV-synthesized videos significantly boosts the performance of recent open-sourced policy models (RoboVLM and SpatialVLA) on SimplerEnv-WidowX tasks.
 - RoboVLM shows a **~13.7% gain** (from 29.8% to 33.9% average success rate).
 - SpatialVLA shows a **~6.5% gain** (from 36.2% to 38.5% average success rate).
- The synthetic data, accounting for ~25% of the augmented dataset, proves highly effective, demonstrating ORV's utility in alleviating data scarcity for policy learning.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study and Analysis

- **Effect of Conditioning Approaches:** Chunk-level action conditioning is found to be crucial, with variations (e.g., using text expert parameters, or no chunking) leading to noticeable performance drops. For occupancy, injecting conditions into the initial diffusion noise is superior to ControlNet-like deep layer injection.
- **Effect of Control Signals:** The introduction of visual priors (depth and semantics derived from occupancy) consistently leads to significant improvements in video generation quality (e.g., 18.72% PSNR gain with full fine conditioning). Even "coarse" occupancy maps achieve comparable performance to "fine" pixel-level conditions, highlighting the value of occupancy. Multiview generation also benefits from visual priors, improving cross-view consistency.
- **Effect of Pretraining:** Training models from the CogVideoX backbone yielded significantly better performance (especially FID and FVD) compared to training from scratch, validating the strategy of leveraging powerful pre-trained foundation models.
- **Robustness of Occupancy Representations:** A critical finding is that models trained on *coarse* occupancy-derived conditions generalize much better to conditions of varying granularity (both coarse and fine) in a zero-shot setting. In contrast, models trained on pixel-aligned "fine" conditions suffer a dramatic performance drop when presented with coarse inputs. This demonstrates that ORV, due to its occupancy-centric design, is less sensitive to inaccuracies in conditioning maps, offering greater robustness for real-world deployment and domain transfer.

Overall, the results robustly validate ORV's capabilities as a high-fidelity, controllable, and versatile generative world model, with direct positive impacts on downstream robotics tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ORV framework represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied intelligence and generative AI for robotics, with several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Advancing Generative World Models:** ORV pushes the boundaries of controllable video generation for robotics. By effectively coupling action priors with 4D occupancy-derived visual priors, it offers a more robust, geometrically grounded, and visually realistic alternative to traditional physical simulators. This directly addresses the long-standing challenge of creating high-fidelity generative world models capable of mimicking real-world dynamics.
2. **Bridging the Representational and Sim-to-Real Gaps:** The core insight of using 4D semantic occupancy as a visual prior is crucial. It effectively bridges the representational gap between sparse robot controls and dense pixel outputs, leading to significantly improved action alignment and temporal consistency. Furthermore, the domain-invariant nature and robustness of occupancy representations enable ORV to facilitate effective sim-to-real visual transfer, reducing the visual realism gap and making policies trained with synthetic data more directly applicable to the real world.
3. **Powerful Data Engine for Embodied AI:** ORV provides a scalable and efficient data generation engine to alleviate the pervasive data scarcity problem in robotics. By synthesizing high-quality, diverse, and controllable robot manipulation videos, it can significantly augment limited real-world datasets. This has a direct positive impact on data-driven robot learning paradigms, including:
 - **Visual Planning:** Improved accuracy in predicting future observations allows for more effective visual planning strategies for robots.
 - **Policy Learning:** Augmented datasets lead to more robust, generalizable, and higher-performing robot policies, as demonstrated by the significant gains observed for RoboVLM and SpatialVLA. This can accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robots.
 - **Imitation Learning:** The ability to generate diverse demonstrations with controlled actions and appearances can make imitation learning more effective and less reliant on human teleoperation.
4. **Novel Dataset Contribution:** The curation of ORV-Data, a large-scale, high-quality 4D semantic occupancy dataset for robot manipulation, is a valuable contribution to the research community. This dataset fills a critical void and will undoubtedly foster further research into occupancy-centric approaches in robotics perception, planning, and control.
5. **Enabling Multiview Understanding:** By supporting multiview consistent video synthesis, ORV allows for a more comprehensive and spatially aware understanding of the robot's environment. This is crucial for complex manipulation tasks that benefit from multiple perspectives and for photorealistic scene reconstruction.
6. **Future Research Directions:** ORV opens up several promising avenues for future research. These include integrating online 4D occupancy generation/forecasting for real-time applications, incorporating richer action representations beyond 7-DoF end-effector poses, developing full multiview initial frame generation from single views, and extending the framework towards long-horizon robot manipulation planning and generation.

In summary, ORV stands as a foundational step towards building more capable and autonomous robots by providing a highly effective tool for realistic simulation and data generation. Its impact is expected to resonate across academic research and industrial applications, accelerating progress in the entire field of embodied AI. The authors also responsibly acknowledge potential dual-use risks and advocate for ethical considerations and safeguards in generative AI technologies.
