# Enhancing the Outcome Reward-based RL Training of MLLMs with Self-Consistency Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS) to improve the faithfulness of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) trained with outcome-reward reinforcement learning. SCS addresses unfaithful reasoning, where models arrive at correct answers via flawed logic, by integrating a differentiable consistency reward, leading to an average absolute performance gain of 7.7 percentage points for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

## Problem

- Outcome-reward reinforcement learning (RL) in multiple-choice MLLM tasks often reinforces "unfaithful trajectories" where models guess correct answers despite flawed or hallucinatory reasoning.
- This leads to limited performance gains in multiple-choice benchmarks compared to open-ended question-answering, indicating a failure to develop robust reasoning abilities.
- Qualitative analysis revealed MLLMs frequently produced incorrect rationales while selecting correct options, and truncated rationales often led to divergent answers, highlighting unstable reasoning.

## Method

- The ==Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS)== framework is proposed, which integrates a ==differentiable consistency reward== into outcome-reward RL training for MLLMs.
- SCS explores "neighboring trajectories" via two mechanisms: ==Truncation–Resampling (TR)==, where initial reasoning is truncated and re-sampled, and ==Visual-Perturbation (VP)==, which introduces small visual noise to input images.
- A consistency reward is calculated based on the number of distinct answers from resampled trajectories, then combined with standard accuracy and format rewards to update the MLLM policy, penalizing inconsistent reasoning.

## Results

- SCS consistently improved MLLM performance, achieving an average absolute gain of **7.7** percentage points for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct over the RLOO baseline across six multimodal benchmarks.
- Models trained with SCS demonstrated a **13.6%** to **15.2%** reduction in unfaithful reasoning occurrences compared to baselines, validated by human and strong LLM evaluations.
- The method generalized across different model scales (3B, 7B, 8B parameters) and various RL algorithms, with both Truncation–Resampling and Visual Perturbation components synergistically contributing to performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-reward alone is insufficient for distinguishing genuinely correct reasoning from unfaithful lucky guesses in multiple-choice MLLM tasks.
> - Perturbing reasoning trajectories through truncation and visual noise can effectively assess and, subsequently, improve the robustness and faithfulness of MLLM reasoning during RL training.
> - Integrating a consistency reward into existing outcome-reward RL frameworks provides a computationally efficient method to enhance reasoning fidelity without requiring complex process reward models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the critical intersection of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), reinforcement learning (RL), and advanced reasoning techniques. With the burgeoning success of large language models (LLMs) and their multimodal extensions (MLLMs), enhancing their reasoning abilities through RL has become a mainstream avenue towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Previous studies have reported significant performance breakthroughs by applying RL to LLMs, particularly in complex domains like mathematics, video comprehension, and visual logical reasoning for MLLMs.

However, the authors identify a significant, yet often overlooked, challenge within the common "outcome-reward" RL paradigm, especially prevalent in multiple-choice multimodal reasoning benchmarks. Traditional outcome-reward RL assesses performance solely based on whether the final chosen option matches the ground truth, completely disregarding the faithfulness or logical soundness of the intermediate reasoning steps. This leads to a crucial flaw: models can receive positive rewards for "unfaithful trajectories" – situations where they guess the correct answer after a faulty, spurious, or even hallucinatory chain of thought. Such behavior, while achieving a correct *outcome*, undermines the model's true reasoning capabilities and hinders its long-term development towards robust intelligence.

The paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a method that allows outcome-reward RL to account for the fidelity of the reasoning process without resorting to the computationally expensive "process reward" models. It builds upon recent advances in consistency-based reasoning, a technique that has shown promise in improving the robustness and reliability of LLM outputs by promoting agreement across multiple generated responses or perturbed inputs. By adapting self-consistency principles to the RL training loop for MLLMs, this work seeks to imbue models with more reliable and interpretable reasoning abilities, moving beyond mere accuracy to foster genuine understanding and logical coherence. This positions the work as an important refinement to prevailing RL training methodologies for MLLMs, making them more effective at learning truly robust reasoning skills rather than superficial task completion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to address and rectify a fundamental flaw in outcome-reward reinforcement learning (RL) when applied to multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in multiple-choice reasoning tasks. The authors aim to enhance the faithfulness of MLLMs' reasoning trajectories, ensuring that models arrive at correct answers through genuinely logical and consistent thought processes, rather than by chance or flawed reasoning.

The primary motivations for this work stem from the observed shortcomings of traditional outcome-reward RL:
1. **Unfaithful Trajectories:** In multiple-choice settings, an MLLM might generate an incorrect or illogical chain of thought but still select the correct final option due to random guessing, spurious correlations, or hallucination. Current outcome-reward RL methods provide a full reward in such cases, inadvertently reinforcing these unfaithful reasoning patterns. This means models can "luck out" and get credit for wrong solutions, hindering the development of reliable reasoning.
2. **Limited Performance Gains in Multiple-Choice vs. Open-Ended QA:** The authors empirically demonstrate that RL training on multiple-choice benchmarks yields significantly smaller performance gains compared to open-ended question-answering settings (e.g., 5.6% vs. 12.0% on Geometry3K). This discrepancy highlights that the multiple-choice format, combined with a naive outcome reward, fails to properly guide the model towards robust reasoning.
3. **Lack of Intermediate Reasoning Fidelity:** Qualitative analysis reveals that models often produce incorrect rationales while still selecting the correct option. Further, truncated rationales frequently lead to divergent answer options upon re-sampling, underscoring the instability and unfaithfulness of the underlying reasoning process.
4. **Computational Efficiency:** While "process reward" models exist to evaluate reasoning steps, they are often resource-intensive. The authors seek a solution that can enhance reasoning faithfulness while maintaining the computational efficiency inherent in outcome-reward formulations.

To achieve these objectives and overcome these motivations, the authors propose Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS), a novel framework designed to correct the issue of unfaithful reasoning by introducing a differentiable consistency reward during policy updates. This reward is intended to down-weight unreliable trajectories, thereby steering the MLLM towards more robust and logically sound reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS)**, a framework designed to introduce a consistency reward into outcome-reward RL training for MLLMs. SCS aims to penalize incoherent or spurious reasoning patterns, thereby encouraging faithful reasoning trajectories. The method operates on the principle that robust reasoning should lead to consistent answers even under minor perturbations or re-sampling.

The SCS methodology is built upon three key assumptions about reasoning:
1. **Uniqueness of the correct trajectory:** There is typically one correct reasoning path.
2. **Leaf/Choice Alignment:** Every reasoning trajectory ends with one of the predefined answer choices.
3. **Relationship between correct/incorrect reasoning and answers:** Correct reasoning *must* lead to the correct option, while incorrect reasoning *may* lead to either a correct (by chance) or incorrect option.

Based on these assumptions, the authors argue that an outcome reward alone cannot differentiate between a genuinely correct reasoning path and an unfaithful one that happens to guess the right answer. To address this, SCS introduces a **consistency reward ($r_{con}$)**. For a given question, after generating an initial reasoning trajectory ($\tau$), SCS explores its "neighboring trajectories" through two integrated mechanisms:

1. **Truncation–Resampling (TR):** The initial trajectory $\tau$ is truncated at a specific point (determined by a truncation ratio $k$) to form an incomplete prefix $\tau_<$. From this prefix, the MLLM is prompted to re-sample multiple continuations, each leading to a new answer ($a_t$). This process is repeated $m$ times, and all sampled answers are collected into a set $A$. The intuition is that if the initial reasoning up to the truncation point is sound, subsequent continuations should consistently lead to the same (correct) answer. If the reasoning is unfaithful, the continuations are likely to diverge, leading to a larger set $A$.

2. **Visual-Perturbation (VP):** To further challenge the model's robustness and consistency, small visual perturbations are introduced to the input image for each resampled trajectory. Instead of fixed noise, the perturbation strength ($\sigma_i$) is drawn independently from a uniform distribution ($U(\sigma_{min}, \sigma_{max})$) for each continuation. This encourages the policy to reason over a broader range of visual evidence and ensures that its reasoning is not fragile to minor input variations.

The consistency reward is then computed as $r_{con} = \frac{1}{N}(N - |A|) \cdot c$, where $N$ is the total number of resampled trajectories, $|A|$ is the number of *distinct* answers among the $N$ resamples, and $c$ is a scaling coefficient. A smaller $|A|$ (meaning more consistent answers) results in a higher consistency reward. This reward is then combined with the standard accuracy reward ($r_{acc}$) and an optional format reward ($r_{for}$) to form the total reward $r = r_{for} + r_{acc} + r_{con}$.

This combined reward is used to update the policy within existing outcome reward-based RL algorithms. The paper demonstrates SCS's integration with several prominent RL algorithms:
- **GRPO (Group-wise Relative Policy Optimization):** A PPO-style method that eliminates the value network.
- **RLOO (Reinforcement Learning with Leave-One-Out):** Generates multiple rollouts and uses peer rollouts for baseline estimation.
- **REINFORCE++ series:** Augments classic REINFORCE with PPO-style optimizations like KL penalties, reward normalization, and advantage normalization.

The algorithm effectively calculates a baseline of the combined reward and computes a policy gradient to update model parameters, steering the MLLM towards trajectories that are not only accurate but also self-consistent under perturbations, thereby enhancing overall reasoning faithfulness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling findings that underscore the effectiveness and significance of Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS) in enhancing the outcome reward-based RL training of MLLMs.

#### 4.1. Empirical Evidence of Unfaithful Reasoning

- The authors first empirically demonstrate the problem of unfaithful reasoning: RL training in a multiple-choice setting yielded only a 5.6% accuracy improvement on Geometry3K, significantly lower than the 12.0% gain in an open-ended QA setting (Figure 2a).
- Qualitative examples (Figure 2b) and quantitative analysis (Figure 2c) confirmed that models often generated incorrect rationales yet selected the correct option, and identical reasoning prefixes frequently led to divergent answer options, indicating unstable and unfaithful reasoning. Human evaluation and strong LLMs (OpenAI-o3-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash) validated the prevalence of unfaithful reasoning (Table 3, Figure 14).

#### 4.2. Significant Performance Gains with SCS

- Integrating SCS with various outcome-reward RL algorithms consistently improved performance across six multimodal benchmarks.
- For **Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct**, SCS yielded an average absolute improvement of **7.7 percentage points** over the RLOO baseline, increasing accuracy from 57.8% to 65.5%.
- SCS also brought gains of 0.9 pp for GRPO, 1.7 pp for REINFORCE++-baseline, and 2.0 pp for REINFORCE++ (Table 2). Every SCS variant statistically outperformed its standard counterpart and the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) baseline.

#### 4.3. Improved Reasoning Reliability (Faithfulness)

- Quantitative analysis of 100 correctly answered questions, evaluated by humans and two powerful LLMs (OpenAI-o3-mini and Gemini-2.5-Flash), showed a significant reduction in unfaithful reasoning. Models trained with SCS exhibited an approximate **13.6% to 15.2% decrease** in the occurrence of unfaithful reasoning compared to their baselines (Table 3).
- Qualitative examples (Figure 4, Appendix D.3) vividly demonstrated that SCS-trained models adopted logically coherent derivation paths, contrasting with the "lucky guess" scenarios and uninformative SFT responses.

#### 4.4. Generalizability Across Models and Architectures

- SCS demonstrated consistent gains on models of different scales and architectures. When applied with RLOO, it improved **Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct** by 3.2 pp and **InternVL3-8B** by 1.6 pp, indicating its broad applicability.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies Confirm Component Importance

- Ablation studies on RLOO-SCS revealed that both components of SCS are crucial:
 - **Truncation–Resampling (TR)** alone provided a 5.2 pp improvement.
 - **Visual Perturbation (VP)** alone yielded a 5.0 pp improvement.
 - Combining both TR and VP recovered the full 7.7 pp gain, confirming their synergistic effect (Table 4).

#### 4.6. Hyperparameter Robustness and Computational Overhead

- Sensitivity analysis for truncation ratio ($k$) and number of resampled trajectories ($m$) showed that SCS performance was robust, with variations remaining within 4 percentage points across different settings. Optimal performance for RLOO was observed around $k=0.8$ and $m=4$ (Figure 5).
- While SCS adds overhead for sampling, the authors state it is "negligible extra computation" and "well-controlled" due to parallel processing with high-performance inference engines. Specifically, for RLOO, SCS increased training time by approximately 38% for a 7.7 pp gain (Table 14), which is an acceptable trade-off for the significant performance and reliability improvements.

Overall, the findings strongly support SCS as an efficient, general, and effective method for improving both the accuracy and, critically, the faithfulness of reasoning in MLLMs trained with outcome-reward RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS) holds significant implications for the development and training of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in enhancing their reasoning capabilities. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

**Addressing a Critical, Overlooked Flaw:** The most significant contribution of this paper is its identification and effective mitigation of the "unfaithful reasoning" problem in outcome-reward RL for MLLMs. This issue, where models achieve correct answers through flawed logic or sheer luck, has been a subtle but critical impediment to building truly intelligent systems. By highlighting and providing a solution for this flaw, the research pushes the field towards more robust and trustworthy AI.

**Enhancing Trustworthiness and Interpretability:** By promoting faithful reasoning, SCS directly contributes to making MLLMs more trustworthy and their outputs more interpretable. When a model consistently arrives at a correct answer through a logical and verifiable chain of thought, users can place greater confidence in its capabilities and understand *how* it reached its conclusions. This is crucial for real-world applications where explainability and reliability are paramount, such as in scientific, medical, or engineering domains.

**Improving the Efficiency of RL Training:** SCS offers an efficient solution that does not rely on computationally expensive "process reward" models, which would require detailed step-by-step supervision or complex reward engineering. Instead, it leverages existing outcome-reward formulations and integrates consistency checks through simple and parallelizable sampling mechanisms. The reported modest increase in training time (~38% for significant gains) suggests a favorable cost-benefit trade-off, making it practical for widespread adoption.

**Generalizability and Broad Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across various MLLM scales (3B, 7B, 8B parameters) and different outcome-reward RL algorithms (RLOO, GRPO, REINFORCE++) demonstrates its generality. This suggests that SCS can be readily integrated into diverse MLLM training pipelines without requiring extensive re-engineering, providing a universal remedy for the identified problem. This broad applicability can accelerate research and development in MLLM reasoning.

**Paving the Way for Consistency-Guided RL:** This work explicitly integrates consistency-based reasoning, a technique previously successful in improving LLM outputs, directly into the RL training loop. This fusion of ideas could inspire new directions in RL algorithm design, where internal consistency and robustness are explicitly optimized alongside external task performance, fostering a deeper form of learning in AI models.

**Advancing MLLM Reasoning for AGI:** By addressing the fundamental challenge of reasoning faithfulness, SCS takes a tangible step towards developing MLLMs with more sophisticated and human-like reasoning abilities, which is a core component of the broader pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Enabling models to consistently arrive at correct solutions through verifiable logic is essential for tackling complex, open-ended problems that require true understanding.

In conclusion, SCS represents a simple, low-cost, and effective solution to a critical problem in MLLM training. By ensuring that models are rewarded not just for *being right* but for *reasoning right*, it has the potential to significantly advance the capabilities and trustworthiness of future MLLMs across a wide range of multimodal reasoning tasks.
