# Enhancing the Outcome Reward-based RL Training of MLLMs with Self-Consistency Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS) to improve the faithfulness of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) trained with outcome-reward reinforcement learning. SCS addresses unfaithful reasoning, where models arrive at correct answers via flawed logic, by integrating a differentiable consistency reward, leading to an average absolute performance gain of 7.7 percentage points for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

## Problem

- Outcome-reward reinforcement learning (RL) in multiple-choice MLLM tasks often reinforces "unfaithful trajectories" where models guess correct answers despite flawed or hallucinatory reasoning.
- This leads to limited performance gains in multiple-choice benchmarks compared to open-ended question-answering, indicating a failure to develop robust reasoning abilities.
- Qualitative analysis revealed MLLMs frequently produced incorrect rationales while selecting correct options, and truncated rationales often led to divergent answers, highlighting unstable reasoning.

## Method

- The ==Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS)== framework is proposed, which integrates a ==differentiable consistency reward== into outcome-reward RL training for MLLMs.
- SCS explores "neighboring trajectories" via two mechanisms: ==Truncation–Resampling (TR)==, where initial reasoning is truncated and re-sampled, and ==Visual-Perturbation (VP)==, which introduces small visual noise to input images.
- A consistency reward is calculated based on the number of distinct answers from resampled trajectories, then combined with standard accuracy and format rewards to update the MLLM policy, penalizing inconsistent reasoning.

## Results

- SCS consistently improved MLLM performance, achieving an average absolute gain of **7.7** percentage points for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct over the RLOO baseline across six multimodal benchmarks.
- Models trained with SCS demonstrated a **13.6%** to **15.2%** reduction in unfaithful reasoning occurrences compared to baselines, validated by human and strong LLM evaluations.
- The method generalized across different model scales (3B, 7B, 8B parameters) and various RL algorithms, with both Truncation–Resampling and Visual Perturbation components synergistically contributing to performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-reward alone is insufficient for distinguishing genuinely correct reasoning from unfaithful lucky guesses in multiple-choice MLLM tasks.
> - Perturbing reasoning trajectories through truncation and visual noise can effectively assess and, subsequently, improve the robustness and faithfulness of MLLM reasoning during RL training.
> - Integrating a consistency reward into existing outcome-reward RL frameworks provides a computationally efficient method to enhance reasoning fidelity without requiring complex process reward models.
