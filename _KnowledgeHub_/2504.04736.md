# Synthetic Data Generation & Multi-Step RL for Reasoning & Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> SWiRL is an offline multi-step reinforcement learning methodology designed to enhance Large Language Models' reasoning and tool-use capabilities. It generates and utilizes synthetic data, applying process-based filtering for step-wise optimization, which leads to substantial performance gains and strong cross-domain generalization in complex tasks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with complex queries requiring multi-step reasoning and strategic interaction with external tools.
- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) methods for LLMs provide sparse, single-step reward signals, which are insufficient for training models on long, sequential reasoning tasks.
- Existing multi-step optimization approaches for LLMs frequently lack integrated tool use or rely on outcome-based filtering of data, limiting their ability to learn robust intermediate processes.

## Method

- ==SWiRL (Step-Wise Reinforcement Learning)== introduces an offline multi-step optimization technique that leverages synthetic data generation with iterative tool execution.
- The methodology employs a ==generative reward model== to provide a granular, ==step-wise reward signal==, evaluating the quality and reasonableness of each individual action within its specific sub-trajectory context.
- It explores and prioritizes ==process-based filtering== of synthetic trajectories, retaining data based on the soundness of intermediate steps rather than solely on the correctness of the final outcome.

## Results

- SWiRL achieved significant relative accuracy improvements of **21.5%** on GSM8K, **12.3%** on HotPotQA, **14.8%** on CofCA, **11.1%** on MuSiQue, and **15.3%** on BeerQA.
- Training SWiRL on HotPotQA improved zero-shot GSM8K performance by a relative **16.9%**, while GSM8K training improved HotPotQA by **9.2%**, demonstrating strong cross-domain and cross-tool transferability.
- SWiRL-finetuned Gemma-2-27b models outperformed their base counterparts and, in several cases, even the stronger Gemini 1.5 Pro reward model on out-of-distribution benchmarks, indicating active learning beyond distillation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Process-based filtering of synthetic data, which evaluates the reasonableness of each intermediate step, is more effective for multi-step reinforcement learning than filtering based on the final outcome.
> - SWiRL demonstrates robust generalization capabilities, improving performance across different multi-step tasks (e.g., question answering to mathematical reasoning) and tool types (e.g., search to calculator) even when trained on a single domain.
> - The method can effectively learn from trajectories with logically sound intermediate steps, even if the final answer is incorrect, showcasing a deeper learning of reasoning processes compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
