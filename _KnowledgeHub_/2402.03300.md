# DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepSeek-AI developed DeepSeekMath, a 7B parameter open-source language model, achieving 51.7% Top1 accuracy on the MATH benchmark, nearing the performance of larger proprietary models like GPT-4. This was accomplished through extensive pre-training on a 120 billion-token mathematical corpus and fine-tuning with a novel Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) struggled with complex, multi-step mathematical reasoning and precise logical deduction.
- A significant performance gap existed between advanced proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4, Gemini-Ultra) and publicly available open-source LLMs in mathematical tasks.
- Achieving high mathematical proficiency in LLMs often required very large parameter counts, leading to high computational costs.

## Method

- **DeepSeekMath** builds upon **DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B**, ==continually pre-training== it on the 120 billion-token DeepSeekMath Corpus, a meticulously curated multilingual dataset of mathematical web content.
- ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== was conducted on a 776K example instruction-tuning dataset covering diverse mathematical fields and solution formats (==CoT==, ==PoT==, tool-integrated).
- A novel ==reinforcement learning== algorithm, ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, was introduced, eliminating the need for a separate ==critic model== to efficiently refine reasoning abilities with process and outcome supervision.

## Results

- DeepSeekMath-RL 7B achieved **51.7%** Top1 accuracy on the competition-level MATH benchmark without external tools, closely approaching proprietary models like GPT-4 (**52.9%**) and Gemini-Ultra (**53.2%**).
- DeepSeekMath-Base 7B surpassed Minerva 540B (a model **77 times larger**) on the MATH dataset (**36.2%** vs. **33.6%**), demonstrating high efficiency.
- The model set new state-of-the-art performance records for open-source models across various English and Chinese mathematical benchmarks, including tool-integrated reasoning and formal mathematics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Code training significantly enhances LLMs' mathematical reasoning abilities, particularly for logical and structured thinking.
> - Pre-training solely on arXiv papers, surprisingly, did not yield noticeable improvements or sometimes led to performance deterioration in their specific experimental setup.
> - Reinforcement learning primarily improves the robustness and consistency of generating correct mathematical solutions rather than boosting the fundamental single-sample reasoning capability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mathematical reasoning has long been recognized as a formidable challenge for language models due to its inherent complexity, structured nature, and the need for precise logical deduction. While cutting-edge proprietary models like OpenAI's GPT-4 and Google's Gemini-Ultra have demonstrated impressive capabilities in this domain, publicly available open-source models have historically lagged considerably. This performance gap poses a significant barrier to democratizing advanced AI research and applications.

The field has seen various approaches to enhance mathematical abilities in LLMs, including:
- **Specialized Pre-training:** Models like Minerva (Google DeepMind) were extensively pre-trained on mathematical texts (e.g., arXiv papers, web pages containing mathematical content). Llemma (from a joint academic/industry effort including researchers from EleutherAI and Stability AI) also focused on mathematical pre-training.
- **Instruction Tuning:** Fine-tuning models on curated datasets of mathematical problems paired with step-by-step solutions (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, Program-of-Thought) to improve problem-solving abilities. Works like WizardMath and MetaMath are examples.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human/AI Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF):** Applying RL techniques (e.g., PPO) to align model outputs with desired performance, often using reward models trained on human preferences or rule-based correctness. Math-Shepherd is a recent example.
- **Tool Integration:** Equipping LLMs with the ability to use external tools like Python interpreters or symbolic solvers to augment their reasoning capabilities.

DeepSeekMath directly addresses the open-source performance gap by combining and significantly advancing several of these approaches. It pushes the boundaries by demonstrating that a relatively smaller 7B parameter model, when subjected to highly effective domain-specific pre-training and novel RL techniques, can rival the mathematical reasoning capabilities of much larger, and often closed-source, models. The paper also contributes to the ongoing debate about optimal pre-training data composition, particularly regarding the utility of code and academic papers for mathematical reasoning. By releasing DeepSeekMath, the authors contribute a powerful new baseline for open research and development in mathematical AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DeepSeekMath is to **significantly advance the mathematical reasoning capabilities of open-source language models**, aiming to bridge the performance gap with state-of-the-art proprietary models like GPT-4 and Gemini-Ultra.

The motivations driving this research are multifaceted:
1. **Democratization of Advanced AI:** Making highly capable mathematical LLMs publicly available fosters open research, innovation, and broader application of AI in scientific and educational contexts.
2. **Addressing Core Challenge:** Mathematical reasoning is a complex cognitive task that tests an LLM's ability to understand, plan, execute multi-step logic, and handle symbolic manipulation. Excelling in this domain signifies a deeper level of understanding and reasoning.
3. **Optimizing Resource Usage:** The paper aims to demonstrate that superior performance can be achieved with a 7B parameter model, challenging the notion that only massive models can achieve high mathematical proficiency. This is crucial for accessibility and reduced computational costs.
4. **Investigating Key Factors:** The authors hypothesize that two core factors are paramount for mathematical reasoning:
 - **High-Quality, Large-Scale Pre-training Data:** Specifically, leveraging publicly available web data (Common Crawl) through a meticulously designed data selection pipeline to create a domain-specific corpus.
 - **Efficient and Effective Reinforcement Learning:** Introducing a novel RL algorithm, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to enhance reasoning abilities while being more memory-efficient than standard PPO.
5. **Gaining Deeper Insights into LLM Training:** Through extensive ablation studies, the authors aim to understand the impact of various training components, such as the utility of code training and arXiv papers for mathematical reasoning, and to develop a unified paradigm for understanding different RL fine-tuning methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepSeekMath's development involves a three-stage training process: foundational pre-training on a coding model, extensive domain-specific pre-training on mathematical data, supervised fine-tuning, and a novel reinforcement learning phase.

#### 3.1. Base Model Selection and Initial Training

DeepSeekMath is initialized from **DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B**, a coding-oriented large language model developed by DeepSeek-AI. The authors hypothesize and later confirm that starting from a model trained on code data provides a better foundation for mathematical reasoning compared to a general-purpose LLM, suggesting a positive transfer of logical and structured thinking from coding tasks.

#### 3.2. Math Pre-training (DeepSeekMath-Base 7B)

The most significant component of the pre-training phase is the creation of the **DeepSeekMath Corpus**, a large-scale, high-quality, multilingual (primarily English and Chinese) dataset of 120 billion math-related tokens.
- **Data Collection Pipeline:** An iterative process is employed:
 1. **Seed Corpus:** OpenWebMath, a collection of high-quality mathematical web texts, serves as the initial seed.
 2. **FastText Classifier Training:** A fastText model is trained on positive examples from OpenWebMath and negative examples from Common Crawl (CC).
 3. **Initial Recall from Common Crawl:** The trained classifier recalls math-related web pages from a deduplicated 40B HTML pages of Common Crawl.
 4. **Iterative Refinement:** To enhance diversity and quantity, domains with a high percentage (>10%) of math pages are identified. Human annotators then refine URLs within these domains to capture more uncollected mathematical content, which is added to the seed corpus, and the classifier is retrained. This process iterated four times, yielding 35.5 million math web pages.
- **Data Decontamination:** Strict filtering criteria (10-gram and 3-gram exact matching) are applied to remove any content overlapping with evaluation benchmarks (English and Chinese math benchmarks) to ensure fair assessment.
- **Corpus Validation:** Comparative pre-training experiments on a smaller 1.3B parameter model against existing math corpora (MathPile, OpenWebMath, Proof-Pile-2) demonstrated the DeepSeekMath Corpus's superior quality, multilingual coverage, and larger scale, leading to steeper and more sustained learning curves.
- **DeepSeekMath-Base 7B Training:** The DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B model is continually pre-trained for 500 billion tokens. The data mix includes 56% from the DeepSeekMath Corpus, 4% from AlgebraicStack (mathematical code), 10% from arXiv (academic papers), 20% from GitHub (code), and 10% natural language from Common Crawl (English and Chinese).

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-Tuning (DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B)

Following pre-training, DeepSeekMath-Base undergoes Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to create **DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B**.
- **SFT Data Curation:** A mathematical instruction-tuning dataset of 776K examples is constructed, covering diverse mathematical fields in English and Chinese. Solutions are provided in various formats: Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Program-of-Thought (PoT), and tool-integrated reasoning. Sources include annotated GSM8K and MATH problems, MathInstruct, and Lila-OOD.
- **Training Settings:** The model is trained for 500 steps with a batch size of 256 and a constant learning rate of 5e-5.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning (DeepSeekMath-RL 7B)

The final stage employs a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, to further refine the model's mathematical reasoning.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** GRPO is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) designed for efficiency. Unlike PPO, which requires a separate critic (value) model to estimate baselines for advantage calculation, GRPO eliminates the critic model. Instead, it estimates the baseline for advantage from the *average reward of multiple sampled outputs (a group) generated for the same question*. This significantly reduces memory and computational burden. GRPO also applies a direct KL divergence regularization term (between the current policy and a reference policy) to the loss function, rather than incorporating it into the reward.
- **Outcome Supervision vs. Process Supervision:**
 - **Outcome Supervision (OS):** Rewards are assigned only at the end of a complete output, based on its correctness. All tokens in the output receive the same normalized reward.
 - **Process Supervision (PS):** Rewards are assigned at the end of *each reasoning step* within an output, providing finer-grained feedback. The advantage for each token is calculated as the sum of normalized rewards from subsequent steps.
- **Iterative RL:** The training process is iterative, where the reward model is continuously retrained with new samples from the policy model (using a replay mechanism), and the policy model is then updated with the refined reward model and a new reference model.
- **DeepSeekMath-RL 7B Training:** GRPO is applied to DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B, using a subset of English instruction-tuning data (GSM8K and MATH, ~144K questions) in chain-of-thought format. The initial reward model is trained based on DeepSeekMath-Base 7B. For each question, 64 outputs are sampled.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DeepSeekMath demonstrates exceptional performance across various mathematical benchmarks, alongside key insights derived from its extensive training.

#### 4.1. DeepSeekMath Performance Overview

- **DeepSeekMath-Base 7B:**
 - **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** Significantly outperforms all open-source base models (e.g., Mistral 7B, Llemma 34B) on English and Chinese mathematical benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, CMATH, etc.). Notably, it surpasses Minerva 540B (a closed-source model 77 times larger) on the competition-level MATH dataset (36.2% vs. 33.6%), demonstrating that parameter count is not the sole determinant of mathematical reasoning ability.
 - **Tool Use:** Achieves state-of-the-art performance in program-aided mathematical reasoning on GSM8K and MATH (e.g., 66.9% on GSM8K+Python), outperforming Llemma 34B.
 - **Formal Mathematics:** Shows strong few-shot autoformalization performance on miniF2F (Isabelle), indicating robust ability in formal theorem proving.
 - **General Capabilities:** Improves performance on MMLU (language understanding) and BBH (reasoning) benchmarks over its precursor, DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5, confirming the positive impact of math training on general reasoning. It also maintains strong coding performance on HumanEval and MBPP.

- **DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B:**
 - **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** Outperforms all comparable open-source instruction-tuned models and most proprietary models (e.g., Inflection-2, Gemini Pro) on the MATH dataset (46.8%). It is competitive with larger Chinese proprietary models like GLM-4 and Baichuan-3.
 - **Tool-Integrated Reasoning:** Achieves nearly 60% accuracy on MATH with tool use (57.4%), surpassing all existing open-source models and being competitive with DeepSeek-LLM-Chat 67B (10x larger).

- **DeepSeekMath-RL 7B:**
 - **Breakthrough MATH Performance:** Achieves an impressive 51.7% Top1 accuracy on the competition-level MATH benchmark without external toolkits or voting, closely approaching the performance levels of Gemini-Ultra (53.2%) and GPT-4 (52.9%). With self-consistency over 64 samples, it reaches 60.9% on MATH.
 - **Significant Improvement:** Despite being trained only on a subset of chain-of-thought data for GSM8K and MATH, DeepSeekMath-RL 7B shows substantial improvements over DeepSeekMath-Instruct 7B on both in-domain (e.g., GSM8K: 82.9%→88.2%, MATH: 46.8%→51.7%) and out-of-domain mathematical tasks (e.g., CMATH: 84.6%→88.8%), highlighting the effectiveness of GRPO.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Lessons from Pre-training

- **Code Training Benefits Mathematical Reasoning:** The research provides strong evidence that code training significantly enhances LLMs' ability to solve mathematical problems, both with and without tool use. Initiating with a code-trained model (DeepSeek-Coder) and including code tokens in continual pre-training proved beneficial. Mixing code and math tokens in one-stage training also showed synergistic effects, particularly for program-aided reasoning.
- **ArXiv Papers Seem Ineffective:** Counter-intuitively, experiments showed that pre-training solely on arXiv papers (even large, cleaned corpora like MathPile or ArXiv-RedPajama) yielded no notable improvements or even led to deterioration across various mathematical benchmarks in their specific setup. The authors caution that this finding has limitations, as it doesn't account for all potential tasks (e.g., informalization) or the effects at larger model scales.

#### 4.3. Insights from Reinforcement Learning

- **Unified Paradigm for RL Methods:** The authors propose a unified paradigm to analyze various fine-tuning methods (SFT, RFT, DPO, PPO, GRPO) based on three key components: Data Source, Reward Function, and Gradient Coefficient. All methods are conceptualized as direct or simplified RL techniques.
- **Online vs. Offline Sampling:** Online sampling (training data generated by the real-time policy model) significantly outperforms offline sampling (data generated by the initial SFT model) in the later stages of training, demonstrating the importance of continuous exploration.
- **Effectiveness of Gradient Coefficients:** GRPO's approach of adjusting gradient coefficients based on reward values (both positive reinforcement and negative penalization) is more effective than methods like Online RFT, which lack this differential treatment. Process supervision (fine-grained, step-aware rewards) further surpasses outcome supervision, indicating the value of detailed feedback.
- **Iterative RL Benefits:** Iterative updates of the reward model and policy model lead to significant performance improvements, especially in the first iteration.
- **Why RL Works (Robustness vs. Fundamental Capability):** Analysis suggests that RL primarily enhances the model's overall performance by making the output distribution more robust, leading to better Maj@K (majority vote accuracy) but not necessarily boosting Pass@K (single-sample accuracy). This implies RL improves the consistency of generating correct solutions rather than a fundamental increase in raw reasoning ability from a single attempt. This aligns with observations in other works that RL helps to correct "misalignment problems" in reasoning outputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepSeekMath represents a significant leap forward for open-source LLMs in the domain of mathematical reasoning, offering several crucial contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Setting a New Open-Source Benchmark:** By achieving 51.7% Top1 accuracy on the MATH benchmark with a 7B model, DeepSeekMath sets a new, highly competitive bar for open-source models, narrowing the gap with proprietary giants. This empowers a wider community to build upon and further advance mathematical AI.
2. **Highlighting Data-Centric AI:** The meticulous and iterative data selection pipeline for the DeepSeekMath Corpus underscores the critical role of high-quality, domain-specific pre-training data. Its large scale (120B tokens, significantly larger than previous math corpora) and multilingual nature are crucial for its success, offering valuable lessons for future data curation efforts in other domains.
3. **Introducing an Efficient RL Algorithm:** GRPO is a novel and practical contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for LLMs. By removing the need for a separate critic model, it makes RL fine-tuning more memory-efficient and accessible, enabling its application in resource-constrained environments.
4. **Providing Key Training Insights:** The detailed ablation studies offer empirical answers to long-standing questions, such as the benefits of code training for mathematical reasoning. The finding regarding the apparent ineffectiveness of arXiv papers, while nuanced, challenges conventional wisdom and encourages further investigation into optimal data sources.
5. **Advancing the Understanding of RL for LLMs:** The unified paradigm for analyzing RL methods and the insights into how RL improves performance (by increasing output robustness rather than solely fundamental capability) provide valuable theoretical and practical guidance for developing more effective alignment strategies. This opens up new research directions for designing robust algorithms, better reward functions, and smarter data sampling strategies.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Specific Math Domains:** DeepSeekMath's capability in geometry and theorem-proving is still relatively weaker compared to closed models, potentially due to data selection bias.
- **Model Scale and Few-Shot Capability:** Restricted by its 7B parameter scale, DeepSeekMath performs worse than GPT-4 in few-shot settings, indicating that larger models still hold an advantage in generalizing from limited examples.
- **arXiv Data Conclusion:** The conclusion about arXiv papers' ineffectiveness is confined to their specific experimental setup and benchmarks, warranting further study in different contexts (e.g., informalization, larger models, mixed data).

Future work will focus on improving the data selection pipeline for even higher quality and broader coverage, and exploring the potential directions for more effective reinforcement learning outlined in their unified paradigm, including advanced sampling techniques, robust algorithms against noisy reward signals (weak-to-strong alignment), and developing better generalized, uncertain-aware, and fine-grained process reward models.
