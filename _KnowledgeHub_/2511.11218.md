# Humanoid Whole-Body Badminton via Multi-Stage Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Beijing Phybot Technology Co., Ltd developed a unified whole-body reinforcement learning policy enabling a humanoid robot to play badminton autonomously. This system, trained with a multi-stage curriculum, achieves sustained rallies in simulation and high-speed returns on real hardware, demonstrating robust dynamic interaction with fast-moving objects.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in dynamic, real-world interactions with fast-moving objects, particularly in tasks requiring precise timing and coordination like badminton.
- Badminton introduces unique complexities such as uncertain aerodynamics, limited decision time, and large swing amplitudes that demand robust whole-body balance recovery.
- Existing robotic racket sport systems often rely on modular or hierarchical approaches, which limit the joint optimization of lower-body footwork and upper-body striking motions for humanoids.

## Method

- A multi-stage reinforcement learning (RL) training pipeline, utilizing ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== in IsaacGym, was developed for a 21-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) humanoid robot.
- A three-stage curriculum (Footwork Acquisition, Precision-Guided Swing Generation, Task-Focused Refinement) progressively trains the whole-body policy by adjusting reward terms and weights.
- Two control variants were explored: a target-known policy relying on an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) for shuttlecock trajectory prediction and a prediction-free policy that observes short-horizon shuttle positions.

## Results

- Two simulated humanoids running the target-known policy successfully sustained a rally of 21 consecutive returns, demonstrating robust performance over extended exchanges.
- The EKF-based target-known policy on real hardware achieved a **91.3%** success rate (42 out of 46 valid returns) for machine-fed shuttlecocks, producing outgoing shuttle speeds up to **19.1** m/s (mean **11.1** m/s).
- The prediction-free policy achieved a **71.7%** success rate on hardware, with outgoing shuttle speeds up to **18.1** m/s (mean **8.2** m/s), indicating robustness to aerodynamic variations despite a modest performance drop compared to the target-known policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The multi-stage RL curriculum effectively enables the humanoid to learn complex, coordinated whole-body skills for dynamic tasks like badminton without requiring motion priors or expert demonstrations.
> - The learned policy demonstrates emergent adaptive coordination patterns, including simultaneous stepping and backswings for distant targets, tiptoe reaching for higher interceptions, and active lower-limb push-off contributing to whole-body impulse generation during strikes.
> - A prediction-free policy, which infers hitting pose and timing implicitly from short histories of shuttlecock observations, is a viable control strategy with comparable performance to prediction-based methods, simplifying deployment.
