# Sample More to Think Less: Group Filtered Policy Optimization for Concise Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Microsoft Research developed Group Filtered Policy Optimization (GFPO), an RL fine-tuning method that addresses response length inflation in large language models by integrating rejection sampling into the training process. This approach enables models to generate substantially shorter and more concise reasoning chains while preserving or improving accuracy, leading to significant inference-time compute savings across various reasoning and coding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large language models fine-tuned with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) often suffer from "length inflation," producing excessively verbose responses that do not correlate with improved accuracy.
- This verbosity leads to increased inference-time compute costs, slower response times, and a higher carbon footprint, hindering practical deployment.
- Existing solutions like token-level loss normalization can inadvertently reinforce verbosity for long correct answers or have limited effectiveness in addressing the core issue.

## Method

- ==Group Filtered Policy Optimization (GFPO)== modifies GRPO by sampling a larger group of candidate responses during training and then filtering them based on desirable attributes like brevity or =="token efficiency"== (reward per token ratio).
- Only the top-k most desirable responses from the filtered group are used to compute policy gradients, effectively zeroing out gradients for rejected samples and implicitly guiding the policy towards conciseness.
- An ==adaptive variant of GFPO== dynamically adjusts the number of retained responses (k) based on real-time estimates of question difficulty, allowing for more exploration on harder problems and more aggressive shortening on easier ones.

## Results

- GFPO achieved significant Excess Length Reductions (ELR) across benchmarks, including **70.9%** on AIME 25 and **84.6%** on AIME 24 with Token Efficiency GFPO, while maintaining or improving pass@1 accuracy compared to the GRPO baseline.
- Adaptive Difficulty GFPO delivered superior ELR on **4 out of 5** benchmarks and improved accuracy on the hardest AIME 25 questions (**27%**), demonstrating effective resource allocation for diverse problem difficulties.
- GFPO variants generalized robustly to out-of-distribution tasks like LiveCodeBench, mitigating length inflation (up to **79.7%** ELR) and sometimes even improving accuracy (e.g., Shortest 8/24 GFPO at **59.2%** vs. **56.7%** GRPO).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strategically increasing compute during the training phase by sampling more responses directly translates to continuous and substantial reductions in inference-time compute, creating more efficient LLMs for deployment.
> - The ratio of retained responses (k/G) acts as a critical control knob to precisely balance the trade-off between response length reduction and reasoning accuracy.
> - GFPO primarily prunes verbosity in the "Solution" and "Verification" phases of reasoning, indicating it targets redundant steps and filler content without compromising core problem-solving logic.
