# Do LLM Modules Generalize? A Study on Motion Generation for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Autolab, Westlake University, UDEER.AI, and Zhejiang University conducted the first systematic study on transferring Large Language Model (LLM) modules to autonomous driving motion generation. The study identifies specific LLM components that are transferable or require adaptation, achieving competitive performance on the Waymo Sim Agents benchmark by improving safety, realism, and diversity in generated agent motions.

## Problem

- A systematic understanding of which Large Language Model (LLM) modules are truly transferable to autonomous driving (AD) motion generation, and why, remains largely unknown despite empirical attempts.
- Autonomous driving motion generation, unlike discrete language, deals with continuous trajectories, complex spatial topologies, and the need for physically plausible, safe, and diverse agent behaviors.
- Previous AD models often struggle with balancing realism, diversity, and safety, leading to issues like causal confusion and compounding errors in closed-loop simulations.

## Method

- A systematic, module-by-module investigation was conducted across five core LLM components: tokenizer design, positional embedding, pre-training paradigms, post-training strategies, and test-time computation.
- Proposed domain-specific adaptations including an agent-centric **Verlet-Agent** tokenizer, a **Global-DRoPE** positional embedding for enhanced spatial reasoning, and a **GRPO** (Group Relative Policy Optimization) post-training strategy for balanced safety and realism.
- Leveraged a ==Transformer==-based autoregressive motion generator and a lightweight GPU-based simulation environment for efficient evaluation and reinforcement learning.

## Results

- The proposed Verlet-Agent tokenizer significantly improved performance (lower **ADE**, **FDE**) over data-driven and scene-centric model-driven tokenizers due to consistent agent-centric encoding.
- Global-DRoPE positional embedding enhanced fine-grained spatial reasoning, reducing the **off-road rate** and achieving superior overall performance compared to vanilla 1D PEs or local DRoPE.
- The GRPO post-training strategy effectively balanced safety and human-likeness, achieving the best overall performance among post-training methods by reducing **collision/off-road rates** while maintaining realism, and the combined optimized model attained competitive results on the Waymo Sim Agents Test leaderboard.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct transfer of LLM modules is often insufficient, necessitating domain-specific adaptations to account for the continuous nature of motion and complex spatial relationships in autonomous driving.
> - Agent-centric tokenization and global-aware relative positional encodings are crucial for consistent and semantically rich motion representation, significantly outperforming scene-centric or standard 1D NLP-style approaches.
> - While LLM scaling laws hold for motion generation to some extent, performance is ultimately constrained by the diversity of real-world training data rather than just model capacity, and post-training with methods like GRPO is vital for balancing safety, realism, and diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Positioning within the Research Landscape

This paper strategically positions itself at the intersection of two rapidly advancing fields: Large Language Models (LLMs) and Autonomous Driving (AD), specifically focusing on motion generation.

**Convergence of LLMs and Autonomous Driving:**
The introduction highlights the "striking similarities" between LLMs and AD motion generation tasks. Both domains involve autoregressive sequence modeling, rely on token-based representations, and demand context-aware decision-making. These fundamental parallels have inspired a growing trend of transferring concepts, architectures, and methodologies from the highly successful LLM paradigm to AD. Examples cited include the use of Transformer-based models for trajectory prediction, traffic simulation, and ego-vehicle planning. This trend is a natural evolution given the success of Transformers in sequence-to-sequence tasks and their ability to capture complex dependencies over long contexts.

**The Need for Systematic Transferability Analysis:**
While previous works have empirically transferred *individual* LLM modules to AD applications (e.g., tokenizers, positional embeddings, autoregressive training schemes), the authors identify a critical gap: a "systematic understanding of which LLM modules are truly transferable" and why others fail or require adaptation. This paper aims to fill this void by providing a comprehensive, system-level investigation rather than focusing on isolated components. It acknowledges that while surface-level similarities exist, "subtle differences" beneath these similarities necessitate careful analysis and adaptation. The use of the Waymo Sim Agents benchmark, which provides a comprehensive evaluation across prediction, simulation, and planning objectives, underscores the paper's commitment to a rigorous and holistic assessment of module transferability.

The research directly addresses the question of *how* and *which* LLM components can be effectively leveraged for autonomous driving, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to a structured comparative study. This systematic approach is crucial for accelerating the development of robust and generalizable AD systems by providing clear guidance on best practices for integrating LLM-inspired techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from the observation of structural similarities between large language models (LLMs) and autonomous driving (AD) motion generation tasks, coupled with a recognized gap in systematic understanding regarding the transferability of LLM components.

The **key motivation** is to provide a comprehensive, systematic investigation into which core LLM modules can be effectively transferred to the domain of motion generation for autonomous driving, and what adaptations are necessary for successful integration. The authors explicitly state: "However, despite promising early attempts, a systematic understanding of which LLM modules are truly transferable remains lacking." This identifies the critical research gap that the paper intends to address.

From this motivation, the **key objectives** are:
1. **Comprehensive Evaluation:** To conduct an in-depth evaluation of five fundamental LLM modules—tokenizer design, positional embedding, pre-training paradigms, post-training strategies, and test-time computation—within the specific context of motion generation for autonomous driving.
2. **Identify Transferable Techniques:** To pinpoint which LLM techniques can be directly or largely transferred to AD motion generation with significant performance improvements.
3. **Analyze Reasons for Failure and Adaptation Needs:** To understand why certain LLM modules or direct transfer attempts might fail in AD scenarios and to propose specific domain-specific adaptations to overcome these limitations. This includes recognizing the inherent differences between continuous motion and discrete language.
4. **Validate Effectiveness on Benchmark:** To demonstrate the effectiveness of the appropriately adapted LLM modules by achieving competitive results on a recognized, challenging benchmark like the Waymo Sim Agents task.
5. **Offer Valuable Insights:** To provide a foundational study that offers valuable insights for the broader autonomous driving community, informing future research and development in areas such as trajectory prediction, traffic simulation, and ego-vehicle planning.

In essence, the paper seeks to move beyond anecdotal module transfers and establish a principled understanding of how to best leverage the architectural and methodological strengths of LLMs to advance autonomous driving capabilities, particularly in generating safe, realistic, and diverse agent motions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper adopts a systematic, module-by-module approach to evaluate the transferability of LLM components to autonomous driving motion generation. The core framework is a Transformer-based autoregressive model, similar to GPT, which predicts next motion tokens.

#### 3.1. Tokenization Strategies

- **Problem:** Language is discrete, motion is continuous. Requires effective discretization of continuous trajectories into tokens.
- **Comparison:**
 - **Data-driven (e.g., SMART):** Clusters representative trajectories into prototypes, creating a motion vocabulary. Suffers from limited coverage and data dependence.
 - **Model-driven (e.g., MotionLM, Verlet Wrapper):** Discretizes motion in acceleration space, ensuring physical plausibility.
 - **Verlet-Scene:** Encodes trajectories in a global scene-centric coordinate system. Leads to inconsistency as identical agent actions map to different tokens due to coordinate variations.
 - **Verlet-Agent (Proposed Adaptation):** Reconstructs the motion vocabulary using an agent-centric encoding. Normalizes trajectory information based on the agent's current position and orientation before tokenization, enhancing consistency.
- **Implementation:** The Verlet Wrapper discretizes X and Y components separately into 128 bins, then computes differences between consecutive segments. These differences (13 discrete values each for X and Y) combine to form 169 motion tokens.
- **Baseline Model:** A GPT-based motion generator with a scene encoder (inspired by LTP) that compresses map and agent history into instance-level tokens. The motion generator uses an autoregressive approach to generate Verlet-formatted motion tokens. The decoder includes temporal self-attention, self-attention among agents, and cross-attention with map features and other agents.

#### 3.2. Positional Embedding Techniques

- **Problem:** NLP uses 1D PE for sequential dependencies. AD needs to capture complex spatial topologies and relative poses between order-agnostic agents and map elements.
- **Comparison:**
 - **No PE:** Baseline without explicit positional encoding (only learnable type and time-wise embeddings).
 - **Vanilla 1D PE:** Standard sinusoidal positional encodings applied to each token.
 - **DRoPE (Directional Relative Positional Embedding):** Borrowed from LLMs, encodes relative positions and orientations using 2D-RoPE and Directional-RoPE. Applies rotation-based transformations to input embeddings.
 - **Global-DRoPE (Proposed Adaptation):** Maintains agent and map encodings in the global coordinate system to preserve rich semantic information, while integrating relative positional cues via the DRoPE framework during cross-instance attention. This aims to overcome the issue of high semantic similarity among map tokens under different local coordinate frames.

#### 3.3. Pre-training and Scaling Law Analysis

- **Approach:** Trains the Transformer-based decoder using a next-token prediction paradigm, similar to LLMs. Generates sequences of motion tokens which are then composed into trajectories.
- **Data Augmentation:** Introduces a novel strategy for autoregressive models by using multiple generation anchors (T_anchor + k * t_interval) within the 8-second prediction horizon. This increases the effective training data by creating token sequences and contexts at different anchor points, simulating 800% of the original dataset.
- **Scaling Law Study:** Evaluates models with varying parameter sizes (0.8M to 11.6M) on different data scales (10%, 100%, 800% augmented data) to assess if scaling laws observed in LLMs hold for motion generation.
- **Base Architecture Selection:** Balances computational cost and diminishing performance gains to select an appropriate model size (5.3M parameters).

#### 3.4. Post-training Strategies

- **Motivation:** Imitation learning (pre-training) suffers from causal confusion and compounding errors, not always leading to safer closed-loop trajectories. Inspired by LLM post-training for alignment.
- **Comparison:**
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Identifies safety-critical collision scenarios from rollouts and fine-tunes the pre-trained model on these specific cases using teacher-forcing.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL) based methods:**
 - **REINFORCE:** Uses discounted returns for gradient ascent. Suffers from sparse and subtle rewards (collision/non-collision), leading to high variance.
 - **A2C (Advantage Actor-Critic):** Employs a critic network to estimate value function and reduce variance through advantage estimation.
 - **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) (Proposed Adaptation):** Inspired by group-based advantage comparisons in LLMs. Eliminates explicit value estimation by performing within-group normalization of returns. Adds a KL divergence term to regularize policy deviation from the pre-trained model (preserving human-likeness) and an entropy bonus for diversity.
- **Implementation:** Developed a lightweight GPU-based simulation environment for efficient collision checking and reward feedback. Parallel generation of multiple trajectories is used for RL. KL divergence is aggregated by mean over rollouts/agents and max over time to maintain sensitivity to sudden unsafe behaviors.

#### 3.5. Test-Time Computing Approaches

- **Motivation:** LLMs use test-time techniques (decoding strategies, re-ranking) to enhance adaptability and controllability without changing model parameters. Applicable to AD by post-selecting from sampled trajectories.
- **Comparison:**
 - **Baseline:** Direct generation.
 - **MoreRollouts + Cluster:** Generates more rollouts in parallel, then applies K-Medoids clustering on the final poses of interest agents to select diverse representatives.
 - **MoreRollouts + Search (Safety Filtering):** Generates more rollouts and evaluates them against cost functions (e.g., collision risk, comfort) to select the safest/most suitable option.
 - **MoreRollouts + Search + Cluster (Proposed Combination):** Combines safety filtering with clustering to achieve a balance of safety and diversity.
- **Implementation:** Leverages the GPT-based architecture for straightforward parallel rollouts. GPU-based environment from RL setup is reused for efficient post-search.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic investigation yields several key findings regarding the transferability and necessary adaptations of LLM modules to autonomous driving motion generation.

#### 4.1. Tokenization Effectiveness (Table 1)

- **Verlet-Agent outperforms others:** Model-based tokenizers (Verlet-Scene, Verlet-Agent) demonstrate higher token prediction accuracy and better overall performance (lower ADE, FDE) compared to data-driven approaches (SMART), especially with limited data. This is attributed to smaller vocabularies and less susceptibility to out-of-distribution issues.
- **Agent-centric encoding is crucial:** Verlet-Agent, by using an agent-centric coordinate system, significantly improves performance over Verlet-Scene (scene-centric). This is because it provides a more consistent mapping from tokens to physical actions, mitigating ambiguity and simplifying the model's learning process.

#### 4.2. Positional Embedding Performance (Table 2, Figure 2, Figure 11, Figure 12)

- **Vanilla 1D PE is detrimental:** Standard 1D positional embeddings, common in NLP, negatively impact autonomous driving performance, even compared to models without any PE. This highlights the unique spatial reasoning requirements of AD.
- **DRoPE enhances spatial reasoning:** DRoPE-based relative pose encoding, when imported from LLMs, significantly reduces the off-road rate, demonstrating enhanced fine-grained spatial reasoning.
- **Global-DRoPE achieves superior performance:** The proposed Global-DRoPE, which preserves global semantic context while incorporating relative positional cues, further improves performance (lower minADE, collision, and off-road rates). Qualitative analysis (Figure 11) shows that global encoding allows lane features to vary more smoothly and capture functional properties, increasing "word" diversity and model expressiveness compared to local coordinate DRoPE where map tokens can become semantically indistinguishable.

#### 4.3. Pre-training and Scaling Law Observations (Table 3, Figure 9)

- **Scaling laws hold within limits:** The autoregressive model exhibits scaling behavior, where increasing model size and data volume generally lead to lower validation loss and higher prediction accuracy. For smaller models, increasing data significantly helps. With sufficient augmented data, larger models show performance gains, consistent with LLM scaling laws.
- **Data diversity is a bottleneck:** However, improvements diminish with excessive parameter increases (e.g., from Big to Large model, 100% parameter increase yields only 2% accuracy gain). The Large model also shows signs of overfitting even on the augmented dataset, suggesting that performance is more constrained by the limited diversity and size of *real* training data than by model capacity. The augmented data, while increasing volume, might lack the true diversity of larger real datasets.
- **Selected Base Model:** A model with approximately 5.3M parameters (Big model) is chosen as the base architecture, balancing computational cost with performance gains.

#### 4.4. Post-training Efficacy (Table 4, Figure 3, Figure 14)

- **SFT is limited:** Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) shows very limited improvement due to the scarcity of safety-critical data and inherent limitations of imitation learning.
- **RL improves safety but impacts realism:** REINFORCE and A2C significantly reduce collision and off-road rates but lead to lower realism scores because they tend to optimize policies conservatively, deviating from human-like behavior.
- **GRPO strikes a balance:** The proposed GRPO method achieves the best overall performance, enabling safer motion generation (reduced collision/off-road rates) without significantly compromising human-likeness (highest realism score). This is due to its group-based advantage comparison, KL divergence regularization, and entropy bonus, which encourage a better balance between safety and diversity.

#### 4.5. Test-Time Computing Outcomes (Table 5, Figure 15)

- **Post-search enhances safety:** Applying test-time search (safety filtering) significantly reduces the collision rate of generated trajectories.
- **Search + Cluster balances safety and diversity:** Combining post-search with clustering yields the highest weighted performance score, offering a balance between safety and diverse plausible outputs.
- **Runtime overhead:** These advanced test-time computing strategies significantly increase computational runtime, indicating a trade-off between performance and efficiency. "MoreRollouts + Search" is highlighted as a more favorable choice for balancing effectiveness and efficiency.

#### 4.6. Overall Benchmark Performance (Table 6)

- The optimized model, combining Verlet-Agent (tokenizer), Global-DRoPE (positional embedding), the Big Model (pre-training), GRPO (post-training), and MoreRollouts-Search-Cluster (test-time computing), achieves competitive performance on the Waymo Sim Agents Test leaderboard. While not the absolute top, its "Combination of Our Ablated Modules" score (Realism 0.778) is very strong, surpassing several established SOTA methods like GUMP, Behavior GPT, SMART, KiGRAS, and DRoPE-Traj, and approaching the top unpublished methods UniMM and Catk. This validates the effectiveness of the adapted LLM modules.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

#### 5.1. Contributions and Impact

This work makes several significant contributions to the fields of autonomous driving and large language models:
- **First Systematic Investigation:** It is the first comprehensive and systematic study into the transferability of *multiple* LLM modules to motion generation in autonomous driving. This moves beyond isolated empirical transfers to a holistic analysis, providing much-needed clarity on the potential and pitfalls of this inter-domain transfer.
- **Identified Key Transferability Factors and Adaptations:** The paper meticulously identifies which LLM techniques are effectively transferable, analyzes reasons for the failure of others, and proposes specific, domain-aware adaptations (e.g., Verlet-Agent tokenizer, Global-DRoPE, GRPO, combined test-time strategies). These adaptations demonstrate that direct transfer is often insufficient, and careful consideration of domain specificities is required.
- **Empirical Validation on Robust Benchmark:** The effectiveness of the proposed adapted modules is rigorously validated on the challenging Waymo Sim Agents benchmark, achieving competitive state-of-the-art results. This provides strong empirical evidence for the practical utility of the findings.
- **Valuable Insights for AD Community:** The findings offer crucial insights for researchers and developers in autonomous driving, guiding the design of future prediction, simulation, and planning systems. By elucidating the synergistic relationship between LLMs and AD, the paper paves the way for more intelligent and robust autonomous systems.

The potential impact is substantial. By demonstrating a principled approach to integrating LLM concepts, this research can accelerate the development of next-generation autonomous driving systems that are not only capable of generating diverse and realistic motions but also robustly safe and human-like. The framework and insights can inform future research directions in applying foundation models to complex, real-world robotic tasks.

#### 5.2. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions

The authors are transparent about the limitations of their study and outline promising avenues for future work:
- **Scope of LLM Mechanisms:** The study did not investigate all LLM-specific mechanisms, such as Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), which could offer further performance gains or efficiency improvements.
- **Scaling Behavior of Larger Models:** Due to limited data and computational resources, the scaling behavior of even larger models (beyond 11.6M parameters) could not be fully explored, leaving open questions about the ultimate limits of performance gains in motion generation.
- **Single-Modality Environment:** The current experiments are restricted to vector-based environments and do not incorporate multimodal sensor inputs (e.g., camera, LiDAR), which are critical for real-world autonomous driving. Future work should explore how these findings generalize to richer perception contexts.
- **Waymo Sim Agents Benchmark Limitations (2024 Scoring):** The paper critically discusses a limitation of the Sim Agents benchmark's 2024 scoring mechanism, particularly its emphasis on likelihood matching. This can penalize safer RL-based methods if ground-truth trajectories contain "false-positive" collisions due to observation noise, as safer generated trajectories would deviate from the (flawed) ground-truth distribution. This highlights a need for more robust evaluation metrics that prioritize safety alongside realism.
- **Handling of Other Agents:** The method's current implementation limits the number of interactive agents fed into the GPT due to observation noise and missing values in "other agent" data from Waymo. While the architecture (DRoPE, GPU memory efficiency) could support more agents, data quality issues restrict training and thus inference on a larger number of obstacles.
- **Failed Cases:** The authors openly present specific failure modes, including collisions near boundaries, uncontrolled rollouts for initially colliding agents (out-of-distribution), and rare instances of "repetition phenomena" (similar to LLM word repetition) leading to unsafe behaviors. These pinpoint areas for further research into robustness and causal reasoning.

Future work will aim to extend the approach to downstream planning tasks and investigate how environment models trained within this framework can facilitate higher-level autonomous driving functions. Addressing the limitations, particularly regarding data quality, multimodal inputs, and robustness to edge cases, will be critical for translating these foundational insights into fully deployable autonomous driving solutions.
