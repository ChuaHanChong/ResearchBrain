# A Survey of Continual Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper provides a comprehensive review of Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL), introducing a novel taxonomy that categorizes methods based on whether they primarily store and transfer knowledge related to policies, experiences, environment dynamics, or reward signals. It identifies the unique challenges of CRL, such as the stability-plasticity-scalability dilemma, and outlines future research directions and applications across various domains.

## Problem

- Traditional Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) agents suffer from "catastrophic forgetting," losing previously acquired knowledge when learning new tasks.
- DRL often requires extensive training data and computational resources, limiting its efficiency and generalization in dynamic, real-world, non-stationary environments.
- Agents struggle to continuously learn and adapt to new tasks while retaining knowledge from prior experiences, necessitating relearning from scratch for each new task.

## Method

- ==Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL)== aims to enable agents to learn new tasks sequentially while retaining knowledge and leveraging prior experiences more efficiently.
- This survey proposes a novel ==taxonomy== that classifies CRL methods based on what knowledge is stored and transferred (==policy==, ==experience==, ==dynamics==, ==reward==) to provide a structured understanding of the field.
- The paper provides a comprehensive review of existing CRL methods within this new framework, standardizes ==scenario settings== and ==evaluation metrics==, and outlines open challenges.

## Results

- The survey classified existing CRL methods into four main categories based on knowledge management: policy-focused (e.g., reuse, decomposition, merging), experience-focused (e.g., direct, generative replay), dynamic-focused (e.g., direct, indirect modeling), and reward-focused (e.g., shaping, intrinsic rewards).
- Key challenges and future research directions were identified, including task-free CRL, the need for improved evaluation and benchmarks, interpretable knowledge, integration with large-scale pre-trained models (including LLMs), and application to embodied agents.
- Common application areas for CRL were summarized, spanning robotics, game playing, natural language processing, medical imaging, finance, and autonomous driving.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) extends traditional Continual Learning by explicitly addressing a "triangular balance" among plasticity (learning new tasks), stability (retaining old knowledge), and scalability (resource efficiency for many tasks).
> - A novel taxonomy categorizes CRL methods based on the type of knowledge they manage for transfer and retention: policy-focused, experience-focused, dynamic-focused, and reward-focused.
> - Standardized scenario settings (Lifelong Adaptation, Non-Stationarity Learning, Task Incremental Learning, Task-Agnostic Learning) and evaluation metrics (average performance, forgetting, transfer, efficiency) are essential for consistent assessment of CRL systems.
