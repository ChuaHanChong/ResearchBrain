# Device-Conditioned Neural Architecture Search for Efficient Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Device-Conditioned Quantization-For-All (DC-QFA), a unified framework for efficiently deploying transformer-based visuomotor policies on diverse robotic hardware. The approach achieves a 2-3x speedup and 2-4x reduction in model size or runtime memory while preserving high task success rates by incorporating real-device constraints and mitigating error accumulation in long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Advanced transformer-based visuomotor policies are computationally and memory intensive, making their deployment challenging on resource-constrained robotic hardware like embedded GPUs or NPUs.
- Existing efficiency methods are often device- or model-specific, requiring extensive and costly retraining or fine-tuning for each new hardware platform, limiting adaptability across heterogeneous systems.
- Prior quantization and 'Once-for-All' frameworks typically do not account for the unique demands of embodied AI, specifically heterogeneous robotic hardware and the critical issue of error accumulation in long-horizon sequential decision-making tasks under low-precision inference.

## Method

- A Device-Conditioned Quantization-For-All (DC-QFA) framework is proposed, training a single quantization-aware supernet that supports a wide range of architectural and precision configurations to amortize deployment effort.
- Real-device latency and memory constraints, obtained from pre-profiled lookup tables, are explicitly integrated into the supernet's training objective through hardware-aware regularization, guiding the optimization toward deployable subnetworks.
- Multi-step On-Policy Distillation (OPD) is introduced to mitigate quantization-induced error accumulation, enhancing the stability and consistency of policies for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks.

## Results

- DC-QFA achieved significant efficiency gains, demonstrating a 2-**3x** speedup in inference latency and a 2-**4x** reduction in model size/runtime memory on Jetson Orin NX, consumer-grade GPUs, and cloud platforms.
- The framework maintained high task success rates across various benchmarks and policies (OpenVLA-OFT, DiffusionPolicy-T, MDT-V), with aggressive W4A4 quantization often performing comparably to FP16 baselines and outperforming other quantization methods.
- Real-world experiments on an Inovo robotic platform confirmed robust deployment in contact-rich manipulation tasks, showcasing DC-QFA's ability to preserve performance on edge hardware even under aggressive 4-bit quantization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a single supernet with device-conditioned objectives and hardware-aware regularization allows for efficient, lightweight specialization of policies for various heterogeneous robotic platforms without per-device retraining.
> - Explicitly modeling and penalizing real-device latency and memory usage during supernet training enables the discovery of subnetworks that are simultaneously high-performing and adhere to specific hardware resource budgets.
> - On-policy distillation, applied over progressively longer horizons, effectively addresses the problem of error compounding in low-precision, closed-loop control, which is crucial for stable long-duration robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with the development of visuomotor policies, particularly those based on transformer architectures. These models, such as Diffusion Policy, MDT, and OpenVLA, enable complex multimodal reasoning and long-horizon planning. However, their computational and memory demands present significant challenges for real-world deployment on resource-constrained hardware commonly found in robotic systems, such as embedded GPUs or NPUs.

Existing research has explored various model compression techniques to address these deployment challenges. These include methods like pruning, knowledge distillation, and quantization. In the robotics domain, approaches such as LightDP and SaliencyQIL have been developed to compress policies for specific tasks. Quantization, in particular, is a widely adopted strategy, with methods categorized into post-training quantization (PTQ), like SmoothQuant and AWQ, and quantization-aware training (QAT), such as HAQ, which integrates hardware models for precision assignment. Beyond model compression, system-level strategies like SmolVLA and Real-Time Chunking (RTC) focus on improving real-time responsiveness through execution scheduling.

A related paradigm, Once-for-All (OFA) frameworks, aims to train a single supernet that can support multiple architectural configurations, allowing for specialized subnetworks to be derived without extensive retraining. Examples include the original OFA, One-QAT (which combines QAT with Neural Architecture Search), and more recent extensions like BatchQuant, QuantNAS, and LLM-QFA, which incorporate quantizer sharing and search-aware quantization.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these fields. While existing model compression and OFA methods improve efficiency, they often require per-device retraining or fine-tuning, making cross-device adaptation time-consuming and suboptimal for heterogeneous hardware. Furthermore, many OFA/QFA frameworks are primarily developed for static vision or language models and do not explicitly consider the unique deployment requirements of embodied policies, which involve real-time interaction, diverse hardware constraints, and the stability challenges of low-precision inference in long-horizon sequential decision-making. This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a unified, device-conditioned framework that amortizes deployment effort, integrates real-device constraints into training, and specifically mitigates error accumulation in long-horizon robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the growing computational demands of advanced visuomotor policies and the limited resources of robotic deployment hardware. Transformer-based policies, while capable of sophisticated manipulation, typically incur high computational and memory costs. This creates a practical barrier for their integration into real-world robotic systems, which often operate under strict latency and memory constraints on edge devices.

Existing approaches to make these policies efficient for deployment have several limitations:
- **Lack of Generalizability:** Many methods are device- and model-specific, requiring distinct optimization for each new hardware platform or policy architecture.
- **High Adaptation Cost:** Adapting policies to different devices often involves time-consuming retraining or fine-tuning processes for each target, which is inefficient for heterogeneous hardware environments.
- **Suboptimal Trade-offs:** Optimizing a fixed compressed model for each target separately may lead to suboptimal accuracy-efficiency trade-offs across a range of hardware budgets.
- **Neglect of Embodied AI Specifics:** Prior Once-for-All (OFA) or Quantization-for-All (QFA) frameworks, primarily designed for vision or language models, do not explicitly account for heterogeneous robotic hardware or the critical issue of stability in long-horizon sequential decision-making tasks under low-precision inference. Quantization errors can accumulate over time, leading to unstable or degraded performance in closed-loop control.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a Unified Framework:** To propose a single, device-conditioned framework (DC-QFA) that can efficiently deploy transformer-based visuomotor policies across heterogeneous robotic hardware platforms.
2. **Amortize Deployment Effort:** To train a single quantization-aware supernet that supports a wide range of architectural and precision configurations, enabling the derivation of deployment-ready policies via a lightweight, once-for-all search, thereby eliminating the need for per-device retraining.
3. **Integrate Hardware Constraints:** To incorporate real-device latency and memory constraints directly into the supernet training process, guiding the optimization toward subnetworks that are both accurate and satisfy specific hardware resource budgets.
4. **Enhance Long-Horizon Stability:** To mitigate quantization-induced error accumulation during closed-loop execution by introducing a multi-step on-policy distillation strategy, thereby improving the consistency and stability of policies for long-horizon manipulation tasks, particularly under low precision.
5. **Achieve Efficiency Gains:** To demonstrate significant acceleration (2-3x speedup) and reduction in model size/runtime memory (2-4x) on various devices (edge, consumer-grade GPUs, cloud platforms) with minimal degradation in task success rate.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Device-Conditioned Quantization-For-All (DC-QFA), is a unified framework designed to facilitate efficient deployment of transformer-based visuomotor policies across heterogeneous robotic hardware. It operates in three main stages: Quantized Supernet Training, Device-aware Subnet Search, and Deployment.

**Problem Formulation:**
The core problem is formulated as finding an optimal subnetwork configuration $c$ for a target device $d$ that minimizes policy loss $L_{policy}(c)$ while satisfying device-specific latency ($C_{lat}(d, c) \le B_{lat}^d$) and memory ($C_{mem}(d, c) \le B_{mem}^d$) budgets. A configuration $c$ specifies architectural parameters (block selection $m_l$, MLP expansion ratio $r_l$, attention head ratio $h_l$) and quantization parameters (weight bit-width $b_W^l$, activation bit-width $b_A^l$) for each layer $l$. The objective is to train a single supernet from which robust, quantized subnetworks can be efficiently specialized.

**Stage I: Device-Conditioned Supernet Training:**
This stage involves training a quantization-aware supernet using demonstration data $D = \{(o_t, a_t)\}$.
- **Joint Sampling:** During training, a target device $d$ is randomly sampled from a set of candidate platforms, along with a subnetwork configuration $c$ from the supernet's search space.
- **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT):** The sampled subnet $\pi_{\Theta,c}$, inheriting weights from the shared supernet, is optimized using QAT to simulate quantization effects during training.
- **Training Objective:** A hardware-aware training objective is adopted, integrating policy loss with regularization terms for latency and memory:
 $L_{base} = E_{c \sim C,(o_t,a_t) \sim D} [L_{SQIL}(o_t, a_t; c) + \alpha R_{lat}(d, c) + \beta R_{mem}(d, c)]$, where $L_{SQIL}$ is the policy loss (e.g., from SQIL) and $\alpha, \beta$ are regularization coefficients.
- **Hardware-aware Regularization:** Latency and memory regularization terms ($R_{lat}$, $R_{mem}$) are introduced using a softplus function that penalizes exceeding device budgets. These costs, $C_{lat}(d, c)$ and $C_{mem}(d, c)$, are estimated using lookup tables (LUTs) pre-profiled on real devices. The LUTs contain block-level measurements, allowing the aggregation of costs for any given subnet configuration.
- **Device Budget Sampling:** By sampling different device budgets during training, the supernet learns to perform well across diverse latency-memory trade-offs, enabling subnetworks to become specialized for various resource constraints.

**Multi-Step On-Policy Distillation (OPD):**
To address error accumulation in long-horizon tasks due to aggressive low-bit inference, OPD is incorporated into the training.
- **Teacher-Student Setup:** A full-precision teacher policy $\pi_T$ (derived from the largest supernet configuration) supervises a quantized student policy $\pi_{\Theta,c}$.
- **On-Policy Rollouts:** The student policy is rolled out for $K$ steps to generate a trajectory $\tau$.
- **Distillation Loss:** At each step in the trajectory, the teacher provides supervision for the student's action distribution. The distillation loss $L_{OPD} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{t=1}^K w_t D(\pi_T(\cdot|o_t), \pi_{\Theta,c}(\cdot|o_t))$ is calculated, where $D$ is a distribution matching operator (e.g., Mean Squared Error for continuous actions).
- **Progressive Horizon:** The rollout horizon $K$ is gradually increased during training, shifting from short-horizon teacher alignment to longer student-driven trajectories.
- **Total Objective:** The final training objective combines the base training loss with the OPD loss: $L_{total} = L_{base} + \gamma L_{OPD}$.

**Stage II: Device-aware Subnet Search:**
After the supernet is trained, NSGA-II (Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II) is used to perform a lightweight, multi-objective search for optimal subnetworks for each target platform.
- **Search Space:** The search considers both architectural parameters (MLP ratio, attention head ratio, block selection) and mixed-precision bit-widths (INT4, INT8, FP4, FP8, FP16).
- **Hardware Guidance:** The search is guided by the pre-generated real-device latency and memory lookup tables, ensuring that selected subnetworks adhere to the target device's resource budgets while maximizing task performance.
- **Benefit:** This approach allows for efficient specialization without any per-device re-optimization or retraining.

**Stage III: Deployment:**
The selected optimal subnet is exported for the target hardware. This involves calibration and compilation for the specific backend (e.g., TensorRT for a Jetson Orin NX) to enable low-bit inference on the device.

**Evaluated Policy Backbones:**
The DC-QFA framework is evaluated on three representative transformer-based visuomotor policies: DiffusionPolicy-T, MDT-V, and OpenVLA-OFT, which represent different network designs and input modalities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive experimental validation across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic deployments, demonstrating the efficacy of DC-QFA.

#### 4.1. Experiments on Simulation Benchmarks

- **OpenVLA-OFT on LIBERO (Table 1):**
 - The FP16 OpenVLA-OFT model achieved an average success rate of 97.1%.
 - DC-QFA with FP8 quantization maintained 96.6% average success (a 0.7% drop), reducing model size by 2x. This was competitive with BatchQuant (96.1%).
 - Under W8A8 quantization, DC-QFA achieved 96.0% average success. This outperformed PTQ methods (SmoothQuant, AWQ, ~93%) and was comparable to QAT methods (SQIL 96.1%, BatchQuant 95.4%).
 - With aggressive W4A4 quantization (4x model size reduction, 3.8GB), DC-QFA achieved 95.9% average success, which was the highest among all quantized methods at this bit-width, surpassing SQIL (95.8%) and BatchQuant (95.4%), and significantly outperforming PTQ methods (~92.6-93.0%).
 - These results indicate that DC-QFA preserves the generalization capability of OpenVLA-OFT under substantial compression.

- **DiffusionPolicy-T on Push-T (Table 2):**
 - The FP16 baseline achieved a 0.772 success rate.
 - DC-QFA with FP8 quantization maintained a 0.770 success rate, nearly matching FP16 performance with a 2x model size reduction. BatchQuant achieved 0.759.
 - In the W8A8 setting, DC-QFA achieved 0.769, outperforming PTQ methods (~0.73) and being comparable to QAT baselines (SQIL 0.766, BatchQuant 0.757).
 - At W4A4 (4x compression), DC-QFA reached 0.770, outperforming all baselines at this quantization level and performing on par with the FP16 model.
 - This demonstrates DC-QFA's ability to preserve closed-loop behavior of DiffusionPolicy-T under aggressive quantization for compact, deployable policies.

- **Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MDT-V) on CALVIN and LIBERO (Table 3):**
 - The FP16 MDT-V baseline achieved 4.52 average rollout length on CALVIN D→D, 3.72 on ABC→D, and 76.5 average success on LIBERO.
 - DC-QFA (FP8) maintained similar performance (D→D 4.47, ABC→D 3.67, LIBERO Avg 76.4), remaining close to the FP16 baseline.
 - At W8A8, DC-QFA achieved D→D 4.48, ABC→D 3.64, and LIBERO Avg 76.0, outperforming PTQ methods (~72 average LIBERO success) and matching QAT methods (SQIL 75.7, BatchQuant 75.8).
 - For W4A4 (4x compression), DC-QFA achieved D→D 4.45, ABC→D 3.66, and LIBERO Avg 75.9, demonstrating strong performance close to the FP16 baseline despite significant compression.
 - The results show DC-QFA consistently preserves MDT-V's multimodal policy behavior.

- **Long-Horizon Stability Analysis with On-Policy Distillation (OPD) (Table 4):**
 - Evaluations on CALVIN with MDT-V demonstrated the effectiveness of OPD in improving long-horizon stability.
 - With FP8 quantization, DC-QFA + OPD increased average rollout length from 4.47 to 4.51 on D→D and from 3.67 to 3.72 on ABC→D, effectively recovering FP16 performance.
 - Similar gains were observed at W8A8 and W4A4, where OPD consistently improved rollout lengths.
 - This indicates that OPD effectively mitigates error accumulation, enhancing the stability of quantized policies in long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.2. Experiments on Real-world Setting

- **Inovo Robotic Platform with Jetson Orin NX (Table 5):**
 - Experiments on four contact-rich manipulation tasks (sweeping beans, egg picking, pepper insertion, carrot pick-and-place) demonstrated real-world robustness.
 - For OpenVLA-OFT, DC-QFA (W8A8) matched the FP16 baseline (e.g., 18/20 successes on sweeping beans), and DC-QFA (W4A4) achieved 15/20 successes, significantly outperforming AWQ (W4A4) which dropped to 10/20.
 - For DiffusionPolicy-T and MDT-V, DC-QFA (W4A4) showed substantial improvements over AWQ (W4A4) in tasks like pepper insertion and carrot pick-and-place, maintaining performance closer to the FP16 baselines.
 - These real-world results confirm that DC-QFA enables reliable deployment of large visuomotor policies on edge hardware, preserving manipulation performance even under aggressive 4-bit quantization in contact-rich tasks.

#### 4.3. Pareto Frontier and Mixed-Precision Analysis

- **Pareto Frontier (Figure 3):** Visualization of MDT-V W8A8 on CALVIN D→D showed that DC-QFA subnets formed a superior Pareto frontier in the parameter-performance plane. DC-QFA consistently identified more parameter-efficient subnets that achieved equal or higher average rollout length compared to existing PTQ/QAT baselines for similar model sizes.
- **Mixed-Precision Deployment (Figure 4):** DC-QFA demonstrated the capability to discover mixed-precision policies (layers with INT4 or INT8) that consistently achieved higher rollout performance across a range of average bit-widths compared to fixed-precision baselines (SQIL, BatchQuant).
- **Deployment Cost (Table 6):** Quantitative measurements on Jetson Orin NX showed significant efficiency gains:
 - FP16: 15.2GB memory, 644.74ms latency.
 - W8A8: 7.9GB memory (1.92x reduction), 297.84ms latency (2.16x speedup).
 - W4A4: 4GB memory (3.80x reduction), 217.38ms latency (2.97x speedup).
 - These figures highlight DC-QFA's ability to offer flexible trade-offs for varied resource constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Device-Conditioned Neural Architecture Search for Efficient Robotic Manipulation" addresses a fundamental challenge in integrating advanced AI with real-world robotics. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas.

### 6. Significance

1. **Bridging the Gap:** The research provides a method to bridge the gap between computationally intensive visuomotor policies and resource-constrained robotic hardware, enabling the practical deployment of sophisticated AI models on edge devices.
2. **Amortized Deployment Effort:** By formulating and implementing a Quantization-For-All (QFA) framework that is device-conditioned, the paper reduces the substantial per-device optimization effort typically required. This represents a move towards more generalizable and less time-consuming deployment across heterogeneous hardware.
3. **Hardware-Aware Optimization:** The explicit integration of real-device latency and memory constraints, via lookup tables and regularization during supernet training, ensures that the resulting policies are optimized not just for accuracy, but also for actual deployability under specific hardware budgets.
4. **Enhanced Long-Horizon Stability:** The introduction of multi-step on-policy distillation specifically addresses the critical challenge of error accumulation in long-horizon, closed-loop robotic tasks under low-precision inference. This contribution is particularly relevant for sequential decision-making in robotics, where small errors can compound over time.
5. **Broad Applicability:** The method's effectiveness is demonstrated across diverse and representative policy backbones (DiffusionPolicy-T, MDT-V, OpenVLA-OFT) and multiple manipulation benchmarks, indicating its generalizability. Real-world validation on a physical robot further substantiates its practical utility.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** The framework can significantly shorten the development and deployment cycles for robotic applications that rely on advanced visuomotor policies. By eliminating the need for extensive per-device retraining, new robotic systems or updated hardware platforms can adopt state-of-the-art AI capabilities more rapidly.
2. **Increased Accessibility of Advanced Robotics:** By making powerful transformer-based policies feasible on more affordable and smaller-form-factor edge hardware, DC-QFA could lower the barrier to entry for complex robotic manipulation, fostering broader adoption in industries like manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics.
3. **More Capable and Robust Autonomous Systems:** Robots equipped with DC-QFA policies can potentially operate more efficiently, with higher responsiveness and improved stability over long durations, leading to more reliable and effective autonomous systems in dynamic and contact-rich environments.
4. **Foundation for Future Embodied AI:** The methodology of integrating real-device constraints and ensuring long-horizon stability for quantized models provides a foundational approach for deploying future large-scale foundation models in robotics and other embodied AI domains.
5. **Energy Efficiency:** Reduced computational load directly translates to lower energy consumption, a critical factor for battery-powered mobile robots and for promoting sustainable AI practices in robotics.

In summary, this work provides a practical and effective solution for deploying high-capacity visuomotor policies on constrained robotic hardware, addressing key challenges in efficiency, generalization, and stability.
