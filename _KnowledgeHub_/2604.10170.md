# Device-Conditioned Neural Architecture Search for Efficient Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Device-Conditioned Quantization-For-All (DC-QFA), a unified framework for efficiently deploying transformer-based visuomotor policies on diverse robotic hardware. The approach achieves a 2-3x speedup and 2-4x reduction in model size or runtime memory while preserving high task success rates by incorporating real-device constraints and mitigating error accumulation in long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Advanced transformer-based visuomotor policies are computationally and memory intensive, making their deployment challenging on resource-constrained robotic hardware like embedded GPUs or NPUs.
- Existing efficiency methods are often device- or model-specific, requiring extensive and costly retraining or fine-tuning for each new hardware platform, limiting adaptability across heterogeneous systems.
- Prior quantization and 'Once-for-All' frameworks typically do not account for the unique demands of embodied AI, specifically heterogeneous robotic hardware and the critical issue of error accumulation in long-horizon sequential decision-making tasks under low-precision inference.

## Method

- A Device-Conditioned Quantization-For-All (DC-QFA) framework is proposed, training a single quantization-aware supernet that supports a wide range of architectural and precision configurations to amortize deployment effort.
- Real-device latency and memory constraints, obtained from pre-profiled lookup tables, are explicitly integrated into the supernet's training objective through hardware-aware regularization, guiding the optimization toward deployable subnetworks.
- Multi-step On-Policy Distillation (OPD) is introduced to mitigate quantization-induced error accumulation, enhancing the stability and consistency of policies for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks.

## Results

- DC-QFA achieved significant efficiency gains, demonstrating a 2-**3x** speedup in inference latency and a 2-**4x** reduction in model size/runtime memory on Jetson Orin NX, consumer-grade GPUs, and cloud platforms.
- The framework maintained high task success rates across various benchmarks and policies (OpenVLA-OFT, DiffusionPolicy-T, MDT-V), with aggressive W4A4 quantization often performing comparably to FP16 baselines and outperforming other quantization methods.
- Real-world experiments on an Inovo robotic platform confirmed robust deployment in contact-rich manipulation tasks, showcasing DC-QFA's ability to preserve performance on edge hardware even under aggressive 4-bit quantization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a single supernet with device-conditioned objectives and hardware-aware regularization allows for efficient, lightweight specialization of policies for various heterogeneous robotic platforms without per-device retraining.
> - Explicitly modeling and penalizing real-device latency and memory usage during supernet training enables the discovery of subnetworks that are simultaneously high-performing and adhere to specific hardware resource budgets.
> - On-policy distillation, applied over progressively longer horizons, effectively addresses the problem of error compounding in low-precision, closed-loop control, which is crucial for stable long-duration robotic tasks.
