# Co-VLA: Coordination-Aware Structured Action Modeling for Dual-Arm Vision-Language-Action Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Donghua University and Samsung R&D Institute China-Beijing developed Co-VLA, a framework that introduces explicit structural priors into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for dual-arm robot systems. This framework enhances the reliability and interpretability of bimanual manipulation by decomposing actions into shared coordination and arm-specific residuals, achieving up to 91% success in a simulated "Handover Block" task and 73% in real-world handovers.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models rely on implicit coordination, which often proves insufficient for tightly coupled, safety-critical, or precisely synchronized bimanual tasks.
- Monolithic action representations conflate common task-level intent with arm-specific execution details, hindering generalization and making coordination failures difficult to diagnose.
- Current systems lack mechanisms to explicitly interpret and adjust synchronization strength, execution asymmetry, or safety behaviors at deployment time.

## Method

- A ==Structured Action Expert== (SAE) replaces the monolithic action head, decomposing bimanual actions into shared coordination latents and arm-specific residual latents, guided by task-adaptive coordination losses.
- A Latent-Aware Controller (LAC) operates at deployment, interpreting the learned latent components to adaptively modulate execution stiffness and refine joint commands for improved smoothness and precision.
- A Collaborative Motion (Co-Motion) demonstration paradigm was explored to generate training data with higher densities of concurrent bimanual coordination, aiming to enrich the learning distribution.

## Results

- In simulation, Co-VLA achieved an **82%** average success rate on "Easy" RoboTwin tasks, improving "Handover Block" to **91%** compared to **64%** for the baseline π0 model.
- In real-world experiments, the full Co-VLA system with LAC enabled reached **73%** success for "Handover ID" and **67%** for "Lift Pot ID," consistently outperforming π0 across various in-distribution and out-of-distribution conditions.
- Latent space analysis demonstrated that the learned shared components positively correlated with inter-arm synchronization, while residual components correlated negatively, confirming the interpretability of the proposed decomposition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bimanual collaboration is best modeled as a structure over actions, rather than a single monolithic action, providing a more interpretable and robust inductive bias for VLA systems.
> - Explicitly separating shared coordination intent from arm-specific execution details, coupled with task-adaptive losses, improves learning of varied bimanual coordination regimes.
> - An adaptive, latent-aware controller operating at execution time can refine robot movements, addressing high-frequency noise while preserving critical micro-adjustments, without requiring specialized hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models for dual-arm systems. VLA models represent a recent advancement in robotics, enabling robots to interpret visual observations and natural language instructions to generate continuous actions. Prior research, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, Diffusion Policy, and RDT-1B, has demonstrated the capacity of large vision–language backbones to implicitly learn coordinated bimanual behaviors from end-to-end learning frameworks. These approaches typically project shared visual and language representations directly into monolithic action vectors for both robot arms.

However, the existing landscape reveals limitations when bimanual tasks become more tightly coupled or subject to critical execution constraints. Implicit coordination often proves insufficient to ensure reliable, interpretable, or deployment-stable behavior in scenarios demanding precise temporal synchronization, asymmetric role assignment, or strict safety protocols (e.g., collision avoidance, motion smoothness). Current VLA systems do not explicitly separate task-level coordination intent from residual execution details, placing the entire burden of resolving collaboration trade-offs on the learned model. This can lead to difficulties in interpreting and adjusting synchronization strength, execution asymmetry, and safety behaviors at deployment.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing an explicit structural prior for VLA models in bimanual manipulation. It builds upon the foundation of existing VLA frameworks (specifically retaining the $\pi_0$ backbone) but introduces architectural and control-level modifications to explicitly model coordination. By introducing a structured action expert and a latent-aware controller, the work bridges the gap between the flexibility of learning-based VLA policies and the need for analytically informed coordination biases, which were more characteristic of classical bimanual control methods (e.g., operational space formulations, virtual linkage models, impedance control). Furthermore, the exploration of a "Collaborative Motion" demonstration paradigm contributes to the ongoing research into data collection strategies for complex multi-robot tasks, acknowledging the limitations of sequential or loosely coupled demonstrations prevalent in methods like ALOHA and RoboTwin.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while VLA models have shown capabilities in bimanual tasks, their implicit coordination mechanisms are often inadequate for tightly coupled, safety-critical, or precisely synchronized bimanual manipulation. The authors contend that "collaboration in bimanual manipulation is not an action itself, but a structure over actions." Existing VLA systems that represent bimanual actions as a monolithic vector conflate a common task-level intent with arm-specific execution details, hindering generalization and making it difficult to diagnose collaboration failures.

The key objectives of this work are to:

1. **Introduce explicit structural priors into VLA models for bimanual manipulation:** This involves designing a Structured Action Expert (SAE) that replaces the monolithic action head of existing VLA backbones. The SAE is intended to decompose bimanual actions into a shared coordination latent and arm-specific residual latents, providing an inductive bias for learning coordinated behaviors. This decomposition aims to make latent representations more interpretable.
2. **Develop a modular coordination-aware loss design:** To complement the structural decomposition, the objective is to introduce task-adaptive coordination losses that enforce semantically meaningful separation between shared and residual components. These losses are designed to shape the latents according to task-specific bimanual coordination structures, addressing variations across different bimanual tasks (e.g., symmetric motion, asymmetric role assignment, tight temporal synchronization).
3. **Design a Latent-Aware Controller (LAC) for deployment-time execution refinement:** The goal is to interpret the learned coordination representations from the SAE to modulate synchronization strength, execution asymmetry, motion smoothness, and safety constraints in real time. This controller operates at the joint-command level and aims to improve deployment reliability without requiring specialized hardware or low-level impedance control, separating learning from execution shaping.
4. **Explore a Collaborative Motion (Co-Motion) demonstration paradigm:** This objective involves investigating a data collection strategy that increases the density of concurrent bimanual coordination samples in the training set. By restructuring task scheduling to enable parallel execution of non-conflicting subtasks, Co-Motion seeks to enrich the training distribution with richer demonstration coverage, thereby complementing the SAE's structural decomposition.

In summary, the research aims to move beyond implicit coordination in VLA models by introducing explicit structural modeling at both the action generation (SAE with coordination losses) and execution (LAC) levels, while also investigating advanced data collection methods (Co-Motion) to enhance learning of complex bimanual tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Co-VLA framework integrates three main components: a Structured Action Expert (SAE), a Latent-Aware Controller (LAC), and an exploratory Collaborative Motion (Co-Motion) demonstration paradigm. The system builds upon existing VLA frameworks, specifically a $\pi_0$ backbone, by modifying its action generation and execution stages.

#### 3.1. A. Structured Action Expert (SAE)

The SAE replaces the monolithic action head found in standard VLA models. Instead of directly mapping hidden states $h_t$ to a concatenated bimanual action $a_t = [a_{t,L}, a_{t,R}]$, the SAE decomposes action generation into shared and residual components.

1. **Shared–Residual Action Decomposition:**
 - From the hidden representation $h_t$, three latent vectors are computed: $z^s_t$ (shared coordination intent), $z^L_t$ (left arm execution-specific information), and $z^R_t$ (right arm execution-specific information). Each latent is generated via separate projection heads ($g_s, g_L, g_R$).
 - The shared latent $z^s_t$ produces common action components for each arm, $a^s_{t,L} = \phi^s_L(z^s_t)$ and $a^s_{t,R} = \phi^s_R(z^s_t)$, using separate projection heads.
 - The residual latents $z^L_t$ and $z^R_t$ produce arm-specific residual action components, $a^r_{t,L} = \phi^r_L(z^L_t)$ and $a^r_{t,R} = \phi^r_R(z^R_t)$.
 - The final joint-level commands for each arm are obtained by additive composition: $a_{t,L} = a^s_{t,L} + a^r_{t,L}$ and $a_{t,R} = a^s_{t,R} + a^r_{t,R}$. This maintains the standard joint-level action interface while introducing explicit structural separation.

2. **Task-Adaptive Coordination Losses:** To guide the learning of semantically meaningful shared and residual components, auxiliary losses are introduced and selected based on the task's coordination pattern:
 - **Sparse Residual Regularization ($L_{sparse}$):** An $\ell_1$ regularization on residual components ($E_t [\|a^r_{t,L}\|_1 + \|a^r_{t,R}\|_1]$) encourages shared-dominant behavior for symmetric tasks by biasing common motion to the shared latent.
 - **Shared Mean Velocity Consistency ($L_{shared}$):** This loss aligns the shared component with the average joint velocity ($E_t [\|a^s_{t,L} - \bar{u}_t\|_2^2 + \|a^s_{t,R} - \bar{u}_t\|_2^2]$ where $\bar{u}_t = \frac{1}{2}(u_{t,L} + u_{t,R})$). It reinforces the shared latent's role in encoding task-level coordination intent, particularly for asymmetric role assignments.
 - **Temporal Synchronization Loss ($L_{sync}$):** Defined as $1 - \text{corr}_{\text{pred}}$, where $\text{corr}_{\text{pred}}$ is the correlation between standardized temporal magnitudes of joint velocity changes for both arms. This loss encourages synchronous acceleration and deceleration, beneficial for temporally coupled motions.
 - The overall training objective is $L = L_{action} + \lambda L_{aux}$, where $L_{aux}$ is selected based on the task type, with $\lambda = 0.001$.

#### 3.2. B. Latent-Aware Controller (LAC)

The LAC operates at deployment time, interpreting the learned shared and residual latent components to refine joint commands before execution. It aims to address high-frequency jitter, overreaction to small residual signals, and inadequate protection of precision-critical motions.

1. **Energy-Based Micro–Macro Analysis:**
 - At each time step, the average magnitudes of shared ($E^s_t$) and residual ($E^r_t$) components are computed.
 - A micro-motion ratio $\rho_t = E^r_t / (E^s_t + \epsilon)$ quantifies the relative importance of residual adjustments.

2. **Residual Opposition and Collaboration Signal:**
 - The cosine similarity $\text{cos}_t$ between residual components ($a^r_{t,L}$ and $a^r_{t,R}$) is calculated.
 - An opposition score $\omega_t = -\text{cos}_t$ is derived. A high $\omega_t$ suggests opposing residual adjustments, indicative of coordinated behaviors (e.g., stabilizing, holding).

3. **Adaptive Stiffness Modulation:** Based on $\rho_t$ and $\omega_t$, an adaptive execution stiffness $\hat{\alpha}_t$ is computed around a base stiffness $\alpha_{base}$:
 - **Macro-dominant motion:** If $\rho_t < \tau_{\rho}$, stiffness increases ($\hat{\alpha}_t = \alpha_{base} + \Delta_{macro}$) for fast, direct execution.
 - **Precision-critical micro adjustment:** If $\rho_t \geq \tau_{\rho}$ and $\omega_t > \tau_{\omega}$, stiffness increases ($\hat{\alpha}_t = \alpha_{base} + \Delta_{prec}$) to preserve meaningful opposite-direction alignments.
 - **Noise suppression:** Otherwise, stiffness decreases ($\hat{\alpha}_t = \alpha_{base} - \Delta_{noise}$) to suppress uncoordinated residual energy.
 - The stiffness is bounded within $[\alpha_{min}, \alpha_{max}]$ and temporally smoothed using an EMA filter ($\alpha_t = (1 - \beta)\alpha_{t-1} + \beta\hat{\alpha}_t$).

4. **Joint-Level Action Refinement:** The raw joint command $q_t = [a_{t,L}; a_{t,R}]$ is refined using a first-order low-pass filter: $\tilde{q}_t = (1 - \alpha_t)\tilde{q}_{t-1} + \alpha_t q_t$. This process ensures smooth execution while adaptively preserving critical motion components. LAC operates without additional learning modules, interpreting the SAE's output structure.

#### 3.3. C. Co-Motion: Collaborative Motion Paradigm

Co-Motion is an exploratory data collection strategy aimed at generating demonstrations with a higher density of concurrent bimanual coordination. It modifies the RoboTwin 2.0 code-generation pipeline.

- **Staged Parallel Scheduling:** Tasks are broken into stages, with non-conflicting subtasks dispatched to both arms in parallel, reducing idle time.
- **Shared Reference Frames:** Both arms reference common spatial anchors (e.g., handover midpoints) to stabilize planning and demonstrations.
- **Look-Ahead Precomputation:** Future interaction targets are precomputed to eliminate stop-and-plan interruptions.
- **Safe Intermediate Targets:** Buffers with explicit clearance margins are used between stages, allowing graceful fallback to sequential execution if parallel dispatch is infeasible.
- Co-Motion is a complementary data strategy, not a prerequisite, to enrich the training distribution.

#### 3.4. D. Training Pipeline

A two-phase training strategy is employed:
1. **Phase 1 (Warm-up):** The pretrained VLA backbone is frozen, and only the new SAE layers (projections and routing modules) are trained for 1,000 steps with a peak learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$.
2. **Phase 2 (Full Fine-tuning):** All parameters are unfrozen, and training continues for 30,000 steps with a reduced peak learning rate of $2.5 \times 10^{-5}$, decaying to $2.5 \times 10^{-6}$. Both phases use a batch size of 32 distributed across 4 GPUs with FSDP.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulation (RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world scenarios, evaluating the performance of Co-VLA, its components, and the Co-Motion data paradigm.

#### 4.1. A. Simulation Evaluation on RoboTwin 2.0

1. **Co-VLA on RoboTwin Tasks:**
 - Co-VLA demonstrated consistent improvements or matched baseline performance compared to $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$ on bimanual manipulation tasks. The average success rate in the "Easy" setting increased from 76% ($\pi_0$) and 73% ($\pi_{0.5}$) to 82% (Co-VLA).
 - Significant gains were observed in tasks requiring tight inter-arm coordination, such as "Handover Block," where Co-VLA achieved 91% success compared to 64% for $\pi_0$ and 44% for $\pi_{0.5}$.
 - The results indicated that increased backbone capacity ($\pi_{0.5}$ over $\pi_0$) did not consistently resolve inter-arm coordination issues without an explicit structural inductive bias, supporting the need for Co-VLA's approach.

2. **Effect of Co-Motion Demonstrations:**
 - **Efficiency:** Co-Motion reduced demonstration generation time by 10–25% across four tested tasks (Handover Block, Scan Object, Place Bread Skillet, Put Object Cabinet) compared to sequential demonstrations. This indicated improved data collection throughput. Models trained on Co-Motion data also yielded faster task completion times during execution, suggesting a transfer of compact motion patterns.
 - **Learnability:** Despite efficiency gains, training with Co-Motion data proved more challenging. The average "Easy-setting" success rate dropped from 76% to 50% for $\pi_0$ and from 82% to 56% for Co-VLA. This suggested an efficiency-vs-learnability trade-off, where denser concurrent trajectories, while more efficient to collect and execute, introduced higher learning difficulty. However, Co-VLA still outperformed $\pi_0$ under Co-Motion training (56% vs. 50%), indicating that SAE's decomposition aids in absorbing complex coordination signals even in challenging data distributions.

#### 4.2. B. Real-World Evaluation

Experiments were conducted on an AgileX Cobot Magic dual-arm robot using 50 human teleoperated demonstrations, with evaluations under both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions (cluttered backgrounds, distractors, low-lighting).

- The full system (Co-VLA with LAC enabled) achieved the highest success rates in five out of six tested settings (ID and OOD across Handover, Pick Bottles, Lift Pot tasks).
- Significant gains were observed in coordination-intensive tasks: "Handover ID" improved from 63% ($\pi_0$) to 73% (Co-VLA with LAC), and "Lift Pot ID" improved from 43% to 67%.
- Comparing Co-VLA without LAC to $\pi_0$ showed task-dependent results: SAE alone improved "Lift Pot ID" (43% to 63%) but slightly decreased "Handover ID" (63% to 57%). This suggested that structured action decomposition alone might not uniformly lead to higher success without execution-time refinement.
- Enabling LAC consistently recovered and surpassed $\pi_0$ across all ID settings, indicating the combined benefit of structured representation and latent-aware control.
- Under OOD conditions, Co-VLA with LAC improved over $\pi_0$ on all three tasks (e.g., "Handover OOD" from 13% to 27%, "Lift Pot OOD" from 33% to 37%). An exception was "Pick Bottles OOD," where Co-VLA without LAC performed marginally better than with LAC (50% vs. 47%), potentially due to LAC's noise suppression filtering out beneficial residual adjustments under distribution shifts.

#### 4.3. C. In-Depth Analysis

1. **Latent–Behavior Alignment:** Analysis revealed a consistent positive correlation between the magnitude of shared action components ($E^s_t$) and inter-arm synchronization ($\text{Sync}_t$), and a negative correlation between residual energy ($E^r_t$) and synchronization. This indicated that the learned decomposition captured interpretable coordination structure, with the shared latent driving aligned motion and residuals encoding arm-specific deviations.
2. **Action Head Ablation and Structural Priors:** A controlled ablation study on auxiliary losses showed task-dependent performance gains.
 - For the role-asymmetric "Put Object Cabinet" task, $L_{shared}$ yielded the largest improvement (49% to 70%), indicating benefits from stabilizing common motion trends.
 - For the temporally coupled "Place Bread Skillet" task, $L_{sync}$ resulted in the highest performance (51% to 62%), emphasizing the importance of aligned motion timing.
 - For the symmetric "Lift" task, all losses maintained high performance, with $L_{sparse}$ showing a slight edge.
 - These results indicated that bimanual manipulation encompasses multiple coordination regimes, and modular structural biases enable targeted adaptation.
3. **Effect of LAC:** Comparing Co-VLA with LAC, without LAC, and with a standard Exponential Moving Average (EMA) filter on the real-world "Pick Bottles" task demonstrated LAC's role. While EMA produced the smoothest trajectories, it led to a notable drop in success (60% for EMA vs. 67% for LAC). This was attributed to EMA's uniform low-pass filtering over-suppressing precision-critical micro-adjustments required for tight bimanual coupling. LAC, by interpreting the semantic structure of SAE's decomposition, adaptively filters high-frequency noise while preserving critical residual adjustments, thus overcoming the trade-off inherent in naive smoothing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Co-VLA, a coordination-aware bimanual manipulation framework that introduces explicit structural priors into Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models. The primary significance of Co-VLA lies in its ability to enhance the reliability, interpretability, and robustness of dual-arm robotic systems for complex, tightly coupled tasks, moving beyond the limitations of purely implicit coordination learned by monolithic VLA models.

The potential impacts include:

- **Improved Performance in Complex Bimanual Tasks:** By decomposing coordination into shared and residual latents within the Structured Action Expert (SAE) and applying task-adaptive losses, Co-VLA demonstrates consistent performance gains, particularly in tasks requiring precise synchronization and role coupling. This could lead to more capable robotic systems for assembly, object handover, and collaborative manipulation in industrial or service robotics.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Diagnosability:** The explicit separation of shared coordination intent from arm-specific residual adjustments provides a more interpretable latent space. This structure could facilitate better understanding of learned behaviors, aid in diagnosing coordination failures, and potentially simplify the design of more targeted interventions or refinements.
- **Robust and Safe Execution:** The Latent-Aware Controller (LAC) operates at deployment time to interpret these learned coordination representations, adaptively modulating synchronization strength, execution asymmetry, motion smoothness, and safety constraints. This approach contributes to safer and more stable execution, particularly under real-world variations and out-of-distribution conditions, without requiring specialized force sensing or impedance control hardware.
- **Guidance for Data Collection Strategies:** The exploration of the Co-Motion demonstration paradigm highlights an efficiency-versus-learnability trade-off. While Co-Motion can generate denser, more coordination-rich demonstrations efficiently, current VLA architectures face increased difficulty in fully learning from such data. This insight guides future research into developing more effective VLA backbones capable of fully exploiting complex, concurrent demonstration data.
- **Modular and Compatible Framework:** Co-VLA is designed as an extension compatible with existing VLA backbones (demonstrated with $\pi_0$), indicating its potential for broader integration into other continuous-action paradigms. The modular design of SAE and LAC allows for targeted improvements without necessitating fundamental changes to the underlying large vision-language models.

Future work outlined by the authors, such as validating SAE and LAC on other continuous-action paradigms (e.g., Diffusion Policy), addressing the learnability challenges of Co-Motion data, and developing automatic task-level coordination descriptors for adaptive loss routing, indicates promising directions for scaling and generalizing the framework further. This research contributes to the development of more intelligent and capable robotic systems that can robustly perform complex bimanual tasks in diverse environments.
