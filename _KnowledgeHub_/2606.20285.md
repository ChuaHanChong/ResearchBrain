# Co-VLA: Coordination-Aware Structured Action Modeling for Dual-Arm Vision-Language-Action Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Donghua University and Samsung R&D Institute China-Beijing developed Co-VLA, a framework that introduces explicit structural priors into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for dual-arm robot systems. This framework enhances the reliability and interpretability of bimanual manipulation by decomposing actions into shared coordination and arm-specific residuals, achieving up to 91% success in a simulated "Handover Block" task and 73% in real-world handovers.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models rely on implicit coordination, which often proves insufficient for tightly coupled, safety-critical, or precisely synchronized bimanual tasks.
- Monolithic action representations conflate common task-level intent with arm-specific execution details, hindering generalization and making coordination failures difficult to diagnose.
- Current systems lack mechanisms to explicitly interpret and adjust synchronization strength, execution asymmetry, or safety behaviors at deployment time.

## Method

- A ==Structured Action Expert== (SAE) replaces the monolithic action head, decomposing bimanual actions into shared coordination latents and arm-specific residual latents, guided by task-adaptive coordination losses.
- A Latent-Aware Controller (LAC) operates at deployment, interpreting the learned latent components to adaptively modulate execution stiffness and refine joint commands for improved smoothness and precision.
- A Collaborative Motion (Co-Motion) demonstration paradigm was explored to generate training data with higher densities of concurrent bimanual coordination, aiming to enrich the learning distribution.

## Results

- In simulation, Co-VLA achieved an **82%** average success rate on "Easy" RoboTwin tasks, improving "Handover Block" to **91%** compared to **64%** for the baseline π0 model.
- In real-world experiments, the full Co-VLA system with LAC enabled reached **73%** success for "Handover ID" and **67%** for "Lift Pot ID," consistently outperforming π0 across various in-distribution and out-of-distribution conditions.
- Latent space analysis demonstrated that the learned shared components positively correlated with inter-arm synchronization, while residual components correlated negatively, confirming the interpretability of the proposed decomposition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bimanual collaboration is best modeled as a structure over actions, rather than a single monolithic action, providing a more interpretable and robust inductive bias for VLA systems.
> - Explicitly separating shared coordination intent from arm-specific execution details, coupled with task-adaptive losses, improves learning of varied bimanual coordination regimes.
> - An adaptive, latent-aware controller operating at execution time can refine robot movements, addressing high-frequency noise while preserving critical micro-adjustments, without requiring specialized hardware.
