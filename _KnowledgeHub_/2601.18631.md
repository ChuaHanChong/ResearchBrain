# AdaReasoner: Dynamic Tool Orchestration for Iterative Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> DAREASONER introduces a framework that enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to learn dynamic, iterative tool orchestration for complex visual reasoning tasks. The approach leverages high-quality trajectory data, a tailored reinforcement learning algorithm, and adaptive learning, allowing smaller MLLMs to achieve state-of-the-art performance, outperforming some larger proprietary models on specific benchmarks and demonstrating robust generalization to unseen tools and tasks.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs struggle with complex, multi-step visual reasoning requiring fine-grained perception and adaptive problem-solving strategies.
- Current tool-use approaches in MLLMs are rigid, lacking dynamic decision-making for tool selection, composition, and multi-turn orchestration.
- Tool-using policies often show poor generalization to unseen tools or novel task distributions due to overfitting specific tool APIs or training scenarios.

## Method

- A ==three-stage data curation pipeline== synthesizes high-fidelity, multi-turn reasoning trajectories, incorporating explicit reflection, backtracking, and tool failure cases for initial supervised fine-tuning.
- ==Multi-Turn Tool Group Relative Policy Optimization (Tool-GRPO)==, an extension of GRPO, refines the MLLM policy using a multi-component reward function optimized for long-horizon strategic planning in multi-turn tool-calling scenarios.
- An ==Adaptive Learning strategy== enhances generalization by applying token-level randomization to functional identifiers and semantic-level paraphrasing to tool descriptions, forcing models to infer functionality rather than memorize syntax.

## Results

- The DAREASONER framework consistently improved 7B base MLLMs by an average of **+38.66%** across tasks, enabling them to achieve competitive or superior performance compared to strong proprietary models, such as surpassing GPT-5 on VSP and Jigsaw (**96.60%** vs. **80.10%**).
- The model demonstrated adaptive tool usage, including zero-shot adoption of new tools (e.g., `ASTAR` boosting VSP navigation by **+17.5 points** with **94.53%** success rate) and dynamic modulation of tool-use frequency based on task relevance.
- Strong generalization was achieved for new tasks and tool definitions through Adaptive Learning, maintaining high tool engagement (e.g., **3.54** calls per sample on Jigsaw) and execution reliability (**98.50%** success) even when tool APIs were randomized or paraphrased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective tool orchestration fundamentally shifts the MLLM performance bottleneck from intrinsic model scale to the utility of external tools, allowing smaller models to achieve state-of-the-art results.
> - Learning tool use as a dynamic and generalizable reasoning skill, rather than a rigid, task-specific behavior, is crucial for developing truly adaptive and versatile AI agents.
> - Explicitly incorporating reflection, backtracking, and the handling of tool failures during data curation and training fosters robust, human-like iterative problem-solving strategies in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on AdaReasoner is situated at the intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), tool learning, visual reasoning, and reinforcement learning, an increasingly vital area in the pursuit of more capable and agentic AI systems. MLLMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding and generating content across various modalities, but they often struggle with fine-grained visual perception, multi-step reasoning, and long-horizon planning, frequently leading to hallucinations or sub-optimal outcomes.

A burgeoning paradigm to address these limitations involves augmenting MLLMs with external tools, mirroring how humans leverage instruments to extend their cognitive abilities. Early efforts in tool-augmented MLLMs, such as SFT- and prompt-based approaches (e.g., Ma et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2024), explored multi-tool usage but were largely characterized by rigid, pre-defined invocation patterns. More recent reinforcement learning (RL)-based methods (e.g., DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner) have shown promise in improving perceptual reasoning through strategies like crop-based search but typically remain constrained to single-tool trajectories or fixed interaction loops. This body of prior work leaves a critical gap: existing methods often lack the ability to adaptively plan and coordinate diverse tools in a flexible, task-aware manner, nor do they treat tool selection, timing, and composition as a core aspect of multimodal reasoning. Furthermore, their tool-use policies tend to be brittle, exhibiting limited generalization to unseen tools or novel task distributions.

AdaReasoner directly addresses this critical gap by proposing a framework that enables MLLMs to learn tool use as a *general reasoning skill* rather than a tool-specific or explicitly supervised behavior. It moves beyond rigid, single-tool paradigms by focusing on adaptive, multi-turn tool planning. The paper builds upon advancements in reinforcement learning for MLLMs, particularly the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 for LLMs, Zhou et al. for MLLMs), extending it to handle complex multi-turn tool-calling trajectories. By doing so, AdaReasoner aims to create more robust, generalizable, and agentic MLLMs capable of dynamic tool orchestration, thus pushing the boundaries of what open-source models can achieve in complex visual reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind AdaReasoner stems from the observation that humans effectively tackle complex problems by utilizing external tools, a valuable parallel for enhancing MLLMs. MLLMs often face limitations in fine-grained visual perception and multi-step reasoning, where precise intermediate verification and long-horizon planning are crucial. Offloading these computational burdens to specialized tools offers a promising path forward.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of AdaReasoner are:

1. **Develop Adaptive Tool Usage:** The central aim is to teach MLLMs not just *how* to use tools, but critically, *when* to invoke them, *which* tools to select from a diverse set, and *how to compose them* effectively over multiple steps. This emphasizes flexible and task-aware decision-making, moving beyond pre-defined, rigid invocation patterns.
2. **Achieve Generalization to Unseen Tools and Tasks:** A significant challenge for existing methods is their brittleness and limited ability to generalize. AdaReasoner seeks to enable models to infer tool utility from task context and intermediate outcomes, allowing them to effectively utilize tools they have never encountered during training and apply learned tool-planning skills to novel task distributions.
3. **Facilitate Long-Horizon, Multi-Step Tool Interactions:** The framework aims to enable models to reason over extended interaction sequences, making coherent tool-use decisions across multiple turns, including reflective self-correction and iterative refinement.
4. **Overcome Intrinsic MLLM Limitations:** By leveraging external, expert tools, AdaReasoner aims to compensate for MLLMs' inherent weaknesses in precise visual perception and complex calculations, thereby improving the reliability and accuracy of the entire reasoning pipeline.
5. **Elevate Performance of Smaller Models:** A core objective is to demonstrate that effective tool orchestration can shift the primary performance bottleneck from the intrinsic scale of the MLLM to the utility and quality of the tools it employs, thereby enabling smaller, open-source models to achieve state-of-the-art results competitive with or superior to much larger proprietary systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AdaReasoner introduces a comprehensive framework built upon three key innovations: a scalable data curation pipeline, a tailored reinforcement learning algorithm, and an adaptive learning mechanism.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Visual Tools

The framework formalizes tool-augmented multimodal reasoning as a sequential decision-making process. An MLLM, acting as a policy $\pi_\theta$, generates a reasoning trajectory by interacting with a predefined set of visual tools $T = \{t_1, \ldots, t_n\}$. A trajectory $\tau$ is a sequence of state-action-observation tuples $\{(s_0, a_0, o_0), \ldots, (s_T, a_T, o_T)\}$, where $s_t$ is the problem state, $a_t$ is a tool-calling action, and $o_t$ is the observation from tool execution. The diverse toolset includes functions for **perception** (e.g., POINT, OCR, DETECTBLACKAREA), **manipulation** (e.g., DRAW2DPATH, INSERTIMAGE, CROP), and **calculation** (e.g., ASTAR), integrating both lightweight offline tools and computationally intensive expert models.

#### 3.2. High-Quality Trajectory Data Curation (Tool Cold Start - TC)

To establish a robust foundation, AdaReasoner employs a scalable, three-stage data curation pipeline to generate high-fidelity, human-like reasoning trajectories for supervised fine-tuning (SFT), termed "Tool Cold Start" (TC).

1. **Abstract Trajectory Design:** For each task (e.g., Visual Spatial Planning (VSP), Jigsaw, GUIQA), an optimal problem-solving blueprint is manually designed. Crucially, these blueprints incorporate complex scenarios like:
 - **Reflection and Backtracking:** Trajectories include explicit self-correction steps, where the model must reflect on sub-optimal outcomes and revise its plan, fostering learning from intermediate failures.
 - **Explicit Tool Failure:** Cases where tools fail or return erroneous results are included, teaching the model to fall back on its intrinsic capabilities when external tools are unhelpful, preventing over-reliance.
2. **Tool Calling Supplements:** These abstract blueprints are grounded by programmatically executing tool calls to populate them with concrete, real-world inputs and outputs.
3. **CoT Data Generation:** A powerful LLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash) is then leveraged to generate corresponding Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning that connects each step, creating rich, tool-augmented trajectories that teach both *what* tools to call and *why/how* to reason between them.

#### 3.3. Multi-Turn Tool GRPO (TG)

The policy is further refined using a tailored reinforcement learning algorithm, "Tool-GRPO" (TG), optimized for long-horizon strategic planning. This extends the GRPO framework to effectively handle multi-turn tool-calling reasoning trajectories.

1. **Multi-turn Reward Accumulation:** The total reward, $R_{total}$, is formulated as $R_{total} = R_{format} \cdot (\lambda_{tool} \cdot R_{tool} + \lambda_{acc} \cdot R_{acc})$.
 - **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** A binary gate (0 or 1) that ensures correct formatting at every step. A single format error nullifies the entire trajectory's reward, enforcing strict adherence to the reasoning structure.
 - **Tool Reward ($R_{tool}$):** A fine-grained hierarchical score (0-4) averaged over all tool-calling turns. It evaluates: (I) Invocation Structure, (II) Tool Name Validity, (III) Parameter Name Correctness (partial credit), and (IV) Parameter Content Validity (partial credit).
 - **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):** A binary reward (0 or 4) granted only for the final turn, indicating correctness of the ultimate answer.
2. **Adaptive Reward for Encouraging Tool Use:** An asymmetric reward structure is designed: full reward for correct final predictions regardless of tool usage (encouraging concise reasoning), but partial credit for informative tool-based reasoning when predictions are incorrect (treating tools as a fallback mechanism under uncertainty).

#### 3.4. Adaptive Learning for Improved Generalization (ADL)

To bolster generalization to unseen tools and new tasks, an Adaptive Learning strategy is integrated throughout both TC and TG stages. This method prevents overfitting to fixed identifiers and encourages semantic understanding of tool functionalities:

1. **Token-Level Randomization for Identifiers:** All functional identifiers (tool names, argument names) are replaced with random alphanumeric strings (e.g., `GetWeather` becomes `Func_X7a2`). This strips away linguistic cues, forcing the model to infer functionality solely from descriptions and context.
2. **Semantic-Level Paraphrasing for Descriptions:** For tool and argument descriptions, Gemini 2.5 Flash is used to rephrase them, altering syntactic structure and lexical choices while strictly maintaining original functional meaning. This creates diverse but semantically equivalent tool definitions, enhancing robustness to variations in documentation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AdaReasoner demonstrates significant advancements in tool-augmented multimodal reasoning, achieving state-of-the-art performance and exhibiting robust adaptive and generalized behaviors.

#### 4.1. Visual Tools Bridge the Capability Gap

- **Consistent Performance Improvements:** The TC + TG training recipe consistently improves base model performance, with the 7B variant achieving an average gain of +38.66%. On tasks like VSP, it transforms performance from an under-optimized baseline (∼31.64%) to near-perfect execution (97.64%), significantly surpassing traditional SFT and Direct GRPO.
- **Outperforming Proprietary Models:** AdaReasoner enables the 7B model to achieve results competitive with, or superior to, strong proprietary models. For instance, on VSP and Jigsaw, it outperforms GPT-5 (96.60% vs. 80.10%).
- **Overcoming Scale-Based Limitations:** Tool augmentation redefines the performance ceiling. While baseline 3B and 7B models show disparate and low performance, their tool-augmented versions both achieve near-perfect accuracy (94.7% and 97.6% on VSP), suggesting that the bottleneck shifts from model scale to the quality of the tools employed.
- **Mechanism of Help:** Visual tools enhance perception (expert tools provide precise, structured observations), verification (manipulation tools allow externalization and checking of hypotheses), and planning (high-quality trajectories align planning behavior with task structure).

#### 4.2. AdaReasoner Exhibits Adaptive Tool Usage

AdaReasoner demonstrates a remarkable ability to adaptively select and regulate tool usage frequency, particularly highlighted in the VSP task:

- **Adaptive Adoption of New Tools (Inference Time):** When a powerful planning tool like A* (deliberately excluded during TC) is introduced solely at inference, AdaReasoner leverages it effectively, providing a significant performance boost (e.g., VSP navigation score from 44.83 to 62.33 for a model without reflection), with a high invocation success rate (94.53%).
- **Learning to Adopt and Master Beneficial Tools (via RL):** By incorporating A* during the TG stage, the model’s invocation frequency for ASTAR progressively increases for path navigation tasks, stabilizing at a high rate (>1.0 call per sample). This mastery translates to a dramatic VSP navigation score increase (to 96.33).
- **Learning to Discard Irrelevant Tools:** Critically, for tasks where A* is irrelevant (e.g., verification), the model learns to suppress its usage, with invocation frequency decaying towards zero. This adaptive pruning prevents performance degradation observed in zero-shot inference scenarios.
- **Learning to Modulate Tool-Use Frequency:** Beyond binary adoption/discarding, the model dynamically modulates the invocation frequency of continuously useful tools. For instance, the POINT tool is used significantly more for navigation (∼3.2 calls/sample) than for verification (∼1.0 call/sample).

#### 4.3. AdaReasoner Exhibits Generalized Tool Usage

The Adaptive Learning strategy (Randomized TC + Randomized TG) proves crucial for robust cross-tool and cross-task generalization:

- **Generalization to New Tasks:** Training with Randomized TC + Randomized TG enables tool-planning capabilities learned from one task (Jigsaw) to effectively transfer to unseen domains (VSP, WebQA). This boosts the VSP score from 28.09 to 78.91 and achieves strong overall generalization across tasks (75.81 vs. 46.50 for base).
- **Generalization to New Tool Definitions:** During evaluation, tool definitions are completely different from training, creating a strict zero-shot tool adaptation scenario. AdaReasoner consistently demonstrates the highest level of effective tool usage (e.g., 3.54 calls per sample (CPS) with 98.50% success on Jigsaw), indicating learning of transferable tool-planning principles rather than overfitting to specific APIs.
- **Robustness in General VQA:** On benchmarks like V* (real-world general VQA without explicit tool-calling supervision), the model still actively invokes tools (1.47 CPS) and achieves the highest accuracy (70.68), demonstrating broad effectiveness.
- **Qualitative Behaviors:** Beyond metrics, AdaReasoner-7B exhibits robust qualitative behaviors, including multi-turn tool planning, reflective correction of imperfect tool outputs, and synergistic tool composition, often outperforming strong baselines like GPT-5.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AdaReasoner framework represents a significant step forward in the development of capable and generalizable multimodal large language models, with several profound implications:

1. **Shifting the Performance Bottleneck:** The most impactful finding is that effective tool orchestration shifts the primary performance bottleneck from the intrinsic scale of the MLLM to the utility and quality of the external tools it wields. This paradigm suggests that achieving state-of-the-art results no longer solely depends on training ever-larger models but can be attained by empowering smaller, more efficient MLLMs with a robust and adaptive tool-using capability.
2. **Empowering Open-Source Models:** By enabling a 7B model to surpass strong proprietary systems like GPT-5 on challenging visual reasoning tasks (e.g., VSP and Jigsaw), AdaReasoner democratizes advanced AI capabilities. This has the potential to accelerate research and development in the open-source community, reducing the reliance on proprietary, resource-intensive models.
3. **Advancing Agentic MLLMs:** AdaReasoner pushes the frontier of agentic MLLMs by teaching models to learn tool use as a general reasoning skill. The ability to autonomously adopt beneficial tools, discard irrelevant ones, modulate usage frequency, and generalize to unseen tools and tasks are hallmarks of truly intelligent agents. This work moves MLLMs closer to becoming flexible, goal-directed problem solvers in complex visual environments.
4. **Robustness and Generalization:** The adaptive learning mechanism, through token-level randomization and semantic paraphrasing, significantly enhances the model's robustness against domain shifts in tool definitions and task contexts. This improved generalization is crucial for real-world deployment, where tool specifications and task requirements can vary.
5. **New Research Directions:** AdaReasoner’s success in integrating high-quality data curation, sophisticated reinforcement learning, and innovative generalization techniques provides a comprehensive blueprint for future research in tool-augmented AI. It encourages further exploration into designing even more powerful and diverse toolsets, refining adaptive learning strategies, and applying these agentic capabilities to a wider array of real-world multimodal problems.

In conclusion, AdaReasoner offers a compelling answer to the challenge of building truly intelligent multimodal agents, demonstrating that with proper training and strategic tool orchestration, MLLMs can autonomously curate effective problem-solving strategies and extend their reasoning capacity in a highly adaptive and generalizable manner.
