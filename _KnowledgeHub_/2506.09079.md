# VidBridge-R1: Bridging QA and Captioning for RL-based Video Understanding Models with Intermediate Proxy Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> VidBridge-R1 introduces a novel training framework that reconciles the paradigm conflict between video Question Answering and captioning tasks within Reinforcement Learning, enabling a single model to excel at both convergent reasoning and divergent generation. The model achieves leading performance on various video understanding and captioning benchmarks, demonstrating enhanced versatility through intermediate proxy tasks.

## Problem

- Developing versatile video understanding models capable of simultaneously excelling in both video Question Answering (QA) and video captioning using Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- A "paradigm conflict" between convergent QA and divergent captioning tasks leads to mutual performance degradation when optimized simultaneously via global reward signals in RL.
- Existing RL-enhanced video Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) typically specialize in either QA or captioning, lacking effective integration of both capabilities within a unified framework.

## Method

- Introduced a novel training framework centered around two intermediate ==proxy tasks==: DarkEventInfer for holistic understanding and MixVidQA for precise information localization.
- Employed the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm with diverse, task-specific, and implicit format reward functions, notably omitting KL divergence regularization.
- Used Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the backbone model and directly applied RL without an SFT cold-start phase, contrasting with conventional MLLM training pipelines.

## Results

- VidBridge-R1 achieved the highest overall score on Video-MME (64.3) and outperformed strongest baselines by an average of **1.9%** on various video reasoning benchmarks.
- Demonstrated leading performance on captioning tasks like DREAM-**1K** and VidCapBench, successfully reconciling divergent generation with other tasks.
- Showcased dramatic improvements on held-out test sets for the proxy tasks (e.g., 117.0 for DarkEventInfer-Test), validating their effectiveness in developing comprehensive video understanding capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naively combining reward signals for convergent QA and divergent captioning tasks in RL results in performance degradation for both, confirming an inherent "paradigm conflict."
> - Intermediate proxy tasks (DarkEventInfer, MixVidQA) effectively bridge this conflict by compelling the model to simultaneously develop both holistic comprehension and precise information extraction.
> - Direct application of RL, especially without an SFT cold-start phase, can more effectively stimulate intrinsic reasoning capabilities and prevent hallucinations compared to models forced to imitate complex reasoning patterns.
