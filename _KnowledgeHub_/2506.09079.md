# VidBridge-R1: Bridging QA and Captioning for RL-based Video Understanding Models with Intermediate Proxy Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> VidBridge-R1 introduces a novel training framework that reconciles the paradigm conflict between video Question Answering and captioning tasks within Reinforcement Learning, enabling a single model to excel at both convergent reasoning and divergent generation. The model achieves leading performance on various video understanding and captioning benchmarks, demonstrating enhanced versatility through intermediate proxy tasks.

## Problem

- Developing versatile video understanding models capable of simultaneously excelling in both video Question Answering (QA) and video captioning using Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- A "paradigm conflict" between convergent QA and divergent captioning tasks leads to mutual performance degradation when optimized simultaneously via global reward signals in RL.
- Existing RL-enhanced video Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) typically specialize in either QA or captioning, lacking effective integration of both capabilities within a unified framework.

## Method

- Introduced a novel training framework centered around two intermediate ==proxy tasks==: DarkEventInfer for holistic understanding and MixVidQA for precise information localization.
- Employed the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm with diverse, task-specific, and implicit format reward functions, notably omitting KL divergence regularization.
- Used Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the backbone model and directly applied RL without an SFT cold-start phase, contrasting with conventional MLLM training pipelines.

## Results

- VidBridge-R1 achieved the highest overall score on Video-MME (64.3) and outperformed strongest baselines by an average of **1.9%** on various video reasoning benchmarks.
- Demonstrated leading performance on captioning tasks like DREAM-**1K** and VidCapBench, successfully reconciling divergent generation with other tasks.
- Showcased dramatic improvements on held-out test sets for the proxy tasks (e.g., 117.0 for DarkEventInfer-Test), validating their effectiveness in developing comprehensive video understanding capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naively combining reward signals for convergent QA and divergent captioning tasks in RL results in performance degradation for both, confirming an inherent "paradigm conflict."
> - Intermediate proxy tasks (DarkEventInfer, MixVidQA) effectively bridge this conflict by compelling the model to simultaneously develop both holistic comprehension and precise information extraction.
> - Direct application of RL, especially without an SFT cold-start phase, can more effectively stimulate intrinsic reasoning capabilities and prevent hallucinations compared to models forced to imitate complex reasoning patterns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in the domain of video understanding, and leverages recent advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for model refinement.

The "Reason-Then-Respond" paradigm, popularized by models like OpenAI O1/O3 and DeepSeek-R1, represents a significant shift in LLM development. This paradigm, which enhances performance through test-time scaling and explicit reasoning steps, has been successfully extended to image-based MLLMs using RL, notably with algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

However, the application of this paradigm to the more complex video modality has presented new challenges. Prior efforts have often resulted in specialized models that excel at either video Question Answering (QA) or video captioning, but rarely both within a unified framework. Existing video-based RL models like Video-R1, VideoRFT, VideoChat-R1, and VideoCap-R1 typically focus on a single task—either enhancing QA capabilities (general or reasoning scenarios) or improving video captioning. This specialization compromises the inherent versatility expected of powerful MLLMs.

The core problem identified by Chen et al. is a "paradigm conflict" between QA and captioning tasks when optimized simultaneously via RL. QA is framed as a *convergent reasoning task*, demanding precise, low-entropy answers. In contrast, captioning is a *divergent generation task*, requiring comprehensive, high-entropy descriptions. Naively combining reward signals for these opposing objectives leads to mutual performance degradation, a critical bottleneck for developing truly general-purpose video MLLMs.

VidBridge-R1 aims to fill this gap by proposing a novel training framework that reconciles this conflict, thereby advancing the field towards more versatile, generalizable, and powerful video understanding models that can master both convergent reasoning and divergent generation within a single framework. This work moves beyond traditional video understanding paradigms, exploring the performance gains achieved through a carefully designed Reason-Then-Respond approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a single, versatile video reasoning model capable of simultaneously excelling in both video Question Answering (QA) and video captioning tasks under the Reason-Then-Respond paradigm.

The motivation stems from several key observations:
- **Versatility of MLLMs:** A core advantage of MLLMs is their ability to perform diverse tasks simultaneously. Over-specializing video MLLMs in either QA or captioning, while enhancing one capability, comes at the cost of overall generalizability.
- **Paradigm Conflict in RL:** When applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to video MLLMs for both QA and captioning, a fundamental conflict arises. QA requires *convergent reasoning* (identifying a unique, low-entropy answer), while captioning demands *divergent generation* (producing diverse, comprehensive, high-entropy descriptions). This inherent opposition in task objectives, when combined through global reward signals in RL, leads to mutual performance degradation, rather than improvement.
- **Limitations of Existing Models:** Current RL-enhanced video MLLMs are narrowly tailored to specific tasks (e.g., Video-R1 for QA, VideoCap-R1 for captioning) and lack effective integration of both capabilities.

To address these issues, the authors propose a novel training framework centered around intermediate proxy tasks. These proxy tasks are designed to compel the model to simultaneously develop both holistic, divergent understanding (crucial for captioning) and precise, convergent reasoning (essential for QA), thereby bridging the identified paradigm conflict and fostering a more balanced and powerful video understanding model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of VidBridge-R1's methodology is a novel training framework built upon two intermediate proxy tasks and a carefully designed Reinforcement Learning strategy.

#### 3.1. Intermediate Proxy Tasks

The paper introduces two novel proxy tasks specifically designed to bridge the gap between divergent captioning and convergent QA:

- **DarkEventInfer**: Inspired by "fill-in-the-middle" in code generation, this task encourages holistic video context understanding. The model is presented with videos where certain event segments are masked by black screens. It must then infer the obscured content based on the surrounding visible contextual cues. This forces the model to perform both divergent understanding (considering all possibilities for the masked event) and convergent reasoning (pinpointing the most plausible event). Data is curated from COIN, with human evaluation to ensure learnability.
- **MixVidQA**: Drawing inspiration from "style mixing" in image generation, this task enhances the model's ability to attend to and extract key information. The model is presented with interleaved sequences from two distinct video clips. It must then answer questions related to *only one* of these clips, requiring it to identify and reason about the relevant content while disregarding irrelevant segments. This task explicitly trains the model in convergent reasoning by demanding precise information localization amidst distraction. Videos are sourced from Kinetics, and QA pairs are generated using Qwen2-VL-72B, with manual review.

By jointly training on these proxy tasks alongside conventional VideoQA and captioning data, the model is encouraged to integrate both holistic understanding and precise information pinpointing, thereby promoting structured video representation and contextual reasoning.

#### 3.2. Data Curation

In addition to the proxy tasks, conventional VideoQA and captioning data are incorporated:
- **VideoQA**: Samples from NExT-QA are used.
- **Captioning**: High-quality videos from Koala-36M (with original captions) and KVQ (with captions generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro) are used to ensure diversity and rich linguistic generation.
- **Data Filtering for GRPO**: A crucial pre-filtering strategy is applied using Qwen2.5-VL-7B to remove samples where candidate answers receive identical rewards, which would render the GRPO algorithm ineffective. This ensures sufficient variance in potential rewards for effective policy updates. The final dataset comprises 10,800 high-quality training instances across all tasks.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Algorithm**: The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm is employed. Notably, the authors find that *omitting the KL divergence regularization* (setting λ=0 in the GRPO objective) is beneficial for more rapidly stimulating reasoning capabilities with a fixed number of training samples, contrasting with typical PPO-like approaches.
- **Reward Function Design**: Diverse, task-specific reward functions are designed:
 - **DarkEventInfer**: A three-tier evaluation by Qwen2.5-72B: 2 for fully correct, 1 for partially correct/error-free, 0 for errors.
 - **MixVidQA & VideoQA**: A two-tier evaluation (Qwen2.5-72B for MixVidQA, regex matching for VideoQA): 1 for correct, 0 for incorrect.
 - **Captioning**: A composite reward combining AutoDQ (event-level recall and precision, with α=0.5 to prevent reward hacking) and a "keywords reward" (R_keywords) that promotes temporally relevant terms and discourages speculative content. GPT-3.5-Turbo is used as the judge model for efficiency in AutoDQ.
 - **Format Reward**: Unlike explicit separate reward components, VidBridge-R1 implements an *implicit* format reward. Task-specific rewards are *only* granted if the model's output conforms to the required Reason-Then-Respond format (`<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`). This design prevents reward hacking (e.g., correct format, incorrect answer; or correct answer, incorrect format) and accelerates convergence to structurally and semantically accurate outputs.
- **Backbone Model**: Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is used as the base model.
- **No SFT Cold-Start**: A key departure from many existing RL-based MLLMs. The authors find that an SFT cold-start phase, particularly when dealing with high-quality reasoning data and proxy tasks, is not only unnecessary but can *impair* the model's inherent reasoning potential by forcing it to learn a specific, potentially suboptimal, reasoning pattern. Direct RL is shown to be more effective in stimulating reasoning abilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VidBridge-R1 demonstrates significant advancements in unified video understanding, outperforming baselines across diverse benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on General Video Understanding and Captioning (Table 1)

- **General Video Understanding (QA tasks):** VidBridge-R1 achieves the highest overall score on Video-MME (64.3), outperforming the strongest baselines by notable margins (e.g., 2.1% on LongVideoBench, 1.6% on MVBench). This demonstrates its robust capabilities across varying video lengths and general comprehension scenarios.
- **Captioning Tasks:** VidBridge-R1 strikes a strong balance between accuracy and comprehensiveness. It achieves leading performance on both DREAM-1K (detailed description) and VidCapBench (utility for text-to-video generation), indicating effective reconciliation of the divergent nature of captioning with other tasks.
- **Impact of Reasoning Paradigm:** The initial observation that forcing reasoning on vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B *degrades* performance on simpler QA tasks highlights the nuanced challenge. However, VidBridge-R1's training successfully overcomes this, showcasing the efficacy of its approach.

#### 4.2. Performance on Video Reasoning Tasks (Table 2)

- VidBridge-R1 achieves outstanding performance on conventional reasoning benchmarks (MMVU, NExT-QA, IntentQA, Causal-VidQA, Video-Holmes), surpassing the strongest baselines by an average of 1.9%.
- Crucially, on the held-out test sets for the proxy tasks, **DarkEventInfer-Test (117.0 score)** and **MixVidQA-Test (49.0 score)**, VidBridge-R1 shows dramatic improvements over all baselines (e.g., 117 vs. 80 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-SFT on DarkEventInfer-Test), validating the effectiveness of the proxy tasks in developing both holistic comprehension and precise information localization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Training Tasks (Table 3)

- **Task Conflict Confirmation:** Naively mixing VideoQA and captioning tasks leads to *performance degradation* across both, even with increased data volume, experimentally confirming the "paradigm conflict" hypothesis.
- **Efficacy of Proxy Tasks:** Progressively incorporating DarkEventInfer and MixVidQA yields substantial performance improvements across all evaluated tasks, demonstrating their critical role in mitigating the conflict and fostering comprehensive capabilities. The largest gains are seen when both proxy tasks are included.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics (Figure 3)

- **General QA:** Rapid initial improvement due to relative simplicity.
- **Reasoning Tasks:** Steady and progressive performance enhancement, confirming consistent activation of reasoning capabilities.
- **Captioning Tasks:** Initial decline followed by oscillatory improvement, suggesting that integrating reasoning into divergent generation is more intricate but ultimately successful.

#### 4.5. SFT Cold-Start Ablation (Table 9 and Figure 12)

- The study reveals that directly applying RL (VidBridge-R1) significantly outperforms methods involving an SFT cold-start, whether with direct answers or reasoning chains (CoT-SFT).
- Forcing models to imitate complex reasoning patterns beyond their current cognitive capacity during SFT can *impair* subsequent RL performance and lead to hallucinations (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-CoT-SFT-RL hallucinating an "hour-long black screen" in a 3-minute video). VidBridge-R1's direct RL approach produces more concise and accurate reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on VidBridge-R1 represents a significant step forward in the development of general-purpose video understanding models, particularly within the Reinforcement Learning framework. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Pioneering a Unified Approach:** VidBridge-R1 is presented as the first versatile video reasoning model capable of simultaneously handling both convergent (QA) and divergent (captioning) tasks under the Reason-Then-Respond paradigm. This directly addresses a critical limitation of previous RL-based video MLLMs that often specialize in one task at the expense of versatility.
- **Resolving the Paradigm Conflict:** The identification and successful mitigation of the "paradigm conflict" between QA and captioning tasks during RL training is a major theoretical and practical contribution. This finding provides valuable insights into the challenges of multi-task optimization with global reward signals and offers a clear path forward using carefully designed intermediate proxy tasks.
- **Innovative Proxy Task Design:** DarkEventInfer and MixVidQA are novel contributions to the methodology of training MLLMs. By compelling models to develop both holistic understanding and precise information localization, these tasks provide a generalizable blueprint for fostering more robust and balanced cognitive abilities in AI. Their effectiveness, particularly on held-out test sets, validates this innovative approach.
- **Rethinking RL Training Pipelines:** The finding that an SFT cold-start can *impair* intrinsic reasoning potential and lead to hallucinations, contrasting with direct RL's ability to stimulate natural reasoning, challenges conventional wisdom in MLLM training. This could lead to a re-evaluation of multi-stage training strategies, encouraging direct RL application, especially when high-quality, task-specific data and rewards are available.
- **Towards More Generalizable AI:** By enabling a single model to excel across diverse video understanding and reasoning scenarios, VidBridge-R1 contributes to the broader goal of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Its multi-task and generalization capabilities demonstrate a pathway to creating more robust and adaptable MLLMs that can handle the complexity and diversity of real-world video content.
- **Practical Applications:** For platforms like Kuaishou, the ability to perform high-quality video QA and captioning within a unified model has immediate practical implications, from enhanced content search and recommendation to automated video summarization and accessibility features. The focus on robust and non-hallucinatory reasoning is crucial for trust and utility in real-world systems.

In conclusion, VidBridge-R1 offers a compelling solution to a fundamental problem in video MLLM development, demonstrating that the strategic integration of intermediate proxy tasks and a refined RL approach can unlock unprecedented versatility and reasoning capabilities, pushing the boundaries of what unified video understanding models can achieve.
