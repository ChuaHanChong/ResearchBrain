# GraspMeanFlow: SE(3)-Equivariant MeanFlow for Few-Step 6-DoF Grasp Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspMeanFlow introduces an SE(3)-equivariant MeanFlow framework for 6-DoF grasp generation, extending the concept of few-step generative modeling to complex Lie groups. The method enables high-quality grasp pose generation using significantly fewer network function evaluations, achieving competitive distributional fidelity and grasp success rates for robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing data-driven generative models for 6-DoF grasp synthesis require many iterative sampling steps (tens of network function evaluations), leading to high computational costs that hinder real-time robotic applications.
- Extending the MeanFlow framework from Euclidean spaces to non-Euclidean manifolds like SE(3) for few-step generation is geometrically non-trivial, especially for the non-commutative SO(3) component.
- Maintaining SE(3)-equivariance, which ensures robustness and generalization, during few-step sampling on SE(3) while dealing with point-cloud conditioning poses a technical challenge.

## Method

- **GraspMeanFlow** rigorously defines ==finite-interval average velocity== on SE(3), employing a ==time-ordered exponential== for the rotational component to correctly accumulate rotation, and a simple integral for translation.
- The model learns this average-velocity field using a novel training objective combining a ==flow-matching boundary term== with a consistency term, instantiated either via a differential MeanFlow identity (GMF-JVP) or a semigroup property (GMF-SG).
- It leverages an SE(3)-equivariant Vector Neuron (VN-DGCNN) backbone, adapted to condition on two time points while preserving equivariance, which ensures generated grasps transform consistently with object pose changes.

## Results

- GraspMeanFlow achieved superior distributional fidelity (EMD of **0.418** for GMF-SG) with just one network function evaluation (**NFE 1**), outperforming existing baselines at comparable low budgets.
- In simulated lift success, GMF-JVP averaged **96.3%** success at **10 NFEs**, surpassing EquiGraspFlow's **91.2%** at the same budget, demonstrating strong performance in the few-step regime.
- The framework significantly reduced inference latency, achieving high-quality results in tens of milliseconds (e.g., **56.3 ms** for **96.3%** success) compared to over a second for some iterative baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Correctly defining average angular velocity on SO(3) requires the time-ordered exponential, as a naive integral does not reproduce the endpoint rotation of a trajectory.
> - SE(3)-equivariance can be preserved in a few-step generative model, ensuring generated grasps are robust to object transformations and reducing the need for extensive data augmentation.
> - The MeanFlow concept can be effectively extended to non-commutative Lie groups like SE(3), enabling a significant reduction in network function evaluations during inference for generative tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of 6-DoF grasp pose synthesis, a fundamental task in robotics that involves generating suitable gripper poses from 3D object observations. This domain has increasingly relied on data-driven generative models, including variational autoencoders, energy-based diffusion models, and stochastic-interpolant bridges, to learn and reproduce complex, multimodal grasp pose distributions.

A notable advancement in this area is the introduction of SE(3)-equivariant flow-based models, such as EquiGraspFlow. These models are designed to generate grasp poses that transform consistently with object rotations and translations, thereby inherently incorporating robustness to object pose changes and reducing the need for extensive data augmentation.

However, a common limitation across these generative approaches, particularly flow-based and diffusion models, is their reliance on iterative sampling. This process typically involves numerical integration of a time-dependent velocity field or repeated denoising steps, requiring tens of network function evaluations (NFE) per grasp. For instance, EquiGraspFlow uses an 80-evaluation solver per grasp. This high computational cost during inference limits their applicability in real-time robotic manipulation pipelines, where hundreds of grasp candidates may need to be generated, filtered, and ranked rapidly.

MeanFlow, an existing framework for Euclidean spaces, offers a method for few-step generation by modeling the average velocity over a finite time interval rather than instantaneous velocities. However, its direct application to the Special Euclidean group SE(3), particularly the Special Orthogonal group SO(3) component (which represents rotations), is not straightforward. The non-commutative nature of SO(3) means that average angular velocity cannot be trivially obtained by integrating instantaneous velocities, as a naive integral does not reproduce the endpoint rotation of a trajectory. Prior manifold MeanFlow extensions have not specifically addressed SE(3) with point-cloud conditioning and the preservation of equivariance.

GraspMeanFlow positions itself within this landscape by extending the MeanFlow concept to SE(3) while explicitly preserving equivariance and addressing the computational efficiency bottleneck. It seeks to enable high-quality grasp generation with significantly fewer network evaluations, thereby enhancing the feasibility of deploying such models in time-sensitive robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an SE(3)-equivariant MeanFlow framework, termed GraspMeanFlow, capable of generating 6-DoF grasp poses with a limited number of sampling steps. This objective is driven by several motivations:

Firstly, current data-driven generative models for 6-DoF grasp synthesis, despite their effectiveness in learning complex grasp pose distributions, are computationally intensive during the sampling phase. Flow-based models integrate a velocity field, and diffusion models denoise iteratively, typically requiring tens of function evaluations per generated grasp. For instance, the reference EquiGraspFlow model requires 80 function evaluations per grasp using a fourth-order Runge–Kutta–Munthe-Kaas solver over 20 steps. This iterative nature renders them less suitable for real-time robotic manipulation scenarios where a high throughput of candidate grasps (e.g., hundreds per scene change) is necessary for robust performance. The authors aim to significantly reduce this computational burden.

Secondly, while the MeanFlow framework offers a route to few-step generation by predicting average velocities over finite intervals in Euclidean spaces, its extension to non-Euclidean manifolds like SE(3) is geometrically non-trivial. The non-commutative property of SO(3) implies that a simple integration of instantaneous angular velocities does not accurately represent the accumulated rotation over an interval. A direct "flattening" of rotations into a Euclidean representation would sacrifice the geometric integrity of the manifold and compromise the SE(3)-equivariance properties that are beneficial for grasp generation (e.g., robustness to object rotations without data augmentation). Therefore, a key motivation is to correctly define and implement average velocities on SE(3) while respecting its Lie group structure.

Thirdly, the maintenance of SE(3)-equivariance is a crucial design principle. Equivariant models ensure that if an object is rigidly transformed, the generated grasps transform identically. This property contributes to the robustness and generalization capabilities of the model, eliminating the need for extensive data augmentation. The authors aim to prove and ensure that this equivariance is preserved under the few-step sampling regime enabled by MeanFlow.

Finally, the implementation of an SE(3)-equivariant MeanFlow model presents specific technical challenges. These include:
- Developing a method to condition the average-velocity field on a pair of times (start and end of interval) within an existing equivariant point-cloud backbone, which typically handles single-time inputs.
- Addressing the numerical fragility of the differential MeanFlow identity on SO(3), particularly issues related to Jacobian-vector products in the Lie algebra.

By addressing these motivations and challenges, the research seeks to provide a computationally efficient and geometrically consistent generative model for 6-DoF grasp poses, suitable for integration into real-time robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GraspMeanFlow frames 6-DoF grasp generation as conditional generative modeling of `p(T | P)` on the SE(3) manifold, where `T = (R, x)` is a grasp pose and `P` is an object point cloud.

**Conditional Prior and Interpolation:** The method utilizes an object-centered prior `p1(T | P)`, which consists of a uniform distribution over SO(3) for rotations (`p1(R)`) and a Gaussian distribution for translations (`p1(x | P)`) centered at the point-cloud mean. Grasp poses are interpolated geodesically on SO(3) and linearly in R^3 between a successful data grasp `T0` and a prior sample `T1`.

**Average Velocity on SE(3):** The core of GraspMeanFlow is the definition of the finite-interval average velocity on SE(3).
- For the **rotational component (SO(3))**, the average angular velocity `ω_avg` over an interval `[s, t]` is defined through the time-ordered exponential `T exp(∫_s^t [ω_τ] dτ)`. This definition ensures that the `exp((t-s)[ω_avg])` exactly reproduces the rigid-body displacement `R_t R_s^T`. This is critical due to the non-commutative nature of SO(3).
- For the **translational component (R^3)**, the average linear velocity `v_avg` is defined by a simple time integral, `(t-s)v_avg = ∫_s^t v_τ dτ`, consistent with Euclidean MeanFlow.

**Training Objective:** The model learns an average-velocity field `fθ(s, t, T_t, P)` and is trained using a combination of a flow-matching boundary term and a consistency term.

1. **Flow-Matching Boundary Term (`L_bd`)**: This term anchors the model at the diagonal `s=t`, ensuring that `ω_avgθ(t, t, T_t)` and `v_avgθ(t, t, T_t)` converge to the instantaneous data velocities `ω_t` and `v_t`, respectively. This provides a fundamental anchor for the learning process.

2. **Consistency Terms (Two Instantiations):**
 - **GMF-JVP (Differential MeanFlow Identity)**: This variant utilizes the differential SE(3) MeanFlow identity `J(ω_s→t) (d/dt)ω_s→t = ω_t`. The objective `L_diff` regresses the model's average velocity `ω_avgθ` against a target derived from this identity, which involves `J(ω_s→tθ)^-1 ω_t` and a Jacobian-vector product (JVP) for `(d/dt)ω_avgθ`. This approach aims for exactness in the differential form but can be numerically sensitive on SO(3). Training for GMF-JVP additionally employs a per-object optimal transport coupling to pair data grasps with prior samples, aiming to straighten interpolation paths.
 - **GMF-SG (Semigroup Property / Consistency Loss)**: This variant exploits the exact multiplicative property of rotations on SE(3): `D(s, t) = D(m, t) D(s, m)` for `s ≤ m ≤ t`. The consistency loss `L_sg` enforces this property on the model's predicted log-displacements. It regresses the direct `exp([ω_s→tθ])` against the composition `exp([ω_m→tθ]) exp([ω_s→mθ])` (with `ω_m→tθ` and `ω_s→mθ` from stop-gradient model evaluations at intermediate states). This approach avoids explicit Jacobian computations and JVP, addressing the numerical stability concerns on SO(3). GMF-SG uses independent sampling for data-prior coupling.

3. **α-Flow Warm-up**: To stabilize initial training, a warm-up phase uses an α-Flow objective. This objective, an interpolation between flow matching and the differential MeanFlow, provides a JVP-free consistency target where the near segment's velocity is provided by the data rather than the model. The α parameter is annealed from 1 down to a small value during this phase.

**SE(3)-Equivariant Conditional Generation:**
- The framework rigorously proves that an SE(3)-equivariant average-velocity field, when combined with an SE(3)-invariant prior, leads to an SE(3)-invariant generated distribution, even in the few-step sampling regime where the transport map may not be a diffeomorphism.
- **Network Architecture**: The model leverages the Vector Neuron (VN-DGCNN) backbone from EquiGraspFlow. To accommodate the two-time conditioning (`s` and `t`), these scalar times are "lifted" into equivariant vectors by multiplying a shared learned equivariant direction (derived from the network's features and pose). This design ensures that the SO(3)-equivariance of the backbone is maintained. The output angular velocity is expressed in the spatial frame, which correctly transforms covariantly under scene rotations.

**Sampling:**
- Sampling is performed in a few steps, with the number of function evaluations (NFE) directly corresponding to the number of steps.
- Two samplers are used: a standard **Euler integration** and an **exp-SO(3) sampler**. The latter, an endpoint-style sampler, uses the interval-averaged field to predict the data endpoint and moves the rotation along the geodesic toward it at a fixed rate.
- **Classifier-free guidance** is implemented by folding it into the training target, avoiding additional function evaluations during inference.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Dataset**: ACRONYM (Bowl, Laptop, Mug, Pencil categories).
- **Baselines**: EquiGraspFlow, SE(3)-DiffusionFields, and BRIDGER.
- **Metrics**: Distributional fidelity (Hungarian Earth Mover's Distance, EMD) and grasp feasibility (simulated lift success in NVIDIA Isaac Gym). Inference efficiency is measured by NFE and wall-clock latency.
- **Training Details**: Two-stage training (α-Flow warm-up followed by boundary + consistency objective).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance of GraspMeanFlow in terms of distributional fidelity, grasp success, and inference efficiency.

**Distributional Fidelity (Earth Mover’s Distance - EMD):**
- **Few-Step Advantage**: GMF-SG demonstrated competitive performance in distributional fidelity, achieving an EMD of 0.418 with a single function evaluation (NFE 1). This is considerably lower than the closest baseline (0.694) at the same budget.
- **Convergence**: GMF-SG maintained the best EMD performance through NFE 5. EquiGraspFlow, a leading iterative model, required five evaluations to approach GMF-SG's single-step EMD and ten evaluations to achieve comparable or slightly better EMD. GMF-JVP, while trailing GMF-SG by 0.04 to 0.06 EMD at various budgets, still significantly outperformed baselines at NFE 1 and 2. This indicates that both GraspMeanFlow configurations generate accurate grasp distributions with substantially fewer evaluations.

**Grasp Success Rate (Simulated Lift Success):**
- **NFE 5 Performance**: At NFE 5, GMF-JVP (using the exp-SO(3) sampler) showed robust performance across most categories. It surpassed EquiGraspFlow's success rate by 21.7 points on Bowl, 24.3 points on Laptop, 3.0 points on Mug, and 12.8 points on Pencil. GMF-SG also performed favorably, only slightly trailing EquiGraspFlow on the Pencil category. BRIDGER was marginally stronger on Mug but lagged significantly on Bowl and Laptop.
- **NFE 10 and 20 Performance**: At NFE 10, GMF-JVP averaged 96.3% success, outperforming EquiGraspFlow (91.2%). At NFE 20, the fully converged EquiGraspFlow achieved 97.7% success, taking a slight lead over GMF-JVP (95.6%). This trend suggests that GraspMeanFlow's performance advantage is most pronounced within the intended few-step regime (NFE 5-10), while iterative methods can eventually close the gap with sufficient evaluations.

**Inference Cost (Wall-Clock Latency):**
- **Efficiency at Matched Quality**: GMF-SG achieved an EMD of 0.418 in 31.5 ms (1 NFE), a quality level that other diffusion baselines did not reach at any budget. GMF-JVP achieved 96.3% grasp success (at NFE 10) in 56.3 ms, whereas SE(3)-DiffusionFields required 1338.7 ms for 96.1% success. These results demonstrate GraspMeanFlow's ability to achieve high-quality results with substantially reduced inference time, particularly at lower NFE counts. The authors note that the point-cloud encoder, used by both EquiGraspFlow and GraspMeanFlow, accounts for a significant portion of the latency at small NFE.

**Model Capacity and Training Budget:**
- GraspMeanFlow's architecture involved minimal changes to the EquiGraspFlow backbone, adding less than 0.1% to the parameter count (633k vs. 633k). Both configurations were trained for 120k steps (approximately 7 hours on an A100 GPU), which is comparable to existing methods. This indicates that the observed performance gains are primarily attributable to the MeanFlow objective and sampling strategy rather than increased model capacity or training budget.

**Equivariance Preservation:**
- Numerical validation confirmed that the trained GraspMeanFlow model maintains SE(3)-equivariance at machine precision (deviations at approximately 8 × 10^-14 for the field and 1 × 10^-12 for samplers). This theoretical guarantee is realized in the practical implementation.

**Ablation Studies:**
- The differential MeanFlow identity for the rotation branch (GMF-JVP) requires a spatial-frame formulation to ensure stable training and avoid divergence (e.g., on rotationally symmetric objects like the Bowl). The flow-matching boundary term was found to be essential for preventing collapsed minimizers in conjunction with consistency losses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GraspMeanFlow framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation and generative modeling:

**Addressing the Real-time Bottleneck in Robotics:** The most prominent impact is the substantial reduction in computational cost during inference for 6-DoF grasp generation. By achieving high distributional fidelity and simulated grasp success rates with only a few (e.g., 5-10) network function evaluations, GraspMeanFlow directly addresses the bottleneck of slow iterative sampling methods. This efficiency makes it a more viable candidate for real-time robotic systems that require rapid generation, filtering, and execution of grasp poses in dynamic environments.

**Advancing Generative Modeling on Lie Groups:** The work significantly extends the MeanFlow framework beyond Euclidean spaces to non-commutative Lie groups like SE(3). The precise definition of average velocity through the time-ordered exponential for rotations is a theoretically rigorous approach to handling the complexities of SO(3). This theoretical development could serve as a foundation for few-step generative modeling in other domains that involve Lie group structures, such as molecular dynamics, protein structure prediction, or rigid-body motion planning.

**Robustness through Equivariance:** GraspMeanFlow’s inherent SE(3)-equivariance, maintained through both its theoretical formulation and network design, ensures that generated grasps transform consistently with object pose changes. This property contributes to the model's robustness, reduces dependence on data augmentation, and enhances its generalization capabilities to novel object orientations and positions in cluttered scenes.

**Practical Implementations and Insights:** The development and evaluation of two distinct instantiations (GMF-SG and GMF-JVP), each with specific strengths (distributional fidelity vs. simulated success), offer practical insights into training MeanFlow models on manifolds. The use of an α-Flow warm-up and the strategies for handling the numerical stability of the differential identity provide valuable recipes for future research.

**Catalyst for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as challenges with highly symmetric objects (like the "Pencil" category) due to the log-displacement target, and the open question of step-budget-aware sampler schedules, highlight avenues for future exploration. Developing symmetry-aware training targets or adaptive sampling strategies could further enhance the model's performance and versatility.

In summary, GraspMeanFlow contributes a computationally efficient, geometrically sound, and equivariant generative model for 6-DoF grasp poses. Its ability to produce high-quality grasps with fewer computational steps represents a step towards more agile and responsive robotic manipulation systems, while also advancing the theoretical and practical application of MeanFlow to complex mathematical structures.
