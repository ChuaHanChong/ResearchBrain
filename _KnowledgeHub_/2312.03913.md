# Controllable Human-Object Interaction Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> CHOIS synthesizes synchronized human and object motion from a language description, initial human/object states, and sparse object waypoints, using a conditional diffusion transformer over SMPL-X poses and object pose. An object geometry loss sharpens waypoint adherence, while test-time reconstruction-guidance terms enforce hand-object contact, feet-floor contact, and no floor penetration. It cuts FID to 0.69 and plugs into path planning for long-horizon 3D-scene interactions.

## Problem

- Human-scene interaction work handles only static objects like chairs, ignoring the dynamic manipulation of large movable objects that dominates everyday behavior.
- Dynamic interaction methods target small hand-held objects or box-lifting policies, failing to generalize across diverse large-object geometries and manipulation styles.
- Large-object methods need past interaction frames or a full object motion trajectory as input, so they cannot generate both motions from initial states alone.

## Method

- ==Conditional diffusion transformer== jointly denoises human pose, object position/relative rotation, and per-hand contact labels, conditioned on ==CLIP== text, masked initial states, sparse waypoints, and ==BPS== object geometry. This is **CHOIS**.
- **Object geometry loss** supervises 100 sampled rest-pose vertices transformed by predicted rotation and translation, penalizing L1 deviation from ground-truth vertices to sharpen waypoint adherence.
- ==Reconstruction guidance== during the final 10 denoising steps applies weighted analytic terms for hand-object contact (gated by predicted contact labels), feet-floor height, and object-floor penetration.

## Results

- On FullBodyManipulation, CHOIS reaches FID **0.69** and contact F1 **0.67** versus **6.16**/**0.53** for adapted MDM and **4.19**/**0.66** for Pred-OMOMO, with end-waypoint error **6.31 cm**.
- On unseen 3D-FUTURE objects, CHOIS gets FID **1.60**, foot height **3.75 cm**, and hand penetration **0.15 cm**, beating Pred-OMOMO's **3.74** FID and **0.18 cm** penetration.
- Ablations: removing the geometry loss raises end-waypoint error from **6.31** to **14.16 cm**; dropping contact guidance cuts contact percentage **0.54** to **0.41**; dropping feet guidance raises foot height to **6.65 cm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparse waypoints plus language give complementary control: geometry grounds trajectories in cluttered scenes while text sets style, letting off-the-shelf planners drive long-horizon synthesis.
> - Expensive physical constraints can move from training losses to test-time guidance gradients, avoiding loss-weight retuning and slow training while still improving contact realism.
> - Joint single-stage generation of object and human motion beats cascaded pipelines that first fix object trajectories, which propagate interpolation errors and add inference stages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The synthesis of realistic human behaviors in 3D environments is a critical area with widespread applications in computer graphics, embodied AI, and robotics. This field aims to create intelligent systems that can simulate, understand, and generate human-like interactions within complex virtual or physical settings.

Historically, research in human motion synthesis has seen significant advancements, particularly with the availability of large-scale motion capture datasets like AMASS. Early works focused on generating human motion alone, often conditioned on actions or language descriptions. The recent success of diffusion models in generative tasks has further propelled text-conditioned human motion synthesis.

However, a significant gap exists when considering human-object interaction within dynamic 3D scenes. Prior works on human-scene interaction were largely restricted to scenarios with *static* objects (e.g., sitting on a chair). While some recent advances model dynamic human-object interactions, they often focus solely on smaller objects where hand motion is paramount, or lack the ability to manipulate diverse objects. More advanced manipulation of larger, diverse objects often relies on sequences of past interaction states or complete sequences of object motion, making them incapable of synthesizing both object motion and human motion purely from initial states and high-level directives.

This paper directly addresses these limitations by focusing on the challenging problem of **synthesizing realistic, synchronized human and *dynamic* object motions for diverse objects in 3D environments, guided by language descriptions and initial states**. The introduction of sparse object waypoints is a key differentiator, bridging the gap between high-level language intent and grounding the interaction within the scene, something that existing methods struggled with. This approach allows for the generation of long-horizon interactions in complex, cluttered 3D environments, a crucial step for more advanced simulations.

The work is also positioned against concurrent research like CG-HOI and HOI-Diff, which also use diffusion models to synthesize human-object interactions from text. CHOIS distinguishes itself by integrating textual conditions with *sparse waypoint conditions*, which is critical for enabling long-term, scene-aware interactions by seamlessly integrating with path planning modules. Furthermore, CHOIS leverages the FullBodyManipulation dataset, which is noted for its superior scale and motion quality compared to datasets like BEHAVE used by concurrent works, providing a robust foundation for the model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **generate synchronized object motion and human motion simultaneously, guided by language descriptions, initial object and human states, and sparse object waypoints in 3D scenes.**

The motivation behind this ambitious objective stems from several critical needs and challenges:

1. **Simulating Realistic Human Behaviors**: To build truly advanced AI systems capable of continuous, natural human behaviors in diverse 3D environments, it is essential to simulate interactions where humans actively manipulate and engage with objects. Current methods often fall short in generating the dynamic coordination required for such tasks (e.g., moving a chair, adjusting a lamp).
2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Methods**:
 - **Static vs. Dynamic Objects**: Many previous works focused on human interaction with static objects, neglecting the common, dynamic manipulations found in daily life.
 - **Small vs. Large Objects**: Methods for dynamic interaction often concentrated on small objects (e.g., grasping a cup), struggling to generalize to larger, more complex objects like furniture.
 - **Dependency on Full Trajectories**: Existing manipulation methods for larger objects often require pre-defined object motion sequences or past interaction states, making them reactive rather than truly generative from initial conditions. This work aims to generate *both* human and object motion from initial states and high-level guidance.
3. **Handling Scene Clutter**: Realistic 3D scenes are often cluttered, imposing significant constraints on feasible motion trajectories. The method must accommodate these environmental constraints, moving beyond the assumption of empty scenes. Sparse waypoints are introduced as an effective mechanism to ground the motion in the scene context, allowing for long-horizon interactions.
4. **Ensuring Realism and Precision**: Generating synchronized human and object motions is inherently challenging. It requires ensuring precise hand-object contact throughout the interaction, maintaining causal relationships between human actions and object movements, and preventing artifacts like foot floating or object penetration with the floor.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a robust, controllable, and generalizable framework for human-object interaction synthesis that overcomes the limitations of prior work by combining expressive language control with scene-aware waypoint guidance and robust physical realism constraints.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, **Controllable Human-Object Interaction Synthesis (CHOIS)**, is a conditional diffusion model designed to generate synchronized object and human motion.

#### 3.1. Data Representation

- **Human Motion (X)**: Represented as global joint positions and 6D continuous rotations, enabling reconstruction via the SMPL-X parametric human model.
- **Object Motion (O)**: Defined by the object's global 3D position (centroid) and relative rotation with respect to its rest pose geometry.
- **Object Geometry (G)**: Utilizes the Basis Point Set (BPS) representation, which encodes the spatial relationship of basis points to the object's surface. This is then projected into a low-dimensional vector.
- **Input Conditions (c)**:
 - Language descriptions: Encoded using CLIP embeddings.
 - Initial states: Human and object pose at the first frame.
 - Sparse object waypoints: 2D object positions every 30 frames, and a 3D object position at the final frame.
 - These conditions (object geometry vector, masked motion conditions, and language embedding) are concatenated and fed into the denoising network.
- **Full Data Representation (τ)**: Includes human motion (X), object motion (O), and contact labels (H) for left and right hands, which are also predicted by the model to guide realism.

#### 3.2. Interaction Synthesis Model (Conditional Diffusion Model)

- **Architecture**: A transformer-based denoising network is employed. It takes the object geometry encoding, masked motion conditions, noisy data representation (τ_n), and a combined noise level/language embedding as input.
- **Training Process**: The model is trained to reverse a forward diffusion process that adds noise to clean data. The objective is to predict the clean data representation (τ_0) from the noisy input.
- **Object Geometry Loss (L_obj)**: An additional L1 loss is introduced during training to improve object motion prediction accuracy. It minimizes the difference between the transformed selected object vertices (using predicted rotation and translation) and their ground truth positions, encouraging consistency with the BPS representation.

#### 3.3. Guidance Terms during Sampling

To overcome challenges with contact realism (hand-object contact, foot floating, object-floor penetration) without complicating the training process with computationally expensive or difficult-to-balance loss terms, CHOIS applies guidance during the inference (sampling) process. This leverages the concept of reconstruction guidance, where the predicted clean data representation is perturbed using gradients from analytical functions.
- **Hand-Object Contact Guidance (F_contact)**: Based on predicted contact labels (H), this L1 loss term minimizes the distance between hand positions and the nearest points on the object mesh when contact is predicted, promoting accurate hand-object interaction.
- **Feet-Floor Contact Guidance (F_feet)**: An L2 loss term that encourages the lowest point of the human's feet to be at a predefined threshold height (h=0.02m) from the floor (z=0), reducing foot floating.
- **Object-Floor Penetration Guidance (F_obj)**: An L1 loss term that penalizes any negative z-coordinates of the object vertices, preventing the object from penetrating the floor.
- **Combined Guidance (F_all)**: These terms are combined using tunable weights (λ1, λ2, λ3) and applied only during the last 10 denoising steps, where the predictions are less noisy and guidance is more effective.

#### 3.4. Application Integration

The paper demonstrates CHOIS's capability to integrate into a broader pipeline for long-term, scene-aware interaction synthesis in 3D environments (e.g., Replica Dataset). This pipeline involves:
1. **High-level Planning**: Language descriptions are parsed (e.g., using GPT-3) to identify interaction objects and target positions.
2. **Scene-Aware Waypoint Extraction**: Primitive functions and semantic labels from scene point clouds are used to determine target 3D positions. Habitat is then employed to generate collision-free paths (sequences of 3D waypoints) within the scene.
3. **Waypoint Adaptation**: Habitat's waypoints are heuristically converted into sparse, time-interval-fixed waypoints suitable for CHOIS's input.
4. **Interaction Synthesis**: CHOIS takes these sparse waypoints, initial states, and a simplified language description (e.g., "Pick up the floor lamp and move it") to generate the detailed synchronized human and object motions.

This modular approach allows CHOIS to bridge high-level semantic instructions with low-level motion control, making it suitable for complex, environment-aware tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of CHOIS is rigorously evaluated using quantitative metrics, qualitative comparisons, and human perceptual studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4)

- **Condition Matching**: CHOIS significantly outperforms baselines (InterDiff, MDM, Lin-OMOMO, Pred-OMOMO) in terms of adhering to sparse object waypoints (lower T_e, T_xy). The `CHOIS w/o L_obj` ablation demonstrates that the object geometry loss is crucial for this improvement.
- **Human Motion Quality**:
 - CHOIS achieves competitive or superior performance in foot sliding (FS) and foot height (H_feet), indicating more realistic ground contact. The ablation study (Table 3) clearly shows that `F_feet` guidance is critical for reducing foot floating.
 - It also shows strong R-precision (R_prec) and low Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), suggesting that the generated human motions are consistent with textual descriptions and have high overall quality.
- **Interaction Quality**:
 - CHOIS excels in hand-object contact metrics (C_prec, C_rec, C_F1, C%), indicating high accuracy and realism in hand-object interactions. The ablation study confirms that `F_contact` guidance significantly boosts contact percentage and F1 score.
 - It also shows low hand-object penetration (P_hand), demonstrating the effectiveness of the guidance terms.
- **GT Difference**: CHOIS generates motions that are close to ground truth in terms of MPJPE, T_root, T_obj, and O_obj, demonstrating fidelity to the desired movement.
- **Generalization to Novel Objects (3D-FUTURE)**: CHOIS consistently outperforms baselines when tested on novel objects from the 3D-FUTURE dataset (Table 2), showcasing its strong generalization capabilities.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results (Figures 3, 5, 6)

- Visual comparisons (Figure 3) demonstrate that CHOIS generates more fluid, natural, and contact-aware interactions compared to baselines which often show unnatural object manipulation or foot floating.
- The application section (Figure 5) beautifully illustrates CHOIS's ability to synthesize long-term, scene-aware interactions (e.g., "Pick up floor lamp, move floor lamp to be close to the sofa") within complex 3D environments like the Replica Dataset.
- Figure 6 further highlights the controllability: given the same text, different waypoints lead to distinct yet plausible interactions (e.g., "Pull the clothes stand and set it back down" with different paths); similarly, different texts with the same waypoints yield varied but consistent interactions (e.g., "Kick the table" vs. "Push the table" with the same path).

#### 4.3. Human Perceptual Study (Figure 4)

- Human evaluators significantly preferred CHOIS over all baselines and even some of its own ablations for both consistency with text input and overall quality of generated interactions. This subjective validation strongly reinforces the quantitative findings, highlighting the perceived realism and controllability of CHOIS.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 3)

- The study confirms the importance of the proposed innovations:
 - `L_obj` (object geometry loss) is critical for improving condition matching, ensuring the generated object motion accurately follows the waypoints.
 - `F_contact` (hand-object contact guidance) is essential for realistic hand-object interactions, preventing noticeable gaps.
 - `F_feet` (feet-floor contact guidance) is crucial for preventing foot floating issues, contributing to more grounded human motion.
 - `F_obj` (object-floor penetration guidance) helps prevent artifacts where objects pass through the floor.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Controllable Human-Object Interaction Synthesis" represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D human behavior generation and interaction. Its significance and potential impact span several domains:

1. **Novelty in Controllability and Integration**: CHOIS is a pioneering approach that effectively combines high-level language descriptions with low-level, sparse object waypoints to simultaneously generate human and dynamic object motion. This unique combination enables fine-grained control over both the style/intent of the interaction (via language) and its grounding in the 3D environment (via waypoints), a capability largely absent in prior work. Its seamless integration with path planning modules for long-term, scene-aware interactions is a major step forward.

2. **Enhanced Realism through Dedicated Mechanisms**: The introduction of the object geometry loss during training and the analytical guidance terms (for hand-object contact, feet-floor contact, and object-floor penetration) during sampling are crucial technical contributions. These mechanisms address critical realism issues that often plague generative models, demonstrating an effective strategy for enforcing complex physical constraints without over-complicating the model architecture or training process.

3. **Robustness and Generalization**: By leveraging the high-quality FullBodyManipulation dataset and demonstrating strong performance on novel objects from 3D-FUTURE, CHOIS proves its robustness and ability to generalize beyond its training data. This is vital for practical applications where interactions with a wide variety of unseen objects are common.

4. **Impact on Computer Graphics and Animation**: CHOIS can dramatically improve the realism and versatility of virtual characters in games, films, and virtual reality. Animators and content creators can use language and simple waypoints to generate complex, dynamic scenes involving human-object manipulation, significantly reducing manual effort and enabling more believable digital human performances.

5. **Advancements in Embodied AI and Robotics**:
 - **Training Embodied Agents**: For embodied AI agents, CHOIS can serve as a powerful tool for generating diverse, realistic interaction trajectories. This synthetic data can be invaluable for training agents to perform complex, multi-step tasks that involve manipulating objects in cluttered environments.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration**: In robotics, particularly for human-robot collaboration, understanding and predicting human intent and motion during manipulation is crucial. CHOIS could be adapted to provide predictive models of human actions, enabling robots to anticipate and assist humans more effectively.
 - **Task Planning**: By connecting high-level language instructions to detailed motion synthesis, CHOIS offers a foundation for more intelligent task planning in complex robotic scenarios, moving from abstract goals to concrete, physically plausible actions.

In conclusion, "Controllable Human-Object Interaction Synthesis" pushes the boundaries of generative AI for 3D human behavior. By offering unprecedented control and realism in dynamic human-object interactions, it lays a strong foundation for future advancements in virtual environments, embodied intelligence, and human-robot interaction.
