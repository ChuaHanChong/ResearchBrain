# Instructions are all you need: Self-supervised Reinforcement Learning for Instruction Following

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and Ant Group developed a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework that enables language models to follow multi-constraint instructions without relying on external supervision. The framework achieves improved constraint-following rates and robust generalization across various in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks while preserving general reasoning abilities.

## Problem

- Language models often struggle to consistently follow multi-constraint instructions, which are critical for complex real-world applications and agentic tasks.
- Existing methods for enhancing instruction following rely heavily on expensive external supervision, such as powerful proprietary models or manual data annotation.
- Multi-constraint tasks inherently yield sparse reward signals during reinforcement learning, making efficient model training challenging and computationally intensive.

## Method

- A ==self-supervised reinforcement learning== framework is proposed that autonomously generates reward signals for multi-constraint instruction following without external human labels or strong external models.
- An ==incremental constraint curriculum== is implemented to progressively introduce constraints, thereby generating dense learning signals to address the sparse reward problem.
- A ==hybrid reward modeling== approach utilizes ==programmatic verification== for hard constraints and a ==self-supervised binary classification== model for soft constraints, trained using pseudo-labels derived from the curriculum.

## Results

- The framework significantly improved constraint-following capabilities across various model sizes (**1.5B** to **14B** parameters), with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-IF achieving a **21.6%** gain on the IFEval benchmark.
- Models trained with the framework demonstrated robust generalization to out-of-domain tasks, including agentic tasks (e.g., Distill-Qwen3-8B-IF gained **6.1** on AgentIF) and multi-turn dialogues.
- The self-supervised **7B** reward model achieved high human alignment (Kendall Tau of **94.0**) and significantly faster inference speeds (approximately **100x** speedup compared to larger generative models) while maintaining strong RL training performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised pseudo-label generation for soft constraints can achieve high consistency with human annotations, effectively replacing external supervision for reward model training.
> - An incremental constraint curriculum successfully provides dense learning signals, making reinforcement learning more efficient and robust for complex multi-constraint instruction following.
> - The method enhances consistency between a model's internal reasoning process and its generated outputs, contributing to both improved constraint adherence and better generalization capabilities.
