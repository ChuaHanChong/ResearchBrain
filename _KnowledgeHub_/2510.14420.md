# Instructions are all you need: Self-supervised Reinforcement Learning for Instruction Following

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and Ant Group developed a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework that enables language models to follow multi-constraint instructions without relying on external supervision. The framework achieves improved constraint-following rates and robust generalization across various in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks while preserving general reasoning abilities.

## Problem

- Language models often struggle to consistently follow multi-constraint instructions, which are critical for complex real-world applications and agentic tasks.
- Existing methods for enhancing instruction following rely heavily on expensive external supervision, such as powerful proprietary models or manual data annotation.
- Multi-constraint tasks inherently yield sparse reward signals during reinforcement learning, making efficient model training challenging and computationally intensive.

## Method

- A ==self-supervised reinforcement learning== framework is proposed that autonomously generates reward signals for multi-constraint instruction following without external human labels or strong external models.
- An ==incremental constraint curriculum== is implemented to progressively introduce constraints, thereby generating dense learning signals to address the sparse reward problem.
- A ==hybrid reward modeling== approach utilizes ==programmatic verification== for hard constraints and a ==self-supervised binary classification== model for soft constraints, trained using pseudo-labels derived from the curriculum.

## Results

- The framework significantly improved constraint-following capabilities across various model sizes (**1.5B** to **14B** parameters), with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct-IF achieving a **21.6%** gain on the IFEval benchmark.
- Models trained with the framework demonstrated robust generalization to out-of-domain tasks, including agentic tasks (e.g., Distill-Qwen3-8B-IF gained **6.1** on AgentIF) and multi-turn dialogues.
- The self-supervised **7B** reward model achieved high human alignment (Kendall Tau of **94.0**) and significantly faster inference speeds (approximately **100x** speedup compared to larger generative models) while maintaining strong RL training performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised pseudo-label generation for soft constraints can achieve high consistency with human annotations, effectively replacing external supervision for reward model training.
> - An incremental constraint curriculum successfully provides dense learning signals, making reinforcement learning more efficient and robust for complex multi-constraint instruction following.
> - The method enhances consistency between a model's internal reasoning process and its generated outputs, contributing to both improved constraint adherence and better generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The capability of large language models (LLMs) to follow complex instructions, particularly those involving multiple constraints, is a significant challenge in natural language processing. This ability is critical for deploying LLMs in practical real-world applications, such as nuanced human conversations and sophisticated agentic tasks. Current instruction-tuned models frequently exhibit insufficient adherence to user instructions, and models specifically trained for reasoning often prioritize reasoning over instruction following.

Existing methodologies aimed at enhancing instruction-following generally fall into several categories:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This approach often relies on distilling high-quality data from more powerful, often proprietary, models.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** This involves training with collected pairwise preference data, which can be resource-intensive to acquire and annotate.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** This paradigm is considered suitable for multi-constraint tasks because complex instructions often contain verifiable elements.

However, current RLVR approaches face several limitations that the present work aims to address:
1. **Dependency on External Supervision:** Many methods rely on external, stronger models (as reward models or data sources for reward model training) or extensive manual annotation, which can be computationally expensive, proprietary, or limit the potential for improvement.
2. **Sparse Reward Signals:** Multi-constraint tasks are difficult to satisfy entirely, leading to infrequent positive rewards that hinder effective learning in RL frameworks.
3. **Computational Inefficiency:** Generative reward models, often employed in these tasks, can be computationally demanding, slowing down training, especially for scenarios with numerous constraints.

This research introduces a self-supervised RL framework that specifically aims to overcome these limitations by eliminating external supervision, providing dense reward signals, and improving computational efficiency, thereby offering a more scalable and practical approach to instruction following.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

1. **Eliminate Dependency on External Supervision:** The central objective is to develop a label-free training framework for reinforcement learning that does not require human annotations, external preference datasets, or reliance on proprietary or computationally expensive "stronger models" to generate reward signals or distill reward model training data. This is motivated by the desire to reduce the cost and logistical challenges associated with obtaining high-quality external supervision.

2. **Address Sparse Reward Signals in Multi-Constraint Tasks:** Complex instructions with multiple constraints are inherently difficult for LLMs to satisfy simultaneously, resulting in a low probability of receiving a full reward. This sparsity impedes effective learning in RL. The motivation is to devise strategies that provide denser, more incremental feedback to guide the model's learning process.

3. **Enhance Computational Efficiency of Reward Modeling:** Existing generative reward models, particularly in multi-constraint contexts, can be computationally intensive, slowing down the training loop. The research seeks to design a more efficient reward modeling approach that can still accurately evaluate constraint satisfaction.

4. **Improve Language Models' Multi-Constraint Instruction Following Capability:** The overarching motivation is to significantly enhance the ability of language models to reliably follow instructions with multiple simultaneous constraints. This is crucial for their practical utility in real-world scenarios, including generating coherent responses in multi-turn conversations and executing complex directives in agentic systems. Current models often fail to consistently adhere to such instructions, leading to unreliable performance.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to propose a more effective, efficient, and scalable training paradigm for instruction-following LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework operates in two main stages: (1) constructing a multi-constraint instruction dataset and training constraint-wise binary classification reward models using pseudo-labels, and (2) optimizing the policy model using composite reward signals.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

The training dataset integrates two primary components:
- **Complex Instruction Synthesis:** To generate diverse multi-constraint instructions, the authors collected 3,000 seed instructions from existing datasets (e.g., OpenAssistant Conversations, Self-Instruct, Super-NaturalInstructions). GPT-4o was then employed to augment these seed instructions by adding multiple constraints. This process included 23 types of "hard" constraints (e.g., JSON format, word frequency) and 25 types of "soft" constraints (e.g., style, role-based tone). Constraint compatibility was managed through a defined taxonomy and flexible selection during generation.
- **General Reasoning Data Integration:** To prevent degradation of general abilities, the dataset was supplemented with 4,501 math problems from the DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset and 1,929 science questions from SciKnowEval.
- **Incremental Constraint Curriculum:** To address sparse rewards, a curriculum-based approach was implemented. For a multi-constraint instruction *x* with *n* constraints (*c*₁ ⊕ *c*₂ ⊕... ⊕ *c*ₙ), curriculum levels *L*ₖ were created. Each level *k* contained sub-instructions *x*ₖ that progressively incorporated the first *k* constraints (*c*₁, *c*₂..., *c*ₖ). This method generates dense learning signals by gradually increasing instruction complexity from single to full multi-constraint scenarios.

#### 3.2. Reward Modeling

The framework employs distinct reward mechanisms for hard and soft constraints to produce constraint-level rewards.

- **Hard Constraint Modeling:** For constraints that can be explicitly verified using programmatic rules (e.g., checking word counts, specific keywords), a binary function *R*ʰ(*o*, *c*) is used. It returns 1 if response *o* satisfies hard constraint *c* and 0 otherwise.
- **Soft Constraint Modeling:** For soft constraints that lack explicit rules, a binary classification reward model *f*(*o*, *c*) is trained. This model estimates the probability that response *o* satisfies soft constraint *c*. Crucially, this model is trained using a self-supervised pseudo-label generation mechanism, eliminating the need for external annotations.
 - **Pseudo-label Generation:** Drawing from the incremental constraint curriculum, a natural relationship is exploited: a response *o*ₖ (generated for an instruction with constraint *c*ₖ) is assumed to exhibit stronger adherence to *c*ₖ than *o*ₖ₋₁ (generated for an instruction without *c*ₖ). This allows for the construction of pseudo-labels: (*o*ₖ, *c*ₖ, label=1) as a positive sample and (*o*ₖ₋₁, *c*ₖ, label=0) as a negative sample.
 - **Training:** The reward model is trained to minimize the Binary Cross-Entropy loss based on these pseudo-labels. The generated pseudo-labels demonstrated high consistency (Kendall Tau Coefficient of 94.0 and Position Consistency of 97.0) with human annotations on a sampled dataset.
 - **Reward Model Training Details:** Three models (Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) were fine-tuned for the binary classification task. Training used FP16 precision, Deepspeed optimization, a batch size of 1, learning rate of 5e-6, and 3 epochs on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

#### 3.3. RL Training

The policy model is optimized using GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization) with composite reward signals.

- **Reward Model Usage:** For soft constraints, the trained reward model *f*(*o*, *c*) outputs logits, which are converted into a scalar probability *R*ˢ(*o*, *c*) ∈ representing satisfaction.
- **Constraint-Level Reward Prediction:** For an instruction *x*ₖ with *k* constraints and a policy-generated response *o*ₖ, the sample-level reward *R*f is aggregated as the average of individual constraint rewards: *R*f = (1/*k*) Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ *r*ᵢ, where *r*ᵢ is *R*ˢ(*o*ₖ, *c*ᵢ) for soft constraints and *R*ʰ(*o*ₖ, *c*ᵢ) for hard constraints. For reasoning tasks, *R*f is 1 for correct answers and 0 otherwise. This composite reward acts as the signal for GRPO optimization.
- **RL Training Details:** Training was performed on 3 nodes (24 NVIDIA A100 GPUs total). Prompts and responses were truncated to 8192 tokens. The optimizer used a learning rate of 1e-6, weight decay of 1e-2, and FSDP with full sharding. Global batch size was 96, with micro-batches of 2 for updates and 16 for experience generation. Gradient clipping was applied. Models were trained for 52 or 260 steps depending on size.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. In-Domain Performance

The proposed method consistently improved instruction-following capabilities across various model architectures and sizes (1.5B to 32B parameters) on in-domain benchmarks (IFEval, CFBench, FollowBench).
- **Non-reasoning models:** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct showed a 21.6% gain on IFEval. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-IF achieved gains of 5.0, 5.0, and 2.4 across the three benchmarks. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct-IF reached 83.0 on IFEval, surpassing SPAR-8B-DPO.
- **Reasoning models:** Distill-Qwen-1.5B-IF achieved gains of 16.5, 3.0, and 3.7. Distill-Qwen-14B-IF showed the highest performance among distilled models with gains of 8.8, 9.0, and 6.4. R1-0528-Qwen3-8B-IF matched VERIF-8B at 87.1 on IFEval.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** The method consistently achieved stronger gains in instruction-following performance and constraint adherence compared to SFT, Self-Rewarding, and ProxyReward, with SFT showing limited or even negative effects on performance.

#### 4.2. Generalizability

The method demonstrated improved instruction-following ability on out-of-domain tasks, including writing tasks, agent tasks (AgentIF), and multi-turn dialogues (MultiChallenge).
- R1-0528-Qwen3-8B achieved performance gains of 6.1 on AgentIF and 7.3 on MultiChallenge after training.

#### 4.3. General Abilities

The trained models largely maintained their general abilities across benchmarks such as GPQA-Diamond, BBEH, AIME, FOLIO, and MMLU-Pro. The study notes that some performance declines on certain general benchmarks can occur due to the distinct nature of instruction-following versus general reasoning abilities.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Incremental Constraint Curriculum:** Removing the incremental constraint curriculum led to performance drops across benchmarks and resulted in sparser reward signals during training, indicating its role in providing dense learning signals.
- **Rule-based Reward Modeling:** Eliminating the explicit rule-based reward for hard constraints also resulted in performance degradation, emphasizing the importance of precise verification for such constraints.
- **Reward Model Comparison:** The self-supervised reward model (Our-RM-7B) achieved competitive human alignment (Kendall Tau 62.7, Position Consistency 83.3 for 4 constraints) compared to LLM-as-a-judge (QwQ-32B) but demonstrated significantly faster inference speed (approximately 100x faster than QwQ-32B and 30x faster than IF-Verifier-7B) and led to better RL training performance. QwQ-32B with instruction-level scoring, while showing higher human alignment, produced sparse rewards hindering RL training.

#### 4.5. Analysis

- **Consistency Analysis:**
 - **Qualitative:** Post-training, models exhibited improved consistency between their internal thinking processes and generated outputs. Before training, models might acknowledge constraints in their "think" trace but fail to incorporate them in the output. After training, the model's thinking became more strategic and aligned with the final, constraint-compliant output.
 - **Quantitative:** GPT-4.1 evaluation revealed that training significantly enhanced the consistency scores between the model’s reasoning and responses on IFEval and CFBench, explaining the observed performance improvements and generalization.
- **Training Dynamics:**
 - Both Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and R1-Distill-Qwen-7B showed steadily increasing rewards, converging around 0.7.
 - Response length dynamics varied: Instruct models generally increased response length over training steps for instruction-following and math tasks, suggesting more explicit reasoning. Distilled models, particularly for math and science, often exhibited an initial expansion followed by a contraction, implying improved efficiency or conciseness in their already strong reasoning abilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework that offers a method for enhancing instruction-following capabilities in large language models without relying on external supervision. This represents a significant contribution by addressing key limitations of existing approaches.

The **significance** of this work lies in:
- **Elimination of External Supervision:** By demonstrating that reward signals can be derived directly from instructions and pseudo-labels generated for reward model training, the framework reduces the dependency on costly human annotation or proprietary, high-capacity models. This makes the approach more scalable and accessible.
- **Improved Training Efficiency:** The constraint decomposition strategy provides dense reward signals, which is crucial for effective learning in multi-constraint tasks where sparse rewards often hinder progress. Furthermore, the constraint-wise binary classification for reward modeling offers computational efficiency compared to generative reward models.
- **Demonstrated Performance and Generalization:** The consistent and substantial improvements observed across diverse in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks, including complex agentic and multi-turn instruction-following tasks, indicate the robustness and versatility of the proposed method. The analysis of improved consistency between a model's thinking and output provides insights into the mechanism of performance enhancement.

The **potential impact** of this work includes:
- **Broader Adoption of RL for LLM Alignment:** A self-supervised, label-free RL framework could facilitate the wider application of reinforcement learning for aligning LLMs with complex user instructions, particularly for organizations with limited access to extensive human annotation resources or powerful proprietary models.
- **Enhanced Real-world Reliability:** LLMs trained with this framework could exhibit more reliable and consistent adherence to complex instructions in practical applications, from conversational AI and content generation to autonomous agents, thereby improving user experience and trust.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The approach to pseudo-label generation and incremental curriculum learning for instruction following may serve as a foundation for further research into self-supervised and efficient RL paradigms for LLM training.

**Limitations** noted by the authors include the lack of validation on larger-scale models (e.g., 72B parameters) due to computational resource constraints, though experiments on smaller models suggest scalability. Additionally, the diversity of the synthetically generated multi-constraint dataset, in terms of task types and constraint types, remains relatively limited compared to the complexity of real-world user instructions.

**Ethical considerations** involved the compensation and privacy of annotators recruited by the institution for evaluating pseudo-label quality, ensuring they were paid above minimum wage and consented to data use for research.
