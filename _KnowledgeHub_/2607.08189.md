# Input-Constrained Spatiotemporal Tubes for Safe Navigation of Unknown Euler-Lagrange Systems in Dynamic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science developed a real-time control framework that extends Spatiotemporal Tubes to guarantee safe navigation for unknown Euler-Lagrange systems, ensuring Finite-Time Reach-Avoid-Stay specifications while explicitly adhering to actuator input limits. The framework provides offline-verifiable conditions and was validated on mobile robots, quadrotors, and spacecraft.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems require robust control for safe navigation in dynamic, uncertain environments, particularly for systems with unknown dynamics and inherent actuator input constraints.
- Prior Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) frameworks for unknown dynamics often overlooked actuator input limits, which can lead to control input violations in challenging scenarios.
- Many existing control strategies struggle to provide formal safety and task completion guarantees while simultaneously respecting physical actuator boundaries in real-time.

## Method

- The framework introduces an **Input-Constrained Spatiotemporal Tube (STT)** whose center and radius dynamics are designed to be compatible with available control authority for unknown Euler-Lagrange systems.
- A ==model-free, closed-form control law== is synthesized using ==bounded transformation functions== to ensure system states remain within the STT while adhering to actuator input limits.
- Offline-verifiable feasibility conditions are derived to systematically design trackable STTs and certify the satisfaction of ==finite-time reach-avoid-stay (FT-RAS)== specifications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of autonomous systems in safety-critical applications, such as autonomous driving, aerial robotics, and industrial automation, necessitates robust control frameworks that can ensure safety in complex, dynamic, and uncertain environments. A significant challenge in this domain is the development of real-time control strategies for systems with unknown dynamics and inherent actuator input constraints.

Existing approaches to autonomous navigation and safety-critical control present various limitations:
- **Classical Planning Approaches (e.g., A*, RRT, Artificial Potential Fields):** These methods often focus on generating collision-free paths without explicitly considering system dynamics or actuator constraints, potentially leading to dynamically infeasible or untrackable trajectories. They typically lack formal safety guarantees and are susceptible to local minima.
- **Model-Based Optimization Approaches (e.g., Model Predictive Control - MPC):** While MPC can account for input and state constraints and ensure recursive feasibility and closed-loop stability, it heavily relies on accurate system models and requires online optimization, which can be computationally intensive and may not provide formal task-level guarantees for reach-avoid objectives.
- **Control Barrier Functions (CBFs):** CBFs are widely used for safety-critical control, but basic implementations may not guarantee safety under actuator constraints due to potential optimization infeasibility. Extensions addressing input constraints or dynamic unsafe sets often depend on accurate models, specific function choices, or offline training, limiting real-time applicability in dynamic environments.
- **Symbolic Control:** This class of methods offers correct-by-construction controller synthesis for known nonlinear dynamics and input constraints. However, it suffers from the curse of dimensionality, with synthesis time growing exponentially with system state dimension, primarily limiting it to offline controller synthesis in static environments.
- **Existing Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) Frameworks:** Previous STT-based approaches have been employed for synthesizing approximation-free, closed-form controllers for Temporal Reach-Avoid-Stay (T-RAS) tasks, even with dynamic unsafe sets and unknown dynamics. However, these frameworks generally do not explicitly account for actuator input constraints, which can lead to control input violations in challenging scenarios or when prescribed-time reaching requirements demand high control effort.

This paper positions itself by addressing the identified gaps. It aims to develop a real-time control framework that combines the benefits of STT (approximation-free, closed-form, unknown dynamics) with explicit consideration of actuator input constraints, providing formal safety and task completion guarantees in dynamic environments, a capability that, according to the authors, is not fully met by existing methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time control framework for unknown Euler–Lagrange (EL) systems that guarantees the satisfaction of Finite-Time Reach-Avoid-Stay (FT-RAS) specifications while explicitly adhering to actuator input limits.

The motivation for this work arises from several critical practical and theoretical challenges:
- **Safety in Autonomous Systems:** The increasing deployment of autonomous robots in safety-critical domains necessitates formal safety guarantees. The ability to guarantee that a system will reach a target, avoid unsafe regions, and stay within the target without collision is fundamental.
- **Unknown System Dynamics:** In real-world applications, precise models of robotic systems are often unavailable or difficult to obtain due to factors like varying payloads, environmental changes, or system degradation. The framework seeks to operate effectively without requiring an accurate system model.
- **Dynamic and Uncertain Environments:** Autonomous systems frequently operate in environments with moving obstacles and disturbances. The framework aims to provide robust performance in such dynamic and uncertain conditions.
- **Actuator Input Constraints:** All physical robotic platforms are subject to limitations on their actuators (e.g., maximum force, torque, velocity). Neglecting these constraints can lead to infeasible control commands, actuator saturation, degraded performance, and ultimately, safety violations. This research is specifically motivated to explicitly integrate and respect these physical limits in the control design.
- **Real-Time Implementation:** For practical deployment, control solutions must be computationally efficient and suitable for real-time operation, avoiding computationally expensive online optimization or extensive offline synthesis.

To achieve these objectives, the paper's key contributions are:
1. **Input-Constrained STT Framework:** Proposing an STT framework designed to ensure that the generated spatiotemporal tubes are compatible with the available control authority for unknown EL systems.
2. **Approximation-Free Control Law:** Developing a closed-form control law based on bounded transformation functions that inherently satisfies actuator input limits and keeps the system state within the synthesized STT.
3. **Offline-Verifiable Feasibility Conditions:** Deriving mathematical conditions that relate actuator limits, system uncertainty bounds, and tube design parameters, enabling systematic design of trackable STTs and certification of FT-RAS satisfaction.
4. **Formal Guarantees:** Proving that when the feasibility conditions are met, the synthesized tube is forward invariant, and the closed-loop system satisfies the FT-RAS specification.
5. **Validation:** Demonstrating the framework's effectiveness through simulations and hardware experiments on various robotic platforms (mobile robot, quadrotor, spacecraft).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology extends the Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) framework to incorporate actuator input constraints for unknown Euler–Lagrange (EL) systems navigating dynamic environments. The approach involves designing a time-varying safe region (the STT) and then synthesizing a model-free, closed-form control law to keep the system state within this tube, while respecting input limits.

#### 3.1. System Definition and Problem Formulation

The system under consideration is an EL system described by $M(x)\ddot{x} + V(x, \dot{x}) + G(x) = \tau + d(t)$, where $x$ is the configuration, $\tau$ is the control input, and $d(t)$ is the external disturbance. The system parameters $M$, $V$, $G$, and disturbance $d$ are unknown, but their boundedness is assumed (Assumptions 1-3). The control input $\tau$ is constrained by a known bound $\bar{\tau}$.

The objective is to satisfy a **Finite-Time Reach-Avoid-Stay (FT-RAS) task** (Definition 2.1): the system must reach a target set $T$ and remain within it after a finite time $t_c$, while continuously avoiding a time-varying unsafe set $U(t)$ composed of dynamic obstacles.

#### 3.2. Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) Design

An STT, denoted $\Gamma(t) = B(c(t), r(t))$, is defined as a time-varying ball centered at $c(t)$ with radius $r(t)$. It must satisfy three conditions (Definition 2.4):
- Positive radius: $r(t) > 0$.
- Reach-and-stay: $\Gamma(0)$ is in the initial set $S$, and $\Gamma(t)$ is in the target set $T$ for $t \ge t_c$.
- Avoidance: $\Gamma(t) \cap U(t) = \emptyset$.

To design the STT, initial and target points $s$ and $\eta$ are chosen, defining initial and target balls $\hat{S}$ and $\hat{T}$.
- **STT Center Dynamics ($ \dot{c}(t)$):** The center dynamics are split into two cases:
 1. **Far from obstacles ($d^{(j)}(c(t), o^{(j)}(t)) > r_a$):** The center is primarily driven towards the target $\eta$ by a term $\gamma(c(t), \eta)$ with a constant $k_1$.
 2. **Near an obstacle ($d^{(j)}(c(t), o^{(j)}(t)) \le r_a$):** A weighted combination of the goal-directed term and obstacle avoidance terms ($m^{(j)}$ for repulsion, $v^{(j)}$ for tangential movement) is used, with constants $k_1, k_2, k_3$. This ensures the tube center maintains a minimum distance $r_{min}$ from obstacles.
 Assumptions 4 and 5 (obstacle separation and eventual safe target passage) are made to facilitate analysis and guarantee task completion.
- **STT Radius Dynamics ($\dot{r}(t)$):** The tube's radius adapts dynamically. It starts at a maximum $r_{max}$ and shrinks when approaching an unsafe set to avoid collision, expanding when moving away. The dynamics are designed using a smooth approximation of the minimum operator, ensuring $r(t) \le \min(r_{max}, d_1^{(j)}(t))$, where $d_1^{(j)}(t)$ is the distance to the $j$-th unsafe set.
- **Theoretical Guarantee (Theorem 3.3):** This theorem formally proves that the designed STT satisfies the FT-RAS conditions: it starts in the initial set and reaches the target in finite time, avoids all unsafe sets, and maintains a positive radius.
- **Bounds on STT Dynamics (Lemma 3.5):** Upper bounds $\bar{c}$ and $\bar{r}$ are derived for the Euclidean norm of the time derivatives of the STT center and radius, respectively. These bounds are crucial for deriving the feasibility conditions.

#### 3.3. Controller Synthesis

A two-stage, backstepping-like procedure is used to synthesize a model-free, closed-form control law:
- **Stage 1 (Virtual Velocity $v_r$):** An intermediate virtual velocity input $v_r(x, t)$ is designed to ensure the system state $x(t)$ remains within the STT. This is achieved by defining a normalized error $e_1(x, t) = \frac{\|x(t) - c(t)\|}{r(t)}$ and using a bounded transformation function $\Psi$ (Appendix A) to drive $e_1$ towards zero, preventing the state from leaving the tube. The parameter $\bar{v}$ controls the magnitude of this virtual velocity.
- **Stage 2 (Acceleration Level Control $\tau$):** An acceleration-level control input $\tau(t)$ is designed to ensure that the actual system velocity $v(t)$ tracks the virtual velocity $v_r(t)$. This is done by controlling the velocity tracking error $e_v(t) = v(t) - v_r(t)$ to remain within an exponentially decaying funnel constraint $\rho_v(t)$. The control law $\tau(t) = -\text{diag}(\Psi(\varepsilon_v))\bar{\tau}$ utilizes the transformation function $\Psi$ on a normalized error $\varepsilon_v$ to directly apply a bounded torque $\bar{\tau}$, ensuring actuator limits are respected. Lemma 4.1 provides a uniform bound on the reference acceleration $\dot{v}_r$, denoted $a_r$.

#### 3.4. Feasibility Conditions

Two key feasibility conditions are derived to establish a relationship between system parameters, STT design, and available control authority (Eqs. 27 and 28):
1. $\bar{v} \ge \|p_v\| + \bar{c} + \bar{r}$: This condition ensures that the maximum allowable virtual velocity $\bar{v}$ is sufficient to keep the state within the STT, considering the initial funnel width $p_v$ and the bounds on STT center and radius dynamics.
2. $\bar{\tau} \ge \frac{1}{m}(V^{\max}_M + m_i \bar{d} + \mu_v(p_v - q_v) + a_r e_n)$: This condition ensures that the maximum allowable torque $\bar{\tau}$ is sufficient to track the virtual velocity, accounting for system uncertainties (Coriolis, gravity, disturbance), funnel decay rates, and the bound on reference acceleration $a_r$.
These conditions are designed to be verifiable offline.

#### 3.5. Main Theorem (Theorem 4.3)

The theorem formally states that if the initial state is within the tube ($x(0) \in \Gamma(0)$) and the feasibility conditions are satisfied, then the proposed control laws ensure that the system state $x(t)$ remains within the STT for all time, thereby satisfying the FT-RAS specification while respecting input constraints. The proof proceeds by contradiction, demonstrating that if the state or velocity error were to violate their respective bounds, it would contradict the feasibility conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents a real-time control framework that extends the Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) concept to explicitly address actuator input constraints for unknown Euler–Lagrange (EL) systems in dynamic environments. The core findings are encapsulated in the theoretical guarantees and validated through comprehensive simulations and hardware experiments.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Contributions

- **Input-Constrained STT Design:** The paper introduces a novel design for STT center and radius dynamics that is explicitly compatible with actuator input limitations, unlike prior STT formulations.
- **Approximation-Free Control Law:** A closed-form control law is developed using bounded transformation functions, ensuring that the generated control inputs always remain within prescribed actuator limits while keeping the system state confined to the STT.
- **Offline-Verifiable Feasibility Conditions:** Crucial feasibility conditions are derived. These conditions link the available control authority (maximum velocity and torque), system uncertainty bounds, and STT design parameters. They provide a systematic method to design trackable STTs and formally certify the satisfaction of finite-time reach-avoid-stay (FT-RAS) specifications.
- **Formal Safety and Task Guarantees:** The framework is proven to ensure that, whenever the derived feasibility conditions are met, the synthesized STT is forward invariant, and the closed-loop system robustly satisfies the FT-RAS specification, including obstacle avoidance, finite-time target reaching, and staying within the target.

#### 4.2. Experimental Validation

The proposed framework was validated through a combination of simulations and real-world hardware experiments across diverse robotic platforms:

- **2D Mobile Robot:**
 - **Simulations:** The framework was tested with a differential drive robot navigating amidst 20 dynamic unsafe sets with random initial positions, radii, and velocities, under bounded disturbances and specified input constraints ($\bar{\tau} =^\top$ N). Results demonstrated that the robot's trajectory remained within the STT, successfully reached the target, and all control inputs consistently stayed within the defined bounds (Figure 2).
 - **Hardware Experiments:** An AgileX differential-drive robot was used in a real-world setting with dynamic obstacles projected onto the floor and state feedback provided by a motion capture system. The robot successfully maintained a safe distance from dynamic unsafe sets, its trajectory remained within the STT, and control inputs respected the $\bar{\tau} =^\top$ N constraint (Figure 3).
- **3D Quadrotor:** Simulations involved a quadrotor in a 3D environment with 4 dynamic unsafe sets and input constraints ($\bar{\tau} =^\top$ N). The results illustrated the quadrotor's ability to safely navigate through the environment, reaching the target while adhering to actuator limits (Figure 4).
- **Rotating Spacecraft:** A 3D simulation of a rigid spacecraft performing attitude reorientation while avoiding a forbidden pointing direction (e.g., Sun-pointing) was conducted. The spacecraft successfully reoriented to the desired attitude, maintaining safety by avoiding the unsafe region and respecting control torque constraints ($\bar{\tau} = [0.23, 0.23, 0.23]^\top$ N·m) (Figure 5).

#### 4.3. Comparison with Existing STT Frameworks

A direct comparison with an existing real-time STT framework that does not consider input constraints was presented. While the unconstrained STT method led to faster target reaching, it exhibited significant violations of input constraints, particularly during evasive maneuvers or when the target became accessible. In contrast, the proposed input-constrained STT framework, despite potentially resulting in slower task completion, consistently guaranteed adherence to input constraints while ensuring safety and eventual task completion (Figure 6). This highlights an inherent trade-off between task completion speed and strict adherence to physical input limits in practical scenarios.

The framework's computational efficiency was noted due to its approximation-free, closed-form expressions for all controller components, supporting real-time operation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research contributes a significant advancement in the field of autonomous system control, particularly for safety-critical applications operating in dynamic and uncertain environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to bridge several gaps in existing methodologies:

- **Enhanced Safety Guarantees in Real-World Conditions:** By formally integrating actuator input constraints into the STT framework, the proposed method provides more realistic and robust safety guarantees. Neglecting these physical limits in control design can lead to actuator saturation, loss of control, and ultimately, system failure or accidents. This framework mitigates such risks by ensuring that control commands are always physically executable.
- **Applicability to Unknown Systems:** The framework's model-free nature for Euler–Lagrange systems is highly significant. Many real-world robotic systems have dynamics that are difficult to model precisely or vary over time. By not requiring an accurate system model, the approach increases deployability in diverse and less-characterized environments.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Operations:** The closed-form control law and offline-verifiable feasibility conditions eliminate the need for computationally intensive online optimization (common in MPC or CBF-QP approaches) or extensive offline synthesis (typical for symbolic control). This characteristic is crucial for real-time deployment on resource-constrained embedded systems in autonomous platforms.
- **Robustness to Dynamic and Uncertain Environments:** The framework's ability to handle dynamic unsafe sets and bounded disturbances ensures robust operation in complex, unpredictable environments where obstacles move or external forces are present.
- **Systematic Design and Certification:** The derived feasibility conditions offer a systematic methodology for designing control parameters and certifying that the control system can fulfill its safety and task objectives given the system's physical limitations and environmental uncertainties. This aids in the verification and validation processes for safety-critical systems.
- **Broadening the Scope of STT Applications:** The work significantly extends the utility of the STT framework by addressing a critical practical limitation (input constraints). This opens avenues for more widespread application of STTs in practical robotics.

The potential impact includes enabling safer and more reliable deployment of autonomous robots in challenging domains such as:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Navigation in urban environments with unpredictable road users and varying vehicle dynamics.
- **Aerial Robotics:** Safe operation of drones in complex airspace with dynamic obstacles and payload variations.
- **Industrial Automation:** Human-robot collaboration where safety of human workers and respect for robot capabilities are paramount.
- **Spacecraft Operations:** Autonomous maneuvering and attitude control while avoiding collisions or sensitive pointing directions under thruster limitations.

Future work suggested by the authors, such as extending the framework to more general nonlinear and stochastic systems, or incorporating more complex task specifications (e.g., LTL, STL), indicates ongoing research directions to further enhance the versatility and applicability of this approach.
