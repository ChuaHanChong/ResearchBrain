# Multi-Layer Visual Feature Fusion in Multimodal LLMs: Methods, Analysis, and Best Practices

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides a systematic analysis of visual feature integration in Multimodal Large Language Models, focusing on optimal layer selection and fusion strategies. It identifies that selecting specific layers from different stages of a visual encoder and employing an external direct fusion method consistently yield the best performance across various multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Visual feature integration strategies in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have not been systematically studied, despite their importance.
- There is a lack of principled methods for effectively selecting visual layers from pre-trained encoders.
- The optimal strategies for fusing extracted visual features into language models remain unclear, often leading to arbitrary design choices.

## Method

- The paper proposes two principled layer selection strategies: ==similarity-based== and ==proportion-based selection==, aiming to capture diverse visual information.
- A ==2x2 framework== categorizes visual-language fusion strategies based on fusion position (==external vs. internal==) and fusion pattern (==direct vs. modular==).
- Extensive experiments were conducted using **Mini-LLaVA**, a lightweight MLLM, to systematically evaluate the proposed layer selection and fusion strategies across multiple benchmarks.

## Results

- Similarity-based selection of one layer from each of the beginning, middle, and ending stages of the visual encoder consistently yielded the best performance.
- External Direct fusion showed the strongest generalization across all tested benchmarks (GQA, MMBench, MME, TextVQA, OCRBench, CV-Bench, POPE).
- Modular fusion strategies consistently underperformed their direct counterparts across different settings, indicating that additional complexity and parameters can hinder training and generalization.
- Internal Direct fusion exhibited the most substantial relative improvement with increased training data, suggesting its efficacy at larger data scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal visual feature selection involves choosing one representative layer each from the beginning, middle, and ending stages of the visual encoder, based on similarity.
> - External Direct fusion consistently demonstrates superior generalization performance across diverse multimodal benchmarks compared to other strategies.
> - Simpler, direct visual feature integration methods generally outperform more complex modular approaches, indicating that added complexity can hinder effective training.
> - Internal Direct fusion shows significant performance improvements with increased training data, suggesting its potential for scalability in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have emerged as powerful AI systems capable of processing both visual and textual information to perform complex reasoning tasks. While significant attention has been given to the language model components of these systems, the visual feature integration strategies have been less systematically studied. This paper addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive analysis of multi-layer visual feature fusion methods in MLLMs.

The research examines two critical aspects of visual feature integration: how to effectively select visual layers from pre-trained encoders, and which fusion strategies are most effective for incorporating these features into language models. These decisions are often made arbitrarily in current research, leading to potentially suboptimal model performance.

### 2. Visual Feature Integration Approaches

Multimodal LLMs typically incorporate visual information by extracting features from pre-trained visual encoders. The extracted features are then fused with language representations to enable reasoning about visual content. This integration can be approached in different ways:

1. **Single-layer vs. Multi-layer Features**: Traditional approaches often extract features from a single layer of a visual encoder, typically the final layer. However, research indicates that different layers capture different levels of visual information, from low-level features (edges, textures) in earlier layers to high-level semantics (objects, relationships) in later layers. Multi-layer approaches aim to leverage this hierarchical information.

2. **Fusion Position**: Visual features can be integrated either externally (processed before entering the LLM) or internally (injected into various layers of the LLM).

3. **Fusion Pattern**: The integration can occur through direct element-wise operations or via additional modular components that transform the visual features before integration.

### 3. Layer Selection Strategies

The paper proposes two principled approaches for selecting visual layers:

1. **Similarity-based Selection**: This method groups visual layers based on the cosine similarity of their feature representations, creating clusters that represent the beginning, middle, and ending stages of visual processing. One representative layer is then selected from each stage.

2. **Proportion-based Selection**: This simpler approach divides the visual encoder into proportional sections (e.g., former layers and latter layers) based on the total number of layers, then selects representative layers from each section.

Both methods aim to capture diverse visual information across the hierarchy of representations in the visual encoder.

### 4. Fusion Strategy Framework

The paper categorizes fusion strategies along two dimensions, creating a 2×2 framework:

1. **Fusion Position**:
 - **External Fusion**: Visual features are processed outside the LLM and typically concatenated with text embeddings at the input level.
 - **Internal Fusion**: Visual features are injected into multiple layers of the LLM during processing.

2. **Fusion Pattern**:
 - **Direct Fusion**: Visual features are directly combined with language representations through element-wise operations (typically addition).
 - **Modular Fusion**: Additional neural network modules transform visual features before integration with language representations.

This framework yields four distinct strategies: Internal Modular, Internal Direct, External Modular, and External Direct.

### 5. Experimental Methodology

To enable rapid experimentation, the researchers developed Mini-LLaVA, a lightweight model based on LLaVA-1.5 that uses MobileLLaMA as its language model foundation. This allowed for extensive ablation studies across different configurations.

The experiments evaluated multiple aspects:

1. **Layer Selection Evaluation**: Various combinations of visual layers were tested, including single layers, layers from different stages based on similarity, and proportional divisions.

2. **Fusion Strategy Comparison**: All four fusion strategies were systematically evaluated to determine their relative performance.

3. **Benchmark Suite**: Performance was measured across a comprehensive set of benchmarks, including GQA (visual reasoning), MMBench (multimodal understanding), MME (multimodal evaluation), TextVQA (text in images), OCRBench (optical character recognition), CV-Bench (computer vision tasks), and POPE (object hallucination).

The training curve analysis revealed that models using multi-layer features (particularly the triple-layer approach with features from beginning, middle, and ending stages) converged to lower loss values compared to single-layer features. Notably, using all layers led to significantly worse performance.

### 6. Key Findings and Results

The experimental results revealed several important insights:

1. **Optimal Layer Selection**: Selecting one representative layer each from the beginning, middle, and ending stages of the visual encoder (based on similarity clustering) consistently delivered the best performance. This approach outperformed both single-layer and all-layer strategies, as well as proportion-based selections.

2. **Fusion Strategy Performance**: External Direct fusion consistently demonstrated the strongest generalization performance across different benchmarks and configurations. This simpler approach of directly combining visual features with text embeddings at the input level proved more effective than more complex alternatives.

3. **Modules vs. Direct Integration**: Fusion strategies that incorporated additional neural network modules (both internal and external modular approaches) generally underperformed compared to their direct counterparts. This suggests that the added complexity and parameter count of these modules can hinder effective training, particularly with limited data.

4. **Internal vs. External Fusion**: External fusion strategies generally outperformed internal fusion approaches. However, Internal Direct fusion showed promise, particularly when trained with larger datasets, suggesting that with sufficient data, the increased expressive power of injecting features throughout the LLM can be beneficial.

5. **Module Position Impact**: For modular fusion strategies, placing modules in the latter layers of the LLM led to more significant performance degradation compared to earlier layers. This indicates a sensitivity to interference with the higher-level reasoning capabilities of the upper LLM layers.

### 7. Training Data Impact

The research also examined how the effectiveness of different fusion strategies changes with training data scale. The results show that all strategies benefit from increased training data, but to different degrees:

1. **External Fusion Advantage**: External fusion strategies (both direct and modular) maintained their performance advantage across different data scales.

2. **Internal Direct Improvement**: Internal Direct fusion showed the most substantial relative improvement with increased data, suggesting that with sufficient training data, it could potentially match or exceed External Direct performance.

3. **Internal Modular Challenge**: Internal Modular fusion consistently underperformed other strategies, though it did show improvement with more data. The gap, however, remained significant, indicating fundamental limitations with this approach.

### 8. Component Analysis

The researchers investigated how changing key components of the MLLM affected fusion strategy performance:

1. **Visual Encoder Impact**: Replacing the standard CLIP ViT-L/14 with the more advanced SigLIP visual encoder improved performance across most fusion strategies. However, the relative performance ordering of the strategies remained consistent, with External Direct and Internal Direct maintaining their advantages.

2. **Language Model Impact**: Upgrading to the more capable MobileLLaMA 2.7B language model similarly improved performance across strategies while maintaining their relative ordering. This suggests that the fusion strategy findings are robust across different model components.

Despite these architectural variations, the general finding that modular approaches underperform compared to direct fusion remained consistent, suggesting that the complexity and additional parameters of modular designs create fundamental optimization challenges.

### 9. Practical Recommendations

Based on the comprehensive analysis, the paper offers several practical recommendations for researchers and practitioners working with MLLMs:

1. **Layer Selection**: Use a similarity-based approach to select representative layers from the beginning, middle, and ending stages of the visual encoder. This captures diverse visual information without overwhelming the model with redundant features.

2. **Fusion Strategy**: For most applications, External Direct fusion offers the best balance of performance, simplicity, and training stability. This approach directly integrates visual features with text embeddings at the input level through element-wise addition.

3. **Data Scaling**: With larger training datasets, consider exploring Internal Direct fusion, as this approach shows promising improvements with increased data and potentially offers higher expressive power.

4. **Avoid Unnecessary Complexity**: The results consistently show that simpler approaches outperform more complex ones. Avoid introducing unnecessary modules or using too many visual layers, as these can hinder effective training and generalization.

5. **Model Component Selection**: While upgrading visual encoders and language models improves overall performance, the choice of fusion strategy remains important regardless of the specific components used.

### 10. Conclusion

This research provides a systematic investigation into the often overlooked aspects of visual feature integration in Multimodal Large Language Models. By analyzing both layer selection strategies and fusion approaches, the paper offers valuable insights and practical guidance for MLLM development.

The key takeaway is that strategic selection of visual features from different processing stages, combined with a direct fusion approach (particularly at the external level), consistently delivers the strongest performance across various benchmarks and model configurations. As training data and model scale increase, more advanced approaches like Internal Direct fusion may become increasingly viable.

These findings contribute to a more principled understanding of MLLM architecture design and help establish best practices for visual-language integration in these increasingly important AI systems. The recommended approaches not only improve performance but also maintain model simplicity and training efficiency, making them valuable guidelines for both research and practical applications.
