# Multi-Layer Visual Feature Fusion in Multimodal LLMs: Methods, Analysis, and Best Practices

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides a systematic analysis of visual feature integration in Multimodal Large Language Models, focusing on optimal layer selection and fusion strategies. It identifies that selecting specific layers from different stages of a visual encoder and employing an external direct fusion method consistently yield the best performance across various multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Visual feature integration strategies in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have not been systematically studied, despite their importance.
- There is a lack of principled methods for effectively selecting visual layers from pre-trained encoders.
- The optimal strategies for fusing extracted visual features into language models remain unclear, often leading to arbitrary design choices.

## Method

- The paper proposes two principled layer selection strategies: ==similarity-based== and ==proportion-based selection==, aiming to capture diverse visual information.
- A ==2x2 framework== categorizes visual-language fusion strategies based on fusion position (==external vs. internal==) and fusion pattern (==direct vs. modular==).
- Extensive experiments were conducted using **Mini-LLaVA**, a lightweight MLLM, to systematically evaluate the proposed layer selection and fusion strategies across multiple benchmarks.

## Results

- Similarity-based selection of one layer from each of the beginning, middle, and ending stages of the visual encoder consistently yielded the best performance.
- External Direct fusion showed the strongest generalization across all tested benchmarks (GQA, MMBench, MME, TextVQA, OCRBench, CV-Bench, POPE).
- Modular fusion strategies consistently underperformed their direct counterparts across different settings, indicating that additional complexity and parameters can hinder training and generalization.
- Internal Direct fusion exhibited the most substantial relative improvement with increased training data, suggesting its efficacy at larger data scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal visual feature selection involves choosing one representative layer each from the beginning, middle, and ending stages of the visual encoder, based on similarity.
> - External Direct fusion consistently demonstrates superior generalization performance across diverse multimodal benchmarks compared to other strategies.
> - Simpler, direct visual feature integration methods generally outperform more complex modular approaches, indicating that added complexity can hinder effective training.
> - Internal Direct fusion shows significant performance improvements with increased training data, suggesting its potential for scalability in MLLMs.
