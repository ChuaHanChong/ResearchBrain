# Discrete Codebook World Models for Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Aalto University and colleagues introduced DC-MPC, a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that utilizes a Discrete Codebook World Model (DCWM) to achieve competitive performance in continuous control tasks. DCWM employs Finite Scalar Quantization for discrete latent spaces and stochastic categorical dynamics, avoiding observation reconstruction, and matches or exceeds TD-MPC2 across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing discrete latent space world models (e.g., DreamerV2/V3) underperform in state-based continuous control tasks compared to continuous latent space methods (e.g., TD-MPC2).
- Prior discrete latent models often rely on observation reconstruction, which can be detrimental for continuous control, especially in state-based settings.
- A gap exists in determining if discrete latent spaces can be effectively designed for continuous control without observation reconstruction, challenging the assumption that continuous spaces are inherently superior.

## Method

![[2503.00653-method.png]]
*The Discrete Codebook World Model (DCWM) architecture. Observations are encoded to continuous vectors, quantized using Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) into discrete codes from a fixed codebook, and used to train stochastic dynamics with cross-entropy loss. The model uses Straight-Through (ST) Gumbel-softmax sampling for multi-step predictions while maintaining gradient flow.*

- Developed **DC-MPC** (Discrete Codebook Model Predictive Control), an MBRL algorithm built on a **DCWM** (Discrete Codebook World Model) that utilizes ==discrete and stochastic latent spaces==.
- Employs ==Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)== for latent state discretization, creating dense, multi-dimensional codebook encodings that preserve ordinal relationships.
- Models probabilistic latent transition dynamics as a ==classifier==, trained with ==cross-entropy loss==, and uses the ==Straight-Through Gumbel-softmax trick== for multi-step gradient flow, critically without observation reconstruction.

## Results

- DC-MPC achieved competitive or superior performance compared to TD-MPC2 and substantially outperformed DreamerV3, SAC, and TD-MPC across **30 DMControl**, **45 Meta-World**, and **5 MyoSuite** tasks.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that classification (cross-entropy) for dynamics and stochasticity improved performance over regression or deterministic models in discrete latent spaces.
- Integrating DCWM's discrete latent space into the TD-MPC2 architecture led to improved performance over vanilla TD-MPC2 on DMControl tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete latent spaces, when properly designed with codebook encodings and stochastic categorical dynamics trained via classification, offer advantages for continuous control, achieving competitive performance against continuous latent space methods.
> - Observation reconstruction loss is detrimental to learning in state-based continuous control tasks and should be avoided for effective world modeling in this context.
> - Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) effectively creates dense discrete codebook representations that preserve ordinal relationships and are computationally more efficient than one-hot encodings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the intersection of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and representation learning, specifically within the realm of world models. World models, which learn to predict environment dynamics, are crucial for MBRL as they enable agents to plan and "imagine" future outcomes, thus improving sample efficiency over model-free methods.

The current landscape of world models for continuous control is characterized by a dichotomy in latent space design:
- **Discrete Latent Spaces:** Pioneered by approaches like DreamerV2/V3 (Hafner et al., 2022; 2023), these models have shown impressive performance in discrete action settings and visual control tasks. DreamerV2/V3 explicitly use one-hot encodings for their discrete latent states and rely on *observation reconstruction* losses for learning representations. However, they have been noted to underperform in state-based continuous control benchmarks.
- **Continuous Latent Spaces:** Methods such as TD-MPC (Hansen et al., 2022) and its successor, TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2023), have achieved state-of-the-art results in state-based continuous control. Crucially, these methods typically employ continuous latent spaces and, importantly, *avoid observation reconstruction* losses, instead relying on self-supervised latent-state consistency objectives. The literature (e.g., Kostrikov et al., 2020; Yarats et al., 2021a; Lutter et al., 2021) has increasingly pointed to the potential detrimental effects of observation reconstruction in continuous control due to irrelevant visual details.

The present work directly addresses a critical gap: whether the benefits of discrete latent spaces, observed in DreamerV2/V3, can be translated and even enhanced for *continuous control*, particularly *without* the reliance on observation reconstruction. It questions the prevailing assumption that continuous latent spaces are inherently superior for continuous control tasks in the absence of visual inputs. The paper further delves into the specifics of discrete encoding schemes (codebook vs. one-hot vs. label) and the nature of transition dynamics (deterministic vs. stochastic, unimodal vs. multimodal), building on recent insights that classification objectives might offer advantages over regression for learning value functions (Farebrother et al., 2024). By challenging existing norms and proposing a novel combination of techniques, this paper aims to advance the state-of-the-art in model-based continuous control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and demonstrate the utility of discrete latent spaces for continuous control tasks within a world model framework, aiming to achieve competitive or superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art algorithms that predominantly use continuous latent spaces.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

1. **Re-evaluating Discrete Latent Spaces for Continuous Control:** Despite the success of DreamerV2/V3 in discrete action and visual control tasks using discrete latent spaces, their performance in state-based continuous control has lagged behind methods like TD-MPC2, which use continuous latent spaces. The authors are motivated to explore if discrete latent spaces, when designed appropriately, can offer unique benefits for continuous control.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Observation Reconstruction:** Previous discrete latent space models (like DreamerV2/V3) rely on observation reconstruction, which has been shown to be unreliable and even detrimental for continuous control, especially in state-based settings. A core motivation is to learn effective discrete latent representations *without* requiring observation reconstruction, similar to how TD-MPC2 operates.
3. **Exploring Novel Discrete Encodings:** The paper aims to demonstrate that discrete *codebook encodings* offer advantages over alternative discrete representations like one-hot (used in DreamerV2/V3) and label encodings. The hypothesis is that codebook encodings, particularly those derived from quantization techniques like FSQ, can preserve ordinal relationships and offer better expressiveness, dimensionality, and density.
4. **Investigating Stochastic Dynamics and Classification Objectives:** The authors are motivated to understand the impact of modeling (i) stochastic vs. deterministic transition dynamics and (ii) learning with classification (cross-entropy) vs. regression (mean squared error) for latent space transitions. They specifically explore if multimodal categorical distributions (inherent in discrete stochastic dynamics trained with cross-entropy) offer benefits over unimodal Gaussian distributions or deterministic dynamics.
5. **Developing a Competitive Model-Based RL Algorithm:** Ultimately, the goal is to integrate these insights into a new model-based RL algorithm, DC-MPC (Discrete Codebook Model Predictive Control), which is both sample-efficient and performs competitively against top-tier algorithms like TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3 across a wide range of continuous control benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **DC-MPC (Discrete Codebook Model Predictive Control)**, is a model-based RL algorithm built upon a novel world model named **DCWM (Discrete Codebook World Model)**. The approach centers on a discrete and stochastic latent space, combined with decision-time planning.

#### 3.1. Discrete Codebook World Model (DCWM)

DCWM is designed with six core components:

1. **Encoder ($e_\theta$):** Maps raw observations ($o$) to continuous latent vectors ($x \in \mathbb{R}^{b \times d}$).
2. **Latent Quantization ($f$):** A crucial step where the continuous latent vector $x$ is quantized into one of the discrete codes ($c \in \mathcal{C}$) from a fixed codebook $\mathcal{C}$. This is achieved using **Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)** (Mentzer et al., 2024). FSQ discretizes each dimension of the latent vector into a predefined set of values, effectively creating a multi-dimensional hypercube codebook. This approach preserves ordinal relationships in multiple dimensions, maintains low dimensionality, and generates dense codes, contrasting with the high-dimensional, sparse nature of one-hot encodings. The quantization function $f$ uses a `round` operation, which is made differentiable via the straight-through gradient estimator (`round_ste`).
3. **Dynamics ($p_\phi$):** Models probabilistic latent transition dynamics $p_\phi(c' | c, a)$ as a classifier. Given the current discrete latent state $c$ and action $a$, an MLP predicts logits over all possible discrete codes in the codebook, which are then converted to probabilities via softmax. This formulation captures potentially multimodal distributions over the next state.
4. **Reward ($R_\xi$):** A neural network that predicts the immediate reward $r$ given the current latent state $c$ and action $a$.
5. **Value ($q_\psi$):** An ensemble of $N_q=5$ action-value functions that estimate the expected future return.
6. **Policy Prior ($\pi_\eta$):** A neural network that suggests an action $a$ given the current latent state $c$.

**World Model Training:**
DCWM is trained in a self-supervised manner using backpropagation through time (BPTT) with the following key loss terms, *without* an observation reconstruction objective:
- **Latent-state Consistency (Cross-Entropy Loss):** The dynamics model $p_\phi$ is trained to predict the next latent code $c_{h+1}$ by maximizing its log-likelihood, using a cross-entropy loss against the target latent code (obtained by encoding the true next observation $o_{h+1}$ and applying `stop_gradient`).
- **Stochastic Dynamics and Gradient Flow:** During multi-step predictions within the world model, uncertainty is propagated by sampling from the categorical dynamics distribution. To allow gradients to flow back through these discrete samples to the encoder, the **Straight-Through (ST) Gumbel-softmax trick** (Jang et al., 2017; Maddison et al., 2017) is employed.
- **Reward Prediction Loss:** The reward model $R_\xi$ is trained with a mean squared error loss to accurately predict rewards in the latent space.

**Policy and Value Learning:**
The policy $\pi_\eta(c)$ and action-value functions $q_\psi(c,a)$ are learned in the discrete latent space using an actor-critic method inspired by TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018). Key features include:
- **N-step Returns:** Augments the loss with N-step returns to reduce bias.
- **Ensemble Critics (REDQ-like):** An ensemble of $N_q=5$ critics is learned, and for TD target calculation, a minimum of two randomly subsampled critics is used (following REDQ, Chen et al., 2021), reducing approximation error and improving stability.
- **Policy Smoothing:** Clipped Gaussian noise is added to target actions for policy smoothing.

#### 3.2. Decision-Time Planning (DC-MPC)

DC-MPC leverages the learned world model for decision-time planning using **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** with a modified **Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)** control (Williams et al., 2015).
- **Trajectory Optimization:** At each environment step, MPPI iteratively samples candidate action sequences, evaluates their returns (using the world model's reward predictor and Q-functions for bootstrapping), and refits a sampling distribution (mean and variance of a Gaussian) based on a weighted average of the best trajectories.
- **Expected Next State for Planning:** Crucially, during planning (unlike training), DC-MPC does *not* sample from the stochastic transition dynamics. Instead, it takes the **expected code** for the next latent state, which is a weighted sum over all possible codes based on their predicted probabilities. This reduces unwanted stochasticity during planning.
- **Exploration:** Exploration noise is added to the selected action before applying it in the environment. The planning process is warm-started using action sequences from the learned prior policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, answering the posed research questions:

1. **Benefits of Discrete Latent Spaces (RQ1):** The experiments (Fig. 3, Fig. 9) conclusively demonstrate that discrete latent spaces, as implemented in DCWM, offer significant benefits in terms of sample efficiency for continuous control tasks compared to continuous latent spaces (e.g., those using MSE regression or even continuous stochastic dynamics like Gaussian/GMM). This challenges the notion that continuous spaces are inherently superior for continuous control.

2. **Importance of Classification, Stochasticity, and Multimodality (RQ2):**
 - **Classification vs. Regression:** Training a discrete latent space with cross-entropy (classification) significantly outperforms training with MSE regression (Fig. 3).
 - **Stochastic vs. Deterministic Dynamics:** Formulating stochastic transition dynamics and training with cross-entropy (using ST Gumbel-softmax sampling for multi-step predictions) further boosts performance over deterministic discrete latent spaces trained with classification (Fig. 3). This highlights the importance of propagating uncertainty and allowing for potentially multimodal transitions.
 - **Continuous Stochastic Dynamics:** While stochastic continuous latent spaces (Gaussian or GMM distributions) sometimes improved performance on DMControl, they drastically underperformed on Meta-World tasks (Fig. 9), suggesting that the benefits of stochasticity are more pronounced and consistent within a discrete latent space framework.

3. **Superiority of Codebook Encodings (RQ3):** Discrete codebook encodings (FSQ) are shown to be superior to one-hot and label encodings (Fig. 4).
 - **One-hot encodings** matched sample efficiency in some tasks but led to significantly slower training due to their high dimensionality and sparsity for networks processing them.
 - **Label encodings** struggled or failed to learn in harder tasks, as they cannot effectively model the multi-dimensional ordinal relationships inherent in the latent space, incorrectly implying simple linear ordering. The codebook's ability to represent multi-dimensional ordinal structures is a key advantage.

4. **State-of-the-Art Performance (RQ4):** DC-MPC performs highly competitively, often matching or exceeding the performance of TD-MPC2, and significantly outperforming DreamerV3, SAC, and TD-MPC across a wide array of challenging benchmarks including 30 DMControl tasks, 45 Meta-World tasks, and 5 MyoSuite tasks (Fig. 5, 14, 16, 18).
 - DC-MPC notably excels in high-dimensional and complex locomotion tasks from DMControl, such as Dog and Humanoid environments (Fig. 13), where baselines like DreamerV3 struggle significantly.
 - A critical finding is that *observation reconstruction harms performance* in continuous control. Adding a reconstruction loss to DC-MPC significantly degraded its performance or prevented learning entirely (Fig. 20), supporting prior work on this issue.
 - **Transferability of DCWM:** When DCWM's discrete and stochastic latent space was incorporated into the TD-MPC2 architecture, it led to improved performance over vanilla TD-MPC2 on DMControl tasks (Fig. 6), further validating the advantages of the proposed latent space design.

5. **Robustness and Efficiency:** DC-MPC's performance was found to be robust to variations in codebook size and latent dimension within reasonable ranges (Fig. 7, 8). FSQ was also shown to be simpler and perform equally well or better than Vector Quantization (VQ) (Fig. 10). The use of an ensemble of critics (REDQ-like) provided slight benefits over standard double Q-learning (Fig. 11).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "DISCRETE CODEBOOK WORLD MODELS FOR CONTINUOUS CONTROL" paper makes several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact:

1. **Re-framing Latent Space Design for Continuous Control:** The most significant impact is challenging the prevailing paradigm that continuous latent spaces are inherently superior for state-based continuous control. By demonstrating that appropriately designed discrete latent spaces, particularly those utilizing codebook encodings and stochastic dynamics trained with classification, can achieve state-of-the-art performance, the paper opens a new avenue for research. It suggests that the benefits of discrete representations (e.g., better generalization, robustness to compounding errors, multimodal modeling capabilities) are not limited to tasks with discrete actions or visual observation reconstruction.

2. **Novel World Model Architecture (DCWM):** The introduction of DCWM, which integrates Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) for discrete codebook generation, stochastic categorical dynamics, and a cross-entropy loss, provides a robust and efficient framework for world modeling. The absence of an observation reconstruction loss, in favor of a latent-state consistency objective, is validated as a crucial design choice for continuous control.

3. **Advancing Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** DC-MPC's competitive performance against powerful baselines like TD-MPC2 and its superiority over DreamerV3 in complex, high-dimensional continuous control tasks (like those in DMControl Dog and Humanoid, Meta-World, and MyoSuite) positions it as a leading algorithm in the field. Its high sample efficiency is particularly valuable for real-world robotics and complex simulation environments where data collection is expensive.

4. **Implications for Representation Learning:** The detailed ablation studies on different discrete encodings (codebook vs. one-hot vs. label) highlight the importance of careful design for discrete latent spaces. The finding that codebook encodings preserve multi-dimensional ordinal relationships and are computationally more efficient than one-hot encodings could influence representation learning beyond world models.

5. **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly outlines several promising avenues for future work, including:
 - **Robustness and Exploration:** Developing more robust hyperparameter tuning and principled exploration mechanisms (e.g., based on epistemic uncertainty) to remove task-specific noise schedules.
 - **Alternative Backbones:** Investigating the integration of DCWM with other world model backbones like Transformers and Diffusion models, which are gaining traction in generative modeling and RL.
 - **Scaling and Generalist Agents:** Exploring how DC-MPC scales to larger models and its effectiveness for developing generalist (multi-embodiment) world models, a critical step towards more versatile AI agents.

Overall, this research offers a compelling case for the re-evaluation of discrete latent spaces in model-based continuous control. By providing a concrete, high-performing algorithm and comprehensive experimental evidence, it lays the groundwork for future innovations in sample-efficient and robust reinforcement learning, potentially impacting the development of advanced robotic systems and AI agents.
