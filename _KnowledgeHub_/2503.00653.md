# Discrete Codebook World Models for Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Aalto University and colleagues introduced DC-MPC, a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that utilizes a Discrete Codebook World Model (DCWM) to achieve competitive performance in continuous control tasks. DCWM employs Finite Scalar Quantization for discrete latent spaces and stochastic categorical dynamics, avoiding observation reconstruction, and matches or exceeds TD-MPC2 across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing discrete latent space world models (e.g., DreamerV2/V3) underperform in state-based continuous control tasks compared to continuous latent space methods (e.g., TD-MPC2).
- Prior discrete latent models often rely on observation reconstruction, which can be detrimental for continuous control, especially in state-based settings.
- A gap exists in determining if discrete latent spaces can be effectively designed for continuous control without observation reconstruction, challenging the assumption that continuous spaces are inherently superior.

## Method

![[2503.00653-method.png]]
*The Discrete Codebook World Model (DCWM) architecture. Observations are encoded to continuous vectors, quantized using Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) into discrete codes from a fixed codebook, and used to train stochastic dynamics with cross-entropy loss. The model uses Straight-Through (ST) Gumbel-softmax sampling for multi-step predictions while maintaining gradient flow.*

- Developed **DC-MPC** (Discrete Codebook Model Predictive Control), an MBRL algorithm built on a **DCWM** (Discrete Codebook World Model) that utilizes ==discrete and stochastic latent spaces==.
- Employs ==Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)== for latent state discretization, creating dense, multi-dimensional codebook encodings that preserve ordinal relationships.
- Models probabilistic latent transition dynamics as a ==classifier==, trained with ==cross-entropy loss==, and uses the ==Straight-Through Gumbel-softmax trick== for multi-step gradient flow, critically without observation reconstruction.

## Results

- DC-MPC achieved competitive or superior performance compared to TD-MPC2 and substantially outperformed DreamerV3, SAC, and TD-MPC across **30 DMControl**, **45 Meta-World**, and **5 MyoSuite** tasks.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that classification (cross-entropy) for dynamics and stochasticity improved performance over regression or deterministic models in discrete latent spaces.
- Integrating DCWM's discrete latent space into the TD-MPC2 architecture led to improved performance over vanilla TD-MPC2 on DMControl tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete latent spaces, when properly designed with codebook encodings and stochastic categorical dynamics trained via classification, offer advantages for continuous control, achieving competitive performance against continuous latent space methods.
> - Observation reconstruction loss is detrimental to learning in state-based continuous control tasks and should be avoided for effective world modeling in this context.
> - Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) effectively creates dense discrete codebook representations that preserve ordinal relationships and are computationally more efficient than one-hot encodings.
