# RLDG: Robotic Generalist Policy Distillation via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> RLDG (Reinforcement Learning Distilled Generalist) proposes a method to enhance the precision and robustness of robotic foundation models by distilling knowledge from specialized RL policies. This is achieved by using RL to autonomously generate high-quality, optimal training data, which is then used to fine-tune generalist models like OpenVLA and Octo, resulting in policies that achieve higher success rates and better generalization on tasks requiring precise manipulation.

## Problem

- Robotic foundation models, while generalist, often lack the high precision required for contact-rich or dexterous manipulation tasks due to inconsistencies in human demonstration data.
- Traditional reinforcement learning excels at precise, task-specific skills but struggles with broad generalization to unseen scenarios.
- Collecting high-quality, consistent human teleoperation data for fine-tuning generalist robotic policies is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and expensive.

## Method

- Train specialized Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies for individual robotic tasks until they achieve high precision and success rates, leveraging sample-efficient real-world RL frameworks.
- Collect high-quality, optimal trajectories by autonomously rolling out the converged ==RL policies==, filtering out failures, and strategically combining with human data for multi-stage tasks.
- Fine-tune pre-trained robotic generalist models (e.g., **OpenVLA**, **Octo**) using the collected RL-generated dataset via a ==supervised learning objective== to distill precision while retaining generalization.

## Results

- RLDG-trained generalist policies (OpenVLA, Octo) achieved significantly higher success rates on precision tasks like Connector Insertion (up to **37%** higher) and FMB Insertion (up to **33%** higher) compared to human-trained baselines, extending to unseen generalization scenarios.
- RLDG demonstrated superior sample efficiency, achieving **100%** success on Connector Insertion with 45 RL episodes compared to 300 human episodes required for the same level of performance.
- RLDG-trained generalists maintained substantially better generalization than original task-specific RL policies, succeeding on unseen Pick and Place scenarios where standalone RL failed, and showing robust performance across multiple unseen connector types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The superior quality and optimality of actions generated by reinforcement learning policies are the primary drivers of performance improvements when fine-tuning robotic generalist models.
> - Reinforcement learning can serve as an automated, scalable source of high-quality training data for foundation models, reducing reliance on human teleoperation.
> - Combining the precision of task-specific RL with the broad generalization of pre-trained foundation models yields policies that excel in both domains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of RLDG (Reinforcement Learning Distilled Generalists) strategically positions itself at the intersection of two rapidly advancing yet distinct paradigms in robotics: robotic foundation models and reinforcement learning (RL).

**Robotic Foundation Models (RFMs):** Recent years have witnessed a surge in the development of generalist robotic policies, often termed "robotic foundation models" (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo). These models leverage large-scale pretraining on vast datasets (often Internet-scale vision-language data) and subsequent fine-tuning on robot demonstrations. This approach has enabled impressive capabilities in understanding natural language instructions and exhibiting zero-shot or few-shot generalization across diverse tasks and environments. However, a significant limitation, as highlighted by the authors, is their performance on tasks requiring high precision, dexterity, and contact-rich interactions. This deficiency often stems from the inherent inconsistencies and lack of precise repeatability in human demonstration data, which forms the basis for their fine-tuning. Human demonstrations, while valuable for capturing task diversity, often fall short in providing the perfectly optimized trajectories needed for fine-grained control.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Robotic Manipulation:** In parallel, reinforcement learning has proven highly effective in learning complex, precise robotic manipulation skills in the real world through direct interaction. RL policies can discover optimal action distributions through trial-and-error, leading to robust and efficient behaviors, especially for challenging tasks like precision insertion or dexterous manipulation. Notable successes include real-world applications of RL for tasks requiring sub-millimeter accuracy. The primary limitation of traditional RL, however, is its struggle with generalization. RL policies are typically task-specific and tend to perform poorly when faced with variations outside their training distribution. Furthermore, scaling RL to process the massive, diverse datasets characteristic of foundation model pretraining remains a significant computational and algorithmic challenge.

**Policy Distillation and Knowledge Transfer:** The concept of distilling knowledge from specialized "expert" policies into more generalist ones is not new in the broader machine learning and RL literature. Methods for transferring skills, combining multiple expert policies, or enhancing generalization through distillation have been explored. However, RLDG introduces a novel application of this concept: distilling knowledge from expert *RL policies* into *pre-trained robotic foundation models*. Crucially, this distillation occurs not through direct policy-to-policy transfer, but indirectly by using RL to *generate superior training data* for the foundation models.

**RLDG's Unique Contribution:** RLDG directly addresses the shortcomings of both paradigms. It leverages RL's strength in optimizing precise behaviors to overcome the "precision deficit" of foundation models trained on inconsistent human data. Simultaneously, it uses foundation models' inherent generalization capabilities to extend the reach of highly specialized RL-learned skills to novel scenarios, a task at which standalone RL policies typically fail. By transforming RL into a *data generation engine* for foundation models, RLDG provides an automated, scalable alternative to human teleoperation for creating high-quality fine-tuning datasets, thus promising a path towards more capable and universally applicable robotic systems that balance precision with broad generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and robustness of generalist robotic policies, specifically pre-trained foundation models, in real-world precision manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and the identification of a critical bottleneck in current robotic learning paradigms:

1. **Addressing the Data Quality Challenge for Robotic Foundation Models:** The authors recognize that while robotic foundation models exhibit impressive flexibility and generalization due to large-scale pretraining, their final performance, particularly on specific, precise tasks, is heavily dependent on the quality of their fine-tuning data. Human teleoperation, the most common source of such data, often suffers from inconsistencies, imperfections, and suboptimality in execution quality and style. These variations are especially detrimental for tasks demanding fine-grained, reactive control, such as contact-rich manipulation (e.g., connector insertion). The motivation is to find a superior, more consistent source of fine-tuning data.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Direct RL Fine-tuning of Foundation Models:** While conceptually appealing, directly fine-tuning large foundation models with reinforcement learning poses significant practical challenges. These include issues of optimization instability, prohibitively high computational costs associated with large model architectures, and the risk of "catastrophic forgetting" of the valuable pre-trained capabilities. This motivates the search for an indirect, more tractable method to imbue foundation models with RL-learned precision.

3. **Leveraging RL's Optimization Capabilities for Data Generation:** The core insight motivating RLDG is that reinforcement learning agents, through reward maximization, can autonomously generate high-quality, optimal trajectories. Unlike human demonstrators, RL agents are capable of discovering and consistently executing highly precise and efficient behaviors, even in complex interaction scenarios. The motivation is to harness this optimization capability to create pristine datasets.

4. **Automating and Scaling Data Collection:** Human teleoperation is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and expensive. By using RL policies to generate data, the authors aim to provide an automated approach that significantly reduces the human effort required for data collection, making the fine-tuning process more cost-effective and scalable.

5. **Improving Generalization Beyond Task-Specific RL:** While RL excels at specific tasks, its policies often lack the broad generalization exhibited by foundation models. The objective is to combine RL's precision with foundation models' generalization, creating a hybrid system that is both highly performant on specific tasks and robust to novel scenarios.

6. **Addressing "Bottleneck" Stages in Multi-Stage Tasks:** For complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks, certain sub-tasks might be particularly challenging and prone to failure (e.g., the precise insertion phase in an assembly task). The motivation is to strategically apply RL data generation to these "bottleneck" segments, while still leveraging human demonstrations for less critical or simpler phases, thereby improving overall task success rates.

In essence, the key objective is to propose a simple yet effective paradigm – RLDG – that uses the strengths of RL (optimal behavior generation) to mitigate the weaknesses of foundation models (data quality sensitivity) and, in turn, uses the strengths of foundation models (generalization) to compensate for the weaknesses of RL (task specificity).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLDG (Reinforcement Learning Distilled Generalist) is a straightforward yet effective method designed to enhance generalist policy performance by distilling knowledge from specialized RL policies into robotic foundation models through high-quality data generation. The approach consists of three main stages:

#### 3.1. Online RL Training

The first step involves training specialized Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies for individual robotic tasks. Each task is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where the state `s_t` includes RGB images and proprioceptive information (e.g., end-effector pose, velocity, wrench measurements), and actions `a_t` represent desired end-effector movements. The objective of the RL policy `π(a_t|s_t)` is to maximize the expected discounted return of a reward function `R(s_t, a_t)` that encodes the task objectives.

While RLDG is algorithm-agnostic, the authors implement it using **HIL-SERL (Human-in-the-Loop Sample-Efficient Real-world Reinforcement Learning)** (Luo et al., 2024d). HIL-SERL is chosen for its high sample efficiency and proven performance in learning precise real-world manipulation skills from pixel inputs. It integrates human interventions (via RLPD - Reinforcement Learning from Policy Data) to accelerate learning, enabling policies to achieve high success rates (often 100%) quickly. For the experiments, RL policies are trained until convergence, typically reaching perfect success rates. The robot setup for RL training (and subsequent data collection and evaluation) involves a Franka Emika Panda arm equipped with a parallel jaw gripper, a RealSense D405 wrist camera for image observations, and a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse for human teleoperation. RL policies operate at a control frequency of 10Hz.

#### 3.2. Experience Collection

Once the specialized RL policies have converged and reliably master their respective tasks, the next step is to collect a high-quality fine-tuning dataset. This is achieved by autonomously rolling out the converged RL policies multiple times. The flexibility of this approach allows for mixing experience from various sources:
- **Multiple RL Policies:** For tasks involving different objects or variants (e.g., different connectors in the Connector Insertion task), separate RL policies can be trained for each, and their generated trajectories combined into a single, balanced fine-tuning dataset.
- **Combining RL and Human Data:** For complex, long-horizon tasks (e.g., FMB Single Object Assembly), RL can be trained specifically on critical "bottleneck" sub-tasks (e.g., the precise insertion phase). The RL-generated data for these critical segments can then be combined with human demonstrations for less precise or easier parts of the task (e.g., grasping and transport). This strategic combination optimizes for performance where it's most needed while minimizing RL training complexity.
Failed trajectories, if any, are filtered out to ensure the dataset's high quality.

#### 3.3. Generalist Policy Fine-tuning

The final stage involves using the meticulously collected, high-quality RL-generated dataset to fine-tune pre-trained robotic generalist models. This process aims to transfer the precise and optimal behaviors learned by the specialized RL policies into the more generalist architecture, while preserving its broad generalization capabilities from initial pre-training. The fine-tuning is performed as a supervised learning objective, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the actions given the states: `L(θ) = -E_{(s_t, a_t)~D} [log π_θ(a_t|s_t)]`.

The authors demonstrate the efficacy of their method by fine-tuning two prominent generalist models:
- **OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024):** A 7-billion parameter vision-language-action model built on Llama 2. It takes a single image and language instruction as input and predicts 7-dimensional actions discretized into 256 bins. For fine-tuning, the public model weights (pre-trained on the 970 thousand-episode Open X-Embodiment dataset) are adapted using **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method. OpenVLA runs at 4Hz due to inference speed limitations.
- **Octo (Octo Model Team et al., 2024):** An open-source generalist robotic policy designed for diverse sensory inputs and action spaces. Unlike OpenVLA, Octo predicts continuous actions using a diffusion head, which is adept at modeling multimodal distributions. The pre-trained Octo-Base model is fine-tuned directly on the RL-generated dataset, with adjustments to input modalities (e.g., using only wrist camera image and masking out image goals). Octo policies also operate at 10Hz.

**Experimental Setup and Tasks:**
The evaluation of RLDG is conducted on a real-world robot platform with four distinct manipulation tasks chosen for their challenges, particularly in precision and generalization:
- **Connector Insertion:** Requires sub-millimeter precision for aligning and inserting electronic connectors (USB, Ethernet, VGA for training; Type-C, HDMI, Display Port, 3-pin XLR for unseen generalization).
- **Pick and Place:** Involves grasping an object from a randomized location and placing it in a bowl, with unseen object/background scenarios for generalization testing.
- **FMB Insertion:** A benchmark task involving inserting a pre-grasped object into a moving board with tight (±1.5mm) tolerance. Used primarily for detailed analysis.
- **FMB Single Object Assembly:** A multi-stage task combining grasping and FMB Insertion, demonstrating RLDG's ability to enhance "bottleneck" phases.

The methodology is robust, leveraging established RL frameworks and prominent foundation models, and evaluating thoroughly across diverse, challenging real-world tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted clearly demonstrate the superior performance of generalist policies fine-tuned with RL-generated data (RLDG) compared to those fine-tuned with human demonstrations. The analysis also sheds light on the reasons behind this performance gain and the generalization capabilities of RLDG.

#### 4.1. RLDG vs. Conventional Fine-tuning (Human Demonstrations)

- **Higher Success Rates:** RLDG consistently led to higher success rates for both OpenVLA and Octo across all evaluated tasks, in both seen and unseen scenarios.
 - For precise tasks like **FMB Insertion** and **Connector Insertion**, OpenVLA with RLDG achieved 23% and 33% higher success rates, respectively, compared to the human-trained baseline. Octo also saw substantial improvements (10% and 37% higher success rates, respectively).
 - Even for tasks like **Pick and Place**, where generalists are often benchmarked, RLDG improved OpenVLA's success from 16/20 to 19/20 and Octo's from 1/20 to 4/20.
 - Crucially, the performance boost extended to **unseen generalization scenarios**. OpenVLA with RLDG achieved over twice the success rate on unseen Connector Insertion compared to the human-trained version. For unseen Pick and Place, Octo went from 0/20 to 4/20 successes.
 - In the **FMB Assembly** task, where RL-generated data was strategically combined with human demonstrations for the "bottleneck" insertion phase, OpenVLA achieved 20/20 successes with RLDG, significantly outperforming the 12/20 successes with purely human demonstrations.

- **Superior Sample Efficiency:** A scaling analysis on the Connector Insertion task revealed that RLDG is significantly more sample-efficient. For the VGA connector, OpenVLA with RLDG achieved 100% success with just 45 RL episodes, whereas human demonstrations required 300 episodes to reach the same level. More strikingly, for the unseen Type-C connector, 150 RL rollouts (trained on multiple connectors) yielded 100% success, while human demonstrations (even 900 episodes) plateaued at 90% success, indicating that human data might inherently limit maximum achievable performance for such precise tasks.

- **Faster Cycle Times:** Generalist policies trained with RLDG generally exhibited faster execution times (cycle times) compared to those trained with human demonstrations. OpenVLA with RLDG reduced cycle times by 0.3 to 2.3 seconds per task. This is attributed to RL's inherent optimization for efficiency (via temporal discounting) being distilled into the generalist policy. However, generalist policies were still slower than the original RL policies, largely due to differences in control frequency (OpenVLA at 4Hz vs. RL at 10Hz) and, for Octo, sub-optimal fitting of the fine-tuning data.

#### 4.2. Generalization of RLDG vs. Original RL Policies

- While the original specialized RL policies achieved near-perfect success on their specific training tasks (e.g., 20/20 on Pick and Place training scenario), they suffered from severe degradation in generalization, dropping to 1/20 success on the unseen Pick and Place scenario.
- In contrast, the RLDG-trained generalists (OpenVLA and Octo) maintained significantly better generalization, achieving 10/20 and 4/20 successes, respectively, on the same unseen Pick and Place task.
- For the multi-task **Connector Insertion**, RLDG-trained OpenVLA and Octo, fine-tuned on multiple connector types, demonstrated robust generalization across four unseen connectors (73/80 and 50/80 successes, respectively), far exceeding the best individual RL policy's performance (49/80 successes). This confirms RLDG's ability to combine RL precision with generalist broadness.

#### 4.3. Analysis: Why is RL Data Better than Human Data?

- **Action Quality as Primary Driver:** A key analysis involved creating a "mixed" dataset (human states, but actions relabeled by RL policy). Fine-tuning on this mixed dataset yielded significantly better success rates than pure human data, closing most of the performance gap with pure RL data. This strongly suggests that the **superior quality and optimality of RL-generated actions** are the primary contributors to RLDG's performance benefits. Visualization of action distributions further supports this, showing RL actions concentrated optimally towards successful movements, whereas human actions are more diffuse and less optimized.
- **Improved State Coverage as Secondary Contributor:** While action quality is dominant, the fact that pure RL data still slightly outperformed the "mixed" dataset indicates that the state distribution explored by RL policies (which might visit more optimal or task-relevant states) also contributes to improved fine-tuning.
- **Qualitative Analysis of Failure Modes:**
 - **Connector and FMB Insertion:** RLDG policies successfully overcame common "stuck" states where human-trained policies failed to align correctly or maintained contact without necessary exploratory movements. RLDG also improved approach trajectories, preventing early contact issues.
 - **Pick and Place:** RLDG policies showed improved grasp reliability and reduced premature gripper closure. An interesting RL-specific failure mode emerged: objects sometimes dropped too early (bouncing out of the bowl), likely due to RL's speed optimization being distilled without perfect timing robustness.
 - **FMB Assembly:** The performance gain of RLDG policies primarily stemmed from the improved insertion phase, consistent with the other insertion tasks. Octo exhibited consistent grasping errors, possibly due to poor depth perception.

In summary, the results unequivocally establish RLDG as a highly effective method for improving the precision and generalization of robotic foundation models, primarily by providing access to optimized, high-quality action data generated autonomously by specialized RL experts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLDG framework presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic learning, offering a promising new paradigm that synergistically combines the strengths of reinforcement learning and robotic foundation models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Automated Generation of High-Quality Training Data:** RLDG offers a simple yet highly effective automated alternative to human demonstrations for generating fine-tuning data for generalist robot policies. This is a crucial step towards reducing the heavy reliance on expensive, time-consuming, and often inconsistent human teleoperation, thus making robotic skill learning more scalable and cost-effective. The ability of RL to discover optimal, precise, and consistent trajectories provides a cleaner, more robust signal for policy learning compared to inherent variability in human-provided data.

2. **Bridging the Generalization-Precision Gap:** One of the long-standing challenges in robotics has been achieving both broad generalization across tasks and high precision for specific, complex manipulation skills. RLDG effectively bridges this gap by leveraging the broad understanding and generalization capabilities of pre-trained foundation models (acquired from vast and diverse datasets) with the precision and optimization prowess of task-specific reinforcement learning. This synergy results in robotic systems that are not only versatile but also capable of performing delicate and contact-rich operations with high success rates.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Sample Efficiency:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that generalist policies fine-tuned with RLDG data achieve substantially higher success rates and improved sample efficiency compared to conventional methods using human demonstrations. For critical tasks requiring sub-millimeter precision, RLDG can push performance to 100% success rates with significantly less data, a level often unattainable or requiring vastly more human data. This directly translates to more reliable and capable robots in real-world deployment.

4. **Flexible and Modular System Design:** RLDG's modular nature, allowing RL policies to be trained on individual sub-tasks or "bottleneck" phases, offers significant flexibility. This enables developers to strategically apply RL where precision is paramount, while still incorporating human demonstrations for other parts of a long-horizon task. This composite approach simplifies RL training complexity and optimizes overall task performance.

5. **Inspiring New Research Directions:** RLDG opens up several exciting avenues for future research.
 - **Automated Task and Reward Specification:** The current method assumes access to well-defined reward functions for RL training. Future work could explore how pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) might autonomously generate fine-tuning tasks and corresponding reward functions, further reducing manual effort.
 - **Robustness in Distillation:** While RL optimizes for success and speed, the paper notes some distillation errors (e.g., early drops in Pick and Place due to aggressive speed optimization). Future research could investigate RL objectives that prioritize robustness to distillation errors or design distillation techniques that better preserve critical timing and robustness aspects.
 - **Scaling Autonomous Improvement of Generalist Policies:** By demonstrating RL's utility as a data generator, RLDG lays groundwork for truly autonomous and scalable methods for continuously improving generalist robot policies without human intervention. This could involve iterative cycles of RL-driven data generation and model fine-tuning.

In conclusion, RLDG represents a pragmatic and powerful step towards developing the next generation of robotic manipulation systems. By re-imagining reinforcement learning as a source of "perfect" demonstrations for foundation models, it offers a pathway to robots that are both intelligent in their understanding of diverse tasks and exceptionally precise in their execution, significantly advancing the state of the art in robotic autonomy.
