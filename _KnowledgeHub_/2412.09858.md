# RLDG: Robotic Generalist Policy Distillation via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> RLDG (Reinforcement Learning Distilled Generalist) proposes a method to enhance the precision and robustness of robotic foundation models by distilling knowledge from specialized RL policies. This is achieved by using RL to autonomously generate high-quality, optimal training data, which is then used to fine-tune generalist models like OpenVLA and Octo, resulting in policies that achieve higher success rates and better generalization on tasks requiring precise manipulation.

## Problem

- Robotic foundation models, while generalist, often lack the high precision required for contact-rich or dexterous manipulation tasks due to inconsistencies in human demonstration data.
- Traditional reinforcement learning excels at precise, task-specific skills but struggles with broad generalization to unseen scenarios.
- Collecting high-quality, consistent human teleoperation data for fine-tuning generalist robotic policies is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and expensive.

## Method

- Train specialized Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies for individual robotic tasks until they achieve high precision and success rates, leveraging sample-efficient real-world RL frameworks.
- Collect high-quality, optimal trajectories by autonomously rolling out the converged ==RL policies==, filtering out failures, and strategically combining with human data for multi-stage tasks.
- Fine-tune pre-trained robotic generalist models (e.g., **OpenVLA**, **Octo**) using the collected RL-generated dataset via a ==supervised learning objective== to distill precision while retaining generalization.

## Results

- RLDG-trained generalist policies (OpenVLA, Octo) achieved significantly higher success rates on precision tasks like Connector Insertion (up to **37%** higher) and FMB Insertion (up to **33%** higher) compared to human-trained baselines, extending to unseen generalization scenarios.
- RLDG demonstrated superior sample efficiency, achieving **100%** success on Connector Insertion with 45 RL episodes compared to 300 human episodes required for the same level of performance.
- RLDG-trained generalists maintained substantially better generalization than original task-specific RL policies, succeeding on unseen Pick and Place scenarios where standalone RL failed, and showing robust performance across multiple unseen connector types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The superior quality and optimality of actions generated by reinforcement learning policies are the primary drivers of performance improvements when fine-tuning robotic generalist models.
> - Reinforcement learning can serve as an automated, scalable source of high-quality training data for foundation models, reducing reliance on human teleoperation.
> - Combining the precision of task-specific RL with the broad generalization of pre-trained foundation models yields policies that excel in both domains.
