# Tactile Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer via Real-to-Sim Image Translation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a fast, open-source tactile simulation environment for deep reinforcement learning and demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer for tactile robots by translating real sensor data to simulated inputs using a Generative Adversarial Network. This approach enabled successful deployment of learned policies on a physical robot for four out of five manipulation and exploration tasks.

## Problem

- Bridging the "sim-to-real gap" for policies learned via deep reinforcement learning, particularly for tactile sensory inputs.
- The computational cost and complexity of high-fidelity tactile simulations, such as Finite Element methods, make them impractical for large-scale DRL training.
- Existing image rendering techniques for tactile sensors often aim for visual realism, which can be difficult to achieve and still leave a domain gap.

## Method

- Developed an open-source, high-throughput ==tactile simulation environment== in PyBullet, generating ==depth images representing contact geometry== rather than detailed sensor physics.
- Trained deep reinforcement learning policies using ==Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO)== directly on these simulated tactile images for various physically-interactive tasks.
- Bridged the sim-to-real gap by training a ==pix2pix GAN== to translate real tactile sensor images into the simulated depth image format, enabling ==zero-shot policy transfer==.

## Results

- PPO acquired control policies for all **five** proposed tasks in the tactile simulation environment, with zero-shot transfer to a physical robot demonstrated for **four out of five**: Edge/Surface Following, Object Rolling/Pushing.
- A pix2pix GAN achieved high SSIM scores (e.g., **0.9955** for Edge) in translating real tactile images to simulated ones and showed superior performance over existing methods in feature prediction.
- Policies on the physical robot exhibited quantitative performance metrics, such as maintaining mean distances of **1.09mm to 1.58mm** from target edges and achieving **100** consecutive successful trials for object rolling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A computationally efficient tactile simulation focusing on contact geometry features, combined with data-driven domain adaptation, can effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap for DRL.
> - The constrained and structured nature of tactile image data makes real-to-sim image translation via GANs highly accurate and robust.
> - Tactile-based policies can achieve more sample-efficient and stable learning in simulation compared to state-based or purely visual policies for contact-rich tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly leveraging deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to enable the acquisition of complex and generalizable control policies. A common approach involves training these policies in simulated environments, which offer advantages such as safe data collection, avoidance of physical damage, access to privileged information, and simplified research dissemination. However, a significant challenge in this paradigm is the "sim-to-real gap," where discrepancies between simulation and reality impede the direct transfer and effective performance of policies learned in simulation to physical robots. Existing methods to address this gap include domain randomisation, network distillation, and domain adaptation.

Much of the current DRL research in robotics predominantly relies on visual and proprioceptive sensory inputs. Tactile information, while crucial for fine manipulation and direct environment interaction in biological systems, has been comparatively underutilized. Tactile data offers advantages such as immunity to occlusion, finer detail in contact situations, and a more constrained observation space, which could simplify the sim-to-real transfer process.

Within the realm of tactile sim-to-real transfer, existing approaches generally fall into two categories:
1. **Finite Element (FE) methods:** These simulate the intricate physics of sensor deformation. While capable of high accuracy, they are computationally intensive, often requiring seconds per simulated sensor output, which is impractical for sample-inefficient methods like DRL unless task-specific approximations are employed.
2. **Image rendering techniques:** These replicate the captured sensor information using rigid-body physics engines, which are generally faster. Previous work in this area has focused on rendering realistic images from specific optical tactile sensors, such as GelSight or TacTip, often employing techniques like Gaussian smoothing or light rendering to better match real images. However, achieving high fidelity in visual matching can add complexity.

This research contributes to the latter category by proposing a high-throughput tactile simulation environment tailored for DRL. A key distinction of this work is its departure from the goal of rendering highly realistic simulated tactile images that closely resemble real sensor outputs. Instead, the approach embraces a simulation that focuses on useful contact geometry features, remaining agnostic to the specific sensor transduction. The sim-to-real gap is then bridged through a data-driven domain adaptation method—specifically, real-to-sim image translation—applied in a subsequent stage. This strategy aims to enhance computational efficiency and potential generalizability across different optical tactile sensors without requiring complex physical modeling of sensor deformation or photorealistic rendering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized potential of tactile sensing in robotics, coupled with the computational and practical challenges of integrating it effectively into deep reinforcement learning frameworks. While simulation is a cornerstone for DRL, the "sim-to-real gap" persists, particularly for modalities like touch, where subtle physical interactions are critical. Traditional tactile simulation methods often face trade-offs between physical accuracy (e.g., FE methods) and computational speed, with few offering a practical balance for large-scale DRL training. Furthermore, existing image-rendering approaches for tactile sensors often strive for visual realism, which can introduce unnecessary complexity or still leave a domain gap that is difficult to bridge.

The specific objectives of this work are articulated through three main contributions:

1. **To provide an open-source suite of reinforcement learning environments designed for tactile robotics.** This involves developing a simple and computationally efficient method for simulating optical tactile sensors, where high-resolution contact geometry is represented as depth images. The motivation behind this is to lower the barrier to entry for researchers in tactile RL and to facilitate the safe and efficient acquisition of data for learning complex policies.
2. **To demonstrate that a standard reinforcement learning algorithm, Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO), can successfully acquire control policies for various physically-interactive tasks using these simulated tactile inputs.** This objective aims to validate the utility and effectiveness of the proposed tactile simulation framework as a training ground for DRL agents, confirming that meaningful behaviors can be learned directly from the simulated tactile observations.
3. **To validate a data-driven approach for achieving zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer.** This involves translating real tactile sensor images into the corresponding simulated depth image format using a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN). The motivation is to overcome the sim-to-real gap without requiring complex modifications to the simulation or fine-tuning on the physical robot. By translating real sensor data into the format the simulated policy was trained on, the learned policies can be deployed directly onto a physical robot for tasks requiring a sense of touch. This method specifically capitalizes on the constrained nature of tactile image space to simplify the translation challenge compared to general visual data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach integrates reinforcement learning in simulation with a data-driven image translation component to enable zero-shot policy transfer to a physical robot. The methodology is structured into three main phases:

**Overall Architecture:**
1. **Reinforcement Learning in Simulation:** A robotic policy is learned in a simulated environment using direct simulated tactile images as input.
2. **Dataset Collection and GAN Training:** A dataset of paired real and simulated tactile images is collected. This dataset is then used to train a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to translate real tactile sensor outputs into the format of simulated images.
3. **Real Robot Evaluation:** For real-world deployment, real tactile images are first passed through the trained GAN generator to produce corresponding simulated images. These generated images are then fed into the reinforcement learning policy, enabling control of the physical robot.

**Tactile Simulation:**
The simulation environment is built using **PyBullet**, chosen for its fast GPU rendering capabilities, support for deformable objects, and efficient kinematics/dynamics solvers. The simulated optical tactile sensor is based on CAD files of a real **TacTip** sensor. The soft tip of the sensor is approximated using rigid-body physics, with limited contact stiffness and damping to allow for approximate penetration of objects, mimicking the deformation of a real soft tip.
- **Image Generation:** The core of the tactile simulation involves rendering depth images using PyBullet's synthetic camera. A "penetration depth map" is created by calculating the difference between the current depth image and a reference image taken when the sensor is not in contact. This difference is then re-scaled to an 8-bit image range, and an artificial border is added to resemble real TacTip images and provide a stable reference point for augmentations.
- **Design Principles:** The simulation prioritizes high throughput (up to 1000 fps for 128x128 images with parallel environments), flexibility (simulating useful contact geometry features rather than exact sensor transduction), and realism (in the sense of supporting data-driven sim-to-real transfer, not physical fidelity). The open-source nature of the suite aims for ease of use.

**Reinforcement Learning Environments:**
The research defines five physically-interactive tasks, categorized into tactile exploration and non-prehensile manipulation. An UR5 robotic arm with Cartesian velocity control (10 Hz, max 10 mm/s linear, 5 °/s angular) is used.
- **Observation Spaces:** Policies are trained and evaluated with various observation spaces:
 - `Env State`: Provides ideal, privileged information from the simulator, serving as an upper performance bound.
 - `Tactile`: Consists solely of 128x128 simulated tactile images, optionally augmented with robot pose information obtainable in reality.
 - `Visual`: Utilizes 128x128 RGB images from a static external camera.
 - `Visual + Tactile`: Combines both visual and tactile image streams.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Edge Following:** Traversal of a randomly oriented edge while maintaining a specific pose.
 - **Surface Following:** Traversal of a randomly generated 3D surface while maintaining a set contact penetration and normal orientation.
 - **Object Rolling:** Manipulating a spherical object to a goal position on a flat sensor tip.
 - **Object Pushing:** Pushing a cube along a randomly generated trajectory.
 - **Object Balancing:** Stabilizing an unstable pole on the sensor tip against random perturbations.
- **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) from the Stable-Baselines-3 library is used for training across all tasks. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) based on the Atari Nature architecture process image-based observations, while fully connected layers handle state observations. Image augmentations (small random translations) are applied to improve training stability and performance.

**Real-to-Sim Image Translation:**
This phase addresses the sim-to-real gap by translating real tactile sensor images into the simulated depth image format.
- **Rationale:** The constrained nature of tactile images (e.g., markers on a uniform background, camera confined within the sensor) makes image translation more tractable than for complex visual scenes, which are affected by external factors like lighting and shadows.
- **Model:** A pix2pix GAN architecture is employed for image-to-image translation, consisting of a U-net generator and a convolutional discriminator.
- **Data Collection:** Three task-specific datasets (Edge, Surface, Probe) are collected, each comprising 5000 training and 2000 validation pairs of real and simulated images. Data collection for real images takes approximately 5 hours per dataset, while simulated data takes about 100 seconds. Crucially, shear perturbations are deliberately induced during real data collection to ensure the GAN output is robust to unavoidable motion-dependent shear in real-world task performance, even though shear is not explicitly modeled in simulation.
- **GAN Training:** Spectral normalisation is used in the pix2pix architecture instead of instance normalisation to mitigate "droplet artefacts" in generated images. The artificial border is excluded from simulated images during training and re-added post-generation, allowing the GAN to focus on the tactile imprint.

**Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer:**
The transfer is performed in a zero-shot manner, meaning no additional training or fine-tuning is conducted on the physical robot. Real-time control loops feed raw real tactile images to the trained GAN generator, whose output (simulated-like image) then becomes the observation for the learned RL policy, which subsequently dictates robot actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding tactile simulation, reinforcement learning, image translation, and zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer.

**Reinforcement Learning in Simulation:**
- Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) successfully learned control policies for all five simulated tasks (Edge Following, Surface Following, Object Rolling, Object Pushing, Object Balancing) using the proposed fast tactile simulation.
- Policies trained with tactile image observations frequently exhibited more sample-efficient and stable training compared to those using ideal environment state observations. This was particularly evident in the Edge-Following and Object-Rolling environments.
- Policies relying solely on visual observations (from an external camera) generally performed worse, which was attributed to the tasks being designed for tactile data and the limitations of a single external camera in capturing crucial contact details.
- Combining visual and tactile observations facilitated successful learning, with notable improvements in sample efficiency for tasks such as Edge Following and Object Pushing.

**Real-to-Sim Image Translation:**
- The pix2pix GAN achieved high accuracy in translating real tactile images to simulated depth images. Mean SSIM scores across validation sets were high: 0.9955 for Edge, 0.9924 for Surface, and 0.9942 for Probe datasets.
- The generator effectively captured and reproduced critical tactile features, such as edge orientation and position.
- The image translation model demonstrated generalization capabilities, processing images with multiple contact sources and handling penetration depths outside the specific range encountered during training, which is important for robustness.
- A comparative analysis (Appendix G) showed that the proposed tactile simulation and domain adaptation method outperformed a previous approach (Ding et al.) using elastic deformation and domain randomisation for TacTip supervised learning tasks. It achieved approximately a 3-fold improvement in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for predicting polar angle and a nearly 4-fold improvement for radial displacement.

**Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer:**
Zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to a physical robot was successfully demonstrated for **four out of the five proposed tasks**.
- **Edge Following:** The learned policy successfully traversed several novel flat shapes (square, circle, clover, foil) on the physical robot, demonstrating generalization to varied contact geometries. The sensor maintained close proximity to the target edge, with mean distances from the ground truth ranging from 1.09mm to 1.58mm in reality, compared to 0.47mm to 1.38mm in simulation. The performance drop in reality was associated with oscillating behavior, potentially due to increased latency in the real control loop and action-range clipping in the PPO algorithm.
- **Surface Following:** The policy accurately traversed a spherical object on the physical robot, maintaining the desired contact penetration and normal orientation. Real-world depth error was 0.57mm (vs 0.30mm sim) and cosine error was 0.00118 (vs 0.00054 sim). Despite a reduction in accuracy compared to simulation, the desired behavior was consistently exhibited.
- **Object Rolling:** The policy successfully manipulated ball bearings (2-8mm diameter) from random initial positions to goal locations on the physical robot, achieving 100 consecutive successful trials. Real and simulated trajectories were similar, though real-world trajectories exhibited greater noise.
- **Object Pushing:** This task presented the most significant challenge for accurate performance on the physical robot, likely due to approximations in the simulated sensor tip, object mechanics, and frictional interactions. Nevertheless, success was achieved across multiple trajectories with objects of different shapes (cube, triangular prism, cylinder). Performance, both in simulation and reality, was sensitive to initial conditions and perturbations.
- **Object Balancing:** This task remained an outstanding challenge for physical robot transfer. Successful training in simulation required physics parameters that exceeded the capabilities of the UR5 robot used for real-world experimentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics by addressing the integration of tactile sensing with deep reinforcement learning and the challenge of sim-to-real transfer. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Validation of Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real for Tactile Robotics:** The work provides evidence that zero-shot policy transfer is a viable approach for tactile-based reinforcement learning agents. This bypasses the need for extensive real-world data collection for policy training or fine-tuning, which can be time-consuming, costly, and potentially damaging to robotic hardware.
- **Accessible and Efficient Tactile Simulation:** By introducing an open-source suite of tactile-focused RL environments and a simple, fast method for simulating optical tactile sensors, the research lowers the barrier to entry for other researchers. The approach of simulating contact geometry rather than detailed sensor transduction physics, combined with data-driven image translation, offers a computationally efficient alternative to traditional high-fidelity physical simulations.
- **Enhanced DRL Capabilities with Touch:** The integration of tactile information, which is critical for intricate physical interaction, into DRL frameworks can enable robots to acquire and perform more complex and robust manipulation skills that rely on a fine sense of touch. This could broaden the range of tasks amenable to RL solutions in areas like industrial assembly, delicate handling, or human-robot interaction.
- **Robustness through Domain Adaptation:** The demonstrated effectiveness of real-to-sim image translation via GANs highlights a robust method for bridging the domain gap between disparate real and simulated sensor outputs. This data-driven approach is shown to be effective even when the simulated images do not precisely match the appearance of real sensor data, reducing the need for complex and brittle hand-tuned simulation parameters.
- **Foundational Platform for Future Research:** The open-source nature of the simulation suite provides a benchmark and development platform for future research. This could facilitate explorations into improved tactile simulation models (e.g., soft-body deformation, global shear), more advanced GAN architectures for image translation, and the application of these methods to multi-sensor configurations (e.g., tactile grippers) and more complex, open-ended manipulation tasks, such as dexterous manipulation and grasping.

In summary, this work advances the state of tactile robotics by providing practical tools and a validated methodology for leveraging simulation and deep reinforcement learning to develop tactile-aware robotic capabilities, paving the way for more dexterous and capable autonomous systems.
