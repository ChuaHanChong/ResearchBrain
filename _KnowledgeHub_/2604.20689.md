# FingerEye: Continuous and Unified Vision-Tactile Sensing for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore and Huazhong University of Science and Technology developed FingerEye, a compact, unified vision-tactile sensor for robotic manipulation, integrating binocular cameras and a compliant structure to provide continuous feedback from pre-contact visual perception to post-contact deformation-based tactile sensing. This system, coupled with a transformer-based learning framework and simulation-augmented representation learning, improved success rates in contact-rich tasks by over 30% compared to vision-only approaches and demonstrated generalization to novel object appearances.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems suffer from discontinuous perceptual feedback, relying on vision before contact and tactile sensing only after, leading to unstable contact initiation.
- Existing vision-based tactile sensors lack pre-contact visual cues, hindering precise fingertip alignment and contact anticipation, while "See-Through-Skin" (STS) sensors often have trade-offs in depth perception, form factor, and sensing fidelity.
- Developing dexterous manipulation skills is challenging due to the high cost of real-world data and the sim-to-real gap, particularly for learning robust behaviors under visual variations.

## Method

- Designed **FingerEye**, a compact wedge-shaped sensor (28.0 x 25.4 x 26.0 mm) with integrated ==binocular RGB cameras==, a ==compliant soft ring== for peripheral contact, and a transparent ==AprilTag-laden acrylic cover==.
- Developed a robust deformation estimation method using ==multi-tag PnP== to track the 6D pose of the AprilTag layout, serving as a proxy for 6D contact wrench.
- Introduced a ==transformer-based imitation learning policy== with ==action chunking== and a ==sim-augmented representation learning== strategy to leverage continuous FingerEye feedback and improve generalization.

## Results

- FingerEye demonstrated robust 6D pose estimation under deformation and low light, with minimal detectable forces of **[4.30, 4.22, 9.93] mN** and torques of **[0.32, 0.13, 8.55] mN-m**.
- Policies using FingerEye achieved **+30% higher success rates** on average across four contact-rich tasks (Coin Standing, Chip Picking, Letter Retrieving, Syringe Manipulation) compared to wrist-camera-only baselines.
- The proposed sim-augmented representation learning effectively generalized to most unseen object colors (e.g., coins), demonstrating improved robustness to visual variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified vision-tactile sensing, providing continuous feedback from pre-contact to post-contact, is crucial for stable and precise dexterous manipulation, bridging the gap between coarse global vision and fine-grained local tactile data.
> - Binocular fingertip vision provides more reliable close-range depth cues compared to monocular setups, enabling superior precision in contact-rich tasks by facilitating accurate fingertip placement.
> - Simulation-augmented representation learning, where a shared visual encoder is trained with auxiliary object-state prediction in simulation, can effectively improve generalization to unseen object appearances in real-world contact-rich tasks without relying on simulated action labels.
