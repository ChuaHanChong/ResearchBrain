# FingerEye: Continuous and Unified Vision-Tactile Sensing for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore and Huazhong University of Science and Technology developed FingerEye, a compact, unified vision-tactile sensor for robotic manipulation, integrating binocular cameras and a compliant structure to provide continuous feedback from pre-contact visual perception to post-contact deformation-based tactile sensing. This system, coupled with a transformer-based learning framework and simulation-augmented representation learning, improved success rates in contact-rich tasks by over 30% compared to vision-only approaches and demonstrated generalization to novel object appearances.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems suffer from discontinuous perceptual feedback, relying on vision before contact and tactile sensing only after, leading to unstable contact initiation.
- Existing vision-based tactile sensors lack pre-contact visual cues, hindering precise fingertip alignment and contact anticipation, while "See-Through-Skin" (STS) sensors often have trade-offs in depth perception, form factor, and sensing fidelity.
- Developing dexterous manipulation skills is challenging due to the high cost of real-world data and the sim-to-real gap, particularly for learning robust behaviors under visual variations.

## Method

- Designed **FingerEye**, a compact wedge-shaped sensor (28.0 x 25.4 x 26.0 mm) with integrated ==binocular RGB cameras==, a ==compliant soft ring== for peripheral contact, and a transparent ==AprilTag-laden acrylic cover==.
- Developed a robust deformation estimation method using ==multi-tag PnP== to track the 6D pose of the AprilTag layout, serving as a proxy for 6D contact wrench.
- Introduced a ==transformer-based imitation learning policy== with ==action chunking== and a ==sim-augmented representation learning== strategy to leverage continuous FingerEye feedback and improve generalization.

## Results

- FingerEye demonstrated robust 6D pose estimation under deformation and low light, with minimal detectable forces of **[4.30, 4.22, 9.93] mN** and torques of **[0.32, 0.13, 8.55] mN-m**.
- Policies using FingerEye achieved **+30% higher success rates** on average across four contact-rich tasks (Coin Standing, Chip Picking, Letter Retrieving, Syringe Manipulation) compared to wrist-camera-only baselines.
- The proposed sim-augmented representation learning effectively generalized to most unseen object colors (e.g., coins), demonstrating improved robustness to visual variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified vision-tactile sensing, providing continuous feedback from pre-contact to post-contact, is crucial for stable and precise dexterous manipulation, bridging the gap between coarse global vision and fine-grained local tactile data.
> - Binocular fingertip vision provides more reliable close-range depth cues compared to monocular setups, enabling superior precision in contact-rich tasks by facilitating accurate fingertip placement.
> - Simulation-augmented representation learning, where a shared visual encoder is trained with auxiliary object-state prediction in simulation, can effectively improve generalization to unseen object appearances in real-world contact-rich tasks without relying on simulated action labels.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous robotic manipulation necessitates continuous and informative perception across various interaction phases, from pre-contact approach to contact initiation and post-contact control. Traditional robotic systems often rely on external vision systems (e.g., environment- or wrist-mounted cameras) for object localization before contact and dedicated tactile sensors for contact information after engagement. This separation, however, results in a "weakly observed" transition phase, where precise contact initiation and force regulation are critical but poorly supported by existing sensing modalities. This limitation hinders the robot's ability to perform delicate and contact-rich tasks with high precision.

Recently, See-Through-Skin (STS) sensors have emerged as a method to integrate both visual and tactile perception within a single sensing surface, aiming to bridge this gap. Despite their potential, the authors identify five critical issues that prevent existing STS designs from achieving the precision and dexterity required for complex manipulation tasks:
1. **Discontinuous or unnatural visual observations**: Many designs suffer from blurry, color-shifted, or intermittent visual feedback due to semi-transparent interfaces, active/non-natural self-illumination, or mechanical reconfigurations.
2. **Fragile contact perception**: Marker- or keypoint-based tactile sensing can be unreliable in dynamic environments with varying backgrounds, lighting, occlusions, and subtle contact motions.
3. **Limited dexterous-hand compatibility**: Existing sensors are often bulky, flat, or front-facing, making them difficult to integrate onto compact dexterous fingertips and limiting their ability to perceive peripheral or non-frontal contacts in confined spaces.
4. **Missing real-and-sim learning infrastructure**: Many systems lack integrated data collection and simulation pipelines essential for scalable learning of multisensory policies and evaluation on dexterous tasks.
5. **Undeveloped end-to-end policy interfaces**: Current STS applications frequently rely on hand-crafted controllers, and when end-to-end learning is attempted, naive modality fusion can lead policies to prioritize easier, less contact-informative global cues over local fingertip feedback.

FingerEye positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified limitations through a joint design of both sensing and learning interfaces. It aims to provide a continuous, robust, compact, and learning-compatible vision-tactile sensing solution specifically for dexterous fingertips, thereby advancing the capabilities of robots in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance robotic dexterity in contact-rich manipulation through a novel framework, FingerEye, which integrates continuous vision-tactile feedback throughout the entire interaction sequence. This overarching goal is motivated by the inherent challenges of dexterous manipulation and the shortcomings of existing sensing and learning paradigms.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the "seeing to touching" gap**: Dexterous tasks, such as standing a coin upright, require precise object localization before contact, accurate detection of contact onset, and stable force regulation after contact. Current systems, which separate pre-contact external vision from post-contact tactile sensing, leave the crucial transition phase weakly observed. FingerEye aims to provide continuous, informative feedback across these phases, ensuring robust perception during precise contact initiation and force regulation.
- **Developing a robust and natural sensing interface**: A major motivation is to overcome the physical design limitations of existing STS sensors. The authors aim to design a fingertip sensor that:
 - Provides clear and natural close-range visual observations without relying on active illumination or mode switching, ensuring sensing continuity.
 - Enables robust contact perception that is resilient to environmental variations (e.g., lighting, background, partial occlusion).
 - Is compact and ergonomically compatible with dexterous robotic hands, allowing for perception of both frontal and peripheral contacts in confined spaces.
 - Is low-cost and reproducible to facilitate wider adoption and research.
- **Establishing comprehensive learning infrastructure**: To enable effective policy learning with multi-sensor FingerEye feedback, a crucial objective is to build integrated real-world and simulation infrastructure. This infrastructure is designed to support systematic data collection, policy training, and scalable evaluation across a diverse set of contact-sensitive tasks.
- **Designing an effective policy interface**: A core motivation on the learning side is to develop policy designs that prevent "modality shortcuts." Naive fusion of sensory data can cause learning algorithms to bypass task-critical local fingertip feedback in favor of easier, but less relevant, global cues (e.g., from wrist cameras or proprioception). The objective is to devise a policy interface that effectively exploits distributed fingertip feedback by ensuring local FingerEye information actively influences action prediction.

By simultaneously addressing these sensing and learning challenges, the research seeks to create an integrated framework that significantly improves the precision and dexterity of robotic manipulation in tasks involving complex contact dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FingerEye framework integrates a novel physical sensing interface with a structured learning interface, along with supporting real-and-sim infrastructure.

#### 3.1. FingerEye Sensing Interface

The sensing interface is designed to provide continuous vision-tactile feedback from a compact fingertip module.

- **Design Highlights**:
 - **Complementary Binocular RGB Cameras**: Each FingerEye module incorporates two RGB cameras. A tip-facing camera, positioned at a short working distance (approximately 10 mm), observes the transparent contact surface, facilitating AprilTag array pose estimation post-contact. A root-side camera operates at a longer working distance (approximately 80 mm) and is tilted by 21.48° to offer a broader view during approach. This binocular configuration provides an extended effective field of view and implicit stereo depth cues for object localization and contact alignment, while maintaining natural RGB observations without active illumination or sensing mode switching.
 - **Peripheral Compliant Ring**: The design features a clear central acrylic window surrounded by a compliant silicone ring. This ring deforms under external forces and torques, inducing measurable changes in the acrylic plate’s pose, serving as a proxy for contact wrench sensing. This peripheral placement ensures the central RGB view remains unobstructed, while allowing both frontal and peripheral contacts to be perceived.
 - **Multi-Tag Pose Tracking**: A custom AprilTag array on the inner surface of the acrylic plate converts ring deformation into a 6D plate-pose change. The system aggregates visible tag corners into a single multi-tag PnP (Perspective-n-Point) problem, providing robust pose tracking even when some tags are partially obscured or affected by varying lighting. This method is noted as being more stable than combining individual tag pose estimates or relying on keyline tracking.
 - **Compact Wedge-Shaped Form Factor**: The assembled module measures approximately 41.7 × 30.0 × 25.0 mm. This wedge shape and compact size ensure compatibility with dexterous hands and allow for entry into confined spaces, as well as enabling specific manipulation behaviors like wedging under thin objects.
 - **Low-Cost and Reproducible Implementation**: FingerEye is constructed using readily available, low-cost off-the-shelf components and 3D-printed parts, with a bill of materials estimated at around $60 per module.
- **Capability Highlights**:
 - **Robust Pose Estimation**: Experiments demonstrate stable detection and pose estimation of the AprilTag array even under frontal/peripheral force perturbations and significant lighting variations, leveraging the redundancy of multiple tags.
 - **Plate-Pose Change as Contact-Wrench Proxy**: Calibration experiments showed a strong linear relationship (R²test ≥ 0.999 for forces, ≥ 0.99 for torques) between the estimated 6D plate-pose change and applied contact wrenches, validating its use as a compact proxy for contact sensing.
 - **Analytical Sensitivity to Small Contact Wrenches**: Based on a subpixel localization accuracy of 0.25 pixels, FingerEye achieves a minimum detectable force F_min of [4.30, 4.22, 9.93] mN and a minimum detectable torque τ_min of [0.32, 0.13, 8.55] mN·m.
 - **Contact-Onset Utility in Delicate Grasping**: The sensor effectively enables contact-aware stopping and damage-free lifting across nine fragile or deformable objects by monitoring fingertip normal deformation (Δz) to detect contact onset.

#### 3.2. FingerEye Learning Interface

The learning interface is designed to effectively leverage multi-sensor FingerEye feedback.

- **Real-and-Sim Learning Infrastructure**:
 - **Robot Platform**: The real-world setup employs a uFactory xArm7 robotic arm equipped with a LEAP Hand. This platform integrates a wrist-mounted RGB camera for global scene context and FingerEye modules on each fingertip for local feedback.
 - **Data Collection Interface**: Demonstrations are collected using a leader–follower teleoperation setup with matched hand kinematics. A passive leader hand guides the robot, which tracks streamed joint positions via PD control. This system records synchronized data including wrist images, multi-fingertip FingerEye observations, plate poses, robot states, and actions at 10 Hz.
 - **Simulation Digital Twin**: A simulated counterpart of the sensorized hand is developed for scalable evaluation and ablation studies. Each FingerEye acrylic plate is modeled as a floating body connected to the sensor base by six virtual joints. These joint gains are calibrated from real-world pose-to-wrench relationships, ensuring consistent plate-pose changes in response to contact forces in simulation.
- **Policy Interface Design Highlights**: The design aims to counteract modality shortcuts by structuring how local FingerEye feedback influences action prediction.
 - **PD1. Modality-Group Encoding for Structured Representations**: Observations are grouped (e.g., wrist RGB, proprioception, per-fingertip FingerEye RGB). Three encoder interfaces are compared:
 - `NoEnc`: Tokenized observations are directly passed to the decoder without pre-decoder self-attention.
 - `FlatEnc`: All observation tokens are mixed together in a shared Transformer encoder (standard approach).
 - `GroupEnc`: Self-attention occurs *within* each modality group first, allowing each group to form a dedicated representation before cross-group fusion in the decoder. This enables local FingerEye views to integrate local geometry and contact-state cues before competing with global cues.
 - **PD2. Group-Conditioned Decoding for Balanced Modality Fusion**: This addresses how action queries integrate information from encoded modality groups.
 - `FlatDec`: Action queries condition on a single sensory context, where all modality groups compete in one cross-attention operation. This can bias the policy towards easier global cues.
 - `GroupDec`: Each modality group produces a separate residual update to the action-query states, and these residuals are averaged. This ensures action prediction is jointly conditioned on all groups, giving each group a direct path into the decoder and reducing competition between local fingertip observations and global cues.
 - **PD3. Cached Visual Summaries for Efficient Multiview Training**: To improve training efficiency with multiple cameras, a frozen RADIO image backbone is used to precompute and cache per-view visual summary vectors offline. Policy training then utilizes these cached summaries as visual tokens, avoiding repeated image backbone inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of FingerEye was evaluated across seven contact-sensitive tasks in both simulation and the real world, including coin standing, chip picking, letter retrieving, syringe manipulation, nut picking, and peg-in-hole pushing. Performance was measured by task success rate (SR).

#### 4.1. Q1–Q2: Sensing Modality Comparison

These experiments compared different observation modalities using a fixed GEnc+GDec policy architecture.

- **O1: FingerEye Visual Feedback Improves Dexterous Manipulation Beyond Wrist-Only Observation.**
 - Adding binocular FingerEye (W+FE) to wrist observations (W) significantly improved mean success rates. W+FE showed a 39.1 percentage point (pp) increase in mean SR in simulation and a 33.8 pp increase in real-world tasks compared to W.
 - The largest performance gains were observed in tasks requiring precise fingertip placement, such as nut picking. The wrist-only policy (W) exhibited approximately 50% lower SR and higher variance in this task, indicating its struggle with occlusion, fine wrist refinement, and coordinated finger motions around small objects. Qualitative observations revealed W failures often stemmed from missing local contact geometry (e.g., offset coin wedging, missed chip/letter edges, nut squeeze-out).
- **O2: Binocular FingerEye Feedback Reduces Monocular Ambiguity Near Contact.**
 - Comparing binocular FingerEye (W+FE) with a monocular tip-camera-only FingerEye variant (W+M), W+FE demonstrated a 6.8 pp improvement in simulation mean SR and a 15.0 pp improvement in real-world mean SR over W+M.
 - Residual failures with W+M included instances where the policy pushed against a syringe edge instead of maneuvering around it, or overshot a peg edge, consistent with limitations of local coverage and single-view ambiguity near contact. The complementary fingertip views in the binocular setup were found to resolve ambiguities in edge side, contact alignment, and object engagement. The performance hierarchy consistently showed W+FE > W+M > W in both simulation and real-world tasks.

#### 4.2. Q3: Continuous Sensing vs. Post-Contact Signals

This experiment compared continuous FingerEye feedback with a post-contact-only tactile map baseline.

- **O3: Continuous Fingertip Vision is More Effective Than Post-Contact-Only Tactile Feedback.**
 - The policy using wrist vision and simulated post-contact tactile maps (W+T) achieved a mean SR of 24.1%, which remained close to the wrist-only baseline (W, 26.7%). In contrast, W+FE achieved 65.9% mean SR.
 - This significant performance gap is attributed to the task requirements. Tasks like Coin Standing and Peg-in-Hole demand precise fingertip pose refinement *before* stable contact. Post-contact tactile maps only provide deformation feedback once contact is established, whereas FingerEye offers continuous local visual feedback during the crucial approach, contact initiation, and contact maintenance phases, enabling the policy to make informed decisions on where and how to engage the object.

#### 4.3. Q4: Policy Interface for Distributed FingerEye Feedback

This section evaluated different policy interface designs while keeping the sensing input fixed to W+FE.

- **O4: Group-Structured Modality Fusion Better Preserves and Exploits FingerEye’s Feedback.**
 - `GEnc+FDec` (Group Encoding, Flat Decoding) improved over `FEnc+FDec` (Flat Encoding, Flat Decoding) by 11.3%, demonstrating the benefit of forming group-level representations before fusion.
 - `NoEnc+GDec` (No Encoding, Group Decoding) improved over `NoEnc+FDec` (No Encoding, Flat Decoding) by 7.1%, indicating the importance of grouped decoder access.
 - The combination of both (`GEnc+GDec`) yielded the best performance, achieving a 65.9% mean SR, which was 13.9 pp higher than the strongest non-grouped baseline.
 - Decoder attention diagnostics (Appendix A.4) indicated that flat decoders exhibited substantial attention dominance from proprioceptive signals, suggesting modality competition and a "proprioceptive shortcut." Policies with higher success rates also maintained higher attention to FingerEye feedback, supporting the hypothesis that preserving direct access to FingerEye observations during decoding is crucial for performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FingerEye framework represents a contribution to dexterous robotic manipulation by offering an integrated solution for continuous vision-tactile sensing and effective policy learning.

### 6. Significance

- **Bridging the Sensing Gap**: FingerEye effectively addresses the critical challenge of continuous perception from pre-contact approach to post-contact control, a limitation of previous systems relying on separate vision and tactile modalities.
- **Robust and Compact Sensor Design**: The research introduces a novel fingertip sensor that provides natural, continuous vision and robust contact deformation sensing, overcoming common issues like unnatural visual feedback, fragile contact perception, and poor dexterous-hand compatibility found in prior See-Through-Skin sensors.
- **Effective Multisensory Learning**: The developed learning interface, particularly the group-structured modality fusion approach, demonstrates how to effectively exploit distributed fingertip feedback, preventing policies from relying on less contact-informative shortcuts. This is validated by significant performance improvements in diverse contact-rich tasks.
- **Reproducible Research**: The open-source release of hardware and software resources for FingerEye fosters further research and development in this area.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation**: FingerEye's capabilities can enable robots to perform more precise, delicate, and complex manipulation tasks that involve intricate contact interactions, expanding the range of applications for robotic systems in manufacturing, service, and healthcare.
- **Foundation for Future Sensing**: The design principles and empirical validation of FingerEye offer a blueprint for developing next-generation integrated fingertip sensors, potentially influencing future hardware designs in robotics.
- **Advancement in Learning for Robotics**: The proposed learning interface and real-and-sim infrastructure provide a robust platform for scalable data generation, policy training, and evaluation, which can accelerate progress in imitation learning and reinforcement learning for contact-rich tasks.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations of the current work:
1. **Manual Sensor Geometry**: The camera placement and AprilTag layout were manually designed. Future work could explore automated optimization of sensor geometry to improve fingertip coverage, reduce blind spots, and enhance handling of diverse contact configurations.
2. **Scope of Manipulation**: The experiments focused on single-arm, fixed-base manipulation. Extending FingerEye to mobile, bimanual, or humanoid robot settings is an avenue for future research.
3. **Sim-to-Real Fidelity and Reinforcement Learning**: While the simulation environment supports ablations and scalable evaluation, improving its fidelity to the real world and integrating reinforcement learning techniques could enable larger-scale data generation, active sensing strategies, and allow for recovery behaviors beyond those demonstrated.
