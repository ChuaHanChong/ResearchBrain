# RoboCerebra: A Large-scale Benchmark for Long-horizon Robotic Manipulation Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboCerebra, a large-scale benchmark from Beihang University, NUS, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, enables comprehensive evaluation of "System 2" capabilities in robotic manipulation. It features long-horizon tasks, averaging 9.1 steps and 2,972 simulation steps per trajectory, and introduces a hierarchical framework that boosts performance in complex, memory-intensive scenarios.

## Problem

- Vision-language models (VLMs) in robotics are predominantly used for reactive "System 1" policies, underutilizing their strengths in deliberative "System 2" planning and reasoning.
- Existing robotic manipulation benchmarks are limited to short horizons (2-5 sub-tasks, <500 action steps), lacking dynamic variations, memory requirements, or partial observability.
- This simplicity restricts the comprehensive evaluation of high-level reasoning, long-horizon planning, and hierarchical subgoal decomposition in complex robotic scenarios.

## Method

![[2506.06677-method.png]]
*HPE framework: VLA trained on paired images and single-step instructions; VLM trained on execution videos with success-labeled instructions. At test time, the VLM updates low-level plans in a memory bank from low-frequency observations while the VLA executes fine-grained actions from high-frequency observations.*

- **RoboCerebra**, a large-scale simulation dataset, provides ==1,000 human-annotated trajectories== across **100 task variants**, featuring significantly extended horizons and dynamic household environments.
- A ==Hierarchical Planning and Execution (HPE)== framework combines a high-level ==VLM planner (System 2)== and a low-level ==VLA controller (System 1)== for structured, closed-loop task decomposition.
- A ==multi-dimensional evaluation protocol== assesses planning accuracy, reflection (task completion assessment), memory utilization, and plan efficiency in addition to standard task success rates.

## Results

- RoboCerebra features tasks averaging **2,972.4 simulation steps** per trajectory, approximately **6×** longer than existing long-horizon manipulation datasets, with over **10,000 fine-grained step-level segments**.
- The HPE framework achieved **13.21%** success in the challenging "Mix" setting, outperforming a fine-tuned OpenVLA (**0.00%**) and a Planner+OpenVLA baseline (**11.48%**).
- **GPT-4o** demonstrated superior VLM planning performance, achieving the best average task success rate of **16.04%** and a planning accuracy of **68.33%**, outperforming other VLM planners by **>4%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pure "System 1" policies, like OpenVLA, struggle significantly with long-horizon tasks, achieving only 4.05% success in ideal settings and failing completely (0.00%) in dynamic, memory-intensive "Mix" scenarios.
> - Integrating "System 2" planning capabilities through the hierarchical framework substantially enhances performance in complex tasks, improving success to 13.21% in "Mix" settings compared to System 1's failure.
> - Language-based reasoning in VLMs, exemplified by GPT-4o's 68.33% plan accuracy, contributes significantly to solving long-horizon tasks even with limited visual grounding, though a notable performance gap remains between VLM planning and ground-truth plans.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has witnessed transformative advances with the advent of large-scale vision-language models (VLMs). These models, by integrating natural language understanding with visual perception, have enabled robotic systems to interpret high-level instructions and generalize across diverse environments, moving beyond conventional, rigidly programmed control paradigms. However, the authors argue that the full potential of VLMs in robotics remains largely untapped.

The current research landscape predominantly applies VLMs as "System 1" policies—fast, reactive vision-language-action (VLA) models that directly map multimodal inputs to low-level control signals. While effective for immediate, short-horizon tasks, this approach underutilizes the VLM's inherent strengths in semantic abstraction, relational understanding, and contextual reasoning. These capabilities align more closely with "System 2" processes, characterized by deliberative, goal-directed thinking, long-horizon planning, and hierarchical subgoal decomposition.

Existing robotic manipulation benchmarks, such as RLBench, VLMBench, ARNOLD, ALFRED, Calvin, RoboCasa, Libero-Long, and VLABench, have made strides in introducing compositional, multi-step tasks. However, the authors critically point out their limitations: they typically involve only 2 to 5 sub-tasks, fewer than 500 action steps, and often lack dynamic variations, memory requirements, or the complexities of partially observable environments. This temporal and structural simplicity restricts the comprehensive evaluation of System 2 capabilities in VLMs, leaving high-level reasoning and long-horizon planning largely unexplored.

RoboCerebra positions itself as a crucial advancement in this landscape by directly addressing these limitations. It provides a novel benchmark explicitly designed to assess "System 2" capabilities, bridging the gap between VLMs' theoretical potential and their practical application in complex, real-world robotic scenarios. By creating a dataset with significantly extended horizons, diverse subgoals, dynamic environments, and explicit memory requirements, RoboCerebra aims to catalyze research into more intelligent, deliberative, and generalizable robotic agents. It shifts the focus from purely reactive execution to intelligent planning, reflection, and memory, leveraging the advanced reasoning capabilities of modern foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind RoboCerebra stems from the observed underutilization of vision-language models' (VLMs) advanced reasoning capabilities in current robotic manipulation research. While VLMs show promise in understanding natural language and visual contexts, their deployment is predominantly confined to reactive, "System 1" behaviors, missing the opportunity to leverage their "System 2" strengths in deliberative, long-horizon planning. The limitations of existing benchmarks—their restricted temporal scale, structural simplicity, and lack of dynamic environmental factors or memory demands—further exacerbate this gap, preventing a thorough evaluation of these higher-order cognitive functions.

To address this critical void, the authors establish the following key objectives for RoboCerebra:

1. **Develop a Large-scale Simulation Dataset for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The foremost objective is to create a dataset that transcends the limitations of prior benchmarks by featuring significantly extended task horizons, diverse subtask sequences, and dynamic household environments. This dataset is designed to inherently demand sophisticated reasoning, memory, and adaptive planning from robotic agents.
2. **Propose a Hierarchical Framework for System 1–System 2 Interaction:** To practically demonstrate and evaluate System 2 capabilities, the paper aims to introduce a baseline hierarchical planning and execution (HPE) framework. This framework explicitly combines a high-level VLM planner (System 2) with a low-level vision-language-action (VLA) controller (System 1), facilitating structured interaction and closed-loop execution of complex tasks.
3. **Establish a Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Protocol:** Beyond simple task success, a crucial objective is to define a comprehensive evaluation protocol. This protocol targets distinct cognitive dimensions pertinent to long-horizon reasoning, specifically planning accuracy, reflection (assessing task completion), and memory utilization, enabling a granular understanding of VLM performance.

In essence, RoboCerebra is motivated by the need to unlock the full potential of VLMs in robotics by providing the necessary infrastructure—a challenging benchmark and an illustrative hierarchical framework—to foster the development of robotic systems capable of truly intelligent, deliberative, and adaptable behavior across extended temporal scales.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of RoboCerebra is founded on a meticulously designed methodology, comprising a robust task setting, an efficient dataset construction pipeline, and a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol, complemented by a hierarchical planning and execution (HPE) framework.

#### 3.1. Task Setting

RoboCerebra tasks are engineered to necessitate long-horizon reasoning, accounting for temporal dependencies, partial observability, and dynamic environments. The benchmark defines six representative sub-task types to capture these complexities:
- **Ideal:** Baseline tasks in static, fully observable settings, serving as a control.
- **Memory Exploration:** Requires active exploration to build internal world representations.
- **Memory Execution:** Demands memory retrieval for task completion, often with obscured information.
- **Random Disturbance:** Introduces unexpected environmental changes requiring adaptation.
- **Observation Mismatching:** Tests robustness to discrepancies between planned and perceived states.
- **Mix:** Combines memory and dynamic factors, demanding continual re-planning under uncertainty.

#### 3.2. Dataset Construction Pipeline

The dataset is constructed in simulation, leveraging the Libero platform, to ensure scalability, reproducibility, and a focus on high-level reasoning rather than sim-to-real gaps. The pipeline consists of three stages:

1. **Cascaded Task Generation:**
 - **Item Sampling:** Objects are randomly sampled from Libero's item library and converted into structured representations detailing category, affordances, and spatial context.
 - **LLM Prompting:** GPT-o3-mini is prompted with these representations to generate high-level task descriptions (e.g., "Heat milk in the microwave").
 - **Hierarchical Decomposition:** These high-level tasks are then decomposed into coherent, step-by-step subtask instructions. The prompting strategy incorporates affordance-aware and spatially grounded reasoning, validating preconditions and postconditions to ensure physical plausibility and temporal consistency. This cascaded approach ensures diverse, semantically meaningful, and executable long-horizon tasks.

2. **Scene Initialization and Verification Execution:**
 - **Simulator Code Generation:** Task plans are translated into executable simulator scenes. Each action step is parsed into spatial and relational constraints (e.g., placing milk from `short_fridge_upper_region` to `coffee_table_top`). Rule-based mappings convert steps into initial and target object placements.
 - **Dual-Loop Verification:**
 - **Symbolic Check:** An internal simulator loop validates object states and relational consistency across steps.
 - **Vision-Language Verification:** GPT-4o evaluates spatial plausibility from multi-view RGB-D renderings, ensuring alignment with human commonsense. This two-tiered verification guarantees functionally correct and semantically plausible environments.

3. **Human Demonstration and Annotation:**
 - **Trajectory Collection:** Human operators execute the subtasks in simulation to collect high-quality, diverse, and realistic action trajectories.
 - **Fine-Grained Annotation:** Each trajectory is annotated with precise subtask boundaries, aligning actions with specific task steps. This temporal segmentation supports granular analysis and instruction grounding.
 - **Quality Control:** Substantial human effort (400 hours for annotation, 200 hours for verification) is dedicated to maintaining consistency, completeness, and accuracy of labeled plans and state transitions.

#### 3.3. Multi-Dimensional Evaluation Protocol

Beyond binary task success, RoboCerebra introduces a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol:
- **Task Success Rate (SR):** Measures if the agent achieves key object state transitions using simulator-internal predicate functions.
- **Average Plan Match Accuracy (Acc_P):** Compares the LLM-generated high-level plan against human-annotated ground-truth plans using exact sequence matching.
- **Plan Efficiency (η):** Calculates success rate normalized by the average plan length, assessing efficiency of symbolic planning.
- **Action Completion Accuracy (Acc_C):** Assesses semantic interpretability and reflection ability via a VideoQA benchmark with human-written binary questions verified against execution.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical Planning and Execution (HPE) Framework

To demonstrate the benchmark's utility, the authors propose an HPE framework:
- **Architecture:** It comprises a high-level **VLM planner** (System 2) that processes low-frequency egocentric observations to generate and update step-level subgoals stored in a memory bank, and a low-level **VLA controller** (System 1) that executes fine-grained actions based on high-frequency visual inputs, conditioned on current subgoals.
- **Training Procedure:**
 - **VLA Training:** OpenVLA is fine-tuned on single-step sequences from long-horizon demonstrations, using (image, instruction) pairs mapped to discretized robot actions (next-token prediction).
 - **VLM Training:** Fine-tuned on video-instruction pairs, annotated for successful or incomplete execution. Contrastive supervision enables progress-aware planning and re-planning based on visual feedback.
- **Hierarchical Task Planning and Execution:** At inference, the VLM decomposes the task, and the VLA executes. The VLM periodically monitors progress, updating the memory bank with the next subgoal or refined instructions upon completion or deviation, enabling closed-loop, robust execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on RoboCerebra reveal significant insights into the capabilities and limitations of current robotic systems and VLMs in long-horizon manipulation.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics

RoboCerebra comprises 1,000 human-annotated trajectories across 100 task variants. Each task averages 9.1 atomic steps (ranging from 2 to over 20), resulting in more than 10,000 step-level segments with fine-grained temporal annotations. A defining feature is its extended temporal scale: the average trajectory length is 2,972.4 simulation steps, approximately **six times longer** than those in existing long-horizon manipulation datasets. The dataset also exhibits high compositional diversity, with over 10% of tasks involving five or more distinct action categories.

#### 4.2. System 1 Struggles in Long-Horizon Tasks

The evaluation of a pure "System 1" model, OpenVLA, demonstrated its substantial limitations in long-horizon tasks. In the "Ideal" setting (static, fully observable), OpenVLA achieved a mere 4.05% success rate. Even with supervised fine-tuning on RoboCerebra data, its performance only marginally improved to 7.84%, still significantly lower than the 21.10% achieved by the proposed Hierarchical Framework. Critically, in the "Mix" setting, which combines memory demands and dynamic scene variations, the fine-tuned OpenVLA failed completely (0.00% success rate). These results underscore that reactive policies inherently lack the capacity to maintain instruction fidelity and adapt to complex, extended temporal dependencies.

#### 4.3. System 2 Enhances System 1 in Complex Tasks

The integration of "System 2" capabilities through the Planner+OpenVLA baseline and the full Hierarchical Planning and Execution (HPE) framework led to notable performance gains. In the challenging "Mix" setting, Planner+OpenVLA achieved 11.48% success, and the HPE framework further improved this to 13.21%. This demonstrates that incorporating VLM-based planning significantly enhances performance in memory-intensive and dynamic scenarios. The iterative reasoning enabled by the hierarchical approach (VLM dynamically monitoring and updating plans) proved beneficial in these complex settings. However, in the simpler "Ideal" setting, the Hierarchical Framework slightly underperformed Planner+OpenVLA (21.10% vs. 21.92%), suggesting that the overhead of dynamic re-planning might introduce unnecessary complexity when tasks are straightforward and static.

#### 4.4. Ablation on Planner Models

A comparative study of different VLM planners (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Next-Video) revealed GPT-4o as the top performer.
- **GPT-4o's Superiority:** GPT-4o achieved the best average success rate (16.04%), outperforming other VLMs by more than 4%. This highlights its robust reasoning capabilities.
- **Importance of Language-based Reasoning:** Even with visual inputs disabled ("Blind" mode), GPT-4o-Blind maintained a relatively stable performance (15.10% average success rate). This finding is crucial, indicating that the language-based reasoning inherent in VLMs contributes significantly to solving long-horizon tasks, even without real-time visual grounding.
- **Performance Gap with Ground Truth:** A persistent performance gap of over 9% remained between the best VLM (GPT-4o) and following the ground-truth plan (GT-plan, 25.16% average success rate). This suggests that limitations in environmental interaction and potential domain gaps in visual understanding still pose significant challenges for current VLMs.

#### 4.5. Evaluation on System 2 Capabilities

A multi-dimensional evaluation focusing on planning accuracy, reflection (observation judgment), success rate, plan length, and plan efficiency further dissected System 2 performance:
- **GPT-4o's Overall Strength:** GPT-4o exhibited the best performance in planning accuracy (68.33%), task success rate (16.04%), and plan efficiency (1.50). This confirms its leading role in general long-horizon task planning and execution.
- **Reflection vs. Planning:** While GPT-4o's observation judgment (Acc_C = 32.66%) was not the highest (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-SFT achieved 66.83% after fine-tuning), it still achieved a substantially higher task success rate. This implies that while reflection is important, strong overall planning capabilities can compensate for some deficiencies in precise environmental observation judgments, or that observation is not yet the decisive factor in current benchmarks.
- **Specialized Models:** Specialized embodied VLMs (e.g., RoboBrain-2.0, Cosmos-Reason1, VeBrain) showed stronger performance on Embodied-QA benchmarks and complex scenarios like Memory Exploration and Mix, compared to general-purpose VLMs. However, this specialization has not fully translated to superior Embodied Planning, indicating a need for deeper integration of perception, reasoning, and long-term planning.
- **Memory Task Performance (Appendix Results):** In fine-grained memory experiments, GPT-4o significantly outperformed Qwen2.5-VL in Memory Exploration and Memory Execution tasks. GPT-4o demonstrated better task completion awareness, exploration efficiency, and decision accuracy, highlighting its superior ability to track progress, make informed decisions, and recall past interactions in memory-dependent scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboCerebra represents a critical advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation, with far-reaching significance and potential impact:

1. **Enabling True VLM Potential in Robotics:** By explicitly focusing on "System 2" capabilities, RoboCerebra moves beyond the reactive "System 1" paradigm that currently dominates VLM applications in robotics. It provides the necessary infrastructure to unlock VLMs' strengths in deliberative reasoning, long-horizon planning, semantic abstraction, and contextual memory, which are crucial for developing truly intelligent and autonomous robotic agents.

2. **Bridging the Benchmark Gap:** RoboCerebra fills a significant gap in existing robotic manipulation benchmarks. Its novel design, featuring significantly longer action sequences (6x longer than previous benchmarks), diverse subtask compositions, dynamic environments, and explicit requirements for memory and reflection, sets a new standard for evaluating advanced robotic intelligence. This allows for a more comprehensive and realistic assessment of a system's ability to handle real-world complexities.

3. **Catalyst for Research in Hierarchical Robotics:** The introduction of the Hierarchical Planning and Execution (HPE) framework, which effectively combines a high-level VLM planner with a low-level VLA controller, provides a strong baseline and a conceptual model for future research. This framework highlights the benefits of structured System 1–System 2 interaction, paving the way for more robust, interpretable, and generalizable robotic policies.

4. **Advancing Cognitive Robotics:** The multi-dimensional evaluation protocol, which goes beyond simple task success to measure planning accuracy, efficiency, and action completion (reflection), offers a more nuanced understanding of robotic cognitive functions. This enables researchers to pinpoint strengths and weaknesses in different aspects of intelligent behavior, fostering targeted improvements in planning, self-monitoring, and memory management.

5. **Directing Future Research:** The findings from RoboCerebra highlight several key areas for future research:
 - **Richer System 1-System 2 Interaction:** The current framework's interaction is limited. Future work can explore more sophisticated bidirectional communication, enabling finer-grained feedback and adaptive control.
 - **Enhanced Evaluation Metrics:** The need for execution-level signals, such as subtask ordering validation and failure recovery mechanisms, is evident to further refine evaluation.
 - **Real-World Validation:** Deploying RoboCerebra in real-world settings would be the ultimate test, introducing new challenges and further validating long-horizon reasoning under realistic, unstructured conditions.
 - **Deeper Integration of Perception and Planning:** The gap between VLM performance and ground-truth plans, especially in visual understanding and environmental interaction, suggests the need for better integration between perceptual input and high-level planning.
 - **Specialized Embodied VLMs:** While specialized VLMs show promise in certain aspects, their full generalization to embodied planning remains an open challenge, requiring continued research into architecture and training paradigms.

In conclusion, RoboCerebra is not merely a dataset but a comprehensive benchmark that redefines the scope of robotic manipulation evaluation. By fostering research into "System 2" capabilities, it promises to accelerate the development of more autonomous, adaptive, and cognitively capable robots that can genuinely understand and operate in complex, dynamic human environments.
