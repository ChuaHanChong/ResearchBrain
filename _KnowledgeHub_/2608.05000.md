# Towards Physics of Multimodal Pretraining: Knowledge Flow, Modality Synergy, Early Unification, and Recipes

> [!summary] Summary
> This research empirically clarifies how knowledge flows between language, visual understanding, and visual generation, the conditions for modality synergy versus competition, and the critical importance of early and simultaneous unification in multimodal pre-training. It derives efficient pre-training recipes, demonstrating strong generative quality with an asymmetric data mix (e.g., L70/U25/G5) and the benefits of architectural decoupling.

## Problem

- Current unified multimodal model design often relies on heuristics rather than principled understanding of how modalities interact during training.
- The intricate design space for unified multimodal pre-training, especially regarding knowledge flow, synergy/competition, and optimal integration timing, remains largely underexplored.
- Many existing approaches retrofit pre-trained LLMs/MLLMs, treating vision as an appended module, which obscures its true role in natively multimodal systems and can lead to suboptimal integration.

## Method

- Systematically explored the **"physics" of unified multimodal pre-training** through controlled experiments on synthetic and large-scale real-world datasets.
- Utilized a decoder-only Transformer architecture (Llama-3-like) with the ==Transfusion framework== to natively integrate vision and language, supporting both text generation and visual generation via ==flow matching==.
- Investigated four key areas: knowledge flow, modality synergy vs. competition, early unification, and efficient pre-training recipes, varying factors like data mixing, ==architectural sharing (Mixture-of-Experts)==, and timing of modality introduction.

## Results

- An optimal, highly asymmetric data mixture (e.g., **L70/U25/G5**) was identified, achieving strong language accuracy, visual understanding, and generative performance with **five times fewer** dedicated generative tokens.
- Architectural decoupling via modality-specific FFNs within a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) setup consistently outperformed dense baselines, demonstrating efficacy in balancing synergy and competition.
- Early unified pre-training consistently provided clear advantages in language accuracy, visual understanding, and visual generation performance over late-fusion approaches, mitigating vision laziness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Knowledge transfer in multimodal pre-training is asymmetric and concept-dependent: language acts as a universal booster, visual understanding is a strong prior for generation, but generation has limited backward transfer.
> - Modality synergy versus competition is governed by task complexity and parameter sharing; decoupled Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) effectively mitigate competition and promote synergy, while shared attention and normalization are crucial for cross-modal benefits.
> - Early and simultaneous multimodal unification is necessary to prevent "vision laziness," a phenomenon where prolonged pure-language pre-training degrades native visual capabilities by reducing visual pathway commitment and integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence is experiencing a significant shift in the development of foundation models, transitioning from unimodal language models to natively unified multimodal systems. Initially, vision was integrated into language models through "late-fusion" approaches, where pre-trained visual encoders were aligned with pre-trained language models. While this method facilitated the initial incorporation of visual capabilities, it inherently constrained the models by forcing rich visual signals into a pre-existing language representation space, thereby bottlenecking their full potential.

A subsequent evolution introduced "early-fusion" methods, which integrate vision from the initial stages of training. This allowed visual and language representations to co-evolve, leading to more native and robust visual understanding capacities. The current trajectory in research is moving towards "unified multimodal models," which extend beyond mere text and visual understanding to treat visual generation as a core, simultaneous objective within a single model architecture. This new paradigm aims for a holistic integration where all modalities are foundational to the model's learning process.

Despite this momentum, the intricate design space and the fundamental mechanisms governing how modalities interact during unified training remain largely underexplored. Much of the current design relies on heuristics rather than principled understanding. Furthermore, many existing approaches construct unified models by retrofitting pre-trained large language models (LLMs) or multimodal large language models (MLLMs), often treating vision as a module to be appended rather than an active participant in shaping the foundational pre-training. This approach obscures the true dynamics of vision's role in a natively multimodal system. This paper directly addresses this gap by seeking to establish a principled, evidence-based foundation for understanding the "physics" of unified multimodal pre-training, aiming to replace intuition with systematic empirical clarity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to move beyond heuristic-driven design in unified multimodal pre-training by providing empirical clarity through systematic exploration. The authors are motivated by the increasing complexity of the design space for these models and the underexplored fundamental mechanisms that govern how different modalities interact during joint training. The overarching goal is to understand the underlying "physics" of multimodal learning to establish a principled foundation for designing, unifying, and scaling future unified multimodal models.

Specifically, the paper identifies four key areas for detailed investigation:

1. **Knowledge Flow:** The study aims to disentangle how knowledge is transferred between language, visual understanding, and visual generation. This involves investigating the patterns of influence and asymmetry in knowledge transfer across these distinct capabilities. The motivation is to understand which modalities act as strong priors or boosters for others, and whether these transfers are symmetric or unidirectional.
2. **Modality Synergy vs. Competition:** The research seeks to identify the conditions under which different modalities either synergize (reinforce each other's learning) or compete for finite model capacity. This includes exploring how data and task complexity influence these interactions, and whether specific architectural choices can promote synergy or mitigate competition. Understanding this dynamic is crucial for efficient resource allocation and model design.
3. **Early Unification:** The authors investigate the temporal and strategic dimensions of integrating modalities during training. They aim to determine whether unifying modalities from the very early stages of training is more effective than late alignment or sequential training. A specific motivation here is to understand the potential consequences of delayed integration, such as reliance on pre-existing language priors and reduced native visual capabilities.
4. **Efficient Pretraining Recipes:** Building on the insights from the first three objectives, a practical motivation is to synthesize these findings into actionable, efficient pre-training recipes. This involves optimizing data mixing ratios and integrating architectural and dynamic insights to achieve strong performance while managing computational budgets. The ultimate goal is to provide a scalable blueprint for developing highly capable unified multimodal foundation models.

Through these objectives, the paper seeks to provide evidence-based guidelines for the fundamental aspects of multimodal pre-training, thereby advancing the theoretical understanding and practical implementation of these complex systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a systematic, empirical approach involving controlled experiments on both synthetic and large-scale real-world datasets. The methodology was designed to isolate fundamental behaviors of multimodal learning by varying specific factors and observing their impact on model performance across different modalities.

**Model Architecture and Training Protocol:**
The core model architecture is a decoder-only Transformer, designed to closely adhere to the Llama-3 architecture. This includes features such as SwiGLU activations, RoPE (Rotary Position Embeddings) with a high base frequency (θ = 500,000), pre-RMSNorm, grouped-query attention, QK-norm, and FlashAttention. The default backbone for controlled experiments is a 1.5B parameter Llama-3-like model, which, with modality-specific split Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) for text and image tokens, totals 2.3B parameters. It consists of 16 layers with a hidden dimension of 2048, GQA with 32 query heads and 8 key-value heads, and an FFN expansion multiplier of 1.5. The Llama-3 BPE tokenizer (128,000 vocabulary) is augmented with multimodal control tokens.

The Transfusion framework is adopted to natively integrate vision and language, unifying discrete next-token prediction for text and continuous flow matching for visual generation within a single model.
Optimization is performed using AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, weight decay=0.1, gradient clipping=1.0). A cosine decay learning rate schedule with a linear warm-up (first 8000 steps) is used. The continuous diffusion flow-matching loss is up-weighted by a factor of 3.0 relative to the discrete text cross-entropy loss. Training timesteps t ∈ are sampled from a logit-normal distribution. Models are trained with a context length of 4096 tokens in bf16 precision under FSDP-2, with training budgets ranging from 100B to 2T tokens. A fixed random seed (0) is used for reproducibility.

**Visual Tokenization Configurations:**
Four distinct visual tokenization schemes were investigated:
1. **RAE (Default):** A frozen SigLIP-2 ViT-400m/14 encoder processes 224x224 images into a 16x16 grid of 256 semantic tokens. These tokens are used for both understanding and generation, with generation performing flow matching in the SigLIP latent space and decoded by a Representation Autoencoder.
2. **Raw Pixels:** The encoder/decoder is removed. 224x224 images are patchified by a single 14x14 convolution into a 16x16 grid of 256 patch tokens, then projected by a lightweight MLP to the transformer hidden dimension. These tokens are consumed for understanding and denoised for generation.
3. **CLIP + VAE:** SigLIP-2 is used for understanding at 224x224 (256 tokens), while generation operates at 256x256 in the Stable Diffusion 3 VAE latent space (8x downsampling, 16 channels), denoising a 32x32 latent grid.
4. **AR (UniTok):** An autoregressive configuration using discrete visual codes from UniTok. Images are represented by residual-quantized codes, predicted autoregressively across spatial positions using a dedicated causal depth head and a cross-entropy objective.

**Pretraining Data:**
Language data is sourced from DCLM. For vision, a collection of approximately 350M image-text pairs from Shutterstock-Image (SSTK) serves as the exclusive source for both image-to-text understanding and text-to-image conditional generation.

**Evaluation Protocols:**
- **Language Evaluation:** Assessed using an unweighted average over 11 standard benchmarks (e.g., ARC-Easy/Challenge, BoolQ, HellaSwag, NaturalQuestions) for few-shot downstream accuracy and validation perplexity on DCLM (in-distribution) and C4 (out-of-distribution).
- **VQA Evaluation:** Visual understanding is comprehensively evaluated across 16 benchmarks categorized into four axes: General, Knowledge, OCR & Chart, and Vision-Centric. Fine-tuning is performed on the Cambrian-7M instruction dataset (one epoch) after pretraining to obtain VQA scores.
- **Visual Generation Evaluation:** Measured by compositional text-to-image capability (GenEval), dense-prompt generation (DPG-Bench), CLIP similarity scores across varying prompt lengths, and held-out diffusion loss.

**Controlled Studies:**
- **Modality Knowledge Flow:** Systematic mixture experiments on real-world data (SSTK/DCLM) varied the ratio of language, visual understanding, and visual generation tokens. A synthetic CLEVR dataset (procedurally generated with controlled concepts like color, shape, relation, size, count) was used to isolate causal statements about concept transfer through concept ablation (removing specific concepts from one modality's training stream while retaining them in others) and fine-tuning experiments to probe latent priors.
- **Synergy vs. Competition:** Experiments varied data and task complexity (e.g., pure backgrounds, noise, simple text, nonsense text, real-world data) while holding other factors constant. Architectural components (FFN, Attention, FinalNorm) were systematically shared or decoupled to study their impact on cross-modal interactions.
- **Early Unification:** The timing of visual data introduction was varied (0 to 800B pure-language tokens before unified training). Different sequences of unimodal training stages (e.g., L→U→G, G→U→L) were compared against joint training, with and without data replay mechanisms.
- **Vision Laziness Analysis:** Four independent mechanistic measurements (training-time FFN activation L2, image-wrapper token embedding L2 norm, inference-time per-element FFN activation, inference-time attention on image tokens) were used to diagnose the internal state of models under different language warm-up durations.

**Scaling Validation:**
The derived insights were validated at scale by training multiple 13.5B MoE models on 2T tokens, using controlled, single-variable comparisons against baselines (balanced data recipe, dense model, late-fusion approach).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded four key insights into the "physics" of multimodal pre-training:

#### 4.1. Knowledge Flow (Finding 1 & 2): Asymmetric and Concept-Dependent Transfer

- **Real-world data:** Knowledge transfer is asymmetric. Language acts as a universal booster, consistently improving all visual tasks (understanding and generation). Visual understanding serves as a strong prior for visual generation, significantly enhancing generation metrics and reducing diffusion loss. However, visual generation yields largely neutral effects on language and visual understanding capabilities, indicating limited backward knowledge transfer.
- **Synthetic data (CLEVR):** Knowledge flow is concept-dependent.
 - **Zero-shot Transfer:** Low-level semantic attributes (e.g., color, shape) show no zero-shot transfer; they must be explicitly learned within each task objective. Higher-level structural concepts (e.g., spatial relations, size, object count) exhibit asymmetric transfer: visual understanding provides zero-shot transfer to generation, enabling the model to generate concepts it only saw during understanding training. Conversely, generation provides minimal zero-shot transfer to understanding, with only a slight exception for counting.
 - **Latent Priors:** For low-level concepts where zero-shot transfer failed, latent knowledge transfer was observed during fine-tuning. Visual generation training established robust, fine-grained visual representations that, while not immediately accessible for zero-shot VQA, significantly accelerated the acquisition of understanding capabilities (e.g., 13-27% higher mean accuracy during recovery). Conversely, understanding-based prior exposure offered minimal advantage for low-level visual generation. This indicates that generative objectives force the learning of dense visual features that are valuable for understanding tasks requiring precision, even if not directly usable without explicit mapping.

#### 4.2. Synergy vs. Competition (Finding 3): Governed by Task Complexity and Parameter Sharing

- **Data and Task Complexity:** Modality interaction is influenced by task difficulty. Simple tasks in one modality (e.g., pure background images, synthetic "nonsense" text) act as cross-modal boosters, improving performance in the other modality (e.g., better language perplexity, lower visual generation loss). Conversely, highly complex, real-world data distributions (e.g., SSTK images, DCLM text) can induce capacity competition, where the benefits of synergy are outweighed by the demands on shared parameters, leading to degradation compared to unimodal baselines.
- **Parameter Sharing:** Architectural choices dictate synergy and competition.
 - **Fully shared (dense) parameters:** Lead to severe modality competition, degrading both language and vision performance.
 - **Decoupled Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs):** Separating FFNs per modality while keeping attention and normalization shared (split_ffn) effectively mitigates competition and promotes strong synergy, significantly improving both language and visual generation. This suggests FFNs act as modality-specific experts.
 - **Shared Attention and Normalization:** These components appear crucial for synergy. Decoupling attention mechanisms or normalization layers significantly diminishes cross-modal benefits, while completely isolating all components results in performance akin to unimodal baselines. This indicates shared attention facilitates cross-modal contextualization, and shared normalization aligns feature scales.
- **Generalization Across Vision Encoders:** The observed modality synergies generalize across diverse visual tokenization designs, including RAE, Raw Pixels, CLIP + VAE, and AR (UniTok). This indicates that the principles of synergy are tied more to learning objectives and architectural parameter-sharing strategies than to specific visual representation choices.

#### 4.3. The Necessity of Early Unification (Finding 4 & 5): Preventing Vision Laziness

- **Timing of Unification:** Early and simultaneous multimodal integration is highly beneficial. Delaying the introduction of visual data (i.e., a longer pure-language pre-training warm-up) consistently degrades native visual capabilities, impacting both understanding and generation. While an initial pure-language phase can provide a foundational text prior, prolonged language-only training significantly cripples visual performance.
- **Sequential vs. Joint Training:** Simultaneous joint training decisively outperforms sequential training approaches, regardless of the order in which modalities are introduced. Even with data replay mechanisms to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, sequential training fails to match the performance of co-evolving modalities. This indicates that modalities actively reinforce each other and treating them as discrete curriculum steps hinders the development of cross-modal synergies.
- **Vision Laziness in Late Alignment (Finding 5):** Delaying multimodal unification induces "vision laziness." The longer the pure-language pre-training, the less the visual pathway commits itself, and the more disconnected it becomes from the language manifold. This was demonstrated by:
 - **Reduced Activations:** Progressively lower L2 norms of image-specific FFN activations during both training and inference.
 - **Weakened Integration:** Shrinking L2 norms of image-wrapper token embeddings, indicating weaker integration into the language manifold.
 - **Diminished Attention to Images:** A sharp drop in the fraction of attention placed on image tokens during inference, suggesting the model relies more on language priors and less on visual input. Qualitatively, late-aligned models struggle with multi-attribute binding and structural details in generation, producing simplified or abstract shapes. This phenomenon suggests that a hardened language pathway can "shortcut" visual problems, preventing the visual components from fully engaging.

#### 4.4. Designing Unified Pretraining Recipes (Finding 6): Asymmetric and Efficient Scaling

- **Optimizing Data Mixing Ratios:** Visual generation is highly data-efficient when trained jointly with more understanding and language data. Extensive grid searches revealed that an optimal data mixture is highly asymmetric (e.g., L70/U25/G5 for Language, Visual Understanding, and Visual Generation, respectively). This leverages the strong unidirectional knowledge flow where language and understanding bootstrap generation, avoiding wasted compute on balanced generation data.
- **Scaling Validation:** Large-scale evaluations (13.5B MoE models on 2T tokens) consistently validated these findings:
 - **Asymmetric Data Mix (L70/U25/G5):** Significantly improved language accuracy, visual understanding, and generative evaluation metrics compared to a balanced mix (L50/U25/G25), even with five times fewer generative tokens. Generative quality, measured by FID, remained highly competitive.
 - **MoE Architecture:** The MoE architecture (with shared attention/normalization and modality-specific FFNs) consistently outperformed a dense baseline, demonstrating the efficacy of architectural decoupling for managing competition while fostering synergy.
 - **Early Unification:** Early unified pre-training consistently provided clear advantages over a late-fusion baseline in language accuracy, visual understanding average, and visual generation performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field by providing a systematic, evidence-based understanding of the "physics" governing unified multimodal pre-training, moving beyond heuristic-driven design. The findings have several significant implications:

1. **Principled Foundation:** The study establishes a principled foundation for designing and scaling multimodal models. By empirically clarifying knowledge flow, synergy/competition dynamics, and optimal training schedules, it provides concrete guidelines for future research and development, replacing intuition with verifiable evidence.
2. **Challenging Conventional Wisdom:** The work challenges the prevalent practice of late-fusion and late-alignment in multimodal model development. By demonstrating the "vision laziness" phenomenon, it highlights the necessity of early and simultaneous unification for robust visual learning, ensuring vision is treated as a first-class modality rather than an afterthought.
3. **Efficiency and Scalability:** The derivation of efficient pre-training recipes, particularly the highly asymmetric data mixing ratios (e.g., L70/U25/G5), indicates that strong generative performance can be achieved with a relatively small fraction of dedicated generation tokens. This is significant because it suggests that native generation capabilities can be integrated into general-purpose foundation models without incurring a disproportionate computational penalty, thereby making multimodal generation more accessible and scalable.
4. **Architectural Guidance:** The findings regarding parameter sharing provide clear architectural guidance, supporting the use of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) designs where shared attention and normalization layers foster synergy, while modality-specific FFNs mitigate capacity competition. This informs the optimal internal structure of unified models.
5. **Advancement Towards Omnimodal Models:** By offering a scalable blueprint for systems with native, high-dimensional I/O, this research accelerates the trajectory towards "omnimodal" foundation models capable of supporting arbitrary input and output formats across text, images, video, audio, and beyond. This deeper integration and co-evolution of modalities are essential for developing more predictive, interactive, and intelligent world models.

In summary, this study provides fundamental insights into the intricate interplay of modalities during unified pre-training, offering practical strategies that can lead to the development of more capable, efficient, and truly multimodal AI systems.
