# RoboMD: Uncovering Robot Vulnerabilities through Semantic Potential Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> ROBOMD enables the diagnosis of robot manipulation policy vulnerabilities by training a reinforcement learning agent to actively search for failure-inducing environmental variations within a learned semantic embedding space. The approach achieved an 80.7% average failure diagnosis accuracy and improved policy robustness by 92.83% with 85% less fine-tuning data compared to untargeted methods.

## Problem

- Identifying vulnerabilities in robot manipulation policies often requires costly, slow, and potentially unsafe physical trials.
- The full range of relevant environmental variations that can cause policy failures is frequently unknown and diverse, making exhaustive testing impractical.
- Existing methods, such as querying large language models or using other DRL approaches, struggle to reliably predict specific manipulation failures or generalize beyond a fixed set of known issues.

## Method

- **ROBOMD** formalizes failure diagnosis as an ==active search problem== within a continuous ==vision-language embedding space==, which functions as a ==semantic potential field== of success/failure likelihoods.
- A separate ==vulnerability prediction policy== (π_MD) is trained using ==Deep Reinforcement Learning (PPO)== to navigate this embedding space and proactively discover failure-inducing environmental conditions.
- The embedding space is constructed using a ==dual-backbone architecture== (==ViT== and ==CLIP==) trained with a ==joint binary cross-entropy and contrastive loss==, allowing for generalization to unseen environmental variations.

## Results

- ROBOMD achieved an average failure diagnosis accuracy of **80.7%** across four manipulation tasks (lift, stack, threading, pick & place), outperforming state-of-the-art VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o below **60%**) and other RL baselines.
- The framework demonstrated generalization by accurately ranking failure likelihoods for entirely unseen environmental variations in both real-world and simulated settings.
- Using RoboMD's prioritized failure modes for targeted fine-tuning improved a pre-trained BC-lift policy's accuracy by **92.83%** while requiring only **1.3 GB** of data, an **85%** reduction compared to **9.0 GB** for untargeted fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A semantically rich embedding space can effectively capture the continuous relationship between environmental variations and robot policy success/failure, enabling diagnosis without direct physical interaction.
> - Active exploration guided by reinforcement learning in this learned continuous space allows for systematic and sample-efficient discovery of novel and subtle failure modes.
> - The theoretical underpinnings of advantage invariance and sample-efficient boundary exploration ensure that the DRL agent can find optimal failure-inducing conditions and converge quickly.
