# Open-AoE: An Open Egocentric Manipulation Dataset and Toolchain for Embodied Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The AoE Team introduced Open-AoE, an open egocentric manipulation dataset and toolchain, providing approximately 2,000 hours of real-world human interaction data captured with commodity smartphones. This resource includes structured annotations and an integrated pipeline to support various embodied learning paradigms, demonstrating superior visual diversity and annotation consistency compared to existing datasets.

## Problem

- Embodied foundation models face data scarcity and quality issues, as real-world interaction data with sufficient geometric and semantic structure is limited.
- Existing egocentric datasets often rely on specialized hardware or controlled capture, limiting scalability and accessibility, while large-scale passive video lacks fine-grained annotations.
- The absence of open, unified data infrastructure and fragmented processing pipelines hinders continuous data collection, systematic processing, and direct support for model training.

## Method

- **Open-AoE** provides a large-scale, diverse egocentric dataset of approximately 2,000 hours, collected using consumer-grade smartphones in natural environments by over 500 participants.
- An end-to-end cloud-side data processing pipeline transforms raw videos into structured training samples, including hand reconstruction (==MANO poses==), ==camera trajectory estimation==, and ==atomic action labeling==.
- A comprehensive downstream toolchain supports data visualization, ==4D hand-object interaction reconstruction==, ==cross-embodiment motion retargeting==, and offers training-ready representations for Vision-Language-Action policies, World Action Models, and World Models.

## Results

- The initial release includes **2,000 hours** of egocentric manipulation data from over **500** contributors, **400+** device models, **400+** scenes, and **8,000+** tasks, with structured text, MANO hand poses, and camera trajectories.
- Open-AoE exhibited higher visual diversity compared to OpenEgo, EgoDex, and EgoXtreme, achieving an Effective Rank of **97.43** and a kNN Domain Mixing of **0.0775**.
- The dataset demonstrated strong image-annotation consistency with a sequence-macro score of **4.583/5** (Idefics2 evaluation) and near-complete temporal annotation coverage of **99.99%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scalable, high-quality egocentric data for embodied learning can be continuously collected using commodity smartphones, enabled by on-device intelligence and robust cloud-side processing.
> - A unified infrastructure integrating systematic processing, reconstruction, and diverse multimodal annotations is crucial for converting raw video into actionable data for robot learning.
> - An open, community-driven ecosystem fostering data contribution and reuse can significantly accelerate the development of embodied foundation models and human-to-robot transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The development of embodied foundation models faces a constraint in the availability and quality of real-world interaction data. Unlike language models, which can leverage internet-scale text, robot learning requires physical demonstrations that capture human perception, hand movements, object interactions, and task completion. This data needs to be not only extensive and diverse but also structured geometrically, temporally, and semantically for effective manipulation learning. Research indicates that diversity across environments and objects can be a significant factor in scaling robot learning.

Egocentric video, captured from the actor's viewpoint, offers a scalable modality for this problem. It records hands, objects, scenes, and action progression within a single visual stream, which aligns with the perceptual structure of robot execution more closely than third-person video. Furthermore, egocentric video can be collected more continuously and economically in everyday environments compared to robot teleoperation.

Recent advancements in egocentric datasets, such as Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS, EgoDex, EgoLive, OpenEgo, and EgoScale, have addressed various aspects of data collection. These efforts have included larger-scale daily activity capture, finer-grained hand pose annotation, richer manipulation scenarios, and improved alignment with robot learning requirements. However, a notable gap persists: the absence of open data infrastructure capable of continuous collection, systematic processing, and direct support for model training. Existing high-quality egocentric datasets frequently rely on specialized hardware (e.g., head-mounted devices, AR/VR) or controlled capture protocols, which can limit accessibility. Conversely, passively collected large-scale video often lacks the detailed hand motion, camera trajectory, action boundaries, and physical structure necessary for effective robot training. While some efforts have introduced dataset-specific pipelines for hand-pose unification or human-to-robot transfer, these pipelines remain fragmented. This fragmentation highlights a distinction between merely releasing video footage and providing data that is readily usable for training embodied models.

Open-AoE positions itself to bridge this gap by introducing an open, community-oriented egocentric manipulation dataset and an integrated toolchain. It aims to provide a unified infrastructure that spans the entire pipeline from commodity smartphone capture to model training, thereby reducing barriers to both data contribution and reuse for the embodied intelligence research community.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of the Open-AoE project is to establish an open data infrastructure that facilitates the continuous collection, systematic processing, and direct utilization of egocentric human manipulation data for embodied learning. This infrastructure aims to address several limitations observed in the current landscape of egocentric datasets and robot learning approaches.

The motivation for Open-AoE stems from the recognition that embodied foundation models are increasingly constrained by the scale and quality of real-world interaction data. While egocentric video offers a promising avenue for scalable data collection, existing resources typically fall short in combining three critical aspects:
1. **Low-cost continuous capture:** Many high-quality datasets necessitate specialized, expensive hardware or controlled lab settings, limiting their accessibility and scalability for diverse, in-the-wild collection. Large-scale passive video, while cheap, often lacks the necessary detailed annotations.
2. **Manipulation-level structured annotations:** Raw egocentric video, even if large-scale, often lacks the fine-grained geometric (e.g., hand poses, camera trajectories) and semantic (e.g., atomic actions, object labels) structures crucial for robot learning. Existing annotation pipelines are often fragmented or specific to particular datasets, hindering broader utility.
3. **Reusable tools for robot learning:** The absence of a unified, open-source toolchain means researchers frequently have to develop their own data processing and model adaptation stacks, which is resource-intensive and creates silos. This fragmentation prevents a direct path from raw video to training-ready data for various embodied learning paradigms.

To address these challenges, Open-AoE sets forth three specific contributions as its key objectives:
- **Release of a large-scale, diverse egocentric dataset:** To provide approximately 2,000 hours of real egocentric human manipulation data. This data is collected using consumer-grade smartphones in natural environments, emphasizing diversity in participants (500+), devices (400+), and everyday manipulation scenarios. This aims to provide a robust foundation for learning.
- **Provision of an end-to-end data processing pipeline:** To open-source a cloud-side pipeline that transforms raw smartphone videos into structured training samples. This pipeline includes quality screening, privacy erasure, temporal action segmentation, semantic labeling, camera-trajectory estimation, hand reconstruction (MANO poses), and multi-stage quality inspection. The goal is to convert unstructured video into high-quality, actionable data.
- **Development of a comprehensive downstream embodied learning toolchain:** To offer a separate toolchain that supports data visualization, 4D hand-object interaction reconstruction, cross-embodiment motion retargeting, and the generation of robotized videos. Furthermore, this toolchain provides training-ready representations and interfaces specifically designed for three major model directions: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, World Action Models (WAMs), and World Models. This aims to lower the technical barrier for researchers to utilize the data for diverse embodied AI tasks.

By integrating scalable capture, structured processing, and downstream adaptation, Open-AoE seeks to reduce friction for both data contribution and data reuse, thereby establishing an open infrastructure for embodied model training, human-to-robot transfer, and world modeling research. The overarching motivation is to move beyond isolated video collections and create a continuous, reusable data production and consumption loop.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The Open-AoE project adopts a modular and end-to-end approach, encompassing data capture, a multi-stage processing pipeline, and a comprehensive downstream toolchain for embodied learning.

**Data Capture: Edge-side Online Detection (AoE Capture App)**
Data collection relies on a lightweight, on-device vision system integrated into a smartphone application. This system performs real-time detection and control to ensure that only relevant, high-quality clips are recorded and uploaded.
- **Hand Visibility Check:** Automatically initiates recording when hands appear in frame and ceases when they exit, prompting users to readjust for full visibility of manipulating subjects.
- **Operating and Wearing Compliance:** Monitors user gestures, scene types (to flag non-standard actions or non-compliant capture environments), wearing poses, and view stability, providing real-time voice feedback for corrections.
- **Lighting Adaptation:** Activates constant fill light and global auto-exposure in low-light conditions.
- **Performance Stability:** Incorporates fixed focal length, motion stabilization, deblurring, and adaptive frame rate to maintain real-time performance, stopping recording if storage is insufficient or the device overheats.
- **Privacy Preservation:** This front-end design primarily retains clips with meaningful hand-object interaction, protecting privacy at the source and minimizing data upload to the cloud.

**Data Processing Pipeline (Cloud-side)**
Raw smartphone recordings undergo a four-stage offline processing pipeline to transform them into structured, anonymized training samples.

1. **Online Process (Edge-side):** (Described above) Valid and permitted clips are uploaded, incorporating privacy-preserving measures.

2. **Offline Quality Check & Scene Labeling:**
 - **Image-based Detection:** Rule-based detectors identify common video defects (aspect ratio, integrity, exposure, duration) and remove unusable recordings. A valid-recording check filters out clips without visible hands, non-egocentric views, or severe camera jitter. Security-information erasure detects and masks (pixelates) faces and sensitive content, replacing personally identifiable information in metadata with anonymized identifiers. A hand re-check verifies clear hand visibility, screening for residual third-person views, truncation, or excessive occlusion. Camera intrinsics are validated for completeness.
 - **Video Slicing and Frame-Rate Fixation:** Videos are segmented into "Parts" based on contiguous qualified or unqualified spans, and their frame rates are standardized. Each Part maintains lineage to its source video and accumulates multi-level labels.
 - **Large-Model Detection:** The Qwen3.7-Plus vision-language model performs semantic-level compliance checks and valid-action detection (ensuring purposeful, complete manipulation). It then constructs a four-level label tree (domain, scene, task, objects) from predefined sets, standardizing outputs into hierarchical labels.

3. **Reconstruction & Annotation:**
 - **Camera Trajectory Estimation:** DROID-W, re-tuned for handheld/wearable capture, estimates 6-DoF camera poses, maintaining stability despite camera shake and torso motion.
 - **Hand Reconstruction:** A specialized two-hand detector, trained on AoE data, provides per-frame bounding boxes and handles missing/occluded hands. HaWoR recovers 3D MANO meshes, metric-scale aligned via SLAM and transformed into world coordinates, with sliding-window optimization for temporal consistency.
 - **Global Bundle Adjustment:** Hand meshes and camera trajectories are jointly optimized with DROID-W dynamic-object masks in a single world coordinate frame, resolving scale drift and coordinate mismatches.
 - **Atomic Action Slicing & Labeling:** Videos are segmented into semantically coherent atomic clips, labeled in English, with human-in-the-loop review for corrections.

4. **Quality Inspection & Data Delivery:**
 - A three-gate quality inspection ensures data usability:
 - **Completeness:** High valid-frame ratio for hand reconstruction.
 - **Correctness:** Low inverse-kinematics failure rate for retargeting to 28-DoF joint space.
 - **Consistency:** Smooth, continuous camera trajectories without abrupt jumps.
 - A randomly sampled subset undergoes human inspection to confirm annotation accuracy and filter out edge cases (e.g., severe occlusion). This process yields the anonymized training corpus, with approximately 2,000 hours curated for open-source release.

**Downstream Toolchain (AoE Toolchain)**
The Open-AoE Toolchain converts synchronized egocentric video, hand geometry, camera motion, and action semantics into research assets.

- **AoE-Visualization:** Provides an interface for synchronized inspection and error diagnosis, overlaying MANO meshes, skeletons, wrist trajectories, and atomic actions on the undistorted egocentric video, alongside a world-frame 3D view and timeline.
- **AoE-Reconstruct-Retarget:** Connects egocentric observation to three outputs:
 - **4D Hand-Object Reconstruction:** Integrates methods like EgoInfinity and Do-as-I-Do to derive time-varying hand geometry, object pose, and interaction assets, leveraging calibrated intrinsics, camera trajectories, MANO priors, and action segments.
 - **Cross-embodiment Retargeting:** Maps human motion to various robot embodiments (e.g., Unitree G1, Galbot/Galaxea, Sharpa, XHand) using IK-based or physics-based approaches, guided by bilateral MANO trajectories, validity masks, and action boundaries.
 - **Robotized Video:** Generates videos where the human arm is removed and a simulated robot is composited into the original scene using video inpainting and MuJoCo rendering, facilitating visual-domain transfer and retargeting diagnosis.
- **AoE-Training-Ready:** Adapts synchronized source signals to model-specific action semantics for different learning paradigms. It provides diverse action representations, including:
 - **Dense MANO State/Action:** A 110D per-hand vector detailing validity, wrist translation, axis-angle, velocity, and MANO pose in camera coordinates.
 - **Robot-Facing Interfaces:** Includes 48D wrist-fingertip trajectories in SLAM world frame, 62D Sharpa robot joint actions, and 20D GR00T gripper actions.
 - **Hand + Camera Dynamics:** A 22D state / 20D-26D ego action format combining hand motion in camera frame with optional camera ego-motion deltas.
 - **Latent/Weak Action:** Supports models that infer action variables from visual changes, providing structured supervision to ground these variables.
 - Recipes and interfaces are provided for VLA policies (e.g., VITRA, H-RDT, GR00T N1.7), World Action Models (e.g., DreamZero, LingBot-VA), and World Models (e.g., DreamDojo, AdaWorld), enabling researchers to use the data for various learning objectives.

### 4. Main findings and results

The Open-AoE project presents a dataset and toolchain characterized by its scale, diversity, and structured multimodal annotations, supported by comparative analysis with existing datasets.

**Dataset Scale and Content:**
The first release of Open-AoE comprises approximately 2,000 hours of egocentric human manipulation video. This data was collected in natural environments by over 500 contributors using more than 400 different smartphone models, spanning over 400 distinct scenes and involving more than 8,000 tasks. The dataset provides structured annotations including text descriptions, MANO-based hand poses, camera trajectories, and temporally localized atomic actions.

**Data Distribution Analysis (based on a 100-hour random sample):**
- **Semantic Diversity:** The sample contains 32,407 distinct natural-language action descriptions, 175 action verbs, 8,030 object strings, and 135 scene labels. This indicates a broad coverage of reusable manipulation primitives and a variety of interaction objects across diverse settings (kitchens, tabletops, offices, bedrooms, and other environments).
- **Temporal Annotation Characteristics:** The temporal annotations achieve 99.99% coverage of the evaluated timeline, with a mean segment duration of 9.64 seconds and an annotation density of 13.97 segments per minute.
- **Contributor Coverage:** The dataset exhibits a long-tailed distribution of contributions across anonymous collector IDs, with a median contribution of 2.6 minutes per ID. The ten largest contributors account for only 13.7% of the sampled duration, suggesting broad variation in manipulation habits and recording environments.
- **Consumer-Camera Domain:** The 100-hour sample covers over 400 consumer smartphone market models, encompassing substantial diversity in device brands and models. This introduces natural camera-domain variation (lens, sensor, image-signal processor, resolution, exposure, firmware) at capture time.
- **Optical Diversity:** The user-weighted horizontal-FOV distribution is multimodal, with concentrations around 65°–75° and 90°–95°, reflecting diverse camera and lens configurations. This variation provides a form of natural sensor-domain randomization.

**Comparative Analysis of High-Dimensional Visual Diversity:**
Using CLIP image embeddings and comparing with OpenEgo, EgoDex, and EgoXtreme (from 3,000 balanced random subsamples of 2,000 embeddings each), Open-AoE demonstrated high visual diversity:
- It achieved the highest mean in all six metrics: Effective Rank (97.43), Participation Ratio (40.47), Meaningful Coverage (19.89), Normalized Cluster Entropy (0.712), Effective Clusters (16.29), and kNN Domain Mixing (0.0775).
- The largest advantages were observed in Effective Rank and kNN Domain Mixing, indicating that Open-AoE spans a greater number of active feature directions and maintains stronger local connectivity to other visual domains.
- Open-AoE consistently ranked first in Effective Rank, Participation Ratio, and kNN Domain Mixing across all 3,000 trials.

**Semantic Breadth and Temporal Completeness:**
Compared to OpenEgo and EgoDex, Open-AoE combines broad natural-language supervision with near-complete temporal coverage.
- It offers 32,407 distinct natural-language action descriptions, significantly more than OpenEgo's 26,864 native labels and EgoDex's 111 categorical action classes.
- Its annotations cover 99.99% of the evaluated timeline, substantially exceeding OpenEgo's 50.1% coverage, while maintaining a high annotation density of 13.97 segments per minute.

**Image-Annotation Consistency:**
An assessment using Idefics2 as a shared vision-language evaluator (comparing with OpenEgo, EgoDex, EgoXtreme) showed that Open-AoE achieved a sequence-macro score of 4.583/5.
- This score substantially exceeded those of OpenEgo (3.029), EgoDex (2.916), and EgoXtreme (2.015).
- 85.4% of Open-AoE sequences attained a mean consistency score of at least 4, indicating strong agreement between annotations and visual content across a majority of sequences.

**Training-Window Retention and Multimodal Completeness:**
- Open-AoE yields approximately 1,760 candidate history-future windows per hour (using 2s history, 2s future, 2s stride), representing 97.8% of the theoretical ceiling. This indicates high temporal continuity suitable for dense sample extraction.
- It exhibits high native hand-signal availability: 98.93% of entries contain at least one valid hand signal, and 98.02% contain valid signals for both hands, which is higher than OpenEgo.
- Open-AoE is the only evaluated release where all five principal supervision modalities (action annotations, bounding boxes, annotation confidence, hand pose, and camera pose) are available with near-universal sequence-level coverage. Bounding box quality is also high, with 100% valid boxes and a mean confidence of 0.947.

These results indicate that Open-AoE provides a dataset with substantial visual and semantic diversity, high annotation quality, and comprehensive multimodal supervision, designed to be readily usable for various embodied learning tasks.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

Open-AoE's development and release aim to address several critical limitations in the field of embodied intelligence, with implications for future research and applications.

**Addressing Data Bottlenecks:** The project's most immediate significance lies in tackling the data scarcity and quality issues that currently constrain the development of embodied foundation models. By providing approximately 2,000 hours of structured, real-world egocentric manipulation data, Open-AoE offers a substantial resource that can support data-intensive learning approaches for robot control and understanding.

**Lowering Barriers to Research:** Open-AoE aims to reduce the technical and logistical barriers for researchers in several ways:
- **Data Contribution:** The use of commodity smartphones for capture simplifies the process of contributing data, potentially enabling a broader community to participate in data collection.
- **Data Consumption:** The integrated data processing pipeline transforms raw video into structured, training-ready samples, eliminating the need for individual research teams to develop complex, custom pre-processing stacks. This unification is intended to streamline the research workflow.
- **Tooling for Diverse Paradigms:** The comprehensive downstream toolchain supports various embodied learning paradigms, including Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, World Action Models (WAMs), and World Models. This flexibility means that researchers can apply the data to their specific areas of interest without rebuilding core infrastructure.

**Fostering Robustness and Generalization:** The dataset's extensive diversity, spanning over 500 contributors, 400 device types, 400 scenes, and 8,000 tasks, is intended to promote more robust and generalizable models. The inherent variations in camera optics (e.g., FOV, imaging pipelines) due to diverse smartphone models introduce a form of natural sensor-domain randomization, which may enhance model robustness when transferring to unseen cameras or robot platforms. This diversity across "what people manipulate, where and how they act, and the consumer-camera domain" is positioned as a foundation for visually robust, cross-domain manipulation learning.

**Establishing an Open Ecosystem:** Open-AoE is presented not as a static data release but as an open, community-oriented ecosystem. This approach encourages ongoing contributions from data collectors, universities, research institutions, and robot developers in terms of new real-world scenes, tasks, reconstruction methods, retargeting techniques, training adapters, and evaluation results. This open-loop development is expected to facilitate the co-evolution of data, tools, and models, accelerating progress in embodied AI.

**Enabling Advanced Embodied AI:** By offering dense, jointly available semantic, geometric, hand-motion, and camera-motion supervision, Open-AoE provides the necessary signals for developing more capable embodied AI agents. Its utility spans various applications, from learning human-to-robot motion transfer to developing models that understand and predict complex human-object interactions in diverse, real-world settings. This can contribute to the creation of robots that learn from natural human demonstrations and operate effectively in unstructured environments.
