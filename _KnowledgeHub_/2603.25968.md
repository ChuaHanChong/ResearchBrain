# Neuro-Cognitive Reward Modeling for Human-Centered Autonomous Vehicle Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Technology Sydney researchers developed an EEG-guided decision-making framework for autonomous vehicles, which predicts human cognitive states from visual scenes to provide implicit reward signals for reinforcement learning. This approach yielded superior driving performance and collision avoidance in simulated emergency braking and left-turn scenarios compared to baselines, including traditional RLHF, by enabling more human-aligned and safer control policies.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end autonomous driving models struggle with human-like reasoning, generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios, and robust decision-making in complex interactive situations.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) lack efficient mechanisms to align AI behavior with human expectations and nuanced cognitive responses, with traditional RLHF being time-consuming and potentially indirect.
- Prior work using physiological signals like EEG to guide RL often requires real-time data collection during inference or responds only after an error occurs, limiting practicality and scalability.

## Method

- A novel ==EEG-guided== decision-making framework was developed to leverage neuro-cognitive signals (Event-Related Potentials, ERPs) as implicit feedback for reinforcement learning in autonomous vehicles.
- A human cognitive reward model, a lightweight CNN, was designed and trained to predict ERP occurrence directly from visual scene information, removing the need for real-time EEG during inference.
- The predicted ERP probability, representing cognitive load or surprise, was integrated as a negative component into the reward function of a TD3-based RL algorithm to guide the autonomous agent's policy.

## Results

- The lightweight CNN for ERP prediction achieved an **82%** average accuracy and a real-time inference speed of 204 frames per second, outperforming established image classification baselines.
- The proposed framework consistently achieved the highest scores in Route Completion, Driving Score, and Infraction Penalty across both emergency braking and left-turn scenarios in the CARLA simulator, surpassing several baselines including traditional RLHF.
- Analysis of machine attention revealed that the proposed model developed more focused attention on critical scene elements (e.g., the lead vehicle) compared to baselines, indicating improved internal representations for decision-making.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human cognitive responses, specifically ERPs, exhibit a statistically significant correlation with driver reaction time and cognitive effort during critical driving events, affirming their utility as an implicit feedback signal.
> - It is feasible to accurately predict human cognitive states (ERPs) from visual scene information using a lightweight CNN, which operates at high inference speeds, enabling scalable neuro-cognitive reward signaling without real-time physiological sensors.
> - Incorporating neuro-cognitive reward signals into RL significantly enhances the autonomous agent's ability to focus on critical scene elements, leading to the learning of safer and more human-aligned driving behaviors.
