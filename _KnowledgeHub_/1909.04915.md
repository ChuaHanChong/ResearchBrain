# Data-efficient Model Learning and Prediction for Contact-rich Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> A hybrid system framework integrates Gaussian Processes and reset maps to enable data-efficient model learning and accurate long-term state prediction for contact-rich robotic manipulation. This approach effectively captures discontinuous dynamics and quantifies uncertainty, outperforming existing methods in low-data regimes across simulated and physical robot tasks.

## Problem

- Learning forward dynamics models for contact-rich manipulation is challenging due to discontinuous dynamics (e.g., velocity jumps during impacts) which most continuous function approximators struggle with.
- Real-world robotic model learning requires high data efficiency, as extensive data collection is costly, time-consuming, and can cause hardware wear.
- Accumulation of errors in long-term predictions and unreliable forecasts in sparse data regions necessitate robust uncertainty quantification and propagation.

## Method

- The system dynamics are modeled as a ==hybrid automaton==, explicitly incorporating dynamics modes, transition relations, guard functions, and ==reset maps== to represent discontinuous state changes during contact.
- ==Dirichlet Process Gaussian Mixture Models (DPGMM)== automatically identify distinct dynamics modes from training data, while ==Gaussian Process Regression (GPR)== learns the individual mode dynamics and probabilistic reset maps.
- Uncertainty is propagated through the learned hybrid model using a combination of the ==Unscented Transform (UT)== for continuous dynamics/reset maps and ==Monte Carlo (MC) sampling== for the deterministic guard functions, managing probabilistic switching and multimodality.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved superior **negative log likelihood (NLL)** and **root mean square error (RMSE)** in both a simulated 1D sliding mass task and a **7-DOF YuMi** robot contact motion task, consistently outperforming standard GP, mGP, and uncertainty-aware ANN baselines.
- It successfully captured and predicted discontinuous state changes, such as velocity jumps during slip and transitions between contact modes, which the baseline models largely failed to handle.
- The approach demonstrated robust performance and accurate uncertainty quantification even with limited training data (e.g., **15 trials**), whereas some baselines showed limitations in scaling or stability with increasing data complexity or prediction horizon.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling hybrid dynamics, particularly incorporating reset maps for discontinuous state evolution, is crucial for accurate long-term prediction in contact-rich manipulation tasks, offering a structural advantage over general, unstructured models.
> - Gaussian Processes prove to be data-efficient for learning local dynamics and representing uncertainty in low-data regimes, making them well-suited for robotics applications where data collection is limited.
> - Combining structured domain knowledge (hybrid automata) with flexible, uncertainty-aware learning methods (GPs) is more effective for handling complex, discontinuous dynamics than relying solely on highly flexible but unstructured models when data is scarce.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), specifically focusing on learning forward dynamics models and multi-step (long-term) state prediction for robotic manipulation tasks involving physical contact. Such models are foundational for planning and control, including model-based policy search and model predictive control (MPC), by enabling the simulation of future system behavior to evaluate control policies.

The broader landscape of robot control extensively uses model learning, but contact-rich manipulation, characterized by discontinuous dynamics, presents unique challenges. Analytical models for these complex interactions are difficult to obtain, making data-driven learning approaches within MBRL particularly relevant. However, existing MBRL methods that address contact-involving tasks often prioritize overall task performance, leaving a gap in direct investigation of effective strategies for model learning and probabilistic long-term prediction in such scenarios.

Current state-of-the-art learning models, such as those based on Gaussian Processes (GPs) like PILCO, struggle with discontinuous dynamics when using standard kernels. Approaches like manifold GP (mGP) attempt to address discontinuities but have primarily been evaluated for single-step predictions. Uncertainty-aware neural network (ANN) models, such as those used in PETS, offer flexibility and scalability but their efficacy in low-data regimes for contact-rich manipulation is not well-established. Deterministic ANN models, lack the explicit uncertainty representation that can contribute to data efficiency. While Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) have been used to learn locally linear dynamics priors, they are less flexible than the nonlinear GP models proposed here.

Hybrid systems approaches, including switched systems (e.g.,), have been explored for robotics. However, many of these do not explicitly support discontinuous *state evolution* in long-term prediction, a critical aspect of rigid body contact, often lacking the concept of "reset maps" that define instantaneous state changes upon mode transitions. This paper aims to bridge these identified gaps by explicitly adopting a hybrid automata structure, incorporating reset maps, and leveraging the data-efficiency and uncertainty representation capabilities of Gaussian Processes, particularly for long-term prediction under low-data conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate data-efficient learning of forward dynamics models and accurate multi-step prediction of state variables (long-term prediction) for contact-rich manipulation tasks within the framework of model-based reinforcement learning.

This objective is motivated by several challenges inherent to contact-rich robotic manipulation and the limitations of existing model learning approaches:

- **Discontinuous Dynamics:** In industrial settings, contact between rigid objects is often modeled as an instantaneous event leading to discontinuous dynamics, frequently involving impulsive velocity updates. Most existing data-driven learning models, optimized for continuous functions, tend to overlook or inadequately handle these discontinuities, which can lead to inaccuracies in predictions and suboptimal control performance. The authors specifically highlight that traditional GP models with squared exponential kernels, or even flexible ANN models, struggle to learn and predict across such abrupt changes.
- **Data-Efficiency:** Model-based reinforcement learning often requires extensive data collection through physical trials. For robotic systems, a high number of trials can result in wear and tear, increase operational costs, and prolong development time. Therefore, developing models that can learn effectively from limited data is a significant practical concern and a key motivation for the research.
- **Prediction Uncertainties:** Long-term prediction involves recursively chaining one-step predictions. Small errors at each step can accumulate and lead to substantial divergence from the true system behavior. Moreover, predictions can become highly unreliable if the system enters regions of the state-action space for which little or no training data has been observed. An effective strategy, as established in prior work, is to adopt a probabilistic modeling approach that explicitly quantifies and propagates uncertainty, which can then be utilized during policy optimization to mitigate the effects of data scarcity.
- **Lack of Direct Evaluation:** While several MBRL methods have demonstrated success in contact-involving tasks, the literature often lacks a direct and detailed evaluation of how effectively these methods perform in terms of model learning and, specifically, probabilistic long-term prediction of discontinuous dynamics in low-data regimes. This paper aims to provide such an evaluation.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a method that explicitly adopts a hybrid system structure, specifically resembling a hybrid automaton. This structure is designed to inherently represent the piecewise continuous nature of contact dynamics and the abrupt transitions between modes. By integrating this hybrid structure with Gaussian Processes (GPs), the method aims to leverage GP's capabilities for data-efficient learning and robust uncertainty representation, including handling the DAgger effect (state distribution mismatch) common in MBRL. A central component of this hybrid approach is the incorporation of "reset maps" to explicitly model the discontinuous state changes that occur during contact.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is structured into two primary components: model learning for the hybrid system and uncertainty propagation through this learned model, as visually depicted in Figure 2 of the paper.

**A. Learning Models with Hybrid Structure**

The method models the system dynamics as a hybrid automaton, comprising dynamics modes, transition relations, guard functions, and reset maps.

1. **Mode Discovery through Clustering:**
 - The approach leverages the observation that data from contact-rich manipulations often exhibits distinct clusters in state space due to discontinuous velocity changes during contact events.
 - **Clustering Algorithm:** Dirichlet Process Gaussian Mixture Model (DPGMM) is applied to the training data (D_x). DPGMM automatically infers the number of clusters (dynamics modes), denoted as K.
 - **Feature Space:** A feature vector consisting of the concatenation of positions and velocities of three non-collinear points fixed on the end-effector is used. This is motivated by the fact that contact impacts are most pronounced at the end-effector, and three points can capture both position and orientation information.
 - The sequential order of data and resulting cluster labels are preserved for subsequent steps.

2. **Dynamics Modes (f_i):**
 - For each identified mode (cluster), an individual dynamics model (f_i) is learned.
 - **Learning Method:** Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) is employed for each f_i. This choice is made to maximize data efficiency and inherently represent uncertainty arising from both system stochasticity and limited training data.
 - **Model Form:** GPR learns the conditional distribution p(∆x_t | i_t, x_t, u_t), where x_{t+1} = x_t + ∆x_t.
 - **Training:** A multi-output GP model (with independent outputs) is trained for each cluster using its respective subset of data {D_xu_i, D_∆x_i}. A standard Squared Exponential (SE) kernel with Automatic Relevance Determination (ARD) and a zero mean function is used, with hyperparameters optimized by maximizing the log marginal likelihood.

3. **Transition Relations (T):**
 - The set of allowed mode transitions (T) is deterministically extracted directly from the sequence of cluster labels obtained during mode discovery. Any observed sequence of mode changes in the training data defines an allowed transition.

4. **Guard Functions (g(.)):**
 - Guard functions (g(.)) determine when a transition between modes occurs. Specifically, ˆg(x_t, u_t) predicts the next mode i_{t+1}.
 - **Learning Method:** This is formulated as a multi-class classification problem. A Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifier is trained to approximate ˆg(.).
 - **Training Data:** The SVM is trained using the dataset {D_xu, z'}, where z' comprises the next mode labels z_{t+1}. The assumption of non-overlapping guard regions (G_i,j ∩ G_i,k = ∅ for j ≠ k) allows for a deterministic guard function.

5. **Reset Maps (R_i,j):**
 - Reset maps are crucial for modeling discontinuous state changes upon mode transitions. For a transition from mode i to mode j, R_i,j maps the state-action pair (x_t, u_t) before transition to the new state x_{t+1} after transition.
 - **Learning Method:** GPR is again used to learn probabilistic reset maps (r_i,j(x_t, u_t)), allowing for uncertainty representation.
 - **Model Form:** GPR learns p(x_{t+1} | i_t, i_{t+1}, x_t, u_t) for cases where i_t ≠ i_{t+1}.
 - **Training:** A multi-output GP ˆr_i,j(.) is learned for each valid transition (i, j) ∈ T, using specific training data {D_xu_ij, D_x'_ij} extracted from the clustered dataset for points where that transition occurred.

**B. Uncertainty Propagation through the Learned Model**

For long-term prediction, uncertainties from initial state distributions and stochastic policies must be propagated through the learned hybrid model.

1. **Dynamics Mode:**
 - **Method:** A particle-based method based on the Unscented Transform (UT) is used to propagate uncertainties through the policy (π) and the mode dynamics (ˆf_i). This accounts for intrinsic uncertainties of the policy and dynamics, as well as propagated input uncertainties.
 - **Process:** The initial state distribution p(x_t|i_t) is propagated through the policy π to obtain p(u_t|x_t, i_t). This is combined with p(x_t|i_t) to form p(x_t, u_t|i_t), which is then propagated through the mode dynamics ˆf_{i_t} (when i_{t+1} = i_t) to yield p(x_{t+1}|i_t, i_{t+1}.).

2. **Guard Function:**
 - **Method:** For the deterministic ˆg(.), a Monte Carlo (MC) approach is employed for uncertainty propagation.
 - **Process:** Samples are drawn from the input distribution p(x_t, u_t). The guard function ˆg(.) is applied to each sample to predict the next mode. The relative frequencies of these predictions approximate the discrete probability distribution p(i_{t+1}|i_t.).

3. **Reset Map:**
 - **Method:** The UT method is also used for propagating input uncertainties through the reset maps (ˆr_i,j).
 - **Process:** When a mode transition is predicted (i_{t+1} ≠ i_t), only the particles from p(x_t, u_t) that triggered the specific guard G_i,j (as determined by the MC process for ˆg(.)) are propagated through the corresponding ˆr_{i_t, i_{t+1}} to obtain p(x_{t+1}|i_t, i_{t+1}.).

**C. Probabilistic Switching and Multimodality in Long-term Prediction**

- **Probabilistic Switching:** Because of uncertainty, the exact time of a mode transition is not deterministic. The guard function may predict non-zero probabilities for multiple modes during transition periods.
- **Segment Management:** The method manages this by maintaining "segments," which are smoothly evolving prediction sequences within a single mode.
- **Splitting and Merging:** When a transition is probabilistically predicted (i.e., multiple modes have non-zero probabilities via ˆg), the input state-action distribution is conceptually "split." One part continues propagation through the current mode's dynamics (ˆf_i), while others are propagated through the appropriate reset maps (ˆr_i,j) for new modes.
- **Multimodality:** If multiple distinct modes are predicted to be active simultaneously (e.g., due to uncertainty in sticking vs. slipping), the system's overall state distribution becomes multimodal and is represented as a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM). Within a single mode, if multiple probabilistic paths converge, their corresponding Gaussian distributions are merged into a single Gaussian approximation weighted by their probabilities.

**Baselines for Comparison:**
The proposed method is compared against three baselines:
- Standard Gaussian Process (GP) with Squared Exponential (SE) kernel and Automatic Relevance Determination (ARD).
- Manifold GP (mGP): Combines an ANN for feature space mapping with an SE kernel.
- Uncertainty-aware ANN (uANN) from PETS: An ensemble of ANN models, where prediction variance reflects model uncertainty.
For all baselines, probabilistic long-term prediction involved propagating 50 particles and subsequent density estimation using DPGMM at each time step.

**Evaluation Metrics:** Performance is evaluated using average negative log likelihood (NLL) and average root mean square error (RMSE) over all time steps, calculated with respect to ground truth test trajectories. RMSE is specifically calculated for the most likely mode.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on two distinct scenarios: a simulated 1D sliding mass task and a 7-DOF industrial robot (YuMi) performing contact-rich motions. The results consistently demonstrated the advantages of the proposed method in handling discontinuous dynamics and achieving data-efficient long-term predictions, particularly in low-data regimes.

**A. The Sliding Mass Experiment**

- **Scenario:** A block controlled to slide on a surface, experiencing three modes: frictionless motion, sticking due to high static friction, and slipping with kinetic friction, including an instantaneous velocity jump during slip.
- **Datasets:** D_15 (15 training trials + 5 test trials) and D_40 (40 training trials + 5 test trials).
- **Proposed Method (Ours):**
 - **Accuracy:** For D_40 (Figure 4a), the method's predictions closely tracked the test trajectories, successfully handling both the free-to-stick and slip discontinuities.
 - **Multimodality & Uncertainty:** It clearly exhibited multimodal behavior during switching (segment overlaps) and for the stick/slip phenomenon. The predicted variances were generally consistent, with the majority of test data falling within the 2σ prediction regions, despite occasional slight underestimations.
 - **Quantitative Performance (Table I):** The proposed method achieved the best NLL and RMSE scores for both D_15 (-2.32 ± 3.8 NLL, 0.63 RMSE) and D_40 (-2.3 ± 3.45 NLL, 0.76 RMSE), indicating superior prediction accuracy and uncertainty quantification.
- **Baselines (GP, mGP, uANN):**
 - **Discontinuity Handling:** None of the baselines successfully handled the free-to-stick discontinuity. uANN and mGP showed partial success with the slip discontinuity, while the standard GP model failed in both instances.
 - **Scaling:** GP and mGP proved unable to scale to the D_40 dataset due to excessive training times.
 - **Uncertainty:** Baselines consistently overestimated prediction variances.
 - **Quantitative Performance:** While the GP model failed to visually capture discontinuities, its average scores over 10 restarts were surprisingly better than uANN and mGP for D_15, though still significantly worse than the proposed method.

**B. The Contact Motion Experiment**

- **Scenario:** A 7-DOF YuMi robot with a peg executing a Cartesian trajectory involving free motion, contact with a wooden platform, sliding on wood, contact with a steel plate, and sliding on steel. Controller gains were perturbed to simulate varied policies in an MBRL setting.
- **Datasets:** D_15 (15 training trials) and D_40 (40 training trials, including perturbed controller data), both evaluated against D_test_40 (5 trials from a -10% gain policy).
- **Proposed Method (Ours):**
 - **Accuracy & Mode Switching (Figure 6a, 6c):** The method's predictions (shown in Cartesian space for clarity) closely followed the ground truth trajectories, accurately reflecting transitions through eight identified modes (indicating some over-clustering, which was considered advantageous for GP training time). It successfully respected both contact instances (free-to-wood and wood-to-steel).
 - **Discontinuity Handling (Figure 6c, 6d):** The full proposed method effectively handled discontinuities in joint velocities (e.g., ˙q_4(t) at 1.5s and 3.5s). In contrast, a simple GP-based switching model failed entirely in long-term prediction, and the proposed method *without* reset maps failed to handle the discontinuities.
 - **Uncertainty:** Variance predictions were generally consistent, with occasional underestimations, but largely encompassed the ground truth within the 2σ region.
 - **Quantitative Performance (Table II):** The proposed method achieved superior NLL and RMSE scores for both D_15 (-22.7 ± 26 NLL, 0.17 RMSE) and D_40 (-24 ± 27 NLL, 0.15 RMSE).
 - **Training Time:** For D_40, training time was 923s.
- **Baselines (GP, mGP, uANN):**
 - **Accuracy (Figure 6b):** None of the baselines could closely follow the ground truth trajectories in the complex contact motion scenario.
 - **Scaling:** Similar to the sliding mass experiment, GP and mGP did not scale to D_40.
 - **Uncertainty & Stability:** uANN, despite its theoretical capabilities, showed instability with its TS∞ propagation scheme beyond short horizons, necessitating an adjustment to average predictions across ensembles. It also exhibited significantly overestimated variances.
 - **Quantitative Performance:** mGP had better scores than uANN but presented noisy mean predictions. GP again outperformed other baselines in scores despite visual inaccuracies.
 - **Training Time:** For D_40, uANN trained in 53s, considerably faster than the proposed method, but with substantially inferior prediction performance.

**Summary of Findings:** The explicit hybrid automata structure, particularly the use of specialized local GP models and reset maps for discontinuous state evolution, proved critical for accurate and data-efficient long-term prediction in contact-rich tasks. This approach consistently outperformed more general, highly flexible models (mGP, uANN) in low-data regimes, which struggled to implicitly learn these complex, discontinuous dynamics without specific structural guidance or significantly more data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and potential impacts, particularly for the development of autonomous robotic systems capable of complex, contact-rich manipulation.

- **Addressing Fundamental Challenges in MBRL:** The work directly tackles two core challenges in MBRL for real-world robotics: the pervasive issue of discontinuous dynamics during rigid contact and the critical need for data efficiency. By providing a robust solution for these, it advances the practical applicability of MBRL in domains like assembly, grasping, and human-robot interaction where contact is inevitable.
- **Enhanced Prediction Accuracy and Data Efficiency:** The demonstrated superior performance in low-data regimes is a key impact. In real-world robotic deployments, collecting large datasets is often costly, time-consuming, and can lead to wear on hardware. A method that learns effectively from limited data significantly reduces the barrier to entry for MBRL and accelerates the development cycle for new manipulation skills. The explicit modeling of hybrid dynamics with GPs is shown to be a more effective strategy for these specific problems than highly flexible, but unstructured, deep learning models when data is scarce.
- **Robust Handling of Discontinuities:** The introduction and effective utilization of "reset maps" within the hybrid automata framework is a crucial innovation. It enables the model to explicitly account for and accurately predict instantaneous, discontinuous changes in state (e.g., velocity jumps during impact), a feature that conventional continuous function approximators inherently struggle with. This allows for more realistic and reliable long-term simulations of contact-rich behaviors.
- **Probabilistic Long-term Prediction:** The ability to generate probabilistic long-term predictions, including multimodal outcomes, provides valuable information for downstream MBRL components. Uncertainty estimates can guide exploration, inform risk-aware planning, and lead to more robust policy optimization strategies, especially under conditions of imperfect models or sparse data.
- **Applicability in Industrial and Real-world Settings:** The assumptions made (rigid objects, static environment, Markovian state representation) align well with many industrial manipulation and assembly tasks. This makes the proposed method a promising candidate for practical applications where precise contact handling and predictable system behavior are paramount.
- **Guidance for Future MBRL Architectures:** The findings suggest that incorporating domain-specific structural knowledge (like hybrid automata for contact) can be highly beneficial when coupled with flexible, uncertainty-aware learners (like GPs). This could inform the design of future MBRL architectures that combine the strengths of structured models with data-driven learning. While currently limited to gradient-free policy search due to particle-based uncertainty propagation, the method's strong model learning performance lays a foundation for its integration into a broader range of MBRL algorithms.
- **Identifying Future Research Directions:** The paper also contributes by highlighting remaining challenges, such as scaling GP-based approaches to very large datasets and potential strategies for addressing these (e.g., parallelization, data windowing). This points towards valuable avenues for subsequent research in making such models even more broadly applicable.
