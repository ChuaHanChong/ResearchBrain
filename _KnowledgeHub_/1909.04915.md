# Data-efficient Model Learning and Prediction for Contact-rich Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> A hybrid system framework integrates Gaussian Processes and reset maps to enable data-efficient model learning and accurate long-term state prediction for contact-rich robotic manipulation. This approach effectively captures discontinuous dynamics and quantifies uncertainty, outperforming existing methods in low-data regimes across simulated and physical robot tasks.

## Problem

- Learning forward dynamics models for contact-rich manipulation is challenging due to discontinuous dynamics (e.g., velocity jumps during impacts) which most continuous function approximators struggle with.
- Real-world robotic model learning requires high data efficiency, as extensive data collection is costly, time-consuming, and can cause hardware wear.
- Accumulation of errors in long-term predictions and unreliable forecasts in sparse data regions necessitate robust uncertainty quantification and propagation.

## Method

- The system dynamics are modeled as a ==hybrid automaton==, explicitly incorporating dynamics modes, transition relations, guard functions, and ==reset maps== to represent discontinuous state changes during contact.
- ==Dirichlet Process Gaussian Mixture Models (DPGMM)== automatically identify distinct dynamics modes from training data, while ==Gaussian Process Regression (GPR)== learns the individual mode dynamics and probabilistic reset maps.
- Uncertainty is propagated through the learned hybrid model using a combination of the ==Unscented Transform (UT)== for continuous dynamics/reset maps and ==Monte Carlo (MC) sampling== for the deterministic guard functions, managing probabilistic switching and multimodality.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved superior **negative log likelihood (NLL)** and **root mean square error (RMSE)** in both a simulated 1D sliding mass task and a **7-DOF YuMi** robot contact motion task, consistently outperforming standard GP, mGP, and uncertainty-aware ANN baselines.
- It successfully captured and predicted discontinuous state changes, such as velocity jumps during slip and transitions between contact modes, which the baseline models largely failed to handle.
- The approach demonstrated robust performance and accurate uncertainty quantification even with limited training data (e.g., **15 trials**), whereas some baselines showed limitations in scaling or stability with increasing data complexity or prediction horizon.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling hybrid dynamics, particularly incorporating reset maps for discontinuous state evolution, is crucial for accurate long-term prediction in contact-rich manipulation tasks, offering a structural advantage over general, unstructured models.
> - Gaussian Processes prove to be data-efficient for learning local dynamics and representing uncertainty in low-data regimes, making them well-suited for robotics applications where data collection is limited.
> - Combining structured domain knowledge (hybrid automata) with flexible, uncertainty-aware learning methods (GPs) is more effective for handling complex, discontinuous dynamics than relying solely on highly flexible but unstructured models when data is scarce.
