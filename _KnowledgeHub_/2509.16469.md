# A Framework for Optimal Ankle Design of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework was developed for the optimal mechanical design of humanoid robot ankle joints, systematically comparing parallel kinematic architectures and actuator types. This methodology produced optimized parallel ankle designs that reduced a scalar performance cost by up to 41% compared to existing serial designs and 14% against conventionally engineered parallel solutions.

## Problem

- Current humanoid robot ankle designs, particularly traditional serial architectures, limit agility, stability, and dynamic performance, hindering operations in human-centric environments.
- Designing parallel ankle mechanisms is complex due to a large number of coupled geometric parameters, with performance metrics varying significantly across their operational workspace.
- A lack of systematic, quantitative methodologies exists for selecting optimal parallel architectures and tuning their parameters based on specific application requirements and performance indicators.

## Method

- Inverse kinematic models were developed for two representative parallel ankle mechanisms, SPU (linear actuated) and RSU (rotary actuated), including a novel reparameterization for the RSU to guarantee workspace feasibility.
- A multi-objective optimization algorithm (==NSGA-II==) was employed to synthesize geometric parameters, minimizing peak actuator forces/torques and velocities over realistic locomotion tasks.
- A scalar cost function was created to aggregate seven architecture-agnostic performance metrics (e.g., speed, torque, mass, compactness) for direct, quantitative comparison and ranking of optimized solutions across different architectures and actuators.

## Results

- The novel RSU reparameterization was validated to guarantee full workspace feasibility across the desired operational region, preventing kinematically impossible configurations during design.
- Optimized parallel ankle designs, notably the RSU mechanism with Synapticon ACTILINK JD 10 actuators, consistently achieved lower scalar costs across evaluated metrics compared to other architectures and actuators.
- Optimized parallel designs reduced the scalar cost by up to **41%** compared to the original serial ankle architecture and by up to **14%** over a conventionally engineered parallel RSU mechanism, validating the framework's effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parallel mechanisms offer inherent advantages over serial architectures for humanoid ankles, including increased rigidity, precision, and better mass distribution, but require sophisticated design approaches.
> - A systematic design framework combining kinematic modeling, workspace reparameterization, multi-objective optimization, and a scalar cost function can effectively overcome the complexity of parallel mechanism design.
> - Ensuring inverse kinematics existence across the entire operational workspace is crucial for parallel mechanisms and can be systematically achieved through dedicated parameter reparameterization, preventing kinematically infeasible designs.
