# A Comprehensive Survey of Reward Models: Taxonomy, Applications, Challenges, and Future

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey consolidates research on Reward Models (RMs) in the Large Language Model (LLM) era, introducing an elaborate taxonomy for their lifecycle components. It details RM types and granularities, explores diverse applications, and identifies challenges alongside future research directions.

## Problem

- Despite the critical role of Reward Models (RMs) in aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human values and safety guidelines via RLHF, a comprehensive and dedicated survey specifically focused on RMs in the LLM era was lacking.
- The rapidly expanding and diverse research on RMs lacked systematic organization and an in-depth analysis, making it challenging for both newcomers and experienced researchers to navigate.
- Existing literature touched upon RMs as part of broader discussions on LLM alignment or RLHF limitations, but did not provide the focused, structured overview necessary to address the complexities of RM development and deployment.

## Method

- The paper introduces the first comprehensive survey specifically focused on parameterized ==Reward Models (RMs)== in the Large Language Model (LLM) era, which are used to reflect human preferences.
- An elaborate ==taxonomy== is developed that systematically reviews RMs across three main dimensions: how preference data is collected (Human Preference vs. AI Preference), the mechanisms of reward modeling (Type Level: Discriminative, Generative, Implicit; Granularity Level: ==Outcome-level, Process-level==), and their various usage scenarios in the LLM lifecycle.
- The survey analyzes the current application landscape of RMs, discusses existing benchmarking and evaluation methodologies, and identifies key challenges and open problems while proposing future research directions to facilitate further advancement in the field.

## Results

- The survey confirms the established role of RMs in LLM alignment and identifies that preference data for training RMs can originate from both human annotation and increasingly from AI models, demonstrating high consistency with human judgment.
- A comprehensive RM taxonomy is presented, categorizing models by type (**discriminative, generative, implicit**) and granularity (**outcome-level vs. process-level**), and their diverse applications across the LLM lifecycle (data selection, policy training, inference) are mapped.
- Significant evaluation challenges are identified, including the lack of general and standardized benchmarks, high cost, and sensitivity to input variations; key challenges like data quality, **overoptimization (reward hacking)**, and evaluation biases are highlighted with proposed future directions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward Models (RMs) are indispensable for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences, acting as crucial proxies for human judgment in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).
> - Preference data for RMs can be sourced from both human annotations (with ongoing research to improve efficiency and quality) and increasingly from capable AI models, which offer scalability for superhuman tasks.
> - A multi-faceted taxonomy is critical for understanding RMs, classifying them by underlying model type (discriminative, generative, implicit) and the granularity of their feedback (outcome-level vs. process-level), each with distinct advantages and challenges.
