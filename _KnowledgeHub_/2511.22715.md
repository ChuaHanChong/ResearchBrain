# ReAG: Reasoning-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> ReAG, a multimodal framework, improves Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering by combining a multi-level retrieval pipeline, a critic model to filter noisy passages, and a generator trained with supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning to produce explicit reasoning traces. It demonstrates accuracy gains of up to 7.8 points on E-VQA and 5.7 points on InfoSeek, while reducing the average number of passages fed to the generator by up to 18%.

## Problem

- Existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods in multimodal contexts often retrieve noisy or irrelevant passages, leading to low precision in Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA).
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle to effectively understand, synthesize, and explicitly reason over retrieved external information for knowledge-intensive queries.
- Current KB-VQA approaches often lack transparent or robust reasoning traces, limiting the interpretability of MLLM predictions.

## Method

- A ==multi-level retrieval== pipeline combines coarse-grained and fine-grained approaches (using GroundingDINO and EVA-CLIP) to enhance recall for relevant knowledge base passages.
- A critic model (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B) filters irrelevant and noisy passages from the retrieved set, providing a cleaner and more precise context to the generator.
- A multi-stage generator training strategy begins with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for initial reasoning capabilities, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) using a modified GRPO framework with rule-based rewards to refine reasoning and answer generation.

## Results

- ReAG achieved state-of-the-art results on E-VQA and InfoSeek benchmarks, outperforming previous methods by up to +7.8 points on E-VQA (Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**) and +5.7 points on InfoSeek (Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**).
- The critic model effectively reduced the average number of passages fed to the generator by **15.9%** to **18.0%** compared to baselines, while maintaining high recall and specificity of relevant passages.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component of ReAG (critic, fine-grained retrieval, SFT, and RL training) contributed positively to the overall performance, validating the integrated approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly filtering noisy retrieved information with a critic model is crucial for improving MLLM performance in KB-VQA, significantly reducing irrelevant context fed to the model.
> - A multi-stage training approach, combining SFT for initial instruction following and RL for refining reasoning capabilities, leads to more robust and accurate knowledge-based generation.
> - Generating explicit natural-language reasoning traces before the final answer enhances both the accuracy and interpretability of MLLM predictions in complex, knowledge-intensive visual question answering tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has demonstrated capabilities in jointly processing and understanding various modalities such as text, images, and videos. Visual Question Answering (VQA) is a common task used to evaluate these models, requiring them to generate responses based on visual content and textual queries.

Despite advances in MLLMs and their extensive pre-training, they often encounter limitations when confronted with domain-specific or knowledge-intensive queries. This challenge is particularly evident in Knowledge-based VQA (KB-VQA), where successful answering necessitates information beyond the MLLM's internal pre-trained knowledge. To address this, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks have become a standard approach, integrating external knowledge sources (e.g., Wikipedia) to condition answer generation.

However, existing RAG approaches in the multimodal domain face several open challenges. These include issues with information retrieval precision, where heterogeneous queries and vast knowledge bases can lead to low recall and the introduction of noisy or irrelevant passages into the MLLM's context window. Furthermore, even with relevant retrieved documents, models often struggle with effectively understanding, synthesizing, and reasoning over this external information to extract the precise details required for an accurate answer.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) strategies have emerged as a significant paradigm for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) and MLLMs with desired behaviors and enhancing their reasoning capabilities. Approaches like GRPO (Generative Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization) have shown promise in improving sample efficiency and training stability for generating coherent and logical outputs, particularly in tasks requiring complex reasoning traces.

This work situates itself at the intersection of these domains, aiming to advance KB-VQA by developing a more robust RAG framework that specifically addresses the precision and reasoning limitations of current MLLMs. By focusing on both refined information retrieval and explicit reasoning augmentation, the research seeks to overcome common failure modes in knowledge-intensive VQA.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and interpretability of MLLMs in Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA). The authors identify two principal limitations in current retrieval-augmented approaches that motivate their proposed solution:

1. **Low Precision and Noisy Retrieval:** Existing RAG methods often struggle with accurately retrieving relevant information from large knowledge bases. Queries can be highly diverse, and knowledge bases can be vast, leading to retrieval systems that suffer from low recall and frequently return noisy or irrelevant passages alongside pertinent ones. When these noisy passages are fed to an MLLM, they can degrade generation quality, introduce hallucinations, or lead to incorrect answers. The motivation here is to develop a more sophisticated retrieval and filtering mechanism that ensures only high-quality, relevant contextual information is provided to the MLLM.

2. **Limited Reasoning over Retrieved Content:** Even when relevant documents are retrieved, current MLLMs often exhibit limited capabilities in explicitly reasoning over this information. They may struggle to synthesize disparate pieces of evidence, identify key facts, or formulate a logical chain of thought to arrive at a correct answer. Many existing methods rely on implicit signals or control tokens, which do not always yield transparent or robust reasoning. The motivation is to equip the MLLM with an explicit, interpretable reasoning capability that can critically evaluate and leverage retrieved evidence, thereby improving answer accuracy and providing insight into the model's decision-making process.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes ReAG (Reasoning-Augmented Generation) with the following key objectives:
- To implement a multi-level retrieval pipeline that combines coarse-grained and fine-grained approaches to improve overall recall.
- To integrate a critic model that filters out irrelevant and noisy passages from the retrieved set, enhancing the precision of the context provided to the generator.
- To develop a multi-stage training strategy for the MLLM generator, leveraging supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as a cold start and subsequently refining reasoning capabilities through a reinforcement learning (RL) framework.
- To enable the generator to produce explicit natural-language reasoning traces prior to the final answer, enhancing the interpretability and explainability of the model's predictions.
- To demonstrate that these combined mechanisms lead to superior performance on challenging KB-VQA benchmarks compared to prior methods.

By achieving these objectives, ReAG aims to provide a more robust, accurate, and transparent solution for knowledge-intensive VQA tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed ReAG model is a multimodal retrieval-augmented generation approach designed to address challenges in Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA). The methodology comprises a multi-level retrieval stage, a filtering stage using a critic model, and a multi-stage training strategy for the generator, which includes a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) cold start followed by reinforcement learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Task Definition

In KB-VQA, a generator model (G) answers a question (q) about an image (I_q), augmented by external knowledge. The knowledge base (KB) consists of N multimodal documents (d_i), each containing text (T_i), an image (I_i), and textual passages (P_i). A retrieval model (R) selects the top-k relevant documents and their passages (P̃), which condition the generator's answer (A ∼ G(A | q, I_q, P̃)). The generator is typically optimized to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth answer tokens.

#### 3.2. Overall Pipeline

ReAG's pipeline involves four main stages:
1. **Multi-level Retrieval:** Gathers candidate passages from the knowledge base.
2. **Filtering:** A critic model selects relevant content from the candidate passages.
3. **Generator Cold Start (SFT):** Instills initial reasoning capabilities in the generator.
4. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Refines reasoning and answer generation.

#### 3.3. Retrieval Stage

This stage identifies potentially informative passages. ReAG's retriever (R) is agnostic, utilizing EVA-CLIP-8B as a cross-modal encoder. Relevance is computed via cosine similarity.

- **Coarse-Grained Retrieval:** The entire query image (I_q) is encoded by EVA-CLIP to retrieve the top-k documents. For InfoSeek, an image-to-text retrieval matches the query image with document metadata (title, summary). For Encyclopedic-VQA, image-to-image retrieval compares the query image with document images. All passages from these top-k documents form P_cg.
- **Fine-Grained Retrieval:** To enhance recall, this step focuses on specific visual regions. GroundingDINO is used to localize the main visual subject of the question (extracted via spaCy) within I_q, generating a cropped image patch (I_q_crop). This patch is then used with EVA-CLIP to retrieve an additional top-k documents. Passages from these documents form P_fg.
- **Final Set of Retrieved Passages (P_noisy):** Passages from P_cg and P_fg are merged, ranked by relevance scores, and all passages from the combined top-k documents are retained. The default k is 20 for each stage.

#### 3.4. Filtering Stage

P_noisy can contain irrelevant information. A critic model (C) is designed to filter these out, yielding P_relevant.

- **Critic Model:** Implemented as an autoregressive MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B), fine-tuned on a curated subset of the ReflectiVA dataset. It receives (I_q, q, p) and predicts whether passage (p) is relevant (y ∈ {Yes, No}) for answering (q) about (I_q). Training uses a next-token prediction objective. The training dataset for the critic consists of 1M samples from InfoSeek and Encyclopedic-VQA, balanced and including soft and hard negatives for robust discrimination.
- **Inference:** At inference, passages (p) from P_noisy are retained in P_relevant only if Pr(Yes|C, q, I_q, p) exceeds a threshold (default 0.1).

#### 3.5. Generator Cold Start (SFT)

This phase prepares the generator (G) for reasoning and mitigates instability during subsequent RL.

- **Collecting Reasoning Traces:** A subset of the ReflectiVA dataset is augmented with explicit reasoning traces (tr). An MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is prompted to generate these traces, explaining how passage (p) contributes to answering (q) given (I_q), conditioned on the final answer and relevance label (y). The traces follow a structured format: `<think> reasoning trace </think> <answer> answer </answer>`.
- **Training Protocol:** The generator (G) is fine-tuned using a next-token prediction objective over both the reasoning trace and the final answer. The SFT loss is L_SFT = αL_A + (1 − α)L_T, where α balances answer and reasoning trace losses (default α=0.8).

#### 3.6. Generator RL Training

The SFT-trained generator's reasoning quality and robustness are further enhanced via reinforcement learning.

- **Task-specific RL:** ReAG uses a modified GRPO framework inspired by DAPO. The objective J_GRPO(θ) encourages policy updates based on advantages (Â_i,t), with a clipped probability ratio. A key modification is the omission of the KL divergence penalty, reducing memory and improving training speed. Loss is computed at the token level.
- **Rule-Based Reward Design:** Two binary reward functions are used:
 - `R_task(o_i)`: Evaluates the accuracy of the generated completion (`o_i`) by parsing the prediction according to question type (numerical, textual, single/multi-answer) and dataset-specific evaluation protocols (BERT-based matching for E-VQA, standard/relaxed accuracy for InfoSeek).
 - `R_fmt(o_i)`: Enforces adherence to the templated output format.
 - The final reward R_i is a weighted sum: R_i = γR_task(o_i) + δR_fmt(o_i) (default γ=1.0, δ=0.2).
- **Advantages:** Advantages (Â_i,t) are computed as the z-score of the reward R_i, guiding policy updates to favor completions with above-average rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on Encyclopedic-VQA (E-VQA) and InfoSeek benchmarks, utilizing Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B as generator backbones.

#### 4.1. Comparison with the State of the Art

- **Zero-Shot MLLMs:** Zero-shot MLLMs (BLIP-2, LLaVA-v1.5, LLaVA-MORE, Qwen2.5-VL) demonstrated low accuracy on KB-VQA benchmarks (e.g., ~20% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B on InfoSeek), highlighting their inability to handle knowledge-intensive queries without external information.
- **Retrieval-Augmented Models:** Existing RAG approaches significantly improved performance (e.g., ~40% for mKG-RAG on InfoSeek), validating the necessity of external knowledge retrieval.
- **ReAG Performance:** ReAG consistently achieved state-of-the-art results on both E-VQA and InfoSeek (Table 1).
 - On E-VQA, ReAG with Qwen2.5-VL-3B outperformed ReflectiVA by +7.7 points, and with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it improved upon VLM-PRF (using InternVL3-8B) by +7.8 points.
 - On InfoSeek, ReAG showed overall improvements of +4.3 and +3.3 points for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 7B, respectively, compared to strong baselines.
These gains underscore the effectiveness and robustness of ReAG's combined retrieval, filtering, and reasoning strategies.

#### 4.2. Results with OMGM Retrieval Mode

When evaluated with OMGM's image-to-text summary retriever for the coarse-grained step, ReAG continued to outperform OMGM and ReflectiVA (Table 2). ReAG with Qwen2.5-VL-3B gained +4.5 and +5.1 points on E-VQA and InfoSeek over ReflectiVA, respectively. With Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ReAG surpassed OMGM by +2.3 points on E-VQA and +5.7 points on InfoSeek, indicating that ReAG's critic-based filtering and reasoning capabilities are effective even with varied initial retrieval modalities.

#### 4.3. Results with Oracle Documents

In an oracle setting, where the ground-truth Wikipedia page was provided, ReAG achieved the highest performance across E-VQA and InfoSeek at all model scales (Table 3). For the 3B variant, ReAG surpassed ReflectiVA by +6.1 points on E-VQA. For InfoSeek, ReAG's 7B version improved over ReflectiVA by +3.7 points, and even by +2.1 points when ReflectiVA used a larger generator (LLaVA-MORE-8B). This suggests ReAG efficiently leverages high-quality information when available.

#### 4.4. Retrieval and Generation Pipeline Analysis

- **Performance with/without Evidence (Fig. 3, left):** ReAG demonstrated superior performance both with and without the direct evidence passage in the context, outperforming comparable models. ReAG consistently achieved closer proximity to the oracle upper bound, indicating effective reasoning and reduced reliance on direct, noisy evidence.
- **Average Number of Passages (Fig. 3, right):** The critic model in ReAG significantly reduced the number of passages fed to the generator. It achieved reductions of 18.0% and 15.9% in the average number of passages compared to ReflectiVA (LLaVA-MORE-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B, respectively), which helps mitigate noise.
- **Effectiveness of Critic (Fig. 7, 8 & Table 6):** The critic model substantially reduced the average number of passages from 128.6 to 5.7 at k=20, demonstrating its ability to filter noisy content while preserving answer-relevant information. The optimal `yes-probability threshold` of 0.1 for the critic balanced precision and recall. Compared to ReflectiVA, ReAG's critic exhibited higher recall (e.g., 94.6% vs. 92.3% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and higher specificity (e.g., 95.7% vs. 95.1%), leading to a cleaner context for the generator.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

The ablation study (Table 4) validated the contribution of each ReAG component:
- Directly passing all top-20 passages without filtering degraded performance significantly.
- The introduction of the critic model provided substantial gains, more than doubling performance in some cases (e.g., 19.2% to 39.3% on E-VQA for the 3B model).
- Adding the fine-grained retriever further improved accuracy.
- Both the SFT cold-start phase and the subsequent RL training stage contributed positively to the final performance, affirming the multi-stage training strategy's efficacy in preparing the model for structured reasoning.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples (Fig. 4, 9, 10) illustrate that ReAG consistently produced accurate answers and generated coherent, structured reasoning traces. In contrast, zero-shot models often produced incorrect or hallucinated answers, and ReflectiVA, while following format, typically lacked explicit reasoning. ReAG's explicit reasoning traces provided insights into its decision-making process, demonstrating its ability to integrate visual and textual cues and assess passage relevance even with noisy or irrelevant evidence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ReAG presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI and knowledge-based systems.

**Advancement in Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering:** ReAG establishes new state-of-the-art performance on prominent and challenging KB-VQA benchmarks such as Encyclopedic-VQA and InfoSeek. This demonstrates a notable step forward in enabling MLLMs to accurately answer complex questions that require external, domain-specific knowledge, moving beyond simple visual recognition tasks.

**Improved Robustness to Noisy Information:** The multi-level retrieval pipeline, combined with the critic model for filtering irrelevant passages, significantly enhances the quality of contextual information fed to the MLLM. This approach reduces the detrimental effects of noisy or low-precision retrieval, a common challenge in RAG systems, making ReAG more robust and less prone to hallucinations or incorrect inferences caused by misleading evidence.

**Enhanced Interpretability and Explainability:** A key contribution of ReAG is its ability to generate explicit natural-language reasoning traces before producing the final answer. Unlike systems that provide implicit reasoning or rely on control tokens, ReAG's structured traces offer transparency into the model's decision-making process. This increased explainability is critical for building trust in AI systems, particularly in applications where accountability and understanding the "why" behind a prediction are important.

**Modular and Adaptable Framework:** The critic model in ReAG is designed to be agnostic to the specific retrieval backbone used. This modularity means that ReAG can integrate with various state-of-the-art retrieval engines, allowing it to benefit from future advancements in retrieval technology without requiring significant architectural changes. The multi-stage training strategy, combining SFT and RL, also provides a flexible paradigm for developing and refining reasoning capabilities in MLLMs.

**Foundation for Future Research:** The success of ReAG in integrating robust retrieval, filtering, and explicit reasoning provides a strong foundation for future research.
- It may inspire further work on designing more sophisticated critic models capable of nuanced contextual evaluation.
- The reinforcement learning framework could be adapted and extended to other multimodal tasks requiring complex reasoning over external knowledge.
- The explicit reasoning traces could serve as valuable data for training more advanced transparent AI systems or for human-in-the-loop validation processes.
- Further investigation into balancing reasoning trace length with generation latency could optimize real-time applications.

In summary, ReAG addresses critical limitations in knowledge-based VQA by offering a robust and interpretable solution. Its methodical approach to information retrieval, filtering, and reasoning-augmented generation represents a significant contribution towards more capable and transparent multimodal AI systems.
