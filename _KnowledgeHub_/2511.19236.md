# SENTINEL: A Fully End-to-End Language-Action Model for Humanoid Whole Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> SENTINEL is an end-to-end language-action model for humanoid whole-body control that directly maps natural language commands and robot proprioceptive inputs to low-level control actions. It achieves a 99.45% success rate in stable execution on a Unitree G1 robot, outperforming previous decoupled and partially integrated methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid control often relies on teleoperation or decoupled modular pipelines, which lack autonomy and struggle to align high-level language commands with physically feasible robot actions.
- Existing text-to-motion generation approaches frequently produce motions that are physically impossible or unstable when transferred to robots due to differing dynamics.
- Prior methods face challenges with robustness, generalizability, and ensuring physical feasibility without requiring explicit, hand-crafted constraints.

## Method

- **SENTINEL** introduces a fully end-to-end language-action model using a transformer-based architecture that directly maps natural language and proprioceptive states to low-level whole-body control actions.
- The model is pre-trained on a large dataset of physically grounded robot trajectories, generated via a high-quality whole-body controller, with extensive domain randomization.
- A post-training residual reinforcement learning stage with a lightweight MLP refines actions, adapting to unmodeled effects and improving sim-to-real transferability.

## Results

- SENTINEL achieved a **99.45%** success rate in physically stable execution in simulation, outperforming baselines such as MDM (**94.94%**) and LangWBC (**81.78%**), and demonstrated superior semantic alignment with a Multi-Modal Distance (MM-Dist) of **0.487**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multi-scale observation history, auxiliary state prediction, and increased model capacity (from 60M to 600M parameters) significantly improved performance metrics like R-precision and MMD.
- The model successfully deployed on a real Unitree G1 robot, performing diverse gestures, locomotion, and waypoint navigation, and exhibited robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and multimodal extensibility by converting visual targets into language commands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly coupling language understanding with low-level whole-body control, without intermediate representations, enhances semantic-kinematic consistency and physical execution stability.
> - Training on large-scale, physically grounded robot data, combined with multi-scale observation history and dynamics-aware action prediction, is crucial for robust and generalizable humanoid control.
> - The integration of a flow matching model for continuous action prediction and a residual reinforcement learning head effectively mitigates open-loop drift and improves real-world adaptability.
