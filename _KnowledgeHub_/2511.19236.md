# SENTINEL: A Fully End-to-End Language-Action Model for Humanoid Whole Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> SENTINEL is an end-to-end language-action model for humanoid whole-body control that directly maps natural language commands and robot proprioceptive inputs to low-level control actions. It achieves a 99.45% success rate in stable execution on a Unitree G1 robot, outperforming previous decoupled and partially integrated methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid control often relies on teleoperation or decoupled modular pipelines, which lack autonomy and struggle to align high-level language commands with physically feasible robot actions.
- Existing text-to-motion generation approaches frequently produce motions that are physically impossible or unstable when transferred to robots due to differing dynamics.
- Prior methods face challenges with robustness, generalizability, and ensuring physical feasibility without requiring explicit, hand-crafted constraints.

## Method

- **SENTINEL** introduces a fully end-to-end language-action model using a transformer-based architecture that directly maps natural language and proprioceptive states to low-level whole-body control actions.
- The model is pre-trained on a large dataset of physically grounded robot trajectories, generated via a high-quality whole-body controller, with extensive domain randomization.
- A post-training residual reinforcement learning stage with a lightweight MLP refines actions, adapting to unmodeled effects and improving sim-to-real transferability.

## Results

- SENTINEL achieved a **99.45%** success rate in physically stable execution in simulation, outperforming baselines such as MDM (**94.94%**) and LangWBC (**81.78%**), and demonstrated superior semantic alignment with a Multi-Modal Distance (MM-Dist) of **0.487**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multi-scale observation history, auxiliary state prediction, and increased model capacity (from 60M to 600M parameters) significantly improved performance metrics like R-precision and MMD.
- The model successfully deployed on a real Unitree G1 robot, performing diverse gestures, locomotion, and waypoint navigation, and exhibited robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and multimodal extensibility by converting visual targets into language commands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly coupling language understanding with low-level whole-body control, without intermediate representations, enhances semantic-kinematic consistency and physical execution stability.
> - Training on large-scale, physically grounded robot data, combined with multi-scale observation history and dynamics-aware action prediction, is crucial for robust and generalizable humanoid control.
> - The integration of a flow matching model for continuous action prediction and a residual reinforcement learning head effectively mitigates open-loop drift and improves real-world adaptability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Existing Approaches and Their Limitations

- **Teleoperation/Joystick Control:** Many humanoids are still controlled by human operators using joysticks or teleoperation interfaces. While effective for specific tasks, this approach requires expert human intervention, lacks autonomy, and offers limited flexibility for complex, high-level commands.
- **Modular Generation Pipelines:** A common approach involves decoupling high-level language understanding from low-level physical execution. This typically entails:
 1. **Text-to-motion Generation:** A model generates human-like motion sequences (e.g., SMPL format) from natural language descriptions. Examples include MDM and T2M-GPT.
 2. **Whole-Body Controller (WBC):** A separate controller then attempts to execute these generated motions on the humanoid robot.
 The primary drawback of this decoupled design is the lack of tight alignment between language commands and physically feasible robot behaviors. Motions generated solely based on aesthetic appeal or human kinematics often lead to physically impossible or unstable actions when transferred to a robot with different dynamics and constraints. Solutions to this typically involve either introducing an "alignment module" to bridge the gap (e.g., by ensuring generated motions are interpretable by the WBC) or a more direct, end-to-end approach.
- **Intermediate Representation-Based Text-Based WBC:** More recent attempts to bridge the gap have involved text-based whole-body control that still relies on some form of intermediate representation. For instance, UH-1 generates robot poses directly from text (though still based on retargeted human data), and LangWBC distills expert policies using a CVAE, but its MLP-based design can limit expressiveness. Other hierarchical modular architectures also fuse vision, language, and motion, but still depend on abstract intermediate representations. These methods struggle with either robustness, generalizability, or ensuring physical feasibility without explicit constraints.

### 2. SENTINEL's Contribution

SENTINEL addresses these limitations by proposing a **fully end-to-end language–action model** that directly maps natural language commands and proprioceptive inputs to low-level whole-body control actions. This approach marks a significant shift from modular or partially integrated systems by:
- **Eliminating Intermediate Representations:** Unlike prior methods, SENTINEL avoids explicit motion representations, latent abstractions, or hand-crafted physical constraints between language understanding and physical execution. This allows for a deeper, more integrated learning process.
- **Stronger Semantic–Kinematic Consistency:** By allowing gradient flow from execution feedback back to language understanding, the end-to-end design ensures that the model learns to generate actions that are inherently physically feasible and semantically aligned with the given command.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The direct mapping and joint optimization across the entire system aim to produce more robust and generalizable humanoid behaviors, capable of handling diverse tasks in dynamic environments.
- **Scalability:** The framework is designed to be scalable, leveraging large datasets and transformer architectures, crucial for complex, general-purpose humanoid control.

This work positions SENTINEL at the forefront of developing truly autonomous and intelligent humanoids that can understand and execute high-level language commands in a physically grounded manner, paving the way for more intuitive human-robot interaction and broader accessibility of humanoid technology.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of SENTINEL stem from the inherent limitations of existing humanoid control paradigms, aiming to create a more intuitive, robust, and scalable solution.

1. **Overcome Limitations of Teleoperation:** The authors note that most humanoids are still controlled via joysticks or teleoperation, which requires expert human operators and lacks flexibility. A key motivation is to move beyond this human-driven paradigm to enable autonomous and intuitive language control, allowing users to specify complex actions and intentions without specialized skills.

2. **Address Decoupling Issues in Modular Pipelines:** Current state-of-the-art approaches often decouple text-to-motion generation from whole-body control. This separation leads to suboptimal control performance, as the generated motions may not be physically feasible or well-aligned with the robot's dynamics. A central objective is to achieve tighter alignment and consistency between language commands and physical execution by integrating these components.

3. **Develop a Fully End-to-End Language-Action Model:** The core objective is to build a system that directly maps natural language instructions and robot proprioceptive states to low-level whole-body control actions. This means avoiding any intermediate motion representations, latent abstractions, or hand-crafted physical constraints, which are seen as potential bottlenecks for robustness and generalizability.

4. **Enhance Semantic Understanding and Stable Execution:** The model is designed to exhibit strong semantic understanding of language commands while ensuring stable and physically feasible execution on humanoid robots. This dual goal is crucial for reliable operation in complex real-world scenarios.

5. **Support Multi-modal Extensions:** A forward-looking objective is to design the framework to naturally support the integration of other sensory inputs, such as visual observations, by converting them into language control signals. This aims to broaden the robot's capabilities, enabling tasks like navigation based on visual targets combined with language instructions.

6. **Achieve Reliable Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Recognizing the gap between simulation training and real-world deployment, the authors aim to develop a method that allows for robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, ensuring that behaviors learned in simulation translate effectively to physical hardware.

In essence, SENTINEL is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of humanoid robots by making them truly intelligent, accessible, and versatile agents, capable of understanding and performing diverse tasks through natural language, directly and reliably.

#### 2.2. Methodology and Approach

SENTINEL's methodology is structured into three main stages: data curation, pre-training of the end-to-end language-action model, and post-training refinement using residual reinforcement learning. It also includes provisions for multi-modal expansion.

##### 2.2.1. Data Curation

1. **Whole Body Controller (WBC) Training:** First, a high-quality WBC policy is trained. This policy is a Mixture-of-Expert (MoE) model trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in the IsaacLab simulation environment. Its purpose is to track diverse human motions on humanoid robots (specifically, the Unitree G1) while adhering to physical constraints. The policy takes a motion-tracking target (retargeted human motion) and the robot's proprioceptive state as input, outputting target joint positions for a low-level PD controller.
2. **Language-Action Dataset Construction:** A large-scale language-action dataset ($D_{robot}$) is created by leveraging existing language-labeled human motion datasets (e.g., a PHC-filtered subset of AMASS, HumanML3D). For each human motion sequence paired with natural language descriptions, the trained WBC is rolled out in simulation. This generates physically grounded robot trajectories (sequences of states and actions) that correspond to the human motions and their language annotations. Approximately 200,000 language-labeled robot trajectories, totaling about 100 million state-action pairs, are collected.
3. **Domain Randomization:** During data collection, domain randomization is extensively used. This introduces variations in physical properties (e.g., center of mass, friction coefficients) and external perturbations (e.g., random pushes, torque noise). This enhances the robustness and generalization capabilities of the model trained on this dataset, preparing it for real-world variations.

##### 2.2.2. Model Training

The training process involves pre-training a transformer-based language-action model followed by a post-training refinement stage.

##### 2.2.3. Pre-training: End-to-End Language-Action Model

1. **Model Architecture:**
 - **Language–State Encoder:** A Transformer processes contextual inputs, including the natural language command (`l`) and the robot's state history (`s_hist`). The language command is first encoded by a CLIP text encoder. Both text and proprioceptive state inputs are projected into the Transformer's hidden dimension, augmented with learnable type and time embeddings. This encoder captures temporal dependencies and cross-modal interactions.
 - **Action Expert (Flow Matching Model):** Implemented as a Transformer decoder, this component predicts an "action chunk" for the next `H` steps. It attends to the key and value caches from the language-state encoder. The core mechanism is **flow matching**, where the model learns to predict the ground-truth velocity field of a continuous action flow. During inference, this velocity field is integrated to generate an action chunk.
2. **Inputs and Outputs:**
 - **Multi-Scale Observation:** The robot's state history (`s_hist`) combines fine-grained short-term states (most recent 10 steps at 50 Hz) for stable control and coarse-grained long-term states (past 10 seconds at 4 Hz) for broader temporal context and task progress awareness.
 - **Dynamics-Aware Prediction:** The action expert not only predicts actions (`a_t`) but also augmented actions (`~a_t`) that include future root linear velocity, root angular velocity, and joint positions. This provides auxiliary supervision, regularizing the policy towards physically grounded behavior by encouraging it to learn environment transition dynamics.
 - **Done Prediction:** A binary "done token" is predicted by an MLP head, indicating whether the command is expected to be completed within the next `H` steps. This allows the model to autonomously terminate tasks.
3. **Training Objective:** The overall loss function combines a primary flow matching loss (minimizing the distance between predicted and target velocity fields) and a binary cross-entropy loss for done prediction. Gradients from the done prediction head do not backpropagate into the language-state encoder to prevent interference with control learning.
4. **Classifier-Free Guidance:** During inference, classifier-free guidance is applied, using both conditioned and unconditioned (masked language) model outputs to amplify the influence of the language command on generated actions.

##### 2.2.4. Post-training: Residual Reinforcement Learning

1. **Residual Action Head (`π_∆`):** A lightweight MLP-based residual action head is introduced. It takes the current state (`s_t`) and the augmented action (`~a_t`) predicted by the pre-trained language-action model and outputs a residual action (`∆a_t`).
2. **Final Action:** The final action applied to the robot is the sum of the pre-trained model's action and the residual action (`a_final = a_t + ∆a_t`). This allows the model to adapt to unmodeled effects and mitigate open-loop drift from action chunking.
3. **Training:** The residual head is trained using PPO in IsaacLab, with the pre-trained language-action model parameters frozen. Extensive domain randomization is applied.
4. **Reward Function:** The reward function encourages the residual action to track the original joint position predictions, penalizes large residual actions, and includes terms for action rate, DoF limits, undesired contacts, and active termination.

##### 2.2.5. Modality Expansion

SENTINEL supports multi-modal extensions by converting other sensory inputs into language signals. For navigation, visual observations from an onboard RGB-D camera (D435) are processed by FoundationPose to estimate the target object's relative position. This estimated waypoint is then inserted into predefined natural-language command templates (e.g., "A person walks to (x m, y m)"). These generated language commands, along with proprioceptive states, are fed into SENTINEL for iterative, closed-loop navigation.

#### 2.3. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and on real humanoid hardware demonstrate SENTINEL's superior performance in text-based whole-body control.

##### 2.3.1. Text-Based Whole Body Control Evaluation

SENTINEL was compared against several baselines: MDM + Retarget, T2M-GPT + Retarget (both decoupled motion generation + WBC), UH-1 (robot pose generation + WBC), and LangWBC (MLP-based text-based WBC).

- **Generation Quality and Execution Success:** SENTINEL consistently outperformed all baselines across various metrics:
 - **Multi-Modal Distance (MM-Dist) ↓:** SENTINEL achieved 0.487, significantly lower than MDM (0.703), T2M-GPT (0.577), UH-1 (0.644), and LangWBC (0.682), indicating better alignment between language commands and robot trajectories.
 - **R-precision at K (R@1, R@2, R@3) ↑:** SENTINEL showed higher retrieval accuracy (R@1: 0.582 vs. 0.338-0.481 for baselines), demonstrating strong semantic understanding.
 - **Diversity ↑:** Maintained high diversity (0.967), comparable to or better than baselines, suggesting a wide range of motion generation.
 - **MMD (1e-2) ↓:** Achieved the lowest MMD (3.438), indicating a closer distribution of generated trajectories to ground truth.
 - **Success Rate (%) ↑:** Most notably, SENTINEL achieved a remarkable 99.45% success rate in physically stable execution, significantly higher than MDM (94.94%), T2M-GPT (89.33%), UH-1 (86.95%), and LangWBC (81.78%).

- **Impact of Physically Grounded Data and Transformer Architecture:** The results indicate that training on physically grounded robot data, combined with the expressive capacity of a transformer-based architecture, is crucial. Methods relying solely on human motion generation (MDM, T2M-GPT) often produced physically infeasible motions, leading to instability. LangWBC, with its MLP-based design, showed limited expressiveness and generalization despite DAgger training.

- **Qualitative Comparison:** For the command "jumps up in a tight twirl," MDM+Retarget generated large-amplitude, unstable motions causing the robot to fall. SENTINEL, however, produced a more moderated, physically feasible jumping-twirling behavior, directly predicting stable actions at the control level.

##### 2.3.2. Ablation Studies

- **Model Design:**
 - **Multi-Scale Observation:** Removing long-term observation history drastically reduced R@1 (0.153) and significantly increased MMD (72.468), showing its criticality for temporal awareness and expressive motion. Even replacing it with a shorter, fixed-frequency observation (2.0s observation) decreased R@1 (0.489) and increased MMD (3.956) compared to the base model, highlighting the benefit of the multi-scale approach.
 - **State Prediction:** Removing auxiliary state prediction slightly decreased the success rate (98.67%) and slightly increased MMD (3.587), indicating its contribution to execution stability.
 - **Done Prediction:** Without done prediction, R@1 dropped (0.522) and MMD increased (5.852), as the model could not actively terminate tasks, leading to undesirable behaviors after command completion.

- **Action Chunk Size:** Experiments with different training chunk sizes (H) and rollout steps (K) revealed that a rollout step of K=5 consistently gave the best performance. Larger training chunk sizes (H=50 vs. H=5) significantly improved both R@1 and MMD, suggesting that training with a longer horizon enables the model to learn longer-range semantic dependencies and maintain physical consistency, even if only a small portion of the chunk is executed.

- **Model Scaling:** Larger model capacities correlated directly with improved performance:
 - 600M parameters (default): R@1 0.582, MMD 3.438, Success 99.45%.
 - 200M parameters: R@1 0.371, MMD 6.485, Success 99.09%.
 - 60M parameters: R@1 0.099, MMD 13.968, Success 22.73%.
 This strongly affirms the importance of model capacity for capturing the complex dynamics and language-action mapping.

##### 2.3.3. Post-Training Evaluation

- **Residual Action Head:** The residual post-training significantly improved performance, especially under domain randomization (more realistic, high-variability environments).
 - Base model with domain randomization: R@1 0.315, Success 95.44%.
 - Base model + residual head with domain randomization: R@1 0.392, Success 99.11%.
 This demonstrates the residual action head's effectiveness in mitigating open-loop drift, enhancing robustness, and improving sim-to-real transferability.

##### 2.3.4. Waypoint Navigation Evaluation

By converting visual target positions into language commands, SENTINEL successfully performed waypoint navigation. The robot's average distance to the target position decreased from 5.06m to 1.99m over two rounds of navigation, confirming its ability to follow positional inputs and its multimodal extensibility.

##### 2.3.5. Real-World Deployment

SENTINEL was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 robot. It reliably interpreted and executed diverse commands, including upper-body gestures (e.g., "playing the violin"), locomotion (e.g., "walking straight forward"), and complex whole-body motions (e.g., "jumping up and down"). The implementation used an asynchronous inference pipeline to manage model latency, ensuring smooth and robust control on real hardware. The system also demonstrated autonomous sequential execution, seamlessly transitioning between multiple commands.

#### 2.4. Significance and Potential Impact

SENTINEL represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics and embodied AI, offering several key contributions and promising substantial future impact:

1. **Pioneering Fully End-to-End Language-Action Control:** SENTINEL is presented as the first fully end-to-end language-action model for humanoid whole-body control that operates without relying on intermediate motion representations or latent abstractions. This direct mapping from language and proprioception to low-level actions is a paradigm shift, enabling a more coherent and integrated learning process.
2. **Enhanced Semantic Grounding and Physical Feasibility:** By jointly optimizing language understanding and physical execution, the model achieves superior semantic alignment and physically stable behaviors. This addresses a critical limitation of previous modular systems, where a disconnect often led to unstable or unexecutable motions.
3. **Robustness through Data and Training:** The meticulous data curation process, involving large-scale robot trajectories generated under extensive domain randomization, coupled with the robust transformer architecture and flow matching technique, contributes to the model's high success rate and ability to perform in varied conditions, including zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
4. **Scalability of the Approach:** The use of large-scale datasets and transformer models, along with demonstrated performance scaling with model size, suggests that SENTINEL can be further improved with more data and computational resources, paving the way for more generalist humanoid control policies.
5. **Multi-modal Extensibility:** The demonstrated ability to integrate visual perception by converting it into language commands (e.g., for waypoint navigation) highlights the framework's flexibility. This opens doors for humanoids to process diverse sensory inputs and translate them into actionable, language-conditioned behaviors, leading to richer interaction capabilities.
6. **Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling natural language control, SENTINEL makes humanoid robots more accessible and intuitive to operate. Users can simply instruct the robot using everyday language, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for interacting with complex robotic systems. This is crucial for their eventual deployment in diverse human environments for tasks ranging from assistance to logistics.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for future research in several areas:
 - **Long-Horizon Planning:** The multi-scale observation and dynamics-aware prediction capabilities could be further developed for more complex, multi-stage tasks.
 - **Generalist Humanoid Agents:** The end-to-end nature makes it a prime candidate for building generalist humanoid agents capable of a wide range of tasks and adaptable to new environments.
 - **Embodied Language Learning:** It offers a platform for studying how robots can acquire a deeper understanding of language through physical interaction and embodiment.

In conclusion, SENTINEL represents a significant leap towards developing truly intelligent and versatile humanoid robots. Its fully end-to-end architecture, combined with robust training and real-world validation, positions it as a promising direction for achieving scalable, general-purpose humanoid control driven by natural language, with profound implications for the future of robotics.
