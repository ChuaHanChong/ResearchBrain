# Think When It Matters: Conditional VLM Reasoning for Social Navigation with RL Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The HUMA framework, developed by Sorbonne Université, enables socially compliant robot navigation by dynamically switching between an efficient RL policy and a Vision-Language Model only when robots are in close proximity to humans. This approach yielded up to a 20% increase in navigation success rates and a 20% reduction in human collisions on the Social-MP3D benchmark, validating its real-time social intelligence.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies often lack generalization for complex, rare, or edge-case social scenarios due to limited semantic understanding.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offer robust semantic understanding but incur high computational costs and slow inference times, limiting real-time deployment in mobile robotics.
- A fundamental trade-off exists between the computational efficiency required for real-time operation and the deep semantic understanding necessary for socially compliant behavior in human-populated environments.

## Method

- **HUMA** (Hybrid Understanding for Multi-modal social Navigation) dynamically switches between an efficient RL navigation policy and a VLM reasoning module based on a ==human-dependent switching mechanism==.
- The RL policy is trained with ==DD-PPO== and a composite reward function, RtsocialnavR_{t}^{socialnav}Rtsocialnav, which includes a ==Social Cognition Penalty== (RtscpR_{t}^{scp}Rtscp) for personal space violations and potential trajectory conflicts.
- The VLM is activated under socially sensitive conditions, processing depth maps converted into a ==JET colormap== with ==Canny edges== and a linguistic goal prompt, and is efficiently fine-tuned using ==Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)== via ==LoRA==.

## Results

- HUMA achieved a **62.07% Success Rate (SR)** on Social-HM3D and **70.35% SR** on Social-MP3D, representing approximately a **3%** and **20%** increase, respectively, over the best baselines.
- The framework significantly reduced **Human Collision Rate (H-Coll)** to **34.48%** on Social-HM3D and **20.82%** on Social-MP3D, corresponding to reductions of approximately **5%** and **20%** against baselines.
- The approach was successfully deployed on the Mirokaï humanoid robotics platform, demonstrating its practical viability and ability to perform socially compliant navigation in a real-world physical setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Selectively engaging computationally intensive VLM reasoning only when socially critical situations arise effectively balances real-time operational efficiency with deep semantic understanding for social navigation.
> - Converting raw depth maps to a JET colormap with Canny edge detection and goal vector overlay for VLM input significantly improves performance (SR, SPL) and reduces inference latency compared to RGB or mixed inputs.
> - An intermediate proximity threshold (e.g., 0.80 m for Personal Space Compliance) for activating the VLM provides an optimal balance, ensuring timely intervention without excessive computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of mobile robots into human-populated environments necessitates advancements in social robot navigation. This field focuses on enabling robots to navigate safely, efficiently, and comfortably while respecting human social norms and ensuring safety. Historically, robot motion planning has relied on fixed geometric rules and, more recently, learning-based paradigms such as Reinforcement Learning (RL). RL policies are favored for their fast inference and reactive behavior, crucial for real-time deployment. However, RL methods often struggle with generalization to complex, rare, or edge-case social scenarios due to their limited semantic understanding and the extensive data requirements for training nuanced social behaviors.

In parallel, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as powerful tools with robust semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities, leveraging multimodal data to interpret complex social contexts such as pedestrian orientation, group dynamics, and intent. While VLMs offer superior contextual awareness and generalization, their high computational cost, slow inference times, and substantial data and training requirements have historically limited their real-time deployment in mobile robot navigation.

This work addresses the fundamental trade-off between the computational efficiency and reactivity of RL-based approaches and the deep semantic understanding of VLM-based methods. It aims to bridge this gap by proposing a hybrid architecture that selectively employs VLMs only when their sophisticated reasoning is deemed critical, thereby attempting to combine the strengths of both paradigms for socially compliant, real-time navigation. The paper positions itself within the ongoing effort to enhance robot autonomy in social settings by exploring adaptive intelligence that balances performance with computational resource management.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robot navigation framework that effectively balances the computational efficiency required for real-time deployment with the advanced semantic understanding necessary for socially compliant behavior in human-populated environments. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of purely RL-based policies, which often lack generalization to complex social scenarios, and purely VLM-based methods, which suffer from high computational overhead and slow inference times that hinder real-time operation.

The core motivation stems from the observation that high-level semantic reasoning is not always necessary for routine navigation tasks in uncrowded environments but becomes critical when a robot operates in close proximity to humans. Existing approaches either prioritize speed over social intelligence or vice versa, leading to a gap in solutions that can offer both. The researchers hypothesize that an adaptive approach, where a robot intelligently decides *when* to engage its deep semantic understanding capabilities, can yield superior performance.

Specifically, the key objectives are to:
1. **Develop a hybrid architecture** (HUMA) that dynamically switches between an efficient RL policy for standard navigation and a more capable, but computationally intensive, VLM for socially sensitive situations.
2. **Enhance social compliance and safety** in robot navigation by leveraging the VLM's contextual reasoning capabilities during critical human interactions, thereby reducing personal space violations and collisions.
3. **Maintain real-time operational efficiency** by restricting the VLM's activation to specific, high-stakes scenarios, thus minimizing its computational impact during routine navigation.
4. **Evaluate the proposed framework** on established social navigation benchmarks to demonstrate its effectiveness compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
5. **Validate the architectural components** through ablation studies to understand their individual contributions to performance.
6. **Demonstrate practical viability** through real-world deployment on a mobile robot platform.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to contribute a robust and pragmatic solution for social robot navigation that integrates advanced AI reasoning without compromising the real-time operational requirements of mobile robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose HUMA (Hybrid Understanding for Multi-modal social Navigation), a hybrid framework designed for human-aware navigation. HUMA dynamically balances the computational efficiency of an RL policy with the semantic understanding of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) by employing a human-dependent switching mechanism. The approach formulates robot navigation as a Goal-Conditioned Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (GC-POMDP).

The HUMA architecture comprises three main components:

#### 3.1. RL Navigation Policy

The base navigation policy adopts the architecture from Falcon, processing egocentric depth images and point-goal coordinates. A two-layer LSTM recurrent state encoder extracts spatio-temporal features to output discrete navigation actions.
- **Training:** The policy is trained using Decentralized Distributed Proximal Policy Optimization (DD-PPO).
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function, $R_{t}^{socialnav} = R_{t}^{pointnav} - R_{t}^{scp}$, drives policy updates.
 - $R_{t}^{pointnav}$: Encourages goal-directed movement, accounting for distance change to target, a step penalty, and a success indicator.
 - $R_{t}^{scp}$ (Social Cognition Penalty): Penalizes socially undesirable behaviors, comprising:
 - $r_{coll}$: Discrete penalties for physical contact with static obstacles or humans.
 - $r_{prox}$: Exponentially scales if the robot breaches a 2.0 m safety envelope around any human, calculated as $\sum_{i=1}^{N} \beta_{prox} \cdot \exp(-d_{t}^{i})$ if $d_{t}^{i} < 2.0$ m, else 0.
 - $r_{traj}$: Penalizes the robot if its immediate path intersects within 0.05 m of a human’s projected H-step future path, with heavier weight for imminent conflicts.
- **Auxiliary Objectives:** A Spatial-Temporal Precognition Module is utilized during training. The total loss function $L_{total} = \beta_{main}L_{main} + \beta_{aux}L_{aux}$ includes $L_{aux} = L_{count} + L_{pos} + L_{traj}$. $L_{count}$ uses cross-entropy for human count estimation, while $L_{pos}$ and $L_{traj}$ use Mean Squared Error (MSE) for current human position tracking and multi-step future trajectory forecasting, respectively.

#### 3.2. Vision Language Model Reasoning Module

The VLM is activated under socially sensitive conditions to provide deeper contextual understanding.
- **Input Domain:** The VLM input, $o_{VLM}^{t}$, consists of a composite image $I_{fused}^{t}$ and a text prompt $P_{t}$.
- **Visual Token Pre-processing:** Raw depth maps $D_{t}$ are converted into information-dense visual tokens:
 1. Normalized to 8-bit unsigned integer space.
 2. Mapped to a 3-channel JET colormap to partition distance zones (close, medium, far).
 3. Processed via a Canny edge detector to map structural boundaries.
 4. Binary edges are superimposed as a white contour mask over the colormap, alongside the rendered GPS goal vector $g_{t}$, to create $I_{fused}^{t}$.
- **Text Prompt Generation:** The GPS goal state is encoded linguistically. $P_{t} = [P_{static}, H_{text}(g_{t})]$, where $P_{static}$ contains static social navigation rules, and $H_{text}(g_{t})$ formats relative goal coordinates into explicit linguistic descriptions.
- **Inference and Action Generation:** The VLM, parameterized by $\theta_{VLM}$, generates a sequence of $n$ actions $a_{t:t+n} \in A$. The model generates specific textual action tokens (e.g., [FORWARD], [LEFT], [STOP]), bridging linguistic output to discrete actions.
- **Efficient Adaptation:** The VLM (Qwen3-VL 2B) is fine-tuned using Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). LoRA applies with rank $r=16$, scaling factor $\alpha=32$, and a dropout rate of 0.05, targeting projection and feed-forward modules, resulting in approximately 17 million trainable parameters (0.81% of the full model). Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) uses category-specific prompts spanning perception, prediction, reasoning, action, and explanation.

#### 3.3. Switching Mechanism

To manage the trade-off between efficiency and social reasoning, HUMA employs a dynamic policy switching mechanism.
- **Proximity Detection:** The minimum Euclidean distance $d_{t} = \min_{h} \|p_{t}^{robot} - p_{t}^{h}\|_{2}$ between the robot and any human is calculated.
- **Personal Space Compliance (PSC) Score:** A binary compliance signal is defined based on a 1.0 m threshold (accounting for human and robot radii). To prevent transient violations from being smoothed, a rolling history of the last $W$ binary signals is monitored. The localized compliance metric, $PSC_{W}$, is formulated as $PSC_{W} = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=t-W+1}^{t} \mathbf{1}[d_{i} > 1.0 \text{ m}]$.
- **Policy Selection:** At each timestep, the active policy is selected based on $PSC_{W}$:
 - If $PSC_{W} \ge \theta$ (social compliance remains above threshold), the efficient RL policy is used.
 - If $PSC_{W} < \theta$ (personal space violations occur), the VLM policy is activated to handle socially sensitive situations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HUMA focused on two main questions: the performance benefits of proximity-based VLM triggering and the optimal conditions for the framework.

#### 4.1. Comparative Evaluation

HUMA was evaluated on the Social-HM3D and Social-MP3D benchmarks against classical and learning-based baselines (A*, ORCA, Habitat-official, Falcon, NavThinker).
- **Success Rate (SR):** HUMA achieved a 62.07% SR on Social-HM3D and 70.35% SR on Social-MP3D, representing approximately a 3% increase on Social-HM3D and a 20% increase on Social-MP3D compared to the best baselines (NavThinker and Falcon, respectively).
- **Human Collision Rate (H-Coll):** HUMA significantly reduced collisions, achieving 34.48% on Social-HM3D and 20.82% on Social-MP3D, corresponding to reductions of approximately 5% and 20% respectively against the best baselines.
- **SPL and PSC:** HUMA maintained competitive performance in Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) and Personal Space Compliance (PSC), demonstrating that improved success rates and safety were not at the expense of path quality or social adherence.

#### 4.2. Ablation Analysis on VLM

- **Model Size vs. Accuracy:** The fine-tuned 2B VLM model achieved accuracy comparable to the highest-performing baseline, with only a 1% difference, while reducing the number of parameters by approximately 33%. This suggests efficient performance for a smaller model.
- **Training Dataset Impact:** Training the VLM (Qwen3-VL-2B) on the SNEI dataset yielded the highest action prediction accuracy (0.850) on the MUSON evaluation set. Training on MUSON alone or in combination with SNEI resulted in better semantic and syntactic alignment (SBERT, ROUGE-1) but lower action accuracy. Given that action prediction is the primary requirement for navigation, SNEI was selected as the preferred training dataset.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis on Augmentation of Visual Input

- **Depth JET Colormap:** Converting raw depth maps to a JET colormap with Canny edge detection and goal vector overlay for the VLM input proved to be the most effective visual modality. This configuration achieved the best SR (62.07%), H-Coll (34.48%), and SPL (54.54) with the lowest latency (2,425 ms/call).
- **RGB and Mixed Inputs:** RGB-based inputs and mixed RGB+depth inputs performed worse across SR, H-Coll, and SPL metrics, and exhibited significantly higher inference latency (4,260 to 5,427 ms/call).
- **Directional Arrows:** Adding directional arrows to depth JET colormap inputs did not provide further performance gains and, in some RGB settings, degraded performance, suggesting the colormap itself provided sufficient spatial context for the VLM.

#### 4.4. Ablation Analysis on Proximity Threshold and Environment Evolution

- **Proximity Threshold ($r$):** An intermediate proximity threshold of $r = 0.80$ m for the PSC switch yielded the optimal balance, achieving the highest SR (62.07%) and SPL (54.54), and the lowest H-Coll (34.48%). This indicates that early but not overly sensitive intervention by the VLM is beneficial.
- **Environment Evolution Strategy during VLM Inference:**
 - **Freeze:** Freezing the environment during VLM inference yielded the best performance (62.07% SR). However, this is considered unrealistic for real-world deployment as the environment would continue to evolve.
 - **Realistic:** Reducing the frequency of environment updates to simulate more realistic scenarios resulted in a modest performance degradation, with SR dropping from 62% to approximately 56%, highlighting a trade-off between decision stability and simulation fidelity.

#### 4.5. Real-World Deployment

HUMA was deployed on the Mirokaï humanoid robotics platform, demonstrating its practical viability. The ROS2-based architecture integrated real-time data from onboard depth cameras and an external OptiTrack motion capture system for human and environment dynamics. The framework translated high-level navigation actions from HUMA into standard linear and angular velocity commands for robot execution. This deployment confirmed the ability of HUMA to perform socially compliant navigation in a physical setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HUMA framework contributes to the field of social robot navigation by presenting a hybrid approach that integrates the complementary strengths of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This work addresses a central challenge in embodied AI: achieving both computational efficiency for real-time operation and deep semantic understanding for complex social interactions.

The primary significance lies in its **dynamic policy switching mechanism**, which allows robots to selectively engage computationally intensive VLM reasoning only when socially sensitive situations arise, such as proximity to humans. This conditional activation directly tackles the VLM's high computational cost and slow inference, making VLM-enhanced social navigation more feasible for real-world deployment where continuous VLM inference is prohibitive.

The **quantitative improvements** demonstrated on standard benchmarks (20% SR increase on Social-MP3D, 3% on Social-HM3D, and significant reductions in human collisions) indicate that HUMA enhances both the safety and success rate of robot navigation. By reducing collisions and personal space violations, the framework contributes to improved human comfort, safety, and trust in human-robot interactions. This is critical for the broader acceptance and integration of mobile robots into public and private spaces.

The **detailed ablation studies** provide insights into optimal VLM configurations, visual input modalities, and switching thresholds. Identifying the effectiveness of depth JET colormap inputs and an intermediate proximity threshold for VLM activation offers practical guidance for future developments in multimodal robot perception and control. The successful real-world deployment on the Mirokaï robot further substantiates the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed architecture.

**Potential impacts** include:
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Robots equipped with HUMA could navigate more intelligently and safely in dynamic, human-dense environments like hospitals, airports, or homes, requiring less human oversight.
- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration:** By understanding and respecting social cues, robots can foster more natural and comfortable interactions, potentially expanding their roles in service, assistance, and companionship.
- **Framework for Future Research:** HUMA provides a foundational framework for further exploration of hybrid AI architectures in robotics. The identified limitations, such as the need for proactive social reasoning (predicting future violations) rather than reactive responses, adaptive trigger thresholds based on environmental context, and addressing simulation-to-reality gaps in VLM inference timing, offer clear directions for future research. Investigating more efficient VLM models or hardware solutions to minimize real-time inference latency will also be a key area.
- **Broader Application of Foundation Models:** The work demonstrates a pragmatic strategy for leveraging large foundation models like VLMs in latency-critical robotic applications, showcasing how their powerful reasoning capabilities can be harnessed without sacrificing real-time performance.

In summary, HUMA represents a step towards more socially intelligent and deployable robotic systems by strategically combining the best features of reactive control and deep semantic reasoning.
