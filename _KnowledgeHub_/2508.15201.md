# Survey of Vision-Language-Action Models for Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper provides a comprehensive review of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied manipulation, categorizing current research by model structures, datasets, training methods, and evaluation. It outlines the field's developmental trajectory, highlights the shift to Transformer-based architectures, and identifies key challenges and future research directions.

## Problem

- The rapidly evolving field of embodied intelligence and robotics, specifically VLA models, suffers from fragmented knowledge and diverse approaches.
- Researchers lack a structured, comprehensive overview to understand the architectural shifts, varied training strategies, and evaluation methodologies.
- Identifying critical gaps, recurring patterns, and promising future directions in VLA model development is challenging without a consolidated synthesis of existing work.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic review, tracing the chronological evolution of VLA architectures from early ==language-conditioned== methods to modern ==Transformer==-based models.
- An in-depth analysis was performed across five critical dimensions: VLA model structures, training datasets, ==pre-training== methods, ==post-training== methods, and model evaluation.
- The survey synthesizes identified challenges (e.g., data scarcity, generalization, real-world deployment) and proposes future research directions to guide the field.

## Results

- VLA models increasingly incorporate diverse sensory inputs, including 3D vision, tactile, and force-torque information, for a more comprehensive understanding of the physical world.
- Significant challenges persist in achieving broad generalization to unseen environments, objects, and tasks, as well as in overcoming the complexities of real-world deployment and the sim-to-real gap.
- The field is actively developing comprehensive evaluation frameworks, encompassing diverse simulation benchmarks and emerging real-world platforms, to assess VLA model performance and generalization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformer architectures have become the standard backbone for VLA models since 2023, enabling multimodal input integration and improved processing for complex robotic tasks.
> - A 'data pyramid' approach, leveraging general VLM/LLM data, human video data, and robot-specific data (including synthetic), is crucial for scalable VLA model training.
> - Pre-training from large foundation models and diverse post-training strategies (e.g., SFT, RLHF, chain-of-thought reasoning) are essential for adapting VLA models and enhancing task-specific performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of embodied intelligence and robotics, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. Embodied intelligence systems aim to develop agents capable of learning and adapting through continuous interaction with their environments. Recent advancements in large foundation models, such as Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have inspired the development of VLA models. These models are designed to serve as universal robotic control frameworks, integrating visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation to facilitate more intuitive and versatile human-robot interaction and broader application scenarios for embodied AI robots.

The survey contextualizes itself by comparing its scope with other existing VLA-related surveys. While other surveys might focus on specific aspects like architectural trends, action tokenization, or post-training methods, this paper distinguishes itself by offering a comprehensive review across five critical dimensions: VLA model structures, training datasets, pre-training methods, post-training methods, and model evaluation. This broad scope aims to provide a holistic understanding of the field, from its foundational principles to its current challenges and future directions, specifically tailored for embodied manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a detailed and comprehensive review of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models within the context of embodied manipulation. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives:

1. **Chronicle Developmental Trajectory:** To trace the historical evolution of VLA architectures, identifying key milestones and shifts in research paradigms. This includes understanding the transition from early language-conditioned methods to the integration of Transformer-based architectures and large foundation models.
2. **Detailed Analysis Across Critical Dimensions:** To conduct an in-depth analysis of the current state of VLA research across five core aspects:
 - **VLA model structures:** Examining the architectural components, including observation encoders, feature reasoning backbones, and action decoders.
 - **Training datasets:** Categorizing and analyzing the various types of data used to train VLA models, from general web-scale data to specific robot demonstration and synthetic datasets.
 - **Pre-training methods:** Describing the strategies employed to leverage large-scale data and pre-trained foundation models for initial knowledge acquisition.
 - **Post-training methods:** Discussing techniques used to adapt pre-trained VLA models to specific robotic tasks, including supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, and prompt engineering.
 - **Model evaluation:** Reviewing the established and emerging benchmarks, simulators, and real-world evaluation methodologies used to assess VLA model performance and generalization capabilities.
3. **Synthesize Challenges and Future Directions:** To identify and articulate the key challenges hindering the further development and real-world deployment of VLA models. Concurrently, the survey aims to outline promising future research directions to address these challenges and advance the field.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors. The rapid advancements in large foundation models have significantly influenced embodied intelligence, making VLA models a focal point for achieving more capable and general-purpose robotic systems. These models are seen as transformative for robotic control, enabling agents to understand complex instructions, perceive their environment richly, and execute precise actions. However, the field is expanding rapidly, leading to a proliferation of diverse architectures, training strategies, and evaluation methods. A structured, comprehensive review is therefore motivated by the need to:
- Organize the fragmented knowledge and provide a clear overview for researchers and practitioners.
- Identify recurring patterns, strengths, and weaknesses across different approaches.
- Highlight critical gaps in current research.
- Guide future research efforts towards more impactful and generalized VLA models for embodied manipulation.
- Facilitate the development of robust and deployable robotic AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper employs a systematic review methodology to survey the landscape of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied manipulation. The authors structure their analysis into a chronological overview followed by a detailed examination across five thematic dimensions.

**I. Developmental Trajectory:**
The survey begins by tracing the evolution of VLA architectures, starting from early language-conditioned robotic control approaches. It highlights key architectural shifts, particularly the adoption of Transformer models, which have become central to VLA development since around 2023. This historical perspective grounds the subsequent detailed analysis.

**II. Detailed Analysis Across Five Critical Dimensions:**

1. **VLA Model Structures:**
 - **Observation Encoder:** The survey categorizes methods for processing visual and other sensory inputs. It discusses the use of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) (e.g., ResNet, EfficientNet in RT-1, ACT) and Vision Transformers (ViT) (e.g., PaLI-X, RT-2, DINOv2, SigLIP, PaliGemma). It also examines approaches for 3D vision (e.g., 3D-VLA, PointVLA, OG-VLA, BridgeVLA) and the integration of diverse multimodal inputs such as tactile (e.g., TLA, VTLA, Tactile-VLA, ForceVLA) and force-torque sensors.
 - **Feature Reasoning Backbone:** The core processing unit of VLA models is analyzed. The Transformer architecture is identified as a dominant choice, including variants like Diffusion Transformers (DiT) (e.g., RDT, GR00T N1). The use of Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures (e.g., ChatVLA) for scalability and efficiency, and emerging State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba (e.g., RoboMamba) are also discussed.
 - **Action Decoder:** This section details how VLA models translate processed features into robotic actions. It covers methods for generating discrete actions (e.g., via clustering or tokenization as in RT-2, OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, BeT, VQ-BeT, FAST) and continuous actions (e.g., behavior cloning, diffusion models, flow matching in ACT, Diffusion Policy, Octo, π0, π0.5). It also explores hierarchical action generation strategies (e.g., HybridVLA, FAST).

2. **Training Datasets:**
 The authors categorize training data into a "data pyramid" based on its source and relevance to robotics:
 - **General VLM/LLM Data:** Large-scale web data (e.g., COCO, VQAv2, GQA, LAION-400M, WebLI) used for pre-training foundation models that are subsequently adapted for VLA tasks.
 - **Human Video Data:** Datasets of human activities (e.g., Something-Something V2, EPIC-KITCHENS-100, Ego-4D, Ego-EXO-4D) providing rich visual and semantic understanding, often used to bridge the gap between human and robot actions.
 - **Robot Demonstration Data:** Task-specific datasets collected from real robots (e.g., BC-Z, Bridge Data, DROID, Mobile ALOHA, OXE, RT-1, RH20T, RoboSet, RoboMIND) providing direct supervision for robotic policies.
 - **Synthetic Data:** Data generated in simulation (e.g., RoboCasa, SynGrasp-1B, RoboTwin, DexMimicGen, FetchBot, UniVoxGen) to overcome real-world data scarcity and enhance generalization through domain randomization.

3. **Pre-training Methods:**
 The survey describes methods for initial knowledge acquisition:
 - **LLM/VLM Fine-tuning:** Adapting large pre-trained language or vision-language models to robotic tasks, leveraging their extensive world knowledge (e.g., RT-2, PaLM-E, RoboFlamingo, OpenVLA, CogACT, π0, PaliGemma, RoboVLMs).
 - **Masked Modeling & Generative Pre-training:** Techniques that involve reconstructing masked parts of inputs (e.g., visual tokens, action sequences) or generating data to learn robust representations from unlabeled data (e.g., UniPi, GR-1, GR-2, LAPA, GO-1, UniVLA, EgoVLA, Being-H0).

4. **Post-training Methods:**
 This section focuses on refining VLA models for improved performance and adaptability:
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Training on task-specific datasets to adapt general models (e.g., GATO, UP-VLA, GR-3).
 - **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** Incorporating human preferences to align model behavior with desired outcomes (e.g., ChatVLA, ChatVLA-2).
 - **Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL):** Utilizing multiple agents for complex tasks.
 - **Prompt Engineering & Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** Using structured prompts or enabling multi-step reasoning processes to enhance task understanding and execution (e.g., ECoT, EMMA-X, RAD, ReasonManip, Inspire, DiVLA, OneTwoVLA, Lite/Fast ECoT).

5. **Model Evaluation:**
 The survey examines the methods and resources for evaluating VLA models:
 - **Real-world Evaluation:** Discussing challenges and platforms for assessing performance in physical environments (e.g., RoboArena, RoboEval, INT-ACT, RobotArena∞). Performance metrics often include success rate, score, standard deviation, and A/B testing results.
 - **Simulation Benchmarks:** Reviewing widely used simulators and benchmarks (e.g., CALVIN, Franka-Kitchen, SimplerEnv, LIBERO, Meta-World, RLBench, RoboMimic, ManiSkill2, AGNOSTOS) that provide controlled environments for testing various skills and generalization capabilities.
 - **Evaluation Metrics:** Highlighting metrics such as success rate, task completion rate, and various error measurements used across different benchmarks (Tables 4, 6, 7 provide examples of comparative performance on DROID, LIBERO, and SimplerEnv).

**III. Synthesis of Challenges and Future Directions:**
Finally, the survey concludes by synthesizing the identified challenges (e.g., data scarcity, generalization, real-world deployment, safety, ethical concerns) and proposing promising avenues for future research (e.g., multimodal data fusion, efficient training, interpretability, human-robot collaboration, robust evaluation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey identifies several key findings and patterns in the development and application of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied manipulation:

1. **Dominance of Transformer Architectures:** Transformer-based architectures have become the standard backbone for VLA models since 2023, enabling the integration of diverse multimodal inputs (vision, language, action). This shift has led to improved capabilities for processing sequential data and learning long-range dependencies, crucial for complex robotic tasks. Emerging architectures like Diffusion Transformers (DiT) and State Space Models (Mamba) are also being explored for efficiency and performance.

2. **Multi-Modal Integration and Embodiment:** VLA models are increasingly incorporating a wider range of sensory inputs beyond just vision and language. This includes 3D vision, tactile sensing, and force-torque information, aiming to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the physical world. The integration of these modalities is seen as essential for robust and dexterous manipulation, especially in contact-rich tasks.

3. **Data Pyramid and Scalability:** The field leverages a "data pyramid" approach for training. Initial knowledge is acquired from massive general VLM/LLM datasets (e.g., WebLI, LAION-400M), followed by human video data (e.g., Ego-4D, EPIC-KITCHENS) to learn human-like behaviors, and finally refined with robot-specific demonstration data (e.g., OXE, DROID) or synthetic data (e.g., RoboCasa, SynGrasp-1B). This hierarchical data strategy addresses the scarcity of robot-specific data while maximizing knowledge transfer from broader domains. Large-scale synthetic data generation is emerging as a critical method to scale robot learning.

4. **Advancements in Pre-training and Post-training:**
 - **Pre-training:** Methods for pre-training have evolved to effectively transfer knowledge from large foundation models (LLMs/VLMs) to the robotic domain. Techniques like masked modeling and generative pre-training on visual and action sequences enable VLA models to learn rich, generalizable representations.
 - **Post-training:** A variety of post-training strategies are employed to adapt VLA models to specific tasks and enhance their performance. These include supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to align with human preferences, and chain-of-thought reasoning (e.g., ECoT, EMMA-X) to improve task-specific decision-making and interpretability. The concept of dual-system VLA models (e.g., Hi Robot, Hume, Fast-in-Slow) is gaining traction, separating fast, reactive control from slow, cognitive reasoning.

5. **Challenges in Generalization and Real-World Deployment:** Despite progress, VLA models face significant challenges in achieving broad generalization, particularly to unseen environments, objects, and tasks. Real-world deployment remains complex due to issues such as sim-to-real gap, safety, robustness to perturbations, and the high cost of data collection. The survey highlights that while models perform well on specific benchmarks (e.g., Hume achieves 98% average success on LIBERO), generalization across diverse, open-ended tasks is still limited.

6. **Developing Evaluation Frameworks:** The field is actively developing more comprehensive evaluation frameworks. These include diverse simulation benchmarks (e.g., CALVIN, LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RLBench) that test different aspects like long-horizon tasks, lifelong learning, and cross-embodiment generalization. Real-world evaluation platforms (e.g., RoboArena, RoboEval) are also emerging to assess performance in actual physical environments, though these are resource-intensive. Metrics such as success rate, score, and error rates are commonly used, but the need for standardized and scalable evaluation methods is emphasized.

7. **Emergence of Industrial Models:** The survey notes the emergence of several VLA models released by major companies and research institutions (e.g., Google's RT-2, NVIDIA's GR00T N1, Figure AI's Helix, Physical Intelligence's π0). This indicates a growing industrial interest and investment, moving VLA research from academia towards practical applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey contributes to the field by providing a comprehensive and structured overview of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several aspects:

1. **Consolidation of Knowledge:** The survey consolidates a large volume of recent research on VLA models, which is crucial in a rapidly evolving field. By organizing diverse approaches into a coherent framework (architectures, datasets, training methods, evaluation), it serves as a valuable resource for researchers, facilitating a deeper understanding of the current state of the art. This can reduce redundancy in research and highlight successful strategies.

2. **Identification of Key Trends and Gaps:** By systematically analyzing VLA models across five critical dimensions, the paper identifies dominant trends, such as the widespread adoption of Transformer architectures and multimodal integration. Crucially, it also highlights significant research gaps and challenges, including data scarcity (especially for robot-specific and tactile data), limitations in generalization capabilities, complexities of real-world deployment, and the need for more robust and scalable evaluation methodologies. This identification is critical for guiding future research efforts.

3. **Guidance for Future Research and Development:** The comprehensive analysis and the outlined future research directions can serve as a roadmap for the embodied AI and robotics community. It points towards promising avenues such as developing more sophisticated multimodal fusion techniques, exploring efficient training paradigms, enhancing interpretability, fostering human-robot collaboration, and advancing embodied world models. By clearly articulating these paths, the survey can help accelerate progress towards more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

4. **Acceleration of VLA Model Development:** Insights gained from this survey into effective model structures, data utilization strategies (e.g., the data pyramid approach), and pre/post-training techniques can directly inform the design and development of next-generation VLA models. This understanding can lead to the creation of more robust, efficient, and generalizable robotic policies.

5. **Broadening Application Scenarios:** As VLA models become more capable and reliable, their potential impact on various application domains is substantial. Improved VLA models could enable robots to perform complex tasks in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, personal assistance, and dangerous environments with greater autonomy and adaptability. This could lead to a broader integration of intelligent robots into human society, enhancing productivity and quality of life.

6. **Educational Resource:** For newcomers to the fields of embodied AI, robotics, and VLA models, this survey provides an accessible entry point. It explains fundamental concepts, outlines the historical context, and details current methodologies, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for aspiring researchers and practitioners.

In summary, this survey provides a timely and exhaustive review of VLA models for embodied manipulation, synthesizing fragmented information and offering strategic guidance for future advancements. Its significance lies in its capacity to accelerate research, improve model development, broaden applications, and serve as a foundational reference for the embodied AI community.
