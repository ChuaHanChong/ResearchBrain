# Fast LeWorldModel

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast-LeWM introduces an action-prefix prediction method for visual world models, enabling parallel forecasting of future latent states. This approach achieves a 3.9x acceleration in dynamics evaluation and a 48% reduction in total planning time while improving planning success rates to 90.5% on average and mitigating error accumulation in long-horizon predictions.

## Problem

- Existing LeWorldModel (LeWM) approaches rely on local one-step latent transition models, necessitating computationally expensive autoregressive rollouts for multi-horizon planning.
- The sequential nature of dynamics evaluation in LeWM leads to accumulated prediction errors over longer planning horizons, decreasing reliability and accuracy.
- Iterative dynamics evaluation renders sampling-based planners, such as CEM, slow due to repeated module invocations.

## Method

- Introduces an ==Action-Prefix Encoder==, a causal Transformer, which processes entire action sequences to generate dense prefix tokens conditioned on the current latent state.
- Develops a Parallel Latent Predictor, a 6-layer MLP, that simultaneously estimates all future latent states for all prefix horizons from the initial latent and corresponding prefix tokens in a single pass.
- Employs a dense prefix prediction objective that supervises all predicted future latents against ground-truth latents across all horizons, supplemented by a SIGReg anti-collapse regularizer.

## Results

- Achieved an average planning success rate of **90.5%** across environments (up to **92.0%** with self-consistency), surpassing LeWM's **85.8%**.
- Reduced dynamics evaluation time by **3.9** times (from 31.4s to 8.0s) and total CEM solve time by **48%** (from 54.4s to 28.3s).
- Exhibited lower initial latent prediction error and a slower growth rate of error over longer horizons, leading to more accurate open-loop predictions and richer, more physically informative latent representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing sequential one-step dynamics prediction with parallel, action-prefix-based multi-horizon prediction significantly accelerates planning and mitigates error accumulation.
> - Dense prefix-level supervision compels the model to learn a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of how state continuously evolves under various action sequences.
> - Conditioning the action-prefix encoder on the current latent state provides essential context, improving the accuracy of future latent state predictions under varying initial conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Fast LeWorldModel" operates within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on visual world models for planning in reinforcement learning environments. World models enable agents to predict the future consequences of their actions, which is a foundational capability for effective planning. A notable development in this area has been the emergence of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). These models learn to predict future embeddings rather than raw pixels, offering a reconstruction-free approach to visual representation learning that can be more efficient and less prone to learning irrelevant visual details compared to reconstruction-based methods.

Recent systems, such as LeWorldModel (LeWM) by Maes et al. (2026), have demonstrated the efficacy of JEPA-style models in supporting reward-free, goal-conditioned planning from pixel observations. These models often operate by encoding observations into compact latent states and then learning dynamics within this latent space. Planning typically involves test-time trajectory optimization methods like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), where candidate action sequences are evaluated based on how closely their predicted future latent state matches a goal latent.

Despite the progress made by LeWM and similar latent world models, a limitation exists in their dynamics modeling approach. Many of these models, including LeWM, employ a local one-step latent transition model. This means they predict only the immediate next latent state from the current latent state and action. Consequently, evaluating a sequence of actions over a longer horizon requires an autoregressive rollout, where the one-step model is applied repeatedly, with each prediction relying on the previously predicted (imagined) latent state. This sequential dependency introduces two primary challenges: it makes planning computationally expensive due to the iterative nature of dynamics evaluation, and it exposes long-horizon predictions to the accumulation of latent errors as errors from earlier steps propagate through the sequence.

"Fast LeWorldModel" directly addresses these identified limitations of existing LeWM-style approaches. By proposing an alternative dynamics modeling paradigm—action-prefix prediction—this work aims to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of latent world models, particularly for tasks requiring multi-horizon planning. It seeks to evolve the capabilities of reconstruction-free visual dynamics models by providing a more robust and faster mechanism for long-term state prediction, thus contributing to the advancement of goal-conditioned planning in complex visual environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the "Fast LeWorldModel" (Fast-LeWM) research are to mitigate the computational expense and accumulated error issues inherent in existing LeWorldModel (LeWM)-style planning approaches.

The motivation stems from two practical limitations observed in LeWM's autoregressive rollout mechanism:
1. **Computational Cost:** LeWM's dynamics model is designed to predict only the next latent state (\(\hat{z}_{t+1}\)) from the current latent (\(z_t\)) and action (\(a_t\)). To evaluate a candidate action sequence over multiple steps, the planner must apply this one-step model autoregressively, composing local transitions. This process involves repeatedly generating intermediate imagined latent states (\(\hat{z}_{t+1}, \ldots, \hat{z}_{t+k-1}\)), which necessitates multiple invocations of the action encoder and latent transition modules. This sequential computation renders the evaluation of numerous candidate action sequences, as required by sampling-based planners like CEM, computationally intensive and slow.
2. **Accumulated Prediction Error:** The autoregressive nature of LeWM's rollout means that prediction errors introduced at any intermediate imagined state can propagate and compound into subsequent predictions. As the planning horizon increases, these accumulated errors can lead to predictions that deviate significantly from the true future trajectory, making long-horizon planning less reliable and prone to inaccuracy.

Fast-LeWM is motivated by the objective to overcome these challenges by reformulating how the dynamics model processes action sequences. Instead of a local, one-step transition model, the goal is to develop a dynamics model that can directly predict future latent states by considering entire action prefixes. This change is intended to:
- **Accelerate Planning:** By enabling parallel prediction of multiple future latents corresponding to different action prefixes, the dynamics model can evaluate candidate action sequences more rapidly, reducing the overall time required for planning.
- **Reduce Error Accumulation:** By anchoring each future latent prediction to the initially observed latent state and the corresponding action prefix, rather than relying on a chain of intermediately predicted latents, the mechanism for error propagation is attenuated. This design aims to produce more stable and accurate long-horizon predictions.
- **Enhance Learning of State Evolution:** By supervising predictions across multiple horizons directly, the model is compelled to learn how action effects accumulate and how the state continuously evolves under various action sequences, rather than merely fitting isolated one-step transitions. This dense, prefix-level supervision is intended to result in a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of world dynamics.

In summary, the key objective is to propose and implement Fast-LeWM as a faster and more robust latent world model that improves planning efficiency and accuracy for reward-free visual control tasks by moving from sequential, one-step dynamics prediction to parallel, action-prefix-based multi-horizon prediction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Fast-LeWM addresses the limitations of autoregressive rollout by introducing a novel approach centered on **action-prefix prediction** and **parallel latent prediction**. The core idea is to predict future latent states directly from the initial observed latent and entire action prefixes, rather than through a step-by-step sequential application of a one-step dynamics model.

The methodology can be broken down into the following components:

#### 3.1. Action-Prefix Encoder

For a given candidate action sequence \(a_{t:t+H-1} = (a_t, \ldots, a_{t+H-1})\), the model needs to generate representations for all its prefixes.
- **Function:** The Action-Prefix Encoder (E\(\psi\)) processes the complete action sequence to output a dense sequence of prefix tokens \(p_{t,1:H} = (p_{t,1}, p_{t,2}, \ldots, p_{t,H})\).
- **Implementation:** It is instantiated as a causal Transformer. A causal mask ensures that the representation for horizon \(k\) (token \(p_{t,k}\)) can only attend to actions \(a_t, \ldots, a_{t+k-1}\), thereby preventing information leakage from future actions into shorter prefixes.
- **Contextualization:** To provide necessary environmental context, the current latent state \(z_t\) (encoded from the current observation \(o_t\)) is mapped through a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) into a "state token." This state token is prepended as the 0-th token to the sequence of action tokens before being fed into the Transformer. This allows the encoder to generate prefix tokens \(p_{t,k}\) that are conditioned on \(z_t\), meaning \(p_{t,k} = E^{(k)}_{\psi}(a_t, \ldots, a_{t+k-1} | z_t)\).
- **Architecture Details:** The action-prefix encoder is a 3-layer Transformer with 6 attention heads, using sinusoidal positional encodings. State and action-prefix token dimensions are set to 192.

#### 3.2. Parallel Latent Predictor

Once the prefix tokens are generated, the Parallel Latent Predictor (G\(\phi\)) takes over.
- **Function:** It estimates all future latents (\(\hat{z}_{t+1:t+H}\)) for all prefix horizons simultaneously in a single forward pass.
- **Mechanism:** For each prefix token \(p_{t,k}\), the predictor uses the anchor latent \(z_t\) and \(p_{t,k}\) to directly predict the corresponding future latent \(\hat{z}_{t+k}\). This means \(\hat{z}_{t+k} = G_{\phi}(z_t, p_{t,k})\).
- **Benefit:** Because each future latent prediction is independently anchored at \(z_t\) and conditioned on its specific prefix token, these predictions do not sequentially depend on one another. This design aims to mitigate the accumulation of prediction errors observed in autoregressive models.
- **Architecture Details:** The predictor is a 6-layer action-modulated residual MLP. It uses AdaLN-zero modulation and dropout, with a latent dimension of 192 and a hidden width of 2048.

#### 3.3. Dense Prefix Prediction Objective

To train Fast-LeWM, a specific loss function is employed:
- **Objective:** Unlike one-step transition learning, Fast-LeWM uses a dense objective that supervises all predicted future latents corresponding to action prefixes. For each horizon \(k \in [1, H]\), the predicted latent \(\hat{z}_{t+k}\) is supervised against the ground-truth future latent \(z_{t+k}\) (encoded from the actual future observation \(o_{t+k}\)).
- **Loss Formulation:** The prefix loss (\(L_{prefix}\)) is defined as the mean squared error (MSE) between predicted and ground-truth latents across all horizons:
 \[L_{prefix} = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{k=1}^{H} \|\hat{z}_{t+k} - z_{t+k}\|^2_2\]
- **Anti-Collapse Regularization:** Similar to other reconstruction-free JEPA-style world models, a SIGReg anti-collapse regularizer (\(\lambda \text{ SIGReg}(Z)\)) is added to prevent latent collapse, resulting in the total training loss \(L_{AP} = L_{prefix} + \lambda \text{ SIGReg}(Z)\). This forces the model to learn a diverse and stable latent space.

#### 3.4. Planning with Action Prefixes

Fast-LeWM integrates with existing planning algorithms, such as CEM, by modifying the dynamics query interface.
- **Candidate Evaluation:** When CEM proposes a candidate action sequence \(a^{(m)}_{t:t+H-1}\), Fast-LeWM evaluates its terminal latent state (\(\hat{z}^{(m)}_{t+H}\)) by encoding the full action sequence into prefix tokens and then using the parallel predictor in a single pass to obtain the final prediction. This contrasts with LeWM's sequential application of a one-step model.
- **Cost Function:** The basic candidate cost \(C^{(m)}_{goal}\) remains the squared Euclidean distance between the predicted terminal latent and the goal latent: \(C^{(m)}_{goal} = \|\hat{z}^{(m)}_{t+H} - z_g\|^2_2\).
- **Optional Self-Consistency:** The prefix-level interface also allows for an optional self-consistency signal during planning. The model can compare the directly predicted terminal latent (\(\hat{z}^{(m)}_{t+H}\)) with an alternative terminal estimate (\(\tilde{z}^{(m)}_{t+H}\)) obtained by first predicting an intermediate latent (e.g., at \(t+H/2\)) and then predicting the remainder of the horizon from that intermediate latent. The discrepancy between these two predictions can be added as a \(\beta\)-weighted penalty to the cost function: \(C^{(m)} = C^{(m)}_{goal} + \beta \|\hat{z}^{(m)}_{t+H} - \tilde{z}^{(m)}_{t+H}\|^2_2\). This term encourages planning stability by favoring action sequences whose long-horizon outcomes are consistently predicted irrespective of the prefix decomposition.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Environments:** Evaluation follows LeWM's protocol, using Two-Room, PushT, Reacher, and OGBench-Cube environments, with reward-free offline datasets.
- **Planning Protocol:** Same goal-conditioned latent planning protocol as LeWM, with a planning horizon H=5 (25 environment steps due to action skip of 5).
- **Training Details:** Models are trained for 10 epochs. The prediction horizon is adaptively determined and clamped to during training. A history size of 1 is used for all environments, as predictions are anchored at the current observed latent. The total model parameters (17.9M) are comparable to LeWM (18.0M).

This methodical approach aims to improve both the efficiency and accuracy of latent world models by fundamentally changing their dynamics prediction mechanism from sequential to parallel, leveraging dense prefix-level supervision for robust learning of multi-horizon state evolution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with Fast-LeWM demonstrate improvements in planning performance, efficiency, and latent space properties compared to existing methods, including LeWorldModel (LeWM).

#### 4.1. Planning Performance and Efficiency

- **Success Rate:** Fast-LeWM consistently achieved higher or equal success rates across all evaluated environments (Two-Room, Reacher, PushT, OGBench-Cube). The average success rate increased from 85.8% for LeWM to 90.5% for Fast-LeWM. When the optional self-consistency term was incorporated into planning, the average success rate further improved to 92.0%, suggesting the utility of this auxiliary signal for candidate selection.
- **Planning Time Reduction:** Fast-LeWM significantly reduced the computational cost associated with planning. The dynamics-evaluation time (comprising action encoding and latent prediction) was reduced from 31.4 seconds (LeWM) to 8.0 seconds, representing a 3.9 times acceleration. The total CEM solve time, which includes additional overheads, was reduced from 54.4 seconds to 28.3 seconds, a 48.0% reduction. These reductions were consistent across tasks, as dynamics evaluation costs are similar for the same image resolution and latent dimensionality.

#### 4.2. Open-Loop Latent Prediction

- **Reduced Initial Error:** Fast-LeWM exhibited substantially lower initial latent prediction error across all four tasks when predicting open-loop trajectories from a true initial frame and future action sequence.
- **Slower Error Growth:** The rate at which prediction error accumulated over longer horizons was consistently smaller for Fast-LeWM compared to LeWM. This indicates that the action-prefix prediction design effectively mitigates open-loop error accumulation, yielding a more accurate and continuous model of state evolution.
- **Qualitative Visualization:** Visualizations of decoded predicted latents showed that Fast-LeWM's predictions remained more consistent with the target trajectory, while LeWM exhibited noticeable drift over longer horizons, attributed to accumulated rollout errors.

#### 4.3. Physical State Probing

To assess the physical information embedded in the learned latent space, probing experiments were conducted on the PushT environment. Lightweight probes (linear and MLP) were trained to predict agent location, block location, and block angle from frozen latent embeddings.
- **Linear Probes:** Fast-LeWM achieved probing performance comparable to LeWM for linear probes (e.g., MSE of 0.048 for agent location and 0.029 for block location/angle) and consistently outperformed PLDM. This suggests that the latent representation preserves a similar level of linearly accessible physical structure.
- **MLP Probes:** For MLP probes, Fast-LeWM demonstrated superior performance across all three physical variables, achieving the lowest Mean Squared Error (MSE) and highest correlation. For instance, the MLP probe for agent location yielded an MSE of 0.001 and a correlation of 1.000. These results indicate that the latent space learned by Fast-LeWM retains richer physical state information in a nonlinearly recoverable form. The dense prefix-level training objective is posited to encourage the learning of fine-grained physical variables crucial for determining future motion and object configuration.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Long-Action LeWM:** A variant that naively extended LeWM's action encoding to a longer temporal span (25 primitive actions) performed poorly (e.g., 76% success on Two-Room), indicating that simply increasing the action block length in a one-step model is insufficient for efficient long-horizon planning.
- **Terminal-only Fast-LeWM:** Removing the dense prefix supervision from Fast-LeWM, so only the final latent prediction is supervised, still resulted in better performance than Long-Action LeWM (e.g., 96% on Two-Room). This validates the effectiveness of the action-prefix representation for long-horizon rollout, even without intermediate supervision. However, it underperformed the full Fast-LeWM, highlighting the importance of dense prefix supervision in constraining intermediate prefix tokens to represent meaningful partial action outcomes.
- **Impact of State Token:** Removing the conditioning of the action-prefix encoder on the current state token led to a decrease in performance (e.g., 94% on Two-Room compared to 98% with the state token). This indicates that providing the current latent context to the action-prefix encoder is beneficial for disambiguating action effects and accurately predicting future latent states under varying initial conditions.

In summary, Fast-LeWM demonstrates quantitative and qualitative improvements in planning success, computational efficiency, and prediction accuracy over baselines, driven by its action-prefix prediction and parallel latent prediction methodology, supported by a dense prefix-level supervision objective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Fast-LeWM represents a significant development in the field of latent world models for visual planning, primarily by addressing critical limitations of existing approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Efficiency for Latent Planning:** By replacing autoregressive one-step rollouts with parallel, action-prefix prediction, Fast-LeWM substantially reduces the computational cost associated with dynamics evaluations during planning. This efficiency gain, demonstrated by a nearly 4-fold acceleration in dynamics-module time and a 48% reduction in total CEM solve time, can enable faster iteration cycles for agents in complex environments or allow for more extensive search within a given time budget. This is particularly impactful for sampling-based planning methods like CEM, where numerous candidate action sequences must be evaluated.
- **Improved Accuracy and Robustness of Long-Horizon Predictions:** The design of Fast-LeWM, which anchors each future latent prediction to the initial observed latent and a specific action prefix, directly mitigates the problem of accumulated latent prediction errors. This results in lower initial prediction error and a slower growth rate of error over longer horizons, leading to more accurate and reliable open-loop predictions. For tasks requiring foresight and long-term planning, this enhanced predictive robustness can translate into more successful task completion.
- **Richer Latent Representations:** The dense prefix-level supervision objective compels the model to learn not just one-step transitions, but how states continuously evolve under varying action sequences. The physical state probing results, particularly the superior performance with MLP probes, suggest that this training paradigm encourages the latent space to capture richer, more fine-grained physical state information relevant to task execution. A more informative latent space can support more precise planning and decision-making.
- **Advancement in Reconstruction-Free Visual World Models:** Fast-LeWM builds upon the foundation of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) by offering a more effective and efficient dynamics interface within this framework. This contributes to the broader goal of developing robust, high-performance world models that can learn from raw visual data without relying on pixel-level reconstruction, which can often be computationally expensive and distract from control-relevant features.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** The principles introduced by Fast-LeWM, such as action-prefix encoding and parallel prediction for multi-horizon dynamics, could be transferable to other areas of model-based reinforcement learning and control. Any system that relies on simulating future states to make decisions could benefit from a dynamics model that offers faster, more accurate, and less error-prone long-horizon predictions. This includes areas like robot manipulation, autonomous navigation, and adaptive control, where efficient and reliable planning is paramount.

In conclusion, Fast-LeWM provides a novel and effective solution to key challenges in latent world modeling, enhancing both the efficiency and accuracy of goal-conditioned planning. Its contributions could foster the development of more capable and deployable intelligent agents in diverse visual environments.
