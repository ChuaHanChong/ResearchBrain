# CVPO: Enhancing LLM Reinforcement Learning Reasoning via Value-Variance Adaptation and Dynamic Curriculum Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> CVPO proposes a reinforcement learning framework for large language models that integrates a stochasticity-aware advantage function correction and a dynamic curriculum learning approach. This method significantly improves mathematical reasoning capabilities, achieving up to 25.2% higher accuracy on AMC23 and 13.7% on MATH500 compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Existing value-based reinforcement learning methods for LLMs provide insufficient precision in feedback for generated answer trajectories, failing to utilize statistical characteristics like value variance for fine-grained guidance.
- LLM training suffers from "difficulty drift," where fixed weighting strategies lead to overfitting on easy problems and neglect of challenging ones as model capabilities evolve, hindering overall reasoning improvement.

## Method

- **CVPO**'s ==stochasticity-aware adaptive advantage function correction== mechanism quantifies intrinsic generation stochasticity using trajectory value-variance, applying asymmetric weight adjustments for correct and incorrect responses.
- A ==difficulty-guided advantage weighting== component dynamically adjusts question importance based on real-time perceived difficulty, using ==Bayesian inference== to track success rates and shifting focus as the model learns.

## Results

- CVPO achieved superior performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including a **25.2%** improvement on AMC23 and **13.7%** on MATH500 compared to state-of-the-art baselines like VAPO.
- Ablation studies confirmed both stochasticity-aware advantage correction and dynamic curriculum weighting individually contributed to performance enhancements.
- The method significantly enhanced exploration, evidenced by a pronounced shift towards higher trajectory value-variance in generated responses, leading to more robust reasoning.
- Dynamic curriculum weighting prevented the "easy-problem trap," ensuring the model consistently focused on appropriately challenging problems throughout training, achieving higher overall reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token-level value-variance within generated trajectories correlates with exploration intensity, and modulating it can provide more precise, adaptive feedback for policy updates.
> - Asymmetric weighting of advantage functions based on both reward type (correct/incorrect) and trajectory stochasticity allows for dynamic regulation of exploration and convergence.
> - Dynamically adjusting question weights based on a Bayesian estimate of real-time perceived difficulty can prevent overfitting to easy problems and strategically focus training on challenging tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates within the rapidly evolving field of enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs) through reinforcement learning (RL), particularly for complex reasoning tasks such as mathematical problem-solving. Recent advancements in this area have demonstrated the potential of RL-based models to achieve significant performance breakthroughs.

The landscape is broadly characterized by two main approaches: value-free and value-based RL methods. Value-free methods, exemplified by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), average rewards from multiple trajectories to compute advantage values. While GRPO has shown pioneering progress in terms of training stability and efficiency by circumventing the need for a value network, it is noted for its limitation in leveraging fine-grained, trajectory-level value information.

In contrast, value-based methods, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) variants like ORZ, VC-PPO, and VAPO, incorporate a value model to estimate advantages more precisely. These methods are recognized for their potential to achieve more accurate credit assignment and higher performance ceilings, making them a promising direction for complex reasoning tasks. However, even these advanced value-based approaches face notable challenges.

The authors identify two key bottlenecks in current value-based RL methods for LLMs. First, existing mechanisms provide insufficient precision in feedback on generated answer trajectories. When trajectories yield homogeneous outcomes (e.g., all correct or all incorrect), the learning gradients lack differentiation, hindering further performance improvement. The statistical characteristics of value estimation, such as variance, are not fully exploited for fine-grained modeling. Second, there is a phenomenon of "difficulty drift" during training. As an LLM's capabilities improve, the relative difficulty of problems changes, leading to inefficiencies if a fixed weighting strategy is maintained. This can result in overfitting to simpler problems and limited attention to more challenging ones, thus restricting the model's overall reasoning capacity. Existing curriculum learning methods are typically static and fail to adapt to this dynamic shift in perceived task difficulty.

CVPO directly addresses these identified limitations by proposing two novel components: a stochasticity-aware advantage function correction mechanism at the trajectory level and a dynamic curriculum weighting method at the question level. By doing so, CVPO aims to build upon the strengths of value-based methods while mitigating their current shortcomings, thereby pushing the performance frontier in LLM reasoning.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) through reinforcement learning, specifically targeting the improvement of performance in complex tasks like mathematical reasoning. This overarching goal is motivated by two principal challenges identified in existing RL-based LLM training paradigms:

First, **insufficient precision in feedback on generated answer trajectories** is a significant bottleneck. Current value-based methods, while powerful, struggle to provide refined and discriminative gradient signals, particularly when a batch of generated trajectories exhibits homogeneous results (e.g., all correct or all incorrect answers). In such scenarios, the lack of differentiated feedback means that the model receives limited guidance on how to adjust its policy. This deficiency restricts the model's ability to learn nuanced improvements, hindering its capacity for further performance gains. The authors highlight that existing approaches fail to effectively utilize trajectory-level statistical characteristics, such as the variance of value estimates, which could offer valuable insights into the intrinsic randomness or exploration intensity of the generation process. Motivated by this, a key objective is to design an advantage function adjustment mechanism that can leverage this intrinsic stochasticity to provide more precise and adaptive feedback.

Second, the phenomenon of **problem difficulty drift** presents another critical challenge. As an LLM undergoes training and its capabilities improve, the relative difficulty of problems in the training set dynamically changes. Problems initially perceived as "hard" may become "medium" or "easy" over time. If the training process continues to use a fixed weighting strategy for all problems, it leads to several inefficiencies: the model may overfit to simple samples in later stages, training efficiency is reduced, and crucially, insufficient attention is given to genuinely difficult problems. This limits the model's ability to break through performance plateaus and achieve its full reasoning potential. This motivates the objective of developing a dynamic curriculum learning approach that can continuously assess sample difficulty and adapt the training focus accordingly, ensuring the model is always challenged at an appropriate level.

To address these motivations, CVPO sets forth the following specific objectives:
- To theoretically and empirically demonstrate that token-level value-variance within a generated trajectory correlates with exploration intensity and influences the magnitude of policy updates.
- To quantify the intrinsic stochasticity of the generation process using estimated trajectory value-variance.
- To design and implement a variance-aware advantage adjustment mechanism that dynamically regulates the weight of trajectories based on their reward types (correct/incorrect) and their quantified stochasticity. This aims to impose stronger constraints on correct samples with high stochasticity while promoting exploration for incorrect samples, thus improving learning efficiency and convergence stability.
- To introduce a dynamic curriculum weighting method that adaptively adjusts problem weights based on their real-time perceived difficulty. This includes maintaining a Bayesian estimate of accuracy for each question and detecting learning bottlenecks.
- To enable the model to strategically focus on tasks that match its current ability at each training stage, preventing it from getting "trapped" on easy problems and fostering sustained learning on complex reasoning tasks.
- To demonstrate, through empirical evaluation on various mathematical benchmarks, that the proposed CVPO method achieves superior performance, stronger exploration capabilities, and more accurate and robust reasoning compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

### 3. Methodology and approach

CVPO (Curriculum-guided Value-Variance Policy Optimization) is built upon the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework and introduces two core mechanisms to address the identified challenges in LLM reinforcement learning: a stochasticity-aware adaptive advantage function correction at the trajectory level and a difficulty-guided advantage weighting at the question level.

#### 3.1. Stochasticity-Aware Adaptive Advantage Function Correction Mechanism

This component focuses on refining the feedback signal for policy updates by considering the intrinsic stochasticity of generated trajectories.

**Theoretical Analysis:** The authors first establish a theoretical link between the magnitude of policy gradients and the variance of the value function (Var(V)). The policy gradient is defined as the expectation of the product of the log-probability gradient and the advantage function. By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz and Jensen's inequalities, they derive an upper bound for the policy gradient norm: `||∇_θ J(θ)||_2 ≤ C * (σ_Q + √Var(V))`, where `C` is a constant related to the policy log-probability gradient norm and `σ_Q` is the standard deviation of the state-action value. This analysis indicates that high value variance leads to larger policy gradients. The implication is that by modulating this variance in relation to trajectory quality, learning signals can be selectively amplified, potentially improving learning efficiency and convergence stability.

**Value Stochasticity Analysis in LLM Reasoning:** The paper posits that token-level value-variance within a trajectory correlates with exploration intensity. High variance suggests active exploration and stochasticity in the generation process, while low variance indicates more rigid or predictable responses. Current advantage function designs are criticized for lacking dynamic response to this intrinsic stochasticity, using fixed weights regardless of trajectory quality (correct/incorrect responses), and failing to integrate value estimation statistics (like variance) into policy optimization. This leads to issues where positive samples might over-explore and negative samples might get stuck in local optima due to rigid penalties.

**Method: Stochasticity-Aware Advantage Correction:**
To address these issues, CVPO quantifies the intrinsic stochasticity of the generation process using the standard deviation of trajectory value estimates, denoted as `σ_i = std(v_i)` for the i-th sample. To provide a contextual reference, this signal is normalized by computing a group-wise mean standard deviation (`μ_k`) for samples originating from the same prompt.

An asymmetric weight adjustment mechanism (`W_S`) is then constructed based on this variance and the reward type (`R(τ)`).
- **For correct responses (R(τ)=1):** The goal is to suppress high stochasticity to accelerate convergence. If `Var(τ)` exceeds `μ_k`, the weight function increases (penalty enhancement). If `Var(τ)` is below `μ_k`, rewards are implicitly reinforced for low-stochasticity strategies. The function is `W_S(Var(τ), 1) = 1.0 + α_P * sigmoid(λ_P * (Var(τ) - μ_k))`, where `α_P` and `λ_P` are fixed gain and steepness parameters, respectively.
- **For incorrect responses (R(τ)=0):** The aim is to retain high stochasticity to expand the exploration space. If `Var(τ)` falls below `μ_k`, the penalty intensity is increased. Conversely, if `Var(τ)` is at or above `μ_k`, the penalty is reduced to encourage high-stochasticity exploration. The function is `W_S(Var(τ), 0) = 1.0 - α_N * sigmoid(λ_N * (μ_k - Var(τ)))`, with `α_N` and `λ_N` being fixed penalty amplitude and sensitivity parameters.

#### 3.2. Difficulty-guided Advantage Weighting

This component addresses the "difficulty drift" and "easy-problem trap" phenomena by dynamically adjusting question weights.

**Difficulty Shift Analysis:** The paper notes that LLMs tend to prioritize easy problems for immediate rewards, neglecting complex ones. As the model improves, initially hard problems become easier. Fixed weighting leads to inefficiencies, overfitting, and limited focus on genuinely challenging tasks. Existing static curriculum learning methods fail to adapt to this dynamic difficulty perception.

**Method: Difficulty-Aware Dynamic Curriculum Weighting:**
CVPO employs a Bayesian inference framework to dynamically track the success rate (`γ_t_τ`) for each sample (question `τ`). A Beta prior distribution `Beta(α_0_τ, β_0_τ)` (initially uniform `α_0_τ=β_0_τ=1`) is recursively updated with observed successes (`s_0_τ`) from model responses.

A trend indicator `∆_t_τ = (¯γ_t_τ - ¯γ_t-1_τ) / ¯γ_t-1_τ` (where `¯γ_t_τ` is the posterior mean accuracy) monitors learning dynamics. Two stagnation conditions are defined:
- For low-accuracy samples (`τ | ¯γ_t_τ ≤ 0.3`): Stagnation if `|∆_t_τ| ≤ 0.05`.
- For high-accuracy samples (`τ | ¯γ_t_τ ≥ 0.8`): Slowdown if `∆_t_τ ≤ 0.03`.
If both conditions are met, a "learning bottleneck" is triggered, signaling a need for strategy shift.

A dynamic curriculum weighting function (`W_D(ρ_q)`) is introduced:
- **Initial Stage:** To promote rapid learning, the model focuses on tasks it can solve but performs poorly on, specifically questions with an accuracy rate around 0.5. This leverages higher reward variance and KL divergence for stronger gradient signals. The function is defined as `W_D(ρ_q) = [2 / (1 + e^(-k1*(ρ_q - c1)))] * [1 / (1 + e^(k2*(ρ_q - c2)))]`, using specific hyperparameters `k1, c1, k2, c2`.
- **Learning Bottleneck Stage:** When a bottleneck is detected, the weighting distribution shifts left (towards lower accuracy), increasing focus on high-difficulty, low-accuracy problems. Weights for fully mastered problems (accuracy=1) are set to zero to avoid redundant training. The function becomes `W_D(ρ_q) = [2 * (1 - ρ_q^2) / (1 + e^(-k'1*(ρ_q - c'1)))] * [1 / (1 + e^(k'2*(ρ_q - c'2)))]`, with adjusted hyperparameters `k'1, c'1, k'2, c'2`.

#### 3.3. Unified Advantage Function

The final weighted advantage function `A'` combines both mechanisms: `A'_t = W_S(Var(τ), R(τ)) * W_D(ρ_q, t_c) * A`, where `A` is the original estimated advantage, `t_c` represents the current curriculum training phase, `Var(τ)` is the trajectory value variance, and `R(τ)` is the trajectory return.

**Experimental Setup:**
The Qwen2.5-7B model is used as the base LLM. Training is conducted on DAPO-Math-17k, and evaluation is performed on AIME 2024, AIME 2025, AMC 2023, AMC 2024, and MATH-500 datasets, with performance measured by `avg@32` accuracy. A rule-based reward system assigns +1 for correct answers (in `\boxed{}` or "Answer:") and -1 otherwise. The implementation details include AdamW optimizer, specific learning rates for actor/critic, batch size of 256, maximum response length of 6144 tokens, and the omission of KL constraints to allow larger model updates for long reasoning traces. Comparisons are made against VAPO and a modified single-step async GRPO (GRPO (ASYN)) due to issues with generic GRPO.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experimental evaluation of CVPO on various mathematical reasoning benchmarks demonstrated its effectiveness in enhancing LLM performance.

**Overall Performance Superiority:**
CVPO consistently outperformed strong baseline methods, specifically GRPO (ASYN) and VAPO, across all tested datasets.
- On AIME24, CVPO achieved a 9.3% improvement over the second-best method.
- On AIME25, CVPO showed a 3.1% improvement.
- On AMC23, CVPO exhibited a substantial 25.2% improvement.
- On AMC24, CVPO registered a 12.9% improvement.
- On MATH500, CVPO demonstrated a 13.7% improvement.
These results indicate that CVPO facilitates more accurate and robust reasoning in complex mathematical tasks compared to prior state-of-the-art methods.

**Ablation Studies on Core Components:**
Ablation studies were conducted to assess the individual contributions of CVPO's two core mechanisms:
- **CVPO (only Stochasticity):** This variant, incorporating only the stochasticity-aware advantage correction, outperformed the VAPO baseline. This suggests that the mechanism for adapting advantage values based on trajectory value-variance is effective on its own. The authors noted that this component contributed a greater performance gain compared to the dynamic curriculum, attributing this to its ability to expand the upper bound of model exploration.
- **CVPO (only Dynamic Curriculum):** This variant, utilizing only the difficulty-aware dynamic curriculum weighting, also surpassed the VAPO baseline. This finding validates the effectiveness of dynamically adjusting problem weights based on perceived difficulty.

**Training Dynamics and Performance Progression:**
Analysis of performance over training steps (Figure 2) on the AIME 2024 benchmark revealed that CVPO maintained a significant advantage throughout the training process compared to VAPO and GRPO (ASYN). CVPO exhibited a rapid initial performance boost by focusing on "medium-difficulty" problems and using trajectory variance for advantage adjustment. Crucially, in the mid-training stage, when performance typically plateaus, CVPO's dynamic curriculum shifted its focus to harder problems. This adaptive strategy allowed CVPO to continue improving in accuracy beyond 1500 training steps, demonstrating a more effective long-term learning trajectory.

**Enhanced Exploration:**
The distribution of trajectory-level value-variance across different models (Figure 3) provided insights into exploration behavior:
- The base Qwen2.5-7B model showed the least average value-variance, indicating limited exploration within response trajectories.
- GRPO-ASYN resulted in a slight increase in responses with higher value-variances.
- VAPO displayed a noticeable shift in value-variance distribution towards higher values.
- CVPO exhibited the most pronounced shift towards higher value-variance, suggesting a significantly greater degree of exploration in its generated response trajectories. This increased exploration is correlated with the model's ability to discover correct answers through a wider range of reasoning pathways.

**Impact of Stochasticity Coefficient:**
Further ablation studies investigated the impact of different value-variance coefficients on CVPO's performance (Figure 4). While higher coefficient values generally led to better performance, there was a point beyond which increasing the coefficients resulted in degraded performance, particularly on harder tasks like AIME. This outcome aligns with the theoretical expectation that excessively large coefficients can lead to larger policy updates (and thus larger policy loss), potentially destabilizing convergence and hindering the model's ability to adapt to tasks requiring complex and elaborate reasoning.

**Effectiveness of Dynamic Curriculum Weighting:**
The comparison of training dynamics with and without dynamic question difficulty weighting (Figure 5) highlighted the mechanism's importance:
- **Without dynamic weighting (Figure 5a):** The model became trapped in an "easy-problem trap," predominantly focusing on simpler questions and allocating insufficient attention to more challenging ones. This constrained the model's overall performance ceiling by limiting its exposure to difficult tasks.
- **With dynamic weighting (Figure 5b):** During early training, the model strategically focused on questions with approximately 50% accuracy, promoting rapid knowledge convergence. Upon detecting a learning bottleneck (around step 1000), the model dynamically adjusted its attention, increasing updates for difficult questions and reducing them for simple ones. This adaptive adjustment allowed the model to achieve a higher performance ceiling by consistently engaging with appropriate levels of challenge.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The development of CVPO represents an advancement in the application of reinforcement learning for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models. The significance and potential impact of this work span several dimensions:

**Advancement in LLM Reinforcement Learning:** CVPO addresses two critical and previously underexplored bottlenecks in value-based RL for LLMs: the imprecision of feedback for homogeneous trajectories and the dynamic nature of problem difficulty. By introducing theoretical insights into value-variance and practical mechanisms for its utilization, CVPO provides a more sophisticated approach to policy optimization than existing methods.

**Improved Reasoning Accuracy and Robustness:** The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated across multiple challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME, AMC, MATH-500) indicate that CVPO can significantly improve the accuracy of LLMs in solving complex logical problems. The enhanced exploration capabilities, driven by the stochasticity-aware mechanism, contribute to a more robust reasoning process, allowing the model to explore diverse solution pathways.

**Enhanced Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** The variance-aware advantage adjustment mechanism offers a novel method to dynamically regulate the balance between exploration and exploitation. By encouraging exploration for incorrect responses and stabilizing convergence for correct ones, CVPO optimizes the learning process, leading to more efficient discovery of effective reasoning strategies. This fine-grained control over exploration is crucial for LLMs tackling intricate, multi-step reasoning tasks.

**Adaptive and Efficient Learning:** The dynamic curriculum weighting method provides LLMs with an adaptive learning strategy. By continuously assessing question difficulty and adjusting training focus accordingly, CVPO prevents the "easy-problem trap" and ensures that the model is consistently challenged at an appropriate level. This not only boosts training efficiency by allocating computational resources to the most impactful learning opportunities but also enables the model to break through performance plateaus, leading to a higher overall reasoning ceiling. This adaptive capability could make RL training more sustainable and effective for models with rapidly evolving abilities.

**Broader Applicability:** While the research demonstrates CVPO's effectiveness in mathematical reasoning, the underlying principles of stochasticity-aware feedback and dynamic difficulty adaptation are generic. These mechanisms could potentially be applied to a wider array of complex reasoning tasks, such as scientific reasoning, code generation, strategic planning, or even conversational AI where similar challenges related to nuanced feedback and evolving task difficulty exist. This work lays a foundation for future research exploring such broad applications.

**Theoretical and Empirical Contributions:** The theoretical analysis linking value variance to policy gradient magnitude provides a principled understanding of how intrinsic stochasticity influences learning. Coupled with robust empirical validation through comprehensive ablation studies and comparative analyses, the paper offers strong evidence for the practical utility of its proposed mechanisms. This provides a valuable blueprint for future research in designing more intelligent and adaptable RL algorithms for LLMs.
