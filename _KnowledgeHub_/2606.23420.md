# Flowing With Purpose: Latent Action Guided Flow Matching Policies For Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from École Centrale de Lyon introduce Latent Action Guided Flow Matching (LAFM), a framework that improves visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation by using an adaptive library of learned prior distributions, guided by high-level motion primitives, instead of a fixed source noise distribution. LAFM achieves an 86.7% average success rate in real-world robotic tasks, a 23.4% absolute increase over standard flow matching, and sets a new state-of-the-art with 93.0% success on the LIBERO-90 benchmark, outperforming larger pre-trained models.

## Problem

- Existing flow matching policies for robotic manipulation typically rely on a single, globally fixed isotropic Gaussian distribution as their source prior.
- Robotic action spaces are often fragmented and multimodal, with multiple valid motor strategies for similar observations, which a single fixed prior struggles to accommodate.
- Demonstrations in robotic manipulation exhibit heteroscedasticity (varying uncertainty and variability), which fixed isotropic priors cannot accurately capture, limiting the model's capacity to represent nuanced variability.

## Method

- **LAFM** replaces the fixed isotropic Gaussian source distribution with a ==dynamic library of learned prior distributions==, each corresponding to a discrete latent action or motion primitive.
- A pre-trained Latent Action Model (LAM) is used to predict a high-level motion primitive from the current observation, which then guides the selection of the appropriate prior distribution from the library.
- The policy is trained with a modified flow matching objective that incorporates an adaptive probability path, a cross-entropy loss for latent action prediction, and a KL divergence regularization to prevent prior overfitting.

## Results

- LAFM achieved an **86.7%** average success rate and **93.9%** completion score in real-world robotic manipulation tasks, marking a **23.4%** absolute increase in success rate over standard Flow Matching and **15.0%** over the π0 baseline.
- On the challenging LIBERO-90 benchmark, LAFM obtained a **93.0%** success rate, an absolute gain of **10.4%** over standard Flow Matching and outperforming all compared state-of-the-art baselines including larger VLA models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that structuring the source distribution with multiple learned priors is crucial, as LAFM (**93.0%** success) significantly outperformed a variant that only conditioned on latent actions without adaptive priors (**85.4%** success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By initializing the generative process from priors already aligned with coarse motion intent, the 'transport burden' on the learned vector field is reduced, leading to shorter and less entangled transport trajectories.
> - An adaptive library of specialized priors effectively captures the varying uncertainties and variances (heteroscedasticity) associated with different types of robotic movements inherent in demonstration data.
> - Integrating latent actions directly as an inductive bias by structuring the source distribution significantly improves the generative denoising process compared to merely conditioning the policy on latent action predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of visuomotor policy learning for robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on generative denoising processes. Recent advancements in this area have seen diffusion models and flow matching policies establish new benchmarks for performance. Flow matching, in particular, is noted for its ability to directly learn a continuous transport field from a simple source distribution to a target action distribution, offering advantages in inference speed and optimization stability compared to diffusion-based methods.

Existing flow matching policies, however, typically assume a globally fixed isotropic Gaussian prior as their source distribution. The authors identify this assumption as a limitation, particularly in robotic manipulation where conditional action distributions are often fragmented and heteroscedastic. Robotic actions can involve multiple valid motor strategies (e.g., different grasp types, distinct motion phases) under similar observations, leading to sharply separated action modes. A single, fixed prior struggles to accommodate this multimodal and heterogeneous nature, forcing the model to learn complex, entangled vector fields, which can hinder training efficiency and limit overall policy performance.

This paper addresses this limitation by introducing a mechanism to structure the source distribution itself, aligning it with coarse motion intent before the transport process begins. It builds upon Latent Action Models (LAMs), which have emerged as unsupervised abstractions for learning temporal transitions from video data, providing discrete motion primitives. Unlike prior work that uses latent actions primarily as pre-training signals for policy learning or for labeling datasets, this research integrates them directly as an inductive bias for the generative denoising process, altering the geometry of the learned transport field itself.

The work differentiates itself from other noise optimization strategies, such as those using reinforcement learning (DSRL, Parrot) to adapt pre-trained policies, by learning adaptive noise distributions directly during policy training. It also aims to compete with and surpass massively pre-trained vision-language-action (VLA) models, often leveraging significantly larger architectures and extensive pre-training on diverse robotics datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and efficiency of flow matching policies for robotic manipulation by addressing a fundamental structural mismatch in existing formulations.

The core motivation stems from the observation that current state-of-the-art flow matching policies for behavior cloning suffer from a systematic limitation: they rely on a single, globally fixed isotropic Gaussian distribution as their source prior. This assumption is deemed problematic for robotic action spaces due to two main characteristics:

1. **Fragmented and Multimodal Action Spaces**: In robotic manipulation, a given observation can correspond to several distinct and valid action sequences or strategies (e.g., different ways to grasp an object, distinct phases of a multi-step task). These diverse action modes imply that the conditional action distribution is not a single, cohesive cluster but rather fragmented. A monolithic Gaussian prior forces the generative model to learn highly complex and entangled vector fields to connect a single, undifferentiated source to these disparate target modes. This entanglement can bottleneck training efficiency and constrain the policy's ability to accurately represent diverse behaviors.
2. **Heteroscedasticity in Demonstrations**: Robotic manipulation demonstrations often exhibit varying degrees of uncertainty or variability. For instance, repetitive motions like reaching or grasp closure might show low variance and strong consistency, while longer-horizon object interactions or exploratory movements might be substantially more diverse. A fixed isotropic prior cannot capture this mode-dependent uncertainty, implicitly imposing homogeneous variance across fundamentally different motion regimes. This limits the model's capacity to accurately represent and reproduce the nuanced variability inherent in human demonstrations.

To address these limitations, the authors propose that the source distribution for flow matching should be structured and adaptive, rather than fixed and global. The key idea is to initialize the denoising process from priors that are already aligned with coarse motion intent. They hypothesize that by predicting the high-level motion primitive (or "latent action") from the current observation, the model can then sample noise from a specialized prior distribution corresponding to that primitive. This approach aims to:

- **Reduce Transport Burden**: By starting the generative process from a distribution already aligned with the target action mode, the "transport burden" on the learned vector field is reduced. This is expected to lead to shorter and less entangled transport trajectories.
- **Accommodate Heteroscedasticity**: An adaptive library of priors, each specialized for a different motion primitive, can naturally capture the varying uncertainties and variances associated with different types of robotic movements.
- **Improve Training Efficiency and Performance**: By simplifying the vector fields and providing a more informed initialization, the framework aims to improve training stability and ultimately lead to higher success rates in complex manipulation tasks.

The work aims to demonstrate that this structural modification to the flow matching process, guided by Latent Action Models, can lead to substantial empirical improvements, outperforming both standard flow matching formulations and larger, pre-trained vision-language-action models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Latent Action Guided Flow Matching (LAFM), extends standard conditional flow matching by replacing the fixed isotropic Gaussian source distribution with a dynamic library of learned prior distributions, guided by latent action predictions.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: The Flow Matching Policy

The paper grounds its approach in the flow matching policy formulation proposed by Black et al.. The objective is to model the conditional distribution of future actions, $p(A_t | o_t)$, where $A_t = [a_t..., a_{t+H-1}]$ is an action sequence of horizon $H$, and $o_t$ is the current observation. A neural network, $v_\theta(A_t^\tau, o_t)$, is trained to minimize a conditional flow matching objective:

$L_{\tau F M}(\theta) = E_{p(A_t|o_t),q(A_t^\tau|A_t)} ||v_\theta(A_t^\tau, o_t) - u(A_t^\tau|A_t)||^2$

Here, $q(A_t^\tau|A_t) = N(\tau A_t, (1-\tau)^2 I)$ defines the optimal transport path, where $\tau \in$ is the flow matching step. Noisy action targets $A_t^\tau = \tau A_t + (1-\tau)\epsilon$ are constructed by linearly interpolating between the true action $A_t$ and standard Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$. The network learns to predict the denoising vector field $u(A_t^\tau|A_t) = A_t - \epsilon$. During inference, actions are generated by numerically integrating the learned vector field from $\tau=0$ to $\tau=1$, starting from a noise sample $A_t^0 = \epsilon$.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Flow Matching with Multiple Priors

LAFM modifies this by replacing the single prior $N(0, I)$ with a library of $K$ learned prior distributions $\{N(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)\}_{k=1}^K$. For each observation $o_t$, the network predicts a latent mode distribution $\pi(o_t)$, where $\pi_k(o_t)$ is the probability that the action belongs to mode $k$. A latent mode $k$ is selected, and noise $\epsilon_k \sim N(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ is sampled from the corresponding prior. This leads to an adaptive probability path:

$q_\theta(A_t^\tau|A_t) = N(\tau A_t + (1-\tau)\mu_k, (1-\tau)^2 \Sigma_k)$

The objective then becomes to regress the adaptive denoising vector field $u_\theta(A_t^\tau|A_t) = A_t - \epsilon_k$:

$L_{\tau AF M}(\theta) = E_{p(A_t|o_t),q_\theta(A_t^\tau|A_t)} ||v_\theta(A_t^\tau, o_t) - u_\theta(A_t^\tau|A_t)||^2$

Proposition 1 is introduced to formalize the intuition that latent-conditioned priors reduce the expected squared transport distance by aligning initial noise samples closer to their target action modes, leading to shorter and less entangled trajectories.

#### 3.3. Latent Action Guided Flow Matching (LAFM)

To make these prior distributions informative, LAFM leverages a pre-trained Latent Action Model (LAM).

1. **LAM Pre-training**: A LAM is pre-trained to compress visual dynamics between two temporal observations ($o_t$ and $o_{t+T}$) into a discrete latent action space $C = \{1..., C\}$. This provides unsupervised abstractions of temporal transitions.
2. **Prior Grounding**: The number of prior distributions $K$ is set equal to the LAM codebook size $C$, establishing a direct mapping between each discrete latent action $\ell_a \in C$ and a specific prior $N(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$.
3. **Latent Action Prediction**: The policy network includes a classification head that predicts a categorical distribution $P_{\theta_{cls}}(k | o_t)$ (equivalent to $\pi_k(o_t)$) from the current observation $o_t$. This prediction is supervised using a Categorical Cross-Entropy (CCE) loss against the discrete latent action $\ell_a$ extracted by the pre-trained LAM encoder:
 $L_{CCE}(\theta_{cls}) = -E_{(o_t,\ell_a)} [\log P_{\theta_{cls}}(k = \ell_a | o_t)]$
 During training and inference, the predicted index $k$ (obtained via `argmax`) determines which prior distribution is used for noise sampling.
4. **Prior Initialization and Regularization**: All learned prior distributions are initialized as standard normal distributions $N(0, I)$ to ensure training stability when latent action predictions are uninformative. To prevent over-specialization and overfitting of priors, a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularization term is added. This term penalizes the divergence between the selected learned prior $N(\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ and a standard normal distribution $N(0, I)$:
 $R_{KL}(\theta) = E_{k \sim P_{\theta_{cls}}(k|o_t)} [\frac{1}{2} \sum_{d=1}^D (\mu_{k,d}^2 + \sigma_{k,d}^2 - 1 - 2 \log \sigma_{k,d})]$
 The weight $\lambda_2$ for this term allows continuous interpolation between a constrained single-prior baseline and a fully adaptive multi-prior ensemble.

The overall objective for LAFM is a weighted sum of these components:
$L_{LAFM}(\theta) = L_{\tau AF M}(\theta) + \lambda_1 L_{CCE}(\theta_{cls}) + \lambda_2 R_{KL}(\theta)$

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The policy utilizes an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture.
- **Observation Input**: Observations $o_t = [I_1^t..., I_n^t, l_t, q_t]$ include RGB images, natural language task descriptions, and robot proprioceptive state. Text is processed by a frozen text encoder, images by a pre-trained (and fine-tuned) image encoder.
- **Policy Encoder**: Preprocessed features and proprioceptive state are projected into a multi-modal token sequence. A dedicated prompt token, processed by a Transformer with full bidirectional attention, outputs a hidden state. This state is linearly projected to the codebook dimension $C$ for latent action classification logits (Eq. 3).
- **Prior Library**: The predicted latent action index $k$ selects parameters ($\mu_k$, $\log \sigma_k$) from two embedding layers $E_\mu, E_{\log \sigma} \in R^{C \times D}$, where $D$ is action space dimensionality. Contextual noise $\epsilon_k$ is then sampled using a location-scale transformation: $\epsilon_k = \mu_k + \sigma_k \odot \zeta$, with $\zeta \sim N(0, I)$.
- **Policy Decoder**: A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) serves as the decoder, conditioned on the encoder's output via cross-attention. It processes the noisy action sequence $A_t^\tau$ and is optimized with the combined LAFM loss.
- **LAM Details**: The LAM (detailed in Appendix B) consists of a Spatial-Temporal Transformer encoder and a traditional Transformer decoder. An NSVQ layer discretizes the latent action space, enforcing an information bottleneck. LAMs are trained on diverse datasets, using frame pairs sampled one second apart to capture coarse movements. The Fractal dataset (with 512 codebook size) is primarily used for experiments, with ablations exploring training data and codebook size.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated LAFM against standard flow matching policies and state-of-the-art baselines in both real-world and simulated robotic manipulation settings.

#### 4.1. Real-World Experiments

Evaluation was conducted on four distinct tasks (picking plates, opening/closing drawer with screwdriver, depositing cans, stacking bowls) using a Franka Emika Panda robotic arm. Each task involved 50 demonstrations. Metrics were success rate and completion score.

- **Comparison with Baselines**: LAFM (0.11B params) was compared against ACT (0.05B params, no pre-training) and $\pi_0$ (3.3B params, VLA pre-training). Standard Flow Matching (FM, 0.11B params, no pre-training) served as a direct baseline.
- **Performance**:
 - LAFM achieved an average success rate of 86.7% and an average completion score of 93.9%.
 - This represents a 23.4% absolute increase in average success rate over FM (63.3%) and a 15.0% increase over $\pi_0$ (71.7%).
 - Completion scores showed similar gains: 18.9% over FM (75.0%) and 11.0% over $\pi_0$ (82.9%).
- **Qualitative Observation**: Policy rollouts from LAFM exhibited smoother and more fluid motions compared to FM.

#### 4.2. LIBERO Benchmarks

The method was evaluated on LIBERO, a simulated robotic manipulation environment.

- **LIBERO Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long (LIBERO-10)**:
 - A single unified model was trained across all 40 tasks.
 - LAFM (96.5% average success rate) showed a consistent, though marginal, performance gain of approximately 1% in success rate over FM (95.4%).
 - LAFM surpassed all other baselines including DP (72.4%), Octo (75.1%), OpenVLA (76.5%), $\pi_0$ (86.0%), SmolVLA (88.8%), UniVLA (95.2%), and ACT (95.9%). The marginal gain for LAFM and FM was attributed to the near-ceiling performance of these benchmarks.
- **LIBERO-90**:
 - This benchmark comprises 90 distinct tasks, allowing for a clearer differentiation in policy performance.
 - LAFM achieved a success rate of 93.0%.
 - This constituted an absolute gain of 10.4% over FM (82.6%).
 - LAFM established state-of-the-art results, outperforming DP (75.4%), VQ-BeT (81.3%), QueST (88.6%), ACT (86.2%), and $\pi_0$ (92.1%).

#### 4.3. Impact of Auxiliary Supervision (FM + LAC)

To distinguish the benefits of structured priors from auxiliary latent action prediction, a variant called FM + LAC was tested on LIBERO-90. This variant used a single standard Gaussian prior but provided the predicted latent action to the decoder via cross-attention, similar to LAFM's latent action prediction objective.

- FM + LAC achieved an 85.4% success rate, improving upon standard FM (82.6%).
- However, LAFM (93.0%) significantly outperformed FM + LAC, indicating that structuring the source distribution with multiple learned priors is crucial and provides benefits beyond simply conditioning the policy on latent action predictions.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

- **Learned Priors Analysis (Figure 3)**:
 - A t-SNE visualization of learned prior means for the LIBERO model showed that latent actions representing similar movements corresponded to similar learned distributions.
 - Priors associated with larger range movements exhibited larger variances, while smaller range movements had more specialized priors with smaller variances. This supports the intuition that latent actions effectively capture the heteroscedasticity of demonstrations.
- **Vector Fields Visualization (Figure 4)**:
 - A comparison of denoising processes for LAFM and FM on LIBERO-90 empirically illustrated Proposition 1.
 - LAFM, with its latent-conditioned priors, showed reduced initial displacement between source noise and target samples, resulting in shorter and less entangled transport trajectories. This reduced the average L1 error (0.45 for LAFM vs. 0.54 for FM) and transport cost (44.9 for LAFM vs. 139.7 for FM).

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Appendix C)

- **Codebook Size**: Experiments on LIBERO-90 showed that both excessively small and excessively large LAM codebook sizes degraded performance, suggesting an optimal range exists for partitioning the latent action space. LAFM consistently outperformed FM across all tested codebook sizes.
- **Training Dataset for LAM**: The choice of dataset for LAM pre-training also impacted performance. Using Fractal alone yielded strong results, outperforming combinations with larger but potentially less consistent datasets like OpenX Embodiment (OxE) + Ego4D. This indicated that the quality and consistency of the LAM training data, ensuring it covers relevant motion primitives, is more critical than sheer quantity or diverse camera views.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a novel framework, Latent Action Guided Flow Matching (LAFM), that addresses a fundamental limitation in existing flow matching policies for robotic manipulation. By replacing the monolithic Gaussian prior with an adaptive library of learned prior distributions guided by latent actions, LAFM provides a structurally informed initialization for the generative denoising process.

The primary significance of LAFM lies in its ability to:

- **Improve Policy Performance**: Empirically, LAFM demonstrates substantial improvements in task success rates, showing a 23.4% increase in real-world robotic deployments and a 10.4% increase on the challenging LIBERO-90 benchmark compared to standard flow matching.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Results with Efficiency**: LAFM surpasses significantly larger vision-language-action (VLA) models (e.g., $\pi_0$, UniVLA) while utilizing comparatively smaller architectures. This indicates a more efficient and scalable paradigm for learning complex robot behaviors.
- **Enhance Generative Process Geometry**: The method intrinsically disentangles the underlying vector fields by reducing the initial displacement between source noise and target actions. This leads to shorter and less entangled transport trajectories, which can improve training stability and robustness.
- **Accommodate Data Heteroscedasticity**: The dynamic adaptivity of the learned priors allows the model to naturally accommodate the varying degrees of variance and multimodality present in human demonstration data, leading to a more accurate representation of diverse motion patterns.

The potential impact of LAFM is in advancing the reliability and capability of robotic manipulation systems. By making visuomotor policy learning more efficient and effective, it could accelerate the deployment of robots in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and service robotics. The ability to learn from demonstrations more effectively, especially in multi-modal and heterogeneous action spaces, could reduce the need for extensive manual feature engineering or task-specific policy designs.

Future work directions include refining the information bottleneck within LAMs to better disentangle agent-specific movements from environmental noise, which could further improve the quality of latent action representations. Additionally, integrating this multi-prior flow matching formulation directly into large-scale pre-training pipelines of foundational VLA architectures is identified as a promising next step, potentially extending the benefits of LAFM to more generalist robot policies.
