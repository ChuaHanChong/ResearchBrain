# Darwin Family: MRI-Trust-Weighted Evolutionary Merging for Training-Free Scaling of Language-Model Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Darwin framework presents a training-free evolutionary merging system designed to enhance large language model reasoning by reorganizing latent abilities within existing checkpoints. Its Darwin-27B-Opus model achieved 86.9% on the GPQA Diamond benchmark, surpassing its strongest fully trained parent model by 1.4 percentage points without additional gradient-based training.

## Problem

- Achieving frontier-level reasoning in large language models typically requires computationally intensive post-training methods like instruction tuning or RLHF, which are costly and difficult to reproduce.
- Existing model merging approaches are often diagnostically blind or static, leading to task interference and inefficient exploration of the vast merge space.
- Core reasoning mechanisms in LLMs are largely established during pretraining, suggesting post-training mainly reconfigures surface behavior rather than fundamentally altering underlying reasoning circuits.

## Method

- The **Darwin** framework employs a ==training-free evolutionary merging== system that recombines frozen parent checkpoints in weight space by selectively mixing parameter deltas.
- It integrates ==Model-layer Response Importance (MRI)== as a diagnostic prior to estimate the functional relevance of individual parameter tensors for reasoning, guiding the evolutionary search.
- An ==MRI-Trust Fusion== mechanism adaptively balances diagnostic guidance with evolutionary exploration using a learned scalar parameter (tau) to control tensor-wise merge ratios.

## Results

- The flagship **Darwin-27B-Opus** model achieved **86.9%** on the **GPQA Diamond** benchmark, outperforming its strongest fully trained parent (Qwen3.5-27B) by **+1.4 pp**.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that MRI-Trust Fusion contributed a cumulative **+2.5 pp** improvement on GPQA accuracy compared to a no-MRI baseline.
- The framework successfully generalized across various model scales and parent compositions, enabling cross-architecture recombination (e.g., **Transformer attention + Mamba FFNs**) that outperformed individual parent models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier-level LLM reasoning capabilities can be substantially improved through training-free weight-space reorganization of latent abilities, offering significant computational savings over gradient-based training.
> - Optimal merging performance arises from an adaptive balance between explicit diagnostic guidance and evolutionary search, with diagnostics providing efficient direction and evolution refining these signals.
> - Consistent structural patterns emerged during evolution, such as selective preservation of attention modules and aggressive recombination in feed-forward components, providing insights into architectural functional specialization for reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated increasing reasoning capabilities, which have largely been achieved through computationally intensive post-training pipelines such as instruction tuning, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), and large-scale distillation. These procedures require significant computational resources and are often challenging to reproduce or adapt across different settings.

A body of research suggests that reasoning ability in LLMs may not be uniformly shaped by post-training. Some studies indicate that supervised and instruction tuning can enhance task-level accuracy while potentially diminishing reasoning faithfulness, robustness, or transferability, particularly in chain-of-thought contexts. Conversely, prompting-based methods like chain-of-thought and least-to-most prompting have shown that reasoning can be elicited at inference time without modifying model parameters, suggesting that fundamental reasoning mechanisms are predominantly established during pretraining. Analysis of internal representations, through layer-wise probing and structural diagnostics, further supports this perspective, indicating that distinct linguistic and reasoning functions are unevenly distributed across model depth, with critical reasoning computations localized to specific layers formed during pretraining and remaining relatively stable under post-training or fine-tuning. More recent diagnostic and causal analyses reinforce the concept of localized and structurally constrained functional importance in neural networks, advocating for selective interventions rather than uniform parameter adjustments. These observations collectively suggest that post-training primarily reconfigures surface behavior rather than fundamentally altering the underlying reasoning circuitry.

This context motivates the exploration of alternative, training-free approaches to enhance reasoning performance. Model merging, which involves directly combining specialized models in weight space, offers such an alternative. Early model merging methods, such as weight averaging or fixed linear combinations, are simple but can suffer from task interference when combining models with substantial representational divergence. Recent advancements in training-free merging employ selective parameter combination and sparsification to improve performance without gradient-based training. Evolutionary approaches further automate the discovery of effective merge configurations by enabling gradient-free optimization across the merge space. However, most existing evolutionary merging methods are "diagnostically blind," meaning they do not explicitly incorporate insights into the functional importance of different model components.

Additionally, the emergence of cross-architecture and hybrid models, which combine different sequence modeling mechanisms (e.g., attention-based transformers with state-space models like Mamba), demonstrates the potential for integrating complementary inductive biases within a single model. This architectural trend provides a precedent for training-free cross-architecture recombination, provided appropriate alignment and selection mechanisms are in place.

The Darwin framework positions itself within this landscape by seeking to improve LLM reasoning capabilities without additional training, specifically by reorganizing latent abilities within existing checkpoints. It aims to overcome the limitations of diagnostically blind evolutionary merging and static merging methods by introducing diagnostic guidance, adaptive merge mechanisms, and support for cross-architecture recombination.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether frontier-level reasoning performance in large language models can be improved without additional gradient-based training, by reorganizing latent capabilities already encoded in existing pretrained checkpoints.

This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **High Cost and Reproducibility of Post-Training:** Current methods for achieving strong reasoning performance in LLMs, such as instruction tuning, reinforcement learning, and large-scale distillation, necessitate substantial computational resources. These pipelines are often difficult to reproduce consistently or adapt across varying research and deployment settings, posing a barrier to broader accessibility and innovation.
2. **Nature of Reasoning Acquisition in LLMs:** A growing body of evidence suggests that core reasoning mechanisms in LLMs are largely formed during the pretraining phase. Post-training procedures, while effective for task-level accuracy, may sometimes degrade reasoning faithfulness, robustness, or transferability, particularly in complex chain-of-thought settings. This implies that post-training primarily reorganizes existing behaviors rather than fundamentally reshaping the underlying reasoning circuitry. This understanding motivates approaches that leverage and reconfigure existing capabilities.
3. **Limitations of Existing Model Merging Approaches:** While model merging offers a training-free alternative, existing methods have limitations. Static heuristic-based approaches (e.g., weight averaging) can suffer from task interference due to uniform treatment of parameters. Evolutionary merging, while flexible in exploring merge configurations, typically lacks diagnostic guidance, leading to potentially inefficient search and reliance on trial-and-error. The motivation is to develop a merging strategy that is both adaptive and informed by the functional importance of model components.
4. **Practical and Reproducible Alternative:** The research seeks to establish a practical and reproducible alternative to costly post-training pipelines for developing reasoning-centric language models. By operating entirely in weight space and employing gradient-free optimization, the proposed framework aims to significantly reduce the computational burden and increase the accessibility of advanced LLM reasoning.
5. **Leveraging Latent Capabilities:** The core hypothesis is that existing pretrained checkpoints already contain latent reasoning capabilities. The goal is to develop a mechanism to effectively identify, combine, and re-organize these capabilities to unlock improved performance without the necessity of introducing new knowledge or abilities through further training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Darwin framework operates as a training-free evolutionary merging system that recombines frozen parent checkpoints in weight space. Its design principles emphasize decoupling diagnostic guidance from evolutionary exploration and reconciling them through an explicit fusion mechanism.

The core components of the Darwin framework are:

1. **Problem Formulation:** The framework assumes two parent models, A and B, share a common pretrained base model, $\theta_{\text{base}}$. Their parameters are represented as $\theta_A = \theta_{\text{base}} + \Delta_A$ and $\theta_B = \theta_{\text{base}} + \Delta_B$, where $\Delta_A$ and $\Delta_B$ denote model-specific deviations. The objective is to construct a merged model $\theta_M$ by recombining $\Delta_A$ and $\Delta_B$ in weight space to improve reasoning performance without gradient-based training.

2. **Merge Kernel and Parameter Recombination:** Darwin assigns tensor-specific merge ratios, $r_{\text{final}}(T)$, which are scalar mixing coefficients applied across all elements of a given tensor $T$. The merged tensor is constructed as $\theta_M(T) = \theta_{\text{base}}(T) + (1 - r_{\text{final}}(T))\Delta_A(T) + r_{\text{final}}(T)\Delta_B(T)$. The primary merge kernel used for final model construction is DARE-TIES (Drop-And-Rescale with Task-Interval Elimination). This kernel computes parameter deltas relative to a shared base, applies Bernoulli sparsity masks to selectively retain informative components, rescales surviving entries to preserve expected magnitude, and then performs weighted recombination. SLERP (Spherical Linear Interpolation) is used as a lightweight alternative for early evolutionary exploration.

3. **Model-layer Response Importance (MRI):** MRI is introduced as a diagnostic prior to estimate the functional relevance of individual parameter tensors for reasoning behavior. For a tensor $T$, MRI combines static tensor statistics and probe-based functional responses: $\text{MRI}(T) = \alpha \cdot \text{Static}(T) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \text{Probe}(T)$.
- **Static Term:** Aggregates normalized entropy, variance, and capped $\ell_2$-norm statistics of the tensor.
- **Probe Term:** Measures the cosine distance between reasoning-conditioned and generic activations induced by a small calibration set.
- The weighting parameter $\alpha$ is fixed at 0.5. MRI-derived ratios, $r_{\text{MRI}}(T) = \text{MRI}_B(T) / (\text{MRI}_A(T) + \text{MRI}_B(T))$, serve as a soft prior.

4. **Architecture-Aware Tensor Alignment (Architecture Mapper):** To enable cross-architecture breeding between heterogeneous model families, an Architecture Mapper establishes tensor-level correspondences. For candidate tensor pairs ($T_A^i$, $T_B^j$), a compatibility score is calculated: $\text{Comp}(i, j) = \beta_1 \text{Type}(i, j) + \beta_2 \text{Dim}(i, j) + \beta_3 \text{Param}(i, j)$. This score considers functional role correspondence, dimensional consistency, and parameter-shape similarity. Fixed heuristic weights are used ($\beta_1=0.5, \beta_2=0.3, \beta_3=0.2$). Layer correspondences are established via constrained greedy matching under a minimum compatibility threshold.

5. **MRI-Trust Fusion and Genome-Based Control:** This mechanism controls the balance between diagnostic guidance (MRI-derived ratios) and evolutionary exploration (genome-derived ratios). A single scalar parameter $\tau \in$ (MRI trust) determines the final tensor-wise merge ratio: $r_{\text{final}}(T) = \tau \cdot r_{\text{MRI}}(T) + (1 - \tau) \cdot r_{\text{genome}}(T)$. Intermediate $\tau$ values allow evolutionary optimization to refine diagnostic signals.

6. **Genome and Evolutionary Optimization:**
- **Genome Representation:** Each merge strategy is encoded by a 14-dimensional adaptive merge genome: $g = (\gamma, \alpha_{\text{attn}}, \alpha_{\text{ffn}}, \alpha_{\text{emb}}, \rho_A, \rho_B, r_0, \dots, r_5, \tau, \lambda)$. This genome controls global merge balance ($\gamma$), component-level mixing ratios ($\alpha_{\text{attn}}, \alpha_{\text{ffn}}, \alpha_{\text{emb}}$), sparsification densities ($\rho_A, \rho_B$), block-level specialization coefficients ($r_0, \dots, r_5$), MRI trust ($\tau$), and merge-kernel interpolation behavior ($\lambda$).
- **Optimization Strategy:** A two-phase strategy is employed to manage the computational cost of evaluating candidate genomes:
- **Phase 1 (Structural Screening):** Candidate merge genomes are evolved using a standard evolutionary optimization loop (e.g., CMA-ES). A lightweight proxy fitness score is used to rapidly filter structurally implausible or low-quality merge configurations without instantiating full models.
- **Phase 2 (Empirical Evaluation):** A small set of high-quality genomes identified in Phase 1 is then instantiated as merged models and evaluated directly on reasoning benchmarks to determine final performance.

Parent models are selected to share a common pretrained base model whenever possible. Evaluation primarily uses GPQA Diamond, ARC-Challenge, and MMLU benchmarks. Comparisons are made against individual parent models, static training-free merging baselines (uniform averaging, TIES-style merging), and evolutionary merging without diagnostic guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results supporting the effectiveness and generalizability of the Darwin framework:

1. **Flagship Performance (Darwin-27B-Opus):**
- The flagship model, Darwin-27B-Opus, achieved a score of 86.9% on the GPQA Diamond benchmark. This performance positioned it at rank #6 among 1,252 evaluated models as of April 22, 2026.
- It outperformed its strongest fully trained parent model (Qwen3.5-27B) by +1.4 percentage points without any gradient-based training, demonstrating that frontier-level reasoning capabilities can be improved solely through weight-space reorganization.
- Compared to static merging methods, Darwin exhibited consistently higher accuracy and reduced variance, suggesting increased robustness against representational interference.
- Relative to evolutionary merging methods lacking diagnostic guidance, Darwin achieved higher peak performance and more reliable convergence, indicating the role of diagnostic signals in navigating the merge space.

2. **Analysis of Learned Genome and Merge Dynamics:**
- The learned MRI trust parameter ($\tau$) consistently converged to intermediate values, typically between 0.35 and 0.55 across various model scales. This suggests that neither pure diagnostic rules (fixed $\tau=1$) nor unconstrained evolutionary search (fixed $\tau=0$) are optimal. Instead, an adaptive balance between diagnostic priors and evolutionary optimization, where diagnostics guide the search while evolution compensates for noise and inter-layer interactions, yields performance benefits.
- The DARE-TIES merge kernel consistently outperformed linear interpolation and SLERP. DARE-TIES's drop-and-rescale mechanism effectively mitigated destructive interference between parent models, making it suitable for merging heterogeneous or specialized models.
- Analysis of evolved genomes revealed stable structural patterns, which recurred across independent runs and model scales. These patterns included selective preservation of attention modules (often from the 'Father' base model) and more aggressive recombination in feed-forward components (often from the 'Mother' specialized model). This suggests that Darwin discovers architectural regularities rather than model-specific artifacts, aligning with prior studies on functional specialization of transformer layers.

3. **Ablation Studies on MRI-Trust Fusion:**
- An ablation study on the Darwin-27B-Opus configuration, varying only the $\tau$ parameter, demonstrated the contribution of MRI-Trust Fusion.
- Using static MRI-based merging (fixed $\tau=1$) resulted in a +1.2 percentage point improvement in GPQA accuracy compared to genome-only merging (fixed $\tau=0$).
- Adaptively learning the trust parameter (evolved $\tau$) yielded an additional +0.9 percentage point gain over a fixed $\tau=0.7$ setting.
- Cumulatively, the full adaptive Darwin variant with MRI-Trust Fusion achieved a +2.5 percentage point improvement on GPQA compared to the no-MRI baseline, indicating its critical role in the observed reasoning gains.

4. **Generalization and Cross-Architecture Capabilities (Darwin Family):**
- The evolutionary principles demonstrated by Darwin-27B-Opus generalized across various model scales (from 4B to 35B parameters), multiple evolutionary generations (recursive merges), and diverse parent compositions.
- Independently evolved Darwin models across these variations consistently exhibited intermediate MRI-trust values and asymmetric recombination patterns (stronger preservation of attention, more aggressive recombination in feed-forward layers). These structural regularities suggest scale-invariant merging principles.
- The framework successfully supported cross-architecture recombination. Darwin-4B-Genesis, for instance, merged Transformer-based attention components with Mamba-style state-space feed-forward components without retraining. This hybrid model outperformed both its parents on targeted reasoning benchmarks, illustrating Darwin's capacity to recombine complementary inductive biases from heterogeneous architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Darwin framework and the Darwin Family of models represent a development in the field of large language model development, with several potential areas of significance and impact:

1. **Cost-Effective LLM Reasoning Enhancement:** The primary significance is the demonstration of achieving and improving frontier-level reasoning performance in LLMs without the extensive computational cost associated with traditional gradient-based training and post-training pipelines. By operating through training-free evolutionary merging in weight space, Darwin offers an alternative that is orders of magnitude (three to six) less expensive in compute, potentially democratizing access to high-performing reasoning models.

2. **New Paradigm for LLM Optimization:** This work establishes diagnostic-guided evolutionary merging as a practical and reproducible pathway to enhance reasoning capabilities. It shifts the paradigm from continuous parameter updates through gradients to discrete, guided recombination of existing latent capabilities, opening a new research program for principled weight-space optimization.

3. **Advancements in Model Merging:** Darwin advances the state of training-free model merging by integrating a multi-dimensional, adaptive genome with explicit diagnostic guidance (MRI-Trust Fusion). This approach addresses limitations of prior static and diagnostically blind evolutionary methods, leading to more robust and higher-performing merged models.

4. **Insights into LLM Internal Functionality:** The consistent observation of stable genome patterns, such as the differential treatment of attention and feed-forward layers (preserving attention, recombining FFNs), provides empirical insights into the functional specialization and importance of different architectural components within LLMs for reasoning tasks. These findings can inform future architectural designs and understanding of LLM mechanisms.

5. **Architectural Flexibility and Hybrid Models:** The demonstrated capability for cross-architecture recombination, exemplified by the successful merging of Transformer and Mamba components, suggests a method for constructing novel hybrid models. This could allow researchers to combine the strengths of different architectural inductive biases without requiring extensive re-training or complex multi-task learning setups.

6. **Reproducibility and Accessibility:** The public release of the Darwin Family models, the V6 codebase, and MRI tooling under the Apache 2.0 license promotes independent verification, further research, and broad adoption. This contributes to the transparency and reproducibility of LLM research, an important aspect given the high resource demands of modern AI development.

7. **Reduced Dependency on Data-Intensive Fine-tuning:** By leveraging latent capabilities from pretrained models, Darwin offers a path to improving reasoning without relying on large, task-specific fine-tuning datasets, which can be expensive to create and curate. This could be particularly impactful for specialized domains or low-resource languages.

Overall, Darwin presents a framework that offers a method for enhancing LLM reasoning with a significantly reduced computational footprint, while also contributing to the understanding of LLM internal workings and fostering community-driven development in model optimization.
