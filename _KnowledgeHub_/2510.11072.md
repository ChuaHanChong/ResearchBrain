# PhysHSI: Towards a Real-World Generalizable and Natural Humanoid-Scene Interaction System

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed PhysHSI, a system enabling humanoid robots to perform diverse, natural interactions with real-world environments through zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. It integrates an adversarial motion prior for lifelike behaviors with a robust coarse-to-fine onboard perception module, achieving high success rates and generalization on a Unitree G1 robot across tasks like carrying, sitting, and lying down.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with generalization and natural motion when interacting with diverse real-world environments.
- Reliable and continuous scene perception using only onboard sensors in unstructured settings is challenging due to limited field of view and occlusions.
- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex, long-horizon humanoid-scene interaction tasks remains a significant hurdle.

## Method

- A simulation training pipeline utilizes ==Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)== and a hybrid Reference State Initialization (RSI) strategy to learn generalizable and lifelike interaction policies.
- A robust coarse-to-fine object localization module combines LiDAR-based odometry for long-range coarse estimates with camera-based AprilTag detection for precise fine localization.
- Extensive domain randomization and observation masking in simulation are employed to facilitate zero-shot transfer to a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Results

- PhysHSI achieved high success rates in simulation (e.g., **84.60%** for Carry Box, **91.32%** for Sit Down in generalized scenes) and demonstrated strong generalization across varied object properties and scene layouts.
- The system successfully performed zero-shot transfer to a Unitree G1 robot, completing tasks like carrying boxes, sitting, and lying down with competitive success rates (e.g., 6/10 for Carry Box, 9/10 for Sit Down) and high precision in diverse real-world settings, including outdoors.
- The coarse-to-fine perception module accurately localized objects, reducing error from approximately 0.35m (LiDAR) to approximately 0.05m (camera), enabling precise interaction while running entirely onboard the robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial Motion Priors, combined with carefully annotated motion data and a hybrid RSI strategy, can effectively train policies for complex, generalized, and natural humanoid-scene interactions, suitable for real-world deployment.
> - A multi-modal coarse-to-fine perception system, leveraging distinct strengths of LiDAR and cameras, is crucial for robust and continuous object localization from onboard sensors in unconstrained environments.
> - Strategic domain randomization and observation masking in simulation are key to achieving successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for intricate manipulation and locomotion tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots to interact naturally with diverse real-world environments represents a grand challenge in robotics, often referred to as Humanoid-Scene Interaction (HSI). This endeavor is considered significantly more complex than achieving basic whole-body skills like standing or walking, as it requires a synergistic combination of advanced motor control, robust scene understanding, and adaptability to varied contexts. PhysHSI directly addresses this complexity by unifying these disparate capabilities into a single, deployable system.

Prior research in HSI can be broadly categorized. **Classical model-based methods** (e.g., motion planning, trajectory optimization) offer stability but suffer from high computational costs and limited generalization due to strong model assumptions, making them unsuitable for diverse, dynamic real-world interactions. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) based methods**, on the other hand, promise broader generalization by learning from diverse simulated experiences. However, learning policies from scratch often demands extensive and intricate reward shaping to achieve natural, lifelike motions.

A significant advancement in achieving motion realism has come from methods that leverage **motion capture (MoCap) priors**, particularly those employing **Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)**. These approaches have been highly effective in physics-based character animation, yielding physically plausible and human-like motions for dynamic interactions in simulation. Yet, a critical limitation has been their confinement to simulated environments, relying on perfect scene observations and struggling with the **sim-to-real transfer** gap. Real-world applications of AMP have primarily focused on regularizing basic locomotion, not complex object interactions.

Concurrently, **scene perception** for humanoid robots remains a persistent challenge. While MoCap systems offer precise global information, their use is restricted to controlled laboratory settings. Onboard RGB and depth cameras, though more practical, often suffer from limited fields of view and occlusions, particularly during long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks. LiDAR-Inertial Odometry (LIO) provides robust global localization but lacks the fine-grained object interaction accuracy needed for precise tasks.

PhysHSI explicitly aims to bridge these critical gaps. It builds upon the strengths of AMP for generating natural, generalizable motions by extending its application to complex, multi-stage interaction tasks in the real world. Crucially, it integrates a novel, robust **coarse-to-fine perception module** that combines LiDAR and camera inputs to provide continuous and accurate object localization, thereby addressing the real-world sensing limitations that have hampered prior MoCap-based or vision-only approaches. By delivering a complete, deployable system capable of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for diverse HSI tasks, PhysHSI represents a significant leap towards enabling humanoids to operate autonomously and naturally in unstructured, everyday environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a **real-world generalizable and natural Humanoid-Scene Interaction (HSI) system**, named PhysHSI. The authors are driven by the vision of deploying humanoid robots in everyday environments to perform diverse and complex tasks, such as carrying objects, sitting on chairs, or lying on beds, with a high degree of autonomy and lifelike behavior.

To achieve this ambitious goal, the work identifies and seeks to address several key challenges:

1. **Generalization:** The system must be able to perform interactions across a wide variety of scenarios and task goals without requiring extensive re-training for each new situation. This includes variations in object properties (size, weight, height), spatial layouts, and environmental conditions.
2. **Lifelike and Physically Plausible Motions:** Humanoid robots should exhibit natural, human-like behaviors rather than stiff, jerky, or artificial movements. This is crucial for seamless human-robot co-existence and for tasks requiring fine motor control.
3. **Robust Scene Perception:** The robot needs reliable and continuous information about surrounding objects and the environment. This is particularly challenging in real-world settings due to limited sensor fields of view, occlusions, and environmental noise.
4. **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Successfully translating policies learned in simulation to physical robots remains a formidable obstacle. The system must be robust to the discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics, sensor noise, and hardware limitations.

The motivation stems from the current limitations of existing methods. Classical control methods lack generalization. Pure RL-based approaches struggle with motion realism. While motion imitation techniques excel in simulation, they fall short in real-world deployment due largely to reliance on idealized observations and the difficulty of sim-to-real transfer. PhysHSI aims to overcome these collective limitations by creating a unified system that robustly combines advanced policy learning with a practical, onboard perception system, pushing the boundaries of what humanoid robots can autonomously achieve in complex real-world interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysHSI is designed as a comprehensive system comprising two main modules: a **simulation training pipeline** and a **real-world deployment system**.

#### 3.1. Simulation Training Pipeline

1. **Data Preparation:**
- The process begins by acquiring human motion data from datasets like AMASS and SAMP (which contain SMPL body model motions) and retargeting them onto the target humanoid robot (Unitree G1).
- To make these motions suitable for HSI, object information (e.g., box position, chair height) is *post-annotated*. This involves identifying key contact frames (e.g., pickup, placement) and inferring object trajectories (e.g., object position set to midpoint of hands during carrying). This manual annotation ensures physically coherent object interactions within the dataset.
- A smoothing filter is applied during retargeting to mitigate jitter, ensuring high-quality motion data.

2. **Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) Policy Training:**
- The HSI problem is framed as a reinforcement learning task, leveraging the AMP framework for both generalization and lifelike behaviors.
- **Policy (πθ):** Takes observations *oπ* (5-step history of proprioception – base angular velocity, gravity direction, joint positions/velocities, end-effector positions, previous action – and task-specific observations – object shape, pose, goal position) and outputs target joint positions for a PD controller.
- **Discriminator (D):** Trained to distinguish between motion clips generated by the policy and those from the prepared reference motion dataset. The discriminator's observation *oD* includes privileged information like base height, linear velocity, and crucially, object position, allowing it to implicitly condition on different task phases (approach, pickup, carry, place).
- **Reward Function:** Comprises task reward (*rG*), regularization reward (*rR*) for joint torques/speeds, and a style reward (*rS*) derived from the discriminator's output, encouraging imitation of natural motions. The style reward is defined as −log(1 − D(*motion_clip*)). PPO is used for policy optimization.
- **Hybrid Reference State Initialization (RSI):** To overcome the challenge of long-horizon tasks and limited demonstration diversity, a hybrid RSI strategy is employed. Episodes are initialized by sampling an initial phase from the motion data while randomizing the scene for subsequent phases. A subset of episodes also starts from a default pose with fully randomized scene parameters, balancing efficient exploration with generalization.
- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic Training:** The actor network is trained using only observations available during real-world deployment, while the critic benefits from richer, privileged state information, enhancing learning stability and efficiency.
- **Motion Constraints:** Early in training, a small style reward weight *wS* is used for exploration, gradually increasing later. L2C2 smoothness regularization is also applied to ensure smooth and stable motions for hardware.

#### 3.2. Real-World Deployment System

1. **Coarse-to-Fine Object Localization:** This module is critical for providing continuous and robust scene perception using only onboard sensors.
- **Initialization:** The target object's initial coarse position is manually specified through LiDAR point cloud visualization. Its orientation is defaulted.
- **Coarse Localization (LiDAR-based):** When the robot is far from the object or the object is outside the camera's FOV, FAST-LIO provides robust odometry (robot's pose relative to its starting point). This odometry is combined with the initial object pose to continuously estimate the object's position and orientation in the robot's base frame. This provides sufficient directional guidance for long-range approach.
- **Fine Localization (Camera-based):** Once the robot approaches the object (and it enters the camera's FOV), AprilTag detection using the Intel RealSense D455 camera provides precise object pose. In case of temporary detection loss (e.g., object temporarily leaving FOV during manipulation), the last observed object pose is propagated using odometry.
- **Static vs. Dynamic Objects:** The system differentiates between static (e.g., chair) and dynamic (e.g., box) objects. For dynamic objects, after grasping, if the object leaves the camera view, its pose information is masked, and the robot relies on proprioception to complete the task.

2. **Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
- **Domain Randomization:** Applied extensively during simulation training. This includes adding random offsets, Gaussian noise, and delays to object poses and forward kinematics (FK) observations.
- **Observation Masking:** The real-world masking mechanism for dynamic objects (when the object is out of camera view during grasping) is replicated in simulation. This involves checking facing condition, FOV condition, and distance condition for object visibility. Standard domain randomization techniques from prior work are also used for physical properties.

3. **Hardware Setup:**
- The system is implemented on a **Unitree G1 humanoid robot**.
- Onboard sensors include a **Livox Mid-360 LiDAR** and an external **Intel RealSense D455 depth camera** mounted on the head.
- All perception modules (point cloud visualization, Fast-LIO, AprilTag detection, FK) and the learned policy run entirely **onboard** a Jetson Orin NX, enabling portable and autonomous deployment.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating sophisticated policy learning with a robust, sensor-fusion-based perception system and extensive sim-to-real transfer strategies, allows PhysHSI to achieve its goal of generalizable and natural humanoid-scene interaction in the real world.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhysHSI demonstrates significant progress in enabling humanoid robots to perform complex real-world interactions. The findings are categorized across simulation validation, real-world deployment, and system analysis.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

1. **Overall Performance (Table I):** PhysHSI consistently outperforms baseline methods (RL-Rewards and Tracking-Based) in both success rate (R_succ) and human-likeness score (S_human) across all four representative HSI tasks (Carry Box, Sit Down, Lie Down, Stand Up).
- **High Success Rates:** For instance, in "full distribution scenes" (more challenging and generalized scenarios), PhysHSI achieves 84.60% for Carry Box and 91.32% for Sit Down, significantly higher than RL-Rewards (63.40%, 73.14%) and Tracking-Based methods (0.02%, 1.12%).
- **Lifelike Motions:** PhysHSI achieves much higher S_human scores (e.g., 3.83 for Carry Box, 4.77 for Sit Down) compared to RL-Rewards (1.17, 3.07), demonstrating its ability to generate natural, human-like movements. Tracking-Based methods can sometimes achieve high S_human if they perfectly track, but their generalization is poor.
2. **Strong Generalization (Fig. 3):** Unlike tracking-based methods that overfit to limited reference trajectories and fail in diverse scenes, PhysHSI, by leveraging AMP, demonstrates robust generalization. It can flexibly recombine motion styles and achieve high success rates even when scene parameters (object positions, sizes, heights) are randomized well beyond the training data.
3. **Ablation Analysis (Table II):**
- **Data Quality:** Smoothing motion data is crucial; "w/o Smoothness" significantly degrades S_human. Object annotations are critical; "w/o Object" drastically reduces R_succ and S_human, as the policy cannot learn task-phase-conditioned behaviors.
- **Hybrid RSI:** This strategy is vital for both generalization and sample efficiency. "w/o RSI" leads to poor efficiency, while "Naive RSI" (initializing solely from fixed reference states) severely limits generalization, performing worse than no RSI in full-distribution scenes.
- **Mask Strategy:** Masking object observations during grasping (to simulate real-world camera limitations) has a minimal impact on the final policy's success rate, validating its effectiveness for sim-to-real alignment without significantly hindering learning.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

1. **Zero-Shot Transfer and High Performance (Table III, Fig. 1, 4):** PhysHSI successfully performs all four HSI tasks on a Unitree G1 robot in real-world environments with zero-shot transfer from simulation.
- Achieves competitive success rates (e.g., 6/10 for the full Carry Box task, 9/10 for Sit Down).
- Demonstrates high finish precision (e.g., 0.19m for Carry Box, 0.07m for Sit Down).
- Handles long-horizon tasks, with execution times ranging from 2.3s (Stand Up) to 10.5s (Carry Box) and maximum movement ranges up to 5.69m.
2. **Generalization in Real World (Fig. 4):** The system generalizes effectively to diverse real-world scenes, including carrying boxes of various shapes, weights, and heights, and interacting with chairs and beds of different dimensions, both indoors and outdoors.
3. **Natural and Expressive Motions (Fig. 1(e)):** PhysHSI produces natural, human-like movements, inheriting catwalk-style locomotion from AMASS data. It can also learn and execute diverse stylized locomotion behaviors (e.g., dinosaur-like walking, high-knee stepping).
4. **Portability:** The entire system runs onboard the robot using only its integrated sensors (LiDAR, camera) and computation (Jetson Orin NX), enabling deployment in outdoor environments without external infrastructure like MoCap.
5. **Object Localization Module Analysis (Fig. 5):**
- The coarse-to-fine perception system is highly effective. The coarse stage (LiDAR-based odometry) provides reliable guidance from long range (localization error ~0.35m).
- The fine stage (AprilTag detection) activates when the robot is within approximately 2.4m, drastically reducing localization error to an average of ~0.05m, enabling precise interaction.
- The system demonstrates robustness with 15 out of 17 real-world HSI trials being successful.

#### 4.3. System Limitations (Table IV)

1. **Hardware Constraints:** The Unitree G1's rubber hand limits manipulation capabilities for larger or heavier boxes. Excessive weight can also lead to motor overheating.
2. **Data Scalability:** The current manual post-annotation of object information in motion data is not scalable for generating large volumes of high-quality HSI data.
3. **Perception Module Automation:** The modular real-world perception system, while robust, introduces complexity. A more automated perception module, potentially with active perception, is desired for greater robustness and simpler deployment.
4. **Specific Task Limits:** For box carrying, the robot is limited to boxes within cm height, [0.6, 3.6] kg weight, and cm maximum size due to camera FOV and arm/hand reach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysHSI represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and humanoid robotics, addressing long-standing challenges in real-world human-scene interaction. Its core contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Breaking the Sim-to-Real Barrier for Complex HSI:** The paper introduces one of the first comprehensive systems to successfully perform diverse, long-horizon humanoid-scene interaction tasks (e.g., carrying, sitting, lying) with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. This is a critical step beyond basic locomotion, showcasing that advanced interaction skills learned in simulation can be reliably deployed on physical robots.

2. **Generalizable and Natural Behaviors:** By leveraging Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) and a hybrid reference state initialization strategy, PhysHSI achieves remarkable generalization across varied task goals and scene configurations while maintaining natural, lifelike motion patterns. This capability is paramount for humanoids to operate effectively and acceptably in unstructured human environments. The ability to learn and reproduce stylized locomotion further highlights the system's expressive potential.

3. **Robust Onboard Perception for Unconstrained Environments:** The novel coarse-to-fine object localization module, integrating LiDAR and camera inputs, provides continuous and robust scene perception solely from onboard sensors. This overcomes the limitations of external MoCap systems and individual camera/LiDAR weaknesses, enabling portable deployment in previously inaccessible or unconstrained environments, including outdoors. This is a crucial step towards truly autonomous and mobile humanoid operations.

4. **Foundation for Future Embodied Intelligence:** PhysHSI's detailed methodology, comprehensive evaluation protocols, and insightful analysis of limitations offer a valuable blueprint and guide for future research. It highlights specific areas for development, such as scalable high-quality HSI data generation, more advanced and automated perception modules (e.g., active perception), and hardware co-design to enhance manipulation capabilities.

5. **Catalyst for Practical Applications:** The demonstrated ability of PhysHSI to perform complex tasks like carrying boxes and interacting with furniture naturally and robustly opens new avenues for practical applications. Humanoids equipped with such capabilities could be deployed in logistics, domestic assistance, elder care, hazardous environments, or even as general-purpose robots, significantly expanding their utility and integration into human society.

In summary, PhysHSI marks a pivotal step towards realizing the vision of intelligent, adaptable, and naturally interacting humanoid robots. It unifies advanced policy learning with practical perception, paving the way for more sophisticated object and scene interaction capabilities in real-world applications.
