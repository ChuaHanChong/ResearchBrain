# VISO-Grasp: Vision-Language Informed Spatial Object-centric 6-DoF Active View Planning and Grasping in Clutter and Invisibility

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology developed VISO-Grasp, a unified framework that integrates Vision-Language Models with active view planning and 6-DoF grasping to handle objects in severely cluttered and occluded environments. The system achieved an 87.5% average final success rate and an average of 3.10 grasp attempts, outperforming all tested baselines.

## Problem

- Robotic grasping in cluttered and occluded environments is challenging, as traditional grasp detectors rely on static viewpoints and often assume sufficient object visibility.
- Existing active grasping frameworks primarily optimize for 3D reconstruction rather than target-driven view search, struggling with heavy clutter or invisible targets.
- Foundation Models typically operate on 2D representations, lacking explicit 3D spatial priors, robust depth reasoning, and real-time viewpoint adaptation needed for 6-DoF grasping in complex 3D spaces.

## Method

- **VISO-Grasp** unifies ==Adaptive Multi-View Open-Vocabulary== 3D Object Detection (AMOV3D), a Target-Guided View Planner (TGV-Planner), and Real-Time Uncertainty-guided Multi-view Grasp Fusion (RT-UMGF).
- AMOV3D leverages Vision-Language Models for evolving spatial reasoning and actively constructs an instance-centric representation of spatial relationships, including occlusion reasoning and target-aware decluttering strategies.
- TGV-Planner employs a novel Velocity-field-based Next-Best-View approach to optimize camera viewpoints for maximizing target visibility, while RT-UMGF refines 6-DoF grasp predictions using Bayesian updates across multiple views for enhanced robustness.

## Results

- VISO-Grasp achieved an Average Final Success Rate (AFSR) of **87.50%** and an Average Grasp Attempts (#AGA) of **3.10**, surpassing static viewpoint methods (**52.5**-**60.0%** AFSR) and a reconstruction-based NBV (**12.5%** AFSR).
- The Target-Guided View Planner demonstrated substantial improvements over static views and traditional NBV, effectively handling severe occlusions by actively adjusting viewpoints and inferring occluding objects.
- The Real-Time Uncertainty-guided Multi-view Grasp Fusion component further enhanced performance, improving AFSR from **70.0%** (without fusion) to **87.5%** and reducing #AGA from **3.43** to **3.10**, indicating increased grasp selection robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Vision-Language Models with active perception and 3D spatial reasoning allows robots to mimic human-like decision-making for sequential manipulation and decluttering in highly occluded scenes.
> - Target-guided active view planning, focused on maximizing target visibility rather than general scene reconstruction, is critical for successful grasping when objects are hidden.
> - Continuous, uncertainty-aware fusion of grasp hypotheses from multiple viewpoints significantly improves the reliability and precision of 6-DoF grasp execution in dynamic, uncertain environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic grasping in cluttered and unstructured environments remains a significant challenge, particularly when target objects are partially or entirely occluded. Traditional learning-based grasp detectors, while advanced, often rely on static viewpoints and assume sufficient object visibility, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios where objects are frequently obscured. Similarly, existing active grasping frameworks, which incorporate Next-Best-View (NBV) planning, primarily prioritize 3D reconstruction over target-driven view search, often necessitating predefined search spaces and struggling with heavy clutter or invisible targets.

In parallel, the rapid advancement of Foundation Models (FMs), including Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has revolutionized robotic perception and planning by enabling zero-shot object detection, multi-modal reasoning, and improved decision-making. However, many FM-driven approaches still primarily operate on 2D representations, lacking explicit 3D spatial priors, robust depth reasoning, and real-time viewpoint adaptation. They often assume minimal occlusions and focus on planar (4-DoF) grasps, overlooking the complexities of 6-DoF grasping in highly occluded, three-dimensional spaces.

VISO-Grasp directly addresses these limitations by proposing the first unified framework that integrates FMs into **target-aware active view planning and 6-DoF grasping** within environments characterized by severe occlusions and even complete target invisibility. Unlike prior work, VISO-Grasp leverages FMs for evolving spatial reasoning and active view planning to construct and continuously update an instance-centric representation of spatial relationships. This allows for a more human-like decision-making process, including sequential grasping strategies for decluttering when direct access to the target is impossible. By incorporating a multi-view uncertainty-driven grasp fusion mechanism, VISO-Grasp also enhances grasp confidence and directional uncertainty in real-time, moving beyond static predictions to dynamic refinement. This work thus bridges the gap between powerful language-vision semantic understanding and robust 3D physical manipulation in complex, real-world conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of VISO-Grasp is to systematically overcome visibility constraints for robotic grasping in environments with severe occlusion and complete invisibility. This is motivated by the observation that humans instinctively adapt their viewpoints and employ intuitive spatial reasoning to locate and grasp objects even when they are initially hidden. Current robotic systems, however, often lack this adaptability, hindering their performance in unstructured, cluttered settings.

The key specific objectives of the VISO-Grasp system are:
- **To leverage Foundation Models (FMs):** Utilize the inherent prior knowledge within VLMs for spatial reasoning and active view planning, enabling human-like decision-making in complex scenes.
- **To construct and update instance-centric spatial relationships:** Develop a framework that continuously integrates spatial and occlusion relationships to build a dynamic understanding of the scene.
- **To enhance grasp success under challenging occlusions:** Improve the robot's ability to successfully grasp target objects despite partial or complete visual obstruction.
- **To facilitate active Next-Best-View (NBV) planning:** Introduce a novel NBV planner that optimizes sensor viewpoints to maximize target visibility, going beyond reconstruction-centric approaches.
- **To optimize sequential grasping strategies:** Implement a VLM-guided strategy for identifying and removing occluding objects when direct grasping of the target is infeasible, thereby enabling progressive scene decluttering.
- **To ensure robust and stable grasp execution:** Introduce a multi-view uncertainty-driven grasp fusion mechanism that refines grasp confidence and directional uncertainty in real-time, improving the reliability of chosen grasp poses.

By achieving these objectives, VISO-Grasp aims to significantly advance robotic manipulation capabilities in unstructured and dynamic environments, making robots more adaptable and efficient in handling real-world clutter.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VISO-Grasp is a unified system composed of three core functional capabilities: Adaptive Multi-View Open-Vocabulary 3D Object Detection (AMOV3D), Target-Guided View Planner (TGV-Planner), and Real-Time Uncertainty-guided Multi-view Grasp Fusion (RT-UMGF).

#### 3.1. Adaptive Multi-View Open-Vocabulary 3D Object Detection (AMOV3D)

The AMOV3D module is responsible for robust 3D object detection and segmentation in cluttered environments, leveraging state-of-the-art Foundation Models.
- **3D Scene Representation Generation:** Given an RGB-D image and a text prompt for the target, a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct-AWQ) generates structured descriptions for detected objects, including label, color, pattern, and spatial relation. Grounding DINO is then used to predict 2D bounding boxes, which are refined by SAM2 for pixel-wise instance segmentation masks. These 2D detections are transformed into 3D world coordinates using depth information and camera intrinsics, and 3D oriented Bounding Boxes (BBs) are computed via Principal Component Analysis (PCA).
- **Multi-View Object Verification and Fusion:** To maintain a consistent scene representation, AMOV3D updates a historical list of perceived objects. New observations from different viewpoints are integrated, and consistency checks are performed to refine object attributes and resolve ambiguities caused by occlusions.
- **Occlusion Reasoning and Scene Exploration:** If the target object is not detected, the system initiates an occlusion reasoning process. It employs Visual-In-Context Learning (VICL) within the VLM, guided by a Mixture-of-Reasoning-Experts (MoRE) paradigm, to analyze spatial configurations, material properties, and geometric constraints. This identifies potential occluders, and the system updates the grasping order to prioritize their removal, incrementally revealing the target object.

#### 3.2. Target-Guided View Planner (TGV-Planner)

The TGV-Planner determines whether the target can be directly grasped or if occluder removal and viewpoint adjustment are necessary.
- **Rule-Based Spatial Reasoning:** Based on the 3D BBs from AMOV3D, objects are assigned spatial relationships (Proximity, Below, High/Low) using predefined geometric criteria. This reasoning dictates the grasping strategy: prioritize objects "Below" the target, or "High" occluders. "Low" occluders are ignored. "High" occluders trigger the Velocity-field-based NBV (V-NBV) module for viewpoint optimization.
- **Velocity-field-based NBV (V-NBV):** This module generates camera motions using a continuous velocity field. For a single occluder, the camera's velocity direction aligns with the vector from the occluder to the target, guiding the camera to a viewpoint that maximizes target visibility (a "stagnation point"). A field strength coefficient (β) ensures smooth movement and convergence. For multiple occluders, the camera motion is determined by a weighted combination (superposition) of individual occluder influences. The planner operates in parallel with grasp inference, executing a grasp immediately if confidence is high, otherwise continuously updating grasps as the viewpoint changes.

#### 3.3. Real-Time Uncertainty-guided Multi-view Grasp Fusion (RT-UMGF)

RT-UMGF continuously refines grasp predictions by fusing multi-view observations using Bayesian updates with a von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution.
- **Grasp Generation:** The system uses vMF-Contact, a pre-trained uncertainty-aware grasp generator, to infer 6-DoF grasp candidates (contact points, baseline vector distributions, approach vectors, grasp widths, and quality scores) from the scene's point cloud at 10Hz.
- **Grasp Categorization:** Newly generated grasps are categorized. If they are significantly distant from existing fused grasps, they undergo self-fusion (using DBSCAN to identify clusters). If they are proximal to existing fused grasps (based on contact point distance and baseline direction cosine similarity), they are cross-fused with the closest existing grasp.
- **Contact-Grasp Fusion:** Both self and cross-fusion processes involve updating grasp parameters. Contact point positions are updated using a weighted sum based on grasp quality. For baseline and approach vectors, Bayesian inference updates the mean direction (μ) and directional precision (κ) of the vMF distribution, increasing precision with more observations.
- **Termination Criteria:** A grasp is executed if its estimated quality and directional precision exceed predefined thresholds, or if the V-NBV velocity field approaches a stagnation point and the quality threshold is met. If the target remains ungraspable, the VLM queries an optimal grasp order for obstructing objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using a UR10e robotic arm equipped with an Orbbec Femto Mega RGB-D camera and a Robotiq 2F85 gripper. The AMOV3D module utilized Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct-AWQ, Grounding DINO, and SAM2, while RT-UMGF employed vMF-Contact with a PointNeXt-B backbone, pre-trained on simulated data. Eight distinct real-world scenes with varying occlusion severity were used for evaluation.

**Ablation studies and comparative baselines included:**
- **Top-down and Init view:** Static viewpoint setups to evaluate performance without V-NBV.
- **w/o GF:** VISO-Grasp without the grasp fusion component, where the best grasp is taken directly from the current NBV inference.
- **Breyer's:** A reconstruction-based NBV planner, given manually annotated bounding boxes around targets for a fair comparison.

The performance was evaluated using metrics such as Final Success (FS - number of successful target grasps), Grasp Attempts (#GA - average attempts per successful trial), Grasp Success Rate (GSR - overall success rate for any grasp), Average Final Success Rate (AFSR), Average Grasp Attempts (#AGA), and Average Grasp Success Rate (AGSR).

**Key findings demonstrated VISO-Grasp's significant superiority:**
- **Overall Performance:** VISO-Grasp (referred to as "Ours") achieved the highest Average Final Success Rate (AFSR) of 87.50% and the lowest Average Grasp Attempts (#AGA) of 3.10, with an Average Grasp Success Rate (AGSR) of 83.86%. This significantly outperformed all baselines.
- **Impact of Active View Planning (TGV-Planner):**
 - **Breyer's NBV** performed poorly (AFSR 12.5%, AGSR 22.5%), highlighting its limitations in target-driven grasping in clutter and the necessity of object-centric reasoning. Even with pre-defined bounding boxes, its reconstruction-focused approach failed to effectively expose targets.
 - **Static Viewpoints (Top-down, Init view)** achieved moderate performance (AFSR 52.5% and 60.0% respectively). They struggled with severe occlusions and lacked the semantic understanding to correctly classify objects (e.g., misclassifying "Pringle box" in Scene 3). VISO-Grasp's multi-view integration through AMOV3D and TGV-Planner effectively refined VLM predictions by gathering diverse observations, leading to higher success rates in these challenging scenarios.
- **Impact of Grasp Fusion (RT-UMGF):**
 - The "w/o GF" baseline, while benefiting from NBV, showed an AFSR of 70.0% and #AGA of 3.43. VISO-Grasp's integration of RT-UMGF further improved performance (87.5% AFSR, 3.10 #AGA). This demonstrates that the real-time uncertainty-guided grasp fusion mechanism effectively refines grasp selection by aggregating uncertain and low-quality grasp hypotheses across multiple views, thereby enhancing robustness and reducing the number of attempts needed. For example, in Scene 4, RT-UMGF increased GSR from 82.4% to 85.7% and reduced #GA from 3.2 to 2.8.
- **Performance in Challenging Scenarios:** VISO-Grasp exhibited robust performance in complex scenes where spatial hierarchy and severe occlusion were present (e.g., "purple cup at the bottom" in Scene 6, or "yellow peach in the green cup" in Scene 7). The synergy between TGV-Planner ensuring target visibility and RT-UMGF refining grasp uncertainty was critical for success in these cases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VISO-Grasp represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks in unstructured and highly occluded environments. Its key contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Novelty in Unifying FMs and Active Grasping:** VISO-Grasp is the first unified framework to integrate state-of-the-art Foundation Models (VLMs) with target-aware active view planning and 6-DoF grasping under conditions of severe occlusion and even complete invisibility. This addresses critical limitations of previous FM-driven robotic systems that often lacked robust 3D spatial understanding, active perception, and capabilities for 6-DoF manipulation in complex settings.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Efficiency:** The system achieves an impressive 87.5% average final success rate while requiring the fewest grasp attempts among all baselines. This demonstrates a substantial improvement in both reliability and efficiency, crucial for practical robotic deployment.
- **Human-like Reasoning for Manipulation:** By leveraging VLMs for semantic scene understanding and rule-based spatial reasoning, VISO-Grasp enables more sophisticated, human-like decision-making for decluttering and optimal viewpoint adjustment. This includes the ability to infer and remove occluding objects sequentially, a capability vital for complex environments.
- **Advancement in Active Perception for Grasping:** The novel Velocity-field-based NBV (V-NBV) planner, which uses continuous velocity fields for object-centric view optimization, represents a significant step forward for active vision in grasping. It moves beyond reconstruction-focused NBV to directly enhance target visibility for manipulation.
- **Reliable Grasp Execution with Uncertainty-Awareness:** The real-time uncertainty-guided multi-view grasp fusion mechanism is critical for ensuring stable and accurate grasp execution. By aggregating and refining grasp hypotheses based on directional uncertainty, the system makes more robust decisions, which is essential for real-world reliability.
- **Broad Practical Applications:** VISO-Grasp has high potential impact across various domains. In industrial settings, it could revolutionize tasks like automated bin picking of unknown objects, intelligent assembly, or recycling and sorting, where items are often cluttered and partially hidden. Its adaptability also extends to logistics, service robotics, and domestic assistance.

Despite its breakthroughs, the authors acknowledge limitations, particularly the computational bottleneck introduced by VLM inference, which can incur latency. Additionally, the current system assumes a quasi-static scene, not accounting for highly dynamic objects or external disturbances. Future work aims to address these challenges by exploring more efficient VLM architectures and developing adaptive NBV strategies for real-time scene variations. Nevertheless, VISO-Grasp lays a robust foundation for intelligent, adaptable robotic manipulation in complex, unstructured environments.
