# CLAMP: Contrastive Learning for 3D Multi-View Action-Conditioned Robotic Manipulation Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> CLAMP, a 3D pre-training framework, integrates multi-view 3D geometric information with robot actions and text descriptions via contrastive learning, enhancing visuomotor policy performance and sample efficiency in robotic manipulation. It significantly improved success rates on simulated tasks by up to 30% and demonstrated superior real-world performance compared to baselines.

## Problem

- 2D image representations in behavior cloning policies often lack the precise 3D spatial information crucial for dexterous robotic manipulation.
- The scarcity of large-scale, diverse robot data hinders the development of robust pre-training methods comparable to those in computer vision.
- Existing pre-training approaches in robotics often underutilize 3D data or fail to leverage robot action information during the pre-training phase.

## Method

- Introduces **CLAMP**, a 3D pre-training framework that uses multi-view re-rendered point clouds (depth, XYZ) and a Vision ==Transformer== with STRING relative positional encoding to capture 3D spatial awareness.
- Employs ==contrastive learning== (SigLIP-style loss) across image, text (task descriptions), and robot action history to learn robust, cross-modal representations.
- Integrates a dual pre-training strategy, concurrently pre-training task-agnostic encoders and a ==Diffusion== Policy, which is then fine-tuned on task-specific demonstrations with frozen encoders.

## Results

- CLAMP-integrated methods achieved up to **30%** higher success rates on simulated tasks, such as **98.0%** on 'Screwdriver in Caddy' compared to **86.7%** for the best baseline, and demonstrated enhanced sample efficiency during fine-tuning.
- In real-world experiments, CLAMP outperformed baselines on four out of five tasks (e.g., Open/Close Drawers, Mug on Plate, Recycle Cans), showing improvements in grasping and multi-step task completion.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of dynamic wrist views, the text encoder, and the ViT with STRING encoding, while highlighting that pre-training both encoders and the policy is essential for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining multi-view 3D geometric information with robot action patterns and text descriptions through contrastive learning creates more effective and functionally relevant representations for robotic policies.
> - Dynamic virtual wrist views are crucial for high-precision manipulation tasks, significantly improving performance compared to static or standard orthogonal views.
> - STRING relative positional encoding, when applied with 3D point cloud coordinates, allows the image encoder to implicitly learn 3D structure by correlating tokens from different views based on their actual 3D proximity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has made progress by leveraging pre-trained 2D image representations in behavior cloning policies. However, these 2D representations often do not adequately capture the 3D spatial information crucial for precise manipulation tasks, leading to limitations in accuracy and generalization. A significant challenge in robotics research is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse robot data, which hinders the development of robust pre-training methods comparable to those in computer vision, where internet-scale image-text datasets are common. Furthermore, there remains an open question regarding which modalities (e.g., RGB, point clouds, voxel grids) are most suitable for pre-training in the context of high-dimensional robot state and action spaces.

Prior works in robotic manipulation have explored using web-scale images, videos, and text in conjunction with simulated and real-world robot data for pre-training. However, the specific contributions of different data sources to policy fine-tuning performance are not fully understood. Many of these approaches do not explicitly pre-train on 3D data or integrate 3D perception, which could provide the spatial understanding robots require for effective physical interaction. For instance, 3D-MVP introduced a 3D multi-view pre-training framework for manipulation that learns image representations via image reconstruction, but it does not incorporate robot action data during its pre-training phase.

This work positions itself to address these identified gaps by developing a 3D pre-training framework that integrates both multi-view 3D geometric information and robot action patterns. It aims to demonstrate that combining these fundamental modalities can create more effective representations for robotic policies, thereby improving learning efficiency and performance on downstream tasks. By contrasting 3D image observations with corresponding robot actions and task descriptions, CLAMP seeks to learn representations that inherently capture the correlations between robot states, environmental geometry, and manipulation behaviors. This approach advances the state of the art by providing a more comprehensive pre-training strategy that bridges the gap between perception and action in a 3D context, moving beyond purely 2D or purely visual 3D pre-training paradigms. The work also contributes to the application of sophisticated attention mechanisms, such as STRING relative positional encoding, within 3D multi-view image encoders.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a novel 3D pre-training framework named Contrastive Learning for 3D Multi-View Action-Conditioned Robotic Manipulation Pretraining (CLAMP) that improves the learning efficiency and performance of robotic manipulation policies.

The motivation for developing CLAMP stems from several limitations in existing approaches to robotic manipulation:

1. **Inadequate 3D Spatial Information in 2D Representations**: While pre-trained 2D image representations have shown success in behavior cloning, they fundamentally lack the precise 3D spatial understanding of objects and scenes that is essential for accurate and dexterous robotic manipulation. Robots operate in a three-dimensional world, and policies relying solely on 2D visual cues may struggle with tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning, such as grasping, placement, or tool use.
2. **Lack of Standardized Pre-training in Robotics**: Unlike computer vision, where large-scale image-text pre-training is standard, a universally adopted pre-training methodology for robotics has not emerged due to the scarcity of extensive, diverse robot interaction data. This necessitates exploring effective modalities and strategies for pre-training using available data.
3. **Underutilization of 3D Data and Robot Actions**: Many existing pre-training efforts for robotics either do not incorporate 3D data or fail to leverage robot action information during the pre-training phase. The researchers hypothesize that both 3D perception (providing geometric and positional understanding) and robot actions (representing patterns of interaction with the environment) are crucial modalities that directly reflect how robots perceive and engage with the physical world. Integrating these modalities is expected to yield more functionally relevant representations.
4. **Improving upon Existing 3D Pre-training**: Prior work, such as 3D-MVP, has explored 3D multi-view pre-training by focusing on image reconstruction, but without explicitly using robot action data to guide the representation learning. CLAMP aims to enhance this by associating 3D visual input directly with robot action patterns through contrastive learning, thereby grounding visual representations in executable behaviors.
5. **Enhancing Downstream Policy Performance and Sample Efficiency**: A core motivation is to develop pre-trained encoders that can provide robust, generalizable representations, and to concurrently pre-train the visuomotor policy itself. This dual pre-training strategy is intended to offer a stronger initialization for the policy, significantly reducing the amount of task-specific demonstration data required during fine-tuning and improving the final task success rates, especially for novel or challenging tasks.

By addressing these motivations, CLAMP seeks to establish a more effective paradigm for learning robotic manipulation policies that can generalize and perform reliably in diverse environments with limited task-specific supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLAMP operates as a 3D pre-training framework that leverages contrastive learning across three modalities: image, text, and robot action. The framework is designed to pre-train task-agnostic encoders and a Diffusion Policy, followed by fine-tuning the policy on specific tasks.

**A. Contrastive Pre-training of Encoders**

1. **Data Generation and Rendering**:
 - **Point Cloud Construction**: RGB-D images from multiple cameras are used, along with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, to construct a scene point cloud. RGB colors are omitted from the point cloud to enhance appearance generalization.
 - **Point Cloud Processing**: The point cloud undergoes 3D bounding-box cropping to remove points outside the workspace and voxel-grid downsampling to reduce density.
 - **Multi-View Re-rendering**: The processed point cloud is re-rendered into five virtual viewpoints, each producing a four-channel image (depth, X, Y, Z coordinates). These views include three fixed viewpoints (overhead, front-left, back-right) and two dynamic wrist views (one attached to each gripper, with a slight offset for improved visibility). The wrist views are chosen to reduce occlusion and provide clear observations for high-precision tasks.
2. **Model Architecture**:
 - **Image Encoder (f)**: A Vision Transformer (ViT) with 94M parameters, 12 layers, and 12 attention heads is used. It incorporates STRING relative positional encoding. For STRING, the 3D coordinates are computed as the average global XYZ of the point cloud points within each image patch. This allows for correlation between tokens from different views corresponding to nearby 3D regions. The five re-rendered views are tiled horizontally to form a single input image of dimensions 224 × 1120 × 4.
 - **Text Encoder (g)**: A CLIP text encoder with 277M parameters, 12 layers, and 12 attention heads processes tokenized text input (256 tokens). The text includes a high-level task description, each object's name, its normalized global coordinate frame position, and an integer indicating task progress. This privileged information is solely used during pre-training.
 - **Action Encoder (h)**: A standard Transformer encoder with 18M parameters, 2 layers, and 12 attention heads. Its input is a history of previous actions (A_t = {A_{t-1}..., A_{t-H}}), where H is the action chunk size.
 - **Output Embeddings**: Each encoder outputs a 768-dimensional embedding.
3. **Contrastive Loss**: The encoders are trained using a SigLIP-style loss across three modality pairs: image-text, image-action, and text-action. This loss minimizes the distance between embeddings of matched pairs (e.g., I_i, T_i) while maximizing the distance between mismatched pairs (e.g., I_i, T_j where i ≠ j). Learnable bias and temperature parameters are used to stabilize training. The total loss is the average of the losses from these three pairs. This encourages the learning of representations that capture mutual information across modalities.
4. **Theoretical Analysis of STRING**: The paper includes a theoretical analysis (Lemma 3.1 and 3.2) detailing how STRING relative positional encoding, applied with 3D point cloud coordinates, modulates attention scores. It suggests that STRING implicitly learns 3D structure by adjusting attention based on the 3D distance between the centers of mass of sub-point clouds corresponding to different image tokens, even from different views. This allows for the correlation of tokens representing spatially close regions in 3D space, irrespective of their 2D projection view.

**B. Diffusion Policy Pre-training and Fine-Tuning**

1. **Diffusion Policy Architecture**:
 - The visuomotor controller is based on the Diffusion Policy, which handles multimodal action distributions. It uses a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) formulation.
 - Architecture: A 192M-parameter Diffusion Policy featuring a 4-layer, 768-wide Transformer encoder and a 7-layer, 768-wide Transformer decoder.
 - Action Chunking: The policy predicts 50 subsequent actions (14 absolute target joint positions) as a chunk.
 - Inputs: RGB images from each camera (processed by separate ResNet-50 backbones pre-trained on ImageNet), robot proprioception features, and unpooled features from the *frozen* pre-trained CLAMP image and action encoders. Positional embeddings are added before feeding tokens to the encoder. The encoder latents are passed to the decoder along with a noised action chunk and diffusion timestep encoding.
2. **Pre-training and Fine-Tuning Strategy**:
 - **Pre-training**: The Diffusion Policy is pre-trained in parallel with the CLAMP encoders. For simulated tasks, it uses the same large-scale simulated dataset. For real-world tasks, it is pre-trained on real-world training data to mitigate the sim-to-real gap. During this stage, the policy is trained independently, without directly using frozen CLAMP embeddings (as the encoders are still learning).
 - **Fine-Tuning**: After pre-training, all weights of the Diffusion Policy (including ResNet backbones) are initialized from the pre-trained checkpoint. The CLAMP image and action encoders are *frozen* and used to extract embeddings that condition the Diffusion Policy. The policy is then fine-tuned on a limited set of task-specific demonstrations. This dual pre-training (encoders and policy) is posited as crucial for efficient and high-performance fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence from both simulated and real-world experiments, along with ablation studies and pre-training retrieval results, to support the effectiveness of CLAMP.

**A. Simulation Results**
- **Performance Improvement**: CLAMP-integrated methods, particularly "ALOHA Unleashed with CLAMP," consistently outperform all baselines across six simulated tasks (Can Opener in Caddy, Screwdriver in Caddy, Pen in Container, Mug on Plate, Plate on Rack, Drawer Open). For example, on "Screwdriver in Caddy," it achieved a 98.0% success rate, compared to 86.7% for "Pretrained ALOHA Unleashed" and 78.0% for "ALOHA Unleashed." Similar significant improvements are observed across other tasks, with gains ranging from 10% to over 30% in success rate compared to the best baselines.
- **Sample Efficiency**: Evaluation curves demonstrate that "ALOHA Unleashed with CLAMP" achieves strong performance at significantly earlier training stages, indicating enhanced sample efficiency during fine-tuning.
- **Full Pre-training Benefit**: Pre-training both the CLAMP encoders and the Diffusion Policy in parallel ("ALOHA Unleashed with CLAMP") yields superior fine-tuning performance compared to pre-training only the encoders ("ALOHA Unleashed with CLAMP Encoders Only").

**B. Real-World Results**
- **Outperformance on Most Tasks**: In real-world experiments, "ALOHA Unleashed with CLAMP" outperforms the baseline "ALOHA Unleashed" on four out of five tasks (Open Drawers, Close Drawers, Mug on Plate, Recycle Cans), while maintaining comparable performance on "Plate on Rack."
- **Specific Task Improvements**: For "Open Drawers" and "Close Drawers," CLAMP successfully completes both sub-tasks (opening/closing both drawers), where the baseline often struggles with the second drawer. On "Mug on Plate," noticeable improvements in grasping and placing the mug are observed. On "Recycle Cans," there were slight improvements in grasping. The authors hypothesize that tasks with comparable performance might be simpler or have less diverse training data, limiting the potential for large gains.

**C. Ablation Studies**
- **Importance of Dynamic Wrist Views**: Eliminating virtual wrist views resulted in performance degradation, particularly on high-precision tasks like "Pen in Container" (-22.0%) and "Mug on Plate" (-28.6%). The custom five-view setup (with dynamic wrist views) also significantly outperformed a standard orthogonal five-view setup.
- **Role of Text Encoder**: Including the text encoder during pre-training provided a minor benefit in simulation but showed a more noticeable improvement in real-world tasks. This is attributed to the text grounding being more valuable in the presence of real-world visual noise and texture variations.
- **Image Encoder Architecture**: Replacing the ViT image encoder with a point-cloud encoder (DP3) or removing STRING relative positional encoding from the ViT led to worse performance, indicating the efficacy of the multi-view, patch-based 3D coordinate-aware attention mechanism.
- **Encoder Freezing and Policy Pre-training**: Not pre-training the encoders or unfrozen encoders during fine-tuning resulted in substantial performance drops. This confirms the benefit of robust, frozen encoder representations and the importance of pre-training the policy.
- **Feature Aggregation**: Using unpooled features from the frozen CLAMP encoders (before MAP pooling) proved more effective than using pooled features.
- **Action Space**: Absolute Cartesian control performed similarly to absolute joint-space control, while relative Cartesian control led to worse performance.
- **Low-Data Regime**: While CLAMP improved learning efficiency and maximum success rate in low-data settings, its full benefits were most apparent with sufficiently large and diverse fine-tuning datasets, suggesting that data quantity still plays a role in maximizing its impact.

**D. Simulation Pre-training Results**
- **Cross-Modal Retrieval**: CLAMP encoders demonstrated high Recall@5 scores on six cross-modal retrieval tasks on validation data, especially in retrieving previous actions.
- **Generalization**: Visualizations of SigLIP pairwise matching probabilities for image-to-previous-action and previous-action-to-image retrieval on an unseen task showed correct retrievals across all examples, indicating that the learned representations generalize and transfer effectively to novel scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents CLAMP, a comprehensive 3D action-conditioned pre-training framework that demonstrates significant advancements in robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in 3D Perception for Robotics**: CLAMP moves beyond conventional 2D image representations by integrating multi-view 3D geometric and positional information. This is critical for tasks requiring precise spatial understanding, contributing to more robust and accurate robotic perception in complex physical environments.
2. **Bridging Perception and Action**: By contrastively pre-training image, text, and action encoders, CLAMP directly associates 3D visual states with robot action patterns and task descriptions. This foundational learning of visuomotor correlations can lead to policies that are more inherently grounded in how robots interact with the world.
3. **Improved Sample Efficiency and Performance**: The empirical results consistently demonstrate that CLAMP pre-training, coupled with parallel policy pre-training, substantially improves the sample efficiency and final success rates of manipulation policies across a range of simulated and real-world tasks. This addresses a key challenge in robotics: reducing the need for extensive, costly task-specific demonstrations.
4. **Novel Application of Relative Positional Encoding**: The work introduces a novel application of STRING relative positional encoding, using 3D coordinates derived from point clouds. This mechanism enables the image encoder to effectively correlate tokens across different camera views based on their actual 3D proximity, thereby enhancing spatial awareness within the transformer architecture.
5. **Importance of Dynamic Views**: The finding that dynamic wrist views are crucial for high-precision manipulation tasks highlights a practical design consideration for robotic camera setups and multi-view perception systems, suggesting an optimal strategy for providing clearer observations of target objects.
6. **Towards Generalizable Robotic Policies**: By learning robust, transferable representations through large-scale simulated data, CLAMP contributes to the development of more generalizable robotic policies. The ability of the pre-trained encoders to generalize to unseen tasks and improve real-world performance indicates a step towards foundation models for robotics that can adapt to diverse and novel scenarios.
7. **Outperformance of State-of-the-Art Baselines**: Consistently outperforming strong baselines, including advanced imitation learning methods like ALOHA Unleashed and ACT, CLAMP establishes a new benchmark for pre-training methods in robotic manipulation. This contributes a concrete, effective framework that researchers and practitioners can build upon.

In conclusion, CLAMP offers a significant contribution to robot learning by providing a principled approach to leverage 3D geometric information and action data for pre-training. Its demonstrated improvements in efficiency and performance have the potential to accelerate the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems.
