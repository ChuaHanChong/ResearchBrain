# Ovis2.5 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> Ovis2.5, developed by the Ovis Team at Alibaba Group, advances multimodal large language models by integrating native-resolution visual processing and reflection-oriented reasoning. The model achieved a state-of-the-art score of 78.3 on the OpenCompass leaderboard for open-source MLLMs under 40B parameters, demonstrating enhanced capabilities in fine-grained visual understanding and deep reasoning.

## Problem

- Fixed-resolution vision encoders in MLLMs lose fine-grained details and global context for high-resolution or visually dense content, like charts or documents.
- Current MLLMs often lack deep, reflective reasoning capabilities such as self-checking and revision, limiting performance on complex, multi-step problems.
- Structural misalignment exists between the continuous nature of visual embeddings and the discrete structure of textual embeddings in many MLLM architectures.

## Method

- Integrates a ==Native-Resolution Vision Transformer (NaViT)== to process images at their original, variable resolutions, preserving fine-grained details and global layout.
- Implements ==reflection-oriented training== to enable an optional 'thinking mode' at inference time, allowing for self-checking and iterative refinement of reasoning.
- Utilizes a learnable ==Visual Embedding Table (VET)== to structurally align visual and textual embeddings, building on the Ovis architecture, and upgrades the LLM backbone to Qwen3.
- Employs a comprehensive ==five-phase training curriculum== (pre-training and post-training with DPO and RL) on a large-scale, high-quality multimodal dataset, supported by an optimized, high-efficiency infrastructure.

## Results

- Ovis2.5-9B achieved an average score of **78.3** on the OpenCompass leaderboard, setting a new state-of-the-art for open-source MLLMs in the sub-40B parameter range.
- The models demonstrated leading capabilities in visual and structured mathematics, ranking first on MathVista (**83.4**) and WeMath (**66.7**), and showed strong performance on academic reasoning benchmarks like MMMU (**71.2**).
- Ovis2.5-9B outperformed all leading open-source competitors and GPT-4o on OCRBench v2 (**63.4** EN, **58.0** CN) and achieved an impressive average grounding score of **90.1** across RefCOCO datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Native-resolution vision processing combined with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) in a ViT significantly improves MLLMs' ability to perceive and reason about fine details and spatial layouts in dense visual content.
> - Explicitly training MLLMs for reflection and self-correction, including the use of '...' tags, enables deeper and more robust reasoning, extending beyond linear chain-of-thought.
> - The sustained 'small model, big performance' philosophy, exemplified by the Ovis2.5-2B model, shows that highly capable multimodal AI can be made accessible for resource-constrained environments.
> - A multi-stage, data-rich training curriculum, including DPO and RL with verifiable rewards, is crucial for progressively building strong perceptual and advanced reasoning skills in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has witnessed an explosion of innovation, with many powerful open-source models emerging and demonstrating impressive capabilities. The prevailing architectural paradigm typically involves connecting a pre-trained vision transformer (ViT) to a large language model (LLM) through a simple projection layer, such as a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).

The Ovis series distinguishes itself in this landscape by addressing a fundamental architectural challenge: the misalignment between the continuous nature of visual embeddings and the discrete structure of textual embeddings. Instead of a simple MLP projector, the original Ovis architecture introduced a **learnable Visual Embedding Table (VET)**. This design structurally aligns the visual and textual embeddings, a contribution that the Ovis team empirically validated as superior to conventional approaches. Ovis2.5 builds directly upon this established foundation, refining it with further architectural and training advancements.

The broader research landscape for MLLMs faces two primary challenges that Ovis2.5 directly targets:
1. **Limitations of Vision Front-Ends:** Many MLLMs rely on fixed-resolution vision encoders, which necessitate image tiling for high-resolution inputs. This tiling process can lead to a loss of fine-grained details and disrupt the global layout of an image, particularly detrimental for visually dense content like complex charts, diagrams, and technical documents. Ovis2.5 directly confronts this by integrating a **native-resolution vision transformer (NaViT)**, which processes images at their original, variable resolutions.
2. **Depth of Multimodal Reasoning:** While many MLLMs can perform linear chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, they often lack the capacity for deeper, reflective reasoning, such as self-checking and revision. This limitation hinders their performance on complex, multi-step problems that require internal evaluation and iterative refinement of solutions. Ovis2.5 addresses this by incorporating **reflection-oriented training**, enabling an optional "thinking mode" at inference time to enhance reasoning robustness.

By tackling these two core issues, Ovis2.5 pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities, particularly for open-source models. It directly competes with and often surpasses leading open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, GLM-4.1V, InternVL3, Keye-VL, MiMo-VL, MiniCPM-V) and aims to narrow the performance gap with advanced proprietary models like Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-4o. The release of two open-source models, Ovis2.5-9B and a more compact Ovis2.5-2B, further contributes to the democratization of advanced MLLM technology, making high-performance multimodal AI more accessible for diverse research and application scenarios, especially those with computational constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing Ovis2.5 stems from the identified shortcomings of its predecessor, Ovis2, specifically its struggles with tasks demanding deep reasoning and precise analysis of visually dense content. These challenges were attributed to two core issues:
1. **Perceptual Degradation from Fixed-Resolution Processing:** Ovis2, like many MLLMs, relied on fixed-resolution vision encoders. This design requires large or high-resolution images to be divided into smaller, fixed-size sub-images or patches, a process that can disrupt the global structure and discard crucial fine-grained details. Such degradation is particularly problematic for complex visuals such as charts, diagrams, and intricate documents, where both macro-level layout and micro-level textual/graphical elements are essential for accurate interpretation.
2. **Limited Reflective Reasoning:** The training schemes in previous models primarily emphasized linear chain-of-thought (CoT), which, while beneficial, lacked explicit supervision for reflective processes like self-checking and revision. This limitation meant that Ovis2 struggled with problems requiring deeper cognitive processes to evaluate and correct its own reasoning steps, leading to suboptimal performance on challenging analytical tasks.

Against this backdrop, the Ovis Team set forth several key objectives for Ovis2.5:
- **Enhance Native-Resolution Visual Perception:** To overcome the limitations of fixed-resolution encoders, a primary objective was to integrate a native-resolution vision transformer (NaViT). This would allow the model to process images at their original, variable resolutions, thereby preserving all fine details and the global layout critical for dense visual content.
- **Strengthen Multimodal Reasoning with Reflection:** A core objective was to move beyond linear CoT by training the model to perform reflection, including self-checking and revision. This capability would be exposed as an optional "thinking mode" at inference time, offering users a strategic trade-off between latency and enhanced accuracy for complex inputs.
- **Develop a Comprehensive Training Curriculum:** To progressively build foundational perception to advanced reasoning skills, the team aimed to devise a structured, multi-phase training strategy.
- **Achieve High-Efficiency Model Scaling:** Given the ambitious architectural and training upgrades, an objective was to develop an optimized infrastructure capable of scaling these processes efficiently, specifically targeting significant speedups in end-to-end training.
- **Establish State-of-the-Art Performance:** The overarching goal was to significantly improve upon Ovis2 and achieve state-of-the-art performance among open-source MLLMs, especially within the sub-40B parameter range, and to maintain the "small model, big performance" philosophy with a highly capable 2B parameter version.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Ovis2.5 builds upon the proven foundational architecture of the Ovis series, while introducing significant architectural and training enhancements to achieve its objectives.

#### 3.1. Architecture

The Ovis2.5 architecture retains three core modules:
- **Visual Tokenizer (VT):** A transformer-based component responsible for extracting features from image patches and projecting them onto a discrete vocabulary of "visual words," yielding a probabilistic visual token.
- **Visual Embedding Table (VET):** Analogous to a textual embedding table, this component stores dedicated embeddings for each visual word, crucially mitigating structural mismatches between visual and textual modalities. The final visual embedding is computed as the expected value of the VET's embeddings, weighted by the VT's probability distribution.
- **LLM:** A pre-trained large language model (LLM) that integrates the visual and textual embeddings to perform cross-modal understanding and generate textual outputs.

To further advance its multimodal capabilities, Ovis2.5 incorporates two key architectural enhancements:
- **Native-Resolution Processing:** The conventional fixed-resolution ViT is replaced with a **Native-Resolution ViT (NaViT)**. This allows Ovis2.5 to directly process images at their native, variable resolutions, eliminating the need for lossy tiling and preserving both fine-grained details and global context. To reinforce spatial awareness, particularly for high-resolution content like charts, NaViT integrates Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) into every ViT block. The NaViT is initialized from the weights of `siglip2-so400m-patch16-512`.
- **Upgraded LLM:** The backbone language model has been upgraded from Qwen2.5 to **Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025a)**. This upgrade leverages Qwen3's superior deep-reasoning capabilities, significantly boosting Ovis2.5's performance on complex tasks and overall multimodal proficiency.

#### 3.2. Model Training

The training of Ovis2.5 follows a comprehensive **five-phase curriculum**, divided into pre-training and post-training stages, each with specific objectives and data compositions.

**Pre-training Stage:**
1. **P1: VET Pre-training:** Focuses on training the Visual Embedding Table (VET) using a dataset of plain image-caption pairs. Most ViT parameters are frozen, with training limited to the final ViT layer, visual head, and VET. Images are resized to 448²-896² pixels, and dynamic position embedding interpolation is used as RoPE is initially disabled.
2. **P2: Multimodal Pre-training:** Transitions to full-parameter training of all modules (VT, VET, LLM). The data is expanded to include conversational data from OCR, image captioning, and visual grounding tasks. The resolution range is significantly expanded to 448²-1792² pixels, and RoPE is activated in every ViT block to enhance spatial awareness.
3. **P3: Multimodal Instruction Tuning:** Continues full-parameter training with an emphasis on following diverse multimodal instructions. The training corpus is enriched with text-only, multi-image, and video inputs, covering domains like general QA, multilingual dialogue, OCR, chart analysis, knowledge-based QA, STEM, and medical. Crucially, "thinking-style" samples with `<think>...</think>` tags are incorporated to cultivate reflective and self-corrective reasoning.

**Post-training Stage:**
1. **P1: Multimodal DPO:** The entire Ovis2.5 model (vision modules and LLM) undergoes full-parameter training using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023), augmented with an auxiliary Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) objective for stability. The DPO dataset comprises multimodal preference data for reasoning and general-purpose tasks, with verifiable scores for reasoning and MLLM-based scoring for general tasks.
2. **P2: Multimodal Reinforcement Learning:** Further enhances reasoning using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) on a Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) dataset. This dataset focuses on open-source mathematical problems, science QA, and visual QA with logical reasoning. To preserve general capabilities, only LLM parameters are updated, while vision modules are frozen.

#### 3.3. Data Composition

The training is underpinned by a large-scale, high-quality multimodal dataset combining public sources (COYO, Laion, Wukong, DataComp, SAM) with extensive in-house and web-sourced data. Specialized data pipelines ensure quality and diversity for:
- **OCR Data:** Documents, charts, posters, screenshots, with MLLM-generated annotations and QA pairs, followed by rigorous filtering.
- **Grounding Data:** Public datasets (RefCoCo) for direct instruction synthesis, complemented by an automated pipeline using SOTA detection models and MLLMs for scalable high-quality data generation.
- **Reasoning Data:** A corpus of vanilla CoT data (existing datasets + MLLM synthesis) and "thinking-style" data with `<think>...</think>` tags for reflection and self-correction, all cross-verified by multiple MLLMs.
- **DPO Data:** Multimodal preference data covering various modalities and task types, leveraging both verifiable scores and MLLM-based scoring.
- **RLVR Data:** Reasoning-centric data primarily focused on math, science, and visual QA, with a significant amount of information embedded in images. Multiple-choice questions are converted to fill-in-the-blank format to mitigate guessing.

#### 3.4. Infrastructure

To handle the computational demands, a high-efficiency infrastructure was developed, delivering a 3–4x end-to-end speedup:
- **Load Balancing via Data Packing:** Addresses computational load imbalance caused by varying data sizes. Multiple shorter samples are combined into longer sequences during preprocessing, minimizing padding, reducing wasted computation, and balancing GPU workloads.
- **Hybrid Parallelism Framework:** Based on Megatron, this framework combines Data Parallelism (DP), Tensor Parallelism (TP), and Context Parallelism (CP) to effectively reduce memory footprint and significantly improve training throughput for large-scale model training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Ovis2.5 demonstrates exceptional capabilities across a broad spectrum of multimodal tasks, establishing new state-of-the-art (SOTA) results for open-source MLLMs at its scale.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **OpenCompass Leaderboard:** Ovis2.5-9B achieved an average score of **78.3**, setting a new SOTA among open-source MLLMs in the sub-40B parameter range. This represents a substantial improvement over its predecessor, Ovis2-8B (71.8).
- **Compact Model Performance:** Ovis2.5-2B, continuing the "small model, big performance" philosophy, scored **73.9** on OpenCompass, establishing SOTA for open-source MLLMs of comparable size.
- The models showed robust performance on general benchmarks like MMBench and MMStar, and significantly narrowed the performance gap with frontier proprietary models such as Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-4o.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Reasoning

- Ovis2.5 excels across a comprehensive suite of reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, MMMU-Pro, MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, LogicVista, WeMath, DynaMath).
- **Ovis2.5-9B** achieved leading capabilities in visual and structured mathematics, ranking first on MathVista (83.4) and WeMath (66.7), and consistently among the top two on other math-focused benchmarks. It also scored strongly on general academic reasoning benchmarks, with 71.2 on MMMU and 54.4 on the more challenging MMMU-Pro.
- **Ovis2.5-2B** delivered SOTA results within its size category, showing substantial gains over comparable open-source models across both general reasoning and math-oriented tasks.

#### 4.3. OCR & Chart Analysis

- **OCRBench v2:** Ovis2.5-9B achieved 63.4 on OCRv2-EN and 58.0 on OCRv2-CN, outperforming all leading open-source competitors and even the proprietary GPT-4o. Ovis2.5-2B also showed strong performance at 60.6 (EN) and 54.0 (CN).
- **Complex Chart Analysis:** On ChartQA Pro, which includes diverse and complex visualizations, Ovis2.5-9B scored 63.8, while Ovis2.5-2B scored 53.1. Across ChartQA, DocVQA, and TextVQA, Ovis2.5-9B consistently demonstrated superior performance, achieving the top average score among its open-source peers.

#### 4.4. Grounding

- Ovis2.5 achieved an impressive average grounding score of **90.1** across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, establishing SOTA among compared open-source models.
- The model demonstrated a pronounced advantage on the highly demanding RefCOCOg dataset (val: 90.3, test: 90.1), which involves complex descriptions of non-salient objects, showcasing its advanced visual-linguistic understanding.

#### 4.5. Multi-Image and Video Understanding

- **Multi-Image:** Ovis2.5-9B achieved 67.3 on BLINK and 69.3 on MMT-Bench, outperforming other prominent models in cross-image reasoning.
- **Video Understanding:** On a diverse set of video benchmarks (VideoMME, MVBench, MLVU, TempCompass), Ovis2.5 maintained strong and consistent performance, demonstrating advanced ability to capture and reason over both spatial and temporal dynamics.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Examples

The report includes several qualitative examples showcasing Ovis2.5's abilities across various domains:
- **World Knowledge:** Accurately identifying plant species and geographic locations with detailed reasoning.
- **Math Problem Solving:** Demonstrating step-by-step solutions for combinatorial patterns and complex geometry problems, often utilizing the "thinking mode" for explicit reasoning paths.
- **Scientific Question Answering:** Solving physics problems (e.g., refraction and reflection) and biology problems (e.g., genetic inheritance patterns) with detailed derivations.
- **OCR:** Precisely extracting structured information from nutritional labels and documents, and performing word-level text localization with bounding box coordinates.
- **Chart Analysis:** Effectively performing conditional searches, comparative analyses, and multi-step reasoning across panels of charts.
- **Grounding:** Accurately locating small objects, referring objects, providing grounded captions, and performing point-level grounding.
- **Multi-Images:** Recognizing art styles and solving jigsaw challenges.
- **Video:** Describing collision processes and providing detailed narrative descriptions of video content.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Ovis2.5 represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal large language models, with profound significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Core Advancements and Significance

- **Revolutionizing Visual Perception:** The integration of a **native-resolution vision encoder (NaViT)** is a critical breakthrough. It directly addresses the long-standing limitation of fixed-resolution encoders that degrade fine details and global context, particularly for complex visuals. This allows Ovis2.5 to achieve superior analysis of visually dense content like charts, diagrams, and documents, moving MLLMs closer to human-like visual understanding.
- **Pioneering Deep Reasoning with Reflection:** The introduction of an optional **"thinking mode"** powered by reflection-oriented training is a major step beyond linear chain-of-thought. By enabling the model to perform self-checking and revision, Ovis2.5 tackles complex tasks with greater robustness and accuracy. This provides a practical mechanism for users to explicitly trade latency for enhanced reliability on challenging inputs, which is invaluable for high-stakes applications.
- **Setting New Open-Source Benchmarks:** Ovis2.5's state-of-the-art performance on the OpenCompass leaderboard for open-source MLLMs (especially in the sub-40B parameter class) and its leading results across STEM, OCR, chart analysis, grounding, and video benchmarks demonstrate a significant leap in capabilities. This elevates the standard for what is achievable with publicly available models, fostering further research and development in the open-source community.
- **Democratizing Advanced MLLMs:** The release of the compact **Ovis2.5-2B model** (achieving SOTA for its size) is particularly impactful. It extends the "small model, big performance" philosophy, making powerful multimodal AI more accessible for resource-constrained environments, edge computing, and on-device applications, thereby broadening the reach and utility of MLLMs.
- **Boosting Training Efficiency:** The optimized training infrastructure, featuring multimodal data packing and hybrid parallelism, provides a **3–4x end-to-end speedup**. This efficiency gain is crucial for accelerating the research cycle, reducing the computational cost of developing large models, and making iterative improvements more feasible for researchers and developers.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Real-World Applications:** Ovis2.5's improved perception and reasoning capabilities can significantly impact various domains. In healthcare, it could lead to more accurate analysis of medical images and complex patient records. In finance and business, it could enable sophisticated interpretation of financial charts, reports, and infographics. For scientific research, it offers a powerful tool for understanding diagrams, experimental results, and complex data visualizations.
- **Advancing Human-AI Interaction:** The model's ability to engage in deeper, reflective reasoning could lead to more intelligent and trustworthy AI assistants that can not only answer questions but also explain their reasoning, self-correct, and provide more robust solutions.
- **Accelerating Open-Source Innovation:** By releasing high-performing models and detailing advanced training methodologies, Alibaba Group contributes significantly to the open-source AI ecosystem. This encourages other researchers to build upon Ovis2.5's innovations, fostering collective progress in multimodal AI.
- **Shaping Future MLLM Development:** The architectural choices, particularly the native-resolution vision and reflection-based reasoning, are likely to influence future MLLM designs, setting new directions for tackling the challenges of complex visual and cognitive tasks.

#### 5.3. Future Directions

The authors acknowledge several promising avenues for future work, indicating ongoing research efforts:
1. **Scaling Perception to 4K-level Images:** Further extending native-resolution perception to even higher resolutions like 4K, which is crucial for extremely detailed visual analysis.
2. **Richer Temporal Reasoning for Long Videos:** Enhancing the model's ability to process and reason over extended video inputs, capturing complex temporal dynamics and relationships.
3. **Tighter Integration of Tool Use:** Developing more sophisticated mechanisms for integrating external tools to enable action-augmented reasoning, expanding the model's capacity for interactive problem-solving and task execution.

In conclusion, Ovis2.5 represents a robust and carefully engineered MLLM that significantly pushes the envelope for open-source capabilities. Its innovations in native-resolution perception and deep reasoning, coupled with efficient training, position it as a foundational work that will likely inspire and enable a new generation of more capable and accessible multimodal AI systems.
