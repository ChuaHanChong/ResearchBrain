# PinchBot: Long-Horizon Deformable Manipulation with Guided Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed PinchBot, a robotic system capable of long-horizon pinch pottery using a goal-conditioned diffusion policy. The system achieved complex pottery shapes, with PointBERT-based policies requiring approximately half the actions compared to other variants, while maintaining high accuracy in goal achievement.

## Problem

- Deformable object manipulation presents challenges due to complex state representation, self-occlusion, non-linear state changes, and the long-horizon nature of many tasks.
- Many existing deformable shaping tasks simplify the problem by involving a limited number of actions or simpler deformation dynamics, not fully testing systems in truly long-horizon, multimodal scenarios.
- Pinch pottery, specifically, is a difficult task characterized by high multimodality, the absence of a fixed action order, and the need for intricate spatial and rotational reasoning.

## Method

- **PinchBot** utilizes a ==Franka Emika== robot with custom soft fingertips and a multi-camera perception system for 3D point cloud state representation of the clay.
- A goal-conditioned diffusion policy is employed, integrating pre-trained 3D point cloud embeddings (PointBERT or a modified PointNet) and novel task progress guidance to inform action generation.
- A collision action projection mechanism prevents catastrophic failures by constraining generated robot actions to safe regions within the clay's estimated diameter.

## Results

- PointBERT-based policies consistently outperformed DP3 PointNet variants across metrics, requiring approximately half the number of actions to complete pottery shaping tasks.
- Task progress guidance improved the adaptability and behavior of DP3 PointNet policies across multiple bowl diameters and qualitatively enhanced PointBERT's control over wall angles.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of pre-training, collision projection, and the diffusion policy framework in achieving high accuracy and robustness for the long-horizon pinch pottery task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained 3D point cloud embeddings, particularly PointBERT, are critical for robust performance in long-horizon deformable manipulation, leading to superior action efficiency and task awareness.
> - Task progress guidance effectively improves policy adaptability and expressiveness, enabling finer control over deformable object properties, such as pottery wall angles.
> - Collision projection is essential for robust and consistent robotic execution, preventing physically harmful or irrecoverable actions during sensitive interactions with deformable materials.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects presents significant challenges in robotics due to complexities in state representation, self-occlusion, non-linear state changes resulting from interaction, and the often long-horizon nature of the tasks. Existing research in deformable object manipulation has addressed various aspects, but many studies focus on tasks that, while challenging, involve a relatively limited number of actions or simpler deformation dynamics.

Previous works in clay shaping, for instance, have explored tasks such as creating alphabet shapes or rolling, folding, and cutting dough. These tasks typically require fewer than 10 actions, simplifying planning and policy learning by reducing the optimization horizon for trajectories and the length of action sequences a policy must learn. While these contributions are valuable, they may not fully test the capabilities of robotic systems in truly long-horizon scenarios where a continuous sequence of precise interactions is required over an extended period.

Point cloud-based behavior cloning, particularly with diffusion policies, has shown promise in various manipulation applications, including some deformable shaping tasks. Researchers have demonstrated the effectiveness of point cloud observations in improving behavior cloning systems and have incorporated large transformer models like PointBERT or modified PointNet architectures. However, these applications often involve tasks where state changes are less complex (e.g., symmetrical deformation in wheel-based pottery) or where the long-horizon aspect is not as pronounced as in freehand sculpting.

This work addresses the gap by focusing on "pinch pottery," a task characterized by its inherently long-horizon nature and high multimodality. Unlike wheel-based pottery, where rotational symmetry simplifies the policy's complexity by applying end-effector actions symmetrically to the clay, pinch pottery requires intricate spatial and rotational reasoning. The absence of an explicit, fixed order of actions that guarantees success, with many combinations leading to the same goal, further amplifies the challenge. By tackling pinch pottery, this research aims to evaluate and benchmark learning frameworks on a task that more rigorously tests their ability to handle complex, long-horizon, and multimodal deformable manipulation. It seeks to push the boundaries of current methods and highlight performance distinctions that might not be apparent in shorter or less complex tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic system capable of creating simple pottery shapes using only pinch-based actions. This "pinch pottery" task serves as a challenging testbed for exploring difficulties inherent in highly multimodal and long-horizon deformable manipulation.

The specific motivations driving this work are as follows:

- **Addressing Long-Horizon Deformable Manipulation**: The authors argue that pinch pottery represents a "true long-horizon" task, requiring a protracted sequence of actions to achieve a goal. This contrasts with many existing deformable shaping tasks that, while called "long-horizon," often involve fewer than 10 actions. By focusing on pinch pottery, the research aims to better evaluate and develop robotic systems that can reliably handle extended manipulation sequences of deformable objects.
- **Navigating High Multimodality**: The pinch pottery task is characterized by its multimodality; numerous combinations of pinch actions can lead to the desired final shape, yet not all action sequences are effective. This presents a challenge for policy learning, as the system must learn to generate diverse yet effective trajectories. The authors specifically aim to assess how well diffusion policy models, known for their ability to handle multimodality, perform in this context.
- **Developing a Goal-Conditioned, Adaptable Policy**: A key objective is to create a single, goal-conditioned policy that can adapt its sculpting sequences to successfully produce a range of final pottery goals (e.g., varying diameters). This moves beyond policies trained for a single, fixed goal, enhancing the versatility and practical applicability of the robotic system.
- **Investigating Impact of Design Choices**: The research is motivated by a desire to understand how specific methodological choices influence performance in long-horizon deformable manipulation. This includes:
 - **Pre-trained 3D Point Cloud Embeddings**: Exploring the effectiveness and behavioral differences between different point cloud embedding models (PointBERT and DP3 PointNet) when pre-trained on a large dataset (ShapeNet) and fine-tuned for the task.
 - **Task Guidance Mechanisms**: Evaluating the impact of different forms of guidance, specifically task-progress prediction (predicting percentage along trajectory) and sub-goal conditioning, on the policy's ability to complete long-horizon tasks.
 - **Collision-Constrained Action Projection**: Developing and assessing a mechanism to prevent catastrophic failures (e.g., punching a hole in the clay) by constraining robot actions to safe regions, thereby improving policy robustness and consistency.
- **Contributing a Public Dataset**: To facilitate further research in this challenging domain, the authors are motivated to provide a public dataset of human demonstrations collected using kinesthetic teaching on a Franka robot. This dataset aims to serve as a resource for the community and enable comparative studies.
- **Benchmarking for Future Work**: Ultimately, the work aims to advocate for and establish pinch pottery as a benchmark task. The authors suggest that evaluating methods on such complex tasks can better reveal nuanced performance differences between approaches compared to simpler, shorter-horizon goals like alphabet shaping.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PinchBot system is designed to perform long-horizon pinch-based pottery using a goal-conditioned diffusion policy. The methodology encompasses task definition, specialized hardware, a sophisticated perception pipeline, extensive data collection, and several key algorithmic components.

**Task Definition:**
The pinch pottery task is defined as transforming an initial clay cylinder of varying dimensions into a target 3D pottery shape (represented by a point cloud) through a sequence of pinch actions. Each pinch action is parameterized by 8 dimensions: end-effector position and orientation (x, y, z, R_x, R_y, R_z), the final distance between fingertips (d_ee), and a termination parameter (γ). The γ parameter indicates whether an action is final (γ=1) or non-final (γ=-1), enabling autonomous stopping conditions. Point cloud states are captured before and after each pinch action.

**Hardware Setup and Finger Design:**
The system utilizes a Franka Emika robotic arm. The workspace includes four fixed Intel RealSense D415 cameras and an additional end-effector mounted camera to provide a comprehensive view, particularly of the pot's interior. To improve the quality of the sculpted pottery, custom fingertips were designed. These consist of 3D-printed concave and convex "bones" cast in soft EcoFlex "skin." This design aims to create smoother, curved surfaces and reduce harsh indentations often associated with robotic pinch shaping. The asymmetrical nature of the fingers necessitates considering the full 360° rotation for the R_z component of the action, unlike some prior works that simplified rotational space.

**Perception System:**
3D point clouds are used as the state representation. The perception pipeline involves:
1. **Multi-Camera Capture**: Point clouds are captured from all five cameras (four fixed, one end-effector mounted).
2. **Coordinate Transformation**: Each point cloud is transformed into the robot's coordinate frame using extrinsic calibration matrices. For the end-effector camera, the current end-effector pose is also incorporated.
3. **Clay Isolation**: Clay points are isolated from the scene using a combination of position and color-based thresholding.
4. **Alignment and Downsampling**: Iterative Closest Point (ICP) is used to fine-tune the alignment between the fixed camera views and the end-effector camera, compensating for proprioceptive errors. The combined point cloud is then uniformly downsampled to a fixed size of 2048 points.

**Data Collection:**
A demonstration dataset of 20 real-world pinch pottery trajectories was collected using kinesthetic teaching on the Franka robot.
- **Variations**: Initial clay cylinder dimensions varied (5 to 8 cm height), and target pottery bowl diameters ranged from 7 to 12 cm. Due to constant clay volume, larger diameters correlated with angled walls, while smaller ones correlated with vertical walls.
- **Trajectory Lengths**: Demonstrations contained between 21 and 31 individual pinch actions.
- **Data Augmentation**: To expand the dataset, a rotation transformation about the z-axis was applied to both the point cloud and actions in 2° increments, resulting in a total of 3600 trajectories for training.

**PinchBot Algorithmic Components:**
The PinchBot method integrates several components built around a goal-conditioned diffusion policy:

- **Point Cloud Embedding Pre-Training**:
 - **Models Compared**: The study compares two point cloud embedding models: PointBERT and a modified PointNet version (referred to as DP3 PointNet).
 - **Pre-training Task**: Both models are pre-trained on the ShapeNet reconstruction task to leverage large-scale 3D data and overcome limitations of small real-world datasets.
 - **Fine-tuning**: After pre-training, the models are fine-tuned on the diffusion policy objective, with a 3-layer MLP head added to project the point cloud embedding into a 512-dimensional latent space.

- **Goal Conditioning**:
 - To enable a single policy to adapt to multiple pottery goals, the target goal point cloud is embedded using the chosen point cloud model. This latent goal embedding is then concatenated with the current state embedding and the previous action. This concatenated vector conditions the iterative denoising process of the action trajectory in the diffusion policy.

- **Subgoal Diffusion Policy (Exploration)**:
 - As an alternative guidance mechanism, the policy is conditioned not just on the final goal, but also on a sequence of `N` intermediate latent sub-goals, along with the current state and previous action. `N` is determined by the action prediction horizon and sub-goal step size (e.g., for a 16-action horizon and 8-step size, N=2). This explores if intermediate state guidance aids long-horizon action generation.

- **Task Progress Guided Diffusion Policy (Proposed Guidance)**:
 - This strategy introduces a simple method for the model to predict its progress along a trajectory. The termination parameter γ is modified to predict a continuous value between -1 (first state) and 1 (final state), representing the percentage of completion. This guides the policy's action generation based on its perceived progress.

- **Collision Action Projection**:
 - To prevent physically harmful or irrecoverable actions (e.g., puncturing the clay wall), a collision detection and projection framework is implemented.
 - **Collision Detection**: A circle is fitted to the current state point cloud by finding the diameter that encloses more than 95% of the points projected into the x,y plane.
 - **Action Projection**: If a generated action's x,y position falls within this estimated clay diameter, its position is projected onto the edge of the circle. This modification only affects the x,y coordinates, preserving the z-component, rotations, and fingertip distance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated PinchBot's performance across different point cloud embedding models, task guidance mechanisms, and through an ablation study. Evaluation metrics included Chamfer Distance (CD) and Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) between the final and goal point clouds, and the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the goal and final pottery diameters. Each variant underwent three real-world runs.

**Impact of Point Cloud Embeddings:**
- **Quantitative Performance**: PointBERT-based policies consistently outperformed DP3 PointNet variants across all quantitative metrics (CD, EMD, MSE) and target bowl diameters (8cm, 10cm, 12cm) (Table I).
- **Action Efficiency**: PointBERT policies required significantly fewer actions to complete the tasks, approximately half the number of actions compared to DP3 PointNet policies, which often took around twice as many actions as the human demonstrations (Table II). This indicates PointBERT's superior ability to determine when a task region was complete and when to terminate.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Qualitatively, DP3 PointNet policies exhibited difficulties in discerning when specific regions of the pot were adequately smoothed or completed, often executing additional, unnecessary pinches. In contrast, PointBERT policies demonstrated better task progress awareness.
- **Latent Space Analysis**: T-SNE visualizations of the latent space (Figure 6) supported these findings, showing a more distinct separation and progression along trajectories in the PointBERT latent space compared to DP3 PointNet, which lacked clear distinctions for trajectory progress.

**Impact of Sub-Goals and Task Guidance:**
- **DP3 PointNet Improvement**: For DP3 PointNet, task progress guidance notably improved policy behavior and adaptability across multiple bowl diameters (Table I).
- **PointBERT Nuances**: For PointBERT, task progress guidance showed minimal quantitative impact, with results falling within the standard deviation of binary prediction variants. However, qualitatively, it improved the policy's ability to differentiate R_x (x-axis rotation) end-effector variations based on the goal point cloud, which directly influences the pottery wall angle. This led to a more expressive policy capable of better adapting wall angles (e.g., for 8cm diameter bowls, producing near-vertical walls as in demonstrations). The binary prediction PointBERT policy achieved similar goal diameters primarily through accurate positional variations.
- **Sub-Goal Policy Limitations**: The sub-goal guided diffusion policy encountered difficulties, particularly with the largest 12cm bowl diameter. This was hypothesized to be due to training data distribution, where intermediate states for larger bowls might resemble final states of smaller diameter bowls, causing ambiguity when only conditioned on sub-goals without the explicit final goal. Its latent space visualization also showed poor separation along trajectory progress and final pottery diameters (Figure 6).

**Ablation Study (on 10cm goal):**
An ablation study examined the individual contributions of pre-training, collision projection, and the diffusion policy training itself.
- **Pre-training**: Removing pre-training for both PointBERT and DP3 PointNet resulted in increased CD, EMD, and MSE metrics (Table III), indicating its importance for the policies to correctly identify goal completion.
- **Collision Projection**: Disabling collision projection led to higher errors (CD, EMD, MSE) for both models, demonstrating its role in improving robustness and preventing irrecoverable states.
- **Diffusion Policy-Styled Training**: Replacing diffusion policy training with a direct regression approach (predicting actions directly) severely degraded performance, resulting in significantly higher error metrics for both PointBERT and DP3 PointNet. This confirms the critical role of diffusion policy in handling the multimodal nature of the action space.

**Summary of Key Findings:**
The research established that PointBERT with pre-training generally yields superior performance in terms of accuracy and action efficiency for long-horizon pinch pottery. Task progress guidance proves beneficial for improving the adaptability and expressiveness of policies, particularly for DP3 PointNet and qualitatively for PointBERT. Collision action projection is essential for robust and consistent execution, preventing catastrophic failures. Finally, the diffusion policy framework itself is fundamental for learning effective policies in this multimodal, long-horizon task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents PinchBot, a robotic system that successfully tackles the long-horizon, multimodal task of pinch pottery, a domain historically challenging for robotic manipulation. The findings have several implications for the field of robotics and deformable object manipulation.

**Advancement in Deformable Manipulation:** The work demonstrates the feasibility of learning complex, goal-conditioned policies for highly deformable objects over extended horizons. By successfully addressing the pinch pottery task, PinchBot provides a concrete example of a robotic system performing intricate shaping operations without explicit tool use or simplifying assumptions like rotational symmetry. This pushes the boundary of what can be achieved with current imitation learning frameworks.

**Importance of Representation and Guidance:** The study highlights the critical role of pre-trained 3D point cloud embeddings, particularly PointBERT, in achieving robust performance in deformable manipulation. The superior performance of PointBERT over DP3 PointNet, especially in terms of action efficiency and task awareness, underscores the value of rich, pre-trained visual representations for understanding complex 3D states. Furthermore, the effectiveness of task-progress guidance for adapting policies to varying goals and improving qualitative behavior (e.g., wall angle control) suggests a promising direction for designing more expressive and controllable manipulation policies.

**Enhanced Robustness through Collision Projection:** The development and validation of collision-constrained action projection is a significant contribution to practical robotic deployment. By preventing catastrophic actions that could lead to irrecoverable states, this mechanism enhances the reliability and safety of deformable manipulation systems, which are inherently prone to sensitive interactions.

**New Benchmark for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The authors propose pinch pottery as a "true long-horizon" benchmark task. By openly providing a dataset of human demonstrations, this work enables future research to directly compare and evaluate new methods on a more challenging and realistic deformable manipulation problem than many existing benchmarks. This can foster the development of more generalizable and capable robotic systems by identifying nuanced performance differences that are not apparent in simpler tasks.

**Potential for Artistic and Functional Applications:** While current capabilities are focused on simple pottery goals, the success of PinchBot lays groundwork for robotic systems capable of more intricate artistic or functional shaping tasks involving soft materials like clay, dough, or even biological tissues. Such capabilities could find applications in manufacturing, culinary arts, medical procedures, or customized fabrication.

**Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as generalizability to a wider range of goals or materials, point towards fruitful avenues for future research. Integrating dynamics prediction into policy learning, creating substantially larger and more diverse datasets, or leveraging techniques like latent space reinforcement learning for rapid policy adaptation could further extend the capabilities of deformable manipulation systems building upon the PinchBot framework.
