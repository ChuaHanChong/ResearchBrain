# ViTacWorld: Scaling Visuo-Tactile World Models for Contact-Rich Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ShanghaiTech University developed ViTacWorld, an action-conditioned visuo-tactile world model, to generate synchronized visuo-tactile-action trajectories for contact-rich robot manipulation. The framework integrates diverse real and task-aligned simulated data, resulting in downstream tactile policies with improved real-robot success rates, for example, increasing from 42.5% to 67.5% on average for a specific policy.

## Problem

- Contact-rich robot manipulation tasks often fail with vision alone due to occlusions, tight tolerances, and subtle force dynamics, necessitating tactile sensing.
- High-quality, synchronized visuo-tactile-action data is scarce and expensive to collect from real robots, limiting the scalability of tactile learning.
- Existing action-conditioned world models are primarily visual, lacking the capability to generate physically meaningful tactile trajectories essential for contact-rich tasks.

## Method

- **ViTacWorld** is an ==action-conditioned visuo-tactile world model== that predicts future visual and tactile observations based on current states and robot actions.
- Its architecture uses a ==view-aware Diffusion Transformer== with ==stream identity embeddings== and ==cross-view attention== to process and generate synchronized multimodal observations (multiple visual views and tactile).
- The model is trained via a ==two-stage pipeline==: pretraining on large-scale public real and ==task-aligned simulated data==, then finetuning with limited real-world expert demonstrations and policy rollouts.

## Results

- Augmenting real expert demonstrations with ViTacWorld-generated rollouts increased the average real-robot success rate for tactile policies (e.g., from **42.5%** to **67.5%** for "π 0.5" + tactile policies).
- The full ViTacWorld model significantly outperformed ablated versions in generating high-quality visual and tactile observations, as measured by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on held-out real data.
- ViTacWorld serves as a lightweight policy evaluator, showing an average **10** percentage point gap between predicted and real success rates, and iterative data augmentation further boosted policy performance (e.g., from **67.5%** to **80.0%** for "π 0.5" + tactile policies).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging task-aligned simulated data, where scenes and sensors are meticulously matched to real systems, effectively reduces the sim-to-real gap for tactile observations.
> - A specialized view-aware Diffusion Transformer, incorporating stream identity embeddings and explicit cross-view attention, enables robust and consistent generation of multimodal visuo-tactile sequences.
> - Synthetically generated, plausible visuo-tactile rollouts can serve as effective data augmentation, improving both broad visual-motor generalization and fine tactile-guided adjustments for downstream robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly tasks involving physical contact, often encounters challenges when relying solely on visual information due to occlusions, constrained interfaces, and subtle force-dependent dynamics. Tactile sensing has emerged as a crucial modality for these "contact-rich" tasks, directly capturing physical interaction cues at the point of contact. However, a significant bottleneck in scaling visuo-tactile robot learning is the scarcity and high cost of obtaining large volumes of task-relevant, synchronized real visuo-tactile-action trajectories. Real-robot teleoperation with tactile sensors is expensive, slow, and constrained by hardware limitations, while existing robot-free teaching systems still require dedicated tactile hardware and may introduce embodiment or viewpoint gaps.

Simulation offers a scalable alternative, with systems like TacSL, TacEx, and UniVTAC enabling visuo-tactile sensor simulation and data collection. Tactile signals, being grounded in local contact geometry and force response, are considered promising for simulation, potentially exhibiting a smaller sim-to-real gap compared to purely visual observations. Nevertheless, simulated contact-rich data can still be limited by task diversity, asset fidelity, and the accuracy of contact modeling, making many real-world interactions difficult to reproduce faithfully.

Recent advancements in video foundation models and action-conditioned robot world models have shown strong capabilities in generating future observations given robot actions. These models typically focus on visual prediction and data generation. Prior visuo-tactile world-modeling work has demonstrated that tactile observations can enhance the physical consistency of imagined contact dynamics, but these models have not been primarily designed as scalable data generators for *downstream tactile policies*.

ViTacWorld positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenge of data scarcity for contact-rich visuo-tactile learning. It aims to bridge the gap between large-scale generative models and contact-rich manipulation by developing an action-conditioned world model that jointly predicts both visual and tactile feedback. Unlike previous works that focus on how policies consume tactile observations or integrate tactile prediction directly into the policy, ViTacWorld focuses on generating additional, task-relevant visuo-tactile data that can be used to *train and improve downstream tactile policies*. It combines diverse data sources—public real tactile datasets, simulation-generated interaction data, and real-world policy rollouts—within a unified framework to overcome the limitations of any single data source and provide a reusable data generator for tactile robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a framework for scalable visuo-tactile robot learning, specifically targeting contact-rich manipulation tasks. The authors propose ViTacWorld, an action-conditioned visuo-tactile world model, to achieve this.

The key objectives are articulated through the following contributions:
1. **Framework Development**: To propose ViTacWorld, an action-conditioned visuo-tactile world-modeling framework that enables the generation of temporally aligned visuo-tactile-action rollouts for contact-rich robot manipulation. This work aims to establish the first world-model-based framework specifically for robot visuo-tactile-action trajectory generation.
2. **Scalable Training Pipeline**: To develop a training pipeline that scales visuo-tactile world modeling by integrating public visuo-tactile-action data, task-aligned simulation-generated visuo-tactile interactions, and real-world policy rollout finetuning.
3. **Policy Improvement and Evaluation**: To demonstrate that ViTacWorld can both improve downstream tactile policies through synthetic rollout augmentation and support action-conditioned policy evaluation by predicting visuo-tactile outcomes under controlled action sequences.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges in contact-rich robot manipulation:
- **Inadequacy of Vision Alone**: Many complex manipulation tasks (e.g., insertion, peeling) require precise physical interaction cues that are often invisible or difficult to infer from camera-based vision alone due to occlusion, tight tolerances, or subtle force dynamics. Tactile sensing is critical for these scenarios.
- **Data Scarcity for Tactile Learning**: High-quality, synchronized visuo-tactile-action data is difficult and expensive to collect at scale from real robots. Tactile sensors can be costly, have limited lifespan, and demonstrations of contact-rich tasks are often hard to reliably execute.
- **Limitations of Simulation**: While simulation offers scalability, existing tactile simulators still face limitations in task diversity, asset fidelity, contact modeling accuracy, and the sim-to-real gap, particularly for everyday tasks involving deformable objects or complex frictional interactions.
- **Lack of Generative Tools for Tactile Data**: Current action-conditioned robot world models, despite advances in video generation, are primarily visual. They are insufficient for contact-rich manipulation where tactile feedback is essential and contact states are weakly observable from cameras. There is a need for generative models that can synthesize physically meaningful visuo-tactile trajectories.
- **Leveraging Tactile Properties**: The observation that tactile signals are directly grounded in physical contact and may exhibit a smaller simulation-to-real gap than purely visual observations motivates the use of simulated tactile feedback as a promising source for scaling data.

By addressing these points, ViTacWorld aims to provide a scalable and robust solution for training and evaluating robot policies in contact-rich environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViTacWorld is designed as an action-conditioned visuo-tactile world model that predicts future visual and tactile observations based on current observations and a sequence of robot actions. The framework extends existing action-conditioned robot video world models to incorporate tactile sensing.

**Problem Formulation:**
Given an initial multimodal observation $o_t = \{I_v^t\}_{v \in V}$, where $V = \{\text{main, wrist, tactile}\}$ represents external camera, wrist camera, and image-like tactile streams, and an H-step robot action sequence $u_{t:t+H-1}$, ViTacWorld predicts the future observations $\hat{o}_{t+1:t+H}$. A view-presence mask $m$ is used to handle heterogeneous training data with varying view availability.

**Action-Conditioned Visuo-Tactile World Model Architecture:**
The model is built upon a pretrained action-conditioned robot video world model, preserving its backbone and action-conditioning mechanism.
- **Multimodal Encoding**: For each time step, observations from the main camera, wrist camera, and tactile sensor are separately encoded into latent tokens using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) encoder. These latent tokens are then concatenated to form a unified visuo-tactile sequence.
- **View-Aware Diffusion Transformer (DiT)**: The core generative component is an adapted Diffusion Transformer, which is made aware of the source stream of each latent token.
 - **Stream Identity Embeddings**: Stream-specific identity embeddings ($e_v$) are introduced for each visual and tactile stream. These embeddings are projected and incorporated into the AdaLN (Adaptive Layer Normalization) modulation pathway, alongside timestep and action conditioning, allowing the DiT to differentiate between modalities.
 - **Stream-Aware Self-Attention**: Within each DiT block, self-attention is first applied *independently within each stream*, modulated by the stream-aware conditioning. This prevents immediate uncontrolled mixing of tokens from different modalities.
 - **Cross-View Attention**: Following intra-stream attention, an explicit cross-view attention module facilitates information exchange *between different streams*. This design ensures cross-modal consistency, allowing the tactile stream to share contact information with the visual streams while maintaining structured interaction.
- **Action Conditioning**: The action sequence is embedded via the inherited action-conditioning pathway and injected into the DiT using the AdaLN modulation. This conditioning is repeated across all visual and tactile streams at corresponding latent timesteps.
- **Training Objective**: ViTacWorld is trained with a latent denoising objective, characteristic of diffusion models. It predicts the denoising target for future visual and tactile latents, conditioned on the current observation, robot actions, stream identities, and the view-presence mask. The loss is applied only to future prediction frames and valid streams.

**Scalable Training Pipeline (Two Stages):**
1. **Stage 1: Pretraining with Large-Scale Real and Simulated Data**:
 - The model is pretrained on a diverse dataset comprising public real visuo-tactile-action data (e.g., over 16K trajectories from OmniViTac, covering various tactile sensors) and *task-aligned simulated data* (over 5K trajectories).
 - **Task-Aligned Simulation**: This simulated data is generated in Isaac Sim. Real-world scenes and objects are reconstructed using 3D Gaussian scanning. The simulated camera-robot setup precisely matches the calibrated extrinsics of the real system. Virtual Xense tactile sensors are integrated, with tactile observations synthesized via the Xense tactile rendering pipeline to match real sensor output characteristics. This simulation is designed to be geometrically and perceptually close to the target real-robot setup, enriching contact patterns and reducing the sim-to-real gap specifically for tactile images. The simulated data also includes variations in object assets and interaction instances beyond the exact target tasks.
 - View-presence masks allow handling trajectories with incomplete modality coverage during pretraining.
2. **Stage 2: Real-World Policy Rollout and Finetuning**:
 - The pretrained model is finetuned on data collected from the target real-world setup, including expert demonstrations (300 trajectories across four tasks) and real policy rollouts (50 per task from a warmed-up tactile $\pi_{0.5}$ policy). This stage adapts ViTacWorld to the specific robot, tactile sensor, task distribution, and policy-induced states of the deployment domain.

**Visuo-Tactile Policy Improvement and Evaluation:**
After training, ViTacWorld serves a dual role:
- **Policy Improvement (Data Augmentation)**: Given a real initial observation and action chunks proposed by a downstream tactile policy, ViTacWorld autoregressively generates contact-rich visual-tactile rollouts. These "imagined" rollouts are filtered for task success and visual-tactile plausibility (currently involving manual inspection, with automated methods planned for future work). Selected successful rollouts (200 high-quality) are then added to the real expert demonstrations, forming an augmented dataset that is used to finetune downstream tactile policies.
- **Policy Evaluation**: The same policy-in-the-loop rollout generation mechanism allows for lightweight policy evaluation. By generating imagined executions under controlled action sequences, ViTacWorld can predict visuo-tactile outcomes (e.g., success or failure) before real-robot deployment. For evaluation, multiple rollouts are typically sampled for each initial condition, and a majority vote determines the predicted outcome.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate ViTacWorld's ability to generate policy-improvement data and serve as a policy evaluation tool for contact-rich robot manipulation tasks.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Robot Platform**: Franka Panda robot with a Robotiq 2F-85 parallel gripper.
- **Sensors**: Xense optical tactile sensors on gripper fingers; Intel RealSense D435 (external view); ZED Mini (wrist-mounted view).
- **Tasks**: Four contact-rich manipulation tasks: Charger Plugging, Cucumber Peeling, U-Block Insertion, and Cuboid Insertion.
- **Data**: 300 real expert demonstrations for policy warm-up and 50 real policy rollouts per task for ViTacWorld finetuning.
- **Generated Data**: 200 high-quality, successful ViTacWorld-generated rollouts were selected to augment training data.
- **Downstream Policies**: Evaluated vision-only $\pi_{0.5}$, tactile $\pi_{0.5}$, and ACT + tactile policies.
- **Evaluation Protocol**: Real-robot success rates over 10 trials per task, assessing completion of task goals.

**Policy Improvement with Generated Visuo-Tactile Data:**
- **Quantitative Results**: Augmenting real expert demonstrations with ViTacWorld-generated rollouts consistently improved real-robot success rates across all tasks and downstream policies. For instance, the average success rate for the $\pi_{0.5}$ + tactile policy increased from 42.5% (expert-only) to 67.5% (expert + ViTacWorld rollouts). Gains were observed for both vision-only and tactile policies, with stronger improvements for tactile policies, suggesting the generated tactile observations provided valuable contact-relevant information.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Policies trained with augmented data exhibited improved behaviors, including better grasp localization, increased robustness to initial pose variations, and for tactile policies, enhanced corrective motions and re-alignment during the contact phase. This indicated that the generated rollouts contributed to learning both broader visual-motor generalization and finer tactile-guided adjustment.

**Visuo-Tactile World Model Quality Analysis:**
- **Ablation Study (Table 2)**: Evaluation on held-out real-world validation clips showed that the full ViTacWorld model significantly outperformed ablated variants (without pretraining or without task-aligned simulation) in terms of PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across visual (main, wrist) and tactile streams.
 - Pretraining with large-scale data substantially improved generation quality, indicating the model learned more stable action-conditioned dynamics.
 - Adding task-aligned simulated tactile trajectories further enhanced the model's performance, suggesting its role in adapting the world model to the target robot domain.
 - While pixel-level metrics (PSNR, SSIM) showed less dramatic gains for the tactile stream due to many non-contact/static frames, LPIPS indicated improved perceptual consistency of generated tactile contact patterns.
- **Qualitative Predictions (Figure 3)**: The full model produced clearer rollouts with better-preserved object geometry, more consistent end-effector motion, and fewer hallucinated contact states compared to ablated versions.

**Additional Evaluation and Improvement Results (Appendix A):**
- **ViTacWorld as a Policy Evaluator (Tables 3 and 4)**: ViTacWorld demonstrated the ability to provide a lightweight policy evaluation signal. For the augmented $\pi_{0.5}$ + tactile policy, ViTacWorld's predicted success rates were close to real-robot execution (average gap of 10 percentage points). The model tended to be slightly conservative, predicting fewer successes than observed in reality, which is deemed preferable for data selection. In an example for U-Block insertion, ViTacWorld showed 90% agreement with real execution, correctly predicting all real-robot failures.
- **Iterative Dream-Data Augmentation (Table 5)**: A second round of augmenting the training data with ViTacWorld-generated rollouts, produced by an already improved policy, led to further improvements in downstream policy performance. For instance, the $\pi_{0.5}$ + tactile policy's average success rate increased from 67.5% (Round 1 augmentation) to 80.0% (Round 2 augmentation), demonstrating the iterative utility of the generated data.
- **Supplementary Tactile Prediction (Figure 6)**: Visualizations with GelSight Mini-style tactile observations (synthesized in simulation and fine-tuned with real data) confirmed the model's ability to generate temporally consistent visual motion and contact-aligned tactile changes, suggesting adaptability to different image-like tactile sensors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViTacWorld addresses a fundamental challenge in robot manipulation: the scarcity of rich, contact-dependent visuo-tactile data for training robust policies. The significance of this work lies in several key aspects:

- **Pioneering Framework for Visuo-Tactile Data Generation**: To the authors' knowledge, ViTacWorld is the first world-model-based framework specifically designed for generating robot visuo-tactile-action trajectories. This introduces a novel approach to overcome data bottlenecks in contact-rich manipulation.
- **Scalable Data Augmentation**: By effectively combining large-scale public datasets, task-aligned simulation, and real-world policy rollouts, ViTacWorld provides a scalable method for synthesizing visuo-tactile-action data. This synthetic data significantly augments real demonstrations, leading to measurable improvements in the performance of various downstream tactile policies. This capability can reduce the reliance on expensive and time-consuming real-robot data collection, accelerating the development of robot learning systems.
- **Enhanced Policy Learning**: The generated "dream data" enables policies to learn both broader visual-motor generalization prior to contact and finer tactile-guided adjustments during interaction. This leads to more robust grasping, better alignment, and improved corrective behaviors in challenging contact-rich tasks.
- **Lightweight Policy Evaluation**: ViTacWorld's ability to predict action-conditioned visuo-tactile outcomes allows it to serve as a lightweight policy evaluation tool. This enables researchers and developers to assess policy performance and identify potential failure modes in a simulated environment before deploying on physical robots, potentially saving time and resources.
- **Technical Advancement in World Modeling**: The proposed view-aware Diffusion Transformer architecture, with stream identity embeddings and explicit cross-view attention, represents a technical advancement in multimodal world modeling. It enables temporally aligned generation of diverse observation streams (multiple visual views and tactile feedback) while maintaining cross-modal consistency.
- **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap for Tactile**: The strategy of using task-aligned simulated data, with careful reconstruction of scenes and calibration of sensor layouts, effectively leverages the smaller sim-to-real gap for image-like tactile observations. This allows simulation to complement real data more effectively for tactile learning.

The potential impact of ViTacWorld extends to various domains requiring dexterous and contact-aware robot manipulation, such as assembly, surgical robotics, and human-robot interaction. By making visuo-tactile data more accessible and usable, this research could foster the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems that can reliably operate in complex, unstructured environments. The iterative data augmentation capability further suggests a path toward continuous policy improvement and adaptation. Future work on automating the curation of generated data could further enhance the scalability and autonomy of the ViTacWorld framework.
