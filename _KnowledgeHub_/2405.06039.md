# Bi-VLA: Vision-Language-Action Model-Based System for Bimanual Robotic Dexterous Manipulations

> [!summary] Summary
> Bi-VLA, developed by the Intelligent Space Robotics Laboratory at Skoltech, is a vision-language-action model system for bimanual robotic dexterous manipulations that interprets natural language instructions and visual inputs to orchestrate coordinated dual-arm actions. The system achieves 100% success in code generation from language commands and up to 83.4% task execution success for bimanual tasks like salad preparation.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation struggles with dynamic, open-ended tasks and natural human communication, especially for bimanual operations.
- Integrating language models for bimanual robotic manipulation has not received sufficient attention, despite many real-world tasks requiring coordinated use of two hands.
- Existing learning-based methods for bimanual tasks often face challenges related to extensive data collection, long training times, or lack of generalization.

## Method

- **Bi-VLA** integrates a ==Language Module== (using Starling-LM-7B-alpha) for semantic planning and code generation with a Vision Language Module (using Qwen-VL) for visual perception.
- The Language Module translates high-level human instructions into detailed bimanual action plans and then into executable API calls for the robotic arms.
- The Vision Language Module identifies objects, verifies ingredient availability, and provides 2D pixel coordinates, which are then converted to 3D world coordinates for accurate robot manipulation.

## Results

- The Language Module achieved a **100%** success rate in generating correct executable code for 100 diverse requests.
- The Vision Language Module demonstrated a **96.06%** success rate in detecting ingredients and an **83.4%** action generation success rate in scenarios where all required ingredients were present.
- In scenarios with missing ingredients, the VLM's detection success rate was **86.53%**, leading to a **71.22%** action generation success rate for task completion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively serve as high-level semantic planners and code generators for orchestrating complex, coordinated bimanual robotic actions from natural language instructions.
> - Robust visual perception and accurate 2D to 3D coordinate conversion are critical bottlenecks and direct determinants of overall task execution success in vision-language-action systems.
> - A modular architecture integrating LLMs for planning and VLMs for perception allows for a less data-intensive approach to bimanual tasks, bridging high-level cognition and low-level control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research by Gbagbe et al. is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and advanced robotic manipulation, particularly bimanual dexterous operations.

Traditionally, robotic manipulation, especially for complex tasks involving dual arms, has relied on meticulous motion planning, predefined trajectories, or highly specialized control algorithms. While effective in structured environments, these methods struggle with dynamic, open-ended tasks and natural human communication. The introduction and rapid advancement of LLMs have revolutionized HRI, allowing robots to understand and process natural language instructions. This has led to the development of systems like CLIPort, ProgPrompt, "Code as Policies", RT-1, and PaLM-E, which translate linguistic commands into robotic actions, often by generating programmatic structures or deriving reward functions. These prior works primarily focus on single-arm manipulation or abstract task planning.

The current paper builds upon this foundation but addresses a critical gap: the integration of language models for **bimanual robotic manipulation**. Many real-world tasks that humans perform, such as cooking, assembly, or handling large objects, inherently require the coordinated use of two hands. While bimanual control has been studied extensively, with approaches ranging from behavior-based control to reinforcement learning (RL) and learning from demonstration (LfD), these often face challenges related to extensive data collection, long training times, or lack of generalization. For instance, Mobile ALOHA explores low-cost teleoperation for bimanual tasks, but still relies on demonstration.

Bi-VLA differentiates itself by aiming for a **less data-intensive approach** that leverages LLMs for strategic semantic planning and orchestration of bimanual actions, rather than relying on extensive training datasets or fine-tuning for specific tasks. By treating LLMs as "strategic semantic planners" that invoke predefined action APIs and allocate tasks between two robotic arms, the system seeks to bridge the gap between high-level human instructions and precise, coordinated bimanual physical execution. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional planning by offering flexibility and natural language understanding, while also providing an alternative to data-hungry learning methods for bimanual tasks. The paper specifically highlights that the application of language models to synthesize bimanual skills has not received significant attention, thereby positioning Bi-VLA as a novel contribution in this underexplored but crucial domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a novel vision-language-action model, named Bi-VLA, specifically engineered for bimanual robotic dexterous manipulations.** This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Enhancing Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) with Natural Language:** The authors aim to enable robots to interpret and act upon complex human instructions given in natural language. This moves beyond predefined scripts or limited command sets, fostering a more intuitive and effective interaction paradigm.
- **Enabling Complex Bimanual Tasks:** Recognizing that many real-world tasks require the coordinated use of two hands, the research seeks to equip robots with bimanual capabilities that can perform intricate operations beyond the scope of single-arm manipulators. The example of preparing a salad, involving picking, placing, and cutting with different tools, vividly illustrates this need.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Traditional Methods:** The paper identifies that traditional planning-based methods are often suboptimal for dynamic tasks, and learning-based approaches like RL and LfD are data-intensive and time-consuming. Bi-VLA aims to provide a more efficient and generalized solution by leveraging the reasoning capabilities of LLMs.
- **Integrating Multimodal Understanding:** A core motivation is the seamless integration of visual scene understanding (identifying objects, their locations, and availability) with language comprehension (interpreting user requests) to generate physical actions. This multimodal fusion is essential for robots to operate effectively in unconstrained environments.
- **Leveraging Generative AI for Action Synthesis:** The research is motivated by the potential of LLMs to act as high-level semantic planners and code generators, translating abstract human intent into executable robotic commands. This shifts the complexity from explicit programming to intelligent interpretation and synthesis.
- **Real-world Applicability:** The chosen application scenario of a "cooking robot" highlights the motivation to develop a practical system capable of performing household tasks, thereby demonstrating the direct utility and potential impact of advanced bimanual manipulation systems.

In essence, the research seeks to create a more intelligent, adaptable, and human-friendly bimanual robotic system by harmoniously integrating cutting-edge AI (LLMs and VLMs) with robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Bi-VLA system is designed as a modular, integrated architecture that grounds natural language instructions into bimanual robot actions through a combination of vision, language, and action generation modules.

**A. General System Overview:**
The system workflow begins with two primary inputs: a user's task request (transcribed into text) and an image from a camera of the workspace.
1. **Request Processing:** The text request serves as a query for a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system, which retrieves relevant information (e.g., recipes from a vector database).
2. **Visual-Linguistic Dialogue:** The RAG output initiates a Visual Question-Answering (VQA) dialogue between the Language Module and the Vision Language Module.
3. **Visual Processing:** The input image is tokenized and combined with VQA question tokens for the Vision Language Module.
4. **Output Generation:** The VLM outputs include an image with captions and a JSON file containing 2D positions of ingredients, a list of all ingredients, and missing ingredients.
5. **Action Generation:** The Language Module then generates a set of actions based on this information, which are subsequently performed by the bimanual robotic system.

**B. Large Language Model (LLM) Module:**
The core of the Bi-VLA's intelligence lies in its LLM module, which uses **Starling-LM-7B-alpha** as its backbone. Starling-LM-7B-alpha is an open-source LLM developed by UC Berkeley’s NEST Lab, known for outperforming GPT-3.5 in several benchmarks.

1. **Semantic Planning:** This component interprets human instructions and generates high-level executable plans for the two robot manipulators. It takes an action description (e.g., "cut the pepper") and produces a detailed plan outlining the sequence of motions for each arm, including which arm performs which part of the task and with what tool. The planner uses a templated structure with "[start of plan]" and "[end of plan]" tags, guided by rules and placeholders filled by the VLM.
2. **Translating Semantic Plan into API Function Calls with the Code Generator:** The generated semantic plan is translated into a sequence of executable Python code using a predefined set of API functions. The LLM is prompted with available functions, the plan structure, and examples (one-shot prompting) to guide it in converting the high-level plan into concrete function calls.
3. **From API Function Call to Action Generation with The Motion Control APIs:** A comprehensive set of API functions controls the physical robot actions. These include:
 - `open_gripper(manipulator_type)`
 - `move_to_object(manipulator_type, object_name)`
 - `grasp(manipulator_type, object_name)`
 - `cut(manipulator_type, object_name)`
 - `pour(manipulator_type, object_name)`
 - `put(manipulator_type, object_name)`
 - `toss(manipulator_type, object_name)`
 - `cut_and_put_in(manipulator_type, object_name)`
 Explicit naming ensures clarity for the language model. The Vision Language Model also uses `get_list_of_objects` and `get_bounding_boxes` to provide necessary object information.

**C. Vision Language Model (VLM) Module:**
The visual perception and understanding are handled by the VLM module, specifically integrating the **Qwen Vision-Language Model**. Qwen-VL is known for its ability to understand both text and images, performing state-of-the-art in image captioning, visual question answering, and visual grounding.

1. **Integration of Qwen VLM:** The Qwen-VL model is used to verify the availability of required items mentioned in the recipe and provide their 2D-pixel coordinates.
2. **Mapping Image Pixel to 3D Coordinates:** A critical step for robotic manipulation is converting 2D pixel information into 3D world coordinates. The system assumes object positions are at their centers and uses standard camera calibration techniques.
 - **Camera Model:** The relationship between 3D world coordinates (`wP`) and 2D pixel coordinates (`pP`) involves the camera's intrinsic matrix (`pcT`) and the transformation matrix from world to camera coordinates (`cwT`).
 - **Distortion Correction:** The Brown–Conrady model is applied to correct radial and tangential distortion errors in the pixel coordinates, ensuring accurate transformation.
 - **Coordinate Conversion Steps:**
 1. Obtain distorted 2D pixel position `[xd, yd]T`.
 2. Calculate undistorted pixel coordinates `[xu, yu]T` using the Brown-Conrady model.
 3. Calculate normalized camera coordinates `[xc/zc, yc/zc, 1]T` using the inverse intrinsic matrix.
 4. Determine real 3D camera coordinates `[xc, yc, zc]T` (assuming objects are on a known z-plane and camera's z-axis is perpendicular to it).
 5. Transform camera coordinates to world coordinates `[xw, yw, zw]T` using the inverse of `cwT`.
These precise 3D coordinates are then sent to the robot for accurate object grasping and manipulation.

**D. Experimental Setup:**
The physical setup includes two **UR3 collaborative robots** mounted on a Siegmund welding table. One robot is equipped with a custom 3D-printed camera holder (with a Logitech HD 1080p webcam) and a two-finger Robotiq gripper. The second robot has a custom knife holder. Ingredients are placed near the first manipulator for camera capture, with a cutting board and a plate/bowl in between the manipulators. A high-performance PC (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090, Intel Core i9, 64GB RAM, Ubuntu 22.04) supervises all processes.

**E. Experiments Workflow:**
Custom cooking recipes (e.g., vegetable, Russian, fruit salads), detailing ingredients and instructions, are stored in a vector database.
1. **User Request:** A user submits a request (e.g., "make a vegetable salad").
2. **RAG Processing:** The request is processed via LLM-Based RAG to retrieve the relevant recipe.
3. **VLM Verification:** The VLM module verifies the availability of all ingredients in the scene based on the recipe.
4. **LLM Action Generation:** If ingredients are available, the LLM module generates a list of actions for the robots. If ingredients are missing or the request is ambiguous, no action is performed.
The system's performance is evaluated on three diverse salad types requiring various actions (picking, placing, cutting, pouring, mixing).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Bi-VLA involved assessing its components individually and collectively, defining a successful task completion as correct code generation by the Code Generator and successful execution of actions. The experiments were structured into two scenarios for the vision module: Scenario 1 (all required ingredients present) and Scenario 2 (some ingredients missing).

The key quantitative results are summarized as follows:

1. **Language Module Performance:**
 - The Language Module was tested with 100 diverse requests (one-third for each of three salad types) generated by GPT-3.5.
 - **Success Rate: 100%** in generating the correct executable code for all requests. This indicates the LLM's robust capability in semantic planning and translating high-level instructions into precise API calls, irrespective of the visual scene context (as this module operates based on VLM output).

2. **Vision Language Module (VLM) Performance:**
 - Evaluated using 100 pictures of ingredients, with half for Scenario 1 and half for Scenario 2 for each salad type. Performance was measured by the correctness of the detected list of ingredients and the accuracy of captions and 2D bounding boxes.
 - **Scenario 1 (All ingredients correct):**
 - **Detection Success Rate: 96.06%** in correctly detecting specific ingredients.
 - **Action Generation Success Rate: 83.4%** in correctly executing the user-requested tasks.
 - **Scenario 2 (Some ingredients missing):**
 - **Detection Success Rate: 86.53%** in identifying ingredients. The paper notes that misidentification of present ingredients as missing ones contributed to this lower rate.
 - **Action Generation Success Rate: 71.22%** in correctly executing the user-requested tasks.

3. **Action Generation Module Performance:**
 - The success rate of the Action Generation Module (physical execution) was stated to be equivalent to the success rate of generating correct 2D bounding box coordinates, given the successful implementation of 2D pixel to 3D world coordinate translation. This implies that if the VLM provides accurate 2D bounding boxes, the system can reliably grasp and manipulate objects.
 - Therefore, the **Action Generation Module's success rate is implicitly tied to the VLM's detection performance**, resulting in 83.4% for Scenario 1 and 71.22% for Scenario 2.

In summary, the Language Module demonstrated perfect performance in generating executable code, highlighting the strength of leveraging LLMs for task planning. The Vision Language Module showed strong, but not perfect, performance, with its accuracy directly impacting the overall task execution success rate. The decline in performance for Scenario 2 underscores the challenges of robust object detection and verification in more complex or incomplete visual scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Bi-VLA system represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for complex bimanual tasks, by seamlessly integrating state-of-the-art vision and language models with robotic action generation. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

**A. Advancing Human-Robot Interaction:**
By enabling robots to interpret and execute complex human instructions provided in natural language, Bi-VLA significantly enhances the naturalness and intuitiveness of HRI. This moves beyond rigid programming or limited command sets, fostering a more collaborative and accessible interaction paradigm for non-expert users. The ability to simply "ask" a robot to prepare a salad and have it autonomously understand and act upon the request is a powerful demonstration of this advancement.

**B. Enabling Dexterous Bimanual Manipulation through AI:**
The paper effectively demonstrates how LLMs can serve as high-level semantic planners for orchestrating coordinated actions between two robotic arms. This is a crucial contribution, as bimanual tasks are inherently more complex than single-arm manipulation but are essential for many real-world applications (e.g., assembly, cooking, medical procedures). By providing a framework for LLMs to generate API calls and allocate tasks between manipulators, Bi-VLA opens new avenues for achieving human-level dexterity and versatility in robotic systems without requiring extensive, task-specific training data often demanded by traditional RL or LfD methods.

**C. Bridging the Gap between High-Level Cognition and Low-Level Control:**
Bi-VLA exemplifies a successful end-to-end system that translates abstract human intent (e.g., "make a salad") into concrete, low-level robot movements. The modular design, where an LLM handles semantic planning and code generation, and a VLM handles perception and grounding, showcases a powerful architecture for intelligent robotic systems. This reduces the need for manual programming of complex task sequences, making robots more adaptable to novel situations and variations in tasks.

**D. Identifying Key Research Directions:**
While the results for the Language Module (100% success in code generation) are outstanding, the performance of the Vision Module, particularly in scenarios with missing ingredients, highlights perception as a critical bottleneck. The paper explicitly states that "the accuracy of the vision module significantly affects task completion." This finding is significant as it provides clear direction for future research in embodied AI: while language models are becoming increasingly powerful at reasoning and planning, robust and versatile visual understanding remains paramount for real-world robotic deployment. Future efforts must focus on improving VLM resilience to visual variations, occlusions, and uncertainties.

**E. Potential for Diverse Applications:**
The success of Bi-VLA in a complex household task like salad preparation suggests its potential applicability across various domains beyond the kitchen. This includes:
- **Manufacturing and Assembly:** Coordinated dual-arm robots can perform intricate assembly tasks that require handling multiple components simultaneously.
- **Healthcare:** Assisting in surgical procedures or patient care requiring precise bimanual manipulation.
- **Logistics:** Efficiently sorting, packing, and moving diverse items in warehouses.
- **Service Robotics:** Expanding the capabilities of robots in homes, restaurants, or other service environments.

In conclusion, Bi-VLA represents a promising paradigm for creating more intelligent, capable, and user-friendly robotic systems. By demonstrating the effective integration of vision, language, and action for bimanual manipulation, this work contributes significantly to advancing the frontier of embodied AI and paves the way for more autonomous and adaptable robotic assistants in diverse real-world settings.
