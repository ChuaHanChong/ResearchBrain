# BiPneu: Design and Control of a Bipolar-Pressure Pneumatic System for Soft Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Michigan State University developed BiPneu, a scalable and cost-efficient multi-channel bipolar-pressure pneumatic system for soft robots, paired with a Dual-Mode Sliding-Mode Controller (DM-SMC). This system achieved precise and responsive pressure regulation across both positive and negative pressure ranges, demonstrating robust performance in dynamic soft robot tasks and seamless integration with high-level robotic software.

## Problem

- Existing pneumatic systems for soft robots struggle with precise, rapid, and robust bipolar-pressure control due to asymmetric inflation-deflation dynamics and strong valve nonlinearities.
- Scalable and cost-effective multi-channel systems often use on/off valves but lack bipolar pressure capability or seamless integration with modern robotic software ecosystems like ROS 2.
- Conventional fixed-gain controllers (e.g., PID) are insufficient for the complex nonlinear oscillatory pressure responses and transient flow disturbances inherent in on/off valve-based pneumatic systems.

## Method

- A 16-channel bipolar-pressure pneumatic system, **BiPneu**, was designed using cost-effective solenoid on/off valves and a shared air source, featuring a Raspberry Pi 4B for embedded control and a ROS 2 server-client architecture for high-level software compatibility.
- A detailed switched nonlinear electro-pneumatic model of BiPneu was developed, incorporating piecewise-continuous orifice-flow dynamics and mode-dependent flow-path switching, with parameters identified experimentally.
- A Dual-Mode Sliding-Mode Controller (DM-SMC) was designed, combining a hysteresis-supervised mode selector with mode-specific sliding-mode control laws, to achieve accurate and computationally efficient bipolar-pressure regulation while mitigating chattering and minimizing mode switches.

## Results

- In multi-step pressure tracking experiments under fixed-volume load, DM-SMC reduced average absolute error by **11.9%** (to **2.53** kPa) and ITAE by **51.4%** (to **28.18** kPa·s²) compared to PID, requiring **51.4%** fewer mode switches.
- DM-SMC demonstrated increased performance advantages at higher frequencies in sinusoidal tracking, reducing average absolute error by **32.19%** at **0.25** Hz and **35.62%** at **0.5** Hz compared to PID under fixed load.
- The system successfully demonstrated dynamic ball maneuvering with a soft parallel manipulator, achieving a final error of **1.4** cm, and real-time FEM-based teleoperation of a soft bellow actuator with an RMSE of **4.1** mm, confirming robust performance and software compatibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a hysteresis-supervised mode selector with mode-specific sliding-mode control effectively manages the discrete switching dynamics and continuous control of bipolar pneumatic systems, improving stability and reducing valve wear.
> - A detailed physics-based switched-system model, despite its complexity, is essential for designing robust controllers like DM-SMC that can account for nonlinear and asymmetric pneumatic dynamics during both inflation and deflation.
> - Utilizing a Raspberry Pi with a ROS 2 server-client architecture bridges the gap between low-level hardware control and high-level robotic software ecosystems, facilitating advanced control development and simulation integration for soft robots.
