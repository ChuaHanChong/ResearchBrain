# BiPneu: Design and Control of a Bipolar-Pressure Pneumatic System for Soft Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Michigan State University developed BiPneu, a scalable and cost-efficient multi-channel bipolar-pressure pneumatic system for soft robots, paired with a Dual-Mode Sliding-Mode Controller (DM-SMC). This system achieved precise and responsive pressure regulation across both positive and negative pressure ranges, demonstrating robust performance in dynamic soft robot tasks and seamless integration with high-level robotic software.

## Problem

- Existing pneumatic systems for soft robots struggle with precise, rapid, and robust bipolar-pressure control due to asymmetric inflation-deflation dynamics and strong valve nonlinearities.
- Scalable and cost-effective multi-channel systems often use on/off valves but lack bipolar pressure capability or seamless integration with modern robotic software ecosystems like ROS 2.
- Conventional fixed-gain controllers (e.g., PID) are insufficient for the complex nonlinear oscillatory pressure responses and transient flow disturbances inherent in on/off valve-based pneumatic systems.

## Method

- A 16-channel bipolar-pressure pneumatic system, **BiPneu**, was designed using cost-effective solenoid on/off valves and a shared air source, featuring a Raspberry Pi 4B for embedded control and a ROS 2 server-client architecture for high-level software compatibility.
- A detailed switched nonlinear electro-pneumatic model of BiPneu was developed, incorporating piecewise-continuous orifice-flow dynamics and mode-dependent flow-path switching, with parameters identified experimentally.
- A Dual-Mode Sliding-Mode Controller (DM-SMC) was designed, combining a hysteresis-supervised mode selector with mode-specific sliding-mode control laws, to achieve accurate and computationally efficient bipolar-pressure regulation while mitigating chattering and minimizing mode switches.

## Results

- In multi-step pressure tracking experiments under fixed-volume load, DM-SMC reduced average absolute error by **11.9%** (to **2.53** kPa) and ITAE by **51.4%** (to **28.18** kPa·s²) compared to PID, requiring **51.4%** fewer mode switches.
- DM-SMC demonstrated increased performance advantages at higher frequencies in sinusoidal tracking, reducing average absolute error by **32.19%** at **0.25** Hz and **35.62%** at **0.5** Hz compared to PID under fixed load.
- The system successfully demonstrated dynamic ball maneuvering with a soft parallel manipulator, achieving a final error of **1.4** cm, and real-time FEM-based teleoperation of a soft bellow actuator with an RMSE of **4.1** mm, confirming robust performance and software compatibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a hysteresis-supervised mode selector with mode-specific sliding-mode control effectively manages the discrete switching dynamics and continuous control of bipolar pneumatic systems, improving stability and reducing valve wear.
> - A detailed physics-based switched-system model, despite its complexity, is essential for designing robust controllers like DM-SMC that can account for nonlinear and asymmetric pneumatic dynamics during both inflation and deflation.
> - Utilizing a Raspberry Pi with a ROS 2 server-client architecture bridges the gap between low-level hardware control and high-level robotic software ecosystems, facilitating advanced control development and simulation integration for soft robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Soft robots are characterized by their inherent compliance and adaptability, which enables their application in unstructured environments and human-robot interaction. Pneumatic actuation is a common method for soft robots, offering a high power-to-weight ratio and rapid response at a relatively low cost. A developing area within soft robotics is the use of bipolar-pressure (positive–negative pressure) pneumatic actuators, which utilize both positive and negative pressures to facilitate bidirectional motion, expand workspace, and enable diverse actuation modes.

However, precise, rapid, and robust pressure control across both positive and negative pressure regimes presents several challenges. These include asymmetric inflation-deflation dynamics, strong valve nonlinearities, and transient flow disturbances induced by switching between pressure polarities.

Existing pneumatic actuation systems for soft robots generally fall into three categories:
- **Volume control:** These systems typically use displacement sources like syringes or piston pumps. While effective, they often provide limited airflow and can be bulky.
- **Continuous pressure control with proportional valves:** Proportional valves offer accurate regulation through continuous analog voltage inputs. They can be integrated with microprocessor units (e.g., Raspberry Pi) and robotic middleware frameworks (e.g., ROS 2), facilitating integration with high-level software. However, these valves are generally larger and more expensive (2–5 times in size and 3–20 times in cost) than solenoid on/off valves, which can restrict their use in compact, multi-channel robot designs.
- **Switching pressure control with solenoid on/off valves:** These architectures use pulse-width modulation (PWM) to drive solenoid on/off valves, allowing for more compact and cost-effective multi-channel implementations. While numerous on/off valve-based systems have been developed, most primarily focus on positive-pressure regulation. A limited number support bipolar pressure operation, with some relying on per-channel mini pumps, increasing system size and complexity, and often constraining pressure range and transient response. Other bipolar systems with on/off valves use low-level microcontroller units (MCUs) for PWM generation, which can impede integration with higher-level software stacks.

In terms of control strategies for on/off valve-based systems, fixed-gain PID controllers often struggle with the nonlinear oscillatory pressure responses caused by air compressibility, asymmetric dynamics, and valve nonlinearities. Researchers have explored advanced strategies such as fuzzy piecewise PID, dual-loop PID tuned by evolutionary algorithms, and model-based reinforcement learning. While model-based control is promising, comprehensive system-level modeling and closed-loop regulation of pneumatic systems remain areas for further investigation.

This work, BiPneu, addresses the identified gaps by developing a scalable, cost-efficient, multi-channel bipolar-pressure pneumatic system based on on/off valves that also ensures compatibility with high-level robotic software ecosystems. Furthermore, it introduces a dual-mode sliding-mode controller (DM-SMC) to mitigate the challenges of nonlinear and asymmetric dynamics, aiming for accurate and responsive pressure regulation while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for embedded real-time implementation. The paper positions BiPneu as an advancement compared to other state-of-the-art bipolar pressure systems (Table I), specifically highlighting its broad regulation range, response time, software ecosystem compatibility, and cost-efficiency per channel.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a pneumatic system and a corresponding control strategy that can achieve accurate, responsive, and robust bipolar-pressure regulation for soft robotic actuators. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified challenges and limitations in current soft robot pneumatic systems:

1. **Enabling Bipolar Actuation for Enhanced Soft Robot Capabilities:** Many emerging soft robotic actuators benefit from bipolar pressure regulation, as it enables bidirectional motion, larger motion ranges, and versatile actuation modes. The development of a system capable of this is essential to fully exploit these capabilities.

2. **Addressing Hardware Limitations in Existing Systems:**
 - **Scalability and Cost:** Existing high-performance systems often rely on expensive proportional valves, limiting their scalability for robots requiring multiple independently controlled channels. Systems using on/off valves are typically more cost-effective and compact but often lack bipolar pressure capability or seamless integration with high-level software.
 - **Integration with High-Level Software:** Many current on/off valve-based bipolar systems use low-level MCUs, which complicate integration with modern robotic software ecosystems like ROS 2, physics-based simulators (SOFA, MuJoCo), and learning-based control policies. There is a need for a platform that natively supports standard network protocols for easier integration.
 - **System Complexity:** Some bipolar systems use per-channel mini pumps, which increase the overall size and complexity of the system and can constrain performance. A shared pressure source architecture is desired for efficiency and compactness.

3. **Overcoming Control Challenges of Bipolar On/Off Valve Systems:**
 - **Asymmetric Dynamics:** Pneumatic systems exhibit distinct and asymmetric dynamics during inflation and deflation due to air compressibility and valve characteristics, making conventional fixed-gain controllers (e.g., PID) insufficient.
 - **Valve Nonlinearities:** The on/off nature and characteristic curves of solenoid valves introduce strong nonlinearities into the system.
 - **Switching-Induced Disturbances:** The act of switching between inflation and deflation modes can generate transient flow disturbances, leading to nonlinear oscillatory pressure responses and hindering stable regulation.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** While advanced control methods like Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MI-NMPC) can address some of these nonlinearities, their computational cost is often prohibitive for real-time embedded implementation on typical robot hardware.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this work are:
- To design and construct "BiPneu," a scalable, cost-efficient, multi-channel bipolar-pressure pneumatic system utilizing solenoid on/off valves and a shared air source.
- To develop a robust and computationally efficient control strategy, the Dual-Mode Sliding-Mode Controller (DM-SMC), based on a hybrid electro-pneumatic model, capable of accurate and responsive pressure regulation across bipolar regimes.
- To ensure the system's compatibility with high-level robotic software ecosystems (e.g., ROS 2, FEM simulators) for ease of application development and research.
- To experimentally validate the performance of BiPneu and DM-SMC under both fixed-volume and varying-volume loads, demonstrating its accuracy, responsiveness, and robustness.
- To showcase BiPneu's utility in relevant soft robotic applications, such as dynamic manipulation and real-time teleoperation, to highlight its practical amenability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research involved the design and implementation of the BiPneu hardware, the development of its software architecture, the formulation of a switched-system model, and the design and evaluation of a dual-mode sliding-mode controller (DM-SMC).

#### 3.1. Hardware Design

BiPneu is a 16-channel bipolar-pressure pneumatic system enclosed in a 3D-printed unit (25×25×19 cm). It features a stackable architecture with two 8-channel boards for scalability. The system uses shared pressure sources: an air compressor for positive pressure (inflation) and a vacuum pump for negative pressure (deflation). Each pneumatic channel consists of two cascaded 3/2 normally-closed solenoid on/off valves (SMC, VQ110U-5M-M5) driven by PWM inputs. Valve 1 acts as a polarity selector, connecting the channel to either the positive pressure or vacuum line. Valve 2 functions as a delivery/hold stage, metering flow to the soft actuator and sealing the channel when de-energized to minimize leakage.

A Raspberry Pi 4B serves as the embedded controller, handling high-level commands via TCP/IP from a host computer. PWM commands are generated at 100 Hz and sent via I²C to a PWM Hat (Adafruit PCA9685), which drives MOSFETs for the 24V solenoids. Pressure feedback is acquired at 60 Hz through I²C analog-to-digital converter (ADC) modules (Adafruit, ADS1015) connected to custom pressure sensing PCBs with NXP MPXHZ6400A transducers, supporting a pressure range of −80 to 300 kPa. A 24V AC/DC adapter powers the system, with a DC-DC converter providing a regulated 5V for the Raspberry Pi and sensor boards.

#### 3.2. Software Architecture

BiPneu employs a distributed ROS 2 server–client architecture. The client-side, running on the embedded Raspberry Pi, performs real-time pressure sensing, executes the local control algorithm (PID or DM-SMC), and handles low-level valve actuation. The server-side, typically a host computer, is responsible for generating desired pressure trajectories, system monitoring, and data logging. This ROS 2-based design enables compatibility with external high-level software ecosystems, including physics-based simulators (SOFA, MuJoCo, Isaac Sim), numerical toolchains (MATLAB/Simulink), and machine learning frameworks.

#### 3.3. Switched System Modeling of BiPneu

The BiPneu system is modeled as a switched nonlinear system due to its distinct airflow dynamics during inflation and deflation. The model is physics-based, combining piecewise-continuous orifice-flow dynamics with mode-dependent flow-path switching, assuming adiabatic conditions. The outlet pressure $P_{out}(t)$ is defined as the system state, and the PWM duty cycle $u_2(t)$ for Valve 2 is the continuous control input. A binary variable $m \in \{0, 1\}$ represents the operating mode (inflation for $m=1$, deflation for $m=0$), selected by Valve 1. The pressure dynamics are expressed as $\dot{P} = f(P) + g_m(P)\bar{x}(u)$, where $f(P)$ accounts for leakage and $g_m(P)$ for mode-specific flow from pressure sources or to vacuum. The terms $A_{po}(P)$, $A_{on}(P)$, $A_{oa}(P)$, and $A_{ao}(P)$ represent effective flow functions for different branches, derived using the critical pressure ratio and shape factor $\phi(r)$. The nonlinear static function $\bar{x}(u)$ maps the PWM duty cycle to the average spool position ratio of Valve 2.

**System Identification:** The model parameters are identified experimentally:
- **Sonic Conductances:** $C_{po}, C_{on}, C_{oa}, C_{ao}$ are identified offline using least-squares fitting on step-response pressure data. This involves isolating specific flow paths (e.g., leakage-only, inflow-only) and fitting the measured pressure trajectories to the corresponding model equations.
- **Spool-Fraction Mapping:** The function $\bar{x}(u)$ is calibrated separately for inflation and deflation using cubic polynomial regression based on step tests across various PWM duty cycles.

#### 3.4. Dual-Mode Sliding-Mode Controller (DM-SMC) Design

The DM-SMC is designed to provide accurate and responsive bipolar-pressure regulation while maintaining computational efficiency. It integrates two main components:
- **Hysteresis-Supervised Mode Selector:** A hysteresis rule (Eq. 4) determines the discrete operating mode ($m \in \{0,1\}$). This rule updates the mode only when the tracking error ($e = P - P_{ref}$) exceeds a hysteresis half-band $h$, preventing rapid mode toggling near the reference pressure.
- **Mode-Specific Sliding-Mode Control (SMC) Law:** For each fixed mode $m$, an SMC law is synthesized. The control objective is to drive the tracking error $e$ to zero by defining a sliding surface $s = \lambda e + k_I \int e(t) dt$. A reaching law, $\dot{s} = -\lambda s - \eta \text{sat}(s/\mu)$, is used to ensure convergence to the sliding manifold while mitigating chattering (with $\mu$ as boundary-layer thickness). By equating the derivative of the sliding surface with the reaching law and solving for the desired average spool-position ratio $\bar{x}$, the continuous PWM command $u$ is obtained through the inverse map $\bar{x}^{-1}(\cdot)$.

#### 3.5. Evaluation and Demonstration

The proposed system and controller are evaluated through both simulations and experiments.
- **Simulation Studies:** Implemented in Python with the identified system model, including Gaussian noise. Comparison is made against PID, Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC), and Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MI-NMPC) for multi-step and sinusoidal (0.1, 0.25, 0.5 Hz) pressure tracking. Performance metrics include steady-state error ($e_{ss}$), average absolute error (AE), integral of time-weighted absolute error (ITAE), PWM energy (PWM-E), number of mode switches, and computational time (CT) on both a host PC and the embedded Raspberry Pi.
- **Experimental Validation:**
 - **Fixed-Volume Load:** Experiments are conducted using a 20 mL syringe as a fixed-volume load. Multi-step and sinusoidal pressure tracking tests are performed, comparing DM-SMC with a well-tuned PID controller.
 - **Varying-Volume Load:** Robustness is assessed by connecting BiPneu to a soft bellow actuator (volume varying from 0 to 25 mL) to simulate load-dependent pneumatic dynamics. Multi-step and sinusoidal tracking tests are repeated, comparing DM-SMC and PID.
- **Soft Robot Demonstrations:**
 - **Ball Maneuvering:** A soft parallel manipulator with three bellow actuators is controlled by BiPneu (DM-SMC) to maneuver a ping-pong ball on a transparent board, requiring dynamic and accurate tilt control. An overhead camera provides ball position feedback for an outer-loop PID controller and an inverse kinematics scheme to generate pressure references.
 - **Real-time FEM-based Teleoperation:** BiPneu's compatibility with high-level software is demonstrated by teleoperating a soft bellow actuator using a SOFA FEM simulator. Desired actuator lengths specified in SOFA are converted to pressure commands via inverse optimization and streamed to BiPneu via ROS 2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of BiPneu and the DM-SMC involved extensive simulations and experiments, demonstrating the system's capabilities and the controller's performance relative to other methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Controller Comparison:** All controllers (PID, NMPC, MI-NMPC, DM-SMC) were able to track reference trajectories in simulation. MI-NMPC showed strong tracking performance and reduced control effort by co-optimizing mode scheduling and PWM inputs. However, its computational cost (3595.999 ms on embedded system) was deemed prohibitive for real-time embedded applications.
- **DM-SMC Performance:** The proposed DM-SMC demonstrated accurate tracking while being computationally lightweight (0.692 ms on embedded system).
 - **Multi-step tracking:** DM-SMC achieved an average absolute error (AE) of 1.59 kPa and an ITAE of 10.79 kPa·s², outperforming PID (AE: 2.48 kPa, ITAE: 22.15 kPa·s²) and NMPC (AE: 1.91 kPa, ITAE: 15.00 kPa·s²). It also exhibited significantly fewer mode switches (0.83 vs. 9.08 for PID and MI-NMPC, 6.08 for NMPC).
 - **Sinusoidal tracking:** DM-SMC outperformed MI-NMPC, NMPC, and PID in most metrics, particularly in reducing AE at various frequencies (e.g., 0.72 kPa at 0.1 Hz, 0.78 kPa at 0.25 Hz, 0.86 kPa at 0.5 Hz). It also significantly reduced the number of mode switches during sinusoidal tracking, which contributes to smoother control and extended valve lifetime, possibly due to its hysteresis-supervised sliding regulation effectively suppressing noise-induced switching.

#### 4.2. Experimental Results

The experimental validation focused on comparing DM-SMC with a well-tuned PID controller on the BiPneu platform, given the computational demands of NMPC and MI-NMPC.

- **Pressure Tracking under Fixed-Volume Load (20 mL syringe):**
 - **Multi-step tracking:** DM-SMC showed improved transient and steady-state tracking. It reduced the average absolute error (AE) by 11.9% (from 2.87 kPa for PID to 2.53 kPa for DM-SMC) and the integral of time-weighted absolute error (ITAE) by 51.4% (from 57.99 kPa·s² to 28.18 kPa·s²), indicating faster error attenuation. DM-SMC also reduced PWM energy by 4.5% and required 51.4% fewer mode switches compared to PID. The average absolute error for DM-SMC was 1.44 kPa (0.5% of the full range).
 - **Sinusoidal tracking:** DM-SMC demonstrated increased performance advantages at higher frequencies. At 0.25 Hz, it reduced AE by 32.19% (from 4.38 kPa to 2.97 kPa) and at 0.5 Hz, by 35.62% (from 6.57 kPa to 4.23 kPa). It also reduced maximum tracking error (max|e|) by 6.37% at 0.25 Hz and 45.50% at 0.5 Hz, while also showing lower PWM-E and reduced switching. The average absolute error for DM-SMC in sinusoidal tests was 4.23 kPa (1.5% of the full range). The observed larger error at 0.1 Hz for DM-SMC was attributed to a higher mismatch in the identified $\bar{x}(u)$ mapping under slowly varying references.

- **Robustness to Varying-Volume Load (Soft Bellow Actuator):**
 - DM-SMC consistently outperformed PID under the deformation-induced varying-volume load, demonstrating robustness to changes in pneumatic dynamics. This was attributed to its mode-specific sliding-mode design and hysteresis-supervised mode switching.
 - **Multi-step tracking:** DM-SMC maintained superior transient responses and fewer mode switches (AE: 2.59 kPa, Switches: 1.33) compared to PID (AE: 2.72 kPa, Switches: 2.75).
 - **Sinusoidal tracking:** DM-SMC's advantage became more pronounced at higher frequencies, where PID exhibited larger phase lag and peak errors. For example, at 0.5 Hz, DM-SMC achieved an AE of 3.60 kPa (vs. 7.17 kPa for PID) and a max|e| of 14.37 kPa (vs. 40.43 kPa for PID).
 - In some cases, DM-SMC's performance under varying load was comparable to or slightly better than fixed-volume results, potentially due to added compliance and damping from the bellow's deformation.

#### 4.3. Soft Robot Demonstrations

- **Ball Maneuvering with a Soft Parallel Manipulator:** BiPneu, using DM-SMC for the inner-loop pressure control, successfully maneuvered a ping-pong ball. It achieved a final error of 1.4 cm (3.3% of board diagonal) in center regulation. For triangular and circular trajectory tracking, RMSEs of 3.0 cm (7.0%) and 3.3 cm (7.7%) were achieved, respectively. This demonstrated the system's capability for accurate and responsive pressure regulation in dynamic interaction tasks.
- **Real-time FEM-based Teleoperation of a Soft Bellow Actuator:** The system demonstrated seamless integration with the SOFA FEM simulator. Across 32 target lengths, the bellow actuator tracked the SOFA reference with an RMSE of 4.1 mm (5.56% of the 73 mm workspace). This indicated low end-to-end latency and confirmed BiPneu's compatibility with high-level software ecosystems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents BiPneu as a contribution to the field of soft robotics, specifically addressing challenges related to pneumatic actuation control.

**Key Contributions:**
- **Scalable and Cost-Efficient Hardware Platform:** BiPneu offers a multi-channel bipolar-pressure pneumatic system utilizing cost-effective solenoid on/off valves, combined with a modular, stackable hardware design that supports scalability (demonstrated with 16 channels, expandable). This addresses the need for compact and economical solutions compared to systems reliant on expensive proportional valves or bulky per-channel pumps.
- **High-Performance Bipolar Pressure Regulation:** The proposed Dual-Mode Sliding-Mode Controller (DM-SMC), built upon a detailed hybrid electro-pneumatic model and incorporating hysteresis-supervised mode selection, achieves accurate and responsive pressure regulation across both positive and negative pressure ranges. This controller demonstrated superior performance in terms of tracking accuracy, control effort, and reduced valve switching compared to well-tuned PID and even some model predictive control strategies in simulations and experiments. Its computational efficiency makes it suitable for real-time embedded applications.
- **Seamless Software Ecosystem Compatibility:** The ROS 2-based server-client architecture facilitates integration with various high-level software stacks, including physics-based simulators and learning-based frameworks. This compatibility reduces development friction for researchers working on advanced control algorithms or complex robotic tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Soft Robot Capabilities:** By providing precise and responsive bipolar pressure control, BiPneu enables soft robotic actuators to achieve larger motion ranges, more versatile actuation modes, and dynamic interactions with their environment. This can lead to the development of soft robots with improved dexterity, adaptability, and force control.
- **Advancement in Control Strategies:** The DM-SMC demonstrates a robust and computationally efficient approach to controlling highly nonlinear, switched pneumatic systems. This can serve as a foundation for future work on advanced control strategies, including model-based reinforcement learning and sim-to-real policy transfer, where accurate and reliable low-level control is crucial.
- **Broader Accessibility for Soft Robotics Research:** The cost-effectiveness and scalability of BiPneu make it more accessible to a wider range of researchers and educators, potentially accelerating innovation in soft robotics. Its user-friendly software interface and integration capabilities reduce the barrier to entry for developing complex soft robotic applications.
- **Applications in Diverse Fields:** The capabilities demonstrated in dynamic ball manipulation and real-time teleoperation suggest potential applications in areas such as rehabilitation devices (e.g., soft exoskeleton gloves), safe human-robot interaction, dexterous manipulation, and exploration in sensitive or unstructured environments. The ability to precisely control both positive and negative pressures expands the design space for soft actuators and their functional roles.

In summary, this work provides a comprehensive solution for bipolar pneumatic control in soft robots, bridging the gap between hardware capabilities, control performance, and software integration, thereby establishing a foundation for advanced soft robotic applications.
