# Class-wise Balancing Data Replay for Federated Class-Incremental Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The FedCBDR framework proposes a method for Federated Class-Incremental Learning that uses global-perspective data replay and task-aware temperature scaling to address class imbalance during incremental training. It consistently improves Top-1 accuracy by 2-15% over state-of-the-art baselines on CIFAR and TinyImageNet datasets while enhancing resistance to catastrophic forgetting in non-IID settings.

## Problem

- Exemplar-based Federated Class-Incremental Learning (FCIL) methods suffer from class imbalance in local replay buffers due to a lack of global data distribution awareness during sample selection.
- A significant imbalance exists between the small number of replayed samples from old tasks and the large number of samples from newly arrived tasks, causing models to forget previous knowledge.
- Current methods for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in FCIL are either computationally costly (generative models) or prone to class imbalance issues in exemplar-based approaches.

## Method

- The Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR) module constructs class-balanced ==replay buffers== using a privacy-preserving mechanism involving ISVD for pseudo-feature construction and SVD for global feature aggregation, followed by importance-driven sample selection.
- The Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS) module dynamically calibrates confidence for old and new classes using distinct temperature factors and re-weights the cross-entropy loss to mitigate imbalance between replayed and new task data.
- The training process combines these modules, first optimizing for initial tasks and then employing the TTS-enhanced loss for subsequent class-incremental learning rounds.

## Results

- FedCBDR consistently achieved the highest Top-1 accuracy, demonstrating 2-**15%** improvement over six state-of-the-art baselines on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and TinyImageNet datasets across various heterogeneity levels.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR) and Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS) modules contribute significantly to performance, with their combination yielding the best overall results.
- The framework exhibited superior resistance to catastrophic forgetting, maintaining higher accuracy and slower degradation throughout the incremental learning process compared to all evaluated baselines.
- Per-class sample distributions in the replay buffer were maintained closer to average by FedCBDR, validating its effectiveness in achieving balanced class-wise sampling and coverage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving global awareness in sample selection for replay buffers, even under privacy constraints, is vital for constructing truly class-balanced memory in federated environments.
> - Dynamically adjusting the model's confidence and loss contribution for samples from old versus new tasks effectively counteracts the forgetting induced by class imbalance during incremental learning.
> - The combination of globally coordinated class-balanced data replay and task-aware loss adjustment provides a synergistic approach to significantly improve resistance to catastrophic forgetting in FCIL.
