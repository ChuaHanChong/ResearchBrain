# Class-wise Balancing Data Replay for Federated Class-Incremental Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The FedCBDR framework proposes a method for Federated Class-Incremental Learning that uses global-perspective data replay and task-aware temperature scaling to address class imbalance during incremental training. It consistently improves Top-1 accuracy by 2-15% over state-of-the-art baselines on CIFAR and TinyImageNet datasets while enhancing resistance to catastrophic forgetting in non-IID settings.

## Problem

- Exemplar-based Federated Class-Incremental Learning (FCIL) methods suffer from class imbalance in local replay buffers due to a lack of global data distribution awareness during sample selection.
- A significant imbalance exists between the small number of replayed samples from old tasks and the large number of samples from newly arrived tasks, causing models to forget previous knowledge.
- Current methods for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in FCIL are either computationally costly (generative models) or prone to class imbalance issues in exemplar-based approaches.

## Method

- The Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR) module constructs class-balanced ==replay buffers== using a privacy-preserving mechanism involving ISVD for pseudo-feature construction and SVD for global feature aggregation, followed by importance-driven sample selection.
- The Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS) module dynamically calibrates confidence for old and new classes using distinct temperature factors and re-weights the cross-entropy loss to mitigate imbalance between replayed and new task data.
- The training process combines these modules, first optimizing for initial tasks and then employing the TTS-enhanced loss for subsequent class-incremental learning rounds.

## Results

- FedCBDR consistently achieved the highest Top-1 accuracy, demonstrating 2-**15%** improvement over six state-of-the-art baselines on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and TinyImageNet datasets across various heterogeneity levels.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR) and Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS) modules contribute significantly to performance, with their combination yielding the best overall results.
- The framework exhibited superior resistance to catastrophic forgetting, maintaining higher accuracy and slower degradation than all baselines, with per-class replay-buffer distributions kept closer to average, validating balanced class-wise sampling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving global awareness in sample selection for replay buffers, even under privacy constraints, is vital for constructing truly class-balanced memory in federated environments.
> - Dynamically adjusting the model's confidence and loss contribution for samples from old versus new tasks effectively counteracts the forgetting induced by class imbalance during incremental learning.
> - The combination of globally coordinated class-balanced data replay and task-aware loss adjustment provides a synergistic approach to significantly improve resistance to catastrophic forgetting in FCIL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the growing field of Federated Class-Incremental Learning (FCIL), a complex area at the intersection of Federated Learning (FL) and Class-Incremental Learning (CIL).

**Federated Learning (FL)** is a distributed machine learning paradigm that enables multiple clients to collaboratively train a shared global model without directly sharing their raw data. This approach is highly valued for its ability to enhance data privacy, leverage diverse data sources, and build generalized models, making it applicable across various sensitive domains like healthcare and finance.

**Class-Incremental Learning (CIL)**, also known as Continual Learning, focuses on enabling a model to learn new tasks and classes sequentially without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. The primary challenge in CIL is "catastrophic forgetting," where a model's performance on older tasks degrades significantly after learning new ones.

**Federated Class-Incremental Learning (FCIL)** combines these two paradigms, introducing the dynamic challenge of continually adding new classes across geographically distributed and data-heterogeneous clients. This amplifies the complexities of both FL and CIL: the global model must integrate evolving knowledge from diverse clients while mitigating catastrophic forgetting, often with non-Independent and Identically Distributed (non-IID) data.

Existing approaches to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in FCIL can be broadly categorized:
- **Generative-based Replay**: These methods use generative models (e.g., GANs, VAEs) to synthesize representative samples from historical tasks. While they avoid storing actual private data, they incur high computational costs for training generative models and are often limited by the quality and fidelity of the synthesized samples. Examples include TARGET and LANDER, which are used as baselines in this paper.
- **Exemplar-based Replay**: These methods directly store a small subset of real samples (exemplars) from previous tasks in a local memory buffer on each client. This approach leverages high-quality raw data but is highly susceptible to **class imbalance**. Due to privacy constraints and the lack of a global view, local clients often select samples that lead to an unbalanced distribution of classes in their replay buffers, undermining the retention of prior knowledge. Re-Fed is a prominent example of this category.
- **Knowledge Distillation-based Methods**: These techniques transfer knowledge from older model versions to the current one, often by aligning output predictions or regularizing important parameters. They avoid storing raw data but can suffer from knowledge degradation over time, especially as the number of tasks increases. FedLwF and FedEWC are examples.

This paper positions itself within the exemplar-based replay paradigm, directly tackling its primary weakness: **class imbalance**. It identifies that existing exemplar-based methods suffer from a lack of global awareness in sample selection, leading to imbalanced replay buffers. Furthermore, it addresses the imbalance between replayed (old) and newly arrived classes during local training. By proposing a method that ensures class-balanced replay through global coordination and dynamically adjusts learning for task imbalance, FedCBDR aims to significantly improve the effectiveness of data replay in FCIL, making it a more robust and practical solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives of this research are motivated by the significant challenges posed by class imbalance in Federated Class-Incremental Learning (FCIL) using data replay strategies. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Address Class Imbalance in Replay Buffers**: Traditional exemplar-based data replay methods in FCIL typically operate with only local information, leading to highly imbalanced distributions of classes within the replay buffers on individual clients. This lack of global awareness means that certain classes might be over-represented while others are severely under-represented or even entirely forgotten, hindering the model's ability to retain knowledge from previous tasks. The first objective is to develop a mechanism that can construct a **class-balanced memory** across clients with a global perspective, ensuring fair representation for all learned classes.
2. **Mitigate Imbalance Between Replayed and New Classes**: Even with a balanced replay buffer, the number of samples from previous tasks (replayed) is often much smaller than the number of samples from the newly arrived task, leading to a long-tail problem during training. This imbalance can cause the model to become overconfident in majority classes (new tasks) and neglect minority classes (old tasks), accelerating catastrophic forgetting. The second objective is to dynamically adjust the learning process to balance the predictive confidence and importance given to samples from previous and current tasks during local model optimization.
3. **Enhance Robustness and Generalization**: Ultimately, the motivation is to develop a FCIL framework that is more resilient to data heterogeneity and task dynamics, leading to improved generalization performance and better resistance to catastrophic forgetting across a sequence of incremental tasks.

By tackling these interconnected class imbalance issues, the authors aim to create a more effective and practical replay-based FCIL system that can maintain high accuracy on both old and new classes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **FedCBDR (Class-wise Balancing Data Replay)**, tackles the challenges of class imbalance in FCIL through two main components: the Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR) module and the Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS) module.

#### 3.1. Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR)

The GDR module addresses the issue of class imbalance within the replay buffer by establishing a privacy-preserving global coordination mechanism for selecting representative samples. This is crucial because local clients lack the global data distribution awareness needed for balanced sampling.

- **Privacy-Preserving Pseudo Feature Construction**: Clients first extract local features `X_k` from their data using the current global model's feature extractor. To preserve privacy, each client `k` encrypts its local feature matrix `X_k` using an Inverse Singular Value Decomposition (ISVD) approach. This involves multiplying `X_k` with a client-specific random orthogonal matrix `P_k` and a globally shared orthogonal matrix `Q`, resulting in a pseudo-feature matrix `X'_k = P_k X_k Q`. These `X'_k` matrices are then uploaded to the server, rather than raw data or original features.
- **Global Feature Aggregation and SVD**: The central server aggregates all `X'_k` from participating clients into a global pseudo-feature matrix `X'`. It then performs Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) on `X'` (`X' = U' Σ' V'`) to capture the underlying global data structure.
- **Importance-Driven Sample Selection**: The server extracts client-specific submatrices `U_k` from the global left singular vectors `U'`. Each client then computes a **leverage score** for each of its samples. A higher leverage score indicates that a sample is more representative and contributes more significantly to the global data structure, making it a better candidate for replay.
- **Class-Balanced Sampling**: Clients send their leverage scores to the server, which aggregates and normalizes them to form a global sampling distribution `p`. The server then performs i.i.d. sampling based on this global distribution, selecting sample indices that are representative of all classes across all clients. To ensure unbiased estimation during aggregation, the sampling weights of selected samples are adjusted.
- **Replay Buffer Update**: The server communicates the selected sample IDs back to the respective clients. Clients then store the corresponding data points in their local replay buffers `B_pre,k`. This process ensures that the replay buffers are populated with samples that are globally representative and class-balanced, mitigating the problem of local-view-induced class imbalance.

#### 3.2. Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS)

The TTS module addresses the class imbalance between samples from previous tasks (replayed) and newly arrived tasks during local training, which often leads to poor retention of older knowledge.

- **Dynamic Confidence Calibration**: TTS introduces a multi-level dynamic confidence calibration strategy. It uses different temperature scaling factors for the logits corresponding to old (`τ_old`) and new (`τ_new`) classes. A lower temperature (`τ_old < 1`) for old classes sharpens their logits, increasing the model's confidence and making it less prone to forgetting. Conversely, a slightly higher temperature (`τ_new > 1`) for new classes can soften their logits, preventing overconfidence and allowing for better generalization.
- **Re-weighted Cross-Entropy Loss**: In addition to temperature scaling, TTS incorporates a re-weighted cross-entropy loss. It assigns adaptive weights (`ω_old` and `ω_new`) to samples from previous and current tasks. By giving a higher weight to samples from previous (minority) tasks, the module amplifies their contribution to the loss function during optimization, ensuring that the model pays sufficient attention to retaining old knowledge. The combined loss function `L_TTS` effectively balances the predictive confidence and learning emphasis between majority and minority classes.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

The training process is divided into two stages:
- **Stage 1: Initial Task Optimization**: For the first task, clients train their local models using a standard cross-entropy loss on their local data `D_k^(1)`.
- **Stage 2: Class-Incremental Optimization**: For subsequent tasks, clients train on a combined dataset `D_k^(s) U B_pre,k` (new task data plus replayed samples). Here, the `L_TTS` loss function is employed, incorporating the task-aware temperature scaling and sample re-weighting to mitigate class imbalance.

By integrating GDR for balanced memory construction and TTS for dynamic loss adjustment, FedCBDR provides a comprehensive solution for effective class-incremental learning in a federated setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of FedCBDR demonstrates its effectiveness across a range of benchmark datasets and heterogeneous settings.

- **Performance Comparison (Tables 2 & 3, Appendix Tables 6 & 7)**:
 - FedCBDR consistently achieves the highest Top-1 accuracy across CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and TinyImageNet datasets, under varying levels of data heterogeneity (Dirichlet β parameters 0.1, 0.5, 1.0) and different task split configurations (3/5, 5/10, 10/20 tasks).
 - It generally outperforms six state-of-the-art baselines (Finetune, FedEWC, FedLwF, TARGET, LANDER, Re-Fed) by a significant margin, ranging from 2% to 15% Top-1 accuracy improvement.
 - While LANDER sometimes performs competitively (e.g., CIFAR100, 5 tasks, β=1.0), it relies on generating a much larger number of synthetic samples (over 10,000 per task), which incurs substantial computational overhead. FedCBDR achieves superior or comparable performance with real, limited exemplars.
 - Exemplar-based methods with class-balanced sampling (FedCBDR) consistently outperform those with class-imbalanced strategies (Re-Fed), highlighting the importance of balancing replay buffer distributions.

- **Ablation Study (Table 4)**:
 - **GDR Module's Contribution**: Incorporating the Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR) module alone significantly improves performance across all cases, particularly in highly heterogeneous settings (β = 0.1). This confirms its effectiveness in mitigating catastrophic forgetting by enabling class-balanced sampling with global awareness.
 - **TTS Module's Contribution**: Using the Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS) module alone also leads to consistent improvements over the baseline Finetune, especially in more challenging multi-task scenarios. This demonstrates its ability to address intra-client class imbalance by dynamically adjusting logit sharpness and sample weights.
 - **Combined Effectiveness**: The full FedCBDR method (GDR + TTS) yields the best overall performance, indicating that the two modules are complementary and synergistically address different aspects of class imbalance and catastrophic forgetting.

- **Performance under Incremental Tasks (Figure 3, Appendix Figure 7)**:
 - FedCBDR consistently maintains higher accuracy curves throughout the incremental learning process compared to all baselines.
 - It exhibits a slower performance degradation as the number of tasks increases, demonstrating stronger resistance to catastrophic forgetting. This is particularly evident in complex settings like CIFAR100 and TinyImageNet with more tasks.

- **Quantitative Analysis of Replay Buffer Size (Table 5, Appendix Table 8)**:
 - FedCBDR shows significant performance advantages over Re-Fed, especially under limited memory budgets. Its performance improvements are more stable and significant as the buffer size increases, demonstrating efficient utilization of memory.
 - FedCBDR even surpasses LANDER (which uses a large generative replay buffer) under certain conditions, underscoring the efficiency of its balanced real-sample replay.

- **Sensitivity Analysis of Hyperparameters (Figure 4)**:
 - The study reveals that while temperature scaling and sample re-weighting are crucial, their optimal performance depends on proper tuning of `τ_old`, `τ_new`, `ω_old`, and `ω_new`.
 - Slightly higher weight and lower temperature for previous-task samples (`ω_old` = 1.1, `τ_old` = 0.9) and lower weight and higher temperature for new samples (`ω_new` = 0.9, `τ_new` = 1.1) generally lead to better performance. Inappropriate choices can hinder performance.

- **Per-Class Sample Distributions (Figure 5, Appendix Figure 8)**:
 - Visualizations confirm that FedCBDR maintains a per-class sample distribution in the replay buffer that is consistently closer to the average across different task stages.
 - In contrast, Re-Fed exhibits noticeable skewness and fluctuations, sometimes failing to retain samples for certain classes entirely. This validates FedCBDR's effectiveness in achieving balanced class-wise sampling and maintaining class coverage.

- **Visualization of Model Attention and Logits Adjustment (Figure 6)**:
 - Grad-CAM visualizations show that without data replay, the model struggles to classify previous task samples and attend to relevant regions.
 - FedCBDR's data replay corrects predictions and guides attention to semantically important areas.
 - The TTS module further improves the model's ability to distinguish confusing classes, enhancing accuracy and prediction stability for tail classes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Class-wise Balancing Data Replay for Federated Class-Incremental Learning" makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact:

- **Addressing a Critical Gap in FCIL**: The paper directly tackles the pervasive problem of class imbalance in data replay for Federated Class-Incremental Learning, a challenge that significantly limits the effectiveness and scalability of existing exemplar-based methods. By proposing a privacy-preserving mechanism for global coordination and a dynamic loss adjustment strategy, FedCBDR offers a robust solution to a core issue in this evolving field.
- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness**: Achieving 2%-15% Top-1 accuracy improvements over six state-of-the-art methods across diverse datasets and heterogeneity levels is a strong indicator of FedCBDR's effectiveness. Its superior resistance to catastrophic forgetting and ability to maintain higher accuracy over numerous incremental tasks demonstrates a significant step forward in building more stable and generalized federated continual learning systems.
- **Privacy-Preserving Global Coordination**: The Global-perspective Data Replay (GDR) module introduces an innovative method to leverage global data distribution insights without compromising client data privacy. The use of ISVD for pseudo-feature construction and SVD for global pattern extraction offers a practical and secure way to achieve balanced replay buffer construction, a crucial aspect for real-world FL deployments.
- **Improved Long-Tail Learning**: The Task-aware Temperature Scaling (TTS) module effectively mitigates the long-tail problem arising from the imbalance between replayed and new task data. By dynamically adjusting logits and re-weighting loss contributions, TTS ensures that minority classes (from older tasks) are not overlooked, fostering better knowledge retention and overall generalization.
- **Practical Implications for Real-World FL**: The demonstrated ability to effectively manage catastrophic forgetting and class imbalance under varying degrees of data heterogeneity makes FedCBDR highly relevant for real-world federated learning applications where data streams are continuous, and client data distributions are naturally non-IID. This could accelerate the adoption of CIL in practical FL scenarios such as healthcare diagnostics, autonomous driving, and IoT device management.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
Despite its impressive performance, the authors acknowledge several limitations and outline promising future directions:
1. **Computational Overhead**: The current approach involves feature transmission and SVD computations, which might incur costs. Future work aims to investigate lightweight sampling strategies to reduce feature transmission and computational costs.
2. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: The TTS module's performance is sensitive to hyperparameter tuning (temperature and weighting factors). Developing more robust post-sampling balancing methods that are less sensitive to hyperparameters is a key area for improvement.
3. **Complex Scenarios**: Extending FedCBDR to more complex scenarios, which may involve additional challenges like device heterogeneity, system failures, or more intricate task relationships, is another promising direction.

In conclusion, FedCBDR represents a significant advancement in Federated Class-Incremental Learning, providing an effective, privacy-preserving, and robust solution to the critical challenge of class imbalance in data replay. Its contributions pave the way for more adaptable and generalizable federated learning systems capable of continuous learning in dynamic, distributed environments.
