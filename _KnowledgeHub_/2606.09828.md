# Latent Spatial Memory for Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Mirage introduces a video world model that uses a latent spatial memory to generate 3D-consistent videos, achieving up to 10.57 times faster end-to-end generation and 55 times lower GPU memory usage compared to prior RGB-based memory approaches while enhancing geometric coherence.

## Problem

- Maintaining 3D spatial consistency across long video sequences generated by world models is challenging, leading to geometric drift.
- Existing 3D memory systems, typically based on explicit RGB point clouds, incur substantial computational costs due to repetitive 'rasterize-and-encode' operations between latent and pixel space.
- Converting latent features to pixel-space RGB and then re-encoding results in information loss, introducing artifacts and providing a suboptimal conditioning signal for generative models.

## Method

- Mirage develops a '==latent spatial memory==' (LSM) that stores scene information as latent-attributed 3D points directly in the diffusion model's latent space, avoiding pixel-space conversion.
- It employs an iterative initialize-readout-update cycle over overlapping video chunks, populating the LSM with VAE-encoded latents and estimated depths, explicitly excluding dynamic objects and sky regions.
- A ControlNet-style side branch is used to integrate the latent memory readouts directly into the video diffusion backbone, efficiently conditioning the generative process without altering the pretrained VAE.

## Results

- Mirage achieved an average score of 70.36 on the WorldScore benchmark, outperforming all compared systems with superior 3D Consistency (92.21) and Photometric Consistency (93.95) on the static axis.
- The system demonstrated up to 10.57 times faster end-to-end video generation and up to 55 times lower GPU memory usage for the 3D cache compared to RGB point cloud baselines.
- On RealEstate10K, Mirage secured the best SSIM (**0.779**) and LPIPS (**0.250**) for novel view synthesis and achieved the highest PSNR_C (20.05) and SSIM_C (**0.825**) in closed-loop consistency tests.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Operating 3D memory directly within the VAE latent space dramatically improves efficiency and preserves feature fidelity compared to RGB-based memory systems.
> - Filtering dynamic objects and sky from the persistent 3D cache is essential for maintaining long-term geometric stability and preventing memory contamination.
> - A two-stage training approach, initially freezing the diffusion backbone, effectively adapts pre-trained video transformers to leverage latent spatial memory while preserving their generative priors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has witnessed substantial advancements through large-scale video diffusion models, which can synthesize photorealistic video sequences. These models are increasingly considered as potential "world simulators" capable of internalizing visual dynamics and generating future observations based on camera trajectories or actions. A significant challenge in this paradigm is maintaining 3D spatial consistency across generated frames. Without an explicit mechanism for spatial memory, even advanced generative models tend to accumulate geometric drift, leading to frames that are individually plausible but collectively inconsistent when viewed within a shared 3D coordinate system.

Previous research has attempted to address this challenge by augmenting video generators with persistent 3D representations, typically in the form of explicit point cloud memories constructed in RGB space. Systems such as WonderJourney, InvisibleStitch, WonderWorld, Voyager, and Spatia store observed scene information as colored 3D points. While this approach effectively enforces multi-view consistency, it introduces two primary bottlenecks. First, the computational cost is substantial, as it requires a repetitive "round trip" between latent and pixel space at each generation step. This involves rendering millions of colored points at full resolution into an RGB image and subsequently re-encoding that image back into a latent tensor for the diffusion model. This process is resource-intensive and dominates the overall generation time. Second, this RGB detour inherently leads to information loss. The translation to pixel space, followed by re-encoding through a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), discards the rich features of the model's native latent representation. The surrogate conditioning signal produced after rasterization and re-encoding may be distorted by VAE reconstruction errors, rasterization artifacts, and distribution mismatches.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by identifying and seeking to overcome these computational and representational limitations of existing RGB-based spatial memory systems. It aims to develop a more efficient and less lossy approach to maintaining 3D spatial consistency in video world models by operating directly within the latent space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to introduce a novel method for maintaining 3D spatial consistency in video world models that addresses the computational and representational bottlenecks of prior explicit RGB-based spatial memory systems. The authors aim to achieve this through the development of a "latent spatial memory."

The motivations for this work are detailed as follows:
- **Computational Efficiency:** Prior systems that use explicit RGB point clouds require a costly "rasterize-and-encode" round trip at every conditioning step. This process involves rendering a high-resolution RGB image from the 3D memory and then encoding it back into the VAE latent space. This is computationally prohibitive, especially as the 3D memory grows, and significantly slows down end-to-end video generation. The authors are motivated to eliminate this per-step pixel-space detour to achieve faster generation.
- **Preservation of Latent Features:** The RGB detour in existing methods is inherently lossy. Converting latent features to pixel-space RGB colors and then re-encoding them discards rich, learned features native to the diffusion model's latent representation. This process can introduce reconstruction errors, rasterization artifacts, and distribution mismatches, leading to a suboptimal conditioning signal for the generative backbone. A key motivation is to preserve the fidelity of the latent representation by operating entirely within the latent space.
- **Reduced Memory Footprint:** Explicit RGB point clouds store millions of colored points, leading to a large memory footprint, particularly as the generation horizon extends. The authors aim to significantly reduce the GPU memory required for the 3D cache by leveraging the compressed nature of the latent space.
- **Enhanced Geometric Consistency:** Despite the computational costs, maintaining robust 3D spatial consistency, especially for long camera trajectories or repeated visits to previously observed regions, remains a challenge. The motivation is to improve the overall geometric coherence and avoid "geometric drift" and "parallax violations" that can occur in videos generated by models without a robust spatial understanding.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ultimate motivation is to combine these efficiency and representational benefits to achieve competitive or state-of-the-art performance in 3D-consistent video generation, as measured by established benchmarks for world modeling and novel view synthesis.

By addressing these points, the research aims to make 3D-consistent video generation more practical, scalable, and higher quality for long-horizon trajectories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, named Mirage, introduces **latent spatial memory**, a persistent 3D cache that stores scene information directly in the diffusion model's latent space, thereby circumventing pixel-space reconstruction. Mirage operates through an iterative initialize-readout-update cycle over overlapping video chunks.

#### 3.1. Latent Spatial Memory Representation

The memory `M` is represented as a set of latent-attributed 3D points, `M = {(p_i, f_i)}`, where `p_i ∈ R^3` is a world-space coordinate and `f_i ∈ R^C` is a full-channel latent feature vector directly from the VAE encoder. This `f_i` matches the native input space of the diffusion backbone.

#### 3.2. Initialization

- The process begins with an initial frame `I_0`.
- `I_0` is encoded into a VAE latent tensor `z_0` using the encoder `E`.
- A metric depth map `D_0` for `I_0` is estimated using a feed-forward reconstructor (e.g., DepthAnything3). The depth map is downsampled to the latent grid resolution, and intrinsics `K_0` are scaled accordingly.
- Each latent cell `(u, v)` from `z_0` is then back-projected into world space using its corresponding depth `D_0(u, v)`, scaled intrinsics `K_ℓ`, and camera pose `E_0` via standard pinhole back-projection (`π⁻¹`). This creates a memory element `(p_uv, f_uv)` where `f_uv` is the latent token `z_0[:, v, u]`.
- Crucially, dynamic objects and sky regions are detected (using an open-vocabulary entity extractor like Qwen3-VL and a video segmenter like SAM3) and excluded from the initial memory construction to ensure only static scene content is cached, preserving geometric coherence.

#### 3.3. Latent-space Memory Readout

- For each target camera view defined by extrinsics `E_t` and intrinsics `K_t`, the latent memory `M` is queried.
- All memory points `p_i` are projected onto the target camera grid at latent resolution.
- For each target latent cell `(u, v)`, a z-buffering mechanism is employed to identify and retain only the frontmost projected point. The latent token `f_i` associated with this frontmost point is retrieved, forming the target-view latent feature tensor `ˆz_t(u, v)`.
- Cells that receive no projected points (i.e., genuinely unseen regions) are zero-filled. A binary visibility mask `m_t` is also generated to indicate which cells received a projected point, allowing the denoiser to differentiate observed from unobserved regions.
- `ˆz_t` and `m_t` are concatenated and fed into a ControlNet-style side branch. This side branch injects the memory features directly into the video diffusion backbone.
- Segment-aware rotary encodings are also used to mark noisy target, clean preceding, and clean reference frames within a single forward pass.
- This readout mechanism operates entirely in latent space, eliminating the need for pixel-resolution rendering of the cache and per-step VAE encoding, which are major bottlenecks in RGB-based methods.

#### 3.4. Autoregressive 3D Cache Update

- After a chunk of frames has been generated by the diffusion model, Mirage updates its persistent memory `M` with the newly observed static scene content.
- Depth and camera parameters for the newly generated frames `I_t` are re-estimated using the feed-forward reconstructor.
- The generated frames `I_t` are re-encoded into clean VAE latents `˜z_t = E(I_t)`.
- These re-encoded latent tokens are then back-projected into the cache using the same depth-guided lifting process as in initialization.
- Similar to initialization, moving objects and sky regions are identified and excluded from this update to prevent transient or geometrically unreliable content from contaminating the persistent cache.
- The latents of the current chunk are also carried forward to serve as short-term temporal context for the subsequent chunk.

#### 3.5. Efficient Adaptation to Existing Diffusion Models

Mirage adapts a pretrained camera-controllable video diffusion transformer (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B) using a two-stage training strategy:
1. **Stage 1:** The backbone and VAE are frozen. Only the ControlNet-style side branch is trained. This step aligns the latent memory readouts with the backbone's feature space without altering the pretrained generative prior.
2. **Stage 2:** Rank-64 LoRA adapters are attached to the self-attention projections of the backbone. These adapters are jointly optimized with the side branch. This allows for lightweight adaptation of the backbone to the memory condition while preserving its pre-trained appearance and motion priors.
Both stages employ the flow-matching objective on the target frames for optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Mirage validates its effectiveness in both generation quality and efficiency across standard benchmarks.

#### 4.1. World Generation on WorldScore

- Mirage achieved the highest **Average Score** of 70.36 on the WorldScore benchmark, outperforming all compared systems, including memory-augmented baselines like Spatia and various foundation video generators.
- The system demonstrated a notable advantage on the **Dynamic Score** partition, while remaining competitive on the **Static Score** partition.
- Mirage exhibited superior performance in **3D Consistency** (92.21) and **Photometric Consistency** (93.95) on the static axis, indicating its ability to maintain geometric scaffolding without the representational loss associated with RGB caches.
- Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4) demonstrated Mirage's ability to maintain 3D coherence on out-of-domain prompts (e.g., outdoor and natural scenes) that differ significantly from its training distribution (RealEstate10K), whereas RGB-cache baselines exhibited stretched textures and memory-free generators showed geometric drift.

#### 4.2. Novel View Synthesis and Closed-Loop Consistency on RealEstate10K

- In the standard novel view synthesis setting on RealEstate10K, Mirage achieved the best **SSIM** (0.779) and **LPIPS** (0.250) scores, and a competitive **PSNR** (18.38), surpassing view memory baselines (SEVA, VMem) and other 3D-aware video generators.
- In the closed-loop evaluation, which specifically assesses long-horizon drift by requiring a return to the starting viewpoint, Mirage achieved the highest **PSNR_C** (20.05) and **SSIM_C** (0.825), and was second-best on **LPIPS_C** (0.228). These results suggest that the latent spatial memory effectively anchors the generator to a coherent geometric representation even after extended camera detours.
- Qualitative results (Figures 6, 7, 8) showed Mirage producing sharper and more structurally consistent frames during camera motion, while baselines frequently displayed geometric drift, texture distortion, or accumulated artifacts. The authors attribute the improved quality to the stored latent feature vectors carrying richer semantic and textural information than three RGB color channels.

#### 4.3. Efficiency

- A core finding is the significant efficiency gains. Mirage achieved up to **10.57× faster end-to-end video generation** compared to RGB point cloud baselines.
- It also demonstrated up to **55× lower GPU memory usage** for the 3D cache compared to RGB-cache baselines (Figure 5).
- The per-frame cache-read time and peak cache VRAM grew slowly for Mirage, reflecting that its operations occur at latent resolution and avoid the costly pixel-space round trip. In contrast, RGB caches exhibited rapid growth in both time and memory, often exhausting GPU memory on longer trajectories.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Latent vs. RGB memory (Table 3):** Replacing the latent cache with an RGB point cloud, while keeping other settings constant, resulted in a decrease in Average Score and weakened 3D and photometric consistency, confirming the representational benefits of latent space.
- **Feature vs. Geometry Downsampling:** Downsampling geometry to the native latent grid was found to be more effective than upsampling features to pixel resolution, as it preserves fidelity to the generator's native latent space and is computationally cheaper.
- **Dynamic Object Filtering (Table 3):** Disabling the mask that excludes moving objects and sky from cache updates led to degraded long-horizon stability and reduced 3D and photometric consistency, indicating its importance in preventing contamination of the persistent memory.
- **Two-Stage Training (Table 3):** A single-stage training schedule yielded lower quality, suggesting that freezing the backbone during initial adaptation of the side branch stabilizes convergence and improves final performance.
- **Depth Source Sensitivity (Table 4):** Mirage demonstrated robustness to the choice of depth estimator. While using a stronger reconstructor like DepthAnything3 yielded the best results, performance remained competitive with noisier depth sources, implying the ControlNet-style branch treats the projected cache as a soft geometric hint.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of latent spatial memory by Mirage represents a notable advancement in the field of video world models, addressing fundamental limitations in maintaining 3D spatial consistency. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several key aspects:

- **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** By operating entirely within the VAE latent manifold and eliminating the computationally intensive "rasterize-and-encode" round trip, Mirage achieves significant speedups (up to 10.57x faster) and substantial reductions in GPU memory usage (up to 55x less). This makes 3D-consistent video generation practical for much longer trajectories and larger scene explorations, which was previously a barrier for RGB-based methods. This efficiency can accelerate research and development in world modeling, simulation, and controllable video synthesis.

- **Enhanced Representational Fidelity:** Storing and querying memory directly in the latent space avoids the information loss inherent in converting latent features to pixel-space RGB values and then re-encoding them. This preserves the rich semantic and textural information encoded in the diffusion model's native latent features, leading to higher quality and more coherent generated videos. This approach leverages the strengths of latent diffusion models more effectively.

- **Robust 3D and Photometric Consistency:** Mirage demonstrates state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance on benchmarks like WorldScore and RealEstate10K, particularly in metrics related to 3D and photometric consistency. Its ability to maintain coherence even after large camera detours or in challenging closed-loop scenarios suggests a more robust understanding of scene geometry. This is critical for applications requiring stable, explorable virtual environments.

- **Foundation for Future World Models:** The proposed latent spatial memory and the Mirage framework offer a more efficient and principled architecture for future video world models. By demonstrating that memory can be effectively managed in latent space, this work opens new avenues for integrating complex 3D scene understanding into generative models without incurring prohibitive computational costs. This could lead to more intelligent agents that can navigate and interact with persistent, consistent virtual worlds.

- **Practical Applications:** The ability to generate long, geometrically consistent videos with controllable camera trajectories has direct applications in virtual reality, gaming, content creation, architectural visualization, and autonomous navigation simulations. The improved efficiency makes these applications more feasible on a wider range of hardware.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges a limitation regarding its handling of dynamic objects. The current approach excludes moving entities from the persistent memory because their geometry is considered unreliable. Consequently, Mirage does not maintain the state of dynamic actors across chunks, meaning scenes dominated by pervasive motion benefit less from the cache. A natural direction for future work is to develop methods for persisting dynamic content across chunks, which would further enhance the realism and consistency of generated worlds containing moving elements.
