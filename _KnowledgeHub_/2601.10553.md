# Inference-time Physics Alignment of Video Generative Models with Latent World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FAIR, Meta Superintelligence Labs introduced WMReward, a method that aligns video generative models with physical principles during inference by leveraging a latent world model (VJEPA-2) as a differentiable reward function. The approach led to a first-place finish in the ICCV 2025 PhysicsIQ Challenge with a score of 62.64% and demonstrated an 11.4% higher human preference for physics plausibility compared to baselines.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art video generative models frequently produce content that violates fundamental physics principles, limiting their real-world utility.
- Physical inaccuracies restrict model applications in critical domains like robotics, autonomous driving, and general world modeling.
- Improving physics plausibility through inference-time methods, using off-the-shelf reward models, remains an underexplored area in video generation.

## Method

- A differentiable reward function, **WMReward**, is derived from a pre-trained ==latent world model (VJEPA-2)== by measuring ==prediction surprise== as the cosine distance between predicted and actual future latent states.
- Three sampling schemes (==gradient-based Guidance==, ==Best-of-N search==, and a combined approach) are employed to steer the denoising trajectory of pre-trained generative models towards higher physics plausibility during inference.
- The method is evaluated across image-and-text-to-video (I2V), video-and-text-to-video (V2V), and text-to-video (T2V) generation using models like MAGI-1 and vLDM.

## Results

- Secured **first place** in the ICCV 2025 Perception Test PhysicsIQ Challenge with a final score of **62.64%**, outperforming previous state-of-the-art by **+7.42%**.
- Human preference studies on PhysicsIQ and VideoPhy benchmarks showed an **+11.4%** higher win rate for WMReward-enhanced videos in terms of physics plausibility over baselines, alongside improvements in visual quality.
- WMReward improved physics consistency on the VideoPhy benchmark by **+8.1%** for MAGI-1 and **+6.9%** for vLDM compared to baseline models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent world models, which learn predictive dynamics in compressed spaces, possess an inherent understanding of physical laws that can be effectively repurposed as a physics plausibility reward signal.
> - Physics alignment of video generative models can be achieved as an inference-time problem without modifying the generative model's architecture or training regimen.
> - Scaling inference-time computation through reward-guided sampling strategies reliably enhances the physical correctness of generated videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

State-of-the-art video generative models have demonstrated capabilities in producing visually coherent content. However, these models frequently generate videos that violate fundamental physics principles. This limitation restricts their practical utility in applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and general world modeling.

Prior research addressing physical implausibility in video generation has largely focused on enhancing the pre-training or post-training stages of generative models. These efforts typically involve injecting explicit physics information or leveraging learned physics priors through fine-tuning or distillation. This approach is based on the premise that the models inherently lack sufficient physics understanding during their initial training, which often relies on minimizing pixel or feature-level reconstruction errors.

In contrast, a less explored area of research posits that physically plausible videos might exist within the data manifold already learned by pre-trained generative models. This perspective suggests that improving physics plausibility could be an inference-time problem, where methods are devised to search or steer the generation process towards more physically correct outcomes. Existing work in this inference-time domain for video generation has primarily involved using vision-language models (VLMs) to rephrase prompts or plan motion, particularly with motion-controllable generative models. In the image generation field, inference-time methods, such as reward models for candidate selection or guidance, have been more extensively developed.

This paper addresses the gap in applying off-the-shelf reward models to enhance the physics plausibility of video generation during inference. It integrates recent advancements in latent world models, which have demonstrated a capacity for physics understanding by learning predictive dynamics in compressed latent spaces. By repurposing such a world model as a reward function, the work aims to align video generative models with physical principles without requiring modifications to the generative model's training. This positions the research as a significant contribution to the inference-time alignment paradigm for improving the realism and utility of generated video content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the physical plausibility of videos generated by state-of-the-art video generative models. The authors frame this as an inference-time alignment problem, aiming to steer the generation process towards outputs that adhere more closely to physical laws, rather than modifying the underlying generative model's architecture or training regimen.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and challenges in current video generation:
1. **Physical Implausibility in SOTA Models**: Despite advancements in visual quality, leading video generative models frequently produce content that violates basic physics. This includes inconsistencies in object interactions, fluid dynamics, gravity, and temporal continuity.
2. **Limitation on Utility**: Such physical inaccuracies restrict the broader utility of these models. For instance, in applications like reliable world modeling, robotics, or autonomous driving, physically inconsistent video outputs can lead to erroneous predictions or unsafe control decisions. Increased user satisfaction also depends on the realism of generated content.
3. **Under-explored Inference-time Solutions**: While much effort has focused on injecting physics knowledge during the pre-training or post-training of generative models, the authors identify that shortcomings in physics plausibility can also arise from suboptimal inference strategies. They note that the use of reward models for inference-time improvement, which has been explored in image generation, remains an underexplored avenue for video generation.
4. **Leveraging Latent World Models' Physics Prior**: The authors observe that latent world models, particularly those based on Joint-Embedding-Predictive-Architecture (JEPA) like VJEPA-2, have exhibited strong physics understanding. These models learn to predict future states in a compressed latent space, concentrating on core physical dynamics and ignoring superficial visual details. This characteristic makes them potentially well-suited to assess the physical plausibility of generated videos.

Therefore, the core motivation is to develop and validate a method, WMReward, that repurposes the inherent physics understanding of a latent world model (VJEPA-2) as a reward signal. This reward signal is used to search and guide multiple candidate denoising trajectories during the video generation process, thereby aligning the outputs with physical principles at inference time. The work aims to demonstrate that scaling test-time computation through such alignment can lead to substantially improved physics plausibility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology focuses on treating physics plausibility improvement as an inference-time alignment problem, where a pre-trained video generative model is steered using a reward function derived from a latent world model.

**Problem Formulation:**
The objective is to sample from a reward-weighted tilted distribution $p^*(x) \propto w(x)p(x)$, where $p(x)$ is the distribution of the pre-trained video generative model and $w(x) > 0$ is a weighting function derived from a physics plausibility reward $r(x)$.

**Latent World Model Reward Signal (WMReward):**
The reward function, WMReward, is based on the concept of "prediction surprise" from a latent world model, specifically VJEPA-2.
- **VJEPA-2 (Joint-Embedding-Predictive-Architecture):** This model learns representations by predicting unmasked video regions from masked ones in a compressed latent space. It comprises a context encoder ($E_\theta$) and a predictor network ($P_\phi$). By focusing on future state prediction in latent space, VJEPA-2 is encouraged to learn high-level spatiotemporal features that capture predictable dynamics and ignore irrelevant pixel-level details, thereby developing physics understanding.
- **Reward Mechanism:** The core intuition is that a robust world model should accurately predict the future in physically plausible videos. Conversely, a large divergence between its prediction and a generated video suggests lower physical plausibility.
 - A sliding window of length (C context frames + M prediction horizon frames) is applied to a generated video $x$.
 - For each window position $k$, VJEPA's context encoder processes $x_{k-C+1:k}$ to produce an encoding.
 - The predictor network $P_\phi$ uses this encoding to predict the latent representations for the future frames $x_{k+1:k+M}$, denoted as $\hat{z}_k$.
 - The encoder $E_\theta$ also processes the full window $x_{k-C+1:k+M}$ to obtain the actual latent representations $z_k$ for the generated future frames.
 - The reward $r(x)$ is calculated as the average cosine distance (1 - cosine similarity) between the predicted future representations ($\hat{z}_{fut_k}$) and the actual generated future representations ($z_{fut_k}$) across all valid windows $K$:
 $r(x) = \frac{1}{|K|} \sum_{k \in K} (1 - \cos(\hat{z}_{fut_k}, z_{fut_k}))$.
 - Lower cosine distance (higher similarity) implies higher reward, indicating better physical coherence. WMReward is designed to be differentiable with respect to the generated video.

**Instantiation of Sampling Schemes:**
Three sampling schemes are employed to draw samples from the reward-weighted distribution, leveraging the differentiability of WMReward:
1. **Guidance ($\nabla$):** This gradient-based method uses $w(x) = \exp(\lambda r(x))$. It modifies the score function of the diffusion model by adding a term proportional to the gradient of the reward function with respect to the denoised estimate $x_{0|t}$. This guides the denoising trajectory towards regions of higher reward (more physically plausible videos).
2. **Best-of-N Search (BoN):** This gradient-free approach generates $N$ independent video samples from the base model $p(x)$ using different random seeds. The WMReward function $r(x)$ then evaluates each of these $N$ candidates, and the sample with the highest reward is selected as the final output. This effectively samples from a distribution $p^*(x) \propto p(x)[F(r(x))]^{N-1}$, where $F(\cdot)$ is the CDF of $r(x)$.
3. **$\nabla$ + BoN:** This combines both approaches. First, $N$ samples are generated using the gradient-based guidance method. Then, from these $N$ guided samples, the one with the highest WMReward score is selected. This strategy aims to achieve stronger alignment without requiring excessively large guidance weights (which can destabilize generation) and further mitigates potential approximation errors from guidance by allowing for selection among multiple refined candidates.

The methodology is evaluated across different video generation setups: image-and-text-to-video (I2V), video-and-text-to-video (V2V), and text-to-video (T2V), using models like MAGI-1, Sora2, and a video latent diffusion model (vLDM).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating the effectiveness of WMReward in improving the physics plausibility of video generation:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance on PhysicsIQ Benchmark:**
 - The approach achieved a final score of 62.64% in the ICCV 2025 Perception Test PhysicsIQ Challenge, securing first place and outperforming the previous state-of-the-art by 7.42%.
 - Specifically, on the PhysicsIQ benchmark, the WMReward($\nabla$+BoN) method yielded a score of 62.0% for V2V generation with MAGI-1, surpassing the previous best by 6.78%. For I2V generation, it improved upon Sora2's performance by 4.13%, reaching 46.43% with Sora2 and WMReward(BoN).
 - This indicates consistent and substantial improvements across both image-conditioned (I2V) and multiframe-conditioned (V2V) video generation tasks.

- **Superiority of Latent World Model Reward:**
 - WMReward consistently outperformed alternative physics plausibility signals derived from other foundation models such as VideoMAE (a self-supervised masked autoencoder using pixel reconstruction error) and VLM-based rewards (Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL, which rely on text-based judgment).
 - This suggests that predictive latent-space surprise, as captured by VJEPA-2, serves as a more effective proxy for physics plausibility compared to pixel-level reconstruction or vision-language model judgments.

- **Validation through Human Preference Study:**
 - A human study on the PhysicsIQ and VideoPhy benchmarks showed significant preference for videos generated with WMReward($\nabla$+BoN) over baselines.
 - For PhysicsIQ, human judges showed an 11.4% higher win rate in physics plausibility for WMReward-enhanced videos from MAGI-1, alongside improvements in visual quality and prompt alignment. Similar trends were observed for vLDM.
 - On VideoPhy, WMReward also demonstrated improved physics plausibility and visual quality by human evaluation.

- **Improved Physics Consistency in Text-conditioned Generation (T2V):**
 - On the VideoPhy benchmark, WMReward significantly improved the physics consistency of MAGI-1 and vLDM by 8.1% and 6.9%, respectively, outperforming all baseline models.
 - While a slight decrease in semantic adherence was noted for vLDM, attributed to the text-agnostic nature of VJEPA surprise, the human study confirmed that the gains in physics plausibility and visual quality generally led to a net overall improvement.

- **Scaling Behavior with Search Space:**
 - The PhysicsIQ score demonstrated a steady improvement as the number of particles (N) for Best-of-N search increased.
 - The $\nabla$+BoN sampling strategy showed stronger scaling, effectively sharpening the distribution of PhysicsIQ scores towards higher-plausibility regions, indicating that increased inference-time compute can reliably enhance physical correctness.

- **Computational Overhead:**
 - WMReward(BoN) parallelizes N sampling trajectories, resulting in computation time scaling linearly with N but maintaining the same memory footprint per trajectory as the baseline.
 - Guidance introduces additional computation primarily from gradient backpropagation, which is proportional to the number of guidance steps. This highlights a compute-performance trade-off adaptable to available resources.

- **Impact on Perceptual Qualities:**
 - VBench evaluators indicated that physics improvements achieved by WMReward did not impair general perceptual quality; instead, they led to small improvements in metrics such as subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness, temporal flickering, imaging quality, and aesthetic quality. This suggests that adherence to physical principles can contribute to overall visual realism and quality.

- **Robustness of WMReward Design:**
 - The effectiveness of WMReward proved relatively stable across variations in VJEPA-2 hyperparameters, including context length, prediction horizon, and stride.
 - Performance gains were observed to scale with the size of the VJEPA backbone (e.g., from ViT-huge to ViT-giant), suggesting that more powerful world models yield better results without requiring fine-tuning of the generative model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work demonstrates the viability of leveraging latent world models for improving the physics plausibility of video generative models, presenting a new direction for enhancing the realism and utility of synthesized videos.

The significance and potential impact of this research include:

- **Advancement in Video Generation Quality:** By significantly improving the adherence of generated videos to physical laws, the research contributes to making synthetic video content more realistic and believable. This addresses a critical limitation of current video generative models, which often prioritize visual fidelity over physical coherence.
- **New Paradigm for Physics Alignment:** The formulation of physics plausibility as an inference-time alignment problem offers a novel and practical approach. Instead of complex retraining or architectural modifications of large generative models, this method allows for post-hoc improvement using an external reward signal. This flexibility can make it easier to adapt existing generative models to new requirements or to incorporate evolving physics understanding from world models.
- **Leveraging Latent World Model Capabilities:** The work highlights the untapped potential of latent world models, like VJEPA-2, as sophisticated physics understanding tools. By repurposing their predictive capabilities as a reward, it demonstrates a transferable knowledge mechanism that can benefit other downstream tasks, specifically video generation. This validates the idea that models trained for prediction in compressed latent spaces develop meaningful representations of physical dynamics.
- **Scalability and Computational Trade-offs:** The study of various sampling schemes (BoN, Guidance, $\nabla$+BoN) illustrates how the method can be adapted to different computational budgets. It shows that increasing inference-time compute can progressively lead to better physics plausibility, offering a spectrum of compute-performance trade-offs for practical deployment.
- **Broader Applications:** Producing physically plausible videos is crucial for enabling more reliable and robust downstream applications.
 - **World Modeling:** Improved physics consistency is fundamental for creating accurate digital twins or simulated environments that mirror real-world dynamics.
 - **Robotics:** Physically coherent video generation can aid in training robotic agents in simulation, allowing them to learn from more realistic interactions and predictions.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Generating physically consistent scenarios can enhance the development and testing of autonomous systems by providing realistic simulations of road conditions, object interactions, and environmental physics.
 - **User Experience:** For general content creation, physically accurate videos enhance immersion and user satisfaction, reducing the presence of jarring or illogical events.

Looking forward, the authors identify several avenues for further improvement. These include developing stronger and more searchable video generative models, enhancing the physics understanding of latent world models to cover a broader range of phenomena (e.g., material properties like weight and friction), and designing more efficient search algorithms, particularly for early stages of denoising where reward estimates can be noisy. Furthermore, integrating semantic information into physics rewards could mitigate observed trade-offs with prompt alignment, paving the way for more comprehensive and controllable physics-aware video generation.
