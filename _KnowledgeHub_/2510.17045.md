# Video Reasoning without Training

> [!summary] Summary
> V-Reason is an inference-time optimization framework that enhances video reasoning in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) without additional training, achieving an average accuracy gain of +1.4% on certain benchmarks while reducing output tokens by 58.6% and inference time by up to 37% compared to RL-trained models. This approach leverages an entropy-based objective to steer the model's internal thinking process, scaling effectively to larger LMMs.

## Problem

- Existing video reasoning methods for LMMs incur high computational costs during both training and inference due to complex architectures, extensive datasets, or verbose reasoning traces.
- There is a limited understanding of the internal reasoning mechanisms within LMMs, making it difficult to interpret, control, or optimize their 'thinking' process effectively.
- Conventional approaches like Chain-of-Thought prompting or reinforcement learning with human feedback exacerbate computational overhead, particularly for high-resolution video data.

## Method

- ==V-Reason== introduces a training-free, inference-time optimization framework that modifies LMM behavior by dynamically controlling the model's output distribution entropy.
- It employs a small, trainable ==Value-Cache Controller (∆V)== added only to the value cache of the last decoder layer for video tokens, preserving the base model's frozen weights.
- A novel ==Entropy Switching Loss== optimizes ∆V, adaptively encouraging either micro-exploration (increasing entropy) or micro-exploitation (decreasing entropy) based on the exponential moving average of entropy.

## Results

- V-Reason consistently improved baseline accuracy, achieving average gains of **+**1.0%** to +1.4%** on various video reasoning benchmarks for the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, and **+3.9%** on the challenging MMVU dataset.
- The method drastically reduced output token length by **58.6%** compared to RL-trained Video-R1-7B and **21.4%** compared to the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, leading to up to **37%** lower inference time.
- V-Reason scaled effectively to larger models, providing a **+3.0%** gain for Qwen2.5-VL-32B on MMVU and a **+0.4%** improvement even for the Qwen2.5-VL-72B model, demonstrating its generalizability and efficiency benefits across scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs exhibit distinct macro-exploration (increasing entropy) and macro-exploitation (decreasing entropy) phases during token generation, observable via output distribution entropy.
> - Higher-quality reasoning models demonstrate lower and delayed entropy maxima during exploration, alongside more pronounced micro-exploration/exploitation cycles, indicating 'deeper thinking'.
> - These internal entropy dynamics can be directly manipulated at inference time to steer an LMM's reasoning process towards higher performance without requiring extensive retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), has seen considerable advancements in their reasoning capabilities. Current approaches for enabling reasoning typically involve several computationally intensive methods. These include Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, supervised fine-tuning with CoT (CoT-SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL) with a thinking-before-answering format. While successful, these techniques, especially CoT-SFT and RL-based methods, demand substantial computational resources during both training and inference. This computational burden is particularly pronounced in video reasoning tasks, which involve high-resolution visual data and multiple frames, leading to longer processing times and increased memory requirements.

Recent research has begun to interpret RL-based reasoning as a sampling process designed to more effectively search for reasoning traces within the pre-trained knowledge of a baseline model. This perspective raises questions regarding whether RL-based models genuinely expand reasoning abilities or primarily enhance the search for existing knowledge. Against this backdrop, there is an emerging interest in developing more efficient, training-free alternatives that can unlock inherent reasoning capabilities without incurring the high costs associated with extensive training and verbose output generation. Inference-time optimization methods have gained traction in other domains, such as diffusion models, for improving control and consistency, and some recent works have explored applying these concepts to LLMs to reduce computational cost and enhance interpretability. However, the specific challenge of achieving video reasoning without additional training has not been extensively addressed in the literature.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper addresses two primary research questions:
1. Can inference-time metrics characterize the thinking process of video reasoning models, and can these metrics differentiate between higher- and lower-quality LMMs?
2. Can such metrics be utilized to formulate novel inference-time optimization objectives for video reasoning, thereby obviating the need for additional model training?

To motivate its approach, the research conducts an analysis of the model's output distribution entropy at each generation step for instruction-tuned LMMs of varying sizes. This analysis reveals a characteristic pattern: all models exhibit an initial phase of increasing entropy, followed by a decrease. This is termed "macro-exploration" (uncertainty and search for solutions) and "macro-exploitation" (identifying and converging on a solution). Crucially, larger and more accurate models display a later entropy peak and a lower final entropy. This indicates a more deliberate and extended exploration phase ("longer thinking") and more confident convergence.

The observation that better models exhibit a delayed and lower entropy peak, along with a lower final entropy, suggests that their "thinking" process involves a series of more pronounced "micro-exploration" and "micro-exploitation" cycles. These cycles involve temporary increases in entropy to consider alternative reasoning paths, followed by decreases as a path is pursued. The research hypothesizes that stronger reasoning models are more decisive in these micro-cycles, leading to a slower increase in overall entropy during macro-exploration and ultimately a lower, delayed peak. Conversely, smaller models tend to peak early, exhibiting a shorter thinking phase and converging prematurely, potentially leading to confident but incorrect answers.

Building on these insights, the key motivation for V-Reason is to develop a training-free inference-time optimization method. This method aims to directly modulate the micro-exploration and micro-exploitation behavior of baseline instruction-tuned LMMs. The objective is to encourage the model to "think longer but more efficiently" by exploring alternatives without generating excessive tokens, thereby mimicking the reasoning patterns of stronger, often RL-trained, models. This approach seeks to bridge the performance gap between baseline and RL-trained models while offering significant efficiency benefits.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named V-Reason (Video-Reason), is an inference-time optimization technique designed to enhance video reasoning in LMMs without requiring supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Inference-time Optimization

The core of V-Reason involves two main components: a value-cache controller and an entropy-based optimization objective.

- **Controller Parameters**: V-Reason augments the LMM with a small, trainable parameter set called the Value-Cache Controller (ΔV). This controller is specifically added to the value cache (V_L) of the *last* decoder layer of the model, targeting only the video token locations. All other model layers remain frozen, and no modifications are applied to input or output text tokens. ΔV is initialized to zero and updated every k-th generated output token (where k > 1). To ensure stability and prevent the controller from destabilizing the pre-trained model, a normalization step is applied: V_new_L = (V_L + ΔV) / ||V_L + ΔV|| * ||V_L||. This normalization preserves the original magnitude of the cache vector, ensuring that ΔV only introduces a directional update.

- **Optimization Objective (Entropy Switching Loss)**: The objective of the optimization is to guide the model's behavior to exhibit patterns similar to high-quality reasoning models. Specifically, this means:
 - Decreasing the rate of entropy growth during the macro-exploration phase by promoting more pronounced micro-exploration and micro-exploitation cycles.
 - Reducing the final entropy during the macro-exploitation phase.

 The **Entropy Switching Loss** (L_switch) is defined as L_switch(ΔV) = -α_k * H_k, where H_k is the entropy of the output distribution for the k-th generated token. The coefficient α_k ∈ {-1, +1} determines whether the loss encourages an increase (α_k = 1) or a decrease (α_k = -1) in entropy.

 The determination of α_k is dynamic and guided by the Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the entropy (H_ema_t = β*H_ema_(t-1) + (1-β)*H_t, with β=0.98). The EMA provides a less noisy, smoothed representation of the entropy trend. The switching coefficient α_k is set as:
 - α_k = +1 if H_ema_k ≥ H_ema_peak (i.e., when EMA is increasing or at a new peak, encouraging exploration).
 - α_k = -1 if H_ema_k < H_ema_peak (i.e., when EMA is decreasing, encouraging exploitation).
 H_ema_peak denotes the maximum EMA value observed before the current step k.

 This mechanism encourages the model to be more decisive in its micro-cycles. During the macro-exploration stage, it promotes "longer thinking" by slowing entropy growth and delaying the peak. Once the EMA reaches a global maximum, α_k switches to -1, initiating the macro-exploitation phase where entropy is minimized for faster convergence to a confident answer. This adaptive approach allows V-Reason to determine the required exploration and exploitation for each sample.

- **Theoretical Guarantees**: The paper provides theoretical propositions stating that under mild smoothness and boundedness assumptions, the entropy updates induced by the Entropy Switching Loss remain stable, and the process stays within the compact interval [0, log n]. Additionally, the EMA smoothing is proven to attenuate high-frequency fluctuations, delay entropy maxima, and enforce bounded oscillations by switching α_k to -1 once a global EMA maximum is reached.

#### 3.2. Efficiency Considerations: V-Reason(Lite)

To address potential memory and computational overheads, V-Reason introduces a "Lite" variant.
- **KV-Cache Pruning**: Before the V-Reason optimization, V-Reason(Lite) optionally prunes 50% of the lowest-norm video tokens from the Key-Value (KV) cache of *all* decoder layers. This significantly reduces KV-cache memory overhead and halves the size of the trainable controller.
- **Value Cache Normalization**: The value update for the Lite variant is V_new_L = (V_pruned_L + ΔV) / ||V_pruned_L + ΔV|| * ||V_L||, which still maintains the magnitude of the unpruned video value cache. This helps mitigate error introduced by pruning.
While V-Reason involves additional computation for optimization, the reduction in output token length typically leads to overall inference efficiency gains compared to verbose RL/CoT-SFT models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate V-Reason on Qwen-2.5-VL-Instruct models, ranging from 3B to 72B parameters, across six video reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MMVU, MVBench, TempCompass, VideoMME). Tasks include Multiple-Choice QA and Regression, evaluated by classification accuracy and Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA).

- **Performance Improvement**:
 - V-Reason significantly outperforms baseline instruction-tuned models, achieving an average improvement of 1.4% over the base model.
 - It narrows the accuracy gap with RL-trained models, such as Video-R1-7B, to within 0.6% on average (excluding MRA scores). For instance, V-Reason(Lite)-7B (128 px) achieved 65.0% on MMVU, surpassing Video-R1-7B (64.0%).
 - The method is most effective when the solution relies on knowledge already present within the pretrained model, which requires better search strategies to surface. For tasks with underrepresented knowledge (e.g., VSI-MRA regression), gains are modest but still positive (+1.4%).
 - Gains are consistent across model scales (3B to 72B parameters) and different LMM backbones (e.g., +5.1% over MMVU for Qwen3-VL-8B).
 - V-Reason shows robustness to varying frame counts (16/32 frames) and resolutions (128/256 pixels), with larger gains often observed with more frames.
 - Both the full V-Reason and V-Reason(Lite) variants surpass the base model, with the Lite variant performing comparably or slightly better in several cases, while offering memory benefits.

- **Efficiency Gains**:
 - V-Reason substantially reduces output token length, generating 58.6% fewer tokens than the RL-trained Video-R1 model and 21.4% fewer than the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model on average.
 - This token reduction translates to significant wall-clock inference time savings, with V-Reason achieving up to 37% lower average inference time compared to Video-R1.
 - V-Reason(Lite) further reduces average GPU memory requirements by 11.6% compared to the full V-Reason, making it suitable for GPUs with more limited memory (e.g., 32GB V100).

- **Ablation Studies and Analysis**:
 - Qualitative analysis and entropy plots confirm that V-Reason successfully induces a delayed EMA peak and lower overall entropy, indicative of longer and more confident exploration.
 - Ablations on optimization step-size demonstrate that accuracy generally increases with more frequent optimization steps, but V-Reason consistently outperforms the base model even with fewer steps.
 - Comparison with alternative optimization objectives (min-entropy, max-entropy) validates the effectiveness of the proposed entropy-switching loss in achieving the desired reasoning dynamics.
 - The observed entropy patterns (macro-exploration/exploitation with micro-cycles, delayed peaks in better models) are shown to generalize to other LLM architectures like Llama and Phi, suggesting broader applicability of these insights.
 - V-Reason demonstrates complementary benefits when combined with state-of-the-art decoding methods like min-p and top-H, indicating its ability to enhance reasoning independently of sampling strategies.
 - The method also provides consistent, albeit smaller, improvements (0.2-0.7%) on models already trained with CoT-SFT or RL, suggesting it refines their existing search processes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a training-free, purely inference-time optimization method for video reasoning, which is a novel contribution to the field. By leveraging theoretically-grounded insights into the entropy dynamics of LMMs, V-Reason offers a computationally efficient alternative to traditional supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning approaches for enhancing reasoning capabilities.

The significance of V-Reason lies in several aspects:
- **Computational Efficiency**: It offers a path to improving video reasoning accuracy without the high computational and data costs associated with extensive training, making advanced reasoning more accessible and deployable on resource-constrained platforms. The substantial reduction in output token length further contributes to inference efficiency.
- **Theoretical Grounding**: The paper provides a formal characterization of reasoning behavior through entropy dynamics, including macro- and micro-exploration/exploitation phases, which could serve as a foundation for future research in understanding and controlling model "thinking" processes.
- **Scalability and Adaptability**: V-Reason's consistent performance gains across various model sizes, architectures, and input resolutions, combined with its compatibility with different decoding strategies, suggests a broadly applicable and robust framework.
- **Bridging Performance Gaps**: The ability to narrow the accuracy gap with RL-trained models to within a small margin, without any additional training, demonstrates a compelling alternative for achieving high-quality reasoning.
- **Potential for Broader Impact**: While demonstrated for video reasoning, the insights into entropy dynamics and inference-time optimization could potentially extend to general multimodal reasoning and even pure large language models, opening new avenues for research in making AI models more adaptable, efficient, and robust.

Overall, V-Reason presents a paradigm shift towards unlocking latent reasoning capabilities in pretrained LMMs through intelligent, dynamic control at inference time, offering a balance between performance and efficiency.
