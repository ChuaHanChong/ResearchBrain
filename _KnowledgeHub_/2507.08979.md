# PRISM: Reducing Spurious Implicit Biases in Vision-Language Models with LLM-Guided Embedding Projection

> [!summary] Summary
> Queen’s University researchers developed PRISM, a data-free and task-agnostic framework that leverages large language models to identify spurious correlations and projects VLM embeddings to mitigate implicit biases. This method achieved state-of-the-art worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds (84.2%) and CelebA (84.0%) datasets, maintaining high overall performance and reducing reliance on confounding background features.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) inherit and amplify spurious correlations from pre-training data, leading to biased, inaccurate, and non-robust predictions.
- Existing debiasing methods often require predefined bias categories, external visual data, or compromise the VLM's overall accuracy.
- There is a lack of truly data-free and task-agnostic debiasing solutions that preserve core semantic information and overall performance.

## Method

- ==PRISM==, a ==two-stage data-free and task-agnostic framework==, is introduced to mitigate VLM biases without predefined categories or external visual data.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) are prompted to dynamically identify spurious attributes and generate controlled scene descriptions encoding both the true class and spurious context.
- A ==learnable projection matrix== is optimized using a novel ==Latent space Debiasing (LD) loss==, designed to minimize spurious attribute influence while preserving core semantic alignment.

## Results

- PRISM achieved state-of-the-art worst-group (WG) accuracy of **84.2%** on Waterbirds and **84.0%** on CelebA, outperforming all data-free baselines and many image-based methods.
- The method preserved high overall accuracy (**93.6%** on Waterbirds, **86.9%** on CelebA), effectively balancing fairness gains with general performance.
- Qualitative analysis through representation maps confirmed that PRISM successfully disentangles features, leading to more structured and separated clusters with reduced influence from background information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can be effectively leveraged to dynamically identify spurious correlations from textual co-occurrence statistics, removing the reliance on human-defined bias categories.
> - A targeted projection of the VLM's embedding space, rather than full model fine-tuning, can effectively remove spurious biases while preserving semantic information and computational efficiency.
> - The novel Latent space Debiasing (LD) loss, with its intra-class invariance and inter-class separation components, is crucial for learning a robust debiasing projection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training), has revolutionized multimodal AI by enabling seamless interaction between visual and textual data. These models excel in tasks like zero-shot classification and cross-modal retrieval by aligning image and text representations in a shared embedding space. However, a critical challenge, and the focus of this paper, is that VLMs inherit and often amplify systemic biases present in their vast pre-training datasets. These biases, stemming from spurious correlations (e.g., associating 'camel' with 'desert' or 'panda' with 'bamboo'), lead to skewed predictions, reduced robustness, and potential real-world harms, making bias mitigation a crucial area of research.

Existing debiasing strategies for VLMs generally fall into three categories:
1. **Feature-space transformations:** Methods like FairerCLIP aim to mathematically transform feature embeddings to remove spurious correlations. While rigorous, they can be computationally expensive and risk removing essential semantic information.
2. **Prompt-based debiasing:** Approaches such as Orth-Cali and Orth-Proj modify textual inputs or projections based on predefined biased prompts to mitigate bias. A major limitation is their struggle to generalize to biases not anticipated or included in the fixed prompts, and they might compromise overall model performance.
3. **Adapter design:** DFR and contrastive adapters introduce lightweight modules or retrain specific layers to adjust representations. These often require external visual data and can also suffer from the trade-off between bias reduction and overall accuracy.

A significant gap in the existing literature is the lack of truly **data-free and task-agnostic** debiasing solutions that do not rely on predefined bias categories or external visual data, and critically, do not compromise the VLM's overall performance. Methods like BendVLM leverage language models but often require labeled images or LLM calls during inference, incurring costs and latency.

PRISM directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel framework that is both data-free and task-agnostic. It leverages the emergent capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to *dynamically identify* spurious correlations, thus circumventing the need for predefined bias categories or external datasets. This LLM-guided approach, combined with a learnable embedding projection, represents a significant step towards more practical and ethically robust VLM deployment, positioning PRISM at the forefront of bias mitigation research for foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the PRISM research is to **develop a novel, effective, and efficient method for mitigating spurious implicit biases in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly CLIP.** This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Addressing Inherent VLM Biases:** VLMs, despite their impressive capabilities, are known to absorb and amplify biases present in their massive training datasets. These "spurious correlations" (e.g., a specific background consistently appearing with a particular object) lead to inaccurate, unfair, and non-robust predictions in real-world scenarios. The core motivation is to rectify this fundamental flaw.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Debiasing Methods:**
 - **Reliance on Predefined Bias Categories:** Many existing methods require explicit knowledge of bias categories (e.g., specific genders, races, or backgrounds), which makes them difficult to scale to task-agnostic settings or adapt to newly discovered biases.
 - **Need for External Data:** A significant portion of debiasing techniques requires auxiliary visual data (labeled or unlabeled) for training, which can be expensive, privacy-sensitive, or simply unavailable.
 - **Performance Compromise:** Often, debiasing methods achieve improved fairness at the cost of overall model accuracy, diminishing the VLM's utility.
3. **Achieving Data-Free and Task-Agnostic Debiasing:** The authors explicitly aim for a solution that does not require additional external visual data (data-free) and can be applied across various tasks without extensive re-configuration (task-agnostic). This would make the debiasing process more practical, scalable, and adaptable.
4. **Preserving Core Semantic Information:** The objective is to remove spurious correlations without discarding essential semantic content, thereby ensuring that the VLM's overall performance and its fundamental ability to align image and text embeddings are not compromised.
5. **Leveraging LLM Capabilities for Bias Discovery:** Motivated by LLMs' ability to capture co-occurrence statistics in text, the research seeks to harness these models to *dynamically generate* descriptions of spurious correlations, moving beyond static, human-defined bias lists.

In essence, PRISM aims to provide a robust, generalizable, and practical debiasing solution that enhances fairness and trustworthiness in VLMs without sacrificing their impressive zero-shot classification capabilities or requiring burdensome additional resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRISM (Projection-based Reduction of Implicit Spurious bias in vision-language Models) is a two-stage, data-free, and task-agnostic debiasing framework designed for VLMs like CLIP. It operates without relying on predefined bias categories or external visual data.

**Stage 1: Leveraging LLMs to Identify Spurious Correlations and Generate Scene Descriptions**

- **Dynamic Bias Discovery:** Instead of relying on human-defined or dataset-specific bias categories, PRISM employs a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM), such as GPT-4o, to identify potential spurious correlations.
- **Prompting the LLM:** The LLM is prompted with simple class labels (e.g., "A photo of a <class y>"). Based on its vast training corpus, where concepts often co-occur spuriously, the LLM is instructed to generate a list of potential spurious attributes associated with these labels. For example, for "waterbird," it might suggest "water background" or "swimming."
- **Generating Controlled Scene Descriptions:** Once the spurious attributes (`A`) are identified, the LLM is further prompted to create a set of "controlled scene descriptions" (`T_a,y`). Each description corresponds to a specific *group* `g = (a, y)`, representing a combination of a true class label (`y`) and a spurious attribute (`a`). For instance, if 'duck' is a class and 'lake' and 'jungle' are spurious attributes, the LLM generates descriptions like "A photo of a duck in a lake" and "A photo of a duck in a jungle." These descriptions are crucial as they explicitly encode both the class and spurious context, allowing the model to learn to disentangle them.

The underlying rationale is that LLMs, by optimizing for likelihood over massive text corpora, learn the statistical co-occurrence of words. When a concept (e.g., "camel") frequently appears with another (e.g., "desert") in the text, the LLM encodes this as a high conditional probability, even if the correlation is spurious rather than causal. PRISM exploits this inherent property of LLMs to dynamically generate bias-aware textual data.

**Stage 2: Learning a Debiasing Projection of the Embedding Space**

- **Objective:** The goal of this stage is to learn a projection matrix `P` that transforms the VLM's embedding space. This projection aims to minimize the influence of spurious attributes while preserving the core semantic alignment between images and text. Importantly, PRISM avoids fine-tuning the entire VLM, thus preserving its generality and computational efficiency.
- **Latent space Debiasing Loss (LD Loss):** A novel contrastive-style loss function, `L_LD`, is introduced to guide the learning of `P`. This loss has two main components:
 - **(a) Intra-class, inter-attribute term:** This term encourages *invariance*. It minimizes the distance between text embeddings of scene descriptions that belong to the *same true class* (`y=y'`) but are associated with *different spurious attributes* (`a != a'`). For example, "A photo of a duck in a lake" and "A photo of a duck in a jungle" should be pulled closer together in the projected space. This ensures that the core class features are robustly represented, irrespective of confounding spurious cues.
 - **(b) Inter-class, intra-attribute term:** This term enforces *separation*. It employs a margin-based penalty to maximize the distance between text embeddings of scene descriptions that share the *same spurious attribute* (`a=a'`) but belong to *different classes* (`y != y'`). For instance, "A photo of a duck in a lake" and "A photo of a finch in a lake" should be pushed further apart. This ensures that even when the spurious context is shared, the class-discriminative features remain distinct.
- **Zero-shot Inference:** After `P` is learned, zero-shot classification is performed in the projected, debiased embedding space. Both the image embeddings `ϕ_I(x)` and the class text embeddings `ϕ_T(t_k)` are transformed by `P` before computing the similarity for prediction: `ˆy = arg max_k ⟨P(ϕ_I(x)), P(ϕ_T(t_k))⟩`.

**PRISM-mini (Variational Approach):**
A computationally lighter variant, PRISM-mini, is also proposed. Instead of optimizing a projection using the LD loss, it directly calculates `P` by orthogonally projecting the embedding space against the embeddings of the spurious attributes identified by the LLM. This makes it highly efficient but potentially less effective than the full PRISM method.

The methodology strategically leverages the strengths of LLMs for dynamic bias identification and employs a targeted, trainable projection to achieve effective debiasing without the need for extensive data or model fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experiments on standard bias benchmark datasets, Waterbirds and CelebA, using the CLIP-ViT-L/14 backbone (and CLIP-RN50 in the appendix), comparing PRISM against a range of baselines. The evaluation metrics include Overall Accuracy (Acc), Worst-Group accuracy (WG), ΔWG (WG improvement over zero-shot), ΔAcc (overall accuracy improvement), and Gap (Acc - WG).

**Key Findings:**

1. **State-of-the-Art Debiasing Performance:**
 - **Waterbirds:** PRISM achieves a WG accuracy of **84.2%**, significantly outperforming all other data-free debiasing methods (e.g., Orth-Cali: 68.8%, VisualDistiller: 42.7%) and even surpassing image-based methods like FairerCLIP (78.1%). It also shows a substantial ΔWG of 47.8%, indicating a dramatic improvement in the worst-performing subgroups.
 - **CelebA:** PRISM achieves a WG accuracy of **84.0%**, again outperforming all data-free baselines (e.g., Orth-Cali: 76.1%, RoboShot: 82.6%) and remaining highly competitive with image-based methods.
 - **Overall Accuracy Preservation:** Crucially, PRISM achieves these WG gains while maintaining high overall accuracy (93.6% on Waterbirds, 86.9% on CelebA), demonstrating its ability to mitigate bias without compromising general performance. The ΔAcc for PRISM is positive (4.3% for Waterbirds) or minimally negative (-0.7% for CelebA), significantly better than other methods that often show large accuracy drops.

2. **Effective Trade-off between Accuracy and Fairness:** Visual comparisons (Figure 1) clearly illustrate that PRISM achieves the most favorable trade-off, delivering the highest worst-group accuracy without a significant drop in overall accuracy, unlike many baselines that either perform well on WG but poorly on Acc, or vice versa.

3. **Improved Per-Group Accuracy and Reduced Disparities:** Per-group accuracy bar plots (Figure 3) show that PRISM consistently improves accuracy across all subgroups compared to the zero-shot baseline, effectively raising the worst-group performance and reducing performance disparities across different biased groups.

4. **Feature Disentanglement:** Qualitative analysis through representation maps (Figure 4) demonstrates that PRISM successfully disentangles features. In the original CLIP space, clusters are highly mixed due to reliance on spurious correlations. After PRISM's projection, the clusters become more structured and well-separated, confirming that the influence of background information is substantially reduced.

5. **Empirical Verification of Textual Bias:** The hypothesis that similar biases exist in the text domain alone is empirically confirmed (Table 2). Zero-shot classification on scene descriptions generated by the LLM (featuring different bird species in various locations) reveals similar WG and accuracy patterns as observed in image-based evaluations, validating the use of LLMs for bias identification.

6. **PRISM-mini Performance:** The lighter variation, PRISM-mini, also performs commendably, achieving the second-best debiasing performance among data-free methods (69.5% WG on Waterbirds, 82.6% on CelebA). This highlights its utility for resource-constrained scenarios.

7. **Ablation Studies and Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
 - **Number of Scene Descriptions:** There's an optimal range for the number of LLM-generated scene descriptions, where too few or too many can negatively impact WG performance (Figure 5).
 - **Margin Hyperparameter `m` in LD Loss:** The `m` hyperparameter influences inter-class separation. An optimal `m` of 0.6 is found to maximize WG performance by balancing semantic preservation and spurious correlation removal (Figure 6).

8. **Role of LLMs:** The choice of LLM significantly impacts PRISM's effectiveness. Using more capable LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) leads to better debiasing performance (WG of 84.0% with GPT-4o vs. 58.9% with Llama 3.2), confirming the importance of high-quality bias identification by the LLM (Table 3).

9. **Comparison with BendVLM (Appendix):** PRISM is highlighted as more practical and efficient than BendVLM, as it requires no labeled image data for training, no task-specific info at inference, no LLM calls at inference, and offers faster inference, while achieving superior WG AUROC (84.0% vs. 77.2% on CelebA).

In summary, PRISM establishes a new state-of-the-art for data-free debiasing in VLMs, effectively mitigating spurious biases without sacrificing overall performance, demonstrating excellent generalizability, and providing a robust, practical solution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of PRISM marks a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, particularly concerning the ethical and robust deployment of Vision-Language Models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Practical and Scalable Bias Mitigation:** PRISM offers a truly **data-free and task-agnostic** solution for debiasing VLMs. This is a monumental practical advantage over previous methods that often require costly labeled images, predefined bias categories, or extensive fine-tuning. By eliminating these dependencies, PRISM makes robust debiasing accessible to a wider range of applications and researchers, especially in scenarios where data collection is expensive, privacy-sensitive, or specific bias categories are unknown.
2. **Preservation of Overall Performance:** A critical achievement of PRISM is its ability to substantially improve worst-group accuracy (debiasing performance) without compromising the overall classification accuracy of the VLM. This addresses a common trade-off in fairness research, ensuring that models remain performant and useful across all demographics and contexts, rather than just becoming fairer.
3. **Dynamic Bias Identification via LLMs:** The novel use of LLMs to dynamically generate descriptions of spurious correlations is a paradigm shift. It moves beyond static, human-curated bias lists, allowing the debiasing process to adapt to unforeseen biases or those specific to particular tasks, thereby enhancing the generalizability and adaptability of the solution. This approach harnesses the semantic understanding encoded within LLMs in an innovative way for ethical AI.
4. **Enhanced Model Robustness and Fairness:** By systematically reducing reliance on spurious correlations, PRISM increases the robustness of VLMs to out-of-distribution data and enhances fairness across different subgroups. This is crucial for the responsible deployment of AI in sensitive domains like healthcare, law enforcement, and hiring, where biased predictions can have severe societal consequences.
5. **Computational Efficiency:** The two-stage design, particularly the learning of a lightweight projection matrix `P` rather than fine-tuning the entire VLM, ensures computational efficiency. The "PRISM-mini" variant further offers a highly resource-efficient option, broadening the applicability of the method. The fact that LLM calls are only needed once during training (not at inference) also makes it cost-effective for deployment.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** PRISM opens new avenues for research, particularly in exploring more sophisticated ways to leverage LLMs for bias discovery and mitigation. The paper itself suggests exploring non-linear projections for debiasing, hinting at the potential for even more nuanced bias removal. It also underscores the potential of synergistic approaches between different foundation models (LLMs and VLMs) for addressing complex AI challenges.

In conclusion, PRISM provides a highly effective, efficient, and ethically significant framework that addresses a core challenge in multimodal AI. By making VLM debiasing more accessible and comprehensive, it paves the way for the development and deployment of more trustworthy, fair, and robust AI systems across diverse applications.
