# ThinkGen: Generalized Thinking for Visual Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> ThinkGen presents a framework for visual generation that applies generalized Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning using a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to guide a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks including reasoning generation, image editing, and text rendering by enabling the model to “think” before generating images.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanisms for visual generation are scenario-specific, requiring significant re-tailoring for different tasks and limiting generalization.
- Prior attempts to integrate Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) into generative processes did not fully exploit their explicit CoT reasoning capabilities, often using them merely as feature providers.
- A general framework was lacking to effectively bridge the verbose CoT outputs of MLLMs with the input requirements of Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for varied visual generation tasks.

## Method

- A decoupled architecture combining a Qwen3-VL-8B-Think MLLM for ==CoT reasoning== and an OmniGen2-DiT-4B for image synthesis, connected by a linear layer, was developed.
- Visual Generation Instruction Refinement (VGI-Refine) extracts essential instruction tokens from MLLM outputs and concatenates learnable Prepadding States to regulate feature distribution, aiding short instructions.
- A novel multi-stage training paradigm, including Separable GRPO (SepGRPO), optimizes the MLLM to generate DiT-preferred instructions (MLLM-GRPO) and refines the DiT for high-quality image generation based on these instructions (DiT-GRPO).

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on reasoning generation (WISEBench), showing a +**21%** improvement with CoT reasoning and establishing a new benchmark.
- Demonstrated superior reasoning editing capabilities (RISEBench), improving from a score of 3.6 to 13.0, competitive with closed-source models like Gemini-2.0.
- Consistently improved metrics across various benchmarks, including semantic consistency (GenEval **0.89**), long-form generation (DPG-Bench 85.87), text rendering (CVTG Word Acc. **0.84**, NED **0.94**), and image editing (ImgEdit 4.21 with CoT).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning by MLLMs, when generalized, dramatically enhances visual generation performance across a wide range of complex tasks, demonstrating a true "thinking" capability.
> - The SepGRPO training strategy effectively decouples and optimizes the reasoning (MLLM) and generation (DiT) components, leading to flexible reward design, reduced learning complexity, and lower training costs.
> - Prepadding States are critical for aligning MLLM output feature distributions with DiT requirements, particularly improving performance for short or concise instructions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in their ability to perform complex reasoning. A key development has been **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning**, where models generate intermediate explicit steps to arrive at a solution. Initially popularized for understanding tasks in areas like mathematics, coding, and vision-language understanding, the extension of CoT reasoning to *generative tasks* is a burgeoning area of research.

Existing work in visual generation, especially with the rise of diffusion models, has largely focused on enhancing image quality, instruction following, or incorporating visual information. MLLMs have been integrated with diffusion models, often serving as powerful feature extractors to condition the generation process. However, a significant limitation of prior attempts to bring CoT to generation, such as those that conceptualize progressive token generation as CoT or decompose the process into specific steps, is their **scenario-specific nature**. These methods often require manual intervention or re-tailoring for different tasks, hindering their generalization and adaptation across diverse generative scenarios like text-to-image generation, image editing, reasoning generation, or reflection.

"ThinkGen" directly addresses this gap by proposing the first *think-driven visual generation framework* that explicitly and *generalizedly* leverages MLLM's CoT reasoning across a wide range of generation tasks. It moves beyond merely using MLLMs as feature providers to actively engaging their reasoning capabilities to inform and improve image synthesis. This positions ThinkGen at the forefront of combining advanced multimodal reasoning with state-of-the-art visual generation capabilities, aiming to create more intelligent and versatile generative AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind ThinkGen are to:

- **Enable Generalized CoT Reasoning for Visual Generation:** The core goal is to overcome the limitations of scenario-specific CoT mechanisms in existing generation models. ThinkGen aims to introduce a unified framework where MLLMs can perform robust "thinking" that is adaptable across various visual generation tasks, without requiring task-specific re-engineering.
- **Explicitly Leverage MLLM's Reasoning Capabilities:** While MLLMs have been used in generative contexts, their advanced CoT reasoning ability has not been fully exploited. ThinkGen seeks to explicitly integrate this "thought process" into the generation pipeline, allowing the MLLM to formulate a coherent plan or refined instruction before the image synthesis begins.
- **Improve Performance in Diverse Generative Scenarios:** The motivation is to achieve state-of-the-art performance across a spectrum of visual generation tasks, including complex reasoning-intensive generation (e.g., world knowledge-informed prompts), precise image editing, and accurate text rendering, by virtue of the integrated CoT reasoning.
- **Develop a Flexible and Efficient Training Paradigm:** Given the complexity of combining MLLMs and Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), a key objective is to create a training strategy that is both effective in aligning the two modules for reasoning-driven generation and computationally efficient. This led to the development of the separable GRPO-based training (SepGRPO).
- **Bridge the Gap Between MLLM CoT and DiT Input Requirements:** The verbose nature of MLLM's CoT reasoning can be redundant for a DiT. A specific motivation is to refine the MLLM's output into concise, effective instructions suitable for guiding the DiT, ensuring optimal information flow.

In essence, ThinkGen is motivated by the vision of intelligent generative models that can "think" and "plan" before "creating," making them more adaptable, robust, and capable of handling complex, abstract user intents in visual generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ThinkGen employs a novel, think-driven framework built upon a **decoupled architecture** and a specialized **reinforcement learning (RL) training paradigm**.

#### 3.1. Decoupled Architecture

The framework consists of two main, decoupled modules:

- **Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM):** ThinkGen initializes its MLLM component with **Qwen3-VL-8B-Think**. This MLLM is responsible for understanding user intent (which can include text prompts or reference images with editing instructions) and performing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. It generates tailored, rewritten generation instructions. The MLLM operates autoregressively, producing explicit intermediate thought steps using a `<think>` formatting. For guiding the DiT, the last two layers of hidden states generated after the `</think>` token are extracted.
- **Diffusion Transformer (DiT):** A standard DiT architecture, initialized with **OmniGen2-DiT-4B**, is used for high-quality image synthesis. It takes the refined instructions from the MLLM as conditional textual input. For image editing tasks, additional reference images are processed by a VAE and incorporated as conditional visual inputs. A simple linear layer acts as a connector to align features from multiple conditional inputs, chosen for its effectiveness over more complex MLP or transformer-based connectors.

#### 3.2. Visual Generation Instruction Refinement (VGI-Refine)

To address the verbosity and potential redundancy of MLLM's CoT outputs, ThinkGen introduces **VGI-refine**. This module performs two crucial steps:
1. **Instruction Token Extraction:** It extracts only the essential instruction tokens that follow the special `</think>` token, thereby isolating the concise CoT results relevant for image generation.
2. **Prepadding States Concatenation:** It concatenates *K* learnable Prepadding States to these extracted instruction tokens. This mechanism helps regulate the representation distribution of the MLLM's output hidden states, particularly benefiting short instructions by ensuring better alignment with the DiT's requirements.

#### 3.3. Training Recipe: Multi-stage with SepGRPO

ThinkGen's training is divided into five distinct stages, blending supervised learning with a novel reinforcement learning approach:

**Supervised Pre-training (Stages 1-3):** These stages focus on aligning the DiT with the MLLM and enhancing image generation quality. Pseudo-CoT templates, where the `<think>` section is empty and the original instruction is repeated, are used to simulate CoT during pre-training.
- **Stage 1: Alignment:** Only the linear connector between MLLM and DiT is trained, with both MLLM and DiT frozen, to align the modules.
- **Stage 2: Pre-training:** All DiT parameters are made trainable on a large corpus (60M samples) covering text-to-image, image editing, text rendering, and in-context generation.
- **Stage 3: High-quality Fine-tuning:** DiT is further fine-tuned on a smaller, high-quality subset (0.7M samples) with higher resolutions (up to 1024x1024) to improve instruction-following and aesthetic quality.

**Separable GRPO-based Training Paradigm (SepGRPO) (Stages 4-5):** This is the core RL phase designed to optimize the MLLM to generate DiT-preferred instructions and to refine DiT for generating superior images based on these tailored instructions. SepGRPO decouples the RL process for text and vision:

- **Stage 4: MLLM-GRPO:** With DiT frozen, GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization) is applied to the MLLM. The MLLM learns to generate rewritten text (CoT reasoning trajectories) that maximizes rewards from task-specific rule models, which leverage metrics like GenEval, HPSv3, Word Accuracy, SigLIP2, and Normalized Edit Distance (NED) across five diverse generation scenarios (semantic composition, reasoning generation, text rendering, image editing, reflection). The advantages are computed in a group-relative manner to stabilize training.
- **Stage 5: DiT-GRPO:** With the MLLM fixed, FlowGRPO is applied to DiT to enhance its instruction-following capability and image quality. DiT performs multiple rollouts to generate images for a given MLLM-generated CoT trajectory, and its policy is updated based on rewards derived from image quality metrics for simple scene and text rendering scenarios. Denoising Reduction is employed to accelerate sampling and collect informative trajectories efficiently.

This separable design offers **flexible reward design**, **reduced learning complexity** (MLLM focuses on instructions, DiT on image quality), and **lower training costs** due to reduced GPU memory usage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ThinkGen demonstrates robust, state-of-the-art performance across various visual generation benchmarks, particularly excelling when CoT reasoning is enabled.

#### 4.1. Performance Across Diverse Scenarios

- **Reasoning Generation (WISEBench):** ThinkGen significantly outperforms previous methods. With CoT reasoning (ThinkGen*), it achieves a substantial +21% improvement (0.55 → 0.76) over its non-CoT counterpart, establishing a new state-of-the-art. This highlights the MLLM's ability to leverage world knowledge for complex image generation tasks.
- **Reasoning Editing (RISEBench):** ThinkGen* shows remarkable improvement, moving from 3.6 to 13.0, far surpassing open-source models and becoming competitive with closed-source models like Gemini-2.0. This indicates strong causal, temporal, spatial, and logical reasoning capabilities for image editing.
- **Text-to-Image Generation (GenEval, DPG-Bench, CVTG):** ThinkGen, especially with CoT reasoning, consistently shows improvements across semantic consistency (GenEval: 0.89), long-form generation (DPG-Bench: 85.87), and text rendering (CVTG Word Acc.: 0.84, NED: 0.94). This demonstrates its superior instruction-following and text-rendering capabilities.
- **Image Editing (ImgEdit):** ThinkGen achieves significantly superior metrics (4.21 with CoT) compared to a range of open-source models, reaching performance comparable to GPT-4o, indicating its proficiency in various editing tasks like adding, adjusting, replacing, removing, changing background, and altering style.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Training Stage Ablations:**
 - Stage 1 (Connector training) yields poor text-rendering, showing limited alignment.
 - Stage 2 (Large-scale pre-training) brings significant improvements across all metrics (e.g., +10% GenEval, +9% WISE, +35% CVTG), establishing strong baseline quality.
 - Stage 3 (High-quality fine-tuning) further enhances image details, boosting CVTG by +12%.
 - Stage 4 (MLLM-GRPO) slightly impacts base image generation but dramatically increases reasoning capabilities (WISE: 0.55 → 0.76 with CoT).
 - Stage 5 (DiT-GRPO) further refines image generation, particularly for fine-grained text rendering (CVTG: 0.79 → 0.84).
- **Prepadding States:** These states are critical for short prompts, significantly improving performance on benchmarks like GenEval (+0.14), WISEBench (+0.09), CVTG (+0.04), and ImgEdit (+0.47), by effectively adjusting MLLM output feature distribution.
- **Training Strategy:** Directly applying Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to DiT with reasoning data does not improve reasoning generation, demonstrating that DiT doesn't inherently gain world knowledge generalization. The significant improvement in reasoning ability (WISE: +0.19) is solely attributable to MLLM-GRPO, confirming that the MLLM is indeed learning to reason.
- **Connector Design and VGI-Refine Extraction:** A simple linear layer for the connector proved most effective. Truncating hidden states before the `</think>` token in VGI-refine consistently improves performance, especially for short prompts, by eliminating redundant information.

#### 4.3. Analysis of the SepGRPO Process

Visualization of the SepGRPO process shows:
- A gradual increase in average CoT length, indicating the MLLM develops more sophisticated reasoning.
- A steady rise in multi-task reward scores, confirming ThinkGen's adaptive learning across diverse scenarios.
- Clear improvements in generated image quality, with richer details and higher fidelity observed at later training steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ThinkGen represents a significant leap forward in the field of generative AI, particularly in bridging the gap between advanced multimodal reasoning and high-quality visual synthesis. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that **explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning can be generalized and effectively leveraged across a diverse array of visual generation tasks**, moving beyond the previous limitations of scenario-specific mechanisms or MLLMs merely serving as feature extractors.

The **decoupled MLLM-DiT architecture** combined with the innovative **SepGRPO training paradigm** offers several critical impacts:
- **Enhanced Intelligence in Generative Models:** ThinkGen endows generative models with a "thinking" capability, allowing them to formulate plans and refine instructions based on complex user intents, world knowledge, and contextual cues. This makes the generated outputs more coherent, logically sound, and aligned with user expectations, especially for reasoning-intensive prompts.
- **Broadened Applicability and Adaptability:** By achieving robust performance across diverse scenarios—from general text-to-image and editing to highly specific reasoning generation and text rendering—ThinkGen showcases the potential for truly unified and adaptable generative AI systems. This reduces the need for specialized models for each task, simplifying development and deployment.
- **Improved User Experience:** Users can provide more abstract, complex, or even slightly ambiguous prompts, and the system can still generate high-quality, relevant images by internally reasoning through the request. This pushes human-AI interaction in generative tasks towards a more natural and intuitive experience.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The separable nature of SepGRPO not only allows for targeted optimization but also significantly reduces GPU memory usage during training, making the development of such sophisticated models more feasible and accessible.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** ThinkGen provides a strong baseline and a new paradigm for integrating reasoning into generative models. Future work can explore even more sophisticated CoT mechanisms, expand the range of reasoning types (e.g., ethical, common sense), or apply this generalized thinking to other generative modalities like video or 3D content.

In conclusion, ThinkGen is a landmark contribution that pushes generative AI from mere "creation" to "intelligent creation," promising a future where AI models can not only generate visuals but also understand, reason, and plan with human-like cognitive steps.
