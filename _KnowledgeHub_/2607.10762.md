# TOLiD: Bridging the Architecture Gap in Vision Foundation Model to LiDAR Pretraining via Token Lifting for Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> TOLiD enhances LiDAR representation learning by coupling a student Vision Transformer with a LiDAR backbone, bridging the architectural gap in VFM-to-LiDAR distillation. This approach achieved a 71.7% mIoU on nuScenes in linear probing, a 3.9% improvement over prior methods, and demonstrated superior robustness to sensor corruptions and cross-sensor domain shifts.

## Problem

- Existing VFM-to-LiDAR distillation methods struggle with a significant architectural gap between dense Vision Transformer (ViT) tokens from large teachers and sparse 3D LiDAR backbones.
- The architectural mismatch leads to information loss, unstable knowledge transfer, and poor generalization of fine-grained semantics, especially under diverse sensor or environmental conditions.
- Heterogeneity of LiDAR sensors (beam count, density) creates cross-sensor domain shifts, making it costly and impractical to collect dense point-wise annotations for every new platform.

## Method

- **TOLiD** introduces a coupled architecture where a LiDAR backbone provides geometry-conditioned features to a student ==Vision Transformer== (initialized from a ==VFM== teacher), allowing distillation over architecturally compatible patch-token representations.
- It converts sparse LiDAR point features into structured, ViT-compatible tokens using ==Frustum Pooling== at initial stages and ==Frustum Attention== at deeper stages for content-aware aggregation.
- A ==visibility-masked distillation== objective ensures that supervision is only applied to patch tokens supported by observed LiDAR geometry, while ==Masked Bilinear Sampling== lifts learned tokens back to per-point features for LiDAR-only deployment during finetuning.

## Results

- TOLiD achieved a **71.7% mIoU** on nuScenes during linear probing, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art by **+3.9%**, and showed improved performance in label-efficient finetuning with **1%** labels on nuScenes (**53.5% mIoU**) and SemanticKITTI (**57.0% mIoU**).
- It demonstrated superior cross-sensor domain adaptation, achieving an average mIoU of **66.7%** across four challenging cross-sensor pairs, surpassing a strong baseline (MuDDoS) by **+2.2%**.
- The framework showed increased robustness to sensor corruptions, achieving **61.34%** average mIoU on the Robo3D benchmark, a **+7.88%** improvement over prior methods, and its performance gains scaled with increased pretraining data volume.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly bridging the architectural gap between Vision Foundation Models and LiDAR backbones through a coupled student ViT significantly improves the effectiveness and stability of cross-modal knowledge distillation.
> - Transforming sparse 3D point features into structured, fixed-dimensional patch tokens via frustum-based mechanisms (Frustum Pooling and Frustum Attention) enables robust integration of geometric information into 2D semantic reasoning.
> - Grounding distillation and feature lifting with visibility masks and content-aware sampling (Masked Bilinear Sampling) prevents the learning of spurious associations and ensures physically relevant feature representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a critical challenge in 3D scene understanding for autonomous robots and self-driving cars, specifically focusing on LiDAR-based semantic segmentation. This field is essential for enabling navigation, mapping, and safe interaction in complex environments.

Traditional LiDAR-based learning models face significant difficulties due to the inherent heterogeneity of sensor suites, which vary in beam count, field of view, point density, and scanning patterns. These variations induce cross-sensor domain shifts, making it impractical and costly to collect new, dense point-wise annotations for every new platform or environment.

In parallel, 2D perception tasks have benefited substantially from the development of Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) pretrained on vast image datasets. However, extending these benefits to LiDAR-based tasks has been constrained by two main factors: the absence of comparable 3D foundation models and a fundamental modality gap when attempting to directly apply VFMs to LiDAR representations.

Recent research has attempted to bridge this gap through VFM-to-LiDAR cross-modal distillation. This self-supervised pretraining strategy leverages VFMs as teachers to distill their representations into LiDAR backbones, aiming to reduce the reliance on extensive labeled 3D data during subsequent finetuning. Existing distillation pipelines typically involve a two-stage process: first, synchronized camera-LiDAR pairs are used to project LiDAR points into image planes, associating them with dense VFM features (e.g., patch tokens or intermediate embeddings). Second, a LiDAR encoder is trained to align with or predict these VFM embeddings, often using feature regression or contrastive learning objectives.

The authors identify a fundamental limitation in these existing VFM-to-LiDAR distillation pipelines: they generally distill knowledge from very large Vision Transformer (ViT) teachers into comparatively smaller 3D backbones with substantially different architectures and feature parameterizations. This forces the student LiDAR network to simultaneously bridge *both* the cross-modal gap (image to LiDAR) and a significant *cross-architecture gap* (dense ViT tokens to sparse 3D encoders). This architectural mismatch can lead to several issues, including information loss, unstable knowledge transfer, and an inability to reliably distill fine-grained semantics crucial for point-wise segmentation, particularly under varying sensor or environmental conditions.

TOLiD directly addresses this architectural gap. Instead of having a heterogeneous 3D backbone directly match fixed image embeddings, TOLiD integrates a student ViT, initialized from the VFM teacher, with the LiDAR backbone. This approach aims to provide supervision over compatible patch-token representations, thereby simplifying the distillation process and potentially enabling more effective and robust transfer of knowledge. This work therefore seeks to improve the fundamental mechanism of cross-modal distillation for 3D perception by ensuring architectural compatibility during knowledge transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of current VFM-to-LiDAR cross-modal distillation methods. While these methods have shown promise in leveraging abundant 2D visual semantics for 3D representation learning, their effectiveness is constrained by a significant architectural discrepancy. Specifically, existing approaches typically treat a large Vision Foundation Model (VFM) as a frozen feature source, extracting its dense ViT token representations and attempting to distil these into a heterogeneous 3D LiDAR backbone. This setup forces the LiDAR network (student) to simultaneously overcome two major challenges: the fundamental modality gap between 2D images and 3D point clouds, and a substantial cross-architecture gap between the teacher’s dense, grid-based ViT embeddings and the student’s sparse, irregular 3D point cloud features.

The authors posit that this architectural mismatch leads to several issues:
1. **Information Loss:** The disparity in feature parameterizations can result in the loss of fine-grained semantic information during transfer, which is crucial for detailed tasks like point-wise semantic segmentation.
2. **Unstable Transfer:** Bridging two distinct gaps simultaneously can make the distillation process unstable, leading to less reliable and less comprehensive learning.
3. **Limited Generalization:** The representations learned under such conditions may not transfer effectively under strong sensor or environmental shifts, hindering the deployment of models across diverse robotic platforms.
4. **Inefficient Data Scaling:** Current methods may not fully leverage the benefits of scaling up pretraining data if the architectural incompatibility acts as a bottleneck for knowledge absorption.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of the TOLiD framework are:
- **To bridge the architectural gap in VFM-to-LiDAR distillation:** This is the core objective, achieved by introducing a student Vision Transformer (ViT) that is initialized from a frozen VFM teacher and then coupled with the LiDAR backbone. This ensures that supervision occurs between architecturally compatible patch-token representations.
- **To enable compatible patch-token representation learning:** Develop mechanisms to convert sparse, unordered LiDAR point features into structured, fixed-dimensional patch tokens that are directly compatible with the ViT architecture. This involves Frustum Pooling for initial token generation and Frustum Attention for content-aware aggregation at deeper layers.
- **To integrate LiDAR features into the student ViT in a multi-stage fashion:** Progressively inject geometry-conditioned LiDAR features at multiple stages within the student ViT blocks, allowing for a richer interplay between 3D geometry and 2D semantics.
- **To ensure geometrically grounded distillation:** Implement a visibility-masked distillation objective that only applies supervision to patch tokens supported by observed LiDAR geometry, preventing the learning of spurious associations in image-only regions.
- **To support LiDAR-only deployment with reliable feature lifting:** Design a Masked Bilinear Sampling (MBS) mechanism to lift learned token features back to per-point representations for downstream 3D tasks. This mechanism specifically avoids contamination from empty or unsupported image patches, ensuring lifted features are physically grounded.
- **To demonstrate improved transferability and robustness:** Empirically validate that the proposed method yields stronger self-supervised representations that enhance performance in linear probing, label-efficient finetuning, cross-dataset generalization, cross-sensor domain adaptation, and robustness to sensor corruptions.

By pursuing these objectives, TOLiD aims to create a more effective, scalable, and robust self-supervised pretraining strategy for LiDAR representation learning, ultimately reducing the annotation burden and accelerating the deployment of 3D perception systems in real-world autonomous applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TOLiD proposes a self-supervised pretraining method designed to bridge the architectural gap in VFM-to-LiDAR cross-modal distillation. Instead of directly aligning a heterogeneous 3D LiDAR backbone with VFM features, TOLiD couples the LiDAR backbone (Lθ) with a student Vision Transformer (ViT) (Gϕ), which is initialized from a frozen VFM teacher (T). This architecture enables supervision to be applied over compatible patch-token representations.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**A. Preliminaries and Notations**
- The system assumes a synchronized multi-sensor setup with a LiDAR scan and a set of calibrated cameras (V) providing 360° coverage.
- LiDAR point cloud P consists of 3D coordinates (xi) and raw attributes (ai).
- RGB images I = {Iv} are captured by cameras.
- Camera intrinsics (Intv) and extrinsics (Extv_ℓ) are used to project each LiDAR point xi into view v, yielding 2D coordinates uv_i = πv(xi).
- A visible subset Pv ⊆ P is defined for points falling within the image bounds of Iv, establishing point-pixel correspondences.

**B. Frustum Pooling (FP): Point-to-Token Mapping**
- The student ViT (Gϕ) processes a regular grid of Np patch tokens. The LiDAR backbone (Lθ) outputs unordered point features fi ∈ R^C_w.
- For each image patch 't' in view 'v', a frustum set Nv(t) is defined, containing all visible LiDAR points whose 2D projections fall within that patch.
- A binary mask Mv ∈ {0, 1}^Np indicates if a patch contains projected LiDAR points (Mv_t = 1 if Nv(t) ≠ ∅, else 0).
- At the initial stage (j=0) of the ViT, standard patch embedding is replaced. Point features fi within a frustum Nv(t) are linearly projected into the ViT embedding space (via Wp ∈ R^C_S × C_w) and then *mean-pooled* to form a single token zv_t.
 - zv_t = (1 / |Nv(t)|) * Σ_{i∈Nv(t)} Wp * fi
- Patches without visible points (Mv_t = 0) are assigned a learnable empty token e ∈ R^C_S.
- The resulting token grid Zv, augmented with positional embeddings and a [CLS] token, is then fed into the student ViT blocks. This allows the transformer to operate in its native token space while incorporating geometric structure from LiDAR.

**C. Frustum Attention (FA): Content-Aware 3D→2D Injection**
- While Frustum Pooling provides a stable initial aggregation, it treats all points equally. For deeper ViT blocks (j>0), where patch tokens encode more contextual representations, Frustum Attention is introduced.
- FA is a cross-attention mechanism that allows each token to selectively aggregate features from the 3D points within its corresponding frustum.
- Given a patch token zt and its associated point features {fi} in Nv(t), point features are first mapped to the ViT embedding dimension (g_i = Wp^(j) * fi).
- Query (Qt) is derived from the patch token (Qt = WQ * zt), while Key (Ki) and Value (Vi) are derived from the point features (Ki = WK * gi, Vi = WV * gi). Separate projection matrices (WQ, WK, WV) are learned for each stage.
- Attention weights are computed, and an aggregated context (∆zt) is formed by summing weighted values. The softmax for attention is computed independently within each frustum.
- The patch token is updated via residual addition and layer normalization: zt ← zt + LN(∆zt).
- FA enables content-aware geometry injection, allowing the token to emphasize informative points and suppress background noise based on its evolving semantic state.

**D. Multi-Stage LiDAR Integration with Student ViT**
- Frustum Pooling and Frustum Attention integrate the LiDAR backbone Lθ at J stages within the student ViT (Gϕ) blocks.
- The LiDAR backbone advances by D_w / J layers between successive stages, and its resulting point features are mapped to view-specific patch-token grids.
- Unlike prior methods where the 3D backbone independently approximates the teacher, here Lθ provides geometry-conditioned features that *complement* the student ViT’s pretrained processing.
- The ViT maintains responsibility for global reasoning and semantic abstraction through its self-attention mechanisms.
- Distillation gradients propagate through the Frustum modules and ViT blocks into Lθ, guiding the LiDAR backbone to learn point-wise features compatible with the teacher-supervised token representations.

**E. Visibility-Masked Distillation**
- For a given camera view 'v', the frozen teacher T processes the RGB image Iv to produce target token representations ZT,v.
- The student ViT Gϕ, conditioned on LiDAR-derived tokens, outputs student tokens ZS,v.
- A learnable projection ψ(·) maps student features (R^C_S) to the teacher embedding space (R^C_T).
- **Distillation Objective:** A combined loss Ldist = 0.5 * (Lcos + Lsl1) is used, comprising cosine similarity (for directional consistency) and smooth-ℓ1 loss (for feature magnitude), applied to pairs of embeddings (p, q).
- **Global Alignment:** Lcls = Ldist(ψ(zS,v_cls), zT,v_cls) aligns the global [CLS] tokens.
- **Visibility-Masked Local Alignment:** Patch-level supervision Lpatch is applied only to tokens with visible LiDAR points, using the occupancy mask Mv. This avoids penalizing regions without geometric evidence:
 - Lpatch = (1 / Σ Mv_t) * Σ_{t=1 to N_p} Mv_t * Ldist(ψ(zS,v_t), zT,v_t)
- The final objective is L = 0.5 * (Lcls + Lpatch), ensuring physically grounded supervision.

**F. Finetuning via Masked Bilinear Sampling (MBS)**
- For LiDAR-only deployment during finetuning, ViT tokens from the final stage are lifted back to per-point representations.
- Naive bilinear interpolation at a point's 2D projection uv_i can aggregate features from all four neighboring tokens, including potentially "empty" patches without LiDAR support, introducing spurious semantics.
- MBS addresses this by masking interpolation using the occupancy map Mv:
 - rv_i = (Σ_{n∈Ω(uv_i)} wn * zv_n * Mv_n) / (Σ_{n∈Ω(uv_i)} wn * Mv_n + ϵ)
 - Here, Ω(uv_i) denotes the four grid neighbors, wn are bilinear weights, and empty tokens (Mv_n=0) are suppressed, with weights re-normalized over only valid support.
- **Cross-view Aggregation and Segmentation:** A single LiDAR point visible in multiple cameras yields multiple lifted features rv_i. These are averaged across all views to produce a final aggregated feature ri. This ri is then concatenated with the point-wise features from the LiDAR backbone Lθ (fi) and passed to a segmentation head for per-point semantic prediction.

The integration of these components allows TOLiD to learn geometry-aware and semantically enriched point representations by leveraging the architectural prior of the VFM teacher, while maintaining geometric grounding through carefully designed cross-modal alignment mechanisms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TOLiD was evaluated extensively across five heterogeneous camera-LiDAR datasets for pretraining, totaling 78,791 LiDAR scans and 341,645 camera-LiDAR pairs, encompassing diverse sensor configurations and driving environments. Performance was assessed using mIoU across various protocols.

**A. Linear Probing and Label-Efficient Finetuning**
- **Linear Probing (LP):** TOLiD, pretrained on four datasets (nuScenes, SemanticKITTI, PandaSet-64, PandaSet-GT), achieved 71.7% mIoU on nuScenes, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (ScaLR) by +3.9%. This substantial improvement with a frozen backbone indicates higher quality and more linearly separable features learned by TOLiD.
- **Label-Efficient Finetuning:** TOLiD also demonstrated improved performance in low-data regimes. On nuScenes, it achieved 53.5% mIoU with 1% labels and 69.2% with 10% labels (matching ScaLR), outperforming prior methods. On SemanticKITTI, it reached 57.0% mIoU with 1% labels, slightly better than ScaLR's 56.8%. These results suggest that TOLiD's representations require less labeled data for adaptation.

**B. Cross-Sensor Domain Adaptation**
- Evaluated under the MuDDoS protocol for unsupervised domain adaptation across four cross-sensor pairs (N→K, K→N, N→W, W→N), using a frozen backbone and a lightweight MLP head trained on the source domain.
- TOLiD, pretrained on all five datasets (including Waymo Open) without self-training (ST) on the target domain, achieved an average mIoU of 66.7%, outperforming MuDDoS (with ST) by +2.2%.
- Significant gains were observed, particularly on the challenging N→K setting (nuScenes 32-beam to SemanticKITTI 64-beam), where TOLiD improved from 52.1% (MuDDoS w ST) to 57.2% (+5.1% mIoU).
- Notably, even when TOLiD was pretrained on only four datasets (excluding Waymo Open), it surpassed MuDDoS (without ST) on N→W (58.4% vs. 54.1%) and W→N (66.8% vs. 53.0%), indicating strong generalization capabilities to previously unseen sensors during pretraining. These findings confirm that TOLiD's self-supervised representations are robust to cross-sensor domain shifts.

**C. Cross-Dataset Generalization and Scaling Pretraining Data**
- The study investigated the impact of pretraining data scale on linear probing performance across four datasets with heterogeneous LiDAR sensors.
- TOLiD consistently outperformed ScaLR, and this performance gap widened with increasing pretraining data volume.
- With a DINOv2-ViT-L/14 teacher and 342k frames (all five datasets), TOLiD achieved 70.9% (nuScenes), 60.1% (SemanticKITTI), 42.0% (PandaSet-64), and 42.6% (PandaSet-GT) mIoU. These results represent gains of +3.4%, +3.3%, +3.3%, and +6.7% mIoU over ScaLR, respectively.
- The most substantial gain on PandaSet-GT (a forward-facing sensor) highlights TOLiD's ability to bridge large domain gaps from predominantly 360° pretraining distributions.
- Figure 4 illustrates this trend: the average LP mIoU gap between TOLiD and ScaLR increased from +2.0% at 169k frames to +4.2% at 342k frames, suggesting that TOLiD's coupled LiDAR-ViT architecture more effectively leverages geometric diversity from heterogeneous sensors as data scales.

**D. Robustness to Sensor Corruptions**
- Evaluated on the Robo3D benchmark, which includes eight real-world corruption types (e.g., fog, snow, beam missing, crosstalk).
- Under Linear Probing, TOLiD achieved 61.34% average mIoU, significantly outperforming ScaLR (53.46%) by +7.88%. TOLiD showed improvements across all eight corruption types, with the largest gain (+17.51%) on crosstalk.
- Under full finetuning, both methods converged to similar performance (TOLiD: 65.72% vs. ScaLR: 65.70%), indicating that TOLiD's advantage lies in its robust frozen representations, making it suitable for faster adaptation with lightweight heads.

**E. Ablation Studies**
- **Masked Bilinear Sampling (MBS):** MBS improved LP mIoU by 0.4% on nuScenes (71.3% to 71.7%) and 0.2% on PandaSet-64 (39.7% to 39.9%) compared to naive bilinear sampling. This confirms the benefit of suppressing empty patches and re-normalizing weights during feature lifting.
- **Frustum Attention (FA) vs. Frustum Pooling (FP) at deeper stages:** FA raised LP mIoU by 0.4% on nuScenes (56.0% to 56.4%) and 0.2% on PandaSet-64 (27.4% to 27.6%) over FP. This indicates that content-aware cross-attention provides richer cues than simple mean pooling at later fusion stages.

In summary, the results demonstrate that TOLiD's approach of bridging the architectural gap through a coupled LiDAR-ViT architecture and specialized frustum-based tokenization leads to superior and more transferable LiDAR representations across various evaluation metrics and challenging real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TOLiD framework introduces a significant advancement in the field of 3D semantic understanding, particularly for autonomous systems relying on LiDAR. Its primary significance lies in explicitly addressing the **cross-architecture gap** that exists in conventional VFM-to-LiDAR distillation methods. By coupling a LiDAR backbone with a student Vision Transformer initialized from a frozen VFM teacher, TOLiD enables knowledge transfer over architecturally compatible patch-token representations, moving beyond direct feature alignment between heterogeneous networks.

The potential impacts of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Improved Representation Quality and Transferability:** The consistent and substantial improvements demonstrated across linear probing and label-efficient finetuning protocols indicate that TOLiD learns inherently stronger, more semantically structured, and linearly separable LiDAR representations. This means the learned features are more effective for downstream tasks and require less task-specific fine-tuning or labeled data, significantly reducing the annotation burden.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Domain Adaptation:** TOLiD's superior performance in cross-sensor domain adaptation, even without self-training on target domains, highlights its ability to learn representations that are robust to variations in LiDAR sensor configurations (e.g., beam count, field of view). This is crucial for robotic fleets that operate with diverse hardware and need to adapt quickly to new platforms or environments without extensive re-annotation. The observed strength in bridging large domain gaps, such as with forward-facing LiDARs, underscores this impact.

3. **Increased Robustness to Real-World Corruptions:** The gains in robustness to sensor corruptions, as evaluated on the Robo3D benchmark, signify that TOLiD's pretrained models are more resilient to real-world operational challenges like adverse weather conditions or sensor malfunctions. This directly contributes to the reliability and safety of autonomous vehicles and robots.

4. **Scalability with Unlabeled Data:** The finding that TOLiD's performance gains widen with increasing volumes and diversity of pretraining data is highly significant. It suggests that this framework can more effectively leverage the continuous accumulation of unlabeled multi-sensor data by robotic systems, translating raw data growth into tangible improvements in perception capabilities without additional human annotation effort. This opens avenues for more sustainable and scalable AI development in robotics.

5. **Deployment-Ready Features:** The effectiveness of TOLiD's frozen backbones with lightweight segmentation heads for various tasks implies that it produces "deployment-ready" features. This feature allows for faster adaptation and deployment of LiDAR perception models onto new robotic platforms, as only a small, task-specific head needs to be trained, rather than the entire deep network. This accelerates the development cycle and reduces computational costs associated with model deployment.

6. **Methodological Innovation:** The introduction of Frustum Pooling and Frustum Attention provides novel mechanisms for effectively converting sparse 3D point features into ViT-compatible tokens while preserving geometric context. Furthermore, the visibility-masked distillation and Masked Bilinear Sampling ensure that cross-modal alignment and feature lifting remain grounded in valid 3D support, preventing the introduction of spurious signals. These techniques contribute to the broader understanding of how to effectively integrate and transfer knowledge between different modalities and architectures.

In conclusion, TOLiD offers a principled solution to a fundamental problem in cross-modal representation learning for 3D perception. By thoughtfully addressing the architectural disparities between VFMs and LiDAR networks, it enables the creation of more transferable, robust, and efficient LiDAR features. This work holds substantial promise for advancing the capabilities of autonomous systems and reducing the barriers to their real-world deployment.
