# Diff-VF: Training-free High-quality Long Video Generation via Diffusion Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Diff-VF introduces a training-free, model-agnostic framework that enables existing short-video diffusion models to generate high-quality long videos. It balances temporal coherence and motion diversity, demonstrating improved performance over prior training-free methods on VBench-Long metrics, and also applies to long-video enhancement.

## Problem

- Existing video diffusion models, trained on short datasets, struggle to generate long videos due to a "training-inference gap."
- Directly extending these models results in quality degradation, temporal flickering, and lack of long-term consistency.
- Prior training-free approaches often compromise between temporal coherence and motion diversity, or are restricted by specific model architectures.

## Method

- Introduces **Diff-VF**, a training-free, plug-and-play framework that operates on noisy latent representations.
- Employs ==Hybrid Noise Initialization (HNI)== for global semantics and content diversity, ==Weighted Window Sampling (WWS)== for local motion smoothness, and ==Temporal Extended Sampling (TES)== for long-range consistency.
- Fuses local and global denoising paths using a timestep-varying weight to balance consistency and detail, and uses ==Skip Residual Guidance== for enhancement.

## Results

- Demonstrated improved balance between temporal coherence and motion diversity on **VBench-Long** metrics across LaVie and HunyuanVideo base models, outperforming prior training-free methods.
- Produced qualitatively superior long videos with visible temporal evolution, dynamic scenes, and consistent identity, avoiding artifacts like flickering and repetitions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of Hybrid Noise Initialization, Weighted Window Sampling, and Temporal Extended Sampling to consistency, smoothness, and long-range dependencies, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global video consistency and content diversity can be effectively initiated by mixing initial noise from the first clip with newly generated random noise.
> - Local temporal continuity and global long-range consistency can be simultaneously achieved by combining weighted overlapping window denoising with temporally re-indexed sampling.
> - A dynamic, timestep-varying fusion of local and global denoising paths is essential to balance overall structure, smooth transitions, and refined details throughout the generation process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Diffusion models have demonstrated capabilities in image generation, surpassing previous methods such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in terms of output quality. This success has led to efforts to extend their application to the video domain, enabling the generation, enhancement, and editing of video content.

However, a significant challenge in video diffusion models is the generation of long videos. Most existing video diffusion models are trained on short video datasets due to factors such as the scarcity of long video datasets and the high computational and memory costs associated with training on longer sequences. Consequently, these models typically produce videos no longer than approximately 10 seconds.

Directly extrapolating short-video diffusion models to generate longer sequences results in a "training-inference gap," leading to quality degradation. Models trained on short videos primarily learn short-term sequential dependencies and local spatio-temporal features. When applied to longer videos, their limited receptive fields and temporal modeling capabilities struggle to maintain long-term temporal consistency and coherent global motion patterns. This often manifests as temporal flickering, over-smoothing of high-frequency content, and a lack of consistent subject or scene evolution over extended durations.

To address this, several training-free methods for long video generation have been proposed. These methods often focus on either improving long-range temporal consistency (e.g., through noise replication, shuffling, or local-global attention decoupling) or enhancing motion diversity (e.g., by modifying temporal attention layers or positional embeddings). However, existing approaches often encounter limitations: some methods overemphasize one aspect at the expense of the other, leading to videos with restricted content or abrupt transitions. Furthermore, certain strategies involving modifications to temporal attention layers are only compatible with specific video diffusion model architectures (e.g., 2D+1D attention modules) and may negatively impact models with coupled spatial-temporal attention (e.g., 3D-attention modules).

Diff-VF positions itself within this landscape by offering a training-free, plug-and-play, and model-agnostic framework designed to convert existing short-video diffusion models into long-video generators. It seeks to provide a balanced solution that simultaneously addresses long-range temporal coherence, motion diversity, and frame-wise quality, while remaining independent of the underlying base model's architecture. The work also extends to long-video enhancement, leveraging similar principles to balance fidelity and realism.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the generation of high-quality, long videos using existing short-video diffusion models without requiring any modification or fine-tuning of the base model. This encompasses achieving a favorable balance between temporal coherence and motion diversity, which is a persistent challenge in long video generation.

The motivations driving this work are several:

1. **Overcoming the Training-Inference Gap:** Short-video diffusion models, while effective for their intended length, exhibit significant degradation when directly applied to generate longer videos. This is due to their limited learned temporal dependencies and receptive fields, which cause issues such as temporal flickering, over-smoothing, and failure to capture global motion patterns. A core motivation is to devise a strategy that allows these pre-trained models to extrapolate effectively to longer sequences.
2. **Balancing Temporal Coherence and Motion Diversity:** Existing training-free methods often focus on either temporal consistency or motion diversity in isolation. For instance, some methods achieve high consistency but produce static or repetitive content, while others introduce diversity at the cost of coherence. The researchers aim to develop a framework that can simultaneously maintain long-range semantic consistency and smooth transitions, while also enabling rich and dynamic motion throughout the video, avoiding monotonous repetitions.
3. **Developing a Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Solution:** Retraining or fine-tuning large video diffusion models for longer video generation is computationally intensive and requires extensive datasets. The motivation is to create a "plug-and-play" framework that operates at the inference stage, abstracting away architectural specifics, thereby making it compatible with a wide range of existing video diffusion backbones (e.g., U-Net based, DiT-based, models with 2D+1D or 3D attention modules) without requiring internal architectural modifications. This reduces computational cost and increases accessibility.
4. **Extending to Video Enhancement:** Beyond generation, there is a motivation to apply similar principles to long-video enhancement. Existing enhancement models, like generation models, are typically trained for short videos. The goal is to extend their capabilities to long videos, ensuring that enhanced outputs maintain high fidelity to the original content while also achieving improved realism and quality.

By addressing these objectives, the researchers aim to provide a practical and effective solution for expanding the utility of current video diffusion models to long-form content, both for creation and quality improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Diff-VF is presented as a training-free, plug-and-play, and model-agnostic framework designed to convert existing short-video diffusion backbones into long-video generators. The framework operates primarily in the latent space and incorporates three complementary strategies during the denoising process, along with an additional mechanism for video enhancement.

The overall approach involves first generating initial noise using a hybrid method. Then, during each denoising step, two distinct sampling strategies are applied and their results are combined using a timestep-varying weight. Finally, a video decoder translates the denoised latents into video frames.

#### 3.1. Hybrid Noise Initialization (HNI)

The paper proposes Hybrid Noise Initialization (HNI) to establish global semantics while enabling content diversity across long videos. Unlike methods that solely replicate or shuffle initial noise, HNI constructs the initial noise for subsequent video clips by mixing the initial noise of the first clip (𝒁0 𝑇) with newly generated random Gaussian noise (𝝐 𝑛 ∼ N (0, 𝑰 )). This process is governed by a mixing weight, *w*, which controls the balance between maintaining consistency with the first clip and introducing new content. A higher *w* increases diversity but may reduce long-range consistency, and vice versa. After mixing, frames within subsequent clips are cyclically swapped in small groups to further enrich content variability while maintaining the overall structure. This entire process generates the initial noisy latent representation for the entire long video, 𝒁 𝑇.

The short-video diffusion backbone then denoises these reorganized clips, allowing its inherent temporal modeling capabilities to establish connections among distant segments of the long video. As denoising progresses, *c(t)* gradually decreases, allowing WWS to dominate in later steps, where the focus shifts to refining local motion continuity and textural details. This soft fusion ensures a balance between global coherence and local smoothness.

#### 3.6. Key Distinctions from Existing Methods

The paper emphasizes that Diff-VF operates exclusively on noisy latent representations, external to the base model's architecture. This contrasts with methods that modify internal modules (e.g., temporal attention layers) or positional encodings. HNI differs from simple noise replication/shuffling by explicitly mixing first-clip noise with random noise, controlled by *w*. WWS uses a position-dependent aggregation rule for overlapping windows, distinct from uniform averaging. TES uniquely re-indexes latents into interleaved subsequences for global context, rather than relying on FIFO queues or positional encoding manipulations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations of Diff-VF, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating high-quality long videos and enhancing existing ones.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison

The evaluations were performed using VBench-Long metrics, which assess temporal coherence (Subject Consistency, Motion Smoothness, Temporal Flickering), motion diversity (Dynamic Degree), semantic consistency (Overall Consistency, Human Action), and frame-wise quality (Imaging Quality, Aesthetic Quality). Experiments were conducted on two base models: LaVie (U-Net-based) and HunyuanVideo (DiT-based).

- **Performance on LaVie:**
 - The base model exhibited high Dynamic Degree but suffered from poor Temporal Flickering and visual instability, indicating that motion was partly due to incoherence.
 - FreeNoise and FreeLong improved temporal consistency but significantly reduced Dynamic Degree, suggesting that strong temporal correlation suppressed content evolution, leading to static or repetitive output.
 - Diff-VF achieved a more balanced performance, maintaining competitive temporal consistency metrics (e.g., Motion Smoothness, Temporal Flickering) while preserving a higher level of motion dynamics compared to FreeNoise and FreeLong. It also demonstrated better semantic alignment (Overall Consistency, Human Action) and comparable Imaging Quality and Aesthetic Quality.

- **Performance on HunyuanVideo:**
 - Similar trends were observed, with methods like RIFLEx and FreeLong achieving high Subject Consistency and Motion Smoothness but very low Dynamic Degree, again indicating overly static generation.
 - FreeNoise showed stronger dynamics but still tended towards content repetition.
 - Diff-VF obtained the highest Dynamic Degree among compared methods while maintaining reasonable temporal and visual quality, corroborating its ability to balance coherence and diversity.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparison

Qualitative results from various base models (LaVie, HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX1.5-5B, VideoCrafter2) visually supported the quantitative findings.

- **Base Models:** Directly applying base models often resulted in noticeable frame-level instability, high-frequency noise, and temporal flickering.
- **Noise-Design-Based Methods (FreeNoise, FreeLong):** These methods frequently produced visually consistent but weakly changing content. Strong correlations in initial noise tended to bias clips towards similar latent trajectories, leading to periodic content, repeated motions, or insufficient temporal evolution.
- **RIFLEx (DiT-based):** While preserving temporal structure, RIFLEx could still lead to repeated or slowed-down motion.
- **Diff-VF:** The proposed method generated more visible temporal evolution, maintaining dynamic scenes and object movements while preserving the identity and background structure. It avoided the abrupt transitions and repetitions observed in other methods, yielding outputs that were both temporally consistent and dynamic.

- **Long Video Enhancement:** The qualitative results for long video enhancement demonstrated that Diff-VF could incorporate faithful and realistic details into videos while preserving long-range temporal consistency and fidelity to the original content.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies validated the contribution of each proposed strategy and hyperparameters.

- **Hybrid Noise Initialization (HNI):** Removing HNI (i.e., using independent Gaussian noise for each clip) led to significantly poorer long-range consistency, motion smoothness, and increased temporal flickering. This confirmed HNI's role in ensuring overall video consistency and smoothness by managing content variation.
- **Weighted Window Sampling (WWS):** Ablating WWS resulted in abrupt transitions at window boundaries, negatively impacting temporal consistency and motion smoothness. This highlighted WWS's importance for local continuity.
- **Temporal Extended Sampling (TES):** Without TES, videos maintained local smoothness but suffered from content drift and reduced global consistency over time, demonstrating TES's role in establishing long-range dependencies.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis:** The studies on *α* (fusion weight scale), *c<sub>s</sub>* (schedule exponent), and *w* (additional noise weight in HNI) confirmed that these parameters serve as explicit control knobs for the consistency-diversity trade-off. Adjusting them allowed for fine-tuning the balance between global structure, local smoothness, and content variation. For instance, increasing *w* introduced more stochastic content and improved motion diversity but could decrease subject consistency if excessive.

Overall, the findings indicate that Diff-VF successfully integrates complementary strategies to achieve a more favorable balance between temporal coherence and motion diversity compared to previous training-free methods, across various base diffusion model architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Diff-VF framework represents a notable contribution to the field of generative artificial intelligence, particularly in video synthesis. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enabling Long Video Generation from Existing Models:** The most direct impact of Diff-VF is its ability to convert pre-trained short-video diffusion models into long-video generators without requiring any further training or fine-tuning. This is significant because training large-scale video diffusion models is computationally expensive and data-intensive. Diff-VF offers a cost-effective and immediate solution for extending the utility of existing models to long-form content, democratizing access to long video generation capabilities.

2. **Addressing the Consistency-Diversity Trade-off:** A core challenge in long video generation has been balancing long-range temporal coherence with motion diversity. Many previous methods tend to prioritize one at the expense of the other, leading to either repetitive/static videos or temporally incoherent ones. Diff-VF's integrated approach, combining Hybrid Noise Initialization, Weighted Window Sampling, and Temporal Extended Sampling, demonstrates a more balanced solution, generating videos that are both semantically consistent over time and exhibit rich, dynamic motion. This balance is crucial for creating engaging and realistic long-form content.

3. **Model-Agnostic and Plug-and-Play Design:** The framework's design, which operates on latent representations outside the base model's architecture, makes it highly versatile. It has been demonstrated to work with different types of video diffusion backbones (U-Net based like LaVie and Transformer-based like HunyuanVideo), including those with varying spatial-temporal attention mechanisms (2D+1D and 3D attention). This model-agnostic nature increases its applicability across a wide range of current and future video diffusion models, making it a broadly useful tool for researchers and developers.

4. **Improved Video Quality and Smoothness:** By effectively removing inter-window discontinuities through weighted fusion and establishing long-range dependencies, Diff-VF significantly enhances the overall visual quality, motion smoothness, and temporal coherence of generated long videos, mitigating common artifacts like flickering and abrupt transitions.

5. **Extension to Video Enhancement:** The integration of Skip Residual Guidance allows Diff-VF to extend its benefits to long-video enhancement. This is important for improving the fidelity and realism of existing long videos, again without the need for model-specific training, offering a versatile tool for video post-processing.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The explicit control mechanisms (e.g., the `w` parameter for noise mixing, `α` and `c<sub>s</sub>` for local-global fusion) provide clear avenues for future research into fine-tuning the consistency-diversity balance. The identified limitations, such as handling abrupt scene cuts or complex temporal dynamics, also open directions for further development, potentially through noise searching or multi-prompt generation techniques.

In summary, Diff-VF's training-free, model-agnostic, and balanced approach to long video generation and enhancement has the potential to significantly advance the practical application of diffusion models in creative industries, content creation, and other areas requiring extended, high-quality video content.
