# CREStE: Scalable Mapless Navigation with Internet Scale Priors and Counterfactual Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> CRESTE presents a scalable mapless navigation framework leveraging visual foundation models for perception and counterfactual inverse reinforcement learning. It enables reliable autonomous navigation in diverse and unseen urban environments, demonstrating significantly reduced human interventions and robust performance over kilometer-scale missions.

## Problem

- Traditional map-centric navigation struggles with scalability, maintenance, and generalization to dynamic or unforeseen urban environments.
- Existing learning-based mapless navigation methods face challenges with data scarcity, generalization to 'open-set' factors (unseen objects, terrains), and brittleness of zero-shot transfer from large visual models.
- Defining explicit, hand-engineered reward functions for complex urban navigation is difficult and often results in brittle performance, while collecting large-scale demonstration datasets for learning is prohibitively expensive.

## Method

- **CREStE** develops a scalable mapless navigation framework comprising a perceptual encoder and a reward function.
- The perceptual encoder distills knowledge from Visual Foundation Models (==Dinov2==, ==SAM2==) to generate robust, structured ==Bird's-Eye-View (BEV)== features from RGB-D inputs.
- Utilizes a novel active learning framework based on ==Counterfactual Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)== to efficiently learn human-aligned navigation costs from limited expert and *suboptimal* counterfactual demonstrations.

## Results

- CRESTE achieved approximately **70%** fewer human interventions compared to the next best baseline in seen environments and **five times** fewer in unseen urban environments.
- Successfully completed a **2-kilometer** long-horizon mission in an unseen urban trail with only **1 intervention**, while the best baseline struggled significantly with **48 interventions**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of structured BEV representations (**28%** more interventions without them) and counterfactual demonstrations (**70%** fewer interventions with them).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) can be effectively distilled into robot-specific perceptual encoders for robust open-set generalization in autonomous navigation.
> - Structured Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representations are crucial for robust and generalizable policy reasoning in urban navigation.
> - Counterfactual Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) offers a data-efficient method for aligning learned navigation costs with human preferences by leveraging easily identifiable suboptimal behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous navigation is broadly divided into two main paradigms: map-centric and mapless approaches. Traditional map-centric solutions rely on high-definition (HD) maps and precise localization, offering high reliability in known environments but struggling with scalability, map maintenance, and generalization to unforeseen factors or dynamic changes in the environment. CRESTE positions itself firmly within the **mapless navigation** paradigm, which aims to achieve goal-reaching without reliance on expensive and fragile HD maps.

Mapless navigation has gained traction due to its potential for greater scalability and adaptability in open-world settings. However, existing mapless solutions face significant challenges, particularly in outdoor urban environments. The paper meticulously outlines these challenges and places CRESTE within this landscape:

- **Traditional Geometric-Only Approaches:** Earlier mapless solutions (e.g.,) primarily focused on geometric obstacle avoidance. While robust for simple static obstacles, they lack the ability to understand semantic cues (e.g., crosswalks, signs) or terrain preferences (e.g., grass vs. concrete), which are crucial for safe and human-aligned urban navigation. CRESTE explicitly aims to go "beyond geometry."
- **Learning-Based Approaches:** The advent of deep learning has led to learning-based mapless methods that can consider more complex factors. However, these methods face their own set of hurdles:
 - **Data Scarcity:** Training robust deep learning models typically requires vast amounts of diverse data, which is expensive and time-consuming to collect for robot deployments.
 - **Robustness and Generalization:** Models often overfit to training data, leading to brittle performance in novel or "open-set" scenarios (unseen objects, terrains, lighting conditions).
 - **Single/Multi-Factor Perception:** Methods like PACER and TerrainNet learn representations for specific factors (terrain, semantics) but require prior knowledge of all relevant classes and manual tuning of cost functions, limiting their "open-set" generalization.
 - **End-to-End Learning (Behavior Cloning, RL, IRL):** While promising for directly learning policies, these methods are highly data-hungry (Behavior Cloning) or require complex reward engineering/preference enumeration (RL/IRL). Overfitting remains a major concern without large-scale datasets.
 - **Zero-Shot Foundation Model Transfer (LLMs/VLMs):** Recent work leveraging large pre-trained models (e.g., PIVOT) offers hope for generalization without extensive robot data, by transferring knowledge from internet-scale data. However, CRESTE empirically demonstrates that these models are "poorly attuned to urban navigation," leading to brittle zero-shot transfer, as they may not correctly identify or prioritize navigation-critical features.

CRESTE addresses these limitations by offering a **scalable learning-based mapless navigation framework** that integrates the strengths of visual foundation models (VFMs) for generalizable perception and a novel counterfactual inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) for efficient reward alignment. This approach marks a significant step towards practical and robust autonomous navigation in the real world, bridging the gap between general-purpose vision models and robot-specific navigation needs. It moves beyond hand-curated features and towards a more adaptable system that learns preferences from limited human feedback.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of CRESTE is to **address the open-world generalization and robustness challenges of outdoor urban navigation** for mapless systems. The authors aim to develop a framework that enables robots to reliably navigate diverse and unseen urban environments without relying on high-definition maps or extensive, task-specific datasets.

This overarching objective is broken down into two critical sub-objectives, which also highlight the core motivations for the CRESTE framework:

1. **Learning Perceptual Representations that Generalize to Open-Set Factors:**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional learning-based methods rely on a pre-defined, hand-curated set of semantic classes or terrain types. This inherently limits their ability to generalize to novel objects (e.g., new types of dynamic entities like scooters, specific street furniture), unforeseen terrains (e.g., unusual types of pavement or ground cover), or unexpected environmental conditions.
 - **CRESTE's approach:** To overcome this, the authors are motivated to leverage the "internet-scale priors" embedded within Visual Foundation Models (VFMs). These models, pre-trained on massive and diverse image datasets, are hypothesized to contain a broad understanding of real-world semantics, geometry, and entities that can be distilled for navigation.

2. **Inferring Expert-Aligned Navigation Costs from Limited Demonstrations:**
 - **Motivation:** Acquiring large-scale, comprehensive robot demonstration datasets is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming for open-world settings. Furthermore, defining explicit, hand-engineered reward functions for complex urban navigation is often challenging and prone to errors or brittleness. The goal is to learn what constitutes "good" navigation behavior (i.e., the underlying cost/reward function) with minimal human effort.
 - **CRESTE's approach:** The authors are motivated to develop a data-efficient method for learning navigation costs that are aligned with human preferences. They propose "counterfactual inverse reinforcement learning" as a novel active learning formulation. The core idea is that humans can more easily identify *undesirable* behaviors (counterfactuals) than provide perfect demonstrations or explicit reward functions. By showing the robot what *not* to do, the system can efficiently infer the desired navigation costs.

In essence, CRESTE is motivated by the need for **scalable, adaptable, and robust autonomous navigation** in dynamic and unpredictable urban environments. It seeks to achieve this by intelligently combining the generalization power of foundation models with a human-in-the-loop reward learning mechanism that is both sample-efficient and interpretable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CRESTE adopts a modular, learning-based approach to mapless urban navigation, composed of two key, trainable components: a perceptual encoder and a reward function. The overall system is designed to be end-to-end trainable, leveraging powerful pre-trained visual foundation models (VFMs) for robust perception and a novel inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) framework for learning human-aligned navigation costs.

#### 3.1. CRESTE Model Architecture

1. **Perceptual Encoder (Θ):**
 - **Input:** Current RGB image (`o_rgb,t`) and sparse depth observation (`o_depth,t`) from the robot's sensors.
 - **Output:** A completed depth image (`y_depth,t`) and a structured Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) feature map (`y_bev,t`).
 - **Components:**
 - **RGB-D Encoder (`f_rgbd`):** Inspired by TerrainNet, it uses an EfficientNet-B0 backbone to extract latent image features (`z_rgbd`) and a depth completion head (`f_depth`) to predict a dense depth map.
 - **Lift-Splat Module (`f_splat`):** Lifts the image features to 3D and "splats" them onto an unstructured BEV feature map (`z_bev,splat`).
 - **BEV Inpainting Backbone (`f_bev`):** A U-Net based network that takes the unstructured BEV features and predicts a *structured* BEV feature map (`y_bev`). This structured map consists of three distinct layers: static panoptic features (`y_bev,static`), dynamic panoptic features (`y_bev,dynamic`), and elevation features (`y_bev,elev`).
 - **Key Architectural Modifications for Open-Set Generalization:**
 - **Semantic Decoder (`f_semantic`):** This head promotes learning semantic and geometric-aware features by regressing image features from **Dinov2**, a powerful VFM. This distillation process allows the encoder to inherit robustness to perceptual aliasing and open-set semantic understanding from Dinov2.
 - **Panoptic Map Decoders (`f_bev,static`, `f_bev,dynamic`):** These decoders are supervised to ensure consistency with BEV instance maps generated by **SegmentAnythingv2 (SAM2)**. Using Supervised Contrastive Loss, features belonging to the same instance are encouraged to be closer in embedding space, providing instance-awareness crucial for urban scenes with dynamic entities. This synergistically unifies Dinov2's semantic understanding with SAM2's instance segmentation capabilities.

2. **Reward Function (`r_ϕ`):**
 - **Input:** The structured BEV feature map (`y_bev,t`) from the perceptual encoder.
 - **Output:** A BEV scalar reward map (`y_reward,t`), which effectively serves as a costmap for planning.
 - **Architecture:** Implemented as a 0.5M parameter Multi-Scale Fully Convolutional Network (MS FCN), a common choice for learning spatially invariant reward functions from visual inputs.

#### 3.2. CRESTE Training Procedure

CRESTE training is a two-stage process: first, the perceptual encoder (Θ) is trained and frozen, and then the reward function (`r_ϕ`) is trained using a novel active learning framework.

1. **Training the Perceptual Encoder (Θ):**
 - **Supervision:** Θ is jointly trained using several loss functions:
 - `L_rgbd`: Combines MSE loss for `f_semantic` (regressing to Dinov2 features) and Cross-Entropy loss for `f_depth` (depth completion, discretized into bins).
 - `L_bev`: Supervises `f_bev` with Supervised Contrastive Loss for static and dynamic panoptic features (aligning with SAM2 instance labels) and L1 regression loss for elevation features (from LiDAR data).
 - **Scalable Label Generation:** A crucial aspect is the *automatic distillation label generation module*. This module leverages VFMs (Dinov2 and SAM2) and raw sensor data (sequential SE(3) robot poses, RGB-point cloud pairs) to generate training labels for the perceptual encoder *without manual annotation*:
 - **Dinov2 for Semantic Labels:** Frozen Dinov2 encoder is used to generate semantic feature map labels for `f_semantic`.
 - **LiDAR/Image Processing for Depth Labels:** Point clouds are projected to images and filtered to generate dense depth maps for `f_depth`.
 - **SAM2/LiDAR for BEV Panoptic Labels:** SAM2 is prompted with dynamic object bounding boxes to generate dynamic BEV instance masks by backprojecting and clustering. For static objects, SAM2 is queried with a grid of points, filtered, and merged across frames for consistency. These are then projected to BEV. Elevation maps are generated by accumulating static 3D points from LiDAR. This automated labeling pipeline is key to CRESTE's scalability.

2. **Training the Reward Function (`r_ϕ`) – Counterfactual Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL):**
 - Once Θ is frozen, `r_ϕ` is trained using a novel objective `L_IRL` within an active learning framework.
 - **Core Idea of Counterfactual IRL:** The objective maximizes the likelihood of expert trajectories while minimizing the likelihood of *counterfactual (suboptimal)* trajectories. Counterfactuals are trajectories that hold all variables constant except for the path taken, allowing precise feedback on undesirable behaviors (e.g., veering off a crosswalk).
 - **`L_IRL` Derivation:** It's derived from the Bradley-Terry model of preferences, which models the probability of one trajectory being preferred over another. The objective `min_π max_ϕ [J_E(r_ϕ) - (αJ_S(r_ϕ) + (1-α)J_π(r_ϕ))]` aims to find a reward function (`r_ϕ`) where the expert's return (`J_E`) is maximized, while returns for suboptimal (`J_S`) and the current policy's (`J_π`) trajectories are minimized. The hyperparameter `α` balances the importance of suboptimal and expert demonstrations. This formulation efficiently incorporates both "good" and "bad" examples.
 - **Active Reward Learning Framework:** This iterative human-in-the-loop process refines `r_ϕ`:
 - **Phase I (Warmstart):** `r_ϕ` is initially trained only on expert demonstrations (setting `α=0`).
 - **Phase II (Synthetic Counterfactual Generation):** The robot executes paths using the current `r_ϕ`. If policy rollouts deviate significantly from expert paths (e.g., using Hausdorff distance), candidate alternate trajectories are generated by perturbing expert paths. A human annotator then reviews these and explicitly labels *undesirable* paths as counterfactuals using a dedicated tool that shows front-view and BEV context. This is highly efficient, as humans easily spot "bad" behavior.
 - **Phase III (Counterfactual Reward Alignment):** `r_ϕ` is retrained using both the original expert demonstrations and the newly collected counterfactuals (`α` is non-zero). This process is repeated until `r_ϕ` aligns with human preferences and the learned policy converges.

#### 3.3. Local Planning and Control

- **Global Planning:** Uses OpenStreetMap to generate coarse GPS goals, then selects the next local subgoal based on proximity to the final goal.
- **Local Planning:** Employs a DWA (Dynamic Window Approach) style method, enumerating constant curvature arcs. Each arc is evaluated by sampling points, calculating a discounted cost from the predicted BEV reward map (converted to a costmap), and adding a goal-reaching cost. The trajectory with the lowest combined cost is selected.
- **Control:** A 1D time optimal control generates low-level actions to follow the chosen local trajectory.

This comprehensive methodology, particularly the combination of VFM-powered perception and counterfactual IRL, allows CRESTE to address the dual challenges of open-set generalization and data-efficient reward learning for complex mapless navigation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive real-world experiments using a Clearpath Jackal mobile robot to evaluate CRESTE's performance, contrasting it against state-of-the-art baselines across diverse urban environments. The evaluation metrics included Average Subgoal Completion Time (AST), Percentage of Subgoals Reached (%S), Normalized Intervention Rate (NIR), Total Distance Driven (Dist. (m)), and Total Interventions (Total Int.).

#### 4.1. Generalizability to Unseen Urban Environments (Q1)

- **Superior Performance:** CRESTE significantly outperforms all state-of-the-art baselines (Geometric Only, PACER+G, ViNT, PIVOT) in both *seen* (in-distribution) and *unseen* (out-of-distribution) urban environments for short-horizon mapless navigation.
- **Reduced Interventions:** CRESTE required approximately **70% fewer human interventions** compared to the next best approach (PACER+G) in seen environments and five times fewer interventions in unseen environments (e.g., NIR of 0.00 for Hemphill Park, an unseen residential park, vs. PACER+G's 9.47-17.93).
- **VLM Limitations:** The VLM-based navigation method (PIVOT) performed poorly relative to other methods, corroborating the authors' claim that VLMs, despite internet-scale priors, are often not well attuned to the specific nuances and critical factors required for robust urban navigation.
- **Sensor Modality Impact:** The combination of **RGB and LiDAR inputs** (CRESTE original) yielded the most favorable results, outperforming stereo RGB and monocular RGB inputs. LiDAR's robustness to challenging lighting conditions (e.g., lens flares) proved crucial for accurate costmap prediction.
- **Distilled vs. Frozen VFMs:** Distilling Dinov2 features (CRESTE-RGB) was found to perform favorably compared to using frozen Dinov2 features (CRESTE-RGB-FROZEN), suggesting that the distillation objective helps the RGB-D encoder learn artifact-free features and adapt VFM knowledge more effectively for navigation.

#### 4.2. Importance of Structured BEV Perceptual Representations (Q2)

- **Significant Performance Drop without Structure:** Ablation studies (comparing CRESTE-st and CRESTE-cfs-st against CRESTE) demonstrated the critical role of structured BEV representations. Without them, CRESTE-st incurred **28% more interventions** on average across all environments.
- **Impact on Reasoning:** The authors hypothesize that structured representations encode higher-level, more generalizable features that are easier for policies to reason about compared to unstructured, lower-level features. This validates the design choice of the BEV inpainting backbone.

#### 4.3. Importance of Counterfactual Demonstrations (Q3)

- **Substantial Reduction in Interventions:** Comparing CRESTE (with counterfactuals) against CRESTE-cfs (without counterfactuals) showed a dramatic improvement. Using counterfactuals reduced the number of interventions by **70% in seen environments and 69% in unseen environments**.
- **Efficiency of Counterfactuals:** This finding strongly supports the effectiveness of the counterfactual Inverse Reinforcement Learning objective in efficiently aligning learned rewards with expert preferences, even with limited additional annotations (3% of the training dataset).

#### 4.4. Performance on Kilometer-Scale Mapless Navigation (Q4)

- **Long-Horizon Success:** In a demanding 2-kilometer long-horizon mission in an *unseen* urban trail, CRESTE achieved remarkable performance, completing the mission with **only 1 intervention** and a high percentage of subgoals reached (99.45%).
- **Outperforming Best Baseline:** The top-performing baseline from short-horizon tests, PACER+G, struggled significantly in this long-horizon task, driving only 1.34 km with **48 interventions** before timing out.
- **Robustness to Perceptual Aliasing:** CRESTE proved robust to perceptual aliasing caused by lighting and weather variations, consistently predicting accurate costmaps. In contrast, PACER+G often failed due to poor costmap predictions under varying conditions or inadequate consideration of geometric factors like curb gaps.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that CRESTE achieves **state-of-the-art performance** in mapless urban navigation across various challenging environments. Its strength lies in its ability to generalize to novel situations and effectively learn human-aligned navigation preferences with high data efficiency, attributing its success to the synergistic combination of VFM-distilled structured BEV representations and counterfactual-guided reward learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CRESTE represents a significant advancement in the field of autonomous mobile robotics, particularly for mapless navigation in complex outdoor urban environments. Its contributions have several far-reaching implications:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By effectively distilling knowledge from visual foundation models (Dinov2, SAM2) into structured BEV representations, CRESTE achieves unprecedented generalization to "open-set" factors like novel semantic classes, terrains, and dynamic entities. This addresses a critical limitation of previous learning-based methods, moving beyond pre-defined taxonomies and enabling robots to reliably operate in the truly unpredictable real world. Its robustness to adverse perceptual conditions (e.g., challenging lighting) further enhances its real-world applicability.

2. **Scalability through Reduced Data and Mapping Requirements:**
 - **Mapless Paradigm:** CRESTE fundamentally reduces reliance on expensive and high-maintenance high-definition maps, which are a major bottleneck for scaling autonomous deployments.
 - **Data Efficiency:** The counterfactual inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) framework drastically reduces the need for massive, perfectly curated expert demonstration datasets. By enabling learning from limited expert demonstrations combined with easily obtainable *suboptimal* counterfactual feedback, it makes the process of teaching robots complex behaviors much more efficient and human-friendly. This "data flywheel" of continuous improvement through targeted human feedback is a powerful enabler for real-world adoption.

3. **Advancement in Reward Learning:** The novel counterfactual IRL formulation is a key methodological contribution. It offers a principled and sample-efficient way to infer complex, human-aligned navigation costs. This shifts the burden from explicit, laborious reward engineering to more intuitive human feedback on "what not to do," accelerating the development and refinement of robotic policies.

4. **Real-World Applicability and Long-Horizon Navigation:** The rigorous real-world validation, including kilometer-scale missions in unseen environments, powerfully demonstrates CRESTE's practical effectiveness. The ability to complete a 2-kilometer mission with only a single human intervention is a compelling testament to its maturity and potential for deployment in various applications, from urban delivery to last-mile logistics and diverse outdoor robotic tasks.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** CRESTE provides a strong foundation for future research in several promising directions:
 - **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The authors identify the need to condition reward maps on embodiment-specific constraints, opening avenues for more versatile robot platforms.
 - **Long-Term Reasoning and Memory:** Extending CRESTE with memory could enable robots to recover from dead ends or navigate more complex, multi-step scenarios, pushing beyond single-timestep local planning.
 - **Automated Counterfactual Generation:** The reliance on human labeling for counterfactuals, while efficient, could be further scaled by exploring automated generation using advanced VLMs/LLMs, democratizing access to this powerful feedback mechanism.
 - **Comprehensive Preference Learning:** Integrating broader preference feedback could enable more nuanced reasoning about different suboptimal paths.

In conclusion, CRESTE represents a significant leap forward in making autonomous outdoor navigation more practical, scalable, and robust. Its innovative integration of foundation models for perception and a human-in-the-loop counterfactual learning approach for preferences addresses critical bottlenecks, paving the way for broader deployment of autonomous robots in challenging and dynamic real-world environments.
