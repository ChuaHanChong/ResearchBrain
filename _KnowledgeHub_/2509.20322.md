# VisualMimic: Visual Humanoid Loco-Manipulation via Motion Tracking and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> VisualMimic presents a visual sim-to-real framework that enables humanoid robots to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks driven by egocentric vision. The system achieves robust zero-shot transfer of diverse skills, such as pushing and kicking, from simulation to a physical Unitree G1 robot, even in unstructured outdoor environments.

## Problem

- Integrating egocentric visual perception with whole-body control for humanoid robots in dynamic, unstructured environments presents a significant challenge.
- Existing methods for humanoid control often rely on external motion capture systems, limiting deployment to controlled lab settings, or struggle with complex whole-body interaction in vision-based reinforcement learning due to vast exploration and action spaces.
- Achieving reliable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for intricate, human-like loco-manipulation skills without real-world fine-tuning remains a major obstacle in robotics.

## Method

- A hierarchical control framework is employed, featuring a low-level task-agnostic keypoint tracking policy that learns human-like dexterity and a high-level task-specific visuomotor policy that generates simplified keypoint commands from visual input.
- A two-stage teacher-student distillation pipeline trains both policies: a privileged teacher for robust motion tracking and another for task-relevant object state interaction, with knowledge distilled into vision-based student policies.
- Training stability is enhanced through techniques like injecting multiplicative noise into keypoint commands during low-level training and clipping high-level actions to remain within a statistically defined "Human Motion Space" (HMS).

## Results

- VisualMimic achieved robust performance across diverse simulated tasks, with the vision-based policy pushing a 3.8-kilogram box an average of 37 meters per minute and dribbling a football 135 meters per minute.
- The visuomotor policies demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to the real Unitree G1 humanoid robot for tasks including lifting, kicking, and pushing, maintaining stable performance in challenging outdoor conditions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical necessity of each design component, showing that removing noise injection, action clipping, or the hierarchical structure significantly degraded performance or led to complete training failure across tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The hierarchical decomposition effectively manages the complexity of vision-based whole-body control, allowing for the learning of both foundational dexterity and task-specific visuomotor skills.
> - Distilling human motion priors into a low-level keypoint tracker is crucial for generating natural, human-like behaviors and significantly stabilizes the training process for high-level policies.
> - Robust sim-to-real transfer for egocentric vision can be achieved by systematically applying heavy random masking to simulated depth images and randomizing camera orientations to mimic real-world sensor noise and variability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is striving to imbue robots with human-like dexterity and adaptability for real-world tasks, particularly loco-manipulation (combined locomotion and manipulation) in unstructured environments. VisualMimic directly addresses a critical challenge in this landscape: the tight integration of egocentric visual perception with whole-body control.

Existing approaches to humanoid control can be broadly categorized. First, **locomotion-focused methods** (e.g., Radosavovic et al.) excel at navigating complex terrains but typically do not address object interaction, limiting their versatility. Second, **approaches relying on external motion capture systems** (e.g., Su et al., Xue et al.) achieve high precision in object state estimation but are confined to controlled laboratory settings, preventing real-world deployment. Third, **vision-based methods for object interaction** represent the most relevant comparison. These can be further divided into:
1. **Imitation learning approaches** (e.g., Ze et al., Ben et al., Li et al.): While promising in task versatility, they are constrained by the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality human demonstration data and exhibit limited generalization capabilities.
2. **Sim-to-real reinforcement learning (RL) methods** (e.g., Allshire et al., Wang et al.): These offer greater robustness and generalizability by leveraging vast amounts of simulated interaction. However, current vision-based RL for humanoids is often limited to simpler interactions like sitting or stair climbing, falling short of complex, human-level object interaction due to the vast exploration and action spaces inherent in whole-body humanoid control.

VisualMimic positions itself as a significant advancement within the **sim-to-real RL paradigm for visual humanoid-object interaction**. It tackles the limitations of existing vision-based RL by introducing a hierarchical control framework that manages the complexity of humanoid exploration and action spaces. Unlike methods confined to upper-body manipulation (e.g., Lin et al.) or perceptive locomotion, VisualMimic aims for *whole-body loco-manipulation* driven by *egocentric vision* with robust *real-world transfer*. The authors specifically highlight their differentiation from works like VideoMimic (which lacks whole-body loco-manipulation) and other methods like Hitter, Recipe, and HEAD (which either lack visual policies or whole-body dexterity) as shown in Table I. Furthermore, while works like HumanoidBench and SkillBlender explore hierarchical frameworks in simulation, VisualMimic distinguishes itself by achieving successful and robust real-world deployment, addressing issues like jitteriness and dependence on privileged object states. By demonstrating zero-shot transfer of complex loco-manipulation skills to physical hardware, VisualMimic pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in practical humanoid robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of VisualMimic is to equip humanoid robots with human-like object interaction capabilities in dynamic and unstructured environments. This broad goal is underpinned by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Integrate Egocentric Vision and Whole-Body Control:** The primary motivation is to seamlessly combine visual perception from the robot's perspective with the intricate demands of whole-body control, mimicking how humans perceive and interact with their surroundings.
2. **Overcome Limitations of External Sensing:** To move beyond reliance on external motion capture systems, which restrict robot deployment to controlled laboratory settings, and enable autonomy in diverse real-world scenarios.
3. **Enhance Generalization and Robustness:** To develop a framework that allows policies to generalize across a wide array of loco-manipulation tasks and exhibit robustness to real-world variabilities (e.g., lighting, uneven terrain), addressing the limitations of task-specific imitation learning and constrained sim-to-real RL.
4. **Facilitate Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A core objective is to ensure that policies trained exclusively in simulation can be directly transferred to physical humanoid robots without requiring further real-world fine-tuning, a significant bottleneck in robotics research.
5. **Enable Complex Loco-Manipulation Tasks:** To demonstrate the ability of humanoid robots to perform diverse and challenging tasks such as lifting, pushing large objects, dribbling, and kicking, requiring intricate coordination of the entire body.
6. **Achieve Human-like Behavior:** To distill human motion priors into robot control, ensuring that the generated movements are not only effective but also fluid and natural, closely resembling human actions.
7. **Address RL Challenges in Humanoids:** To mitigate the challenges of large exploration and action spaces in vision-based reinforcement learning for humanoids through a hierarchical design and novel training stabilization techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VisualMimic is a visual sim-to-real framework built on a hierarchical design, allowing for effective training and zero-shot transfer of visuomotor policies to real humanoid robots. The approach comprises two main policies and several key training stabilization and sim-to-real transfer techniques.

**A. Hierarchical Policy Design:**
The framework employs a two-level hierarchical control structure:
1. **Low-Level Task-Agnostic Keypoint Tracking Policy ($\pi_{tracker}$):** This policy is responsible for precise, whole-body motion tracking. It learns human-like dexterity priors from a large dataset of human motion and is designed to be task-agnostic, meaning it can be reused across different high-level tasks.
2. **High-Level Task-Specific Visuomotor Policy ($\pi_{generator}$):** This policy generates simplified keypoint commands for the low-level tracker, conditioned on egocentric visual and proprioceptive inputs. It is trained specifically for each task, leveraging the underlying dexterity provided by the low-level policy.

**B. General Keypoint Tracker Training (Stage 1):**
To ensure human-like behaviors while tracking simplified keypoint commands, a two-stage teacher-student distillation pipeline is used:
- **Teacher Motion Tracker ($\pi_{tracker}^{tea}$):** A privileged teacher policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with access to full future whole-body motion reference frames (2 seconds ahead) and privileged proprioceptive information (e.g., foot contact forces). This allows it to achieve highly accurate and smooth motion tracking. The reward function encourages accurate motion tracking and penalizes artifacts like jitter. Human motion data from AMASS and OMOMO is retargeted using GMR to generate suitable humanoid motions.
- **Student Keypoint Tracker ($\pi_{tracker}^{stu}$):** The knowledge from the teacher is distilled into a student policy using DAgger. The student operates solely on current proprioception and a compact set of keypoint commands ($c_{kp_t}$). These commands consist of root position error and relative position errors for five keypoints: the head, two hands, and two feet. This distillation ensures that the student policy captures human-like motion despite the simplified command interface.

**C. Task-Specific Keypoint Generator Training (Stage 2):**
To train the high-level policy efficiently for diverse tasks, another two-stage teacher-student distillation is applied to overcome the challenges of visual RL (partial observability, slow simulation):
- **Teacher State-based Keypoint Generator ($\pi_{generator}^{tea}$):** A teacher policy is initially trained using PPO with privileged access to task-relevant object states (e.g., positions, contact forces) alongside proprioceptive information. This "privileged" information accelerates learning and enables the policy to effectively solve the task. Task-specific reward functions are designed to guide behavior, including rewards for `Approach` (contact with target points), `Forward progress` (object movement in the intended direction), `Force` (sufficient force exertion), `Look at object` (robot facing the object), and `Drift` (penalizing lateral deviation).
- **Student Vision-based Keypoint Generator ($\pi_{generator}^{stu}$):** The state-based teacher policy is then distilled into a student policy via DAgger. This student policy is designed for real-world deployment, relying only on egocentric depth images (processed by a CNN encoder) and proprioceptive inputs, bypassing the need for external object state estimation. RGB images are avoided due to their significant sim-to-real gap.

**D. Stabilizing Training with Human Motion Space (HMS):**
To address instability during RL training, where policies might explore actions outside feasible human motion:
- **Noised Keypoint Commands for Low-Level Student Training:** Multiplicative noise (sampled uniformly between 0.5 and 1.5) is injected into keypoint commands during the student tracker's training. This enhances the low-level policy's robustness, allowing it to adapt to slightly noisy or varied commands from the high-level policy.
- **Action Clipping for High-Level Policies:** The output actions of the high-level keypoint generator are clipped to remain within a statistically defined "Human Motion Space" (HMS). This range, $\mu \pm 1.64\sigma$, is derived from the mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$) of keypoint commands observed during low-level policy training, covering approximately 90% of human motion variations.

**E. Real-World Deployment Enhancements:**
Critical steps are taken to ensure successful zero-shot transfer to the Unitree G1 humanoid robot:
- **Vision-Based Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To bridge the gap between clean simulated depth images and noisy real-world data from the RealSense D435i camera, heavy random masking (fixed bottom-left mask, multiple rectangular masks with varying values) is applied to simulated depth images during training. Real-world depth images are pre-processed with spatial and temporal filters. Randomization of the camera's orientation (up to ±5°) is also introduced to account for potential physical sensor drift.
- **Safe Real-World Deployment via Binary Commands:** A binary command signal (0 for pause, 1 for execute) is integrated into the policy training. This allows safe starting, pausing, and ending of tasks, preventing the robot from falling when the program terminates. The robot always starts in a pause state, and the reward system is adjusted accordingly for each command state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VisualMimic demonstrates significant success in enabling visuomotor humanoid loco-manipulation, validated through extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world.

1. **Effective Training for Diverse Human-like Tasks (Q1):**
 - In simulation, VisualMimic achieved robust performance across a wide range of tasks including Push Box, Kick Box, Lift Box, Reach Box, Large Kick, Kick Ball, Balance Ball, and Push Cube. For instance, the vision-based policy could push a 3.8-kilogram box an average of 37 meters per minute and dribble a football 135 meters per minute, demonstrating both effectiveness and straightness (Table II).
 - The hierarchical design was identified as crucial for this success, decomposing the complex problem into manageable sub-problems of motion tracking and generation.
 - The teacher-student training for the low-level tracker resulted in natural, human-like behaviors, in contrast to non-human-like motions observed without this component (Fig. 6).

2. **Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer (Q2):**
 - Visuomotor policies trained in simulation successfully transferred zero-shot to the real Unitree G1 humanoid robot for tasks such as Lift Box, Kick Ball, Kick Box, and Push Box (Fig. 3, Fig. 4).
 - Crucially, the policies maintained stable performance even in challenging outdoor environments, demonstrating robustness to fluctuating lighting, irregular ground surfaces, and environmental distractions.

3. **Demonstrated Whole-Body Dexterity (Q3):**
 - The framework enables the robot to utilize its entire body, including lower limbs, for object interaction. This was exemplified by adaptive strategies in tasks like Push Box, where the robot varied its posture (bending to push with both hands, leaning with a shoulder, or switching to one-handed pushing) based on friction conditions or box height (Fig. 7).
 - An ablation study confirmed that using only upper-body keypoints significantly hampered performance on tasks requiring lower-body engagement (e.g., Balance Ball), underscoring the importance of the 6-point keypoint design (head, hands, feet, root).

4. **Effective Utilization of Egocentric Vision (Q4):**
 - VisualMimic heavily relies on the robot's egocentric vision for object interaction, particularly with randomized object positions. An ablation study comparing vision-enabled policies with a variant trained without visual input showed a substantial performance drop for the latter (Table II), unequivocally highlighting the effectiveness of VisualMimic in leveraging vision.

5. **Necessity of Design Choices (Q5):**
 - Comprehensive ablation studies (Table III, Fig. 9) confirmed the critical role of each design component:
 - **Noised Keypoint Commands:** Removing noise during low-level training degraded performance in Kick Box and Kick Ball, and led to complete failure in Push Box.
 - **Action Clipping:** Eliminating action clipping caused return collapse in later training stages across all tasks, indicating that the unconstrained high-level action space became unmanageable.
 - **Keypoint Interface:** Using a 23-DoF interface instead of the compact 6-point keypoint interface significantly reduced performance or prevented learning entirely.
 - **Global-Frame Tracking:** Training trackers in the local frame resulted in substantial performance drops, confirming the benefit of global-frame tracking for reducing drift and facilitating high-level policy training.
 - **Teacher-Student Distillation for High-Level Policy:** Direct Visual RL (without the state-based teacher) failed entirely in Kick Box and Kick Ball, underscoring the efficiency gains from the teacher-student approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VisualMimic represents a substantial step forward in the realm of humanoid robotics, with far-reaching significance and potential impact:

1. **Advancement in Humanoid Loco-Manipulation:** The framework pushes the state-of-the-art by enabling humanoid robots to perform complex, whole-body loco-manipulation tasks purely from egocentric vision, a capability previously challenging to achieve robustly in the real world. This unlocks new possibilities for autonomous humanoid interaction in dynamic environments.
2. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The robust zero-shot transfer capabilities, achieved through innovations like systematic depth image masking and camera pose randomization in simulation, provide a valuable blueprint for bridging the simulation-to-reality gap in vision-based robotics. This is critical for practical deployment, as real-world data collection and training are often prohibitive.
3. **Efficient and Stable Learning:** The hierarchical teacher-student distillation approach, combined with stabilization techniques like noise injection and action clipping within the Human Motion Space, offers an efficient and effective method to train complex visuomotor policies for high-dimensional humanoid robots. This addresses a core challenge of scalability in reinforcement learning for complex physical systems.
4. **Enhanced Dexterity and Adaptability:** By demonstrating versatile whole-body dexterity and adaptive strategies in varying environmental conditions (e.g., friction changes), VisualMimic moves humanoids closer to truly intelligent and adaptable agents. This capacity for flexible interaction with the environment is crucial for real-world utility.
5. **Generalizability:** The success across a diverse range of tasks and robustness to unstructured outdoor environments highlights the generalizability of the learned policies. This suggests that the framework is not limited to specific, pre-defined tasks but can be extended to new scenarios with relatively simple task-specific reward engineering.

The potential impact of VisualMimic is considerable. It could accelerate the development of versatile humanoid robots capable of operating in various unstructured settings, from assisting in homes and workplaces to performing tasks in hazardous or remote environments. The framework lays a foundation for future research into even more complex interactions, such as those involving deformable objects, long-horizon tasks, or human-robot collaboration, as acknowledged in the paper's limitations. Ultimately, VisualMimic brings the vision of general-purpose, physically embodied AI closer to reality, paving the way for humanoids to become truly indispensable agents in society.
