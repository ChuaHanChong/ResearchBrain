# SpatialTree: How Spatial Abilities Branch Out in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, ByteDance Seed, and Beijing Jiaotong University introduce SpatialTree, a cognitive-science-inspired hierarchical taxonomy and benchmark for evaluating multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) spatial abilities. The work reveals distinct hierarchical dependencies among spatial skills and demonstrates that targeted training, particularly with a differentiated "auto-think" reinforcement learning strategy, can systematically improve spatial intelligence across all cognitive levels.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for MLLMs treat spatial capabilities as fragmented and isolated tasks, leading to a shallow understanding of spatial intelligence.
- A lack of a unifying, hierarchical framework prevents a comprehensive understanding of how spatial abilities emerge, interact, and transfer within MLLMs.
- Current evaluation methods do not effectively reveal the underlying structure and cross-level dependencies of spatial intelligence in MLLMs, making targeted development challenging.

## Method

- Developed ==SpatialTree==, a ==four-level hierarchical taxonomy== (Perception, Mental Mapping, Mental Simulation, Agentic Competence) inspired by cognitive science to structure MLLM spatial abilities.
- Constructed SpatialTree-bench, a capability-centric benchmark with ==27 fine-grained sub-abilities==, by reorganizing existing data, leveraging expert perception models for new data generation, and curating novel datasets for agentic competence.
- Implemented targeted training interventions including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on foundational skills and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a hierarchy-aware =="auto-think" reward mechanism== to optimize for different levels of spatial complexity.

## Results

- Targeted SFT on single L1 abilities (e.g., Geometry Distance) led to positive cross-level transfer to higher-level skills (e.g., 'Goal Execution' improved by **+3.4**), while multi-ability SFT provided synergistic gains (**+1.1** overall) and mitigated negative intra-level transfer.
- The hierarchy-aware "auto-think" RL strategy achieved a consistent performance gain of **+3.6** across all SpatialTree levels compared to the SFT baseline, outperforming uniform RL approaches by adapting to the cognitive demands of each spatial task.
- MLLMs augmented with explicit reasoning mechanisms generally performed better, with Gemini 3 Flash achieving the highest overall score of **57.8** on SpatialTree-Bench, followed by Gemini 3 Pro (**56.5**) and Qwen3VL-235B (**40.0**) among open-source models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial abilities in MLLMs exhibit a clear hierarchical structure: lower-level perception skills are largely orthogonal, while higher levels (Mental Mapping, Mental Simulation, Agentic Competence) show strong interdependencies.
> - Targeted SFT on foundational L1 abilities can induce non-trivial positive cross-level transfer to higher-level spatial competence, with training on multiple L1 abilities synergistically boosting overall performance.
> - Effectively scaling spatial intelligence in MLLMs requires differentiated learning strategies, balancing "thinking" (deliberative reasoning) for complex tasks and "perceiving" (intuitive processing) for basic perception, as demonstrated by the "auto-think" RL strategy.
