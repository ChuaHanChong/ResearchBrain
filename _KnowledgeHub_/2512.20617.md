# SpatialTree: How Spatial Abilities Branch Out in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University, ByteDance Seed, and Beijing Jiaotong University introduce SpatialTree, a cognitive-science-inspired hierarchical taxonomy and benchmark for evaluating multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) spatial abilities. The work reveals distinct hierarchical dependencies among spatial skills and demonstrates that targeted training, particularly with a differentiated "auto-think" reinforcement learning strategy, can systematically improve spatial intelligence across all cognitive levels.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for MLLMs treat spatial capabilities as fragmented and isolated tasks, leading to a shallow understanding of spatial intelligence.
- A lack of a unifying, hierarchical framework prevents a comprehensive understanding of how spatial abilities emerge, interact, and transfer within MLLMs.
- Current evaluation methods do not effectively reveal the underlying structure and cross-level dependencies of spatial intelligence in MLLMs, making targeted development challenging.

## Method

- Developed ==SpatialTree==, a ==four-level hierarchical taxonomy== (Perception, Mental Mapping, Mental Simulation, Agentic Competence) inspired by cognitive science to structure MLLM spatial abilities.
- Constructed SpatialTree-bench, a capability-centric benchmark with ==27 fine-grained sub-abilities==, by reorganizing existing data, leveraging expert perception models for new data generation, and curating novel datasets for agentic competence.
- Implemented targeted training interventions including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on foundational skills and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a hierarchy-aware =="auto-think" reward mechanism== to optimize for different levels of spatial complexity.

## Results

- Targeted SFT on single L1 abilities (e.g., Geometry Distance) led to positive cross-level transfer to higher-level skills (e.g., 'Goal Execution' improved by **+3.4**), while multi-ability SFT provided synergistic gains (**+1.1** overall) and mitigated negative intra-level transfer.
- The hierarchy-aware "auto-think" RL strategy achieved a consistent performance gain of **+3.6** across all SpatialTree levels compared to the SFT baseline, outperforming uniform RL approaches by adapting to the cognitive demands of each spatial task.
- MLLMs augmented with explicit reasoning mechanisms generally performed better, with Gemini 3 Flash achieving the highest overall score of **57.8** on SpatialTree-Bench, followed by Gemini 3 Pro (**56.5**) and Qwen3VL-235B (**40.0**) among open-source models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial abilities in MLLMs exhibit a clear hierarchical structure: lower-level perception skills are largely orthogonal, while higher levels (Mental Mapping, Mental Simulation, Agentic Competence) show strong interdependencies.
> - Targeted SFT on foundational L1 abilities can induce non-trivial positive cross-level transfer to higher-level spatial competence, with training on multiple L1 abilities synergistically boosting overall performance.
> - Effectively scaling spatial intelligence in MLLMs requires differentiated learning strategies, balancing "thinking" (deliberative reasoning) for complex tasks and "perceiving" (intuitive processing) for basic perception, as demonstrated by the "auto-think" RL strategy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "SpatialTree" addresses a critical gap in the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly concerning their spatial intelligence. MLLMs, exemplified by models like GPT-4V, LLaVA, and Gemini, have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding and reasoning about visual information alongside language. However, the systematic evaluation and development of their *spatial abilities* remain a significant challenge.

**Current Landscape and Identified Gap:**
Prior research on spatial abilities in MLLMs has largely followed a "task-centric trajectory." This means evaluations have focused on narrow, specific tasks such as relative object positioning, size estimation, 3D grounding, or spatiotemporal reasoning in multi-view and video settings. While these efforts have pushed the boundaries of MLLM capabilities, the authors argue that "these task-centric benchmarks remain fragmented and often treat spatial capabilities as isolated or overlapping skills." This fragmentation hinders a comprehensive understanding of how spatial intelligence *emerges*, *interacts*, and *transfers* within MLLMs. Existing benchmarks, like BLINK, SpatialEval, VSI-Bench, and Omnispatial, while valuable, tend to be disparate and lack a unifying framework.

**Inspiration from Cognitive Science:**
The authors draw direct inspiration from cognitive science, a field with a long history of studying spatial ability. Theories from Piaget, Tolman, and Kuipers highlight the *hierarchical* and *developmental* nature of spatial cognition in humans—progressing from basic perception to complex reasoning and interaction. This insight motivates the paper's central paradigm shift: moving "from fragmented tasks to a capability-centric framework."

**Contribution to the Landscape:**
"SpatialTree" positions itself as the first effort to bridge this gap by introducing a cognitive-science-inspired hierarchical taxonomy of spatial abilities for MLLMs. This framework, organized into four progressive levels (Perception, Mental Mapping, Mental Simulation, and Agentic Competence), provides a structured lens through which to analyze and develop MLLM spatial intelligence. By constructing the "first capability-centric hierarchical benchmark" and conducting systematic evaluations and targeted training interventions, the paper aims to:
1. **Unify Evaluation:** Provide a consolidated framework for evaluating a broad spectrum of spatial skills, moving beyond isolated tasks.
2. **Reveal Structure and Dependencies:** Uncover the inherent hierarchical structure and cross-level dependencies of spatial abilities in MLLMs, mirroring cognitive development.
3. **Guide Development:** Inform future MLLM design and training strategies by identifying how foundational skills transfer and compose to support higher-level intelligence.

In essence, "SpatialTree" represents a foundational contribution that seeks to bring order and systematicity to the study of spatial intelligence in MLLMs, offering a deeper, more principled understanding than purely task-driven approaches. It aims to elevate the discussion from "can models perform task X?" to "what underlying cognitive capabilities do models possess, and how do they interact?"

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "SpatialTree" research is to systematically understand, evaluate, and ultimately scale spatial abilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by establishing a comprehensive, cognitive-science-inspired framework.

The primary motivations driving this research stem from several critical observations and identified limitations in the current state of MLLM development:

1. **Fragmented Understanding of Spatial Intelligence:** While MLLMs are increasingly capable of visual understanding and basic spatial reasoning, their spatial abilities are often studied in a narrow, task-specific manner. Existing benchmarks focus on isolated tasks, leading to a fragmented understanding of what constitutes "spatial intelligence" in these models. There is a lack of a unified view on how different spatial skills relate to each other.
2. **Lack of Hierarchical Perspective:** Cognitive science strongly suggests that human spatial ability develops progressively, from basic perception to complex reasoning and interaction. This inherent hierarchy, however, remains poorly understood and systematically unaddressed in the context of MLLMs. The authors note that the proliferation of spatial tasks makes it difficult to uncover inherent structure and cross-level dependencies.
3. **Need for Capability-Centric Evaluation:** Current "task-centric" evaluations do not effectively reveal *how* spatial abilities emerge, *interact*, and *transfer* within MLLMs. This makes it challenging to diagnose specific strengths and weaknesses, and to design effective strategies for enhancing overall spatial intelligence. The authors explicitly pose the question: "Can we move beyond disjointed, task-centric evaluations to uncover a compact set of atomic capabilities that reveal how spatial abilities emerge, interact, and transfer?"
4. **Inspiration from Cognitive Development:** The core motivation is to draw direct inspiration from the cognitive science insight that "intelligence is a dynamic structure built through successive stages" (Piaget). This provides a theoretical underpinning for developing a hierarchical framework for MLLMs.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:

- **Objective 1: Develop a Cognitive-Science-Inspired Taxonomy:** To introduce "SpatialTree," a novel, hierarchical taxonomy that organizes spatial abilities into four distinct, progressively complex levels: L1 Perception, L2 Mental Mapping, L3 Mental Simulation, and L4 Agentic Competence. This provides a structured framework for conceptualizing spatial intelligence in MLLMs.
- **Objective 2: Construct a Capability-Centric Benchmark:** To build the "first capability-centric hierarchical benchmark" (SpatialTree-bench) that rigorously evaluates mainstream MLLMs across 27 fine-grained sub-abilities derived from the SpatialTree taxonomy. This involves reorganizing existing data, leveraging expert models, and curating new data for underexplored areas, especially agentic competence.
- **Objective 3: Analyze Hierarchical Dependencies and Transfer Dynamics:** To thoroughly evaluate MLLMs on SpatialTree-bench to uncover the structural relationships among spatial skills, including how foundational abilities (L1) relate to higher-level reasoning and agentic competence (L2-L4). This involves using methods like Pearson correlation and targeted training interventions.
- **Objective 4: Identify Effective Scaling Strategies:** To explore and validate methods for improving spatial intelligence across the hierarchy. Specifically, to investigate the transfer dynamics of spatial skills through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and to address the trade-off between "thinking" (deliberation) and "perceiving" (intuition) in Reinforcement Learning (RL), proposing differentiated inference strategies.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a "proof-of-concept framework" for understanding and systematically scaling spatial abilities, paving the way for MLLMs with more robust and human-like spatial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "SpatialTree" is comprehensive, spanning taxonomy design, benchmark construction, and empirical analysis through targeted training interventions. The core approach is to shift from a fragmented, task-centric view to a unified, capability-centric perspective of spatial intelligence in MLLMs.

#### 3.1. The SpatialTree Taxonomy Design

Inspired by cognitive science, the authors propose a four-level hierarchy of spatial abilities:
- **L1 Perception:** Focuses on innate sensing of geometric and physical attributes (Distance, Size, Shape, Egocentric Motion, Allocentric Motion, Gravity, Object Orientation, Topology, Correspondence, Detection, Grounding). This level represents direct visual processing without deep linguistic or symbolic reasoning.
- **L2 Mental Mapping:** Bridges perception with language, involving grounding spatial concepts in linguistic semantics and forming language-structured spatial memory (Spatial Captioning, Semantic Relations, Motion Interpretation, Perspective Taking, Affordance Understanding, Cognitive Map, Memory Retrieval).
- **L3 Mental Simulation:** Supports internal reasoning and planning about space (Causal Reasoning about Geometry, Motion Prediction, Semantic-Spatial Relations, High-Level Planning, Route Planning). This level aligns with Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
- **L4 Agentic Competence:** Represents the culmination of spatial intelligence, executing actions in dynamic environments (Goal Execution, Open Exploration). This integrates perception, language, and reasoning for real-world interaction.

#### 3.2. SpatialTree-Bench Construction

To instantiate this taxonomy, the authors built a hierarchical benchmark.
- **Data Curation:**
 - **Reorganization of Existing Datasets:** Data from numerous prior works (e.g.,) were reorganized and mapped to the SpatialTree framework, addressing scattered capability coverage. Complex reasoning tasks were converted to hybrid multi-option + LLM-as-a-Judge formats.
 - **Spatial Engine for New Data Generation:** To fill gaps and enrich annotations, a "Spatial Engine" was constructed. This engine integrates multiple expert perception models (e.g., DepthAnything3, SpatialTracker, GeoCalib, OrientAnything) to extract intermediate representations (depth, correspondences, tracking, gravity direction). These representations were then processed using QA templates and rephrased by LLMs to create diverse L1-L3 QA samples, including new sub-capabilities like orientation and affordance.
 - **L4 Agentic Competence Curation (SpatialPlus):** L4 required extensive new data curation as agentic interactions were largely absent. Data covering manipulation and navigation across diverse embodiments (human hands, robotic grippers, game characters) were collected from internet sources (e.g., in-game footage, egocentric manipulation videos, robotics data).
 - An **Action Mapping Strategy** was designed to discretize low-level, embodiment-specific actions into unified high-level motion primitives (e.g., 6-DoF camera movements for navigation, gripper open/close for manipulation), making them executable by MLLMs.
 - Human annotators reformatted human-object interaction sequences into multi-step multiple-choice tasks.
- **Evaluation Metrics Designs:** Diverse metrics were tailored to each task type:
 - **Multiple-Choice Questions (70.7%):** For basic spatial relations, object properties, causal dynamics.
 - **Numeric Accuracy:** For continuous quantities like distances, angles, 3D coordinates (e.g., Mean Squared Error, angular difference).
 - **LLM-as-a-Judge:** For open-ended or complex reasoning tasks (e.g., trajectory description, action sequence explanation), assessing correctness and partial correctness.
 - **Agentic Evaluation:** For L4 tasks, measured success rates, positional error, and orientation error against ground-truth trajectories in interactive simulated environments.
 - **Hierarchical Aggregation:** A bottom-up, weighted summation methodology was employed to derive a single comprehensive score, with weights guided by cognitive psychology principles and empirical correlation analysis (e.g., L1 assigned largest weight).

#### 3.3. Experimental Approach

- **Benchmarked Models:** A broad range of 16 mainstream MLLMs were evaluated, categorized into "Thinking Models" (augmented with Chain-of-Thought), "Non-Thinking Models," and "Open-Source Models" to provide a holistic view.
- **Ability Dependencies Analysis:** Pearson correlation coefficients were computed from benchmark scores to reveal inter-capability dependencies, showing stronger correlations for higher-level skills.
- **Targeted Training Interventions:**
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Selected L1 abilities (Geometry Distance, Geometry Size, Relative Correlation) that showed strong correlations with higher-level performance. Models were fine-tuned on dedicated datasets (approx. 0.25M QA samples per ability) blended with general visual-instruction data. The impact on both intra-level and cross-level transfer was analyzed.
 - **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) was used to scale spatial abilities. A critical innovation was the **Hierarchy-Aware Reward Mechanism** (auto-think strategy):
 - For **Intuitive Perception (L1):** Rewards for "thinking processes" were removed, and a length penalty was introduced to discourage over-reasoning on direct visual signals.
 - For **Complex Reasoning (L3-L4):** Rewards for explicit reasoning steps were retained and amplified to encourage deeper computation.
 - **Strict RL Evaluation:** Data decontamination ensured no overlap between RL training and SpatialTree-Bench testing data. The training objective (MCQ selection) was distinct from evaluation metrics (continuous/semantic), ensuring generalization.
- **Ability Transfer via Prompting:** Explicit prompts providing low-level visual information (e.g., correspondence, depth, size) were used to investigate their direct influence on high-level tasks like navigation.

This multi-faceted methodology allowed the researchers to systematically dissect, evaluate, and enhance spatial intelligence in MLLMs from both a cognitive and engineering perspective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings regarding the structure, dependencies, and transfer dynamics of spatial abilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):

#### 4.1. Hierarchical Structure and Dependencies of Spatial Abilities

- **Clear Structural Revelation:** Initial evaluations on SpatialTree-Bench revealed a distinct hierarchical structure. Lower-level abilities (L1 Perception), such as Geometry and Motion, were found to be largely orthogonal, exhibiting weak correlations among themselves. This suggests they function as relatively independent, foundational components.
- **Increasing Interdependency at Higher Levels:** In stark contrast, higher-level capabilities (L2 Mental Mapping, L3 Mental Simulation, and L4 Agentic Competence) showed strong positive correlations. This indicates that complex tasks at these levels are highly interdependent and likely rely on overlapping, underlying sub-skills, validating the cognitive-science-inspired hierarchical taxonomy. For example, the correlation matrix in Fig. 5 (A) showed strong inter-correlations for L3 and L4 tasks.

#### 4.2. Cross-Ability Transfer via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

- **Finding 1: Negative Intra-Level Transfer, Strong Cross-Level Transfer:** Targeted SFT on single L1 abilities (e.g., Geometry Distance, Geometry Size, Relative Correlation) had a surprising dynamic. While it often led to negligible gains or even *negative transfer* within the same L1 level (e.g., improving Geometry but hurting Motion or Localization, as seen in Table 2), it induced *non-trivial and positive cross-level transfer* to higher-level abilities. For instance, SFT on distance improved 'Understanding' (+2.0) and 'Goal Execution' (+3.4), demonstrating that foundational perception acts as a crucial stepping stone for complex tasks.
- **Finding 2: Multi-Ability Synergy:** Training a model jointly on multiple fundamental L1 abilities (Distance, Size, Correspondence) resulted in synergistic gains that significantly surpassed the effects of individual SFT interventions. This blended SFT not only yielded an overall performance gain (+1.1) but also mitigated the performance drops observed in other L1 abilities when trained individually. For example, L1.Motion, which dropped under single-ability SFT, showed a positive improvement (+0.7) with compositional training. This suggests that a holistic approach to foundational training is more beneficial than isolated skill development.

#### 4.3. Reasoning vs. Perception Trade-off in Reinforcement Learning (RL)

- **Limitations of Naive RL:** Initial experiments with standard Reinforcement Learning (using GRPO) showed that a uniform RL strategy was insufficient. Naive RL, especially when applied to single low-level abilities or with uniform data mixing, tended to overfit to specific reward signals, resulting in siloed improvements with poor generalization and negligible or detrimental transfer to higher-level capabilities.
- **Dichotomy of "Thinking" and "Perceiving":** A critical finding was the trade-off between "thinking" (deliberative reasoning) and "perceiving" (intuitive, direct perception). While extensive "thinking" (encouraged by traditional CoT-based RL) was indispensable for complex reasoning and planning tasks (L3-L4), it proved *detrimental* to intuitive perception tasks (L1), such as numerical estimation, where over-thinking degraded precision.
- **Effectiveness of Auto-Think Strategy:** The proposed **hierarchy-aware "auto-think" strategy** successfully addressed this dichotomy. By suppressing rewards for explicit "thinking processes" and introducing length penalties for intuitive perception tasks (L1), and conversely, amplifying reasoning rewards for complex tasks (L3-L4), RL consistently improved performance across *all* levels of the SpatialTree. This differentiated approach enabled significant gains, as shown in Table 3 (e.g., Full RL@auto-think yielded an overall +3.6 gain compared to the SFT Baseline, outperforming Full RL@think). This validates that different levels of spatial intelligence require distinct cognitive modes during training.

#### 4.4. Overall MLLM Performance

- **Leading Models:** Gemini 3 Flash achieved the highest overall score (57.8) on SpatialTree-Bench, closely followed by Gemini 3 Pro (56.5). Among open-source models, Qwen3VL-235B led with a score of 40.0.
- **Impact of "Thinking" Mechanisms:** Models augmented with explicit reasoning or Chain-of-Thought generation mechanisms ("Thinking Models") generally performed better than "Non-Thinking" and "Open-Source" counterparts, especially on higher-level spatial tasks, underscoring the importance of reasoning capabilities for complex spatial intelligence.

These findings provide crucial insights into the internal workings of spatial intelligence in MLLMs and offer a roadmap for their systematic development and scaling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "SpatialTree" research makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the development and understanding of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and the broader field of artificial intelligence.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Spatial Intelligence Evaluation

- **From Task-Centric to Capability-Centric:** The most profound impact is the introduction of the SpatialTree framework itself. By shifting from fragmented, task-specific benchmarks to a unified, cognitive-science-inspired, capability-centric hierarchy, the paper establishes a new paradigm for evaluating and understanding spatial intelligence in MLLMs. This enables researchers to ask *what underlying abilities* models possess rather than just *what tasks* they can perform.
- **Granular Diagnosis and Understanding:** The 27-sub-ability hierarchical benchmark allows for a much more granular diagnosis of MLLM strengths and weaknesses across the spectrum of spatial cognition. This is invaluable for identifying specific areas requiring improvement and for tracking the developmental trajectory of spatial intelligence in future models.

#### 5.2. Deep Insights into MLLM Learning and Architecture

- **Unveiling Hierarchical Dependencies:** The finding that L1 perception skills are largely orthogonal but become increasingly interdependent at higher levels (L2-L4) provides fundamental insights into how spatial abilities might be organized within MLLM architectures. This validates the cognitive science principle of progressive development within an AI context.
- **Optimizing Training Strategies:**
 - **Cross-Level Transfer as a Development Path:** The demonstration of strong cross-level transfer from foundational L1 abilities to higher-level competence (e.g., robot manipulation improving after distance SFT) offers a clear roadmap for MLLM development. It suggests that targeted, high-quality training on basic perceptual skills can unlock advanced capabilities, potentially leading to more efficient and effective pre-training or fine-tuning strategies.
 - **Synergistic Multi-Ability Training:** The discovery of synergistic gains when multiple foundational abilities are trained jointly highlights the importance of holistic learning, rather than isolated skill acquisition. This can guide the design of curriculum learning or multi-task learning approaches for spatial AI.
 - **Differentiated Reasoning (Auto-Think Strategy):** The identification of the "thinking" vs. "perceiving" dichotomy in RL and the success of the "auto-think" strategy is a critical contribution. It implies that a "one-size-fits-all" training approach is suboptimal for complex cognitive abilities. Future MLLMs and embodied agents can be designed to dynamically adjust their inference and learning modes, encouraging deliberation for complex tasks while promoting intuitive, "fast" processing for basic perception. This could lead to more robust, efficient, and human-like decision-making.

#### 5.3. Advancing General Spatial Intelligence and Embodied AI

- **Towards Human-like Cognition:** By systematically mapping and evaluating spatial abilities inspired by human cognitive development, SpatialTree paves the way for MLLMs to develop more robust and generalizable spatial intelligence, akin to human capabilities. This is a crucial step toward building truly intelligent agents that can understand and interact with the 3D world.
- **Empowering Embodied Agents:** The focus on L4 Agentic Competence, including navigation and manipulation, directly contributes to the field of embodied AI. By enabling MLLMs to better plan and execute actions in dynamic environments, SpatialTree's framework and findings will support the creation of more capable robots, virtual agents, and interactive systems.
- **Bridging Cognitive Science and AI:** This work strengthens the interdisciplinary link between cognitive science and AI. It demonstrates how theories of human cognition can inform the design and evaluation of artificial intelligence, offering a valuable feedback loop where AI experiments can, in turn, provide computational insights into cognitive theories.

In summary, "SpatialTree" is not merely another benchmark; it is a foundational framework that provides a deeper scientific understanding of spatial intelligence in MLLMs. Its insights into ability hierarchies, transfer dynamics, and differentiated learning strategies promise to significantly impact the architectural design, training methodologies, and ultimate capabilities of future AI systems.
