# KITE: Keyframe-Indexed Tokenized Evidence for VLM-Based Robot Failure Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> KITE is a training-free, keyframe-indexed front-end that converts long robot-execution videos into compact evidence bundles: motion-salient keyframes paired with schematic pseudo-BEV layouts, open-vocabulary detections, contact-transition tokens, and serialized robot-profile context, letting an off-the-shelf VLM detect, identify, localize, explain, and correct failures without fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Raw long execution videos bury subtle failure cues in dense visual detail, diluting temporal context needed for post-hoc diagnosis by VLMs.
- Prior failure-summarization and failure-specific VLM methods depend on task-specific training or additional memory structures rather than a general front-end.
- No simple training-free front-end exists that converts a long execution into compact spatiotemporal evidence immediately legible to an off-the-shelf generalist VLM.

## Method

- ==Motion-based keyframe selection== uses dense optical-flow magnitude peaks with temporal non-maximum suppression, backfilled with uniform frames under a fixed budget M.
- ==Pseudo-BEV schematic== renders non-metric top-down layout per keyframe with confidence-scaled circles, consistent instance IDs, axes, and timestamps as a layout prior.
- **KITE context** serializes robot profile, plan, keyframe tags, GAIN/LOSS/STABLE contact-transition tokens, and global scene-graph relations into one prompt for all QA tasks.

## Results

- On RoboFAC simulation, **KITE** with Qwen2.5-VL-7B gains **+36** points failure detection, **+18** failure identification, **+33** failure localization over vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- Real-world MCQ gains are smaller but positive (**+1** detection, **+5** identification, **+2** localization); QLoRA fine-tune pushes KITE near or beyond RoboFAC-tuned baseline.
- Ablations show removing pseudo-BEV drops failure explanation ROUGE-L by **0.05**, and uniform keyframe sampling degrades success rate more than any other component swap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Externalizing when/where/what as explicit tokens (keyframes, schematic layout, contact transitions) can substitute for costly fine-tuning on failure-reasoning tasks.
> - Schematic non-metric layout cues can be more useful to VLM reasoning than photorealistic reconstruction, since legibility matters more than geometric fidelity.
> - Motion-saliency-driven keyframe selection matters more than any single perception module; poor temporal sampling degrades downstream reasoning most.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly integrating large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) to enhance capabilities such as planning, control, and human-robot interaction. These foundation models offer open-vocabulary perception, natural language understanding, and commonsense reasoning, which are valuable for complex robotic tasks. However, a significant challenge arises when these models are tasked with analyzing robot failures from long, raw execution videos. Such videos often contain subtle, temporally distributed, and visually dense failure cues that are difficult for current VLMs to reliably process and interpret for diagnostic purposes.

Prior research has addressed aspects of this problem through various approaches. Some works have focused on summarizing robot experiences for LLMs to aid in diagnosis and recovery. Others have involved training failure-specific VLMs or fine-tuning existing models for robotic failure reasoning. While these contributions are valuable, many depend on extensive task-specific training, which can limit their generalizability and increase development costs. Additionally, some methods still leave open the "representation problem"—how to convert a raw video into a compact form that preserves the critical spatiotemporal evidence needed for effective post-hoc diagnosis without requiring significant re-training of the VLM itself. The challenge is to provide a unified, interpretable interface that makes diagnostic facts immediately accessible to an off-the-shelf generalist VLM.

KITE (Keyframe-Indexed Tokenized Evidence) addresses this specific gap. It proposes a training-free, model-agnostic front-end designed to preprocess raw robot execution videos into a structured, compact, and interpretable format consumable by general-purpose VLMs. This positions KITE as a method that leverages the existing reasoning capabilities of foundation models by optimizing their input representation, rather than requiring extensive re-training. It draws inspiration from work on structured scene abstractions, such as 3D scene graphs, bird's-eye-view (BEV) interfaces, and diagrammatic abstractions, to inject useful inductive biases into multimodal reasoning without full retraining. By focusing on the representation interface, KITE distinguishes itself from approaches that primarily summarize memories or rely on heavy fine-tuning, aiming for broader applicability and interpretability in robot failure analysis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enhance the capability of general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in performing comprehensive robot failure analysis, encompassing detection, identification, localization, explanation, and correction. This is pursued within the context of analyzing long robot execution videos.

The primary motivation stems from the inherent difficulty VLMs face when attempting to diagnose subtle, time-sensitive, and visually complex robot failures directly from raw video data. Specifically, the authors identify several challenges that VLMs encounter:

- **Subtle and Distributed Failure Cues:** Robot failures in real-world settings can be nuanced, involving slight misalignments, mistimed actions, or contact issues. These cues are often distributed across time and space within a long video, making them easily overlooked or misinterpreted by VLMs that process raw visual streams.
- **Temporal Context Dilution:** Long videos can dilute temporal context, making it hard for VLMs to identify the precise "when" (the moment of deviation) of a failure event. Relevant evidence might be buried amidst extensive, less informative footage.
- **Lack of Legible Evidence Representation:** Raw video alone does not present the necessary diagnostic evidence (e.g., object layout, relative poses, contact states, task intent) in a form that is immediately legible or structured for VLM reasoning. This necessitates complex inference from the VLM, which may not be reliable.
- **Dependency on Task-Specific Training:** Many existing VLM-based approaches for failure analysis require significant task-specific fine-tuning or training. This limits their generalizability to novel tasks or robot platforms and increases the development overhead for new applications. The authors aim to minimize this dependency by focusing on a training-free front-end.
- **Need for Multi-modal Integration:** Explaining failures often requires combining information about "where" (spatial layout, contact), "when" (temporal deviation), and "what/how" (task intent, robot capabilities). A unified representation that integrates these multimodal cues is crucial for holistic analysis.
- **Desire for Interpretability:** For effective human-robot interaction and debugging, the failure analysis should not only identify problems but also provide understandable explanations and suggest actionable corrections. This requires an interpretable intermediate representation that allows both the VLM and human operators to trace the reasoning.

To address these motivations, KITE aims to develop a compact, interpretable front-end that explicitly externalizes the critical evidence a VLM needs. This involves distilling long videos into motion-salient keyframes, pairing them with schematic bird’s-eye-view (BEV) representations, integrating robot-profile information and scene context, and serializing all these cues into a unified, structured prompt format. The central objective is to enable an off-the-shelf VLM to perform robust and detailed failure analysis without requiring it to learn to extract these complex cues from raw video itself.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KITE operates as a training-free, model-agnostic front-end that processes a robot execution video into a compact bundle of multimodal evidence. The methodology is designed to externalize crucial information that a Vision-Language Model (VLM) would otherwise struggle to infer from raw video. The pipeline comprises several sequential steps, culminating in a structured prompt for the VLM.

**A. Keyframe Selection:**
The process begins by reducing a long video into a small set of up to $M=8$ motion-salient keyframes. To achieve this, KITE calculates dense optical flow between consecutive video frames. Each frame is then scored based on its average optical flow magnitude, providing a scene-wide motion saliency signal. Keyframes are selected as local peaks in this score using temporal non-maximum suppression. If fewer than $M$ salient frames are identified, the remaining slots are filled with uniformly spaced frames to ensure broad temporal coverage. This motion-based approach prioritizes frames where significant action or change occurs, which are often indicative of critical task events or failures.

**B. Per-Keyframe Perception:**
For each selected keyframe, several perception modules are employed:
- **Open-Vocabulary Detection (OVD):** GroundingDINO is used to detect objects of interest and robot parts (arms, grippers). The detected bounding boxes, class labels, and confidence scores are collected. Detections are temporally linked across keyframes to establish short instance tracks, and timestamps are overlaid onto the RGB keyframes.
- **Single-View Depth Estimation:** Depth-Anything-V2-Large estimates relative depth for each keyframe. This depth information is associated with each object detection and is primarily used as a coarse ordering cue rather than for metric geometry, with extreme outliers suppressed.
- **Contact-Transition Proxy:** Between consecutive keyframes where a robot gripper and a candidate object are detected, a coarse contact-transition token is generated. This token, $\gamma_{k \to k+1} \in \{\text{GAIN, LOSS, STABLE}\}$, summarizes changes in interaction. It is determined by analyzing trends in bounding-box Intersection over Union (IoU) and nearest-center distance between the gripper and the object, using defined thresholds ($\tau_{IoU} = 0.1$, $\tau_d = 0.15$). This proxy provides an informative interaction signal without requiring precise force sensing.

**C. 3D Scene Graph:**
A lightweight 3D scene graph is constructed for each keyframe. Nodes in this graph correspond to the detected objects and robot parts. Their 3D centroids are approximated using the estimated relative depth and camera geometry. Pairwise spatial relations, such as {LEFT_OF, ABOVE, IN_FRONT_OF}, are encoded based on the sign and magnitude of these centroid offsets with small tolerance thresholds. Local scene graphs are then aggregated into a global graph by maintaining instance tracks across all keyframes. This coarse 3D representation helps disambiguate spatial ordering that might be ambiguous in 2D images.

**D. Robot Description:**
A concise textual profile of the robot performing the task is included. This profile details the robot's morphology (e.g., number of arms, type of grippers, end-effectors), sensors, workspace characteristics, and salient embodiment constraints. This information allows the VLM to contextualize its explanations and proposed corrections based on the specific robot platform.

**E. Pseudo-BEV Schematic (Layout Prior):**
For each keyframe, a schematic, non-metric top-down pseudo-Bird’s-Eye-View (pseudo-BEV) is rendered. This visualization externalizes relative object layout in an interpretable format. Key features of the pseudo-BEV include:
- Fixed axes (X-right, Z-forward) with arrows.
- Circles representing tracked objects, with radii proportional to their detection confidence (clipped to pixels).
- Object class labels and instance IDs, consistent with those in the RGB keyframe overlays.
- Overlaid timestamp and keyframe index.
The pseudo-BEV is designed to be a legible spatial cue for the VLM, emphasizing relationships over precise metric geometry.

**F. KITE: Keyframe-Indexed Tokenized Evidence:**
All the extracted information is serialized into a compact, structured context string, denoted as $T$. This string acts as a unified front-end prefix for VLM prompts across all query types. The structure of $T$ includes:
- `[ROBOT]` section: Contains the robot's morphological description.
- `[PLAN]` section: Provides high-level plan steps, if available.
- `[KF i_k @ t_k]` tags: Indicate timestamped keyframes.
- `[CONTACT k -> k+1]` tokens: Summarize contact transitions (GAIN, LOSS, STABLE).
- `[GLOBAL_SCENE]` section: Lists tracked objects and their relations, with consistent IDs across RGB and pseudo-BEV.

**G. Prompting and Failure Localization:**
When querying the VLM, an input bundle is provided, consisting of the KITE context string $T$ and a set of images. This image set includes both the RGB keyframes with object detection overlays and their corresponding pseudo-BEVs. The textual prompt includes a brief instruction explaining the schematic nature of the pseudo-BEV. For tasks like failure localization, the VLM is instructed to output a strict JSON format listing candidate failure frames by their index and a confidence score. This structured output facilitates automated parsing and subsequent analysis.

**H. Narrative Summary:**
For a comprehensive response, KITE prompts the VLM to generate a concise causal narrative. This narrative explicitly references keyframe IDs and timestamps from the provided evidence and suggests both a high-level and a low-level correction for the observed failure. The entire perception pipeline's cost scales linearly with the number of keyframes ($M$), making it efficient irrespective of the original video length.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates KITE's effectiveness using the RoboFAC benchmark, which includes both simulated and real-world robot manipulation sequences, as well as qualitative evaluations on real dual-arm robots in a laboratory setting (DART and ALOHA-2). The primary VLM backbone used for quantitative evaluation is Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with comparisons against vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Gemini-2.0, GPT-4o, and the RoboFAC-tuned baseline.

**Quantitative Results on RoboFAC Benchmark:**
The results demonstrate that KITE, in a training-free setting, significantly enhances the performance of an off-the-shelf VLM for robot failure analysis tasks.

- **Training-free KITE vs. Vanilla VLM:**
 - **Simulation Tasks (MCQ):** KITE with Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieved substantial improvements over vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B. For Failure Detection (FD), KITE showed a 36 percentage point gain (0.88 vs. 0.52). For Failure Identification (FI), there was an 18 percentage point gain (0.44 vs. 0.26). For Failure Locating (FL), KITE improved by 33 percentage points (0.55 vs. 0.22).
 - **Real-world Tasks (MCQ):** Gains were smaller but consistently positive. FD improved by 1 point (0.84 vs. 0.83), FI by 5 points (0.43 vs. 0.38), and FL by 2 points (0.74 vs. 0.72).
 - **Free-Language Tasks (ROUGE-L and Sentence-BERT Similarity):** KITE consistently improved ROUGE-L scores across all relevant tasks (Task Identification - TI, Failure Explanation - FE, High-Level Correction - HL, Low-Level Correction - LL) in both simulation and real-world settings. For Sentence-BERT cosine similarity, KITE either improved upon or closely matched the vanilla VLM’s performance across most tasks.

- **KITE with Lightweight Fine-tuning (QLoRA):**
 - Applying a lightweight QLoRA fine-tune to KITE + Qwen2.5-VL-7B further improved performance across most metrics. This adapted model frequently matched or, in some cases, slightly surpassed the performance of the heavily tuned RoboFAC-7B baseline, particularly in simulation FD (0.93 vs. 0.91) and FI (0.69 vs. 0.63). This suggests that KITE’s structured evidence representation is amenable to efficient adaptation.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies conducted on real-world tasks highlighted the contribution of specific KITE components:

- **Impact of Pseudo-BEV:** Removing the pseudo-BEV schematics resulted in a reduction in performance. This effect was most pronounced in Failure Explanation (FE), where ROUGE-L dropped by 0.05 points, and also negatively impacted FD, FI, and FL. This indicates the pseudo-BEV’s role in providing explicit, legible layout cues is significant for reasoning.
- **Impact of Keyframe Selection:** Replacing the motion-based keyframe selection with uniform sampling caused a more substantial degradation in overall performance. This was particularly evident for tasks requiring precise temporal and spatial localization of failures. This outcome supports the effectiveness of motion saliency for identifying critical moments.

**Qualitative Analyses:**
Qualitative results from both the RoboFAC simulation and real-world lab robots (DART and ALOHA-2) visually demonstrated KITE's ability to create a legible chain of evidence.
- In simulation, the generated explanations explicitly referenced elements within the KITE structured context, pseudo-BEV, and keyframes.
- On real dual-arm robots, KITE facilitated failure localization and generated explanations that specifically tied the failure to the robot's embodiment and observed sequence, leveraging the robot-profile information in the structured context. For instance, in an ALOHA-2 scenario where an object was dropped during a dual-arm handover, the explanation incorporated robot-specific details to analyze the failure.

In summary, KITE substantially improves the diagnostic capabilities of general-purpose VLMs for robot failure analysis without requiring task-specific training of the front-end. Its compact, interpretable, and multimodal evidence representation enables VLMs to perform better across various failure analysis tasks, from detection to correction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of KITE holds several points of significance and presents various potential impacts within the fields of robotics, artificial intelligence, and human-robot interaction.

**1. Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Reliability:**
By providing an effective, training-free mechanism for VLMs to analyze robot failures, KITE contributes directly to improving robot autonomy and reliability. The ability to automatically detect, identify, localize, explain, and suggest corrections for failures enables robots to operate more robustly in dynamic and unstructured environments, reducing downtime and human intervention.

**2. Bridging the Gap for General-Purpose VLMs:**
KITE addresses a critical bottleneck in the application of powerful, off-the-shelf VLMs to complex robotic tasks. Instead of requiring extensive re-training or fine-tuning of foundation models for specific failure analysis scenarios, KITE's front-end makes the necessary spatiotemporal evidence immediately legible. This expands the practical utility of generalist VLMs in robotics, making them more adaptable to new tasks and platforms without prohibitive development costs.

**3. Improved Interpretability and Explainable AI in Robotics:**
The structured, tokenized evidence, including keyframe visuals, schematic pseudo-BEVs, and serialized textual context, enhances the interpretability of VLM reasoning. This legibility is crucial for explainable AI (XAI) in robotics. Human operators can review the exact evidence presented to the VLM and understand the basis of its diagnosis and proposed corrections, fostering greater trust and facilitating more efficient debugging and recovery processes. The ability to generate causal narratives referencing specific keyframes and timestamps is a step towards more transparent robot intelligence.

**4. Practical Applicability and Generalizability:**
The training-free nature of the KITE front-end promotes broad applicability. It can be readily integrated with various robot platforms and manipulation tasks without requiring a new training dataset for each scenario. The qualitative demonstrations on real dual-arm robots (DART and ALOHA-2), which extend beyond the single-arm tasks of the RoboFAC benchmark, suggest that the representation generalizes to diverse real-world failure modes. This reduces the barrier to entry for deploying VLM-based failure analysis in practical robotic systems.

**5. Foundation for Future Robotic Systems:**
KITE’s modular design lays a foundation for future advancements. Researchers can build upon this framework by incorporating stronger perception modules, richer relational vocabularies for scene graphs, multi-view layout cues, or more sophisticated, adaptive keyframe selection policies. The work also highlights the importance of human-centered evaluation for explanation quality, which is a significant avenue for future research.

**6. Cost-Efficiency in Development and Deployment:**
By avoiding heavy task-specific VLM fine-tuning, KITE offers a more cost-efficient approach to developing and deploying failure analysis solutions. This is particularly relevant for small-to-medium enterprises and research labs with limited computational resources, enabling them to leverage state-of-the-art foundation models for complex robotic problems.

In conclusion, KITE significantly advances the field of robot failure analysis by providing an effective, interpretable, and adaptable solution that maximizes the capabilities of existing Vision-Language Models. Its impact is likely to be seen in more robust robotic deployments, improved human-robot collaboration, and accelerated development of intelligent autonomous systems.
