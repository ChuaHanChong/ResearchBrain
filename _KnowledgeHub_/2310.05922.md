# FLATTEN: optical FLow-guided ATTENtion for consistent text-to-video editing

> [!summary] Summary
> FLATTEN introduces an optical flow-guided attention mechanism into latent diffusion models, significantly enhancing visual consistency in text-to-video editing by ensuring precise temporal correspondence between features. This training-free method achieves superior qualitative and quantitative performance, making it a practical plug-and-play solution for existing T2V frameworks.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-video editing methods struggle to maintain visual consistency across frames, leading to flickering or unnatural appearance changes.
- Current spatio-temporal attention mechanisms in diffusion models often aggregate irrelevant temporal information, reducing precision in edits.
- Preserving the original motion and structure of a video while applying diverse textual edits remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- Integrates an ==optical flow==-guided ==attention mechanism== into the ==U-Net== of pre-trained ==latent diffusion== models, operating in a training-free manner.
- Restricts attention to only temporally corresponding patches identified by optical flow, rather than dense, unconstrained spatio-temporal attention.
- Utilizes **DDIM** inversion and feature injection to preserve source video structure and improve latent noise estimation.

## Results

- FLATTEN achieved state-of-the-art S_edit scores (57.01 on TGVE-D, 84.49 on TGVE-V), demonstrating superior balance of textual adherence and visual consistency.
- It recorded very low E_warp values (e.g., 4.92 on TGVE-D), indicating high visual consistency, and was rated highest in user studies across consistency, alignment, and motion preservation.
- The method functions as a plug-and-play module, improving other T2V editing frameworks like ControlVideo, where it reduced E_warp from 6.81 to 4.78.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly guiding attention with optical flow ensures that information exchange occurs only between temporally corresponding visual elements.
> - Restricting attention to patch trajectories across frames prevents the introduction of irrelevant temporal data, a common issue with dense spatio-temporal attention.
> - Enhancing consistency does not require re-training large video diffusion models but can be achieved through a clever, plug-and-play modification of existing attention modules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-video (T2V) editing has rapidly emerged as a critical extension of the highly successful text-to-image (T2I) generation and editing paradigms. With the proliferation of short-form video content on social media, the demand for efficient and flexible video editing tools has skyrocketed. T2V editing promises to revolutionize content creation by allowing users to modify video appearance based on natural language prompts, offering unparalleled flexibility compared to traditional manual editing.

However, T2V editing presents significantly greater challenges than T2I editing. The primary hurdles include:
1. **Visual Consistency:** Ensuring that edits are smooth and coherent across all frames, preventing flickering or abrupt changes in appearance, which is notoriously difficult when applying frame-by-frame T2I techniques.
2. **Motion Preservation:** Faithfully retaining the original motion and temporal dynamics of the source video, avoiding structural distortions or unnatural movements in the edited output.
3. **Computational Cost:** Training large-scale video generation models is computationally intensive and requires vast video datasets, making them often inaccessible or impractical for general use.

Recent advancements in T2I diffusion models have paved the way for T2V editing by adapting these powerful generative architectures. Most contemporary T2V editing methods attempt to "inflate" 2D spatial attention mechanisms within the diffusion model's U-Net architecture into spatio-temporal attention. This involves extending the attention mechanism to consider features across multiple frames, aiming to inject temporal context. Examples include methods like Tune-A-Video, Text2Video-Zero, ControlVideo, FateZero, and TokenFlow.

While effective to some degree, the paper argues that this "dense spatio-temporal attention" approach has a significant limitation: each patch in a given frame attends to *all* other patches across the entire video. This indiscriminate aggregation can introduce "irrelevant information," leading to inconsistencies and a lack of precise control over the edited video's temporal coherence. TokenFlow, for instance, tries to enforce consistency through linear combinations of diffusion features based on source correspondences but can still suffer from high-frequency flickering. ControlNet-based methods (e.g., Text2Video-Zero, ControlVideo) excel at preserving per-frame structure but may lack fine-grained control over visual consistency.

FLATTEN addresses this specific limitation by introducing **optical flow-guided attention**. Instead of dense, unconstrained spatio-temporal attention, FLATTEN explicitly uses optical flow to guide the attention mechanism, ensuring that patches attending to each other are indeed temporally corresponding, i.e., they lie on the same "flow path" across different frames. This is a novel integration of optical flow directly into the attention module of a diffusion model's U-Net for T2V editing, providing a more intelligent and targeted way to achieve visual consistency. Furthermore, its "training-free" nature and plug-and-play compatibility distinguish it as a highly practical and accessible solution within the evolving T2V landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly improve the visual consistency of edited videos generated through text-to-video editing models**, while faithfully preserving the original motion of the source video.

The motivation stems from the inherent challenges faced by existing diffusion-based T2V editing methods:
- **Inherent Inconsistency in Existing Spatio-Temporal Attention:** Current approaches that extend 2D spatial attention to spatio-temporal attention often involve aggregating features from all patches across all frames. This "dense" attention, while introducing some temporal context, can also incorporate irrelevant information for a given patch, leading to visual inconsistencies (e.g., flickering, appearance changes) in the edited video.
- **Lack of Precise Temporal Guidance:** Without an explicit mechanism to link corresponding visual elements across time, diffusion models struggle to maintain a stable appearance of objects or styles throughout a video sequence.
- **Computational and Accessibility Barriers:** Many state-of-the-art T2V generation models are computationally intensive, require vast training data, or are not publicly available, limiting broader research and application. Editing methods built upon existing T2I models aim to circumvent this, but still need robust solutions for video-specific challenges.

FLATTEN's core motivation is to overcome these limitations by introducing a novel, **optical flow-guided attention mechanism** that ensures patches on the same underlying motion path across frames attend to each other. This targeted information exchange is hypothesized to stabilize the generated content and enforce visual consistency. A secondary but crucial motivation is to offer this improvement in a **training-free** and **seamlessly integrable** manner, making it a practical enhancement for any diffusion-based T2V editing framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FLATTEN's methodology builds upon latent diffusion models (LDMs) and introduces a novel optical flow-guided attention mechanism within their U-Net architecture. The approach is training-free and designed for seamless integration.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The method leverages the principles of:
- **Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs):** These models operate in a compressed latent space using an auto-encoder, which makes them computationally more efficient than pixel-space diffusion models. They employ a U-Net to progressively denoise a noisy latent representation back to an image or video, conditioned by a textual prompt.
- **DDIM Inversion:** Deterministic Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIMs) allow for the inversion of an input image/video `z_0` into a corresponding latent noise `z_T`. This `z_T` can then be used as a starting point for controlled generation or editing, ensuring that the edited output is rooted in the original content. The paper notes that DDIM inversion can also benefit from FLATTEN.

#### 3.2. Overall Framework

The FLATTEN framework aims to edit a source video `V` based on an editing prompt `τ`, producing a visually consistent output. The core steps involve:

1. **U-Net Inflation:** The 2D U-Net of a pre-trained T2I diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion) is expanded along the temporal axis. This involves converting 2D convolutional kernels to 3D (1x3x3) and replacing 2D spatial attention with "dense spatio-temporal attention." In this dense attention, patches from all frames in the video are combined as queries (Q), keys (K), and values (V), allowing for a comprehensive, albeit potentially noisy, temporal context. Parameters are inherited from the original T2I model to avoid new training.

2. **FLATTEN Integration:** This is the central contribution. To address the inconsistency issue of dense spatio-temporal attention, FLATTEN is integrated into the extended U-Net blocks. Crucially, FLATTEN does not introduce new trainable parameters. Instead, it reuses the output of the dense spatio-temporal attention (`H`) as input. For a given patch (query), FLATTEN gathers its corresponding keys and values *only* from patches that lie on the same trajectory across different frames, as determined by optical flow. This ensures that attention is focused on temporally relevant features, stabilizing content appearance.

3. **DDIM Inversion with FLATTEN:** The source video is inverted into its latent noise representation `z_T` using the modified U-Net (which now includes FLATTEN). The paper explicitly states that using FLATTEN during inversion helps in estimating a more accurate latent noise by introducing temporal dependencies, which improves the fidelity of reconstruction and subsequent editing.

4. **Feature Injection:** Following previous T2I editing methods (e.g., PNP-Diffusion), features saved during the DDIM inversion process are injected back into the U-Net during the DDIM sampling (denoising) phase. This helps preserve the structural integrity and original details of the source video while applying edits.

5. **DDIM Denoising/Sampling:** The edited video is generated by denoising from `z_T` using the target prompt `τ` and the FLATTEN-augmented U-Net.

#### 3.3. Flow-Guided Attention (FLATTEN) Details

The core of FLATTEN lies in its intelligent use of optical flow:

1. **Optical Flow Estimation:** A pre-trained optical flow prediction model, specifically RAFT (Recurrent All-Pairs Field Transforms), is used to estimate dense pixel displacement fields between consecutive frames of the source video. These fields indicate how each pixel moves from one frame to the next. The estimated flow fields are then downsampled to match the resolution of the latent space (e.g., HxW).

2. **Patch Trajectory Sampling:** Based on the downsampled optical flow, the trajectories of patches in the latent space are computed. For a patch at `(x_0, y_0)` in the first frame, its corresponding coordinates `(x_k, y_k)` in subsequent frames `k` are tracked. This creates a sequence of patch locations, forming a "trajectory." To simplify implementation and ensure unique assignment, occlusion handling is performed: when a patch disappears or multiple trajectories converge, a single trajectory is randomly selected to continue, while others are stopped. This ensures each patch belongs to exactly one trajectory.

3. **Attention Process:** Multi-head attention is performed *along these sampled patch trajectories*. For a given patch embedding `z(x_k, y_k)` serving as a query (Q), its corresponding keys (K) and values (V) are *only* the other patch embeddings that lie on the *same trajectory*. Unlike dense spatio-temporal attention where K and V would come from all patches in all frames, FLATTEN restricts K and V to only temporally corresponding patches. This targeted attention facilitates accurate information exchange between consistent visual elements, preventing interference from irrelevant temporal contexts and significantly improving visual consistency. The output of this attention updates the latent features `z`.

By explicitly linking features across frames based on their motion paths, FLATTEN effectively "flattens" the temporal inconsistencies that plague traditional spatio-temporal attention mechanisms, achieving high-quality and consistent T2V editing without requiring additional training or fine-tuning of the diffusion model itself.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that FLATTEN achieves state-of-the-art performance in text-to-video editing, particularly excelling in visual consistency.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison

The method was evaluated on two subsets of the LOVEUTGVE dataset: TGVE-D (16 videos from DAVIS) and TGVE-V (37 videos from Videvo). FLATTEN was compared against Tune-A-Video, Text2Video-Zero, ControlVideo, FateZero, and TokenFlow using several metrics:
- **Textual Alignment:** CLIP-T (average cosine similarity between edited frames and prompt).
- **Visual Consistency:** E_warp (flow warping error).
- **Overall Editing Score:** S_edit = CLIP-T / E_warp (proposed by the authors).
- **Overall Quality:** CLIP-F (average cosine similarity between all frames in a video), PickScore (alignment with human preferences).

**Key Quantitative Results:**
- **Superior S_edit:** FLATTEN achieved the highest S_edit scores on both TGVE-D (57.01) and TGVE-V (84.49), significantly outperforming all other methods (e.g., TokenFlow: 50.21 on TGVE-D, 81.17 on TGVE-V). This indicates a strong balance between textual adherence and visual consistency.
- **Excellent E_warp:** FLATTEN recorded very low E_warp values (4.92 on TGVE-D, 3.16 on TGVE-V), indicating high visual consistency, comparable to or slightly better than TokenFlow, which also emphasizes consistency.
- **High CLIP-T and PickScore:** FLATTEN achieved competitive or highest CLIP-T and PickScore values, demonstrating strong adherence to the text prompt and alignment with human preferences.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons in Figure 5 and the supplementary material clearly illustrate FLATTEN's advantages:
- **Consistent Appearance:** FLATTEN consistently produces edited videos where the generated appearance (e.g., "wooden trucks," "ship... 2D vector art," "colored wolf") remains stable and free of flickering across frames, unlike some competitors (e.g., TokenFlow's intermittent white sun).
- **Structure Preservation:** The method largely preserves the motion and structure of the source video while applying the textual edits.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Tune-A-Video:** Often struggles with structure preservation (e.g., wrong number of trucks).
 - **FateZero:** Can fail to apply edits, resulting in videos almost identical to the source.
 - **Text2Video-Zero & ControlVideo:** Rely on external control maps (e.g., edge maps) which, if of low quality (e.g., due to motion blur), can degrade editing quality.
 - **TokenFlow:** While good at consistency, its pre-defined combination weights can sometimes lead to artifacts or less coherent results.

#### 4.3. Plug-and-Play FLATTEN

A significant finding is FLATTEN's ability to be seamlessly integrated into other diffusion-based T2V editing methods. When incorporated into ControlVideo, FLATTEN remarkably improved its visual consistency, reducing E_warp from 6.81 to 4.78 and boosting S_edit from 40.70 to 56.42, demonstrating its generalizability and practical utility.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the effectiveness of both dense spatio-temporal attention (DSTA) and FLATTEN:
- Adding DSTA to the baseline significantly improved S_edit (from 21.16 to 42.06), showing its role in temporal reasoning.
- Adding FLATTEN alone to the baseline also improved S_edit (from 21.16 to 44.69), highlighting the benefit of flow guidance.
- Combining DSTA and FLATTEN yielded the best results, especially when FLATTEN directly used the output of DSTA (method II), leading to the highest S_edit (57.01) and best visual consistency.

#### 4.5. User Study

A user study involving 16 participants rated FLATTEN highest across all perspectives:
- **Semantic Alignment:** 31.46% preference (vs. TokenFlow 18.65%).
- **Visual Consistency:** 41.12% preference (vs. TokenFlow 26.74%).
- **Motion and Structure Preservation:** 41.59% preference (vs. TokenFlow 24.30%).
This corroborates the automatic metrics and confirms FLATTEN's superior perceived quality by humans.

#### 4.6. DDIM Inversion with FLATTEN

The paper also showed that using FLATTEN during DDIM inversion improved the quality of estimated latent noise, leading to better reconstruction fidelity (higher PSNR and SSIM), underscoring its benefit throughout the diffusion process.

#### 4.7. Limitations and Robustness

- **Limitations:** FLATTEN is less effective for "dramatic structure editing" (e.g., transforming sharks into drones), excelling more in style, texture, and category changes. This is because it relies on preserving the *motion* of the source video, which might conflict with radical structural changes.
- **Robustness:** The method demonstrated robustness to noise in the optical flow field and to using different optical flow estimators (RAFT vs. GMA), indicating that minor inaccuracies in flow do not severely degrade performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FLATTEN represents a significant advancement in the field of text-to-video editing, offering a novel and highly effective solution to the critical challenge of visual consistency. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novelty in Consistency Enforcement:** By introducing optical flow-guided attention directly into the U-Net of diffusion models, FLATTEN offers a fundamentally new and more intelligent approach to ensure temporal coherence. This is a departure from dense spatio-temporal attention, which often introduces irrelevant information, and represents a more principled way to connect corresponding visual elements across frames.
- **Training-Free and Plug-and-Play:** The training-free nature of FLATTEN makes it incredibly practical and accessible. It can be seamlessly integrated into existing diffusion-based T2V editing frameworks (as demonstrated with ControlVideo) without requiring expensive re-training or fine-tuning. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers to improve their T2V models, accelerating progress in the field.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The quantitative and qualitative results, alongside the user study, unequivocally demonstrate FLATTEN's superior performance, particularly in visual consistency and overall editing quality. This sets a new benchmark for the task and provides a robust foundation for future work.
- **Enhanced User Experience:** For end-users, FLATTEN promises to deliver edited videos that are smooth, visually stable, and free from distracting flickers or inconsistencies. This directly translates to a more professional and aesthetically pleasing output, making text-to-video editing a more viable tool for content creation.
- **Efficient Resource Utilization:** By building upon pre-trained T2I models and operating in a training-free manner, FLATTEN avoids the massive computational costs associated with training entirely new video diffusion models. This efficiency makes advanced video editing more accessible.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The successful integration of explicit motion guidance (via optical flow) into the attention mechanism opens new avenues for research. Future work could explore more advanced ways to leverage optical flow, integrate other forms of motion priors, or develop hybrid approaches that combine FLATTEN's consistency with capabilities for more dramatic structural edits. The robustness of the method to noisy flow also suggests its potential for real-world application where perfect flow estimation is challenging.

In conclusion, FLATTEN's innovative use of optical flow for guided attention is a key step forward in making text-to-video editing more consistent, reliable, and practically deployable. Its training-free nature and plug-and-play capability position it as an influential contribution that will likely be adopted and built upon by the wider research community.
