# Natural Language Can Help Bridge the Sim2Real Gap

> [!summary] Summary
> Lang4Sim2Real bridges the sim2real visual gap by pretraining a ResNet-18 image encoder on automatically generated language descriptions of sim and real images, either regressing frozen LLM embeddings or matching pairwise BLEURT language distances. The encoder then backs a multitask, multidomain behavioral-cloning policy trained on abundant sim plus 25-100 real demonstrations, beating prior sim2real methods, CLIP, and R3M by 25-40%.

## Problem

- Image-conditioned policies need vast visual data, but real-world demonstrations are expensive, and policies transfer poorly when simulation and target domains differ drastically in appearance.
- System identification is engineering-intensive and intractable for deformable objects, while domain randomization needs huge diverse data and yields conservative policies over an overly broad state distribution.
- Off-the-shelf vision-language encoders collapse all trajectory frames into a narrow embedding region, lacking the gripper-object spatial granularity that low-level control demands.

## Method

- **Automatic language labeling**: scripted-policy stage indices, or off-the-shelf ==VLM== gripper and object detection (==GroundingDINO==), index template strings to produce per-image scene descriptions in both domains without manual annotation.
- **Language-Regression** pretraining trains a ==ResNet-18== plus a linear predictor head to regress frozen ==miniLM== embeddings of each image's description, making the visual space mirror the language space.
- **Language-Distance** variant matches normalized ==BLEURT== similarity of cross-domain description pairs to image-embedding dot products; the frozen encoder then feeds a ==FiLM==-adapted, language-conditioned multitask behavioral-cloning policy.

## Results

- With **100** real demonstrations, language regression reached **90%** success on multi-step real pick-and-place versus **55%** for no-pretraining on sim plus real, and **55%** for R3M.
- Prior sim2real baselines collapsed on wrap wire: domain randomization scored **0%** at every demo count and ADR+RNA at most **10%**, while language distance reached **75%**.
- In sim2sim, the language variants averaged **43-44%** on stacking and **63-64%** on pick-and-place, outperforming CLIP by **25-40** points and R3M by **15-30**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language is a usable domain-invariant supervision channel: similarly described observations should map to nearby features, so the policy draws similar action distributions across visually dissimilar domains.
> - A few hundred in-domain trajectories with templated labels can outscale internet-pretrained encoders, which plateau because their features lack control-relevant spatial granularity.
> - Tighter regression supervision beat looser distance matching once the domain gap widened; label granularity must segment trajectories finely or the representation collapses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of image-conditioned robotic policies faces a significant challenge in acquiring suitable visual representations for low-level control. This is primarily due to the high dimensionality of image data, necessitating extensive visual data for robust representation learning. In real-world robotic applications, data collection is often resource-intensive and costly.

Sim-to-Real (Sim2Real) transfer learning has emerged as a strategy to mitigate this data scarcity. The paradigm involves training policies in data-rich simulated environments and then transferring them to the real world. However, a major impediment to effective Sim2Real transfer is the "visual gap" – the substantial visual dissimilarities between simulated and real-world domains. Policies trained on images from a simulator frequently fail to generalize to real-world images due to this domain shift.

Current approaches to Sim2Real typically involve:
- **Domain Randomization**: Varying physical and visual parameters in simulation to expose the policy to a broader range of states, hoping to encompass the target real-world domain. This often requires large amounts of diverse data and can lead to conservative policies.
- **System Identification**: Meticulously tuning simulation parameters to match the real world, a process that is often labor-intensive and may not fully capture all real-world complexities, especially for deformable objects.
- **Domain Adaptation with GANs**: Using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to map images between domains at the pixel level, which can be unstable and data-hungry.
- **Semantic Representations**: Utilizing representations like semantic segmentation or depth maps as a common ground, though their efficacy has been demonstrated primarily in simpler navigation tasks.

Concurrently, research in vision-language pretraining has shown that large-scale datasets with image-text pairs can yield powerful visual representations. Models like CLIP and R3M leverage natural language supervision to learn representations that capture high-level semantic information. In robotics, language has been widely used for various purposes, including conditioning policies on instructions, high-level planning, and reward shaping in reinforcement learning. However, these methods often struggle with the fine-grained spatial-temporal understanding required for low-level manipulation. Crucially, the application of natural language specifically to *bridge visual representations across dissimilar domains* in Sim2Real, as proposed in this paper, represents a distinct and less explored area within this landscape.

This work addresses the challenge of visual domain shift in Sim2Real by proposing a novel application of natural language: using language descriptions of images as a domain-invariant semantic signal to unify visual representations across simulation and reality. The intent is to facilitate the learning of visual representations that are robust to pixel-level differences but maintain semantic consistency, thereby improving few-shot imitation learning performance in real-world robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that effectively bridges the visual sim2real gap by leveraging natural language, enabling data-efficient, few-shot imitation learning for robotic manipulation tasks in the real world.

The core motivation stems from the inherent difficulties in learning image-conditioned robotic policies:
- **Data Scarcity in the Real World**: Acquiring the vast amounts of visual data necessary to train robust image representations for low-level control is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming in real-world robotic setups.
- **Visual Dissimilarity in Sim2Real**: While simulation offers a cheap source of data, policies trained purely in simulation often fail to transfer to the real world due to significant pixel-level differences in visual observations (e.g., textures, lighting, camera angles, object appearances) and discrepancies in physical dynamics between the two domains. This "sim2real gap" prevents learned policies from generalizing effectively.

The authors hypothesize that natural language can serve as a "unifying signal" across visually disparate domains. The key insight is that if two image observations, one from simulation and one from the real world, are described by similar natural language, they should semantically represent similar states and thus lead to similar optimal actions. For example, "gripper holding bread over square" should evoke similar underlying semantic states regardless of whether the image is from a simulator or a real camera, even if their pixel appearances are vastly different.

This insight motivates the development of a domain-agnostic visual representation. Such a representation would:
- Allow policies to leverage abundant simulated data as an inductive bias.
- Require only a limited number of real-world demonstrations for successful adaptation.
- Capture task-relevant semantic state from images, while being granular enough for low-level control.

Specifically, the approach aims to overcome several limitations of existing Sim2Real methods:
1. **Eliminating System Identification**: The proposed method seeks to bypass the need for intensive engineering effort to precisely match simulation environments to the real world, both visually and semantically.
2. **Handling Deformable Objects**: It targets tasks involving deformable objects, which are notoriously difficult to simulate accurately in terms of dynamics and visual appearance.
3. **Bridging Wide Domain Gaps**: The method is designed to perform well even with substantial differences between simulation and reality, including variations in camera viewpoint (first-person vs. third-person), friction and damping coefficients, task goals, robot control frequencies, and initial robot/object positions.

By demonstrating that training an image encoder to align with language descriptions or their semantic distances can create a domain-invariant image representation, the research seeks to improve the sample efficiency and performance of Sim2Real transfer in few-shot settings on complex, long-horizon multi-step tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Lang4Sim2Real framework employs a two-stage, pretrain-then-finetune paradigm: (1) cross-domain image-language pretraining and (2) multitask, multidomain behavioral cloning.

#### 3.1. A. Automatic Language Labeling of Images

To acquire the image-language pairs necessary for pretraining, two automated labeling pipelines are utilized:
1. **Synchronous with Scripted Policy**: During scripted policy demonstration collection, each image observation is assigned a "stage index" corresponding to an `if-case` in the policy (e.g., gripper moving above object, gripper closing). These stage indices map directly to predefined template strings describing the scene's semantic state (e.g., "gripper open, reaching for {objName}, out of {contName}").
2. **Decoupled with Off-the-Shelf VLMs**: An alternative, more scalable method allows labeling previously collected images without action or state information. This involves using an open-vocabulary object detector (GroundingDINO) to locate objects and the gripper, combined with a small CNN-based gripper state predictor (trained on 100 trajectories). This information is then used to infer the stage number and corresponding language template. Empirically, this method did not degrade performance.

A frozen miniLM model is used as the language encoder ($f_{lang}$) to map these language descriptions into a fixed-dimensional embedding space.

#### 3.2. B. Cross-Domain Image-Language Pretraining

The first stage focuses on learning a domain-invariant image encoder ($f_{cnn}$), based on a ResNet-18 architecture initialized from scratch. The goal is for $f_{cnn}$ to embed semantically similar scenes from both simulation ($D_s$) and the real world ($D_t^{target}$) into proximate regions of the learned embedding space, despite pixel-level differences. The pretraining dataset $D_{VL}$ consists of image-language pairs from both $D_s$ and $D_t^{target}$ (around 100 demonstrations per task, totaling ~10k images per domain).

Two variants for image-language pretraining are proposed:
1. **Language-Regression**: This variant trains $f_{cnn}$ to directly predict the language embedding ($f_{lang}(l_d)$) of the corresponding image description ($l_d$). A linear layer ($g$) acts as a language predictor. The loss function, $L_{cnn,reg} = ||g(f_{cnn}(I_d)) - f_{lang}(l_d)||^2_2$, encourages the image encoder to align its output directly with the frozen language embedding space.
2. **Language-Distance Learning**: This variant focuses on preserving pairwise semantic distances. It encourages the dot product similarity between the embeddings of two images ($f_{cnn}(I_s)$ and $f_{cnn}(I_t)$) to match the semantic similarity of their language descriptions ($l_s$ and $l_t$). The language similarity is measured using BLEURT, a learned metric for text generation, normalized between 0 and 1. The loss function is $L_{cnn,dist} = (f_{cnn}(I_s)^T f_{cnn}(I_t) - d(l_s, l_t))^2_2$, where $d(\cdot, \cdot)$ is the BLEURT similarity. This provides a softer form of supervision, emphasizing relative semantic distances rather than exact embedding values.

#### 3.3. C. Multitask, Multidomain Behavioral Cloning

The second stage involves training a language-conditioned behavioral cloning (BC) policy ($π$). After pretraining, $f_{cnn}$ is largely frozen to preserve the learned domain-invariant representation, with the exception of its last layer, which is finetuned. Trainable FiLM layer blocks are inserted into $f_{cnn}$ as adapter modules to condition the policy on the task-specific language instruction ($l_{task}$). A policy head, consisting of fully-connected layers, processes the image features ($f_{cnn}(I_t)$) and robot proprioceptive state ($s_t$) to predict the mean and standard deviation of a multivariate Gaussian action distribution.

The policy is trained on a combined dataset $D_{BC} = D_s \cup D_t^{target}$, which includes action-labeled demonstrations. This constitutes a $k+1$ multi-task problem, with $k$ tasks from simulation (thousands of trajectories) and one target task from the real world (25-100 trajectories). The standard BC negative log probability loss is used for training.

#### 3.4. D. Experimental Setup

- **Settings**: Experiments are conducted in both a Sim2Sim setting (two different simulated environments) and a Sim2Real setting (simulation to real-world hardware).
- **Environments**: Robosuite (Mujoco) for simulation, and a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda arm for real-world tasks. Both use a common action space (continuous xyz delta displacement, continuous gripper closure) and proprioceptive state (22-dim). Image observations are 128x128 RGB.
- **Domain Gap**:
 - **Sim2Sim**: Differences in camera viewpoint (third vs. first person), and significantly different joint friction and damping coefficients.
 - **Sim2Real**: Wider gap including differences in control frequency (50Hz sim vs. 2Hz real), object types, visual appearance (backgrounds, camera angles), and initial object/robot positions.
- **Task Suites**:
 1. **Stack Object**: Simple pick-and-place (sim: 4 objects, real: move object to mat).
 2. **Multi-step Pick and Place**: Longer-horizon, two-step task (sim: object in pot, pot on stove; real: carrot in bowl, bowl on plate).
 3. **Wrap Wire**: Deformable object manipulation (sim: chain around cylinder; real: plug cord around blender).
- **Data**: Scripted policies generate demonstrations. $D_s$ contains hundreds to thousands of trajectories per task. $D_t^{target}$ contains 25, 50, or 100 real-world demonstrations.
- **Metrics**: Task success rate (for all tasks), and average number of consecutive subtasks completed (for multi-step).
- **Baselines**: Non-pretrained (only $D_t^{target}$, or $D_s+D_t^{target}$), prior Sim2Real methods (MMD, Domain Randomization, ADR+RNA), and pretrained vision-language models (CLIP, R3M with frozen backbones).
- **Ablations**: "Stage Classification" pretraining (predicting stage index instead of language embedding/distance) to isolate the effect of language.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of Lang4Sim2Real in both Sim2Sim and Sim2Real settings, particularly in few-shot scenarios.

- **Overall Performance**: Lang4Sim2Real consistently achieved the highest success rates across the three task suites in both Sim2Real (Table I) and Sim2Sim (Table II), often outperforming baselines by a substantial margin (25-40%).
- **Impact of Language-Guided Pretraining**:
 - The proposed language-guided pretraining significantly improves performance compared to non-pretrained baselines. In Sim2Real, it nearly doubles the success rate in most task suites. This indicates its ability to bridge a wide Sim2Real gap by ensuring semantically similar images across domains map to similar embedding regions, which in turn influences similar action distributions.
 - For instance, in the "Multi-step Pick and Place" Sim2Real task with 100 real trajectories, "Lang Reg. (ours)" achieved 90% success, compared to 55% for "No Pretrain ($D_s+D_t$)".
- **Comparison to Prior Sim2Real Baselines**:
 - Lang4Sim2Real outperformed all tested prior Sim2Real methods (MMD, Domain Randomization, ADR+RNA). These baselines generally performed poorly in the challenging setups used, highlighting the difficulty of the wide domain gap addressed.
 - MMD showed competitive performance on simpler tasks like "Stack Object" but did not scale well to more complex tasks or with increased data, possibly due to stability issues in mean embedding alignment. Domain randomization and ADR+RNA showed limited or negative impact, suggesting that a simple increase in simulation diversity does not necessarily close wide visual gaps.
- **Comparison to Pretrained Vision-Language Models (CLIP, R3M)**:
 - In Sim2Real, Lang4Sim2Real surpassed both frozen CLIP and R3M. While R3M performed as the strongest among the pretrained baselines, achieving up to 65% success on some tasks, both CLIP and R3M exhibited performance plateaus. Lang4Sim2Real scaled better with increasing amounts of real-world data, achieving up to 90% success, despite CLIP and R3M being pretrained on internet-scale datasets. This suggests that domain-specific, language-aligned pretraining with fewer examples can be more effective for robotic manipulation.
 - In Sim2Sim, our method also outperformed R3M by 15-30% and CLIP by 25-40% on average for stacking and multi-step tasks. Performance was comparable for the "Wrap Wire" task in Sim2Sim, which was generally easier.
- **Effect of Language (Ablation: Stage Classification)**:
 - In Sim2Sim, "Stage Classif." (predicting stage index) performed similarly to "Lang Reg." pretraining.
 - In Sim2Real, however, language pretraining provided a measurable benefit over stage classification across all task suites. This suggests that the semantic richness of natural language (vs. simple stage indices) is particularly advantageous in bridging larger domain gaps.
- **Comparison of Pretraining Variants**:
 - In Sim2Sim, there was no clear winner between "Language-Regression" and "Language-Distance Learning".
 - In Sim2Real, "Language-Regression" generally outperformed "Language-Distance Learning." This indicates that the more direct and constraining regression loss, which aligns image embeddings directly to LLM embeddings, is more effective for transfer across wider Sim2Real gaps.
- **Robustness to Reduced Language Granularity**: Experiments (Table VII) showed that while decreasing language granularity slightly impacted performance, the method remained robust, suggesting that even coarse language alignment contributes to learning useful representations.
- **Pretraining without Target Task Data (D_t_prior)**: When pretraining on a prior real-world task ($D_t^{prior}$) and training the policy on the target task ($D_t^{target}$), Lang4Sim2Real still largely outperformed baselines (Table IV). This indicates the learned representation does not overfit to the pretraining task and generalizes to related unseen tasks. R3M showed stronger performance in the lowest data regime (25 demonstrations) when baselines were given additional $D_t^{prior}$ action-labeled data.
- **Combining with R3M**: Finetuning R3M with the proposed pretraining procedure did not yield improvements in Sim2Real (Table X), possibly because the adapters learned simulation-specific features that did not transfer effectively from R3M's real-world pretraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to addressing the Sim2Real gap in robotics, particularly for visual imitation learning in data-scarce real-world environments.

The key significance lies in demonstrating that:
- **Data-Efficient Sim2Real Transfer**: Natural language descriptions can serve as an effective, data-efficient bridge for transferring visual policies from simulation to reality. The method successfully leverages language-annotated images from a limited number of real-world demonstrations (25-100 trajectories) alongside abundant simulation data.
- **Domain-Invariant Representation Learning**: The proposed language-guided pretraining helps learn visual representations that are robust to substantial pixel-level domain shifts while preserving task-relevant semantic information. This is crucial for enabling policies to generalize from simulated to real-world observations.
- **Performance Beyond State-of-the-Art**: Lang4Sim2Real consistently outperforms both established Sim2Real techniques (e.g., MMD, Domain Randomization) and powerful vision-language foundation models (e.g., CLIP, R3M) in few-shot settings across various complex robotic manipulation tasks. This suggests a more tailored and effective approach for robotic control contexts.
- **Reduced Engineering Overhead**: By effectively bridging wide domain gaps, the method can alleviate the intensive engineering effort typically required for system identification or fine-tuning simulation parameters to match real-world dynamics and visuals. This could accelerate the development and deployment of robotic systems.
- **Applicability to Challenging Scenarios**: The demonstrated success on tasks involving long-horizon, multi-step operations and deformable objects (which are difficult to simulate accurately) expands the scope of practical Sim2Real applications.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment**: By reducing the reliance on costly real-world data collection, this method could enable faster iteration and deployment of robotic manipulation skills in diverse real-world settings.
- **Democratization of Robotics Research**: Researchers and practitioners with limited access to extensive real-world robot platforms and data collection resources could leverage this approach to develop and test policies more effectively using simulation.
- **Advancement in Foundational Models for Robotics**: While current large-scale foundation models provide general visual understanding, this work indicates that specialized, language-guided fine-tuning or pretraining with task-relevant data can yield superior performance for downstream robotic control tasks, potentially leading to the development of more robot-centric visual representations.
- **New Avenues for Multimodal Learning**: This research highlights the power of language as a cross-modal alignment signal beyond traditional vision-language tasks, pointing towards future investigations into how language can integrate with other modalities (e.g., tactile, force) to enhance robotic learning.

Future work suggested by the authors, such as scaling the method to larger datasets and combining it with other representation learning techniques, could further enhance its generalizability and fine-grained understanding, cementing its role as a significant step towards more robust and data-efficient Sim2Real transfer.
