# JanusVLN: Decoupling Semantics and Spatiality with Dual Implicit Memory for Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> JANUSVLN introduces a dual implicit neural memory system that decouples visual semantics and spatial geometry to enhance Vision-Language Navigation. The framework achieves state-of-the-art navigation accuracy and robust spatial understanding from RGB inputs on VLN-CE benchmarks and in real-world robot deployments.

## Problem

- Existing explicit memory in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) models, such as textual cognitive maps or historical frames, suffers from spatial information loss, redundancy, and exponential memory inflation.
- Visual encoders of many Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are primarily pre-trained on 2D image-text pairs, limiting their comprehension of 3D geometric structures and spatial relationships necessary for robust navigation.
- Computational overhead from reprocessing entire historical observations at each action prediction step hinders the scalability and real-time performance of VLN agents.

## Method

- JANUSVLN proposes a dual implicit neural memory that decouples visual semantics and 3D spatial geometry into separate, compact, fixed-size neural representations, preventing memory inflation and computational redundancy.
- It integrates a ==Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT)== as a 3D spatial-geometric encoder to extract 3D prior knowledge directly from RGB video streams, without requiring explicit 3D data.
- A hybrid incremental update strategy combines a sliding window queue for recent frames with an initial window retaining early trajectory information, serving as stable 'Attention Sinks' for long-term memory.

## Results

- Achieved SR **60.5%**/SPL **56.8%** on the R2R-CE Val-Unseen split (outperforming prior explicit-memory methods by up to **10.8%** and 3D-depth methods by up to **16.7%** SR) and SR **56.2%**/nDTW **62.1%** on RxR-CE Val-Unseen.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both dual implicit memory components are essential, with the removal of spatial-geometric memory leading to a substantial **8.3** point drop in SPL (from **49.2** to **40.9**).
- Successfully deployed on a Unitree Go2 robotic platform, executing complex real-world navigation tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding, validating its practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual semantics and spatial geometry into distinct implicit neural memories allows for more efficient and robust environmental understanding compared to unified explicit memory systems.
> - 3D spatial-geometric priors can be effectively extracted and utilized from standard 2D RGB video input by leveraging models like VGGT, significantly enhancing spatial reasoning without specialized hardware.
> - Fixed-size, incrementally updated implicit memory significantly reduces inference overhead by 69-90% and maintains competitive performance for long navigation trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a fundamental task within embodied AI, requiring an agent to navigate real-world environments guided by natural language instructions and visual input. Recent advancements in VLN have been significantly influenced by Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which offer robust semantic understanding capabilities. These MLLM-driven approaches typically employ explicit semantic memory mechanisms, such as constructing textual cognitive maps or storing sequences of historical visual frames.

However, existing methodologies face several limitations within the current research landscape:
1. **Spatial Information Loss and Redundancy in Explicit Memory:** Methods relying on textual cognitive maps often struggle to convey precise spatial relationships and object orientations, leading to the loss of crucial visual, spatial-geometric, and contextual information. Additionally, repetitive descriptions contribute to redundancy and noise. Approaches that store historical observation frames necessitate reprocessing the entire history at each action prediction step, resulting in significant computational overhead. In both scenarios, explicit semantic memory tends to expand exponentially with navigation time, impeding the efficient extraction of relevant information.
2. **Deficiency in 3D Spatial Understanding:** A prevalent issue is that the visual encoders of many existing MLLMs are primarily pre-trained on 2D image-text pairs, following paradigms like CLIP. While these encoders excel at capturing high-level semantics, they exhibit limitations in understanding 3D geometric structures and spatial information. This is despite 2D images inherently containing various 3D spatial cues such as perspective, occlusion, and geometric structures, which human observers can readily interpret. This oversight constrains the spatial reasoning capabilities of models in complex navigation tasks.

JanusVLN addresses these limitations by proposing a fundamental departure from explicit, single-memory systems. It introduces a dual, implicit neural memory approach, aiming to decouple and explicitly model both visual semantics and spatial geometry. This approach seeks to provide a more compact, efficient, and spatially-aware memory system for continuous VLN, thereby contributing to the development of embodied agents with enhanced spatial reasoning from standard RGB inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were motivated by the identified limitations in current Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) systems, particularly those leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The key objectives and underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Explicit Memory in VLN:**
 - **Motivation:** Current MLLM-based VLN methods typically rely on explicit semantic memory (e.g., textual cognitive maps or storing historical visual frames). These explicit memory structures suffer from several drawbacks: memory inflation (growing exponentially with trajectory length), computational redundancy (requiring reprocessing of historical data), and loss of fine-grained spatial information. Such inefficiencies hinder the scalability and real-time performance of VLN agents in complex, long-duration navigation tasks.
 - **Objective:** To develop a novel memory paradigm that avoids these issues by creating a fixed-size, compact neural representation for historical information, thereby enabling efficient and incremental updates without recomputing past observations.

2. **Enhance 3D Spatial Reasoning from RGB-Only Input:**
 - **Motivation:** Navigation is an inherently 3D physical interaction. However, the visual encoders of many existing MLLMs are predominantly trained on 2D image-text datasets, making them proficient in high-level semantics but deficient in comprehending 3D geometric structures and spatial relationships. This 2D-centric approach limits their ability to effectively reason about spatial layouts and depth, even though 2D RGB images contain implicit 3D cues. Relying on auxiliary 3D data (e.g., depth maps, point clouds) requires specialized hardware, restricting real-world applicability.
 - **Objective:** To improve the spatial reasoning capabilities of VLN models by integrating 3D prior knowledge directly from RGB video input, without requiring any explicit 3D data. This involves extending MLLMs to incorporate a feed-forward 3D visual geometry foundation model.

3. **Introduce a Dual Implicit Memory Framework:**
 - **Motivation:** The human brain’s navigation system provided inspiration for this work. Human cognition implicitly represents scenes, with hemispheric specialization for semantic understanding (analogous to the left brain) and 3D spatial cognition (analogous to the right brain). This dual processing allows for efficient and robust navigation. Current single, explicit memory systems in AI do not fully emulate this integrated, yet specialized, cognitive architecture.
 - **Objective:** To propose JanusVLN, a novel framework featuring a dual implicit neural memory. This framework models spatial-geometric and visual-semantic memory as separate, compact, and fixed-size neural representations. This design is intended to mirror the human brain’s efficient memorization within finite capacity while enabling a synergistic understanding of both "what" objects are and "where" they are located in 3D space.

4. **Establish a New Paradigm for VLN Research:**
 - **Motivation:** The existing focus on 2D semantics-dominated approaches limits the potential for developing truly spatially-aware embodied agents. A fundamental shift is necessary to advance the field.
 - **Objective:** To demonstrate that the proposed dual implicit memory, as a novel paradigm, can effectively replace conventional textual cognitive maps and historical frames, thereby steering VLN research from a 2D semantics-dominant approach towards 3D spatial-semantic synergy. The ultimate aim is to contribute significantly to building the next generation of spatially-aware embodied agents capable of more robust and intelligent navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JanusVLN is presented as a novel VLN framework that employs a dual implicit neural memory system to address the limitations of explicit memory and enhance spatial understanding from RGB-only video streams. The methodology centers on decoupling visual semantics and spatial geometry into distinct, fixed-size neural representations.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Concepts

- **Navigation Task Definition:** The task involves an embodied agent navigating through unseen continuous environments. At each timestep `t`, the agent receives a natural language instruction `I` and an ego-centric RGB video stream `O_T = {x_0..., x_t}`. The agent predicts a low-level action `a_{t+1}` from a defined action space `A = {Move Forward, Turn Left, Turn Right, Stop}`. Actions correspond to fine-grained physical changes (e.g., 30° rotation, 25 cm forward step). The process iterates until the `Stop` action is executed at the target.
- **Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT):** VGGT is a learning-based, end-to-end model designed for 3D reconstruction. It uses a transformer feed-forward architecture comprising an encoder for single-image feature extraction, a fusion decoder for cross-frame interaction to generate geometric tokens, and a task-specific prediction head for 3D attributes (e.g., point map, confidence map). For JanusVLN, the pre-trained encoder and fusion decoder components of VGGT are leveraged as a 3D visual geometry encoder to extract 3D prior information from RGB input, without directly outputting 3D attributes.

#### 3.2. Dual Implicit Memory

The core of JanusVLN is its dual implicit memory, designed to prevent memory inflation, computational redundancy, and spatial information loss inherent in traditional explicit memory systems.

- **Implicit Neural Representation:** Instead of storing raw, high-dimensional historical frames, JanusVLN caches historical Key-Value (KV) pairs that have been processed by neural networks. These KVs, derived from the output of attention modules (e.g., transformers), represent high-level semantic abstractions and structured representations of the past environment. This design creates a compact, knowledge-refined memory for efficient retrieval and reasoning.
- **Hybrid Incremental Update Strategy:** To maintain fixed-size memory and manage long navigation sequences, a hybrid caching mechanism is employed:
 - **Sliding Window Queue (M_sliding):** A First-In, First-Out (FIFO) queue of capacity `n` that stores the KV caches of the `n` most recent frames. This provides the model with immediate, relevant contextual information. The oldest KV is evicted when a new frame is added.
 - **Initial Window (M_initial):** This component permanently retains the KV cache from the initial few frames of the trajectory. These initial frames act as "Attention Sinks," providing stable global anchors for the entire navigation task and contributing to long-term memory.
 - **Efficient Retrieval:** For each new incoming frame, cross-attention is computed between its current image tokens and the combined implicit memory (`M_initial` and `M_sliding`). This directly retrieves historical information without reprocessing past frames, significantly reducing inference overhead. The spatial geometry encoder's global attention process integrates the current frame's tokens (as Query) with concatenated historical KVs (from `M_initial` and `M_sliding`).

#### 3.3. JanusVLN Architecture

The JanusVLN framework integrates the dual implicit memory with a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to enhance spatial understanding.

- **Decoupling Visual Perception: Semantics and Spatiality:**
 - **2D Visual Semantic Encoder:** The original visual encoder from Qwen2.5-VL (a specific MLLM) is used to encode the input RGB frame `x_t` into 2D semantic tokens (`S_t`). This encoder groups spatially adjacent 2x2 patches into a single image token to manage computational cost.
 - **3D Spatial-Geometric Encoder:** The pre-trained encoder and fusion decoder of VGGT are used to encode the input RGB frame `x_t` into 3D spatial-geometric tokens (`G_t`), interacting with its dedicated spatial memory.

- **Spatial-Aware Feature Fusion:**
 - After obtaining semantic features (`S_t`) and spatial-geometric features (`G_t`), a spatial merging strategy (similar to Qwen2.5-VL) concatenates 2x2 feature blocks within `G_t` to align its shape with `S_t`, producing `G'_t`.
 - These aligned spatial features (`G'_t`) are then projected through a lightweight two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and subsequently added to the semantic features (`S'_t`). The fusion formula is `F_t = S'_t + λ * MLP(G'_t)`, where `λ` is a weight for the spatial-geometric features.
 - The resulting `F_t` represents the final spatially-geometrically enhanced visual features.
 - These fused visual features, alongside the text embedding of the navigation instruction `I`, are then fed into the MLLM backbone to predict the agent's next action.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Base Models:** JanusVLN is constructed upon Qwen2.5-VL 7B and the VGGT model.
- **Training:** The model is trained for one epoch. Only the LLM and the projection layer are fine-tuned, with learning rates of 2e-5 and 1e-5, respectively. The semantic and spatial encoders remain frozen.
- **Memory Configuration:** The initial window size is set to 8 frames, and the sliding window size is set to 48 frames. The spatial geometric feature weight `λ` is set to 0.2.
- **Data:** In addition to standard datasets, 155K trajectories from ScaleVLN and 14K DAgger-collected trajectories from R2R-CE and RxR-CE are incorporated for training, totaling approximately 10.7 million image-action pairs.
- **Real-World Setup:** Evaluation on a Unitree Go2 robotic platform equipped with an Insta360 X5 camera. JanusVLN runs on a remote server with an A10 GPU for continuous processing and action inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of JanusVLN was evaluated through extensive experiments on standard benchmarks and real-world scenarios, alongside ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results on VLN-CE Benchmarks

Experiments were conducted on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE (Val-Unseen splits) datasets, using metrics such as Navigation Error (NE), Oracle Success Rate (OS), Success Rate (SR), Success-weighted Path Length (SPL), and normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW). SR and SPL are considered primary indicators.

- **R2R Val-Unseen (Table 1):**
 - **Against Multi-Input Methods:** JanusVLN achieved an SR of 60.5%, demonstrating an improvement of 10.5-35.5% compared to methods that utilize multiple input types like panoramic views, odometry, or depth.
 - **Against 3D Depth Data Methods:** JanusVLN reported an SR improvement of 12.6-16.7% over methods relying on explicit 3D depth data, such as g3D-LF (47.2% SR) and NaVid-4D (43.8% SR), while operating solely on RGB input.
 - **Against Explicit Memory Methods (RGB-only):** The framework surpassed methods using explicit textual cognitive maps (e.g., MapNav, 39.7% SR) and historical frames (e.g., NaVILA, 54.0% SR; StreamVLN, 56.9% SR) by 20.8%, 10.8%, and 3.6% in SR, respectively, often with comparable or less auxiliary data.
 - **Performance with Limited/No Extra Data:** JanusVLN* (without additional data) achieved an SR of 52.8% and SPL of 49.2%, outperforming NaVILA* (49.7% SR, 45.5 SPL with 12574K external data) and StreamVLN* (45.5% SR, 41.6 SPL with 10033K external data) by 3.1-7.3% in SR and 3.7-18.8% in SPL.
 - **Overall:** JanusVLN achieved the highest reported SR (60.5%) and SPL (56.8%) on the R2R-CE Val-Unseen split, indicating strong performance.

- **RxR Val-Unseen (Table 2):**
 - JanusVLN reported an SR of 56.2%, an improvement of 3.3-30.7% over previous methods. It also achieved the highest nDTW at 62.1%. This suggests superior generalization capabilities across different datasets.

#### 4.2. Real-World Qualitative Results (Figure 4, Figure 11)

The model was evaluated on a Unitree Go2 robotic platform in real-world environments. JanusVLN successfully executed navigation tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding, such as identifying the "farthest yellow stool," distinguishing between "beside the green potted plant rather than in front of it," and recognizing a "stool beside the orange cabinet." This indicates the framework's ability to enhance spatial reasoning in practical applications.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (R2R-CE, no additional data unless specified)

- **Ablation of the Dual Implicit Memory (Table 3):**
 - Removing spatial-geometric memory resulted in a substantial drop in SPL (from 49.2 to 40.9) and SR (from 52.8 to 47.0), demonstrating its role in spatial understanding.
 - Removing semantic memory led to a 13.8% decrease in SR (from 52.8 to 45.5).
 - The simultaneous removal of both memory modules caused a significant degradation in performance (SR 24.8, SPL 16.8). These results collectively affirm the complementary and essential nature of the dual implicit memory.

- **Ablation of 3D Geometric Priors (Table 4):**
 - Replacing the VGGT spatial geometric encoder with other 2D-pretrained visual encoders (DINOv2, SigLIP 2) or a randomly initialized VGGT did not yield significant performance improvements (SR ~47-47.9). This suggests that the performance gains are not solely due to increased model parameters but specifically from the VGGT's embedded 3D spatial-geometric priors, which are acquired from training on pixel-to-3D point cloud pairs.

- **Ablation on Memory Size (Table 5):**
 - The original VGGT model without caching showed exponentially increasing inference time with sequence length, becoming impractical.
 - JanusVLN's cached memory strategy reduced inference overhead by 69-90% while simultaneously offering slight performance improvements.
 - Performance progressively improved with increasing memory size, saturating at 48 frames, suggesting that a compact, fixed-size implicit memory is sufficient.
 - Omitting the preservation of initial window KVs resulted in a slight performance degradation (SR drop from 52.8 to 51.0), highlighting the importance of these initial frames as long-term "Attention Sinks."

- **Real-world Quantitative Results (Appendix C, Figure 6):** In real-world tests, JanusVLN outperformed its variant without spatial memory, showing a 23.6% improvement in tasks requiring spatial understanding.

- **Results on HM3D-OVON (Appendix C, Table 6):** On the HM3D-OVON benchmark, JanusVLN achieved an SR of 44.9%, surpassing previous methods (SOTA 40.8%), which indicates its generalization capabilities to more diverse environments.

- **Ablation of Fusion Strategies (Appendix C, Table 7):** An additive fusion strategy with a spatial feature weight (λ) of 0.2 yielded optimal performance. Cross-attention performed competitively but was marginally inferior.

- **Data Ablation (Appendix C, Table 8):** JanusVLN demonstrated robust intrinsic navigation capabilities, outperforming prior methods that used partial supplementary datasets even without any additional data. Performance further improved with the inclusion of ScaleVLN and DAgger data, indicating efficient data utilization.

#### 4.4. Statistical Analysis (Appendix D)

- **Success and Strengths Analysis (Figure 7):** JanusVLN exhibited more pronounced superiority in tasks specifically requiring spatial understanding (e.g., instructions with terms like 'farthest,' 'nearest,' 'rightmost'), compared to its average gain across all tasks.
- **Performance by Instruction Length (Figure 8):** For highly complex and lengthy instructions (400-550 words), JanusVLN maintained performance, outperforming StreamVLN, potentially due to its dual implicit memory effectively processing detailed, step-by-step guidance.
- **Failure Case Analysis (Figure 9):** Two common failure types were identified: inadequate self-correction when deviating from the optimal path, and premature stopping, possibly due to an overly aggressive stopping policy or inaccurate distance estimation from spatial information lacking real-world scale.

#### 4.5. Visualization Analysis of Spatial Geometric Tokens (Appendix E, Figure 10)

Visualizations of spatial geometry tokens as depth maps and point clouds demonstrated their ability to capture precise depth information, clarify object positions, sizes, and relationships (e.g., localizing the "farthest chair," revealing a "chair behind the sink counter," representing "door size," and focusing on the "rightmost house"). This confirms that the spatial information embedded in these tokens is crucial for spatial understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

JanusVLN introduces a novel framework for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) that has implications for the efficiency, robustness, and capabilities of embodied AI agents.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Novel Memory Paradigm:** The work presents a dual implicit neural memory paradigm for VLN, representing a departure from explicit, growing memory structures. This paradigm is inspired by the human brain’s specialized cognitive functions for semantic understanding and spatial cognition, offering a more biologically analogous approach to scene representation.
- **Advancement in Spatial Understanding:** JanusVLN demonstrates a method to acquire 3D spatial geometric structural information directly from standard RGB video streams, without requiring auxiliary 3D data (such as depth maps or point clouds) or specialized hardware. This capability addresses a fundamental limitation of many MLLM-based VLN systems that are often biased towards 2D semantic understanding.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** The design of fixed-size, compact neural memory, coupled with a hybrid incremental update mechanism (initial and sliding window KV caches), significantly reduces memory inflation and computational redundancy. This efficiency enables more practical and scalable real-time navigation for embodied agents.
- **Shift in VLN Research Direction:** The success of JanusVLN in integrating spatial-geometric and visual-semantic information suggests a shift in VLN research from being primarily 2D semantics-dominated towards a 3D spatial-semantic synergy. This reorientation is presented as critical for developing more capable and context-aware embodied agents.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **More Capable Embodied Agents:** By providing agents with a more robust and efficient understanding of both "what" objects are and "where" they are in 3D space, JanusVLN could lead to the development of embodied agents capable of more complex and fine-grained navigation tasks in unfamiliar environments.
- **Reduced Hardware Requirements:** The ability to extract rich 3D spatial information solely from RGB input reduces the reliance on expensive auxiliary 3D sensing hardware, potentially lowering the cost and increasing the accessibility of advanced navigation systems for real-world deployment.
- **Applications in Diverse Domains:** As articulated in the ethical statement, the technology could advance beneficial applications of embodied AI. Examples include providing navigational assistance for individuals with visual impairments, enhancing the autonomy and task efficiency of domestic service robots, and improving search and rescue operations in challenging or hazardous environments.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The dual implicit memory framework and the approach to integrating 3D priors from 2D input may inspire further research into more cognitively plausible and computationally efficient memory architectures for various embodied AI tasks beyond navigation.

#### 5.3. Ethical Considerations

The authors acknowledge the potential for dual-use of advanced autonomous navigation technology. While highlighting beneficial applications, they recognize the risk of misuse in negative contexts such as unauthorized surveillance or military operations. The research is motivated by scientific progress and social welfare, and the authors condemn unethical or malicious uses of the technology, calling for adherence to guidelines for responsible AI development and application within the academic community.
