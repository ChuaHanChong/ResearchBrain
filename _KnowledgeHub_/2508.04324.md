# TempFlow-GRPO: When Timing Matters for GRPO in Flow Models

> [!summary] Summary
> TempFlow-GRPO introduces a reinforcement learning framework that enhances human preference alignment for text-to-image flow matching models by explicitly considering the temporal dynamics of the generative process. The method achieves a Geneval score of 0.97, outperforming Flow-GRPO's 0.88, and demonstrates up to a 3.75x speedup in convergence while maintaining lower and more stable KL divergence.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) methods for flow matching models treat all generative timesteps uniformly, neglecting their varying impact and exploration potential.
- Previous GRPO (Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization) approaches rely on sparse terminal rewards, leading to imprecise and inefficient credit assignment across the multi-step image generation process.
- Training 'process reward models' for intermediate, semantically ambiguous noisy image states is difficult and computationally expensive.

## Method

- ==Trajectory Branching==: Introduces controlled stochasticity at specific 'branching timesteps' to precisely attribute final reward differences to actions at that step, effectively creating a process reward.
- Noise-Aware Policy Weighting: Adapts the optimization intensity for each timestep proportionally to its intrinsic noise level, amplifying learning in early, high-impact stages and refining later stages.
- Seed Group Strategy: Groups trajectories sharing an identical initial noise seed to improve the signal-to-noise ratio of reward variations, ensuring changes are attributed solely to intermediate exploration.

## Results

- Achieved a Geneval score of **0.97**, substantially outperforming Flow-GRPO (**0.88**) and state-of-the-art models like GPT-4o and FLUX.1 Dev.
- Demonstrated superior human preference alignment, improving PickScore by ~**1.7%** over Flow-GRPO and matching Flow-GRPO's performance with a **3.75x** speedup on HPSv3.
- Consistently exhibited faster convergence, reducing training time to **33%** to **50%** of Flow-GRPO at 1024x1024 resolution while achieving superior final performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The impact and exploration potential of generative steps in flow models vary significantly across timesteps; early steps are more critical for structure, while later steps are for refinement.
> - Precise credit assignment for intermediate actions in a multi-step generative process can be achieved by strategically introducing stochasticity at controlled points, circumventing the need for specialized process reward models.
> - Aligning reinforcement learning optimization pressure with the intrinsic noise level of each generative step leads to more efficient exploration and stable learning dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: text-to-image (T2I) generation, flow matching models, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

**Text-to-Image Generation and Diffusion Models:** The past few years have seen an explosion in the capabilities of T2I models, particularly those based on diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, Midjourney). These models excel at generating high-quality, diverse images from textual prompts. More recently, flow matching models (like FLUX and SANA) have emerged as an alternative to diffusion models, often offering faster inference and competitive or superior generation quality by learning a continuous-time "flow" that transforms simple noise into complex data. The paper specifically references recent advancements in flow matching models (Esser et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024a; Labs et al., 2025), positioning itself within this leading-edge generative paradigm.

**Human Preference Alignment (RLHF):** Despite the impressive capabilities of these generative models, aligning their outputs precisely with nuanced human preferences remains a significant challenge. Purely unsupervised or self-supervised training often leads to outputs that are aesthetically lacking, semantically incorrect, or biased in ways that humans dislike. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has proven highly effective in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human instructions and preferences. The application of RLHF to diffusion models, often termed "Diffusion RL" (Wallace et al., 2024; Black et al., 2023; Fan et al., 2023), is a burgeoning area aimed at solving this alignment problem for image generation. Methods like D3PO and Diffusion-DPO adapt DPO for diffusion models, while Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) and DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025) specifically apply online reinforcement learning to flow matching models.

**The Gap Addressed by TempFlow-GRPO:** The authors identify a crucial limitation in existing RLHF approaches for flow matching models: **temporal uniformity**. Previous methods, including Flow-GRPO, treat all timesteps of the generative process equally and typically rely on sparse terminal rewards (feedback only on the final image). This overlooks the fact that different stages of image generation (e.g., early structural decisions vs. late fine-grained refinements) have vastly different impacts on the final output and exploration potential. This "black box" approach to the multi-step generation process leads to inefficient exploration and suboptimal convergence. Some works, like SPO (Liang et al., 2025), attempt to use process reward models, but these are difficult to train due to the semantic ambiguity of intermediate noisy images.

TempFlow-GRPO directly addresses this fundamental oversight. By explicitly considering the temporal dynamics and varying criticality of decisions across timesteps, this work aims to significantly improve the efficiency and effectiveness of RLHF for flow models, pushing the boundaries of human preference alignment in T2I generation beyond the state-of-the-art uniform approaches. It represents a principled step towards more "temporally-aware" reinforcement learning in continuous generative processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of TempFlow-GRPO is to develop a principled reinforcement learning framework for flow matching text-to-image models that effectively aligns generated outputs with human preferences by explicitly accounting for the temporal dynamics of the generative process.

The motivation stems from two critical limitations observed in existing GRPO (Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization) frameworks for flow models:

1. **Temporal Uniformity Assumption:** Current approaches treat all timesteps in the multi-step generation process uniformly, applying the same optimization pressure regardless of the stage of generation. This is a significant oversight because:
 - **Varying Criticality:** Decisions made in early stages, when the image is highly noisy and abstract, establish the fundamental structure and composition. These decisions have a much higher impact on the final image quality and semantic content than fine-tuning adjustments made in later, less noisy stages.
 - **Non-uniform Exploration Potential:** The intrinsic noise level in the underlying SDE sampler varies dramatically across timesteps. Early, high-noise stages offer substantial exploration potential, while late, low-noise stages are primarily for stable refinement. Uniform optimization squanders these high-impact exploration opportunities and can lead to instability in later stages.

2. **Sparse Terminal Rewards and Inefficient Credit Assignment:** Existing methods typically provide a single reward signal only upon the completion of image generation. Assigning credit for this terminal reward uniformly across all intermediate actions (timesteps) is imprecise and inefficient. It becomes challenging to discern which specific actions or stages contributed positively or negatively to the final outcome. While some attempt to use "process reward models" for intermediate steps, training such models on semantically ambiguous noisy images is notoriously difficult and computationally expensive.

To remedy these shortcomings, the authors are motivated to design a GRPO framework that:
- Enables **precise credit assignment** to intermediate actions without requiring specialized intermediate reward models.
- Adapts **optimization intensity** to each timestep's intrinsic exploration capacity and impact on the final output.
- Ultimately leads to **more efficient exploration, faster convergence, and superior alignment** with human preferences and compositional capabilities in text-to-image generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TempFlow-GRPO addresses the identified limitations by introducing three key innovations that collectively enable temporally-aware optimization within the GRPO framework for flow matching models:

#### 3.1. Trajectory Branching for Process Rewards

The core idea here is to achieve precise credit assignment for intermediate actions without the need for complex, hard-to-train process reward models that evaluate noisy intermediate states.
- **Key Insight:** Instead of evaluating intermediate states, TempFlow-GRPO strategically leverages the hybrid deterministic-stochastic nature of flow matching sampling (ODE for deterministic evolution, SDE for stochasticity).
- **Mechanism:** A trajectory is generated deterministically (using ODE) up to a designated "branching timestep" *k*. At this specific point, stochasticity is intentionally introduced (using SDE) via a noise variable. The rest of the trajectory, from *k-1* down to *0*, is then completed deterministically (using ODE).
- **Credit Localization (Theorem 1):** By concentrating all stochasticity and model controllability at this single branching point, any difference in the final reward can be unambiguously attributed to the exploratory action taken at timestep *k*. This creates an effective "process reward" signal for that specific timestep, based on an existing, high-quality outcome-based reward model.
- **Practical Implementation:** For each step *k*, the reward *R* for that step is defined as *R(ODE<sub>k-1</sub>(SDE(x<sub>k</sub>, ϵ<sub>i</sub>)), c)*, which means generating from *x<sub>k</sub>* with one SDE step followed by deterministic ODE steps until *x<sub>0</sub>*. This effectively provides a temporally-aware reward signal for each *k*.

#### 3.2. Noise-Aware Policy Weighting

This innovation addresses the "uniform optimization" limitation by aligning optimization pressure with the varying exploration potential across timesteps.
- **Problem:** The SDE sampler injects varying magnitudes of noise (*σ<sub>t</sub>√∆t*) across timesteps. Early stages have high noise and thus high exploration capacity, while late stages have low noise, focused on refinement. Standard GRPO applies uniform optimization, ignoring this.
- **Observation:** The authors demonstrate a strong correlation between the reward standard deviation (indicating exploration impact) and the intrinsic noise level (*σ<sub>t</sub>√∆t*) at each timestep (Figure 5, Left). This suggests the noise level is a natural proxy for a weighting factor.
- **Mechanism:** TempFlow-GRPO modifies the policy loss function by introducing a weighting factor proportional to the noise level at each timestep (*Norm(σ<sub>t</sub>√∆t)*).
- **Intuition:** This weighting amplifies the learning signal during early, high-noise, high-impact stages, encouraging macroscopic exploration. Conversely, it reduces optimization pressure in later, low-noise stages, preventing aggressive updates from corrupting high-fidelity image states and encouraging fine-grained adjustments.
- **Theoretical Justification (Appendix A.1):** The policy gradient derivation shows that without reweighting, the "scale term" modulating gradient contributions is imbalanced. Noise-aware reweighting simplifies this term to be proportional to step size *∆k*, achieving a more balanced gradient contribution across timesteps, especially when "flow shift" equals 1 (Figure 5, Right).

#### 3.3. Seed Group Strategy

This strategy enhances the robustness and attribution of reward variations.
- **Background:** Traditional GRPO uses prompt-level or batch-level grouping for normalization. Trajectory branching involves *K* distinct explorations at each timestep.
- **Mechanism:** TempFlow-GRPO introduces a novel seed-level grouping strategy. Trajectories originating from the same prompt are further grouped if they share an identical initial noise seed (*x<sub>T</sub>*).
- **Benefit:** By controlling for the influence of the initial noise, this strategy ensures that any observed variations in reward can be more confidently attributed solely to the stochastic exploration introduced during the branching process at intermediate timesteps, rather than initial random fluctuations. This improves the signal-to-noise ratio of the reward for policy optimization.

By integrating these three innovations, TempFlow-GRPO creates a comprehensive, temporally-aware RL framework that respects the underlying generative dynamics of flow models, leading to more efficient and effective human preference alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TempFlow-GRPO demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across various benchmarks, showcasing significant improvements in sample quality, human preference alignment, and computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Compositional Image Generation (Geneval)

- TempFlow-GRPO substantially improves the base model's performance on the Geneval benchmark, raising the overall score from 0.63 to 0.97.
- It significantly outperforms Flow-GRPO, achieving a score of 0.97 in just 3,800 steps, whereas Flow-GRPO only reached 0.88 under the same conditions.
- The method converges much faster, reaching a score of 0.95 in approximately 2,000 steps, while Flow-GRPO required 5,600 steps for the same level.
- It surpasses other state-of-the-art models like GPT-4o, FLUX.1 Dev, and SD3.5-Medium on this benchmark (Figure 3, Right; Table 1).

#### 4.2. Enhanced Human Preference Alignment (PickScore, HPSv3)

- On the PickScore benchmark, TempFlow-GRPO achieves the highest performance, outperforming the original Flow-GRPO by ~1.7% and an improved baseline (Flow-GRPO Prompt) by ~1.0% (Figure 3, Left).
- It demonstrates remarkable training efficiency, matching Flow-GRPO's performance in 100-200 steps and Flow-GRPO (Prompt)'s performance in 300-400 steps.
- When evaluated on FLUX.1-dev with HPSv3 as the reward model, TempFlow-GRPO matches Flow-GRPO's 300-step performance in merely 80 steps, a 3.75x speedup (Figure 7, Figure 10).

#### 4.3. Faster Convergence and Computational Efficiency

- Across all evaluated benchmarks (Geneval, PickScore, HPSv3 on FLUX.1-dev), TempFlow-GRPO consistently exhibits faster convergence in terms of training steps and GPU hours (Figure 3, Figure 12).
- At higher resolutions (1024x1024), it achieves Flow-GRPO's final performance using only 33% to 50% of the total training time.
- The method also achieves superior final performance, indicating a higher performance ceiling than Flow-GRPO even with extended training.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Confirming Component Effectiveness

- **Trajectory Branching:** Introducing trajectory branching significantly improves Flow-GRPO (Prompt) performance on both PickScore and Geneval (Figure 8).
- **Noise-Aware Policy Weighting:** Applying noise-aware reweighting on top of trajectory branching further boosts performance, particularly on Geneval (a 10% improvement over Flow-GRPO).
- **Seed Group Strategy:** The seed group strategy yields additional performance gains, especially in PickScore (Figure 8, right two plots) and further validates its role in isolating exploration effects.
- A larger number of branches generally yields better results in later training stages (Figure 8, Left).

#### 4.5. Qualitative Improvements

- Qualitative comparisons show that TempFlow-GRPO generates images with noticeably finer details, superior photorealism, and fewer visual artifacts or mistakes compared to FLUX.1-dev and Flow-GRPO (Prompt) (Figure 1, Figure 9, Figures 15-19).
- The approach demonstrates superior capability in preserving complex structures and realistic textures, and better adherence to complex prompts.

#### 4.6. Training Stability and KL Divergence

- TempFlow-GRPO maintains lower and more stable KL divergence values throughout the training process compared to Flow-GRPO, indicating better preservation of the original model's distribution while effectively aligning with preferences (Figure 7, Figure 10, Figure 14). This suggests improved training dynamics.

#### 4.7. Robustness and Generalizability

- The method's effectiveness is validated across different base models (SD3.5-M, FLUX.1-dev), reward models (PickScore, HPSv2, HPSv3, Geneval), and resolutions (512x512, 1024x1024).
- It also demonstrates strong capability in **multi-reward optimization**, achieving substantial improvement on HPSv3 while maintaining gains on PickScore (Table 2).
- Comparison with DanceGRPO also shows TempFlow-GRPO achieving higher scores and faster convergence (2x speedup) without specialized hyperparameter tuning (Figure 11, Left).

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that TempFlow-GRPO successfully addresses the temporal uniformity limitation in existing GRPO methods, leading to a more efficient, stable, and performant RL framework for high-quality text-to-image generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TempFlow-GRPO framework represents a significant step forward in the field of text-to-image generation, particularly in the realm of human preference alignment with flow matching models. Its innovations address fundamental limitations of previous reinforcement learning approaches, leading to several important implications and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. More Efficient and Effective RLHF for Generative Models

The core contribution of TempFlow-GRPO lies in its principled approach to incorporating temporal dynamics into policy optimization. By enabling precise credit assignment (trajectory branching) and adapting optimization intensity based on exploration potential (noise-aware weighting), it makes the RLHF process dramatically more efficient. This efficiency means:
 - **Faster Convergence:** Models can achieve high-quality alignment with significantly fewer training steps and GPU hours, reducing computational costs and time-to-market for new models.
 - **Superior Final Performance:** The method not only converges faster but also achieves a higher performance ceiling, leading to text-to-image models that generate images of objectively better quality, higher photorealism, and better adherence to complex compositional prompts.
 - **Improved User Experience:** Ultimately, more finely aligned models translate to a better user experience for text-to-image platforms, as generated content will more consistently match user expectations and preferences.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Gap in Process Reward Learning

The trajectory branching mechanism offers an elegant solution to the challenging problem of obtaining process rewards for intermediate, semantically ambiguous states in generative models. By circumventing the need to train specialized process reward models—which are notoriously difficult and expensive—TempFlow-GRPO simplifies the RLHF pipeline while still providing fine-grained feedback. This approach could inspire similar strategies in other multi-step generative processes where intermediate states are hard to evaluate.

#### 5.3. Advancing the Understanding of Generative Model Dynamics

The theoretical justification for noise-aware weighting, particularly the analysis of the "scale term" in the policy gradient, provides valuable insight into the inherent dynamics of flow matching models. It highlights how the intrinsic noise levels dictate exploration potential and how uniform optimization can be suboptimal. This deeper understanding could inform future architectural designs or training strategies for continuous generative models.

#### 5.4. Broader Applicability and Robustness

The method's demonstrated effectiveness across different base models (SD3.5-M, FLUX.1-dev), diverse reward models (PickScore, HPSv3, Geneval), varying resolutions, and even in multi-reward optimization scenarios underscores its robustness and generalizability. This suggests that TempFlow-GRPO could be a foundational improvement for online RL applied to flow-based generative models, with wide applicability in various T2I applications and potentially other generative tasks beyond images.

#### 5.5. Impact on Industry and Commercial Applications

For industry players like Tencent (WeChat Vision), achieving highly aligned and efficient generative models is crucial. Faster training, better quality, and more consistent outputs directly translate to competitive advantages in products like content creation tools, personalized visual assistants, or advanced image editing features within platforms like WeChat. The ability to quickly adapt models to specific user preferences or evolving trends without extensive retraining is highly valuable.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as noted by authors):**
The authors acknowledge that their current work primarily focuses on algorithmic innovations rather than reward model enhancements. Future work will explore incorporating multi-modal rewards from more powerful foundation models. This suggests that while TempFlow-GRPO significantly optimizes *how* RL is applied, there's still room to improve the *quality and comprehensiveness* of the reward signals themselves, potentially leading to even more sophisticated alignment capabilities.

In conclusion, TempFlow-GRPO makes a substantial contribution by introducing a principled and highly effective method for bringing temporal awareness to reinforcement learning for flow-based generative models. Its innovations are likely to be influential in the ongoing quest to align AI-generated content with complex human preferences, accelerating the development of more capable and user-centric generative AI systems.
