# RGBSQGrasp: Inferring Local Superquadric Primitives from Single RGB Image for Graspability-Aware Bin Picking

> [!summary] Summary
> RGB-SQGrasp enables robust robotic bin picking by inferring local superquadric primitives from a single monocular RGB image, overcoming limitations of depth sensors and partial views. The framework achieves a 92% grasp success rate on a UR5e manipulator in cluttered, real-world environments.

## Problem

- Robotic bin picking in cluttered environments faces challenges from occlusions, limited camera views, and the need for complete object models.
- Existing grasping methods often rely on high-quality depth sensors, which are prone to noise and artifacts with reflective or translucent objects, or lack generalization to novel objects.
- Traditional superquadric fitting techniques typically require comprehensive point clouds, making them unsuitable for partial observations from single camera views in unstructured settings.

## Method

- ==The RGB-SQGrasp== framework employs a superquadric fitting network trained on a diverse, cross-platform synthetic dataset to infer geometric primitives from partial point clouds.
- It leverages pre-trained vision foundation models (Depth Pro for metric depth, SAM 2 for segmentation) to generate robust instance-aware partial point clouds from a single monocular RGB image, circumventing depth sensor dependencies.
- Grasp poses are sampled based on the inferred superquadric primitives, then refined by collision checking and a novel quality score that accounts for local fitting accuracy.

## Results

- The RGB-SQGrasp framework achieved a **92%** grasp success rate on a UR5e manipulator over 189 picks in real-world bin-picking tasks across eight diverse, cluttered scenes.
- It significantly outperformed baseline methods (PS-CNN and MMPS), which achieved **79.7%** and **80.5%** success rates respectively, demonstrating higher accuracy in superquadric fitting and improved robustness, particularly with reflective or translucent objects.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of pre-processing, post-processing (ICP alignment), and the combined local and global feature encoders to the framework's overall high performance and fitting accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Superquadric primitives offer a compact and interpretable shape representation that enables graspability-aware reasoning, which is crucial for synthesizing stable robot grasps.
> - Strategic integration of vision foundation models with a monocular RGB camera can effectively generate robust depth maps and instance segmentations, mitigating sim-to-real gaps and real-world depth sensor limitations.
> - A hybrid neural network architecture combining local (DGCNN) and global (PointNet-adapted) feature extraction is highly effective for accurately inferring superquadric parameters from partial point clouds.
