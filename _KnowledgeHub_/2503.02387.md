# RGBSQGrasp: Inferring Local Superquadric Primitives from Single RGB Image for Graspability-Aware Bin Picking

> [!summary] Summary
> RGB-SQGrasp enables robust robotic bin picking by inferring local superquadric primitives from a single monocular RGB image, overcoming limitations of depth sensors and partial views. The framework achieves a 92% grasp success rate on a UR5e manipulator in cluttered, real-world environments.

## Problem

- Robotic bin picking in cluttered environments faces challenges from occlusions, limited camera views, and the need for complete object models.
- Existing grasping methods often rely on high-quality depth sensors, which are prone to noise and artifacts with reflective or translucent objects, or lack generalization to novel objects.
- Traditional superquadric fitting techniques typically require comprehensive point clouds, making them unsuitable for partial observations from single camera views in unstructured settings.

## Method

- ==The RGB-SQGrasp== framework employs a superquadric fitting network trained on a diverse, cross-platform synthetic dataset to infer geometric primitives from partial point clouds.
- It leverages pre-trained vision foundation models (Depth Pro for metric depth, SAM 2 for segmentation) to generate robust instance-aware partial point clouds from a single monocular RGB image, circumventing depth sensor dependencies.
- Grasp poses are sampled based on the inferred superquadric primitives, then refined by collision checking and a novel quality score that accounts for local fitting accuracy.

## Results

- The RGB-SQGrasp framework achieved a **92%** grasp success rate on a UR5e manipulator over 189 picks in real-world bin-picking tasks across eight diverse, cluttered scenes.
- It significantly outperformed baseline methods (PS-CNN and MMPS), which achieved **79.7%** and **80.5%** success rates respectively, demonstrating higher accuracy in superquadric fitting and improved robustness, particularly with reflective or translucent objects.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of pre-processing, post-processing (ICP alignment), and the combined local and global feature encoders to the framework's overall high performance and fitting accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Superquadric primitives offer a compact and interpretable shape representation that enables graspability-aware reasoning, which is crucial for synthesizing stable robot grasps.
> - Strategic integration of vision foundation models with a monocular RGB camera can effectively generate robust depth maps and instance segmentations, mitigating sim-to-real gaps and real-world depth sensor limitations.
> - A hybrid neural network architecture combining local (DGCNN) and global (PointNet-adapted) feature extraction is highly effective for accurately inferring superquadric parameters from partial point clouds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic bin picking is a critical task in various industrial applications, including warehousing and manufacturing. However, environments characterized by randomly placed, overlapping, or entangled objects pose significant challenges for robotic manipulation. These challenges stem from limited camera views that hinder complete object model reconstruction and tight spatial constraints with occlusions leading to recognition and grasping failures. Addressing these issues often requires techniques that can infer grasping actions from partial or incomplete data, such as partial point clouds obtained from limited perspectives.

Classical approaches to robotic grasping are categorized into analytical and data-driven methods. Analytical methods rely on complete physical models to perform force-closure or form-closure analysis, offering intrinsic descriptiveness and grasp stability. However, their dependence on often-unavailable object models restricts their generalization to novel objects. Data-driven methods, conversely, generate grasp hypotheses by training models on diverse datasets. While these approaches improve generalization, the acquisition of high-quality datasets for real-world scenarios is challenging, and reliance on synthetic data often introduces a significant sim-to-real gap.

Shape primitives, originating from analytical approaches, offer a way to represent objects by basic geometric forms. Minimum Volume Bounding Boxes (MVBBs) and shape primitive templates have been used, but these often lack the expressiveness for complex objects or rely on robust depth sensing. Superquadric (SQ) primitives have gained attention due to their compact parametrization and ability to represent a wide range of shapes (cubes, cylinders, spheres, ellipsoids) within a continuous parameter space. This balance between computational efficiency and expressiveness makes them suitable for data-driven methods and for understanding object properties relevant to grasping.

Previous work on superquadric fitting typically frames it as an optimization problem requiring nearly complete point clouds, which is impractical in bin-picking settings with limited views. While depth images can provide richer geometric information for primitive recognition, methods relying on them are susceptible to noise and discrepancies from real-world sensors, leading to performance degradation and reliance on expensive hardware. Some approaches have utilized multi-view data or object-specific prior knowledge (e.g., symmetry) to recover shapes or define grasps, but these are often not practical for unstructured bin-picking environments.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing methods. It aims to leverage the benefits of superquadric primitives for graspability assessment while overcoming the challenges of limited sensor data and sim-to-real transfer, specifically by using a single monocular RGB camera and incorporating vision foundation models to bridge the gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a grasping framework, named RGB-SQGrasp, that can infer robust grasp poses for bin picking from a single monocular RGB camera. This objective is driven by several motivations related to the limitations of existing robotic grasping systems in cluttered industrial environments:

- **Eliminating Depth Sensor Dependency:** Traditional shape primitive recognition and grasp planning methods often depend on high-quality depth sensors. These sensors can introduce noise and artifacts, particularly along object edges and on reflective or translucent surfaces, leading to inconsistent and degraded performance in real-world scenarios. The paper aims to circumvent this dependency by inferring geometric information solely from a monocular RGB input, thereby reducing hardware costs and improving robustness to challenging surface properties.
- **Addressing Limited Camera Views and Occlusion:** In bin-picking environments, objects are frequently occluded or partially visible due to clutter. This makes it difficult for traditional methods to reconstruct complete object models or accurately fit shape primitives, which often require comprehensive point clouds. The framework is motivated to infer superquadric representations from *locally visible geometry*, enabling effective grasping even with incomplete observations without relying on multi-camera setups or prior object knowledge.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Many data-driven grasping approaches rely on synthetic datasets for training, which can lead to inconsistencies when deployed on real robots due to differences in rendering, sensor noise, and environmental conditions. The paper aims to mitigate this sim-to-real gap by developing a cross-platform dataset generation pipeline that introduces variations in rendering effects and by integrating state-of-the-art vision foundation models for robust scene understanding, which can enhance generalization from synthetic data to real-world deployment.
- **Leveraging Superquadrics for Graspability-Aware Reasoning:** Superquadrics provide a compact, interpretable shape representation that captures physical properties essential for understanding an object's graspability. The motivation is to fully exploit these advantages by developing a data-driven superquadric fitting network that can accurately extract superquadric primitives from partial point clouds, thus enabling geometric reasoning for efficient grasp synthesis and evaluation.
- **Improving Grasp Success Rates in Cluttered Environments:** The ultimate goal is to enhance the reliability and success rate of robotic bin picking in complex, unstructured environments. By integrating a robust superquadric inference mechanism with an intelligent grasp pose sampling strategy that accounts for collision avoidance and fitting quality, the framework seeks to achieve higher success rates compared to existing methods that struggle with occlusions and sensor limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RGB-SQGrasp framework operates through a multi-phase process designed for bin-picking tasks, from data generation to real-world deployment.

#### 3.1. Phase 1: Dataset Generation

The initial phase focuses on creating a diverse synthetic dataset to train the superquadric fitting network. Building upon MetaGraspNetV2, a large-scale dataset, a primitive dataset is constructed using cross-platform simulators (Isaac Sim, MuJoCo, PyBullet). For each object in a simulated scene, partial point clouds (PCDs) are captured from six varying camera viewpoints (one top-down, five slightly altered perspectives). Each PCD is then segmented to isolate individual objects, and ground truth superquadric (SQ) parameters are sampled from the object's model, forming PCD-SQ pairs. The use of multiple simulators introduces variations in rendering, point cloud density, sparsity, and noise, which helps reduce the sim-to-real gap. Data augmentation techniques, including Gaussian noise addition (standard deviation: 0.001–0.005), scaling (0.5–2.0 along each axis), and random translation within the bin, are applied to enhance generalization. The dataset comprises 36,000 PCD-SQ ground truth pairs for training.

#### 3.2. Phase 2: Superquadrics Fitting Network

This phase details the architecture and training of a neural network designed to infer superquadric parameters from partial point clouds.
- **Input Preparation:** Input point clouds undergo filtering for outlier noise removal and are then downsampled to 2000 points using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS). Ground truth is generated by uniformly sampling 2000 points from the superquadric surface corresponding to the ground truth SQ parameters.
- **Network Architecture:** The network integrates both local and global feature extraction.
 - **Local Feature Extractor:** Utilizes a DGCNN architecture, processing point clouds through four EdgeConv layers followed by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP).
 - **Global Feature Extractor:** Adapted from PointNet, it focuses on capturing the object's overall geometry, particularly for scale prediction.
 - Both extractors produce 1024-dimensional permutation-invariant feature vectors, which are then passed through an MLP with layers of dimensions (512, 256) and leaky ReLU activations.
 - **Prediction Heads:** The network employs four heads: a shape head (outputs ε1, ε2), a scale head (outputs α1, α2, α3), a rotation head (outputs a rotation matrix R), and a translation head (outputs a translation vector t). For translation and rotation, a weighted ensemble of outputs from both local and global branches refines predictions.
- **Loss Function:** The training loss compares the point cloud sampled from the predicted superquadric surface with the ground truth point cloud using Chamfer Distance (CD) loss. This permutation-invariant loss ensures alignment of predicted and ground truth shapes.

#### 3.3. Phase 3: SQ-guided Scene Understanding

This phase describes the inference process during real-robot deployment, where geometric information is extracted from a monocular RGB camera input.
- **RGB Input and Foundation Models:** A Microsoft Azure Kinect RGB camera serves as the monocular sensor. To overcome issues with noise and artifacts common in real depth sensor data and RGB variations, the framework leverages pre-trained vision foundation models: Depth Pro for metric depth estimation and Segment Anything 2 (SAM 2) for depth-based segmentation. This approach aims to produce depth maps that resemble real depth sensor outputs while benefiting from the robustness and generalization of foundation models.
- **Instance-Aware Partial Point Cloud Generation:** The metric depth map generated by Depth Pro is projected onto the segmentation map produced by SAM 2, resulting in instance-aware partial point clouds (PCDs) for individual objects.
- **Pre-processing Stage:** Each instance PCD is preprocessed by downsampling to 2000 points using FPS and applying RANSAC for outlier removal. The preprocessed PCDs are then fed into the trained superquadric fitting network to obtain SQ parameters.
- **Post-processing Stage:** This stage refines the SQ fitting through Iterative Closest Point (ICP) alignment, further improving accuracy. The refined SQ primitives are then projected back into the scene for grasp sampling.

#### 3.4. Phase 4: Grasp Pose Sampling

This final phase involves generating and selecting grasp poses based on the inferred superquadric primitives.
- **Grasp Candidate Generation:** For each superquadric primitive, a family of grasp pose candidates is sampled. This strategy, based on previous work, utilizes the SQ shape (ε1, ε2) to define base grasp types and scale (α1, α2, α3) to define the number of possible grasps along each axis. It involves sampling antipodal grasp poses and moving the gripper along principal axes, leveraging the superquadric's symmetry.
- **Grasp Selection Rules:** Generated candidates are filtered and prioritized:
 1. **Collision Checking:** Poses are checked against the bin and other objects using MoveIt!'s Planning Scene module.
 2. **SQ Quality Score:** To account for local inaccuracies when fitting complex shapes with a single superquadric, a quality score (Ci) is computed for each point on the predicted SQ surface based on its distance to the nearest neighbor in the partial object point cloud. The SQ surface is divided into N regions, and an average score (¯Ck) is computed for each region. Only grasps from regions where ¯Ck exceeds a tunable hyperparameter σ are considered high-quality.
 3. **Grasp Prioritization:** Within high-quality regions, top-down grasps are prioritized based on the dot product of the gripper's approach direction and the positive z-axis. If multiple grasps share similar approach directions, the one closest to the superquadric's center of mass (COM) is selected for stability.
- **Execution:** The selected grasp poses are then executed by the UR5e manipulator in a sequential process until the bin is cleared or a maximum number of grasps is reached.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the RGB-SQGrasp framework through both simulation and real-robot experiments.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation:** PyBullet was used for initial proof-of-concept, with thirty objects from YCB and GraspNet-1B datasets. Four bin-picking scenes were generated by freely dropping objects.
- **Real-Robot:** Experiments used a UR5e manipulator with a Robotiq 2F85 gripper and a Microsoft Azure Kinect RGB camera. Eight randomly designed scenes of varying complexity were tested, using sixteen distinct objects (four seen, twelve unseen) from supermarket settings. Three repetitions were conducted per scene.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Superquadric fitting accuracy was measured by mean Chamfer Distance (mCD), mean Rotational Error (mRE), and mean Translational Error (mTE). Grasp success rate (GSR) was defined as the ratio of successfully picked, transported, and placed objects to the total objects.
- **Baselines:** RGB-SQGrasp was compared against PS-CNN, which uses predefined shape templates and ICP with bidirectional image filtering, and MMPS, a multi-modal primitive shape grasping method using both RGB and depth.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiment Results (Table I)

RGB-SQGrasp demonstrated superior performance compared to the baseline methods across eight diverse scenes.
- **Overall Grasp Success Rate:** RGB-SQGrasp achieved an average GSR of 92%. This significantly outperformed PS-CNN (79.7%) and MMPS (80.5%).
- **Fitting Accuracy:** RGB-SQGrasp consistently exhibited lower mRE, mTE, and mCD values, indicating higher accuracy in superquadric fitting and pose estimation. For example, in Scene 1, RGB-SQGrasp recorded mRE 5.39, mTE 1.47, mCD 13.62, while PS-CNN had mRE 12.77, mTE 4.58, mCD 28.86, and MMPS had mRE 11.24, mTE 3.14, mCD 17.85. Similar trends were observed across other scenes.
- **Robustness to Challenging Objects:** The paper observed that in scenes containing reflective or translucent objects (e.g., glass bottles), PS-CNN, which relies heavily on raw depth data, experienced a notable performance drop due to corrupted depth capture. MMPS, incorporating RGB, partially mitigated this but still struggled with depth inconsistencies. RGB-SQGrasp, by leveraging foundation models for depth estimation, maintained robust primitive recognition and higher grasp success rates in these challenging scenarios, highlighting its effectiveness with various object materials.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table II)

Ablation studies were conducted on object-specific tasks (seen and unseen objects) to quantify the contribution of individual framework components.
- **Full Model Performance:** The full RGB-SQGrasp model demonstrated the best performance across all metrics for both seen and unseen objects (seen: mRE 0.42, mTE 0.81, mCD 4.43, GSR 100%; unseen: mRE 1.21, mTE 2.15, mCD 8.54, GSR 94.4%).
- **Importance of Pre-processing:** Removing pre-processing ("Ours w/o Pre") resulted in a decrease in performance (e.g., unseen objects: mRE 3.12, mTE 3.51, mCD 16.73, GSR 88.9%), indicating the value of outlier removal and downsampling for input point clouds.
- **Criticality of Post-processing:** Removing post-processing refinement ("Ours w/o PP") led to a substantial degradation, particularly in fitting accuracy and grasp success (e.g., unseen objects: mRE 9.35, mTE 5.31, mCD 37.63, GSR 80.6%). This suggests that ICP alignment is crucial for aligning the estimated superquadric with the partial point cloud.
- **Contribution of Feature Encoders:** Removing either the global encoder ("Ours w/o GE") or the local encoder ("Ours w/o LE") negatively impacted performance. The paper observed that global features are effective for overall geometric scale, while local features capture fine-grained details. Their combined use was noted as providing superior results, validating the hybrid feature extraction approach.

These results indicate that RGB-SQGrasp successfully leverages superquadric primitives and foundation models to achieve robust grasping in complex bin-picking scenarios, outperforming depth-reliant methods and confirming the importance of its integrated components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RGB-SQGrasp framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts in the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Reduced Hardware Dependency:** By enabling robust scene understanding and grasp synthesis from a single monocular RGB image, the framework eliminates the need for expensive and often unreliable depth sensors. This has the potential to significantly lower the hardware cost and complexity of robotic bin-picking systems, making them more accessible and deployable in a wider range of industrial settings.
- **Enhanced Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The integration of vision foundation models (Depth Pro for metric depth estimation and SAM 2 for segmentation) addresses the long-standing challenge of sim-to-real gap and inconsistencies arising from real-world depth sensor noise, especially with reflective or translucent objects. This approach leads to more reliable primitive recognition and higher grasp success rates in cluttered and optically difficult scenes, which are common in manufacturing and warehousing.
- **Generalization to Novel Objects without Prior Knowledge:** The framework's ability to infer superquadric representations from locally visible geometry without requiring prior object knowledge (e.g., CAD models) or multi-camera views significantly enhances its generalization capabilities. This is critical for industrial applications where robots encounter a diverse and often unknown set of objects.
- **Graspability-Aware Geometric Reasoning:** Leveraging superquadric primitives provides a compact, interpretable representation that inherently captures physical and graspability understanding. This geometric reasoning allows for efficient grasp synthesis and evaluation, contributing to more stable and successful grasps compared to methods relying on less expressive shape approximations or purely data-driven black-box approaches.
- **High Performance in Bin Picking:** Achieving a 92% grasp success rate over 189 picks in varying occlusion scenes, outperforming baseline methods, demonstrates the practical effectiveness of RGB-SQGrasp. This high success rate indicates its potential for improving automation efficiency and reliability in bin-picking operations, which are often bottlenecks in supply chains and manufacturing.

The framework's methodology, particularly the use of cross-platform synthetic data generation and the strategic incorporation of foundation models, offers a path forward for developing robust perception systems for robotics that are less susceptible to real-world sensor variability. Future extensions of this work could involve adapting the graspability analysis to other end-effectors (e.g., suction cups, multi-fingered hands), further broadening its applicability and impact on diverse manipulation tasks.
