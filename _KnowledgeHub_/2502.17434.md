# V-HOP: Visuo-Haptic 6D Object Pose Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> V-HOP presents a visuo-haptic 6D object pose tracking system that adaptively fuses visual and haptic data for robust and generalizable performance. The framework, developed at Brown University, achieves real-time tracking across diverse grippers, sensor types, and unseen objects, enhancing robotic manipulation capabilities in occluded and contact-rich environments.

## Problem

- Visual-only 6D object pose tracking methods struggle with accuracy and stability in scenarios involving heavy occlusion, rapid object movement, or in-hand manipulation.
- Existing visuo-tactile approaches often lack generalization across different robotic gripper embodiments, tactile sensor types, and unseen objects, hindering their real-world applicability.
- Previous visuo-tactile systems face limitations in computational efficiency, often being too slow for real-time robotic control, and encounter difficulties with sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- **V-HOP** introduces a ==unified haptic representation== that converts diverse tactile sensor data (taxel-based and vision-based) and gripper kinesthetics into a coherent point cloud, suitable for various gripper designs.
- A ==transformer-based== network adaptively fuses visual features from a pretrained foundation model (FoundationPose) with haptic embeddings from a ==PointNet++== encoder, allowing dynamic weighting of modalities based on contextual reliability.
- The system is trained end-to-end on a large, multi-embodied synthetic dataset generated using NVIDIA Isaac Sim, enabling robust sim-to-real transfer without real-world data collection.

## Results

- V-HOP consistently surpassed FoundationPose by **4%** in ADD and **5%** in ADD-S metrics on a multi-embodied synthetic dataset, demonstrating strong generalization to novel objects and grippers.
- The system achieved a **32%** lower ADD-S error and was approximately **10 times** faster (**32 FPS**) than prior optimization-based visuo-tactile methods like NeuralFeels, while generalizing to a novel vision-based tactile sensor (DIGIT).
- In real-world experiments, V-HOP enabled robotic manipulation tasks such as bimanual handovers with an **80%** success rate (compared to **40%** for FoundationPose) and improved success in can-in-mug insertion tasks, even under heavy occlusion and human perturbation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified haptic representation that combines tactile readings with gripper kinematics into a point cloud significantly improves cross-embodiment and domain generalization for visuo-haptic tracking.
> - Adaptive fusion of visual and haptic modalities via Transformer encoders allows the system to intelligently prioritize sensory input, relying more on haptics during occlusion and contact, and vision otherwise, mimicking human multisensory integration.
> - Leveraging powerful visual priors from frozen pretrained visual foundation models, combined with specialized haptic processing, creates a robust and efficient framework for precise 6D pose estimation in complex scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate 6D object pose tracking is a cornerstone capability for advanced robotic manipulation, enabling dexterous and contact-rich interactions. Historically, 6D object pose estimation has been dominated by visual-only approaches, which have seen significant advancements with large-scale datasets and foundation models like FoundationPose. However, these methods inherently struggle in scenarios characterized by severe occlusion, rapid object dynamics, or in-hand manipulation, where visual information alone becomes insufficient or unreliable.

Humans, in contrast, master object manipulation by seamlessly integrating visual and haptic (touch) information. This multisensory integration allows for robust object perception, where vision provides global context and initial hypotheses, while haptics refines precise grasps and tracks objects during intimate contact or occlusion. This human capability has long inspired roboticists to develop visuo-haptic systems.

Prior research in visuo-tactile object pose estimation has explored various data-driven approaches, combining visual input with tactile feedback from sensors. While these efforts have shown promise in controlled environments or synthetic datasets, they face critical limitations that hinder their real-world applicability and generalization:
1. **Cross-embodiment Generalization:** Many existing methods are highly specialized, overfitting to specific gripper designs or tactile sensor layouts, making them difficult to deploy across diverse robotic platforms.
2. **Domain Generalization and Sim-to-Real Gap:** Compared to vision-only approaches, visuo-tactile methods often exhibit poorer generalization, struggling with novel objects, sensor types, or differences between simulated and real environments, often due to insufficient data diversity.
3. **Temporal Coherence:** Many approaches estimate object pose on a per-frame basis, leading to less coherent tracking over sequences, which is problematic for dynamic manipulation tasks.
4. **Computational Efficiency:** Optimization-based visuo-tactile methods can be computationally intensive, limiting their use in real-time applications.

V-HOP directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel framework that bridges the gap between powerful visual foundation models and robust haptic feedback. By proposing a unified haptic representation and an adaptive visuo-haptic fusion architecture, V-HOP aims to achieve generalizable, robust, and real-time 6D object pose tracking that can operate effectively across different grippers, sensor types, and objects, including those not seen during training. This work represents a significant step towards enabling robots to perceive and manipulate objects with human-like robustness and versatility, pushing the boundaries of in-hand manipulation and dexterous robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the V-HOP research are to:

1. **Develop a unified haptic representation:** Create a novel representation that can effectively integrate tactile signals and gripper kinesthetics from diverse gripper embodiments and sensor types (both taxel-based and vision-based tactile sensors) into a coherent, generalizable format. This aims to overcome the "cross-embodiment" challenge, a major bottleneck in existing visuo-haptic systems.
2. **Introduce a robust visuo-haptic object pose tracker:** Design a transformer-based model that seamlessly integrates this unified haptic input with visual feedback, leveraging the strong priors from pretrained visual foundation models. The goal is to achieve accurate and temporally coherent 6D object pose tracking, especially in challenging scenarios like high occlusion and dynamic in-hand manipulation.
3. **Achieve strong generalization:** Ensure the proposed framework generalizes effectively to novel objects, novel gripper embodiments, and novel sensor types, significantly improving upon the "domain generalization" limitations of prior visuo-tactile methods.
4. **Demonstrate real-world applicability:** Validate the approach through extensive experiments, including sim-to-real transfer, showcasing its robustness, real-time performance, and ability to enable complex manipulation tasks.

The motivation for V-HOP stems from the observed limitations of current state-of-the-art object pose tracking methods. While vision-only approaches have made remarkable progress, their reliance solely on visual cues makes them fragile in scenarios common in manipulation, such as object occlusion, rapid movement, or intimate contact. Inspired by the human ability to integrate vision and touch for superior object perception, the authors seek to imbue robots with analogous multisensory capabilities. However, previous attempts at visuo-tactile integration in robotics have often failed to achieve broad applicability due to issues with generalization across hardware and environments. V-HOP is motivated by the need to address these persistent challenges, making visuo-haptic perception a practical and impactful solution for real-world robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V-HOP proposes a data-driven approach for generalizable visuo-haptic 6D object pose tracking, built on a novel unified haptic representation and a transformer-based fusion network.

**Problem Definition:** The work tackles model-based visuo-haptic 6D object pose tracking. Given an object's CAD model, a sequence of RGB-D images, an initial 6D pose, the gripper's URDF, joint positions, tactile sensor data (positions and readings), and camera-robot transformation, the model estimates the object's 6D pose at each timestep.

**Unified Haptic Representation:** This is a core contribution, designed to be flexible across multiple gripper embodiments and sensor types.
- **Integrating Tactile and Kinesthetic Feedback:** Unlike prior works that often treat tactile signals in isolation, V-HOP integrates tactile data with the gripper's kinesthetic information (its posture) into a unified point cloud. This provides a more comprehensive understanding of the gripper-object interaction.
- **Handling Diverse Sensor Types:**
 - **Taxel-based Sensors:** For sensors like the Barrett Hand (capacitive taxels), contact points (`S_p,c`) are derived by thresholding raw tactile readings (`S_r`).
 - **Vision-based Sensors:** For sensors like DIGIT (GelSight-type), tactile depth estimation models convert tactile images (`S_I`) into tactile point clouds (`S_p,c`).
- **Gripper Representation:** The gripper's mesh (`M_h`) is generated from its URDF and current joint positions via forward kinematics. This mesh is downsampled into a 9-D gripper point cloud (`P_h`), where each point includes 3D coordinates, 3D surface normals, and a one-hot encoded label (contact, no-contact, or object point). The tactile point cloud (`S_p,c`) is then mapped onto `P_h` to label gripper points that are "in contact" within a certain radius. This combines cutaneous and kinesthetic information.
- **Object Representation:** The object's CAD model is also represented as a 9-D point cloud (`P_Φ`). For tracking, this model point cloud is transformed into a "hypothesized" object point cloud (`P_o`) based on the object's estimated pose from the previous timestep.
- **Gripper-Object Point Cloud Fusion:** The gripper point cloud (`P_h`) and the hypothesized object point cloud (`P_o`) are fused (`P = P_h ∪ P_o`) to create a holistic representation of the gripper-object interaction. This design follows the "render-and-compare" paradigm, where the hypothesized object pose is implicitly compared against observed gripper contact.

**Network Design - Visuo-Haptic Transformer:**
- **Visual Modality:** V-HOP leverages a pretrained visual foundation model, specifically the visual encoder (`f_v`) of FoundationPose, and freezes its weights during training. This extracts high-quality visual embeddings (`Z_v`) from RGB-D observations, benefiting from robust visual priors.
- **Haptic Modality:** A PointNet++ (`f_h`) network is used as the haptic encoder to process the canonicalized gripper-object point cloud (`P`), generating a haptic embedding (`Z_h`). Canonicalization centers the point cloud around the gripper centroid for efficiency.
- **Visuo-Haptic Fusion:** Inspired by optimal integration in human multisensory perception, V-HOP uses self-attention mechanisms within Transformer encoders for adaptive fusion. The visual (`Z_v`) and haptic (`Z_h`) embeddings are fed into Transformer encoders, which are fine-tuned from the pretrained FoundationPose weights. This allows the model to dynamically adjust the weighting between modalities based on context (e.g., relying more on haptics during occlusion, more on vision when not in contact).
- **Output:** The Transformer encoders feed into two disentangled output heads to estimate 3D translation and 3D rotation, consistent with FoundationPose.

**Training Paradigm:**
- **Pose Estimation:** The model is trained to estimate the relative pose difference (`ΔT`) between a noisy ground-truth pose (hypothesis) and the actual observation.
- **Loss Function:** The overall loss (`L`) combines:
 - **`L_T` (L2 Loss):** For the estimated relative 3D rotation and 3D translation.
 - **`L_a` (Attractive Loss):** Encourages activated tactile points to be in contact with the estimated object surface, acting as a single-direction Chamfer distance.
 - **`L_p` (Penetration Loss):** Penalizes interpenetration between the estimated object point cloud and the gripper point cloud, promoting physically plausible poses.

**Multi-embodied Dataset:** To enable cross-embodiment learning and generalization, the authors created a large synthetic dataset (1.55 million images) using NVIDIA Isaac Sim, featuring eight different grippers and thirteen YCB objects. This synthetic dataset, enriched with domain randomization, is used for all training, following the sim-to-real paradigm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

V-HOP demonstrates significant improvements in 6D object pose tracking, especially in generalization and robustness, validated across synthetic and real-world experiments.

- **Performance on Multi-embodied Dataset (Simulated):**
 - V-HOP consistently outperforms state-of-the-art visual tracker FoundationPose (FP) and visuo-tactile estimator ViTa on its custom multi-embodied dataset. On average, V-HOP achieves a **4% improvement in ADD metric and a 5% improvement in ADD-S metric** compared to FoundationPose.
 - **Generalization to Novel Objects and Grippers:** V-HOP shows strong performance even on objects (e.g., pudding box) and grippers (e.g., D’Claw) that were entirely unseen during training, underscoring the effectiveness of its unified representation and training strategy for generalization.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Modality Contribution:** Both visual and haptic modalities are critical. Removing tactile feedback significantly degrades performance, making reasoning about gripper-object contact difficult. Removing visual input leads to an even more substantial drop, highlighting the dominance of vision for overall pose estimation, consistent with human perception studies. The full V-HOP model combining both modalities yields the best results.
 - **Fusion Strategies:** Early fusion (integrating modalities at the feature level within the transformer) significantly outperforms a late fusion strategy (where modalities are processed separately and results are combined). Early fusion leads to a **30.97% higher ADD score and 18.69% higher ADD-S score**, confirming the benefit of deep, adaptive integration.

- **Robustness to Occlusion:** V-HOP consistently outperforms FoundationPose across various occlusion ratios. As occlusion severity increases, the integration of haptic feedback becomes more crucial, enabling V-HOP to maintain stable tracking where visual-only methods struggle or fail.

- **Performance on FeelSight Dataset (Novel Embodiment and Sensor Type):**
 - V-HOP generalizes effectively to the FeelSight dataset, which features a novel embodiment (Allegro hand with DIGIT sensors) and a novel vision-based tactile sensor type, along with a novel object (Rubik’s cube).
 - V-HOP achieves a **32% lower ADD-S error** compared to NeuralFeels, a prior optimization-based visuo-tactile tracker, while maintaining similar ADD-S-0.1d scores.
 - Crucially, V-HOP achieves this superior performance *without* requiring ground-truth segmentation masks, which NeuralFeels utilizes.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** V-HOP is approximately **10 times faster than NeuralFeels (32 FPS vs. 3 FPS)** on an NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU, demonstrating its practicality for real-time robotic applications.

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer Experiments (Barrett Hands on Franka Emika Robots):**
 - **Pose Tracking:** In challenging real-world scenarios involving stable grasping with human-guided dynamic motion and heavy occlusion, FoundationPose often loses track. V-HOP maintains stable object tracking throughout, demonstrating its robustness due to visuo-haptic sensing.
 - **Bimanual Handover Task:** When integrated into motion planning, V-HOP enables more reliable bimanual object handover. It allows the robot to detect grasp failures and reattempt, and to maintain precise tracking for accurate handover. V-HOP achieves an **80% success rate** compared to FoundationPose's 40% (average across objects), indicating a **40% higher average task success rate**. The system also proves robust to human perturbations.
 - **Can-in-Mug Task:** For a single-arm and bimanual insertion task, V-HOP again yields higher success rates (60% vs. 20% for single-arm, 20% vs. 0% for bimanual) compared to FoundationPose, emphasizing its precise pose estimation for contact-rich tasks.

- **Contribution of Each Modality (Grad-CAM Analysis):** Analysis using Grad-CAM reveals that V-HOP dynamically adapts its reliance on visual and haptic inputs. When the gripper is not in contact or occlusion is low, the model predominantly uses visual cues. As contact is established and occlusion becomes severe, the model intelligently shifts its attention towards haptic inputs, confirming the "optimal integration" principle.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

V-HOP represents a significant advancement in 6D object pose tracking for robotic manipulation, addressing several long-standing challenges that have limited the real-world deployment of visuo-haptic systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking Generalization Barriers:** The most impactful contribution is V-HOP's unprecedented generalization capability. It is the first data-driven visuo-haptic approach to demonstrate robust performance across:
 - **Novel gripper embodiments:** Handling different gripper designs without retraining.
 - **Novel objects:** Tracking objects not seen during the training phase.
 - **Novel sensor types:** Adapting seamlessly between taxel-based (Barrett Hand) and vision-based (DIGIT) tactile sensors.
 This level of generalization is critical for developing truly versatile and adaptable robotic systems, drastically reducing the need for costly and time-consuming custom development and data collection for each new robot or task.

2. **Enabling Robust In-Hand and Contact-Rich Manipulation:** By fusing visual and haptic information in an adaptive and coherent manner, V-HOP provides stable and accurate 6D pose tracking even under severe occlusion, high dynamics, and intimate contact – conditions where vision-only systems notoriously fail. This directly empowers robots to perform complex dexterous manipulation tasks, such as bimanual handovers and precise insertions, with higher success rates and greater reliability in unstructured environments.

3. **Mimicking Human Multisensory Integration:** The adaptive fusion mechanism, inspired by human "optimal integration" of vision and touch, is a key architectural highlight. The Grad-CAM analysis empirically confirms that the model dynamically weighs sensory modalities based on contextual reliability. This not only improves robustness but also deepens our understanding of how artificial intelligence can emulate sophisticated biological perception.

4. **Practicality for Real-World Robotics:** V-HOP's real-time performance (32 FPS) makes it suitable for dynamic robotic applications, contrasting with many prior optimization-based visuo-tactile methods that were too slow for online control. Furthermore, its successful sim-to-real transfer, relying solely on synthetic data for training, demonstrates a viable path to deploying advanced perception systems without extensive real-world data collection.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** V-HOP provides a strong foundation for future research in several areas:
 - **Model-Free Visuo-Haptic Tracking:** The paper acknowledges the model-based limitation and suggests integration with simultaneous object reconstruction methods, pushing towards even broader applicability.
 - **Learning Dexterous Skills:** More robust pose tracking directly facilitates the development of advanced robotic skills through imitation learning or reinforcement learning, as accurate object state estimation is crucial for learning complex policies.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Improved object perception could enhance human-robot interaction, allowing robots to understand and adapt to human manipulation actions more effectively.

In conclusion, V-HOP pushes the state-of-the-art in robotic perception by delivering a highly generalizable, robust, and real-time 6D object pose tracking solution. Its ability to effectively integrate and adapt to diverse sensory inputs, across various hardware and objects, marks a significant step towards more autonomous, capable, and human-like robotic manipulation.
