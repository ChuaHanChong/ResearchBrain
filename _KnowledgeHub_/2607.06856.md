# Gen4U: Unifying Video Generation and Understanding via Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind's Gen4U framework repurposes frozen, state-of-the-art video diffusion models to serve as unified encoders for both video generation and understanding, extracting rich semantic and geometric features with a single forward pass. The approach achieves competitive performance across diverse downstream tasks like video classification, captioning, depth, and pose estimation, challenging previous findings on diffusion models' semantic limitations.

## Problem

- Existing visual representation learning paradigms for video struggle to unify fine-grained geometric understanding with broad, high-level semantic reasoning in a single model.
- Current methods often present a trade-off: Vision-Language Models excel at semantics but neglect spatio-temporal details, while pixel-level reconstruction methods preserve geometry but lack broad semantic generalization.
- Prior research indicated that while diffusion models could capture low-level geometry, they might not effectively encode high-level semantic information for understanding tasks.

## Method

- The **Gen4U** framework extracts ==intermediate spatiotemporal activations== from frozen, large-scale ==video diffusion models== (**Veo3**, **Wan 2.2**) during a single forward pass at specific noise levels.
- These extracted features, conditioned on generic text, are then fed into lightweight, task-specific decoders (linear and attention probes, a ==Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT)== readout head) for diverse downstream applications, keeping the generative backbone fixed.
- This approach leverages the implicit understanding acquired by generative models during training to serve as a competitive general-purpose video encoder without fine-tuning the generative core.

## Results

- Gen4U achieved **72.6%** Top-1 accuracy on Something-Something V2 video classification with data augmentation, surpassing several established baselines in this evaluation setup.
- The framework delivered competitive performance on image and video captioning, notably outperforming SigLIP baselines on SSv2 (CIDEr **289.5**), and strong results on monocular depth estimation (**0.075** AbsRel on ScanNet) and camera pose estimation (**1.10** EPE on ScanNet).
- Analysis revealed that optimal feature extraction for semantic tasks occurs around **60%** noise levels, while geometry tasks often benefit from features in mid-to-late network stages and moderate noise levels (**30-60%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video diffusion models exhibit a 'semantic bottleneck' at approximately 60% noise level, where abstract semantic information is optimally represented and strongly aligns with text embeddings.
> - The latent spaces of advanced video diffusion models contain rich hierarchical representations that capture both high-level semantic understanding and fine-grained geometric and motion details.
> - Stronger generative models, like Google DeepMind's Veo3, learn more robust and versatile representations, indicating a correlation between generative capability and the quality of features for perception tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the ongoing challenge of developing a unified, task-agnostic foundation model for video that can reconcile both geometric precision and high-level semantic understanding. Current paradigms in visual representation learning typically exhibit a trade-off:
- **Contrastive methods and large Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as CLIP or SigLip, tend to excel at broad semantic understanding by operating primarily in language space, but often neglect precise spatio-temporal details.
- **Pixel-level reconstruction approaches**, like Masked Autoencoders (MAE) and their video counterparts (e.g., VideoMAE), are effective at preserving local geometry and motion, but may not generalize effectively to diverse high-level semantic tasks.

Previous investigations into diffusion models for representation learning have yielded mixed conclusions. Earlier works, such as those by Vélez et al. (2025) and Zhu et al. (2026), suggested that while diffusion models could capture low-level geometry, they struggled with high-level semantics. This raised questions about their suitability as general-purpose perception encoders. However, other research explored the understanding capabilities of generative models in specific contexts, ranging from zero-shot classification to learning world models in simulated environments, hinting at their potential for implicit world knowledge.

Gen4U directly addresses the perceived limitation regarding semantic understanding in diffusion models. The authors contend that as video diffusion models mature and become more capable of generating physically and temporally consistent videos, they implicitly learn a more comprehensive understanding of the physical world. This work aims to demonstrate that state-of-the-art video diffusion models can, in fact, overcome the previously identified semantic limitations, thereby unifying the generation and understanding paradigms within a single, frozen model. It extends the exploration of how generation can inform understanding beyond low-level geometric tasks to a broader spectrum of visual perception objectives.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized gap in current visual representation learning: the inability of a single model to robustly support both fine-grained geometric understanding and broad, high-level semantic reasoning for video. The authors are inspired by predictive coding theory, which posits that the brain's ability to predict is central to cognition. They hypothesize that for a generative model to produce realistic and consistent video, it must implicitly acquire a deep understanding of underlying physical principles, such as motion dynamics, object permanence, and part-object relationships.

Despite this intuition, earlier studies had cast doubt on the semantic capabilities of diffusion representations when repurposed for understanding tasks. The core objectives of the Gen4U project were therefore multi-faceted:

1. **Latent Space Characterization**: To systematically investigate the internal representations of state-of-the-art video diffusion models (specifically, the proprietary Veo3 and the open-source Wan 2.2). This involved probing their intermediate activations across varying network depths and noise levels to map the hierarchical structure of their latent spaces.
2. **Optimal Feature Extraction**: To identify specific layers and noise levels within these generative models that yield representations most suitable for diverse understanding tasks, encompassing both global semantics and entangled, spatially-distributed fine-grained details.
3. **Framework Development (Gen4U)**: To introduce a framework that efficiently repurposes these identified generative representations as video encoders. The design goal was to enable this repurposing with a single forward pass through the frozen generative backbone, avoiding the computational cost associated with iterative denoising during inference.
4. **Performance Validation**: To empirically demonstrate that these frozen, large-scale video diffusion models, when coupled with lightweight decoders, can function as highly competitive and general-purpose video encoders across a wide array of downstream tasks. These tasks were selected to cover both semantic objectives (e.g., video classification, image/video captioning) and non-semantic objectives (e.g., depth estimation, camera pose estimation).
5. **Unification**: Ultimately, the aim was to show that this approach effectively unifies video generation and understanding paradigms by achieving strong perception performance while fully preserving the generative model's original ability to produce high-quality video, all without fine-tuning the generative backbone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centered on analyzing and repurposing intermediate activations from advanced video diffusion models. The study primarily utilized a proprietary model, Veo3 (Google DeepMind, 2025), and an open-weight model, Wan 2.2-T2V-A14B (Wan et al., 2025). These models are either Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) or flow-matching models, both operating by learning to reverse a corruption process in a compressed latent space.

**Feature Extraction and Gen4U Framework:**
During inference, the Gen4U framework operates by performing a single forward pass through the frozen diffusion model. Intermediate spatiotemporal activations, denoted as h(l)t, are extracted from the l-th layer at a chosen noise level t. To handle the inherent stochasticity of diffusion, a fixed random seed was used for all experiments. The models were conditioned on a generic text embedding (e.g., "A video of a scene") to prevent any leakage of ground-truth task information. These extracted features were then fed into lightweight, task-specific decoders for various downstream applications, with the generative backbone remaining entirely frozen.

**Latent Space Analysis:**
To understand the structure of the latent embeddings, the authors conducted several analyses:
- **PCA Visualization**: Principal Component Analysis was applied to activations extracted from Veo3 at different depths and noise levels. The top three principal components were mapped to RGB channels for qualitative assessment of feature evolution.
- **Mutual k-Nearest Neighbors (MkNN) Alignment**: This zero-shot metric, adapted from previous work, quantitatively assessed the similarity of neighborhood structures between diffusion features and established reference encoders. Video-level embeddings were obtained by global average pooling of spatiotemporal activations from the diffusion model. Reference encoders included the Gemma-2-9b-it text encoder for semantic alignment, and DINOv2 (image) and VideoMAEv2 (video) for visual alignment. The MkNN score (k=10, N=1024 videos on VATEX dataset) measured the average overlap of k-nearest neighbors between the two embedding spaces. This analysis involved sweeping across all intermediate layers of both the diffusion model and reference encoders to find the pair yielding maximal alignment.
- **Linear and Attention Probes**: For a more direct evaluation of representation quality for specific tasks, linear and attention-based decoders were trained on top of the frozen diffusion features.
 - **Linear Probe**: Applied global spatiotemporal pooling followed by a linear projection.
 - **Attention Probe**: Employed a cross-attention head with task-specific queries to selectively aggregate information from the features. These probes were used for video classification on the Something-Something V2 (SSv2) dataset.
- **Preview Decoding (Appendix C)**: Linear and attention decoders were trained to reconstruct the original RGB video frames from activations at different stages, using mean L2 error as the loss. This provided qualitative insights into the information content at various depths and noise levels.

**Downstream Task Evaluations:**
The Gen4U framework, primarily utilizing Veo3 features due to its higher generative capability, was evaluated across diverse tasks:
- **Semantic Understanding**:
 - **Video Classification**: Evaluated on the SSv2 dataset using one-block attention decoders. Experiments included data augmentation applied directly to the intermediate activations (temporal masking, dropout, label smoothing).
 - **Image and Video Captioning**: Performed on MS-COCO, SSv2, and VATEX datasets. A Gemma-2 (2B) text-only Large Language Model (LLM) served as a decoder, connected via a cross-attention adapter that projected visual tokens into learnable query tokens. The adapter and LLM were trained on dataset-specific splits, with LLM weights initially frozen and gradually thawed. Data augmentation involved concatenating features from multiple noise injection levels. An ablation study also investigated the impact of higher input resolutions for COCO.
- **Geometry Understanding**:
 - **Monocular Depth Estimation**: Assessed on the ScanNet dataset. A Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT) readout head was trained on frozen features, using the scale-invariant logarithmic (SiLog) loss. Metrics included Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel) and threshold accuracy (δ1).
 - **Camera Pose Estimation**: Evaluated on ScanNet. A one-block attention decoder predicted the 6DoF relative camera pose (a 12D vector for SE(3) transformation) between frames in a clip. The End-Point-Error (EPE) metric was used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the structure of diffusion model latent spaces and the performance of Gen4U on various downstream tasks.

**Latent Space Analysis:**
- **Feature Evolution**: PCA visualizations indicated that at high noise levels, features encoded simple, low-frequency global shapes. As noise levels decreased, features became more complex, capturing higher-frequency, refined details.
- **Semantic Bottleneck at 60% Noise**: Quantitative MkNN alignment with the Gemma-2-9b-it text encoder consistently peaked at approximately **60% noise level** for both Veo3 and Wan 2.2 (Figure 3). This suggests the emergence of a "semantic bottleneck" at this specific noise level, where abstract semantic information is optimally represented. Crucially, this strong alignment with text embeddings, without explicit text alignment training, contradicts prior findings that diffusion models struggle with high-level semantics.
- **Bimodal Depth Pattern in Veo3**: Veo3 exhibited a distinct bimodal pattern in alignment scores across network depth (Figure 3 left, Figure 4). Alignment peaked in early and late layers, with a dip in the middle layers. This contrasts with Wan 2.2's unimodal pattern, which peaked earlier (around 25% depth). The authors hypothesize that Veo3's bimodal pattern might reflect information routing mechanisms observed in large-scale transformers.
- **Alignment with Visual Encoders**: Veo3's intermediate activations demonstrated strong alignment with both DINOv2 (image model) and VideoMAEv2 (video model) (Figure 4), further supporting their potential as versatile features.
- **Feature Complexity and Probes**: Linear and attention probes for SSv2 video classification (Figure 5) revealed optimal extraction points around **70-80% of model depth** and **30-60% noise level**. Attention probes generally outperformed linear probes, particularly at deeper blocks and lower noise levels. This indicates that at lower noise, semantic information may become spatially scattered and requires more sophisticated pooling mechanisms, like attention, to be effectively decoded, rather than being discarded.
- **Generative Capability Correlation**: Veo3, as a stronger generative model, consistently achieved higher alignment scores than Wan 2.2, suggesting a correlation between generative power and the richness of learned representations.

**Downstream Task Performance (Veo3):**
- **Video Classification (SSv2)**:
 - Gen4U achieved **71.3% Top-1 accuracy** without data augmentation, surpassing several established baselines from MAE, contrastive, and even the diffusion-based V-Walt models (Table 1).
 - With data augmentation (applied to activations), performance further improved to **72.6% Top-1 accuracy**, setting a new state-of-the-art in this evaluation setup, outperforming V-JEPA-H.
- **Image and Video Captioning**:
 - Gen4U excelled on SSv2 (CIDEr 289.5, BLEU@4 45.5), significantly outperforming SigLIP baselines (Table 2).
 - On COCO and VATEX, while producing reasonable captions, its initial performance was lower than SigLIP baselines, which are heavily optimized for vision-language alignment.
 - **Noise Augmentation**: Augmenting training data by concatenating features from multiple noise injection levels (10%, 30%, 60%) significantly improved COCO (CIDEr 69.3) and VATEX (CIDEr 56.7) performance.
 - **High Resolution**: Using higher input resolution for COCO images further boosted CIDEr to 102.0, reducing the gap to specialized discriminative models.
 - **Frozen LLM**: Freezing the Gemma2 LLM during adapter training resulted in only a slight performance decrease on VATEX (CIDEr 40.2), still outperforming VideoPrism.
- **Monocular Depth Estimation (ScanNet)**:
 - Achieved an AbsRel of **0.075** and δ1 accuracy of **0.952** (Figure 6 left). This represents a 10.7% relative improvement over a strong frozen-feature baseline (4DS).
 - The optimal extraction point was identified at approximately **80% depth and 30% noise level**.
- **Camera Pose Estimation (ScanNet)**:
 - Attained an End-Point-Error (EPE) of **1.10** (Figure 6 right), which is comparable to the DINOv2 baseline (1.08 EPE).
 - Performance peaked around **75% depth and 60% noise level**.

Across geometry tasks, the optimal extraction points generally aligned with the "sweet spots" identified through the zero-shot alignment and classification probes (mid-to-late network stages, medium noise levels).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Gen4U: Unifying Video Generation and Understanding via Diffusion" represents a significant contribution to the field of video AI, primarily by challenging and updating existing perceptions of diffusion model capabilities.

**Unification of Paradigms**: The most significant contribution is the successful demonstration of unifying video generation and understanding within a single, frozen, large-scale diffusion model. This addresses a long-standing challenge in visual representation learning and moves closer to the development of truly general-purpose foundation models for video. By repurposing generative representations, Gen4U shows that a model trained exclusively for high-quality video generation can simultaneously serve as a competitive video encoder for diverse perception tasks.

**Rethinking Diffusion Representations**: The findings revise the understanding of features learned by advanced diffusion models. Contrary to earlier reports suggesting limitations in capturing high-level semantics, this research establishes that state-of-the-art video diffusion models do encode rich semantic information, alongside geometry and motion. The observed strong alignment with both text and other visual encoders indicates a broad applicability of these representations.

**Computational Efficiency**: Gen4U's ability to extract high-quality features with a single forward pass through the generative backbone is a practical advantage. This bypasses the iterative denoising process inherent in generative inference, making the model as computationally efficient for understanding tasks as conventional discriminative encoders. This could facilitate broader adoption and deployment in resource-constrained environments or real-time applications.

**Catalyst for Future Research**: The detailed latent space analysis, particularly the identification of a semantic bottleneck at a specific noise level and the bimodal representation pattern in Veo3, provides novel insights into how complex information is processed and organized within these models. These findings can guide future research into the theoretical underpinnings of diffusion representations and potentially lead to more targeted methods for feature extraction and manipulation.

**Practical Implications**: From a practical standpoint, the concept of a single visual model serving both generation and understanding roles could streamline model development and deployment. It may reduce the computational resources and data required to train separate models for these distinct tasks, offering efficiencies for organizations working across both domains. The competitive performance on various tasks, including state-of-the-art results on SSv2 video classification and strong geometry estimation, underscores the immediate utility of the Gen4U framework.

**Limitations**: The primary limitation noted by the authors is the reliance on a proprietary model (Veo3) for a significant portion of the main experiments. While some analyses were replicated with the open-source Wan 2.2, the full reproducibility of the study by the broader research community is partially constrained. However, the systematic methodology and strong empirical evidence contribute valuable insights nonetheless.
