# Video-STAR: Reinforcing Open-Vocabulary Action Recognition with Tools

> [!summary] Summary
> Video-STAR enhances open-vocabulary action recognition by integrating contextual sub-motion decomposition with tool-augmented reinforcement learning, resulting in state-of-the-art performance with significant improvements (e.g., 26.3% absolute on K-400 (HM)) and a 22% reduction in inference time through dynamic tool selection.

## Problem

- Existing Open-Vocabulary Action Recognition (OVAR) methods often overfit static features or exhibit limited generalization, especially for motion-centric tasks and fine-grained distinctions.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently generate cross-modal hallucinations in OVAR due to a mismatch between their text-centric Chain-of-Thought reasoning and dynamic visual input.
- Current MLLMs struggle to differentiate semantically similar actions because they lack the ability to analyze fine-grained, category-specific sub-motion dynamics.

## Method

- The **Video-STAR** framework employs a two-stage inference process where a ==policy network selects tools== from a library of four domain-specific components (e.g., Human Detection, Pose Estimation).
- A novel training data construction method uses multimodal Chain-of-Thought reasoning and tool-augmented decomposition to generate high-fidelity sub-motion breakdowns and action explanations.
- The model is trained with agentic Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a hierarchical reward function.

## Results

- Video-STAR achieved state-of-the-art results on five action recognition benchmarks, with Video-STAR-**7B** demonstrating a **26.3%** absolute improvement in K-400 (HM) accuracy.
- The framework significantly improved generalization to novel action categories, often matching or exceeding base category performance, and reducing the base-to-novel performance gap.
- Dynamic tool selection reduced inference time by approximately **22%** compared to a static pipeline, while maintaining comparable accuracy, proving the efficiency of pruning redundant tool calls.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex actions into fine-grained sub-motion primitives, guided by context, significantly enhances the ability to discriminate between semantically similar actions.
> - Integrating dynamic, tool-augmented reasoning via reinforcement learning provides visually grounded evidence, effectively reducing cross-modal hallucinations inherent in text-centric MLLM approaches.
> - A hierarchical reward mechanism, which explicitly incentivizes correct tool invocation and relevant sub-motion analysis only when the final prediction is accurate, is crucial for robust agentic learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Open-Vocabulary Action Recognition (OVAR), a challenging area that seeks to identify action classes not explicitly seen during model training. Traditional action recognition systems are typically limited by closed vocabularies, making them less adaptable to real-world scenarios where novel actions frequently occur.

Recent advancements in vision-language pre-training, notably through models like CLIP, have enabled initial progress in zero-shot action understanding. However, prior methods, including those based on fine-tuning CLIP (e.g., ActionCLIP, ViFi-CLIP), parameter-efficient strategies (e.g., Adaptformer, ST-Adapter), or prompt engineering (e.g., AIM, VPT), have faced limitations. These limitations include overfitting to static features, restricted capacity, architectural rigidity, and suboptimal cross-architecture generalization, particularly when dealing with motion-centric tasks or distinguishing fine-grained actions. More recent efforts, such as Open-VCLIP and FROSTER, have aimed to improve generalization but are often constrained by static assumptions or rely heavily on CLIP's inherent static generalization.

Concurrently, multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have shown capabilities in visual reasoning and cross-modal understanding, utilizing unified architectures to process visual and textual signals. While these MLLMs have demonstrated performance in tasks like image captioning and video understanding, their application to OVAR still presents challenges. Existing MLLM approaches often rely on text-centric chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, which can lead to misalignments between temporal visual features and discrete textual representations, resulting in cross-modal hallucinations. They also frequently struggle with differentiating semantically similar actions due to a lack of category-specific reasoning.

The paper introduces Video-STAR to address these limitations by integrating contextual sub-motion decomposition with tool-augmented reinforcement learning. This approach aims to transition from text-centric reasoning to visually grounded inference, thereby enhancing fine-grained action discrimination and mitigating cross-modal hallucinations, moving beyond the static or purely text-based reasoning paradigms prevalent in earlier works.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Video-STAR is to enhance open-vocabulary action recognition (OVAR) capabilities, particularly in scenarios involving semantically similar or complex actions, and to reduce instances of cross-modal hallucination in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). The motivation stems from two critical limitations observed in existing MLLMs when applied to OVAR:

1. **Cross-modal Hallucinations due to Text-centric Reasoning:** Current MLLMs often over-rely on text-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for visual understanding. This reliance can lead to a misalignment between the temporal dynamics of visual features and the discrete nature of textual representations. As a result, models may generate incorrect or ungrounded reasoning steps, producing "hallucinations" that do not accurately reflect the visual content of the video. The paper highlights this as a significant barrier to robust visual understanding, where the model's textual priors can override or misinterpret actual visual cues.

2. **Inability to Distinguish Semantically Similar Actions:** In open-vocabulary settings, actions can be complex, involving subtle temporal dynamics, spatial relationships, and nuanced semantic similarities. Existing methods typically treat actions as monolithic entities, making it difficult for models to learn and apply discriminative patterns for closely related actions. For example, distinguishing between "throwing" and "punching" requires fine-grained analysis of body mechanics and object interaction, which current MLLMs often lack, leading to misclassifications. The unpredictability of novel actions in open-vocabulary scenarios further exacerbates this issue, as models cannot learn discriminative patterns directly from predefined categories.

To address these challenges, the research posits that effective OVAR requires two key capabilities:
- **Enabling Fine-grained Action Discrimination through Contextual Sub-motion Decomposition:** This involves breaking down holistic actions into their constituent, discriminative sub-motion primitives. By analyzing individual body-part interactions and their sequential phases (e.g., "leg bending" → "torso jumping" → "arm extending" for "shoot basketball"), the model can develop a more granular understanding of action mechanics, thereby resolving ambiguity in semantically similar actions.
- **Enhancing Cross-modal Interleaving through Multimodal CoT Reasoning with Tool-Augmentation:** This capability guides the model to autonomously invoke domain-specific tools to extract concrete visual information, thereby enriching visual-semantic representation. This approach moves beyond frame-level manipulations to explicitly model motion continuity and hierarchical sub-motion dependencies, ensuring both spatial precision and semantic coherence. The objective is to anchor reasoning to direct visual evidence rather than solely relying on textual priors, which helps to mitigate cross-modal hallucinations.

In summary, the detailed motivation is to overcome the limitations of MLLMs in OVAR by developing a framework that can perform fine-grained action analysis via sub-motion decomposition and reduce hallucinations through dynamic, visually-grounded tool invocation, moving towards a more robust and adaptable action recognition system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Video-STAR is a unified framework that combines contextual sub-motion decomposition with tool-augmented reinforcement learning (RL) for open-vocabulary action recognition. The methodology is structured around a three-stage pipeline: training data construction, agentic supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and agentic reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Tool Library

The task is to identify an action $A$ given a video $V$ and a query $Q$, potentially for unseen action categories. The framework employs a two-stage inference process:

- **Stage 1: Tool Selection:** The model analyzes contextual cues from $V$ and $Q$ to select the most relevant tool from a predefined toolset $T = \{T_p, T_d, T_a, T_v\}$. This stage produces a selected tool call $C \subseteq T$ and intermediate results $R$, guided by a policy network $\pi_\theta$.
- **Stage 2: Result Integration & Prediction:** The tool outputs $R$ are integrated with the original inputs to refine action recognition. For visual tools ($T_p$, $T_d$), extracted features $F$ are concatenated with raw video frames $V$. For semantic reasoning tools ($T_a$, $T_v$), generated explanations $E$ are appended to the original query $Q$. The final action prediction $A$ is then computed based on these enhanced inputs.

The **Tool Library** comprises four domain-specific components:
- **Human Detection ($T_d$):** Utilizes YOLO 11, an anchor-free detector with a hybrid spatial attention mechanism, to identify and localize human subjects in video frames. This helps isolate action-relevant regions from background distractions.
- **Pose Estimation ($T_p$):** Leverages YOLO 11's 17-keypoint skeletonization capability (COCO format) to capture sub-pixel joint positions (e.g., elbows, wrists, knees) for fine-grained motion analysis and structured kinematic representation.
- **Action Explanation ($T_a$):** Employs the Qwen API for online Retrieval-Augmentation (RAG) to provide detailed, category-specific explanations for action labels. It redefines actions through transitional phases (e.g., "stand" is "transit from sit or lay to stand"), offering a deeper semantic understanding.
- **Video Description ($T_v$):** Extracts temporally salient frames (e.g., frames with maximum joint displacement) and uses the Qwen API with RAG to generate concise textual descriptions of actions at specific moments, disambiguating multi-phase action sequences (e.g., "sit-stand-walk").

#### 3.2. Training Data Construction

To overcome the text-centric biases and limitations in distinguishing similar actions, a novel training dataset is constructed through multimodal CoT reasoning and tool-augmented decomposition.

- **Data Collection:** An initial pool of approximately 7,000 video-query pairs from the HMDB-51 dataset is curated. Qwen2.5-VL-72B synthesizes initial CoT reasoning sequences. A rigorous filtering stage, utilizing the closed-source Qwen-VL-Max model as an expert validator, scores and reviews each chain, discarding those with factual errors or logical inconsistencies. This process yields about 5,000 high-fidelity reasoning chains for SFT.

- **Generating Pipeline (Three-Stage Logic Chain):**
 1. **Sub-motion Decomposition:** Breaks down holistic actions into discriminative sub-motion primitives by identifying key body-part interactions (e.g., "shoot ball" into "body bending → leg extending → feet-ball interaction"). This isolates perceptually distinct motion patterns.
 2. **Candidate Selection:** Maps each sub-motion to pre-defined action definitions, generating 2-3 semantically relevant candidate actions (e.g., "body bending" suggests "run," "leg extending" implies "hurdle"). This narrows the search space while maintaining semantic diversity.
 3. **Matching Scoring:** Compares all sub-motions against each candidate’s detailed definition, evaluating spatial precision and description alignment. The highest-scoring candidate is ultimately selected.

- **Data Assessment:** A specialized LLM evaluates the factual accuracy and logical coherence of the constructed data, removing entries with inconsistent reasoning or unverified claims.

#### 3.3. Agentic Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

Before RL, a cold-start phase of SFT is performed. Initial experiments indicated that direct RL optimization led to a decline in tool invocation frequency, likely due to a distributional discrepancy between tool-enhanced visual features and the model's pretraining data.
SFT formalizes each training instance as a tuple $(X, I, S, Y)$, where $X$ is input modality, $I$ is task instructions, $S$ are reasoning steps, and $Y$ is the target output. The objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the reasoning process, enabling the model to generate reasoning chains aligned with task instructions and ground-truth during pre-training.

#### 3.4. Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL)

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** The GRPO algorithm is adopted for policy optimization. For each query, GRPO generates a set of rollout trajectories $\{o_1, \dots, o_G\}$ based on the previous policy. The policy is refined by optimizing an objective function that considers the ratio of new to old policy probabilities, a clipping mechanism, an advantage estimator, and a KL divergence term for stability. The advantage estimator $A_i$ is calculated using normalized rewards from the trajectory group.

- **Reward Design:** The total reward $R(\tau)$ is a hierarchical function integrating four components:
 1. **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):** Evaluates the correctness of the final action classification.
 2. **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** Penalizes unstructured or incomplete reasoning chains.
 3. **Tool-usage Reward ($R_{tool}$):** Activated only when correct answers are produced alongside valid tool invocations, promoting meaningful tool use.
 4. **Sub-motion Reward ($R_{sub}$):** Employs a hierarchical weighting mechanism for semantically salient sub-motions. If $n$ sub-motions are generated, ordered by relevance, the $k$-th ranked candidate receives a weight $w_k = n - k + 1$. If $m$ predicted sub-motions match, $R_{sub} = (\sum_{i=1}^m w_{k_i}) / (\sum_{i=1}^n w_{k_i})$. This prioritizes higher-priority sub-motions.

 The total reward is defined as: $R(\tau) = R_{acc}(\tau) + R_{format}(\tau) + I_{R_{acc}(\tau)>0} \cdot (R_{tool}(\tau) + R_{sub}(\tau))$. The indicator function $I_{R_{acc}(\tau)>0}$ ensures that tool usage and sub-motion prioritization are only rewarded when the final answer is correct, discouraging redundant tool invocations or irrelevant sub-motions for incorrect predictions. This coupling ensures agents invoke tools meaningfully while maintaining coherent reasoning chains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations of Video-STAR were conducted on five standard action recognition benchmarks: UCF-101, HMDB-51, Kinetics-400 (K-400), Kinetics-600 (K-600), and Something-to-Something V2 (SSv2). Top-1 accuracy was reported, and experiments were implemented using Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B models.

#### 4.1. Base-to-Novel Performance

In the base-to-novel setting, Video-STAR was fine-tuned only on the base set of HMDB-51 and evaluated zero-shot on K-400, UCF-101, and SSv2, a more challenging setup compared to other methods fine-tuned on each respective dataset's base set.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Video-STAR consistently achieved new state-of-the-art results across all datasets. For instance, Video-STAR-7B demonstrated a 26.3% absolute improvement in K-400 (HM) and 27.0% in HMDB-51 (HM) over the previous best method.
- **Generalization to Novel Categories:** The performance gap between base and novel sets was significantly reduced by Video-STAR. In K-400 and UCF-101, the accuracy for novel classes sometimes surpassed that of base classes, indicating strong generalization capabilities.
- **Scaling Effects:** Model scaling from 3B to 7B led to substantial absolute increases in harmonic mean (HM) accuracy (e.g., 10.5% on K-400 and 3.2% on SSv2), although performance on HMDB-51 and UCF-101 was already near saturation.
- **MLLM Limitations:** Generic MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-7B showed competitive performance on K-400 but exhibited considerable performance degradation on HMDB-51 (HM 45.6%) and SSv2 (HM 11.6%), unlike Video-STAR, which maintained high performance across all datasets.

#### 4.2. Cross-Dataset Performance

Under the cross-dataset setting, where models are trained on a source dataset and evaluated on a different target dataset, Video-STAR also demonstrated robust performance.
- **Stability and Robustness:** Video-STAR exhibited comparable results on full validation sets and split evaluations, suggesting that tool usage and sub-motion decomposition contribute to stability.
- **Model Efficiency:** The smaller Video-STAR-3B model outperformed previous state-of-the-art methods and even the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed framework.
- **Case Studies:** Comparative case studies showed Video-STAR-3B accurately identified actions like "turn" while Qwen2.5-VL-3B misclassified them as "smile," illustrating the benefit of sub-motion decomposition and tool augmentation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Stages:** SFT-only training significantly improved accuracy over zero-shot, and outperformed RL-only training, highlighting the importance of SFT in providing a structured reasoning foundation before RL optimization. The full SFT-then-RL pipeline yielded the best results.
- **Tool-Usage and Sub-Motion:** Removing tool usage from the framework caused a notable performance degradation (at least 9.1% on all datasets), confirming the role of external tools in resolving cross-modal ambiguities. Removing sub-motion decomposition similarly reduced accuracy (8.2% to 9.4%), underscoring its importance for breaking down complex actions into discriminative primitives. The full model, integrating both, achieved the highest scores.
- **Reward Design:** Ablations on the hierarchical reward function revealed that solely relying on accuracy and format rewards produced the lowest performance. Including sub-motion reward or tool-usage reward individually improved results, with the complete reward function leading to the highest accuracy, validating its efficacy in guiding RL optimization.
- **Number of Generations:** Increasing the number of candidate responses generated during RL training (from 2 to 6) consistently improved performance, with diminishing returns beyond six. A setting of Num=4 was selected for balancing accuracy and efficiency.
- **Data Assessment and Sub-motion Weighting:** Removing the data assessment stage from SFT led to a decrease in accuracy (1.9% to 3.9%), indicating its importance for data reliability. Assigning equal weights to sub-motions (instead of hierarchical weighting) also reduced accuracy (2.5% to 4.8%), confirming the value of prioritizing semantically discriminative primitives.
- **Individual Tool Contributions:** Removing individual tools (Human Detection, Pose Estimation, Action Explanation, Video Description) each resulted in performance drops (1.4% to 6.8%), affirming that all components of the tool library are complementary and essential.
- **CoT Data Generation Model:** Replacing the teacher model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) with more powerful MLLMs (InternVL-2.5-78B or Gemini-1.5-Pro) for CoT data generation further improved Video-STAR's performance (up to 97.5% on UCF), demonstrating the framework's robustness and scalability with the quality of the teacher model.
- **Comparison with Frontier SOTA Models:** Video-STAR-7B significantly outperformed powerful general-purpose frontier models like Qwen3-VL-8B and Gemini-1.5-Pro on specialized OVAR tasks, particularly HMDB-51 (92.1% vs. 73.6% for Gemini-1.5-Pro), suggesting that a task-specific reasoning framework can be more effective than relying solely on larger, general-purpose models for such tasks.

#### 4.4. Efficiency and Robustness

- **Agentic System vs. Static Pipeline:** The dynamic tool-selection policy of Video-STAR achieved a comparable accuracy (96.7% vs. 97.2% on UCF-101) to a static pipeline that invokes all tools, but reduced total inference time by approximately 22% (from 4.10s to 3.18s). This demonstrated efficiency gains by pruning redundant tool calls.
- **Computational Overhead:** Video-STAR-3B achieved a +38.6% absolute accuracy improvement on UCF-101 over its base model (Qwen-2.5VL-3B) with a moderate increase in latency (primarily from tool invocation, 1.43s) and GFLOPs, indicating a favorable performance-cost trade-off.
- **Robustness to Tool Selection:** Swapping default tools (e.g., YOLO 11 with OpenPose, Qwen API with Gemini-1.5-Pro) maintained consistently high performance, albeit with varying latency. This indicated that the underlying agentic logic for orchestrating tools is the primary contribution, rather than reliance on a specific tool stack.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Video-STAR represents a development in open-vocabulary action recognition by integrating contextual sub-motion decomposition with tool-augmented reinforcement learning. Its significance stems from several key contributions and their potential implications:

- **Bridging Text-centric Reasoning and Visually Grounded Inference:** The framework addresses a core limitation of many existing MLLMs, which often suffer from cross-modal hallucinations due to an over-reliance on textual priors. By compelling the model to build its logic from concrete visual evidence extracted by domain-specific tools (e.g., pose estimation keypoints, human detection bounding boxes), Video-STAR anchors its reasoning to the visual world. This approach reduces speculative textual reasoning and enhances the fidelity of visual understanding.

- **Enhanced Fine-grained Action Discrimination:** The sub-motion decomposition mechanism enables the model to disentangle complex, holistic actions into a sequence of discriminative primitives. This capability is particularly important for differentiating semantically similar actions, which has been a persistent challenge in OVAR. By analyzing specific body-part movements and their temporal interactions, Video-STAR can achieve a more granular and accurate classification.

- **Dynamic and Efficient Tool Integration:** The development of a tool-augmented agentic system, coupled with a hierarchical reward mechanism, allows for the dynamic and efficient invocation of external tools. This not only resolves visual-semantic ambiguities but also optimizes tool usage, activating them only when they contribute meaningful insights. The comparison with static pipelines demonstrates that this dynamic approach offers substantial efficiency gains while maintaining high accuracy, making the system more practical for real-world deployment.

- **Empirical Performance:** The consistent state-of-the-art performance across multiple challenging datasets (HMDB-51, UCF-101, SSv2, Kinetics-400, Kinetics-600) validates the effectiveness and robustness of the Video-STAR framework. The significant improvements over existing CLIP-based methods and even powerful general-purpose frontier MLLMs underscore the value of a specialized, task-specific reasoning framework for OVAR.

- **Modularity and Generalizability:** The robustness demonstrated when swapping core tools (e.g., YOLO 11 with OpenPose, Qwen API with Gemini-1.5-Pro) suggests that the underlying agentic logic is modular and can flexibly orchestrate various tools. This indicates generalizability of the approach beyond a specific set of tools.

The potential impact of Video-STAR includes advancements in various real-world applications such as:
- **Surveillance Systems:** Improved ability to identify complex or unusual activities.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** More nuanced understanding of human intentions and actions for better collaboration.
- **Content Analysis Platforms:** Enhanced capacity for categorizing and searching video content based on fine-grained action descriptions.
- **Assistive Technologies and Sports Analytics:** Providing detailed feedback on human movement and performance.

Future work directions include exploring cross-modal generalization to broader video understanding applications and optimizing the computational overhead of tool invocation through techniques like model distillation, quantization, and parallelization for more lightweight and faster inference. The framework also opens avenues for further research into sophisticated reward designs and the integration of even more diverse domain-specific tools.
