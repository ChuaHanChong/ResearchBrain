# Reinforcement Fine-Tuning Naturally Mitigates Forgetting in Continual Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) fundamentally addresses catastrophic forgetting in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) during continual post-training, outperforming Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). The GRPO variant of RFT achieved a forgetting measure of -2.3% and improved multi-discipline understanding by 2.1%, while SFT showed significant performance degradation on old tasks and general capabilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) face catastrophic forgetting when continually learning new tasks, leading to performance drops on previously acquired knowledge.
- Current Continual Post-Training (CPT) research primarily relies on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), neglecting the intrinsic forgetting properties of the underlying learning paradigm itself.
- Sequential fine-tuning with SFT often degrades both task-specific performance and the model's fundamental general-purpose capabilities.

## Method

- The research directly compares Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) against Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as the core learning paradigm for Continual Post-Training (CPT) of MLLMs.
- A comprehensive evaluation framework was established using a sequence of seven diverse multimodal tasks and benchmarks for general capabilities like MMMU and MMLU-Pro.
- Rollout-based Instance Filtering for RFT (RIF-RFT) was introduced to enhance the efficiency and stability of RFT in CPT by dynamically filtering out uninformative samples.

## Results

- SFT showed severe catastrophic forgetting (FM of -10.4%) and significant degradation of general capabilities (16.9% drop on MMLU-Pro), whereas RFT (GRPO) maintained a low FM of -2.3% and improved MMMU performance by 2.1%.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the KL penalty in RFT primarily contributes to training stability, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mainly boosts performance rather than being the source of inherent forgetting mitigation.
- The proposed RIF-RFT method improved RFT's computational efficiency by approximately 44% (153.8 hours vs. 86.62 hours total wall-clock time) while retaining strong anti-forgetting properties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) inherently resists catastrophic forgetting in MLLMs during continual post-training, demonstrating superior knowledge preservation compared to SFT without explicit continual learning strategies.
> - RFT safeguards and often enhances the model's general capabilities, such as multi-discipline understanding and reducing hallucination tendencies, unlike SFT which causes significant degradation.
> - RFT's anti-forgetting property stems from an implicit regularization mechanism where updates are dynamically scaled by reward variance, dampening changes for uncertain samples and preserving established knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) and, more recently, multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has introduced advanced capabilities in understanding and interacting with complex data. A critical aspect for the practical deployment and longevity of these models is their ability to adapt to new tasks and evolving data streams without degrading performance on previously acquired knowledge. This continuous adaptation process is referred to as Continual Post-Training (CPT).

A central challenge in CPT is catastrophic forgetting, a phenomenon where a model's performance on previously learned tasks significantly diminishes when it is trained on new tasks. The existing research landscape for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in LLMs and MLLMs primarily focuses on three categories of strategies: data replay, which involves re-exposing the model to a subset of old data; model expansion, which entails adding new parameters or modules for new tasks; and parameter regularization, which imposes constraints on parameter updates to preserve prior knowledge. These methods are predominantly integrated into the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) paradigm, where models are updated by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of ground-truth responses.

The role of the fundamental fine-tuning paradigm itself, however, has received less direct investigation in the context of CPT. Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), which optimizes models based on feedback from generated outputs rather than direct supervision, has gained prominence in aligning foundation models with human preferences and improving performance on specific objectives in single-task settings. This paper situates itself by exploring whether RFT offers inherent advantages over SFT in mitigating catastrophic forgetting during continual learning, moving beyond the traditional focus on add-on strategies within the SFT framework. The work aims to fill this gap by providing a direct comparative analysis of SFT and RFT in a CPT setting for MLLMs, assessing their impact on both task-specific knowledge retention and the preservation of general model capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a systematic comparative analysis between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) as core post-training paradigms, specifically in the context of Continual Post-Training (CPT) for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The investigation centers on their respective impacts on knowledge retention.

More detailed objectives include:
- **Evaluate Knowledge Retention on Downstream Tasks:** To determine whether SFT or RFT is more effective in preserving performance on previously learned tasks when an MLLM continually adapts to a sequence of new downstream tasks. This addresses the challenge of catastrophic forgetting in task-specific knowledge.
- **Assess Preservation of General Capabilities:** To analyze how each fine-tuning paradigm affects the model's pre-existing general knowledge and capabilities on standard, broad benchmarks after sequential training on downstream tasks. This aims to understand whether CPT degrades or enhances foundational model abilities.
- **Investigate Mechanisms of Forgetting Mitigation:** To empirically identify the underlying reasons for any observed differences in forgetting mitigation, particularly within the RFT paradigm. This involves scrutinizing the roles of specific components such as KL divergence penalties and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, as well as analyzing the learning dynamics and gradient updates.
- **Propose an Enhanced RFT Algorithm:** To develop and evaluate a new algorithm, Rollout-based Instance Filtering for RFT (RIF-RFT), aimed at improving the training efficiency of RFT while maintaining its robustness in CPT.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several factors:
- **Evolving Demands of MLLMs:** MLLMs are increasingly deployed in dynamic environments that require them to continuously learn new skills and incorporate evolving domain knowledge. An efficient and scalable CPT paradigm is essential for their real-world applicability.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting as a Barrier:** Catastrophic forgetting remains a significant impediment to effective continual learning. While existing strategies address forgetting, they typically operate within the SFT paradigm, leaving the role of the foundational learning paradigm largely unexplored.
- **Potential of RFT:** RFT has demonstrated success in aligning foundation models and enhancing specific capabilities. The researchers hypothesize that its feedback-driven optimization might inherently offer better knowledge preservation properties compared to SFT's direct supervision.
- **Addressing Base Model Degradation:** Previous studies have indicated that SFT can degrade the general capabilities of foundation models. Investigating whether RFT can mitigate or even reverse this degradation is a key motivation.
- **Lack of Comparative Analysis:** Prior to this work, a direct comparative investigation into SFT versus RFT for knowledge preservation in the challenging CPT setting for MLLMs was absent.
- **Improving Efficiency:** Given that RFT methods can be computationally intensive, there is a motivation to enhance their efficiency, particularly for continuous deployment scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a comparative experimental approach to evaluate Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) in a Continual Post-Training (CPT) setting for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

**Base Model and Datasets:**
The open-source Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model was selected as the base MLLM due to its reported vision-language comprehension capabilities and moderate resource footprint. The model underwent continual fine-tuning on a sequence of seven diverse multimodal vision-language datasets: ScienceQA, TextVQA, VizWiz, GQA, Geometry3K, PathVQA, and Super-CLEVR. These datasets cover a range of tasks, including multimodal reasoning, text reading in images, object hallucination assessment, and complex relational understanding.

**Learning Algorithms:**
The study compared:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Implemented as standard negative log-likelihood minimization, optimizing the probability of ground-truth responses.
- **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT):** Three representative RFT algorithms were used:
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Generates a group of responses, computes normalized rewards relative to the group mean, and optimizes an advantage-weighted log-probability, typically with a KL-divergence penalty against a reference policy.
 - **ReMax:** Uses the reward of a greedy decoding response as a baseline to normalize rewards for a single sampled response.
 - **RLOO (Reinforcement Learning with Leave-One-Out Baseline):** Generates multiple samples and uses the average reward of the other samples as a baseline for each individual sample.

**Reward Function for RFT:**
For GRPO, a combined reward function `r_overall = 0.9 * r_acc + 0.1 * r_format` was designed.
- `r_acc` (accuracy reward) was 1 if the generated answer matched the ground truth, 0 otherwise.
- `r_format` (format reward) was 1 if the generated response adhered to the expected structure (e.g., Chain-of-Thought reasoning within `<think>` tags and the final answer in `\boxed{}`), 0 otherwise.

**Prompt Templates:**
The Qwen-VL-7B-Instruct model utilized two types of input prompt templates:
- **NoCoT (non-chain-of-thought):** A basic question-answering format requiring a direct final answer.
- **CoT (Chain-of-Thought):** Instructs the model to engage in a reasoning process within `<think>` tags before providing a final answer in `\boxed{}`.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Task-Specific Performance:** After learning all tasks, performance on each previously encountered task's test set was evaluated.
 - **Average Accuracy (AvgAcc):** The average accuracy across all tasks after training on the final task.
 - **Forgetting Measure (FM):** Calculates the average difference between the final accuracy of a task and its best accuracy achieved throughout the training sequence (`P*i = max(P_k,i)` for `k` from task `i` to `T`). A higher AvgAcc and an FM closer to zero indicate better performance and less forgetting.
- **General Capabilities Evaluation:** At the end of the CPT sequence, models were evaluated on three general benchmarks, with and without CoT prompting:
 - **MMMU:** A comprehensive multi-discipline multimodal understanding and reasoning benchmark.
 - **MMLU-Pro:** An enhanced, more challenging multi-task language understanding benchmark.
 - **POPE:** A benchmark specifically designed to assess object hallucination in vision-language models.

**Implementation Details:**
All experiments employed full-parameter fine-tuning. SFT experiments used the `llamafactory` framework with a learning rate of 1e-5 and a batch size of 24. RFT methods were implemented using the `easyR1` framework (building on `Verl`), with consistent settings: learning rate of 1e-6, rollout batch size of 512, sampling temperature of 1.0, and a KL-divergence coefficient `β = 0.01` for GRPO. GRPO used a group size of 8. Model outputs were normalized by disregarding extraneous whitespace and ignoring case sensitivity.

**Ablation Studies:**
To understand RFT's stability, ablation studies were conducted on GRPO:
- **GRPO w/o KL:** GRPO trained with CoT prompts but without the KL divergence penalty.
- **GRPO w/o CoT:** GRPO trained without CoT prompts (using question-answering format) but retaining the KL penalty.
- **Learning Dynamics Analysis:** Training samples were categorized by the base model's success rate in rollouts (easy, hard, mixture) to analyze gradient update behavior.

**Proposed Algorithm (RIF-RFT):**
A Rollout-based Instance Filtering (RIF-RFT) algorithm was proposed to enhance RFT efficiency. For each new training instance, a small number of policy rollouts were performed. If the average reward from these rollouts exceeded a minimal threshold (τ=0 by default), the instance was retained for RFT training; otherwise, it was discarded.

**Robustness Analysis:**
Additional experiments were conducted to assess the generality of findings across:
- **Model Scales:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct.
- **Task Domains:** Text-only tasks (GSM8K, USMLE) using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- **Task Orderings:** Two different sequences of tasks for 3B and 8B models.
- **Comparison to Experience Replay:** SFT with Experience Replay (ER) at a 25% replay ratio was evaluated as a baseline for established continual learning techniques.
- **Random-Matched Baseline for RIF-RFT:** RIF-RFT was compared against GRPO trained on a randomly subsampled dataset of equivalent size.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The investigation yielded several key findings concerning the performance of SFT and RFT in continual post-training (CPT) for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

#### 4.1. RFT Inherently Resists Catastrophic Forgetting

- **SFT exhibits severe catastrophic forgetting:** Sequential SFT resulted in a significant degradation of performance on previously learned tasks, indicated by a Forgetting Measure (FM) of -10.4%. For example, performance on ScienceQA dropped from a peak of 95.2% to 76.1% after completing the entire task sequence. The final average accuracy (AvgAcc) for SFT was 54.0%, notably lower than the 62.9% achieved by multi-task learning (MTL) SFT, which served as an upper bound. This confirms SFT's susceptibility to forgetting in CPT.
- **RFT preserves task knowledge effectively:** In contrast, all RFT methods demonstrated resilience against forgetting. GRPO achieved an FM of -2.3% and a final AvgAcc of 60.0%, which was close to the MTL SFT upper bound. For instance, GRPO maintained ScienceQA performance at 93.0% after all tasks, a minimal drop from its peak of 95.6%. This suggests that the RFT paradigm inherently possesses properties that mitigate forgetting without requiring explicit continual learning strategies.
- **RFT outperforms Experience Replay:** When compared to Experience Replay (ER), a common continual learning technique, GRPO (FM -0.4%) showed superior forgetting mitigation compared to SFT + ER (FM -2.8%) on the 3B model, even with ER using a 25% replay ratio. RFT achieved this without the storage overhead associated with replay buffers.
- **Robustness across conditions:** The forgetting-mitigation effect of RFT was found to be robust across different model scales (3B and 8B models), text-only tasks (GSM8K and USMLE), and various task orderings. RFT maintained near-zero forgetting (FM consistently close to zero) across these variations, whereas SFT experienced substantially larger performance drops.

#### 4.2. RFT Protects and Enhances General Capabilities

- **SFT degrades general capabilities:** SFT training, both sequentially and in a multi-task setting (MTL (SFT)), led to a notable degradation of the model's foundational, general-purpose abilities. For example, SFT caused a 16.9% performance drop on MMLU-Pro (from 47.5% to 30.6%) and a 12.0% drop on MMMU. This suggests that the SFT paradigm itself can be detrimental to the base model's broader capabilities.
- **RFT preserves and enhances general capabilities:** Conversely, RFT effectively safeguarded and often enhanced the model's general abilities. GRPO improved performance on MMMU by 2.1% (from 52.1% to 54.2%) and on POPE (a benchmark for object hallucination) by 1.9% (from 86.6% to 88.5%), indicating a reduction in hallucination tendencies. This difference highlights RFT as a more robust paradigm for maintaining and improving general capabilities during CPT. This trend held across random, popular, and adversarial splits of the POPE benchmark.

#### 4.3. Understanding RFT’s Forgetting Mitigation: Ablation Studies and Empirical Insights

Ablation studies using the GRPO algorithm investigated the roles of specific components:

- **KL penalty is not the primary factor for task-specific knowledge preservation:** Removing the KL-divergence penalty (`GRPO w/o KL`) did not degrade performance on the continual learning sequence, maintaining a similar average accuracy. However, training without the KL penalty introduced significant instability in later stages of the task sequence, suggesting its primary role is in aiding optimization stability rather than directly preventing task-specific forgetting.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is a performance booster, not a forgetting mitigator:** `GRPO w/o CoT` still strongly resisted forgetting, maintaining high average accuracy. It even outperformed standard GRPO on some tasks (e.g., VizWiz 63.8% vs. 51.8%). On general benchmarks, `GRPO w/o CoT` remained robust, achieving the highest POPE score (88.7%) in non-CoT evaluation. This indicates that while CoT can enhance performance, it is not the mechanism responsible for RFT's inherent resistance to catastrophic forgetting.

- **RFT's stability stems from selective gradient updates:** Empirical analysis of learning dynamics, categorizing samples by difficulty, revealed that RFT's advantage comes from its selective learning mechanism. Policy gradient methods, like GRPO, generate minimal parameter updates for samples where all sampled responses yield identical rewards (i.e., when `σ_r = 0`), meaning the model's behavior is already stable or the sample is beyond its current competence. This prevents arbitrary parameter changes that could interfere with established knowledge. In contrast, SFT always computes gradients towards the ground-truth response, forcing updates regardless of the model's current state or sample learnability. This inherent property of RFT focuses gradient signals on informative samples.

#### 4.4. RIF-RFT: Enhancing Efficiency of RFT

- **Improved Efficiency and Comparable Performance:** The proposed Rollout-based Instance Filtering for RFT (RIF-RFT) algorithm demonstrated a practical trade-off between performance and computational efficiency. While full GRPO achieved the highest overall performance, RIF-RFT achieved an AvgAcc of 57.5% (compared to 60.0% for GRPO and 54.0% for SFT) by retaining an average of only 50% of the training data. This led to a significant reduction of approximately 44% in total wall-clock time compared to standard GRPO, with filtering overhead being negligible.
- **Effectiveness of Filtering:** The filtering mechanism, by focusing on learnable samples, allowed RIF-RFT to outperform a `GRPO (Random Match)` baseline (which used a randomly subsampled dataset of the same size), suggesting that the gains come from intelligent sample selection rather than merely reducing data volume.
- **Filtering Threshold:** An ablation study on the filtering threshold `τ` showed that a default `τ=0` (retaining samples with any non-zero reward) achieved the highest performance, indicating that even low-reward informative samples contribute positively to learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a comprehensive investigation into the role of the learning paradigm itself—Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) versus Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)—in mitigating catastrophic forgetting during continual post-training (CPT) of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The findings carry several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Paradigm Shift for Continual Learning:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration that RFT inherently mitigates catastrophic forgetting and preserves, and in some cases enhances, general model capabilities in MLLMs during CPT. This suggests that the choice of the fine-tuning paradigm plays a more fundamental role in knowledge retention than previously recognized. This insight could guide future research towards RFT as a default, robust paradigm for continual learning, potentially reducing reliance on complex, task-specific, or data-intensive continual learning strategies.
- **Enhanced MLLM Adaptability and Longevity:** By naturally preserving both task-specific skills and general intelligence, RFT can make MLLMs more adaptable to ever-evolving downstream tasks and user requirements. This would allow for more seamless, long-term deployment of MLLMs without costly full retraining or the degradation of critical core abilities. This directly addresses the practical need for MLLMs to incorporate new information continuously in real-world scenarios.
- **Deeper Understanding of Learning Dynamics:** The empirical analysis of RFT's learning dynamics, particularly the observation that its stability arises from selective gradient updates on informative samples, provides a mechanistic explanation for its forgetting mitigation. This insight contributes to the theoretical understanding of policy gradient methods and how they interact with complex neural network architectures. It offers a foundation for further theoretical investigations into cross-task gradient interference and optimal learning signal utilization.
- **Improved Computational Efficiency for RFT:** The proposed Rollout-based Instance Filtering for RFT (RIF-RFT) offers a practical solution to enhance the training efficiency of RFT, which is often computationally intensive. By focusing RFT on learnable samples, RIF-RFT significantly reduces computational time while largely preserving performance. This improvement in efficiency makes RFT a more viable option for real-world applications, especially in resource-constrained environments or for models requiring frequent updates.
- **Mitigation of Base Model Degradation:** The finding that RFT not only preserves but can also enhance the base model's general capabilities (e.g., on MMMU and POPE benchmarks) is notable. This contrasts with SFT, which was shown to severely degrade these foundational abilities. This aspect is crucial for maintaining the robustness and trustworthiness of deployed MLLMs, particularly by reducing tendencies like object hallucination.
- **Guidance for Post-Training Strategy Selection:** The work offers clear guidance for researchers and practitioners in selecting post-training paradigms. For scenarios demanding continuous adaptation and robust knowledge retention, RFT emerges as a strong candidate, especially when combined with efficiency enhancements like RIF-RFT.

**Potential Negative Impacts and Future Considerations:**
The paper acknowledges that while RFT can improve model reliability, preserving model behavior might also retain undesirable biases or unsafe capabilities. Therefore, continual post-training should be coupled with careful evaluation, safety filtering, and targeted unlearning when necessary. Furthermore, RFT remains computationally expensive, and future work is needed to explore more efficient training protocols and transparent reporting of compute usage.
