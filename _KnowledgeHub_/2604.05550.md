# AutoSOTA: An End-to-End Automated Research System for State-of-the-Art AI Model Discovery

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoSOTA is an end-to-end automated research system that takes an unstructured top-tier AI paper and returns an improved executable repository. Eight specialized agents ground the paper to code and dependencies, build and repair the execution environment, construct an evaluation rubric, then propose, schedule and validity-check optimization ideas. It discovered 105 new SOTA models across 125 papers, averaging about five hours and nearly 10% gain per paper.

## Problem

- Prior AutoML and NAS systems optimize hyperparameters or architectures only, never starting from unstructured literature or recovering a runnable baseline from a released repository.
- Recent iterative LLM frameworks confine agents to improving one bounded code module inside a sanitized sandbox, entirely sidestepping environment setup and dependency recovery.
- Academic artifacts are fragmented: repository links buried in footnotes, missing scripts, hardcoded paths, dense result tables, so no prior system defines the target metric autonomously.

## Method

- **AgentResource** grounds papers to repositories, datasets and base models via web interaction; **AgentObjective** builds a ==tree-structured recursive rubric== with ==hierarchical context injection== turning the macro goal into dense verifiable feedback.
- **AgentInit** initializes environments, **AgentMonitor** tracks long-horizon execution with ==external-memory context management== and breaks deadlocks, and **AgentFix** repairs runtime dependency and traceback failures.
- **AgentIdeator** builds a ==constraint-aware hypothesis library==, **AgentScheduler** manages code states, program database, prompt samplers and LLM ensembles under compute budgets, and **AgentSupervisor** enforces ==red-line checks== against evaluation leakage.

## Results

- From **2,347** screened candidate papers and **745** tested, **125** survived cost filters; AutoSOTA optimized **105** baselines, averaging roughly **10%** gain on each paper's primary metric.
- Case gains span domains: FR-Spec throughput **674.58** to **765.27** tok/s (**13.44%**), protein binding correlation **0.4897** to **0.5655** (**15.48%**), CVRP optimality gap **0.914%** to **0.444%** (**51.4%**).
- Time-series Neural MJD test MAE fell from a **17.127** reproduced baseline to **14.096** (about **19%** below the paper's **17.411**) via antithetic variates and loss reweighting, discarding gains that helped only NLL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large empirical gains often hide in implementation defects, not hyperparameters: an incorrect draft-token reuse was the single biggest throughput win in a published inference system.
> - Ensembling existing repository artifacts, such as precomputed physics-based predictions, can beat single-model ceilings once subtle data-alignment bugs are fixed.
> - Autonomous optimization needs an explicit validity supervisor and metric-specific filtering, since improvements on secondary metrics frequently fail to transfer to the target metric.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence (AI) research is characterized by a continuous pursuit of State-Of-The-Art (SOTA) performance. Achieving these advancements typically involves extensive cycles of implementation, replication, debugging, evaluation, and iterative refinement, which often require significant human effort. This process is further complicated by the fragmented nature of modern AI research artifacts, including papers, code repositories, datasets, checkpoints, and often undocumented engineering assumptions. This reliance on repetitive experimental iteration diverts human researchers from more conceptual and disruptive explorations.

Existing efforts to automate aspects of this research pipeline include several categories:
- **Automated Machine Learning (AutoML)**: Traditional AutoML systems, such as those focusing on Hyperparameter Optimization (HPO) and Neural Architecture Search (NAS), have demonstrated the ability to optimize model design within predefined search spaces. However, these systems are constrained by their "closed-world assumption," preventing them from inventing novel algorithmic structures, synthesizing external knowledge, or debugging complex software environments beyond their fixed operational boundaries.
- **LLM-Driven Algorithm Discovery and Code Optimization**: Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have enabled systems like SWE-agent and AutoCoder to generate and optimize programmatic logic, navigate multi-file repositories, and resolve software engineering issues. Other systems, such as FunSearch and Eureka, leverage LLMs for discovering mathematical heuristics, algorithms, and optimizing reward functions. Evolutionary frameworks like AlphaEvolve aim to continuously mutate algorithmic code for performance improvement, and open-source initiatives like AutoResearch explore iterative experiment loops. A common limitation across these approaches is their tendency to operate within sanitized, sandbox environments or confine interventions to bounded code modules. They frequently bypass the complexities of environment initialization, dependency resolution, and long-horizon debugging inherent in unstructured academic codebases. Furthermore, they often lack robust supervisory mechanisms necessary to maintain protocol comparability when modifying complex SOTA repositories, leading to potential spurious empirical gains.
- **Autonomous AI Scientists**: More ambitious efforts aim to automate the entire scientific research lifecycle, leading to systems referred to as "AI Scientists." Examples include Sakana AI's AI Scientist and AI Scientist v2, DeepMind’s AI Co-Scientist, DeepScientist, and ResearchAgent. These frameworks typically focus on macro-level activities such as conceptual ideation, hypothesis generation, experimental execution, and manuscript drafting. However, they often do not provide the robust, long-horizon execution grounding required to reliably advance highly competitive empirical baselines. Their focus is often on the conceptual or documentation stages rather than the detailed, fault-tolerant optimization of code to surpass existing SOTA.

AutoSOTA addresses these limitations by developing an end-to-end automated research system that integrates disparate stages of the research pipeline. It aims to bridge the gap from unstructured research papers to functional, empirically improved, and reproducible executable repositories. Unlike prior work, AutoSOTA explicitly tackles the challenges of environment setup, baseline reproduction, and valid, open-ended methodological reflection under realistic constraints, providing a comprehensive solution for accelerating SOTA model discovery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AutoSOTA is to establish an end-to-end automated research system capable of discovering State-Of-The-Art (SOTA) models. This involves autonomously converting unstructured academic literature into empirically superior algorithms that surpass existing published baselines. The system aims to map a top-tier AI paper (P) to an advanced, executable repository (R*) containing a novel algorithmic variant that demonstrably outperforms the original method presented in P.

The motivation for this research stems from several critical observations within the current AI research ecosystem:
- **Bottleneck in SOTA Pursuit**: The pursuit of SOTA performance is a central driver in AI research. However, achieving these advancements is rarely the result of singular theoretical insights. Instead, it involves a protracted cycle of implementation, replication, debugging, evaluation, and refinement, consuming significant human effort over months. This iterative process constitutes a fundamental bottleneck, diverting scientific attention from more original and disruptive explorations.
- **Fragmentation of Research Artifacts**: Modern AI research artifacts are often distributed across various sources—papers, GitHub repositories, datasets, model checkpoints, and implicit engineering assumptions. This fragmentation makes the reproduction and further optimization of reported methods challenging and time-consuming.
- **Limitations of Existing Automation**: While AutoML has enhanced hyperparameter tuning and architecture search, and LLM-based systems have improved code generation and debugging, these efforts often operate within narrow problem formulations. Existing frameworks frequently overlook the complexities of environment setup, the challenges of reproducing baselines from incomplete academic code, and the need for open-ended methodological reflection under realistic constraints. They typically fail to provide a comprehensive, end-to-end solution for scientific discovery from raw literature to improved executable models.

To address this complex, open-ended discovery problem, AutoSOTA decomposes the overall objective into three tightly coupled sub-problems:

1. **Resource Preparation and Goal Setting**: This initial phase aims to translate a theoretical paper into a concrete experimental context. This involves locating the corresponding codebase (C), necessary datasets (D), base models (M), and precisely defining the target performance metric (g*). Key challenges include the severe fragmentation and implicit coupling of academic resources, the difficulty in standardizing repository links, and the chaotic presentation of empirical results in literature, which requires semantic reasoning to identify the most scientifically meaningful benchmark.

2. **Experiment Evaluation**: Following resource acquisition, this phase focuses on synthesizing a functional repository state (R_rep) whose empirical evaluation closely approximates the target metric (eval(R_rep) ≈ g*). This stage transforms static resources into a dynamic execution pipeline. The primary difficulty lies in managing long-horizon reasoning under partial observability, navigating complex environment configurations, implicit hardware dependencies, and deprecated libraries. Open-source code often suffers from incompleteness (missing scripts, hardcoded paths, undocumented preprocessing), requiring the agent to diagnose silent failures, interpret cryptic tracebacks, and synthesize missing logic from abstract paper descriptions.

3. **Reflection and Ideation**: This final and most demanding phase elevates the system from a replication engine to an autonomous scientific discoverer. The goal is to uncover genuine algorithmic improvements, including structural innovations, novel learning objectives, and optimized implementation strategies, while intelligently balancing open-ended exploration against high computational costs. The main challenge involves navigating an expansive search space, managing the severe imbalance between exploration and exploitation, and ensuring that all metric gains are methodologically valid, thereby moving beyond trivial hyperparameter tuning.

These three sub-problems collectively define the core technical and scientific bottlenecks that AutoSOTA aims to solve through an integrated, end-to-end automated research system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoSOTA is designed as a multi-agent system, structured to emulate the division of labor observed in human AI research. It systematically decomposes the cognitive and operational workload across eight specialized agents, organized into three core macro-modules: Resource Preparation and Goal Setting, Experiment Evaluation, and Reflection & Ideation. These modules do not operate in a strictly sequential pipeline; Experiment Evaluation and Reflection & Ideation are tightly coupled in an iterative cycle to drive algorithmic evolution. The mechanical generation of syntax modifications, often referred to as Code Optimization, is treated as an operational bridge within this framework.

#### 3.1. Architecture Design

The overall architecture comprises eight specialized agents: AgentResource, AgentObjective, AgentInit, AgentMonitor, AgentFix, AgentIdeator, AgentScheduler, and AgentSupervisor.

#### 3.2. Resource Preparation and Goal Setting Module

This module bridges the gap between unstructured academic literature and actionable engineering constraints.
- **AgentResource: Bridging Static Literature and Executable Environments**
 - **Paper-to-Repository Grounding and Curation**: This sub-module processes conference-level metadata from eight top-tier AI conferences (ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS, AAAI, NAACL, ICCV, ACL, CVPR) from 2025. It converts raw paper collections into normalized local representations with standardized metadata and parsed text. An LLM-based classifier identifies methodological papers, filtering out theoretical or review articles. For these candidates, it extracts GitHub links from PDFs and project pages, using an LLM selector to identify official repositories and shallow-cloning them locally. A lightweight readiness assessment (abstract, file tree, README) determines if a repository is actionable for reproduction.
 - **External Resource Acquisition: Bridging the Dependency Gap**: This stage handles heavyweight external dependencies (datasets, pre-trained weights).
 - **Symbolic Resource Discovery**: It parses the repository structure and evaluation manifests (e.g., `rubric.csv`) to catalog external requirements under a zero-download constraint, populating a Global Resource Registry with source URLs, resource taxonomies, and estimated file sizes (`S_total`).
 - **Physical Acquisition and Orchestration**: A Download Execution Agent operates on the validated registry. It employs gated selection based on `S_total` (within `[S_min, S_max]`) and redundancy elimination. Resources are routed to a standardized directory structure (e.g., `datasets/`, `models/`). It supports polymorphic retrieval (e.g., `huggingface-cli`, `curl`, LLM-driven custom Python scripts) and ensures concurrency and fault tolerance with paper-level parallelism, asynchronous telemetry, and in-memory URL mapping for de-duplication and breakpoint resumption.
- **AgentObjective: Automated Objective Rubric Construction**
 - This agent automates the construction of a structured evaluation space. It maps the macro research goal (g*) into a quantifiable dense feedback library (DFL), providing explicit evidentiary support derived from the paper (P) and codebase (C).
 - **Tree-Structured Recursive Decomposition**: A Breadth-First Search (BFS) algorithm, guided by LLMs, recursively decomposes macro-replication objectives into multi-dimensional, granular atomic sub-tasks. Each parent node (e.g., Result Match, Methodology Implementation) is refined into binary (Pass/Fail) children. A weight-driven termination mechanism ensures evaluative depth while controlling search space, terminating decomposition when a node's weight falls below a threshold or max depth is reached.
 - **Hierarchical Context Injection**: A multi-dimensional knowledge injection pool integrates PDF Markdown, VLM-extracted visual knowledge (tables/figures), and engineering logic from the codebase. A tiered on-demand context delivery mechanism dynamically injects information: shallow strategic layers (ToC, abstract), intermediate technical layers (methodology, experimental sections, VLM evidence), and deep implementation layers (repository-level context, specific data preprocessing, utility configurations).

#### 3.3. Experiment Evaluation Module

This module focuses on robust operational resilience to transform noisy academic repositories into executable baselines.
- **AgentInit: Initialize Experiment Environment**
 - This agent transforms a paper-grounded task interface into an actionable execution plan and runnable repository state (R0, E0, Π0). It operates through a preset workflow: input analysis, sub-task decomposition, environment construction (using tools like Repo2Run, Docker fallback), dependency resolution, resource acquisition, repository inspection, command discovery, and execution preparation. It is equipped with a reusable skill library for tasks like GPU/CUDA validation, Python/PyTorch compatibility, path repair, and glue logic synthesis, enabling protocol-preserving repository repair within original evaluation constraints.
- **AgentMonitor: Tracking the state of experiment and preventing deadlock**
 - This external supervisory agent performs real-time state tracking and high-level intervention. It parses the execution trace to estimate the current phase (setup, installation, launch, etc.), detecting dead-end trajectories (e.g., repeated environment edits, infinite loops of hallucinated bug-fixing). Upon detection, it provides high-level corrective guidance (e.g., switch to manual Docker, consult failure-handling skill) without collapsing the main agent's autonomy. It also acts as a global budget and safety controller, terminating execution on timeouts and managing resource cleanup.
 - **Persistent Context Management via External Memory**: AgentMonitor maintains a structured external memory for long-horizon optimization: `code_analysis.md` (code cognition map), `idea_library.md` (dynamic idea pool), and `research_report.md` (distilled SOTA insights). This retrieval-augmented paradigm controls context growth and enables coherent global view.
- **AgentFix: Resolving Runtime Conflicts**
 - This module is responsible for structured diagnosis, protocol-preserving repair, and cross-task reuse of failure knowledge. It maps observed failures to validated repair actions (Δt) using a memory of historical failures (M_err). The design emphasizes "retrieval before repair," where agents consult explicit repair protocols from a structured skill repository for known failure categories (e.g., package installation errors, CUDA toolchain mismatches, network issues). This prevents cyclical reasoning by storing normalized error signatures and associated remedies, guiding the system toward unexplored repair strategies while ensuring repairs do not alter scientific protocols.

#### 3.4. Reflection & Ideation Module

This module elevates the system to an autonomous scientific discoverer, operating in continuous synergy with the evaluation phase.
- **AgentIdeator: Constraint-Aware Hypothesis Construction**
 - This agent constructs a structured search space of admissible improvement hypotheses. It queries an external, research-capable model for a task-relevant prior report, grounding hypotheses in SOTA literature, empirical heuristics, and best practices. It enforces a "red-line system" to filter out proposals that violate experimental integrity, ensuring alignment across metric, implementation, constraint, and lever dimensions. The output is a systematized hypothesis library (H_adm), categorized by granularity (PARAM, CODE, ALGO) and risk level, along with an audit record.
- **AgentScheduler: Lifecycle Management for Optimizing Research Codebases**
 - This agent is the orchestration core, managing the entire lifecycle of a reproduced paper.
 - **Optimization Strategy**: The workflow proceeds through four phases:
 - **Phase 0 (Initialization & Baseline Measurement)**: Launches Docker, initializes version control (`_baseline` tag), executes full evaluation to obtain measured baseline metrics (recorded in `scores.jsonl`).
 - **Phase 1 (Code & Paper Understanding)**: Systematically explores the repository, identifies algorithmic branches, maps the reasoning pipeline, estimates runtime, and enumerates hard constraints in `code_analysis.md`.
 - **Phase 2 (Idea Library Construction)**: Generates an initial Idea Library with categorized ideas (micro, meso, macro granularity), prioritized by feasibility and potential. A mandatory Red Line Audit filters ideas against the seven constraints, marking violations as REJECTED.
 - **Phase 3 (Iterative Optimization Loop)**: Comprises seven steps: Pre-Iteration Reflection, Idea Selection, Git Snapshot, Code Implementation, Evaluation, Debugging, Result Recording, and Idea Library Update. It features a "Leap Path" anti-stagnation mechanism, forcing structural innovation if recent iterations were only parameter tuning. A "Honeymoon Period" allows exploration on Leap-derived states. An internal version control layer ensures state atomicity and rollback capability.
 - **Resource Management**: Manages a global resource framework for multiple papers and GPUs, using two-GPU units. It ensures persistent tracking of scheduling state, enabling recovery from crashes. Tasks run in independent processes, decoupled from the scheduler, ensuring uninterrupted operation. Dynamic GPU assignment and artifact-driven completion verification maximize resource utilization.
- **AgentSupervisor: Preventing Invalid Optimization Behaviors**
 - This agent acts as the guardian of scientific integrity, enforcing a "Red Line System" to ensure that optimization proceeds under consistent, comparable, and methodologically valid conditions. This system comprises seven non-negotiable constraints:
 - **R1**: Evaluation Metric Parameters Must Not Change.
 - **R2**: Evaluation Script Integrity Must Be Maintained.
 - **R3**: Integrity of Algorithm Outputs Must Not Be Violated.
 - **R4**: No Unfair Trade-offs Between Metric Dimensions.
 - **R5**: Dataset Integrity Must Be Preserved.
 - **R6**: No Modification of the Dataset.
 - **R7**: Paper-Specific Constraints Must Be Explicitly Identified and Strictly Followed.
 - These constraints are enforced through a multi-layered supervision mechanism: during Phase 1 (identifying R7), after Phase 2 (Red Line Audit of initial ideas), when the Leap Path is triggered in Phase 3 (explicit check for dynamic ideas), and as global rules in the main prompt. This ensures that only legitimate, scientifically valid improvements are pursued.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

The evaluation benchmark for AutoSOTA was constructed from recent papers published in eight top-tier AI conferences (NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, CVPR, ICCV, ACL, NAACL, AAAI) from 2025. A systematic multi-stage screening protocol was applied:
1. **Methodological Filtering**: Excluded theoretical, review, or purely analytical papers, retaining only those proposing novel, empirically validated methods.
2. **Artifact Availability Filter**: Retained only papers with explicit links to public code repositories.
3. **Readiness Assessment**: Filtered out incomplete repositories or placeholders.
4. **Computational Cost Constraint**: Filtered out papers with insurmountable environment installation failures or those requiring more than 4 GPU hours for a single complete experimental replication.
This rigorous process yielded a final dataset of 125 method-driven research papers, serving as the primary testbed.

#### 4.2. Main Results

AutoSOTA demonstrated performance in automated replication and subsequent optimization across this diverse set of papers:
- **SOTA Discovery**: The system successfully optimized 105 out of the 125 baselines, leading to the discovery of 105 new SOTA models that surpassed the original reported methods.
- **Performance Improvement**: An average performance improvement of approximately 10% was achieved over the original main methods.
- **Computational Efficiency**: The system required an average of approximately five hours of execution time per paper.
- **Cross-Domain Robustness**: AutoSOTA exhibited consistent robustness across various multidisciplinary tasks, including medical time-series analysis and protein binding energy prediction, validating its generalized potential for large-scale scientific advancement. The improvements showed lower variance and stronger consistency compared to traditional research data.

#### 4.3. Categorical Analysis of Optimization Outcomes

A systematic audit and categorical analysis of the 125 experimental trials classified outcomes into three tiers (Figure 8):
- **Class A (Algorithmic Innovation)**: Represented 51% (64 out of 125) of the results. These involved substantive methodological modifications, such as loss function redesign, model operator replacement, or architectural restructuring. Class A schemes yielded an average performance enhancement of 8.82%, indicating the system's capability for logical restructuring beyond conventional machine learning tool operations.
- **Class B (Parameter Optimization)**: Comprised 33% (41 out of 125) of the results, involving hyperparameter tuning (e.g., learning rates, sampling scales). Class B optimizations achieved a mean improvement of 5.67%.
- **Class C (Optimization Failure)**: Accounted for 16% (20 out of 125) of cases, where no improvements were recorded or invalid optimizations occurred. These instances indicate a failure rate in the self-supervised constraint mechanisms, potentially due to implicit violations or limitations in handling complex cross-agent interactions. However, the fact that approximately 84% of tasks maintained methodological consistency and evaluation comparability under the supervision mechanism suggests the overall effectiveness of AgentSupervisor.

#### 4.4. Case Studies

Six representative case studies further illustrated the nature of AutoSOTA's improvements:
- **LLM Research (FR-Spec)**: Achieved a 13.44% throughput improvement. AutoSOTA identified and fixed a hidden error in the speculative decoding path (incorrect reuse of `tree_draft_ids`) and implemented runtime improvements (GPU-side terminal-token checks, precomputed padding paths), demonstrating optimization beyond routine hyperparameter tuning.
- **NLP Research (Comparing Moral Values...)**: Obtained an 8.59% improvement in `correlation_care`. The system redesigned the moral information propagation in a Graph Moral Network by applying a triple-log1p transformation for edge weights (smoothing) and evolving from a single-scale to a multi-scale propagation design.
- **Biology Research (Predicting mutational effects...)**: Realized a 15.48% increase in per-interface Pearson correlation. AutoSOTA leveraged pre-computed physics-based predictions (FoldX), autonomously resolved a data-alignment bug, and formulated a hybrid ensemble strategy (60% ML-based StaB-ddG, 40% FoldX).
- **CV Research (Reasoning as Representation...)**: Achieved a 2.68% improvement in Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC) for Image Quality Assessment. The system recognized and restructured a representation bottleneck in a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture, redesigning the token fusion mechanism to capture multi-scale quality degradation by concatenating global CLS tokens with both average-pooled and max-pooled spatial patch tokens.
- **Time Series Research (Neural MJD...)**: Demonstrated a 19.0% reduction in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for financial data prediction. AutoSOTA dynamically scaled Monte Carlo sampling, introduced antithetic variates for variance reduction, and reallocated conditional mean loss weight, exhibiting dynamic optimization and self-correction under resource constraints.
- **Optimization Research (MoSES...)**: Achieved a 51.4% reduction in optimality gap for the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP). Interventions included modifying the neural architecture's gating mechanism, aggressively scaling test-time symmetric augmentations, dynamically downscaling batch size, and injecting classical Operations Research heuristics (intra-route 2-opt, cross-route Or-opt relocations), showcasing hybrid optimization and expert-level orchestration.

These results indicate that AutoSOTA is capable of identifying and implementing substantive architectural, algorithmic, and workflow-level interventions, moving beyond simple hyperparameter tuning to achieve empirical advancements across diverse AI domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of AutoSOTA carries implications beyond achieving improved benchmark results. The system is positioned as a novel research infrastructure for AI science, designed to absorb the repetitive and time-consuming experimental iteration cycles, thereby amplifying human creativity.

The work suggests a shift in the established research paradigm, fostering a tightly coupled human-AI collaboration model. In this framework, automated agents are delegated tasks such as large-scale execution, baseline reproduction, and empirical reflection. This delegation allows human scientists to reallocate their attention toward higher-level conceptual innovation, causal reasoning, and disruptive scientific questions, redirecting human effort to the core of scientific discovery.

AutoSOTA addresses a critical execution gap within the broader vision of "AI Scientists." While existing autonomous AI scientist frameworks often focus on macro-level ideation or manuscript generation, AutoSOTA provides the empirical grounding necessary to ensure that AI-driven research is rigorously anchored in verifiable and reproducible code execution. This specialized, fault-tolerant infrastructure for SOTA model optimization complements abstract scientific creativity with robust, long-horizon implementation and validation.

Potential future avenues for this research include extending the automated objective rubric to encompass more complex modalities, such as hardware-in-the-loop systems or physical robotics. Furthermore, integrating AutoSOTA’s robust execution engine with fully autonomous macro-ideation frameworks, such as OmniScientist, could lead to a completely closed-loop research paradigm where AI agents not only optimize existing state-of-the-art methods but also proactively invent, empirically validate, and publish entirely new algorithmic paradigms.
