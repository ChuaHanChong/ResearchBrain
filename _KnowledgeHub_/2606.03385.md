# Grasp-Then-Plan with Failure Attribution: A Closed Two-Stage Framework for Precise and Generalizable Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Grasp-Then-Plan with Failure Attribution (GTP-FA) is a closed-loop framework for robotic manipulation that diagnoses failures in tightly coupled grasp-then-plan systems, attributing them to either grasp selection or downstream policy. This diagnosis guides targeted optimization, achieving average terminal success rate improvements of up to 54.0 percentage points in simulation and 65.6 percentage points on a real Franka robot across various long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Difficulty in attributing failures in robotic manipulation to either grasp selection or downstream planning, leading to inefficient trial-and-error learning.
- Task-incompatible grasps, which are geometrically stable but hinder subsequent manipulation, often get misdiagnosed as planning failures.
- Contaminated learning signals when using failure data, as end-to-end fine-tuning may incorrectly assign grasp-induced errors to the downstream policy.

## Method

![[2606.03385-method.png]]
*The GTP-FA framework operates in a loop: executing a task, diagnosing the failure mode, and routing updates to either the grasp selection or the planning policy.*

- A closed-loop execute-diagnose-update framework (**GTP-FA**) that explicitly links grasp selection to downstream task outcomes.
- A Failure Attribution Discriminator trained with weak pseudo-labels, predicting whether a failure is due to task-irrelevant grasp, unstable grasp, or planning insufficiency.
- A Grasp-Conditioned Diagnostic Representation Space combined with neighborhood priors to generalize failure attribution to unseen grasps and fuse with the discriminator's prediction for robust diagnosis.
- A diagnosis-driven bidirectional optimization strategy that routes targeted updates: augmenting grasp scores with task priors and risk penalties, and reshaping training data and reweighting policy objectives for planning-side improvement.

## Results

- GTP-FA consistently improved terminal success rates across five base learners in ManiSkill3 simulations, with an absolute gain of up to 54.0 percentage points (Behavior Cloning) and 31.3 percentage points (PPO).
- On a real Franka Research 3 robot, GTP-FA-π_0.5 achieved an average task-level success rate of **76.8%** across five long-horizon tasks, an absolute increase of 65.6 percentage points compared to the original π_0.5 baseline (**11.2%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the full attribution-guided bidirectional optimization is critical, as planning-only, grasp-only, or naive joint optimization approaches generally performed worse than the full framework or the baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly attributing failures to either grasping or planning is crucial for effective and stable optimization in coupled robotic manipulation systems.
> - Naive joint optimization without specific failure attribution can be unstable and ineffective, potentially misassigning optimization pressure.
> - Integrating task-level priors and diagnostic risk assessment into grasp selection significantly improves task compatibility and downstream execution reliability.
