# 3D FlowMatch Actor: Unified 3D Policy for Single- and Dual-Arm Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The 3D FlowMatch Actor (3DFA) introduces a unified 3D policy that drastically accelerates the training and inference of 3D generative models for robot manipulation. It achieves state-of-the-art performance in both single- and dual-arm tasks by leveraging Flow Matching and system-level optimizations, reducing training time by over 30x and increasing inference speed to 18.2Hz on benchmarks like PerAct2.

## Problem

- Prior 3D diffusion-based robot policies suffered from prohibitively slow inference speeds and extremely long training times, hindering real-time deployment and research.
- Learning robust and generalizable coordinated policies for bimanual manipulation is challenging, with existing approaches struggling with generalization and precise spatiotemporal coordination.
- Current robotic systems often rely on specialized policies or multi-stage pipelines for different task types, adding complexity and limiting versatility.

## Method

- Replaced the ==Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)== in 3DDA with a Flow Matching approach, specifically Rectified Flow, for trajectory generation, significantly reducing the required denoising steps.
- Extended the architecture to handle bimanual manipulation by tokenizing noisy trajectory estimates and proprioceptive information for both arms jointly within a unified 3D Relative Denoising Transformer.
- Implemented system-level optimizations, including accelerated dataloading, Density-Biased Sampling (DBS), mixed-precision training, and CUDA graph compilation, to boost efficiency.

## Results

- Achieved over **30x** faster training (from 21 days to 16 hours) and **30x** faster inference (from 0.5Hz to 18.2Hz) on the PerAct2 bimanual benchmark compared to previous 3D diffusion policies.
- Set a new state of the art on the PerAct2 bimanual benchmark with an **85.1%** average success rate, outperforming π0-keypose by **41.4%** absolute with nearly 1000 times fewer parameters.
- Demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on the HiveFormer 74-task benchmark (**90.3%** success rate) for planner-free dense trajectory prediction, and showed superior real-world performance over π0 and iDP3 on bimanual tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Flow Matching offers a superior generative modeling paradigm for robotics compared to DDPMs, enabling significant inference speedups with minimal performance degradation (e.g., 5 steps vs. 100 steps).
> - Explicit 3D visual representations remain highly effective for precise robot manipulation, outperforming large 2D vision-language-action models even with vastly fewer parameters, especially for complex 3D interactions.
> - Meticulous system-level optimizations, alongside advanced generative models, are crucial for making powerful, 3D-aware robot policies practical for real-time deployment and reducing development cycles.
