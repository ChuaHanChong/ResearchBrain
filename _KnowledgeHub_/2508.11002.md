# 3D FlowMatch Actor: Unified 3D Policy for Single- and Dual-Arm Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The 3D FlowMatch Actor (3DFA) introduces a unified 3D policy that drastically accelerates the training and inference of 3D generative models for robot manipulation. It achieves state-of-the-art performance in both single- and dual-arm tasks by leveraging Flow Matching and system-level optimizations, reducing training time by over 30x and increasing inference speed to 18.2Hz on benchmarks like PerAct2.

## Problem

- Prior 3D diffusion-based robot policies suffered from prohibitively slow inference speeds and extremely long training times, hindering real-time deployment and research.
- Learning robust and generalizable coordinated policies for bimanual manipulation is challenging, with existing approaches struggling with generalization and precise spatiotemporal coordination.
- Current robotic systems often rely on specialized policies or multi-stage pipelines for different task types, adding complexity and limiting versatility.

## Method

- Replaced the ==Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)== in 3DDA with a Flow Matching approach, specifically Rectified Flow, for trajectory generation, significantly reducing the required denoising steps.
- Extended the architecture to handle bimanual manipulation by tokenizing noisy trajectory estimates and proprioceptive information for both arms jointly within a unified 3D Relative Denoising Transformer.
- Implemented system-level optimizations, including accelerated dataloading, Density-Biased Sampling (DBS), mixed-precision training, and CUDA graph compilation, to boost efficiency.

## Results

- Achieved over **30x** faster training (from 21 days to 16 hours) and **30x** faster inference (from 0.5Hz to 18.2Hz) on the PerAct2 bimanual benchmark compared to previous 3D diffusion policies.
- Set a new state of the art on the PerAct2 bimanual benchmark with an **85.1%** average success rate, outperforming π0-keypose by **41.4%** absolute with nearly 1000 times fewer parameters.
- Demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on the HiveFormer 74-task benchmark (**90.3%** success rate) for planner-free dense trajectory prediction, and showed superior real-world performance over π0 and iDP3 on bimanual tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Flow Matching offers a superior generative modeling paradigm for robotics compared to DDPMs, enabling significant inference speedups with minimal performance degradation (e.g., 5 steps vs. 100 steps).
> - Explicit 3D visual representations remain highly effective for precise robot manipulation, outperforming large 2D vision-language-action models even with vastly fewer parameters, especially for complex 3D interactions.
> - Meticulous system-level optimizations, alongside advanced generative models, are crucial for making powerful, 3D-aware robot policies practical for real-time deployment and reducing development cycles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is rapidly advancing, moving beyond simple pick-and-place tasks performed by single-arm systems. The broader research landscape is increasingly focused on enabling robots to perform complex, dexterous, and coordinated tasks, often inspired by human capabilities. This paper directly addresses several critical challenges within this evolving landscape:

**a. The Rise of Bimanual Manipulation:** While single-arm robots have seen success, many real-world tasks require the coordinated action of two arms. Bimanual manipulation offers greater dexterity and ability to handle complex objects or scenarios (e.g., carrying large items, stabilizing an object while operating on it). However, it introduces significant challenges in spatiotemporal precision and coordination, making policy learning substantially more difficult. This work positions itself as a significant step forward in robust bimanual policy learning, moving beyond existing approaches that struggle with generalization.

**b. The Impact of Generative Models, Specifically Diffusion Models:** Recent years have witnessed the transformative power of generative models, particularly diffusion models, in various AI domains, including robotics. In single-arm manipulation, diffusion models have proven adept at capturing multimodal behaviors, achieving high-precision action prediction through 3D scene understanding, and showing impressive generalization. Models like 3D Diffuser Actor (3DDA) pioneered the combination of diffusion-based action generation with 3D scene representations. This paper recognizes this success and explicitly aims to extend these powerful techniques to bimanual scenarios.

**c. Addressing the Efficiency Bottleneck of Diffusion Models:** A critical barrier to the widespread adoption of diffusion models in real-world robotics has been their computational cost. Traditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) require numerous iterative denoising steps during inference, leading to slow control frequencies unsuitable for real-time interaction. Similarly, their training times can be prohibitively long. This paper directly confronts this "efficiency bottleneck," seeking to make these powerful generative policies practical for deployment.

**d. Emergence of Flow Matching Techniques:** To overcome the efficiency issues of DDPMs, the paper leverages Flow Matching, a newer generative modeling paradigm. Flow Matching offers a way to generate data by solving an optimal transport problem between distributions, often resulting in significantly faster sampling (fewer steps) compared to DDPMs. This aligns with a growing trend in the generative modeling community to find more efficient sampling strategies for high-quality generation. The paper explores the application of Rectified Flow, a specific instantiation of flow matching, to robot policy learning.

**e. The Importance of 3D Scene Representations vs. 2D Vision:** The paper strongly advocates for the use of 3D pretrained visual scene representations. While foundational 2D vision-language-action (VLA) models (like π0) have shown remarkable capabilities, they can sometimes struggle with precise 3D relationships, occlusions, or scenarios where depth information is critical. By explicitly grounding visual and action tokens in a common 3D coordinate frame, 3DFA continues the lineage of 3D-aware policies that leverage geometric priors for more robust and precise manipulation.

**f. Towards Generalist and Planner-Free Robotics:** The development of a unified policy capable of handling both single- and dual-arm tasks, and predicting both sparse keyposes and dense end-effector trajectories, signals a move towards more generalist robotic systems. The ability to predict dense trajectories directly eliminates the need for separate motion planners (e.g., RRT), which can simplify the control architecture, reduce computational overhead at runtime, and enable more nuanced, continuous interactions that planners might not account for (e.g., following a door's rotational arc).

In essence, 3DFA represents an evolutionary leap in robot manipulation by making cutting-edge 3D diffusion-based policies practical for bimanual and complex tasks, bridging the gap between powerful generative models and the demands of real-time robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of 3D FlowMatch Actor (3DFA) is driven by a clear set of objectives, motivated by the limitations of existing state-of-the-art robot manipulation policies, particularly those based on 3D diffusion models.

**a. Overcome Efficiency Bottlenecks of 3D Diffusion Policies:**
 - **Motivation:** Prior work, especially 3D Diffuser Actor (3DDA), demonstrated the power of 3D diffusion models for precise manipulation. However, 3DDA suffered from prohibitively slow inference speeds (e.g., 0.5Hz) and extremely long training times (e.g., 21 days for a benchmark). These limitations make such policies unsuitable for real-time robotic deployment, where rapid response is critical, and hinder iterative research and adaptation to new tasks.
 - **Objective:** The primary goal is to drastically accelerate both the training and inference times of 3D diffusion-based policies by orders of magnitude, without compromising performance.

**b. Enable Robust and Generalizable Bimanual Manipulation:**
 - **Motivation:** While bimanual systems offer enhanced dexterity for complex tasks, learning coordinated policies for them is inherently more challenging than for single-arm systems. Existing bimanual approaches often struggle with robust generalization across diverse tasks and precise spatiotemporal coordination.
 - **Objective:** To adapt state-of-the-art single-arm 3D generative policies to effectively handle bimanual manipulation tasks, establishing new benchmarks for success rates and robustness.

**c. Develop a Unified Policy Architecture:**
 - **Motivation:** Current robotic systems often rely on specialized policies or multi-stage pipelines for different task types (e.g., single-arm vs. dual-arm, keypose prediction vs. full trajectory generation). This complexity adds overhead and limits versatility.
 - **Objective:** To create a single, general-purpose framework capable of addressing both unimanual and bimanual manipulation, and flexible enough to predict either sparse keyposes or dense end-effector trajectories, thus simplifying the robot control architecture.

**d. Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse Benchmarks:**
 - **Motivation:** Advancements in robot learning require empirical validation against challenging and standardized benchmarks, as well as real-world evaluations.
 - **Objective:** To demonstrate superior performance over existing strong baselines on leading bimanual (PerAct2) and unimanual (PerAct, HiveFormer) simulation benchmarks, and to validate this performance on challenging real-world bimanual tasks. This includes outperforming models with significantly more parameters or extensive pretraining, highlighting the value of 3D-aware design choices.

**e. Facilitate Planner-Free Trajectory Generation:**
 - **Motivation:** The reliance on external motion planners (like RRT) to interpolate between predicted keyposes can introduce limitations. Planners might not account for complex object kinematics or continuous interactions, leading to unnatural or unfeasible motions.
 - **Objective:** To enable direct, planner-free prediction of dense end-effector trajectories, allowing for more compliant and accurate execution, particularly for tasks requiring continuous interaction with the environment.

In summary, 3DFA is motivated by the need for powerful, yet practical, 3D-aware generative policies that can efficiently tackle the complexities of both single- and dual-arm robot manipulation, moving towards a more unified and deployment-ready robotics paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 3D FlowMatch Actor (3DFA) is engineered as an efficient and unified 3D policy for robot manipulation, built upon the foundation of the 3D Diffuser Actor (3DDA) architecture while introducing significant innovations in generative modeling and system-level optimizations.

**a. Foundational Architecture (Building on 3DDA):**
3DFA inherits the core architectural principles of 3DDA, a 3D diffusion policy that learns from demonstrations. Its central idea is to frame trajectory generation as a denoising process. The model conditions its predictions on:
- **Multi-view RGB-D Observations:** Visual information from posed cameras and sensed depth maps are processed.
- **Language Instructions:** Task-specific instructions are encoded into latent embeddings.
- **Proprioception History:** Information about the robot's current and past joint states or end-effector poses.
All these inputs are converted into 3D tokens. 2D image features are lifted to 3D by calculating average 3D locations for feature patches. Noisy action tokens and proprioception tokens are also grounded in 3D. A key component is the **3D Relative Denoising Transformer**, which fuses all 3D tokens (visual, action, proprioception) using 3D relative attention, augmented with standard attention for language tokens. Finally, MLPs decode the refined trajectory tokens into end-effector translations, rotations, and gripper states.

**b. Core Generative Modeling Shift: From DDPM to Flow Matching:**
The most substantial architectural change in 3DFA is replacing 3DDA's Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)-based diffusion mechanism with a **Flow Matching** approach, specifically **Rectified Flow**.
- **Rectified Flow (RF):** Instead of iteratively denoising Gaussian noise, RF transforms a sample from a simple source distribution (e.g., Gaussian noise) to a complex target data distribution (real robot actions) by following straight-line paths in the sample space. This is governed by an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) that describes a continuous, time-differentiable trajectory.
- **Velocity Field Prediction:** During training, the model learns to approximate a time-dependent velocity field that points directly from an interpolated noisy sample (between noise and the true action) towards the true action. This is achieved by minimizing an L2 loss between the predicted velocity field and the ideal straight-line velocity field. Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss supervises gripper openness.
- **Accelerated Inference:** This shift drastically reduces the number of denoising steps required during inference. While DDPMs typically need 100 steps, Flow Matching allows 3DFA to achieve comparable performance with as few as 5 steps, or even 3 or 1 step with minor performance degradation, leading to significant inference speedups.

**c. Extension to Bimanual Manipulation:**
To support dual-arm control, 3DFA redefines robot actions to include separate end-effector poses for both left and right arms.
- Noisy trajectory estimates and proprioceptive information for both arms (τᵢ,L, τᵢ,R and cL, cR) are tokenized independently.
- These arm-specific 3D tokens are then jointly processed by the same 3D Relative Denoising Transformer, allowing the model to learn coordinated actions between the two arms within a unified framework.
- During training, the loss function is extended to supervise the velocity fields and gripper openness for both arms simultaneously.
- A key detail: Positional embeddings for 3D relative attention are computed in the global unnormalized frame, while trajectory predictions are done in per-arm normalized space, preventing spatial distribution compression that would harm bimanual performance.

**d. System-Level and Architectural Optimizations for Efficiency:**
Beyond Flow Matching, 3DFA incorporates a suite of practical optimizations to further boost training and inference efficiency:
- **Accelerated Dataloading:**
 - **Random Keypose Sampling:** Instead of loading entire episodes, which often involves unused data, 3DFA samples keyposes randomly across episodes. This is managed efficiently using the `zarr` Python library for lazy loading and simultaneous access to data indices across all episodes, ensuring batch diversity and fixed batch sizes.
 - **Optimized Data Types:** Images are loaded as `uint8` and depth maps as half-precision, converting them to `float32` only after loading onto the GPU. This significantly reduces data loading time.
 - **GPU-Accelerated Preprocessing:** Depth unprojection to point clouds and data augmentations are performed directly on the GPU, leveraging faster, batched operations.
- **Faster Point Sampling:** Replaces Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) with **Density-Biased Sampling (DBS)** for sparsifying 3D scene tokens. DBS promotes sampling in sparser neighborhoods and can be implemented in a fast, batched PyTorch version, halving the cost associated with this step.
- **Mixed-Precision Training:** Enables automatic casting of operations to half-precision where possible, reducing memory footprint and allowing for larger batch sizes during training.
- **Efficient Attention Implementation:** Utilizes modern PyTorch implementations of attention (likely leveraging optimized C++ backends like FlashAttention) that are significantly faster, especially with larger batch sizes and half-precision.
- **CUDA Graph Compilation:** Compiles the model's non-dynamic operations into a static graph, allowing for runtime optimizations. This required refactoring the original 3DDA codebase to remove dynamic logic branches, CPU operations, in-graph language tokenization, and custom kernel operations that prevented graph compilation.

This comprehensive approach, combining a powerful new generative modeling technique with meticulous system-level optimizations, enables 3DFA to achieve unprecedented efficiency without compromising the high performance characteristic of 3D-aware policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for 3DFA demonstrate significant advancements in efficiency and performance across various robot manipulation benchmarks, both simulated and real-world.

**a. Dramatic Efficiency Gains:**
The most striking findings revolve around the speed improvements:
- **Training Time Reduction:** 3DFA reduces training time by over 30× compared to previous 3D diffusion-based policies. For instance, on the PerAct2 bimanual benchmark, training time was cut from approximately 21 days for an adapted 3DDA to just 16 hours for 3DFA (using 4 L40S GPUs).
- **Inference Speed Acceleration:** Inference speed increased by over 30×. On PerAct2, 3DDA's 0.5Hz inference rate was boosted to 18.2Hz for 3DFA (on a single L40S GPU), making real-time deployment feasible.
- **Flow Matching Robustness:** Ablation studies confirm that replacing DDPM with Flow Matching is crucial. Flow matching maintains peak performance (85.1% SR) even with only 5 denoising steps, and respectable performance (83.9% with 3 steps, 75.2% with 1 step), while DDPM requires 100 steps and DDIM suffers sharp performance drops with fewer steps.
- **Impact of Optimizations:** Each system-level optimization contributed significantly: optimized data loading reduced training time by two-thirds initially, and combined optimizations yielded the 10× speedup on top of the flow matching benefits, reaching the total 30× speedup. For inference, Flow Matching provided a 9× speedup, and replacing FPS with DBS doubled the control frequency by halving scene encoding cost.

**b. State-of-the-Art Bimanual Manipulation (PerAct2 Benchmark):**
- 3DFA establishes a new state of the art on the challenging PerAct2 bimanual simulation benchmark. It achieves an average success rate of 85.1%, outperforming the next-best method, π0-keypose, by an absolute margin of 41.4%.
- Notably, 3DFA, with only 3.8 million parameters, significantly outperforms π0-keypose, which has nearly 1000 times more parameters and extensive pretraining. This highlights the effectiveness of 3D-aware policies over purely 2D, large-scale pretraining for precise 3D manipulation.
- When compared to other strong baselines (ACT, RVT-LF, PerAct-LF, PerAct2, DP3, KStarDiffuser, PPI, AnyBimanual), 3DFA shows large absolute improvements across individual tasks.

**c. High Performance in Unimanual Settings:**
- **PerAct Benchmark (18 tasks):** 3DFA achieves performance on par with 3DDA. Critically, it does so with a 28× faster inference speed and requires 6× less training time when using four cameras. Further optimization to two cameras results in 14× less training time and 30× faster inference with only a minor performance drop.
- **HiveFormer 74-task Benchmark (Planner-Free Trajectory Prediction):** 3DFA sets a new state of the art on this benchmark with a success rate of 90.3%, surpassing Act3D by 7.3%.
 - On a subset of 8 particularly challenging tasks requiring continuous interaction (e.g., "unplug charger," "open fridge"), 3DFA outperforms the two-stage ChainedDiffuser by 6.8% and PointFlowMatch by 23.5% absolute. This demonstrates its strong capability for direct, dense trajectory prediction, eliminating the need for motion planning.

**d. Real-World Validation:**
- 3DFA was evaluated on a custom 10-task bimanual benchmark using the Mobile ALOHA platform. It consistently outperformed two strong baselines: π0 and iDP3.
- Despite π0 being a 2D policy pretrained on 10,000 hours of robot demonstration data and iDP3 being a variant of DP3 for humanoid systems, 3DFA (3.8M parameters, 54ms inference) achieved higher success rates across most tasks compared to π0 (3238M parameters, 100ms inference) and iDP3 (68.8M parameters, 420ms inference).
- Real-world tests confirmed that π0 struggled with occlusions and precise object localization, while iDP3 often failed due to imprecise grasping. 3DFA showed greater consistency in these challenging real-world scenarios.

**e. Ablation Studies:**
Comprehensive ablation studies rigorously validate the contribution of each design choice, showing how flow matching and various system-level optimizations cumulatively lead to the observed efficiency and performance gains.

In conclusion, the main findings highlight 3DFA as a highly efficient and effective unified 3D policy that sets new performance standards in both bimanual and unimanual manipulation, validating its design choices through extensive empirical evaluation across diverse benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of 3D FlowMatch Actor (3DFA) carries profound significance and offers several potential impacts on the field of robotics and AI.

**a. Enabling Real-Time, Practical Robot Deployment:**
The most immediate and significant impact of 3DFA is its ability to dramatically accelerate both the training and inference of 3D diffusion-based policies. Reducing training from weeks to hours and increasing inference speed to real-time (18.2Hz) removes a major bottleneck that has hindered the practical deployment of powerful generative models in robotics. This paves the way for robots to perform complex tasks in dynamic, unstructured environments where rapid decision-making and continuous control are essential. It also enables faster iteration cycles for researchers and developers.

**b. Advancing Generalist Robotics and Unified Policies:**
3DFA's design as a unified framework for both single- and dual-arm manipulation, capable of predicting either sparse keyposes or dense end-effector trajectories, marks a significant step towards generalist robot policies. By consolidating different control paradigms into a single architecture, it reduces the complexity of robot control systems, promotes reusability, and facilitates the development of robots that can adapt to a wider range of tasks without requiring specialized models. This aligns with the broader vision of creating more versatile and autonomous robotic agents.

**c. Reaffirming the Power of 3D Visual Representations:**
Despite the rise of massive 2D vision-language-action (VLA) models pretrained on vast datasets (like π0), 3DFA demonstrates that explicit 3D scene understanding remains a powerful inductive bias for robotic manipulation. Its superior performance, even with a significantly smaller parameter count, underscores that incorporating geometric and spatial reasoning directly into the policy architecture is highly effective for tasks requiring precise 3D interaction. This reinforces the importance of research into robust 3D perception and scene understanding for embodied AI.

**d. Democratizing Advanced Generative Models in Robotics:**
By making 3D diffusion policies significantly more efficient, 3DFA makes these powerful techniques more accessible to a wider range of researchers and applications. The lower computational burden for training and inference reduces hardware requirements and allows more researchers to experiment with and build upon these advanced generative models, potentially accelerating innovation in robot learning.

**e. Simplifying the Robot Control Stack with Planner-Free Trajectory Prediction:**
The capability of 3DFA to directly predict dense end-effector trajectories, rather than relying on external motion planners to interpolate between keyposes, simplifies the robot's control stack. This "planner-free" approach can lead to more natural, continuous, and compliant robot motions, especially for tasks that involve complex interactions with objects (e.g., opening a drawer along its arc, extracting a charger smoothly). It removes a potential source of errors and computational overhead associated with traditional planning modules.

**f. Foundation for Future Research and Industrial Adoption:**
3DFA provides a strong baseline and a practical framework for future research in several directions. It highlights the potential for further integration of advanced generative models (like Flow Matching) with robust 3D perception. The collaboration with NVIDIA hints at the potential for industrial adoption, as efficient, high-performance robot policies are crucial for automation in manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics. Identified limitations, such as dependence on accurate depth sensing and calibration, and challenges with fine force control, open clear avenues for future work, including hybrid 2D/3D architectures, learning from noisy depth, or integrating force feedback.

In summary, 3DFA represents a significant leap towards developing high-performance, real-time, and general-purpose robot manipulation policies. Its dual focus on efficiency and 3D-awareness not only pushes the state of the art but also makes these advanced capabilities viable for real-world robotic applications, setting a new standard for how complex tasks can be learned and executed by autonomous systems.
