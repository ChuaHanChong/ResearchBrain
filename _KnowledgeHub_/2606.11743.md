# TacCoRL: Integrating Tactile Feedback into VLA via Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TacCoRL integrates tactile feedback into pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies using a simulation-to-real pipeline to improve robot performance in contact-rich manipulation. The system learns contact-guided corrective behaviors from simulated "near-failure" states, increasing average real-world success rates from 50.0% (vision-only) to 72.5% across tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often fail in contact-rich manipulation tasks due to visual occlusions and ambiguities, which prevent them from capturing crucial local contact states.
- Collecting large-scale, diverse real-world tactile demonstrations is costly, time-consuming, and typically lacks crucial 'near-failure' states necessary for learning corrective behaviors.
- Actively exploring and collecting data for off-nominal contact scenarios on physical robots is risky, expensive, and involves slow, costly resets.

## Method

- A two-stage ==sim-to-real pipeline== integrates tactile feedback into existing VLA policies, underpinned by meticulous alignment of controller dynamics, camera poses, and tactile sensor statistics between simulation and reality.
- The VLA policy is augmented with a tactile encoder and a dual-path tactile fusion mechanism, employing a contact-aware gate to condition actions based on a history of tactile readings.
- The policy is initially warm-started with sim-real co-training and then refined through reinforcement learning (RL) post-training in simulation, which is anchored by real-world data to prevent simulator overfitting.

## Results

- The calibrated simulator closely matched real-robot controller responses and exhibited similar active-taxel reading distributions from matched contact rollouts.
- RL post-training in simulation increased average success rates from **40.5%** (after co-training) to **78.5%** for visuo-tactile policies, significantly outperforming vision-only policies.
- Real-world evaluation demonstrated that integrating tactile feedback boosted the average success rate of post-trained policies from **50.0%** (vision-only) to **72.5%** across four bimanual contact-rich manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate sim-to-real alignment across visual, control, and tactile modalities is fundamental for effectively transferring learned contact-guided behaviors from simulation to physical robots.
> - Combining sim-real co-training for initialization with real-data-anchored RL post-training effectively enables learning critical error recovery actions in simulation that generalize to real-world tasks.
> - Tactile feedback proves essential for correcting localized contact errors, especially when visual information is ambiguous or occluded, leading to significantly enhanced robustness in manipulation tasks.
