# TacCoRL: Integrating Tactile Feedback into VLA via Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TacCoRL integrates tactile feedback into pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies using a simulation-to-real pipeline to improve robot performance in contact-rich manipulation. The system learns contact-guided corrective behaviors from simulated "near-failure" states, increasing average real-world success rates from 50.0% (vision-only) to 72.5% across tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often fail in contact-rich manipulation tasks due to visual occlusions and ambiguities, which prevent them from capturing crucial local contact states.
- Collecting large-scale, diverse real-world tactile demonstrations is costly, time-consuming, and typically lacks crucial 'near-failure' states necessary for learning corrective behaviors.
- Actively exploring and collecting data for off-nominal contact scenarios on physical robots is risky, expensive, and involves slow, costly resets.

## Method

- A two-stage ==sim-to-real pipeline== integrates tactile feedback into existing VLA policies, underpinned by meticulous alignment of controller dynamics, camera poses, and tactile sensor statistics between simulation and reality.
- The VLA policy is augmented with a tactile encoder and a dual-path tactile fusion mechanism, employing a contact-aware gate to condition actions based on a history of tactile readings.
- The policy is initially warm-started with sim-real co-training and then refined through reinforcement learning (RL) post-training in simulation, which is anchored by real-world data to prevent simulator overfitting.

## Results

- The calibrated simulator closely matched real-robot controller responses and exhibited similar active-taxel reading distributions from matched contact rollouts.
- RL post-training in simulation increased average success rates from **40.5%** (after co-training) to **78.5%** for visuo-tactile policies, significantly outperforming vision-only policies.
- Real-world evaluation demonstrated that integrating tactile feedback boosted the average success rate of post-trained policies from **50.0%** (vision-only) to **72.5%** across four bimanual contact-rich manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate sim-to-real alignment across visual, control, and tactile modalities is fundamental for effectively transferring learned contact-guided behaviors from simulation to physical robots.
> - Combining sim-real co-training for initialization with real-data-anchored RL post-training effectively enables learning critical error recovery actions in simulation that generalize to real-world tasks.
> - Tactile feedback proves essential for correcting localized contact errors, especially when visual information is ambiguous or occluded, leading to significantly enhanced robustness in manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is advancing through the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models provide robust visual representations, language grounding, and action priors, primarily derived from large-scale pretraining datasets. This foundation is valuable for a range of robotic tasks. However, for contact-rich manipulation scenarios, such as insertion, assembly, or in-hand manipulation, visual observations alone frequently prove insufficient. Cameras may not reliably capture critical local contact states, including precise alignment, contact locations, or distributed pressure, often due to occlusions or inherent visual ambiguities.

Tactile sensing emerges as a complementary modality, offering information about local contact location, pressure distribution, and misalignment, which is not readily available through vision. Prior research has explored tactile capabilities through various approaches, including the creation of large tactile datasets, the development of dedicated tactile-language-action models, or the use of inference-time tactile guidance.

This work, TacCoRL, differentiates itself by focusing on integrating tactile feedback into an *existing* VLA backbone. Its objective is to augment the capabilities of already pretrained VLA policies rather than requiring extensive tactile pretraining or the development of entirely new tactile-specific policies from scratch. The authors address the challenges associated with collecting real-world tactile demonstrations, particularly the rarity of "near-failure" states that are crucial for learning corrective behaviors but are costly and risky to collect on physical hardware.

The method leverages simulation as a primary learning environment for contact interaction. This approach aligns with the broader trend of sim-to-real transfer and post-training methods in robotics, which aim to refine learned policies beyond static demonstrations. TacCoRL contributes to this landscape by proposing a specific sim-to-real post-training framework designed for adapting existing VLA policies to contact-rich manipulation tasks, incorporating a feedback loop within a real-aligned simulated environment. It combines elements of sim-real co-training and reinforcement learning, with a mechanism to anchor the policy to real-world data distributions during refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of robot manipulation in contact-rich tasks by integrating tactile feedback into existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. The authors identify several key motivations for pursuing this objective:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Vision-Only VLA Models:** While VLA models provide strong foundational priors, their reliance on visual observations alone often renders them inadequate for tasks requiring precise contact interactions. Visual sensors frequently suffer from occlusions (e.g., by the gripper or manipulated object) and inherent ambiguities, preventing them from capturing crucial local contact states, such as fine alignment, contact points, or pressure distribution. Tactile feedback is essential for resolving these localized interactions.

2. **Overcoming Challenges of Real-World Tactile Data Collection:**
 - **Cost and Scalability:** Collecting large-scale, diverse real-world robot demonstrations, especially those incorporating tactile feedback, is resource-intensive and difficult to scale.
 - **Data Distribution Bias:** Real-world demonstrations tend to emphasize successful, nominal task executions. They typically underrepresent "near-failure" states (e.g., slight misalignments, contact on incorrect surfaces, unstable grasps) where corrective actions guided by tactile feedback are most critical.
 - **Risk and Expense of Off-Nominal Exploration:** Actively exploring and collecting data for these critical off-nominal scenarios on physical hardware is risky, potentially leading to sensor damage, and involves slow, costly resets.

3. **Leveraging Simulation for Learning Off-Nominal Contact Behaviors:** To circumvent the difficulties of real-world data collection for critical near-failure states, the authors are motivated to utilize simulation. A properly aligned simulated environment offers a safe, resettable, and verifiable platform where contact conditions can be systematically varied. This allows for closed-loop learning of how tactile readings should modulate action responses, particularly in error recovery situations that are rare in successful demonstrations.

Based on these motivations, the research outlines three specific contributions:

1. **Development of a Tactile-Conditioning Interface:** To adapt pretrained VLA policies for tactile input without necessitating large-scale tactile pretraining or training new tactile policies from scratch.
2. **Design of a Sim-to-Real Post-Training Pipeline:** This pipeline integrates sim-real co-training with verifiable rewards in simulation, anchored by real data, to learn contact-guided action refinements that transfer to the physical robot.
3. **Real-Robot Evaluation:** To demonstrate improved success and robustness of the developed visuo-tactile policies compared to vision-only and imitation-only baselines across several bimanual contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TacCoRL framework integrates tactile feedback into pretrained VLA policies through a two-stage sim-to-real pipeline. This pipeline involves initial co-training followed by reinforcement learning (RL) refinement, both leveraging a meticulously aligned simulation environment.

**Policy Input and Output:** At each time step $t$, the policy receives a language instruction $\ell$, visual observations $o_v_t$, proprioceptive data $q_t$, and tactile readings $o_τ_t$. It then predicts an $H$-step action chunk $A_t = a_{t:t+H-1}$.

### 4. Simulation Environment Alignment (Section 3.1)

A cornerstone of the methodology is ensuring that the simulated environment accurately reflects the real-world setup, enabling effective closed-loop learning and transfer. This alignment focuses on three policy-facing interfaces:
- **Scene Configuration and Camera Alignment:** Visual task contexts are matched between simulation and reality. Camera extrinsics are calibrated offline by optimizing pose offsets around nominal camera mounts using synchronized real and simulated episodes.
- **Controller Response:** The dynamics of how robot actions translate into robot state changes are aligned. This is achieved through controller system identification (SysID). Short single-joint frequency sweeps are recorded on the real robot and replayed in simulation. The simulated MIT-style impedance controller parameters (proportional gain $K_p$, derivative gain $K_d$, feed-forward torque $T_{ref}$) are optimized using Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA) to minimize the difference in target-tracking errors and response speeds between the real and simulated joints.
- **Tactile Statistics Alignment:** The characteristics of contact perception are aligned. Tactile readings, represented as flattened taxel arrays, are modeled in simulation using a spring-damper model, mapping penetration depth and rate to a normal contact reading ($f^{sim}_{t,k} = [k_n \delta_{t,k} + k_d \dot{\delta}_{t,k}]_+$). Parameters ($k_n, k_d$) and policy-facing normalization (e.g., noise threshold $\lambda_{noise}$) are calibrated by replaying matched real-to-sim contact trajectories and comparing active-taxel masks, counts, centroids, histograms, and temporal differences.

### 5. Tactile-Augmented VLA Policy (Section 3.2)

The policy architecture is built upon a $\pi_{0.5}$-style VLA model, which utilizes a vision-language backbone for semantic and visual context and a flow policy-based action expert. The tactile integration involves:
- **Tactile Encoder:** A tactile encoder $E_τ$ processes a recent history window of tactile readings $h_τ_t = o_{τ_{t-L+1:t}}$. This history (length $L$, $K$ taxels) captures the evolution of contact (loading, release).
- **Projection to Tokens:** The encoded tactile history is projected via $W_τ$ into $M$ tactile tokens $Z_τ_t$, with a hidden size $d$.
- **Contact-Aware Gating:** A binary contact gate $c_t$ is implemented to suppress tactile token influence when only background noise is present. The gate is active if the maximum number of taxels with readings above an activation threshold $\lambda_f$ within the history window exceeds a count threshold $m$. When the gate is inactive, tactile tokens are removed.
- **Dual-Path Tactile Fusion:** The gated tactile tokens ($\tilde{Z}_τ_t$) condition the model through two paths:
 1. **Context Modulation:** Cross-attention allows $\tilde{Z}_τ_t$ to update the vision-language-proprioception context tokens ($\tilde{Z}^{base}_t$).
 2. **Action Expert Conditioning:** The $\tilde{Z}_τ_t$ tokens are directly concatenated with the modulated base tokens ($[\tilde{Z}^{base}_t, \tilde{Z}_τ_t]$) and made available as conditioning tokens for the action expert at each denoising step of the flow policy.
This design maintains the VLA's pretrained behavior in the absence of contact while enabling tactile evidence to modify the action chunk during contact interactions.

### 6. Sim-Real Co-Training (Section 3.3)

This initial stage warm-starts the tactile-augmented policy.
- **Data Aggregation:** The process utilizes real-world demonstrations ($D_{real}$) and simulated demonstrations ($D_{sim}$). $D_{sim}$ comprises human tele-operation data ($D^{teleop}_{sim}$) and additional demonstrations synthesized using MimicGen under varied initial object configurations ($D^{Mimic}_{sim}$), filtered for success.
- **Co-Training Objective:** The policy is trained using a conditional flow-matching loss ($\ell_{flow}$) on demonstrated action chunks. The co-training objective $L_{co}(\theta)$ is a weighted sum of losses from simulated and real datasets: $L_{co}(\theta) = \alpha E_{(x,A^*) \sim D_{sim}}[\ell_{flow}(\theta; x, A^*)] + (1-\alpha) E_{(x,A^*) \sim D_{real}}[\ell_{flow}(\theta; x, A^*)]$. The coefficient $\alpha$ balances the contribution of simulated data, aiming to preserve task and contact structure without diluting real-world cues.
- **Purpose:** This stage provides the policy with an initial tactile-conditioned action prior grounded in both simulated and real observations, serving as a viable initialization for subsequent RL.

### 7. Post-Training with a Real-Data Anchor (Section 3.4)

This stage refines the policy using on-policy simulation rollouts.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Standard Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used, with sparse task-level success and failure predicates defining rewards. This encourages learning successful contact behaviors through interactive exploration in simulation.
- **Real-Data Anchor:** To prevent the policy from overfitting to simulator specifics or drifting away from the real-world distribution, the RL objective is optimized jointly with a supervised real-data anchor. The combined loss is $L_{RL}(\theta, \omega) = L_{PPO}(\theta, \omega; E_{sim}(\psi)) + \beta E_{(x,A^*) \sim D_{real}}[\ell_{flow}(\theta; x, A^*)]$. Here, $\omega$ represents critic parameters, $L_{PPO}$ is the clipped PPO loss, and $\beta$ weights the real-data behavior cloning loss.
- **Deployment:** After post-training, the critic, reward functions, and privileged simulation states are discarded, and the refined policy is directly deployed to the real robot.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate the efficacy of TacCoRL across several contact-rich bimanual tasks: Test Tube Insertion, Do Puzzle, Assembly #1, and Assembly #2.

#### 8.1. Controller and Tactile Alignment (Section 4.1)

- **Controller Response:** Through system identification (SysID), the calibrated simulator demonstrated close agreement with the real robot's trajectory and tracking error during single-joint sweeps (Figure 4A). This alignment was particularly noted in joints where gravity compensation mismatch was initially significant, indicating improved fidelity for action execution.
- **Tactile Statistics:** Following tactile calibration, the normalized active-taxel reading distributions from matched real and simulated contact rollouts exhibited similar patterns (Figure 4B). This suggests that the simulated contact observation interface provides a reasonable representation of real-world tactile feedback, which is crucial for learning contact-guided behaviors.

#### 8.2. Sim-Real Co-Training and Simulator RL Post-Training (Section 4.3)

- **Co-Training as Initialization:** Direct application of RL from a base VLA model, without prior co-training, resulted in a 0.0% success rate across all tasks in simulation. This indicates that sparse rewards alone are insufficient to bootstrap the complex task sequences and precise contact corrections required. Sim-real co-training effectively provided a strong initialization, raising the average simulator success rate to 33.0% (vision-only) and 40.5% (visuo-tactile) immediately after co-training. With exploration noise, these initial success rates were 22.5% and 29.3% respectively.
- **Tactile Feedback Improves RL:** After RL post-training, the average simulator success rates increased to 60.5% for vision-only policies and 78.5% for visuo-tactile policies (Table 1). Visuo-tactile policies consistently achieved higher success rates than vision-only policies across all tasks during simulator RL (Figure 6). This improvement was particularly pronounced in tasks like Test Tube Insertion and Assembly #2, where visual occlusions and subtle misalignments are common, suggesting that tactile history effectively guided error correction.

#### 8.3. Real-World Evaluation (Section 4.4)

- **Qualitative Transfer:** Real-robot rollouts demonstrated that the post-trained policy utilized tactile feedback to correct local contact errors, such as incremental translations and reorientations, especially when the gripper or held object obscured the target contact region (Figure 5).
- **Quantitative Success:** Tactile feedback significantly increased the average real-world success rate of RL post-trained policies from 50.0% (vision-only) to 72.5% across the four tasks (Table 2).
- **Performance vs. Baselines:** For tasks requiring longer horizons, such as "Do Puzzle," RL post-training with visuo-tactile input achieved a 45% success rate, which was substantially higher than real-only fine-tuning (15%) and sim-real co-training (25%). This indicates the value of on-policy refinement in simulation for robust task completion.

#### 8.4. Ablation of Co-Training and Real-Data Anchoring Weights (Section 4.5)

- **Co-Training Ratio ($\alpha$):** Varying $\alpha$ on the Assembly #2 task showed that a balanced contribution of simulated and real demonstrations during co-training was beneficial. Lowering $\alpha$ from 0.95 to 0.5 improved the best simulator success rate from 42.9% to 70.3% and real-world deployment success from 40% to 45%. When $\alpha = 0$ (no simulated demonstrations during co-training), performance was significantly degraded in both domains (14.1% sim success, 25% real success), indicating that the initial policy was too far from the simulation's interaction distribution for effective post-training.
- **Real-Data Anchor Weight ($\beta$):** With $\alpha=0.5$ fixed, anchor weights of $\beta=0.1$ and $\beta=1.0$ resulted in significant improvements in real-world deployment success (from 45% to 80%) and simulator success (above 92%). The real-data anchor proved crucial in preventing simulator-specific exploitation and drift, ensuring learned corrections were transferable to the real robot. Conversely, an overly strong anchor ($\beta=5.0$) suppressed RL refinement, pushing the policy back towards imitation and leading to reduced success in both simulation and the real world. Absence of the anchor ($\beta=0$) also led to suboptimal performance due to overfitting to simulator peculiarities.

### 9. Significance and Potential Impact

The TacCoRL framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving contact. Its potential impacts are multi-faceted:

- **Practical Integration of Tactile Feedback:** TacCoRL offers a scalable and practical method for incorporating tactile feedback into existing VLA policies. By avoiding the necessity for large-scale tactile-specific pretraining or the development of entirely new tactile policies, the framework lowers the barrier to entry for augmenting robot capabilities with touch, making such enhancements more accessible to a broader range of robotic systems.
- **Enhanced Robustness in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The ability to learn and transfer contact-conditioned corrective actions directly addresses a core limitation of vision-only systems in tasks like assembly and insertion. This leads to policies that exhibit improved robustness and success rates in scenarios where visual occlusion, ambiguity, or fine-grained interaction details are critical for task completion. This impact is directly relevant to industrial automation, service robotics, and other applications requiring dexterous manipulation.
- **Efficient Leveraging of Simulation for Learning:** The methodology demonstrates an effective strategy for utilizing simulation as a primary learning environment for complex contact interactions. By focusing on "near-failure" states that are challenging, costly, or risky to collect in the real world, the framework enables efficient learning of crucial error recovery behaviors. This reduces the reliance on extensive and hazardous real-world exploration, accelerating policy development cycles.
- **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The meticulous approach to simulation-to-real alignment (controller, camera, tactile), combined with a two-stage training paradigm (sim-real co-training followed by RL with a real-data anchor), provides a robust blueprint for bridging the sim-to-real gap for contact-rich tasks. This contributes to the broader research effort in making policies learned in simulation reliably deployable on physical robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations of the current work also highlight important avenues for future research. These include further reducing the need for real tactile data through advanced representation learning and tactile simulation, automating the creation and alignment of task-level digital twins, and extending the framework to handle more complex contact dynamics involving highly deformable objects or fluids. Addressing these areas could broaden the applicability and autonomy of tactile-enhanced robotic systems.
