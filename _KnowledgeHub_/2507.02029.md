# RoboBrain 2.0 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboBrain 2.0, an embodied vision-language foundation model from BAAI, integrates perception, reasoning, and planning to empower intelligent interaction in physical environments. It demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across numerous spatial and temporal reasoning benchmarks, including object placement prediction and multi-robot planning, establishing a new baseline for embodied AI capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI models face limitations in accurately understanding spatial relationships, identifying object affordances, and modeling relative and absolute spatial positions in dynamic physical environments.
- A lack of robust temporal modeling hinders current models from performing multi-stage, cross-agent temporal planning and executing robust closed-loop control in interactive scenarios.
- Many models struggle to extract causal logic from complex human instructions and align it with dynamic environmental states, restricting their generalization to open-ended embodied tasks.

## Method

- The system employs a heterogeneous encoder-decoder architecture, utilizing a lightweight vision encoder for dynamic-resolution images/videos and a Qwen2.5-VL-initialized LLM decoder for core reasoning, supporting diverse outputs including text, spatial coordinates, and reasoning traces.
- A massive, meticulously curated training dataset is constructed, spanning general MLLM VQA, detailed spatial understanding (visual grounding, object pointing, affordance, 3D/2D spatial concepts), and complex temporal tasks (ego-view planning, multi-robot coordination, closed-loop interaction).
- A progressive three-stage training strategy is implemented, moving from foundational spatiotemporal learning to embodied spatiotemporal enhancement, and culminating in Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) using the Reason-RFT framework.

## Results

- The 32B variant of RoboBrain 2.0 consistently achieved state-of-the-art or near state-of-the-art results across most spatial reasoning benchmarks, including BLINK (83.63), RoboSpatial (72.43), RefSpatial-Bench Location (54.00), and Where2Place (73.59), often surpassing proprietary models like Gemini and GPT-o4-mini.
- On temporal reasoning benchmarks, RoboBrain 2.0 (7B and 32B variants) demonstrated outstanding performance, achieving SOTA in Multi-Robot Planning (81.50 for 7B), Ego-Plan2 (57.23 for 32B), and RoboBench (72.16 for 7B).
- The lightweight 7B variant also exhibited strong competitive performance across benchmarks, showcasing its versatility and potential for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying perception, reasoning, and planning within a single vision-language foundation model is effective in overcoming long-standing bottlenecks in spatial understanding, temporal modeling, and causal reasoning for embodied AI.
> - Developing a diverse and high-quality embodied dataset, encompassing fine-grained spatial and complex temporal aspects, alongside a multi-stage training curriculum, is crucial for achieving advanced embodied intelligence.
> - Leveraging robust, scalable infrastructure for both large-scale training (FlagScale) and reinforcement fine-tuning (VeRL) significantly contributes to the efficient development and deployment of complex multimodal foundation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of RoboBrain 2.0 is situated at a critical juncture in AI research, aiming to bridge the gap between "digital intelligence" and "physical intelligence." In recent years, Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated extraordinary capabilities in digital domains, excelling in tasks such as multimodal question answering, image generation, GUI control, and video understanding. These advancements have propelled AI into various practical applications, from intelligent assistants to healthcare.

However, a significant frontier remains: enabling AI models to meaningfully interact with and understand the physical world. This "embodied AI" challenge requires models to perceive their surroundings, comprehend complex embodied tasks, and execute actions in real-world environments. Existing embodied foundation models, while making progress, face three fundamental bottlenecks that RoboBrain 2.0 specifically aims to address:
1. **Limited Spatial Understanding:** Current models struggle with accurate modeling of relative and absolute spatial relationships, and the identification of object affordances in dynamic physical environments, which limits their real-world applicability.
2. **Weak Temporal Modeling:** A lack of understanding of multi-stage, cross-agent temporal dependencies and feedback mechanisms hinders long-horizon planning and robust closed-loop control in interactive scenarios.
3. **Insufficient Reasoning Chains:** Many models lack the capacity to extract causal logic from complex human instructions and align this logic with dynamic environmental states, thereby restricting their generalization to open-ended embodied tasks.

RoboBrain 2.0 directly tackles these limitations, positioning itself as a "latest generation" embodied vision-language foundation model designed to unify perception, reasoning, and planning. It builds upon previous efforts in multimodal fusion, perception, and action execution by introducing a more comprehensive architecture, an extensive and diverse training dataset, and a sophisticated multi-stage training strategy. By open-sourcing its components, BAAI aims to accelerate the collective progress towards building truly generalist embodied agents capable of robust and intelligent interaction within complex physical environments. This work contributes significantly by pushing the boundaries of what current multimodal foundation models can achieve in a robotic context, moving beyond purely digital tasks to tangible physical interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RoboBrain 2.0 is to introduce an advanced embodied vision-language foundation model that unifies perception, reasoning, and planning for complex tasks in physical environments. This overarching goal is driven by a clear motivation to overcome the critical limitations observed in existing embodied AI models, as identified in the broader research landscape:

1. **Enhance Spatial Understanding:** A key motivation is to improve the model's ability to accurately perceive and reason about spatial relationships in the physical world. This includes precise affordance prediction (understanding how objects can be interacted with), spatial referring (identifying specific locations or objects based on complex descriptions), and trajectory forecasting (predicting movement paths). The lack of robust spatial understanding in previous models severely limits their practical utility in robotics.
2. **Strengthen Temporal Decision-Making:** The project aims to develop capabilities for effective decision-making over time. This involves enabling closed-loop interaction (where the model can adapt its actions based on real-time feedback), multi-agent long-horizon planning (coordinating actions across multiple robots or over extended periods), and dynamic scene graph updating (maintaining an internal representation of the changing environment). Addressing these temporal challenges is crucial for agents operating in dynamic, interactive settings.
3. **Improve Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** A core objective is to endow the model with stronger reasoning capabilities, allowing it to extract causal logic from complex, open-ended human instructions and align these with the dynamic states of the environment. This aims to move beyond simple command execution to more intelligent, adaptable problem-solving.
4. **Advance Towards Generalist Embodied AI:** Fundamentally, RoboBrain 2.0 is motivated by the broader vision of building truly generalist embodied agents. By unifying perception, reasoning, and planning, the authors aspire to create a foundation model that can generalize across a wide spectrum of embodied tasks, reducing the need for task-specific engineering.
5. **Facilitate Research and Deployment:** The team is motivated to offer practical solutions by releasing two variants (a lightweight 7B model and a full-scale 32B model) to cater to diverse deployment needs and resource constraints. Furthermore, the commitment to open-sourcing the code, checkpoints, and benchmarks is a strong motivation to foster reproducible research, accelerate community development, and enable wider adoption of embodied AI technologies.

In essence, RoboBrain 2.0 seeks to move embodied AI beyond rudimentary capabilities, enabling more sophisticated, robust, and adaptable interaction with the physical world, ultimately accelerating the path toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) in embodied contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboBrain 2.0 adopts a sophisticated and multi-faceted methodology encompassing architecture design, extensive data construction, a progressive multi-stage training strategy, and robust infrastructure.

#### 3.1. Architecture

The model employs a **heterogeneous encoder-decoder architecture** designed for unified multimodal processing:
- **Vision Encoder and Projection:** A lightweight vision encoder (approximately 689M parameters) handles dynamic-resolution images and multi-view video inputs. It leverages adaptive positional encoding and windowed attention for efficient high-resolution processing. Frame-wise visual tokenization with multi-dimensional RoPE (Rotary Positional Encoding) is used for spatiotemporal encoding. Visual embeddings are then projected into the language model's token space via a lightweight MLP projector. Multi-view inputs are serialized and augmented with view-specific positional identifiers.
- **LLM Decoder:** A decoder-only language model, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL, serves as the core reasoning engine. It comes in 7B and 32B parameter variants. Unlike conventional VLMs, it flexibly supports both concise responses and multi-step Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. Its outputs can be diverse, including free-form text (for task decomposition, scene graph updates, dialogue), spatial coordinates (for pointing, bounding boxes, trajectories), and optional reasoning traces for transparency.

#### 3.2. Input Modalities and Tokenization

RoboBrain 2.0 processes a rich set of input modalities:
- **Language instructions:** Natural language commands ranging from high-level goals (e.g., "Carry the apple to the nearest table") to low-level motor commands (e.g., "Grasp the apple").
- **Scene graph:** Structured JSON representation of the environment, detailing objects, categories, locations, and robot configurations.
- **Multi-view static images:** Images from various camera perspectives, processed independently and concatenated.
- **Video frames:** Sequences of egocentric views, optionally with timestamp tokens for temporal grounding.

Language inputs are tokenized by the language tokenizer, while visual inputs are processed by the vision encoder and projected into the LLM's token space.

#### 3.3. Training Data

A core strength of RoboBrain 2.0 is its massive and meticulously curated training dataset, categorized into three types:
- **General MLLM VQA:** Comprising 873K high-quality samples from LLaVA-665K and LRV-400K, covering standard VQA, region queries, OCR-based VQA, and visual dialogues, filtered and augmented for efficiency.
- **Spatial Data:**
 - **Visual Grounding:** 152K high-resolution images from LVIS with bounding box coordinates and 86K conversational sequences.
 - **Object Pointing:** 190K QA pairs from 64K images, derived from Pixmo-Points (filtered by GPT-4o for indoor relevance) and RoboPoint (347K QA), using 28 human-designed templates.
 - **Affordance:** 561K QA pairs from PACO-LVIS part-level annotations (46K images, 75 object, 200 part categories) and RoboPoint region reference data (270K images, 14 spatial relations), with questions generated by GPT-4o.
 - **Spatial Understanding:** 826K samples covering 31 spatial concepts, constructed via RefSpatial pipeline from 2D web images (OpenImage, RAM, GroundingDINO, Qwen2.5-VL, UniDepth V2, WildeCamera for pseudo-3D scene graphs) and 3D scene/embodied videos (MMScan, 3RScan, ScanQA, SQA3D, SpaceR, CA-1M).
 - **Spatial Referring:** 802K samples from RefSpatial, focusing on unambiguous single-target referring for robotic applications.
- **Temporal Data:**
 - **Ego-View Planning:** 50K samples from EgoPlan-IT, extracting multiple frames for task progress, with diverse prompt templates and distractor actions.
 - **ShareRobot Planning:** 1M QA pairs from 51K instances, covering 102 scenes, 12 robot embodiments, and 107 atomic tasks (human-annotated in RoboVQA format).
 - **AgiBot Planning:** 9,148 QA pairs from AgiBot-World across 19 manipulation tasks, with 109,378 first-person images, providing step-by-step instructions.
 - **Multi-Robot Planning:** 44,142 samples generated from RoboOS-based simulations across household, supermarket, and restaurant scenarios, using structured templates, workflow graphs, and agent-specific tool plans (generated by DeepSeek-V3).
 - **Closed-Loop Interaction:** Large-scale synthetic Observation-Thought-Action (OTA) trajectories from AI2Thor (based on Embodied-Reasoner) across 120 indoor environments, with GPT-4o generating detailed thought processes.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

RoboBrain 2.0 employs a progressive three-stage training strategy:
1. **Foundational Spatiotemporal Learning:** Initial fine-tuning on large-scale multimodal datasets to build general capabilities in spatial perception and temporal understanding (dense captioning, object localization, video QA, referring expressions).
2. **Embodied Spatiotemporal Enhancement:** Integration of high-resolution, multi-view, and egocentric video datasets, along with instruction-augmented navigation and interaction data. This stage emphasizes long-horizon temporal dependencies, multi-agent coordination, 3D affordance estimation, and scene graph construction, using extended sequence lengths and multi-camera inputs.
3. **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Embodied Contexts:** A two-phase approach using the Reason-RFT framework:
 - **CoT-SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning):** 10% of the training data is annotated with GPT-4o-generated CoT rationales, followed by supervised fine-tuning.
 - **RFT (Reinforcement Fine-Tuning):** Another 10% of data is used for collecting model responses. Incorrect answers are curated into a reformatted training set. Optimization uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) guided by a composite reward for accuracy and format correctness.

#### 3.5. Infrastructures

BAAI leverages its advanced infrastructure for efficient training and inference:
- **Large-Scale Training Infrastructure (FlagScale):** Features multi-dimensional hybrid parallelism (non-uniform pipeline parallelism, separate recompute for ViT to prevent OOM), memory pre-allocation (padding to max sequence length), optimized data pre-processing (JSON-only, aligned with Qwen2.5-VL preprocessor), distributed data loading (reducing redundancy in DP-PP-TP setups), and robust fault tolerance mechanisms.
- **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning Infrastructure (VeRL):** Utilizes VeRL, an open-source RL framework for LLMs/VLMs, based on the HybridFlow architecture, ensuring efficient and scalable execution of RFT workflows.
- **Inference Infrastructure (FlagScale):** Employs FlagScale for optimal inference engine selection and configuration. A mixed-bit quantization strategy is used: full-precision for the vision encoder (for accuracy) and 8-bit weights/16-bit activations for the language module (for efficiency), resulting in approximately 30% latency reduction.

This comprehensive methodology, from architectural choices to training data and infrastructure, enables RoboBrain 2.0 to achieve its ambitious goals in embodied AI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboBrain 2.0 demonstrates exceptional performance across a wide array of embodied spatial and temporal reasoning benchmarks, often setting new state-of-the-art (SOTA) records. The report highlights results for two variants: the lightweight 7B model and the full-scale 32B model, with the 32B variant generally achieving superior performance.

#### 4.1. Spatial Reasoning Capabilities (Tables 2 & 3)

RoboBrain 2.0 was evaluated on nine spatial reasoning benchmarks, showcasing its robust understanding of physical environments:
- **BLINK (Depth Perception & Spatial Relation Understanding):** RoboBrain-7B-2.0 achieved SOTA with an average score of 83.95, closely followed by RoboBrain-32B-2.0 at 83.63. Both variants significantly outperformed all prior open-source and proprietary general baselines (e.g., GPT-o4-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen2.5-VL) and embodied baselines (e.g., VeBrain-8B, Cosmos-Reason1-7B).
- **CV-Bench (2D/3D Spatial Understanding):** RoboBrain-7B-2.0 secured SOTA accuracy at 85.75, with RoboBrain-32B-2.0 at a strong 83.92, both exceeding all listed baselines.
- **EmbSpatial (Embodied Spatial Tasks):** RoboBrain-32B-2.0 achieved a near SOTA score of 78.57, slightly behind Gemini-2.5-Pro, but surpassing all other general and embodied baselines. RoboBrain-7B-2.0 also performed competitively at 76.32.
- **RoboSpatial (Spatial Reasoning in Robot Environments):** RoboBrain-32B-2.0 achieved a clear SOTA with 72.43, substantially ahead of all baselines. RoboBrain-7B-2.0 scored 54.23, outperforming most general baselines.
- **RefSpatial-Bench (Spatial Referring Expressions):** RoboBrain-32B-2.0 achieved SOTA in both Location (54.00) and Placement (54.00) accuracy, significantly outperforming all general baselines. RoboBrain-7B-2.0 (Loc: 36.00, Pla: 29.00) also showed competitive precision.
- **SAT (General Spatial Reasoning):** RoboBrain-32B-2.0 achieved a clear SOTA score of 86.67, significantly outperforming all general baselines.
- **VSI-Bench (Visual-Spatial Integration):** While Gemini-2.5-Flash achieved SOTA (48.83), RoboBrain-32B-2.0 was competitive at 42.69, outperforming many other models.
- **Where2Place (Object Placement Prediction):** RoboBrain-32B-2.0 achieved SOTA with an average of 73.59 across seen and unseen scenarios, substantially surpassing all baselines. RoboBrain-7B-2.0 also performed strongly at 63.59.
- **ShareRobot-Bench-Affordance (Object Functionality/Interaction):** RoboBrain-32B-2.0 secured SOTA with 35.28% accuracy. RoboBrain-7B-2.0 achieved 28.05%, outperforming other baselines.
- **ShareRobot-Bench-Trajectory (Navigation/Motion Prediction - DFD↓):** RoboBrain-32B-2.0 achieved a remarkable SOTA DFD of 0.2368 (lower is better), significantly outperforming all general and embodied baselines. RoboBrain-7B-2.0 followed with a competitive 0.5512.

#### 4.2. Temporal Reasoning Capabilities (Table 4)

RoboBrain 2.0 also demonstrated outstanding performance on key temporal reasoning benchmarks:
- **Multi-Robot Planning (Coordination across Scenarios):** RoboBrain-7B-2.0 achieved the SOTA average score of 81.50, outperforming all general baselines. RoboBrain-32B-2.0 followed closely with an average of 80.33. This highlights the strong multi-agent coordination capabilities.
- **Ego-Plan2 (Egocentric Daily Activity Planning):** RoboBrain-32B-2.0 secured SOTA with an average score of 57.23, significantly outperforming all general and embodied baselines in breaking down complex tasks into actionable steps. RoboBrain-7B-2.0 (33.23) was competitive with embodied baselines.
- **RoboBench (Robotic Mobile Manipulation Planning):** RoboBrain-7B-2.0 achieved SOTA with a score of 72.16, surpassing all general and embodied baselines. RoboBrain-32B-2.0 (68.33) also outperformed several baselines.

In summary, the 32B variant of RoboBrain 2.0 consistently achieved state-of-the-art or near state-of-the-art results across most spatial and temporal reasoning benchmarks, often significantly outperforming proprietary models like Gemini and GPT-o4-mini, and well-established open-source models. The lightweight 7B variant also demonstrated strong competitive performance, making RoboBrain 2.0 a powerful and versatile embodied foundation model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboBrain 2.0 represents a significant stride forward in the field of embodied AI, with profound implications for research and practical applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancing Generalist Embodied AI:** By unifying perception, reasoning, and planning within a single framework, RoboBrain 2.0 moves closer to the vision of a generalist embodied agent. This is crucial for creating AI systems that can adapt to and operate effectively in diverse, unconstrained physical environments, rather than being limited to narrow, predefined tasks.
2. **Addressing Core Bottlenecks:** The model directly tackles the identified limitations in spatial understanding, temporal modeling, and chain-of-thought reasoning, which have historically hindered the progress of embodied AI. Its superior performance on benchmarks specifically designed for these challenges validates its effectiveness in resolving these long-standing issues.
3. **Setting New Performance Benchmarks:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on a wide range of public embodied spatial and temporal reasoning benchmarks, often surpassing both advanced open-source and proprietary models (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-o4-mini), demonstrates the model's robustness and capability. This sets a new bar for future research and development in the field.
4. **Enabling Real-World Robotic Applications:** The specific capabilities demonstrated by RoboBrain 2.0 – including precise affordance prediction, accurate spatial referring, reliable trajectory forecasting, intelligent multi-agent coordination, and adaptive closed-loop interaction – are directly applicable to complex real-world robotics scenarios. This could lead to more autonomous and intelligent robots in industries such as manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and domestic assistance.
5. **Comprehensive Data and Training Innovations:** The meticulous construction of a vast and diverse dataset, spanning general VQA, detailed spatial perception (pointing, affordance, 3D understanding, referring), and complex temporal modeling (ego-view, multi-robot, closed-loop planning), provides a rich foundation for embodied learning. The progressive three-stage training strategy, integrating foundational learning, embodied enhancement, and sophisticated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), represents a robust methodology for developing advanced embodied intelligence.
6. **Scalable and Efficient Infrastructure:** The development and utilization of BAAI's open-source FlagScale framework for large-scale training and inference, alongside VeRL for RFT, highlight significant engineering prowess. Innovations like hybrid parallelism, memory optimization, and mixed-bit quantization ensure that these powerful models can be trained and deployed efficiently and at scale, reducing the computational barrier for future research and practical adoption.
7. **Fostering Open Science and Collaboration:** The decision to open-source the model checkpoints, training recipes, and evaluation tools is a pivotal contribution. This promotes reproducibility, allows the broader research community to build upon RoboBrain 2.0's advancements, and accelerates the collective pursuit of generalist embodied AI. It also facilitates comparison and benchmarking, leading to faster innovation cycles.
8. **Future Impact on System-Level Integration:** The outlined future directions, particularly the integration with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks and system-level integration with advanced robotics platforms for "intelligence app stores," suggest a pathway towards highly modular, adaptive, and easily deployable robotic AI solutions. This vision could revolutionize how robotic skills are developed, shared, and utilized.

In conclusion, RoboBrain 2.0 is a testament to the synergistic power of large-scale vision-language models, specialized embodied datasets, and advanced training methodologies. Its impact is poised to be transformative, pushing the boundaries of what embodied AI can achieve and significantly contributing to the realization of intelligent agents capable of seamless interaction with our physical world.
