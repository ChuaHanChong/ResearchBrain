# NeoHorse-1: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement via Agentic Post-Training with Routing Harness

> [!summary] Summary
> NeoHorse-1 is a family of agent-native models post-trained on trajectories harvested from a deployed LLM routing harness. Routing-predicted capability tiers order user-turn training examples into a three-stage supervised curriculum, then schedule contexts for on-policy distillation from a teacher, while evaluation feedback reallocates the next data mixture. Macro-average scores rise from 58.94 to 64.87 (4B) and 65.60 to 69.04 (9B) across ten benchmarks.

## Problem

- Recursive self-improvement lacks a concrete mechanism: prior systems improve scaffolds or policies without an operational source of capability evidence linking deployment back to the next training round.
- Trajectory supervised fine-tuning imitates a fixed teacher policy, leaving a distribution gap because deployed students condition on their own generated prefixes rather than recorded responses.
- Curriculum methods depend on explicit difficulty labels or dataset-specific heuristics that are costly to obtain, and static instruction-response pairs poorly represent agentic execution trajectories.

## Method

- **Agentic supervision** serializes harness trajectories into user-turn examples keeping interleaved reasoning, tool calls and harness context, with prediction loss masked to current-turn assistant spans, after structural validation and six-dimensional semantic evaluation.
- Routing-derived capability scores over tiers C0-C3, hard or score-weighted soft, order examples across a **three-stage curriculum**, and the same progression schedules starting contexts for ==on-policy distillation== under a top-K ==reverse-KL== teacher objective.
- **Capability-guided allocation** profiles checkpoint deficiencies across semantic attributes, quality dimensions, outcomes and routing tiers, then shifts the next training mixture toward weak regions, closing the evaluation-selection-update loop.

## Results

- Post-training lifts the 4B macro-average from **58.94** to **64.87** and the 9B from **65.60** to **69.04**, with NeoHorse-1-4B beating its base model on every comparable benchmark.
- Under matched curriculum, optimizer, seed and budget, routing-harness trajectories beat the public Toucan corpus by **6.26** average points, including **+11.31** on τ²-Bench and **+8.54** on HumanEval.
- Scaling unique supervised tokens raises the five-benchmark development average from **69.31** to **71.45**; on a PinchBench case the 9B model cuts token usage **83.6%** versus the 4B.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A router's predicted capability demand, not the tier actually served, is a free difficulty label, since executed routes also reflect user overrides, availability and policy.
> - Data provenance can outweigh recipe: real harness interactions transfer better than synthetic tool-agent data even under an identical curriculum and matched training budget.
> - Gains concentrate on interactive execution rather than static instruction compliance, and only one loop iteration was tested, so compounding self-improvement stays unproven.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the broader field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) for AI systems. RSI describes a direction where AI systems increasingly contribute to their own improvement, from refining responses to automating aspects of AI research itself. The paper argues that realizing RSI requires a concrete mechanism for an AI system to observe its capabilities and convert that evidence into learning.

The authors propose that a deployed routing harness, which manages an agent's context, tools, and environment interaction, inherently contains such a mechanism. This harness generates execution trajectories that include observable evidence of a model's strengths and limitations, beyond just task outputs. NeoHorse-1 aims to leverage this interaction data and routing signals to guide agentic post-training.

The work builds upon and integrates several existing research areas:

- **Agentic Model Post-Training:** Previous work in this area involves trajectory-based supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to transfer planning and tool use capabilities into models, as seen in FireAct, AgentTuning, Agent-FLAN, and Llama 3. The paper also notes the importance of on-policy distillation (OPD) for reducing the distribution gap between SFT (learning from fixed teacher trajectories) and deployment (student generating its own trajectories), and the impact of harness design on agent performance (SWE-agent, Terminal-Lego). NeoHorse-1 extends these by incorporating routing signals to organize and prioritize training data.
- **LLM Routing and Curriculum Learning:** LLM routing systems assign queries to appropriate models, often balancing quality and cost (e.g., FrugalGPT, RouteLLM). Agentic routing extends this to multi-agent systems where a decision layer selects the best model. Curriculum learning is a training strategy that presents examples based on difficulty, which has shown effectiveness in LLM post-training. NeoHorse-1 utilizes routing signals to estimate capability demand and create a curriculum, offering an alternative to explicit difficulty labels.
- **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** Existing RSI research explores improving agent behavior, scaffolds, and training processes. NeoHorse-1 contributes to this by proposing a system-level feedback loop where deployed routing harnesses continuously supply interaction experience and capability-level feedback to guide subsequent model updates, thus influencing what the system learns from next based on what it has learned to do.

NeoHorse-1 distinguishes itself by concretizing the mechanism for RSI within an agentic routing harness, leveraging its inherent data generation and capability assessment to drive iterative model improvement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of NeoHorse-1 is to develop and demonstrate a concrete mechanism for Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) in AI systems, specifically within the context of agent-native models. The motivation stems from the recognition that for AI systems to recursively improve, they require a systematic way to observe their own performance, identify gaps in their capabilities, and translate this evidence into subsequent learning cycles.

The core motivation and objectives can be detailed as follows:

- **Establishing a Concrete RSI Mechanism:** The authors contend that the operational environment of a deployed routing harness naturally provides the necessary signals for RSI. Beyond just producing task outputs, agentic interactions within a harness generate rich execution trajectories. These trajectories contain records of decisions, tool interactions, and task outcomes, which serve as observable evidence of what a model can and cannot yet accomplish. The objective is to harness this continuous stream of operational data to inform model improvement.
- **Agentic Post-Training for Capability Development:** To leverage this mechanism, the project aims to develop agent-native models that are specifically designed to learn from these rich interaction trajectories. This involves a post-training regimen that converts deployment-generated data into training examples capable of teaching interleaved reasoning, tool use, and response generation within their original execution context.
- **Routing-Guided Learning Curriculum:** A key objective is to use the routing signals—which predict capability demand for each interaction—to organize the training experience. This allows for a structured, curriculum-based approach to supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and on-policy distillation (OPD), progressively introducing more complex or demanding examples as the model's capabilities evolve. This aims to optimize learning efficiency and target specific capability improvements.
- **Closing the Evaluation-Selection-Update Loop:** The project seeks to implement a feedback loop where model evaluation directly informs the allocation of new training data. By analyzing model deficiencies on stratified evaluation suites (based on routing signals and outcome feedback), the system can dynamically adjust the composition of the next training mixture. This closes an "evaluation–selection–update" loop, ensuring that the system's learning is guided by its actual performance and needs, and that updated models, when redeployed, generate new data that further informs subsequent iterations.
- **Prototyping Harness-Mediated RSI:** NeoHorse-1 is presented as an initial prototype to validate this conceptual framework. The objective is to demonstrate that this feedback-driven process can lead to measurable improvements in agentic capabilities across various benchmarks and that the approach lays the groundwork for sustaining these gains across successive model generations.

In essence, the motivation is to move RSI from a theoretical concept to practical implementation by embedding its core feedback mechanisms directly into the operational infrastructure of agentic AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NeoHorse-1 system integrates a data pipeline from a routing harness with specific agentic post-training methods and a capability-guided data allocation strategy to facilitate recursive self-improvement.

#### 3.1. System Design and Data Pipeline

The system couples a heterogeneous model pool with intelligent routing. This setup records, for every user turn, the predicted capability demand, the selected service tier, and the subsequent interaction. This process forms the basis of the "harness-native data flywheel."

- **Data Composition and Granularity:**
 - **Trajectory:** A complete interaction executed by the deployment harness, including user requests, model responses, tool calls, environment observations, recovery attempts, and outcomes.
 - **User Turn:** The basic serialized training unit, starting with a user request and ending at the next user request or task termination. It retains the current request's interleaved reasoning, tool calls, and observations, along with earlier visible responses and tool interactions as context.
 - **Subscene:** Groups adjacent user turns sharing a local goal, serving as the unit for semantic characterization.
 - The primary corpus consists of 10⁵–10⁶ harness-generated trajectories, augmented with publicly available data covering instruction following, dialogue, reasoning, tool use, code, agent interaction, and preference learning.
- **Data Quality Control:**
 - **Deduplication and Decontamination:** Exact and near-duplicate records are removed. Training data is screened against evaluation suites to ensure disjointness.
 - **Structural Validation:** Rule-based validation reconstructs and verifies event sequences within trajectories, checking for payload readability, message structure, event order, and tool-call closure. Trajectories are categorized as "internally complete," "partially recoverable," or "quarantined."
 - **Semantic Evaluation:** For structurally valid trajectories, a semantic judge evaluates six independent quality dimensions: goal attainment, instruction adherence, tool use, evidence consistency, error recovery, and termination. Each dimension is assigned PASS, WARN, FAIL, or NOT_EVALUATED, grounded in trajectory evidence.
- **Data Characterization (Subscene-Level):** Each subscene is characterized along three axes:
 - **Scene:** Describes the user's activity and context (task type, application domain, use context, asking/doing).
 - **Goal:** States the user's objective, acceptance criteria, and cross-turn relations (new, continued, modified).
 - **Outcome:** Records the verifiable result of the attempt against the goal.
- **Agentic Routing Signals:** The harness router estimates capability demand at the user-turn level based on the current request and interaction history. It assigns turns to one of four service tiers (C0-C3), reflecting increasing capability demand. Routing records include the router's raw prediction, the policy-adjusted decision, and the tier actually served. These signals, combined with semantic attributes, allow for stratification of the corpus by user intent, service allocation, and observed result.
- **Capability-Guided Data Allocation:** Evaluation feedback from a stratified suite (kept disjoint from training data) is used to form a model-deficiency profile. This profile guides the reallocation of existing and new training data, shifting the training mixture towards underperforming regions while maintaining broad coverage. Successful trajectories provide positive supervision, while informative failures guide rebalanced coverage.

#### 3.2. Agentic Post-Training Methods

The post-training involves two main components: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and On-Policy Distillation (OPD), both guided by routing signals.

- **Agentic Supervision (SFT):**
 - **User Turns as Training Units:** Each training example is a user turn, containing a user request and the subsequent assistant responses and tool interactions.
 - **Serialization and Context:** Examples are serialized using a chat template, retaining within-turn reasoning and visible historical context (earlier user requests, assistant responses, tool calls, results). System instructions and harness context are also preserved.
 - **SFT Objective:** A binary token-level loss mask is applied, setting to one for tokens in the retained assistant target spans (reasoning, serialized tool calls, visible responses, end-of-response tokens) of the current turn. The objective minimizes the masked negative log-likelihood, normalizing by the count of supervised tokens.
- **Routing-Guided Curriculum Learning (for SFT):**
 - **Routing Scores:** For each training example, a capability demand score (𝑠ᵢ) is estimated from the request and history *before* the first supervised assistant response. This can be a hard assigned tier index or a soft score (score-weighted mean tier index).
 - **Curriculum Scheduling:** Training is conducted in three stages. Each stage gradually introduces examples with higher routing scores, while reserving some lower-scored examples for later stages to prevent high-demand interactions from dominating the end of training.
- **Routing-Guided On-Policy Distillation (OPD):**
 - **Context Scheduling:** The same three-stage routing-guided curriculum is applied to organize starting contexts for OPD, progressively introducing higher-scored contexts.
 - **Student Generation and Teacher Supervision:** At each stage, a student model checkpoint generates an assistant response for contexts drawn from the scheduled distribution. A fixed teacher model provides next-token distributions for these student-generated prefixes. The rollout checkpoint is refreshed as training progresses.
 - **Distillation Objective:** The objective minimizes the response-normalized reverse KL divergence between the student's and teacher's coarsened next-token distributions (retaining top-K tokens and an "other" bin). Only the student's probabilities on collected prefixes are updated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of NeoHorse-1 focused on quantitative benchmark performance, qualitative analysis of agent trajectories, and an examination of training data impact.

#### 4.1. Overall Results (Benchmark Performance)

NeoHorse-1 was evaluated at 4B and 9B scales across ten benchmarks covering agentic capabilities, code generation, and instruction following.

- **Broad Gains at Both Model Scales:**
 - **4B Model:** NeoHorse-1-4B achieved a macro-average score of 64.87, an improvement from the Qwen3.5-4B baseline of 58.94. It outperformed Qwen3.5-4B on every benchmark with available results. The most significant gains were observed on harness-based agent tasks and coding benchmarks, with consistent improvements also in tool use and instruction following.
 - **9B Model:** NeoHorse-1-9B achieved a macro-average score of 69.04, up from the Qwen3.5-9B baseline of 65.60. Gains were concentrated on harness-based agent tasks and tool interaction, with more stable performance on instruction-following benchmarks.
- **Larger Configurations Remain Beneficial:** NeoHorse-1-9B consistently outperformed NeoHorse-1-4B across all comparable benchmarks. These additional gains were more pronounced on tasks requiring sustained state tracking, interaction with external tools, and iterative decision revision (agentic tasks, function calling, challenging coding). Differences were smaller on static instruction compliance tasks. Notably, NeoHorse-1-4B matched or exceeded Qwen3.5-9B on several benchmarks, indicating that the post-training can partially compensate for model scale differences.

#### 4.2. Agent Trace Analysis (Qualitative Behavioral Differences)

Qualitative analysis of agent trajectories revealed behavioral mechanisms underlying the benchmark performance:

- **Training Closes the End-to-End Execution Loop:** The post-training enabled NeoHorse-1-4B to transform locally plausible actions into coherent, complete workflows. For example, in a QwenClawBench project-scheduling task, Qwen3.5-4B failed to incorporate updated dependency constraints, leading to an invalid schedule. NeoHorse-1-4B, however, retrieved and recognized the new evidence, recomputed, verified, and correctly saved the schedule.
- **Scale Pays Off Under Feedback and Failure:** The 9B model exhibited greater robustness in tasks requiring iterative debugging, recovery from failures, and sustained state maintenance.
 - **WorkBuddy (Code Repair):** NeoHorse-1-4B stopped after a single attempt, failing to establish an effective testing and repair loop. NeoHorse-1-9B, conversely, followed a complete edit–test–inspect–repair cycle, incorporating execution feedback until successful verification.
 - **PinchBench (Strategy Adaptation):** When encountering an unavailable dependency (pandas), NeoHorse-1-4B repeatedly attempted failed installations and patching. NeoHorse-1-9B recognized the blocked approach, abandoned the unavailable dependency, and switched to standard Python libraries, successfully completing the task while significantly reducing requests, execution time, and token usage. This demonstrated an ability to revise strategy and allocate interaction budget effectively.

#### 4.3. Data Analysis

- **Routing-Harness Data vs. Public Agent Data:** A controlled experiment compared training with routing-harness data against public synthetic tool-agent data (Toucan), using the same routing-guided training configuration. The routing-harness data yielded consistently stronger performance, improving the unweighted average across five comparable benchmarks by 6.26 percentage points. The largest gains were observed on HumanEval (+8.54) and 𝜏²-Bench (+11.31), indicating that routing-mediated interactions provide more transferable agentic supervision.
- **Scaling Routing-Harness Supervision:** Increasing the amount of routing-harness supervision (measured in unique supervised tokens on a logarithmic scale) demonstrated consistent aggregate gains. The average score across five benchmarks steadily increased from 69.31 for the base model to 71.45 at the largest data scale, suggesting that scaling high-quality agentic supervision leads to continued performance improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NeoHorse-1 represents an initial step towards realizing recursive self-improvement (RSI) by demonstrating a concrete mechanism within a deployed agentic routing harness. The research highlights the potential of leveraging operational interaction data and routing signals as feedback for continuous model improvement.

The work's significance and potential impact include:

- **Operationalizing RSI:** The study provides an operational framework for RSI, moving it from a conceptual design to a practical implementation. By converting real-world execution trajectories, capability demand signals, and observable outcomes from a routing harness into structured training data, NeoHorse-1 establishes a feedback-driven process for AI model evolution.
- **Enhanced Agentic Capabilities:** The consistent performance gains observed across diverse agentic, coding, and instruction-following benchmarks for both 4B and 9B models indicate the effectiveness of the proposed agentic post-training methods. These improvements extend beyond isolated answers to more complete workflows, iterative verification, error recovery, and strategy adaptation, which are critical for robust AI agents.
- **Data-Centric AI Development:** The findings underscore the value of high-quality, context-rich interaction data generated in deployment environments. The superiority of routing-harness data over public synthetic datasets suggests that real-world interaction experience, especially when structured and prioritized by routing signals, is a potent source of supervision for developing advanced agentic capabilities.
- **Curriculum Learning and On-Policy Distillation:** The integration of routing-guided curriculum learning and on-policy distillation provides a structured approach to leveraging varied interaction experience. This methodology helps models learn effectively from a wide range of capability demands, ensuring both foundational skills and advanced problem-solving are addressed.
- **Path Towards Continuous Improvement:** While NeoHorse-1 is presented as a prototype reflecting a single pass of the evaluation-selection-update loop, it establishes a foundation for iterative self-improvement. The capability-guided data allocation mechanism, combined with continuous data generation from updated models in the harness, outlines a clear path for sustained, compounding gains across successive model generations.

Future work directly addresses the current limitations, focusing on iterating the feedback loop across multiple model generations, extending the recipes to a broader range of tasks and execution environments served by the harness, and refining the routing signals to create even more calibrated estimates of difficulty and deficiency. This research positions the everyday operation of an AI routing harness not just as a service delivery mechanism, but as a critical component in the recursive self-improvement of AI systems.
