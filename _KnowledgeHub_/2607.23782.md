# $N_0$-VTLA: Scaling Vision-Tactile-Language-Action Model with Latent Tactile Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> A vision-tactile-language-action (VTLA) foundation model, N0-VTLA, integrates anticipatory tactile perception through latent tactile tokens and utilizes an offline reinforcement learning method (ALTER) for continuous policy improvement. This approach achieved an average success rate of 47.2% on the NeoReal benchmark and 83.1% on UniVTAC tasks, outperforming previous VLA models and showing enhanced performance with offline learning.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action (VLA) models exhibit limitations in contact-rich robotic manipulation due to the absence of scaled, effective tactile perception.
- Current tactile integration approaches are often reactive, treating sparse tactile data as additional visual streams or only acting on already-occurred contact.
- Continuously improving robotic policies from diverse, imperfect real-world deployment data presents a challenge for offline reinforcement learning.

## Method

- Introduces **N0-VTLA**, a ==vision-tactile-language-action (VTLA)== foundation model that extends a VLA backbone with a novel ==latent tactile pathway==.
- This pathway employs =="latent tactile tokens"== trained to predict net tactile change over future action chunks, enabling anticipatory control for contact-rich tasks.
- Proposes **ALTER**, an ==advantage-conditioned offline reinforcement learning== method, to continuously improve policies using dense progress and sparse preference labels derived from deployment data.

## Results

- N0-VTLA achieved an average success rate of **47.2%** on the real-world NeoReal benchmark, surpassing the π0.5 baseline (**29.4%**) and demonstrating improved performance across all nine contact-rich tasks.
- In simulation, the model attained an **83.1%** average success rate on UniVTAC tasks, outperforming previous strong baselines by approximately **16** percentage points, and showed superior overall performance across 20 tasks (**63.8%** vs **44.0%** for π0.5).
- The ALTER offline reinforcement learning method consistently improved task policies, with N0-VTLA+ALTER reaching success rates of **95%** on "Towel Folding" and **80%** on "Bag Packing," demonstrating effective policy enhancement from deployment data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent tactile tokens, trained to predict future contact, effectively enable anticipatory robotic control, demonstrating significantly better future contact prediction accuracy (92.3%) than current tactile readings (57%).
> - Tactile perception, integrated anticipatorily, allows for contact-aware feedback loops and fine gripper force control, leading to adaptive behaviors like retries and delicate object manipulation.
> - The influence of tactile input on robotic actions is evident as predicted action paths diverge from touch-removed counterparts precisely at moments of firm contact, indicating direct tactical conditioning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the development of vision-language-action (VLA) models. These models, often fine-tuned from large-scale pre-trained vision-language backbones, have demonstrated an ability to follow instructions and generalize across various tasks, scenes, and robot embodiments. Examples include models built on architectures like ACT and Diffusion Policy, as well as generalist policies like RT-1, RT-2, and Octo. These systems primarily rely on camera-based visual observations, language instructions, and robot proprioceptive state.

Despite progress in visual-language grounding and generalist policies, a persistent challenge for current VLA models lies in contact-rich manipulation. Tasks requiring fine-grained interaction, precise force control, or handling of deformable objects often prove difficult. This limitation stems from the relative absence of tactile perception in large-scale VLA systems. Existing attempts to integrate touch have typically been limited to small, task-specific datasets, often comprising tens of hours of data for a few skills.

Approaches to integrating tactile sensing in VLA policies have generally followed two paths. One method involves concatenating tactile tokens into the vision-language context, treating the tactile stream as another camera input. However, tactile signals are often sparse and only informative during brief contact events, potentially adding little value to an information-dense visual and linguistic prefix. A second approach injects current tactile readings directly into the action pathway to guide denoising. This method is primarily reactive, meaning the policy is guided by contact that has already occurred, which may not be optimal for anticipatory manipulation where actions need to predict impending contact.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a vision-tactile-language-action (VTLA) foundation model that integrates tactile perception at scale and in an anticipatory manner. It builds upon established VLA backbones, specifically a flow-matching architecture, and extends it with a dedicated tactile pathway. The research also contributes to the growing area of offline reinforcement learning (RL) by proposing a method, ALTER, for improving policies using stored deployment data, addressing the challenge of continuous learning and adaptation in real-world robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the N$_{0}$-VTLA model are detailed as follows:

- **Address Weakness in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Current vision-language-action (VLA) models often exhibit limitations in tasks that require precise physical interaction, such as inserting objects into tight spaces, manipulating deformable materials, or exerting controlled force. This deficiency is largely attributed to the lack of effective tactile perception. The motivation is to overcome this "persistent weakness" by integrating tactile sensing into a foundation model framework.

- **Scale Tactile Pre-training:** A significant gap identified in existing work is the absence of VLA policies pre-trained on tactile data at scale. Prior tactile integrations were primarily trained on small, task-specific datasets. A key objective is to pre-train a VTLA model, N$_{0}$-VTLA, on a large-scale visuo-tactile dataset, NeoData, aiming to establish broad contact priors and generalize tactile understanding.

- **Develop an Anticipatory Tactile Integration Mechanism:** Existing tactile integration methods are often either insufficient (treating sparse tactile data as another visual stream) or reactive (using current tactile readings to inform actions already taken). The motivation is to move beyond reactive control towards an anticipatory mechanism. N$_{0}$-VTLA aims to achieve this by predicting future tactile changes and conditioning actions on these predictions, allowing the policy to anticipate and proactively adjust for contact events.

- **Introduce Latent Tactile Tokens:** To achieve anticipatory control, the paper proposes a novel method of representing tactile information through "latent tactile tokens" (z). These tokens are intended to estimate the net tactile change over a future action chunk, rather than simply reflecting the current contact state. This approach is motivated by the hypothesis that actions should be conditioned on the contact they are *about to cause*, which requires an internal estimate of future tactile states.

- **Ensure Stable and Efficient Tactile Pathway Integration:** Integrating a new modality, especially a sparse and predictive one like tactile sensing, into a large pre-trained VLA model can be challenging due to potential instability and degradation of existing capabilities. The research is motivated to develop a structured, multi-stage training recipe that brings the newly initialized tactile pathway online progressively and stably, grounding the predictive latent tokens before full end-to-end training.

- **Enable Offline Policy Improvement from Deployment Data:** Beyond initial training and supervised task adaptation, real-world robotic systems generate vast amounts of deployment data, which often contains valuable information about both successes and failures, as well as human corrections. A key objective is to leverage this data for continuous policy improvement without requiring additional environment interactions. This led to the development of ALTER (Advantage Labeling from Trajectory Events and Relative Progress), an advantage-conditioned offline reinforcement learning method.

- **Validate Performance on Contact-Rich Benchmarks:** The ultimate motivation is to demonstrate that the proposed N$_{0}$-VTLA model, with its novel tactile integration and offline improvement capabilities, can significantly outperform existing strong baselines on real-world and simulated contact-rich manipulation tasks, thereby laying a foundation for more versatile and robust robotic policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The N$_{0}$-VTLA model is designed as a vision-tactile-language-action (VTLA) foundation model for contact-rich manipulation, integrating tactile perception in a predictive manner and leveraging offline policy improvement.

**Model Architecture (N$_{0}$-VTLA)**

1. **Base Architecture:** N$_{0}$-VTLA is built upon a $\pi_{0.5}$ vision-language-action backbone, which utilizes PaliGemma for its vision-language capabilities and a flow-matching action expert for generating action chunks. Camera views, language instructions, and robot state (discretized) form the model's prefix. The action expert denoises an action chunk over a horizon of H=50 steps in a canonical 32-dimensional action space.
2. **Latent Tactile Pathway:** This is a core addition to the base VLA model.
 - **Tactile Encoder:** Raw tactile frames are not directly used. Instead, a "contact-difference image" is generated by subtracting an episode-start baseline (zero-contact reference) from the current tactile frame. This difference image is then encoded by a frozen, self-supervised visual encoder (DINOv2) followed by a trainable linear projection. Each tactile image yields 10 tokens (1 class token, 9 spatial tokens), which are concatenated across active views. Freezing the encoder preserves its representation and reduces memory.
 - **Latent Tactile Predictor:** A lightweight module reads the current tactile difference tokens (g) in the context of the vision-language prefix and emits "latent tactile tokens" (z). These tokens are designed to estimate the *net tactile change expected over the coming action chunk*. The action expert is directly conditioned on `z`; the raw tactile tokens `g` do not enter the vision-language prefix or the action expert directly.
 - **Predictive Rationale:** This design choice is motivated by the goal of anticipatory control. Rather than reacting to current contact, the policy acts on predicted contact changes, which is beneficial for anticipatory behaviors like precise grasping or slip recovery.

**Data**

- **NeoData Corpus:** N$_{0}$-VTLA is pre-trained on NeoData, a large-scale, curated, multi-platform visuo-tactile corpus. This dataset includes data from single- and dual-arm robot manipulators (ARX X5, UR5e, Flexiv, Franka, Piper) and a UMI-style handheld collection gripper.
- **Vision-Based Tactile Sensing:** Each gripper finger is equipped with a self-developed visuo-tactile sensor (GelSight-like), capturing contact as an image stream.
- **Canonical Action and State Schema:** All embodiments are unified into a fixed 32-dimensional state and action container, inherited from $\pi_{0.5}$. This allows single- and dual-arm data to coexist. Actions are stored as absolute end-effector poses, then converted to chunk-relative motions during training.
- **Tactile Collection Convention:** Each episode begins with a short, static, zero-contact baseline period to establish a reference frame for tactile difference calculations.
- **Data Quality Verification:** A rigorous verification process ensures data completeness, validity, and adherence to specified schemas and statistics.
- **Simulated Data:** Data from the UniVTAC simulator and the NeoSim suite are converted into the canonical schema and integrated alongside real data.

**Training**

N$_{0}$-VTLA's training proceeds through several phases:

1. **Base Pre-training:** Initial pre-training of the vision-language-action backbone is conducted at cluster scale on the NeoData corpus, ensuring stability across diverse data.
2. **Three-Stage Latent-Tactile Training:** This recipe brings the new tactile pathway online without destabilizing the pre-trained base policy.
 - **Stage 1: Grounding the Predictor:** The entire base policy is frozen. Only the latent tactile predictor, its tactile projection, and a lightweight reconstruction head are trained. The predictor's output `z` is trained to match a future-tactile target `z*` (mean tactile change over the next H steps) using a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss and an auxiliary L1 reconstruction loss on a coarse future-tactile-difference field. This grounds `z` in anticipatory tactile information.
 - **Stage 2: Aligning Latents with the Action Expert:** The tactile perception stack (encoder, predictor) and VLA backbone are frozen. Only the latent-to-expert projection and the action expert are trained under the base action objective. Crucially, the vision-language prefix is masked for the action queries, forcing the action expert to learn to consume `z` as its primary conditioning for action prediction.
 - **Stage 3: End-to-End Joint Training:** All components, except the always-frozen tactile encoder backbone, are unfrozen and trained jointly under the standard pre-training action objective. The vision-language mask is removed, allowing the expert to see both the VLA prefix and `z`. This refines the entire policy.

3. **Supervised Task Adaptation (SFT):** The pre-trained N$_{0}$-VTLA checkpoint is fine-tuned on a few hundred task-specific demonstrations for downstream tasks, following the pre-training recipe at a reduced scale.

4. **Offline RL from Deployment Data with ALTER (Advantage Labeling from Trajectory Events and Relative Progress):** This optional procedure improves the task policy using stored deployment experience without new environment interaction.
 - **Deployment Corpus:** Includes clean teleoperation demonstrations, autonomous rollouts, and human-in-the-loop (HIL) rollouts (including staged-recovery episodes).
 - **Offline Annotations:**
 - **Dense Progress Supervision:** Clean demonstrations receive detailed stage-progress annotations. A VLM maps signal-grounded intervals (identified by tactile changes, kinematics, gripper state, visual cues) to a shared task template with L1/L2/L3 stages. Stages are weighted by their mean durations to calculate duration-calibrated progress $\phi_t$.
 - **Sparse Preference Supervision:** Imperfect deployment trajectories provide sparse before-and-after preferences based on tactile-detected object-drop events and logged human corrections.
 - **Event-Aware Pairwise Progress Learning:** A $\pi_{0.5}$-based paired-observation progress model A$_{\theta}$(x$_{a}$,x$_{b}$) is trained. It jointly encodes observations from two time points and predicts their relative task progress. Training uses a regression loss for demonstration pairs (targets $\phi(x_a) - \phi(x_b)$) and a margin loss for event preference pairs (e.g., before-drop vs. after-drop). Tactile, kinematic, and event signals are used to construct targets but are not direct inputs to the progress model.
 - **Advantage-Conditioned Offline Policy Learning:** The frozen pairwise progress model is applied to every stored trajectory to estimate global progress $\hat{\phi}_t$ and local execution change $\hat{r}_t$. Samples are assigned to duration-calibrated stages, and within each stage, samples are ranked by local change. The top $\rho = 0.3$ samples receive a "Advantage: positive" binary label, appended as a text input to the task prompt during policy training. Other samples receive "Advantage: negative". At deployment, the prompt always uses "Advantage: positive".

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of N$_{0}$-VTLA involved real-world and simulated benchmarks, demonstrating the efficacy of its tactile integration and offline policy improvement.

**Real-World Results: NeoReal Benchmark**
N$_{0}$-VTLA was evaluated on nine fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation tasks from the NeoReal benchmark against ACT and $\pi_{0.5}$.
- **Success Rate:** N$_{0}$-VTLA achieved an average success rate of 47.2%, outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ (29.4%) and ACT (0%). It surpassed the strongest baseline on every individual task.
- **Progress Score:** On a 100-point rubric, N$_{0}$-VTLA averaged 56.8 points, leading $\pi_{0.5}$ (42.3 points) and ACT (10.2 points). This indicated better progress even on long-horizon tasks where full success was less frequent.
- **Specific Examples:** For "Socket Plugging," N$_{0}$-VTLA reached 85% success compared to 60% for $\pi_{0.5}$. On "Board Insertion," N$_{0}$-VTLA achieved 25% while both baselines registered 0%, indicating its ability to handle precision insertion.

**Simulation Results: UniVTAC and NeoSim Benchmarks**
The model was tested on a suite of 20 tasks, including 8 UniVTAC tasks and 12 NeoSim tasks, which specifically stress pre-contact and in-contact regimes.
- **UniVTAC Tasks:** N$_{0}$-VTLA achieved an average success rate of 83.1%, leading the strongest external baseline, InternVLA-A1, at 67.1%. It showed near-saturating success on several insertion tasks and 100% on "Grasp Classify."
- **NeoSim Tasks:** These tasks were generally more challenging. N$_{0}$-VTLA averaged 50.8% success, compared to 45.8% for $\pi_{0.5}$. Specialist baselines performed considerably worse (8.6% to 23.4%). The gap between single-arm (73.8% for N$_{0}$-VTLA) and dual-arm tasks (39.4% for N$_{0}$-VTLA) was notable, with dual-arm tasks proving difficult for all methods.
- **Overall Simulation Performance:** Across all twenty tasks, N$_{0}$-VTLA achieved 63.8% mean success, against 44.0% for its strongest overall baseline, $\pi_{0.5}$.

**Offline Policy Improvement with ALTER**
The ALTER method was evaluated for improving policies using deployment data on three real-robot tasks: "Towel Folding," "Bag Packing," and "Cardboard Box Folding."
- **Progress Model Efficacy:** The pairwise progress model effectively predicted global task progress and detected local execution degradations (e.g., towel drops, unrecovered crumpling errors), with predicted progress decreasing during errors and rising during recovery.
- **Policy Performance:**
 - N$_{0}$-VTLA-SFT (supervised fine-tuning) consistently outperformed $\pi_{0.5}$-SFT on all three tasks.
 - When trained with ALTER, both backbones showed improvement. N$_{0}$-VTLA+ALTER achieved success rates of 95%, 80%, and 75% on "Towel Folding," "Bag Packing," and "Cardboard Box Folding," respectively.
 - $\pi_{0.5}$+ALTER achieved 90%, 75%, and 60%. This indicated that the performance advantage of the stronger N$_{0}$-VTLA (VTLA backbone) persisted and improved under the ALTER offline RL procedure.

**Representation Analyses**
Analyses probed the learned tactile representation after Stage 1 pre-training.
- **Anticipatory Capability:** The predicted latent tokens `z` retrieved their matching future-tactile target `z*` with 92.3% top-1 accuracy within a pool of candidates (chance: 3.2%). This significantly outperformed using only the current tactile encoding `g` for retrieval (57% top-1). This suggests the latent tokens carry predictive future contact information beyond the current observation.
- **Tactile Sensitivity:** A perturbation probe showed that swapping tactile input changed `z` significantly more (cosine distance ~0.9) than swapping RGB views and language prompts (cosine distance ~0.2). The tactile-to-vision-language sensitivity ratio remained high (4.3 after Stage 1, ~1.4 after end-to-end joint training), indicating `z` retains its tactile grounding.

**Summary of Key Findings (from Section 6)**

1. **Contact-Aware Retry Loops:** Tactile perception enabled N$_{0}$-VTLA to detect failed insertions, lift the arm, realign, and re-attempt, transforming tasks like "Socket Plugging" from a single visual commitment into a contact-aware feedback loop.
2. **Fine Gripper Force Control:** Tactile deformation signals allowed N$_{0}$-VTLA to make continuous, small adjustments to gripper aperture, enabling delicate tasks like "Bottle Standing" without excessive force.
3. **Action Divergence at Contact-Critical Moments:** Counterfactual probes demonstrated that the predicted action path with tactile input diverged from its touch-removed counterpart precisely at moments of firm contact and grasp, while coinciding in free space. This indicated the tactile pathway's direct influence on actions during critical interaction phases.
4. **Persistent Advantage with Offline RL:** The stronger performance of the N$_{0}$-VTLA backbone was maintained and further improved when combined with the ALTER offline policy improvement method, indicating that robust tactile pre-training provides a better starting point for subsequent learning from deployment data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The N$_{0}$-VTLA research presents several significant contributions with potential broad impact on the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Advancing Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The work directly addresses a known limitation of current VLA models by demonstrating that large-scale tactile pre-training and anticipatory tactile integration can significantly improve performance on fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation tasks. This is crucial for real-world robotics, where many practical applications involve precise physical interaction with objects, often under uncertainty.
- **Novel Tactile Integration Paradigm:** By proposing "latent tactile tokens" that predict future contact changes, N$_{0}$-VTLA shifts the paradigm from reactive to anticipatory tactile control. This is a conceptually significant move, as it enables robots to proactively adjust their actions based on expected contact, which is essential for dexterous and precise manipulation.
- **Stable Training Methodology:** The three-stage training recipe offers a practical and robust method for integrating a new, predictive sensory pathway into an existing, complex VLA foundation model without compromising its stability. This curriculum-based approach can inform future efforts to incorporate additional sensory modalities or complex internal representations into large-scale robot learning systems.
- **Leveraging Deployment Data for Continuous Improvement:** The ALTER offline RL method provides a scalable solution for extracting valuable learning signals from diverse deployment data, including imperfect rollouts and human interventions. This capability for continuous policy improvement without costly online interaction is vital for creating adaptable and robust robotic systems that can learn and refine skills over long operational periods in dynamic environments.
- **Foundational Model Development:** N$_{0}$-VTLA represents a step towards truly multimodal foundation models for robotics, expanding beyond vision and language to include tactile intelligence at scale. The introduction of the NeoData corpus also provides a valuable resource for future research in visuo-tactile learning.
- **Increased Robustness and Generalization:** The enhanced ability to handle unexpected contact, perform retries, and exert fine force control suggests that N$_{0}$-VTLA policies are more robust to real-world variations and errors. This increased robustness contributes to improved generalization capabilities across different scenarios and objects.

The findings lay a foundation for developing versatile, tactile-driven manipulation policies, which could have a substantial impact on applications requiring high precision, dexterity, and adaptability, such as industrial assembly, assistive robotics, and handling of delicate or deformable goods. The open-source release of code and datasets (as indicated by the paper references) could further accelerate research and adoption of these methods within the robotics community.
