# TelePhysics: Physics-Grounded Multi-Object Scene Generation from a Single Image with Real-Time Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> TelePhysics, a training-free framework developed by Fudan University and TeleAI, converts a single image into a physically consistent and controllable multi-object video. Human evaluation ranked its outputs highest across categories, including Semantic Adherence (6.93 Borda score), Physical Commonsense (6.90 Borda score), and Overall Preference (6.91 Borda score), while maintaining competitive visual quality.

## Problem

- Generative video models often lack explicit understanding of 3D geometry and physical laws, leading to physically implausible object interactions and limited user control.
- Single-image-to-3D reconstruction methods frequently reconstruct objects in isolation, resulting in interpenetration and spatial misalignment when assembling multi-object scenes.
- Physics-based 3D generation approaches commonly sacrifice photorealism, producing stylized or 'cartoonish' visual appearances rather than maintaining fidelity to the input image.

## Method

- A training-free framework that integrates ==Scene Perception== for instance-level 3D mesh reconstruction and background expansion, and Scene Alignment for transforming objects into a unified world coordinate frame.
- Employs a Vision-Language Model for automatic material parameter estimation and a multi-physics backend (Genesis engine) with specialized solvers (RBD, MPM, PBD) for robust and diverse physical simulations.
- Utilizes WonderTrace, which leverages video generation models and a partial denoising strategy, to translate simulated physical states into photorealistic video outputs, preserving spatiotemporal structure.

## Results

- Achieved the highest Borda scores in human evaluation for Semantic Adherence (6.93), Physical Commonsense (6.90), Video Quality (6.64), and Overall Preference (6.91) compared to seven other methods.
- The framework supports real-time interactive physics previews at approximately 15 frames per second (FPS) after a one-time scene initialization cost of about 46 seconds.
- Scene-aware pose alignment, including the Anchor-Guided Ground Plane Estimation (AGMF) strategy, achieved a **0.00** Penetration Rate and **0.90** Interaction Success Rate, ensuring valid initial physical states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D scene reconstruction and simulator-based dynamics are essential for achieving physical plausibility and fine-grained control over object interactions in video generation.
> - A unified world coordinate frame, with robust ground plane estimation (AGMF) and coarse-to-fine perspective alignment, is crucial for resolving object interpenetration and ensuring geometric consistency between 2D inputs and 3D scenes.
> - Decoupling the physical simulation from the photorealistic rendering pipeline enables real-time interactive previews, significantly accelerating the iterative design and manipulation of dynamic scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "TelePhysics" is situated within the rapidly evolving field of generative AI, specifically focusing on the generation of dynamic video content from static images. Recent advancements in generative video models, often employing diffusion or autoregressive architectures, have led to high visual realism. However, these models typically operate primarily on appearance, lacking explicit understanding of 3D geometry, mechanical laws, or physical consistency. This limitation restricts their ability to produce videos with reliable physical interactions and fine-grained control over object movements.

Existing approaches in this landscape generally fall into a few categories:
- **Appearance-driven video generators**: These models excel at visual quality but offer limited or no explicit physical control, leading to outputs that may be visually appealing but physically implausible (e.g., objects interpenetrating, ignoring gravity). Attempts to incorporate physics often rely on soft constraints or manually specified parameters, resulting in simplistic or short-horizon motions.
- **Single-image-to-3D conversion**: Methods in this area reconstruct 3D objects from 2D images. A common challenge is that objects are often reconstructed independently within egocentric coordinate systems. When these isolated 3D models are combined into a scene, they frequently exhibit spatial misalignment and inter-object penetration, leading to physically incorrect scene configurations.
- **Physics-based scene-level 3D generation**: These approaches aim to model object layouts and physical plausibility. While they can provide explicit 3D representations, they frequently encounter difficulties in accurately aligning generated scenes with the input image. This can manifest as spatial misalignment in object pose, scale, and orientation, stylized artifacts, or inconsistencies with the input data, especially in scenarios involving occlusion or limited viewpoints. Additionally, some methods in this category may produce "cartoonish" visual appearances, diverging from photorealism.
- **Physics-based 3D generation and control**: While some methods have explored integrating 3D generation with physical control, they often lead to outputs that lack photorealistic appearance, appearing more like renderings from a simplified simulator than real-world video.
- **Latency constraints**: Physics-based simulations, especially when coupled with complex rendering pipelines or large generative models, can be computationally intensive, hindering real-time interaction or rapid iteration.

TelePhysics aims to address these identified limitations by proposing a training-free framework that integrates robust 3D scene reconstruction, precise scene alignment, a multi-physics simulation engine, and photorealistic video re-rendering. It seeks to bridge the gap between visual fidelity and physical accuracy, enabling controllable and interactive video generation from a single image while mitigating issues such as object interpenetration, spatial misalignment, and appearance inconsistencies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of TelePhysics is to transform a single static image into a dynamic, physically consistent, and controllable video. This goal is motivated by several limitations observed in current generative video and 3D reconstruction methodologies. The paper details the following specific objectives:

1. **Achieve Physical Consistency and Controllability in Video Generation**: Current generative video models often lack explicit mechanical reasoning. This results in videos where objects move without adhering to physical laws, and user control over object trajectories and interactions is minimal. TelePhysics aims to provide fine-grained, long-horizon physical control over multi-object scenes, enabling diverse mechanics-based manipulations such as various force fields (e.g., gravity, wind, vortex) and explicit 3D velocities. The motivation is to move beyond appearance-driven synthesis to generate outcomes that are both visually realistic and physically plausible.

2. **Resolve Object Interpenetration and Spatial Misalignment**: Traditional single-image-to-3D conversion methods frequently reconstruct objects in isolation. When these independently reconstructed objects are placed into a single scene, they often interpenetrate or are spatially misaligned, leading to physically impossible configurations. A key objective is to reconstruct the entire scene geometry within a unified spatial coordinate system to inherently resolve these ambiguities and ensure coherent object layouts.

3. **Ensure Photorealistic Visual Fidelity**: Physics-based 3D generation methods can sometimes produce outputs with stylized artifacts or a "cartoonish" appearance, diverging from the photorealism of the input image. TelePhysics aims to preserve the photorealistic visual fidelity of the input image throughout the dynamic simulation and rendering process, ensuring that the generated video maintains high visual quality while adhering to physical laws.

4. **Enable Real-Time Interaction**: Existing methods often rely on latency-heavy priors or computationally intensive rendering processes, making real-time interaction difficult. A significant objective is to decouple the physical simulation from the rendering pipeline. This separation is intended to allow for real-time interactive physics previews, enabling users to manipulate scene elements and observe immediate, physically accurate responses without significant computational delay.

5. **Develop a Unified, Training-Free Framework for Holistic Scene Reconstruction**: The paper motivates the creation of a single, cohesive framework that integrates all necessary steps—from scene perception and 3D reconstruction to physical simulation and video synthesis—without requiring extensive training for each new input image. This aims to simplify the generation process and improve consistency across different stages.

By addressing these objectives, TelePhysics seeks to overcome critical barriers in physics-grounded scene generation, moving towards more interactive, realistic, and controllable synthetic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TelePhysics proposes a training-free unified framework that converts a single image into a physically consistent and controllable video. The pipeline operates through four main stages: Scene Perception, Scene Alignment, Physics Simulation, and WonderTrace (video generation).

#### 3.1. Scene Perception

This module decomposes the input image into instance-level foreground geometry and a consistent background.
- **Instance-level Mesh Reconstruction**: Given an input image and semantic prompts, the Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM3) is used to generate high-fidelity instance masks for foreground objects. These 2D masks are then lifted into explicit 3D geometric meshes using SAM-3D-Objects. The use of explicit mesh-based representations is specified to facilitate stable mechanical simulation by providing precise contact interfaces, in contrast to implicit or point-based representations that can lead to ghosting artifacts.
- **Background Synthesis and Expansion**: To create a complete and spatially consistent environment, occluded regions in the background are first synthesized using the LaMa inpainting model. This produces a restored background (B_rest) with foreground objects removed. Subsequently, the field of view is expanded via an outpainting function (F_outpaint) to create an extended panoramic context (B_ext). This expansion is intended to accommodate dynamic camera viewpoints and resolve scale ambiguity, thereby mitigating the "cardboard" effect in localized reconstruction.

#### 3.2. Scene Alignment

This stage addresses spatial inconsistencies arising from egocentric object reconstruction and camera estimation, establishing a unified world coordinate frame.
- **4.2.1. Pose Coordinate Alignment**: This mechanism transforms egocentric mesh representations into a unified world coordinate frame and anchors them to a canonical ground plane to ensure coherent spatial alignment.
 - **Single-Object Canonical Alignment**: For individual objects, Global Centroid Normalization translates the mesh to the world origin. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to determine principal axes, which are then aligned with the world vertical axis (Z-up) via a rotation matrix. Finally, Ground-Contact Correction shifts the object so its lowest point rests on the Z=0 plane.
 - **Multi-Object Scene Alignment**: For scenes with multiple objects, vertices from all meshes are aggregated into a global point cloud. The RANSAC algorithm is then used to fit a dominant plane, representing the ground. An orthogonal transformation aligns the estimated ground normal with the world vertical axis, and the scene is translated so the ground plane coincides with z=0.
 - **Anchor-Guided Ground Plane Estimation (AGMF)**: To overcome the issue of standard RANSAC converging on dense vertical structures instead of the true ground, AGMF extracts vertices from the lower extrema of each object mesh to form an "anchor set" (P_base). A robust M-estimator is then minimized over this subset to determine the ground plane parameters, ensuring the estimated normal aligns with the collective gravitational base.

- **4.2.2. Perspective Alignment**: This module refines camera parameters to ensure photometric and geometric consistency between the reconstructed scene and the input image. It formulates perspective alignment as a continuous optimization problem over camera parameters.
 - **Loss Functions**: Optimization minimizes a combination of three loss terms: a Region-Aware Appearance Loss (L_obj) for pixel-wise fidelity within object regions, another for background regions (L_bg), and a Mask Alignment Loss (L_mask) using the Dice coefficient to enforce geometric consistency between rendered and target silhouettes.
 - **Coarse-to-Fine Optimization**: The optimization strategy combines a coarse global search (uniform sampling of candidate poses within bounded space) to identify a reliable initialization, followed by a local refinement step using the derivative-free Powell method to achieve precise pose adjustment.

#### 3.3. Physical Simulation

This stage computes physically compliant trajectories and collision responses for the aligned scene.
- **VLM-Based Material Parameter Estimation**: A Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, is used to automatically estimate categorical (e.g., material type) and continuous (e.g., mass, friction, Young's modulus) material parameters for each reconstructed entity. The VLM processes the full scene image and per-object crops, guided by a system prompt that specifies material types and their associated parameters.
- **Multi-Physics Backend**: The Genesis engine serves as a unified backend, routing objects to appropriate solvers based on VLM classifications:
 - **Rigid Body Dynamics (RBD)**: Handles non-deformable objects and robotic manipulators using articulated body algorithms for stable, penetration-free interactions.
 - **Material Point Method (MPM)**: Simulates fluids and hyperelastic materials (e.g., sand, snow) as a hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian method, capable of modeling large volumetric deformations and phenomena like fracture or splashing.
 - **Position-Based Dynamics (PBD)**: Manages thin-shell structures like cloth and cables by directly modifying object positions to satisfy geometric constraints, ensuring stability in stiff systems.
 - These solvers operate concurrently with GPU-based parallelism and adaptive time-stepping, exchanging force and state information to manage multi-material interactions.

#### 3.4. WonderTrace

This module bridges the domain gap between coarse physical simulations and photorealistic video outputs.
- **Photorealistic Re-rendering**: WonderTrace leverages the latent space of video generation models (specifically Wan2.1 VACE or Wan2.2 VACE 14B) as a rendering prior. It translates simulated physical states and trajectories (e.g., depth maps, optical flow, object masks) into conditional control signals.
- **Partial Denoising Strategy**: To balance computational efficiency with visual fidelity, the module perturbs simulation frames with moderate noise and applies only the final denoising stages of the diffusion process. This approach is intended to preserve the spatiotemporal structure of the simulation while enriching the scene with high-frequency visual details.
- **Novel View Synthesis and FOV Expansion**: By extending the background environment (from Scene Perception) into the video re-rendering stage, WonderTrace supports novel camera viewpoints and extensive virtual camera movements without out-of-bounds artifacts. The re-rendered, expanded background is seamlessly composited with dynamic foreground elements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TelePhysics involved qualitative, quantitative, and ablation studies, demonstrating its performance in controllability, physical consistency, visual quality, and runtime efficiency.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Comparisons

Qualitative comparisons with existing video generation baselines (e.g., Sora2-pro, Veo3.1, CogVideoX1.5, Wan2.2-A14B, WonderPlay, PhysCtrl) indicate that TelePhysics generates motions that are temporally coherent and mechanically consistent with specified events. This is particularly evident in scenarios involving complex multi-object interactions such as support, collision, and stacking, where TelePhysics avoids the drift, interpenetration, and identity inconsistency observed in appearance-driven video generation models. Appearance-driven methods, while visually appealing, frequently show a lack of reliable control over object trajectories and often produce ambiguous collisions or motions that deviate from specified forces. Physics-aware baselines like PhysCtrl, though improved in controllability, can still struggle with long-horizon stability and spatial consistency in multi-object environments.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison (GPT-5 Evaluation)

A GPT-5-based 5-point Likert scoring framework was used to evaluate 60 generated videos across three criteria:
- **Semantic Adherence (SA)**: TelePhysics achieved the highest score (3.29), indicating strong alignment between generated video content and text prompts, including specified initial forces or velocities.
- **Physical Commonsense (PC)**: TelePhysics also obtained the highest score (3.15), reflecting that generated motions and interactions are plausible under fundamental physical principles.
- **Video Quality (VQ)**: While Veo3.1 achieved the highest video quality score (3.71), TelePhysics remained competitive (3.61).

These results suggest that the explicit scene reconstruction and simulator-based dynamics employed by TelePhysics significantly enhance controllability and physical plausibility, while maintaining competitive visual quality. The performance of earlier physics-aware methods (PhysCtrl, WonderPlay) was noted to be lower in multi-object scenes, highlighting a strength of TelePhysics in such scenarios.

#### 4.3. Human Evaluation

A ranking-based human study corroborated the automated evaluation. Participants ranked videos from seven methods based on Semantic Adherence, Physical Commonsense, Video Quality, and Overall Preference. TelePhysics consistently received the highest Borda scores across all four criteria: SA (6.93), PC (6.90), VQ (6.64), and Overall Preference (6.91). This indicates that human annotators perceived TelePhysics outputs as more controllable and physically plausible, contributing to a better overall viewing experience.

#### 4.4. Generation Speed

The framework's runtime on an NVIDIA H100 GPU was analyzed. Scene perception (segmentation, 3D mesh reconstruction) and scene alignment (physics config estimation, pose, and camera optimization) incur a one-time initialization cost of approximately 46 seconds per scene. After this initialization, the system supports a real-time interactive physics preview at approximately 15 frames per second (FPS). The optional high-fidelity offline diffusion re-rendering stage is not included in these real-time FPS statistics.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Scene-aware Pose Alignment**: This component was shown to be critical. Without alignment, objects were poorly placed (Mask IoU 0.11), while single-object alignment led to severe geometric conflicts (Penetration Rate 0.50). Vanilla RANSAC improved spatial grounding but still resulted in Support Violation Rate (SVR) of 0.15. The full AGMF strategy achieved 0.00 Penetration Rate and the highest Interaction Success Rate (0.90), demonstrating its necessity for valid initial physical states.
- **Perspective Alignment**: The coarse-to-fine camera pose optimization strategy proved essential. Relying solely on an initial camera guess resulted in high Reprojection Error (38.95 pixels). Combining global search with local Powell optimization (the coarse-to-fine approach) achieved the lowest Reprojection Error (14.33 pixels) and competitive visual metrics (SSIM 0.76, LPIPS 0.13), confirming its role in aligning 2D and 3D spaces.
- **Force Field Configuration**: While purely appearance-based metrics (Motion Amplitude, Smoothness) might favor unconstrained motions, the "Full forces" configuration, which includes gravity and primary forces, is necessary to impose strict physical laws and prevent unnatural object drifting, despite potentially lower "smoothness" scores due to realistic interactions.
- **Inference Efficiency in Video Synthesis**: For the video synthesis stage, 10 denoising steps provided optimal visual fidelity (SSIM 0.82, LPIPS 0.05) with the lowest wall-clock time (539.03 s per video), indicating that the conditional signals from explicit dynamics are highly informative and facilitate rapid convergence.
- **Geometric Fidelity of Re-rendering**: Evaluation confirmed that depth-conditioned video re-rendering reliably preserves the underlying physical geometry of the raw simulation. Re-rendered outputs maintained robust scene layouts (overall SSIM 0.72) with minimal object centroid displacement (52.5 pixels on average) and low silhouette drift for deformable objects, indicating structural consistency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TelePhysics represents an advancement in the domain of physics-grounded scene generation, offering a method to convert a single image into a controllable and physically consistent video. The framework addresses several limitations present in prior appearance-driven video generation and object-centric 3D reconstruction methods.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to generate dynamic video content that adheres to physical laws while maintaining visual fidelity to the input image. This is achieved through a holistic scene-level 3D reconstruction, which resolves issues like inter-object penetration and spatial misalignment common in systems that reconstruct objects independently. By unifying all scene elements within a shared 3D representation and enforcing consistent spatial constraints, TelePhysics contributes to the creation of more plausible and stable interactive environments.

The framework's enhanced controllability is a notable impact. Unlike generative video models that provide limited physical control, TelePhysics enables fine-grained, long-horizon manipulation of multi-object interactions through diverse force fields and 3D velocities. This capability is crucial for applications requiring precise control over object dynamics, such as virtual prototyping, robotics simulation, and interactive content creation for games or virtual reality.

Furthermore, the decoupling of the physical simulation from the rendering pipeline contributes to real-time interaction capabilities. While an initial setup cost is present, the subsequent real-time physics preview at approximately 15 FPS allows for immediate feedback on user-defined interactions, accelerating creative workflows and facilitating iterative design in dynamic virtual scenes.

The use of a Vision-Language Model for automatic material parameter estimation and a multi-physics backend (Genesis) with specialized solvers (RBD, MPM, PBD) provides a robust and adaptable approach to simulating diverse material behaviors. This modularity contributes to the system's ability to handle complex multi-material interactions effectively.

Potential impacts extend across various fields:
- **Content Creation**: Artists and designers could rapidly prototype dynamic scenes for film pre-visualization, animation, or game development, with assurance of physical realism.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI**: The ability to generate physically accurate and interactive environments from single images could provide valuable synthetic data and testing grounds for training and evaluating robotic agents or embodied AI systems.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality**: The creation of dynamic, interactive scenes with physical consistency can enhance immersion and realism in VR/AR applications.
- **Scientific Visualization**: Generating physically accurate simulations from real-world imagery could aid in studying and understanding complex physical phenomena.

Despite these advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations, including dependence on the accuracy of monocular segmentation and 3D reconstruction, challenges with highly articulated or transparent objects, and the latency associated with high-quality offline re-rendering. Nevertheless, TelePhysics establishes a practical foundation for future research in jointly inferring geometry, material properties, and contact attributes from visual and linguistic cues, and for developing uncertainty-aware scene reconstruction techniques. The open-source availability of the code is expected to foster further innovation in this area.
