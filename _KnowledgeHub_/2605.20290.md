# TelePhysics: Physics-Grounded Multi-Object Scene Generation from a Single Image with Real-Time Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> TelePhysics, a training-free framework developed by Fudan University and TeleAI, converts a single image into a physically consistent and controllable multi-object video. Human evaluation ranked its outputs highest across categories, including Semantic Adherence (6.93 Borda score), Physical Commonsense (6.90 Borda score), and Overall Preference (6.91 Borda score), while maintaining competitive visual quality.

## Problem

- Generative video models often lack explicit understanding of 3D geometry and physical laws, leading to physically implausible object interactions and limited user control.
- Single-image-to-3D reconstruction methods frequently reconstruct objects in isolation, resulting in interpenetration and spatial misalignment when assembling multi-object scenes.
- Physics-based 3D generation approaches commonly sacrifice photorealism, producing stylized or 'cartoonish' visual appearances rather than maintaining fidelity to the input image.

## Method

- A training-free framework that integrates ==Scene Perception== for instance-level 3D mesh reconstruction and background expansion, and Scene Alignment for transforming objects into a unified world coordinate frame.
- Employs a Vision-Language Model for automatic material parameter estimation and a multi-physics backend (Genesis engine) with specialized solvers (RBD, MPM, PBD) for robust and diverse physical simulations.
- Utilizes WonderTrace, which leverages video generation models and a partial denoising strategy, to translate simulated physical states into photorealistic video outputs, preserving spatiotemporal structure.

## Results

- Achieved the highest Borda scores in human evaluation for Semantic Adherence (6.93), Physical Commonsense (6.90), Video Quality (6.64), and Overall Preference (6.91) compared to seven other methods.
- The framework supports real-time interactive physics previews at approximately 15 frames per second (FPS) after a one-time scene initialization cost of about 46 seconds.
- Scene-aware pose alignment, including the Anchor-Guided Ground Plane Estimation (AGMF) strategy, achieved a **0.00** Penetration Rate and **0.90** Interaction Success Rate, ensuring valid initial physical states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D scene reconstruction and simulator-based dynamics are essential for achieving physical plausibility and fine-grained control over object interactions in video generation.
> - A unified world coordinate frame, with robust ground plane estimation (AGMF) and coarse-to-fine perspective alignment, is crucial for resolving object interpenetration and ensuring geometric consistency between 2D inputs and 3D scenes.
> - Decoupling the physical simulation from the photorealistic rendering pipeline enables real-time interactive previews, significantly accelerating the iterative design and manipulation of dynamic scenes.
