# Touch in the Wild: Learning Fine-Grained Manipulation with a Portable Visuo-Tactile Gripper

> [!summary] Summary
> A portable visuo-tactile gripper and a masked autoencoding framework for multimodal representation learning enable robots to perform fine-grained manipulation tasks in diverse real-world environments with improved success rates. For example, the system increased the success rate for test tube collection from 0.25 (vision-only) to 0.85.

## Problem

- A scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and real-world datasets that integrate vision and touch for robotic manipulation.
- Existing advanced tactile sensors are often bulky, fragile, or confined to labs, hindering their deployment in diverse, unstructured environments.
- Current robot learning methods, often vision-only, lack the fine-grained contact information critical for precise, human-like manipulation in ambiguous scenarios.

## Method

- Development of a lightweight, portable **visuo-tactile gripper**, integrating ==flexible piezoresistive sensors== with a fisheye camera, designed for 'in-the-wild' data collection.
- Curating and open-sourcing a large-scale dataset comprising over 2.6 million synchronized visuo-tactile pairs from 2,700 human demonstrations across 43 tasks in 12 diverse environments.
- A self-supervised ==masked autoencoding== framework with ==cross-attention== for visuo-tactile representation learning, which fuses modalities while preserving fine-grained tactile information.

## Results

- The proposed system achieved the highest success rates across four challenging manipulation tasks, improving Test Tube Collection success from **0.25** (vision-only) to **0.85**.
- The pretrained visuo-tactile encoder consistently and accurately reconstructs tactile images from partial inputs and focuses visual attention on contact areas, even for out-of-distribution examples.
- Pretraining dramatically improved policy performance in low-data and low-epoch training regimes, demonstrating faster convergence, lower training loss, and higher real-world success rates compared to non-pretrained baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The masked autoencoding objective effectively reconstructs tactile images and generates interpretable representations that consistently focus on contact-relevant visual regions.
> - Visuo-tactile integration provides crucial in-hand state information and improves the detection of subtle state transitions, mitigating issues of visual occlusion and ambiguity.
> - Self-supervised pretraining yields generalizable visuo-tactile representations that significantly enhance sample efficiency and robustness for downstream policies, especially in low-data regimes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, largely driven by progress in reinforcement learning and imitation learning. However, a significant bottleneck remains: the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and real-world datasets that integrate multiple sensory modalities, particularly vision and touch. This paper directly addresses this critical gap, contributing to several key areas in robotics and AI.

Historically, robotic manipulation research has often relied on either vision-only systems or specialized tactile sensors confined to structured laboratory environments. The limitation of vision-only systems becomes apparent in "in-the-wild" settings, where visual cues can be unreliable due to occlusion, poor lighting, or cluttered backgrounds. Tactile feedback, while crucial for human fine-grained manipulation (e.g., inserting a key or adjusting a pipette), has been difficult to integrate scalably into robotic systems. Existing tactile sensors often suffer from issues of bulkiness, rigidity, lack of robustness for mobile deployment, or difficulties in synchronization with visual data.

This work specifically positions itself to bridge the divide between lab-centric research and real-world deployment by tackling two primary challenges:
1. **Portable Tactile Hardware:** Most advanced tactile sensors (e.g., soft bubble sensors, optical-based sensors like GelSight) are often too large, rigid, or fragile for robust, long-term use in diverse, unstructured "in-the-wild" environments. The paper introduces a lightweight, robust, and portable visuo-tactile gripper that overcomes these limitations, enabling scalable data collection outside of controlled lab settings.
2. **Learning from Multimodal Data:** Integrating tactile and visual signals is challenging due to their distinct characteristics—vision provides global, semantic context, while touch offers local, high-resolution physical interaction feedback. Previous approaches have often used contrastive learning, which can sometimes suppress the fine-grained, geometry-sensitive information inherent in tactile signals. This paper proposes a novel masked autoencoding approach that better preserves modality-specific features while fostering cross-modal understanding.

Furthermore, the paper aligns with a broader trend in robot learning that emphasizes collecting human demonstrations "in the wild" to transfer complex skills to robots (e.g., Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI)). However, these prior "in-the-wild" systems have predominantly been vision-centric. By incorporating tactile sensing, this research significantly enriches the fidelity of human demonstrations, allowing robots to learn the contact-rich strategies essential for precise manipulation. The creation and commitment to open-sourcing a large-scale, diverse visuo-tactile dataset also makes a substantial contribution to the community, addressing the data scarcity problem that has hampered multimodal robot learning. The acknowledgment of concurrent work like ViTaMIn highlights the growing recognition within the research community of the importance of portable visuo-tactile systems for real-world manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that humans seamlessly integrate vision and touch for effective and precise manipulation in the physical world. This multimodal capability is particularly critical in scenarios where visual information is ambiguous, occluded, or unreliable, necessitating local, high-resolution feedback provided by touch. The authors note a significant deficiency in current robotic systems and data collection methodologies:
- Most existing handheld grippers used for collecting human demonstrations lack tactile sensing, thus failing to capture the crucial contact-rich strategies employed by humans in real-world tasks.
- Reliance on vision alone makes robotic systems vulnerable to environmental variability (e.g., lighting changes, background clutter), whereas tactile sensing offers a robust and complementary signal.

To address these limitations, the paper sets forth three primary objectives, which also encapsulate its core contributions:

1. **Develop a portable, lightweight visuo-tactile gripper:** The first objective is to engineer a compact and ergonomic handheld gripper that integrates flexible piezoresistive tactile sensors with a vision system (fisheye camera). This hardware is designed to facilitate synchronized collection of visual and tactile data in diverse, real-world, and "in-the-wild" environments, enabling large-scale human manipulation demonstration capture.

2. **Propose a multimodal representation and policy learning framework:** The second objective is to design a learning framework that effectively fuses visual and tactile signals. The proposed framework aims to integrate these distinct modalities while preserving their unique characteristics, specifically through a masked autoencoding approach utilizing cross-attention. This framework seeks to enable more efficient and accurate policy learning for fine-grained manipulation tasks by allowing interpretable representations to emerge, focusing consistently on relevant contacting regions.

3. **Curate and open-source a diverse, in-the-wild multimodal manipulation dataset:** The third objective is to create and make publicly available a substantial dataset comprising synchronized visuo-tactile pairs. This dataset, collected using the developed hardware across numerous tasks and environments, is intended to support effective visuo-tactile pretraining and significantly improve downstream policy learning.

Ultimately, the overarching goal is to validate this integrated system on challenging, fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks (e.g., test tube insertion, pipette-based fluid transfer) to demonstrate improved accuracy and robustness, thereby bridging the gap between human demonstrations and robot learning in complex, real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented is a comprehensive two-stage pipeline, encompassing hardware design, large-scale data collection, and a novel multimodal learning framework.

#### 3.1. Visuo-Tactile Data Collection System (Hardware)

The authors designed a **lightweight, handheld visuo-tactile gripper** that builds upon the triple-layer piezoresistive sensor architecture of 3D-ViTac. Key modifications include:
- **Scalable Flexible Tactile Sensors:** Thin, matrix-based tactile pads are embedded into soft, fin-shaped gripper fingers. They achieve higher spatial resolution (12x32 taxels per pad, 2x2mm² each) and improved robustness by replacing stainless-steel electrodes with flexible printed circuit (FPC) electrodes. The FPC design also enables rapid, tool-free fabrication and mounting.
- **Portable Multi-Modal Sensing System:** Each tactile pad connects to a custom Arduino-based PCB housed within the gripper's palm. The complete unit, including batteries, weighs approximately 962g, making it suitable for prolonged "in-the-wild" use. A fisheye RGB camera is integrated for visual input.
- **Multi-Modal Data Synchronization:** To ensure precise alignment between visual and tactile data streams (tactile at 23 Hz, GoPro at 60 Hz), a hardware-free synchronization strategy is employed. QR codes displaying host timestamps are shown to the camera before each demonstration, while tactile data is timestamped by the microcontroller and published via ROS2. Offline post-processing decodes QR codes and aligns data using the shared host-clock reference.

Using this system, the authors curated an **in-the-wild multimodal manipulation dataset** of over 2.6 million synchronized visuo-tactile pairs, comprising more than 2,700 demonstrations across 43 manipulation tasks in 12 diverse indoor and outdoor environments. This dataset is committed to being open-sourced.

#### 3.2. Visuo-Tactile Representation and Policy Learning (Software)

The learning framework is divided into two stages:

**Stage 1: Visuo-Tactile Representation Learning (Self-Supervised Pretraining)**
- **Problem Formulation:** The goal is to learn a multimodal encoder $E_\phi$ that fuses RGB image $I$ and tactile image $T$ into a joint representation $z_{fusion}$ while preserving modality-specific structure.
- **Masked Autoencoding Objective:** Instead of contrastive learning, which can suppress fine-grained tactile signals, the authors use a masked autoencoding objective. The encoder $E_\phi$ and decoder $D_\psi$ are jointly optimized to reconstruct full tactile images from partially observed tactile input and corresponding visual frames.
- **Tactile Encoder:** The 1x24x32 tactile image (vertically stacked left and right fingertip readings, converted to 3-channel via colormap) is divided into 4x4 patches. During training, 60-80% of patches are randomly masked in 95% of samples. A 3-layer CNN processes the masked input to produce a 768-dimensional tactile embedding ($z_{tac}$).
- **Vision Encoder:** An RGB image $I$ is processed by a ViT-B/16 encoder, initialized from CLIP and finetuned. The final [CLS] token serves as the 768-dimensional visual embedding ($z_{img}$).
- **Cross-Modal Fusion:** Two rounds of multi-head cross-attention (MHAttn) are applied:
 - $z_{tac}$ queries $z_{img}$ to get $z'_{tac}$.
 - $z_{img}$ queries the updated $z''_{tac}$ to get $z'_{img}$.
 - The updated embeddings are concatenated to form the 2D-dimensional fused representation ($z_{fusion}$).
- **Tactile Reconstruction Decoder:** The $z_{fusion}$ is passed through a two-layer MLP with sigmoid activation to reconstruct the tactile image ($\hat{T}$). The loss is the L2 norm between the ground truth $T$ and $\hat{T}$.
- **Stabilization:** An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of online weights is used for the target encoder.

**Stage 2: Policy Learning via Behavior Cloning**
- **Integration:** The pretrained visuo-tactile encoder $E_\phi$ is integrated into a conditional diffusion policy.
- **Observation Space:** At each timestep $t$, the robot receives raw sensory inputs ($I_t$, $T_t$, $p_t$), where $p_t$ is the proprioceptive state (end-effector pose, gripper width). $I_t$ and $T_t$ are passed through $E_\phi$ to produce the fused visuo-tactile embedding $z_t = E_\phi(I_t, T_t)$. The diffusion policy is conditioned on $o_t = (z_t, p_t)$.
- **Diffusion Policy:** A conditional diffusion policy (convolutional U-Net with DDIM inference) is adopted. It predicts noise ($\hat{\epsilon}^k_t$) for a noisy action ($a^k_t$) based on the conditioning observation $o_t$. Training uses an MSE loss between added and predicted noise.
- **Training Details:** All encoder components, including the CLIP backbone, tactile CNN, and cross-attention layers, are finetuned during this stage.

#### 3.3. Experiments

The system is evaluated on four challenging real-world robotic tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation: Test Tube Collection, Pencil Insertion (both requiring in-hand state information), Fluid Transfer, and Whiteboard Erasing (both requiring fine-grained force information). Performance is compared against vision-only, Ours w/o Cross-Attention, and Ours w/o Pretraining baselines over 20 trials for each task. Ablation studies further analyze the impact of pretraining data quantity and epochs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across hardware validation, representation learning, and downstream manipulation performance.

#### 4.1. Hardware Validation and Data Collection

The developed portable, lightweight visuo-tactile gripper successfully enables synchronized collection of visual and tactile data in diverse, real-world, and "in-the-wild" environments. The dataset curated from this system comprises over 2.6 million visuo-tactile pairs from 2,700 demonstrations across 43 tasks in 12 environments, highlighting the scalability and versatility of the hardware. Supplementary experiments confirm strong cross-sensor consistency, indicating the reliability and interchangeability of the fabricated tactile sensors.

#### 4.2. Visuo-Tactile Representation Learning Quality

- **Effective Reconstruction:** Qualitative results (Figure 5) demonstrate that the pretrained encoder, using the masked autoencoding objective, accurately reconstructs tactile images from partially masked tactile and RGB inputs. This performance is consistent for both in-distribution and out-of-distribution examples, suggesting the model learns meaningful visuo-tactile associations that generalize.
- **Interpretable Representations:** Visualizations of self-attention maps from the final ViT layer (Figure 5, Figure 10) show that the model consistently attends to contact-relevant regions of the RGB images (i.e., the gripper-object contact area). This focus is maintained across diverse scenarios, independent of background clutter or object familiarity, indicating that the learned representations are interpretable and task-relevant.
- **Impact of Pretraining Data Scale:** Ablation studies (Supplementary Figure 8) reveal that larger pretraining datasets lead to lower reconstruction losses and more stable training, resulting in cleaner and more structured tactile reconstructions and more precise visual attention on contact regions.
- **Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** Critically, pretraining ablations where task-specific demonstrations were *excluded* from the pretraining dataset (Supplementary Table 3) showed nearly identical downstream policy performance. This strong result indicates that the learned visuo-tactile representations generalize effectively to novel manipulation tasks, rather than overfitting to specific examples in the pretraining data.

#### 4.3. Downstream Manipulation Task Performance

The system was validated on four challenging real-world robotic tasks: Test Tube Collection, Pencil Insertion, Fluid Transfer, and Whiteboard Erasing. Table 1 presents a clear performance hierarchy:
- **Superiority of Visuo-Tactile with Pretraining:** The `Ours w/ Pretraining` method consistently achieved the highest success rates across all tasks and subtasks. For instance, in Test Tube Collection, it reached 0.85 success compared to `Vision-Only`'s 0.25. In Fluid Transfer, it achieved 0.90 success versus `Vision-Only`'s 0.55.
- **Importance of Cross-Attention:** The `Ours w/o Cross-Attention` baseline, which simply concatenates features, performed worse than `Ours w/ Pretraining`, highlighting the necessity of effective cross-modal fusion.
- **Value of Pretraining:** `Ours w/ Pretraining` significantly outperformed `Ours w/o Pretraining` (encoder initialized from scratch or CLIP only), emphasizing the benefits of the self-supervised pretraining stage for learning robust representations.

#### 4.4. Analysis and Discussion of Key Benefits

The authors identify three key benefits derived from incorporating touch and leveraging pretrained representations (Section 5.3):
1. **Explicit In-Hand State Information:** Tactile feedback mitigates issues of visual occlusion and ambiguity inherent in single-camera setups. For example, in Test Tube Collection, tactile sensing provided robust orientation information, unaffected by changes in cork color that confused vision-only policies.
2. **Improved Detection of Critical State Transitions:** In force-controlled tasks like Fluid Transfer, tactile pressure feedback enabled precise detection of subtle force changes, crucial for distinguishing between action phases (e.g., whether squeezing has ended). This led to smoother transitions and improved accuracy compared to vision-only policies that struggled with ambiguous visual cues.
3. **Coordinated Use of Vision and Touch:** The jointly trained visuo-tactile encoder, facilitated by cross-attention, enables a harmonious integration of modalities. Unlike naive concatenation methods, which led to over-reliance on one modality (e.g., excessive force in Whiteboard Erasing causing safety stops), the proposed fusion allows for modulated force application based on both visual context and tactile feedback.

#### 4.5. Pretraining Ablations (Low-Data and Low-Epoch Regimes)

Ablation studies on the Test Tube Collection task (Figure 7) demonstrated that pretraining provides substantial benefits, especially in resource-constrained scenarios:
- **Low-Data Regime:** With only 30 or 60 demonstrations, the pretrained policy significantly outperformed the non-pretrained policy, exhibiting smoother trajectories and fewer failures, especially during complex reorientation. This suggests pretraining helps the encoder learn visuo-tactile correlations early, allowing the policy to focus on action trajectories.
- **Low-Epoch Regime:** Pretraining made policies more robust to initial environmental configurations and imperfect grasps, preventing over- or under-reorientation failures. This indicates that prior knowledge from pretraining, particularly emphasizing tactile cues, enhances robustness to disturbances.
- **Faster Convergence and Higher Success:** Pretrained policies consistently achieved lower training loss values and higher real-world success rates, underscoring improved sample efficiency and stability (Figure 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for real-world, unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact span several critical dimensions:

**1. Enabling Scalable "In-the-Wild" Robotic Learning:**
The development of a **portable, lightweight, and robust visuo-tactile gripper** is a crucial hardware innovation. It addresses a major bottleneck in robot learning by facilitating the collection of high-fidelity, multimodal human demonstrations in diverse, uncontrolled settings—a capability previously limited to structured labs or vision-only systems. This hardware democratizes access to complex interaction data, moving robotic research closer to real-world applicability.

**2. Providing a Foundational Dataset:**
The creation and commitment to open-sourcing a **large-scale, diverse, and synchronized "in-the-wild" visuo-tactile dataset** (over 2.6 million pairs from 2,700 demonstrations across 43 tasks) represents a tremendous contribution to the research community. This dataset will serve as a vital resource for training and benchmarking multimodal learning algorithms, accelerating progress in areas such as visuo-tactile representation learning, sim-to-real transfer, and robust policy development. Its diversity in tasks and environments helps ensure learned policies are generalizable.

**3. Advancing Multimodal Representation Learning:**
The proposed **masked autoencoding framework with cross-attention** offers a powerful and effective method for integrating visual and tactile signals. By prioritizing the preservation of fine-grained tactile information while leveraging visual context, this approach overcomes limitations of simpler fusion strategies and traditional contrastive learning methods. Its ability to generate interpretable representations that consistently focus on contact regions is a key methodological advance, leading to more robust and accurate policy learning.

**4. Bridging the Gap to Human-Level Manipulation:**
The demonstrated significant improvements in accuracy, robustness, and sample efficiency on challenging fine-grained manipulation tasks (e.g., test tube insertion, fluid transfer, whiteboard erasing) highlight the practical utility of integrated visuo-tactile sensing. This work enables robots to perform delicate and precise interactions that mimic human capabilities more closely, particularly in scenarios where vision is insufficient, thereby reducing the dependency on highly structured environments.

**5. Future Trajectories for Robotic Systems:**
This research lays a solid foundation for future work. The authors explicitly mention extending this approach to **multi-finger dexterous hands**, which could unlock even richer and more complex manipulation skills. The success in a parallel gripper setting suggests strong potential for highly articulated hands, pushing the boundaries of what robots can accomplish in terms of dexterity and adaptability.

In summary, "Touch in the Wild" significantly advances both the hardware and algorithmic aspects of visuo-tactile robotic manipulation. By enabling scalable data collection in the real world and developing effective learning strategies, it provides critical tools and insights that will undoubtedly impact the development of more capable, robust, and human-like robotic systems for a wide array of applications.
