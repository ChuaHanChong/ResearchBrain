# MultiPLY: A Multisensory Object-Centric Embodied Large Language Model in 3D World

> [!summary] Summary
> MultiPLY presents the first multisensory object-centric embodied large language model, capable of actively exploring 3D environments to gather and reason with visual, audio, tactile, and thermal data. The model consistently demonstrates improved performance across various embodied tasks requiring interactive, fine-grained understanding of physical objects.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal LLMs primarily process passive 2D visual data, lacking understanding of the 3D physical world and active interaction capabilities.
- There was a scarcity of large-scale datasets providing embodied agents with diverse, actively collected multisensory feedback in 3D environments.
- Current LLMs lack architectural and training paradigms to connect words, actions, and various percepts in a continuous, interactive loop within an embodied setting.

## Method

- Proposed **MultiPLY**, a novel multisensory embodied LLM that integrates object-centric visual, audio, tactile, and thermal interactive data within a 3D world.
- Created "==Multisensory Universe==," a large-scale dataset of 500,000 multisensory interaction data points, generated by an LLM-powered embodied agent in enriched 3D environments.
- Developed an architecture featuring specific action tokens to command agent interactions and state tokens to integrate multisensory observations, enabling end-to-end instruction tuning for interactive tasks.

## Results

- MultiPLY achieved **56.7%** accuracy in object retrieval, outperforming the closest baseline, PointBind-LLM (Finetuned), at **48.9%**, by disentangling specific sensory attributes.
- The model demonstrated **41.6%** accuracy in tool-use tasks, surpassing PointBind-LLM (Finetuned) at **32.1%**, by effectively reasoning about object functionality through interactive sensory data.
- MultiPLY generated richer multisensory captions (BLEU1: **48.9**, BLEU4: **20.1**) and showed a **30.2%** success rate in task decomposition, highlighting the benefits of active interaction and multisensory integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active interaction and the integration of diverse multisensory information are crucial for an LLM to form a robust, human-like understanding of and reasoning about a 3D physical world.
> - Object-centric representations, combined with specialized action and state tokens, allow LLMs to dynamically gather and process fine-grained sensory details, effectively resolving ambiguities that passive models cannot.
> - Step-by-step interaction significantly enhances an embodied agent's ability to decompose complex tasks and reduces hallucination by grounding decisions in real-time sensory feedback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of MultiPLY is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal AI, Embodied AI, and 3D Scene Understanding.

**Current Landscape:**
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** Recent advancements have led to powerful MLLMs like LLaVA, Flamingo, BLIP-2, and PaLM-E, which excel at vision-language tasks (e.g., visual question answering, image captioning). However, these models primarily operate on passive 2D sensory data (images or video frames) and lack an understanding of the 3D physical world.
- **3D Scene Understanding:** Some efforts, such as 3D-LLM, have begun to integrate 3D visual information (e.g., point clouds) into LLMs to enable spatial reasoning. While a step forward, these models often still process 3D data passively and struggle with object-level detail and efficiency.
- **Multisensory Learning:** Independent research areas have explored combining different sensory modalities, such as visual-audio learning (e.g.,) and visual-tactile learning (e.g.,). These often focus on specific modalities or single objects, not comprehensive integration within an interactive 3D environment.
- **Embodied AI:** This field aims to create agents that can interact with and learn from their physical environments. Prior work often focuses on navigation and manipulation, but typically with limited sensory input (e.g., primarily vision).

**The Gap MultiPLY Addresses:**
The paper identifies a critical limitation of existing MLLMs and 3D-LLMs: their passive nature. Humans navigate a "multisensory world" by *actively exploring and interacting* with objects, gathering a rich "mélange of sensory data" (visual, auditory, tactile, thermal) to perform tasks. Current models "passively absorb sensory data," meaning they lack the ability to:
1. **Actively interact with objects** in a 3D environment.
2. **Dynamically collect diverse multisensory information** (beyond just vision and language).
3. **Establish fine-grained correlations** among words, actions, and varied percepts in a dynamic environment.

**MultiPLY's Contribution to the Landscape:**
MultiPLY directly addresses this gap by proposing the first multisensory embodied LLM that actively integrates visual, audio, tactile, and thermal interactive data within a 3D world. It represents a significant step towards developing more human-like AI agents that can not only perceive but also physically engage with their surroundings to gather information and solve complex tasks. By focusing on active interaction and object-centric multisensory representations, MultiPLY pushes beyond static perception to enable dynamic, interactive reasoning. This work contributes to bridging the gap between perception-only LLMs and fully embodied, intelligent agents capable of complex decision-making in the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind MultiPLY stems from a fundamental observation about human intelligence and a critical limitation of current AI systems. Humans possess an innate ability to process and combine various sensory cues (sight, sound, touch, temperature) while actively exploring and interacting with their 3D surroundings to understand the world and accomplish tasks. For example, determining if a donut is ready involves seeing it, potentially hearing the microwave, then touching it to feel its temperature and hardness.

The paper identifies three key challenges that prevent current large language models from replicating this human-like capability:

1. **Paucity of Multisensory Interaction Data:** Training such an LLM requires a vast dataset of interactions where agents actively explore and collect multisensory feedback. Such large-scale, diverse datasets were largely non-existent.
2. **Appropriate Representations for 3D Scenes and Multisensory Information:** LLMs need a flexible way to represent 3D scenes – both an abstract, object-centric overview and detailed multisensory information about specific objects, accessible through interaction. Humans fluidly switch between these levels of detail.
3. **LLMs Not Tailored for Interaction Data:** Existing LLMs are designed to process passive inputs and generate single-step outputs. They lack the architectural and training paradigms to connect words, actions, and percepts in a continuous, interactive loop within an embodied environment.

Based on these motivations and challenges, the key objectives of the MultiPLY project are:

- **To propose MultiPLY:** A novel multisensory embodied large language model capable of encoding object-centric multisensory representations (visual, audio, tactile, thermal) by deploying an agent to actively engage with a 3D environment.
- **To establish correlations:** Enable the LLM to understand the complex relationships among words, actions, and percepts collected through interaction.
- **To collect "Multisensory Universe":** A large-scale, comprehensive dataset comprising 500,000 multisensory interaction data points, generated by an LLM-powered embodied agent interacting in enriched 3D environments. This dataset is crucial for training embodied MLLMs.
- **To develop a novel architectural approach:** Introduce action tokens to denote agent actions and state tokens to represent multisensory observations, enabling end-to-end instruction tuning of a pre-trained LLM for interactive tasks.
- **To demonstrate superior performance:** Evaluate MultiPLY on a diverse set of embodied tasks, including object retrieval, tool use, multisensory captioning, and task decomposition, proving its effectiveness over existing baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology behind MultiPLY involves a multi-pronged approach: the creation of a novel, large-scale dataset, a specialized architecture for integrating multisensory data, and a two-stage training and inference procedure tailored for embodied interaction.

#### 3.1. The Multisensory-Universe Dataset

The core of MultiPLY's training is the **Multisensory Universe**, a large-scale dataset comprising 500,000 multisensory interaction data points. Its generation pipeline consists of three main steps:

1. **Inputting Interactive Objects into 3D Scenes:**
 - **Foundation:** Built upon Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) dataset, which provides 3D spaces and rooms.
 - **Enrichment:** HM3D objects lack interactivity and sensory data. New interactive objects (1-10 per scene) are added from ObjectFolder (with impact sounds and materials) and Objaverse (diverse 3D objects).
 - **ChatGPT for Scene Generation:** ChatGPT is prompted to assign proper bounding boxes, material categories (e.g., ceramic, plastic, steel), properties (deformation, elasticity, hardness), and temperature labels (hot, cold, room temperature) to these newly added objects. This includes creating ambiguous scenarios (e.g., two similar-looking bottles, one plastic, one steel) to necessitate multisensory interaction.

2. **Object Sensor Data Acquisition:**
 - **Tactile:** Uses DiffTactile, a physics-based differentiable tactile simulator. Object meshes are "touched" by a simulated bubble gripper, and marker deformation (initial vs. final positions) provides tactile readings, converted into 2D heatmap images. ChatGPT helps parameterize material properties (Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, Yield Strength) for DiffTactile based on object descriptions.
 - **Ambient Sound:** ChatGPT matches semantic labels of objects with appropriate sounds from AudioSet, which provides a hierarchical ontology of audio events.
 - **Impact Sound:** For ObjectFolder objects, impact sounds are retrieved by querying their implicit sound fields at specific striking locations and forces.
 - **Temperature:** ChatGPT assigns specific numerical temperatures (e.g., 85°C for "hot cup") based on the labels it generated during scene construction.

3. **Embodied Agents for Data Collection:**
 - **Task Generation:** Inspired by, ChatGPT is prompted to generate diverse tasks (multisensory captioning, QA, dialogue, manipulation, tool use, task decomposition, rearrangement) based on the scene's objects, their properties, and available actions. It also generates pseudo ground-truth action sequences and language reasoning outputs.
 - **Interaction Data Collection:** An embodied agent (in Habitat-sim) first randomly explores to gather initial RGBD data. Then, it executes the generated actions (e.g., "touching an object"), and the environment returns the corresponding sensory feedback (e.g., tactile readings, temperature). This interaction history, paired with the language data, forms the Multisensory Universe dataset.

#### 3.2. MultiPLY Architecture

MultiPLY builds upon the LLaVA (LLaVA-v1.5-7b checkpoint) as its backbone multimodal large language model, enhancing it with object-centric representations and interaction-specific tokens.

1. **Object-Centric Scene Representations:**
 - **Initial Scene Impression:** The 3D environment is abstracted into an object-centric representation. Concept Graphs (powered by CLIP encoder and multi-view association) are used to encode objects from observed images, creating `O x 1024` visual features for `O` objects. Position embeddings are added.
 - **Ambient Sound:** If an object emits ambient sound, it's encoded using a CLAP audio encoder, yielding a 1024-dim feature.
 - **Tokens:** These initial inputs are enclosed by `<SCENE>`, `</SCENE>` and `<AMBIENT SOUND>`, `</AMBIENT SOUND>` tokens for the LLM.

2. **Action Tokens:** A novel set of tokens is devised to allow the LLM to command the agent's interactions:
 - `<SELECT>`: Chooses an object based on attention between LLM's language features and CLIP visual features of objects.
 - `<NAVIGATE>`: Instructs the agent to move to the selected object.
 - `<OBSERVE>`: Scrutinizes an object to get its detailed point cloud.
 - `<TOUCH>`: Interacts with an object to obtain tactile and thermal information.
 - `<HIT>`: Strikes an object to obtain its impact sound.
 - `<PICK-UP>`, `<PUT-DOWN>`, `<LOOK-AROUND>`: For manipulation and environmental exploration.

3. **State Tokens:** These tokens convey the agent's multisensory observations back to the LLM:
 - `<OBJECT>`: Encodes detailed object point cloud features (aggregated 3D features from 2D CLIP + position embeddings).
 - `<IMPACT SOUND>`: Encoded impact sound (CLAP + linear projector).
 - `<TACTILE>`: Encoded tactile information (tactile heatmap -> CLIP -> mean-pool -> linear projector).
 - `<TEMPERATURE>`: Encoded temperature (temperature heatmap -> CLIP -> mean-pool -> linear projector).

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

1. **Model Architecture:** LLaVA serves as the backbone. LLaVA’s vision-to-language projector is reused for visual features. For other modalities (sound, tactile, temperature), lightweight one-layer linear projectors (adapters) map their 1024-dim features into LLaVA’s text token embedding space.

2. **Modality Alignment (Stage 1):** Sensor-to-language adapters are trained for alignment. Audio-language alignment uses AudioSet and AudioCaps. For impact sound, tactile, and thermal data, ChatGPT generates one-sentence captions for alignment. During this stage, the image encoder and LLM weights are frozen.

3. **Instruction Tuning with Multisensory Universe (Stage 2):** LLaVA is fine-tuned end-to-end on the Multisensory Universe dataset. The training loss includes the standard LLM loss and an additional Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss that forces the model to select the correct object based on attention scores. The entire model is unfrozen. Training is conducted efficiently on 128 V100 GPUs using FSDP.

4. **Inference:**
 - MultiPLY receives a task prompt and abstract scene representation.
 - It generates a sequence of tokens. If an action token is generated, the embodied agent executes it in Habitat-sim.
 - The resulting multisensory observation is then encoded into state tokens and appended back to the LLM.
 - The LLM continues generating text or subsequent action tokens, creating an interactive dialogue loop.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MultiPLY demonstrates significant advancements over existing baselines across a diverse suite of embodied tasks, validating its approach of active multisensory interaction and object-centric reasoning.

#### 4.1. Object Retrieval

- **Performance:** MultiPLY achieves 56.7% retrieval accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, PointBind-LLM (Finetuned), scores 48.9%, while single-modality models (CLIP, CLAP) and even finetuned binding-based models (ImageBind-I, PointBind-I) lag significantly (e.g., ImageBind-I (Finetuned) at 36.7%).
- **Analysis:**
 - **Multisensory Advantage:** Models using multiple sensory inputs dramatically outperform single-modality models, highlighting the necessity of diverse sensory data for resolving ambiguities between similar-looking objects.
 - **3D vs. 2D:** 3D-based models consistently surpass their 2D counterparts due to better information capture and reduced occlusion issues.
 - **LLM Power:** LLM-based approaches generally outperform similarity-based retrieval methods. MultiPLY's key advantage lies in its ability to interact and *disentangle* individual sensory attributes (e.g., material, temperature) step-by-step to resolve ambiguities, whereas binding-based methods fuse everything into a single embedding, making fine-grained reasoning difficult.

#### 4.2. Tool Use

- **Performance:** MultiPLY achieves 41.6% accuracy, outperforming PointBind-LLM (Finetuned) at 32.1% and significantly surpassing binding-based methods (e.g., ImageBind-I (Finetuned) at 16.4%).
- **Analysis:** Binding-based methods struggle notably in tool-use tasks. This is attributed to their difficulty in disentangling specific sensory information (like material) from fused representations and then reasoning about how these properties enable an object's functionality as a tool or a substitute. MultiPLY's interactive and disentangled processing allows for more nuanced functional reasoning.

#### 4.3. Multisensory Captioning

- **Performance:** MultiPLY demonstrates strong performance with BLEU1: 48.9, BLEU4: 20.1, and METEOR: 24.2. These metrics are considerably higher than PointBind-LLM (Finetuned) (BLEU1: 36.7), 3D-LLM (Finetuned) (BLEU1: 31.2), and LLaVA (Finetuned) (BLEU1: 28.6).
- **Analysis:** 3D-based LLMs generally outperform 2D VLMs. MultiPLY's ability to actively interact with objects and dynamically switch between abstract and detailed representations allows it to generate richer, more accurate multisensory descriptions, unlike models that rely on a holistic, static scene representation.

#### 4.4. Task Decomposition

- **Performance:** MultiPLY achieves a 30.2% success rate, outperforming 3D-LLM with Interaction (22.4%) and LLaVA with Interaction (14.5%). Models without interaction perform very poorly (e.g., LLaVA wo Interaction at 4.0%).
- **Analysis:** The results underscore the critical importance of interaction in embodied task decomposition. Models that attempt to generate all actions at once suffer from significant hallucination (e.g., proposing to retrieve non-existent objects). MultiPLY's step-by-step interaction, coupled with its multisensory understanding and ability to attend to nuanced object details, significantly improves its ability to decompose tasks into feasible, context-aware actions.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Examples

Qualitative examples (Figure 4, Figure 11 in Supplementary) visually demonstrate MultiPLY's capabilities. It can engage in multisensory dialogues, navigate to sound sources, assess object states (e.g., temperature of a CD player, softness of a towel, material of a knife), and make informed decisions based on gathered multisensory feedback, reflecting a more intelligent and adaptable embodied agent.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MultiPLY represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI and multimodal large language models, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Perception and Embodiment:** MultiPLY pioneers the integration of active interaction with a broad spectrum of multisensory inputs (visual, audio, tactile, thermal) within a large language model framework. This moves beyond passive observation, enabling LLMs to actively explore, query, and understand their 3D physical environment in a more human-like manner. This is crucial for developing truly intelligent agents that can operate effectively in the real world.

2. **Novel Dataset for Embodied Multisensory Learning:** The introduction of **Multisensory Universe**, a large-scale (500k data points) dataset of embodied multisensory interactions, is a major contribution. This dataset addresses the critical "paucity of multisensory interaction data" challenge, providing a much-needed resource for training future embodied LLMs and fostering further research in this complex domain. Its creation using LLM-powered agents and enriched 3D environments is innovative and scalable.

3. **Innovative Architectural and Training Paradigm:** The proposed framework, with its object-centric scene representations, specialized action tokens for commanding interactions, and state tokens for integrating multisensory feedback, offers a flexible and effective blueprint for end-to-end instruction tuning of LLMs in embodied settings. This approach allows LLMs to dynamically adapt their reasoning based on real-time sensory observations.

4. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By integrating diverse sensory data, MultiPLY demonstrates superior performance across complex embodied tasks like object retrieval, tool use, multisensory captioning, and task decomposition. Its ability to disentangle and reason about individual sensory attributes (e.g., material from impact sound, temperature from touch) allows for more nuanced decision-making and problem-solving, which is beyond the scope of vision-only or binding-based multimodal models.

5. **Foundation for Future Embodied Agents:** MultiPLY lays the groundwork for developing more capable and versatile embodied AI agents. By equipping LLMs with the ability to actively perceive and understand the physical properties of objects through multiple senses, this research paves the way for applications in:
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Robots that can accurately identify objects, understand their properties (e.g., "is this cup hot?"), and perform tasks requiring fine-grained sensory feedback (e.g., handling fragile items).
 - **Interactive Virtual Environments:** More realistic and responsive virtual agents that can engage with users through nuanced multisensory understanding.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** Agents capable of richer communication and collaboration, understanding human queries that involve multisensory information.
 - **General-Purpose Embodied AI:** Moving towards agents that can learn and adapt to a wide array of tasks in unstructured environments.

While the authors acknowledge a limitation in MultiPLY's reliance on pre-defined policies for detailed navigation and control, viewing these as orthogonal aspects that can be integrated later, the core contribution of enabling active multisensory perception and reasoning within an LLM remains highly significant. MultiPLY successfully pushes the boundaries of embodied AI by bringing LLMs into a richer, more interactive 3D world, offering a compelling vision for the future of intelligent agents.
