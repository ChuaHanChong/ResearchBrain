# CombatVLA: An Efficient Vision-Language-Action Model for Combat Tasks in 3D Action Role-Playing Games

> [!summary] Summary
> CombatVLA is an efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed for real-time combat tasks in 3D Action Role-Playing Games, which achieves a 50-fold acceleration in inference speed to 1.85 seconds. The model demonstrates superior combat understanding and higher task success rates than human players in complex in-game scenarios, surpassing previous VLM-based agents.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based game agents face severe latency (60-90 seconds) for real-time tasks, making them impractical for dynamic combat in ARPGs requiring second-level responses.
- There is a lack of high-quality, action-labeled training data and a standardized benchmark specifically for evaluating 'combat understanding' in 3D ARPGs.
- Current methods struggle with high-resolution perception, complex tactical reasoning, and generalization under the real-time constraints of dynamic combat environments.

## Method

- A Python Action Tracker was developed to efficiently collect human gameplay data, capturing screenshots and keyboard/mouse actions with millisecond precision, used to create the ==Combat Understanding Benchmark== (==CUBench==).
- The **CombatVLA** 3B parameter model, built on Qwen2.5-VL, is trained using a ==three-stage progressive learning== paradigm (Video-AoT, Frames-AoT, Frames-Truncated-AoT) with an ==adaptive action-weighted loss== function.
- An action execution framework integrates CombatVLA, utilizing a novel ==truncated Action-of-Thought== (==AoT==) inference strategy with a `⟨TRUNC⟩` token to significantly reduce latency during real-time gameplay.

## Results

- CombatVLA achieved the highest average score of **63.61%** on the CUBench, outperforming GPT-4o-0513 (**57.29%**) and Gemini-2.0-flash (**57.90%**), particularly excelling in the 'Reasoning' category (**69.71%**).
- It demonstrated a **50-fold** acceleration in inference speed, requiring only **1.85 seconds** of delay per action, a significant improvement over previous VLM-based agents that incurred delays of **60–90 seconds**.
- The model achieved a higher task success rate than human players in various challenging 'Black Myth: Wukong' and 'Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice' tasks, also showing strong zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks and different games.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a specialized VLA model with a truncated Action-of-Thought inference strategy effectively overcomes the latency barrier, enabling sophisticated reasoning for real-time, dynamic combat scenarios.
> - Progressive learning from coarse-grained video understanding to fine-grained frame-level decision-making, coupled with adaptive action-weighted losses, significantly enhances both combat understanding and action prediction accuracy.
> - High-quality human gameplay data, transformed into semantically rich Action-of-Thought sequences, provides crucial guidance for the model to develop robust tactical reasoning and strong generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and embodied intelligence, specifically addressing a critical gap in their application to real-time, complex 3D environments like Action Role-Playing Games (ARPGs). VLAs have made "groundbreaking progress" by integrating visual perception, semantic reasoning, and physical action control, excelling in tasks such as UI operations and navigation. However, the paper highlights "significant challenges" in scenarios demanding "second-level responses, high-resolution perception, and tactical reasoning under dynamic conditions."

The existing landscape of AI-driven game agents can be broadly categorized:

- **API-based Methods:** Some pioneers, like Wang et al. with Minecraft agents (Voyager), leverage game APIs to directly access memory for environmental information. While effective, this approach fundamentally differs from human interaction, which relies on visual perception, limiting its generalizability to scenarios where APIs are unavailable or impractical.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Approaches:** Methods like the "Black Myth: Wukong" AI use algorithms like DQN and PPO with pure visual input. While these allow autonomous learning, they require extensive predefined reward designs and substantial trial-and-error training, making them data-intensive and sometimes difficult to adapt to diverse scenarios. JARVIS-1 and VPT similarly mimic human interaction but struggle with generalization due to predefined action spaces.
- **Large Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based Agents:** Recent works like Cradle and VARP have demonstrated the potential of VLMs in playing video games. These models benefit from the advanced reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) combined with visual perception. However, a major impediment is their reliance on "ultra-large-scale VLMs" such as GPT-4o, leading to "delays that can exceed 60 or even 90 seconds." Such high latencies render them impractical for real-time, latency-sensitive combat games where decisions must be made in seconds or fractions of a second.

CombatVLA directly tackles this critical latency issue, proposing an *efficient* VLA model. It aims to combine the sophisticated reasoning capabilities of VLM-based agents with the real-time responsiveness required for ARPG combat. By developing a specialized 3B parameter model and optimizing its inference through novel data formats and execution strategies, CombatVLA seeks to bridge the gap between high-level AI reasoning and low-latency action execution in dynamic 3D environments, a challenge that has largely remained "underexplored." This positions CombatVLA as a significant advancement towards building truly agile and intelligent embodied agents for complex interactive tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **CombatVLA**, an efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model specifically optimized for real-time combat tasks in complex 3D action role-playing games (ARPGs). This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of existing VLA and VLM-based approaches:

1. **Address Latency in Real-time Decision-Making:** The most critical motivation is the severe latency problem (60-90 seconds) observed in existing VLM-based game agents (e.g., Cradle, VARP) when applied to ARPGs. Combat tasks demand "second-level responses," making such delays unacceptable and hindering practical applicability. CombatVLA aims for a significant speedup in inference and execution.
2. **Enhance High-Resolution Perception and Tactical Reasoning:** ARPGs present dynamic conditions requiring sophisticated visual perception (e.g., enemy and self positioning, movement, environmental awareness) and complex combat reasoning (e.g., recognizing enemy attack patterns, prioritizing actions). Current models often struggle with these high-dimensional demands under real-time constraints.
3. **Overcome Data Scarcity and Benchmarking Gaps:** There is a lack of high-quality, action-labeled training data and a standardized benchmark specifically for assessing "combat understanding" in 3D ARPGs. This work aims to contribute both a data collection tool and a comprehensive evaluation benchmark.
4. **Improve Generalization and Robustness:** While some RL-based agents can master specific tasks, they often struggle with generalization to new scenarios or games. VLM-based agents have better generalization but are too slow. CombatVLA seeks to combine the best of both worlds: strong generalization with real-time performance.

To achieve these motivations, the paper outlines several concrete objectives, summarized as contributions:

- **Develop an Action Tracker:** To efficiently collect human video-action data pairs in games, addressing data scarcity.
- **Establish a Combat Understanding Benchmark (CUBench):** To rigorously evaluate models' "combat IQ" across gathering, comprehension, and reasoning tasks in a VQA format.
- **Create a Three-Stage Action-of-Thought (AoT) Dataset:** To enable progressive learning of combat skills, from coarse-grained video-level understanding to fine-grained frame-level decision-making and efficient truncation strategies.
- **Design the CombatVLA Model:** A 3B model trained with a progressive learning paradigm and adaptive action-weighted losses to achieve optimal combat understanding and efficiency.
- **Integrate into an Action Execution Framework:** To deploy CombatVLA for real-time PC gameplay, leveraging a novel truncated AoT inference strategy for fast action execution.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to significantly advance the capabilities of embodied intelligence in highly dynamic and complex virtual environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CombatVLA introduces a comprehensive methodology encompassing data collection, model training, and an execution framework, all designed for efficient, real-time combat decision-making.

#### 3.1. Action Tracker and Combat Understanding Benchmark (CUBench)

To address the scarcity of labeled action data, the authors developed a lightweight Python **Action Tracker**. This tool runs in the background, simultaneously capturing game screenshots and monitoring keyboard/mouse activities with millisecond precision. A multi-threaded architecture and timestamping ensure accurate alignment of frames with actions, where each action is aligned with the nearest future frame. The tracker parses raw hardware events into semantic action descriptors and stores them as JSON metadata.

Building on the collected data, the authors established the **Combat Understanding Benchmark (CUBench)**. This benchmark evaluates a model's "combat IQ" across three key capabilities:
- **Gathering:** Single-image judgments (e.g., enemy/own health, abnormal status).
- **Comprehension:** Multi-image judgments (e.g., understanding enemy action intention, current stun state).
- **Reasoning:** Multi-image multiple-choice questions requiring tactical decision-making (e.g., choosing the most suitable next action to prioritize health while depleting enemy health).
The evaluation data comprises 914 QA pairs derived from 200 hours of human gameplay recordings in "Black Myth: Wukong," manually annotated and cross-verified by a team of experts and GPT-4o-0513 for question generation.

#### 3.2. Action-of-Thought (AoT) Construction

Inspired by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, the collected video-action data is transformed into **Action-of-Thought (AoT)** sequences. These sequences are formatted as JSON, explicitly including the `[action]` (e.g., "press space") and an `[explanation]` (semantic description of the game state and the rationale behind the action). A critical innovation is the introduction of a special `⟨TRUNC⟩` token, which marks the point where output can be truncated during inference to speed up execution.

#### 3.3. CombatVLA Model and Three-Stage Progressive Learning

CombatVLA is a **3B parameter model** built upon the **Qwen2.5-VL-3B** backbone. Its training follows a **three-stage progressive learning paradigm**:
1. **Stage 1: Coarse-Grained Video-AoT Tuning:** The model is trained on Video-AoT data, where actions are aligned with entire video segments (n frames at m FPS). This initial stage helps the model develop a broad understanding of the combat environment and stabilizes training.
2. **Stage 2: Fine-Grained Frames-AoT Tuning:** The focus shifts to precise, frame-level decision-making. Frames-AoT data pairs are created, tracing back `k` frames from an action's timestamp. This enables the model to understand the sequence and logic of combat scenarios for second-level reaction times.
3. **Stage 3: Fine-Grained Frames-Truncated-AoT Tuning:** To mitigate the inference time associated with verbose AoT explanations, this stage introduces the `⟨TRUNC⟩` token into the AoT data. During real-time operation, any output following `⟨TRUNC⟩` is truncated, allowing the model to retain AoT's reasoning benefits while accelerating inference.

During training, the vision encoder's parameters are frozen, and only the language model's parameters are fine-tuned.

#### 3.4. Adaptive Action-Weighted Loss

To address action distribution imbalance, CombatVLA is trained with a composite loss function:
- **Language Modeling Loss (L_lang):** Standard loss for sequence generation.
- **Action Alignment Loss (L_align):** Enforces accuracy in action prediction.
- **Modality Contrastive Loss (L_con):** Aligns visual and action representations. It minimizes cosine distance between visual and action embeddings (`ˆv_EOS`, `ˆa_EOS`) for matched action pairs and maximizes separation for mismatched pairs.
A **priority-aware matching criterion** `M(A_l, A_o)` is introduced, which evaluates if the highest-priority action in the ground truth (`A_l`) exists in the model's output (`A_o`), based on a predefined action sequence `P`. This sequence assigns exponential weights (`α`) to prioritize rare but critical actions, ensuring balanced learning across diverse action categories.

#### 3.5. Action Execution Framework

CombatVLA is integrated into a lightweight and fast action execution agent. This framework captures real-time game footage (e.g., 60+ FPS), samples relevant frames (e.g., 3 frames from the last 9), and resizes them for CombatVLA. The key to efficiency here is the **Truncated Inference and Execution** strategy: the framework monitors CombatVLA's output and stops token generation upon encountering the `⟨TRUNC⟩` token, converting the preceding tokens into actions. It uses the "pyautogui" library to automate keyboard and mouse controls, pausing the game during the approximate 1.85-second inference period and resuming for action execution. This setup ensures human-like interaction with the game environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results presented in the paper demonstrate CombatVLA's superior performance in combat understanding, real-time execution, and generalization, often surpassing both human players and existing state-of-the-art models.

#### 4.1. Combat Understanding Evaluation

- **CUBench Performance:** CombatVLA achieved the highest average score of **63.61%** on the Combat Understanding Benchmark (CUBench), outperforming top closed-source models like GPT-4o-0513 (57.29%) and Gemini-2.0-flash (57.90%). It also significantly improved upon its own backbone, Qwen2.5-VL-3B (55.87%), by 7.74 points.
- **Reasoning Capability:** CombatVLA excelled particularly in the "Reasoning" category of CUBench, scoring **69.71%**, which was 14.28 points higher than the second-highest model, Claude35-sonnet. This highlights the effectiveness of the Action-of-Thought (AoT) data in enhancing the model's high-level tactical reasoning. While competitive, its scores in "Gathering" and "Comprehension" were not always the absolute highest, but still strong.

#### 4.2. General Benchmark Evaluation

- Despite being fine-tuned on specific combat tasks, CombatVLA demonstrated comparable performance to its backbone model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) on general benchmarks like MME, VideoMME, and OCRBench. This indicates that the specialized training did not degrade its broader visual-language understanding capabilities, confirming the robustness of the approach.

#### 4.3. Task-Level Practical Evaluation

- **Superior Game Performance:** In task-level practical tests on "Black Myth: Wukong" and "Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice," CombatVLA not only significantly outperformed existing VLM-based agents (Cradle and VARP) but also achieved a **higher task success rate than human players** in many scenarios, especially on hard and very hard tasks (e.g., 90% vs. 60% for Task 9, 80% vs. 50% for Task 10).
- **Baselines' Limitations:** Cradle, which relies heavily on explicit text prompts, performed worst due to the implicit visual nature of combat tasks. VARP showed poor performance on difficult tasks and low generalization to different games, highlighting its limitations.
- **Strong Generalization:** CombatVLA, fine-tuned only on very hard tasks (BMW tasks 9 and 10), demonstrated strong generalization ability in zero-shot tests on easier tasks within the same game (BMW tasks 1-8) and entirely different games (SSDT tasks 11-13).

#### 4.4. Inference Latency

- CombatVLA achieved a remarkable **50-fold acceleration** in inference speed. It required only **1.85 seconds** of delay and a single model call per inference, vastly superior to Cradle (61.68 seconds, 5 model calls) and VARP (90.23 seconds, 10 model calls). This dramatic reduction in latency is crucial for real-time applications.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Progressive Learning:** Demonstrated that each stage of progressive learning incrementally improved performance on CUBench. Stage 1 (coarse-grained) yielded 57.27%, Stage 2 (fine-grained) 61.43%, and Stage 3 (truncated AoT) the highest at 63.61%. Stage 3 also halved the inference time compared to Stage 2 (1.85s vs. 3.73s), confirming the efficiency gains of truncation.
- **Adaptive Action-Weighted Loss:** The inclusion of `L_con` and `L_align` significantly enhanced the model's reasoning performance, increasing it from 62.86% (without loss) to 69.71% in the reasoning category and boosting the overall average score by 2.18 points. This validates the importance of this loss mechanism in balancing action categories and improving vision-action alignment.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Visualization

- Visual examples illustrated CombatVLA's ability to make precise and context-aware decisions, such as healing when health is low, immobilizing enemies for follow-up attacks, dodging attacks opportunistically, and performing complex maneuvers like blocking then executing a shinobi deathblow. The AoT explanations provided clear semantic guidance for these complex actions.

In summary, CombatVLA establishes a new benchmark for efficiency and effectiveness in VLA models for complex 3D combat, demonstrating a practical and robust solution for real-time embodied AI in gaming.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of CombatVLA represents a significant leap forward in the field of embodied intelligence and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with profound implications for both academic research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Breaking the Latency Barrier for Real-time VLAs

The most critical contribution of CombatVLA is its unprecedented efficiency. By achieving a **50-fold acceleration** in inference speed (from 60-90 seconds down to 1.85 seconds) compared to existing VLM-based agents, CombatVLA effectively breaks the latency barrier that has previously limited VLAs from operating effectively in real-time, latency-sensitive environments like competitive 3D ARPGs. This makes sophisticated VLA reasoning practical for scenarios demanding immediate, second-level responses, opening up new possibilities for dynamic human-computer interaction.

#### 5.2. Advancing State-of-the-Art in Embodied AI

CombatVLA not only achieves superior performance on combat understanding benchmarks, outperforming powerful closed-source models like GPT-4o, but also demonstrates a **higher task success rate than human players** in complex combat scenarios. This underscores its advanced capabilities in high-resolution visual perception, tactical reasoning, and efficient action execution, setting a new benchmark for embodied AI agents in complex virtual worlds.

#### 5.3. Robust Generalization Capabilities

The model's ability to generalize to unseen tasks within the same game and even to entirely different games (e.g., "Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice" without specific training) is highly significant. This suggests that CombatVLA has learned fundamental combat logic and visual patterns rather than just memorizing specific sequences, paving the way for more universally applicable embodied agents.

#### 5.4. Innovative Methodological Contributions

The paper introduces several innovative components that have broader applicability:
- **Action-of-Thought (AoT) Data Format:** Extends the Chain-of-Thought paradigm to embody agents, providing semantic guidance for action reasoning.
- **Three-Stage Progressive Learning:** A robust training methodology that efficiently guides the model from coarse-grained understanding to fine-grained, real-time decision-making.
- **Truncated AoT Strategy:** A clever inference optimization that significantly reduces latency without sacrificing the benefits of rich semantic reasoning.
- **Adaptive Action-Weighted Loss:** A principled approach to handle imbalanced action distributions and prioritize critical actions, enhancing learning effectiveness.

#### 5.5. Acceleration of Research Through Open-Sourcing

The commitment to open-source all resources—including the action tracker, dataset, CUBench benchmark, model weights, training code, and framework implementation—is a monumental contribution to the research community. This will foster transparency, reproducibility, and accelerate further research and development in embodied AI, VLA models, and AI for gaming by providing researchers with high-quality tools and data.

#### 5.6. Broader Impact and Future Directions

The success of CombatVLA in ARPGs has significant potential to impact other domains requiring intelligent, real-time decision-making in complex 3D environments. This could include:
- **Robotics:** Developing robots capable of real-time perception and nuanced action control in dynamic physical spaces.
- **Autonomous Systems:** Enhancing the responsiveness and reasoning capabilities of self-driving cars, drones, or industrial automation.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Creating more intuitive and intelligent virtual assistants or interactive agents.
- **Simulations and Training:** Providing highly realistic and challenging AI opponents or collaborators in training simulations.

While the current research focuses on games, its core innovations in efficiency and intelligent decision-making lay crucial groundwork for the next generation of truly intelligent and responsive embodied AI. Future work will naturally aim to enhance the model's understanding of broader game scenarios and expand its application to an even wider array of games and real-world challenges.
