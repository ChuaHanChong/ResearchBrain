# OccSim: Multi-kilometer Simulation with Long-horizon Occupancy World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> OccSim introduces a data-driven generative simulator for autonomous driving, utilizing a long-horizon occupancy world model with a novel W-DiT block to stably generate multi-kilometer 3D occupancy maps. This approach overcomes previous generation length limitations, creating data that improves downstream perception model training by up to 22.1% relative in mIoU compared to asset-based simulators.

## Problem

- Existing autonomous driving simulators face fundamental trade-offs among realism, content diversity, and interactivity, often relying on pre-recorded driving logs or HD maps which limit scalability.
- Current state-of-the-art occupancy world models are limited to short stable generation horizons (typically fewer than 50 frames), making them insufficient for constructing multi-kilometer maps or entire city blocks.
- Achieving 3D-consistent static environment generation and populating it with reactive, diverse dynamic agents remains a challenge, as many generative models suffer from 'memoryless visual hallucination'.

## Method

- **OccSim** employs a ==decoupled strategy==, modeling simulation as two generative tasks: multi-kilometer rollout for static scenes and conditional generation of dynamic traffic flow.
- A novel ==W-DiT (Warp-DiT)== block is used for long-horizon static ==occupancy generation==, incorporating explicit ==3D rigid transformations== and a ==mask-injected conditioning== mechanism to bound geometric error accumulation.
- A ==two-pass keyframe-based fusion== heuristic and a robust ==topology extraction== algorithm construct consistent, multi-kilometer road maps, while a compact ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT-S)== generates plausible initial agent layouts.

## Results

- **OccSim** demonstrated stable generation of over **3,000** continuous frames, constructing large-scale 3D maps spanning more than **4 kilometers**, representing an **80-fold** improvement in stable generation length over previous state-of-the-art models.
- The simulator exhibited higher generation diversity (measured by Vendi Scores) and maintained superior structural realism and stability over long-term rollouts compared to prior generative baselines.
- Data collected from **OccSim** enabled 4D semantic occupancy forecasting models to achieve up to **67%** zero-shot performance on unseen data, outperforming models trained on an asset-based simulator (CARLA) by **22.1%** relative in average mIoU when scaled fivefold.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly leveraging 3D geometric priors and rigid transformations within a world model is crucial for achieving ultra-long-horizon stable generation of static environments, preventing rapid error accumulation seen in prior models.
> - A decoupled approach that separately models static environment dynamics and dynamic agent behaviors allows for efficient construction of large-scale, consistent worlds and flexible traffic flow generation.
> - A novel SNR-scaled perception loss, combined with dual-head injection in the W-DiT architecture, effectively refines fine-grained geometric details and enhances robustness for long autoregressive rollouts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Level 4 autonomous driving systems necessitates robust closed-loop evaluation methods. Existing simulation approaches present a fundamental trade-off among realism, content diversity, and interactivity.

Traditional open-loop replay of recorded data offers high authenticity but inherently lacks the capacity for reactive responses to ego-vehicle behaviors. Conversely, geometry-based simulators, such as CARLA, provide interactivity but frequently exhibit a significant sim-to-real gap concerning sensor realism and the diversity of generated content.

Recent advancements in data-driven simulations aim to reconcile this dilemma by leveraging large-scale real-world datasets. However, existing data-driven simulators exhibit limitations when applied to the comprehensive and scalable requirements of autonomous driving. The paper identifies four key desiderata for a comprehensive data-driven simulator:
1. **Sensor realism and semantic richness:** The ability to generate high-fidelity sensor data and dense semantic labels without manual annotation.
2. **Reactive and diverse agents:** The capacity to simulate interactive agent behaviors learned from data, rather than relying on heuristic rules or simple trajectory playback.
3. **3D-Consistent World Dynamics:** Maintenance of global spatial persistence, ensuring static elements remain structurally identical during long-horizon closed-loop driving, even upon revisiting previously observed locations. This addresses "memoryless visual hallucination" common in standard generative models.
4. **Log-independent Simulation:** The capability for open-ended generation in novel scenarios, overcoming the scale limitations imposed by reliance on pre-recorded driving logs or high-definition (HD) maps.

The research landscape includes several categories of world models and data-driven simulators:
- **Long-horizon RGB world models** (e.g., GAIA-1, GAIA-2, Cosmos-Drive-Dreams, Vista, DrivingWorld, Epona) primarily focus on generating realistic video sequences. While offering rich visual features, these models often face challenges in accurately capturing and predicting complex 3D geometry and physical depth.
- **Occupancy World Models** (e.g., OccWorld, Occllama, DOME, COME, DynamicCity, OccFM, UniScene) address 3D geometry by using voxelized and densified LiDAR data representations. Earlier works used discrete encoding and deterministic auto-regressive transformers, while more recent approaches leverage generative modeling techniques like VAEs, diffusion models, and flow matching. A notable limitation of current state-of-the-art occupancy world models is their short stable generation horizon, typically fewer than 50 frames. This length is sufficient for vehicle control decisions but insufficient for constructing multi-kilometer maps or entire city blocks.
- **Data-driven simulation with generative models** (e.g., Nocturne, SLEDGE, Scenario Dreamer, ReSim, DriveArena, SemCity, InfiniCube, X-scene) aim to generate realistic scenarios and sensory observations. Some approaches utilize video generation techniques and HD maps, while others explore generative modeling of road layouts and vehicle agents, or leverage new rendering techniques like 3D Gaussian Splatting. However, many still depend on pre-existing data or spatial priors, limiting their open-ended generation capabilities.

OccSim positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical challenge of achieving multi-kilometer, long-horizon, 3D-consistent environment generation, specifically using an occupancy world model. It seeks to overcome the reliance on continuous driving logs or HD maps, which currently constrains the scalability and diversity of data-driven autonomous driving simulations. The paper highlights that current occupancy world models suffer from architectural designs that implicitly learn complex camera motions without accounting for the inherent geometric rigidity of 3D space, leading to error accumulation and model collapse during long autoregressive rollouts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a data-driven generative simulator capable of satisfying the aforementioned desiderata for comprehensive and scalable autonomous driving evaluation. This involves constructing a simulator that can efficiently generate realistic, diverse, and interactive driving scenarios with 3D consistency over extended horizons, without requiring extensive pre-recorded driving logs or HD maps.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Overcoming Log and HD Map Dependency:** The fundamental dependency on pre-recorded driving logs or HD maps severely constrains scalability and open-ended generation capabilities. OccSim aims to obviate this requirement, conditioning only on a single initial frame and a sequence of future ego-actions.
- **Achieving Ultra-Long-Horizon Stable Generation:** Previous state-of-the-art occupancy world models are limited to stably generating fewer than 50 frames. This horizon is insufficient for constructing large-scale maps spanning multiple city blocks or several kilometers. A key objective is to enable stable generation over thousands of continuous frames to construct multi-kilometer 3D occupancy maps. This requires addressing the progressive accumulation of errors that leads to model collapse in existing autoregressive models.
- **Ensuring 3D-Consistent Static Environment Generation:** For long-horizon closed-loop driving, static elements (e.g., road topology, buildings) must preserve strict spatiotemporal consistency. The simulator must generate environments where, for instance, a revisited location appears structurally identical. This contrasts with "memoryless visual hallucination" observed in some generative models.
- **Populating Reactive and Diverse Dynamic Agents:** After generating a large-scale static environment, the simulator needs to populate it with realistic, interactive agents whose behaviors reflect human-like distributions. This requires moving beyond rule-based heuristic algorithms or simple trajectory playback, which lack diversity and realism.
- **Maintaining Sensor Realism and Semantic Richness:** The generated environment and its dynamic agents must approximate the distribution of real-world sensor data and inherently provide dense semantic labels, avoiding the need for manual annotation typically associated with asset-based simulators.

In summary, the research aims to establish a new paradigm for autonomous driving simulation, driven by an occupancy world model that can synthesize massive, diverse simulation streams spanning kilometers, thereby enabling more comprehensive and scalable testing of autonomous driving systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OccSim addresses its objectives through a decoupled strategy for static and dynamic voxels, modeling the simulation process as two synergistic generative tasks: multi-kilometer rollout for static scenes and conditional generation of dynamic traffic flow.

#### 3.1. Long-Horizon Static Occupancy World Models with W-DiT

The core of OccSim's static environment generation is the **W-DiT (Warp-DiT)** block. This module is designed to achieve ultra-long-horizon, stable generation of static maps by explicitly leveraging 3D geometric priors, which differentiates it from prior occupancy world models that implicitly learn camera motions.

- **Latent Representation:** An occupancy VAE, consisting of a spatial compressor (E) and a decoder (D), maps occupancy frames ($O_t$) to latent representations ($z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C_z}$). The objective is to auto-regressively estimate the future latent states given an initial state ($z_0$) and a sequence of future ego-trajectories ($J_{0:K+\sigma}$).
- **Rigid Transformation and Warping:** W-DiT assumes planar motion on the XY-plane, deriving deterministic rigid transformation matrices ($T_{t+1}^t$) from the ego-actions. These matrices are used to forward-warp the previous latent representation ($z_t$) to the viewpoint at $t+1$, yielding a warped latent ($\hat{z}_{t+1}$). This explicit transformation aims to bound geometric error accumulation.
- **Mask-injected Conditioning:** To account for regions falling outside the sensor's field of view or becoming occluded due to ego-motion, a binary visibility mask ($M_{vis}$) is deterministically computed. Additionally, a random mask ($M_{rand}$) sampled from a Bernoulli distribution is introduced to encourage robust representation learning by preventing the model from trivially copying inputs. The final conditioned latent ($\tilde{z}_{t+1}$) is formed by applying these masks to the warped latent: $\tilde{z}_{t+1} = (M_{vis} \odot M_{rand}) \odot \hat{z}_{t+1}$.
- **Future Action Conditioning:** Future ego-actions are rasterized into physical waypoints ($P_t$) projected onto the BEV plane, anchored in the ego coordinate system at $t+1$. These waypoints form a continuous geometric polyline ($l_{t+1}$) representing the intended path. A U-Net refines the concatenation of $l_{t+1}$ and $\tilde{z}_{t+1}$ to form a feature ($\hat{f}_{t+1}$).
- **Token-wise Scaling and Shifting:** The W-DiT block integrates both global (from timestep $\tau$) and token-wise spatial control signals (from $\hat{f}_{t+1}$) into its Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) and MLP layers. This dual-conditioned design allows for precise control of spatial rigid transformations during long-horizon generation.
- **SNR-weighted Occupancy Perception Loss:** The model is optimized using a velocity loss objective, typical for flow models. To mitigate the "mean trap" issue in continuous VAE-based generative models, a novel SNR-scaled Perception Loss is introduced. This loss projects denoised predictions back into the discrete occupancy space for direct supervision using Cross-Entropy (CE) loss. A time-dependent scaling factor ($\tau^2$) is applied to the perception loss, increasing its weight exponentially in low-noise regimes to refine fine-grained geometric boundaries and semantic details.

#### 3.2. Multi-kilometer Road Map Fusion from Single Frames

OccSim fuses individual frames into a global, consistent map using a two-pass keyframe-based fusion heuristic (Algorithm 1 in Appendix).
- **Keyframe Selection:** Generated sequences are subsampled into keyframes based on a spatial distance threshold, projected onto a unified SE(2) BEV plane.
- **First Pass (Keyframe Sinking & Fusion):** A deterministic column sinking operation aligns ground-level voxels to the absolute zero-elevation plane, rectifying pitch, roll, and elevation drifts. Keyframe voxels are assigned to an initialized global map ($M_{global}$).
- **Second Pass (Non-Keyframe Voting Inpainting):** Non-keyframes are used to robustly inpaint spatial gaps through a spatial majority voting mechanism.
- **Morphological Refinement:** Binary closing and connected-component area filtering operations are applied to ensure spatial continuity of road topologies and remove isolated noise clusters.

To enable unbounded map expansion and support multi-agent simulation, a robust **topology extraction and graph refinement algorithm** (Algorithm 2 in Appendix) is employed:
- **Graph Initialization:** A 2D drivable surface mask is extracted, filtered, and skeletonized to form an initial graph structure.
- **Artifact Removal:** Spurious triangular loops are eliminated, and a rigorous graph cleaning protocol prunes short spurs and merges dense junction clusters into unified super-nodes.
- **Dual-Probe Filtering:** A topology probe identifies and discards artificial endpoints, while a semantic probe uses the 3D elevation profile to detect physical occlusions or obstacles. Valid extensible frontiers are identified, and W-DiT synthesizes missing occupancy for new segments, closing the loop for unbounded procedural generation.

**Lane Topology Extraction** (Algorithm 3 in Appendix) is then performed:
- **B-Spline Smoothing & Parallel Offsetting:** Skeletal segments are converted into smooth reference centerlines using 3rd-degree B-Spline interpolation. Parallel lanes are generated via orthogonal offsetting, with topological boundary constraints enforced to ensure physical feasibility.
- **Spatial Intersection Resolution:** Geometric overlaps at complex intersections are resolved by identifying conflict points, severing trajectories, retaining the longest continuous sub-trajectory, and applying secondary B-Spline interpolation to eliminate artifacts.

#### 3.3. Agent Generation and Control with Static Road Map

After constructing the city-scale static map, OccSim populates the environment with plausible initial agent states.
- **Layout Generator:** Unlike log-replay methods, OccSim uses a generative approach for traffic layouts. It models the conditional probability distribution of agent layouts given a static environment ($P(A|z_{static})$). This is achieved by mapping the discrete set of agents into a continuous 2D spatial heatmap ($H$), encoding static and dynamic vehicles as Gaussian kernels. A compact Diffusion Transformer (DiT-S) is trained to learn this distribution.
- **Data Augmentation:** To prevent overfitting and encourage robustness, a two-stage data augmentation pipeline is used during training, comprising agent-level perturbations (e.g., capping vehicle count, positional shifts) and global spatial transformations (e.g., rotations, reflections) applied to both the occupancy map and the heatmap.
- **Inference:** The sampled heatmap is discretized via Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) to extract individual vehicle instances. Each vehicle is projected onto the nearest lane to establish a kinematically feasible initial yaw.
- **Agent Control:** A* routing to randomly selected junctions in $M_{global}$ and an extended 2D Intelligent Driver Model (2D-IDM) (Algorithm 5 in Appendix) are employed to roll out microscopic kinematic trajectories. The framework is designed for plug-and-play compatibility with learning-based trajectory forecasting models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate OccSim focused on static realism and stability, generation diversity, and dynamic fidelity for downstream utility.

#### 4.1. Static Realism & Stability

- **Long-Horizon Generation:** OccSim demonstrated the ability to stably generate over 3,000 continuous frames of static occupancy, constructing large-scale 3D maps spanning over 4 kilometers. This represents an improvement of greater than 80 times in stable generation length compared to previous state-of-the-art occupancy world models, which typically achieve fewer than 50 stable frames.
- **Quantitative Realism (Fig. 4):**
 - Under conditional fidelity (evaluating structural accuracy at each timestep against ground truth) over a 30-frame constraint, OccSim showed superior stability and structural realism, measured by MMD and KID metrics, in both UniScene and OccFM latent spaces.
 - For unconditional realism (measuring distance between generated samples and the global marginal distribution of ground truth data) over a 1000-frame long-horizon rollout, OccSim maintained low KID and FID scores, indicating that its long-term rollouts consistently reside on the valid data manifold and outperform baselines that suffer from rapid error accumulation.
- **2D Realism under Varying Trajectories (Fig. 5):** When evaluating 2D projections of the 3D generated occupancy across challenging ego-actions (straight, closed-loop, continuous turning), OccSim consistently performed below a defined "chaos" threshold (representing a structurally collapsed, unusable state). In contrast, previous state-of-the-art models exceeded this threshold significantly earlier (e.g., around the 35th-40th frame). OccSim's performance on complex trajectories surpassed the previous SOTA's results even on simpler straight-line trajectories.

#### 4.2. Generation Diversity

- **Quantitative Diversity (Fig. 6):** OccSim exhibited higher generation diversity over long-term rollouts compared to prior generative baselines (COME, DOME).
 - **Pairwise mIoU Diversity:** OccSim showed a rapid increase in diversity at early timesteps and maintained a high and stable level without performance degradation as autoregressive generation extended.
 - **Vendi Scores:** In both OccFM and UniScenes latent spaces, OccSim achieved the highest Vendi Scores, indicating a richer variety of generated geometric and semantic structures, reflecting the model’s multimodality in modeling environmental uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Dynamic Fidelity & Downstream Utility

- **Zero-Shot Transfer Performance (Table 1):** OccSim's utility was demonstrated by using data collected directly from the simulator to pre-train 4D semantic occupancy forecasting models.
 - Models trained on OccSim data achieved up to 67% zero-shot performance (in terms of average IoU/mIoU) on unseen data, outperforming models trained on data from an asset-based simulator (CARLA) by 11% relative in average mIoU.
 - When the OccSim dataset size was scaled up by 5 times, the zero-shot performance increased to approximately 74%, expanding the improvement over asset-based simulators to 22.1% relative in average mIoU.
 - This indicates that even with a straightforward dynamics algorithm like 2D IDM for agent control, OccSim provides more effective training signals for simulating overall traffic flow and background dynamics than CARLA.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 2)

- The ablation study on the W-DiT design confirmed the critical role of three components: the perception loss, the dual-head injection structure, and SNR weighting.
- Without any of these components, achieving stable quality output at a 500-frame scale proved challenging, with the model's output degrading rapidly. The feature injection structure was identified as the most significant contributor to performance. The SNR-scaled perception loss, with its $\tau^2$ weighting, was shown to significantly improve results compared to a perception loss without this scaling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OccSim represents a development in data-driven simulation for autonomous vehicles, addressing several limitations of previous methods and offering significant potential impacts:

- **Novel Simulation Paradigm:** OccSim introduces the first occupancy world model-driven simulator capable of generating diverse and realistic rollouts spanning thousands of frames. This is achieved without reliance on Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) layouts or High-Definition (HD) maps as input, which are common constraints in existing approaches.
- **Enhanced Scalability and Open-Ended Generation:** By achieving ultra-long-horizon stable generation (over 3,000 frames) and supporting multi-kilometer map construction, OccSim liberates data-driven simulation from the confines of finite pre-recorded driving logs. This enables the generation of novel, unseen scenarios on a large scale, fostering more comprehensive testing and reducing the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection for diverse scenarios.
- **Improved 3D Consistency and Realism:** The explicit incorporation of rigid transformations and a robust fusion methodology within W-DiT ensures spatiotemporal consistency of static environments over extended durations. This addresses the "memoryless" nature of some generative models, providing a more reliable foundation for autonomous system evaluation where environmental persistence is crucial. The generated static environments and dynamic agent behaviors exhibit higher fidelity, bridging the sim-to-real gap more effectively than traditional asset-based simulators.
- **Direct Downstream Utility:** A key finding is the demonstrated utility of data collected from OccSim for pre-training downstream perception tasks, such as 4D semantic occupancy forecasting. The ability to outperform conventional simulators like CARLA in this zero-shot transfer setting suggests that OccSim-generated data provides more relevant and robust training signals, accelerating the development and validation of autonomous driving algorithms.
- **Foundation for Advanced Agent Behavior:** While currently utilizing a 2D Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) for agent control, OccSim's framework is designed to be compatible with complex learning-based policies. This offers a path toward integrating more sophisticated, data-driven agent behavior models, further enhancing the realism and reactivity of simulated traffic.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies avenues for future work, including replacing heuristic-based fusion and lane graph extraction with end-to-end approaches, exploring lossless rotational operations directly in occupancy space to improve upon the latent space assumption of W-DiT, and scaling model performance with larger datasets as more semantic occupancy data becomes available. These directions suggest that OccSim could serve as a foundational step for further advancements in data-driven autonomous driving simulation.
