# Generating Images with Multimodal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GILL (Generating Images with Large Language Models) introduces a multimodal language model that generates novel images, retrieves existing images, and produces text, all conditioned on interleaved image and text inputs. The model leverages frozen foundation models and lightweight mapping networks to improve image generation coherence in complex contexts like visual storytelling and dialogue, outperforming baselines in various metrics.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal language models could process diverse inputs and generate text, but none could synthesize novel images within a dynamic, conversational context.
- State-of-the-art text-to-image models struggle with longer, more nuanced, or conversational language inputs, lacking the deep contextual understanding of Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Training large multimodal models from scratch is computationally expensive, necessitating efficient adaptation methods that combine existing powerful models.

## Method

- GILL efficiently adapts ==frozen LLMs (OPT-6.7B), visual encoders (CLIP ViT-L), and text-to-image generators (Stable Diffusion v1.5)== by learning ==lightweight mapping networks (~50M trainable parameters)==.
- A novel ==GILLMapper (4-layer Transformer)== is introduced to translate LLM's high-level visual intent from special output tokens into the Stable Diffusion text encoder's embedding space for novel image generation.
- A multi-task loss function combines objectives for image captioning, predicting image output tokens, novel image generation, and image retrieval, alongside a ==decision module to choose between generation and retrieval==.

## Results

- GILL demonstrates superior contextual image generation, with CLIP Similarity improving from **0.581** to **0.641** on the VIST dataset when moving from single-caption to full multimodal context, outperforming Stable Diffusion (**0.598** with 5 captions).
- On the VisDial dataset, GILL's image generation performance consistently improves with increased dialogue rounds, achieving a CLIP Similarity of **0.645** at 10 rounds, surpassing Stable Diffusion (**0.622**).
- GILL maintains strong performance on other multimodal tasks, performing comparably or better than similar-sized models on VQA (**0.3178** zero-shot accuracy) and image captioning (**0.1059 BLEU-4**), while also matching or exceeding FROMAGe in image retrieval (**20.3 Recall@1** on VIST).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging the long-range contextual understanding of frozen LLMs significantly enhances the relevance and coherence of generated images in multi-turn dialogues and visual storytelling scenarios.
> - An efficient modular architecture, by freezing large foundation models and training only small connecting components, enables powerful multimodal capabilities with substantially reduced computational training costs.
> - A dedicated mapping module (GILLMapper) is crucial for effectively bridging the semantic gap between LLM representations and the specific input requirements of a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper, GILL, sits at the nexus of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal AI, and Text-to-Image Generation.

**Evolution of Multimodal Language Models:** Historically, LLMs were text-only, demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and generating human language. The challenge has been to effectively ground these models in other modalities, particularly vision, to enable more comprehensive AI systems. Early multimodal LLMs focused on processing image and text inputs to generate *text outputs* (e.g., image captioning, visual question answering). Works like Frozen, Flamingo, and BLIP-2 pioneered methods to adapt frozen text-only LLMs to vision-and-language tasks by mapping visual features into the LLM's embedding space. More recently, models like FROMAGe extended this by allowing LLMs to generate *retrieved images* alongside text. GILL directly builds on this trajectory by adding the critical capability of *novel image generation* to the multimodal output repertoire, thus creating a more complete multimodal dialogue agent.

**Advancements in Text-to-Image Generation:** Concurrently, text-to-image generation has seen explosive growth, with models like DALL-E, GLIDE, Imagen, and Stable Diffusion achieving unprecedented levels of realism and control. These models typically excel at generating images from concise text prompts but struggle with longer, more complex, or conversational language contexts. They are also generally limited to text-only inputs, unable to leverage visual context from interleaved images.

**GILL's Unique Contribution:** GILL bridges these two powerful research streams. It leverages the strong language understanding and contextual reasoning capabilities of frozen LLMs for complex, long-form, and multimodal dialogue (a strength of LLMs) and fuses them with the high-fidelity image synthesis abilities of frozen, state-of-the-art text-to-image models (like Stable Diffusion). This fusion is achieved efficiently, without requiring expensive end-to-end retraining of these massive foundational models. The ability to handle *arbitrarily interleaved image and text inputs* and produce *coherent sequences of generated text, retrieved images, and novel generated images* is a significant step beyond existing models. It moves towards truly conversational and creative multimodal AI assistants that can visually illustrate points or respond to complex requests with newly synthesized imagery, making it a critical advancement in multimodal generative AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a multimodal language model capable of generating novel images, in addition to text and retrieved images, while conditioning on arbitrarily interleaved image and text inputs. The authors were motivated by several key limitations in the existing landscape of multimodal AI:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Multimodal Output:** While prior multimodal language models could process diverse inputs and generate text, and some could retrieve existing images (like FROMAGe), none could synthesize *novel images* within a dynamic, conversational context. This limited their utility in scenarios requiring creative or context-dependent visual responses.
2. **Leveraging LLM's Contextual Understanding for Image Generation:** State-of-the-art text-to-image models excel at generating high-quality images from short prompts but often fall short when dealing with longer, more nuanced, or conversational language inputs. LLMs, on the other hand, are masters of long-range context and dialogue. The authors aimed to harness the LLM's superior contextual understanding to drive more relevant and coherent image generation, especially in dialogue settings.
3. **Computational Efficiency:** Training large multimodal models from scratch, as done by models like Flamingo, requires immense computational resources. The authors sought an efficient adaptation method that could combine the strengths of existing, frozen foundation models (LLMs, visual encoders, text-to-image generators) with minimal additional training, thereby making advanced multimodal capabilities more accessible.
4. **Enabling Coherent Multimodal Dialogue:** The ultimate motivation was to create a model that could engage in natural, flowing multimodal conversations, where it could seamlessly interleave text, relevant retrieved images, and newly generated images based on the ongoing dialogue. This allows for a richer and more intuitive user experience, akin to human-like communication.
5. **The "Retrieve or Generate" Dilemma:** An additional objective was to equip the model with the intelligence to decide, at inference time, whether retrieving an existing image or generating a new one would be more appropriate for a given context, a capability absent in prior models that were limited to only one mode of image output.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GILL's methodology centers on efficiently adapting frozen text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) and pre-trained image models by learning lightweight mapping networks between their embedding spaces. The core components and training approach are:

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview (Figure 2)

GILL consists of a frozen LLM backbone (OPT-6.7B), a frozen visual encoder (CLIP ViT-L), and a frozen text-to-image generation model (Stable Diffusion v1.5). The trainable parameters are limited to small mapping networks: `W_cap` for processing images, `E_img` for producing image tokens, `W_t2i` and `W_i2t` for retrieval, and the novel `GILLMapper` module for image generation.

#### 3.2. Learning to Process Images

To enable the LLM to understand visual inputs, a linear mapping `W_cap` is learned. This mapping takes the pooled visual embeddings `v_ϕ(x)` from the frozen CLIP visual encoder and projects them into a sequence of `k` vectors in the LLM's input embedding space. This is trained using a standard negative log-likelihood (captioning) loss (`l_c`) on image-caption pairs, allowing the LLM to effectively "see" and describe images.

#### 3.3. Learning to Produce Images (Retrieval and Generation)

To allow GILL to output images, special `[IMG]` tokens (`[IMG1]..., [IMG{r}]`) are added to the LLM's vocabulary. The LLM is trained to predict these tokens, indicating when an image output is desired (`l_p` loss). The hidden states corresponding to these `[IMG]` tokens are then used for either retrieval or generation.

- **Novel Image Generation:** This is a core innovation. A lightweight 4-layer encoder-decoder Transformer, named **GILLMapper** (`f_ω`), is introduced. GILLMapper conditions on the `[IMG]` token hidden states from the LLM and a set of learned query embeddings (`q_1..., q_L`). Its purpose is to translate the LLM's high-level visual intent (encoded in the `[IMG]` hidden states) into the specific input embedding space required by the frozen Stable Diffusion text encoder (`T_ψ`). This mapping is learned by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss (`l_g`) between GILLMapper's output and the actual text embeddings produced by Stable Diffusion's text encoder for the corresponding caption. Crucially, this "distillation" training does not require running the computationally expensive Stable Diffusion *decoder* during training, making the process highly efficient. At inference, GILLMapper's output feeds directly into the Stable Diffusion decoder to synthesize a novel image.
- **Image Retrieval:** Similar to prior work like FROMAGe, GILL learns linear mappings (`W_t2i` and `W_i2t`) to project the `[IMG]` token embeddings and visual encoder outputs into a shared `p`-dimensional space. An InfoNCE loss (`l_r`) is used to train these mappings, enabling retrieval of the most semantically similar image from a candidate set during inference via cosine similarity.

#### 3.4. Deciding to Generate or Retrieve

To intelligently choose between generating a novel image and retrieving an existing one, a separate decision module is trained. This module is a linear classifier that takes the LLM's `[IMG]` hidden states as input. It is trained on a human-annotated dataset derived from PartiPrompts, where each prompt is labeled as either favoring "generation" or "retrieval." This allows GILL to dynamically select the most appropriate image output modality based on context.

#### 3.5. Data and Implementation Details

- **Training Data:** Conceptual Captions (CC3M) dataset (3.3M image-text pairs).
- **LLM Backbone:** OPT-6.7B (frozen).
- **Visual Encoder:** CLIP ViT-L (frozen).
- **Text-to-Image Generator:** Stable Diffusion v1.5 (frozen).
- **Key Parameters:** `k=4` visual tokens for input, `r=8` learned `[IMG]` tokens for output, GILLMapper query embedding dimension `m=512`, retrieval embedding dimension `p=256`.
- **Efficiency:** Only ~50M parameters are trained, compared to the ~8B parameters of the frozen foundational models. Training is performed on 2 A6000 GPUs for 2 days.
- **Loss Function:** A multi-task loss combining captioning (`l_c`), image token prediction (`l_p`), generation (`l_g`), and retrieval (`l_r`) losses. The decision model is trained separately.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate GILL's enhanced capabilities in multimodal interaction, particularly in contextual image generation, while maintaining strong performance on retrieval and text generation tasks.

#### 4.1. Contextual Image Generation

- **VIST Dataset (Visual Storytelling):** GILL significantly outperforms baseline text-to-image models (GLIDE, Stable Diffusion) when provided with longer and multimodal contexts.
 - With a single caption, GILL's CLIP Similarity (0.581) is comparable to Stable Diffusion (0.592), and LPIPS is similar.
 - When provided with all 5 story captions, GILL improves substantially (CLIP Sim: 0.612, LPIPS: 0.696), outperforming Stable Diffusion (CLIP Sim: 0.598, LPIPS: 0.704). This highlights the LLM's ability to leverage long-form text.
 - With full multimodal context (5 captions, 4 images), GILL achieves its best performance (CLIP Sim: 0.641, LPIPS: 0.693), demonstrating its ability to effectively process and benefit from interleaved image and text inputs. Stable Diffusion cannot handle such inputs.
- **VisDial Dataset (Visual Dialogue):** GILL shows superior performance in generating images from dialogue-like text.
 - Similar to VIST, with shorter dialogue contexts, Stable Diffusion might slightly outperform GILL.
 - However, as the number of dialogue rounds increases (e.g., to 10 rounds), GILL's performance consistently improves and surpasses Stable Diffusion (CLIP Sim: 0.645 vs. 0.622; LPIPS: 0.714 vs. 0.723). This indicates GILL's effectiveness in processing long conversational contexts for image generation.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 5 & 7):** GILL generates images that are more sensitive to complex, multi-turn, and multimodal prompts, often producing more relevant and coherent visuals than Stable Diffusion, especially when the context extends beyond a simple text prompt. It also effectively blends generated and retrieved images into coherent dialogue flows.

#### 4.2. Contextual Image Retrieval

- GILL performs comparably to or better than prior dedicated retrieval models like FROMAGe on the VIST dataset (Recall@1: 20.3 vs. 18.2; Recall@5: 45.0 vs. 42.7), demonstrating that the image generation objective does not compromise retrieval capabilities.

#### 4.3. Other Multimodal LM Capabilities

- GILL performs comparably to or slightly better than similar-sized multimodal LMs (e.g., Frozen, MAGMA, FROMAGe) on standard tasks like VQA (zero-shot accuracy: 0.3178 vs. 0.2553-0.2851 for others) and image captioning (BLEU-4: 0.1059 vs. 0.1023), despite its broader set of functionalities.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Effect of Context:** Performance generally improves with increasing input context (both textual and multimodal), validating the benefit of the LLM backbone's contextual understanding.
- **Generation-Only Objective:** Removing the retrieval loss had minimal impact on generation performance, suggesting GILL has sufficient capacity for both tasks.
- **GILLMapper Module:** GILLMapper is critical for effective image generation, substantially outperforming simpler mapping networks (linear layer, MLP, Transformer Encoder) in both FID on CC3M and CLIP Similarity on VIST. This highlights its role in translating LLM representations effectively into the Stable Diffusion input space.
- **Number of `[IMG]` Tokens:** Generation performance generally improved as the number of `[IMG]` tokens (`r`) increased, plateauing around `r=4` or `r=8`, indicating that multiple tokens provide richer expressive capacity for visual generation.
- **Decision Model:** The trained linear classifier for deciding between retrieval and generation achieved an F1 score of 0.547, outperforming simple baselines and heuristic methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GILL represents a significant step forward in the development of truly multimodal AI systems, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Advanced Multimodal Dialogue and Interaction:** GILL is the first model capable of processing arbitrarily interleaved image and text inputs to generate coherent sequences of text, retrieved images, *and novel generated images*. This breakthrough opens the door for more sophisticated and human-like AI assistants that can not only understand complex multimodal queries but also respond creatively and visually. Imagine an AI assisting in design, storytelling, or education by dynamically generating illustrative images based on a multi-turn conversation.

2. **Efficient Multimodal Integration:** The modular "frozen model fusion" approach is highly impactful. By keeping large foundation models (LLM, visual encoder, text-to-image generator) frozen and only training lightweight mapping networks, GILL achieves impressive capabilities with significantly reduced computational cost for training compared to end-to-end multimodal models. This makes advanced multimodal AI development more accessible and allows for rapid integration of future, even more powerful, foundational models.

3. **Leveraging LLM's Contextual Prowess for Image Generation:** GILL effectively demonstrates that the deep contextual understanding and long-range reasoning abilities of LLMs can be harnessed to improve the relevance and coherence of generated images, especially in complex scenarios like dialogue or visual storytelling. This is a crucial validation of LLMs' versatility beyond purely textual tasks.

4. **Intelligent "Retrieve vs. Generate" Decision-Making:** The model's ability to autonomously decide whether to retrieve an existing image or generate a novel one based on context is a practical advancement. This flexibility allows GILL to provide the most appropriate visual response, enhancing its utility in diverse applications where some visual concepts might be best represented by existing images, while others require novel creation.

5. **Future Modularity and Scalability:** The modular design ensures that GILL can readily benefit from ongoing advancements in its constituent components. As stronger LLMs, more powerful visual encoders, or higher-fidelity text-to-image generation models emerge, they can be seamlessly integrated into the GILL framework to enhance its overall capabilities, making it a robust and future-proof research direction.

**Potential Impact and Ethical Considerations:**
The enhanced generative capabilities of GILL also necessitate careful consideration of broader societal impacts. As highlighted by the authors, GILL inherits the limitations of its LLM backbone (e.g., potential for hallucinations, biases, text degeneration) and its image generation backbone (e.g., potential for disinformation, propagation of stereotypes). The ability to generate realistic, context-dependent images alongside persuasive text could amplify concerns about synthetic media and misinformation. The authors acknowledge these risks and suggest mitigation strategies like watermarking generated images or using calibrated base models.

Overall, GILL represents a significant advancement towards building more capable and versatile multimodal AI agents, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in human-computer interaction and creative AI applications, while also calling for responsible development and deployment.
