# Large Multimodal Agents: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey systematically reviews Large Multimodal Agents (LMAs), structuring the field by deconstructing their core components and proposing a four-type taxonomy. The work consolidates existing evaluation methodologies and identifies future research directions, providing a unified framework for understanding and advancing multimodal AI.

## Problem

- The rapidly expanding field of Large Multimodal Agents (LMAs) suffered from fragmented understanding, inconsistent terminology, and a lack of standardized evaluation methods.
- Existing LLM-powered agents were limited to text-based interactions, unable to effectively interpret and respond to diverse real-world multimodal information (e.g., visual, audio).
- Research on LMAs was often conducted in isolation, hindering effective comparison and cumulative progress across different systems.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic and comprehensive review of LLM-driven multimodal agents, consolidating scattered knowledge and proposing a coherent ==taxonomy==.
- The survey deconstructed LMAs into fundamental building blocks: ==perception, planning, action, and memory==, providing a granular understanding of their architecture and evolution.
- It compiled and analyzed diverse ==evaluation methodologies== and highlighted frameworks for ==multi-agent collaboration==, while also delineating future research directions.

## Results

- A novel **four-type** taxonomy for LMAs was proposed, categorizing agents based on their planner type (closed-source vs. fine-tuned open-source LLM) and long-term memory integration (none, indirect, native).
- Detailed analysis of LMA core components (perception, planning, action, memory) revealed the evolution from simple modality conversion to sophisticated multimodal processing and native multimodal memory systems.
- A comprehensive overview of LMA applications across domains like GUI automation, robotics, autonomous driving, video understanding, and complex visual reasoning was provided.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMAs are complex systems requiring sophisticated perception (multimodal data interpretation), planning (reasoning, plan formulation), action (tool use, embodied actions), and memory (multimodal long-term storage).
> - LMA development has progressed through four types, from early agents leveraging closed-source LLMs without memory to advanced agents with native multimodal long-term memory, enabling enhanced generalization.
> - Evaluating LMAs remains challenging due to diverse methods, necessitating standardized objective benchmarks and consideration of subjective metrics like versatility, scalability, and safety.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The broader research landscape is currently dominated by the transformative advancements of Large Language Models (LLMs). These models have revolutionized text-based AI agents, imbuing them with unprecedented capabilities in decision-making, reasoning, and knowledge assimilation, often mirroring human cognitive processes. Examples include agents capable of command interpretation, complex natural language processing tasks, and environment interaction purely through language.

However, the real world is inherently multimodal, requiring perception and interaction beyond just text. A significant and rapidly emerging trend in AI research is the extension of these LLM-powered agents into multimodal domains. This evolution aims to enable AI agents to interpret and respond to diverse user queries involving various modalities, such as visual (images, videos), audio, and potentially tactile information. This multimodal capability is crucial for handling more intricate, nuanced, and real-world tasks that transcend the limitations of text-only interaction. The paper refers to these advanced entities as Large Multimodal Agents (LMAs).

Within this rapidly expanding field, the authors identify a critical gap: while several surveys exist for general LLM-powered agents, very few specifically focus on the unique challenges and advancements in the *multimodal* domain. Furthermore, existing research on LMAs has often been conducted in isolation, leading to a fragmented understanding of the field, inconsistencies in terminology, and a lack of standardized evaluation methodologies, which hinders effective comparison and progress.

This survey paper directly addresses this void. By systematically reviewing the nascent field of LLM-driven multimodal agents, it aims to consolidate scattered knowledge, propose a coherent taxonomy, synthesize diverse evaluation methods, and delineate future research trajectories. It positions itself as a foundational resource that not only summarizes the current state-of-the-art but also provides a much-needed framework for standardizing research and fostering more meaningful comparisons within this dynamic area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to conduct a systematic and comprehensive review of LLM-driven multimodal agents, which the authors term Large Multimodal Agents (LMAs). This overarching goal is motivated by several critical needs within the rapidly evolving AI landscape.

The specific objectives of the survey are to:
- **Introduce Essential Components:** Deconstruct LMAs into their fundamental building blocks: perception, planning, action, and memory, providing a granular understanding of their architecture.
- **Categorize Current Research:** Develop a novel taxonomy that classifies existing LMA studies into four distinct types, offering a structured way to understand their development and capabilities.
- **Review Collaborative Frameworks:** Explore frameworks where multiple LMAs interact and cooperate, emphasizing how collective intelligence can enhance efficacy.
- **Compile Evaluation Methodologies:** Aggregate and analyze the diverse evaluation methods currently used for LMAs, aiming to establish a comprehensive framework that can standardize assessments and facilitate more effective comparisons between different agents.
- **Highlight Applications:** Provide an exhaustive overview of the extensive real-world applications and tasks that LMAs are capable of addressing.
- **Propose Future Research Directions:** Identify unresolved challenges and promising avenues for future research, offering valuable insights and guidance for the community.

The motivation behind undertaking this comprehensive survey stems from the recognition that while LLMs have empowered text-based agents, real-world scenarios inherently demand multimodal interaction. Existing LLM-powered agents fall short when faced with information beyond text, especially visual data, necessitating the evolution towards LMAs. However, the burgeoning research in LMAs has progressed in a somewhat uncoordinated manner. Individual studies often operate in isolation, leading to a fragmented understanding, a lack of cohesive terminology, and, critically, disparate evaluation metrics. This disarray complicates direct comparisons between different LMA systems and impedes cumulative progress. Therefore, this survey is motivated by the urgent need to bring order and clarity to the field, serving as a consolidated resource to bridge existing knowledge gaps, standardize practices, and accelerate the development of more robust, adaptive, and human-like AI agents capable of operating effectively in complex, multisensory environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic review approach to analyze the emerging field of Large Multimodal Agents. Their methodology is structured to provide a comprehensive overview by breaking down LMAs into their constituent parts, classifying existing models, examining collaborative paradigms, scrutinizing evaluation techniques, and mapping out diverse applications.

The core components of their approach include:

- **Component-wise Deconstruction:** The survey begins by dissecting LMAs into four fundamental components:
 - **Perception:** How LMAs acquire and interpret multimodal information from their environment. The authors trace the evolution from simple modality conversion (e.g., image to text) to more sophisticated sub-task tools and the extraction of salient multimodal "vocabulary."
 - **Planning:** The central cognitive process akin to the human brain, responsible for reasoning and plan formulation. This involves analyzing the types of models used as planners (e.g., GPT-3.5, GPT-4, LLaMA, LLaVA), the format of their plans (natural language, programs, hybrid), mechanisms for inspection and reflection (e.g., memory, human-made plans, Monte Carlo search), and planning methodologies (dynamic vs. static).
 - **Action:** The execution mechanism that translates plans into concrete operations. The authors categorize actions into tool use (e.g., visual foundation models, APIs, Python), embodied actions (e.g., robot movements, virtual character actions), and virtual actions (e.g., web clicks, scrolling). They also discuss how agents learn to perform these actions (prompting vs. self-instruction fine-tuning).
 - **Memory:** Crucial for adaptability and generalization in complex environments. The survey distinguishes between short and long-term memory, highlighting the unique challenge of storing multimodal information and different approaches (textual conversion vs. native multimodal storage using embeddings like CLIP).

- **Taxonomical Classification:** A key aspect of their methodology is the introduction of a novel four-type taxonomy for existing LMAs, illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper. This classification is based on the nature of the planner (closed-source vs. fine-tuned open-source LLMs) and the presence and type of long-term memory integration:
 - Type I: Closed-source LLMs as Planners w/o Long-term Memory.
 - Type II: Finetuned LLMs as Planners w/o Long-term Memory.
 - Type III: Planners with Indirect Long-term Memory (access via tools).
 - Type IV: Planners with Native Long-term Memory (direct interaction).

- **Analysis of Multi-Agent Collaboration:** Beyond individual agents, the authors investigate frameworks where multiple LMAs collaborate, each with distinct roles and responsibilities, to achieve complex goals, illustrated in Figure 3. This section underscores the benefits of distributed intelligence in LMA systems.

- **Evaluation Framework Development:** Recognizing the diverse and often incomparable evaluation methods, the paper dedicates a significant section to compiling and structuring existing assessment techniques. This includes:
 - **Subjective Evaluation:** Focusing on human-centric metrics like versatility, user-friendliness, scalability (lifelong learning, unseen tools), and crucially, value and safety.
 - **Objective Evaluation:** Discussing quantitative metrics (task-specific accuracies, new LMA-specific metrics) and benchmarks (standardized testing environments like SmartPlay, GAIA, VisualWebArena) that allow for systematic comparisons.

- **Application Mapping:** The survey comprehensively outlines the wide array of real-world applications where LMAs demonstrate significant utility, categorizing them into domains such as GUI automation, robotics, game development, autonomous driving, video understanding, visual generation/editing, complex visual reasoning, and audio editing/generation.

- **Future Directions Elucidation:** Based on their comprehensive review, the authors synthesize open challenges and propose promising future research trajectories concerning framework evolution, evaluation standardization, and application expansion.

The survey draws upon a broad spectrum of recent research papers, primarily published between November 2022 and February 2024, sourced from top AI conferences. This focus on very recent literature ensures the survey is up-to-date in a rapidly evolving field. The accompanying GitHub repository mentioned in the abstract further supports their methodological transparency and provides a living resource for the community.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey presents a structured and comprehensive overview of the emerging field of Large Multimodal Agents (LMAs), yielding several key findings across its core sections:

- **Core Components of LMAs:**
 - **Perception:** LMAs process diverse modalities (text, image, video, audio). Initial approaches relied on simple modality conversion, often leading to irrelevant information. Recent advancements involve sub-task tools for complex data types and methods for extracting "visual vocabulary" to enhance environmental understanding for LLMs.
 - **Planning:** This is the "brain" of LMAs, more challenging than in language-only agents due to multimodal complexity. Planners utilize various LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5/4, open-source LLaMA/LLaVA), formulate plans in natural language or programs, and incorporate inspection/reflection mechanisms (e.g., memory retrieval, human-guided plans, Monte Carlo search). Planning strategies can be static (pre-defined sub-plans) or dynamic (re-planning based on real-time feedback).
 - **Action:** LMAs execute plans via tool use (e.g., visual foundation models, APIs, Python), embodied actions (e.g., robots, virtual characters), or virtual actions (e.g., web interactions). Actions are enabled either through prompting the agent with tool information or by fine-tuning open-source models with action-related data.
 - **Memory:** Crucial for complex, realistic environments. Unlike language-only agents, LMAs require multimodal long-term memory. Current approaches either convert all modalities to text for storage or employ native multimodal memory systems, such as key-value pairs where visual information is encoded by models like CLIP for similarity-based retrieval.

- **Taxonomy of LMAs:** The paper proposes a novel four-category classification, reflecting the evolutionary path of LMAs:
 - **Type I (Closed-source LLMs as Planners w/o Long-term Memory):** Early LMAs that use prompts to leverage closed-source models (e.g., GPT-3.5) for planning in simpler tasks like image editing or VQA.
 - **Type II (Finetuned LLMs as Planners w/o Long-term Memory):** More recent LMAs that fine-tune open-source LLMs or multimodal models (e.g., LLaMA, LLaVA) with instruction-following data, enabling them to serve as both planner and executor for similar tasks to Type I.
 - **Type III (Planners with Indirect Long-term Memory):** These LMAs integrate long-term memory, but LLM planners access it indirectly by invoking specific tools (e.g., querying a memory bank for spatiotemporal attributes in video processing).
 - **Type IV (Planners with Native Long-term Memory):** Represents the most advanced type, where LLM planners directly interact with multimodal long memory, bypassing tools. This enables enhanced generalization and adaptation in complex, open-world environments (e.g., Minecraft agents proficient in over 200 tasks).

- **Multi-Agent Collaboration:** The survey highlights that multiple LMAs can cooperatively address complex tasks. By assigning different roles and responsibilities to individual agents (e.g., a "perceiver agent," "patroller," "exploration agent"), the collective framework distributes the cognitive burden and significantly enhances overall task performance, as demonstrated by systems like MP5 and MemoDroid.

- **Evaluation Methodologies:** The authors identify a significant challenge in LMA evaluation due to diverse methods. They categorize existing approaches:
 - **Subjective Evaluation:** Human assessment based on metrics like versatility (tool/action diversity), user-friendliness (satisfaction, efficiency, accuracy), scalability (ability to learn new competencies), and value/safety (ethical alignment).
 - **Objective Evaluation:** Quantitative assessment using specific task-related metrics (e.g., VQA accuracy) and, increasingly, dedicated benchmarks designed for LMAs (e.g., VisualWebArena for visually guided web tasks, SmartPlay for game-based evaluation, GAIA for general AI assistant questions requiring multimodal reasoning and tool use).

- **Applications:** LMAs demonstrate broad utility across numerous real-world domains:
 - **GUI Automation:** Simulating human actions in user interfaces for repetitive tasks and complex workflows.
 - **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Integrating perception, reasoning, and physical interaction for comprehensive environmental understanding and action.
 - **Game Development:** Creating intelligent and realistic AI agents for engaging player experiences.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Overcoming perception and interpretation challenges in complex scenarios, enabling safer navigation.
 - **Video Understanding:** Analyzing and comprehending video content by extracting essential information.
 - **Visual Generation & Editing:** Creating and manipulating visual content based on multimodal inputs.
 - **Complex Visual Reasoning Tasks:** Primarily focusing on multimodal Question Answering (QA) by leveraging visual and textual modalities.
 - **Audio Editing & Generation:** Integrating expert models for efficient music and audio creation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the rapidly evolving field of Large Multimodal Agents (LMAs) and broader artificial intelligence research. Its potential impact spans foundational understanding, research acceleration, and practical application.

**1. Structuring a Nascent Field:** The most immediate significance is its role in bringing structure and clarity to a fragmented and rapidly expanding domain. By meticulously defining core components (perception, planning, action, memory) and proposing a coherent four-type taxonomy, the paper provides a much-needed common language and conceptual framework. This systematization is crucial for new researchers entering the field and helps existing practitioners understand how their work fits into the broader landscape.

**2. Standardizing Evaluation:** The detailed compilation and analysis of existing subjective and objective evaluation methodologies represent a critical contribution. The identified lack of standardized evaluation criteria has been a major impediment to meaningful comparisons among different LMA systems. By highlighting this challenge and synthesizing diverse metrics and benchmarks, the survey advocates for, and implicitly guides the development of, a more systematic and comparable evaluation paradigm. This will foster more rigorous research, enable reproducible results, and accelerate progress by clearly identifying superior approaches.

**3. Guiding Future Research:** The paper's conclusion, with its proposed future research directions, serves as a roadmap for the community. It pinpoints crucial avenues for development, including:
 - **Framework Evolution:** Encouraging the creation of more unified single agents with direct multimodal interaction and native memory, as well as advancing multi-agent collaboration mechanisms (communication protocols, strategic task distribution). This will push the boundaries of what a single or collective LMA can achieve.
 - **Robust Evaluation:** Emphasizing the continuous development of comprehensive, relevant, and realistic evaluation tasks and datasets, coupled with lucid, non-repetitive metrics, to accurately assess the multifaceted capabilities of LMAs.
 - **Application Expansion:** Identifying promising real-world applications, particularly in human-computer interaction and complex automation tasks like web browsing, that can leverage LMAs' multimodal understanding for enhanced utility and improved human-machine synergy.

**4. Bridging Theoretical and Applied AI:** By extensively detailing both the theoretical components and the vast array of practical applications, the survey helps bridge the gap between fundamental research and real-world deployment. It showcases the tangible benefits and transformative potential of LMAs in domains ranging from robotics and autonomous driving to GUI automation and content creation.

**5. Facilitating Collaboration and Resource Sharing:** The provision of an up-to-date resource list on GitHub (https://github.com/jun0wanan/awesome-large-multimodal-agents) is a practical and valuable contribution. This open resource fosters community engagement, encourages collaboration, and facilitates access to cutting-edge research, further accelerating progress in the field.

In essence, "Large Multimodal Agents: A Survey" is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a foundational document that aims to shape the future trajectory of LMA research. By providing a clear context, a structured understanding, a critical analysis of evaluation, and forward-looking guidance, it empowers researchers to develop more intelligent, versatile, and human-like AI agents capable of navigating and interacting with our complex, multimodal world.
