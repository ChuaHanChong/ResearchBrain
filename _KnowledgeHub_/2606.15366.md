# Robust Conformal CBF and CLF Controllers via Iterative Policy Updates

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zürich and the University of Pennsylvania developed an episodic learning framework for robust Conformal Control Lyapunov/Barrier Function (rCLF/rCBF) controllers, designed to maintain probabilistic stability and safety guarantees even with policy-induced distribution shifts. The framework utilizes Adversarially Robust Conformal Prediction (ARCP) and trajectory sensitivity analysis to iteratively update the robustness margin, demonstrating effective convergence and 100% empirical safety in robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Robust CLF/CBF controllers rely on a worst-case bound for model error, which is difficult to obtain reliably from data-driven models without strong assumptions.
- Conformal Prediction (CP) provides probabilistic error bounds but these guarantees are invalidated by 'distribution shift' when the control policy changes the system's trajectory distribution.
- This 'circular dependence' means a deployed CP-based controller can compromise its own safety and stability assurances by altering the data distribution from which its robustness margin was derived.

## Method

- An ==episodic learning framework== iteratively updates ==robust conformal CLF/CBF policies== and their associated ==robustness margins== across episodes.
- The framework integrates ==Adversarially Robust Conformal Prediction (ARCP)== to maintain ==probabilistic validity of uncertainty bounds== despite policy changes.
- A ==distribution shift budget (M)== is explicitly quantified through ==closed-loop trajectory sensitivity analysis==, which bounds the potential increase in model error due to a policy update, allowing for an adjusted robustness margin.

## Results

- The proposed 'Robust' method successfully converged its robustness margin and maintained **100%** empirical score coverage and stability for an inverted pendulum, outperforming non-robust baselines.
- In a multi-obstacle planar maze navigation task, the 'Robust' method achieved **99.8%** score coverage and **100%** safety, effectively handling complex model errors and demonstrating superior performance compared to 'calibrate-once' baselines that failed to adapt to distribution shifts.
- For quadcopter obstacle-avoidance, the algorithm enabled safe and consistent navigation through a track, where baselines like 'calibrate-once' exhibited degenerate behavior, highlighting the framework's practical utility in high-dimensional, safety-critical applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Probabilistic stability and safety guarantees for robust conformal CLF/CBF policies can be formally maintained across policy-induced distribution shifts by leveraging ARCP.
> - The sensitivity of closed-loop trajectories to policy changes can be effectively bounded by analyzing the Lipschitz continuity of dynamics and policies, enabling the quantification of distribution shift.
> - Under specific Lipschitz conditions, explicit and implicit update rules for the robustness margin can be derived, with the explicit rule shown to converge to a neighborhood of the optimal population quantile.
