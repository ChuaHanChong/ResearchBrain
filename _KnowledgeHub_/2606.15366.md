# Robust Conformal CBF and CLF Controllers via Iterative Policy Updates

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zürich and the University of Pennsylvania developed an episodic learning framework for robust Conformal Control Lyapunov/Barrier Function (rCLF/rCBF) controllers, designed to maintain probabilistic stability and safety guarantees even with policy-induced distribution shifts. The framework utilizes Adversarially Robust Conformal Prediction (ARCP) and trajectory sensitivity analysis to iteratively update the robustness margin, demonstrating effective convergence and 100% empirical safety in robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Robust CLF/CBF controllers rely on a worst-case bound for model error, which is difficult to obtain reliably from data-driven models without strong assumptions.
- Conformal Prediction (CP) provides probabilistic error bounds but these guarantees are invalidated by 'distribution shift' when the control policy changes the system's trajectory distribution.
- This 'circular dependence' means a deployed CP-based controller can compromise its own safety and stability assurances by altering the data distribution from which its robustness margin was derived.

## Method

- An ==episodic learning framework== iteratively updates ==robust conformal CLF/CBF policies== and their associated ==robustness margins== across episodes.
- The framework integrates ==Adversarially Robust Conformal Prediction (ARCP)== to maintain ==probabilistic validity of uncertainty bounds== despite policy changes.
- A ==distribution shift budget (M)== is explicitly quantified through ==closed-loop trajectory sensitivity analysis==, which bounds the potential increase in model error due to a policy update, allowing for an adjusted robustness margin.

## Results

- The proposed 'Robust' method successfully converged its robustness margin and maintained **100%** empirical score coverage and stability for an inverted pendulum, outperforming non-robust baselines.
- In a multi-obstacle planar maze navigation task, the 'Robust' method achieved **99.8%** score coverage and **100%** safety, effectively handling complex model errors and demonstrating superior performance compared to 'calibrate-once' baselines that failed to adapt to distribution shifts.
- For quadcopter obstacle-avoidance, the algorithm enabled safe and consistent navigation through a track, where baselines like 'calibrate-once' exhibited degenerate behavior, highlighting the framework's practical utility in high-dimensional, safety-critical applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Probabilistic stability and safety guarantees for robust conformal CLF/CBF policies can be formally maintained across policy-induced distribution shifts by leveraging ARCP.
> - The sensitivity of closed-loop trajectories to policy changes can be effectively bounded by analyzing the Lipschitz continuity of dynamics and policies, enabling the quantification of distribution shift.
> - Under specific Lipschitz conditions, explicit and implicit update rules for the robustness margin can be derived, with the explicit rule shown to converge to a neighborhood of the optimal population quantile.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of robust control theory, machine learning for control, and uncertainty quantification. Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) are established tools for ensuring stability and safety in nonlinear dynamical systems. Traditionally, their application relies on precise knowledge of the system dynamics. However, in many practical scenarios, only approximate or learned models are available, leading to model errors that can compromise stability and safety guarantees.

Previous work has addressed model uncertainty through robust CLF and CBF frameworks, which embed a worst-case bound on model error into the control design. A significant challenge in this approach is obtaining a reliable, non-heuristic bound on this model error, especially in data-driven settings. Conformal Prediction (CP) has emerged as a statistical technique to quantify uncertainty and provide probabilistic error bounds with finite-sample guarantees, without requiring distributional assumptions beyond exchangeability.

Recently, research such as that presented in (referenced in the paper) began integrating CP with robust CLF/CBF frameworks to derive probabilistic bounds on model error from data. This approach aimed to provide statistically guaranteed robustness margins for controllers. However, a critical limitation arises from "distribution shift": the CP bounds are typically calibrated on data collected under an *initial* or *fixed* policy. Once the CLF/CBF controller is deployed with this bound, it alters the closed-loop system's trajectory distribution. This shift can invalidate the probabilistic guarantees of the original CP bound, leading to a loss of the very stability and safety assurances that the robust control framework was intended to provide. This phenomenon is highlighted as a "circular dependence" where the controller's policy affects the data distribution from which its robustness margin is derived.

Other research has explored various forms of CP under distribution shifts, including adaptive CP for non-exchangeable time series, weighted CP for covariate shifts, distributionally robust CP, and adversarially robust CP (ARCP). Additionally, performative prediction literature examines scenarios where model predictions influence future data generation. This paper distinguishes itself by specifically addressing *policy-induced distribution shifts* in the context of CLF/CBF control policies, aiming to maintain *probabilistic stability/safety guarantees* across iterative policy updates. It leverages ARCP and explicitly quantifies the worst-case distribution shift arising from controller changes, providing a novel approach to ensure control guarantees under interactive learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a fundamental challenge in integrating data-driven uncertainty quantification methods, specifically Conformal Prediction (CP), with robust control techniques like Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs). While CP offers probabilistic guarantees on model error bounds, its direct application to robust CLF/CBF synthesis faces a critical limitation: the distribution shift problem.

**Detailed Motivation:**

1. **Model Error in Data-Driven Control**: When control policies are synthesized using learned or identified nominal models (ˆf), an unknown model error (ε) exists between the nominal and true system dynamics (f). This error can lead to unstable or unsafe system behavior if not properly accounted for.
2. **Limitations of Robust CLF/CBF with Unknown Bounds**: Robust CLF/CBF frameworks require a known, worst-case bound (r) on the model error (||ε(x, u)|| ≤ r). Data-driven estimates of this bound are often heuristic or under-approximate, potentially rendering safety and stability guarantees invalid.
3. **Conformal Prediction's Promise and Pitfall**: CP can provide statistically rigorous, probabilistic upper bounds on model error without strong distributional assumptions. However, for these bounds to be valid, the calibration data (used to derive the bound) and the test data (generated by the deployed system) must be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.).
4. **The Distribution Shift Problem**: When a CP-derived robustness margin (r) is embedded into a CLF/CBF controller, the resulting policy (π_r) changes the closed-loop system's trajectory distribution. This new distribution differs from the one used for CP calibration, violating the i.i.d. assumption. Consequently, the probabilistic guarantees of the CP bound no longer hold for the *deployed* controller, as exemplified by "Example 1" in the paper, where a CP-based controller can render a system unsafe. This creates a "circular dependence": the policy depends on the robustness margin, which in turn depends on the data generated by the policy itself (Figure 1 in the paper).
5. **Lack of Guaranteed Robustness under Shift**: Current conformal CLF/CBF approaches do not formally retain stability/safety guarantees under these interaction-induced distribution shifts.

**Key Objectives:**

The main objective of this work is to develop a framework that addresses the distribution shift problem in conformal robust CLF/CBF control, thereby enabling the retention of probabilistic stability and safety guarantees across iterative policy updates. Specifically, the objectives are:

1. **Highlight and Analyze the Distribution Shift Issue**: Clearly articulate the problem of distribution shifts for conformal CLF and CBF policies and provide analytical examples illustrating when stability/safety guarantees cannot be maintained (e.g., Example 1).
2. **Propose an Episodic Framework for Policy Updates**: Introduce an iterative learning framework where the robust conformal CLF/CBF policy is updated across episodes.
3. **Integrate Adversarially Robust Conformal Prediction (ARCP)**: Employ ARCP to maintain probabilistic validity despite policy changes. This involves quantifying how much the nonconformity score can increase between policy updates.
4. **Quantify Distribution Shift Budget**: Develop a method to explicitly quantify a "distribution shift budget" (M) that bounds the potential increase in model error due to a policy update. This budget is derived through a closed-loop trajectory sensitivity analysis.
5. **Derive Update Rules for the CP Bound**: Based on the distribution shift budget, establish both implicit and explicit update rules for the robustness margin (r_j+1) for the next episode.
6. **Prove Episodic Probabilistic Guarantees**: Demonstrate that the proposed framework provides probabilistic stability and safety certificates that hold *across* episodes, conditional on the calibration data.
7. **Analyze Algorithm Convergence**: Investigate the convergence properties of the iterative update algorithm, particularly for the explicit update rule.
8. **Empirical Validation**: Demonstrate the effectiveness and theoretical claims of the framework through various case studies, comparing its performance against baseline approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is an episodic framework that iteratively updates robust Conformal Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) and Control Barrier Function (CBF) policies while maintaining probabilistic stability and safety guarantees in the presence of distribution shifts. The methodology integrates robust control, adversarial conformal prediction, and sensitivity analysis.

**A. Problem Formulation Context:**
The paper considers systems described by continuous-time dynamics `˙x(t) = f(x(t), u(t))`, where `f` is unknown. A known nominal model `ˆf` is available, leading to an unknown model error `ε(x, u) = f(x, u) - ˆf(x, u)`. Control policies `π` map states `x` to inputs `u`. Robust CLFs and CBFs (rCLF/rCBF) are used to enforce stability and safety, respectively, by satisfying conditions that depend on a robustness margin `r`. This margin `r` is supposed to upper-bound the model error, i.e., `||ε(x, u)|| ≤ r`. The policies `π_r(x)` are synthesized via quadratic programs (QPs), which minimize a control objective while satisfying the rCLF/rCBF constraints.

Conformal prediction (CP) is introduced to obtain a probabilistic bound for the *trajectory-level* error `s_ct(τ) = sup_t ||ε(x(t), u(t))||`. A key insight is that `s_ct(τ) ≤ r` suffices for stability/safety. CP uses a calibration dataset of `n` i.i.d. trajectories (and their scores `s^(i)`) to determine a conformal threshold `s_[k]`. If calibration and test trajectories are truly i.i.d. under a fixed policy `π`, then `P(s_ct(τ) ≤ s_[k]) ≥ 1-α`. However, this "fixed policy" assumption is typically violated in practical iterative control design, leading to the distribution shift problem.

**B. Episodic Framework for Iterative Policy Updates:**
To address the circular dependence and distribution shift, the authors propose an episodic framework.
1. **Episode `j`**: A robustness margin `r_j` is used to synthesize policy `π_j = π_r_j`.
2. **Data Collection**: `n_j` initial states are sampled, and `n_j` calibration trajectories `τ^(i)_j` are collected under `π_j`. The nonconformity scores `s^(i)_j = s_ct(τ^(i)_j)` form the calibration dataset `D_cal_j`.
3. **Conformal Threshold `q_j`**: The empirical conformal threshold `q_j` is computed from `D_cal_j` at a tightened miscoverage level `1 - ¯α_j` (where `¯α_j = α - √ln(1/δ)/(2n_j)`), as required by Adversarially Robust Conformal Prediction (ARCP).

**C. Bounding Policy-Induced Distribution Shifts via ARCP:**
The core innovation lies in transferring probabilistic guarantees from policy `π_j` to `π_j+1`.
1. **Adversarially Robust Conformal Prediction (ARCP)**: The framework employs a calibration-conditional version of ARCP (Lemma 3). ARCP allows for guarantees on a perturbed test score `˜s` if `˜s ≤ s + M` almost surely, where `M` is a non-negative perturbation term.
2. **Quantifying `M`**: To use ARCP, the authors define a "coupled" nonconformity score `˜s_j+1 := s_ct(x_j(0), π_j+1)`. This score uses the *same initial condition* `x_j(0)` as `s_j` but applies the *new policy* `π_j+1`. The crucial step is bounding the difference `|˜s_j+1 - s_j|`. This difference is bounded by `M_j+1 = ρ(π_j+1, π_j)`.
3. **Trajectory Sensitivity Analysis (Assumption 3 & Proposition 3)**:
 - **Lipschitz Assumptions**: The analysis relies on the assumption that the true dynamics `f`, nominal dynamics `ˆf`, and the policy `π_r(x)` are Lipschitz continuous on relevant compact sets `Ω` and input sets `U`. The policy `π_r(x)` is also assumed to be Lipschitz continuous with respect to the state `x` (`L_π`).
 - **State Deviation**: Using Grönwall's inequality, the state deviation `||x'(t) - x(t)||` between two trajectories generated by policies `π_r'` and `π_r` (from the same `x_0`) is bounded by `β_T_x ||π_r' - π_r||_Ω`, where `β_T_x` depends on time horizon `T` and Lipschitz constants.
 - **Nonconformity Score Deviation**: Based on the Lipschitz continuity of `ε(x, u)` and the bounded state deviation, the difference in nonconformity scores `|s(x_0, π_r') - s(x_0, π_r)|` is bounded by `β_T ||π_r' - π_r||_Ω`. This `β_T` acts as the `ρ` function in ARCP.
4. **Policy Sensitivity in `r` (Assumption 4 & Proposition 2)**:
 - To enable an *explicit* update rule, the policy `π_r(x)` is further assumed to be Lipschitz continuous with respect to the margin `r` (with constant `L_U`). This is justified by analyzing the sensitivity of the QP solutions (Eq. 5, 6) to the parameter `r` under strong regularity conditions of the QP.
 - Combining these, the difference in nonconformity scores can be bounded by `κ |r' - r|`, where `κ = β_T L_U`.

**D. Algorithmic Update Rules for `r_j+1`:**
Using `M_j+1 = κ |r_j+1 - r_j|` (or `M_j+1 = β_T ||π_r_j+1 - π_r_j||_Ω`), the robustness margin `r_j+1` for the next episode is chosen to minimize the margin while satisfying the ARCP inequality:
1. **Implicit Update (Eq. 11)**: `r_j+1 ∈ argmin {r : r ≥ q_j + β_T ||π_r - π_r_j||_Ω}`. This is a fixed-point problem solvable via numerical methods like grid search.
2. **Explicit Update (Eq. 14)**: If `π_r(x)` is Lipschitz in `r` (Assumption 4), leading to `M_j+1 = κ |r_j+1 - r_j|`, then for `κ < 1`, `r_j+1` can be computed in closed form:
 `r_j+1 = (q_j - κr_j) / (1 - κ)` if `q_j ≥ r_j`
 `r_j+1 = (q_j + κr_j) / (1 + κ)` if `q_j < r_j`

This explicit rule is central to the algorithm's practical implementation and convergence analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical guarantees and empirical validations for its proposed framework.

**A. Theoretical Guarantees:**

1. **Episodic Validity (Theorem 1)**: This theorem establishes that if the robustness margin `r_j+1` for episode `j+1` is chosen such that `r_j+1 ≥ q_j + M_j+1` (where `q_j` is the conformal threshold from episode `j`'s data, and `M_j+1` is the distribution shift budget), then the probability of the actual nonconformity score `s_j+1` in episode `j+1` being less than or equal to `r_j+1` is at least `1-α`, with a high outer confidence `1-δ`. This means `P_{n_j}(P(s_{j+1} ≤ r_{j+1} | D_{cal,j}) ≥ 1-α) ≥ 1-δ`. This result is fundamental, ensuring that the updated margin `r_j+1` provides statistical coverage despite the policy change.
2. **Conformal Safety and Stability Certificates (Theorem 2)**: Building on Theorem 1, this theorem extends the episodic validity to provide probabilistic guarantees for control objectives. If `r_j+1` satisfies the condition from Theorem 1:
 - **Safety**: If `h(x)` is an rCBF with margin `r_j+1` and the policy `π_j+1` enforces the CBF constraint, then `P_{n_j}(P(x_{j+1}(t) ∈ C, ∀t∈[0,T] | D_{cal,j}) ≥ 1-α) ≥ 1-δ`. This means the system remains in the safe set `C` with high probability over the horizon `T`.
 - **Stability**: Similarly, if `V(x)` is an rCLF with margin `r_j+1` and `π_j+1` enforces the CLF constraint, then `P_{n_j}(P(||x_{j+1}(t)|| ≤ α_V^{-1}(e^{-ct}α_V(||x_{j+1}(0)||)), ∀t∈[0,T] | D_{cal,j}) ≥ 1-α) ≥ 1-δ`. This guarantees stability (boundedness of state norm) with high probability.
 These theorems are crucial as they formally link the statistical guarantees of ARCP to the deterministic control guarantees of rCLF/rCBF, preserving them across policy updates.
3. **Convergence of the Explicit Update Rule (Theorem 3 & Corollary 1)**: The paper analyzes the convergence of the explicit update rule (Eq. 14) as a stochastic recursion.
 - It defines a population `(1-α)`-quantile `Q_r(1-α)` as a fixed point `r* = Q_{r*}(1-α)`.
 - If the Lipschitz constant `κ < 1`, the mapping `r ↦ Q_r(1-α)` is a contraction, implying a unique fixed point `r*`.
 - The error `e_j = |r_j - r*|` is shown to propagate as `e_j+1 ≤ λ_κ e_j + B_κ |η_j|`, where `λ_κ = 2κ / (1-κ)` and `B_κ = 1 / (1-κ)`. The term `η_j` represents the finite-sample quantile estimation error.
 - If `κ < 1/3` and `|η_j|` is uniformly bounded, the `lim sup e_j` is bounded, meaning the iterative margin `r_j` converges to a neighborhood of `r*`.
 - Furthermore, if `η_j → 0` (which occurs as the number of calibration samples `n_j → ∞`), then `r_j → r*` with high probability (conditional on the initial sequence of calibration data `D_cal_0:J-1`). This result provides a theoretical basis for the long-term behavior and accuracy of the algorithm.

**B. Empirical Validations (Case Studies):**
The framework is demonstrated on three control benchmarks:

1. **Inverted Pendulum (rCLF stability)**:
 - **Task**: Stabilize an inverted pendulum using an rCLF with a learned nominal model.
 - **Results**: The proposed "Robust" method (iterative update) successfully converges its robustness margin `r_j` to a stable value near the 1-α score quantile, maintaining 100% empirical score coverage and stability. The "calibrate-once" baseline also performed well here (a scenario similar to that explored in prior work). In contrast, the "non-robust" baseline (r=0) failed to maintain stability, highlighting the need for robustification.
 - **Key Finding**: Demonstrated effective convergence of `r` and maintenance of stability guarantees.

2. **Multi-Obstacle Planar Maze Navigation (rCBF safety)**:
 - **Task**: Navigate a single-integrator system through a maze of 17 circular obstacles with a complex, barrier-localized vortex field as model error, using an rCBF for collision avoidance.
 - **Results**: The "Robust" method effectively converged its margin `r_j` rapidly (within 2 episodes, consistent with `κ = 0.3 < 1/3`), achieving 99.8% score coverage and 100% safety. The "calibrate-once" and "naive" baselines, while maintaining safety, failed to achieve the target (1-α = 0.9) score coverage (66.8% and 87.2% respectively), illustrating the inadequacy of approaches that do not account for distribution shifts explicitly. The "non-robust" baseline resulted in significant collisions.
 - **Key Finding**: Demonstrated the necessity of the iterative update and the explicit handling of distribution shift for maintaining coverage and safety in a complex, multi-constraint environment.

3. **Quadcopter Obstacle-Avoidance (Higher-Order rCBF safety)**:
 - **Task**: An ego quadcopter navigating a track with fixed obstacles, where model error arises from ignored physical effects and environment interactions (e.g., stopping behavior exacerbating model mismatch). A 4th-order ECBF-QP is used for motor thrust control.
 - **Results**: The "Robust" algorithm successfully converged on an `r` value that allowed the quadcopter to navigate the maze safely and consistently. Baselines like "calibrate-once" exhibited degenerate behavior, getting stuck or attempting inappropriate paths due to fixed or improperly adapted `r` values. This highlights how distribution shifts can significantly impair performance in complex, dynamic tasks.
 - **Key Finding**: Illustrated the practical utility of the framework in a high-dimensional, safety-critical robotic application where policy-induced distribution shifts significantly impact controller performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a critical and previously unaddressed challenge at the intersection of data-driven control and rigorous safety/stability guarantees. By providing a framework that explicitly accounts for policy-induced distribution shifts, the work significantly advances the state of the art in robust learning-based control.

**Key Significance:**

1. **First Probabilistic Guarantees Under Distribution Shift**: To the best of the authors' knowledge, these are the first results that provide formal stability and safety guarantees for robust conformal CLF/CBF policies *despite policy-induced distribution shifts*. This is a major theoretical contribution, closing a critical gap in the practical deployment of learning-based controllers with statistical assurances.
2. **Bridging Statistical and Formal Methods**: The work successfully integrates Adversarially Robust Conformal Prediction (ARCP) with the formal methods of CLFs and CBFs. This combination leverages the statistical power of CP for uncertainty quantification and the deterministic guarantees of CLF/CBF for control, offering a powerful paradigm for designing safe and reliable autonomous systems.
3. **Quantifiable Distribution Shift Management**: The methodology introduces a principled way to quantify the distribution shift between successive policy updates through a closed-loop trajectory sensitivity analysis. This quantification (via `M` or `κ`) is crucial, as it enables the iterative adjustment of the robustness margin while retaining statistical validity.
4. **Actionable Update Rules**: The derivation of both implicit and, importantly, explicit update rules for the robustness margin (e.g., Equation 14) provides practical, implementable algorithms for controller adaptation. The analysis of the convergence properties of these rules further bolsters their utility and predictability.
5. **Enhanced Reliability for Autonomous Systems**: In fields such as robotics, aerospace, and autonomous driving, where learned models are increasingly common, ensuring verifiable safety and stability is paramount. This framework offers a method to build controllers that can adapt to learned model uncertainties and interactive environments while providing high-confidence guarantees, thereby increasing the reliability and trustworthiness of autonomous systems.
6. **Addressing the Circularity Problem**: The work effectively breaks the "circular dependence" where a controller's parameters are derived from data generated by the controller itself, by providing a principled way to manage the resulting distribution shifts across iterations.
7. **Foundation for Adaptive Safety-Critical Systems**: This research lays a foundation for future work on adaptive safety-critical control systems. It opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated mechanisms for managing distribution shifts, extending to more complex system dynamics, various forms of uncertainties (e.g., stochastic disturbances), and alternative learning paradigms.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Safer Autonomous Navigation**: Applications in drones, self-driving cars, and industrial robots can benefit from controllers that continuously adapt to environmental changes and learned model inaccuracies while rigorously maintaining safety guarantees.
- **Robust Human-Robot Interaction**: In interactive settings, where robot policies influence human behavior (and vice-versa), this framework could help design adaptive controllers that remain safe and stable despite the co-adaptation of agents.
- **Trustworthy AI in Control**: By providing transparent and quantifiable probabilistic guarantees, the work contributes to building more trustworthy AI-driven control systems, fostering greater confidence in their deployment in safety-critical domains.
- **Advancement in Learning-Based Control**: The methodology represents a significant step towards combining the flexibility and performance advantages of learning-based control with the rigorous assurances demanded by formal control theory.
