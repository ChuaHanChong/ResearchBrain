# ProDrive: Proactive Planning for Autonomous Driving via Ego-Environment Co-Evolution

> [!summary] Summary
> ProDrive introduces a framework for proactive autonomous driving planning that tightly couples a query-centric trajectory planner with a BEV world model, enabling ego-environment co-evolution. The system achieves a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 86.6 on the NAVSIM benchmark, demonstrating improved safety and efficiency by anticipating future scene evolution and interactions.

## Problem

- Current autonomous driving planners are predominantly reactive, making decisions based only on immediate sensor observations.
- This reactive nature often leads to myopic decisions, safety-critical failures, and suboptimal efficiency in dynamic and complex driving environments.
- Existing integrations of world models are frequently auxiliary or loosely coupled, failing to deeply influence the planner's internal decision-making process with future scene dynamics.

## Method

- **ProDrive** jointly trains a ==query-centric trajectory planner== (Ego Module) and a ==BEV world model== (Environment Module) end-to-end.
- Implements ==Ego-Environment Coupling== by injecting ==planner-aware ego tokens== into the world model to condition future scene predictions on the ego vehicle's intended actions.
- Maintains ==unbroken gradient flow== from the world model's future outcome assessments back to the planner, letting predicted states and rewards shape decision-making during training.

## Results

- **ProDrive** achieved **PDMS 86.6** on NAVSIM v1, outperforming reactive and other world-model-integrated baselines.
- Strong safety: **NC 98.0**, **DAC 95.4**, **TTC 93.7**.
- Enhanced efficiency: **Ego Progress 80.7**; ablations confirm world model, ego token injection, and proactive gradient feedback are all necessary.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional interaction between a planner and a world model, where each informs the other, is crucial for enabling proactive and safer autonomous driving.
> - Injecting rich, planner-aware semantic context (ego tokens) into the world model significantly enhances the accuracy of future scene prediction and improves the collaborative optimization of the system.
> - Maintaining an end-to-end gradient path from future-aware reward signals (e.g., collision avoidance, progress) back to the planner is vital for learning anticipatory behaviors and making globally optimal decisions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous driving systems has progressed through various paradigms, from modular pipelines to end-to-end learning approaches. This paper positions ProDrive within the context of moving beyond reactive planning, which primarily relies on current observations, towards proactive planning that incorporates future scene understanding.

Historically, autonomous driving research involved distinct stages such as perception, prediction, and planning. Trajectory prediction, a key component, evolved to consider ego vehicle intentions or candidate plans, as seen in works like PRECOG and Trajectron++. However, these approaches often remained prediction-centric, with the planner acting as a downstream consumer of predictive outputs rather than being intrinsically shaped by future reasoning.

Concurrently, end-to-end autonomous driving systems, exemplified by TransFuser, UniAD, and VADv2, have gained prominence. These systems aim to directly map raw sensor inputs to planning outputs, reducing compounding errors inherent in sequential modular designs. Despite their advancements, many end-to-end systems still operate reactively, generating trajectories based primarily on the current observed state of the environment.

The integration of world models into autonomous driving represents a more recent development to incorporate anticipatory reasoning. Existing world model applications include:
- **Generative World Models:** Such as GAIA-1 and DriveDreamer, which synthesize realistic future driving videos. While offering high fidelity, these models often operate in high-dimensional pixel spaces, which can be computationally intensive and challenging to integrate directly with planning modules.
- **Structured World Models:** These models, including LAW, SeerDrive, and ImagiDrive, predict compact, task-relevant representations of future states. They are more amenable to integration with planning. However, many current implementations use world models in an auxiliary capacity, serving as data generators, simulators, or post-hoc evaluators for candidate trajectories. The direct coupling between the planner's internal decision representations and the predicted future scene evolution often remains limited.

ProDrive aims to bridge this gap by establishing a tighter, bidirectional coupling between a query-centric planner and a BEV world model. This approach seeks to move beyond traditional reactive decision-making and loosely coupled world model integration, allowing the planner to be directly influenced and optimized by anticipated future scene dynamics. The work specifically relates to the structured world modeling direction but differentiates itself by injecting planning-aware features directly into the world model to guide future prediction, rather than relying on generic latent features or decoupled action tokens.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for ProDrive stems from a recognized limitation in current autonomous driving planners: their predominantly reactive nature. End-to-end planners typically generate trajectories based solely on immediate sensor observations. In dynamic real-world driving scenarios, this reactive approach can lead to several challenges:
- **Myopic Decisions:** Without explicit consideration of how the environment will evolve, or how surrounding agents will react to the ego vehicle's actions, planners may make short-sighted decisions that are locally optimal but globally suboptimal or unsafe.
- **Safety-Critical Failures:** The inability to anticipate future interactions and potential hazards can result in safety-critical situations, such as unexpected collisions or breaches of traffic rules.
- **Suboptimal Efficiency:** Reactive planning may also lead to inefficient driving, as opportunities for smoother maneuvers or earlier adjustments to traffic flow might be missed if future states are not modeled.

To address these limitations, the central objective of ProDrive is to enable **proactive planning** for autonomous driving. This involves developing a framework that allows the ego vehicle to anticipate future scene evolution and its interactions with surrounding agents, thereby facilitating more informed and safer decision-making.

The specific objectives outlined by the authors to achieve this proactive planning capability are:
1. **Jointly Train a Query-Centric Trajectory Planner and a Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) World Model End-to-End:** This involves creating an integrated system where both modules are optimized together, rather than sequentially or in a decoupled manner.
2. **Enable Ego-Environment Co-Evolution:** This core concept implies a reciprocal relationship where the planner influences future predictions, and these predictions, in turn, guide the planner's decisions.
3. **Inject Planner-Aware Ego Tokens into the World Model:** The planner is designed to generate diverse candidate trajectories along with "planning-aware ego tokens." These tokens, representing the planner's internal decision semantics, are fed into the world model to condition future scene prediction. This ensures that the future environment evolution is predicted in the context of the ego vehicle's specific intended actions.
4. **Evaluate Candidate Trajectories in Parallel:** The world model is utilized to assess the potential outcomes of multiple candidate trajectories simultaneously, considering their impact on future scene evolution.
5. **Preserve End-to-End Gradient Flow:** A crucial objective is to maintain an unbroken gradient path from the world model's future outcome assessments back to the planner. This allows the feedback from predicted future states (e.g., potential collisions or desired progress) to directly shape the planner's internal decision-making process during training.
6. **Achieve Bidirectional Coupling:** The framework aims for a tight, two-way interaction where the planner informs future prediction, and future prediction directly influences the planner's learning and refinement.

By achieving these objectives, ProDrive aims to move beyond current-observation-driven decision-making, enabling autonomous vehicles to act with foresight in complex and dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ProDrive is structured around two main components: an Ego Module (the planner) and an Environment Module (a BEV world model), connected through a bidirectional Ego-Environment Coupling mechanism.

**Problem Formulation:**
The system receives multi-view camera images, a LiDAR point cloud, and ego vehicle status (velocity, acceleration, yaw rate). Its objective is to predict a future trajectory consisting of *T* waypoints, each specifying position (x, y) and heading (θ), within the ego-centric coordinate frame.

**Ego Module: Query-Centric Planner:**
This module is responsible for generating diverse candidate trajectories. It is based on a BEVFormer-like architecture:
1. **Input Processing:** Multi-view images are encoded using a ResNet-34 backbone. Ego vehicle state is projected into ego-conditioned features.
2. **Ego Tokens:** A set of learnable ego tokens, initialized as `Q^(0)`, are employed. These tokens will progressively encode planning-aware information.
3. **Iterative Cross-Attention Refinement:** The ego tokens undergo *L* cascaded refinement stages with shared parameters.
 - **Trajectory Decoding:** At each stage *l*, an MLP head decodes each ego token into a trajectory waypoint, resulting in *K* distinct trajectory proposals (`τ^(l)`).
 - **Feature Refinement:** An `EgoRefiner` block updates the ego tokens (`Q^(l+1)`). This block includes deformable self-attention among the ego tokens and deformable cross-attention with multi-view image features. The decoded trajectory waypoints from `τ^(l)` serve as 3D reference points projected onto camera views, guiding the attention mechanism to relevant image features.
4. **Output:** After *L* stages, the module outputs `K` final candidate trajectories (`{τ_k}`) and the refined ego tokens (`Q^(L)`).

**Ego-Module Training:** The Ego Module is trained with a multi-task loss incorporating:
- **Trajectory Loss (`L_traj`):** A winner-take-all regression loss comparing the *K* proposals at each stage to the expert trajectory (`τ*`), combined with a diversity regularizer (`L_div`) to encourage varied proposals.
- **Scoring Loss (`L_score`):** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for a planner-side scorer to predict online Predictive Driver Model (PDM) targets, validity indicators, key-agent states, and ego-area occupancy.
- **Auxiliary Perception Losses (`L_aux`):** Cross-Entropy for predicted BEV semantic maps, detection classification, and detection bounding box regression from ego queries. These stabilize ego token representations and improve candidate generation.

**Environment Module: BEV World Model:**
This module predicts future scene states and evaluates the candidate trajectories generated by the Ego Module.
1. **Initial BEV State:** A separate camera-LiDAR backbone, similar to TransFuser, generates an initial BEV state (`B_0`).
2. **Action Tokens:** For each candidate trajectory `τ_k`, an action token `a_k` is created to encode its geometry and ego kinematics.
3. **Recurrent Future Prediction:** The world model predicts future BEV states over *N* iterations. At each iteration *i*, the input sequence `F_i` consists of the action token (`a_k^i`), an ego token (`s_k^i`, derived from the planner), and the current BEV feature (`B_k^i`). This sequence, augmented with positional embeddings, is processed by a Transformer encoder to predict the future action token, an enriched state token, and the future BEV state (`B_k^(i+1)`).
4. **Trajectory Selection and Reward:** For each candidate trajectory, multi-step BEV features and action tokens are aggregated. Two heads predict:
 - **Imitation Reward:** Supervised by the distance to the expert trajectory.
 - **Simulation-Oriented Rewards:** These include collision avoidance, drivable-area compliance, progress, time-to-collision (TTC), and comfort.
 The final score (`R_k`) is a weighted sum of the logarithmic rewards. The trajectory with the highest `R_k` is selected.

**Environment-Module Training:** The Environment Module is trained with:
- **Imitation Reward Loss (`L_im`):** Cross-entropy between predicted imitation reward and a soft target derived from the proximity of `τ_k` to `τ*`.
- **Simulation Reward Loss (`L_sim`):** BCE between predicted simulation reward and precomputed simulator metrics from the nearest anchor trajectory.
- **Alignment Loss (`L_align`):** Mean Squared Error (MSE) to align the planner-side score with the normalized world-model score, explicitly coupling the two modules.
- **World Model Loss (`L_wm`):** Focal loss for supervising current and future BEV semantic maps (`ˆM_0`, `ˆM_f`). The future target is conditioned on the proposed ego trajectory, rendering the ego box at the sampled proposal position on the future BEV canvas.

**Ego-Environment Coupling:**
This mechanism facilitates the bidirectional interaction between the Ego and Environment Modules.
1. **Ego Token Injection:** The refined ego tokens (`Q^(L)`) from the Ego Module, which carry planning-relevant semantic context, are reshaped and projected into specific ego tokens (`s_k^i`) that are injected into the Environment Module for each world model iteration. This allows the world model to condition its future predictions not only on the planned trajectory but also on the underlying rationale and context from the planner.
2. **Joint Training Objective:** ProDrive is trained end-to-end using a combined multi-task objective: `L = L_traj + L_score + L_reward + L_wm + L_aux`. This ensures that the future-aware gradients from the Environment Module's reward evaluation and BEV prediction directly propagate back to optimize the Ego Module, enabling proactive planning.

**Implementation Details:**
The system is implemented using PyTorch and PyTorch Lightning. Both modules utilize ResNet-34 backbones. The agent processes a synchronized sensor slice from the NAVSIM history, including multi-view cameras and LiDAR. A 4-second planning horizon with 0.5-second sampling results in 8 waypoints. The planner generates 64 candidate trajectories. Training involves Adam optimization with different learning rates for the Ego (10^-4) and Environment (10^-5) modules, trained for 15 epochs on 8 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ProDrive was conducted on the NAVSIM v1 benchmark, which emphasizes challenging and interactive planning scenarios, using the Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) as the primary metric. PDMS integrates five factors: No At-Fault Collision (NC), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), Time-to-Collision (TTC), Comfort (C), and Ego Progress (EP), providing a comprehensive measure of planning performance.

**Quantitative Performance (Table 1):**
ProDrive demonstrated performance improvements over several baselines. It achieved a PDMS of 86.6, which was higher than all compared methods.
- **Safety Metrics:** ProDrive exhibited strong safety performance, with 98.0 NC (no at-fault collisions), 95.4 DAC (drivable area compliance), and 93.7 TTC (time-to-collision). These values indicate effective collision avoidance and adherence to road rules.
- **Efficiency Metric:** An Ego Progress (EP) score of 80.7 was achieved, suggesting improved long-horizon planning efficiency.
- **Comparative Performance:** ProDrive outperformed leading reactive-planning baselines such as TransFuser, UniAD, VADv2, and PARA-Drive. It also exceeded the performance of other proactive or world-model-integrated methods like LAW, World4Drive, FSDrive, and Epona, particularly in overall PDMS and several sub-metrics.

**Qualitative Analysis (Figure 3):**
Qualitative examples illustrated ProDrive's behavior in complex interactive driving scenarios. The system was observed to anticipate the future motions of surrounding vehicles, generating more foresighted plans compared to reactive approaches. This anticipatory capability allowed ProDrive to select trajectories that balanced safety and efficiency, proactively mitigating potential collision risks while maintaining appropriate driving progress. These observations suggest that the planner is capable of reasoning beyond the immediate scene.

**Ablation Studies (Table 2):**
To assess the contribution of key architectural components, three ablation studies were performed:
1. **"w/o World Model":** Replacing the Environment Module with a simple MLP-based reward predictor resulted in a substantial performance drop across all critical metrics. PDMS decreased to 83.5, NC to 94.9, DAC to 93.9, TTC to 90.8, and EP to 79.5. This indicated that explicit modeling of future scene evolution, rather than just predicting rewards from trajectory tokens, is important for safety and long-horizon planning efficiency.
2. **"w/o Ego Token Injection":** Removing the mechanism that injects planner-refined ego tokens into the Environment Module led to a reduction in performance. PDMS dropped to 85.5, NC to 97.8, DAC to 94.3, TTC to 93.0, and EP to 80.3. This suggests that conditioning the world model on rich, planner-aware semantic context is beneficial for effective future prediction and for maintaining stable collaborative optimization between the modules.
3. **"w/o Proactive Gradient":** Blocking the gradient flow from the Environment Module (reward loss) back to the Ego Module (planner) caused a performance decline. PDMS decreased to 85.8, NC to 97.6, DAC to 94.9, and TTC to 93.1. This finding supports the argument that future-aware gradient feedback is necessary for directly shaping the planner's internal decision process and for enabling it to make safe and effective decisions.

The ablation studies collectively affirmed the effectiveness of the proposed Ego-Environment Coupling mechanism. Both directions of interaction—ego token injection and proactive gradient feedback—were shown to be relevant for the overall performance of ProDrive.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ProDrive framework represents an advancement in the field of autonomous driving by addressing the limitations of reactive planning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Shift from Reactive to Proactive Planning:** ProDrive demonstrates a viable method for moving beyond current-observation-driven reactive decision-making. By integrating explicit future scene prediction, the system enables autonomous vehicles to anticipate environmental evolution and potential interactions, which can lead to more informed and less myopic planning.
- **Enhanced Safety and Efficiency:** The quantitative results on the NAVSIM benchmark indicate measurable improvements in safety-critical metrics (No At-Fault Collision, Drivable Area Compliance, Time-to-Collision) and planning efficiency (Ego Progress). These improvements are particularly relevant in complex, interactive driving scenarios where anticipatory reasoning is crucial for safe and smooth navigation.
- **Tighter Integration of World Models:** The proposed Ego-Environment Coupling mechanism provides a method for deeply integrating a world model with a trajectory planner. This moves beyond using world models merely as auxiliary tools (e.g., for data generation or post-hoc evaluation) by making them integral to the planner's learning and decision-making process through feature injection and end-to-end gradient feedback.
- **Bidirectional Co-Evolution:** The concept of ego-environment co-evolution, where the planner's internal representations guide future prediction and vice-versa, offers a framework for developing more sophisticated autonomous systems. This bidirectional interaction allows for mutual refinement and adaptation between the ego vehicle's intentions and the anticipated environment dynamics.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** ProDrive establishes a baseline for future research into proactive planning architectures. The authors suggest extending ProDrive to more expressive world models, indicating an avenue for further development in anticipating broader ranges of environmental states and agent behaviors. The framework could serve as a reference for integrating advanced predictive capabilities into end-to-end driving systems.
- **Potential for Real-World Deployment:** Improvements in safety and robustness, particularly in challenging interactive scenarios, could contribute to the development of more reliable and trustworthy autonomous driving systems, potentially accelerating their acceptance and deployment in real-world urban and complex environments.

By demonstrating the efficacy of tightly coupled ego-environment co-evolution, ProDrive contributes to the ongoing development of autonomous driving systems that can exhibit more human-like foresight and adaptability.
