# Dexonomy: Synthesizing All Dexterous Grasp Types in a Grasp Taxonomy

> [!summary] Summary
> Dexonomy presents an efficient, two-stage pipeline for synthesizing 9.5 million physically plausible, type-aware dexterous grasps across 10,700 objects and 31 grasp types in under three days. The work further demonstrates training a generative model on this comprehensive dataset, achieving an 82.3% real-world success rate for type-conditional grasping with a Shadow hand.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with 'true dexterous grasping,' which requires selecting and executing appropriate grasp types for specific tasks, moving beyond mere object acquisition.
- Creating large-scale, high-quality, and type-aware datasets for multi-fingered hands is challenging, hindering the development of data-driven learning approaches for dexterous manipulation.
- Existing grasp synthesis methods often lack the ability to generate a diverse range of grasp types, ensure physical plausibility, or generalize effectively across various objects and hand configurations.

## Method

- A ==two-stage optimization pipeline== (**Dexonomy**) is introduced, beginning with lightweight global alignment of the object's pose to a human-annotated grasp template, followed by simulation-based local refinement of the hand's pose.
- The pipeline leverages ==differentiable physics simulations (Warp and MuJoCo)== for efficient optimization and rigorous validation, ensuring contact-rich, penetration-free, and physically plausible grasps.
- A ==self-improving template library== mechanism iteratively enhances the synthesis process by incorporating successful grasps as new templates, broadening the range of graspable objects and improving robustness.

## Results

- A large-scale Dexonomy dataset was constructed, containing **9.5 million grasps** for **10,700 objects** across **31 GRASP taxonomy types**, synthesized efficiently in less than **three days** using 8 NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs.
- The pipeline achieved a **60.50%** grasp success rate on a fingertip grasp benchmark, outperforming existing baselines such as BODex (**49.23%**) and demonstrating superior contact richness with zero penetration.
- A type-conditional generative model trained on the Dexonomy dataset achieved an **82.3%** real-world success rate for type-aware grasping with a Shadow hand on 13 unseen objects, demonstrating the practical utility of the generated data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining parallelized global object-centric alignment with local hand-centric refinement in a differentiable physics environment efficiently generates high-quality, type-aware grasps.
> - The iterative self-improvement of the grasp template library significantly enhances synthesis success rates and generalizability by continuously learning from successfully generated grasps.
> - Rigorous, contact-aware simulation validation and a unified grasp quality metric are crucial for ensuring the physical plausibility and stability of synthesized grasps for real-world application.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous grasping is a fundamental capability for intelligent robots, enabling intricate interactions with the physical world. However, the field has largely focused on whether a dexterous hand can successfully grasp an object, often treating multi-fingered hands as glorified parallel grippers. This work critically argues that true dexterous grasping requires selecting and executing *appropriate grasp types* based on task requirements, a capability that remains largely underexplored.

The broader research landscape for grasp synthesis can be categorized into:
- **Analytical Grasp Synthesis:** These methods typically focus on optimizing wrench-based force closure metrics. While applicable to any object, they are often type-unaware, generate a limited range of grasp types (e.g., fingertip grasps), and can produce unnatural hand poses as they prioritize force closure over human-like grasping habits. For dexterous hands with high degrees of freedom, random sampling is inefficient, necessitating complex optimization, which can be prone to local minima.
- **Functional Grasp Transfer:** Approaches like aim to generate more human-like grasps by mapping contact regions from demonstrations. However, their scalability is limited, as they often require geometrically similar or axis-aligned objects to the initial demonstration.
- **Learning-based Grasp Synthesis:** Modern approaches leverage generative models (CVAE, diffusion models, normalizing flows) to handle partial observations and real-world deployment. Their performance, however, is heavily dependent on the availability of large-scale, high-quality training datasets. Collecting such data for multi-fingered hands, especially with diverse grasp types and contact-rich scenarios, is extremely challenging, whether through teleoperation, human-to-robot transfer, or reinforcement learning (RL).

Dexonomy positions itself as a critical advancement that addresses the limitations of existing methods, particularly the data bottleneck for learning-based approaches. It proposes an *efficient, generalizable pipeline* to synthesize high-quality, type-aware dexterous grasps, thereby enabling the creation of the necessary large-scale datasets. Unlike analytical methods, it integrates human-annotated templates to ensure natural and type-specific grasps, and unlike functional transfer methods, it is not limited by object similarity. By building upon robust simulation environments and differentiable optimization techniques, Dexonomy offers a scalable solution for generating diverse and physically plausible grasps that reflect the full dexterity of multi-fingered hands, thus moving closer to enabling robots to perform "true dexterous grasping."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for Dexonomy stems from the recognition that for robots to be truly intelligent and capable, they must move beyond simply "grasping with dexterous hands" to "grasping dexterously with appropriate grasp types based on the task requirement." This entails a semantic understanding of *how* an object should be grasped, differentiating between tasks like securely holding an apple (power grasp) versus delicately picking up a flat object (precision grasp).

The authors identify two key challenges in achieving this goal:
1. **Selecting the appropriate grasp type based on the task:** This is acknowledged as a high-level decision-making problem, which might be partially addressed by advancements in large vision-language models like GPT-4o.
2. **Generating high-quality grasps for specified types and objects:** This challenge is the primary focus of the paper and represents a significant bottleneck.

To tackle the second challenge, particularly with data-driven methods, the indispensable first step is the creation of a large-scale, high-quality dataset that encompasses a wide variety of grasp types, at least those categorized by established taxonomies like GRASP. However, collecting and annotating such data for multi-fingered hands is extremely difficult.

Therefore, the key objectives of the Dexonomy project are:
- To propose an **efficient and generalizable pipeline** capable of synthesizing contact-rich, penetration-free, and physically plausible dexterous grasps for *any grasp type, any object, and any articulated hand*. This pipeline is designed to operate efficiently, starting from only a single human-annotated grasp template per hand and grasp type.
- To utilize this pipeline to **construct a large-scale, high-quality dataset** (named Dexonomy) that covers a comprehensive range of grasp types (specifically, 31 types from the GRASP taxonomy). This dataset aims to support the development of data-driven methods for type-aware grasp synthesis.
- To **demonstrate the utility of the constructed dataset** by training a type-conditional generative model that can predict desired grasp types for novel objects from single-view point clouds, and validate its performance in real-world scenarios.
- To develop an **annotation user interface (UI)** based on the proposed algorithm, simplifying the process of collecting semantic grasp data with minimal human effort.

In essence, the project aims to solve the fundamental data scarcity problem for type-aware dexterous grasping, providing both the tools for data generation and the resulting dataset to accelerate research in intelligent robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Dexonomy pipeline is an efficient, two-stage optimization and validation framework designed to synthesize high-quality, type-aware dexterous grasps. An overview of the pipeline is shown in Figure 2 of the paper.

**1. Grasp Template Definition:**
The process begins with a "Grasp Template Library," which initially requires just one human-annotated template for each hand and grasp type. A template comprises the hand's joint configuration (q), hand contact points (p_h_i), their corresponding surface normals (n_h_i), and the specific hand link names for each contact point. These templates serve as an initial, human-intuitive representation of a desired grasp type.

**2. Lightweight Global Alignment of Object Pose:**
This is the first optimization stage, designed for efficiency and parallelism. Instead of directly moving the hand to fit the object, this stage samples and optimizes the object's pose (scale, rotation, translation) to align with the *fixed* contact information defined in a selected grasp template.
- **Dense Sampling:** A random grasp template, a random hand contact from that template, and a random object with a random surface point are selected. The object is initialized by aligning the sampled hand and object contacts (points matched, normals opposite). Random scales and in-plane rotations are also sampled. This process is highly parallelized on a GPU, generating hundreds of thousands of initial samples.
- **Optimization:** The object pose is optimized by minimizing a simple energy function `L = k_p ||p_h_i - p_o_i||^2 + k_n ||n_h_i - n_o_i||^2`, which penalizes the mismatch between the hand's template contact points/normals (p_h_i, n_h_i) and the nearest points/normals on the object's surface (p_o_i, n_o_i). The differentiable library Warp is used for efficient nearest-point queries.
- **Post-filtering:** Results are filtered based on four criteria: a low final energy function value, avoidance of severe hand-object penetration (detected by collision skeletons), sufficient object contact quality (measured by Eq. 6), and removal of duplicate object transformations using a farthest point sampling-like approach. This stage outputs a small number of promising object poses.

**3. Simulation-based Local Refinement of Hand Pose:**
With the object pose now fixed and well-aligned, this stage locally refines the hand's pose to improve contact.
- **Virtual Force Application:** A virtual force `f_i = k_f (p_h_i - p_o_i)` is calculated for each hand contact point, pushing it towards its corresponding nearest object point.
- **Transposed Jacobian Control:** These virtual forces are translated into hand joint torques `τ = Σ J_h,i^T f_i` using a simplified transposed Jacobian control within MuJoCo. This method, despite its simplicity, is highly effective for achieving rich contacts without complex custom energy functions.
- **Penetration Avoidance:** Crucially, MuJoCo's built-in 1mm contact margin is leveraged to ensure strictly penetration-free hand poses by repelling the hand if it gets too close to the object. This results in contact distances typically within [0, 2mm].
- **Post-filtering:** Final hand poses are filtered to ensure no penetration (via collision meshes), that annotated fingers are indeed in contact with the object (to maintain template fidelity), and that the grasp quality (Eq. 6) meets a threshold.

**4. Simulation Validation with Contact-Aware Control:**
To ensure physical plausibility and robustness, the synthesized grasps are validated in MuJoCo.
- **Contact-Aware Control Strategy:** Unlike heuristic squeezing methods, Dexonomy employs a principled approach. It calculates desired contact forces for each point using the grasp quality metric (Eq. 3 with g=0, minimizing overall wrench error) and converts these into joint torques via transposed Jacobian control.
- **Stability Test:** A grasp is deemed successful if the object remains stable under six orthogonal external forces (e.g., gravity directions) for 2 seconds in simulation, with thresholds for object movement (5cm translation, 15° rotation). The object mass is set at 100g.

**5. Construction of New Grasp Templates:**
Successful grasps from the validation stage are used to enrich the Grasp Template Library. The joint configuration of the new template comes directly from the successful grasp, and its contact information is updated only if actual contacts are detected near the original contact points on the same hand link. This self-improvement mechanism iteratively broadens the range of graspable objects and enhances the robustness of the synthesis process.

The overall methodology combines parallelized global sampling and optimization with physics-based local refinement and rigorous validation, enabling efficient generation of a vast, high-quality dataset of type-aware dexterous grasps. The "Preliminaries of Grasp Quality Metric" section defines a unified force closure metric (Eq. 6), which is used in post-filtering and validation, providing a consistent standard for grasp quality assessment throughout the pipeline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive set of experimental results validating the efficacy and superiority of the Dexonomy pipeline.

**Dexonomy Dataset Construction:**
- Using the proposed pipeline, the authors constructed a large-scale dataset featuring **9.5 million grasps** across **10,700 distinct object assets**. These objects include 5,697 from DexGraspNet and 5,000 new, diverse objects from Objaverse.
- The dataset covers **31 distinct grasp types** from the GRASP taxonomy (excluding two task-specific types).
- The entire dataset was synthesized in less than **3 days** using a server equipped with 8 NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs, demonstrating high efficiency.
- Each data point includes three critical poses: **grasp pose**, **pre-grasp pose** (for collision-free motion planning), and **squeeze pose** (for applying force), providing a complete set for robotic manipulation.

**Type-Unaware Grasp Synthesis (Allegro Hand):**
- **Comparison with Baselines (Table II):** Dexonomy significantly outperforms four open-source baselines (DexGraspNet, FRoGGeR, SpringGrasp, BODex) in terms of grasp quality metrics for fingertip grasps.
 - It achieved the highest **Grasp Success Rate (GSR) of 60.50%**, substantially higher than the next best, BODex (49.23%).
 - It demonstrated superior contact richness (highest **CLN of 4.38**), consistency (lowest **CDC of 0.21 mm**), and **zero penetration depth (PD and SPD of 0.00 mm)**, indicating highly stable and physically plausible grasps.
 - While its speed (323.4 s⁻¹) was slightly lower than BODex (403.9 s⁻¹), this was attributed to MuJoCo's CPU-based local refinement.
- **Harder Benchmark (Table III):** When tested under more challenging conditions (lower friction coefficients and more diverse objects from Objaverse), Dexonomy's advantage became even more pronounced.
 - It significantly outperformed BODex, achieving an **Object Success Rate (OSR) exceeding 94%** for Objaverse objects (vs. 73.53% for BODex), showcasing its stronger generalizability to complex, in-the-wild objects. Its GSR also improved with more attempts due to the template-adding strategy, while BODex's performance remained static.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 5):** Visualizations confirmed that Dexonomy consistently synthesizes human-like and stable grasps, even for complex geometries and objects requiring large adjustments in hand joint angles, where baselines often failed or produced unnatural poses.

**Type-Aware Grasp Synthesis:**
- **Comparison with Functional Grasp Transfer Baselines (Figure 6):** Lacking available code for direct quantitative comparison, a qualitative assessment against figures from papers like LHFG and CCFG showed Dexonomy's grasps to be superior. They involved more contact points (e.g., ~10 links for scissors, ~7 for mugs) and exhibited no penetration, in contrast to the fingertip-dominant or penetrating grasps from baselines.
- **Statistics for GRASP Taxonomy (Table IV):** The success rates for broader grasp types (power, intermediate, precision) were generally lower than fingertip grasps due to the specificity of certain types to object shapes. Power grasps demonstrated higher stability under "hard" test conditions (reduced friction) due to their rich contacts, while precision grasps showed the highest success rate under "normal" conditions. The overall diversity of grasps (D) was improved over previous work.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Modular Ablation (Table V):**
 - The **post-filtering step in the global alignment stage** was found to be more critical than its optimization, effectively discarding suboptimal samples.
 - The **optimization in the local refinement stage** was crucial for improving grasp quality, while its post-filtering was essential for ensuring penetration-free grasps.
 - The **construction of new grasp templates** significantly enhanced the synthesis success rate (from 41.5% to 60.5% GSR), demonstrating the self-improving nature of the pipeline.
- **Control Strategy Ablation (Table VI):** The proposed **contact-aware control strategy** consistently improved grasp success rates during simulation validation compared to simpler scaling or fixed squeeze approaches. Power grasps were less sensitive, likely due to their inherent robustness from rich contacts.
- **Robustness to Initial Templates (Table VII, Figure 7):** The pipeline proved robust to noisy human-annotated templates and variations in initial hand joint angles, primarily due to the effectiveness of the template-adding strategy, which generates high-quality templates from successful grasps.

**Learning-based Grasp Synthesis from Partial Observation:**
- **Simulation Performance (Table VIII):** A type-conditional generative model trained on the Dexonomy dataset significantly outperformed the BODex baseline by ~10% (GSR 63.9% vs. 54.0%) when generating grasps from single-view point clouds in simulation. This highlights the value of the comprehensive and type-aware Dexonomy dataset.

**Real-World Experiment (Shadow Hand):**
- **Success Rate (Figure 9):** The trained type-conditional generative model achieved an impressive **82.3% success rate** in real-world experiments with 13 unseen objects and selected grasp types.
- **Qualitative Performance:** The model successfully generated physically plausible and desired grasp types. Failures were primarily attributed to unsuitable grasp types chosen for particular objects or the inherent fragility of some grasp types (e.g., Tip Pinch) to real-world noise.
- **Hardware and Control Considerations:** The authors noted a sim-to-real misalignment in the Shadow hand's under-actuation and challenges with motion planning using CuRobo, suggesting areas for future improvement.

**Application: Annotation UI:**
- The algorithm forms the basis for an **efficient annotation UI (Figure 10)**, allowing users to collect semantic dexterous grasp data with minimal input (two mouse clicks to specify contact and grasp type). This tool can significantly reduce human annotation effort compared to teleoperation methods.

In summary, Dexonomy delivers on its objectives, providing a robust, efficient, and generalizable method for synthesizing high-quality, type-aware dexterous grasps, culminating in a large-scale dataset and a demonstrably capable real-world robotic system.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Dexonomy" project represents a significant stride forward in the field of robotic manipulation, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling "True Dexterous Grasping":** By focusing on synthesizing *all dexterous grasp types* from the GRASP taxonomy, this work directly addresses the critical need for robots to perform functionally appropriate and human-like grasping. It elevates the discussion from mere successful object acquisition to intelligent, task-oriented interaction, unlocking the full potential of multi-fingered robot hands.

2. **Addressing the Data Scarcity Bottleneck:** The primary impact lies in its ability to efficiently generate **large-scale, high-quality, and type-aware datasets** for dexterous grasping. Data collection is arguably the biggest challenge for learning-based robot manipulation. Dexonomy provides a scalable, automated pipeline that largely overcomes this bottleneck, significantly accelerating research in data-driven dexterous grasping, policy learning, and generative models. The release of the **9.5 million-grasp Dexonomy dataset** itself is a major contribution to the robotics community.

3. **Generalizability and Scalability:** The pipeline's ability to synthesize grasps for *any grasp type, object, and articulated hand* from a single human-annotated template is a testament to its exceptional generalizability. This contrasts sharply with prior methods limited by object geometry or grasp type specificity, paving the way for more universally applicable robotic grasping solutions.

4. **Advancing Methodological Prowess:**
 - The **two-stage optimization strategy** (object-centric global alignment followed by hand-centric local refinement) is a novel and highly efficient approach to tackle the complex, high-dimensional problem of dexterous grasp synthesis, effectively avoiding local minima.
 - The integration of **differentiable physics simulation** (Warp, MuJoCo) for both optimization and rigorous validation ensures physical plausibility, contact richness, and penetration-free grasps, crucial for real-world reliability.
 - The **contact-aware control strategy** for simulation validation provides a robust and principled method for assessing grasp stability, moving beyond ad-hoc heuristics.
 - The **self-improving template library** mechanism, where successful grasps contribute to new templates, enhances the robustness and adaptability of the synthesis process over time.

5. **Direct Real-World Application:** The successful deployment of a type-conditional generative model trained on the Dexonomy dataset, achieving an **82.3% success rate in real-world experiments**, demonstrates the practical utility and robustness of the generated data and the learning approach. This offers a tangible pathway for robots to perform intelligent, type-specific grasping in unstructured environments.

6. **Facilitating Semantic Grasping Research:** The proposed **annotation UI**, leveraging the Dexonomy algorithm, significantly simplifies the process of collecting semantic grasp data. This tool can democratize access to high-quality annotated data, fostering advancements in understanding human-object interaction, task-oriented grasping, and bridging the gap between high-level human commands and low-level robot actions.

In conclusion, "Dexonomy" not only pushes the boundaries of dexterous grasp synthesis by providing a comprehensive and efficient solution but also lays foundational groundwork for future research in generalizable, intelligent robot manipulation. Its impact will likely be felt across robotic perception, planning, control, and learning, enabling a new generation of robots capable of more dexterous and human-like interactions with the world.
