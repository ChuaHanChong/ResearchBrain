# ManiSoft: Towards Vision-Language Manipulation for Soft Continuum Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiSoft, a new benchmark for vision-language manipulation using soft continuum robots, provides a specialized simulator, language-conditioned tasks, and expert demonstrations. Initial evaluations on ManiSoft show that current VLM models achieve approximately 30% success rates, struggling with unique challenges such as visual proprioceptive estimation and leveraging soft robot compliance.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language manipulation (VLM) research and benchmarks primarily focus on rigid robotic arms, which are limited in environments requiring significant shape adaptation.
- Soft robotic arms offer continuous deformability but introduce challenges like unreliable proprioception and complex, high-dimensional action spaces.
- A dedicated benchmark combining VLM with soft continuum robots was absent, hindering systematic evaluation and progress in this specific domain.

## Method

- **ManiSoft** introduces a hybrid simulator combining Cosserat rod dynamics for soft body deformation with MuJoCo for end-effector interaction and Blender for visual rendering.
- It defines four language-conditioned manipulation tasks (Collecting, Alignment, Stacking, Arrangement) across clean and randomized scenes to test various aspects of soft robot control.
- A hierarchical pipeline generates expert demonstration trajectories using a rule-based high-level planner and an RL-trained low-level executor to provide data for learning-based policies.

## Results

- Benchmarked policy models (Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA-OFT) achieved average success rates around **30%** (**31.6%** and **30.4%** respectively), demonstrating the difficulty of soft robot VLM tasks.
- All models experienced significant performance degradation in randomized, cluttered scenes, with OpenVLA-OFT showing stronger generalization by outperforming Diffusion Policy by **13.1%** in these conditions.
- Primary failure modes identified include visual proprioceptive state ambiguity leading to unstable motion, limited exploitation of soft arm compliance for obstacle avoidance, and a "stop-moving" behavior observed in OpenVLA-OFT after successful grasping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM policies for soft robots must infer complex arm configurations and internal states primarily from visual observations due to the lack of precise proprioception.
> - The distributed, low-level actuation of soft arms leads to a high-dimensional, coupled action space, complicating the generation of stable and coordinated behaviors.
> - Effective soft robot manipulation requires policies to not only perform actions but also strategically exploit the arm's deformability for tasks like obstacle avoidance and grasping delicate objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current landscape of vision-language manipulation (VLM) research primarily focuses on rigid robotic arms. Existing benchmarks such as RLBench, ManiSkill, CALVIN, LIBERO, RoboVerse, and RoboTwin predominantly feature rigid arm embodiments. Similarly, advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like RT-1, RT-2, DexVLA, RDT-1B, OpenVLA, and the π series have been primarily developed and evaluated within the context of rigid arm manipulation.

While rigid arms offer advantages in terms of accurate proprioception and low-dimensional kinematics, their fixed morphology imposes fundamental limitations in environments requiring significant shape adaptation, such as cluttered or confined spaces. In these scenarios, rigid arms may be unable to reach targets or interact effectively due to their inherent joint constraints.

Soft robotic arms, constructed from elastic materials, represent an alternative paradigm. Their continuous deformability allows them to adapt their geometry, enabling the execution of manipulation policies that are infeasible for rigid counterparts. However, incorporating soft arms into VLM presents distinct challenges. Soft arms often lack the accurate proprioception common in rigid arms, leading to complex kinematic control. Their distributed actuation, typically relying on low-level commands like pressures, tendon tensions, or torques, results in a higher-dimensional and more coupled action space compared to the intuitive kinematic targets of rigid arms. These factors complicate the generation of stable and coordinated behaviors.

This work addresses a significant gap in the current research by focusing on vision-language manipulation for deformable robotic embodiments. While low-level control of soft arms has seen advancements, high-level vision-language reasoning combined with the unique dynamics of soft arms remains relatively underexplored. ManiSoft aims to fill this void by providing a standardized benchmark that facilitates the systematic investigation and comparison of methods specifically designed for VLM with soft continuum robots. It is designed to expose and encourage solutions to challenges unique to deformable robotics, such as interpreting visual observations for complex soft body states and developing strategies that leverage deformability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce ManiSoft, a benchmark specifically designed to catalyze vision-language manipulation research for soft robotic arms. To achieve this, ManiSoft aims to provide several core components:
1. **A simulation and rendering stack** tailored for soft-arm manipulation.
2. **A suite of language-conditioned tasks** presented within diverse scenes.
3. **A collection of expert demonstration trajectories** to support the development and training of imitation learning and offline reinforcement learning policies.

The motivation for developing ManiSoft stems from several identified limitations and challenges within the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Limitations of Rigid Robot Paradigms:** The majority of existing VLM research and benchmarks focus on rigid robotic arms. While effective for many tasks, the fixed morphology of rigid arms inherently limits their adaptability in complex, cluttered, or confined environments. Scenarios requiring significant shape adaptation, such as reaching around obstacles or navigating tight spaces, often lead to failure for rigid systems.
- **Untapped Potential of Soft Robots:** Soft robotic arms offer an appealing alternative due to their continuous deformability. This characteristic enables them to conform to their environment, grasp delicate objects, and access spaces inaccessible to rigid robots, thereby expanding the capabilities of robotic manipulation.
- **Specific Challenges of Soft Robotics in VLM:** Integrating soft arms into VLM frameworks introduces substantial technical hurdles:
 - **Unreliable Proprioception:** Unlike rigid arms with precise joint sensors, soft arms often lack accurate internal state sensing, making it difficult to ascertain their exact configuration and deformation from conventional means. Policy models must infer these states primarily from visual observations.
 - **Distributed Low-Level Actuation:** Soft arms are typically actuated by low-level commands (e.g., torques, pressures) rather than direct kinematic targets (e.g., joint angles). This distributed actuation results in a higher-dimensional and more coupled action space, which complicates the generation of stable and coordinated manipulation behaviors.
 - **Absence of Dedicated Benchmarks:** Despite advancements in both VLM and soft robotics, a comprehensive benchmark specifically combining these two domains was lacking. This void hindered systematic evaluation, comparison, and acceleration of research into vision-language-conditioned control for soft deformable robots.
- **Need for a Diagnostic Testbed:** ManiSoft is motivated by the need for a standardized platform to evaluate existing policy models on soft arms, identify novel failure modes unique to deformable embodiments, and guide the development of new methods that can address challenges such as accurate visual estimation of proprioceptive states and effective exploitation of deformability for adaptive obstacle avoidance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ManiSoft is designed as a comprehensive benchmark for vision-language manipulation with soft robotic arms, incorporating a specialized simulator, a diverse set of tasks, and an automated data generation pipeline.

**Simulator Design:**
The development of a robust simulator was a central technical challenge. Traditional soft-body simulators often lack detailed environmental interaction capabilities, while rigid-body simulators do not support continuous deformation. To overcome this, ManiSoft employs a hybrid simulator approach:
- **Soft Body Dynamics:** The soft arm is modeled as a Cosserat rod, a common approach for slender deformable bodies. It is discretized into multiple segments, and its dynamics, including axial, shear, bending, and torsional strains, are simulated using Elastica. External actuation torques (τ_e) generate internal forces (f_i) and moments (τ_i) that govern the arm's deformation.
- **End-Effector (EEF) Interaction:** The end-effector and its interactions with rigid environmental elements (e.g., objects, table) are handled by MuJoCo, a physics engine known for efficient and stable simulation of contact-rich scenarios.
- **Coupling Mechanism:** The soft body and the end-effector are coupled via an elastic force constraint. This constraint is implemented as a virtual spring, stretchable and twistable with zero rest length, connecting the two components. Hooke's law is applied to generate restoring forces (F = -k_F ∆x) and torques (M = -k_M ∆θ) based on relative translations (∆x) and rotations (∆θ) between the attachment points. This mechanism ensures coordinated yet compliant motion between the soft arm and its end-effector.
- **Visual Rendering:** Blender is utilized to render visual observations. Based on the simulated states, Blender generates RGB images of the tabletop scenes from fixed camera viewpoints, including the soft arm, target objects, and any obstacles.

**Task Definition:**
ManiSoft defines four distinct manipulation tasks, each designed to highlight different aspects of deformable control and VLM challenges:
- **Collecting (COLL):** The robot is instructed to grasp a specified object and place it into a container. This task evaluates fundamental trajectory control and basic end-effector coordination.
- **Alignment (ALN):** The objective is to precisely position a target object to a specified 6-DoF pose, testing capabilities for fine-grained orientation and placement.
- **Stacking (STK):** This task requires assembling tableware items, from largest to smallest, into a stable vertical pile. It assesses precision control during elevated and contact-rich interactions.
- **Arrangement (ARR):** The robot must place multiple objects according to a given spatial configuration, demanding integrated visual perception, spatial reasoning, and obstacle avoidance.
For all tasks, policy models receive a language instruction (L) and visual observations (V_t) and output actions (A_t), which consist of external torques (τ_e) and the end-effector state (S, indicating grasp/release). Crucially, internal soft-body states are deliberately excluded from policy inputs, forcing models to infer arm configuration and deformation solely from visual data. Performance is evaluated based on success rate and efficiency (number of steps).

**Scene Generation:**
An automated pipeline generates diverse tabletop scenes:
- **Object Library:** An asset library derived from RoboTwin-OD provides 263 3D objects, including 109 manipulable objects across 17 categories and 154 obstacles across 35 categories. Each manipulable object has pre-annotated 6-DoF end-effector poses for interaction.
- **Difficulty Levels:** Scenes are generated in two settings:
 - **Clean:** Contains only task-relevant objects in fixed layouts and appearances.
 - **Randomized:** Introduces additional irrelevant objects as obstacles, varies object placements, and applies scene randomization (diverse textures, lighting intensity, brightness). This setting evaluates generalization under increased visual and physical complexity.
- **Language Instruction Generation:** Instructions are generated from a curated template library, instantiated with object attributes (e.g., color, shape). In randomized scenes, multiple attribute-aware descriptions are used to enhance linguistic richness.

**Trajectory Generation:**
Expert trajectories are generated using a hierarchical mechanism to ensure scalability and quality:
- **High-Level Planner:** A task-specific rule-based planner generates a sequence of waypoints, each defining a desired 6-DoF end-effector pose. These waypoints guide the soft arm through semantic intermediate configurations (e.g., approach, grasp, retract).
- **Low-Level Controller (RL-trained Executor):** An Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy, trained using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), serves as the executor. It receives the target end-effector pose, proprioceptive states of arm segments, and the current end-effector pose, then outputs torque commands to drive the soft arm to each waypoint.
- **Reward Function for Executor:** The executor is trained with a dense reward function that includes:
 - **Pose Difference Reward (R_d):** Penalizes the pose discrepancy between the current and target end-effector poses, calculated using the SE(3) logarithm map. It includes bonuses for achieving small pose differences.
 - **Stability Reward (R_s):** Provides a signal based on the rate of change of the pose difference when the end-effector is close to the target. This term encourages smooth and stable convergence, penalizing oscillations.
- **Data Statistics:** The pipeline generates 6,300 scene-trajectory pairs (2,100 clean, 4,200 randomized), with an average of 40 language instructions per scene. Trajectories are long (average 1,272 steps) due to the precision required for torque-based control. Object distribution is diverse, and grasp positions are widely distributed across the workspace.

**Benchmarked Models:**
Three representative policy models were benchmarked on ManiSoft: Diffusion Policy (DP), RDT, and OpenVLA-OFT. DP and RDT were trained from scratch, while OpenVLA-OFT was fine-tuned using LoRA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the three policy models on the ManiSoft benchmark revealed distinct performance characteristics and identified specific failure modes associated with soft robotic manipulation.

**Overall Performance:**
- Diffusion Policy (DP) and OpenVLA-OFT achieved comparable average success rates (31.6% and 30.4% respectively), requiring average execution lengths of 520 and 527 steps.
- RDT performed significantly worse, with an average success rate of 9.2% over 496 steps. This discrepancy is attributed to RDT's larger parameter count (1B vs. approximately 400M for DP and OpenVLA-OFT), which potentially leads to overfitting and reduced generalization on the test set.
- The COLL task consistently yielded the highest success rates across all models, suggesting it is comparatively less challenging, likely due to lower demands for precise orientation perception and spatial reasoning.

**Performance in Clean Settings:**
- DP demonstrated superior performance on the COLL task (63.0% success rate), outperforming OpenVLA-OFT by 17.6%.
- However, OpenVLA-OFT surpassed DP on the remaining tasks: ALN (25.0% vs. 18.3%), STK (20.0% vs. 15.0%), and ARR (31.3% vs. 30.0%). This suggests that OpenVLA-OFT, likely benefiting from its pretrained weights, exhibits stronger reasoning and generalization capabilities in more complex, multi-step scenarios.

**Performance in Randomized Settings:**
- All models experienced a substantial decline in success rates under randomized conditions, confirming the increased task difficulty introduced by obstacles, varied object placements, and diverse visual attributes.
- DP showed the largest performance drop, with its success rate decreasing by 29.4%.
- RDT and OpenVLA-OFT experienced more moderate declines of 7.6% and 3.4%, respectively.
- In the randomized setting, OpenVLA-OFT consistently outperformed DP, achieving an average improvement of 13.1%. This indicates that OpenVLA-OFT maintains stronger generalization performance when confronted with environmental and instruction-level variations.

**Object Category Performance (ARR task):**
- The Rubik's Cube was consistently the easiest object to manipulate, yielding the highest success rates. In the clean setting, its success rate was 5-30% higher than other categories across methods. In the randomized setting, OpenVLA-OFT achieved a 15.0% success rate, double that of other models.
- The shoe proved to be the most challenging object, with success rates 5-30% lower than other categories in the clean setting. Under randomization, success rates for the shoe dropped below 10% for all methods. These results indicate that object geometry significantly influences task difficulty, though the relative performance trends across models remained consistent.

**Identified Failure Modes:**
Through visualization and failure-case analysis, three primary failure modes were identified:
1. **Proprioceptive State Ambiguity:**
 - **Description:** Reliable torque control requires precise estimation of the soft arm's configuration and internal forces. Soft arm deformation induces internal torques that must be accurately compensated to allow the residual torque to drive the arm towards the target. Small errors in visual state estimation can overwhelm this delicate balance.
 - **Observation:** Policies often failed when large arm deformation was required (e.g., reaching near the arm base), leading to significant internal torques. The models struggled to compensate for these loads, resulting in unstable motion, lateral drift of the end-effector, and ultimately failure to reach the target.
2. **Limited Exploitation of Soft Arm Compliance:**
 - **Description:** Soft arms possess inherent flexibility and compliance, which should enable them to adapt their shape to navigate around obstacles. However, the evaluated policies frequently failed to leverage this key advantage.
 - **Observation:** Instead of deforming the arm to reach behind an obstacle, policies often attempted to move the arm directly towards the target, resulting in collisions. This suggests that the models did not effectively plan interaction strategies that utilize the soft arm's shape-adaptive capabilities. Potential solutions include incorporating more obstacle-specific expert data or physical priors during training.
3. **"Stop-Moving" Behavior (Specific to OpenVLA-OFT):**
 - **Description:** OpenVLA-OFT occasionally exhibited a behavior where, after successfully grasping an object, the robot would cease further movement, failing to initiate subsequent actions required to complete the task.
 - **Observation:** This behavior is hypothesized to stem from subtle visual changes occurring during the grasping phase, which may create a feedback loop that suppresses further action generation. This explains why OpenVLA-OFT had a lower success rate and longer execution lengths compared to DP on the simpler COLL task. This highlights a difference between stochastic (diffusion-based) and deterministic policies, where the former may be more capable of escaping such feedback loops.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManiSoft represents a contribution to the fields of robotics and artificial intelligence by addressing a currently underexplored area: vision-language manipulation with soft continuum robots.

**Contribution to Research:**
- **Bridging a Research Gap:** The benchmark directly addresses the limitations of rigid-robot-centric VLM research by providing a dedicated platform for soft arms, thereby expanding the scope of embodied AI.
- **Standardized Testbed:** ManiSoft offers a standardized environment, tasks, and data, enabling systematic evaluation and comparison of new policy models for soft robotic manipulation. This facilitates reproducible research and accelerates progress in this challenging domain.
- **Exposure of Unique Challenges:** By benchmarking existing models, the work has successfully exposed fundamental challenges specific to soft robotics in VLM, such as the difficulty of visually estimating proprioceptive states, the limited exploitation of deformability in planning, and certain policy-specific behaviors like "stop-moving." This clear articulation of bottlenecks can guide future research efforts.
- **Data Generation Efficiency:** The hierarchical trajectory generation pipeline provides a scalable method for producing high-quality expert demonstrations, which is crucial for training learning-based policies for complex soft robot control.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**
- **Safer and Human-Friendly Robotics:** Soft robots are inherently safer due to their compliance, making them suitable for direct human-robot interaction. By enabling better control and reasoning for soft arms, ManiSoft can support the development of robots that can operate safely alongside humans in service, assistive, or collaborative manufacturing roles.
- **Compliant Interaction in Complex Environments:** The benchmark promotes research into robots that can effectively navigate and interact with cluttered, confined, or unstructured environments, leveraging their deformability to avoid obstacles and grasp delicate or irregularly shaped objects.
- **Assistive and Medical Robotics:** The enhanced capabilities in soft robotic manipulation could have significant implications for assistive technologies (e.g., for individuals with disabilities) and medical applications (e.g., minimally invasive surgery, rehabilitation), where precise and compliant interaction is paramount.
- **More Adaptable Robotic Technologies:** By pushing the boundaries of VLM to soft embodiments, this work contributes towards developing more versatile and adaptable robotic systems capable of performing a wider range of tasks in diverse real-world settings.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**
The authors acknowledge that ManiSoft is an initial step. Current limitations include a focus on a subset of actuation mechanisms and relatively simple tabletop scenarios, with potential for expansion to more diverse actuation types, dynamic, and long-horizon tasks. Furthermore, opportunities exist to strengthen sim-to-real consistency and physical validation. These areas are identified for future improvements to enhance the benchmark's coverage, realism, and evaluation diversity.
