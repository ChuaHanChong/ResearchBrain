# VQActFlow: Vector-Quantized Action Mode Steering for Multi-Task Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VQActFlow introduces a robot manipulation policy that employs vector-quantized action tokens and Variational Flow Matching to explicitly maintain and steer a preference over discrete action modes during generation. This approach, incorporating Classifier-Free Guidance and a novel contrastively-trained codebook critic, enhances task success and generates smoother robot trajectories across various manipulation platforms.

## Problem

- Multi-task robot manipulation policies often struggle with ambiguous observations, making it difficult to definitively commit to one distinct action mode from multimodal demonstration data.
- Existing continuous action generation policies lack explicit representation of action modes, limiting the ability to directly steer towards a specific discrete behavior or resolve ambiguity.
- Prior action tokenization methods typically lose the explicit discrete action mode structure during the generation phase, hindering effective inference-time guidance.

## Method

- A two-stage framework using a Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) for action tokenization, followed by a ==Variational Flow Matching (VFM)== policy to generate discrete code sequences.
- The VFM policy is trained with categorical supervision over code indices, outputting logits for codebook entries at each integration step to maintain an explicit preference for discrete action modes.
- An inference-time guidance interface directly manipulates these categorical preferences, utilizing an adapted Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) for language conditioning and a novel contrastively-trained codebook critic for scene-conditioned feasibility.

## Results

- VQActFlow achieved an **80.5%** success rate on the LIBERO-90 benchmark with combined guidance, surpassing Continuous Flow Matching DiT (**77.2%**) and Discrete Policy (**60.3%**).
- On a Unitree G1 humanoid, Classifier-Free Guidance improved task success from **23.8%** to **57.5%** by reducing "wrong task" errors from **41.3%** to **2.5%**.
- Deployed on an ALOHA-style bimanual platform, VQActFlow with guidance achieved **77.5%** success, outperforming Discrete Policy, and generated significantly smoother trajectories with peak joint jerk of ~2x10^2 rad/s^3 compared to ~10^4 rad/s^3 for continuous flow matching baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly maintaining a preference over discrete action modes throughout the generation process provides a more effective and direct interface for inference-time guidance in multi-task robotics.
> - Integrating categorical supervision into Variational Flow Matching enables direct steering of action mode commitment, leading to improved task disambiguation and robustness.
> - The combination of Classifier-Free Guidance (for language-conditioned task selection) and a scene-conditioned, contrastively-trained codebook critic (for action feasibility) offers complementary signals that significantly enhance overall policy performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of multi-task robot manipulation, specifically focusing on visuomotor policy learning from demonstration data. The broader research landscape for robot policies is characterized by an increasing demand for models capable of handling multiple, qualitatively distinct manipulation tasks based on language instructions and visual observations.

A prevailing paradigm in visuomotor manipulation involves diffusion and flow matching policies. These methods, such as Diffusion Policy, DiffuseLoco, and π0, operate by generating actions in a continuous action or latent space. While effective, a challenge with these continuous approaches in a multi-task setting is that action modes—which can differ by target object, contact strategy, or coordination—are not explicitly represented. Consequently, inference-time guidance mechanisms like Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) typically reshape a single continuous distribution rather than directly selecting among discrete action modes.

Another lineage of work addresses action tokenization using vector-quantized codebooks. These approaches learn a finite vocabulary of action primitives, utilizing a discrete bottleneck to separate different modes at a representational level. Prior work such as Discrete Policy tokenizes actions using a VQ-VAE but then generates these codes by diffusion in a continuous embedding space. The discrete indices are only recovered at the end of the sampling process via nearest-neighbor lookup. This approach abandons the explicit discrete structure during the critical generation phase, which can limit the ability to directly steer mode commitment. Other methods like OpenVLA, VQ-BeT, FAST, and OAT also explore various forms of action tokenization.

Guidance techniques for generative policies, including classifier guidance, CFG, Diffuser, and SafeDiffuser, are standard tools for biasing generative models. However, in robotics applications, these typically steer continuous action distributions or trajectories.

VQActFlow positions itself at the intersection of these domains by integrating vector-quantized action tokenization with Variational Flow Matching (VFM). Crucially, it distinguishes itself by maintaining an *explicit preference over discrete action modes* throughout the entire generation process, rather than only tokenizing or generating in a continuous latent space. This explicit mode commitment provides a direct interface for inference-time guidance, which VQActFlow leverages with both an adapted CFG and a novel, contrastively-trained codebook critic. This distinct approach addresses the challenge of resolving ambiguous observations and ensuring commitment to the correct, feasible action mode in complex multi-task scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind VQActFlow arises from the challenges inherent in learning multi-task robot manipulation policies from demonstration data, particularly when such data is multimodal. The core problem is that a robot policy, given language and visual context, must select one specific action mode from a distribution that encompasses qualitatively different behaviors. These modes could represent distinct target objects, varying contact strategies, or different bimanual coordination patterns.

The paper identifies two main limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Implicit Mode Representation in Continuous Policies:** Current continuous action generation policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, Flow Matching) operate in continuous spaces where action modes are not explicitly represented. When an observation is ambiguous (e.g., multiple objects present, multiple valid ways to interact), a continuous policy's guidance mechanisms reshape a single continuous distribution. This makes it difficult to direct the policy to commit definitively to one discrete action mode over others. A "wrong mode selection" in this context is not a minor numerical error but results in the robot executing a coherent but incorrect task behavior, or performing a task in an infeasible manner, leading to task failure.

2. **Loss of Discrete Structure During Generation in Prior Tokenization Approaches:** While vector-quantized codebooks, offer a discrete bottleneck that can separate action modes at the representation level, existing methods like Discrete Policy generate these action tokens by diffusion in a *continuous* embedding space. The discrete indices are only recovered by nearest-neighbor lookup at the conclusion of the sampling process. This means that during the intermediate steps of generation, the policy does not maintain an explicit representation of which discrete action mode it is committing to. This lack of continuous mode commitment hinders the ability to steer the policy effectively when resolving ambiguities towards the instructed task.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of VQActFlow are:

- **Develop a multi-task manipulation policy that explicitly maintains a preference over discrete action modes throughout the entire generation process.** This ensures that the policy's commitment to a particular action mode is represented at every step, rather than being inferred or recovered post-hoc.
- **Generate action tokens via Variational Flow Matching (VFM) with categorical supervision over code indices.** This approach allows for a continuous transport process that produces logits over codes at each integration step, directly reflecting an explicit preference for action modes.
- **Design an inference-time guidance interface that directly acts on this explicit mode preference to steer the policy's commitment.** This allows for dynamic redirection of probability toward the correct action mode at deployment.
- **Instantiate this guidance interface with two complementary mechanisms:**
 - **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** To amplify the difference between task-conditioned and unconditional predictions, thereby steering the policy toward the language-instructed action mode.
 - **A learned codebook critic:** To provide a complementary feasibility signal, pushing the policy toward action modes that are feasible within the current scene. This critic is trained via contrastive learning against diverse negative examples.
- **Empirically evaluate VQActFlow across various robot manipulation platforms and benchmarks** (LIBERO simulation, Unitree G1 humanoid, ALOHA-style bimanual platform) to demonstrate its performance advantages over both continuous and discrete baselines.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more direct and transparent mechanism for action mode selection and steering in multi-task robotics, leading to more reliable and controllable robot behaviors in complex, ambiguous environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VQActFlow employs a two-stage framework: first, it tokenizes continuous actions into discrete code sequences using a Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE); second, it generates these code sequences via a Variational Flow Matching (VFM) policy, explicitly maintaining and steering a preference over discrete action modes during generation.

**A. VQ-VAE Action Tokenization**
The initial stage involves encoding continuous action chunks into discrete tokens.
- **Input:** An action chunk `a_1:H` comprising `H` timesteps and `d_a` action dimensions.
- **Encoder (E):** A temporal encoder maps `a_1:H` to a sequence of continuous latents `z ∈ R^(L x d_e)`, where `L` is a compressed sequence length (`H` divided by a temporal downsampling factor `2^S`), and `d_e` is the codebook embedding dimension.
- **Codebook (C):** A learned codebook `C = {e_k}_(K=1)^K` contains `K` discrete embedding vectors, `e_k ∈ R^(d_e)`.
- **Quantization:** Each latent `z^(l)` from the encoder is quantized to its nearest codebook entry `e_k*l`, producing a sequence of quantized embeddings `z_q`.
- **Decoder (D):** A temporal CNN decoder reconstructs the original action chunk `a_1:H` from `z_q`.
- **Training:** The VQ-VAE is trained jointly with a reconstruction loss and a commitment loss, with the codebook updated by exponential moving average. Upon training completion, the VQ-VAE is frozen and utilized to generate supervision targets for the VFM policy.

**B. Variational Flow Matching Policy**
This policy generates sequences of discrete action tokens, maintaining an explicit preference over the codebook entries.
- **Training:**
 - **Targets:** The frozen VQ-VAE encoder provides target embeddings `x_1 = z_q` and corresponding ground-truth indices `{k*l}_(l=1)^L`.
 - **Process:** Gaussian noise `x_0 ~ N(0, I)` and a timestep `t ~ U(0, 1)` are sampled. An optimal-transport interpolant `x_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1` is constructed.
 - **Policy Network (f_θ):** A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) processes `x_t` conditioned on `t`, an observation encoding `o`, and language conditioning `c`. It outputs logits `h^(l) ∈ R^K` for each position `l` in the sequence, representing a categorical distribution over the `K` codebook entries.
 - **Loss:** Training minimizes the cross-entropy between the predicted categorical posterior (derived from `h^(l)`) and the ground-truth codebook indices `k*l`. This trains the network to discriminate the correct action mode.
- **Inference:**
 - **Velocity Derivation:** At each integration step, the categorical output (logits `h`) is converted to probabilities `p_k^(l)`. The posterior mean `μ_1^(l)` in the embedding space is computed as a weighted sum of codebook embeddings, using `p_k^(l)` as weights. This `μ_1` serves as the predicted endpoint to compute the optimal transport (OT) velocity `v_θ(x_t, t) = (μ_1 - x_t) / (1-t)`.
 - **Integration:** Starting from `x_0 ~ N(0, I)`, the ODE `ẋ = v_θ(x, t)` is integrated from `t=0` to `t=1` using Euler steps.
 - **Decoding:** The terminal state `x_1` is quantized position-wise to yield code sequence `{k*l}_(l=1)^L`, which is then decoded by the VQ-VAE decoder `D` into continuous actions `a_1:H`.
- **Architecture:** The policy network `f_θ` is a DiT with cross-attention for visual (ResNet-18 encoder) and text (frozen CLIP-B/32 encoder) conditioning, and adaptive layer normalization for the timestep embedding.

**C. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) for Language Conditioning**
CFG is applied directly to the policy's categorical logits to steer it towards the language instruction.
- **Mechanism:** During training, language conditioning `c` is replaced by a null embedding `∅` with a certain probability `p_drop`, enabling the network to learn both conditional (`h_c`) and unconditional (`h_∅`) logits.
- **Inference:** At each integration step, the logits are linearly extrapolated: `h̃ = h_∅ + w * (h_c - h_∅)`, where `w` is the guidance weight. Guided probabilities `p̃_k^(l)` are then computed from `h̃` and used in Eq. 7 for velocity calculation. This biases the policy's mode preference towards codes consistent with the language.

**D. Codebook Critic for Scene-Conditioned Feasibility**
A learned critic provides a feasibility signal, biasing the policy's mode preference towards actions feasible in the current scene.
- **Guided Velocity:** During ODE integration, the critic's gradient adjusts the policy's probability distribution. The guided distribution `p̃` is obtained by projecting `p + λ(t) * Π_T[∇_p C_ψ(o, c, p, t)]` onto the probability simplex, where `Π_T` ensures the gradient is tangent to the simplex.
- **Guidance Weight (λ(t)):** The weight `λ(t)` adapts to the entropy of the current distribution `p`, strengthening as `p` becomes more concentrated over integration. This prevents pushing towards arbitrary codes early in generation.
- **Critic Architecture:** `C_ψ(o, c, p, t)` is a transformer encoder. It takes a visual observation `o`, language conditioning `c`, the sequence of `L` simplex positions `p ∈ R^(L x K)`, and flow timestep `t`. `o` and `c` are fused into a conditioning token. Each `p^(l)` is linearly projected, combined with positional and sinusoidal timestep embeddings, processed by a transformer encoder, and finally mapped to a scalar feasibility score by an MLP head.
- **Critic Training:** Trained using an InfoNCE loss. It contrasts positive `(o, c, p)` triples against diverse negative examples designed to violate various aspects of trajectory validity: temporal coherence (shuffled codes), code distribution (random replacement), observation grounding (mismatched observation), language grounding (mismatched language), cross-demonstration consistency, and in-episode temporal shifts. These negatives ensure the critic learns a broad feasibility signal. Samples are noised to random flow timesteps to expose the critic to varying distributional sharpness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VQActFlow was evaluated across LIBERO simulation benchmarks and on two hardware platforms, demonstrating improvements over both continuous and discrete baselines.

**A. CFG Evaluation on LIBERO-Goal**
On the LIBERO-Goal benchmark, VQActFlow’s performance was assessed with varying Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) weights (`w`).
- **Guidance Effectiveness:** VQActFlow achieved an 81.0% success rate at `w=4`, improving from 73.0% without guidance. Performance degraded monotonically at higher `w`, reaching 63.0% at `w=8`. This indicates an optimal guidance strength.
- **Comparison to Baseline:** The Discrete Policy baseline, under matched conditions, peaked at a lower 61.5% success rate with `w=6`, suggesting VQActFlow requires less aggressive guidance to achieve higher performance. VQActFlow also showed tighter standard deviation bands, indicating more consistent gains across tasks.
- **Failure Modes:** Analysis of rollouts revealed a dichotomy: at low `w`, failures were often due to executing coherent actions on the wrong object (insufficient task disambiguation). At high `w`, failures involved reaching the correct object but missing the grasp (degraded execution precision). The optimal `w` thus represented a trade-off.

**B. Multi-task Scaling on LIBERO-90**
Evaluation on LIBERO-90, a more extensive benchmark with 90 multi-task scenarios, assessed the scalability and the contribution of the codebook critic.
- **Overall Performance:** VQActFlow with CFG (`w=2`) and critic guidance (`λ=1.0`) achieved an 80.5% success rate.
- **Baseline Comparison:** This performance surpassed Continuous Flow Matching DiT (CFM) at 77.2% and MT-ACT at 72.4%. Among VQ-based baselines, VQActFlow significantly outperformed VQ-BeT (24.1%) and Discrete Policy (60.3% with `w=2`), demonstrating a 17.3% margin over Discrete Policy.
- **Complementary Guidance:** Critic guidance alone contributed a +2.0% improvement over unguided VQActFlow (`w=1, λ=0`). When combined with CFG (`w=2`), the critic added a further +2.9% improvement. This suggests that CFG, which sharpens task identity, and the critic, which injects a broader feasibility signal, provide non-redundant contributions.
- **Codebook Size Ablation:** A codebook size of K=512 yielded optimal performance (72.3%) without guidance, indicating a balance between vocabulary granularity and the difficulty of categorical prediction.

**C. Humanoid Whole-Body Manipulation (Unitree G1)**
VQActFlow was deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid for four pick-and-place tasks.
- **Impact of CFG:** CFG significantly increased aggregate success from 23.8% (unguided, `w=1`) to 57.5% (`w=6`).
- **Error Type Correction:** The improvement was primarily driven by a reduction in "wrong task" errors, which decreased from 41.3% to 2.5%. The rate of "missed grasp" errors remained approximately consistent. This finding supports the role of CFG in improving task disambiguation, aligning with observations from LIBERO-Goal.

**D. Bimanual Manipulation**
The policy was evaluated on an ALOHA-style bimanual platform for four contact-rich tasks.
- **Performance vs. Discrete Policy:** VQActFlow with CFG (`w=4`) achieved 73.8% success, outperforming Discrete Policy at 61.3% under matched guidance weight. Without guidance, both policies had similar success rates, highlighting the differential impact of CFG on VQActFlow.
- **Critic Contribution:** Critic guidance, independently (λ=1.0, w=1.0), improved VQActFlow's success from 45.0% to 60.0%, with notable gains in precision-demanding tasks like peg-in-hole. The combination of CFG (`w=4`) and critic guidance (`λ=1.0`) yielded the highest success rate at 77.5%.
- **Inference Latency:** The full configuration (CFG + critic) incurred an average inference latency of 350.2 ms per action chunk, which is well within the 1.28 s duration of each action chunk, allowing for asynchronous inference and smooth execution.
- **Motion Smoothness:** A key finding was the significantly smoother trajectories generated by VQActFlow (peak joint jerk ~2x10^2 rad/s^3) compared to a Continuous Flow Matching (CFM) baseline (~10^4 rad/s^3). The CFM baseline failed all tasks due to severe high-frequency oscillations. This smoothness in VQActFlow is attributed to the VQ-VAE's temporal compression and learned compositional vocabulary, which inherently encodes trajectory smoothness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in VQActFlow demonstrates a significant advance in multi-task robot manipulation, particularly in how robot policies commit to and are steered toward specific action modes.

**Core Contributions:**
The central significance lies in the architectural choice to maintain an *explicit preference over discrete action modes* throughout the action generation process, rather than operating solely in continuous embedding spaces or recovering discrete modes only at the final step. This explicit representation provides a more effective and direct interface for inference-time guidance.

**Enhanced Guidance Efficacy:**
- **Superior CFG Performance:** The research empirically demonstrates that Classifier-Free Guidance, when applied to categorical preferences within VQActFlow, yields larger performance gains, operates optimally at lower guidance weights, and achieves higher success rates compared to its application in continuous-embedding alternatives (e.g., Discrete Policy). This suggests a more efficient and precise control over task selection.
- **Complementary Feasibility Signal:** The introduction of a novel, contrastively-trained codebook critic provides a distinct feasibility signal that complements the task-specific guidance from CFG. This critic, trained to identify violations of temporal coherence, observation grounding, and language grounding, enhances the policy's ability to select actions that are not only aligned with instructions but also feasible within the current scene. The cumulative effect of these two guidance mechanisms, operating through a unified categorical interface, leads to improved robustness.

**Improved Performance and Robustness in Diverse Environments:**
- **Benchmark Advancement:** VQActFlow achieved leading success rates across a range of benchmarks, including the LIBERO-Goal and LIBERO-90 simulation suites.
- **Hardware Deployment:** The successful deployment on distinct hardware platforms—a Unitree G1 humanoid and an ALOHA-style bimanual system—for complex, contact-rich tasks underscores its practical viability.
- **Trajectory Smoothness:** A notable impact on hardware is VQActFlow's ability to generate significantly smoother trajectories compared to continuous flow matching policies. This is attributed to the VQ-VAE's learned compositional vocabulary encoding, which inherently promotes trajectory smoothness, a critical factor for stable and safe robot operation, particularly in contact-rich scenarios. This robustness addresses a common failure mode in continuous action generation.

**Potential Impact:**
The methodology presented in VQActFlow has several potential impacts:
- **Enhanced Reliability:** The explicit mode commitment and multi-faceted guidance could lead to more reliable robot behavior in ambiguous environments, reducing errors like "wrong task" execution.
- **Increased Control and Interpretability:** By steering discrete action modes, it may offer a more intuitive and potentially interpretable control mechanism for robot policies compared to manipulating continuous latent spaces. This could be valuable for debugging and understanding policy decisions.
- **Foundation for Generalist Robots:** The ability to effectively steer multi-task policies could contribute to the development of more general-purpose robots capable of performing a wide array of complex manipulation tasks with greater precision and autonomy.
- **Integration with Large Language Models (LLMs):** The discrete, tokenized action space aligns well with the token vocabularies of large language models. This creates a natural interface for integrating VQActFlow as an action head for future Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, potentially enabling more sophisticated language-conditioned robotic control.

While the VQ-VAE bottleneck might impose an inherent information loss, future work could explore more expressive action tokenization schemes to further relax this constraint, while preserving the advantages of the categorical interface. This research establishes a strong foundation for future advancements in explicit action mode control for multi-task robot manipulation.
