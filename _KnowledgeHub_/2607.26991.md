# RL$^2$-VLA: Adaptive RL Latent Compositional Steering with Test-Time Scaling for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RL^2-VLA introduces an adaptive inference-time steering framework that augments Vision-Language-Action models with a reinforcement learning-based latent policy, selectively diversifying actions in predicted failure states to enhance performance in challenging and out-of-domain manipulation tasks, achieving up to a 17.5% improvement in real-world settings.

## Problem

- Pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit limited robustness and generalization when faced with challenging or out-of-domain (OOD) tasks.
- Existing inference-time steering and test-time scaling methods for VLAs often generate action samples that lack diversity, leading to correlated failure modes.
- Current intervention strategies are typically fixed and non-adaptive, applying steering indiscriminately regardless of whether the base VLA is likely to succeed or fail.

## Method

- A lightweight, offline reinforcement learning (RL) ==flow-matching== policy (**π_RL**) is trained on expressive VLA latents to generate a differentiable guidance function.
- During VLA inference, a ==compositional steering== mechanism combines the velocity fields of the base VLA and the trained RL policy, allowing for diverse action generation.
- An adaptive failure detection module, using ==SAFE== and ==Conformal Prediction==, triggers compositional steering only when the VLA is predicted to be in a failure state.

## Results

- Adaptive RL^2 enhanced average success rates in simulated OOD tasks: **+7.5%** over baseline with OpenVLA on in-domain prompts, **+10.1%** for OOD language prompts, and **+10.9%** for OOD task environments.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of adaptiveness, with adaptive steering yielding an average **+8.9%** gain over non-adaptive approaches, and RL training surpassing Behavior Cloning by up to **+4.5%**.
- Real-world experiments on a PiperX manipulator demonstrated an average **+17.5%** performance gain over the Rephrase baseline in four OOD tasks, validating the framework's practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An analysis of test-time scaling laws revealed that RL-induced action diversity significantly reduces error in failure states but can be detrimental in success states, necessitating adaptive intervention.
> - Conditioning the RL steering policy on VLA's internal latent features is crucial for effective steering, outperforming conditioning on raw observations.
> - Training the steering policy with RL (e.g., QAM) introduces more beneficial diversity than Behavior Cloning, moving beyond mere demonstration replication.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in general-purpose robotics, leveraging large-scale robot demonstrations and priors from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to achieve visuomotor capabilities. Research efforts have historically focused on improving VLAs during pretraining, through methods such as data scaling and architectural design, and during post-training, via techniques like preference alignment and chain-of-thought reasoning.

More recently, attention has shifted to enhancing VLA performance during deployment, specifically through inference-time steering and test-time scaling. These approaches aim to improve robustness in challenging or out-of-domain (OOD) scenarios, where VLA success rates often decline, without requiring extensive data collection or model retraining.

Existing inference-time steering methods generally fall into two categories:
- **Discrete action selection:** These methods sample multiple action candidates and select the most suitable one using external verifiers. While preserving the policy's learned action distribution, they are often limited by sample diversity, which can restrict adaptation to novel OOD situations.
- **Differentiable steering:** This approach directly influences action generation using continuous guidance signals, such as VLM-derived scoring functions or metrics from world model predictions. These methods can steer policies beyond their original action distribution but are reliant on external guidance models that may suffer from imperfect physical grounding or prediction errors.

In parallel, test-time scaling (TTS) research has explored repeatedly sampling actions or generating diverse samples from rephrased language instructions. However, a common limitation across these methods is that all generated samples originate from the same base policy, potentially inheriting correlated biases and failure modes. Furthermore, many existing steering and scaling approaches apply a fixed intervention strategy indiscriminately, failing to differentiate between situations where the base VLA is likely to succeed and those where intervention is necessary.

The reported work, RL^2, positions itself within this landscape by addressing these limitations. It proposes an adaptive inference-time steering framework that aims to generate diverse actions beyond dominant demonstration modes and existing correlated failure patterns. RL^2 distinguishes between success and failure states, applying intervention adaptively, thereby combining the behavioral priors of large-scale imitation learning with the action diversity capabilities of reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptive inference-time steering framework that enhances the robustness and generalization of pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly in challenging and out-of-domain (OOD) tasks. This objective is motivated by observed limitations in current VLA deployment strategies:

1. **Limited Action Diversity and Correlated Failure Modes:** Existing test-time steering and scaling methods often generate action samples that are concentrated around similar behaviors. This can lead to correlated failure modes, where even multiple sampled actions fail in similar ways, especially when the base policy struggles with novel or complex tasks. The motivation is to introduce a mechanism that can explore a broader range of effective actions, moving beyond the patterns learned from potentially imperfect demonstration data.
2. **Non-Adaptive Intervention Strategies:** Current methods typically apply a fixed intervention strategy at every timestep, regardless of the VLA's likelihood of success. This undifferentiated approach can be inefficient, introducing unnecessary computation or even perturbing already accurate actions when the base policy is performing well. The researchers observe that human behavior, when faced with uncertainty, involves broadening the diversity of alternatives considered only when default actions are likely to fail, rather than constantly deliberating. This human-like adaptive behavior motivates the development of a selective intervention strategy.

To address these motivations, the research poses a central question: "How and when should we steer pretrained VLAs toward diverse action candidates to enhance robustness in challenging / OOD scenarios?" This question is broken down into two components:

- **How to steer:** The motivation for the "how" component is to combine the strengths of imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL). VLAs, trained via IL, provide strong behavioral priors from large-scale demonstrations. However, these demonstrations can be inherently imperfect or incomplete. RL, in contrast, offers the ability to discover high-value behaviors beyond the confines of demonstration data, thereby inducing greater action diversity. The objective is to integrate these two paradigms through a compositional steering strategy that leverages an RL policy.
- **When to steer:** The motivation for the "when" component stems from an empirical observation. The researchers hypothesize that the benefits of increased action diversity are most pronounced when the VLA is likely to fail, while such diversity might be detrimental to already accurate actions. This hypothesis is investigated through an analysis of test-time scaling laws, aiming to quantify how steering impacts performance differently under success and failure states. The findings from this analysis directly inform the design of an adaptive steering mechanism that activates only when failure is predicted.

By addressing these questions, the research aims to establish a framework that not only generates more diverse and high-quality actions but also applies these interventions intelligently and efficiently, leading to improved task performance and generalization in real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RL^2 framework is an adaptive inference-time steering method that employs Reinforcement Learning on VLA Latents. Its methodology is structured to address both "how" and "when" to steer pretrained VLAs.

#### 3.1. "How to steer": RL Latent Policy Training and Compositional Steering

1. **RL Latent Policy Training:**
 - **Goal:** To generate a differentiable guidance function for steering VLA action samples by training a lightweight RL flow-matching policy, denoted π_RL.
 - **Conditioning:** π_RL is conditioned on expressive latents (e_t) extracted from the frozen VLA action expert, specifically hidden-state vectors from the VLA's action expert. Different aggregation strategies (e.g., first, last, mean, concatenate) for these internal features are explored to form a single embedding vector e_t.
 - **Data Augmentation:** Existing large-scale datasets (BridgeV2, DROID) are augmented with these policy latents to create a training dataset D = {o_i, a_i, ℓ_i, e_i}.
 - **Training Algorithm:** The training of flow-matching actor-critic networks for π_RL utilizes Q-learning with Adjoint Matching (QAM). QAM is selected because it mitigates numerical instability typically encountered when backpropagating through a policy's multi-step flow-matching process. It employs a lean adjoint state (˜g_t) as a time-dependent guidance signal, computed via a reverse Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE), to optimize the policy's target velocity field. This approach encourages convergence to an optimal behavior-regularized distribution while maintaining expressive capabilities.
 - **Compatibility:** The framework is designed to be compatible with other RL methods suitable for VLA composition, including non-flow-based methods like Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) for autoregressive VLAs.
 - **Computational Overhead:** The RL policies are lightweight and trained efficiently (e.g., 500k-1M steps on two NVIDIA L40 GPUs), with training times largely influenced by the VLA latent dimensionality.

2. **Compositional Steering:**
 - **Mechanism:** During VLA inference, the VLA action distribution π_VLA is steered by injecting guidance from the trained RL policy π_RL. This is achieved by composing the velocity fields of the VLA (v_VLA) and RL (v_RL) policies.
 - **Velocity Composition:** The enhanced VLA velocity (v_comp) is computed as a weighted average: v_comp ← w * v_VLA + (1 - w) * v_RL.
 - **Weighting:** Composition weights 'w' are sampled from a Gaussian distribution (μ = 0.5, σ = 0.25), clipped between 0 and 1. This variable weighting allows action samples to benefit from both the VLA's demonstration-aligned priors and the diversity introduced by RL steering.
 - **Autoregressive VLAs:** For autoregressive VLA architectures (e.g., OpenVLA), where direct flow-matching composition is not applicable, a Gaussian perturbation strategy is adopted. This involves fitting a Gaussian distribution over an equal mix of VLA and RL action samples and then resampling a larger batch from this fitted distribution.

#### 3.2. "When to steer": Failure Detection for Adaptive Steering

1. **Multi-Task Failure Detector:**
 - **Rationale:** Based on insights from the test-time scaling law analysis (described below), compositional steering is most effective during VLA failure states and potentially detrimental during success states. Therefore, an adaptive mechanism is needed.
 - **Implementation:** The framework integrates SAFE, a lightweight multitask failure detector. SAFE is an LSTM-based model that takes a sequence of VLA features (e_0:t) up to the current timestep and outputs a failure score s_t ∈.
 - **Training:** SAFE is trained on collected rollouts (containing both successes and failures) using a binary cross-entropy loss across all timesteps.

2. **Conformal Prediction (CP) for Thresholding:**
 - **Adaptive Trigger:** Steering is triggered dynamically when the predicted failure score s_t exceeds a time-varying threshold δ_t. Otherwise, the system defaults to the original VLA action distribution.
 - **Calibration:** The threshold δ_t is calibrated using a one-sided time-varying Conformal Prediction (CP) formulation. This involves calibrating a CP band C_α = {[-∞, upper_t]} using successful rollouts from a validation set, such that new successful rollouts satisfy s_t < upper_t with a specified probability (1 - α). Both per-task and combined CP bands are calibrated.

3. **CP Alpha Selection Heuristic:**
 - **Challenge:** The optimal CP significance level α (which dictates steering frequency) varies by task.
 - **Heuristic:** To avoid exhaustive sweeps, a test-time heuristic is introduced. Candidate α values are evaluated on a validation set using balanced accuracy (BalAcc(α) = 0.5 * (TPR(α) + TNR(α))). The top three α values based on this metric are then subjected to a calibration sweep, and the best-performing value is selected for final evaluation.

#### 3.3. Action Verification

- **Role:** After candidate actions (either VLA or steered) are generated, an action verifier (V_θ) serves as a reward model.
- **Selection:** The verifier assigns a score r_n,t = V_θ(o_t, a_n,t, l_t) to each candidate action. The action with the highest score is selected for execution.
- **Flexibility:** RL^2 is designed to be adaptable to various verifiers, including those trained via preference learning (e.g., RoboMonkey) or contrastive learning (e.g., CoVer).

#### 3.4. Inference-Time Scaling Law (Informative Analysis)

Prior to developing the adaptive steering, an analysis of test-time scaling laws was conducted to understand how different steering approaches affect action error under success and failure states.
- **Method:** Using π^0 as the base VLA and BridgeV2 dataset, normalized root-mean-squared error (NRMSE) between policy and ground truth actions was computed. Top and bottom 1,024 tuples based on NRMSE defined failure and success sets.
- **Evaluation:** Various baseline steering approaches (Repeated, Rephrase, RBF, RL, Concat, Residual, RLT) and RL^2 were evaluated.
- **Key Insight:** The analysis revealed an exponential power law relationship between NRMSE and the number of generated action samples. Crucially, RL^2 compositional steering and other diversity-inducing methods significantly reduced action error in failure states, demonstrating strong scaling behavior. However, these methods performed worse than baselines in success states, indicating that diversity can unnecessarily perturb accurate actions. This insight directly motivated the adaptive steering approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated RL^2 across various simulation benchmarks (SIMPLER, PolaRiS) and real-world tasks, utilizing different VLAs and verifiers.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

1. **Compositional Steering Robustness to Language Prompts:**
 - **In-domain SIMPLER (OpenVLA):** Adaptive RL^2 improved average success rates by +7.5% (up to +19.4% task-wise) over the Repeated baseline.
 - **OOD Language Prompts (SIMPLER, π^0):** Adaptive RL^2 achieved an average +10.1% gain (up to +14.7% task-wise) in success rates over the Rephrase baseline for OOD language instructions.
 - **OOD Language Prompts (PolaRiS, π^0.5):** Adaptive RL^2 demonstrated an average +10.9% gain in success rates and +7.8% gain in progress rates (up to +17.3% success rate for specific tasks) over the Rephrase baseline.
 - **Qualitative Observation:** Visualizations indicated that during failure states, VLA actions (even with rephrases) often directed away from the task object or towards distractors, leading to oscillatory behaviors. RL^2 steered actions were more diverse, with some higher-quality samples directed towards the task object, which were then selected by the verifier.

2. **Compositional Steering Generalization to OOD Task Environments:**
 - **OOD Task Environments (SIMPLER, π^0):** RL^2 achieved an average +8.5% performance gain (up to +14.6% task-wise) over the Rephrase baseline in environments with unseen objects, distractors, or backgrounds.
 - **Qualitative Observation:** In OOD environments, Rephrase π^0 action samples tended to be misguided towards distractor objects during failure. RL-steered samples provided diversity that shifted the action distribution closer to the main task object, facilitating successful grasping.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

1. **Importance of Adaptiveness:**
 - Removing the failure detection module (non-adaptive steering) generally resulted in lower performance compared to adaptive RL^2. Across OpenVLA and π^0.5, adaptive steering yielded an average +8.9% and task-wise +16.7% gain over non-adaptive steering. This supports the finding that compositional steering is most beneficial when applied to failure states and can be detrimental otherwise.

2. **Effectiveness of Latents and RL Training:**
 - **RL vs. BC:** Using RL for steering policy training consistently outperformed Behavior Cloning (BC) by up to +4.5% in performance, indicating that RL introduces necessary diversity beyond demonstration modes.
 - **VLA Latents:** Conditioning the steering policy on VLA latents significantly improved performance (e.g., +38.8% for OpenVLA) compared to conditioning on raw observations. This suggests that VLA latents provide a more expressive representation for effective steering.

3. **Failure Detection Trigger:**
 - SAFE as a failure detector outperformed an external VLM (CoVer) used as a trigger, by up to +3.5%. This is attributed to SAFE being conditioned on informative latents directly from the action expert, resulting in better separability between success and failure scores.

#### 4.3. Additional Studies

1. **Scaling Samples and Rephrases:** RL^2 demonstrated improved performance when scaling both the quantity of sampled actions and the diversity introduced by rephrased language instructions. Combining both types of scaling yielded up to +18.7% performance gain, validating the scaling laws established in the paper.

2. **CP Alpha Selection Heuristic:** The proposed balanced-accuracy heuristic proved effective, achieving most (up to 88.5% relative to maximum) performance gains by selecting from the top-3 candidate alpha values for Conformal Prediction.

3. **Minimal Additional Latency:** The QAM steering policy and SAFE failure detector introduced minimal latency during inference compared to the VLA forward pass, indicating efficiency suitable for real-world deployment.

4. **Qualitative Analysis (PCA):** Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on VLA action chunks in failure states from the BridgeV2 dataset showed that compositional steering shifted the action distribution significantly closer to the ground truth action, effectively correcting failures.

#### 4.4. Real-World Evaluation

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a PiperX manipulator for four OOD real-world tasks (two with OOD prompts, two with OOD environments), using π^0, QAM steering, and CoVer verifier.
- **Performance:** Adaptive RL^2 achieved an average +17.5% performance gain over the Rephrase baseline and +14.2% over non-adaptive RL^2.
- **Observations:** Real-world results confirmed that adaptive RL^2 initially used the base VLA for accurate approaches, then triggered compositional steering upon failure detection to avoid common failure modes such as collisions with the toolbox or distraction by other objects, significantly improving replanning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RL^2 framework contributes to the field of robotic learning by enhancing the robustness and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models during deployment. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Improved Robustness in OOD Scenarios:** The research demonstrates significant performance improvements (up to +17.3% in simulation and +17.5% in real-world settings) for VLAs operating on challenging and out-of-domain tasks. This capability is critical for deploying general-purpose robots in varied and unconstrained environments, where unseen objects, environments, or instructions are common.

2. **Adaptive and Efficient Intervention:** RL^2 introduces an adaptive inference-time steering mechanism that applies compositional steering only when the base VLA is likely to fail. This is a key departure from prior methods that apply interventions indiscriminately. By avoiding unnecessary perturbations during success states, the framework enhances efficiency and prevents degradation of already accurate actions, aligning with a more intelligent and human-like adaptive control strategy.

3. **Novel Scientific Insight on Scaling Laws:** The work establishes distinct test-time scaling laws for VLA steering under success and failure states. This finding provides empirical evidence that RL-induced diversity is most beneficial when a policy is underperforming, but can be detrimental during successful execution. This insight can guide future research in designing more targeted and effective intervention strategies for foundation models.

4. **Enhanced Action Diversity:** By training a lightweight offline RL policy conditioned on expressive VLA latents, RL^2 effectively generates diverse and high-quality action candidates beyond the dominant demonstration modes. This addresses a critical limitation of existing methods, which often produce action samples that suffer from correlated biases and failure modes inherent to the base imitation learning policy.

5. **Modular and Practical Framework:** RL^2 is presented as a modular framework compatible with diverse pretrained VLAs and action verifiers. Its lightweight RL policy and failure detection module introduce minimal additional latency, making it a practical solution for real-world deployment on robotic hardware. The successful transfer of gains from simulation to a real-world manipulator underscores its applicability.

6. **Pathway to General-Purpose Robotics:** The ability to adaptively steer and improve pretrained VLAs without extensive retraining or data collection positions adaptive test-time steering as a promising pathway toward the development of more robust and truly general-purpose robotics foundation models. This could reduce the cost and complexity of deploying capable robotic systems.

While acknowledging limitations such as the need for further exploration of steering functions (e.g., large VLM-based differentiable steering), improvements in failure detection (e.g., leveraging large-scale offline datasets for training), and potential co-training of RL policy with verifiers, RL^2 represents a step toward more intelligent and reliable robot autonomy.
