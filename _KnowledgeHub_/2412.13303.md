# FastVLM: Efficient Vision Encoding for Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FastVLM, an efficient Vision Language Model developed by Apple, employs a novel FastViTHD vision encoder designed for rapid high-resolution image processing. This approach achieves over 2.5 points higher average performance on VLM benchmarks while being up to 3 times faster on an M1 MacBook Pro, significantly improving on-device VLM efficiency.

## Problem

- Vision Language Models (VLMs) suffer from high latency (Time-To-First-Token) at high image resolutions, particularly for text-rich content, due to the large number of visual tokens generated.
- Existing VLM vision encoders, often ViT-based, are computationally expensive and inefficient for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.
- Many VLM pipelines rely on complex, separate token pruning or sampling modules to manage visual token count, adding complexity and overhead.

## Method

- Developed ==FastViTHD==, a novel 5-stage ==hybrid convolutional-transformer vision encoder== with an increased 64x downsampling factor to inherently reduce the number of visual tokens.
- Integrated multi-scale features through ==depth-wise convolutions== within the FastViTHD architecture to enhance feature granularity and information capture.
- Employed a systematic multi-stage LLaVA-1.5-like training pipeline to adapt FastViTHD with various LLMs and input resolutions, optimizing the accuracy-latency trade-off.

## Results

- FastVLM achieves over **2.5 points** higher average performance on key VLM benchmarks (Avg-5) for a given runtime budget compared to prior efficient VLMs.
- FastVLM reaches target VLM performance up to **3 times** faster and demonstrates up to **85 times** faster Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) on an M1 MacBook Pro compared to larger, less efficient models like LLaVA-OneVision.
- FastViTHD, FastVLM's vision encoder, inherently produces fewer visual tokens and outperforms models that use multiple encoders or explicit token pruning, such as MM1 and MiniGemini-HD, on various benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An efficiency-first architectural design for the vision encoder, specifically a hierarchical hybrid convolutional-transformer, can inherently reduce visual tokens and latency more effectively than post-hoc pruning.
> - The vision encoder's latency becomes the dominant bottleneck in VLM performance at high resolutions, necessitating optimized vision backbone design.
> - Directly setting static input resolution for the VLM model generally offers a better accuracy-latency trade-off compared to dynamic tiling strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as a pivotal area in AI, enabling machines to understand and generate responses based on both visual and textual inputs. These models typically integrate a vision encoder (often a pre-trained backbone like a Vision Transformer, ViT) with a Large Language Model (LLM) via a projection layer. Recent breakthroughs in LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Qwen2, Llama) and large-scale image-text pre-training (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP) have fueled rapid advancements in VLM capabilities.

However, a critical challenge in scaling VLMs, particularly for tasks involving text-rich images (e.g., document understanding, chart analysis), is the **inefficiency at high image resolutions**. Increasing resolution leads to a combinatorial explosion in the number of visual tokens processed by the vision encoder and subsequently fed to the LLM. This dramatically increases both the vision encoding latency and the LLM's "prefilling time" (the time taken for the LLM to process all input tokens before generating the first output token), collectively known as Time-To-First-Token (TTFT). High TTFT impedes real-time interactive applications.

The broader research landscape has explored several avenues to address this:
- **Resolution Adaptation:** Techniques like continuous pretraining (e.g., PaliGemma) adapt vision backbones to higher resolutions. Tiling strategies (e.g., Sphinx, S2, AnyRes) divide large images into smaller sub-regions, processing them independently before aggregation. While these methods allow for high-resolution input, they often incur significant computational overhead due to multiple inferences or the inherent inefficiency of processing large token sequences.
- **Efficient Image Encoding:** Many VLMs still rely on standard ViT-based architectures (e.g., SigLIP, EVA-CLIP) which, despite their representational power, are not inherently optimized for high-resolution efficiency due to their isotropic nature (uniform processing of all patches).
- **Token Reduction:** To manage the large number of visual tokens, methods like LLaVA-PruMerge, Matryoshka-based sampling, and perceiver-style resamplers/pooling have been proposed. These approaches prune or condense visual tokens *after* the initial encoding, adding complexity to the model pipeline.
- **Hierarchical Architectures:** Some works (e.g., ConvNeXt, FastViT) have explored hierarchical vision backbones, which naturally downsample inputs and produce fewer tokens. ConvLLaVA, for instance, specifically uses a pure-convolutional backbone. These designs offer a simpler path to token reduction.

**FastVLM's contribution fits into this landscape by directly addressing the fundamental efficiency bottleneck at the vision encoder level for high-resolution inputs.** Unlike methods that add post-hoc token pruning, FastVLM integrates efficiency into the core architecture design of its vision encoder, FastViTHD. It systematically explores the complex Pareto optimal trade-off between resolution, latency, and accuracy, demonstrating that a carefully designed *hybrid convolutional-transformer architecture* can outperform existing ViT-based and even other hierarchical models, while simplifying the overall VLM pipeline. Its on-device benchmarking focus further distinguishes it, aiming for practical deployability rather than just theoretical performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind FastVLM are deeply rooted in the practical challenges of deploying high-performance Vision Language Models (VLMs), particularly on resource-constrained devices:

- **Optimize the Resolution-Latency-Accuracy Trade-off:** The paramount objective is to achieve the best possible balance among these three critical factors. While high resolution is crucial for detailed image understanding (especially text-rich content), it often comes at the cost of increased latency. The authors aim to find the "Pareto optimal curve" for VLM design, maximizing accuracy within a given runtime budget (TTFT) across various resolutions and LLM sizes.

- **Reduce Vision Encoding Latency for High-Resolution Images:** Popular visual encoders, particularly ViTs, become computationally expensive at high resolutions due to the sheer number of tokens generated. A primary goal is to design a vision encoder that can efficiently process high-resolution inputs, thereby significantly cutting down the image encoding time.

- **Minimize Visual Token Count for Efficient LLM Processing:** The large number of visual tokens produced by high-resolution images directly impacts the LLM's prefilling time, contributing significantly to overall latency. FastVLM aims to generate fewer, yet high-quality, visual tokens, reducing the computational burden on the downstream LLM.

- **Simplify Model Design by Eliminating External Token Pruning:** Instead of relying on separate, often complex, token pruning or sampling modules, a key motivation is to incorporate token reduction directly into the vision encoder's architecture. This "end-to-end" design simplifies the VLM pipeline and potentially improves efficiency.

- **Demonstrate Strong On-Device Performance:** Unlike much academic research that focuses solely on theoretical performance metrics or GPU-server benchmarks, a strong motivation for FastVLM is to validate its efficiency and performance on actual consumer hardware (e.g., M1 MacBook Pro). This ensures the model is truly practical for real-world applications where latency and energy consumption are critical.

- **Leverage Hybrid Architectures:** The authors are motivated by the promising characteristics of hybrid convolutional-transformer architectures (like FastViT) for efficient vision encoding, building upon their prior work. They believe these architectures offer a superior foundation for VLMs due to their native resolution scaling capabilities and efficiency in token generation.

In essence, the motivation is to build VLMs that are not only capable but also **fast and resource-efficient enough for widespread, interactive deployment**, especially in scenarios demanding fine-grained visual understanding like text and document analysis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology adopted for FastVLM involves a systematic, empirical approach to optimize VLM efficiency and performance, rooted in architectural innovation and rigorous benchmarking.

#### 3.1. Comprehensive Efficiency Analysis

The work begins by recognizing the complex interplay between image resolution, vision encoder latency, visual token count, and LLM size on overall VLM performance and Time-To-First-Token (TTFT). This understanding guides the architectural design decisions.

#### 3.2. Vision Encoder Design - From FastViT to FastViTHD

- **Initial Exploration with FastViT:** The authors first explore the existing FastViT architecture (specifically, the MCi2 encoder from MobileCLIP) as a VLM backbone. FastViT is a hybrid convolutional-transformer model, known for its efficiency and ability to scale resolution. They demonstrate that FastViT already offers advantages over ViT-L/14 in terms of speed and token count, even at higher resolutions.
- **Multi-Scale Feature Integration:** To further enhance FastViT's VLM performance, they introduce "architectural interventions." A key intervention is the incorporation of multi-scale features. By aggregating features from different stages of the hierarchical encoder, more granular information can be captured. They ablate two pooling strategies (AvgPooling and 2D Depth-wise Convolutions), finding depth-wise convolutions to be more effective.
- **Introducing FastViTHD (High-Resolution Design):** Recognizing that simply scaling up FastViT (by increasing self-attention layers or width) is suboptimal for high-resolution VLMs, they design a novel hybrid encoder, FastViTHD.
 - **5-Stage Architecture:** FastViTHD features a 5-stage design, with an extra downsampling layer compared to standard 4-stage hybrid models. This ensures self-attention operates on tensors downsampled by a factor of 64 (rather than 16), significantly reducing token count.
 - **Mixed Block Types:** The first three stages utilize efficient RepMixer blocks (convolutional), while the last two stages employ multi-headed self-attention (transformer) blocks, effectively combining local feature extraction with global reasoning.
 - **Parameter Efficiency:** Despite being larger than the original FastViT variants, FastViTHD is designed to be more parameter-efficient than popular ViT alternatives.
 - **CLIP Pretraining:** FastViTHD is pre-trained using a CLIP-like setup (specifically, the MobileCLIP approach) on the DataCompDR-1B dataset.

#### 3.3. VLM Training Strategy (FastVLM)

- **LLaVA-1.5 Setup Adaptation:** FastVLM models are trained following the established LLaVA-1.5 2-stage (and in later experiments, 3-stage and 4-stage with more data) visual instruction tuning pipeline.
 - **Stage 1 (Connector Training):** Only the vision-language projector is trained, using a smaller alignment dataset (LLaVA-558K). Vision encoder and LLM are frozen.
 - **Stage 1.5 (Resolution Scaling/Intermediate Pretraining):** For scaled-up experiments, an intermediate pretraining stage is introduced using large, densely captioned datasets (e.g., Recap-CC3M + 10.6M/12.5M samples), where all modules (vision encoder, projector, LLM) are tuned. This stage adapts the vision encoder to higher resolutions and aligns it further with the LLM.
 - **Stage 2 (Supervised Fine-tuning):** All modules (vision encoder, projector, LLM) are fine-tuned on a supervised instruction tuning dataset (LLaVA-665K or larger custom datasets like 1.1M, 6.5M, 11.9M, 12.5M samples) at the target input resolution.
 - **Stage 3 (High-Quality Instruction Tuning):** For the best performing models, an additional fine-tuning stage is performed on a high-quality instruction tuning dataset with chain-of-thought reasoning (MammothVL).
- **Diverse LLM Integration:** The methodology systematically evaluates FastViTHD with various LLM backbones (Vicuna-7B, Qwen2-0.5B, Qwen2-1.5B, Qwen2-7B) and a range of input resolutions to comprehensively map the accuracy-latency landscape.

#### 3.4. Resolution Scaling Strategies

- **Static vs. Dynamic Input Resolution:** The paper analyzes two ways to handle high-resolution inputs: directly scaling the model's input resolution (static) or using tiling strategies (dynamic, like AnyRes). They empirically determine the optimal operating points for each.

#### 3.5. Benchmarking and Evaluation

- **On-Device Benchmarking:** A crucial aspect is the rigorous on-device benchmarking conducted on an M1 MacBook Pro. The vision encoder's latency is measured using Core ML, and the LLM's prefilling latency using MLX, providing realistic TTFT (Time-To-First-Token) measurements.
- **Comprehensive VLM Evaluation:** Models are evaluated across a diverse set of mainstream VLM benchmarks, including general knowledge (GQA, SeedBench), text-rich (TextVQA, DocVQA, ChartQA, OCRBench, InfoVQA), hallucination (POPE), and complex reasoning tasks (MMMU, MMVet, ScienceQA, AI2D, MathVista). The "Avg-5" metric (average of GQA, TextVQA, POPE, DocVQA, SeedBench) is used for ablation studies due to its low variance and quick evaluation time.

This multi-faceted methodology, combining architectural innovation, systematic training and scaling, and real-world performance measurement, underpins FastVLM's claims of superior efficiency and performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a compelling set of findings that support FastVLM's superior efficiency and performance in Vision Language Models:

#### 4.1. FastViTHD: A Superior Vision Encoder for VLMs

- **Efficiency over ViT and ConvNeXt:** FastViTHD significantly outperforms conventional ViT-based and even other hierarchical convolutional backbones like ConvNeXT. Compared to SigLIP-SO400M (a popular ViT), FastViTHD achieves 3.2× faster TTFT and is 3.6× smaller. Against ConvNeXT, it's 2.3× faster and 1.7× smaller. Despite being smaller and faster, FastViTHD maintains or achieves higher VLM accuracy.
- **Competitive CLIP Pretraining Performance:** Despite its smaller size (125.1M parameters vs. 304M for ViT-L/14) and higher speed (6.9× faster than ViT-L/14), FastViTHD achieves comparable performance on 38 multi-modal zero-shot CLIP tasks. This indicates its strong general visual representation capabilities.
- **Effectiveness of Architectural Interventions:** The architectural optimizations, such as the 5-stage design with increased downsampling (64x factor) and multi-scale feature aggregation using depth-wise convolutions, are shown to be crucial for FastViTHD's efficiency and accuracy. The additional downsampling stage directly contributes to fewer visual tokens.

#### 4.2. Optimized Accuracy-Latency Trade-off (Pareto Optimality)

- FastVLM (powered by FastViTHD) significantly shifts the Pareto-optimal curve for accuracy versus TTFT. For a given runtime budget, FastVLM achieves over **2.5 points higher average performance** (on the Avg-5 metric) compared to FastViT-based VLMs. Conversely, FastVLM can reach a target VLM performance up to **3× faster**. This demonstrates a fundamental improvement in the efficiency frontier.

#### 4.3. Latency Breakdown and Token Efficiency

- **Vision Latency Dominance:** At higher input resolutions, the vision encoder's latency becomes the dominant factor in the overall Time-To-First-Token (TTFT), more so than the LLM prefilling time. This finding validates the focus on vision encoder efficiency.
- **Superior Token Reduction to Pruning Methods:** FastViTHD's architectural design inherently leads to fewer visual tokens. The paper shows that FastVLM, by simply operating at lower *input* resolutions with its hierarchical design, produces fewer tokens and achieves better accuracy-latency trade-offs than explicit token pruning/downsampling methods applied to isotropic architectures like ViT. Even with as few as 16 visual tokens, FastViTHD outperforms several token pruning techniques.

#### 4.4. Comparison with State-of-the-Art VLMs

- **Against ConvLLaVA:** FastVLM consistently outperforms ConvLLaVA, another VLM using a hierarchical backbone, particularly on text-rich benchmarks, while being faster (e.g., 22% faster at similar settings, and 2× faster at higher resolutions).
- **Against LLaVA-OneVision (0.5B LLM):** At the 0.5B LLM scale, FastVLM matches or surpasses LLaVA-OneVision (which uses a large SigLIP-SO400M vision encoder and 1152x1152 resolution) but achieves an **85× faster TTFT** and uses a vision encoder that is **3.4× smaller**. This is a stark demonstration of FastVLM's efficiency gains.
- **Against MM1 (1-2B LLM):** FastVLM matches or surpasses MM1, even while generating 5× fewer visual tokens. With larger instruction tuning datasets (12.5M samples), FastVLM outperforms MM1 and LLaVA-NeXT across various benchmarks, including text-rich ones, despite having a smaller encoder and fewer tokens.
- **Against Multi-Encoder Models:** FastVLM, using a single vision encoder, outperforms models like Cambrian-1 and MiniGemini-HD (which use multiple vision encoders) on several benchmarks, while being significantly faster (e.g., 7.9× faster than Cambrian-1) and using fewer visual tokens. This challenges the notion that multiple encoders are necessary for top performance.
- **Impact of LLM Quality:** The results demonstrate that pairing FastViTHD with higher-quality LLMs (e.g., Qwen2 vs. Vicuna) consistently improves VLM performance across a wide range of benchmarks, underscoring the interplay between vision and language components.

#### 4.5. Resolution Scaling Strategies

- **Static Resolution Preferred:** Empirically, directly setting the model's input resolution (static resolution) generally offers the best accuracy-latency trade-off compared to dynamic tiling strategies (AnyRes). Dynamic resolution is only beneficial at extreme resolutions (e.g., 1536x1536) and only when using fewer tiles (e.g., 2x2 grid) to avoid significant overhead.

In summary, FastVLM's findings consistently highlight its ability to achieve state-of-the-art VLM performance with unprecedented efficiency, particularly for high-resolution inputs and on-device deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FastVLM represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision Language Models, with profound implications across several dimensions:

#### 5.1. Enabling On-Device AI/ML and Ubiquitous Deployment

The most direct and impactful significance of FastVLM is its strong performance on resource-constrained hardware like the M1 MacBook Pro. By drastically reducing Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) and vision encoder size, FastVLM makes high-performance VLMs viable for deployment on smartphones, tablets, and other edge devices. This capability is crucial for delivering real-time, interactive AI experiences directly to users, without relying on cloud inference. For a company like Apple, this aligns perfectly with their strategy of privacy-preserving on-device processing.

#### 5.2. Redefining Efficiency in VLM Design

FastVLM shifts the paradigm from simply scaling up existing, often inefficient, architectures or relying on post-hoc token pruning. It champions a **"efficiency-first" architectural design** for the vision encoder. By demonstrating that architectural innovations (like the 5-stage hybrid FastViTHD and optimized downsampling) can inherently reduce token count and latency while maintaining or improving accuracy, the paper sets a new standard for how efficient VLMs should be built. This foundational insight can guide future research in designing lightweight yet powerful multimodal models.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance on Text-Rich and Detailed Understanding Tasks

The paper consistently shows FastVLM's superior performance on benchmarks demanding fine-grained visual understanding, especially those involving text (e.g., DocVQA, TextVQA, ChartQA, InfoVQA). The ability to efficiently process high-resolution images, combined with effective token generation, means FastVLM can excel in applications like:
- **Document AI:** Automated understanding of invoices, forms, reports, and other text-heavy documents.
- **Data Visualization:** Interpreting charts, graphs, and infographics.
- **Accessibility:** Assisting visually impaired users by describing complex visual content, including text in images.
- **Real-time Visual Q&A:** Providing quick answers to questions about images encountered in daily life.

#### 5.4. Systematic Benchmarking and Open-Source Contribution

The rigorous and transparent benchmarking methodology, particularly the on-device measurements of TTFT, provides a valuable and realistic metric for comparing VLM efficiency. Many papers report FLOPs or theoretical throughput, but real-world latency is often different. FastVLM's commitment to releasing code and models (R4, R5, R12, R13, R41, R42 checkpoints) is a significant contribution to the open-source community. This enables other researchers and developers to reproduce results, build upon FastVLM, and accelerate further innovation in efficient multimodal AI.

#### 5.5. Challenges the Need for Overly Complex Architectures

By outperforming models that rely on multiple vision encoders or very large, resource-intensive backbones, FastVLM demonstrates that a single, highly optimized, parameter-efficient vision encoder can be sufficient. This simplifies model training, deployment, and potentially reduces the overall carbon footprint of VLM development and operation.

In conclusion, FastVLM's primary impact lies in making high-performing Vision Language Models truly practical and deployable for a wide range of real-world applications, especially on consumer devices. Its innovative architectural design and empirical validation of efficiency metrics set a new benchmark for the field, paving the way for more responsive, accessible, and resource-friendly multimodal AI.
