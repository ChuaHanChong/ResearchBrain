# Llama-Nemotron: Efficient Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA's Llama-Nemotron introduces a family of open-source language models designed for advanced reasoning, achieving state-of-the-art performance on complex tasks while delivering superior inference efficiency. The models integrate a dynamic reasoning toggle and surpass benchmarks like GPQA-Diamond with significantly higher throughput compared to existing models.

## Problem

- Existing state-of-the-art reasoning models are computationally expensive, leading to high inference costs and longer response times, hindering practical deployment.
- Users lack dynamic control over the verbosity and depth of reasoning, leading to inefficient resource allocation for simpler queries and suboptimal user experience.
- A need exists for commercially permissive open-source models that combine advanced reasoning capabilities with practical inference efficiency.

## Method

- Developed a family of heterogeneous reasoning models (Nano, Super, Ultra) based on Llama 3.1/3.3, featuring a ==dynamic reasoning toggle== to control output verbosity.
- Implemented a ==multi-stage training pipeline== encompassing Neural Architecture Search (NAS), FFN Fusion for efficiency, knowledge distillation, continued pretraining, and supervised fine-tuning.
- Utilized large-scale, ==curriculum-driven Reinforcement Learning (GRPO, RLOO)== and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to enhance reasoning capabilities, alignment, and surpass teacher model performance.

## Results

- LN-Ultra (253B) achieves state-of-the-art open-source performance on GPQA-Diamond (**76.0%** accuracy), surpassing DeepSeek-R1 (**71.5%**) and demonstrating significantly higher inference throughput (e.g., **4x-1.9x** speedup).
- LN-Super (49B) delivers a **5x** throughput speedup over Llama 3.3-70B-Instruct on a single H100 GPU at batch size 256 (TP1) while maintaining competitive reasoning performance.
- The models successfully support a dynamic reasoning toggle, effectively modulating response styles based on user prompts, and exhibit strong generalization on out-of-distribution tasks like LLM-as-a-Judge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A comprehensive multi-stage training methodology, integrating architectural optimization (NAS), distillation, SFT, and large-scale RL, is crucial for achieving both state-of-the-art reasoning and high inference efficiency.
> - Large-scale, curriculum-driven Reinforcement Learning is essential for models to surpass their teacher's reasoning capabilities on challenging tasks, overcoming a performance ceiling.
> - Implementing a dynamic reasoning toggle provides users with effective control over response style and computational resources, enhancing model versatility and practicality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of the Llama-Nemotron series is situated at the nexus of two critical trends in large language model (LLM) research: the pursuit of advanced reasoning capabilities and the imperative for inference efficiency. In recent years, LLMs have demonstrated remarkable progress across diverse NLP tasks, with the advent of "reasoning models" like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1 marking a new frontier. These models excel at complex problem-solving, often by generating elaborate "chains of thought," self-verification, and reflection. However, this depth of reasoning typically translates into significantly longer response generation times and higher computational costs, posing a substantial challenge for practical deployment and the viability of agentic AI systems.

This paper directly addresses this critical trade-off. While many research efforts focus solely on maximizing performance, Llama-Nemotron aims to achieve state-of-the-art reasoning performance *while simultaneously* optimizing for inference throughput and memory efficiency. This emphasis on deployability and cost-effectiveness distinguishes it within a landscape increasingly dominated by ever-larger, more computationally intensive models. The introduction of a dynamic "reasoning toggle" is a novel contribution, providing users with fine-grained control over resource allocation and response verbosity, a feature largely absent in many existing models.

Furthermore, by releasing the models, comprehensive post-training dataset, and training codebases (NeMo, NeMo-Aligner, Megatron-LM) under an open license, NVIDIA contributes significantly to the open-source AI community. This open approach contrasts with the proprietary nature of some leading models and fosters a collaborative environment for further research and development, allowing researchers and enterprises to build upon NVIDIA's advancements without incurring prohibitive licensing costs or being locked into closed ecosystems. This move reinforces the growing trend of large tech companies contributing to the open-source AI commons, accelerating the pace of innovation across the industry.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Llama-Nemotron project is to introduce an open family of heterogeneous reasoning models that simultaneously achieve exceptional reasoning capabilities, superior inference efficiency, and an open license suitable for enterprise use. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Advance Reasoning Capabilities:** The authors aim to push the boundaries of LLM intelligence, particularly in complex reasoning tasks such as PhD-level STEM questions and competition-level mathematics. They strive for models that can "think deeply about problems before answering," offering performance competitive with or surpassing existing state-of-the-art reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1.

2. **Maximize Inference Efficiency:** A core motivation is to tackle the critical bottleneck of inference cost associated with advanced reasoning. The paper highlights that inference efficiency is no longer merely a deployment concern but a "core limiting factor for overall model intelligence and the viability of agentic pipelines." The goal is to design models that can run efficiently on readily available hardware (e.g., LN-Ultra on a single 8xH100 node), offering high throughput and memory efficiency, thus making advanced reasoning more practical and accessible.

3. **Provide User Control over Reasoning:** Recognizing that not all queries benefit from verbose, multi-step reasoning, the project aims to empower users with dynamic control. The "reasoning toggle" allows users to switch between standard chat and detailed reasoning modes, ensuring that inference resources are judiciously allocated and response styles are appropriate for the task at hand. This enhances the model's versatility and user experience.

4. **Promote Open Research and Development:** A significant motivation is to contribute to the open-source AI community. By releasing the Llama-Nemotron models, the complete post-training dataset, and the underlying training codebases (NeMo, NeMo-Aligner, Megatron-LM), NVIDIA seeks to facilitate further research, development, and enterprise adoption of reasoning models, lowering barriers to entry and accelerating innovation across the field.

5. **Establish Leadership in Efficient AI:** Through this series, NVIDIA aims to demonstrate its leadership in developing not only powerful AI models but also highly optimized, deployable solutions that leverage its expertise in both hardware and software.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Llama-Nemotron models (Nano, Super, Ultra) are built upon the Llama 3.1 and Llama 3.3 series and are developed through a sophisticated, multi-stage training procedure designed to optimize for both reasoning capabilities and inference efficiency.

**Stage 1: Inference Optimization with Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and FFN Fusion**
The process begins by enhancing inference efficiency, primarily for LN-Super and LN-Ultra. This stage leverages the **Puzzle framework**, a neural architecture search (NAS) system that transforms large language models into hardware-efficient variants.
- **Block-wise local distillation:** Puzzle first creates a "library" of alternative transformer blocks by applying distillation to approximate the function of original Llama 3 blocks while improving computational properties. Variants include attention mechanism removal and variable Feed-Forward Network (FFN) dimensions.
- **Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP):** A MIP solver then assembles a complete, heterogeneous model by selecting one block per layer, optimizing for quality under deployment constraints such as throughput, latency, and memory usage.
- **FFN Fusion (for LN-Ultra):** An additional technique for LN-Ultra, FFN Fusion identifies consecutive FFN blocks (a structural property emerging after attention layer removal) and replaces them with fewer, wider FFN layers that can execute in parallel. This reduces sequential depth, improving latency and compute utilization.

**Stage 2: Post-NAS Recovery Training (Knowledge Distillation and Continued Pretraining)**
Following architecture optimization, models undergo further training to recover any quality degradation and improve inter-block compatibility.
- **Knowledge Distillation:** Both LN-Super and LN-Ultra are trained using a knowledge distillation objective on specialized datasets (e.g., Distillation Mix) for tens of billions of tokens.
- **Continued Pretraining (CPT):** LN-Ultra receives an additional 88 billion tokens of continued pretraining on a large dataset (Nemotron-H phase 4 pretraining dataset). This step is crucial for the model to not only match but *surpass* the performance of its original reference model (Llama 3.1-405B-Instruct).

**Stage 3: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
SFT is critical for distilling reasoning behavior from powerful "teacher" models like DeepSeek-R1 and enabling the dynamic reasoning toggle.
- **Synthetic Data Curation:** A large, high-quality post-training dataset is curated for both "reasoning on" and "reasoning off" modes.
 - **Math:** Problems from Art of Problem Solving (AoPS) are extracted, classified, decontaminated, and solved by teacher models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct) multiple times to generate diverse reasoning and non-reasoning solutions, which are then filtered for correctness.
 - **Code:** Competitive programming questions from various sources are collected, decontaminated, and solutions are generated by DeepSeek-R1, explicitly prompting for reasoning steps. Solutions are post-processed and refined. The authors emphasize the importance of large-scale, challenging data for coding.
 - **Science:** Open-ended and multiple-choice questions (MCQs) are sourced (StackOverflow) and synthetically generated (using Nemotron-4-340B-Instruct and Qwen2.5 models), decontaminated, and solved by DeepSeek-R1 to generate reasoning traces.
 - **General:** Synthetic prompts and real-world user prompts are used, with DeepSeek-R1 generating multiple responses and Llama-3.1-Nemotron-70B reward model performing rejection sampling.
- **Reasoning Toggle Training:** Paired data (reasoning and non-reasoning responses for the same prompt) is used, tagged with "detailed thinking on/off" system instructions, enabling the model to learn context-dependent reasoning modulation.
- **Training Details:** Models are trained with token-level cross-entropy loss, using Adam optimizer and a cosine learning rate decay with linear warmup. Specific learning rates, batch sizes, and epoch counts vary by model size.

**Stage 4: Large-Scale Reinforcement Learning (RL for Reasoning)**
This stage is applied exclusively to LN-Ultra, specifically to enable it to *surpass* its teacher's reasoning capabilities, particularly on challenging benchmarks like GPQA-Diamond.
- **GRPO Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed.
- **Rewards:** Accuracy rewards (comparing predictions to ground truth via Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) and format rewards (ensuring correct usage of `<think>` tags for reasoning mode).
- **Curriculum Training:** Data is preprocessed by discarding easier prompts (pass rate > 0.75) and a progressive batching strategy is used, starting with easier examples and gradually moving to harder ones, based on pre-calculated pass rates. This stabilizes training and improves accuracy.
- **Infrastructure:** Training utilizes NeMo-Aligner for RL, vLLM for generation, and Megatron-LM for training, all co-located on 72 nodes of 8xH100 GPUs. Extensive memory profiling and optimization (tensor, pipeline, context, sequence parallelism, and releasing/reloading memory between stages) are critical.
- **FP8 Inference Generation:** Custom vLLM loaders and meta-weight tensor initialization enable FP8 inference generation, achieving a 1.8x speedup over BF16 and significantly improving throughput.

**Stage 5: RL for Preference Optimization (Alignment Phase)**
A final, shorter alignment phase focuses on instruction following and human preference.
- **Instruction Following (RLOO):** For LN-Super and LN-Ultra, a short RL run using the RLOO algorithm with an instruction following verifier as a reward significantly boosts performance on instruction following benchmarks.
- **RLHF (Iterative Online RPO/GRPO):** For general helpfulness and chat capabilities, iterative online RPO (for LN-Super/Nano) or GRPO (for LN-Ultra) is used to maximize reward predicted by the Llama-3.1-Nemotron-70B-Reward model on HelpSteer2 prompts. This improves conversationality and general task performance, even on out-of-domain tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Llama-Nemotron series of models demonstrates remarkable achievements across efficiency, reasoning capabilities, and broad task performance, positioning them as leading open-source models.

1. **State-of-the-Art Reasoning Performance:**
 - **LN-Ultra (253B):** Consistently matches or outperforms all existing open-weight models across a wide range of reasoning benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, AIME24/25, MATH500, LiveCodeBench). It achieves state-of-the-art performance on GPQA-Diamond among open models (76.0% accuracy), surpassing DeepSeek-R1 (71.5%).
 - **LN-Super (49B):** Performs competitively in its weight class, outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Distilled-Llama-70B in reasoning-on mode (e.g., 66.7% vs. 65.2% on GPQA-Diamond) and maintaining strong instruction following.
 - **LN-Nano (8B):** Despite its compact size, LN-Nano achieves strong performance across reasoning benchmarks (e.g., 54.1% on GPQA-Diamond), demonstrating the effectiveness of the SFT pipeline in transferring structured reasoning.

2. **Superior Inference Efficiency:**
 - **LN-Ultra:** Optimized to run efficiently on a single 8xH100 node (compared to DeepSeek-R1 requiring 8xH200). Figure 4 clearly illustrates LN-Ultra's superior accuracy-throughput Pareto curve, consistently outperforming DeepSeek-R1 with significantly higher throughput (e.g., 4x-1.9x speedup) at equivalent or higher accuracy on GPQA-Diamond. It achieves a 1.71x latency improvement over Llama 3.1-405B-Instruct.
 - **LN-Super:** Achieves a 5x throughput speedup over Llama 3.3-70B-Instruct at batch size 256 and TP1 on a single H100 GPU. Even against Llama 3.3-70B-Instruct at its optimal TP4 configuration, LN-Super at TP1 delivers a ≥2.17x throughput advantage.
 - **FP8 Inference Generation:** The implementation of FP8 inference generation during RL training provided a 1.8x generation speedup against BF16, contributing to the overall efficiency.

3. **Dynamic Reasoning Toggle:** The models successfully support a dynamic reasoning toggle, allowing users to switch between "detailed thinking on" and "detailed thinking off" modes using a lightweight system prompt. This feature provides effective control over response style and computational resources.

4. **Efficacy of Multi-Stage Training:**
 - SFT is shown to be highly effective in distilling reasoning capabilities from strong teacher models into student models, especially for smaller models.
 - Large-scale, curriculum-driven Reinforcement Learning is confirmed as essential for enabling models, particularly LN-Ultra, to *surpass* teacher performance on complex reasoning tasks like GPQA-Diamond, highlighting its role in breaking the "teacher performance ceiling."
 - The comprehensive multi-stage post-training pipeline (NAS, distillation, CPT, SFT, RL for reasoning, RL for preference) is deemed necessary to produce a well-rounded model performing well across a wide variety of benchmarks.

5. **Strong Generalization and Alignment:**
 - The models demonstrate strong generalization capabilities on out-of-distribution tasks, such as LLM-as-a-Judge (JudgeBench), where LN-Ultra outperforms DeepSeek-R1 and other leading open-source models, trailing only behind top proprietary models.
 - RLHF (preference optimization) significantly improves general helpfulness and chat capabilities (e.g., LN-Super achieves an Arena Hard score of 88.3, beating Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o-2024-05-13), often enhancing performance across other benchmarks simultaneously.

6. **Open-Source Contributions:** The successful release of the models, the comprehensive Llama-Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset, and the training codebases (NeMo, NeMo-Aligner, Megatron-LM) provides a substantial contribution to the open-source community.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Llama-Nemotron series of models represents a significant advancement in the field of large language models, with profound implications for both research and practical applications.

1. **Democratization of State-of-the-Art Reasoning:** By releasing highly capable reasoning models under a commercially permissive open license, along with their training data and code, NVIDIA significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, developers, and enterprises. This move democratizes access to powerful AI, fostering innovation and allowing a broader community to build upon and customize these advanced reasoning capabilities.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Performance and Practicality:** The primary impact lies in demonstrating that cutting-edge reasoning capabilities do not have to come at the expense of prohibitive inference costs. The Llama-Nemotron models, particularly LN-Ultra, offer a compelling solution to the critical challenge of making advanced LLMs deployable and economically viable for real-world applications, especially those requiring complex, agentic behaviors. This efficiency could accelerate the adoption of LLMs in resource-constrained environments or high-throughput scenarios.

3. **Enabling Controlled and Adaptive AI Systems:** The dynamic "reasoning toggle" is a game-changer for user experience and resource management. It empowers users to adapt the model's behavior to specific task requirements, preventing unnecessarily verbose outputs for simple queries while enabling deep, multi-step reasoning when needed. This feature is crucial for building more intelligent, responsive, and resource-aware AI agents and applications.

4. **Advancement in LLM Training Methodologies:** The paper showcases a sophisticated, multi-stage training pipeline that effectively combines architectural optimization (NAS, FFN Fusion), knowledge distillation, supervised fine-tuning, and large-scale, curriculum-driven reinforcement learning. This integrated approach provides a robust blueprint for future LLM development, demonstrating how to achieve both high performance and efficiency, and how to enable models to surpass their teachers through structured RL. The detailed insights into infrastructure optimizations, including FP8 inference generation, are invaluable for scaling large model training efficiently.

5. **Shaping the Future of Open AI Ecosystems:** NVIDIA's commitment to open-sourcing not just the models but also the training data and tools (NeMo, Megatron-LM) reinforces its role as a key enabler of the open AI ecosystem. This approach encourages transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative progress, contrasting with more closed-source development paths and fostering a vibrant community around NVIDIA's AI platforms.

6. **Benchmarking and Competitive Landscape:** The Llama-Nemotron series sets new benchmarks for open-source models on challenging reasoning tasks, pushing the competitive boundaries in the AI research landscape. Its strong performance against both open and proprietary models (e.g., outperforming DeepSeek-R1 and competing with Claude/GPT models on various metrics) solidifies its position as a top-tier contender.

In conclusion, the Llama-Nemotron series significantly contributes to making highly intelligent, efficient, and controllable reasoning AI accessible and practical, thereby accelerating the broader development and deployment of advanced AI systems.
