# MVP-Nav: Multi-layer Value Map Planner Navigator

> [!summary] Summary
> MVP-Nav is an RGB-only navigation framework from Shanghai Jiao Tong University that reconstructs explicit 3D spatial occupancy from monocular observations, integrating high-level semantic reasoning with low-level physical constraints for Zero-shot Object Goal Navigation. It achieves a 65.4% Success Rate on the HM3D dataset, outperforming RGB-only baselines and competing with RGB-D systems, and demonstrates real-world success rates of 45-47.5% on an Agibot G1 robot.

## Problem

- RGB-only navigation systems face physical uncertainty from the absence of explicit depth information, leading to ambiguities in object distance and scale.
- High-level semantic reasoning in depth-free methods often misaligns with low-level physical constraints, resulting in physically infeasible or unsafe navigation plans.
- Existing RGB-only solutions lack a unified representation that coherently integrates discrete semantic understanding with continuous physical boundaries for robust navigation.

## Method

- **MVP-Nav** reconstructs explicit 3D physical occupancy from monocular RGB observations by projecting 2D semantic instances into 3D Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBBs), forming a ==Global Spatial Semantic List (GSSL)==.
- Introduces a ==Multi-layer Value Map (MVM)== that unifies VLM-generated semantic priorities, directional guidance, and low-level physical constraints into a shared cost space for geometric path planning.
- Employs a ==decoupled, recursive navigation framework== with a parallelized pipeline to integrate high-level reasoning and low-level execution, using semantic re-projection for safety verification.

## Results

- Achieved a **65.4%** Success Rate (SR) and **27.9%** Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) on the HM3D dataset, outperforming RGB-only baselines and rivaling several state-of-the-art RGB-D systems.
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of semantic re-projection, exploration memory, and LLM-based rating, with their removal leading to significant performance decreases (e.g., SR dropped from **65.4%** to **53.2%** without semantic re-projection).
- Demonstrated **45.0%** and **47.5%** success rates in challenging real-world university library and office environments on an Agibot G1 robot, showcasing drift-resistant execution and semantic safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured physical priors, explicitly reconstructed from monocular RGB via 3D foundation models and OBBs, can effectively compensate for the absence of active depth sensors in ZSON, achieving competitive performance.
> - The Multi-layer Value Map (MVM) provides a robust mechanism to align high-level semantic reasoning with low-level physical constraints through multiplicative fusion, enabling physically grounded geometric path planning.
> - A decoupled, recursive framework with parallelized high-level reasoning and real-time semantic re-projection for safety verification significantly improves efficiency and robustness against monocular depth estimation failures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Zero-shot Object Goal Navigation (ZSON). Historically, object navigation systems relied heavily on active sensors such as LiDAR or RGB-D cameras to acquire precise metric information for tasks like mapping and path planning. Examples include geometry-driven paradigms and methods like Stubborn, which leveraged explicit geometric baselines.

More recently, the research community has observed a shift towards RGB-only navigation. This transition is motivated by objectives such as reducing hardware costs, improving the accessibility of robotic platforms, and challenging agents to develop higher-level visual reasoning capabilities that mimic biological organisms' reliance on passive vision and internal priors for 3D structure perception.

Current RGB-only navigation approaches generally fall into two categories:
- **Semantic-driven methods:** These often utilize Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for panoramic parsing or heuristic exploration, as seen in works like PanoNav and PixNav. A limitation of these methods is the "Semantic-Physical Gap," where treating space as discrete semantic labels can lead to distorted topological relationships and semantically plausible but physically unreachable goal points.
- **End-to-end learning paradigms:** These approaches learn direct mappings from raw RGB pixels to control signals, with examples including OmniNav and NavFoM. While efficient, they typically treat spatial relations as implicit latent features, which can result in "Physical Uncertainty" (ambiguity of object distance and scale) and "Semantic-Physical Misalignment" due to the lack of explicit, persistent 3D environmental awareness.

MVP-Nav positions itself to address these limitations by proposing a physical-aware paradigm for RGB-only ZSON. The paper identifies a critical bottleneck in existing depth-free methods: the inability to explicitly recover 3D scale from 2D sequences, which leads to physical uncertainty and semantic-physical misalignment. MVP-Nav aims to bridge the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level physical constraints by grounding vision-language model (VLM) decisions within a reconstructed 3D volume, thereby providing a unified representation that aligns discrete semantic reasoning with continuous physical boundaries. This approach seeks to enable navigation that is both goal-directed and physically grounded, potentially achieving performance comparable to or surpassing methods that rely on active depth sensors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust and safe Zero-shot Object Goal Navigation (ZSON) using only RGB-only perception, without relying on active depth sensors. The motivation stems from several identified challenges inherent in depth-free navigation:

1. **Addressing Physical Uncertainty:** The absence of explicit depth information in RGB-only perception introduces significant ambiguity regarding object distance and scale. Current methods either operate at a high semantic level without sufficient geometric grounding or learn policies that lack explicit physical constraints, often leading to behaviors that are semantically logical but physically unsafe or inefficient.
2. **Mitigating Semantic-Physical Misalignment:** High-level semantic reasoning, particularly with Vision-Language Models (VLMs), can become disconnected from the low-level physical constraints of the environment. This misalignment manifests as agents proposing plausible routes that are obstructed or lead to collisions, because their "mental plan" is not consistently grounded in the latent 3D layout of the environment.
3. **Unifying Disparate Information Layers:** Existing RGB-only solutions often decouple physical perception from high-level planning or sacrifice geometric constraints during low-level control. There is a perceived lack of a unified representation that can integrate discrete semantic reasoning with continuous physical boundaries in a coherent manner.
4. **Mimicking Human-like Perception and Navigation:** The motivation also includes moving towards a navigation paradigm that emulates biological organisms, which primarily rely on passive vision and internal priors to infer 3D structure and navigate. This necessitates methods that can infer complex semantic meanings and latent geometric structures from monocular visual cues.
5. **Enhancing Accessibility and Reducing Hardware Costs:** By demonstrating effective navigation with RGB-only input, the research aims to reduce the hardware complexity and cost associated with advanced robotic systems, thereby enhancing the accessibility and deployability of embodied agents in consumer-grade and real-world scenarios.

To address these motivations, MVP-Nav sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- Reconstruct explicit physical occupancy from monocular RGB observations by leveraging 3D foundation models to project 2D semantic instances into 3D Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBB). This process aims to transform ambiguous depth cues into deterministic spatial representations, forming a global spatial semantic representation.
- Introduce a Multi-layer Value Map (MVM) mechanism to unify high-level VLM-generated semantic priorities and low-level physical constraints into a shared cost space. This is intended to enable physically grounded geometric path planning.
- Develop a framework that facilitates geometric path planning governed by both reconstructed physical occupancy and semantic guidance.
- Demonstrate that structured physical priors, as reconstructed by MVP-Nav, can effectively compensate for the absence of active depth sensors, achieving performance that is competitive with or superior to traditional depth-sensing methods in ZSON benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MVP-Nav operates as a decoupled, recursive navigation framework designed to integrate high-level semantic reasoning with low-level physical constraints. The system follows a loop-based architecture, alternating between high-level decision stages and low-level execution loops.

**A. Episode Initialization and Termination:**
The navigation episode begins with a Vision-Language Model (VLM) parsing a target image into a main goal and several sub-goals. The robot then performs a 360-degree in-place rotation to gather initial observations. The episode concludes when the VLM enters a "Judge" mode, indicating a potential goal discovery. A final 360-degree panoramic scan is performed, and LightGlue is used to match the goal image with historical views. The VLM verifies the target's presence, and a low score is assigned to misdetected goals to prevent re-attraction.

**B. MVP-Nav Loop Components:**
The core of MVP-Nav consists of four interconnected logical components:

1. **Physical Perception for 3D Reconstruction and Global Spatial Semantic List (GSSL):**
 - This module transforms monocular RGB image sequences into a structured, persistent 3D spatial memory, the Global Spatial Semantic List (GSSL).
 - It uses the visual geometry foundation model VGGT to process image sequences, regressing pseudo-depth maps and camera parameters. Pixels are back-projected to construct a local 3D point cloud.
 - Grounded-SAM generates semantic masks for target objects, general objects, and the floor.
 - The raw point cloud is projected into Bird's-Eye View (BEV) space to extract non-floor occupancy, which undergoes morphological inflation to create a local occupancy map for path planning. Floor points are isolated for real-time semantic re-projection verification.
 - Point clusters covered by target and general object masks are explicitly decoupled to fit separate Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBB). This prevents semantic cross-contamination and ensures critical target entities remain distinct in the GSSL.
 - GSSL entities are verified through majority voting on associated detections and require association across at least three independent frames to ensure temporal consistency.
 - Scale ambiguity is resolved by correlating the VGGT-predicted trajectory with the robot's metric trajectory (from onboard localization) using a similarity transform.
 - The GSSL is dynamically updated via 3D Intersection-over-Union (IoU). Consistent semantics and sufficient spatial overlap trigger a weighted fusion to refine entity properties.

2. **VLM-based High-level Reasoning:**
 - A VLM (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) acts as the cognitive core, processing the GSSL and navigation goal.
 - It assigns semantic importance scores (α_i ∈) to newly detected entities in the GSSL, leveraging common-sense priors to assess relevance to the goal. These scores are stored in the GSSL.
 - The VLM dynamically determines the navigation mode:
 - **Explore:** Activated when no high-value entities are identified. The VLM provides coarse directional suggestions (e.g., left-front, front, right-front) to guide the agent towards unmapped areas.
 - **Find:** Activated when the target or a strongly correlated cue is detected. The system plans a trajectory directly towards the spatial center of the entity.
 - **Judge:** Activated when the agent is near the main target. Multi-view observations are collected, and the VLM verifies the entity against the goal image before task termination.

3. **Multi-layer Value Map (MVM) Planning:**
 - This component integrates GSSL, VLM scores, and local occupancy information into a unified cost space to determine an optimal midterm goal (g_mid).
 - Three independent value layers are constructed in a 2D grid space:
 - **Semantic Value Map (Φ_sem):** Aggregates spatial contributions of GSSL entities, weighted by their VLM-assigned scores (α_i) and an exploration status (η_i = 1 for new, 0.5 for detected). The sum is clipped at 1.0, and values decay exponentially with distance from the entity's projected center.
 - **Directional Value Map (Φ_dir):** Biases movement towards the VLM-suggested orientation. It uses a Gaussian function of angular deviation (∆θ) and restricts exploration gain to the forward 180° sector.
 - **Traversability Value Map (Φ_trav):** Derived from the geometric occupancy map. It is inversely proportional to the distance to the nearest obstacle, ensuring safe navigation. Obstacles have a higher penalty weight (k=1.0) than unknown regions (k=0.5).
 - The final total value map (Φ_total) is generated by an element-wise product: Φ_total = Φ_sem ⊙ Φ_dir ⊙ Φ_trav. This multiplicative fusion ensures that the selected g_mid satisfies all semantic and physical constraints simultaneously; non-traversable areas result in a zero total value.
 - The midterm goal (g_mid) is set as the global maximum within Φ_total.

4. **Low-level Execution Loop:**
 - This layer translates g_mid into motor commands.
 - An A\* search is performed on the local occupancy map to establish a topological trajectory towards g_mid.
 - For high-frequency reactivity, a sliding-window Fast Marching Method (FMM) identifies a short-term goal (g_st) by finding the intersection of the A\* path and the window boundary. FMM solves the Eikonal equation on the MVM to generate a continuous potential field, guiding the robot smoothly while maintaining distance from hazards.
 - A **Safety Verification** step is implemented: before executing a FORWARD action, g_st is re-projected onto the image plane. If the re-projected point is not within the semantic floor mask (M_floor), the agent halts and rotates until the goal aligns with a traversable region.
 - To minimize latency, a parallelized pipeline is employed. High-level reasoning for the next stage is initiated when the agent approaches the current g_mid (within a Euclidean distance threshold of ϵ = 3m), overlapping VLM inference with physical movement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MVP-Nav was conducted on widely adopted indoor object-goal navigation benchmarks (HM3D, MP3D, and RoboTHOR) and in real-world environments.

**A. Benchmark Performance:**
- **Superiority over RGB-only baselines:** MVP-Nav demonstrated performance gains compared to existing depth-free methods. On the HM3D dataset, it improved the Success Rate (SR) from 43.5% (PanoNav) to 65.4% and the Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) from 23.7% to 27.9%. This suggests that its multi-view geometric perception framework constructs more effective spatial representations than traditional monocular schemes.
- **Competitiveness with RGB-D systems:** Despite relying solely on RGB input, MVP-Nav achieved performance levels comparable to or, in some cases, superior to several state-of-the-art RGB-D systems. For instance, its SR surpassed that of SG-Nav (54.0%) and UniGoal (54.5%), both of which utilize physical depth sensors and offline-trained scene graphs. This indicates that semantic planning grounded in physical constraints can enable capabilities approaching those of active depth sensing.

**B. Ablation Study and Analysis (on HM3D Dataset):**
- **Full-Sequence Reconstruction:** This approach resulted in Out-of-Memory (OOM) errors, cumulative drift, and geometric warping, supporting the necessity of MVP-Nav's recursive, stage-wise framework that reconstructs at smaller spatial scales.
- **Without Semantic Re-projection:** Removing this module led to a decrease in SR (from 65.4% to 53.2%) while marginally increasing SPL (from 27.9% to 30.5%). This trade-off suggests that without real-time floor traversability verification, agents adopt riskier, more direct trajectories, resulting in reduced physical safety and higher collision rates.
- **Without Exploration Memory:** Disabling exploration memory caused a significant drop in SR (to 38.2%) and SPL (to 13.4%), indicating that this component is critical for preventing repetitive search patterns and ensuring effective zero-shot navigation.
- **Without LLM Rating:** Removing LLM-based rating led to a decrease in SR (to 45.7%) and SPL (to 20.1%). This finding supports the value of LLM-driven common-sense reasoning for prioritizing candidate frontiers and guiding exploration.

**C. Component Selection Comparison:**
- **Physical Perception:** VGGT was identified as the optimal choice, balancing SR (65.4%) with moderate latency (5.31 s) and VRAM consumption (15.72 GB), outperforming Depth-Anything V3 (lower accuracy) and Map-Anything (prohibitive VRAM).
- **VLM Reasoning:** GPT-4o-mini exhibited the lowest end-to-end latency (5.59 s) and superior logical robustness in following spatial semantic instructions compared to alternatives like Llama3.2-vision and Gemini-3-flash.

**D. Computational Efficiency and Latency Analysis:**
- The primary bottlenecks were Physical Perception (5.31 s) and MLLM Semantic Reasoning (5.59 s), contributing to a total latency of 13.19 s per stage.
- MVP-Nav's parallelized pipeline, which overlaps high-level reasoning with physical execution, effectively mitigates this latency. By initiating planning for the next stage when the agent is within 3 meters of the current midterm goal, the system achieves an average actual delay of 1.6 s per stage, considered negligible compared to the average stage duration of 50.31 s. This validates the effectiveness of hierarchical decoupling in reconciling computational demands with fluid navigation requirements.

**E. Real-World Experiments:**
- MVP-Nav was deployed on an Agibot G1 wheeled robot in challenging university library corridor and office environments.
- It achieved an overall success rate of 45.0% (18/40 trials) in the library and 47.5% (19/40 trials) in the office, for tasks involving navigating over distances exceeding 15 meters to locate specific objects.
- The primary failure mode in long corridors (up to 60 meters) was attributed to the inherent difficulty of long-range monocular depth estimation, leading to occasional point cloud drifting and map distortion.
- **Key strengths observed in real-world deployment:**
 - **Drift-Resistant Execution:** The recursive stage-wise update mechanism reduced cumulative drift from monocular vision, enabling the robot to maintain precise alignment with physical corridor boundaries.
 - **Semantic Safety under Monocular Failure:** The semantic floor mask re-projection module effectively prevented collisions in environments with depth ambiguity (e.g., glass partitions, textureless walls) by triggering proactive recovery when projected goals fell outside traversable regions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MVP-Nav framework presents several significant contributions to the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics:

1. **Advancement in RGB-Only Navigation:** This work offers a method for achieving effective and safe zero-shot object goal navigation using solely RGB input, addressing fundamental challenges of physical uncertainty and semantic-physical misalignment that hinder existing depth-free approaches. By demonstrating performance comparable to or superior to some RGB-D systems, MVP-Nav suggests that well-structured physical priors can substantially compensate for the absence of active depth sensors.
2. **Novel Integration of Perception and Planning:** The proposed physical-aware paradigm, which reconstructs explicit 3D spatial occupancy from monocular observations using 3D foundation models and Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBBs), represents a method for generating structured geometric priors. The Multi-layer Value Map (MVM) architecture further provides a mechanism to align discrete semantic reasoning from MLLMs with continuous physical constraints within a unified cost space, facilitating a tri-objective goal selection policy.
3. **Enhanced Accessibility and Cost-Effectiveness:** By eliminating the reliance on expensive active depth sensors, MVP-Nav contributes to reducing the hardware costs and complexity associated with advanced robotic navigation. This potentially broadens the accessibility and applicability of embodied agents for consumer-grade robots and in environments where depth sensing might be impractical or unavailable.
4. **Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive evaluations across multiple simulation benchmarks (HM3D, MP3D, RoboTHOR) and successful deployment on a physical robotic platform (Agibot G1) in challenging real-world indoor and semi-outdoor environments underscore the framework's robustness and ability to generalize beyond simulated settings. The observed drift-resistant execution and semantic safety mechanisms in the face of monocular depth estimation failures highlight the system's practical reliability.
5. **Mimicking Biological Navigation:** The approach moves towards mimicking how biological organisms infer 3D structure and navigate using passive vision, fostering research into higher-level visual reasoning and spatial inference for embodied agents.

**Potential Impact:**
MVP-Nav's ability to perform complex navigation tasks with minimal sensor input could accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous robots in diverse applications, from household assistants to industrial inspection, where cost, size, and power consumption are critical factors. The framework also provides a foundation for future research in several directions:
- Improving reconstruction fidelity through fine-tuning 3D foundation models with navigation-specific priors.
- Developing hybrid memory structures that integrate implicit neural features with explicit, interpretable geometric outputs.
- Exploring tighter coupling between physical features and the execution layer to further reduce latency and enhance the agility of sensor-minimalist systems.

In summary, MVP-Nav represents an advancement in embodied AI by offering a practical and effective solution for depth-free object navigation, thereby contributing to more economical and versatile robotic capabilities.
