# Block-wise Adaptive Caching for Accelerating Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University developed Block-wise Adaptive Caching (BAC), a training-free inference acceleration method for Transformer-based Diffusion Policy models in robotics. This technique significantly reduces inference latency, achieving up to 3x speedup on real-world tasks and over 3.4x in simulations without degrading action generation quality, making Diffusion Policy practical for real-time robotic control.

## Problem

- Diffusion Policy's iterative denoising process incurs substantial computational overhead, rendering it impractical for real-time robotic control which requires high action frequencies (e.g., 30-50 Hz).
- Existing cache-based acceleration methods for image/video generation diffusion models are not directly transferable due to Diffusion Policy's Transformer-based denoisers and distinct data characteristics.
- A lack of customized feature caching methods specifically designed to leverage redundancies within the Diffusion Policy framework for efficient action generation.

## Method

- Introduced ==Block-wise Adaptive Caching== (**BAC**), a training-free plugin that adaptively updates and reuses cached intermediate action features at the individual block level.
- Developed an Adaptive Caching Scheduler (ACS) using dynamic programming to optimally identify cache update timesteps for each block, maximizing global feature similarities between cached and skipped features.
- Designed the Bubbling Union Algorithm (BUA) to mitigate the 'error surge phenomenon' in Feed-Forward Network (FFN) blocks by ensuring upstream blocks prone to high errors also update their caches when downstream FFNs do.

## Results

- Achieved up to **3x** speedup in real-world robotic pick-and-place tasks, reaching **71%** success rate at **39.2 Hz** with S=7 cache update frequency, significantly outperforming baselines (e.g., DDPM at **3%** success rate, **7.8 Hz**).
- Delivered 'lossless acceleration' on simulation benchmarks, maintaining average success rates (e.g., **0.79** on PH data) comparable to or slightly better than full-precision models while consistently providing over **3.4x** speedup.
- Demonstrated strong generalization by accelerating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (RDT-**1B** on ManiSkill) by up to **3.55x** with negligible performance drops, making them more practical for real-time deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature similarities within Diffusion Policy models exhibit non-uniform temporal dynamics and distinct patterns across different architectural blocks (Self-Attention, Cross-Attention, FFNs), necessitating block-wise adaptive caching.
> - Direct application of block-level adaptive schedules can lead to an 'error surge phenomenon,' particularly in FFN blocks, due to error amplification caused by upstream caching errors.
> - High episode homogeneity within single tasks allows for efficient, offline pre-computation of block-specific optimal caching schedules, incurring minimal real-time computational cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have emerged as a prominent paradigm in machine learning, initially demonstrating capabilities in image and video generation. More recently, this framework has been adapted to robotic control through Diffusion Policy, enabling the modeling of complex action distributions via conditional denoising processes. This approach has also been integrated into vision-language-action (VLA) models, facilitating dexterous and sophisticated robotic tasks.

Despite the demonstrated visuomotor modeling strengths of Diffusion Policy, its iterative denoising procedure introduces substantial computational overhead. This computational burden frequently renders Diffusion Policy impractical for real-time robotic control applications, which typically require high action frequencies (e.g., 30–50 Hz). For instance, a robotic arm task requiring 50 denoising steps at 1 millisecond per step would limit the action update rate to 10 Hz, falling short of real-time control needs.

The acceleration of diffusion models has been an active area of research, particularly for image and video generation tasks, with cache-based methods showing promise. However, existing cache-based acceleration techniques are not directly transferable to Diffusion Policy. This limitation is attributed to fundamental differences in data characteristics and model architectures, as Diffusion Policy often employs Transformer-based denoisers (Diffusion Transformers, DiT) rather than the U-Net architectures prevalent in image generation. Existing Transformer-based caching methods typically apply uniform caching schedules across all model blocks or require additional training, which may not be suitable for the specific dynamics of action generation.

This research addresses the gap in acceleration techniques specifically tailored for Diffusion Policy. It aims to develop a customized feature caching method that accounts for the architectural and data particularities of Diffusion Policy, thereby improving its computational efficiency without compromising action generation quality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the computational intensity of Diffusion Policy, which impedes its deployment in real-time robotic control scenarios. The iterative nature of the denoising process in Diffusion Policy, while effective for modeling complex action distributions, necessitates numerous computational steps for each action prediction. This results in inference latencies that are incompatible with the high action update rates required for smooth and responsive robotic operation. Current acceleration methods, largely designed for image or video generation diffusion models, are not directly applicable due to architectural dissimilarities (e.g., U-Net versus Transformer backbones) and distinct data characteristics inherent to action sequences.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To develop a customized feature caching method for Diffusion Policy:** The central goal is to create an acceleration technique that is specifically designed to leverage the redundancies within the Diffusion Policy framework.
2. **To achieve lossless action generation acceleration:** The method aims to significantly reduce inference time without degrading the quality or success rate of the generated actions. This is crucial for maintaining effective robotic control.
3. **To implement block-wise adaptive feature caching:** Based on observations of non-uniform temporal feature similarities and distinct block-specific patterns within Diffusion Policy models, the research aims to introduce a fine-grained caching strategy that adapts update schedules at the individual block level.
4. **To design an Adaptive Caching Scheduler (ACS):** A specific objective is to create a scheduler that can optimally identify the timesteps at which cached features should be updated, maximizing the similarity between cached and skipped features.
5. **To develop a mechanism for mitigating inter-block error propagation:** Acknowledging that naive block-wise caching can lead to error amplification, particularly within Feed-Forward Network (FFN) blocks, an objective is to devise an algorithm that truncates these caching errors.

The overall motivation is to transform Diffusion Policy from a computationally demanding framework into a practical solution for real-time robotic control by enhancing its inference efficiency as a training-free plugin, readily integratable with existing Transformer-based Diffusion Policy and VLA models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Block-wise Adaptive Caching (BAC), is a training-free plugin designed to accelerate Transformer-based Diffusion Policy by adaptively updating and reusing cached intermediate action features at the block level. The methodology is structured around two key observations and two main algorithmic components:

**Key Observations on Feature Similarities:**
The authors initially identified two characteristics of feature similarities within Diffusion Policy models:
1. **Non-uniform temporal dynamics:** Feature similarities across denoising timesteps do not change uniformly.
2. **Block-specific patterns:** Different architectural blocks (Self-Attention, Cross-Attention, Feed-Forward Networks) exhibit distinct temporal similarity patterns. These observations underpin the need for a block-wise adaptive caching strategy rather than a uniform one.

**Core Components of BAC:**

**A. Adaptive Caching Scheduler (ACS):**
The ACS aims to determine an optimal set of timesteps for cache updates that maximizes the global feature similarities between cached and skipped features for each block.
- **Optimization Objective:** The chosen metric for feature similarity is cosine similarity, which measures directional consistency. Consecutive similarity `s_k` is defined as the cosine similarity between features at timestep `k` and `k-1`. Interval similarity `ϕ(i, j)` is the sum of consecutive similarities over an interval `[i, j]`. The objective is to maximize the sum of interval similarities across `M` update steps, subject to the total number of updates. A larger `ϕ(i, j)` indicates lower caching error from reusing `b_j` over `[i, j]`.
- **Optimal Schedule Solver (Dynamic Programming):** Given the combinatorial complexity of selecting `M` update steps from `K` timesteps, a dynamic programming (DP) approach is employed.
 - `DP[m][j]` is defined as the maximum cumulative similarity when the `m`-th cache update occurs at timestep `j`.
 - The state transition equation `DP[m][j] = max_{0≤i<j} {DP[m-1][i] + ϕ(i, j)}` allows for efficient computation.
 - A pointer matrix `PTR[m][j]` is used to backtrack and reconstruct the optimal update schedule `C*` after the DP table is filled.
- **Offline Computation:** Leveraging the observed high episode homogeneity within a single task, the ACS computes these block-specific optimal schedules once before inference, incurring minimal additional computational cost during real-time operation.

**B. Error Surge Phenomenon and Analysis:**
Directly applying block-level adaptive schedules derived from ACS can lead to unexpected performance degradation, termed the "error surge phenomenon." This is particularly prevalent in FFN blocks.
- **Identification:** Experiments showed that block-wise updates, instead of reducing errors, sometimes amplify them in FFN blocks, resulting in sudden error surges where update-induced errors exceed reuse-induced errors.
- **Theoretical Analysis (Proposition 3.1):** The error propagation through FFN blocks is formally analyzed. For an FFN defined as `FFN(X) = W_out ϕ(W_in LN(X) + b_1) + b_2`, an upstream error `δ` propagates such that the output error `∆` is `∆ = W_out diag(ϕ'(U)) W_in (A - B) δ + O(∥δ∥^2)`. This indicates that FFNs introduce upstream caching errors during updates, partly due to the lack of intermediate normalization.
- **Empirical Confirmation:** Experiments demonstrated a strong positive correlation (Pearson r = 0.9894) between upstream and downstream FFN errors. The magnitudes of `W_1` and `W_2` in FFNs were also found to be approximately three times larger than those in attention blocks (`W_V`), suggesting a higher potential for error amplification. A complete error propagation chain showed that when a downstream block updates while its upstream blocks have large caching errors, a surge occurs that persists even after subsequent upstream updates.

**C. Bubbling Union Algorithm (BUA):**
To counteract the error surge phenomenon and truncate inter-block error propagation, the BUA revises the ACS-generated schedules. The core principle is that if an FFN block updates its cache, its upstream blocks with significant errors should also update.
- **Stage 1: Selecting Upstream Blocks with Large Caching Errors:** To identify upstream blocks that are major error sources, the average L1 norm of feature differences `ℓ_j` across all pairs of denoising timesteps is computed for each block `j`. The top `n` blocks with the largest `ℓ_j` (indicating large reuse-induced errors) are selected into a set `U`.
- **Stage 2: Unioning Update Timesteps:** For each upstream block `u` in `U`, its update schedule `C(u)` is augmented by unioning it with the update schedules `C(v)` of all FFN blocks `v` that are downstream of `u` (i.e., `C(u) = C(u) ∪ (∪_{v∈D(u)} C(v))`). This mechanism ensures that upstream blocks prone to high error also update their caches when their downstream FFNs do, thereby suppressing the propagation of significant errors.

**Implementation Details:**
BAC is implemented as a training-free plugin. Hyperparameters `n` (number of top upstream blocks) is set to 5 for simulation tasks and 3 for real-world experiments. All experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090D GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Block-wise Adaptive Caching (BAC) was evaluated through extensive experiments across real-world and simulation benchmarks, including its application to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, along with ablation studies.

**Real-world Benchmark Evaluation:**
Experiments on a Franka Research 3 arm performing a pick-and-place task demonstrated BAC's practical benefits.
- **Superior Performance:** BAC significantly outperformed all baselines in both success rate and inference frequency. With a cache update frequency (S=7) and `n=3` upstream blocks, BAC achieved a 71% success rate at 39.2 Hz.
- **Aggressive Caching:** Employing a more aggressive caching strategy (S=5) further boosted inference frequency to 45.1 Hz while retaining a 63% success rate.
- **Baseline Comparison:** In contrast, a standard DDPM with K=100 steps yielded a 3% success rate at an impractical 7.8 Hz. Accelerated baselines like DDIM (K=50) and Uniform caching (S=20) achieved limited success rates of 52% and 40%, respectively, attributed to degraded action generation quality.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** BAC (S=5) facilitated smooth and successful task execution with high generation fidelity and low latency. Baselines exhibited severe failure modes, such as premature picking, immobility due to kinematically unreachable joint poses, and early releases. These failures were linked to high end-to-end latencies (up to 54 seconds) and observation-action desynchronization, which caused stale predictions and induced erratic robotic motions.

**Simulation Benchmark Evaluation:**
BAC was assessed on standard robotic manipulation benchmarks including Robomimic (PH/MH data), Push-T, Multimodal Block Pushing, and Kitchen tasks.
- **Lossless Acceleration:** With S=10 cache update steps, BAC achieved "lossless acceleration," maintaining an average success rate of 0.79, 0.77, and 0.98 across Proficient Human (PH), Mixed Human (MH), and multi-stage tasks, respectively. These results were comparable to, or slightly better than, the full-precision DP-T model (0.76, 0.76, 0.98).
- **Consistent Speedup:** BAC consistently delivered acceleration rates exceeding 3.4x across most tasks.
- **Robustness:** BAC demonstrated superior robustness compared to Uniform and TeaCache baselines, especially on challenging tasks like Kitchen where other methods failed to generate correct actions. This was attributed to BAC's ability to reduce reuse-induced errors via ACS and mitigate update-induced errors via BUA.

**Acceleration on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:**
BAC was tested with RDT-1B on the ManiSkill benchmark, focusing on tasks such as PegInsertionSide and StackCube.
- **Generalization:** BAC achieved up to 3.55x acceleration with a negligible performance drop at S=2, and maintained lossless performance at S=1. These findings indicate BAC's strong generalization capabilities across Diffusion Policy-based VLA models.

**Ablation Study:**
The effectiveness of ACS and BUA was analyzed through three variants:
- **Effectiveness of ACS:** A "Unified ACS" schedule (ACS applied only to the first self-attention block, then universally used) showed performance improvement over Uniform caching, confirming the necessity of reducing reuse-induced errors.
- **Effectiveness of BUA:** A naive "Block-wise ACS" (ACS applied to each block independently) unexpectedly performed worse than Unified ACS, empirically substantiating the "error surge phenomenon." Integrating BUA into the block-wise schedule ("Block-wise ACS + BUA") recovered full-precision performance (average score of 0.79), demonstrating BUA's effectiveness in truncating error propagation.

**Additional Findings:**
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The choice of `n` (number of upstream blocks selected by BUA) was found to be crucial for mitigating update-induced errors, especially in challenging tasks, and its effectiveness was tied to `S` (cache update frequency).
- **Similarity Metric:** Cosine similarity performed better than Mean Squared Error (MSE), L1-Norm, and Wasserstein-1 distance in overall success rate, particularly for noisy data and complex tasks, while maintaining similar computational costs.
- **Episode Homogeneity:** Extensive qualitative and quantitative analyses demonstrated high inter-episode feature similarity within individual tasks (average per-matrix mean similarity ≥0.96, 95th-percentile entries ≥0.99), validating the efficiency of pre-computing schedules offline.
- **Integration with DDIM:** BAC provided additional speedup (2.2-3.5x) even when combined with the DDIM sampling scheduler (K=40), reinforcing its inherent acceleration capabilities beyond just denoising step reduction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Block-wise Adaptive Caching (BAC) as a method addressing a computational bottleneck in Diffusion Policy, with several implications for robotic control and broader applications of diffusion models.

**Enhanced Real-time Robotic Control:** The primary significance of BAC lies in its ability to significantly accelerate Diffusion Policy inference, achieving up to 3x speedup without compromising action generation quality. This advancement directly addresses the critical need for higher action frequencies (e.g., 30–50 Hz) in real-time robotic control, making Diffusion Policy a more practical and deployable solution for complex visuomotor tasks. The ability to achieve smooth and successful task execution, as demonstrated in real-world experiments, suggests a direct pathway for its integration into active robotic systems.

**Broadened Application of Diffusion Policy and VLA Models:** By offering a training-free and readily integrable plugin, BAC facilitates the adoption of Transformer-based Diffusion Policy across a wider range of applications, including sophisticated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The demonstrated effectiveness of BAC with RDT-1B on ManiSkill tasks indicates its potential to enhance the deployment of these large, expressive models in scenarios requiring real-time responsiveness. This could accelerate research and development in areas such as generalist robotics and human-robot interaction.

**Methodological Contributions to Deep Learning Optimization:** The work introduces novel methodological contributions to optimizing iterative deep learning models. The identification of non-uniform temporal feature dynamics and block-specific patterns within Diffusion Policy models motivates a more fine-grained, adaptive caching strategy. The Adaptive Caching Scheduler (ACS) provides an efficient dynamic programming approach for optimal cache schedule determination. Furthermore, the detailed analysis of the "error surge phenomenon" and the development of the Bubbling Union Algorithm (BUA) to mitigate inter-block error propagation offer insights into the stability and robustness challenges of caching in complex neural architectures. These concepts may be applicable to other iterative deep learning processes beyond diffusion models.

**Facilitating Reproducible Research:** The commitment to open-sourcing the implementation and detailed descriptions of experimental setups, theoretical assumptions, and ablation studies enhances the reproducibility of the research. This transparent approach supports further investigation and development within the community.

In summary, BAC contributes a practical and theoretically grounded solution to a significant performance challenge in Diffusion Policy, potentially enabling more widespread and effective application of this powerful paradigm in real-world robotic systems and advanced AI models.
