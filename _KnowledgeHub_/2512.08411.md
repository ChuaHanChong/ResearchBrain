# Prismatic World Model: Learning Compositional Dynamics for Planning in Hybrid Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> The Prismatic World Model (PRISM-WM) is a structured latent world model that decomposes hybrid physical dynamics—continuous motion punctuated by discrete events such as contacts and impacts—into composable primitives. Conventional monolithic latent world models enforce global continuity and over-smooth distinct dynamic modes (sticking vs. sliding, flight vs. stance), causing compounding errors in long-horizon planning rollouts. PRISM-WM uses a context-aware Mixture-of-Experts where a gating mechanism implicitly identifies the current physical mode and specialized experts predict the associated transition dynamics, with a latent orthogonalization objective enforcing expert diversity and preventing mode collapse. Across DMControl, Humanoid-Bench, and DiffRL continuous-control benchmarks, PRISM-WM provides a higher-fidelity substrate for trajectory optimizers such as TD-MPC and reduces rollout drift.

## Problem

- Physical robotic dynamics are hybrid: continuous motion is punctuated by discrete events (contacts, impacts), producing distinct dynamic modes such as sticking vs. sliding and flight vs. stance.
- Conventional monolithic latent world models enforce global continuity, over-smoothing these distinct modes and blurring transitions at physical boundaries.
- For a planner, this smoothing causes compounding errors during long-horizon lookaheads, making model-predictive search unreliable exactly where contact dynamics matter most.

## Method

- Introduces **PRISM-WM**, a latent world model that factorizes complex hybrid dynamics into non-redundant composable primitives without manual mode engineering.
- Uses a ==context-aware Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== forward-dynamics model where a ==gating mechanism== implicitly identifies the current physical mode and specialized experts predict the associated transition dynamics.
- Adds a ==latent orthogonalization objective== that enforces expert diversity, compelling experts to specialize in mutually exclusive dynamics and preventing ==mode collapse==.
- Retains the standard latent-world-model components—encoder $q(z_t|o_t)$, forward dynamics $p(z_{t+1}|z_t,a_t)$, and reward model $p(r_t|z_t,a_t)$—so it drops in as a substrate for both online planning and direct policy learning.
- Serves as a backbone for trajectory optimizers such as ==TD-MPC==, reducing rollout drift and stabilizing gradient propagation for policy learning.

## Results

- PRISM-WM outperforms **vanilla MoE** baselines by enforcing latent orthogonalization, with the gating mechanism capturing **semantic physical regimes** and exhibiting distinct mode transitions under perturbations.
- Evaluated across continuous-control tasks spanning multiple simulators: **DMControl** locomotion, **Humanoid-Bench** whole-body control, and high-dimensional contact-rich **DiffRL** environments, including high-dimensional humanoids and multi-task settings.
- Provides a **high-fidelity substrate** for trajectory optimization, improving planning reliability over extended horizons and mitigating negative interference in multi-task settings.
- Demonstrates robustness in **zero-shot sim-to-sim transfer**, supporting its claim as a domain-independent dynamics backbone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structurally disentangling hybrid dynamics into orthogonal expert primitives is presented as fundamentally essential for mastering contact-rich control, rather than an incremental MoE tweak.
> - The latent orthogonalization objective is the load-bearing component: without it, MoE experts collapse and the structural benefit disappears.
> - By acting as a model-agnostic dynamics substrate, PRISM-WM is complementary to downstream control paradigms (Dreamer, TD-MPC, PWM) rather than a competing policy method.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and planning, specifically focusing on the development of world models for robotic domains. World models are predictive models that learn to forecast future states and rewards within a learned representation space, acting as surrogate environments for training control policies or facilitating planning.

A significant challenge in robotic domains is the hybrid nature of physical dynamics, where continuous motion is interspersed with discrete events such as contacts, impacts, and changes in contact modes (e.g., sticking vs. sliding, flight vs. stance). Conventional latent world models typically employ monolithic neural networks. While these models are universal approximators, they often enforce global continuity, leading to an over-smoothed representation of these distinct dynamic modes. This over-smoothing is problematic for planning algorithms, as small prediction errors accumulate over long horizons, compromising the reliability of trajectory search, particularly at physical boundaries. It also results in gradient mismatch, hindering the precise propagation of planning signals during optimization.

Previous research in MBRL has seen advancements in latent dynamics models for synthetic rollouts (e.g., Dreamer series), value estimation, and representation learning. Algorithms like TD-MPC (Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control) and PWM (Policy Learning with Multi-Task World Models) conduct online control and policy learning within latent spaces. However, these methods often rely on monolithic world models that struggle with the discontinuities of hybrid systems.

The Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture has been applied in reinforcement learning for various purposes, including policies, value functions, multi-task transfer, and hierarchical or continual RL. Some works, like NMOE, have explored nested mixtures for identifying hybrid dynamical systems. However, this paper distinguishes itself by proposing a flat, context-aware MoE with a novel latent orthogonalization objective specifically designed to learn a non-redundant basis for residual dynamics in world models. This approach aims to address the mode collapse and compounding error issues prevalent in existing monolithic and vanilla MoE world models when dealing with hybrid dynamics, thereby providing a more robust and high-fidelity dynamics substrate for downstream MBRL algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the predictive fidelity and robustness of world models used in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and planning, particularly in robotic environments characterized by hybrid dynamics.

The core motivations stem from the limitations of current latent world models:

1. **Addressing Over-smoothing of Hybrid Dynamics:** Robotic tasks, such as legged locomotion and manipulation, involve continuous interaction with the environment, leading to frequent switches between distinct physical contact modes. These interactions introduce non-smooth, highly non-linear dynamics with sharp discontinuities in the underlying vector field. Existing world models, which typically use single, monolithic neural networks, tend to learn an "averaged" representation that over-smoothes these discontinuities. This architectural choice, while offering universal approximation capabilities, practically hinders the accurate modeling of piecewise physics.

2. **Mitigating Compounding Errors in Long-Horizon Planning:** The over-smoothing of dynamic modes results in persistent and compounding errors when planners perform long-horizon lookaheads. Small, single-step inaccuracies in the dynamics model accumulate significantly over time, leading to predictions of physically implausible states and ultimately compromising the reliability of the planning process. This forces planning algorithms to operate with restricted lookahead horizons, limiting their ability for long-term reasoning and decision-making.

3. **Improving Gradient Quality for Policy Learning:** For direct policy learning methods that use world models as differentiable simulators, a non-smooth or biased dynamic loss landscape due to over-smoothing can yield noisy gradients. These noisy gradients can destabilize policy optimization and impede convergence, especially in high-dimensional and contact-rich environments.

4. **Enabling Structural Decomposition of Dynamics:** The authors propose that world models need to incorporate structural priors derived from hybrid dynamical systems. Rather than forcing a global function to approximate piecewise physics, the motivation is to learn a data-driven decomposition of specialized latent regimes. This decomposition should allow distinct physical modes to be modeled separately without interference, leading to more accurate predictions.

5. **Preventing Mode Collapse and Ensuring Expert Diversity:** When employing Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, a common challenge is ensuring that different experts specialize and do not learn redundant features (mode collapse). This work is motivated to introduce a mechanism that promotes diversity among experts, compelling them to capture distinct components of the dynamics.

By addressing these issues, the research aims to create a foundational world model that can provide a high-fidelity substrate for various MBRL algorithms, enabling them to tackle complex, contact-rich control tasks that are currently beyond the reliable capabilities of monolithic models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Prismatic World Model (PRISM-WM) is introduced as a structured architecture designed to decompose complex hybrid dynamics into composable primitives. Its methodology hinges on a context-aware Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework coupled with a latent orthogonalization objective.

**A. Core Architecture Components:**

1. **Standard Latent World Model Setup:** PRISM-WM operates within the established framework of latent world models, comprising:
 - An **encoder** `q(z_t|o_t)` that maps high-dimensional observations `o_t` (e.g., pixels) to a compact latent state `z_t`.
 - A **forward dynamics model** `p(z_{t+1}|z_t, a_t)` which is the primary focus of PRISM-WM's innovation.
 - A **reward model** `p(r_t|z_t, a_t)` for predicting rewards in the latent space.

2. **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Dynamics Model:**
 - **Gating Network (Router):** A neural network `G_φ(c_t)` that takes a context vector `c_t` as input. For single-task settings, `c_t = [z_t, a_t]`; for multi-task settings, `c_t = [z_t, a_t, e_task]` (where `e_task` is a task embedding). The gating network outputs `K` raw scores, which are then passed through a softmax function with a temperature `τ_t` to produce normalized weights `w_t ∈ R^K`. These weights `w_{t,k}` represent the contribution of each of the `K` experts to the prediction, implicitly identifying the active physical regime.
 - **Expert Networks:** A set of `K` specialized neural networks `{E_k}_{k=1}^K`. Each expert `E_k` takes an input `x_t` (e.g., `[z_t, a_t]`) and outputs a penultimate-layer vector `u_k(x_t) ∈ R^d`. These experts are designed to specialize in the continuous flow dynamics within specific physical regimes.

3. **Latent Orthogonalization Objective:**
 - To prevent experts from learning redundant features and ensure each expert contributes uniquely to the dynamics, a differentiable **Gram-Schmidt process** is applied to the output `u_k` of the expert networks.
 - For each expert output `u_k`, an orthogonal residual `~v_k` is computed by subtracting its projections onto the previously normalized orthogonal vectors `v_i` (for `i < k`).
 - `~v_k = u_k - Σ_{i=1}^{k-1} ⟨v_i, u_k⟩ v_i`
 - The residual `~v_k` is then normalized to obtain `v_k`: `v_k = ~v_k / (||~v_k||_2 + ϵ)`, where `ϵ` is a small constant for numerical stability and well-conditioned Jacobian during backpropagation.
 - This process ensures that the set `{v_k}` forms an orthogonal basis, spanning a maximal-rank, non-redundant subspace for the residual dynamics `∆Z`. This structural prior prevents "mode collapse," where multiple experts might converge to model the same dynamic behavior.

4. **Next State Prediction:**
 - The predicted next latent state `ˆz_{t+1}` is computed as a residual addition to the current state `z_t`, formed by a weighted sum of the orthogonal expert outputs `v_k`:
 - `ˆz_{t+1} = z_t + Σ_{k=1}^K w_{t,k} ⋅ v_k(x_t)`
 - This formulation allows the gating network to dynamically combine the orthogonal bases, effectively reconstructing the unordered physical modes identified by the experts.

**B. Integration with MBRL Paradigms:**

PRISM-WM is designed to be a versatile backbone for existing MBRL frameworks:

- **Online Planning (e.g., TD-MPC):** By providing a dynamics model that accurately decomposes hybrid transitions, PRISM-WM aims to reduce the one-step model residual (from `ε` to approximately `ε/K^α`). This significantly curbs the compounding drift over long planning horizons, leading to more reliable trajectory optimization.
- **Direct Policy Learning (e.g., PWM):** The orthogonal basis constraint is intended to maintain a well-conditioned Jacobian. This ensures stable gradient propagation across discontinuous physical boundaries, transforming complex loss landscapes into more functionally smooth convex blends, which facilitates stable policy optimization.

**C. Mathematical Formalization and Stability Analysis:**

The model formalizes dynamics as `∆z_t = Σ_{k=1}^K w_{t,k} ⋅ proj_{V_k}(Φ(x_t))`, where `Φ(x_t)` is a shared encoding and `proj_{V_k}` ensures non-redundant components. The authors emphasize evaluating Mutual Information between gating weights `w_t` and ground-truth boolean contact tensors from the MuJoCo simulator to confirm that the learned decomposition aligns with physical events. The use of a stabilized Gram-Schmidt process with `ϵ`-perturbation ensures a well-conditioned Jacobian, preventing gradient explosion and maintaining the stability of the convex combination of Lipschitz experts.

**D. Experimental Design:**

The experimental evaluation involves benchmarking PRISM-WM against state-of-the-art model-based (TD-MPC2, DreamerV3, PWM) and model-free (SAC) methods across diverse continuous control tasks. These include:
- Standard locomotion tasks from **DeepMind Control Suite**.
- Whole-body control tasks from **Humanoid-Bench**.
- High-dimensional, contact-rich environments from **DiffRL**.

PRISM-WM is integrated into its target paradigms (TD-MPC2 and PWM) by replacing their monolithic dynamics and reward models. The number of experts `K` is primarily set to 4, with ablation studies exploring `K=2, 4, 6`. Evaluation metrics include sample efficiency, asymptotic performance, multi-task generalization scores, zero-shot sim-to-sim transfer robustness, latent predictive fidelity, reward estimation accuracy, and real-time inference efficiency. Qualitative visualizations are used to interpret the mode-switching behavior and whole-body control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that PRISM-WM exhibits enhanced performance and robustness across various continuous control benchmarks compared to monolithic baselines.

**A. High-Dimensional Locomotion on DiffRL:**
PRISM-WM achieved higher sample efficiency and asymptotic returns across five proprioceptive locomotion tasks from the DiffRL suite. On quadrupedal (Ant, Anymal) and Hopper tasks, it showed faster convergence. Crucially, in high-dimensional humanoid environments (Humanoid, SNU-Humanoid), monolithic TD-MPC2 and model-free baselines experienced policy collapse, which the authors attribute to compounding prediction errors caused by over-smoothing non-smooth contact events. PRISM-WM, by decomposing these dynamics, prevented such failures, leading to stable optimization.

**B. Multi-Task Generalization:**
In a benchmark of 30 DeepMind Control Suite tasks, PRISM-WM attained a mean normalized score of 0.531, representing a 23.5% improvement over the monolithic TD-MPC2 baseline (0.430). It outperformed the baseline on 26 out of 30 tasks, with notable gains on `cheetah-run-backwards` (+0.31) and `reacher-three-hard` (+0.28). Performance on established manipulation tasks remained stable. This robust generalization is attributed to the gating network's ability to cluster tasks with similar physical properties into distinct expert active regions, thus mitigating negative interference between conflicting global dynamics.

**C. High-Dimensional Humanoid Control:**
PRISM-WM surpassed baselines across four tasks (Run, Slide, Pole, Maze) from the Humanoid-Bench suite, which involve unstable hybrid dynamics and require accurate predictions of continuous locomotion with contact discontinuities. While monolithic TD-MPC2 exhibited learning volatility and policy collapses, PRISM-WM maintained stable gaits and reward growth, particularly in tasks requiring sustained locomotion alongside interactive objectives like balancing a pole or avoiding boundaries. This indicates its capacity to model complex whole-body behaviors by isolating distinct dynamic modes.

**D. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Sim Transfer:**
For zero-shot transfer from IsaacGym (soft-contact) to MuJoCo (stiff-contact) on humanoid-bench tasks, PRISM-WM demonstrated significant robustness. The monolithic MLP baseline suffered a catastrophic 47.8% performance drop and a 12% success rate, mainly due to over-smoothing contact boundaries. A vanilla MoE showed only marginal improvement. In contrast, PRISM-WM maintained a high target return (386.3) with only an 11.8% drop and achieved an 88% success rate. The authors highlighted that orthogonalization constrained vertical support forces and horizontal friction residuals to distinct basis subspaces, allowing the gating network to adapt to changes in friction coefficients without compromising global dynamics. Qualitatively, under lateral perturbation, the gating mechanism exhibited a sharp phase shift to stabilization experts, indicating a deliberate transition to recovery modes and a rapid return to nominal gait.

**E. Ablation Study:**
- **Fidelity of Long-Horizon Value Estimation:** PRISM-WM, with its orthogonalization layer, maintained higher latent cosine similarity (directional preservation of state trajectories) and superior reward estimation fidelity over extended planning horizons compared to monolithic models and even a vanilla MoE. While a scaled vanilla MoE could match dynamics fidelity, it showed higher predictive variance and degraded reward accuracy due to expert redundancy and overfitting. PRISM-WM prevented rapid manifold collapse and physically infeasible states.
- **Robustness to Expert Count (K):** All PRISM-WM variants with varying expert counts (K=2, 4, 6) outperformed the monolithic baseline. K=4 was identified as optimal, balancing representational capacity and computational efficiency. The study concluded that performance gains primarily stem from the principle of structural decomposition rather than precise hyperparameter tuning of K.
- **Real-time Inference Efficiency:** PRISM-WM (K=4) achieved a throughput of 7,712 Hz, which was slightly faster than the monolithic MLP baseline (7,499 Hz) on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, with minimal memory overhead. This indicates that the MoE architecture, by activating only a subset of experts per step, does not introduce significant latency overheads for real-time control.

**F. Qualitative Visualization (Agile Whole-Body Control):**
A stroboscopic visualization of the humanoid in the MuJoCo Pole task demonstrated PRISM-WM's ability to synthesize sharp torso-dodging maneuvers while maintaining stable foot-ground contacts. This structural fidelity, resulting from the orthogonal decomposition, allowed the agent to avoid falls typical of monolithic models during obstacle avoidance, which usually struggle with latent manifold collapse when approximating simultaneous free-space movements and stiff ground reactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Prismatic World Model (PRISM-WM) represents a notable contribution to Model-Based Reinforcement Learning, particularly for applications in robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation in MBRL:** The research directly addresses the problem of over-smoothing hybrid dynamics by monolithic world models, a long-standing bottleneck that limits the predictive accuracy and planning horizons in complex, contact-rich robotic environments. By explicitly decomposing dynamics into specialized primitives, PRISM-WM mitigates compounding errors, thus improving the reliability of MBRL algorithms.

2. **Enhanced Predictive Fidelity for Planning:** PRISM-WM provides a higher-fidelity dynamics substrate for trajectory optimization algorithms. By reducing rollout drift and producing more physically consistent predictions, it enables planners (e.g., TD-MPC) to operate over longer horizons and make more informed decisions, potentially leading to the solution of tasks previously intractable for conventional world models.

3. **Stabilized Policy Learning:** The latent orthogonalization objective contributes to maintaining a well-conditioned Jacobian, which is crucial for stable gradient propagation during policy learning (e.g., in PWM frameworks). This can lead to more robust and faster convergence of policies in domains with discontinuous physical interactions.

4. **Improved Generalization and Multi-task Learning:** The context-aware Mixture-of-Experts architecture, combined with orthogonalization, enables PRISM-WM to implicitly cluster tasks by their shared physical properties. This structural disentanglement allows for the isolation of task-specific knowledge, significantly reducing negative interference in multi-task settings and fostering the development of more scalable and capable generalist agents.

5. **Robustness to Reality Gap:** The demonstrated success in zero-shot sim-to-sim transfer, especially between simulators with different contact models (IsaacGym to MuJoCo), highlights PRISM-WM's capacity for learning physically grounded and transferable dynamics. This robustness is critical for deploying agents trained in simulation to real-world robotic systems, where discrepancies in physics models are common.

6. **Interpretability of Dynamic Modes:** The gating mechanism's ability to exhibit distinct mode transitions under perturbations and implicitly identify physical regimes (e.g., locomotion vs. balance) offers a degree of interpretability. This provides insights into how the model perceives and switches between different physical behaviors, which can be valuable for understanding and debugging complex robotic systems.

7. **Foundational Model for Embodied AI:** By offering a method to robustly model complex physics through orthogonal primitives, PRISM-WM establishes a structural foundation for future general-purpose embodied agents. This could pave the way for more sophisticated robotic behaviors that seamlessly integrate continuous motion with discrete interaction events.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the manual selection of expert count K and the need to test in real-world robotics, the demonstrated capabilities suggest that PRISM-WM is a significant step towards building more accurate, robust, and generalizable world models for artificial intelligence in physical environments.
