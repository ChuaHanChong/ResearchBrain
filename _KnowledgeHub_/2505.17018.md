# SophiaVL-R1: Reinforcing MLLMs Reasoning with Thinking Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> SophiaVL-R1 improves multimodal reasoning by integrating a "thinking reward" that evaluates the quality of the model's reasoning process alongside traditional outcome-based rewards. This dual-reward approach allows smaller models, such as SophiaVL-R1-7B, to surpass larger models like LLaVA-OneVision-72B on benchmarks including MathVista and MMMU.

## Problem

- Current reinforcement learning approaches for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) rely solely on outcome-based rewards, neglecting the quality of the reasoning process.
- Models can arrive at correct answers through flawed or unreliable reasoning strategies, which are not penalized by outcome-only rewards, potentially reinforcing non-generalizable patterns.
- This limitation makes it difficult to ensure that MLLMs develop robust and trustworthy reasoning capabilities, especially in complex cross-modal understanding tasks.

## Method

- SophiaVL-R1 introduces a ==dual-reward system== that evaluates both the final outcome and the quality of the intermediate reasoning steps (a "thinking reward").
- A dedicated ==thinking reward model==, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and trained on manually annotated reasoning examples, assesses reasoning quality based on logical soundness and error identification.
- The ==Trust-GRPO algorithm== dynamically adjusts the influence of thinking rewards using a trustworthiness weight (γ) and an annealing schedule to ensure reliable integration and prevent reward exploitation.

## Results

- SophiaVL-R1-7B outperforms LLaVA-OneVision-72B on the MathVista benchmark despite having significantly fewer parameters and achieves **4.5 points** higher on MMMU.
- Ablation studies confirm that the trained thinking reward model, trustworthiness weight (γ), and annealing strategy are all crucial components contributing to performance.
- Training curves show that SophiaVL-R1 consistently achieves higher rewards and demonstrates faster improvement throughout training compared to baseline approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evaluating the quality of the reasoning process, in addition to the final outcome, is essential for developing robust and reliable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
> - Model-generated rewards for reasoning quality can be effectively integrated into training through trustworthiness mechanisms, mitigating risks like reward hacking and ensuring stable learning.
> - Advanced training methodologies, such as dual-reward systems and trustworthy policy optimization, can enable smaller MLLMs to achieve performance competitive with or superior to significantly larger models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) and, more recently, multimodal large language models (MLLMs), has demonstrated progress in eliciting reasoning capabilities. A prevalent paradigm involves reinforcement learning (RL) guided by rule-based outcome rewards, as exemplified by models like DeepSeek-R1 for LLMs and R1-OneVision, OpenVLThinker, and Video-R1 for MLLMs. These methods primarily focus on the correctness of the final answer, using a binary or score-based rule to provide reward signals.

However, a recognized limitation of this outcome-reward-centric approach is its insufficient supervision over the intermediate "thinking process." Models may arrive at correct answers through flawed or sub-optimal reasoning trajectories (e.g., "wrong thinking, correct answer" scenarios), which can hinder their generalization ability. This issue has motivated research into "process reward models" (PRMs), which aim to provide feedback at intermediate reasoning steps. Prior PRM approaches, such as those by Lightman et al. (2023) and VisualPRM (Wang et al., 2025b), often impose rigid step-wise constraints or face challenges in evaluating the correctness of individual steps, potentially leading to reward exploitation (e.g., generating repetitive or meaningless steps).

This work positions itself within this landscape by attempting to integrate reward signals for the thinking process in MLLM reasoning, specifically within the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) RL framework. In contrast to existing step-wise PRMs, SophiaVL-R1 proposes a holistic evaluation of reasoning quality. Furthermore, it addresses the potential unreliability and "reward hacking" issues associated with model-generated rewards, a challenge observed in various applications of LLM-as-a-judge paradigms (Ye et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025a). The proposed Trust-GRPO method aims to mitigate these risks, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and generalizable MLLM reasoning systems. The research contributes to the ongoing effort to guide MLLMs towards not only producing correct answers but also employing sound and coherent reasoning strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning capabilities and generalization of multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) by explicitly incorporating supervision over their thinking processes during reinforcement learning (RL) training. The authors identify a critical limitation in current rule-based RL paradigms, which primarily rely on outcome rewards to evaluate only the final answer. This approach, while effective for achieving correct results, often fails to assess the quality of the intermediate reasoning steps, potentially leading models to adopt sub-optimal, non-generalizable, or even flawed reasoning strategies that coincidentally yield correct answers.

To address this, the specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **To introduce thinking process supervision:** The central motivation is to guide MLLMs towards sound reasoning processes, not just correct final outcomes. This involves developing a mechanism to evaluate the quality of an MLLM's entire "chain-of-thought" or thinking trajectory. The authors aim to move beyond rigid step-wise process rewards, which can be overly restrictive or exploitable, and instead focus on a holistic assessment of reasoning quality.

2. **To develop a robust thinking reward model:** A key objective is to train a model capable of generating high-quality "thinking rewards." This model must be able to discern logical soundness, correct application of methods, identification of errors, language consistency, and redundancy in a given reasoning process. The motivation is to provide a granular reward signal that distinguishes between favorable and flawed reasoning, even if both lead to the same final answer.

3. **To mitigate the unreliability of model-generated rewards:** Model-generated rewards, particularly those from other LLMs, can be prone to "reward hacking" or inconsistencies, where high rewards might be assigned to sub-optimal processes or even incorrect answers. A crucial objective is to develop a mechanism, named "Trust-GRPO," to assess and adaptively adjust the influence of these thinking rewards based on their perceived trustworthiness. This is motivated by the need to prevent the learning process from being derailed by erroneous reward signals, ensuring that the model learns from reliable feedback.

4. **To balance thinking rewards with outcome rewards:** As training progresses, rule-based outcome rewards typically become more reliable indicators of overall performance. Therefore, an objective is to design a training strategy that gradually transitions the model's reliance from thinking process rewards to outcome rewards. This annealing strategy is motivated by the desire to leverage the exploratory guidance of thinking rewards in early stages while emphasizing the precision of outcome rewards in later stages, thereby optimizing learning efficiency and stability.

5. **To demonstrate enhanced reasoning and generalization:** Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to create an MLLM (SophiaVL-R1) that exhibits superior reasoning and generalization capabilities across a diverse range of multimodal tasks, including mathematical reasoning and general vision-language understanding. The aim is to show that incorporating trustworthy thinking process supervision leads to a more capable and robust MLLM, even outperforming significantly larger models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SophiaVL-R1 framework integrates model-generated thinking rewards with rule-based outcome rewards within a modified Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training algorithm, termed Trust-GRPO. The approach is structured around three main components: dataset composition, thinking reward model, rule-based outcome reward, and the Trust-GRPO training algorithm.

#### 3.1. Dataset Composition (SophiaVL-R1-130k)

To support training, a multimodal reasoning dataset named SophiaVL-R1-130k was curated. This dataset comprises 130,000 examples, aggregated from various publicly available text-only and multimodal sources. It is organized into five categories: Math, OCR, Chart, Knowledge, and General, covering diverse reasoning scenarios to ensure robust model performance and generalization.

#### 3.2. Thinking Reward Model

The research develops a thinking reward model designed to evaluate the quality of an MLLM's reasoning process at a holistic level, rather than step-by-step. This model outputs a score between 0 and 1, independent of the final answer's correctness.

- **Training Data Collection:** A dataset of 470,331 (question, response) pairs was collected from the outputs of Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct during its initial GRPO training on SophiaVL-R1-130k. This data captures both effective and flawed reasoning patterns.
- **Annotation:** Each response in the collected data was annotated by a more advanced MLLM, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, which assigned a "thinking reward" based on five criteria: Logical Soundness, Correct Reasoning, Error Identification, Language Consistency, and Redundancy. A specific prompt (detailed in Appendix A) was used for this evaluation.
- **Data Filtering and Balancing:** To ensure label quality and a balanced distribution across reward levels, rule-based filtering removed noisy samples, and uniform sampling was applied, resulting in a high-quality dataset of 156,703 samples, named SophiaVL-R1-Thinking-156k.
- **Model Training:** The thinking reward model was initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and fine-tuned using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the SophiaVL-R1-Thinking-156k dataset. During training, the model learned to predict a thinking reward score given a question and its reasoning process.

#### 3.3. Rule-Based Outcome Reward

Consistent with prior work (e.g., DeepSeek-R1), rule-based functions are employed to generate outcome reward signals. These task-specific functions assess the final answer's correctness by comparing it against ground-truth answers. Reward definitions vary by task type:
- **Numerical:** Binary reward for exact match.
- **Multiple Choice:** Reward based on matching the ground-truth choice.
- **OCR:** Negative Word Error Rate (WER).
- **Free-form Text:** Average of ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L scores.

#### 3.4. Trustworthy Group Relative Policy Optimization (Trust-GRPO)

This is the core RL algorithm designed to integrate both thinking rewards and outcome rewards while mitigating the risks associated with potentially unreliable model-generated thinking rewards. The reasoning model is initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and trained on SophiaVL-R1-130k.

- **Combined Reward Formulation:** For each response *i*, the total reward *R_i* is defined as a combination of the outcome reward *R_o_i* and the thinking reward *R_t_i*:
 *R_i = R_o_i + γαe^(-steps/T) * R_t_i*
 Here, *α* is a hyperparameter controlling the initial impact of the thinking reward, *steps* is the current global training step, and *T* is the total number of training steps, used for the annealing strategy.

- **Trustworthiness Weight (γ):** This weight dynamically adjusts the influence of the thinking reward based on its perceived reliability for a given question's response group.
 - Responses to a question *q* are grouped into *G_correct* (those with *R_o_i ≥ 0.5*) and *G_wrong* (those with *R_o_i < 0.5*).
 - The average thinking rewards for these groups, *μ_c* and *μ_w*, are calculated.
 - The trustworthiness weight *γ* is defined:
 - *γ = 1* if *μ_c ≥ μ_w* (thinking reward aligns with outcome)
 - *γ = e^(μ_c - μ_w)* if *μ_c < μ_w* (thinking reward is higher for wrong answers, indicating unreliability, thus *γ* is reduced exponentially).
 This mechanism assigns a lower *γ* when thinking rewards are abnormally high for incorrect answers, signaling potential misalignment or unreliability without additional computational overhead.

- **Time-based Annealing Strategy:** The term *e^(-steps/T)* implements an exponential decay schedule, gradually reducing the influence of the thinking reward as training progresses. This encourages the model to primarily rely on the more accurate rule-based outcome reward in later stages, while leveraging the thinking reward for initial exploration and guidance.

- **Advantage Calculation:** The advantage *A_i* for each response is computed by normalizing its reward *R_i* against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within its response group.

- **GRPO Objective:** The policy *π_θ* is updated by maximizing the GRPO objective function, which incorporates the calculated advantages, a clipping mechanism for policy ratios, and a KL divergence regularization term against a reference policy *π_ref*.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of SophiaVL-R1 across various multimodal reasoning and general benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Math Reasoning Benchmarks

SophiaVL-R1-7B consistently achieved competitive performance on mathematical reasoning tasks:
- **MathVista:** SophiaVL-R1-7B attained an accuracy of 71.3%. This surpassed the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct models trained with GRPO (69.9%) and SFT+GRPO (66.8%). Notably, it also outperformed the significantly larger LLaVA-OneVision-72B (68.4%).
- **MathVerse:** SophiaVL-R1-7B achieved 48.8% accuracy. This represented an 18.1-point improvement over InternVL2.5-8B-VisualPRM (30.7%), indicating that the proposed Trust-GRPO method, particularly its holistic thinking reward, provided a more effective reward signal compared to PRM-based methods. SophiaVL-R1 also consistently outperformed other models across all sub-tasks within MathVerse.

#### 4.2. Performance on General Benchmarks

Beyond mathematical reasoning, SophiaVL-R1-7B demonstrated strong generalization capabilities across a suite of general multimodal benchmarks:
- **MMMU (Multi-discipline Multimodal Understanding):** SophiaVL-R1-7B scored 61.3%, outperforming LLaVA-OneVision-72B (56.8%) by 4.5 points.
- **MME, ChartQA, MMBench, MMStar:** SophiaVL-R1-7B achieved scores of 2403.8, 88.5%, 85.4%, and 66.7% respectively. These results consistently surpassed those of Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with GRPO or SFT+GRPO, and generally outperformed other contemporary MLLMs, including URSA-8B and R1-Onevision-7B. This suggests that the reasoning improvements fostered by SophiaVL-R1 are transferable to a broader range of multimodal tasks.

#### 4.3. Performance of Thinking Reward Model

The quality of the trained thinking reward model (3B parameters) was evaluated on VLRewardBench, a benchmark for multimodal reward models:
- The SophiaVL-R1 thinking reward model achieved an overall accuracy of 48.6% and a macro accuracy of 48.9%. These scores were higher than those of larger models like GPT-4o-mini (41.5% overall, 44.8% macro) and Qwen2-VL-72B (39.5% overall, 43.0% macro), as well as the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (43.1% overall, 43.0% macro).
- Notably, the model demonstrated strong performance in detecting "Hallucination," indicating its ability to identify unreliable responses, which is a key requirement for the trustworthiness mechanism.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contributions of key components of SophiaVL-R1:
- **Effect of the Trained Thinking Reward Model:** Replacing the trained thinking reward model with an untrained Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model (SophiaVL-R1-wo-trained-TRM) resulted in performance comparable to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B+GRPO baseline. This indicated that an untrained reward model offered limited guidance and highlighted the critical importance of the SophiaVL-R1-Thinking-156k dataset and the SFT training pipeline for generating effective thinking rewards.
- **Effect of the Trustworthiness Weight γ:** Removing the trustworthiness weighting (SophiaVL-R1-wo-trust) led to a performance drop across all benchmarks compared to the full SophiaVL-R1 model. This result supports the effectiveness of the trustworthiness weighting in ensuring reliable thinking process rewards.
- **Effect of the Time-based Annealing Strategy:** The model without both trustworthiness weighting and annealing (SophiaVL-R1-wo-trust-and-annealing) generally performed worse than SophiaVL-R1-wo-trust. This suggests that without annealing, the model might over-exploit potentially unreliable thinking rewards, indicating the benefit of gradually reducing the thinking reward's influence in later training stages.
- **Training Curve Analysis:** Analysis of mean rule-based outcome rewards during training showed that SophiaVL-R1 achieved the highest reward and exhibited faster improvement. The variant directly combining thinking and outcome rewards without trustworthiness weighting or annealing performed worse during training, reinforcing the necessity of these proposed strategies for stable RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces SophiaVL-R1, an MLLM trained with the Trust-GRPO algorithm, representing an advancement in reinforcing reasoning capabilities. The work's significance and potential impact can be summarized in several areas:

1. **Advancement in Reasoning Supervision:** The study highlights the value of providing explicit supervision over the intermediate "thinking process" in MLLMs, moving beyond sole reliance on final outcome rewards. This approach can lead to models that not only produce correct answers but also employ more logically sound and generalizable reasoning strategies. This is a notable step in addressing the limitation where models might achieve correct answers through flawed internal logic.

2. **Mitigation of Reward Model Unreliability:** The introduction of the Trust-GRPO algorithm, with its trustworthiness weighting (γ) and time-based annealing strategy, offers a practical solution to a known challenge in RL with model-generated rewards: their potential unreliability and susceptibility to reward hacking. By dynamically assessing the credibility of thinking rewards and adjusting their influence, the method enhances the stability and effectiveness of the learning process, making it more robust against noisy or misleading feedback.

3. **Improved Generalization and Performance:** SophiaVL-R1-7B demonstrates performance advantages over existing MLLMs, including larger models (e.g., outperforming LLaVA-OneVision-72B on several benchmarks despite having 10 times fewer parameters). This suggests that enhancing reasoning through process-level supervision can lead to more efficient and capable models. Its strong performance across both specialized mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse) and general multimodal benchmarks (MMMU, MME) indicates improved generalization, which is crucial for real-world applications.

4. **Contribution to Multimodal RL Research:** The work contributes to the growing body of research on applying reinforcement learning to multimodal models. It offers insights into designing effective reward mechanisms and training strategies for complex reasoning tasks in vision-language domains. The holistic approach to thinking reward design, avoiding rigid step-wise constraints, presents an alternative to traditional PRMs.

5. **Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to making all code, models, and datasets publicly available promotes reproducibility and facilitates further research in this area. This allows other researchers to build upon SophiaVL-R1's foundations, potentially accelerating advancements in MLLM reasoning and RL techniques.

The potential impact extends to applications requiring robust and explainable reasoning, such as advanced educational tools, scientific discovery platforms, and intelligent assistants capable of complex problem-solving. By fostering more human-like reasoning processes, this research contributes to the development of more trustworthy and capable artificial intelligence systems.
