# UniLM-Nav: A Unified Framework for Zero-Shot Last-Mile Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> UniLM-Nav introduces a unified zero-shot framework that leverages a multimodal large language model (MLLM) to infer manipulation-ready base poses for robots during last-mile navigation. This approach achieved an overall success rate of 23.77% on the HomeRobot OVMM benchmark, outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods in open-vocabulary mobile manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robot navigation systems typically provide only approximate object locations, often insufficient for precise 'manipulation-ready' base poses.
- Existing last-mile navigation methods rely on manual annotations or extensive task-specific training data, which limits their scalability and generalization to novel, open-vocabulary tasks.
- Prior MLLM-based approaches primarily use object-level cues, struggling with fine-grained spatial reasoning and adherence to geometric constraints critical for many manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **UniLM-Nav** is a zero-shot MLLM framework that decomposes last-mile navigation into three sequential, MLLM-driven stages: ==view selection==, ==task-conditioned affordance grounding==, and ==geometry-aware base-pose reasoning==.
- The framework explicitly integrates 3D geometric information (e.g., lifting 2D affordance points to 3D, robot configuration) with MLLM visual-linguistic reasoning to overcome limitations of 2D visual inference for metric properties.
- The MLLM selects an optimal reference view, grounds task instructions to a precise 2D ==affordance point==, and predicts a robot base position, while the heading is geometrically computed towards the 3D affordance point.

## Results

- UniLM-Nav achieved a **23.77%** Overall Success Rate on the HomeRobot OVMM benchmark, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art MoTo (**20.64%**) and other baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness in zero-shot open-vocabulary settings.
- Ablation studies confirmed that active last-mile navigation is essential for successful manipulation (Overall SR dropped below **5%** without it) and highlighted the importance of MLLM-based view selection and geometry-aware base-pose reasoning.
- Real-world experiments on a Unitree B2 quadruped robot successfully demonstrated pick-and-place capabilities for simple tasks, while also revealing challenges for MLLMs in handling highly fine-grained spatial instructions in complex environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the complex last-mile navigation problem into distinct MLLM-driven stages (view selection, affordance grounding, base-pose reasoning) improves overall performance compared to merging these steps.
> - Providing explicit 3D geometric context to MLLMs for base-pose reasoning is crucial for predicting physically feasible and reachable manipulation poses, as MLLMs struggle with implicit metric inference from 2D images.
> - MLLM-based view selection from recent observations significantly enhances the reliability of subsequent affordance grounding and base-pose reasoning steps, with specialized or 'thinking' MLLM variants showing benefits for reasoning-intensive stages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Mobile manipulation, the ability of robots to navigate to objects and perform interactions, is a foundational capability for applications in various domains, including household service, industrial automation, and logistics. This field typically involves two main phases: navigation to a target region and subsequent manipulation. A significant challenge in this paradigm is the "last-mile navigation" problem, which arises from a granularity mismatch: while object navigation systems can bring a robot to the general vicinity of a target (e.g., within 1-2 meters), this proximity often does not guarantee a manipulation-ready base pose. A manipulation-ready pose requires precise alignment, sufficient reachability, and clearance from obstacles to successfully execute the intended manipulation.

Prior work on last-mile navigation has employed various strategies. Early methods often relied on manual annotation of task-specific base poses, which limited their scalability and generalization to novel tasks or environments. Learning-based approaches have attempted to address this by implicitly learning joint navigation-manipulation policies or explicitly learning manipulation-conditioned pose preferences. However, these methods are typically data-intensive and struggle to generalize in open-vocabulary settings where tasks and objects are not predefined.

More recently, the advent of vision foundation models and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has introduced new possibilities for open-vocabulary perception and visual reasoning in robotics. Some approaches have begun to leverage these models for base pose reasoning in last-mile navigation, using object-level cues. However, object-level reasoning can be insufficient for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial constraints, such as placing an object in a specific region of a receptacle relative to other objects. The broader research landscape seeks more flexible, training-free methods that can handle open-vocabulary tasks with nuanced spatial requirements.

UniLM-Nav positions itself within this evolving landscape by proposing a unified zero-shot MLLM framework that explicitly addresses task-aware spatial reasoning for last-mile navigation. It builds upon the visual reasoning capabilities of MLLMs but seeks to overcome the limitations of object-level cues by decomposing the problem into distinct MLLM-driven stages that incorporate explicit geometric information. This work contributes to the development of more generalizable and robust mobile manipulation systems capable of operating in diverse, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework for zero-shot, open-vocabulary last-mile navigation that enables a robot to infer manipulation-ready base poses through task-aware spatial reasoning. This objective is motivated by several key challenges in mobile manipulation:

1. **Granularity Mismatch in Navigation:** Conventional object navigation systems aim to bring a robot to the approximate vicinity of a target. However, achieving successful manipulation often requires a base pose that is not merely close, but precisely oriented, positioned, and free from obstructions to allow the robot's manipulator to reach and interact with the target object or receptacle effectively. The current robot pose after object navigation might be too far, poorly aligned, or have obstacles blocking the arm's workspace, necessitating a "last-mile" adjustment.

2. **Limitations of Prior Last-Mile Navigation Methods:**
 - **Lack of Scalability and Generalization:** Early methods that relied on manual annotation of base poses or task-specific training data are not scalable to the vast number of potential objects, tasks, and environments encountered in real-world open-vocabulary settings.
 - **Data Intensity:** Learning-based approaches, while more flexible than manual methods, often require extensive data for training, which can be costly and time-consuming to acquire for diverse mobile manipulation tasks.
 - **Insufficient Spatial Reasoning:** Recent MLLM-based methods have shown promise for zero-shot generalization but often focus on object-level cues. Many manipulation tasks, however, involve fine-grained spatial constraints (e.g., "place *in front of* the monitor," "place at the *bottom-left corner*"), which go beyond simple object localization and require detailed reasoning about relative positions, affordance regions, and scene geometry.

3. **Leveraging MLLM Capabilities for Embodied AI:** MLLMs have demonstrated advanced capabilities in visual reasoning, understanding natural language instructions, and grounded perception. The motivation is to fully exploit these capabilities to address the complexities of last-mile navigation, specifically for open-vocabulary scenarios where task instructions can be diverse and novel. This involves moving beyond MLLMs as mere perception modules to using them as active reasoners for complex embodied decision-making.

To address these motivations, UniLM-Nav sets out to:
- Decompose the complex problem of last-mile navigation into a series of more manageable sub-problems (view selection, affordance grounding, base-pose reasoning), each solvable by a shared MLLM backend.
- Integrate explicit geometric information (e.g., 3D affordance points, robot configuration) with MLLM visual-linguistic reasoning to overcome the limitations of purely 2D visual inference for metric properties and reachability.
- Develop a framework that operates in a zero-shot manner, minimizing the need for task-specific training or annotations, thereby enhancing scalability to open-vocabulary settings.
- Demonstrate that such a framework can achieve state-of-the-art performance on relevant benchmarks and validate its applicability in real-world robotic deployments.

### 3. Methodology and approach

UniLM-Nav is structured as a zero-shot multimodal large language model (MLLM) framework designed to infer manipulation-ready base poses. The methodology decomposes last-mile navigation into three sequential stages, all resolved by a common MLLM backend.

#### 3.1. View Selection

Upon completion of object-goal navigation, the robot is in the vicinity of the target. However, the immediate field of view may not be optimal for precise manipulation. To address this, UniLM-Nav maintains a short-term memory buffer, `M_t`, storing the `K` most recent egocentric RGB-D observations `o_t-k` and corresponding robot states `s_t-k` (robot and camera poses).

The MLLM is prompted with these `K` candidate observations and the task instruction. Its role is to select a reference view, `o_sel`, that best supports subsequent manipulation. The selection criteria are:
1. **Clear Visibility:** The target object or receptacle must be clearly visible, unoccluded, and identifiable.
2. **Feasible Reachability/Navigability:** The selected view should provide visual evidence of a clear approach path for the robot to navigate close enough for manipulation, avoiding obstacles or cluttered areas.
The MLLM outputs the ID of the selected observation, which then serves as the visual reference for the subsequent stages.

#### 3.2. Task-Conditioned Affordance Grounding

After selecting the optimal reference view `o_sel`, the MLLM is tasked with grounding the natural language instruction `T` to a specific image-space affordance point `(u, v)` within that view. This step goes beyond mere object detection, requiring the MLLM to identify the precise location on the object or receptacle surface where the intended manipulation should occur.

For "pick" tasks, `(u, v)` is expected to lie within a graspable region of the target object. For "place" tasks, it should correspond to a safe, stable, and unoccupied placement location on the target receptacle surface. This involves the MLLM reasoning about:
- **Task instruction:** Understanding the semantic requirements of the action.
- **Object semantics:** Identifying relevant features of the object.
- **Local spatial layout:** Interpreting the immediate environment.
- **Manipulation constraints:** Implicitly considering what constitutes a "safe" or "graspable" point.
The MLLM outputs the `(u, v)` coordinates, typically normalized, which are then scaled to the original image resolution.

#### 3.3. Geometry-Aware Base-Pose Reasoning

Instead of asking the MLLM to directly infer a target base pose from a 2D image—a task that requires difficult implicit metric and geometric reasoning—UniLM-Nav converts visual information into explicit geometric quantities.

First, the grounded 2D affordance point `(u, v)` from `o_sel` is combined with its corresponding depth map and camera parameters to lift it into a 3D affordance point `p_a` in the robot-centric coordinate frame of the selected observation. This `p_a` is a precise 3D target for interaction. The selected observation `o_sel` is also visually annotated with the `(u, v)` point, creating `~o_sel`, to provide visual context to the MLLM.

The MLLM is then provided with:
- The visually prompted observation `~o_sel`.
- The lifted 3D target coordinate `p_a`.
- The robot's configuration `R` (e.g., kinematic limits).
- The task instruction `T`.

Conditioned on these explicit inputs, the MLLM predicts a manipulation-ready base position `(x, y)` in a local coordinate frame relative to the robot's current pose (from `o_sel`). The heading angle `θ` is not directly predicted by the MLLM; instead, it is geometrically computed by orienting the robot toward the lifted 3D affordance point `p_a`. This forms the complete local base pose `(x, y, θ)`.

This local base pose is then transformed into the global frame using the robot pose corresponding to `o_sel`. This final global target pose is then sent to a low-level navigation policy, which is responsible for driving the robot to the specified pose while avoiding collisions. For placement tasks, the MLLM also predicts arm extension and lift height, which are used by a simple manipulation policy.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results, including benchmark evaluations, ablation studies, and real-world robot deployments.

#### 4.1. Evaluations on OVMM Benchmark

UniLM-Nav was evaluated on the HomeRobot OVMM benchmark, which assesses a robot's ability to perform open-vocabulary mobile manipulation tasks, including finding, picking, navigating to a receptacle, and placing objects.
- **Overall Performance:** UniLM-Nav, using Gemini-3-Flash-Preview as its MLLM backend, achieved an Overall Success Rate (SR) of 23.77%, which surpassed the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) method, MoTo (20.64%), and other baselines like MoManipVLA (15.80%). This indicates its effectiveness in zero-shot open-vocabulary settings.
- **Stage-wise Success:** UniLM-Nav also recorded the highest FindObj success rates (69.47% with Gemini-3-Flash-Preview and 68.97% with RoboBrain-2.5-4B), suggesting that its last-mile navigation strategy facilitates more reliable target approaches after initial object navigation.
- **MLLM Backend Impact:** Even with a lightweight MLLM backend, RoboBrain-2.5-4B (4B parameters), UniLM-Nav achieved a competitive Overall SR of 19.19%. This performance exceeded most other baselines and demonstrated a practical balance between deployability, inference efficiency, and task performance, potentially due to RoboBrain-2.5-4B's fine-tuning on robotic data.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A comprehensive ablation study was conducted on a 20% subset of the OVMM benchmark to assess the contribution of each component of UniLM-Nav and the influence of MLLM choices.

- **Necessity of Last-Mile Navigation:** Removing the last-mile navigation stage (i.e., merely turning the robot toward the grounded affordance point) caused the Overall SR to drop below 5%. This result confirms that active base pose adjustment is crucial for successful manipulation, even after reaching the target's vicinity.
- **Effectiveness of View Selection:** Disabling the MLLM-based view selection module reduced the Overall SR from 25.42% to 20.42%. This indicates that selecting an optimal reference view from recent observations is beneficial for improving the reliability of subsequent affordance grounding and base-pose reasoning.
- **Effectiveness of Base-Pose Reasoning:** The variant without geometry-aware base-pose reasoning (where the MLLM directly grounded a floor point) substantially underperformed in both Pick success and Overall SR. This finding highlights the importance of providing explicit robot-centric geometric context to the MLLM, rather than relying on its implicit visual inference of metric distances and reachability from 2D observations.
- **Effect of MLLM Backend Choice:** Proprietary models (e.g., Gemini-3-Flash-Preview, GPT-5.4) generally showed strong performance. Within the Qwen3-VL-Instruct family, larger models tended to achieve higher Overall SR. Notably, RoboBrain-2.5-4B, which is specialized for robotics, achieved a competitive 20.50% Overall SR, outperforming generic larger models like Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct, suggesting the value of domain-specific adaptation.
- **Merging View Selection and Affordance Grounding:** When view selection and affordance grounding were merged into a single MLLM call, performance generally decreased across different MLLM backends. This supports UniLM-Nav's design choice of explicitly decomposing these steps into separate MLLM calls, allowing each to be handled more effectively.
- **Geometrically Computed Heading vs. MLLM-Predicted Heading:** Using a geometrically computed heading (orienting the robot toward the lifted affordance point) consistently outperformed MLLM-predicted heading angles, leading to a higher Overall SR. This suggests MLLMs currently struggle with direct heading prediction, and geometric constraints are more reliable.
- **Thinking vs. Non-Thinking Models:** Replacing the "instruct" model with a "thinking" model (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking) specifically in the base-pose reasoning module yielded the most significant improvement in Overall SR (from 9.58% to 20.83%), indicating this stage benefits most from enhanced spatial reasoning. However, simply adding a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompt did not provide similar improvements, suggesting that intrinsic "thinking" capabilities are more critical.

#### 4.3. Error Analysis

Analysis of failed episodes revealed common failure modes:
- **Navigation Errors (61.0%):** The largest category, occurring before last-mile navigation, including failures to find the target object or receptacle, or navigating to an unsuitable receptacle (e.g., one that is physically inaccessible due to robot constraints).
- **Manipulation Errors (18.1%):** Occurring after reaching the predicted base pose, these included low-level policy failures (e.g., releasing an object from an excessive height) and placement instability (object failing to meet strict stability criteria of the benchmark).
- **Last-Mile Navigation Errors (20.9%):** Failures specific to the UniLM-Nav framework included:
 - **Affordance Grounding Errors:** MLLM predicting an affordance point too close to the edge of a receptacle, leading to unstable placement.
 - **View Selection Errors:** MLLM selecting an occluded or inaccessible view, despite better alternative views being available in memory.
 - **Base-Pose Reasoning Errors:** MLLM predicting a base pose that resulted in poor alignment with the target or limited manipulator reachability.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

UniLM-Nav was deployed on a Unitree B2 quadruped robot with a 6-DoF Unitree Z1 manipulator in an office environment for four representative mobile manipulation tasks.
- **Success on Simple Tasks:** UniLM-Nav achieved 7/10 success for "Pick Cup from Table" and 6/10 for "Place Cake on Plate," demonstrating its ability to effectively bridge object navigation and manipulation in physical scenes.
- **Challenges with Fine-Grained Spatial Reasoning:** Performance was lower (4/10 for both) on tasks requiring more fine-grained spatial reasoning, such as "Place Bottle in Front of Monitor" or "Place Cake at Bottom-left Corner of the Table." This suggests that while MLLMs show promise, their spatial reasoning capabilities can still be a bottleneck for highly specific relative placement instructions in complex real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

UniLM-Nav represents a significant contribution to the field of mobile manipulation, particularly in addressing the critical "last-mile navigation" problem. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Zero-Shot, Open-Vocabulary Mobile Manipulation:** The framework’s ability to operate in a zero-shot manner for open-vocabulary tasks directly tackles a major limitation of prior data-intensive or manually-annotated methods. This greatly enhances the scalability and flexibility of robotic systems, allowing them to adapt to novel objects and instructions without extensive retraining. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in dynamic environments like homes, warehouses, and factories, where predefined tasks are rare.

2. **Unified MLLM-based Reasoning:** UniLM-Nav demonstrates a coherent and unified approach to solving complex sub-problems of last-mile navigation (view selection, affordance grounding, base-pose reasoning) using a single MLLM backend. This simplification in architecture and reliance on a powerful, general-purpose model reduces implementation complexity and leverages the rapidly advancing capabilities of MLLMs for embodied intelligence.

3. **Improved Robustness through Geometry-Aware Reasoning:** The explicit integration of 3D geometric information (lifting 2D affordance points to 3D and using them for base pose reasoning) is a key strength. This approach mitigates the challenges MLLMs face in implicitly inferring metric distances and physical constraints from 2D images, leading to more reliable and physically feasible base pose predictions. This architectural choice enhances the robustness of the system in practical applications.

4. **Valuable Empirical Insights for MLLM-Robotics Integration:** The extensive ablation studies provide empirical evidence and insights into how MLLMs perform in specific robotic reasoning contexts. Findings regarding the benefits of decomposing complex tasks, the impact of MLLM architecture (e.g., "thinking" models), the value of specialized fine-tuning (e.g., RoboBrain-2.5), and the utility of explicit geometric context offer crucial guidance for future research and development in MLLM-powered robotic systems. These insights can inform the design of more effective prompting strategies and model architectures.

5. **Validation in Real-World Scenarios:** The successful deployment and validation on a physical quadruped robot in an office environment demonstrate the practical applicability of UniLM-Nav. While challenges remain for highly fine-grained spatial tasks, the framework's performance on fundamental pick-and-place operations highlights its potential for real-world tasks and underscores the feasibility of using MLLMs for complex mobile manipulation in unstructured settings.

6. **Paving the Way for More Intelligent Embodied Agents:** By addressing the last-mile problem effectively, UniLM-Nav contributes to building more capable and intelligent embodied agents that can seamlessly transition from high-level navigation goals to precise physical interactions. This work lays a foundation for future advancements in areas such as human-robot collaboration, autonomous task execution, and versatile robotic assistance.

In summary, UniLM-Nav advances the state of the art in mobile manipulation by providing a robust, flexible, and intelligent framework for zero-shot last-mile navigation. Its unified MLLM-centric design, combined with explicit geometric reasoning, offers a promising path towards more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems in open-vocabulary environments.
