# Learning from Active Human Involvement through Proxy Value Propagation

> [!summary] Summary
> Proxy Value Propagation (PVP) introduces a reward-free method for training reinforcement learning agents that directly leverages active human involvement. It enables agents to efficiently learn human preferences from real-time interventions and demonstrations, achieving superior performance with significantly less human effort and higher user satisfaction compared to traditional RL and other human-in-the-loop techniques across various control tasks and environments.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) struggles with the complexity and cost of designing reward functions that accurately capture human intents, often leading to AI misalignment (specification gaming).
- RL's sample inefficiency and trial-and-error exploration pose significant safety risks, making it impractical for safety-critical real-world applications like autonomous driving.
- Existing human-in-the-loop (HL) methods, particularly those with active human involvement, often do not fully leverage both human intervention and demonstration data, or lack generalizability across diverse tasks and control devices.

## Method

- **Proxy Value Propagation (PVP)** introduces a ==reward-free framework== that integrates active human interventions and demonstrations directly into a modified ==value-based RL algorithm==.
- It learns a ==proxy value function== by directly labeling Q-values: human actions receive a +1 proxy Q-value, while agent actions leading to intervention receive a -1.
- A ==temporal difference (TD) loss== propagates these human-defined desirability signals across state-action space, combining with agent-explored data in a ==balanced buffer== to ensure efficient learning and prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- PVP consistently outperformed vanilla RL baselines, achieving high episodic returns in tasks like MetaDrive with approximately **350** returns in **37,000** steps and solving CARLA within **30 minutes**, significantly faster than baselines.
- It required the lowest human effort among active HL baselines while achieving higher test-time performance, with a user study reporting PVP as the most user-friendly method (**4.8/5.0** for Compliance, Performance, and Stress).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of TD learning for value propagation, balanced buffers for stable training, and the deterministic policy for user experience, demonstrating that a separate reward function was not necessary for performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human interventions and demonstrations can be effectively transformed into direct, reward-free Q-value labels (+1 for desired, -1 for undesired actions) to guide policy learning, circumventing the need for reward engineering.
> - Propagating these proxy values through a reward-free TD loss allows the agent to learn from both human data and its own exploration, ensuring efficiency and generalizability.
> - Using a deterministic novice policy, coupled with balanced buffers for human and agent data, significantly enhances user-friendliness, reduces human stress, and improves learning stability by minimizing 'jittery' agent behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of reinforcement learning (RL) and human-in-the-loop (HL) policy optimization, specifically addressing challenges related to AI alignment, safety, and sample efficiency in real-world applications.

Traditional RL methods typically rely on manually designed reward functions to guide an agent's learning process. However, the paper identifies several limitations with this approach:
1. **Reward Engineering Complexity**: Designing reward functions that fully encapsulate complex human intents and preferences is challenging and costly.
2. **AI Misalignment**: Faulty or incomplete reward functions can lead to agents exhibiting biased, misguided, or undesired behaviors, a phenomenon sometimes referred to as "specification gaming."
3. **Sample Inefficiency**: RL often requires a vast number of interactions with the environment, making it impractical for many real-world scenarios.
4. **Safety Concerns**: The trial-and-error exploration inherent in RL poses significant safety risks in critical applications such as autonomous driving.

Human-in-the-loop learning emerges as a promising paradigm to mitigate these issues by incorporating human subjects into the learning process. This allows for direct conveyance of human preferences, potentially leading to better-aligned, more efficient, and safer AI agents. The existing HL literature can be broadly categorized by the nature of human involvement:
- **Passive Human Involvement**: Here, humans provide feedback intermittently or upon request, such as advising actions or offering preference-based assessments of collected trajectories. While improving alignment, these methods often lack real-time intervention capabilities, posing unbounded risks in safety-critical tasks.
- **Active Human Involvement**: This approach focuses on scenarios where human subjects actively intervene and provide real-time demonstrations during an agent's execution. This proactive engagement is intended to enhance AI alignment and ensure training-time safety.

The current paper specifically focuses on learning from *active human involvement*. It aims to advance this sub-field by proposing a method that addresses the limitations of prior active HL techniques, which often do not fully leverage both human intervention and demonstration, may not incorporate agent-collected data efficiently, or lack generalizability across diverse task settings and control devices. The proposed Proxy Value Propagation (PVP) method seeks to provide a simple, reward-free, and highly efficient solution that can be seamlessly integrated into existing value-based RL algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient, reward-free method for policy optimization that learns directly from active human involvement, thereby improving AI alignment, safety, and learning efficiency. The motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

1. **Overcoming Reward Function Limitations**: The paper is motivated by the inherent difficulties and potential pitfalls of handcrafting reward functions in traditional RL. Such functions are often incomplete or misaligned with true human preferences, leading to agents that exploit "loopholes" or exhibit unintended behaviors. The authors argue that human behaviors themselves are the "ultimate source of information about human preferences," making direct learning from human demonstrations and interventions a more direct and robust approach to AI alignment.

2. **Enhancing Training-Time Safety**: For safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, uncontrolled agent exploration in a real or high-fidelity simulated environment poses significant risks. Active human involvement, where a human supervisor can immediately intervene and take control, is seen as crucial for ensuring safety during the learning process. The method aims to leverage these interventions as explicit signals of undesirable agent behavior.

3. **Improving Learning Efficiency and Reducing Human Effort**: Traditional RL is notoriously sample-inefficient, requiring extensive environmental interactions. While human involvement can improve efficiency, existing active HL methods may still demand substantial human effort or fail to generalize effectively. This research seeks to develop a method that maximizes learning efficiency by effectively utilizing all available data (both human demonstrations and agent explorations) and minimizes the cognitive load and total intervention time required from human supervisors.

4. **Promoting Generalizability and User-Friendliness**: The authors identify a need for HL methods that are broadly applicable across various types of tasks (continuous vs. discrete action spaces) and compatible with different human control interfaces (gamepads, keyboards, racing wheels). Furthermore, the motivation includes designing a system that is user-friendly, reducing stress and fatigue for human participants during training. This involves addressing issues like erratic or "jittery" agent behaviors that can complicate human supervision.

5. **Simplicity and Integrability**: A key motivation is to design a method that requires minimal modification to existing, well-established value-based RL algorithms. This would facilitate easier adoption and integration into current RL frameworks, allowing practitioners to leverage the benefits of active human involvement without extensive architectural changes.

In essence, the research aims to bridge the gap between human intuition and machine learning by transforming human corrective feedback and demonstrations into a direct, reward-free signal for policy learning, leading to more trustworthy and capable AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Proxy Value Propagation (PVP), is a reward-free active human involvement approach designed for policy optimization in value-based reinforcement learning. It integrates human feedback directly into the learning process to shape an agent's policy.

The methodology is built around a modified value-based RL framework, specifically extending algorithms like TD3 for continuous action spaces and DQN for discrete action spaces. The core mechanism involves learning a "proxy value function" that encodes human intents.

**PVP Workflow:**

1. **Agent Exploration and Human Oversight (Fig. 1 A & B):**
 - During training, the AI agent interacts with the environment. If the human subject does not intervene, the agent's exploratory transitions `(s, a_n, s')` are stored in a **Novice Buffer (B_n)**, where `s` is the state, `a_n` is the agent's action, and `s'` is the next state.
 - At any point, the human subject can intervene by taking over control (e.g., by pressing a button on a control device). During human intervention, both the agent's proposed action `a_n` and the human's demonstrated action `a_h` are recorded along with the state and next state `(s, a_n, a_h, s')` into a **Human Buffer (B_h)**. Critically, only the human's action `a_h` is applied to the environment to ensure safety and demonstrate desired behavior.

2. **Proxy Value Labeling and Propagation (Fig. 1 C):**
 - **Proxy Value (PV) Loss (`J_PV(θ)`):** For transitions sampled from the Human Buffer (`B_h`) where intervention occurred, PVP directly labels the Q-values. The human's demonstrated action `a_h` is assigned a high proxy Q-value of `+1`, signifying a desired behavior. Conversely, the agent's action `a_n` that prompted the intervention is assigned a low proxy Q-value of `-1`, indicating an undesired behavior. This is achieved through an L2 loss:
 `J_PV(θ) = E_((s,a_n,a_h)∼B_h)[|Q_θ(s, a_h) - 1|^2 + |Q_θ(s, a_n) + 1|^2] * I(s, a_n)`
 where `I(s, a_n)` is an indicator function for intervention. This loss explicitly penalizes Q-values that do not align with human preferences.
 - **Temporal Difference (TD) Loss (`J_TD(θ)`):** To propagate these labeled proxy values across the state-action space and to leverage data from non-intervened agent exploration, a reward-free TD loss is applied to all transitions (from both `B_h` and `B_n`). The absence of a traditional reward function `r(s,a)` in the Bellman backup is a defining feature:
 `J_TD(θ) = E_((s,a,s')∼B_h ∪ B_n)[|Q_θ(s, a) - γ max_{a'} Q_ˆθ(s', a')|^2]`
 This loss enables the Q-values to learn temporal dependencies and propagate the human-defined desirability signals (the `+1` and `-1` labels) to unlabeled state-action pairs generated during agent exploration.
 - **Combined Value Loss (`J(θ)`):** The total loss for updating the Q-network (`Q_θ`) is the sum of the Proxy Value Loss and the Temporal Difference Loss: `J(θ) = J_PV(θ) + J_TD(θ)`.
 - **Policy Optimization:** Following the learning of the Q-function, the agent's policy (`μ_n`) is updated to maximize the learned Q-values, consistent with standard value-based RL. For deterministic policies, this is typically `μ_n(s) = arg max_a Q(s, a)`.

**Key Design Choices and Considerations:**

1. **Connection to Conservative Q-Learning (CQL):** The `J_PV(θ)` objective, particularly the `(Q_θ(s, a_n) - Q_θ(s, a_h))` component, bears resemblance to CQL. However, PVP augments this with an L2 regularization on the Q-values for human-involved transitions. This regularization is crucial in a reward-free setting to prevent unbounded Q-value growth and potential overfitting, as Q-values are not grounded by true environmental rewards.

2. **Alternative to Direct Reward Assignment:** The paper explicitly discusses why a direct reward assignment (e.g., `+1` for human action, `-1` for agent action during intervention) is impractical. Assigning a `-1` reward to `a_n` during intervention is problematic because `a_n` is *not* executed, so its resulting `s'` is unknown, preventing a valid Bellman backup. Furthermore, a simple reward signal could incentivize the agent to deliberately perform poorly to elicit positive human rewards. PVP circumvents these issues by directly manipulating Q-values.

3. **Deterministic Novice Policy:** For both continuous and discrete action spaces, PVP employs a deterministic novice policy (e.g., `arg max_a Q(s, a)`). This design choice aims to enhance user-friendliness by reducing the "jitter" and unpredictable behaviors often associated with stochastic exploration, thereby minimizing human fatigue and stress during supervision.

4. **Balanced Buffers:** To address the issue of sparse human intervention data being overwhelmed by agent-generated data (leading to catastrophic forgetting of critical behaviors), PVP maintains separate Human (`B_h`) and Novice (`B_n`) Buffers. During each training iteration, equally sized mini-batches are sampled from both buffers, ensuring a 1:1 ratio of human-involved to agent-exploratory data in the SGD updates. This helps in consistently reinforcing human demonstrations.

By combining these elements, PVP creates a reward-free, efficient, and user-friendly framework for learning from active human involvement, aiming to produce policies that faithfully emulate desired human behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental results to validate the effectiveness and generalizability of the Proxy Value Propagation (PVP) method across various control tasks, action spaces, and human control devices.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison with RL Counterparts

- **Superior Learning Efficiency and Performance:** PVP consistently demonstrated higher performance and superior learning efficiency compared to vanilla RL baselines (TD3, DQN, SAC, PPO) across all tested environments. For instance, in MetaDrive-Gamepad, PVP achieved approximately 350 episodic returns in 37,000 environment steps (about one hour of human involvement), whereas TD3 failed to achieve comparable results even after 300,000 steps. Similarly, in CARLA, PVP agents learned to drive proficiently within 30 minutes, a task that TD3 could not solve. In GTA V, PVP solved the task with only 1,200 human data points and 20,000 total data points, significantly outperforming TD3 which required 300,000 steps for similar performance. For discrete tasks like MiniGrid Two Room, PVP successfully solved the task, while vanilla DQN failed to learn an effective exploratory solution.
- **Generalizability across Control Devices:** PVP exhibited strong generalizability across different human control devices (Gamepad, Keyboard, Racing Wheel) in the MetaDrive benchmark, consistently outperforming baselines in all settings (Table 6).

#### 4.2. Performance Comparison with Human-in-the-Loop Baselines

- **Low Safety Violations during Training:** Compared to vanilla RL and safe RL methods, all tested HL methods, including PVP, achieved extremely low safety violations during training, empirically supporting the notion that active human involvement enhances safety (Table 1).
- **Lower Human Effort and Higher Test Performance:** PVP required the lowest human effort in terms of human data usage and overall intervention rate among active HL baselines (HG-DAgger, IWR, HACO). Despite less human effort, PVP significantly outperformed these baselines in test-time performance metrics such as episodic return, success rate, and route completion (Table 1 and 2).
- **Generalizability in Unseen Environments:** In MetaDrive, which features a training/test environment split, PVP demonstrated the ability to learn high-quality, generalizable agents.
- **Smoother Actions:** Qualitative visualization (Fig. 3 and 10) showed that PVP agents produced notably smoother action sequences and trajectories compared to baselines like HACO, contributing to a better user experience.

#### 4.3. User Study Results

A user study questionnaire, evaluating "Compliance," "Performance," and "Stress" on a 5-point scale, indicated that PVP was the most user-friendly method (Table 3):
- **Compliance:** PVP scored highest (4.8 ± 0.5), indicating agents' behaviors satisfied human intents more effectively.
- **Performance:** PVP scored highest (4.8 ± 0.5), suggesting agents learned faster and performed better in solving tasks.
- **Stress:** PVP scored highest (4.7 ± 0.6), implying it caused the least fatigue and stress for human subjects during training, attributed to its deterministic novice policy and effective learning of desired behaviors.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of individual components of PVP (Table 1 and 4):
- **TD Learning Importance:** Disabling the TD loss (PVP w/o TD) significantly degraded performance, confirming the critical role of propagating human intent information to unlabeled states.
- **Reward Function Not Necessary:** Adding the environmental reward function to PVP (PVP w/ Reward) did not yield significant improvements in performance, suggesting that native reward functions might not always align perfectly with human preferences and that PVP's reward-free approach is effective.
- **Balanced Buffers are Crucial:** Disabling balanced buffers (PVP w/o BB) led to unstable training and poor performance, validating its role in preventing catastrophic forgetting of critical human demonstrations.
- **Novice Buffer Value:** Removing the Novice buffer (PVP w/o NB) resulted in poor performance, indicating that agent exploratory data, even when not intervened, contains valuable information for learning forward dynamics and implicitly human preferences.
- **Deterministic Policy Advantage:** Using a stochastic policy (PVP w/ SP) significantly reduced performance and increased human stress due to agent oscillations and unpredictable behaviors, highlighting the benefits of a deterministic policy for human-AI interaction.
- **PVP vs. Vanilla CQL Objective:** Implementing the vanilla CQL objective with PVP's design (PVP w/ CQL) resulted in worse performance. Analysis (Fig. 4) showed that PVP's explicit L2 regularization on Q-values helped bound their magnitude, creating a clearer distinction between desired and undesired actions and leading to more stable training compared to CQL's potentially unbounded Q-values for extreme actions.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that PVP is a robust, efficient, and user-friendly method for policy learning from active human involvement, capable of aligning AI behaviors with human preferences across diverse and challenging tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Proxy Value Propagation (PVP) offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the fields of reinforcement learning and human-AI collaboration:

1. **Advancement in AI Alignment without Reward Engineering**: PVP provides a practical and effective method for aligning AI agents with human preferences without requiring the manual design of complex reward functions. This addresses a fundamental challenge in RL, as faulty or misaligned reward functions are a known source of undesirable AI behaviors. By directly learning from human interventions and demonstrations, PVP offers a more intuitive and potentially more robust path to preference alignment.

2. **Enhanced Safety in Policy Learning**: For safety-critical domains such as autonomous driving, active human involvement during training inherently increases safety. PVP leverages these real-time interventions not merely as safeguards but as direct learning signals. This approach contributes to developing safer RL systems by allowing humans to prevent undesirable actions before they cause significant harm, and by penalizing those actions in the learning process.

3. **Improved Learning Efficiency and Reduced Human Effort**: The method demonstrates superior learning efficiency compared to both traditional RL and other human-in-the-loop baselines. This efficiency is achieved by effectively utilizing all available data (human demonstrations and agent explorations) and by propagating human intent through proxy values. The reduced amount of human data required and the lower intervention rate signify a decreased cognitive and time cost for human supervisors, making the training process more sustainable and scalable.

4. **Broad Generalizability**: PVP's demonstrated compatibility with various task settings (continuous and discrete action spaces) and different human control devices (gamepad, keyboard, racing wheel) highlights its generalizability. This broad applicability suggests that the method could be adopted across a wider range of human-robot interaction and autonomous system development scenarios.

5. **User-Friendliness and Practicality**: The user study results underscore PVP's user-friendly nature, with human subjects reporting higher compliance, better performance, and significantly less stress compared to other methods. This is a critical factor for the real-world deployment of human-in-the-loop systems, as the success often hinges on the quality of human interaction and the sustainability of human involvement. The method's minimal modification requirement for existing value-based RL algorithms also enhances its practical implementability.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Development of Autonomous Systems**: By providing a more efficient and safer way to train agents, PVP could accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous systems in complex environments, from robotic manipulation to autonomous vehicles.
- **Democratization of RL Training**: The reduced reliance on expert reward engineering and improved user experience could make advanced RL training more accessible to non-experts or domain specialists who can provide demonstrations but not formulate reward functions.
- **Foundation for Trustworthy AI**: By building policies directly from human input, PVP contributes to the development of AI systems that are inherently more understandable and trustworthy, as their behaviors directly reflect human intent.
- **Future Research Directions**: The identified limitations, such as handling suboptimal human demonstrations, integrating with more advanced RL techniques, and addressing continuous human availability, open avenues for further research. This includes combining PVP with offline RL, passive human involvement, or constrained optimization methods to further enhance its robustness and applicability.

In summary, PVP represents a notable step towards creating more effective, safe, and human-aligned AI agents by directly learning from the nuanced and corrective feedback inherent in active human involvement.
