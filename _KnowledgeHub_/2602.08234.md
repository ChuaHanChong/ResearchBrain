# SkillRL: Evolving Agents via Recursive Skill-Augmented Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill and collaborators developed SKILLRL, a framework enabling Large Language Model (LLM) agents to learn and recursively evolve abstract skills from diverse experiences. SKILLRL achieves state-of-the-art performance with a 89.9% success rate on ALFWorld, 72.7% on WebShop, and an average score of 47.1% on search-augmented QA tasks, outperforming larger closed-source models and memory-augmented baselines.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) agents often operate episodically, failing to learn and generalize from past experiences, which limits their scalability and adaptability.
- Existing memory-augmented LLM agents predominantly store raw, verbose, and noisy interaction trajectories, leading to "context bloat" and inefficient extraction of high-level behavioral patterns.
- Current memory mechanisms typically function as external references without fundamentally adapting the agent's internal policy or evolving to address new failure modes and challenges.

## Method

- Employs a teacher model to distill raw interaction trajectories, including both successful episodes and failures, into concise ==strategic patterns== and ==failure lessons==.
- Constructs a hierarchical ==SKILLBANK== that organizes distilled knowledge into general skills (universal strategies) and task-specific skills, designed for efficient retrieval and significant token compression.
- Utilizes ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for cold-start initialization==, followed by ==recursive skill evolution== during Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to dynamically refine the skill library and agent policy based on ongoing performance.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art success rates, including **89.9%** on ALFWorld, **72.7%** on WebShop, and an average score of **47.1%** on search-augmented QA tasks.
- Outperformed strong baselines, including GPT-4o by **41.9%** and Gemini-2.5-Pro by **29.6%** on ALFWorld, demonstrating that intelligent skill learning can compensate for model scale.
- Reduced prompt token usage by approximately **10.3%** compared to raw memory retrieval, while also exhibiting faster convergence and a dynamic expansion of the skill library from **55** to **100** skills during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstracting experiences into high-level, hierarchical skills provides superior performance and context efficiency for LLM agents compared to storing or compressing raw interaction trajectories.
> - A dynamic and recursive skill evolution mechanism, where the skill library co-evolves with the agent's policy, is critical for continuous improvement, adaptability, and effectively addressing emergent failure modes.
> - Initial Supervised Fine-Tuning on skill-augmented reasoning traces is essential for enabling the base LLM agent to effectively utilize and integrate abstract skills into its decision-making process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

SKILLRL is situated at the intersection of Large Language Model (LLM) agents, memory-augmented AI, and reinforcement learning, aiming to address critical limitations in current agent capabilities.

The emergence of **LLM agents** has marked a significant advance in AI, enabling autonomous systems to perform complex tasks by interacting with environments through natural language. Pioneering works like ReAct (Yao et al., 2022b) and Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) demonstrated impressive reasoning and acting capabilities. However, as the authors highlight, a fundamental challenge remains: these agents often operate in isolation, treating each task execution as an episodic event, largely failing to learn from accumulated experience or generalize knowledge effectively. This lack of continuous learning significantly hinders their scalability and adaptability in dynamic, open-ended environments.

To mitigate the limitations of finite context windows and the absence of continuous learning, **memory mechanisms** have been integrated into LLM agents. Early approaches primarily relied on retrieving raw trajectories or episodic memories (e.g., Mem0, ExpeL, MemP), often employing a static Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigm. While providing some context, these methods suffer from inherent drawbacks: raw trajectories are frequently lengthy, redundant, and noisy, leading to "context bloat" and difficulty in extracting high-level, reusable patterns. This can result in suboptimal performance or even degradation, as the agent struggles to discern critical information from noise. Current research in this area is trending towards self-improving memory systems that distill interactions into higher-level insights, but many still struggle with the trade-off between information density and noise, or fail to adapt the agent's internal policy based on this memory.

SKILLRL directly challenges these limitations by introducing **skill abstraction** as a superior alternative to raw memory storage. It draws inspiration from how human experts develop "skills" – compact, reusable strategies that capture the essence of accomplishing subtasks. This positions SKILLRL within the burgeoning field of learning and evolving agentic skills, which is increasingly viewed through the lens of Continual Learning (CL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Unlike traditional CL, which focuses on knowledge preservation in predefined tasks, SKILLRL contributes to the self-evolving agents paradigm, where agents actively acquire and refine skills in open-ended settings.

Furthermore, SKILLRL integrates **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to optimize the agent's policy. While RL is widely used for LLM alignment, applying it effectively to agentic skill acquisition has been challenging due to sparse rewards and long horizons. SKILLRL's key innovation here is treating the **skill library as a dynamic component that co-evolves with the agent's policy**, leveraging RL to refine structured skills through recursive failure analysis. This distinguishes it from prior memory-augmented RL methods that often treat memory as a static or auxiliary source, providing a more integrated and adaptive learning framework. By focusing on distilling abstract skills and facilitating their recursive evolution, SKILLRL pushes the boundaries of how LLM agents can achieve robust, generalizable, and context-efficient performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind SKILLRL stems from a critical observation regarding the current state of Large Language Model (LLM) agents: despite their remarkable capabilities in complex tasks, they often operate in isolation, failing to learn effectively from past experiences. This episodic nature means that each new task execution often starts from scratch, hindering their ability to generalize, adapt, and improve over time.

The authors identify several specific problems that drive the need for SKILLRL:
1. **Inefficient Memory Utilization:** Existing memory-based methods for LLM agents primarily store raw trajectories. These raw experiences are frequently verbose, redundant, and heavily laden with noise, making it difficult for agents to extract meaningful, high-level behavioral patterns. This inefficiency prevents the distillation of core principles essential for generalization across tasks.
2. **Lack of Policy Adaptation:** Current memory mechanisms largely serve as external reference databases or in-context prompts, mimicking past solutions without fundamentally adapting the agent’s internal policy. The agent does not truly "learn" from memory in a way that guides its decision-making process or improves its underlying behavioral strategy.
3. **Context Bloat and Performance Degradation:** The sheer length and noise of raw trajectories lead to significant "token footprint" issues. This problem is exacerbated by the limited context windows of LLMs, forcing a trade-off between the amount of information provided and its utility, often leading to sub-optimal performance or even a decline as task complexity increases.
4. **Static Knowledge Bases:** Memory banks in many systems are static, failing to evolve with the agent's improving capabilities or adapt to new challenges encountered during exploration. This prevents the agent from addressing emergent failure modes or discovering new, more efficient strategies.

In response to these challenges, SKILLRL's key objectives are:
- **To bridge the gap between raw experience and efficient policy improvement:** This involves moving beyond simple memorization to a system that enables agents to extract actionable knowledge.
- **To achieve automatic skill discovery:** The framework aims to automatically identify and distill high-level, reusable behavioral patterns (skills) from diverse interactions, including both successes and failures.
- **To implement recursive skill evolution:** A crucial objective is to enable the skill library to be a dynamic component that continuously co-evolves with the agent's policy during reinforcement learning, ensuring adaptability and long-term improvement.
- **To reduce the token footprint while enhancing reasoning utility:** By abstracting experiences into concise skills, SKILLRL seeks to provide richer guidance to the agent within limited context windows, thereby improving efficiency without compromising performance.
- **To enhance generalization and robustness:** The ultimate goal is to enable LLM agents to learn and transfer knowledge effectively, leading to superior performance, faster convergence, and greater resilience as task complexity increases.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SKILLRL proposes a comprehensive framework designed to integrate skill discovery and evolution with reinforcement learning for LLM agents. Its methodology is structured around three core components: Experience-based Skill Distillation, Hierarchical Skill Library Construction, and Recursive Skill Evolution.

#### 3.1. Experience-based Skill Distillation

The first step involves transforming raw, verbose interaction trajectories into concise, actionable knowledge.
- **Trajectory Collection:** A base LLM agent (π_base) is deployed in the environment to collect a diverse set of trajectories. Critically, SKILLRL collects *both* successful (T+) and failed (T-) episodes, recognizing that failures provide valuable insights into boundary conditions and flawed reasoning.
- **Teacher Model Distillation:** A high-capability teacher model (M_T, implemented using OpenAI o3) is employed to process these trajectories:
 - For **successful trajectories (τ+)**, M_T extracts "strategic patterns" – critical decision points, underlying reasoning for correct actions, and generalizable behaviors that led to task completion.
 - For **failed trajectories (τ-)**, M_T synthesizes "concise failure lessons." This involves identifying the point of failure, the flawed reasoning or action, what should have been done (counterfactuals), and general principles to prevent similar errors in the future. This approach avoids the direct inclusion of noisy, lengthy failed episodes.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Skill Library (SKILLBANK) Construction

The distilled knowledge is then organized into a structured, hierarchical library to facilitate efficient retrieval and utilization.
- **Skill Organization:** SKILLBANK is designed with two levels of abstraction:
 - **General Skills (S_g):** These capture universal strategic principles applicable across all task types within a given environment (e.g., systematic exploration, state management, goal-tracking heuristics). They provide foundational guidance.
 - **Task-Specific Skills (S_k):** These encode specialized knowledge for particular task categories (e.g., domain-specific action sequences, preconditions, common failure modes unique to a task type). They are extracted by grouping trajectories by task type during collection.
- **Skill Structure:** Each skill is formatted with a concise name, a principle describing the strategy, and explicit "when to apply" conditions.
- **Skill Retrieval:** During inference, given a task description (d), general skills (S_g) are always included. Task-specific skills (S_ret) are adaptively retrieved from S_k using semantic similarity (TopK) between embeddings of the task description and the skills. This selective retrieval significantly reduces the context window footprint (achieving 10–20x token compression compared to raw trajectories) while providing targeted guidance.

#### 3.3. Recursive Skill Evolution

Recognizing that a static skill library is insufficient for continuous improvement, SKILLRL introduces a mechanism for dynamic skill refinement.
- **Cold-Start Initialization:** Before full RL training, the base LLM agent (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) undergoes Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). The teacher model (M_T) generates skill-augmented reasoning traces (D_SFT) that explicitly demonstrate how to retrieve, interpret, and apply skills during decision-making. This SFT phase is critical for the agent to learn how to effectively *utilize* the abstracted skills. The resulting fine-tuned model (π_θ_sft) serves as the starting policy for RL and the reference policy (π_ref) for KL regularization.
- **Recursive Evolution during RL:**
 - After each validation epoch, the success rate for different task categories is monitored.
 - If a category's success rate falls below a threshold (δ), failed validation trajectories (T_val-) are collected using a diversity-aware stratified sampling strategy.
 - The teacher model (M_T) analyzes these failures to identify gaps in the current SKILLBANK. It then proposes new skills (S_new) or suggests refinements to existing ones.
 - SKILLBANK is dynamically updated by incorporating these new or refined skills. This forms a "virtuous cycle" where policy improvement leads to encountering new challenges, which in turn drives skill library expansion and further policy refinement.
- **RL-based Policy Optimization:** The skill-augmented policy is optimized using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** (Shao et al., 2024).
 - For each task, the agent retrieves relevant skills (S_g, S_ret).
 - It samples a group of (G) trajectories from its current policy.
 - Binary rewards (0 for failure, 1 for success) are assigned to each trajectory.
 - Normalized advantages are computed based on intra-group relative rewards.
 - The policy is updated using a PPO-style clipped objective, with a KL penalty to the SFT-initialized reference policy (π_ref) to prevent catastrophic forgetting of skill utilization capabilities while improving task performance.

The combination of these components allows SKILLRL to iteratively learn, abstract, and refine its knowledge base and policy, enabling more robust and efficient performance in complex, interactive environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SKILLRL demonstrates significant advancements in LLM agent performance across a variety of challenging benchmarks. The experimental results validate the framework's core hypotheses regarding the utility of skill abstraction and recursive evolution.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Overall Superiority:** SKILLRL consistently achieves state-of-the-art results, significantly outperforming strong baselines across all tested environments.
 - On **ALFWorld**, SKILLRL achieves a success rate of 89.9%.
 - On **WebShop**, it secures a success rate of 72.7% and an average score of 85.2.
 - For **seven search-augmented QA tasks**, SKILLRL attains an impressive average score of 47.1%.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

- **Against Prompt-based Methods:** SKILLRL drastically outperforms prompt-based agentic or memory-based methods (e.g., ReAct, Reflexion, Mem0, ExpeL, MemP, SimpleMem) by a substantial margin. For instance, on ALFWorld, SKILLRL's 89.9% success rate is significantly higher than Reflexion's 42.7% and Mem0's 33.6%. This gap underscores that skill abstraction and policy adaptation are more effective than relying on raw in-context learning or simple memory retrieval.
- **Against Vanilla RL:** Compared to standard RL baselines like RLOO (75.5% on ALFWorld) and GRPO (77.6% on ALFWorld), SKILLRL (89.9%) shows a clear advantage. Notably, since GRPO is the base optimizer for SKILLRL, the 12.3% absolute improvement on ALFWorld is directly attributable to the skill-augmentation mechanism. This gain is particularly evident in complex subtasks like "Cool" (+23.0%) and "Pick2" (+22.8%), demonstrating the effectiveness of structured skill priors.
- **Against Memory-Augmented RL:** SKILLRL substantially outperforms existing memory-augmented RL frameworks such as MemRL (21.4% on ALFWorld) and EvolveR (43.8% on ALFWorld). Even hybrid approaches like Mem0+GRPO (54.7% on ALFWorld) and SimpleMem+GRPO (62.5% on ALFWorld) are significantly outmatched, with SKILLRL demonstrating an absolute success rate gap of about 35.2% over Mem0+GRPO. This strongly supports the paper's core hypothesis that high-level skill abstraction and co-evolving libraries are superior to simple trajectory storage or compression.
- **Against Closed-Source Models:** Using the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct base model, SKILLRL remarkably outperforms much larger closed-source models. On ALFWorld, it exceeds GPT-4o by 41.9% and Gemini-2.5-Pro by 29.6%. This highlights that intelligent skill learning can compensate for model scale, allowing smaller, open-source models to achieve superior performance through structured experiential knowledge.
- **On Search-Augmented QA:** SKILLRL maintains its lead, achieving an average score of 47.1%, surpassing strong baselines like Search-R1 (38.5%) and EvolveR (43.1%). It particularly excels in multi-hop reasoning tasks like Bamboogle, outperforming EvolveR by 19.4%, demonstrating the effectiveness of hierarchical skills in guiding multi-step information synthesis.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies rigorously confirm the contribution of each SKILLRL component:
- **Hierarchical Structure:** Removing the hierarchical structure (using only task-specific skills) led to a 13.1% and 11.3% drop on ALFWorld and WebShop, respectively, validating the importance of general strategic principles.
- **Skill Library vs. Raw Trajectories:** Replacing the skill library with raw trajectories resulted in the largest performance degradation (up to 25%), providing direct evidence that abstraction is significantly superior to direct memorization.
- **Cold-Start SFT:** The absence of cold-start SFT caused a 20% drop, proving its criticality in teaching the base model how to effectively utilize skills.
- **Dynamic Evolution:** Removing the dynamic evolution mechanism led to a 5.5% decrease, confirming that the continuous refinement and expansion of the skill library is crucial for iterative improvement and adaptability.

#### 4.4. Context Efficiency and Evolution Dynamics

- **Context Efficiency:** SKILLRL maintains a significantly leaner prompt, averaging less than 1,300 tokens, which is approximately a 10.3% reduction compared to raw memory retrieval (averaging ~1,450 tokens). This demonstrates that skill abstraction effectively mitigates the context-bloat problem.
- **Skill Library Growth:** The skill library dynamically grows from an initial 55 skills to 100 skills during training, primarily driven by the expansion of task-specific skills (from 43 to 80). This balanced expansion reflects the agent's ability to develop specialized expertise while encountering new scenarios.
- **Faster Convergence:** Recursive skill evolution leads to a notably higher learning rate and superior asymptotic performance. SKILLRL achieved over 80% success within 60 training steps, whereas the baseline without evolution required 90 steps to reach a lower peak, highlighting the acceleration in convergence provided by dynamic skill refinement.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

Case studies on WebShop and ALFWorld illustrate how SKILLRL's agent adaptively retrieves and integrates both general and task-specific skills into its reasoning process. This seamless integration confirms that the agent develops a structured understanding of task logic, leading to more robust and efficient problem-solving rather than merely memorizing steps.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that SKILLRL's innovative approach of distilling experiences into hierarchical, evolving skills, combined with RL-based policy optimization, represents a substantial leap forward in developing capable, efficient, and continuously learning LLM agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SKILLRL introduces a paradigm shift in how Large Language Model (LLM) agents learn and evolve, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence:

**1. Bridging the Gap from Raw Experience to Abstract Knowledge:** The most profound significance of SKILLRL lies in its ability to effectively abstract verbose and noisy raw interaction trajectories into concise, actionable skills. This moves beyond the limitations of traditional memory-based methods that merely store or compress raw data. By enabling agents to distill high-level, reusable behavioral patterns, SKILLRL empowers them with a more human-like capacity for learning, where core principles and strategies are extracted rather than just memorized sequences of actions.

**2. Dynamic and Co-Evolving Knowledge Base:** SKILLRL's recursive skill evolution mechanism is a crucial innovation. Unlike static knowledge bases or memory banks, the SKILLBANK dynamically expands and refines itself in tandem with the agent's policy improvement. This continuous co-evolution ensures that the agent's knowledge remains relevant, addresses emergent failure modes, and adapts to increasingly complex scenarios. This virtuous cycle of learning and self-improvement is essential for building truly adaptive and robust AI agents capable of operating in open-ended, dynamic environments.

**3. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** The framework significantly reduces the "token footprint" by distilling experiences into compact skills, achieving comparable or superior performance with substantially less context. This context efficiency is vital for practical LLM agent deployment, as it mitigates the costs and latency associated with long context windows. Furthermore, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance with a relatively smaller open-source LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) compared to much larger closed-source models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro) highlights that intelligent skill learning can effectively compensate for model scale, making advanced agent capabilities more accessible and democratized.

**4. Improved Generalization and Robustness:** By learning abstract skills, agents gain a deeper understanding of task logic and strategic principles, leading to better generalization across task instances and increased robustness to variations. The hierarchical structure of SKILLBANK, separating general strategies from task-specific heuristics, further enhances this ability, allowing agents to apply foundational knowledge while adapting to specific domain requirements. This results in faster convergence rates and higher asymptotic performance, particularly in complex, multi-step tasks.

**5. Broad Applicability Across Domains:** The successful evaluation across diverse benchmarks—embodied AI (ALFWorld), web navigation (WebShop), and search-augmented QA tasks—underscores the broad applicability of the SKILLRL framework. This versatility suggests its potential to be adopted in various real-world scenarios requiring intelligent agents, from robotics and digital assistants to complex decision-making systems.

**Potential Future Impact:**
SKILLRL lays fundamental groundwork for the next generation of self-evolving AI agents. It opens avenues for future research in:
- **Automated Skill Discovery without Human Supervision:** Further reducing the reliance on human-curated prompts or teacher models for skill distillation.
- **Transfer Learning with Skill Libraries:** Exploring how learned skill libraries can be more effectively transferred across different environments or even modalities.
- **Explainable Agent Behavior:** The structured nature of skills could lead to more interpretable agent reasoning, allowing developers to understand *why* an agent made a particular decision.
- **Continual and Lifelong Learning:** SKILLRL's recursive evolution mechanism is a stepping stone towards agents that continuously learn and accumulate knowledge over their entire operational lifetime, rather than being trained once and deployed statically.

In conclusion, SKILLRL represents a pivotal advancement in the development of intelligent agents, moving beyond rudimentary memory to a sophisticated system of abstract, evolving skills. Its contributions promise to usher in an era of more capable, efficient, and truly adaptive LLM agents, capable of navigating and mastering increasingly complex challenges.
