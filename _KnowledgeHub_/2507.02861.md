# LiteReality: Graphics-Ready 3D Scene Reconstruction from RGB-D Scans

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Cambridge, The University of Hong Kong, and Technical University of Munich developed LiteReality, a pipeline that converts RGB-D scans of indoor environments into compact, realistic, and interactive 3D virtual replicas. The system reconstructs scenes with high-quality physically based rendering materials, articulated geometry, and physical properties, demonstrating improved performance in object retrieval, material recovery, and full-scene reconstruction compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing 3D reconstruction methods produce '3D photographs' that lack the structural and functional information required for interactive applications like robotics, AR/VR, gaming, and embodied AI.
- Outputs from current reconstruction techniques are often incompatible with standard computer graphics pipelines due to missing object individuality, articulated geometry, PBR materials, and integrated physical properties.
- Challenges persist in producing faithful and functional reconstructions from 'in-the-wild' RGB-D scans due to clutter, severe occlusions, varied lighting, and the high computational cost of material estimation for large scenes.

## Method

- LiteReality employs a four-stage pipeline: Scene Perception and Parsing, Object Reconstruction, Material Painting, and Procedural Reconstruction, designed to convert raw RGB-D scans into graphics-ready scenes.
- A training-free hierarchical retrieval mechanism, combining semantic filtering, image-based comparisons, pose-aware rendering, and contextual language model selection, is used to retrieve high-fidelity 3D models for detected objects.
- A novel material painting module utilizes auto-crop mapping with SAM and MLLMs for semantic guidance, followed by a lightweight albedo-only optimization in CIE LAB color space to recover high-quality PBR materials robustly.

## Results

- LiteReality's training-free object retrieval method achieved a lower L1 Chamfer Distance (0.0986 avg/CAD) on the ScanNet dataset, indicating more accurate shape matches than prior approaches.
- The material painting module demonstrated competitive performance in object-centric PBR material recovery (RMSE: 0.2163, SSIM: 0.4353, LPIPS: 0.5854), producing visually plausible results even under challenging lighting and image styles.
- The full-scene reconstruction achieved strong overall performance (RMSE: 0.2664, SSIM: 0.5818, LPIPS: 0.6522) on diverse iPhone-scanned scenes, surpassing Phone2Proc and Digital Cousin baselines in perceptual similarity metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured scene graph representation combined with constraint-based collision resolution effectively addresses noise and ensures physical plausibility in raw RGB-D scan detections for scene parsing.
> - Training-free, multi-modal hierarchical retrieval can achieve competitive 3D model matching performance without extensive training, enhancing adaptability and scalability to expanding object datasets.
> - Lightweight albedo-only optimization, guided by MLLMs, offers a robust and computationally efficient method for transferring high-fidelity PBR materials onto 3D models even under challenging real-world imaging conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D reconstruction has seen advancements, particularly with differentiable rendering techniques like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and Gaussian Splatting, which achieve high degrees of photorealism. However, the outputs of these methods often function as "3D photographs," capturing geometry and texture but lacking the structural and functional information necessary for interactive applications. Existing techniques typically produce representations that are not directly compatible with standard computer graphics pipelines, limiting their utility in domains such as robotics, virtual/augmented reality (AR/VR), gaming, and embodied AI.

Prior research efforts have addressed various aspects of creating virtual environments. In simulation, methods generate interactive scenes from text descriptions, room layouts, or single images, primarily for training embodied AI. These often prioritize diversity over faithful reconstruction of real-world scenes. Other approaches focus on realism through compact representations, such as CAD retrieval and alignment, or material estimation from images. However, these often encounter challenges when dealing with real-world indoor scans characterized by clutter, occlusion, and varied lighting, and frequently overlook the integration of components into a coherent, functional system. The incompatibility with graphics pipelines stems from a lack of object individuality, articulated geometry, physically based rendering (PBR) materials, and integrated physical properties.

LiteReality addresses this gap by proposing a pipeline that moves beyond passive, geometry-focused reconstructions to create "graphics-ready" environments. It aims to bridge the divide between photorealistic 3D reconstruction and the requirements of interactive, simulation-ready digital replicas, specifically by incorporating structural, functional, and material information for compatibility with graphics pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of LiteReality is to develop a novel pipeline that converts RGB-D scans of indoor environments into compact, realistic, and interactive 3D virtual replicas suitable for integration into standard graphics pipelines. This goes beyond visual resemblance to reality by supporting features essential for graphics applications.

The motivation for this work stems from several key limitations in existing 3D reconstruction and scene generation methods:
- **Lack of Interactivity and Structure:** While modern 3D reconstruction achieves visual fidelity, outputs typically lack semantic richness, object-centric representations, and functional properties necessary for user interaction, manipulation, and simulation. For instance, doors cannot be opened, and objects lack realistic physics.
- **Incompatibility with Graphics Pipelines:** Traditional 3D reconstruction methods produce geometry and texture data that are not directly compatible with common graphics engines. A truly "graphics-ready" scene requires:
 - **Object-centricity:** Each object must be an individual entity to allow for physically plausible manipulation.
 - **Functional Modeling:** Objects should support interactive behaviors (e.g., opening drawers, operating appliances).
 - **PBR Materials:** Photorealistic appearance under diverse lighting conditions requires high-quality physically based rendering materials.
 - **Physical Properties:** Integration of mass, gravity, and collision dynamics is essential for realistic interactions.
- **Challenges with Real-World Scans:** Existing systems often struggle to produce faithful reconstructions from "in-the-wild" scans due to issues like clutter, severe occlusions, and poor lighting. Many methods assume curated or controlled environments, limiting their applicability.
- **Computational Cost and Scalability:** Differentiable rendering for material estimation can be computationally expensive, particularly for large scenes with numerous objects, hindering scalability.

LiteReality seeks to overcome these challenges by providing a comprehensive system that automatically transforms noisy RGB-D scans into editable, simulation-ready digital replicas, thereby enabling applications in AR/VR, gaming, robotics, and digital twins. The project also aims to introduce a training-free object retrieval module with competitive similarity performance and a robust material painting module capable of transferring appearances even under challenging conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LiteReality operates through a four-stage pipeline designed to convert RGB-D scans into graphics-ready scenes.

#### 3.1. Scene Perception and Parsing

The pipeline begins by extracting key spatial information from RGB-D images. For real-life scans, Apple's RoomPlan system with an iPhone is used to capture room layouts and oriented bounding boxes (O-Bbox). To address noise (e.g., collisions, misalignments) inherent in raw detections, a structured scene graph representation is employed.
- **Scene Graph Representation:** Nodes represent elements like walls, windows, doors, and detected objects, each with spatial (location, dimensions, orientation) and appearance (top-k most visible cropped images) attributes. Edges define spatial relationships (e.g., "attached to walls," "on top of," "table-chair pair," "connecting to") to enforce physical plausibility.
- **Parsing Process:** Walls are parsed into connected components forming room boundaries, with noisy detections aligned to a grid. Spatial relationships between objects are inferred based on their geometry.
- **Constraint-Based Collision Resolution:** An iterative approach applies virtual forces to separate intersecting objects while respecting scene graph constraints (e.g., wall-attached objects only move along walls, table-chair pairs maintain relative positions).

#### 3.2. Object Reconstruction Stage

This stage focuses on retrieving artist-designed 3D models to represent detected objects, providing high-fidelity geometry and articulated functionality. A training-free hierarchical retrieval mechanism is used, leveraging multiple information modalities:
- **Semantic Filtering:** Objects are initially categorized into fine-grained subcategories to narrow down candidate models.
- **Image-Based Retrieval:** Cropped 2D views of the input object are compared against pre-rendered images of candidate 3D models from a curated database using a pre-trained feature encoder (DINOv2). The top 10 visually similar matches are selected.
- **Pose-Aware Rendering and Comparison:** The top candidates are placed in the scene based on detected poses and re-rendered from the same camera angles. These rendered views are then re-encoded and compared, further reducing candidates to the top four.
- **Contextual Selection:** A language model is employed to evaluate higher-level attributes like style, proportion, and visual coherence, selecting the best-matched object.
- **Object Clustering:** Identical or similar objects are grouped using DINOv2 features and KMeans clustering. Clusters are further subdivided by dominant color to ensure visual consistency for joint retrieval, reducing redundant processing. The LiteReality database comprises 5,283 assets from 3D-Future, AI2-THOR, and Sketchfab, structured to align with Apple’s RoomPlan.

#### 3.3. Material Painting Stage

This module enhances realism by recovering high-quality, spatially varying PBR materials for retrieved 3D models, addressing challenges like misalignment, poor lighting, and diverse image styles.
- **Auto-crop Mapping:** SAM-guided segmentation extracts meaningful image patches. These patches are filtered, and a Multi-Modal Large Language Model (MLLM) semantically maps 3D material segments to the most visually representative image patches.
- **Semantic and Visual Guided Material Search:** High-quality materials are initialized through a multi-step retrieval process:
 1. Language-guided prompting refines material category predictions.
 2. CLIP embeddings from cropped image patches are compared with embeddings of candidate materials.
 3. GPT-4 evaluates the visual compatibility of albedo maps with reference patches.
- **Albedo-Only Optimization:** Instead of computationally intensive full PBR parameter optimization, a lightweight albedo-only adjustment is performed in the CIE LAB color space. This shifts the global color distribution of the albedo to match target LAB values inferred by an MLLM from part-specific RGB values, preserving high-frequency details while correcting hue and brightness.

#### 3.4. Procedural Reconstruction Stage

The final stage assembles the scene within Blender, applying physical properties for interactivity.
- **Scene Assembly:** Walls are constructed, followed by windows and doors, and then objects are placed within the room.
- **Physics Integration:** Rigid-body properties are assigned to each object, with mesh geometry used for collision boundaries. Structural elements (e.g., walls) are passive rigid bodies, while movable items are active.
- **Mass Estimation:** An MLLM predicts object mass from cropped images to enable realistic rigid-body dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LiteReality's effectiveness was evaluated both end-to-end and at individual stages, using custom benchmarks for retrieval similarity, object-centric PBR material estimation, and full-scene graphics-ready reconstruction.

#### 4.1. Retrieval Similarity Benchmarking

- **Dataset and Metric:** Evaluated on the ScanNet dataset, comparing retrieved models against Scan2CAD ground-truth annotations over the entire ShapeNet database (300–8,000 models per category). L1 Chamfer Distance was used to measure geometric difference between normalized models.
- **Performance:** LiteReality's training-free retrieval method achieved a lower L1 Chamfer Distance (0.0986 avg/CAD, 0.1067 avg/class) compared to prior approaches like MSCD (0.1103, 0.1188), Digital Cousin (0.1411, 0.1246), and ScanNotate (0.1042, 0.1110), indicating more accurate shape matches across multiple object categories (Table 1).

#### 4.2. Object-Centric Material Recovering Benchmarking

- **Dataset and Setup:** Five indoor scenes captured with an iPhone 13 Pro Max (RoomPlan mode), containing 111 objects across eight categories. Each object had four cropped, representative frames, reflecting real-world challenges (occlusion, poor lighting, misalignment). PBR materials from each method were applied to ground-truth CAD models, rendered with global illumination, and compared to cropped RGB images using RMSE, SSIM, and LPIPS.
- **Performance:** LiteReality demonstrated competitive performance in material painting (RMSE: 0.2163, SSIM: 0.4353, LPIPS: 0.5854) when compared to baselines like Make-It-Real (RMSE: 0.2377, SSIM: 0.3981, LPIPS: 0.6111), PhotoShape (RMSE: 0.3225, SSIM: 0.2371, LPIPS: 0.6558), and Make-It-Real + Albedo-Only (RMSE: 0.2156, SSIM: 0.4203, LPIPS: 0.5899). Its results were visually plausible even under challenging conditions and diverse image styles (Figures 6, 7, 8; Table 2, Table 5 in Appendix).

#### 4.3. Graphics-Ready Reconstruction Benchmarking

- **Setup:** Five diverse iPhone-scanned scenes were used for end-to-end evaluation. Rendered images from reconstructed scenes (under original camera poses) were compared to real RGB captures using perceptual similarity metrics (RMSE, SSIM, LPIPS).
- **Performance:** LiteReality exhibited strong overall performance in full-scene reconstruction (RMSE: 0.2664, SSIM: 0.5818, LPIPS: 0.6522). This outcome surpassed Phone2Proc (RMSE: 0.3604, SSIM: 0.5512, LPIPS: 0.7338) and Digital Cousin (RMSE: 0.3653, SSIM: 0.5531, LPIPS: 0.7364), as well as enhanced Digital Cousin variants (Table 3, Figures 5, 9).

#### 4.4. Runtime Analysis

A complete room-scale scene reconstruction takes 20 to 60 minutes on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. Scene parsing takes 1–3 minutes, object retrieval 2–5 minutes, material painting 15–50 minutes, and procedural reconstruction/export less than two minutes. The material painting stage constitutes the majority of the runtime.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LiteReality represents a notable step in 3D scene reconstruction by providing a comprehensive system capable of converting RGB-D scans of indoor environments into graphics-ready digital replicas. The work addresses a critical gap between photorealistic geometric reconstructions and the functional requirements of interactive applications.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **First Graphics-Ready System:** LiteReality is presented as the first system capable of reconstructing room-level RGB-D scans into compact, realistic CAD representations that include high-quality PBR materials and support for simulation and virtual interaction. This generalization across a range of real-world scenes is a significant advancement.
- **Training-Free Object Retrieval:** The proposed training-free retrieval pipeline achieved competitive similarity performance on the Scan2CAD benchmark, enhancing adaptability and scalability to expanding datasets.
- **Robust Material Painting:** The novel material painting approach reliably transfers high-fidelity PBR materials onto 3D models, demonstrating robustness to shape misalignment, challenging lighting, and diverse image styles, and outperforming existing methods in quantitative evaluation.
- **Interactive and Editable Environments:** By integrating PBR materials, articulated geometry, and physical properties (mass, collision dynamics), the reconstructed scenes are immediately usable in simulation engines, enabling dynamic relighting, physics-based interactions, and modular scene editing.

**Potential Impact:**
The capabilities demonstrated by LiteReality have broad implications across several application domains:
- **AR/VR and Gaming:** Facilitates the creation of immersive and interactive virtual experiences by rapidly generating high-fidelity digital twins of real-world spaces.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Provides realistic and physically plausible simulation environments for training and testing robots, reducing the need for expensive real-world experiments.
- **Digital Twins and Visualization:** Enables the generation of compact and editable digital replicas of physical spaces, useful for facility management, architectural visualization, and planning.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including the dependence on upstream perception accuracy (object detection, layout estimation), the inherent challenges of object retrieval (occasional mismatches, though correctable due to object-centricity), and the current constraint-based collision solver's potential struggle with complex arrangements.

Future research directions include extending detection and retrieval to smaller items for enriched scene detail, incorporating learning-based light estimation with full SVBRDF recovery for more faithful relighting, scaling the pipeline to multi-room and building-level reconstructions, and optimizing the entire system for real-time performance to enhance its utility for interactive and on-device applications. This work lays a foundation for future exploration in graphics-ready 3D scene reconstruction.
