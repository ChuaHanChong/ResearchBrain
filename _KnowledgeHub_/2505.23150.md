# Bigger, Regularized, Categorical: High-Capacity Value Functions are Efficient Multi-Task Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> The Bigger, Regularized, Categorical (BRC) approach enables online temporal-difference learning to scale effectively to billion-parameter models across hundreds of tasks. This method consistently outperforms strong single-task specialists and demonstrates efficient knowledge transfer capabilities, challenging the assumption that high-capacity value models require offline datasets or behavioral cloning.

## Problem

- Scaling value-based reinforcement learning (RL) to high-capacity models and multiple tasks faces challenges like gradient instability and task interference.
- Different tasks often have vastly different reward scales and structures, leading to reward imbalance that can dominate learning signals.
- Sparse rewards in many real-world tasks make it difficult for agents to learn effectively across diverse scenarios simultaneously.

## Method

- **Categorical Q-Learning** uses cross-entropy loss to predict the full distribution of returns, providing scale independence and stable gradients across diverse reward scales.
- Scaled Q-Value Models employ significantly increased model capacity (up to billion parameters) with normalization techniques (e.g., BroNet, Impala with LayerNorm) to maintain stability in online TD learning.
- Learnable task embeddings, concatenated with state representations, enable parameter efficiency, shared structure discovery, and facilitate transfer by allowing the model to learn meaningful task representations.

## Results

- BRC-trained multi-task agents consistently outperformed strong single-task specialists across seven benchmarks, with performance gains up to **62%** on MetaWorld and **257%** on HumanoidBench-Medium.
- Performance continued to improve with model size, even beyond **250M** parameters, indicating that multi-task learning effectively utilizes large model capacities.
- BRC demonstrated high computational efficiency, requiring **40x** fewer gradient updates and achieving comparable performance to offline methods with **100x** fewer transitions on challenging tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-capacity models can intrinsically handle task interference better, as the rate of gradient conflicts decreases with increasing model capacity.
> - The 'Scaled Q-value model' component, encompassing larger, regularized architectures, contributes over 60% of the performance improvements in the BRC approach.
> - Learned task embeddings naturally cluster similar tasks, revealing semantic structure in the task space and enabling effective transfer learning by manipulating the input rather than retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed remarkable success in scaling models across natural language processing and computer vision, where large models trained on diverse datasets demonstrate exceptional performance and generalization capabilities. However, this scaling paradigm has had limited impact in value-based reinforcement learning (RL), where attempts to train high-capacity models on multiple tasks often encounter fundamental challenges including gradient instability, reward imbalance, and task interference.

This paper introduces "Bigger, Regularized, Categorical" (BRC), a comprehensive approach that enables online temporal-difference learning to scale effectively to billion-parameter models across hundreds of tasks. The research challenges the prevailing assumption that high-capacity value models require offline datasets or behavioral cloning, demonstrating that online multi-task RL can not only match but surpass strong single-task specialists.

### 2. The Challenge of Scaling Online Value-Based RL

Traditional multi-task reinforcement learning faces several critical obstacles that prevent effective scaling:

**Gradient Conflicts**: When training a single model on multiple tasks, gradients from different tasks can interfere with each other, leading to unstable optimization and degraded performance across tasks.

**Reward Imbalance**: Different tasks often have vastly different reward scales and structures. Tasks with larger reward magnitudes can dominate the learning signal, causing the model to implicitly prioritize certain tasks over others.

**Sparse Rewards**: Many real-world RL tasks provide sparse reward signals, making it difficult for agents to learn effectively when dealing with diverse scenarios simultaneously.

These challenges have historically pushed practical approaches toward offline learning methods, policy distillation from single-task experts, or specialized gradient surgery techniques. The BRC approach directly addresses these fundamental issues through three key design principles.

### 3. The BRC Approach

The BRC methodology integrates three complementary design choices that work synergistically to enable scalable multi-task learning:

#### 3.1. Categorical Q-Learning ("Categorical")

Rather than using traditional mean squared error (MSE) loss for Q-value regression, BRC employs cross-entropy loss through distributional reinforcement learning. This approach treats Q-value prediction as a classification problem, learning the full distribution of returns rather than just the mean.

The categorical approach provides several advantages:
- **Scale Independence**: Cross-entropy loss is inherently more robust to different reward scales compared to MSE
- **Gradient Stability**: The classification formulation provides more stable gradients across diverse tasks
- **Outlier Resistance**: The approach is less susceptible to extreme values that can destabilize learning

To accommodate the fixed support domain required by categorical RL, rewards are dynamically normalized using per-task normalization factors, ensuring consistent learning signals across all tasks.

#### 3.2. Scaled Q-Value Models ("Bigger, Regularized")

The second pillar involves significantly increasing model capacity while incorporating normalization techniques to maintain stability. BRC employs different architectures depending on the input modality:

**For proprioceptive control**: The BroNet architecture, which consists of MLPs with residual connections and layer normalization, enables stable scaling to high capacities.

**For vision-based control**: A scaled Impala architecture with layer normalization added between the encoder and Q-network modules.

The normalization components are crucial for preventing the divergent behavior typically associated with scaling TD learning. The research demonstrates that these regularized architectures allow for steady performance improvements with increasing capacity in both single and multi-task scenarios.

#### 3.3. Learnable Task Embeddings

Instead of using separate network heads for each task, BRC employs learnable task embeddings that are concatenated with state representations. This design choice offers several benefits:

- **Parameter Efficiency**: More efficient use of parameters compared to separate heads
- **Shared Structure Discovery**: Enables the model to discover and leverage shared underlying dynamics
- **Task Clustering**: The learned embeddings naturally cluster similar tasks, facilitating transfer

The embeddings are optimized through backpropagation of the temporal difference loss, allowing the model to learn meaningful task representations that support both task-specific performance and cross-task generalization.

### 4. Experimental Validation

The BRC approach was extensively evaluated across seven multi-task benchmarks comprising over 280 unique tasks:

**Benchmarks Tested**:
- DeepMind Control Suite (high-DoF locomotion, 7 tasks)
- HumanoidBench (low and high-DoF humanoid control, 9-20 tasks)
- MetaWorld (dexterous manipulation, 50 tasks)
- ShadowHand (high-DoF manipulation, 85 tasks)
- Atari100k (vision-based discrete control, 26 tasks)

The experiments used a single set of hyperparameters across all benchmarks, demonstrating the robustness and generalizability of the approach. Multi-task agents interacted with all tasks in parallel while maintaining fixed update-to-data ratios, ensuring fair comparison with single-task baselines.

### 5. Key Findings

#### 5.1. Superior Multi-Task Performance

BRC-trained multi-task agents consistently outperformed strong single-task specialists across all evaluated benchmarks. The performance improvements were substantial, with multi-task models showing gains of 62% on MetaWorld, 257% on HumanoidBench-Medium, and significant improvements across other domains.

#### 5.2. Effective Model Scaling

The research demonstrated that performance continues to improve with model size, even beyond 250M parameters where single-task performance typically saturates. This indicates that the diversity provided by multi-task learning enables more effective utilization of large model capacities.

#### 5.3. Reduced Gradient Conflicts

Counterintuitively, the rate of gradient conflicts between tasks decreases as model capacity increases. This suggests that high-capacity models can intrinsically handle task interference better than smaller models, potentially reducing the need for explicit gradient surgery techniques.

#### 5.4. Component Importance Analysis

Using Shapley value analysis, the research identified that the "Scaled Q-value model" component contributes over 60% of the performance improvements, highlighting the critical importance of high-capacity, regularized critics in enabling robust multi-task learning.

### 6. Transfer Learning Capabilities

#### 6.1. Model Transfer Effectiveness

BRC agents demonstrate exceptional transfer learning capabilities. Model transfer (fine-tuning pretrained weights) significantly outperforms learning from scratch and converges faster than data transfer approaches, indicating that pretrained weights encode valuable generalizable features.

#### 6.2. Embedding-Tuning Transfer

A particularly compelling finding is the effectiveness of "embedding-tuning transfer." By adjusting only the low-dimensional task embeddings while keeping policy and value networks frozen, BRC agents achieve over 90% success rates on new manipulation tasks with as few as 300 target-task transitions.

#### 6.3. Task Embedding Structure

The learned task embeddings naturally cluster similar tasks, demonstrating that the model successfully discovers semantic structure in the task space. This clustering enables effective transfer by manipulating the input space rather than retraining large networks.

#### 6.4. Computational Efficiency

BRC achieves remarkable computational efficiency, performing 40× fewer gradient updates than single-task methods to reach equivalent performance on challenging humanoid control tasks. The approach also demonstrates superior sample efficiency, achieving performance comparable to offline methods with 100× fewer transitions.

### 7. Implications and Future Directions

This research represents a significant advancement in multi-task reinforcement learning, demonstrating that online value-based methods can scale effectively to high capacities and numerous tasks. The findings challenge the prevailing notion that generalist RL agents require offline datasets or behavioral cloning.

**Key Contributions**:
1. **Scalability Demonstration**: Proof that online TD learning can scale to billion-parameter models across hundreds of tasks
2. **Generalist Advantage**: Multi-task agents that surpass single-task specialists while using fewer computational resources
3. **Transfer Capabilities**: Effective knowledge transfer to new tasks through both model fine-tuning and embedding manipulation
4. **Architectural Insights**: Identification of key design choices that enable stable scaling of value-based methods

The work opens new avenues for developing truly generalist RL agents that learn efficiently from diverse online experience. The embedding-tuning transfer paradigm, in particular, offers a practical approach for rapid adaptation to new tasks without extensive retraining.

Future research directions include understanding the theoretical foundations of task synergy in multi-task learning, exploring the limits of model scaling in RL, and investigating how these principles apply to other RL paradigms beyond value-based methods. The demonstrated success of online multi-task learning suggests a promising path toward building versatile AI systems capable of learning and adapting across broad spectrums of tasks and environments.
