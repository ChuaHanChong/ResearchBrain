# VIMPO: Value-Implicit Policy Optimization for LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> VIMPO introduces a policy optimization framework for large language models that bridges the simplicity of critic-free methods with the fine-grained credit assignment of actor-critic approaches. The method, developed at UC Berkeley and Yale, achieves higher validation accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and demonstrates greater robustness to noisy reward signals compared to existing critic-free baselines.

## Problem

- Existing RL methods for LLMs face a trade-off between the training simplicity and stability of critic-free approaches and the fine-grained credit assignment of actor-critic methods.
- Critic-free methods typically assign coarse, trajectory-level advantages, often overlooking critical steps in multi-step, long-form reasoning tasks.
- Actor-critic methods, while providing dense token-level feedback, can introduce training instabilities and are sensitive to the quality of the learned critic's value function.

## Method

- Formalizes autoregressive generation by LLMs as a deterministic-transition Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- Derives a policy-implied value function and a closed-form, ==critic-free one-step TD advantage== directly from the optimality conditions of KL-regularized RL.
- Introduces a value loss that incorporates outcome-level verifiable rewards by anchoring the derived value recurrence with a terminal condition of zero future reward.
- Integrates the policy-derived advantage into a PPO-style actor update, explicitly separating outcome-based reward incorporation from policy improvement.

## Results

- Achieved superior average performance across four mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with up to a **3.2%** absolute improvement over GRPO baselines (e.g., **20.8%** vs **17.6%** on AIME 2025).
- Demonstrated increased robustness to noisy reward signals, maintaining a consistent performance advantage over GRPO when rewards were corrupted by a **25%** flipping rate.
- Exhibited stronger learning dynamics and faster convergence, leading to higher validation accuracy on benchmarks like MATH-500 and AIME 2024 during training.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed VIMPO's token-level advantage signal aligns with Monte Carlo estimates, providing fine-grained credit assignment for correct and incorrect reasoning steps within trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The perceived trade-off between critic-free training simplicity and dense, token-level credit assignment is not fundamental, as both can be achieved simultaneously.
> - The optimal value function for a KL-regularized policy can be implicitly represented via policy log-likelihood ratios against a reference policy, obviating the need for a separate explicit critic network.
> - A closed-form, one-step temporal-difference (TD) advantage, expressed entirely through policy log-ratios, naturally emerges from the same optimality conditions, enabling fine-grained credit assignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become a central post-training paradigm for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs), particularly for complex tasks like mathematical problem-solving and code generation. Within this domain, existing RL post-training methods for LLMs generally fall into two categories: actor-critic and critic-free approaches.

Actor-critic methods, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Value-Aware Policy Optimization (VAPO), incorporate a learned value function. This enables the provision of dense, token-level supervision, offering fine-grained credit assignment. However, this approach introduces challenges, including sensitivity to the quality of the learned critic and potential instabilities arising from critic-policy co-adaptation.

In contrast, critic-free methods, including Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Dynamic Advantage Policy Optimization (DAPO), eliminate the need for a separate value network. They estimate advantages from groups of rollouts associated with the same prompt, simplifying the training process by replacing a learned state-dependent baseline with a sample-based group baseline. This simplification, however, often comes at the cost of coarser credit assignment, as a single trajectory-level advantage is typically broadcast uniformly across all tokens of a completion. This practice can treat a complex multi-step reasoning process as an indivisible unit, potentially overlooking the importance of specific reasoning steps.

Recent research has attempted to refine this coarse signal in critic-free settings. For instance, Future-KL Influenced Policy Optimization (FIPO) re-weights GRPO advantages using a future-KL factor, while attention-based methods aim to identify "anchor" tokens heuristically. These approaches introduce token-level granularity as an auxiliary add-on rather than deriving it inherently from the optimization objective.

VIMPO (Value-Implicit Policy Optimization) is introduced into this landscape as an approach that seeks to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It aims to retain the practical simplicity and stability of critic-free methods while simultaneously recovering the dense, token-level credit assignment typically associated with actor-critic models. The work positions itself as developing a mechanism to connect outcome-based verifiable rewards with policy-derived internal learning signals, thereby providing a more coherent and efficient training framework for LLMs engaged in long-form reasoning. The paper also draws connections to log-ratio parameterizations in preference optimization (e.g., DPO, IPO, TDPO) and classical consistency-based RL (e.g., Path Consistency Learning, soft Q-learning), emphasizing its use of policy-reference log-ratios in an online RL setting with rollout rewards and Bellman-style identities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for VIMPO stems from an identified trade-off in current reinforcement learning paradigms for LLM post-training. Existing methods face a tension between the simplicity and training stability offered by critic-free approaches and the dense, token-level credit assignment capabilities of actor-critic methods. This trade-off is particularly pertinent for tasks requiring multi-step, long-form reasoning, where fine-grained feedback on individual reasoning steps is beneficial. Critic-free methods, despite their simplicity, often assign a uniform, trajectory-level advantage to all tokens, which may not adequately differentiate critical reasoning steps from less significant tokens. Actor-critic methods, while providing denser signals, introduce complexities such as training instabilities and sensitivity to the quality of the learned value function.

The key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Overcome the Fundamental Trade-off:** The authors hypothesize that the perceived trade-off between critic-free simplicity and dense credit assignment is not inherent. VIMPO aims to demonstrate that it is possible to achieve both simultaneously.
2. **Develop a Critic-Free, Policy-Implied Value Function:** The core objective is to derive a representation of the optimal value function for a KL-regularized policy within a deterministic-transition Markov Decision Process (MDP) that models autoregressive generation. This derivation should express the value function implicitly through policy log-likelihood ratios against a frozen reference model, thereby eliminating the need to train a separate, explicit critic network.
3. **Incorporate Outcome-Level Verifiable Rewards Effectively:** The method seeks to establish a simple value loss that directly incorporates outcome-level verifiable rewards. This is achieved by anchoring the derived value recurrence with a terminal condition, stating that no future reward remains at the end of a trajectory, and using a Monte Carlo estimate for the initial value. This objective aims to align the policy-implied value function with observed outcomes.
4. **Derive a Critic-Free Actor Advantage for Token-Level Credit Assignment:** A further objective is to extract a closed-form, one-step temporal-difference (TD) advantage directly from the same derivation of optimality conditions. This advantage, expressed entirely through policy log-ratios, is intended to provide dense, token-level learning signals for policy improvement, similar to those in actor-critic methods, but without a learned critic.
5. **Separate Reward Incorporation from Policy Improvement:** VIMPO aims to achieve a coherent mechanism where outcome-based supervision (through the value loss) is distinctly separated from policy improvement (through a PPO-style actor update using the policy-derived advantage). This separation is hypothesized to improve training efficiency and robustness.
6. **Empirically Demonstrate Superior Performance and Robustness:** The method's empirical objectives include demonstrating improved training efficiency and higher validation accuracy compared to existing critic-free baselines (specifically GRPO) on mathematical reasoning benchmarks. Additionally, the research aims to show increased robustness to noisy reward signals, which is a practical consideration in real-world RLVR settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VIMPO's methodology is structured around four principal steps, beginning with the formalization of autoregressive generation and culminating in a PPO-style actor update.

#### 3.1. Setup: Autoregressive Generation as a Deterministic MDP

The process of autoregressive generation by an LLM is formalized as a deterministic-transition Markov Decision Process (MDP). For a given prompt `x`, a policy `π` generates a sequence of tokens `a_0..., a_{T-1}`. A state `s_t` is defined as the prefix `(x, a_0..., a_{t-1})`, with `s_0 = x` and `s_T` being the terminal state. Transitions are deterministic, meaning `P(s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t) = 1`. The method uses the standard finite-horizon expected-return action-value function `Q_π(s_t, a_t)` and value function `V_π(s_t)`, distinct from entropy-regularized soft values. This formulation allows for control over how KL divergence contributes to value and actor objectives, and avoids requiring estimates of future KL contributions when constructing advantages.

At optimality, a KL-regularized policy `π*` is defined by a local optimality condition (Equation 1), which involves maximizing the expected action-value while regularizing against a reference policy `π_ref`. Solving this maximization yields an expression for `π*(a | s_t)` that depends on `π_ref(a | s_t)` and the optimal action-value function `Q*(s_t, a)` (Equation 2).

#### 3.2. From KL-Regularized Optimality to Value Recurrence

The deterministic transition property simplifies the Bellman equation to `Q*(s_t, a_t) = r(s_t, a_t) + γV*(s_{t+1})` (Equation 3). By substituting this simplified Bellman equation into the optimality condition (Equation 2) and performing algebraic manipulation involving `ln Z(s_t)`, the paper derives a central identity (Equation 4):
`β ln(π*(a_t | s_t) / π_ref(a_t | s_t)) = r(s_t, a_t) + γV*(s_{t+1}) - V*(s_t) + β KL*(s_t)`.
This identity establishes that, under optimality, the per-token log-ratio relative to the reference policy is equivalent to a Bellman residual plus a KL correction. This identity provides three key insights: a closed-form representation for the value function `V*`, a zero-mean signal property for the log-ratio and KL terms, and a closed-form one-step TD advantage.

#### 3.3. Training the Policy-Implied Value

The central identity is a first-order linear recurrence. Solving it forward yields an expression for `V*(s_t)` in terms of an initial anchor value `V*(s_0)` and a sum of terms involving log-ratios, KL divergence, and rewards (Equation 5). To create a training objective, the optimal policy `π*` is replaced by the trainable policy `π`, defining a policy-implied value function `V_π(s_t)` (Equation 6).

A critical element is the "terminal anchor": at the terminal state `s_T`, no future reward remains, so `V_π(s_T) = 0`. This condition provides a parameter-free supervision target. The method then trains `π` by minimizing the squared error between `V_π(s_T)` and zero, leading to the value loss `L_V(π) = 0.5 * V_π(s_T)^2` (Equation 7).

For the common LLM RL setting with outcome-only rewards (where `r(s_k, a_k) = 0` for `k < T-1` and `r(s_{T-1}, a_{T-1}) = R_final`), `γ = 1`, and `V*(s_0)` estimated by the group-mean return `R_final`, the value loss specializes to Equation 8. This loss encourages the cumulative policy-reference log-ratio along a trajectory to track the centered final reward. A stop-gradient operator is applied to the KL term to ensure it functions as a centering baseline rather than being directly manipulated by the loss.

#### 3.4. PPO Integration via the Closed-Form Advantage

The same central identity also provides a one-step advantage estimator. For a trainable policy `π_θ`, the policy-implied TD advantage `bA_TD_t` is defined as `β log(π_θ(a_t | s_t) / π_ref(a_t | s_t)) - β KL_{π_θ}(s_t)` (Equation 9). This quantity is structurally equivalent to a Bellman residual when the policy-implied value satisfies its recurrence.

These one-step estimates are then accumulated using a Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE)-style approach to form `bA_λ_t` (Equation 10). Before being used in the PPO surrogate objective, the detached advantages are normalized across valid response tokens in each minibatch (Equation 11). This normalized advantage `eA_λ_t` is used in the PPO actor loss `L_A`.

The overall VIMPO training objective combines the value loss and the actor loss: `L_VIMPO(π) = L_V(π) + c_A L_A(π)` (Equation 12), where `c_A` is an actor update strength coefficient. This design ensures that external reward information is incorporated through the value loss, while the actor update for policy improvement utilizes the induced log-ratio advantage, thereby separating these two aspects of the learning process. ### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VIMPO focused on mathematical reasoning tasks in an RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) setting, comparing it against GRPO baselines.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** Qwen3-4B-Base.
- **Training Data:** Math subset of Guru (54.4K examples).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** MATH-500, AIME 2024, AIME 2025, and OlympiadBench. Evaluation metrics included `avg@1` and `avg@32` accuracy, depending on the benchmark.
- **Baselines:**
 - **GRPO:** A token-level loss aggregation variant, following the DAPO training recipe, with no KL reward penalty.
 - **Naive GRPO:** The original sequence-mean-token-mean GRPO loss.
- **Hyperparameters:** Shared hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate) were kept consistent between VIMPO and GRPO to isolate the impact of the objective differences. VIMPO-specific coefficients (`β` and `c_A`) were tuned coarsely.
- **Noisy Rewards Test:** Training rewards were corrupted by flipping the binary correctness signal with 25% probability to assess robustness.

#### 4.2. Main Comparison (Clean Rewards)

- **Performance Improvement:** All RL methods significantly improved over the Qwen3-4B-Base model (Table 1). VIMPO exhibited stronger learning dynamics and achieved higher validation accuracy compared to both GRPO baselines on MATH-500 and AIME 2024 throughout training (Figure 3).
- **Final Accuracy:** VIMPO achieved the best average performance across all four benchmarks and the highest individual score on each (Table 1). For example, on AIME 2025, VIMPO improved from 17.6% (GRPO) to 20.8%. This suggests its policy-implied value loss and actor update are particularly beneficial for harder, competition-style reasoning evaluations.
- **Response Length:** VIMPO explored longer responses in early training, but its response length subsequently decreased while validation accuracy remained higher than GRPO, indicating that performance gains were not simply due to increased completion length (Figure 3).

#### 4.3. Robustness to Noisy Rewards

- **Consistent Advantage:** Under a 25% reward flipping rate, both VIMPO and GRPO experienced performance degradation. However, VIMPO consistently retained an advantage over GRPO across all benchmarks (Table 1). The performance gap was notably larger on the AIME evaluations (e.g., AIME 2025: VIMPO noisy 14.9% vs. GRPO noisy 11.7%).
- **Learning Dynamics Under Noise:** VIMPO showed faster improvement in training accuracy and maintained stronger AIME 2024 validation performance during the initial training phase under corrupted rewards (Figure 4). This behavior is attributed to VIMPO's mechanism of separating reward incorporation (via the value loss target) from policy improvement (via a policy-implied log-ratio advantage), making the actor update less directly dependent on individual potentially corrupted reward labels.

#### 4.4. Ablations

- **Contribution of Actor Update:** A value-only VIMPO variant (with `c_A = 0`) improved, but more slowly than the full VIMPO objective. Adding the PPO-style actor update (`c_A = 0.005`) substantially accelerated learning and achieved the highest training accuracy among the tested settings (Figure 5).
- **Policy Shift and Regularization:** The main VIMPO setting resulted in a higher "VO KL" (KL divergence from the reference policy), indicating a larger policy shift, while a high-`β`, high-`c_A` setting kept VO KL nearly flat at the cost of slower learning. This suggests that while the actor update speeds up learning, the choice of coefficients is critical to avoid over-constraining the policy.
- **Response Length in Ablations:** The main VIMPO setting, which optimized fastest, also produced longer responses by step 200, highlighting the interplay between coefficients, optimization speed, policy movement, and generation-length dynamics (Figure 7 in Appendix B.2).

#### 4.5. Additional Metrics (from Appendix)

- **Training Entropy:** The GRPO baselines showed a largely monotonic decrease in average token-level policy entropy, consistent with "entropy-collapse" observations. VIMPO, conversely, while entering a low-entropy regime, exhibited small fluctuations and recoveries in later training stages, suggesting it might be less tightly coupled to monotonic entropy collapse and allow for more local policy adjustments (Figure 6 in Appendix B.1).
- **Qualitative Token-Level Signal:** A case study on a MATH-500 problem provided qualitative evidence that VIMPO's GAE advantage signal aligns with Monte Carlo rollout-based temporal-difference estimates. It highlighted favorable signals for correct reasoning steps and adverse signals for incorrect branches (e.g., overcounting) within a completion, illustrating its capability for fine-grained credit assignment (Figures 8 and 9 in Appendix C).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VIMPO introduces a method for policy optimization that addresses a central tension in the application of reinforcement learning to large language models (LLMs): the trade-off between the simplicity of critic-free approaches and the dense credit assignment provided by actor-critic methods.

The key technical contribution is the derivation of a policy-implied value function and a critic-free actor advantage directly from the optimality conditions of KL-regularized reinforcement learning within a deterministic-transition MDP model of autoregressive generation. This allows for token-level credit assignment without the complexities associated with training a separate critic network.

The empirical results demonstrate several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning Performance:** VIMPO achieves higher validation accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks compared to established critic-free baselines like GRPO. The observed larger gains on competition-style evaluations (e.g., AIME) suggest its particular utility for more challenging, multi-step reasoning tasks.
2. **Improved Robustness to Noisy Rewards:** The method exhibits greater resilience to corrupted reward signals. This characteristic is valuable in practical RLVR settings where verifiers and reward functions may be imperfect or noisy, potentially leading to more reliable model training.
3. **Training Efficiency:** VIMPO demonstrates stronger learning dynamics, leading to faster improvement during training compared to the baselines. This can reduce the computational resources and time required for LLM fine-tuning.
4. **Separation of Learning Signals:** By separating outcome-level reward incorporation through the value loss from policy improvement via a policy-derived actor advantage, VIMPO offers a structured approach that may contribute to training stability and efficiency.
5. **Insights into Policy Dynamics:** Observations regarding VIMPO's training entropy suggest different policy learning dynamics compared to GRPO, potentially indicating a mechanism that mitigates monotonic entropy collapse and allows for finer policy adjustments.
6. **Qualitative Credit Assignment:** The qualitative case study illustrates that VIMPO's token-level advantage signal can effectively highlight correct and incorrect reasoning steps within a generated trajectory, providing interpretable feedback crucial for improving complex reasoning.

The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also suggest avenues for future impact. These include exploring adaptive coefficient schedules for `β` and `c_A` (which might allow for better optimization throughout training), investigating approximate KL estimators for scalability with larger models, and dynamically updating the reference policy. Extending VIMPO beyond mathematical reasoning to other verifiable domains (e.g., code generation, tool use) and evaluating it against carefully tuned actor-critic baselines across a wider range of model scales and random seeds are identified as important future directions. Addressing these points could further broaden VIMPO's applicability and solidify its position as a robust and efficient method for enhancing LLM capabilities.
