# CoV: Chain-of-View Prompting for Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> CoV: Chain-of-View Prompting enhances Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Embodied Question Answering by enabling active, iterative viewpoint adjustment within 3D environments at inference time. The training-free framework significantly boosts spatial reasoning, achieving an average +11.56% improvement on the OpenEQA benchmark and state-of-the-art results on ScanQA.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Embodied Question Answering (EQA) methods are limited by a fixed, passive set of input viewpoints in 3D environments.
- This passive observation restricts VLMs' ability to acquire sufficient, question-relevant context, especially when information is partially occluded or distributed across multiple views.
- Traditional EQA systems struggle with complex spatial reasoning and multi-step inferences due to incomplete visual information derived from static observations.

## Method

- The ==Chain-of-View (CoV) prompting== framework transforms passive VLMs into active viewpoint reasoners via a two-stage, training-free, test-time approach.
- A ==coarse-grained view selection agent== initially filters a large set of egocentric frames to identify a reduced subset most relevant to the question, using the VLM itself.
- A ==fine-grained CoV agent== then iteratively adjusts viewpoints by generating discrete camera actions (translation and rotation) to acquire additional environmental details and refine its understanding of the 3D scene.

## Results

- CoV achieved an average **+11.56%** improvement in LLM-Match across four mainstream VLMs (Qwen3-VL-Flash, GLM-4.6V, Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-4o-Mini) on the OpenEQA benchmark.
- The framework secured state-of-the-art results on the ScanQA validation set, achieving **116** CIDEr and **31.9%** EM@1, and strong performance on SQA3D with **51.1%** EM@1.
- Performance scaled with increased exploration budget, showing an average additional **+2.51%** improvement across models when allowing more action steps compared to a minimum of one.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active visual exploration, analogous to Chain-of-Thought for language, can significantly improve spatial reasoning in VLMs by dynamically acquiring better-selected visual data.
> - Endowing AI agents with an "observational agency," allowing them to strategically gather information, is crucial for robust human-like interaction and understanding in complex 3D environments.
> - The framework demonstrates test-time scaling, where increasing the budget for active exploration steps directly correlates with progressive improvements in the agent's performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of **Embodied Question Answering (EQA)**, particularly within 3D environments. EQA aims to enable AI agents to process textual questions and perceive/reason within a given environment (often represented by egocentric images or 3D scene data) to derive correct answers, mimicking human-like interaction with the physical world. This capability is paramount for future advancements in robotics, autonomous navigation, and sophisticated human-computer interaction.

The broader landscape of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has seen significant progress, with models like LLaVA-3D, LL3DA, and Scene-LLM integrating 2D multimodal perception with 3D spatial context. However, the authors identify a substantial limitation: most existing VLMs and EQA methods rely on a **fixed and finite set of input viewpoints**. This passive observation paradigm restricts their ability to acquire sufficient, question-relevant context, especially when information is partially occluded, distributed across multiple views, or requires multi-step reasoning. For instance, finding an occluded object or understanding complex spatial relationships often necessitates dynamically changing one's perspective.

"CoV: Chain-of-View Prompting" positions itself as an innovative solution that bridges this gap by transforming passive VLMs into **active viewpoint reasoners**. It moves beyond simply processing given visual data to actively *exploring* the 3D scene at inference time. This approach is inspired by the success of **test-time reasoning** strategies like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting in Large Language Models (LLMs), which have demonstrated that eliciting step-by-step reasoning can significantly improve performance without additional training. CoV extends this concept to the visual domain, proposing a "Chain-of-View" where the model not only reasons step-by-step but also dynamically adjusts its visual input.

The paper contrasts CoV with prior 2D VLM approaches that use predetermined video frames, highlighting CoV's ability to explore an "open-ended view space" constructed from a 3D scene. By enabling dynamic selection and adjustment of viewpoints, CoV directly tackles the problem of **spatial ambiguity and incomplete visual information** that plagues traditional static-view EQA systems. It contributes to the growing body of research on **agentic AI**, where models are endowed with capabilities to interact with their environment to gather information and refine their understanding, rather than just acting as static processing units. This work effectively pushes the boundaries towards more intelligent, interactive, and human-like AI agents in complex 3D environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Chain-of-View (CoV) prompting stems from a critical bottleneck in existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) systems: their inability to actively and dynamically acquire visual information from 3D environments. Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are largely constrained to a fixed set of input views, which severely hampers their capacity for complex spatial reasoning. When answers are not immediately apparent, or when context is distributed and partially occluded, a static set of observations is insufficient. For instance, answering "Where can I get some pop drinks?" might require an agent to infer the need to locate a refrigerator and then actively navigate to find it, rather than simply identifying a soda can in a pre-given image. This limitation reduces the practicality and robustness of EQA systems in real-world applications like robotics and autonomous agents.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of the CoV prompting framework are:

1. **Transforming VLMs into Active Viewpoint Reasoners:** To enable pre-trained VLMs to move from passive observation to active exploration, allowing them to dynamically adjust their perspective within a 3D scene to gather relevant visual evidence.
2. **Improving Spatial Reasoning in EQA Tasks:** To significantly enhance the performance of VLMs on complex spatial reasoning tasks by providing a mechanism for coarse-to-fine view selection and adjustment.
3. **Developing a Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Framework:** To achieve these improvements purely through an inference-time reasoning framework, without requiring additional model training or dataset-specific fine-tuning, thereby ensuring broad applicability and cost-effectiveness across various mainstream VLMs.
4. **Enabling Multi-Step and Iterative Context Acquisition:** To facilitate a process where the agent can iteratively refine its understanding of the scene by performing discrete camera actions (e.g., rotations, movements) and incorporating new observations into its reasoning chain.
5. **Demonstrating Test-Time Scaling:** To show that the agent's performance can progressively improve with an increased budget for exploration steps, indicating the efficacy of extended, active reasoning.

In essence, CoV aims to overcome the inherent limitations of static visual inputs by endowing VLMs with an "observational agency," allowing them to "look around" and strategically gather the most pertinent visual cues needed to answer spatially complex questions in 3D environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Chain-of-View (CoV) prompting framework is a two-stage, training-free, test-time reasoning approach designed to enable VLMs to actively explore 3D environments for Embodied Question Answering (EQA). It operates in a coarse-to-fine manner, inspired by chain-of-thought prompting but applied to visual context acquisition.

#### 3.1. Problem Setting

The task involves answering a natural language query `Q` based on a 3D scene representation `S`. Instead of direct access to `S`, the agent receives a sequence of egocentric frames `V = {v1..., vT}` sampled from a video episode of the scene, along with camera poses. The goal is to generate an answer `A` by maximizing `P(A | S, V, Q)`, but critically, CoV allows for dynamic generation of new views.

#### 3.2. Coarse-Grained View Selection

The initial input `V` often contains many redundant frames with low information density. To address this, the first stage employs a **View Selection Agent**.
- **Purpose:** To filter the large set of initial views and identify a reduced subset `V' = {vi1..., viK}` (where `K << T`) that are most relevant to the question `Q`. This step significantly reduces the search space and focuses on potentially informative views.
- **Mechanism:** The VLM acts as this agent, guided by a prompt template (Figure 8 in the Appendix). This prompt instructs the agent on logic for view selection, such as identifying key objects, evaluating spatial relationships, handling occlusion, and reducing redundancy.
- **Additional Context:** Crucially, a bird-eye view of the entire scene is also provided to the agent to facilitate a global understanding of the environment, complementing the local egocentric views.

#### 3.3. Fine-Grained View Adjustment

Once the initial set of relevant views `V'` is selected, the second stage, the **Chain-of-View (CoV) Agent**, takes over to perform iterative, fine-grained view adjustments.
- **Purpose:** To dynamically generate new perspectives and acquire additional environmental details that might be occluded or unclear in the initially selected views. This addresses the limitation of passive, one-step answer generation.
- **Action-Reasoning Loop:** The CoV agent operates in an iterative loop:
 1. **Context Definition:** At the start, the agent's context `C0` includes the question `Q` and the selected views `V'`.
 2. **Action Instruction Generation:** At each step `t`, the CoV agent (a VLM, using the prompt in Figure 9) analyzes the current observation (`vti`) and the question (`Q`) to generate an *action instruction* `at`.
 3. **Action Execution and New Observation:** The action instruction `at` is mapped to an `SE(3)` rigid-body camera transformation. This transformation updates the camera pose relative to the 3D scene representation `S`, generating a new viewpoint `v(t+1)i`.
 4. **Context Update:** The newly generated view `v(t+1)i` is appended to the agent's context (`C(t+1) = {Q, V', v1i..., v(t+1)i}`), which is then fed back to the VLM for the next reasoning step.
- **Action Space:** The agent's action space `A` includes discrete translational and rotational actions:
 - **Translational:** `move forward/backward/left/right/up/down` (fixed displacement).
 - **Rotational:** `yaw (rotate left/right)`, `pitch (look up/down)`, `roll (tilt clockwise/counterclockwise)` (adjusts orientation).
 - **View Switch:** `switch to view N` (allows the agent to jump to any anchor view from `V'`).
- **Termination Condition:** The reasoning process continues until the CoV agent determines it has gathered sufficient information to answer the question, or when a predefined maximum number of action steps (`L`) is reached. At this point, the final answer is produced.
- **Prompting Strategy:** The CoV agent's prompt explicitly requires a minimum number of action steps (`min_action_steps`) before answering and a "verification phase" to encourage thorough exploration.

By interleaving discrete camera actions with iterative reasoning, CoV enables the VLM to build a more comprehensive and grounded understanding of the 3D scene, leading to more accurate and spatially aware answers without modifying the underlying VLM architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on challenging embodied question answering benchmarks demonstrate the significant efficacy of the Chain-of-View (CoV) prompting framework.

#### 4.1. Performance on OpenEQA Benchmark

The OpenEQA benchmark, designed for evaluating EQA capabilities in the era of foundation models across 180+ real-world environments, served as a primary evaluation platform. CoV was tested with four mainstream VLMs: Qwen3-VL-Flash, GLM-4.6V, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and GPT-4o-Mini, using the LLM-Match metric.
- **Significant Average Improvement:** CoV achieved an impressive average improvement of **+11.56%** in LLM-Match across all evaluated models compared to their baselines (without CoV).
- **Maximum Gain:** The highest performance gain was observed on **Qwen3-VL-Flash**, which showed a remarkable **+13.62%** improvement. GLM-4.6V, GPT-4o-Mini, and Gemini-2.5-Flash also saw substantial gains of 8.50%, 12.40%, and 11.70%, respectively.
These results underscore CoV's ability to boost spatial reasoning in a training-free manner across diverse VLM architectures.

#### 4.2. Performance on ScanQA and SQA3D Benchmarks

CoV also delivered strong performance on two representative datasets for spatial reasoning: ScanQA (object-grounded QA) and SQA3D (situated reasoning). When compared against state-of-the-art 3D LMMs and zero-shot 2D LMMs, CoV, using GPT-4.1, Gemini Pro Flash, and InternVL for comparison:
- **ScanQA (val):** Achieved state-of-the-art results with **116 CIDEr** (compared to LEO's 101.4) and **31.9% EM@1**. It also showed superior scores across BLEU-4 (16.9), METEOR (24.5), and ROUGE-L (50.4).
- **SQA3D (test):** Demonstrated strong performance with **51.1% EM@1**.
These results indicate CoV's effectiveness not only in generating human-like responses (CIDEr) but also in providing strictly accurate answers (EM@1) for complex 3D scene understanding tasks.

#### 4.3. Test-Time Scaling Ability

A crucial finding is the empirical verification of CoV's test-time scaling capability. By increasing the minimum number of action steps (exploration budget) allowed for the CoV agent, performance gradually improved.
- **Average Additional Improvement:** Compared to a minimum of 1 action step, increasing the action-step limit yielded an average additional improvement of **+2.51%** across the tested models on OpenEQA.
- **Peak Scaling Gain:** Gemini-2.5-Flash showed the highest scaling gain, with an additional **+3.73%** improvement.
This demonstrates that allowing more computational budget for active exploration and multi-step reasoning directly translates into better performance, validating the core hypothesis of the framework. Analysis of action step distribution (Figure 5) showed that most questions were answered in 1-3 steps, but questions requiring more steps exhibited higher scores, further supporting the value of deeper exploration.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

An ablation study on the OpenEQA benchmark investigated the role of the coarse-grained view selection component:
- **Impact of View Selection:** Removing the view selection agent resulted in an average performance drop of **4.59%**. This indicates that the initial filtering of redundant frames and selection of question-relevant anchor views is a critical component for both efficiency and accuracy, preventing the CoV agent from being overwhelmed by irrelevant information.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples (Figures 4, 10-13) further validate that CoV produces more coherent, interpretable reasoning chains and answers that are well-grounded in the spatial context. It effectively localizes objects, reasons about spatial relationships, and aligns predictions with question semantics, even in scenes with occlusion or distributed context.

In summary, the findings robustly support that Chain-of-View prompting is a highly effective, training-free, and model-agnostic strategy that significantly enhances VLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities in 3D EQA by enabling active, iterative viewpoint adjustment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Chain-of-View (CoV) prompting framework represents a significant advancement in Embodied Question Answering (EQA) and the broader field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its core significance lies in a conceptual shift from passive observation to **active, viewpoint-aware reasoning**, fundamentally altering how AI agents interact with and understand 3D environments.

#### 5.1. Conceptual Paradigm Shift

The most profound impact of CoV is its departure from the traditional paradigm where VLMs are fed a static, fixed set of images. Instead, CoV introduces an **"observational agency"** where the model can strategically decide *what* to look at and *how* to adjust its perspective to gather optimal information. This parallels human cognitive processes in complex environments, where we instinctively move our heads or change our position to resolve ambiguities. By extending Chain-of-Thought reasoning to the visual domain as "Chain-of-View," the work highlights that improved reasoning can come not just from processing more data, but from processing *better-selected and dynamically acquired* data.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Spatial Reasoning and Generalization

CoV directly addresses a critical limitation of current VLMs: their struggle with complex spatial reasoning due to occlusions, distributed context, and the need for multi-step inferences in 3D environments. By enabling coarse-to-fine view selection and fine-grained camera adjustments, CoV empowers VLMs to build a more comprehensive and grounded understanding of spatial layouts and object relationships. The significant performance gains on diverse EQA benchmarks (OpenEQA, ScanQA, SQA3D) across multiple underlying VLMs underscore its robust effectiveness and generalizability.

#### 5.3. Training-Free and Model-Agnostic Practicality

A major practical advantage of CoV is its **training-free nature**. It leverages existing, powerful pre-trained VLMs without requiring costly fine-tuning or architectural modifications. This makes CoV a highly accessible and cost-effective solution for improving EQA capabilities, allowing researchers and practitioners to immediately apply it to their existing models. Its model-agnostic design ensures compatibility with a wide range of current and future VLMs, boosting their performance on spatial tasks without needing specialized model development for each new VLM.

#### 5.4. Test-Time Scaling as a Performance Lever

The demonstration of **test-time scaling** is another crucial contribution. The finding that performance gradually improves with an increased budget for action steps provides a clear pathway for enhancing agent capabilities purely at inference time. This is valuable for applications where computational resources can be flexibly allocated, allowing agents to perform more thorough explorations for higher-stakes or more complex queries. It implies a robust mechanism where performance is directly correlated with the "effort" of visual exploration.

#### 5.5. Broad Impact on Embodied AI Systems

The potential impact of CoV extends broadly to various embodied AI applications:
- **Robotics:** Robots could autonomously explore environments, identify objects, and understand spatial relationships more effectively for navigation, manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
- **Autonomous Navigation:** Self-driving cars or drones could better perceive and interpret complex scenarios, especially when critical information is partially hidden or requires a change in perspective.
- **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Agents in virtual worlds could provide more context-aware assistance or answer queries about the digital environment more intelligently.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** More intuitive and spatially grounded interactions could be developed, as AI systems better understand the physical context of human commands.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

While highly impactful, CoV also opens several avenues for future research, as noted in its limitations. These include developing more sophisticated view selection algorithms to avoid suboptimal choices, devising adaptive reasoning mechanisms to handle highly dynamic or cluttered environments more effectively, and mitigating the risk of noise or hallucination from excessive exploration. Further research into optimizing action trajectory and integrating continuous action spaces could also enhance the framework.

In conclusion, CoV significantly advances the state-of-the-art in embodied spatial reasoning. By focusing on *how* an AI agent sees and reasons through active, iterative viewpoint adjustments, it offers a powerful and practical solution that pushes towards more intelligent, adaptive, and human-like AI systems capable of interacting meaningfully with the complex 3D world.
