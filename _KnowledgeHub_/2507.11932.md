# Hyperphantasia: A Benchmark for Evaluating the Mental Visualization Capabilities of Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hyperphantasia benchmark evaluates multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) mental visualization capabilities through synthetic interpolation and extrapolation puzzles. It reveals that current MLLMs largely lack robust internal visual construction and simulation, performing significantly below human levels and often resorting to brittle heuristics.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) primarily assess passive visual perception, such as visual question answering or object recognition.
- There is a gap in evaluating MLLMs' active cognitive processes like mental visualization, which involves internally constructing, manipulating, and simulating visual representations without direct external stimuli.
- Human intelligence relies heavily on mental visualization for complex tasks like spatial reasoning, trajectory prediction, and problem-solving through imaginative simulation, a capability not systematically tested in MLLMs.

## Method

- Researchers developed ==Hyperphantasia==, a new synthetic benchmark comprising four ==procedurally generated puzzle types== designed to test interpolation and extrapolation abilities.
- The benchmark includes tasks like =='Seven Segments'== (inferring digits from partial connections), =='Connect the Dots'== (identifying objects from sparse contours), =='Linear Trajectory'== (extrapolating lines), and =='Parabolic Trajectory'== (predicting ball impact points).
- Reinforcement learning (RL) was applied to a Qwen-VL 2.5 7B model on extrapolation tasks to investigate whether training can enhance mental visualization, using varied datasets in terms of difficulty and composition.

## Results

- Most MLLMs achieved near **0%** accuracy on the 'Seven Segments' task, and even with explicit visual cues, many still failed, indicating issues with basic visual interpretation of out-of-distribution patterns.
- On extrapolation tasks, MLLMs often performed near random guessing on medium and hard difficulties, frequently employing overly simplistic and incorrect heuristics for trajectory prediction.
- Reinforcement learning on diverse, moderately difficult datasets significantly improved Qwen-VL 2.5 7B's performance, for example, achieving **40%** accuracy on Hard Parabolic Trajectory after training on a 'Parabolic Medium' mix, demonstrating some cross-task transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a profound deficiency in mental visualization, with performance significantly deteriorating as task difficulty increases, contrasting sharply with high human accuracy.
> - Failure modes extend beyond a mere lack of mental visualization, with models often unable to interpret even simple geometric patterns when presented in slightly out-of-distribution visual formats.
> - Reinforcement learning can improve generalization for mental visualization, particularly when models are trained on diverse and moderately challenging problems rather than just easy examples, which tend to foster brittle, non-transferable heuristics.
