# Hyperphantasia: A Benchmark for Evaluating the Mental Visualization Capabilities of Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hyperphantasia benchmark evaluates multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) mental visualization capabilities through synthetic interpolation and extrapolation puzzles. It reveals that current MLLMs largely lack robust internal visual construction and simulation, performing significantly below human levels and often resorting to brittle heuristics.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) primarily assess passive visual perception, such as visual question answering or object recognition.
- There is a gap in evaluating MLLMs' active cognitive processes like mental visualization, which involves internally constructing, manipulating, and simulating visual representations without direct external stimuli.
- Human intelligence relies heavily on mental visualization for complex tasks like spatial reasoning, trajectory prediction, and problem-solving through imaginative simulation, a capability not systematically tested in MLLMs.

## Method

- Researchers developed ==Hyperphantasia==, a new synthetic benchmark comprising four ==procedurally generated puzzle types== designed to test interpolation and extrapolation abilities.
- The benchmark includes tasks like =='Seven Segments'== (inferring digits from partial connections), =='Connect the Dots'== (identifying objects from sparse contours), =='Linear Trajectory'== (extrapolating lines), and =='Parabolic Trajectory'== (predicting ball impact points).
- Reinforcement learning (RL) was applied to a Qwen-VL 2.5 7B model on extrapolation tasks to investigate whether training can enhance mental visualization, using varied datasets in terms of difficulty and composition.

## Results

- Most MLLMs achieved near **0%** accuracy on the 'Seven Segments' task, and even with explicit visual cues, many still failed, indicating issues with basic visual interpretation of out-of-distribution patterns.
- On extrapolation tasks, MLLMs often performed near random guessing on medium and hard difficulties, frequently employing overly simplistic and incorrect heuristics for trajectory prediction.
- Reinforcement learning on diverse, moderately difficult datasets significantly improved Qwen-VL 2.5 7B's performance, for example, achieving **40%** accuracy on Hard Parabolic Trajectory after training on a 'Parabolic Medium' mix, demonstrating some cross-task transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a profound deficiency in mental visualization, with performance significantly deteriorating as task difficulty increases, contrasting sharply with high human accuracy.
> - Failure modes extend beyond a mere lack of mental visualization, with models often unable to interpret even simple geometric patterns when presented in slightly out-of-distribution visual formats.
> - Reinforcement learning can improve generalization for mental visualization, particularly when models are trained on diverse and moderately challenging problems rather than just easy examples, which tend to foster brittle, non-transferable heuristics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence, particularly in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), has seen rapid advancements. MLLMs integrate linguistic and visual information, enabling them to perform a variety of tasks such as visual problem-solving, visual mathematical reasoning, and visual question answering (VQA). Numerous benchmarks, including VQAv2, MMMU, MathVista, and SEED-Bench, have been developed to evaluate these capabilities. These benchmarks primarily assess models' ability to process and interpret visual information directly presented in images, focusing on tasks that require passive visual perception, classification, or direct inference from explicit visual cues.

However, human cognition involves a more active form of visual processing known as mental visualization. This capability allows individuals to internally construct, manipulate, simulate, and extrapolate visual representations without direct external stimuli. Cognitive science research has extensively documented this human ability, citing examples such as mentally rotating 3D objects, predicting physical trajectories, and inferring occluded information. While some recent benchmarks, such as those evaluating mental rotation or spatial reasoning (e.g., Xu et al., SRBench, LEGO-Puzzles), have begun to probe more cognitively demanding visual manipulation, they have not systematically assessed the core capacity of MLLMs to visually construct, simulate, and extrapolate information from incomplete visual inputs.

This work addresses a recognized gap in the evaluation of MLLMs by focusing on their mental visualization capabilities. It posits that current benchmarks offer limited insight into MLLMs' active visual reasoning and imagination, which are crucial for real-world scenarios requiring dynamic visual understanding, such as anticipating object trajectories in autonomous systems. By introducing a benchmark specifically designed to isolate and evaluate these abilities, the paper aims to provide a more comprehensive assessment of MLLMs' cognitive skills beyond passive perception, positioning itself at the intersection of multimodal AI evaluation and cognitive science.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate the mental visualization capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models. The authors define mental visualization as the cognitive process of internally constructing and manipulating visual representations without explicit external stimuli.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
- **Gap in Current MLLM Evaluation:** Existing benchmarks for MLLMs predominantly focus on passive visual perception, captioning, or retrieval. They provide limited insight into whether MLLMs can actively reason about, simulate, extrapolate, or construct latent visual structures. This leaves a critical aspect of intelligence unassessed.
- **Importance of Mental Visualization in Human Cognition:** Mental visualization is a fundamental human cognitive skill, vital for tasks involving complex reasoning, prediction, abstraction, spatial navigation, inferring physical trajectories, and solving problems through imaginative simulation. Without this ability, human-like intelligence in AI remains incomplete.
- **Limitations for Real-world Applications:** The absence of robust mental visualization capabilities could hinder MLLMs in real-world applications that demand dynamic visual understanding, such as autonomous vehicles needing to anticipate object movements or robots performing complex manipulation tasks based on partial visual information.
- **Need for a Targeted Benchmark:** There is a recognized need for a benchmark specifically designed to probe these active visual reasoning and imagination abilities in a controlled and systematic manner, allowing for detailed analysis of model performance across varying levels of complexity.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines several key contributions:
- Introduction of Hyperphantasia, a novel synthetic benchmark comprising four distinct puzzle types, each with three difficulty levels, designed to assess MLLMs' mental visualization.
- Comprehensive evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs on Hyperphantasia to reveal their current capabilities and limitations in mental visualization.
- Exploration of reinforcement learning (RL) as a method to improve visual simulation capabilities in MLLMs, analyzing how task difficulty and diversity in training affect generalization.
- Identification of specific failure modes in MLLMs beyond the lack of mental visualization, such as issues with visual robustness and interpretation of slightly out-of-distribution visual formats.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Hyperphantasia, a synthetic benchmark composed of four procedurally generated puzzles designed to evaluate the mental visualization abilities of MLLMs. These puzzles are organized into two main categories: Interpolation and Extrapolation, with each containing two sub-categories. Each puzzle is presented at three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard), with 100 samples per difficulty level for each task, totaling 1,200 samples.

#### 3.1. Hyperphantasia Dataset Design

The difficulty levels are controlled by specific hyperparameters for each task:
- **Interpolation Tasks:** These assess the model's ability to infer internal boundaries and recognize visual concepts from partial information.
 - **Seven Segments:** Models are given a grid of numbered dots and a list of edges to connect. They must identify the digit implicitly formed. Difficulty is modulated by the number of digits in the final shape (Easy: 3 digits, Medium: 4, Hard: 5).
 - **Connect the Dots:** Models receive a set of dots sampled from an object's external contour and must identify the object from four options. Difficulty increases as the minimum distance between sampled dots increases, reducing visual fidelity (Easy: 10 pixels, Medium: 20, Hard: 25). Objects are sourced from the Clipart subset of the DomainNet dataset. Incorrect options are generated using GPT-4o and manually verified.
- **Extrapolation Tasks:** These evaluate the model's capacity to anticipate and extend visual structures beyond explicit information, predicting trajectories.
 - **Linear Trajectory:** Models are shown a triangle with a portion of one edge removed and must identify the correct node for the missing segment from four options. Difficulty is controlled by the visible portion of the edge (Easy: 90% visible, Medium: 60%, Hard: 40%).
 - **Parabolic Trajectory:** Models are presented with a partial trajectory of a thrown ball and must determine its impact point on a horizontal surface from four options. Difficulty is modulated by the length of the visible trajectory (Easy: 90% visible, Medium: 60%, Hard: 40%). Puzzle generation involves random sampling of parameters like angles and initial height.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup

- **Models Evaluated:** A range of state-of-the-art MLLMs were evaluated, including proprietary models (o4-mini, GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet) and open-source models (Qwen-VL-2.5 (7B, 32B), LLaMA 3.2 (11B, 90B), LLaVA-OneVision (7B, 72B), Molmo (7B, 72B), Deepseek-VL2).
- **Human Evaluation:** Human performance was established as a baseline. For Seven Segments, 100% accuracy was reported due to its perceived triviality for humans upon manual inspection. For other puzzles, human participants answered each question at least three times, and average accuracy was reported across tasks and difficulty levels.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Models were prompted to explain their reasoning but required to enclose the final answer within `<ANSWER>` tags. A temperature of 0 was used for deterministic outputs, with up to three regeneration attempts if the format was not followed. Deepseek-VL2 was an exception, with answers extracted via response parsing from free-form output. Images were 384x384 pixel JPGs.

#### 3.3. Improving Mental Visualization via Reinforcement Learning (RL)

- **Focus:** RL experiments were restricted to the Extrapolation tasks (Linear and Parabolic Trajectory).
- **Rationale:** RL was chosen over Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to enhance reasoning abilities without requiring detailed thinking traces, which are typically unavailable. SFT often fails to generalize from easy to hard examples without such supervision.
- **Training Data:** Multiple training datasets were constructed, varying in difficulty and task composition (e.g., "Linear Easy," "Parabolic Medium," "Linear Mix," "All Mix," "Hard Mix") to investigate generalization.
- **Model and Framework:** Qwen-VL 2.5 7B was trained using the GRPO implementation.
- **Evaluation:** The best RL-trained model checkpoint was selected based on test loss and evaluated on the original Hyperphantasia extrapolation puzzles.

#### 3.4. Investigating Model Failure on Seven Segments Puzzles

- **Experiment:** To understand the poor performance on Seven Segments, an additional experiment was conducted where the input images for the Easy set were augmented by explicitly drawing the edges between specified dots.
- **Purpose:** This intervention aimed to remove the need for internal construction, making the intended digit immediately recognizable through visual cues, and thereby isolating whether the failure was due to a lack of mental visualization or other visual recognition issues.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies (Appendix)

- **Prompt Sensitivity:** Different prompt formats (shortened, instructing for extra care, single-letter answers, defining an AI expert persona) were tested on the Easy Linear Trajectory task to assess their impact on model performance.
- **Multi-shot Prompting:** An experiment with 3-shot prompting was conducted on Easy Linear Trajectory puzzles to investigate if providing examples would improve performance.
- **Language-only Seven Segments:** The Seven Segments task was converted into a text-only format and presented to both MLLMs and language-only models to evaluate their ability to solve the puzzle without visual input, relying solely on symbolic descriptions of dot connections.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs on Hyperphantasia revealed a consistent pattern of limitations in mental visualization, with performance generally degrading as task difficulty increased. A significant gap was observed between MLLM and human performance across most tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Model Performance and Limitations

- **Performance Degradation with Difficulty:** All models exhibited a clear decline in accuracy from Easy to Medium and Hard difficulty levels across most puzzle types.
- **Task-Specific Strengths/Weaknesses:** Models displayed uneven capabilities, performing better on some puzzle types than others, indicating a lack of a unified internal mechanism for general visual reasoning.
- **Human-Model Gap:** Human accuracy consistently remained high, even on harder tasks (e.g., 94-95% on Connect the Dots Hard, 89% on Linear Trajectory Hard), while models often struggled to surpass random guessing levels.

#### 4.2. Interpolation Tasks

- **Seven Segments:** This task proved to be particularly challenging. Most models, with the exceptions of Gemini 3 Pro Preview (98% Easy) and o4-mini (83% Easy), failed almost completely (0-4% accuracy), even on the Easy setting. Analysis of responses showed models frequently produced fixed guesses (e.g., Qwen-VL 2.5 7B outputting "012" in 32% of Easy examples), suggesting a failure to engage in visual reasoning and instead relying on memorized patterns or default completions.
- **Connect the Dots:** Models performed relatively well on the Easy setting, with Gemini (97%) and GPT-4o (96%) showing high accuracy. However, performance significantly deteriorated on Medium and Hard sets (Gemini, the best, achieved 75% on Hard). Some models (e.g., Molmo, Deepseek) performed near random guessing on harder sets. A notable failure mode was the hallucination of non-existent visual features to justify incorrect answers, indicating limitations in visual grounding and fine-grained perceptual reasoning.

#### 4.3. Extrapolation Tasks

- **Linear Trajectory:** This task was challenging for MLLMs. Claude achieved the highest accuracy on Easy (60%), but most models were below 50% on Easy and dropped to near-random guessing on Medium and Hard. Human accuracy for Easy Linear Trajectory was 100%, highlighting a substantial disparity. Some models resorted to simplistic heuristics, such as selecting the rightmost point as the impact location, demonstrating a failure in genuine trajectory reasoning.
- **Parabolic Trajectory:** This was the most difficult task for models. While Claude again showed the best performance on Easy (40%), its accuracy, along with most other models, rapidly deteriorated to near-random guessing on Medium and Hard. LLaMA 3.2 72B was an exception, showing 31% on Medium. Human accuracy on this task dropped from nearly 100% on Easy to approximately 50% on Medium and Hard, suggesting that even humans might struggle with the fine distinction between difficult options.

#### 4.4. Improving Mental Visualization via Reinforcement Learning

- **Effectiveness of RL:** RL training improved mental visualization abilities, particularly for the Qwen-VL 2.5 7B model.
- **Training Data Composition:**
 - Training solely on Easy puzzles resulted in weak generalization; models adopted superficial heuristics that failed on harder tasks (e.g., 9% accuracy on Medium Linear Trajectory after training only on Easy Linear Trajectory).
 - Training on Medium puzzles exhibited stronger generalization. For example, a model trained on Medium Parabolic Trajectory surpassed o4-mini, achieving 40% accuracy on Hard Parabolic Trajectory.
 - Combining Easy and Medium puzzles (mixed datasets) further improved generalization and demonstrated cross-task transfer (e.g., "Parabolic Mix" model showed high performance on Parabolic Trajectory and 44% on Easy Linear Trajectory).
- **Importance of Diversity and Challenge:** These findings suggest that training on diverse and moderately challenging problems is crucial for developing robust and transferable mental visualization capabilities, rather than relying on large-scale training on simple examples.

#### 4.5. Investigating Model Failure on Seven Segments Puzzles

- **Impact of Explicit Visual Cues:** When input images for Seven Segments were augmented by explicitly drawing the edges, only GPT-4o (76%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (98%) were able to leverage these cues. All other models continued to fail (0% accuracy).
- **Beyond Mental Visualization:** This result suggests that for many models, the failure on Seven Segments is not solely due to a lack of mental visualization but also reflects an inability to extract and interpret simple geometric patterns when presented in a slightly out-of-distribution visual format. This points to a broader limitation in visual robustness.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies (Appendix Findings)

- **Prompt Sensitivity:** Variations in prompt formatting had minimal to no significant effect on model performance on the Easy Linear Trajectory task, indicating that the core limitations were not simply due to prompting.
- **Multi-shot Prompting:** Providing 3-shot examples also resulted in minimal to no improvement in performance for most models, suggesting that this technique alone is insufficient to overcome fundamental mental visualization limitations. LLaVA-OneVision 72B notably failed to process multi-image inputs.
- **Language-only Seven Segments:** Language-only models failed this task entirely, as expected. MLLMs that performed well on the image-based version (o4-mini, GPT-4o) also performed well in the text-only format, while others that failed the image-based version continued to struggle, reinforcing that some MLLMs might possess an inherent capability that is not solely reliant on the explicit visual input.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and development of Multimodal Large Language Models by introducing Hyperphantasia, a novel benchmark specifically designed to assess mental visualization capabilities—a critical aspect of cognition that is largely unexplored in current MLLM evaluations.

The **significance** of this work lies in:
- **Highlighting a Critical MLLM Limitation:** The comprehensive evaluation demonstrates that state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit substantial limitations in mental visualization, visual grounding, and robustness to minor visual shifts, confirming a significant gap between AI and human cognitive abilities. This suggests that current MLLMs may often mimic reasoning through learned patterns rather than possessing a true, generalized understanding of underlying visual content and its dynamics.
- **Introducing a Focused Evaluation Tool:** Hyperphantasia provides a standardized and procedurally generated dataset for systematically probing and comparing MLLMs on tasks requiring active visual reasoning, simulation, and extrapolation. This allows for controlled analysis across varying complexity levels.
- **Demonstrating Learning Pathways:** The reinforcement learning experiments indicate that mental visualization abilities can be acquired through training, provided that the training data is diverse, appropriately challenging, and structured to promote robust and transferable skills rather than superficial heuristics. This offers a tangible direction for future MLLM training paradigms.
- **Identifying Broader Visual Recognition Failures:** The targeted experiment on Seven Segments puzzles reveals that MLLM failures are not solely due to a lack of mental visualization but also stem from an inability to robustly interpret and extract structure from slightly out-of-distribution visual inputs. This points to a fundamental limitation in visual processing beyond just advanced reasoning.

The **potential impact** of this research includes:
- **Guiding MLLM Development:** The findings underscore the necessity for MLLM research to move beyond passive visual perception and focus on developing active visual thinking and internal representation capabilities. This could lead to architectural innovations and training methodologies that foster more robust and flexible visual reasoning.
- **Advancing AI for Complex Real-world Applications:** Improved mental visualization is crucial for MLLMs to excel in domains requiring dynamic understanding and prediction, such as autonomous navigation (anticipating object trajectories), robotics (planning manipulation in uncertain environments), scientific discovery (simulating physical phenomena), and creative design (imagining novel forms).
- **Inspiring New Research Directions:** The paper suggests exploring "visual thinking tokens" or other forms of internal visual representations that do not necessarily need to be human-interpretable but can facilitate internal visual simulation. Hyperphantasia is positioned as an ideal testbed for such explorations, encouraging interdisciplinary research at the intersection of AI, cognitive science, and computer vision.
- **Improving Trustworthiness of AI:** By exposing limitations in visual robustness and grounding, the work contributes to identifying areas where MLLMs can fail in unexpected ways, informing efforts to build more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

While Hyperphantasia focuses on a specific subset of mental visualization tasks, it provides a foundational benchmark for future work to expand into a broader spectrum of visual imagination capabilities in multimodal models.
