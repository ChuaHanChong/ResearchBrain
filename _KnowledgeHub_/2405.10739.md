# Efficient Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A collaborative team from Tencent Youtu Lab, SJTU, BAAI, and ECNU presents a comprehensive survey categorizing and analyzing methods for developing efficient Multimodal Large Language Models. The survey demonstrates how diverse architectural and training strategies enable models with reduced computational costs to achieve performance competitive with, or sometimes exceeding, much larger general MLLMs across 14 visual-language benchmarks.

## Problem

- Contemporary Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) achieve high performance due to scaling laws, but incur immense computational and memory costs for both training and inference.
- The extensive resource demands restrict MLLM access to a few dominant corporations, raising concerns about the democratization of AI and user privacy.
- Deploying powerful MLLMs on resource-constrained edge devices remains a significant challenge, hindering widespread adoption and real-world application.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic literature review, synthesizing and organizing existing research on ==efficient MLLMs==.
- A ==six-primary category taxonomy== was developed to classify diverse research efforts: Architecture, Efficient Vision, Efficient LLMs, Training, Data and Benchmarks, and Application.
- A detailed comparative analysis was performed on 22 efficient MLLMs and 13 larger MLLMs across 14 established visual-language benchmarks, providing empirical context and a public GitHub repository.

## Results

- Architectural advancements, including compact vision encoders, efficient vision-language projectors (e.g., Mamba-based), and Small Language Models (SLMs) under **3 billion** parameters, are fundamental to constructing efficient MLLMs.
- Vision token compression methods, such as multi-view input and specialized token processing modules, effectively manage the computational burden of high-resolution images and long video sequences.
- Empirical comparisons show that many efficient MLLMs achieve performance comparable to, and occasionally surpass, much larger general MLLMs on **14** established visual-language benchmarks, enabling their practical use in resource-sensitive applications like biomedical analysis and document understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficiency in MLLMs is achieved through a multi-faceted approach, combining architectural innovations, optimized training methodologies, and strategic data utilization, rather than solely relying on smaller base models.
> - Techniques like vision token compression, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), and Mamba architectures are crucial for effectively processing high-resolution inputs and improving inference speed while maintaining scalability.
> - High-quality pre-training and instruction-tuning data, coupled with parameter-efficient transfer learning techniques, are essential for adapting MLLMs to specific tasks with reduced resource expenditure and preserving pre-trained knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The past year has witnessed a revolutionary surge in the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with models like OpenAI’s GPT-4V and Google’s Gemini showcasing unprecedented performance in complex visual question answering, understanding, and reasoning tasks. This success is deeply rooted in the "scaling law," which posits that model performance improves with increased data, computational power, and model size. MLLMs effectively leverage the pre-training knowledge of both Large Language Models (LLMs) and modality-specific encoders (e.g., vision transformers), circumventing the need for training from scratch and inheriting robust language generation and transfer learning abilities.

However, this scaling comes at a significant cost. The immense model sizes and the high computational demands for both training and inference (e.g., MiniGPT-v2 requiring over 800 GPU hours for training, LLaVA-1.5 with Vicuna-13B demanding 18.2T FLOPS and 41.6G memory for a single inference) create substantial barriers. These resource requirements limit access to only a few dominant corporations, raising concerns about democratization and privacy, especially as mainstream MLLMs primarily operate in the cloud. Running these powerful models on edge devices, which is crucial for widespread adoption and privacy protection, becomes a formidable challenge.

This survey directly addresses these critical limitations by focusing specifically on *efficient* MLLMs. It distinguishes itself within the broader MLLM research landscape by systematically reviewing the strategies and advancements aimed at reducing the resource consumption of MLLMs while minimizing performance degradation. This is not merely a survey of MLLMs, but a focused examination of *how to make them practical and accessible*. The work traces the evolution of efficient MLLMs from simply replacing large LLMs with lightweight counterparts to developing sophisticated architectural optimizations, specialized components, and data compression techniques. It positions itself as a vital guide for researchers and practitioners looking to overcome the inherent resource-intensiveness of state-of-the-art MLLMs and deploy them in real-world, resource-constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this survey stems directly from the paradoxical success and inherent limitations of contemporary MLLMs. While these models have demonstrated "remarkable performance" across a spectrum of multimodal tasks, their "extensive model size and high training and inference costs" pose significant obstacles to their widespread adoption in both academic research and industrial applications. This is particularly acute in burgeoning areas like edge computing, where computational and memory resources are severely constrained. The authors explicitly highlight the democratization and privacy concerns arising from the control of powerful, cloud-based MLLMs by a handful of large corporations, emphasizing the urgent need for more accessible and deployable alternatives.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the survey are:
1. **Comprehensive and Systematic Review:** To offer an exhaustive and structured overview of the current research landscape dedicated to efficient MLLMs. This involves organizing the diverse methodologies and models into a coherent framework.
2. **Summarize Research State:** Specifically, to detail:
 - The **timeline** of representative efficient MLLMs, illustrating the historical progression of this sub-field.
 - The current state of **efficient structures and strategies** employed in MLLMs, categorizing and explaining various architectural, training, and data optimization techniques.
 - The **applications** where efficient MLLMs are particularly relevant or have demonstrated significant impact, especially those requiring resource-sensitive deployments.
3. **Identify Limitations and Future Directions:** To critically analyze the shortcomings of current efficient MLLM research and propose promising avenues for future investigation, thereby serving as a roadmap for the community.
4. **Resource Provision:** To provide a publicly accessible GitHub repository that compiles and organizes the discussed papers, ensuring the survey remains a living document that can be actively maintained and updated with emerging research.

In essence, the authors are motivated to distill the rapidly growing knowledge in efficient MLLMs into an organized, accessible resource that can catalyze further research and development towards practical, democratized, and privacy-preserving multimodal AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology primarily involves a comprehensive literature review, synthesis, and organization of existing research on efficient MLLMs. The authors adopt a systematic approach to categorize and present the advancements in this fast-evolving field.

The core of their methodology is the development of a **six-primary category taxonomy** to structure the diverse research efforts:
1. **Architecture:** This category delves into the fundamental design of MLLMs, focusing on how different components (vision encoders, vision-language projectors, small language models) and overall structures are optimized for efficiency. It also covers techniques like vision token compression and novel efficient architectural designs (e.g., MoE, Mamba).
2. **Efficient Vision:** This section reviews general strategies for making vision models more efficient, drawing from broader computer vision research. It covers compact architectures, pruning, knowledge distillation, and quantization as potential methods adaptable for MLLMs.
3. **Efficient LLMs:** Analogous to Efficient Vision, this part surveys general techniques for improving the computational efficiency and scalability of language models, including optimizations in attention mechanisms, alternative frameworks (like Mixture of Experts or State Space Models), and fine-tuning strategies.
4. **Training:** This category focuses on the methodologies used to develop efficient MLLMs, encompassing pre-training, instruction-tuning, diverse training steps (e.g., single-stage vs. multi-stage), and parameter-efficient transfer learning techniques.
5. **Data and Benchmarks:** This section addresses the role of datasets (pre-training and instruction-tuning) and evaluation benchmarks in the development and assessment of efficient MLLMs. It evaluates the trade-offs in dataset characteristics and the suite of benchmarks used for performance comparison.
6. **Application:** This final category explores the practical implications of efficient MLLMs in various real-world domains, emphasizing scenarios where balancing performance with computational cost is crucial, such as biomedical analysis, document understanding, and video comprehension.

Within each category, the authors further break down specific techniques and provide examples of representative models. They illustrate key concepts with diagrams (e.g., timeline of efficient MLLMs, architectural diagrams, MoE and Mamba structures, S2-Wrapper, GQA). A crucial part of their approach is a **detailed comparative analysis** of 22 mainstream efficient MLLMs and 13 larger MLLMs across 14 well-established visual-language benchmarks, providing empirical context to the theoretical discussions.

Finally, the authors commit to maintaining a **GitHub repository** (https://github.com/lijiannuist/Efficient-Multimodal-LLMs-Survey) that mirrors their taxonomy and compiles the featured papers. This demonstrates a forward-looking approach, recognizing the dynamic nature of the field and aiming to provide a continuously updated resource for the community.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey systematically presents a wealth of findings across various dimensions of efficient MLLMs:

#### 4.1. Architectural Innovations (Section 2)

- **Vision Encoders:** While a minor component in terms of parameters, the choice of vision encoder significantly impacts efficiency. The trend involves using well-aligned pre-trained models like CLIP, DINOv2, or SigLIP. Some works (BRAVE, Cobra, SPHINX-X) propose combining multiple encoders with diverse inductive biases to capture a wider range of visual representations, while lightweight models like ViTamin offer significant parameter reduction with competitive accuracy.
- **Vision-Language Projectors:** This bridging component has seen evolution from simple MLPs to more complex, efficient designs. Attention-based projectors (Q-Former, Perceiver Resampler, MEQ-Former) efficiently extract visual features. CNN-based projectors (LDPv2 in MobileVLMv2) and Mamba-based approaches (VL-Mamba with 2D VSS) offer parameter efficiency and faster processing. Hybrid structures like Honeybee's C/D-Abstractor balance local context modeling and adaptability in visual token count.
- **Small Language Models (SLMs):** As the largest component, SLM selection is paramount for efficiency. The survey highlights the use of models with less than 3 billion parameters (e.g., Phi-2, Gemma-2B, MobileLLaMA, TinyLlama, Qwen-1.8B, Phi-1.5). These SLMs, often trained with specialized data recipes, demonstrate competitive performance against much larger LLMs, forming the backbone of many efficient MLLMs.
- **Vision Token Compression:** Addressing the quadratic scaling of Transformer costs with image resolution, this area is critical for high-resolution images and videos. Techniques include:
 - **Multi-view Input:** Using global (low-res) and local (high-res patches) views (LLaVA-UHD, InternLM-XComposer2-4KHD).
 - **Token Processing:** Modules to condense tokens (LLaVA-UHD's compression, LLaVA-PruMerge, MADTP for adaptive reduction), or specialized merging/resampling for document tasks (TinyChart, TextHawk).
 - **Multi-Scale Information Fusion:** Twin encoders for high/low resolution (Mini-Gemini) or multi-scale pre-training (S2-Wrapper).
 - **Vision Expert Agents:** Leveraging specialized agents (OCR Agent, Grounding Agent in P2G, MoVA) to enhance detail perception, compensating for single encoder limitations.
 - **Video-Specific Methods:** Techniques like T-Selector (Elysium) and unified representation (VideoLLaVA, LLaMA-VID, MA-LMM) to manage long video sequences efficiently.
- **Efficient Structures:**
 - **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):** MoE-LLaVA and MM1 leverage sparse activation to increase model capacity without proportional inference cost.
 - **Mamba:** Cobra and VL-Mamba integrate the Mamba architecture for linear-time scaling, competitive performance, and faster inference, addressing sequential modeling challenges.
 - **Inference Acceleration:** Methods like speculative decoding (SPD), dynamic token pruning (FastV), and visual token withdrawal in deeper layers (VTW) significantly reduce computational overhead during inference.

#### 4.2. Efficient Vision (Section 3) and Efficient LLMs (Section 4)

The survey identifies a range of general efficiency techniques from the broader vision and language model domains, which are applicable to MLLMs. These include compact architectures (e.g., Reformer, EfficientFormer), pruning (unstructured, structured, hybrid), knowledge distillation (homomorphic, heteromorphic), and quantization (post-training, quantization-aware, hardware-aware) for vision, and attention optimization (sharing-based, feature reduction, approximate attention), alternative frameworks (MoE, State Space Models like RWKV, Mamba), and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT like LoRA, (IA)3) for LLMs. These areas offer a rich toolkit for future MLLM efficiency research.

#### 4.3. Training Methodologies (Section 5)

- **Pre-training:** Focuses on aligning modalities using large-scale image-caption pairs. The choice of which parameters to unfreeze (only projector, partial encoder/SLM) depends on the dataset quality and desired outcome, with VILA demonstrating the importance of updating the base LLM. Multi-stage pre-training (Idefics2) optimizes compute efficiency.
- **Instruction-Tuning (IT):** Crucial for task adaptation, often using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on instruction-based dialogue datasets. LaVIN introduces Mixture-of-Modality Adaptation for cost-effective IT, while HyperLLaVA explores dynamic tuning strategies.
- **Diverse Training Steps:** Some works (SPHINX-X, Cobra) argue for single-stage training to simplify pipelines, while others (TinyGPT-V) adopt multi-stage specialized processes.
- **Parameter Efficient Transfer Learning (PEFT):** LoRA-based methods (EAS, MemVP) are commonly adopted to protect pre-trained knowledge and reduce resource expenditure during fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Data and Benchmarks (Section 6)

- **Pre-training Data:** High-quality, large-scale image-caption pairs (CC3M, LAION-5B) are fundamental. There's a growing trend towards using powerful MLLMs (like GPT-4V) to generate more detailed, fine-grained instruction-tuning data (ShareGPT4V, ALLaVA). Interleaved multimodal data (MMC4, Obelics) is also recognized as beneficial.
- **Instruction-Tuning Data:** Derives from task-specific datasets (VQA, DocVQA) or self-instruction methods using LLMs for multi-turn conversations. Integrating text-only instructional data also improves conversational skills (VILA).
- **Benchmarks:** The survey provides a comprehensive comparison of models on 14 established VL benchmarks (VQAv2, GQA, MMMU, MME, MM-Vet, etc.), demonstrating that many efficient MLLMs achieve performance comparable to, and sometimes exceeding, much larger general MLLMs. For example, MM1-3B (3B parameters) shows competitive results against GPT-4V and Gemini Pro (much larger models) on several benchmarks.

#### 4.5. Applications (Section 7)

Efficient MLLMs are crucial for specialized, resource-intensive tasks:
- **Biomedical Analysis:** LLaVA-Rad (radiology reports) and MoE-TinyMed demonstrate rapid, lower-parameter performance suitable for clinical settings.
- **Document Understanding:** TinyChart, TextHawk, HRVDA, and Monkey address challenges of high-resolution images and fine-grained visual perception in document processing.
- **Video Comprehension:** mPLUG-video, Video-LLaVA, LLaMA-VID, and MA-LMM develop methods to handle the extensive visual tokens in long videos efficiently.

In summary, the main findings highlight a robust and diverse landscape of techniques making MLLMs more efficient, ranging from architectural choices and training strategies to data curation and application-specific optimizations. The empirical comparisons underscore that efficiency does not necessarily mean a significant compromise in performance, enabling broader applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey on "Efficient Multimodal Large Language Models" carries significant implications and potential impact across the AI ecosystem:

1. **Democratization of Advanced AI:** The most profound impact lies in lowering the barrier to entry for MLLM research and development. By detailing strategies to reduce computational costs and model sizes, the survey empowers researchers in academia, startups, and smaller organizations to participate in the MLLM revolution. This fosters a more diverse and innovative research landscape, moving beyond the exclusive domain of well-resourced tech giants.
2. **Enablement of Edge Computing and Privacy-Preserving AI:** The focus on efficiency is critical for deploying MLLMs on resource-constrained edge devices such as smartphones, smart cameras, and IoT devices. This transition from cloud-centric to on-device processing has direct implications for user privacy (as data processing occurs locally) and latency (real-time responses). This opens up vast new application spaces for intelligent agents in everyday life, from personalized assistants to autonomous systems.
3. **Sustainability in AI Development:** The exponential growth of large AI models raises serious concerns about their environmental footprint. By promoting efficient architectures and training methodologies, this survey contributes to more sustainable AI development, reducing energy consumption and carbon emissions associated with the creation and deployment of powerful models.
4. **Expansion of MLLM Application Domains:** The survey highlights how efficient MLLMs are crucial for specialized, resource-intensive tasks that were previously infeasible due to computational bottlenecks.
 - **Healthcare:** Efficient MLLMs can power on-device diagnostic tools and intelligent medical assistants, providing rapid analysis of medical images and clinical data without transferring sensitive information to the cloud.
 - **Document Analysis:** Industries reliant on extensive document processing (legal, finance, administration) can leverage efficient MLLMs for faster and more accurate understanding of high-resolution documents, charts, and tables.
 - **Video Understanding:** Real-time analysis of long video streams, critical for surveillance, autonomous vehicles, and content moderation, becomes more feasible with efficient video comprehension MLLMs.
5. **Roadmap for Future Research:** By systematically identifying current limitations (e.g., handling extended multimodal contexts, supporting more diverse input/output modalities beyond image-text, leveraging a broader range of lightweight LLMs, improving instruction-tuning data quality) and proposing concrete future directions (e.g., embodied agents on edge devices), the survey acts as a valuable guide. It helps researchers pinpoint critical challenges and promising avenues, accelerating progress in the field.
6. **Consolidation and Accessibility of Knowledge:** The rapidly evolving nature of MLLM research can be overwhelming. This survey consolidates disparate findings into a structured, accessible format, complete with a living GitHub repository. This makes it easier for newcomers to grasp the state-of-the-art and for experienced researchers to identify gaps and build upon existing work efficiently.

In essence, this work is significant because it tackles the critical bottleneck preventing MLLMs from realizing their full potential beyond specialized, highly resourced environments. By championing efficiency, it paves the way for a more accessible, sustainable, and widely applicable era of multimodal artificial intelligence.
