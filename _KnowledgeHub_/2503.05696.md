# A Multi-Fidelity Control Variate Approach for Policy Gradient Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Texas at Austin, The University of British Columbia, and DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory developed Multi-Fidelity Policy Gradients (MFPG), a framework that leverages abundant low-fidelity data as a control variate to significantly reduce the variance of policy gradient estimates. The method improves sample efficiency and robustness for reinforcement learning in high-fidelity, data-scarce environments, even with dynamics mismatch or reward misspecification.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning requires substantial amounts of data, which is often expensive, time-consuming, or unsafe to collect from real-world systems or high-fidelity simulations.
- The inability to efficiently collect high-fidelity data creates a barrier to the widespread development and deployment of RL solutions.
- Cheap, abundant low-fidelity simulation data exists, but is typically too coarse or inaccurate for direct policy training or deployment.

## Method

- A **MFPG** (==Multi-Fidelity Policy Gradients==) framework is proposed, using ==control variates== to combine limited high-fidelity data with extensive low-fidelity data to estimate policy gradients.
- A ==policy reparameterization trick== is employed to generate correlated trajectories across high- and low-fidelity environments, enhancing the statistical relationship between samples.
- The framework provides an ==unbiased, variance-reduced estimator== for the on-policy gradient of the high-fidelity objective, instantiated with a ==multi-fidelity REINFORCE== algorithm.

## Results

- MFPG significantly improved mean performance over High-Fidelity Only baselines in **8 out of 8** tested scenarios with mild to moderate dynamics gaps, achieving **1** final return collapse and **0** AUC collapses across **15** main scenarios.
- In a task with anti-correlated low-fidelity rewards, MFPG achieved substantially better performance than both High-Fidelity Only and Low-Fidelity Only baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the policy reparameterization trick was critical for achieving strong cross-fidelity correlation and accelerating learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MFPG empirically achieves substantial variance reduction in policy gradient estimates, narrowing the performance gap to methods with significantly more high-fidelity samples.
> - The approach demonstrates strong reliability and robustness across a wide range of dynamics mismatches between fidelities, outperforming other methods that often suffer performance collapses with inaccurate low-fidelity data.
> - MFPG remains effective even when low-fidelity rewards are severely misspecified (e.g., anti-correlated to high-fidelity rewards), leveraging statistical relationships rather than requiring qualitative agreement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated significant capabilities across diverse applications, including nuclear fusion control, aerial vehicle piloting, and molecular discovery. However, a pervasive challenge is the substantial data requirement for training RL policies. Often, this data must be collected from real-world operational systems or expensive high-fidelity simulations, which can be infeasible, unsafe, or prohibitively costly. This limitation presents a barrier to the widespread development and deployment of RL solutions, particularly in applications requiring "sim-to-real" transfer where policies trained in simulation need to perform well in the real world.

Concurrently, various low-fidelity simulation tools are frequently available that can generate large volumes of data cheaply. These tools, such as reduced-order models, linearized dynamics, heuristic reward functions, or generative world models, can provide useful information for RL training, even if their approximations are too coarse for direct policy deployment or accurate representation of the target environment.

This work addresses the challenge of data scarcity in high-fidelity environments by integrating low-fidelity data. It positions itself within several established lines of research:

- **Variance Reduction in RL via Control Variates:** The method of control variates (CVs) is a standard technique for reducing variance in Monte Carlo estimation. In RL, CVs have a long history, typically involving the subtraction of reward baselines or state-value functions from Monte Carlo returns to accelerate learning. This research extends the application of control variates to a multi-fidelity setting, distinguishing itself from prior work that predominantly focuses on single-environment contexts. Specifically, it differs from existing multi-fidelity CV approaches by addressing Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with function approximation and introducing a novel policy reparameterization trick to enhance the correlation of trajectories across fidelities, thereby improving variance reduction.

- **Multi-Fidelity RL and Fine-Tuning for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** This area focuses on designing training curricula that leverage data from multiple simulation fidelities to train high-performing policies for the most accurate (highest-fidelity) environment while minimizing computational costs. Existing multi-fidelity RL methods and sim-to-real transfer techniques often involve bootstrapping value functions or models from simulation, guiding real-world exploration, or aligning state distributions. The current work contributes to this landscape by proposing a new policy gradient estimator that seamlessly incorporates cross-domain data, complementing existing paradigms for fine-tuning in target domains.

- **Off-Dynamics RL:** This field deals with scenarios where multi-fidelity environments exhibit mismatched transition dynamics. Traditional approaches include system identification or domain randomization. More recent policy adaptation methods, such as DARC and PAR, attempt to compensate for dynamics gaps by learning action transformations, estimating importance weights, or augmenting rewards based on dynamics mismatch. This paper differentiates its approach by fundamentally grounding policy learning in high-fidelity samples and using low-fidelity data *exclusively* as a variance-reduction mechanism. This design choice provides theoretical guarantees of convergence to locally optimal policies in the high-fidelity environment, irrespective of the dynamics mismatch, a characteristic that often degrades in other off-dynamics RL methods as dynamics gaps grow. Additionally, the proposed method demonstrates effectiveness even in the presence of reward misspecification, which is not a primary focus of many dynamics-mismatch specific techniques.

In summary, this work introduces a novel multi-fidelity policy gradient framework that enhances the sample efficiency of high-fidelity data by systematically integrating abundant low-fidelity data through a variance-reduction mechanism, thereby offering a reliable and robust approach to RL training in data-scarce, real-world relevant scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from the practical limitations encountered when deploying Reinforcement Learning algorithms in complex, real-world systems or when training with high-fidelity simulations.

**Problem Statement and Motivation:**
Many high-impact applications of RL, such as autonomous systems (robotics, aerial vehicles), complex control systems (nuclear fusion reactors), and scientific discovery (material design), necessitate policies that perform reliably and optimally in their target environments. However, acquiring the vast amounts of interaction data typically required by RL algorithms from these target environments poses significant challenges:
- **Cost:** Running high-fidelity simulations can be computationally expensive.
- **Feasibility:** Interactions with real-world operational systems may be difficult or time-consuming to obtain.
- **Safety:** Experimenting with real-world hardware can be unsafe or lead to undesirable outcomes during the learning process.

These constraints often lead to a "data-scarce" regime for high-fidelity samples, significantly impeding the development and deployment of RL policies. The dilemma is that while RL algorithms need rich experience, obtaining this experience from the most accurate source is often impractical.

**Opportunity:**
A common observation is the availability of "low-fidelity" simulation tools. These tools, which might include simplified physics models, heuristic reward functions, or generative models, can generate data much more cheaply and in greater abundance than high-fidelity counterparts. While individually too coarse for direct "sim-to-real" transfer or for accurately solving the target task, these low-fidelity data sources often contain useful structural or behavioral information related to the target environment. The motivation, therefore, is to effectively leverage this cheap, abundant, but imperfect low-fidelity data to overcome the limitations imposed by scarce high-fidelity data.

### 3. Key Objectives

To address this, the paper sets out the following key objectives:

1. **Develop a Novel Multi-Fidelity RL Framework:** Design a framework that strategically combines limited high-fidelity data with extensive low-fidelity data.
2. **Construct an Unbiased, Variance-Reduced Estimator:** Propose "multi-fidelity policy gradients (MFPGs)" as a mechanism to create an estimator for on-policy policy gradients that is unbiased with respect to the high-fidelity objective and substantially reduces variance. This is to be achieved by using the low-fidelity data to form a control variate.
3. **Instantiate a Practical Algorithm:** Develop a concrete, multi-fidelity variant of a classical on-policy algorithm (REINFORCE) that implements the MFPG estimator, demonstrating its practical applicability.
4. **Provide Theoretical Guarantees:** Establish theoretical foundations for the proposed MFPG estimator, proving asymptotic convergence to locally optimal policies in the target environment and demonstrating faster finite-sample convergence rates under standard assumptions, provided there is non-zero correlation between high- and low-fidelity samples.
5. **Empirical Validation:** Evaluate the MFPG algorithm across a suite of simulated robotics benchmark tasks. This evaluation aims to demonstrate its effectiveness in high-fidelity data-scarce regimes, specifically assessing:
 - Its ability to reduce variance in policy gradient estimates.
 - Its reliability and robustness when faced with varying dynamics gaps between high- and low-fidelity environments, including scenarios where low-fidelity data might be neutral, beneficial, or even harmful.
 - Its performance when the low-fidelity environment exhibits reward misspecification (e.g., anti-correlated rewards).

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to offer a principled method for managing the trade-off between policy performance and data collection costs, thereby enabling more efficient and reliable training and deployment of RL policies in real-world scenarios.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around the Multi-Fidelity Policy Gradients (MFPGs) framework, which employs control variates to combine data from high-fidelity (target) and low-fidelity environments.

**Modeling Multi-Fidelity Environments:**
The work considers two environments:
- **High-Fidelity Environment ($M_h$)**: An MDP $(S, A, \Delta_s^I, \gamma, p_h, R_h, T)$ representing the true target environment or a highly accurate simulator. Sampling from $M_h$ is assumed to be expensive and limited ($N_h$ samples).
- **Low-Fidelity Environment ($M_l$)**: An MDP $(S, A, \Delta_s^I, \gamma, p_l, R_l, T)$ with potentially different transition dynamics ($p_l$) and reward functions ($R_l$) compared to $M_h$. Sampling from $M_l$ is assumed to be cheap and abundant ($N_l \gg N_h$ samples). Crucially, $M_l$ must support resetting to arbitrary user-specified states to facilitate correlated trajectory sampling.

**Multi-Fidelity Policy Gradient Estimators via Control Variates:**
The objective is to maximize the expected total reward $J(\theta)$ in $M_h$ by estimating its policy gradient $\nabla_\theta J(\theta)$. Standard policy gradient methods suffer from high variance when $N_h$ is small.
The MFPG framework proposes to reduce this variance using the control variate technique. A new random variable $Z_{\pi_\theta}(c)$ is constructed:
$$Z_{\pi_\theta}(c) := X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h} + c(X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l} - \mu_l)$$
where:
- $X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h}$ is a random variable representing the contribution to the policy gradient from a high-fidelity trajectory $\tau_h$.
- $X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l}$ is a similar random variable from a low-fidelity trajectory $\tau_l$.
- $\mu_l := E_{\tau_l \sim M_l(\pi_\theta)}[X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l}]$ is the expected value of the low-fidelity random variable.
- $c \in \mathbb{R}$ is a coefficient.

This construction guarantees that $Z_{\pi_\theta}(c)$ is an **unbiased** estimator for $E[X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h}]$ (and thus $\nabla_\theta J(\theta)$). The optimal coefficient $c^*$ that minimizes the variance of $Z_{\pi_\theta}(c)$ is given by:
$$c^* = - \frac{\text{Cov}(X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h}, X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l})}{\text{Var}(X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l})}$$
If the Pearson correlation coefficient $\rho(X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h}, X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l})$ is non-zero, then $\text{Var}(Z_{\pi_\theta}(c^*)) = (1 - \rho^2) \text{Var}(X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h})$, implying a **strictly smaller variance** than using high-fidelity samples alone. In practice, $c^*$ and $\mu_l$ are estimated from samples. The paper uses moving-average statistics (Eq. 3, 4) for $c^*$ estimation, updating correlation and variance estimates iteratively.

**Sampling Correlated Trajectories (Algorithm 1):**
For effective variance reduction, $X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h}$ and $X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l}$ must be correlated. This is achieved by coupling specific sources of stochasticity across fidelities:
1. **Shared Initial States**: For each of the $N_h$ correlated trajectories, an initial state $s_0^h$ is sampled from $\Delta_s^I$. The low-fidelity environment $M_l$ is then reset to the *same* initial state $s_0^l = s_0^h$.
2. **Correlated Action Sampling via Policy Reparameterization Trick**: To ensure the policy's action selections are correlated, a reparameterization trick is used. In continuous action spaces, action-noise samples $\omega_{\pi_t} \sim N(0,1)$ are drawn and then transformed by the policy's state-dependent mean and standard deviation to produce actions $a_t$. The *same* sequence of $\omega_{\pi_t}$ samples is used to generate actions in both $M_h$ and $M_l$ at corresponding timesteps. For discrete actions, the Gumbel-Max trick is employed.
3. **Independent Dynamics and Rewards**: The transition dynamics ($p_h, p_l$) and reward functions ($R_h, R_l$) for $M_h$ and $M_l$ remain independent.

This process ensures that while the environments might evolve differently due to their distinct dynamics, the initial conditions and the policy's action generation mechanism are shared, creating a statistical dependency between the resulting trajectories.

**Multi-Fidelity REINFORCE Algorithm (Algorithm 2):**
The MFPG framework is instantiated with the REINFORCE algorithm.
- The random variable $X_{\pi_\theta}^\tau$ is defined as $\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} G_t \log \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)$, where $G_t$ is the return-to-go.
- To further reduce variance, state-value function subtraction is incorporated: $X_{\pi_\theta}^\tau = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} (G_t - V_\phi(s_t)) \log \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)$, where $V_\phi$ is a shared value function learned from high-fidelity samples.
- **Per-iteration steps**:
 1. Sample $N_h$ correlated trajectories from $M_h$ and $M_l$ using Algorithm 1.
 2. Sample $N_l$ additional uncorrelated trajectories from $M_l$ to estimate $\mu_l$.
 3. Compute $X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h}$ and $X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l}$ for the correlated samples.
 4. Estimate $c^*$ using moving averages and compute the MFPG gradient estimate.
 5. Perform a policy update $\theta_{k+1} \leftarrow \theta_k + \alpha_k \hat{g}_{MFPG_k}$.
 6. Fit the value function $V_\phi$ using high-fidelity samples.

**Theoretical Analysis (Section 5):**
The theoretical analysis establishes convergence properties under standard assumptions for MDPs and parameterized policies (bounded rewards, differentiable/Lipschitz/bounded score functions, unbiased/bounded-variance high-fidelity PG estimator). A key assumption is **non-zero correlation** between $X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_h}$ and $X_{\pi_\theta}^{\tau_l}$ (Assumption 6).
- **Asymptotic Convergence**: Theorem 1 proves that MFPG REINFORCE guarantees asymptotic convergence to a locally optimal policy (satisfying first-order optimality conditions) in the target high-fidelity environment.
- **Faster Finite-Sample Convergence**: The theorem also shows that when a non-zero correlation exists, MFPG achieves faster finite-sample convergence rates compared to training with high-fidelity samples alone. This improvement directly relates to the correlation coefficient $\rho$, with higher correlation leading to greater variance reduction. The proof leverages the Lipschitz continuity of the policy gradient and the supermartingale convergence theorem.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of the Multi-Fidelity Policy Gradients (MFPG) algorithm was conducted across a suite of simulated robotics benchmark tasks, primarily focusing on scenarios with scarce high-fidelity data but abundant low-fidelity data. The experiments used 20 random seeds per setting, employing non-parametric bootstrap confidence intervals for statistical significance.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Tasks:** MuJoCo environments: Hopper-v3, Walker2d-v3, and HalfCheetah-v3.
- **Dynamics Variation:** High-fidelity environments featured changes in gravity or friction across five levels (0.5x, 0.8x, 1.2x, 2.0x, 5.0x) relative to the low-fidelity environment (original dynamics).
- **Data Regimes:** All methods were restricted to 100 high-fidelity samples per policy update, with multi-fidelity methods having access to up to 100x additional low-fidelity samples. Training ran for up to 1M high-fidelity environment steps.
- **Baselines:**
 - **High-Fidelity Only (HFO):** Standard REINFORCE with state-value subtraction, using only limited high-fidelity data.
 - **Low-Fidelity Only (LFO):** REINFORCE trained on 100x abundant low-fidelity data, evaluated in the high-fidelity environment.
 - **DARC:** Off-dynamics RL method using domain classifiers to augment low-fidelity rewards.
 - **PAR:** Off-dynamics RL method using latent space dynamics mismatch for reward augmentation.
- **Metrics:** Final evaluation return (average of last 20 evaluation steps), Area Under Curve (AUC) for accumulated performance, and number of performance collapses (median below 50% of HFO median).

**Key Findings:**

1. **Insight I1: Empirical Variance Reduction (Section 6.2)**
 - In high-fidelity data-scarce regimes with mild dynamics gaps (e.g., Hopper-v3 with 1.2x friction), MFPG substantially reduced the variance of policy gradient estimates compared to single-fidelity baselines.
 - This variance reduction was often more significant than that achieved by standard state-value baseline subtraction alone.
 - MFPG's variance reduction effectively narrowed the performance gap to baselines that had access to significantly more (e.g., 10x) high-fidelity samples.

2. **Insight I2: Reliability and Robustness Across Dynamics Variation (Section 6.3)**
 - **Mild to Moderate Dynamics Gaps (0.5x to 2.0x)**: In tasks where low-fidelity data were neutral or beneficial (Hopper-gravity, Walker2d-friction), MFPG consistently achieved statistically significant improvements in mean performance over the High-Fidelity Only baseline. MFPG was the only evaluated method to do so in 8 out of 8 such scenarios.
 - **Comparison with Off-Dynamics RL Baselines**: While DARC and PAR could achieve high performance in some scenarios where low-fidelity data were strongly beneficial (e.g., Walker2d), they exhibited aggressive exploitation of low-fidelity data, leading to less consistent performance and severe failures in other contexts. MFPG, by design, used low-fidelity data solely for variance reduction, resulting in more reliable and consistent gains, albeit sometimes more modest than the peak performance of aggressive baselines.
 - **Harmful Low-Fidelity Data (HalfCheetah-friction)**: When low-fidelity data proved detrimental, MFPG demonstrated the strongest robustness against performance degradation among all evaluated methods. DARC and Low-Fidelity Only experienced consistent and significant performance drops. PAR could achieve high performance in some mild-to-moderate gap settings but required extensive per-task hyperparameter tuning and still exhibited large cross-seed variance.
 - **Extreme Dynamics Shifts (5.0x)**: At extreme dynamics shifts where cross-fidelity correlation severely diminished, MFPG's performance became effectively neutral compared to High-Fidelity Only. Other baselines struggled significantly more, often failing to achieve meaningful performance.
 - **Quantified Robustness**: Across 15 main scenarios, MFPG experienced 1 final return collapse and 0 AUC collapses, significantly fewer than PAR (1/1), Low-Fidelity Only (5/3), and DARC (5/3). Similar trends were observed in additional experiments across 24 settings.

3. **Insight I3: Effectiveness with Misspecified Reward (Section 6.4)**
 - An experiment with a Hopper-v3 task where the low-fidelity reward was the negative (anti-correlated) version of the high-fidelity reward demonstrated MFPG's effectiveness even under severe reward misspecification.
 - The Low-Fidelity Only baseline completely failed, while High-Fidelity Only made limited progress. MFPG successfully combined these two data sources, leveraging the negative correlation, to achieve substantially better performance than both baselines. This highlights MFPG's ability to exploit statistical relationships even when low-fidelity data is qualitatively very different from the target.

**Additional Findings (Section 6.5):**

- **Impact of High-Fidelity Sample Amount**: MFPG yielded larger benefits over High-Fidelity Only in data-scarce regimes (e.g., 100-200 high-fidelity samples per update), with its advantage diminishing as high-fidelity samples became abundant (above 500 samples).
- **Impact of Low-Fidelity Sample Amount**: MFPG exhibited robustness to the amount of low-fidelity data, showing consistent improvements or neutral performance across a wide range of low-fidelity sample sizes. Larger amounts of low-fidelity data were more beneficial in mild-gap settings.
- **Sensitivity to $\eta_{ma}$ (Moving-Average Weight)**: MFPG's median performance consistently exceeded or matched High-Fidelity Only across tested values of $\eta_{ma}$, indicating robustness to this hyperparameter.
- **Role of Reparameterization Trick**: Ablation studies confirmed that the policy reparameterization trick was critical for achieving strong cross-fidelity correlation and accelerating learning, especially in settings with mild dynamics gaps.
- **Variance-Bias Trade-off with Reconstraining**: Periodically "reconstraining" low-fidelity rollouts to match high-fidelity states could strengthen correlation and accelerate learning in some cases, but also introduced bias, leading to performance degradation in others. This technique was not adopted in the main evaluations.

In summary, the empirical results validate MFPG as a reliable and robust method for improving sample efficiency in RL by leveraging multi-fidelity data, particularly in high-fidelity data-scarce environments and even under challenging conditions like reward misspecification.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Multi-Fidelity Policy Gradients (MFPG) framework offers a significant methodological contribution to Reinforcement Learning, with implications for both theoretical understanding and practical applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency in Data-Scarce Regimes**: The core contribution of MFPG is its ability to substantially improve the sample efficiency of high-fidelity data by systematically incorporating readily available, cheap low-fidelity data. This is particularly impactful in real-world scenarios where interactions with the target environment are expensive, time-consuming, or unsafe (e.g., robotics, autonomous vehicles, industrial control, molecular discovery, nuclear fusion). By requiring only a small amount of high-fidelity data to anchor policy learning, MFPG reduces the overall data collection burden.

2. **Reliable and Robust Policy Learning**: The empirical results demonstrate MFPG's reliability and robustness across various dynamics gaps and even with misspecified rewards. Unlike other off-dynamics RL methods that may aggressively exploit low-fidelity data (leading to potential instability or severe failures when models are inaccurate), MFPG's design principles ensure that low-fidelity data primarily serve as a variance-reduction mechanism for an unbiased high-fidelity gradient estimate. This cautious approach yields consistent performance improvements and strong resilience against performance degradation, which is crucial for deployment in operational systems where reliability is paramount.

3. **Principled Approach to Sim-to-Real Transfer**: MFPG provides a principled statistical technique for managing the "sim-to-real gap." By enabling efficient learning with limited real-world (high-fidelity) data, it offers a pathway to accelerate the fine-tuning of policies initially developed in simulation for real-world deployment. The framework's ability to operate even with large discrepancies in low-fidelity dynamics or rewards means that even coarse simulators can provide valuable information, broadening the utility of existing simulation tools.

4. **Theoretical Foundations for Multi-Fidelity RL**: The theoretical analysis, establishing asymptotic convergence to locally optimal policies and faster finite-sample convergence rates for multi-fidelity REINFORCE, provides a solid mathematical grounding for the approach. These guarantees strengthen confidence in the method's behavior and performance under specified conditions.

5. **Broadened Applicability of Low-Fidelity Models**: The finding that MFPG remains effective even when low-fidelity rewards are anti-correlated to high-fidelity rewards highlights a unique strength. It implies that low-fidelity models do not need to be accurate representations of the real world in terms of absolute dynamics or rewards; merely possessing a consistent statistical relationship (correlation) with the high-fidelity environment can be sufficient for them to be useful. This significantly expands the range of low-fidelity data sources that can be effectively leveraged.

**Potential Future Impact**:
The research opens several avenues for future work, which could further amplify its impact:
- **Extension to Modern RL Algorithms**: Applying the MFPG framework to state-of-the-art actor-critic, off-policy, and offline RL algorithms could address current limitations in these methods, particularly regarding bias-variance trade-offs.
- **Generalization to Complex Multi-Fidelity Settings**: Expanding MFPG to handle an arbitrary number of fidelity levels, different state/action spaces, or infinite-horizon MDPs would increase its flexibility and applicability.
- **Real-World Deployment**: Direct application of MFPG to real robotic systems would be a critical step in demonstrating its practical value for efficient sim-to-real transfer and reducing the cost and risk associated with robot learning.
- **Systematic Correlation Enhancement**: Developing methods to systematically enhance the correlation between multi-fidelity samples could lead to even greater variance reduction and performance gains.

In conclusion, MFPG represents a robust and theoretically sound step towards making RL more practical and deployable in data-constrained real-world environments. Its emphasis on reliability and efficient data utilization positions it as a promising paradigm for future RL research and application.
