# Working Backwards: Learning to Place by Picking

> [!summary] Summary
> Limoyo et al. from Samsung AI Centre and the University of Toronto developed Placing via Picking (PvP), a self-supervised method that autonomously collects expert demonstrations for complex robotic object placement tasks by reversing successful picking trajectories. The approach enables training vision-based policies that achieve 87.78% success in dish rack loading, outperforming policies trained via kinesthetic teaching by approximately 20%.

## Problem

- High human effort and time required to collect large, diverse expert demonstration datasets for robotic imitation learning (data collection bottleneck).
- Difficulty in addressing full, contact-constrained object placement tasks with varied, complex objects, which are common in real-world scenarios.
- Limitations of existing automated data collection methods that often simplify tasks, rely on scripted policies, or require human supervision.

## Method

- Developed Placing via Picking (PvP), a self-supervised data collection pipeline that leverages the time-reversal symmetry of pick-and-place tasks.
- Integrated Compliant Control for Grasping (CCG) and Tactile Regrasping (TR) modules to ensure robust, uninterrupted data collection in contact-rich environments.
- Utilized a language-driven grasp planning pipeline with vision-language models (Grounding-Dino, Segment Anything) and a grasp planner (Contact-GraspNet) for flexible object grasping.

## Results

- The full PvP system (with CCG and TR) collected all 128 episodes without human intervention, a substantial improvement over the 9 episodes achieved by the naive approach.
- Policies trained with noise-augmented PvP data achieved an **87.78%** success rate for dish rack loading with a Gaussian mixture model, improving policy performance by 10-**15%** compared to training without noise.
- PvP-trained policies consistently outperformed policies trained through human kinesthetic teaching by approximately **20%** across various dataset sizes, suggesting superior data quality and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reversing successful picking trajectories can generate high-quality expert demonstrations for object placement without human intervention.
> - The combination of compliant control and tactile sensing is essential for achieving robustness in autonomous data collection for contact-constrained manipulation tasks.
> - Noise augmentation during data collection, particularly when combined with multimodal action representations, significantly enhances policy performance and generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a significant challenge within robotic manipulation, specifically the data collection bottleneck for imitation learning (IL) in complex, real-world object placement tasks. Imitation learning is a paradigm that aims to teach robots new skills by observing human or expert demonstrations. While effective in other domains, its application to robotics has been constrained by the substantial human time and effort required to collect large, diverse datasets in physical environments.

Previous research in automatic data collection for robotic manipulation has explored several avenues. Some methods leverage trajectory optimization or simple scripted pick-and-place policies to generate data for reinforcement learning or other policy learning frameworks. Examples include using scripted policies to bootstrap datasets for off-policy deep reinforcement learning or combining scripted, learned, and tele-operated policies for language-specified manipulation, as seen in systems like AutoRT. However, these prior works often simplify the problem by focusing on small objects placed on flat, horizontal surfaces, typically without significant environment contact constraints. The authors of explicitly noted the limitation of lacking robust and diverse autonomous collection policies.

Another line of work involves "working backwards" or time-reversal, which has been applied in generative modeling, learning visual predictive models, and reinforcement learning. In robotics, this concept has been used for tasks like insertion, where objects are assumed to be in their desired configuration, picked up, and then moved back to an initial goal location to generate training data. Similar work also used time-reversed trajectories for generating assembly data from disassembly. However, these methods typically focus on local interactions or simpler 2D open-loop actions, and often require human guidance for the initial manipulation, or do not consider the complexities of "placement" as distinct from "insertion," which involves reasoning over larger scenes with potential for multiple place poses and complex objects requiring robust grasp planning.

This paper distinguishes itself by focusing on the full, contact-constrained placing problem involving larger, more complex objects that require robust grasp planning. It aims to develop a fully self-supervised data collection method that eliminates human intervention, directly addressing the limitations of existing automated data collection techniques and the more constrained applications of the "working backwards" principle. By leveraging the inherent symmetry of pick-and-place tasks and integrating advanced components like compliant control, tactile sensing, and language-driven grasp planning, this work attempts to scale IL for realistic, challenging manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an autonomous and scalable method for collecting real-world demonstrations for robotic object placement tasks, particularly those involving specific, contact-constrained target locations. The authors term this method "placing via picking" (PvP).

The motivation behind this work stems from several key challenges in current robotic imitation learning:
1. **Data Collection Bottleneck:** A major impediment to scaling imitation learning in robotics is the significant human effort and time required to collect large, diverse expert demonstration datasets in physical environments. This bottleneck limits the robustness and generalization capabilities of learned policies.
2. **Complexity of Placing Tasks:** Unlike many prior works that simplify placing to flat surfaces and small objects, the authors aim to address the full placing problem. This includes scenarios where objects have varied shapes and weights, and must be manipulated into specific, often contact-constrained locations (e.g., loading a dishwasher, setting a table). These constraints are seen not as obstacles but as potential guides for learning.
3. **Limitations of Existing Automated Methods:** While some methods automate data collection, they often rely on scripted policies that are not flexible enough for complex, contact-rich environments, or require significant bootstrapping or human supervision. Methods based on "working backwards" typically focus on insertion or simpler tasks, often still requiring initial human input or lacking sophisticated grasp planning.
4. **Need for Robustness:** Autonomous data collection pipelines need to be robust to real-world inaccuracies, such as sensor noise, controller errors, and imperfect grasp planning. Failures during data collection necessitate human intervention, undermining the goal of scalability.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines several key contributions:
- **PvP, a self-supervised data collection method:** This method is designed for 6-degrees-of-freedom (6-DOF) robotic object placements, exploiting the cyclical nature and inherent symmetry of pick-and-place tasks.
- **Robust data collection modules:** The integration of compliant control for grasping (CCG) and tactile-driven regrasping (TR) is presented as crucial for achieving uninterrupted pick-and-place data collection, especially in contact-constrained environments. These modules allow the robot to leverage environmental contacts as guides.
- **Language-specified grasp planning pipeline:** The work incorporates recent advancements in vision-language models (Grounding-Dino, Segment Anything) and grasp planners (Contact-GraspNet) to enable a flexible and specified approach to object grasping.
- **Real-world experimental validation:** The proposed PvP method is validated through experiments on practical home robot scenarios, specifically dishwasher loading and table setting, demonstrating its capability to train vision-based policies that generalize to varied object placement scenarios. The policies trained with PvP data are shown to outperform those trained with kinesthetic teaching in terms of success rate and data efficiency, without requiring human supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, Placing via Picking (PvP), is a self-supervised pipeline for autonomously collecting expert demonstrations for robotic object placement. It leverages the inherent symmetry of pick-and-place tasks by reversing successful grasping trajectories. The methodology can be broken down into several phases:

**A. Self-Supervised Data Collection (PvP Cycle)**
The PvP cycle consists of four main, sequential phases:

1. **Grasp Planning:**
 - The manipulator positions a camera to obtain a clear view of objects initially located at their target placement locations.
 - A language-driven grasp planning pipeline is used:
 - **Object Detection:** Grounding-Dino identifies object bounding boxes based on text descriptions (e.g., "plates," "green plate").
 - **Segmentation:** Segment Anything generates per-object masks from these bounding box crops.
 - **Grasp Generation:** Contact-GraspNet uses these masks to generate candidate 6-DOF grasp poses (T_{g,i}) that are specific to the objects of interest. Grasps not on the target objects are filtered.
 - A single grasp pose (T_{grasp}) is randomly selected, and a pregrasp pose (T_{pregrasp}) is defined with a translational offset above it to ensure collision-free approach.

2. **Grasping:**
 - The manipulator sequentially moves to T_{pregrasp} and then T_{grasp} using linear and spherical linear interpolation.
 - Two critical modules ensure robustness during grasping:
 - **Compliant Control for Grasping (CCG):** Before closing the gripper, the manipulator's Cartesian impedance controller is set to minimal translational and rotational stiffness. This allows the arm to comply with environmental contact constraints, minimizing reaction forces that could cause emergency stops, damage, or object displacement. This compliance guides the manipulator towards a natural grasping pose when objects are already in contact with the environment.
 - **Tactile Regrasping (TR):** A visuotactile Finger-STS sensor detects grasp stability. If partial or unstable contact is detected (e.g., based on marker displacements or RGB image analysis showing insufficient contact surface area), the TR module calculates the required end-effector (EE) pose change to achieve a larger, more stable contact area and executes a regrasp. A simpler random regrasp strategy is also noted as a potential alternative.

3. **Retrieving:**
 - Once a stable grasp is achieved, the retrieval phase begins. The manipulator moves from T_{grasp} to T_{pregrasp} and then to a randomly sampled clearance pose (T_{clearance}) above the scene.
 - During this process, the poses of the EE and corresponding timestamps are recorded at 120 Hz, forming a dense expert retrieval trajectory (τ^expert_r).
 - Rotational compliance of the EE is maintained during the movement to T_{pregrasp}.
 - The dense trajectory is then downsampled to a control frequency of 5 Hz (∆t = 0.20 s) to create a sparse retrieval trajectory (τ̄^expert_r).

4. **Placing by Reversing:**
 - The sparse retrieval trajectory (τ̄^expert_r) is time-reversed to obtain the desired place trajectory (τ̄^expert_p).
 - Global poses from this reversed trajectory are converted into relative end-effector pose changes (∆T_i) which serve as expert actions.
 - A binary gripper command (a_gripper,i) is also defined: the gripper remains closed during the entire placement trajectory, and then opens for N (N=5) time steps at the end to release the object.
 - An episode of expert data consists of a sequence of observations (RGB images from a wrist camera, downsampled to 128x128 and stacked three previous frames) and actions (relative EE pose change and gripper command) collected during the rollout of these reversed commands.

**B. Noise-Augmented Data Collection**
To enhance robustness and data coverage, the authors introduce a noise augmentation variant. During the data collection, the first 75% of the pose commands (∆T) are perturbed with isotropic Gaussian noise. Both the translation vector (t) and the rotation vector (θ, defined by angle θ and axis e) of the pose commands are perturbed:
- Translation: `t_perturbed = t + δt`, where `δt` is sampled from `N(0, σ_t^2)` (σ_t = 0.5 cm).
- Rotation: `θ_perturbed = (θ + δθ)(e + δe)`, where `δe` is sampled from `N(0, σ_e^2)` (σ_e = 0.5 cm) and `δθ` is sampled from `N(0, σ_θ^2)` (σ_θ = 0.5°).

**C. Policy Learning**
- A policy π_φ(a_t | o_t) parameterized by φ is trained using the autonomously collected data.
- **Behavioral Cloning (BC)** is used as the learning algorithm, minimizing a likelihood-based loss function.
- The policy network architecture consists of a ResNet18 convolutional neural network (CNN) as the backbone, followed by a multilayer perceptron (MLP) that outputs the parameters for the action distribution.
- Two action representations are explored for the distribution π_φ(a | o): a unimodal Gaussian and a multimodal mixture of Gaussians.
- During inference, a low-noise evaluation scheme is employed by setting the standard deviation of the Gaussian distributions to an arbitrarily small value, making the unimodal Gaussian effectively a deterministic policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PvP was evaluated through real-world experiments on two household tasks: dishrack loading and table setting. The quantitative results focused primarily on the dishrack loading task due to its contact-constrained nature.

**A. PvP Data Collection Robustness**
An ablation study on the Compliant Control for Grasping (CCG) and Tactile Regrasping (TR) modules was conducted to assess their impact on the robustness of the data collection loop.
- **Without CCG and TR:** The naive approach resulted in 15 failures over 128 episodes, averaging approximately 9 autonomously collected episodes before human intervention was required. Failure modes included stiff grasping causing object movement and large force preloads against the environment, leading to inaccurate placements upon release, and unstable grasps causing significant relative motion between the object and gripper.
- **With CCG only:** The number of failures reduced to 3, increasing the average to 43 episodes before intervention. CCG mitigated issues arising from stiff grasping.
- **With both CCG and TR:** The system successfully collected all 128 episodes without any human intervention. TR addressed unstable grasps by preemptively detecting and correcting them.
These results indicate that both CCG and TR are crucial for achieving robust and uninterrupted autonomous data collection in contact-constrained environments.

**B. Noise Augmentation Ablation**
The effect of noise-augmented data collection on policy performance was evaluated. Two datasets (128 demonstrations each) were collected: one with noise augmentation and one without. Policies were trained using both deterministic and Gaussian mixture action representations.
- **Deterministic Policy:** Training with noise-augmented data improved the average success rate from 71.67% to 81.11% (an approximate 10% increase).
- **Gaussian Mixture Policy:** Training with noise-augmented data improved the average success rate from 72.22% to 87.78% (an approximate 15% increase).
The authors hypothesized that noise augmentation accentuates the multimodal nature of placement tasks (where multiple valid actions can exist for similar observations), and that a mixture-of-Gaussians policy could better capture this multimodality. Policies trained with noise-augmented data also qualitatively demonstrated better re-adjustment capabilities during placement.

**C. Comparison to Kinesthetic Teaching**
Policies trained using data collected by PvP (specifically the noise-augmented variant) were compared against policies trained using human kinesthetic teaching. Both policies utilized a Gaussian mixture model for action representation.
- **Performance Comparison:** Policies trained with PvP data consistently outperformed those trained with kinesthetic teaching across various dataset sizes, showing an advantage of approximately 20%. For example, with 100 demonstrations, the PvP-trained policy achieved a success rate above 0.8, while the kinesthetic-trained policy remained below 0.6.
- **Observed Failure Modes:** Kinesthetic-trained policies frequently misplaced objects (e.g., incorrect angle, unstable resting) and struggled with gripper timing (opening at incorrect moments). The latter error mode was not observed in PvP-trained policies.
- **Data Quality Hypothesis:** The authors hypothesized that the lower performance of kinesthetic policies was due to the overall lower quality of human demonstrations compared to machine-generated ones. Kinesthetic demonstrations were found to be longer on average (41.66 timesteps vs. 29.41 timesteps for PvP) and exhibited greater variance in length, indicating potential sub-optimality, mistakes, or unwanted movements. PvP's systematic noise augmentation, which humans cannot consistently replicate, was also cited as a contributing factor to its superior data quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, Placing via Picking (PvP), offers a method for addressing critical challenges in scaling robotic manipulation through imitation learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Autonomous Data Collection for Complex Tasks:** PvP introduces a fully self-supervised pipeline for collecting expert demonstrations for robotic object placement. This is particularly significant because it targets contact-constrained tasks with complex objects, which are more representative of real-world scenarios compared to the simpler tasks often addressed by prior automated data collection methods. By eliminating the need for human intervention in data collection, PvP significantly reduces the labor and cost associated with training robotic policies.

- **Enhanced Robustness and Scalability:** The integration of Compliant Control for Grasping (CCG) and Tactile Regrasping (TR) is crucial. These modules enhance the robustness of the data collection process by mitigating common failure modes like unstable grasps or excessive forces against the environment. This robustness allows for the uninterrupted collection of hundreds of demonstrations, which is essential for scaling imitation learning approaches to large and diverse datasets.

- **Improved Policy Performance and Data Efficiency:** The experimental results demonstrate that policies trained with PvP-generated data outperform those trained through traditional human kinesthetic teaching. This suggests that the quality of autonomously generated demonstrations, especially with noise augmentation, can surpass that of human demonstrations for specific metrics. The higher success rates with fewer demonstrations also indicate improved data efficiency, meaning robots can learn effective placement skills faster and with less initial data.

- **Leveraging Vision-Language Models and Grasp Planning:** The use of advanced components like Grounding-Dino, Segment Anything, and Contact-GraspNet within the grasp planning pipeline enables language-driven and object-specific grasping. This integration allows for more flexible task specification and generalization to a variety of objects and placement contexts, moving towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** PvP provides a promising foundation for future work in several areas. Its self-supervised nature makes it a candidate for integration with high-level language-based planners, potentially enabling robots to autonomously define and collect data for a broader range of complex, language-specified tasks. The framework also opens avenues for exploring the use of tactile sensing as an additional modality for the learned policy, potentially leading to even more precise and robust manipulation skills.

While the method relies on effective grasping and has limitations regarding camera viewpoint and unmodeled object dynamics, PvP represents a pragmatic direction towards making real-world robotic placement learning more accessible, efficient, and scalable by minimizing human involvement.
