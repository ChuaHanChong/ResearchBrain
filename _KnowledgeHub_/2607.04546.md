# Mask2Real-WM: Segmentation Masks as a Sim-to-Real Bridge for Controllable Dexterous World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Mask2Real-WM, a two-stage action-conditioned world model, utilizes segmentation masks as a sim-to-real bridge to enable controllable dexterous manipulation. The approach achieves approximately 0.95 in-distribution and 0.87 out-of-distribution action controllability with fewer than 2.5 hours of real-world data, while producing sharper video predictions.

## Problem

- Developing world models for dexterous manipulation is challenging due to the high dimensionality of action spaces (e.g., 23-DoF robot hands).
- Acquiring large-scale, high-quality real-world datasets for dexterous manipulation tasks is resource-intensive and time-consuming.
- Existing video prediction models struggle to accurately represent fine-grained interactions, such as finger-object contacts, often resulting in blurry predictions, and face a significant sim-to-real gap for visual observations.

## Method

- A two-stage action-conditioned world model, **Mask2Real-WM**, decouples pixel prediction into a ==dynamics model== (WM1) for segmentation masks and a ==rendering model== (WM2) for RGB images.
- WM1 is extensively pretrained on over 50 hours of synthetic data in segmentation space, leveraging its reduced sim-to-real gap to learn robust action dynamics.
- WM2 is trained exclusively on limited real-world data to render photorealistic RGB images, conditioned on past RGB frames, action sequences, and the segmentation masks predicted by WM1 via a ==ControlNet== module.

## Results

- The full Mask2Real-WM achieved a high action controllability score of approximately **0.95** for in-distribution frames and **0.87** for out-of-distribution frames, significantly outperforming a monolithic baseline.
- The model generated sharper, more accurate video predictions, particularly for intricate finger-object contacts, with sharpness levels closer to ground truth compared to the blurring observed in monolithic models.
- Mask2Real-WM demonstrated effective generalization to unseen objects, maintaining high mask fidelity (SSIM ≈ **0.86**) due to the sim-pretrained dynamics model, suggesting that only the renderer needs adaptation for new object appearances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Utilizing segmentation masks as an intermediate representation effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap, enabling large-scale synthetic pretraining for dynamics modeling despite limited real-world data.
> - Decoupling dynamics prediction from visual rendering allows each component to specialize: WM1 learns action-conditioned geometric changes, while WM2 focuses on photorealistic appearance.
> - Extensive simulation pretraining is crucial for achieving high per-DoF action controllability and robustness, as it exposes the dynamics model to a wider range of joint configurations and independent finger actuations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of robot learning, specifically focusing on action-conditioned world models. World models are computational systems that predict future observations given sequences of actions, enabling functions such as policy evaluation, planning, and data augmentation for robotic systems. While world models are increasingly utilized in robotics, applying them to dexterous manipulation tasks presents several challenges. These include the high dimensionality of action spaces (e.g., 23 degrees of freedom (DoF) for a dexterous hand), the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality real-world datasets for dexterous tasks, and the difficulty of accurately predicting intricate finger-object contacts, which often manifest as blurring in pixel-space predictions.

Existing action-conditioned video generation models, such as Genie, NVIDIA Cosmos, and WAN, have demonstrated capabilities in learning interactive environments or physical AI applications using diffusion or flow-matching architectures. Within robotic manipulation, previous efforts like Ctrl-World have explored controllable manipulation rollouts, but primarily for lower-DoF parallel-jaw grippers. DexWM addressed dexterous manipulation by conditioning on 3D hand keypoints and camera poses within a DINOv2 feature space, requiring a separate decoder for visualization. Other works, like those leveraging ControlNet, have explored conditioning video generation on structural signals such as depth or edges. Concurrent research includes the Mask World Model (MWM), which decouples mask prediction from image generation but targets parallel-jaw grippers and operates as a World Action Model (WAM), and BridgeV2W, which conditions on embodiment masks derived from kinematics, thereby bypassing learned mask dynamics or robot-object interactions.

Mask2Real-WM differentiates itself by directly addressing dexterous manipulation through a two-stage approach that decouples pixel prediction into a mask-space dynamics model and an image-space rendering model. This decomposition, particularly the use of segmentation masks as an intermediate representation, is presented as an effective sim-to-real bridge. This approach enables the dynamics model to benefit from extensive simulation pretraining while the rendering model learns photorealistic appearance from limited real data, a strategy that is less explored for image-space world models. Unlike methods that rely on pre-defined kinematic masks, Mask2Real-WM learns mask dynamics, which supports modeling robot-object interactions and facilitates per-DoF action controllability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an action-conditioned world model capable of predicting the future consequences of candidate actions for dexterous manipulation tasks without requiring extensive physical interaction. This model is intended to support functions such as policy evaluation, planning, and data augmentation in robot learning.

The motivation for Mask2Real-WM stems from several specific challenges inherent in applying world models to dexterous manipulation:
- **High-Dimensional Action Space:** Dexterous hands, such as the 23-DoF setup used in this study, possess a complex action space that is difficult for traditional world models to control precisely. A key objective is to achieve per-DoF action controllability across all these dimensions.
- **Scarcity of Real-World Data:** Collecting large-scale datasets for dexterous manipulation in the real world is resource-intensive and time-consuming. The project aims to achieve high performance with a limited amount of real-world interaction data (fewer than 2.5 hours).
- **Prediction Fidelity for Contacts:** Existing video prediction models often struggle with accurately representing fine-grained interactions, such as finger-object contacts, frequently resulting in blurry or imprecise predictions. An objective is to produce sharper, more accurate predictions in these critical regions.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Directly transferring models trained on synthetic RGB data to the real world is challenging due to discrepancies in appearance, lighting, and texture. The motivation is to leverage a more domain-invariant representation to bridge this gap effectively.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a decomposition strategy:
1. **Exploiting the Smaller Sim-to-Real Gap in Segmentation Space:** Segmentation masks are less sensitive to variations in texture, lighting, and material properties compared to RGB images. This property is hypothesized to enable large-scale pretraining of a dynamics model on synthetic data, reducing the reliance on extensive real-world interaction.
2. **Decoupling Dynamics from Rendering:** By separating the prediction of future geometric configurations (masks) from the rendering of photorealistic appearance (RGB images), the system can specialize each component. The dynamics model focuses on action-conditioned object and robot motion, while the rendering model focuses on visual fidelity.

The specific contributions outlined by the authors are:
1. The use of segmentation space as a sim-to-real bridge, facilitating large-scale synthetic pretraining of the dynamics model on over 50 hours of simulation data.
2. The development of Mask2Real-WM, a two-stage action-conditioned world model comprising a simulation-pretrained dynamics model (WM1) and a real-data-trained rendering model (WM2).
3. Experimental validation demonstrating that simulation pretraining of the dynamics model improves action controllability and that subsequent fine-tuning on real data further enhances performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mask2Real-WM addresses the action-conditioned video prediction problem for dexterous manipulation within a multi-view setup. An observation at time *t* consists of an RGB image *I*<sub>*t*</sub> from *V* camera views and a corresponding 3-class segmentation mask *m*<sub>*t*</sub> (hand, object, background). The action *a*<sub>*t*</sub> is a 23-DoF vector concatenating a 6-DoF end-effector pose and a 17-DoF ORCA hand joint configuration. The model's objective is to predict future RGB images and masks given a context window of past observations and a sequence of past and future actions. The system uses *V*=2 views, a context window of *k*=5 frames, and a prediction horizon of *H*=5 steps, with autoregressive rollout for longer sequences. Images are processed at 135 × 240 pixels.

The core of the methodology is a factorization of the joint distribution into two stages:
1. **WM1: Dynamics Model:** This model predicts future segmentation masks *m*<sub>*t+1:t+H*</sub> conditioned on past masks *m*<sub>*t-k:t*</sub> and the full action sequence *a*<sub>*t-k:t+H*</sub>. It operates in mask space, which has a reduced sim-to-real gap, allowing for large-scale synthetic pretraining.
2. **WM2: Rendering Model:** This model predicts future RGB images *I*<sub>*t+1:t+H*</sub> conditioned on past RGB images *I*<sub>*t-k:t*</sub>, the past and predicted future segmentation masks *m*<sub>*t-k:t+H*</sub>, and the action sequence *a*<sub>*t-k:t+H*</sub>. It is trained exclusively on real-world data to learn photorealistic appearance.

**Data Pipeline:**
- **Simulation Data:** Over 50 hours of synthetic demonstrations were generated using IsaacLab. This included ground-truth segmentation labels. Data generation involved a MimicGen-based pipeline for structured pick-and-place tasks and an exploratory sinusoidal joint generator to cover the full 17-DoF ORCA joint range. PD gains and friction parameters were tuned to match a physical robot. Synthetic masks were blurred (σ=1.5 px) to mitigate rasterization artifacts and reduce the domain gap.
- **Real-World Data:** Fewer than 2.5 hours of demonstrations were collected using an ORCA-hand teleoperated with a Rokoko glove and two synchronized RGB camera views. Segmentation masks for real data were automatically generated by SAM 3 using text prompts, with an auxiliary clip to bootstrap the tracker for occluded wrist views.

**Stage 1: Dynamics Model (WM1):**
- **Architecture:** WM1 is a video diffusion model initialized from Stable Video Diffusion (SVD). It adopts a multi-view, action-conditioned formulation.
- **Conditioning:** At each denoising step, VAE latents of past segmentation masks are concatenated with noisy future latents. The 6-DoF end-effector pose and 17 hand joint angles, after projection by separate Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) into 1024-dimensional per-frame embeddings, are summed and injected into every SVD U-Net block via frame-wise cross-attention.
- **Training:** WM1 is pretrained on the simulation dataset for 55,000 steps with a learning rate of 10<sup>-4</sup>. Subsequently, it is fine-tuned on the real-world dataset for 45,000 steps with LoRA (rank 16, α=16) at a learning rate of 5×10<sup>-6</sup>. The objective is a standard denoising loss.

**Stage 2: Appearance Model (WM2):**
- **Architecture:** WM2 utilizes the same SVD backbone as WM1, but its weights are frozen and adapted via LoRA (rank 16, α=16).
- **Conditioning:** WM2 receives three primary conditioning signals:
 - Past RGB frames, encoded through SVD’s VAE.
 - Segmentation masks (both past and future, predicted by WM1): These are processed by a dedicated lightweight CNN encoder, which operates in pixel space and specializes in hand and object boundaries, followed by a ControlNet module. The features are injected into SVD’s encoder and middle blocks via zero-initialized convolutions.
 - Actions: These are conditioned via the same dual-branch MLP cross-attention mechanism used in WM1.
- **Training:** WM2 is trained exclusively on the real-world dataset for 70,000 steps with a learning rate of 10<sup>-4</sup>. The CNN encoder and ControlNet are fully fine-tuned. Independent mask and action conditional dropout (each 10%) is applied to enable classifier-free guidance during inference. The objective is a denoising loss.

**Inference:**
At inference, SAM 3 first processes a 5-frame context window of real RGB observations to obtain initial segmentation masks. WM1 then autoregressively rolls out future mask sequences. Subsequently, WM2 renders photorealistic RGB images conditioned on these predicted masks, the initial past frames, and the action sequences. For long-horizon predictions, the last predicted frame and mask are added to the context window for subsequent autoregressive chunks.

**Experimental Setup:**
The real-world platform consists of a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda arm equipped with a 17-DoF ORCA hand, totaling 23 actuated DoF. Observations are captured by two Luxonis OAK-D RGB cameras: a fixed workspace camera and a wrist-mounted camera. The manipulation environment is a custom arena with tilted, colored walls designed to facilitate continuous pick-and-place tasks with everyday objects. This arena configuration allows for continuous data collection, diverse visual textures, and flexibility in background and object sets. The simulation counterpart, built in Isaac Sim, focuses on geometric and kinematic fidelity rather than photorealism, reading ground-truth masks directly from instance segmentation output.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate Mask2Real-WM focused on action controllability, video prediction quality, and robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions, comparing the full model against ablated variants and a monolithic baseline.

**Action Controllability:**
- The full Mask2Real-WM (WM1: sim→real, WM2: real) achieved a controllability score of approximately 0.95 for in-distribution (ID) frames and approximately 0.87 for OOD frames. This performance was achieved by applying a sinusoidal signal to individual action components and scoring the model's response.
- This significantly outperformed the monolithic Ctrl-World baseline, which scored approximately 0.6 ID and 0.44 OOD, indicating that the baseline struggled with fine-grained per-DoF control.
- Simulation pretraining of WM1 alone (WM1: sim, WM2: real) improved controllability from an initial real-only baseline (0.68 ID, 0.51 OOD) to approximately 0.85 ID and 0.73 OOD. This improvement is attributed to the large-scale synthetic data providing exposure to a broader range of joint configurations and independent per-finger actuation, which was largely absent in the limited real dataset.
- Further fine-tuning WM1 on real data (WM1: sim + real, WM2: real) refined the model's mask predictions, aligning them with the SAM3 mask distribution used by WM2, and contributed to the highest controllability scores. Qualitative analysis of generated videos supported these findings, demonstrating wider angular range actuation for individual finger joints without unintended coupling.

**Video Prediction Quality:**
- **Pixel Metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS):** The real-only WM1 variant exhibited the highest ID pixel metrics, but its performance degraded most severely under OOD conditions. This suggests that without substantial synthetic exposure, the dynamics model could overfit to the visual and kinematic statistics of its training environment. Pretraining WM1 on simulation data substantially reduced OOD degradation, and subsequent real fine-tuning (the full model) further narrowed this gap while maintaining robust ID performance.
- **Sharpness Analysis:** A Laplacian-variance analysis revealed that the monolithic baseline consistently produced less sharp videos across all evaluation groups, with high-frequency content significantly below ground truth. In contrast, the two-stage Mask2Real-WM models maintained sharpness levels closer to ground truth. This indicated that the baseline's competitive pixel metrics were partially an artifact of its tendency to produce blurrier, temporally averaged outputs, a known bias in pixel-level evaluation metrics.
- **Mask-space Quality (WM1):** In WM1's segmentation space, real-only training performed best on ID data, but the simulation-midtrained and fine-tuned WM1 (ours) demonstrated superior generalization under distribution shift (OOD PSNR 21.7 versus 20.5 for real-only). This highlighted the benefit of the synthetic corpus in covering contact-rich motions not present in the smaller real dataset.

**WM2 Conditioning Ablation:**
- Mask conditioning via ControlNet was identified as the primary factor driving spatial quality and sharpness in the generated videos. Removing mask conditioning resulted in blurry, temporally averaged outputs.
- Combining both mask and action conditioning signals yielded the best overall perceptual quality and motion consistency, particularly for the wrist-mounted camera view where scale might otherwise be ambiguous from segmentation masks alone.

**Error Accumulation:**
- In autoregressive rollouts, LPIPS error was observed to accumulate most rapidly during the initial ~10 steps before plateauing. OOD scenarios generally exhibited a steeper increase in error accumulation.

**Failure Modes:**
- The primary failure modes were localized to WM1 (the dynamics model). These included objects vanishing under hand occlusion (due to a lack of depth information) and object duplication or erroneous spawning, indicating uncertainty in the object's predicted state.
- When WM2 was conditioned on ground-truth segmentation masks, its rendering quality significantly improved, confirming that WM1's mask prediction accuracy was the current bottleneck rather than WM2's rendering capabilities.

**Generalization to Unseen Objects:**
- The system demonstrated generalization to objects (banana, cup, cylinder) that WM1 had encountered in simulation but WM2 had never rendered from real data. WM1, operating in segmentation space, transferred effectively, maintaining high mask fidelity (SSIM ≈0.86). This suggests that for new object appearances, only the WM2 renderer would need to be updated, not the dynamics model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Mask2Real-WM presents a significant development in robot learning by addressing critical challenges in dexterous manipulation with world models. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating an effective strategy for overcoming the limitations of data scarcity and the sim-to-real gap, particularly for high-DoF robotic systems.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

- **Enabling Dexterous Manipulation with Limited Real Data:** The core innovation of using segmentation masks as a sim-to-real bridge allows the dynamics model to be extensively pretrained on synthetic data. This approach significantly reduces the reliance on large, difficult-to-acquire real-world dexterous manipulation datasets, which can accelerate research and development in this domain.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Robustness:** The achieved high per-DoF action controllability (≈0.95 ID, ≈0.87 OOD) represents a substantial improvement over monolithic baselines. This fidelity in action prediction and control is crucial for complex tasks requiring precise finger-object interactions, enabling robots to execute fine-grained manipulations reliably even in unseen scenarios.
- **Improved Simulation-to-Real Transfer:** By isolating dynamics prediction to a more domain-invariant segmentation space, the work provides a robust framework for transferring learned behaviors from simulation to reality. This method could be broadly applicable to other robot learning problems where the domain gap between simulation and reality is a bottleneck for visual observations.
- **Modular and Flexible Architecture:** The two-stage design offers practical advantages in terms of modularity. WM1 can function as a lightweight dynamics checker without the computational cost of full rendering, while WM2 can be independently fine-tuned to adapt to new visual environments or object textures by adjusting only its ControlNet weights. This modularity streamlines adaptation and deployment.
- **Foundation for Advanced Robot Learning:** Mask2Real-WM provides a controllable and realistic world model that can serve as a powerful tool for policy evaluation, planning, and "dreaming" in imagination. This capability can facilitate the development of more sophisticated robot policies that learn and adapt within simulated environments before being deployed in the physical world.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the lack of depth information for object occlusion and object identity drift, point to clear avenues for future research, including depth conditioning, object-ID conditioning, and the use of richer data generation techniques. Furthermore, the computational cost of two sequential diffusion passes suggests exploration into distillation methods to enable near-real-time inference, which would further broaden the practical applicability of such models.

In summary, Mask2Real-WM offers a method for creating controllable and robust world models for dexterous manipulation, mitigating challenges associated with data scarcity and domain transfer through a novel segmentation-space bridge. This approach has the potential to influence the development of robotic systems capable of more versatile and adaptive physical interaction.
