# Eva-VLA: Evaluating Vision-Language-Action Models' Robustness Under Real-World Physical Variations

> [!summary] Summary
> The Eva-VLA framework systematically evaluates the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models under physically plausible real-world variations, revealing substantial vulnerabilities in state-of-the-art systems. It demonstrates that optimized 3D object transformations, illumination changes, and adversarial patch placements can increase VLA model failure rates from under 25% to over 80% in simulated and real environments, and the generated adversarial examples improve model robustness through adversarial training.

## Problem

- VLA models show promise for robotic manipulation, but their robustness to dynamic, physically plausible variations (e.g., object poses, lighting) in real-world environments remains underexplored.
- Existing robustness evaluation methods, often gradient-based or pixel-level, lack physical realism, have a limited scope of variations, or require white-box model access, making them unsuitable for black-box VLA systems.
- Discovering worst-case scenarios across a vast space of physical variations in the real world incurs prohibitive data collection and experimentation costs.

## Method

- **Eva-VLA** proposes a unified framework that parameterizes diverse, physically plausible real-world variations, including ==3D object rotations==, ==dynamic point light source illumination==, and spatial placement of ==natural adversarial patches==.
- An adversarial objective is defined using ==cosine similarity== between clean and perturbed action vectors, augmented with a substantial terminal reward for task failure, to provide a continuous optimization signal.
- A query-based, ==gradient-free black-box optimization== algorithm (==CMA-ES==) is employed to efficiently discover worst-case configurations of physical variations without requiring model gradients or extensive real-world data.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA, UniVLA) exhibited severe vulnerabilities, with failure rates surging from baseline levels (**4.0-23.5%**) to over **80%** under optimized 3D object transformations, illumination, and adversarial patches.
- 3D object transformations consistently emerged as the most disruptive variation, leading to widespread spatial misalignment and incorrect object placement, particularly in long-horizon tasks.
- Adversarial training using Eva-VLA's generated examples significantly improved π0.5 model robustness against adversarial patches (**45.5%** to **24.3%** failure), illumination (**12.3%** to **6.3%**), and 3D transformations (**85.8%** to **56.8%**), with minimal impact on clean performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-world physical variations can be effectively parameterized as continuous optimization problems, enabling systematic and reproducible robustness evaluations in simulation.
> - Gradient-free black-box optimization techniques are efficient for identifying critical vulnerabilities in VLA models, even without access to internal model gradients, which is crucial for black-box system evaluation.
> - Adversarial examples derived from physically plausible variations are actionable and can be effectively used for adversarial training, leading to quantifiable improvements in VLA model robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotic manipulation by integrating visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation into unified end-to-end systems. These models, exemplified by architectures such as RT-2, OpenVLA, CogACT, TraceVLA, SpatialVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, π series, and UniVLA, have shown capabilities in various applications, including manufacturing, healthcare, and service robotics. They often leverage pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and fine-tune them with robot action data.

Despite advancements and deployments, a critical area of investigation remains the robustness of VLA models to the physical variations inherent in real-world environments. Existing research has explored adversarial attacks in robotics, predominantly through methods like adversarial patches or pixel-level perturbations. These attacks, often gradient-based and designed for digital settings, face limitations in physical plausibility and applicability to black-box systems. For instance, some prior work introduced localized perturbations via gradient-based attacks or focused on predefined threat categories and constrained 2D perturbations. While these provided foundational analysis, they did not fully capture the continuous, multi-dimensional nature of real-world physical shifts or the black-box operational settings of many VLA models. Challenges include ensuring physical realism of perturbations and efficiently discovering worst-case scenarios without extensive real-world data collection.

This work, Eva-VLA, aims to address these gaps by proposing a unified framework that systematically evaluates VLA robustness under physically plausible and diverse real-world variations. It extends beyond pixel-level or predefined attacks to consider continuous physical transformations (3D object transformations, illumination changes, and adversarial patch placements) and employs a black-box optimization strategy suitable for models where gradients are inaccessible. By focusing on a systematic, reproducible, and efficient vulnerability discovery pipeline, Eva-VLA contributes to bridging the identified gap between VLA models' laboratory performance and their robustness in unpredictable real-world deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically evaluate the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models when exposed to real-world physical variations. The authors propose Eva-VLA as a unified framework to achieve this.

The motivation for this work stems from two core observations:
1. **VLA Models' Promise vs. Real-World Challenges:** VLA models are emerging as solutions for robotic manipulation, showing transformative potential across various sectors. However, real-world deployment environments are dynamic and introduce inevitable physical variations such as changes in object poses, lighting conditions, or visual scene disruptions. These variations can significantly alter robot behavior, potentially without immediate detection, and pose safety risks. The current understanding of VLA models' robustness under such diverse physical conditions is underexplored.
2. **Limitations of Existing Robustness Evaluation Methods:** Prior research into VLA-based robotic system robustness has limitations:
 - **Lack of Physical Plausibility:** Many existing adversarial attack methods, particularly gradient-based, pixel-level perturbations, generate changes that may not be physically realistic or reproducible in the real world.
 - **Limited Scope of Variations:** Current methods often focus on specific, constrained types of visual interference (e.g., localized adversarial patches) that do not capture the broad spectrum of continuous and multi-dimensional physical changes encountered in real environments.
 - **Dependence on White-Box Access:** Many gradient-based attacks require access to the model's internal gradients, making them unsuitable for evaluating black-box VLA models deployed in real-world scenarios where such access is often restricted.
 - **Prohibitive Real-World Data Costs:** Systematically exploring a vast space of physical variations to discover worst-case scenarios in the real world would incur prohibitive data collection and experimentation costs.

To address these motivations, the research specifically aims to tackle two fundamental challenges in VLA models' physical robustness evaluation:
1. **Characterizing Diverse Physical Perturbations:** To systematically describe diverse physical variations encountered in real-world deployments while maintaining reproducibility for evaluation. This involves identifying key dimensions of variation and parameterizing them in a way that is both comprehensive and physically plausible.
2. **Efficient Worst-Case Scenario Discovery:** To efficiently discover worst-case scenarios (i.e., configurations of physical variations that lead to task failure) without incurring prohibitive real-world data collection costs. This necessitates an optimization mechanism that can effectively search a continuous parameter space in a black-box setting.

By addressing these challenges, Eva-VLA seeks to expose critical fragilities in state-of-the-art VLA models, bridge the gap between their laboratory performance and real-world operational resilience, and provide a framework that can inform the development of more robust robotic manipulation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Eva-VLA framework is designed to systematically evaluate the robustness of VLA models by formulating real-world physical variations as continuous optimization problems. The methodology comprises preliminaries, parameterization of physical variations, formalization of an adversarial objective, and a query-based optimization algorithm.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Vision-Language-Action Models

VLA models integrate visual observation ($V$), language instructions ($L$), and action generation ($A$) into an end-to-end system, represented as $F: V \times L \rightarrow A$. The action space typically encodes 7 degrees of freedom (DoF) for a robotic manipulator, including positional changes ($\Delta P_{x,y,z}$) and rotational changes ($\Delta R_{x,y,z}$) along Cartesian axes, and gripper control (gripper).

#### 3.2. Eva-VLA Framework Design

The core of Eva-VLA involves parameterizing three distinct categories of physical variations and defining an adversarial objective.

- **Parameterization of 3D Object Transformations ($\Theta$):**
 - **Focus:** Rigid 3D transformations, specifically rotations, of important objects in the scene.
 - **Parameterization:** A 3-dimensional vector $\Theta = \{\alpha, \beta, \gamma\}$ representing Tait-Bryan angles (yaw, pitch, roll) in the Z-Y-X sequence.
 - **Constraints:** Transformations are constrained to a bounded interval $[\Theta_{min}, \Theta_{max}]$ to ensure physical plausibility within the environment. This allows exploration of arbitrary object orientations.

- **Parameterization of Illumination Variations ($\Lambda$):**
 - **Focus:** Realistic illumination changes in the environment.
 - **Parameterization:** A 4-dimensional vector $\Lambda = \{x, y, \sigma, I\}$ defining a dynamic point light source.
 - **Model:** A Gaussian falloff function $L(z) = I \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{\|z - (x, y)\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$ is used, where $(x, y)$ are the center coordinates, $\sigma$ is the spread radius, and $I$ is the intensity. Parameters are optimized within physically plausible limits to avoid trivial sensor failures and isolate genuine perceptual vulnerabilities.

- **Parameterization of Adversarial Patch Placement ($\phi$):**
 - **Focus:** Disrupting scene understanding through strategically placed visual elements.
 - **Approach:** Instead of optimizing pixel textures, natural images (e.g., barcodes) are used as patches, and their spatial placement on the tabletop surface is optimized. This ensures physical realizability via standard UV mapping.
 - **Parameterization:** A 2-dimensional vector $\phi = \{x, y\} \in [\phi_{min}, \phi_{max}]$, where $(x, y)$ denotes the patch's coordinates within the table's texture map. Constraints are defined by the physical dimensions of the workspace.

- **Adversarial Objective ($L_{adv}$):**
 - **Purpose:** To quantify the degradation of VLA model performance under physical variations and provide a continuous signal for optimization.
 - **Metric:** Cosine similarity is used to measure the deviation between predicted adversarial action vectors ($A^{adv}$) and nominal clean action vectors ($A^{clean}$) over $N$ timesteps.
 - **Task Failure Integration:** To account for policy redundancy (where different action sequences can succeed) and target genuine failures, a substantial terminal reward is added. A binary indicator $I_{fail} \in \{0, 1\}$ is 1 if the task ultimately fails, multiplied by a large positive scalar $\lambda$.
 - **Formulation:** $L_{adv} = -\sum_{i=1}^{N} \cos(A_i^{clean}, A_i^{adv}) + \lambda \cdot I_{fail}$.
 - **Goal:** The objective is to discover an optimal distribution $p^*(\mathcal{T})$ over the transformation parameters $\mathcal{T} \in \{\Theta, \Lambda, \phi\}$ that maximizes the expected adversarial impact: $p^*(\mathcal{T}) = \arg \max_{p(\mathcal{T})} E_{\mathcal{T} \sim p(\mathcal{T})} [L_{adv}(A^{clean}, A^{adv})]$.

#### 3.3. Query-Based Optimizing Algorithm

To efficiently discover the most potent physical variations, Eva-VLA employs a probabilistic, black-box optimization approach.

- **Distribution Search Problem:** Instead of finding a single worst-case configuration, the problem is framed as searching for an optimal distribution of parameters. Each variation type $\mathcal{T}$ is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian distribution: $\mathcal{T} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathcal{T}}, \Sigma^2_{\mathcal{T}} C_{\mathcal{T}})$, where $\mu_{\mathcal{T}}$ is the mean configuration, $\Sigma_{\mathcal{T}}$ controls exploration scale, and $C_{\mathcal{T}}$ captures parameter correlations.
- **Optimization Algorithm:** Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) is utilized. This gradient-free evolutionary algorithm is suitable for black-box evaluation as it relies solely on model inference outputs, bypassing the need for differentiable simulation environments or model gradients.
- **Efficiency Techniques:**
 - **Learning Rate Adaptation (LRA):** Dynamically adjusts the step size ($\Sigma$) during optimization to balance exploration and exploitation, enhancing convergence speed.
 - **Early Stopping Policy:** Monitors convergence and terminates optimization when improvements plateau, preventing computational waste.
- **Optimization Loop:** The process involves iteratively: 1) sampling candidate configurations from the current parameter distribution, 2) evaluating their adversarial effectiveness by querying the VLA model within the simulation, and 3) updating the distribution parameters based on the most successful candidates.

This methodology combines physically plausible parameterization with an efficient, gradient-free black-box optimization strategy to systematically uncover vulnerabilities in VLA models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using the Eva-VLA framework on the LIBERO dataset, involving several state-of-the-art VLA models, revealed significant vulnerabilities under real-world physical variations.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Widespread Vulnerability:** VLA models, including OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, UniVLA, and $\pi^{0.5}$, exhibited substantial increases in failure rates when subjected to optimized physical variations, despite having low failure rates (4.0% to 23.5%) in clean, controlled environments. This indicates a significant gap between laboratory performance and real-world robustness.
- **Impact of 3D Object Transformations:** Object 3D transformations ($\Theta$) consistently emerged as the most disruptive physical variation. OpenVLA's average failure rate surged to 83.0%, UniVLA to 88.0%, and $\pi^{0.5}$ to 86.0% under these attacks. This highlights a critical fragility in models' spatial reasoning capabilities.
- **Impact of Illumination and Adversarial Patches:** Illumination variations ($\Lambda$) and adversarial patch placements ($\phi$) also caused significant performance degradation across nearly all evaluated models. For instance, OpenVLA's average failure rates rose to 63.0% under illumination changes and 71.0% under adversarial patches.
- **Necessity of Optimization:** The results demonstrated that random environmental perturbations, while causing some performance drops (e.g., 33.0%-56.0% for OpenVLA), were significantly less effective than the optimized perturbations discovered by Eva-VLA. This validated the framework's efficiency in locating worst-case scenarios and exposing extreme bounds of VLA brittleness.
- **Compounding Effects in Long-Horizon Tasks:** The vulnerability was particularly pronounced in long-horizon tasks, where failure rates approached near-total collapse under optimized object transformations (e.g., for OpenVLA and UniVLA). This suggests that adversarial effects can compound over extended action sequences.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Distinct Failure Modes:** Visualizations of robot movement trajectories under physical variations showed distinct failure modes:
 - **Object 3D Transformations:** Led to spatial misalignment and incorrect object placement, even if initial movement patterns were similar. The robot might attempt the full task but mislocalize target positions.
 - **Illumination Variations:** Resulted in degraded object recognition, manifesting as incomplete grasping actions or premature trajectory termination due to interference with visual perception.
 - **Adversarial Patches:** Induced oscillatory behaviors and intermittent loss of object contact, causing the robot to deviate from intended trajectories and disrupt task-relevant interactions.
- These qualitative observations reinforced that each variation type effectively disrupted robot actions through specific mechanisms.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study and Additional Results

- **Optimization Efficiency:** Ablation studies on optimization iterations (0 to 60 steps) showed rapid convergence of failure rates. Most severe vulnerabilities were discovered within the first 40 iterations, demonstrating the efficiency of the black-box optimization in systematically locating worst-case physical states without excessive computational overhead.
- **Specificity of Attacks:** Analysis of scaling ratios of the optimal distribution confirmed that VLA models are susceptible to specific, critical geometric and visual configurations, rather than just general sensitivities to arbitrary noise. Scaling the optimal distributions (e.g., from $\times$1.0 to $\times$10.0) significantly reduced attack effectiveness, indicating the precision of the discovered vulnerabilities.
- **Robustness Improvements through Adversarial Training:** The worst-case scenarios generated by Eva-VLA were used to improve model robustness. Adversarial training on $\pi^{0.5}$ using these examples reduced vulnerability to adversarial patches (from 45.5% to 24.3%), illumination variations (from 12.3% to 6.3%), and 3D transformations (from 85.8% to 56.8%). This improvement came with a negligible increase in clean task failure rate (from 4.0% to 5.0%), validating the practical utility and actionable quality of the identified vulnerabilities.
- **Real-World Performance:** Physical experiments confirmed that the three types of variations induced severe task failures in real-world scenarios. Objects were misplaced, visual perception was obscured, and scene understanding was interfered with, leading to unstable, jerky, and oscillatory robot motions, consistent with simulation results.

In summary, the main findings highlight that leading VLA models possess severe vulnerabilities to physically plausible real-world variations, with 3D object transformations posing the most significant threat. The Eva-VLA framework effectively and efficiently uncovers these weaknesses, and the generated adversarial examples are valuable for enhancing model robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Eva-VLA framework and its findings carry several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of robotic manipulation and VLA model development:

- **Systematic Robustness Evaluation:** Eva-VLA introduces a unified and systematic framework for evaluating VLA models' robustness against continuous, physically plausible real-world variations. By parameterizing 3D object transformations, illumination changes, and adversarial patch placements, it provides a structured approach to stress-testing these models beyond conventional digital or constrained physical attacks. This systematic approach contributes to more comprehensive and reproducible vulnerability assessments.
- **Exposure of Critical Vulnerabilities:** The research quantitatively and qualitatively demonstrates that state-of-the-art VLA models, despite strong performance in controlled environments, exhibit severe vulnerabilities to physical perturbations. The high failure rates (e.g., over 90% in some cases for OpenVLA on LIBERO-Long tasks under 3D transformations) expose critical systemic fragilities, particularly in spatial reasoning and visual perception under variable conditions. This highlights a significant gap between current VLA capabilities and the requirements for reliable real-world deployment.
- **Efficiency in Worst-Case Scenario Discovery:** By formulating physical variations as continuous optimization problems and leveraging gradient-free optimization (CMA-ES), Eva-VLA provides an efficient mechanism to discover worst-case scenarios without requiring prohibitive real-world data collection or white-box access to model gradients. This efficiency is crucial for practical evaluation and development in robotics.
- **Guidance for Robust Model Development:** The identified vulnerabilities are not merely theoretical flaws; the research demonstrates that the adversarial examples generated by Eva-VLA can be utilized for adversarial training. This quantifiably increases model robustness (e.g., significant reductions in failure rates for $\pi^{0.5}$ against all three variation types with minimal impact on clean performance). This positions Eva-VLA not just as an evaluation tool but also as an effective data augmentation method to enhance the resilience of robotic manipulation systems.
- **Bridging Simulation to Reality:** The validation through real-world experiments, showing consistent task failures and unstable robot motions under the proposed variations, reinforces the practical relevance of the simulation-based findings. This bridging of the simulation-to-reality gap underscores the urgency and necessity of addressing these physical vulnerabilities before widespread deployment.
- **Enhancing Operational Safety and Reliability:** The observed instability, jerky, and oscillatory motions in both simulated and real-world perturbed scenarios highlight potential risks to human safety and the operational environment. By systematically identifying and characterizing these vulnerabilities, Eva-VLA contributes to a pathway for developing VLA-based robotic systems that are safer and more reliable in unpredictable physical environments.

In conclusion, Eva-VLA significantly advances the understanding of VLA model robustness, providing a crucial framework for both evaluating existing models and guiding the development of future generations of robotic systems that can operate reliably in complex, dynamic, and physically variable real-world settings.
