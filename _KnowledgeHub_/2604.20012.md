# EmbodiedMidtrain: Bridging the Gap between Vision-Language Models and Vision-Language-Action Models via Mid-training

> [!summary] Summary
> EmbodiedMidtrain introduces a data-centric mid-training method that selectively curates general Vision-Language Model (VLM) data based on its proximity to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) task distributions. This approach consistently improves VLA performance across robot manipulation benchmarks, enabling smaller VLM-initialized VLAs to achieve results competitive with or superior to significantly larger models.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which control robots, are typically initialized from general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs).
- A significant data distribution mismatch exists between the broad pretraining data of VLMs and the specific data required for embodied action generation in VLAs.
- This mismatch leads to suboptimal VLM initializations for VLAs, limiting their performance and requiring extensive, often inefficient, fine-tuning.

## Method

- **EmbodiedMidtrain** characterizes the data-distribution gap between VLM and VLA data quantitatively with ==Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)== and qualitatively with ==t-SNE visualizations==.
- Introduces a ==data engine== that employs a lightweight ==binary classifier== to score VLM samples by proximity to the VLA distribution, then curates a ==mid-training== dataset from the top-K scored samples to adapt the VLM backbone.
- Fine-tunes the adapted VLM as the backbone for a VLA model on standard robot manipulation benchmarks, predicting continuous arm and binary gripper actions.

## Results

- Achieved consistent performance improvements across **three** robot-manipulation benchmarks (Calvin ABC-D, SimplerEnv Bridge, LIBERO-10) with two VLM backbones, reaching higher performance levels earlier in fine-tuning and maintaining this advantage despite similar loss curves.
- A smaller mid-trained **InternVL3.5-1B** model (**1.1B** parameters) surpassed several expert VLA baselines and off-the-shelf VLMs **3-8×** larger, demonstrating improved resource efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the ==proximity-based data selection== method is critical, outperforming random sampling and various hand-crafted metrics for VLM data curation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A quantifiable and visually distinct data distribution gap exists between general VLM pretraining data and embodied VLA data, which is non-uniform.
> - Selectively mid-training VLMs on a curated subset of VLM data, chosen via a learned proximity estimator for its closeness to VLA distributions and its spatial-grounding/reasoning content rather than purely text-centric content, yields a significantly stronger initialization for VLA models.
> - The performance gains from this data-centric mid-training approach are transferable across different VLM backbones, indicating generalizable embodied alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robotics foundation models have led to the development of Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs), which integrate visual perception, language understanding, and action generation for generalist robot control. Many VLAs, such as OpenVLA, π0, and GR00T N1, leverage Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as their foundational backbones, inheriting broad visual and linguistic representations. This approach aims to facilitate rapid adaptation to embodied settings.

However, a fundamental challenge exists in that most VLAs are initialized from general-purpose, off-the-shelf VLMs that have not been specifically adapted for embodied action generation. VLM pretraining typically encompasses diverse vision-language tasks like captioning, visual question answering, and document understanding. In contrast, VLA training involves robotic manipulation trajectories grounded in physical interaction. This discrepancy results in a data distribution mismatch between the VLM pretraining domain and the embodied action generation domain.

Previous research efforts have explored improving VLMs' embodied capabilities through specialized dataset construction (e.g., RefSpatial, Robo2VLM, RoboPoint) and model-level adaptations. However, studies such as Zhang et al. (2026) indicate that performance gains from fine-tuning VLMs on embodied tasks do not consistently translate to improved downstream VLA performance. This suggests that existing embodied VLM fine-tuning might capture signals different from those required for effective VLA execution, or that current approaches demand substantial amounts of in-domain robot data.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by identifying and directly addressing the data distribution gap between general VLMs and target VLA domains. It proposes a mid-training paradigm, a concept analogous to intermediate training stages in Large Language Models (LLMs) used to adapt foundation models toward specific domains or capabilities before final fine-tuning. Unlike prior work that either focuses on VLM-side embodied benchmarks or relies on extensive task-specific data, this research focuses on curating and leveraging existing, diverse VLM data to create a more aligned initialization for VLAs. The contribution is a data-centric mid-training strategy that selectively adapts VLMs to the embodied domain without requiring architectural changes or solely relying on new, specific embodied datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the identified data distribution gap between general Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) to enhance the performance of VLAs in robot control tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:

1. **Distributional Mismatch:** VLAs commonly utilize off-the-shelf VLMs as their backbones, inheriting visual and linguistic understanding from broad VLM pretraining. However, VLM pretraining data covers a wide array of general vision-language tasks (e.g., image captioning, visual question answering, document understanding) that often differ significantly from the specific requirements of embodied action generation, which involves robotic manipulation trajectories and physical interaction. This creates a fundamental distributional mismatch between the source VLM data and the target VLA domain.

2. **Suboptimal VLM Initialization for VLAs:** Despite providing a strong foundation for general language and visual understanding, the internal representations of general VLMs may not be optimally suited for the nuanced embodied reasoning necessary for effective action generation. This misalignment means that current VLMs provide a less-than-ideal starting point for VLA fine-tuning, potentially limiting their downstream performance.

3. **Limitations of Existing Adaptation Methods:** Simply fine-tuning VLMs on curated embodied data has not reliably translated into substantial improvements in VLA performance, as noted in previous work. This indicates that a more principled and effective method for adapting VLMs to the VLA domain is required beyond direct fine-tuning or the creation of new embodied-specific datasets.

4. **Heterogeneity of the Data Gap:** An initial analysis conducted by the authors reveals that the VLM-VLA data distribution gap is not uniform. While VLA data tends to occupy compact regions separated from the broader VLM distribution, some VLM samples demonstrate a higher degree of alignment with the VLA domain than others. This heterogeneity suggests that merely training on all available VLM data indiscriminately might dilute the embodied signal, while selective use of the most relevant VLM data could be beneficial.

These motivations collectively underscore the need for a mechanism that can selectively reshape the VLM training data distribution to be more aligned with the VLA domain, thereby providing a stronger and more relevant initialization for downstream VLA fine-tuning. The authors propose mid-training as a strategy to achieve this, specifically through a data engine that identifies and curates VLM samples exhibiting higher proximity to the VLA domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of EmbodiedMidtrain involves a multi-stage approach: first, characterizing the data distribution gap, then developing a data engine for proximity-based data selection, followed by VLM mid-training, and finally VLA fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Data Distribution Gap Characterization (Section 3)

The research begins by analyzing the distributional mismatch between VLM and VLA data.
- **Representation Space:** The last hidden states of a VLM (specifically, InternVL3.5-1B was used for analysis) are extracted as feature representations, h(·), for both VLM and VLA data samples.
- **Quantitative Analysis (MMD):** The Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) metric (Equation 1) is used to quantify the distance between pairs of datasets. A Gaussian RBF kernel applied over the extracted features measures similarity. The results show generally smaller MMD distances within VLM or VLA groups compared to cross-group distances, indicating a clear distributional mismatch.
- **Qualitative Visualization (t-SNE):** t-SNE (t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding) is employed to visualize the high-dimensional feature distributions. The visualization indicates that VLA datasets form compact and concentrated clusters largely separated from the broader and more dispersed VLM distributions.
- **Internal Heterogeneity:** The analysis also reveals that the gap is not uniform; some VLM data sources and individual samples are more aligned with the VLA domain than others, suggesting that sample-level selection could be beneficial.

#### 3.2. Data Engine for EmbodiedMidtrain (Section 4)

To bridge this gap, the paper proposes a mid-training data engine that selects a subset of VLM data exhibiting higher alignment with the VLA domain.
- **Proximity-Based Data Selection:** This process is framed as a domain-membership problem. The goal is to select a subset of VLM data, D*VLM, that minimizes distributional divergence from the target VLA data, D_VLA (Equation 2). This is relaxed to a per-sample scoring and top-K selection approach (Equation 3).
- **Lightweight Proximity Estimator:** A binary classifier is trained on frozen VLM features to distinguish VLA data (positive samples) from VLM data (negative samples).
 - The classifier's output, s(x) (Equation 5), is interpreted as a continuous measure of a VLM sample's closeness to the VLA domain. This score is monotonically increasing with the density ratio p_VLA(x) / p_VLM(x) (Equation 4).
 - The estimator applies a learnable scoring function, f, (a linear layer) on top of the frozen VLM's last hidden state, ϕ(x), followed by a sigmoid activation: s(x) = σ(f(ϕ(x))).
 - It is trained using binary cross-entropy loss (Equation 6) with early stopping at 90% validation accuracy.
- **Curated Mid-training Mixture:** After training, the proximity estimator scores all candidate VLM samples. The top-K highest-scoring samples are selected to form a curated mid-training dataset, D*VLM. This process focuses the training on VLM samples most compatible with the embodied domain while preserving diversity.

#### 3.3. VLM Mid-training

- The chosen VLM backbone (InternVL3.5-1B or Qwen3VL-2B) undergoes a mid-training stage on the curated D*VLM dataset. This involves full-parameter supervised fine-tuning using the LLaMA-Factory framework.
- The objective of this stage is to adapt the VLM's internal representations to be more suitable for embodied tasks, providing a stronger initialization (θ_mid) for subsequent VLA fine-tuning.

#### 3.4. VLA Fine-tuning

- Following VLM mid-training, the resulting VLM is used to initialize a VLA model.
- The VLA architecture follows the VLM4VLA pipeline, cascading the VLM backbone with a two-branch MLP action decoder. This decoder predicts continuous arm actions (supervised with Huber loss) and binary gripper actions (supervised with binary cross-entropy loss).
- The VLA is fine-tuned end-to-end on standard robot manipulation benchmarks. Input images are standardized and resized, and only single-view images are used without robot state information.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Benchmarks and Baselines

- **Benchmarks:** The system is evaluated on three simulated robot manipulation benchmarks: Calvin ABC-D (testing generalization to novel scene configurations), SimplerEnv Bridge (real-to-sim, tabletop manipulation), and LIBERO-10 (challenging long-horizon tasks).
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against:
 - **Expert VLA Models:** OpenVLA and π0.
 - **Off-the-shelf VLM Baselines:** VLAs directly fine-tuned from diverse VLMs like Qwen2.5VL, Qwen3VL, Paligemma, and KosMos-2 families, spanning various architectures and scales.
- **Resource Efficiency:** Comparisons include model size and total number of samples seen during training to assess efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the VLM-VLA data distribution gap, the effectiveness of EmbodiedMidtrain, and insights into its mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Data Distribution Gap Characteristics

- **Separation and Compactness:** Quantitative (MMD) and qualitative (t-SNE) analyses confirm a substantial data distribution gap between general VLM data and VLA data. VLA datasets form compact, concentrated clusters that are largely separated from the broader, more dispersed distributions of VLM datasets (Figure 2).
- **Internal Heterogeneity:** The gap is not uniform; some VLM samples and datasets exhibit higher proximity to the VLA domain than others, indicating a spectrum of alignment rather than a binary distinction.

#### 4.2. Consistent VLA Performance Gains

- **Overall Improvement:** EmbodiedMidtrain consistently yields significant performance improvements across all three robot manipulation benchmarks (Calvin ABC-D, SimplerEnv Bridge, and LIBERO-10) for both InternVL3.5-1B and Qwen3VL-2B backbones (Table 1).
- **Competitive with Larger Models:** The mid-trained InternVL3.5-1B, which is the smallest model in the comparison (1.1B parameters), surpasses several expert VLA baselines and off-the-shelf VLMs (e.g., Paligemma-1, Paligemma-2, KosMos-2) that are 3-8 times larger on Calvin ABC-D. On SimplerEnv Bridge and LIBERO-10, it achieves performance comparable to much larger Qwen family VLMs, while utilizing substantially fewer training samples (Table 1). This suggests that data alignment during mid-training contributes more than sheer model scale or data volume.

#### 4.3. Cross-Backbone Transferability of Selected Data

- The same mid-training data mixture, curated using features from InternVL3.5-1B, also produced performance gains for Qwen3VL-2B across all three benchmarks after mid-training (Table 1). This indicates that the proximity-based selection captures general distributional properties relevant to embodied VLA tasks, rather than being specific to a single VLM backbone's feature space.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on Data Engine Components

- **Proximity-based Selection vs. Random Sampling:** Randomly sampling data from the candidate VLM pool for mid-training consistently underperforms the proximity-based selection method across all benchmarks (Table 2). This demonstrates that the gains are not merely from additional mid-training steps, but from the deliberate identification and selection of VLM data better aligned with the VLA domain.
- **Learned Estimator vs. Hand-crafted Metrics:** The proposed learned proximity estimator outperforms hand-crafted alternatives, including feature-space average distance, VLA-conditioned perplexity, and delta perplexity, in terms of consistent performance across tasks (Table 2). This suggests the learned estimator captures VLA alignment more robustly than rule-based or simple statistical measures.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics During VLA Fine-tuning

- **Stronger Initialization:** Analysis of VLA fine-tuning trajectories shows that models initialized with EmbodiedMidtrain achieve higher performance from the earliest fine-tuning checkpoints (Figure 3). This provides direct evidence that mid-training yields a superior starting point for VLA learning.
- **Persistent and Widening Advantage:** The performance gap between mid-trained and baseline models persists and widens throughout the entire fine-tuning process, indicating that the benefits of mid-training are not transient but foundational.
- **Discrepancy with Training Loss:** Notably, this performance advantage is not clearly reflected in the training loss, which remains similar between the two initializations. This suggests that training loss alone may not fully capture the quality of a VLM initialization for VLA tasks.

#### 4.6. Analysis of Selected VLM Data

- **Dataset and Sample-level Preferences:** The proximity estimator exhibits preferences at both the dataset and sample levels (Figure 4a). For example, RefSpatial (embodied-oriented) generally receives higher average scores, while VCR (general-purpose) receives lower scores. Within datasets, there is significant score spread, indicating fine-grained sample-level selection.
- **Content Alignment:** High-scoring samples often involve spatial grounding and reasoning crucial for manipulation (e.g., "Which object is directly in front of you?"), whereas low-scoring samples are typically text-centric (e.g., "Who wrote this book?") (Figures 4b, 4c). This indicates the estimator learns to identify visual reasoning patterns relevant to embodied control.
- **Distribution Shift and Diversity Preservation:** The selected data subset shows a clear shift towards higher proximity scores compared to the full candidate pool (Figure 5). Simultaneously, the selected data retains high diversity, comparable to the original general VLM data and significantly higher than embodied-oriented VLM or VLA data (Table 6). This implies that EmbodiedMidtrain improves alignment with embodied tasks while preserving broad visual and linguistic coverage.
- **Mixture Composition:** The mid-training mixture comprises a balance of general and embodied-oriented sources. LAION-400M contributes the largest share due to its scale, but embodied-oriented datasets like RoboPoint and RefSpatial are retained at much higher rates relative to their original sizes, reflecting the estimator's preference (Figure 6).

#### 4.7. Capability Retention after VLM Mid-training

- Mid-training largely preserves the VLM's original capabilities. Performance on some spatial reasoning VLM benchmarks (VisuLogic, 3DSRBench) improves, while others (BLINK, SpatialEval) show moderate decreases, and POPE remains stable (Table 7). This suggests a reorientation of the model towards skills relevant for embodied adaptation rather than a uniform degradation or improvement of all VLM capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EmbodiedMidtrain framework presents a notable contribution to the field of robotics and embodied AI, with several potential impacts:

1. **Principled VLM-VLA Alignment:** The work provides a principled, data-driven approach to address the fundamental distributional mismatch between general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs). By actively curating VLM data that aligns with embodied tasks, it offers a systematic way to prepare VLMs for downstream robot control.

2. **Improved VLA Performance and Efficiency:** The demonstrated consistent performance gains across multiple robot manipulation benchmarks suggest that a well-aligned VLM initialization significantly benefits VLA learning. The ability of smaller, mid-trained models to achieve competitive results with substantially larger models and lower training budgets indicates a pathway for more computationally efficient and resource-effective development of high-performing VLAs. This could democratize access to advanced robot learning models.

3. **Enhanced Understanding of Embodied Learning:** The detailed analysis of the data distribution gap, the preferences of the proximity estimator, and the training dynamics provides insights into the nature of embodied reasoning required by VLAs. The finding that the learned estimator favors spatial reasoning over purely text-centric tasks highlights the specific capabilities that transfer effectively from general VLM data to embodied control.

4. **Transferability Across Architectures:** The successful transfer of the curated mid-training data across different VLM backbones (InternVL3.5-1B to Qwen3VL-2B) suggests that the proximity-based selection identifies general, backbone-agnostic properties of embodied alignment. This enhances the generalizability and utility of the proposed data engine.

5. **Leveraging Existing Data Effectively:** Instead of requiring the creation of new, large-scale embodied datasets, EmbodiedMidtrain demonstrates how to derive greater value from existing, abundant VLM data. This approach is scalable and can extend the utility of current and future VLMs for embodied intelligence without necessarily increasing model size or developing specialized architectures.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The findings regarding the non-uniformity of the VLM-VLA gap, the importance of sample-level selection, and the specific types of reasoning captured by the proximity estimator can guide future research in designing more effective VLM pre-training strategies and VLA adaptation techniques. The release of code, data, and models will further facilitate reproducibility and exploration by the research community.

In summary, EmbodiedMidtrain offers a practical and effective method for improving VLA performance by enhancing the readiness of VLM backbones for embodied tasks. Its focus on data-centric adaptation provides a promising direction for developing more capable and efficient generalist robot control systems.
