# SCALE: Self-uncertainty Conditioned Adaptive Looking and Execution for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A method named SCALE enhances the test-time robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by dynamically adjusting visual perception and action decoding based on the model's self-uncertainty. This training-free, single-pass strategy yields consistent performance improvements across diverse robotic manipulation tasks in simulation and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with robustness during test-time in diverse, real-world environments.
- Existing Test-Time Scaling (TTS) methods for VLAs demand additional training, often degrade under domain shifts, and require multiple forward passes, hindering real-time robotic control.
- Current TTS approaches primarily refine action decoding, neglecting adaptive visual perception, which is crucial for handling perceptual ambiguity.

## Method

- **SCALE** is proposed as a training-free, single-pass inference strategy for VLA models that jointly modulates visual perception and action decoding.
- It employs a novel self-uncertainty measure, derived from the Kullback-Leibler divergence to high and low uncertainty reference distributions, to quantify both distributional ambiguity and top-1 decisiveness.
- The method adaptively adjusts the vision encoder's attention temperature and action sampling temperature based on this uncertainty, balancing exploration and focused execution.

## Results

- SCALE demonstrated consistent success rate improvements, such as **5.8** percentage points (p.p.) for OpenVLA on LIBERO and **19.5** p.p. in real-world in-distribution tasks, across diverse VLA backbones and robotic manipulation benchmarks.
- The method outperformed greedy decoding, naive fixed-parameter sampling, and training-required Test-Time Scaling methods (e.g., MG-Select by **10.7** p.p. on LIBERO), all while maintaining single-pass inference efficiency.
- Ablation studies showed that both adaptive action decoding and adaptive visual attention contributed positively, with their combination yielding synergistic gains (e.g., **10.6** p.p. on LIBERO-Long for OpenVLA from a baseline of **52.7%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An intrinsic self-uncertainty signal can effectively drive adaptive modulation of both visual perception and action in VLA models, enhancing test-time robustness.
> - Dynamically adjusting visual attention based on uncertainty allows VLA models to adapt how they perceive the environment, particularly under perceptual ambiguity.
> - Jointly adapting both perception and action provides synergistic performance improvements, outperforming individual component contributions.
