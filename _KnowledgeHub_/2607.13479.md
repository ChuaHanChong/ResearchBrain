# Topology-Agnostic Mesh Reconstruction of Deformable Objects from Sparse Touch

> [!summary] Summary
> A topology-agnostic estimator reconstructs a deformable object's full vertex mesh from only sparse noisy touches and no vision. Touch tokens are mixed by self-attention, then a fixed bank of grid-position queries cross-attends to them, so swapping the query grid covers 1D rope, 2D cloth, and 3D soft body. Error drops roughly two-thirds versus non-learned baselines; deep-ensemble uncertainty drives a learned next-touch policy.

## Problem

- Deformable shape estimation almost always assumes vision, failing in darkness, inside bags, under gripper occlusion, or on crumpled cloth with folded-under layers.
- Active tactile shape estimation has been studied mostly for rigid objects; deformable work is vision-led, heuristic, and outputs 1D splines rather than full meshes.
- Non-learned completion, such as Gaussian-process implicit surfaces, over-smooths sharp folds, and separate models are normally needed per object topology.

## Method

- ==Grid-query cross-attention== estimator: touch tokens pair noisy 3D coordinates with normalized grid index, mix via one ==self-attention== layer, then per-vertex queries cross-attend and decode positions.
- ==Topology-agnostic design==: only the query-grid dimensionality changes, so one implementation yields rope, cloth, and volumetric soft-body estimators with permutation invariance over any touch count.
- ==Learned acquisition==: a 5-member ==deep ensemble== gives per-vertex variance; a small MLP regresses an expected-error-reduction oracle on epsilon-greedy rollouts to score next-touch candidates.

## Results

- Estimator cuts error by roughly two-thirds: **+66.6** to **+77.9%** versus GPIS and **+45.3** to **+66.6%** versus IDW across cloth and rope budgets; soft body **+71** to **+80%**.
- Cross-attention beats a global-pool set encoder under identical training, with the margin widening from **+4.7%** to **+22.4%** on cloth as touch budget grows.
- Learned acquisition gains **+5.6** to **+7.0%** over random on cloth (oracle **+14** to **+21%**); ensemble-variance proxy falls to **-11.4%** at **30** touches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Locality matters: per-vertex cross-attention over individual observations, not a pooled summary, is what makes reconstruction sensitive to where contacts land.
> - Raw uncertainty maximization is a poor acquisition rule; it chases persistently uncertain outliers and can perform worse than random as budget grows.
> - Active placement pays off only when state is underdetermined; once vision plus a learned prior pins the shape down, where you touch barely matters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Manipulating deformable objects, such as cloth, rope, or soft bodies, is a complex task for robots, primarily due to the infinite-dimensional nature of their configurations. Accurate state estimation, or understanding the object's current shape, is a prerequisite for almost all deformable manipulation tasks, including folding, routing, or grasping.

The majority of existing research in deformable object perception relies heavily on vision-based sensing. Techniques often involve garment-mesh completion from depth data, cloth-mesh reconstruction with occlusion reasoning, diffusion models for generating cloth states, or rope state estimation from occluded point clouds. Other related work focuses on learning dynamics across various topologies, but these approaches typically assume near-full visual state information and predict dynamics rather than reconstructing mesh from sparse contact. These vision-led methods are effective in environments where visual data is consistently available and unobstructed.

However, there are numerous practical scenarios where vision is degraded or entirely unavailable. Examples include reaching into opaque containers (bags or drawers), operating in low-light or dark environments, manipulating objects directly in-hand where the gripper occludes the object, or handling heavily crumpled materials where self-occlusion prevents external cameras from perceiving hidden layers. In these challenging contexts, touch emerges as a natural and often the only reliable sensor modality.

The utilization of touch, however, presents its own set of challenges. Tactile observations are inherently sparse and local, providing information only at the point of contact. Furthermore, acquiring each touch observation can be computationally or mechanically costly. This necessitates efficient strategies for both interpreting sparse tactile data and actively selecting informative touch locations.

Prior work on active tactile shape estimation has largely focused on rigid objects, employing methods like learned next-best-touch strategies with vision and touch, classical Gaussian Process (GP) uncertainty sampling, or active visuo-haptic completion. For deformable objects, some research has explored probing visually occluded cable segments, but these approaches are typically vision-led, use geometric heuristics, and output simpler representations like 1D splines rather than full meshes. A notable gap exists in the literature for a tactile-only full-mesh estimator that can operate across various deformable object topologies (1D, 2D, and 3D) without relying on vision, and for a learned active sensing policy in this specific context. This paper aims to address these identified limitations by developing a unified, topology-agnostic approach for deformable mesh reconstruction from sparse touch and an accompanying active sensing strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a single, topology-agnostic estimator capable of reconstructing the full mesh of a deformable object solely from a limited number of sparse tactile observations, without the aid of vision. This estimator is designed to handle diverse deformable objects, specifically a 1D rope, a 2D cloth, and a 3D volumetric soft body, using a unified architectural approach.

A secondary objective is to investigate and develop a learned active sensing strategy. This policy aims to intelligently decide where a robotic system should touch next to maximize information gain and minimize reconstruction error, especially under sparse tactile data budgets.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several critical limitations in current robotic perception and manipulation capabilities:

1. **Addressing Vision Limitations:** The dominant paradigm for deformable object perception relies on vision. However, many real-world manipulation scenarios occur in environments where vision is compromised or unavailable (e.g., in darkness, inside opaque containers, under heavy self-occlusion, or when an object is manipulated in-hand and occluded by the gripper). These situations render vision-based methods ineffective, necessitating alternative sensing modalities like touch.
2. **Exploiting Touch for Full Shape Estimation:** While touch is a natural sensor in vision-deprived settings, its inherent sparsity and locality pose significant challenges for estimating the full, high-dimensional shape of deformable objects. Existing tactile-only methods are limited in scope, often targeting rigid objects or simpler deformable representations (e.g., 1D splines). There is a need for a method that can infer the complete mesh structure across different deformable object types from minimal tactile input.
3. **Efficiency in Data Collection:** Each tactile observation is generally costly in terms of time and robot actuation. Therefore, a practical system must operate with only a handful of touches. This motivates the need for both an effective estimator that can reconstruct from sparse data and an intelligent strategy to select the most informative touch locations, thereby minimizing the total number of required contacts.
4. **Generality and Unification:** Developing separate perception models for different deformable topologies (1D, 2D, 3D) is inefficient. The research is motivated by the desire to create a single, unified architecture that can generalize across these different object types, simplifying development and deployment.
5. **Understanding Active Sensing Benefits:** The study also aims to quantify the benefits of active tactile sensing, especially in vision-free contexts. It seeks to determine if intelligent touch placement significantly improves reconstruction accuracy compared to random touching, and how these benefits are influenced by factors like self-occlusion or the availability of visual information. This includes understanding the potential gap between ideal (oracle) and deployable active sensing policies.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need to enable robust deformable object perception in challenging, vision-limited environments, striving for efficient and generalizable tactile-only mesh reconstruction complemented by an intelligent active sensing strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the problem of reconstructing the full mesh of a deformable object from sparse tactile observations without vision. The object is represented as a mesh of $N$ vertices, lying on a regular grid $G$ (e.g., $(H, W)$ for a sheet, $(H, 1)$ for a linear object, or $(D, H, W)$ for a volume). Its configuration $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$ is drawn from a distribution of settled states. Touch at vertex $i$ yields a noisy position $o_i = x_i + \epsilon$, where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$. Given observations $O_S$ from touched vertices $S$, the goal is to reconstruct the full mesh $\hat{X} = f(O_S)$. Reconstruction error $E(S)$ is measured as the mean per-vertex squared error: $\frac{1}{N} \sum_i \| \hat{x}_i(O_S) - x_i \|^2$.

**Topology-Agnostic Estimator:**

The core of the methodology is a single, permutation-invariant cross-attention network designed to be topology-agnostic. This means the same underlying model architecture can be applied to 1D ropes, 2D cloths, and 3D soft bodies with minimal adaptation.

1. **Observation Encoding:** Each tactile observation is first encoded into a token. This token concatenates the noisy 3D coordinates of the touched point with the normalized grid index of the corresponding vertex. The key aspect of topology-agnosticism here is that the dimensionality of the grid index (1 for rope, 2 for cloth, 3 for soft body) is the only component that changes across different object types.
2. **Self-Attention Layer:** These observation tokens are then processed through an MLP to embed them into a higher-dimensional space (width $d=128$). Subsequently, a multi-head self-attention layer mixes these embedded tokens. This step allows each observation to be contextualized by all other observations, capturing relationships within the sparse input set.
3. **Cross-Attention Layer:** A fixed bank of query tokens is introduced, one for each output vertex of the target mesh. Each query token's features consist of the normalized grid coordinates of its respective vertex. These query tokens then cross-attend to the contextualized observation tokens. This design ensures that each output vertex individually "reads" from the aggregated observation information, maintaining sensitivity to local touch placement.
4. **Decoder MLP:** Finally, a decoder MLP maps each attended query token to its predicted 3D position, forming the reconstructed full mesh $\hat{X}$.
5. **Topology-Agnostic Mechanism:** The mechanism for adapting to different object topologies is solely through setting the dimensions of the query grid (e.g., $(H, W)$ for cloth, $(H, 1)$ for rope, or $(D, H, W)$ for a volumetric soft body). This allows a single implementation to function across 1D, 2D, and 3D deformable objects. The network is also permutation-invariant, accommodating any number of touches in any order.

**Belief and Learned Acquisition:**

To provide a measure of uncertainty and guide active sensing, a deep ensemble approach is utilized.

1. **Deep Ensemble for Uncertainty:** $M=5$ independently initialized estimators (instantiations of the topology-agnostic network) are trained. The ensemble mean of their reconstructions constitutes the final shape estimate, $\hat{X}$. The per-vertex variance across the ensemble's predictions serves as an epistemic uncertainty measure, indicating regions where observations provide less constraint on the shape.
2. **Expected-Error-Reduction (EER) Oracle:** An EER oracle is defined to establish an upper bound for achievable active sensing performance. For each potential next touch candidate, this oracle tentatively adds the touch, reconstructs the mesh (using ground truth), and measures the true error reduction. This oracle provides an idealized, ground-truth-aware performance metric.
3. **Learned Acquisition Policy:** A deployable agent needs to select the next touch point using only its current belief state, not ground truth. A small MLP is trained for this purpose. It takes a set of belief features for each candidate vertex: its ensemble-mean 3D position, ensemble variance, grid position, the fraction of observed vertices, and the distance to the nearest existing observation. This MLP is trained via regression against the EER oracle's scores on on-policy rollouts, incorporating $\epsilon$-greedy exploration ($\epsilon=0.4$) to prevent covariate shift during training. The learned policy thus amortizes the oracle's per-candidate lookahead into its weights, enabling efficient selection at deployment time by requiring only a single ensemble reconstruction followed by a fast scoring pass for candidates. The per-candidate feature design also allows the same acquisition policy to transfer across 1D, 2D, and 3D objects.

**Experimental Setup:**

Experiments are conducted in the MuJoCo flex simulator. Configurations include a $20 \times 20$ cloth draped over randomized obstacles (leading to self-occlusion), a 40-vertex rope settled into random curves, and a $6 \times 6 \times 6$ (216-vertex) volumetric soft body. Approximately $10^3$ configurations are generated per object type, with a held-out test split. Five-member ensembles are trained (2500 steps, Adam optimizer, observation noise $\sigma=0.02$). Results are reported as mean $\pm$ std over multiple seeds.

**Baselines:**

- **Estimator Baselines:** Inverse-Distance Weighting (IDW), Gaussian-Process Implicit Surface (GPIS) reconstruction, and a global-pool (DeepSets-style) set encoder are used for comparison.
- **Active Sensing Baselines:** Random touching, a GP uncertainty baseline, and an ensemble-variance proxy are compared against the learned acquisition policy and the EER oracle.
- **Metrics:** Error reduction percentage ($100 (E_{ref} - E) / E_{ref}$) is reported against appropriate references (random for active sensing, named baseline for estimation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding both the topology-agnostic mesh estimator and the learned active sensing policy.

**Estimator Performance:**

1. **Significant Error Reduction:** The learned topology-agnostic estimator substantially reduces reconstruction error compared to non-learned geometric baselines. On average, it cuts error by approximately two-thirds.
 - For cloth, the estimator achieves error reductions over GPIS ranging from +66.6% (highest budget) to +74.8% (low budget), and over IDW from +65.1% (mid budget) to +66.5% (low budget).
 - For rope, reductions over GPIS range from +68.7% (highest budget) to +77.9% (low budget), and over IDW from +45.3% (highest budget) to +66.6% (low budget).
2. **Superiority to GPIS:** The margin of improvement over the Gaussian-Process Implicit Surface (GPIS) baseline is comparable to that over IDW. This suggests that GPIS, while a learned method, tends to over-smooth sharp features like folds in crumpled meshes, a limitation that the cross-attention architecture effectively addresses.
3. **Importance of Cross-Attention:** An ablation study comparing the cross-attention network against a simpler global-pool (DeepSets-style) encoder shows that cross-attention consistently outperforms the global-pool encoder. The performance gap increases with the number of touches (from +4.7% to +22.4% on cloth as touches accumulate). This indicates that the locality-preserving nature of cross-attention, which allows individual output vertices to read observations separately, is crucial for reconstruction quality, not merely the use of a learned set encoder.
4. **Topology-Agnosticism Validated:** The same architecture, with only its query grid dimensions adjusted, successfully reconstructs volumetric objects. For a 3D soft body, the estimator reduces error over GPIS by +71% to +80%, a margin consistent with the performance on 1D rope and 2D cloth. This confirms the method's ability to generalize across different object topologies from a single implementation.

**Active Sensing Performance:**

1. **Modest Average Gain:** The learned active sensing policy provides a real but modest average gain in error reduction compared to random touching.
 - On cloth, the learned policy reduces error over random by +5.6% to +7.0% across various touch budgets (K=10 to K=30).
 - On rope and the 3D soft body, the learned acquisition shows similar behavior, achieving +17% to +18% error reduction over random at sparse budgets.
2. **Outperformance of Baselines at Sparse Budgets:** The learned policy outperforms the GP uncertainty baseline at sparse budgets. The GP baseline only catches up at the largest budget, where comprehensive coverage might become more important than precise selection.
3. **Degradation of Variance Proxy:** A simple ensemble-variance proxy, which aims to select touches in areas of high uncertainty, degrades below random touching as the budget increases. This is because maximizing variance can lead to repeatedly touching persistently uncertain outliers rather than truly informative locations that reduce overall uncertainty effectively.
4. **Significant Headroom:** The Expected-Error-Reduction (EER) oracle reveals substantial headroom for active sensing, demonstrating error reductions of +14% to +21% on cloth. This indicates that the gap between deployable and ideal (ground-truth-aware) selection strategies, rather than an inherent lack of potential improvement, is the primary factor limiting the observed gains.

**Contextual Benefits of Active Sensing:**

1. **Increased Benefit with Occlusion:** The advantage of active sensing intensifies under conditions of self-occlusion. When cloth and rope configurations are stratified by crumple severity, the learned policy's error reduction rises from +6.0% on the least-occluded third of cloth configurations to +11.8% on the most-occluded third. Similarly, the oracle's headroom also increases (from +17.8% to +22.8%), indicating that active selection is most impactful when the object's hidden state is highly ambiguous.
2. **Diminished Benefit with Vision:** When a noisy depth camera is available in conjunction with a learned shape prior, the benefits of active touch largely diminish. In this visuo-tactile setting, active touch lowers occluded-region error by only about 4% to 6%, and the choice of where to touch barely affects the outcome; random and oracle placement perform almost identically. This implies that the camera and prior information already constrain most of the hidden state, leaving minimal additional information to be gained from strategic tactile exploration. This finding motivates the primary focus on the vision-free setting in this research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic perception and manipulation by addressing the challenging problem of deformable object state estimation in environments where traditional vision-based methods are ineffective.

1. **Enabling Perception in Vision-Deprived Settings:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable approach for reconstructing the full mesh of deformable objects from sparse tactile input alone. This is particularly impactful for scenarios such as robots operating in darkness, reaching into opaque containers (e.g., bags, drawers), manipulating objects that are heavily self-occluded, or when the manipulating gripper itself occludes the object from external cameras. This capability broadens the range of environments and tasks where robots can effectively interact with deformable materials.
2. **Generalization Across Deformable Topologies:** The development of a single, topology-agnostic estimator capable of handling 1D ropes, 2D cloths, and 3D volumetric soft bodies using a unified architecture is a notable advancement. This generality simplifies model design and deployment, as a single learned model can be applied across diverse deformable object types without requiring specialized architectures for each.
3. **Advancement in Deformable Mesh Reconstruction:** The learned cross-attention estimator achieves substantial accuracy improvements (roughly two-thirds error reduction) over non-learned geometric baselines. This demonstrates that deep learning, particularly attention mechanisms, can effectively extract meaningful global shape information from highly localized and sparse tactile data, outperforming methods that struggle with the intricate features of deformable objects (e.g., sharp folds).
4. **Insights into Active Sensing Efficacy:** The research provides critical insights into when and where active tactile sensing yields benefits. It establishes that while active sensing offers real, albeit modest, average gains, its impact is significantly amplified in scenarios characterized by high self-occlusion and the absence of visual information. Conversely, the finding that active touch offers minimal advantage when vision is available suggests that in vision-rich environments, the estimator's quality might be more critical than the precision of touch placement. This guides future research efforts to focus active sensing development on the most impactful contexts.
5. **Foundation for Downstream Tasks:** Accurate full-mesh estimates are crucial inputs for deformable object planners and controllers. By providing a robust method for state estimation, this work lays a foundation for more sophisticated and autonomous deformable object manipulation tasks in challenging environments.
6. **Feasibility for Hardware Implementation:** The position-only touch model assumed in the study aligns with standard robot arm capabilities (descend-until-contact with forward kinematics), implying that the methodology does not require specialized, complex tactile skins for hardware validation, potentially easing its transition to real-world robotic systems.

Future work includes closing the gap between the oracle and deployable active sensing policies, especially under realistic probing constraints, and validating the approach on real robotic hardware to confirm its applicability outside of simulation.
