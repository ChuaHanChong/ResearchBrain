# Learning to Reason as Action Abstractions with Scalable Mid-Training RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Apple and Northwestern University introduce Reasoning as Action Abstractions (RA3), a mid-training method that teaches large language models to discover and utilize high-level "action abstractions" for reasoning, leading to improved generalization and accelerated reinforcement learning (RL) convergence in code generation tasks. RA3 achieves an average 4-point accuracy improvement over standard next-token prediction during mid-training and demonstrates faster and higher asymptotic performance in subsequent RL fine-tuning on complex coding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Mid-training (continued pre-training on expert data) is a crucial but "poorly understood" phase in LLM development, lacking principled design and theoretical guarantees for improving downstream RL performance.
- Applying RL to LLMs is inefficient due to vast action spaces (vocabulary), long planning horizons for complex tasks, and the inefficiency of exploration in such large spaces.
- Existing mid-training algorithms often rely on heuristic metrics, failing to provide guarantees for subsequent RL improvements or efficient learning of complex reasoning.

## Method

- The work formalizes mid-training as an ==action space optimization== problem, aiming to identify an action subspace that minimizes both pruning approximation error and post-training RL error.
- It proposes **RA3** (==Reasoning as Action Abstractions==), a scalable ==self-supervised RL== algorithm that extracts temporally consistent latent reasoning structures (==action abstractions==) directly from raw expert demonstrations.
- **RA3** uses an ==EM-style optimization== to iteratively discover latents and fine-tune the LLM, incorporating a ==KL penalty== mechanism to control the frequency of "thinking" (generating new rationales) for scalability.

## Results

- RA3 consistently improves generalization during mid-training, achieving an average **4-point** accuracy improvement over standard Next-Token Prediction and **8 points** over base models on benchmarks like HumanEval and MBPP.
- Models mid-trained with RA3 exhibit faster convergence and achieve higher asymptotic performance during subsequent Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) on challenging code generation tasks (HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, Codeforces).
- An ablation study confirms the KL penalty effectively regulates reasoning frequency, allowing RA3 to produce concise, optimal rationales, leading to the best performance and demonstrating the scalability mechanism's effectiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mid-training is most effective when it learns and operates on "action abstractions" rather than primitive actions, as these compact the decision space and shorten the effective planning horizon, thus accelerating RL convergence.
> - The theoretical analysis shows that effective action space pruning requires minimizing a tradeoff between approximation error and post-training RL error.
> - A self-supervised RL approach can automatically discover meaningful, transferable high-level reasoning steps (e.g., "BFS" in code) from raw expert demonstrations without explicit labels, enhancing the LLM's policy prior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: large language models (LLMs), reinforcement learning (RL), and hierarchical abstraction. It addresses a critical, yet "poorly understood," aspect of modern LLM training: the role and design of the "mid-training" stage.

Historically, LLMs have progressed from large-scale pre-training (e.g., next-token prediction on vast text corpora) to supervised fine-tuning and, more recently, to reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or verifiable rewards (RLVR) for aligning models with complex objectives. The paper notes the "remarkable success" of RL in LLM applications like preference optimization, mathematics, code generation, and agentic tasks. However, it identifies a crucial intermediate step, "mid-training" (or continued pre-training on expert data), as a "central factor" behind these successes. Despite its widespread use, the principled design and theoretical understanding of mid-training's impact on subsequent RL performance remain a significant gap. Existing mid-training algorithms often rely on heuristic metrics, lacking guarantees for downstream improvements.

The paper steps into this gap by proposing that effective mid-training should move beyond mere next-token prediction on primitive actions. Instead, it advocates for learning "action abstractions" or "temporally extended actions." This concept draws heavily from the long-standing field of hierarchical reinforcement learning and Markov Options, where agents learn high-level skills or policies that operate over extended periods, rather than making decisions at every primitive time step. This approach is motivated by the inherent challenges of applying RL to LLMs: vast action spaces (the entire vocabulary), long effective planning horizons for complex tasks (like generating multi-line code or multi-step reasoning), and the inefficiency of exploring such large spaces. Prior work has also observed benefits from learning action hierarchies and using abstraction-based reasoning, especially in code generation and agentic tasks, but often relies on synthetic reasoning distilled from frontier LLMs, which introduces distributional shift and high costs.

By formalizing the benefits of action space pruning and accelerated RL convergence through temporal abstractions, this work provides a theoretical grounding for a more principled mid-training approach. The proposed algorithm, Reasoning as Action Abstractions (RA3), leverages self-supervised RL to discover these latent, temporally consistent "reasoning" steps directly from expert demonstrations, without requiring costly manual labeling or distillation from larger models. This positions RA3 as an innovative and scalable method to strengthen LLM policy priors, enabling more efficient and effective post-training RL, thereby contributing to the development of more capable and autonomous AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations driving this research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Theoretical Gap in Mid-Training:** The primary motivation stems from the observation that while mid-training is widely used and demonstrably effective for strengthening policy priors in LLMs, its precise role and theoretical underpinnings for shaping post-training RL are "poorly understood." The authors aim to provide the "first theoretical analysis" to formalize how mid-training design choices impact downstream RL.

2. **Minimizing RL Errors through Action Space Optimization:** A key objective is to demonstrate that an ideal mid-training algorithm should identify an optimal "action subspace." This subspace should simultaneously minimize two types of errors:
 - **Pruning Approximation Error:** The error induced by reducing the vast original action space to a more compact, useful subset.
 - **Post-Training RL Error:** The error incurred during subsequent planning and optimization within this pruned, more tractable space.

3. **Enhancing RL Efficiency through Action Abstractions:** Motivated by the theoretical insights, a major objective is to establish that mid-training is most effective when it operates in the space of "action abstractions" rather than primitive actions. The hypothesis is that learning high-level, transferable "skills" (temporal abstractions) leads to:
 - A more compact decision space, making pruning more efficient.
 - A reduced effective planning horizon, accelerating RL convergence.

4. **Developing a Scalable and Principled Mid-Training Algorithm:** Based on the theoretical findings, the authors aim to design and implement a novel, scalable mid-training algorithm called Reasoning as Action Abstractions (RA3). This algorithm should be capable of:
 - Automatically extracting temporally consistent latent reasoning structures (action abstractions) from raw expert demonstrations.
 - Leveraging these learned abstractions to improve LLM performance and generalizability during mid-training.
 - Accelerating the convergence and improving the asymptotic performance of subsequent RL fine-tuning.

5. **Validation on Complex Reasoning Tasks:** The ultimate motivation is to empirically demonstrate the effectiveness of RA3 on challenging, multi-step reasoning domains, specifically code generation tasks, using various base LLMs and benchmarking its performance against standard mid-training approaches (like next-token prediction).

In essence, the research is driven by a desire to transform mid-training from a heuristic-driven process into a theoretically grounded, efficient, and powerful stage for building more capable and adaptable LLMs by explicitly learning and utilizing hierarchical reasoning structures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper combines rigorous theoretical analysis with a novel algorithmic design and comprehensive empirical validation.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework: How Mid-Training Shapes Post-Training RL

The authors begin by formalizing the role of mid-training through a regret decomposition (Lemma 3.2). They assert that mid-training should aim to identify an action subspace, $\hat{Z}$, that minimizes two error sources:
1. **Action-set pruning error:** The approximation error resulting from restricting the full action space $Z$ to $\hat{Z}$.
2. **Post-training RL error:** The error incurred during subsequent RL planning within $\hat{Z}$.

Corresponding to these errors, two key factors determining mid-training effectiveness are identified:

- **Pruning Efficiency (Theorem 3.4):** This factor governs the quality of the initial RL policy prior. The theory shows that the number of expert samples required to prune suboptimal actions decreases as the size of the *minimal $\epsilon$-optimal action subset* ($|Z_\epsilon|$) and the overall action space ($|Z|$) shrink. This highlights the benefit of a compact decision space, motivating the use of action abstractions.
- **Impact on RL Convergence (Theorem 3.5):** This factor dictates how efficiently the policy can be improved through online interactions. The analysis, based on policy iteration, reveals that using temporally extended actions (where each abstraction spans $\tau$ primitive steps) effectively reduces the discount factor $\gamma$ to $\gamma^\tau$. This shortens the "effective planning horizon" and accelerates RL convergence.

Together, these theoretical results advocate for operating in a space of "action abstractions" rather than primitive actions.

#### 3.2. Algorithmic Approach: Reasoning as Action Abstractions (RA3)

Building on the theoretical insights, the authors propose **Reasoning as Action Abstractions (RA3)**, a scalable mid-training algorithm.

- **Temporal Variational Bound (Theorem 4.1):** The core of RA3 is a novel sequential Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) derived for the Next-Token Prediction (NTP) objective. This ELBO introduces a sequence of latent variables $z_{0:T}$ to model hidden thoughts or intentions behind the observed primitive actions $a_{0:T}$. Maximizing this ELBO amounts to estimating latent trajectories that rationalize expert behavior.

- **Expectation-Maximization (EM) Style Optimization:** The ELBO is optimized iteratively using an EM-like procedure:
 - **E-step (Self-Supervised RL):** Given the current language model $\pi^i$, a variational posterior $q$ is updated. This is framed as a T-horizon RL problem where the "reward" at each step is the log-likelihood of the observed expert actions, $log \pi^i(a_t|s_t, z_t)$, augmented with a KL penalty. This step aims to discover latent sequences that "explain" the expert behavior.
 - **M-step (Supervised Fine-tuning):** Given the updated $q^i$, the language model $\pi$ is fine-tuned to $\pi^{i+1}$ via imitation learning on expert trajectories that have been "bootstrapped" with the inferred latents $z_t$ sampled from $q^i$.

- **Temporally-Consistent Latents as Action Abstractions:** To ensure the latents represent temporally extended abstractions, a specific prior distribution for $z_t$ is designed (Equation 4.3). This prior assigns a high probability (controlled by hyperparameter $\alpha$) to reusing the previous latent $z_{t-1}$ (enforcing temporal consistency) and a uniform distribution to sampling new latents (promoting diversity).

- **Scalability Mechanism:** To address the computational cost of full RL rollouts for mid-training-sized data (billions of tokens), RA3 introduces two types of latents:
 - `<act>`: Denotes temporal consistency, meaning the current action shares the same high-level intention as the previous one, and no new rationale is sampled.
 - `<think>`: Initiates a new rationale, requiring a full rollout.
 - **KL Penalty Regulation (Proposition 5.1):** The KL divergence term in the ELBO naturally decomposes into a Bernoulli KL regularizer and an entropy term. This allows the algorithm to control the frequency of "thinking" (generating new rationales) by assigning a penalty (hyperparameter `c`) to $z_t \neq <act>$. This reward shaping mechanism discourages unnecessary reasoning, making RA3 computationally efficient and scalable. If `c` is too high, RA3 degenerates to NTP.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Domain:** Python code generation.
- **Primitive Actions:** Single lines of code.
- **Latent Mapping:** `<act>` is mapped to a newline character (`\n`), and `<think>` is mapped to a comment line (`\n#`), enforcing correct syntax through a "format reward."
- **Base Models:** Qwen-2.5-1.5B, Llama-3.2-1B, and Llama-3.1-8B.
- **Mid-Training Data:** 3.5M code snippets (1B tokens) from a Python subset of a high-quality continued pre-training corpus.
- **RL Step:** T-horizon objective replaced with a 5-step truncated return. Asynchronous rollout with SGLang engine used for efficiency. Policy gradient for optimization.
- **Post-Training RLVR:** GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm used with the AReaL-boba-2-RL-Code dataset.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Cross-entropy loss during mid-training, pass@k on HumanEval, MBPP, HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, and Codeforces for mid-training and RLVR performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and scalability of RA3 across various metrics and stages of LLM training.

#### 4.1. Mid-Training Results

- **Efficient Latent Discovery:** The RL training reward curve shows that the model quickly learns to maximize rewards in the E-step, efficiently discovering latent reasoning structures.
- **Meaningful Abstractions:** Examples illustrate that RA3 successfully extracts high-level, transferable skills (e.g., "dummy head creation," "BFS") from raw expert code, enriching the training data with implicit reasoning.
- **Reduced Cross-Entropy Loss:** Fine-tuning on reasoning-bootstrapped data (M-step) significantly reduces cross-entropy loss and accelerates learning compared to standard Next-Token Prediction (NTP), confirming that learning from these enhanced trajectories is more efficient.
- **Improved Generalization (Pass@k):** RA3 consistently outperforms NTP and the base models across HumanEval, MBPP, HumanEval+, and MBPP+ benchmarks. On average, RA3 achieves a 4-point improvement over NTP and an 8-point improvement over the base models in mean accuracy, underscoring the generalization benefits of learning temporal action abstractions. This improvement is observed across all tested model sizes (1B to 8B).

#### 4.2. Post-Training RLVR Results

- **Enhanced RLVR Performance:** Both NTP and RA3 mid-training lead to significant improvements in RLVR performance compared to the base models.
- **Faster Convergence and Higher Asymptotic Performance:** Crucially, RA3-trained models demonstrate faster convergence and achieve higher asymptotic performance during subsequent RLVR (using GRPO) on complex benchmarks like HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, and Codeforces. This empirically validates the theoretical prediction that temporal abstractions accelerate RL convergence and lead to stronger final policies.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on KL Penalty (c)

- **Regulation of Reasoning Frequency:** The ablation study confirms that the KL penalty `c` effectively controls the frequency of reasoning (when a `<think>` token is generated):
 - Low `c` (e.g., 0.01, 0.03): Leads to redundant `z` generation at nearly every timestep, offering no significant advantage over primitive-action NTP, as neither the decision space nor the effective planning horizon is effectively reduced.
 - High `c` (e.g., 0.2): Forces the policy to output only `<act>` tokens, degenerating RA3 into NTP, as new rationales are too heavily penalized.
 - Optimal `c` (e.g., 0.05): Strikes a balance, triggering reasoning for fewer than 40% of lines, producing short, effective rationales, and achieving the best mean accuracy. This demonstrates the scalability mechanism's effectiveness in controlling computational overhead while maintaining performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the development of advanced large language models and AI agents:

1. **Foundational Theoretical Understanding:** The paper provides the first formal analysis of how mid-training shapes post-training RL, offering a principled framework to understand the interplay between action space pruning and RL convergence. This theoretical grounding moves mid-training from a heuristic-driven practice to a well-understood component of the LLM training pipeline. The insights into how a compact decision space and a shorter effective planning horizon enhance learning are crucial for designing future training paradigms.

2. **Innovative and Scalable Algorithm (RA3):** RA3 offers a novel, theoretically-grounded algorithm for learning hierarchical action abstractions in LLMs. Unlike previous approaches that might rely on costly human labeling or distillation from larger models, RA3 autonomously discovers these reasoning structures from raw expert demonstrations using self-supervised RL. Its built-in scalability mechanism, which regulates reasoning frequency via a KL penalty, is critical for applying this method to the massive datasets typical of mid-training.

3. **Enhanced LLM Capabilities and Efficiency:** By learning and leveraging action abstractions, RA3 significantly improves LLM performance in complex, multi-step reasoning tasks like code generation. The demonstrated improvements in generalization (pass@k scores) and the ability to accelerate RL convergence and achieve higher asymptotic performance during post-training RLVR indicate that RA3 produces stronger, more capable policy priors. This translates to LLMs that are not only better at generating correct code but also learn more efficiently from subsequent interactive feedback.

4. **Broader Applicability to Agentic AI:** While validated on code generation, the core principles of RA3 – learning temporally extended action abstractions – are highly relevant to other domains requiring multi-step reasoning and planning, such as agentic tasks, mathematics, and complex decision-making. This work lays a foundation for building more intelligent and autonomous AI agents that can reason at a higher level of abstraction, making them more adaptable and efficient in diverse environments.

5. **Reduced Training Costs and Complexity:** The ability of RA3 to learn reasoning without explicit labels or expensive distillation steps, combined with its mechanism to control computational cost by regulating reasoning frequency, can lead to more efficient and less resource-intensive LLM development cycles. This makes advanced training techniques more accessible and scalable.

In summary, this research advances both the theoretical understanding and practical methodology of LLM training, particularly in the crucial mid-training phase. By championing the learning of action abstractions, it paves the way for LLMs that possess a deeper, more structured reasoning capability, leading to more robust, efficient, and ultimately, more intelligent AI systems.
