# Agentic World Modeling: Foundations, Capabilities, Laws, and Beyond

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a "levels × laws" taxonomy for agentic world modeling, categorizing capabilities into Predictor, Simulator, and Evolver levels and world types into physical, digital, social, and scientific regimes. The framework unifies disparate research efforts, providing criteria for decision-usable simulation and autonomous model revision, and highlights the shift from passive prediction to active, adaptable environmental understanding for AI agents.

## Problem

- Conceptual fragmentation of "world model" across diverse AI fields, leading to inconsistent definitions, evaluation practices, and challenges in comparing progress.
- Existing surveys on world models typically lack a coherent progression of capabilities across modalities and domains or a precise framework to analyze large generative models as simulators.
- The role of world modeling in emerging agentic AI applications (e.g., web agents, multi-agent systems) is often underrepresented within unified frameworks.

## Method

- Introduces a comprehensive ==levels × laws taxonomy== organizing world modeling into three levels (==L1 Predictor==, ==L2 Simulator==, ==L3 Evolver==) and four governing-law regimes (physical, digital, social, scientific).
- Defines clear, testable boundary conditions for elevating capabilities, such as ==long-horizon coherence== and ==intervention sensitivity== for L2, and ==evidence-grounded diagnosis== for L3.
- Proposes a shift towards ==decision-centric evaluation metrics== (Action Success Rate, Counterfactual Outcome Deviation) and standardized evaluation protocols.

## Results

- The proposed taxonomy unifies world modeling literature, synthesizing methodologies and challenges across **>100 representative systems**.
- Analysis revealed that **many current systems operate as L1 Predictors**, effective for local dynamics but lacking long-horizon coherence or intervention sensitivity for robust L2 simulation.
- The **L3 Evolver** concept, exemplified by autonomous laboratories in the scientific domain, formalizes the critical role of continuous, evidence-driven model adaptation for self-improving AI agents.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World modeling capabilities can be categorized into a progressive hierarchy: L1 for local prediction, L2 for decision-usable multi-step simulation, and L3 for evidence-driven model revision.
> - The nature of "governing laws" in physical, digital, social, and scientific environments dictates distinct constraints and failure modes that world models must accommodate.
> - Advanced agentic AI requires world models capable not just of prediction, but also reliable counterfactual reasoning, adherence to environmental constraints, and autonomous self-revision based on new evidence.
