# On the Sample Efficiency of Inverse Dynamics Models for Semi-Supervised Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research unifies existing Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) approaches and explains their superior sample efficiency over Behavior Cloning (BC) in semi-supervised imitation learning. It demonstrates that IDMs are often inherently simpler and less stochastic than expert policies, leading to improved performance with limited labeled data across diverse robotic and game environments.

## Problem

- Expert demonstrations for imitation learning are expensive and scarce, especially for complex robotic tasks, limiting the scalability of methods like Behavior Cloning (BC).
- Despite empirical success of Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) in semi-supervised imitation learning, a clear theoretical and empirical explanation for their superior sample efficiency compared to BC was lacking.
- Existing IDM-based methodologies (e.g., VM-IDM, IDM labeling) were not formally unified, leading to a fragmented understanding of their underlying mechanisms.

## Method

- The authors formally unified ==VM-IDM== and ==IDM labeling==, demonstrating their convergence to a consistent ==IDM-based policy== under ideal conditions.
- They theoretically and empirically investigated two core hypotheses for IDM's sample efficiency: ==reduced complexity== of the ground-truth IDM and ==reduced stochasticity== compared to the expert policy.
- They proposed **LAPO+**, an improved ==latent action policy learning== algorithm, by integrating IDM-based decoding to leverage identified statistical advantages in latent representations.

## Results

- IDM labeling consistently outperformed Behavior Cloning (BC) in **9 out of 16** ProcGen environments, showing orders of magnitude improvement in low-data regimes.
- The proposed LAPO+ algorithm achieved significantly better performance than both BC and standard LAPO in low-data settings across ProcGen tasks.
- On robot manipulation benchmarks (Push-T, Libero10), IDM-based policies (VM-IDM and IDM labeling) generally surpassed BC, and VM-IDM sampling within the UVA architecture performed on par with or better than standard policy sampling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Under ideal conditions, VM-IDM and IDM labeling learn the same consistent "IDM-based policy," offering a unified conceptual framework for these methods.
> - The ground-truth Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) is often fundamentally simpler (lower complexity) and less stochastic than the expert policy, making it easier to learn from limited labeled data.
> - Observing the next state (s') provides critical information that can significantly reduce the inherent uncertainty (stochasticity) of the action, thereby simplifying the learning task for the IDM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated at the intersection of imitation learning, semi-supervised learning, and robotics. It addresses a core challenge in learning intelligent behaviors: the scarcity and high cost of collecting action-labeled expert demonstrations, especially in complex domains like robotics.

**Behavior Cloning (BC)**, a foundational technique where a policy is learned directly from state-action pairs, serves as the primary baseline. While capable, its reliance on extensive labeled data makes it impractical for scaling to generalist policies or complex real-world scenarios.

The growing availability of **action-free data**, such often in the form of videos (e.g., from YouTube, dashcams, or simulation recordings), has spurred interest in **Semi-Supervised Imitation Learning (SSIL)**. SSIL methods aim to leverage abundant unlabeled data alongside a small set of labeled demonstrations to learn robust policies.

Within SSIL, **Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs)** have emerged as a powerful tool. An IDM predicts the action taken given the current and next states (`a | s, s'`). Existing IDM-based SSIL approaches include:
1. **IDM Labeling:** Training an IDM on the small labeled dataset, then using it to label the large unlabeled dataset (`s, s' -> a`) for subsequent BC training.
2. **VM-IDM (Video Model - IDM):** Coupling an IDM with a video model (VM) that predicts the next state (`s' | s`). The policy then involves sampling a next state from the VM and inferring the action via the IDM.

While IDM-based methods have empirically shown superior sample efficiency compared to BC, the reasons behind this advantage have remained partially understood. Previous hypotheses ranged from the IDM being "non-causal and simpler" to analogies with model-based reinforcement learning. This paper aims to provide a more rigorous and comprehensive theoretical and empirical explanation for this observed sample efficiency.

This work directly contributes to the development of more data-efficient and scalable methods for robotic control and general agent learning, aligning with the broader trend of learning from large, diverse, and often uncurated datasets, similar to successes seen in natural language processing and computer vision with large language models and foundation models. It also connects to recent advancements in **latent action learning** and **unified video-action prediction models** (like UVA), which represent cutting-edge approaches in leveraging multimodal data for policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate and explain the empirically observed superior sample efficiency of Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) learning compared to Behavior Cloning (BC) within the context of Semi-Supervised Imitation Learning (SSIL).

The primary motivations driving this work are:

1. **Bridging the Explanatory Gap:** While IDM-based methods have consistently demonstrated better performance than BC, especially with limited labeled data, a complete theoretical and empirical understanding of *why* this happens has been lacking. Previous explanations were often partial or heuristic. The authors aim to provide a more formal and comprehensive framework.
2. **Unifying Existing Approaches:** The paper seeks to formally connect different IDM-based SSIL methodologies, specifically VM-IDM and IDM labeling, by demonstrating that they converge to the same "IDM-based policy" under ideal conditions. This unification provides a clearer conceptual foundation for the field.
3. **Identifying Core Contributing Factors:** The authors hypothesize that the IDM's advantage stems from the inherent properties of the ground-truth IDM itself, specifically its reduced complexity and stochasticity compared to the expert policy. Motivations for exploring these factors include:
 - **Complexity:** Investigating if the mapping from `(s, s')` to `a` is fundamentally simpler to learn than `s` to `a`.
 - **Stochasticity:** Examining if IDMs are less prone to the inherent randomness or multi-modality of expert policies.
4. **Guiding Future Algorithmic Design:** By uncovering the fundamental reasons for IDM's sample efficiency, the research aims to provide insights that can inform the design of more effective SSIL algorithms. This includes recognizing when and how IDM learning can be most advantageous and leveraging its statistical properties.
5. **Improving State-of-the-Art Methods:** The motivation extends to proposing concrete improvements to existing IDM-based algorithms, such as LAPO, to capitalize on the identified statistical advantages. They also aim to study how these insights apply to modern, high-capacity architectures for video and action prediction.

In essence, the paper is motivated by a desire to move beyond empirical observations to a deeper, more principled understanding of IDM-based semi-supervised learning, ultimately enabling the development of more robust and data-efficient AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a mixed-methods approach, combining theoretical derivations with extensive empirical validation across diverse environments.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Unification and Formalization

- **Connecting VM-IDM and IDM Labeling:** The authors first formally demonstrate that, in the limit of infinitely many unlabeled `(s, s')` pairs and sufficient model capacity, the policies learned by VM-IDM and IDM labeling converge to the same "IDM-based policy." This is achieved by showing that the video model (VM) converges to the ground-truth next-state distribution, and the subsequent policy learning objectives become equivalent.
- **Consistency of IDM-based Policy:** They further prove that this unified IDM-based policy is consistent, meaning it recovers the expert policy `π*` when given infinite action-labeled data, similar to Behavior Cloning.
- **Formalizing Sample Efficiency:** The core claim of IDM learning's superior sample efficiency is formalized using Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. They demonstrate that if the learned IDM is closer to the ground-truth IDM (in KL divergence) than BC's policy is to the expert policy, then the IDM-based policy will also be closer to the expert policy. A key inequality `E[D_KL(π* || π_VM-IDM)] <= E[D_KL(h* || h_IDM)]` is derived to support this, implying that efficient IDM learning directly translates to efficient policy learning.

#### 3.2. Investigating Causes for IDM Sample Efficiency (Claims 1 & 2)

The authors propose two main causes for IDM's sample efficiency:
- **Claim 1: Expert policy is more complex than IDM.**
 - **Theoretical Argument:** They argue that the ground-truth IDM `h*(a | s, s')` often resides in a lower-complexity hypothesis class than the expert policy `π*(a | s)`. This implies a better bias-variance trade-off for IDM learning. Lower complexity hypothesis classes lead to lower variance without introducing significant bias if the ground truth is contained within.
 - **Analytic Example (Mazes):** They provide an explicit mathematical construction for a simple maze environment, showing that `h*` can be expressed as a linear classifier (or a simple 1-layer CNN for image states), while `π*` cannot.
 - **Experimental Validation (Mazelab):** They conduct experiments on procedurally generated maze environments, systematically varying:
 - **Environment Complexity:** Maze size (10x10 to 50x50).
 - **Goal Complexity:** Fixed vs. varying goal locations.
 - **State Representation:** Position coordinates (S_pos) vs. image representations (S_img).
 - **Model Capacity:** Low (linear classifier/1L CNN) vs. high (MLP/5L CNN).
 They compare BC and VM-IDM (using a ground-truth VM, `v*`) to isolate the IDM learning advantage.
 - **Discussion on Generalization:** They link these findings to statistical learning theory concepts (capacity control via VC dimension, Rademacher complexity) and the "simplicity bias" of neural networks in overparameterized regimes.
- **Claim 2: Expert policy is more stochastic than IDM.**
 - **Theoretical Argument:** They argue that `h*(a | s, s')` is often less stochastic than `π*(a | s)`, i.e., `H(a | s, s') <= H(a | s)` is often a strict inequality. This means observing the next state `s'` provides crucial information that often disambiguates actions, making `h*` more deterministic.
 - **Analogy (Linear Regression):** They draw an analogy to linear regression, where estimator variance increases with residual noise, suggesting higher stochasticity negatively impacts sample efficiency.
 - **Experimental Validation (Grid Environment):** They test this claim on a simple grid environment where the expert policy's stochasticity can be controlled (e.g., varying the probability of choosing between two equally optimal actions). They compare BC and IDM labeling.

#### 3.3. Benchmarking and Algorithmic Improvements

- **ProcGen Environments:** They evaluate BC, IDM labeling, and a proposed improvement to a latent action policy learning algorithm called LAPO (Latent Action Policy Learning from Observations) across 16 Atari-like ProcGen environments.
 - **LAPO+ (Proposed Improvement):** They identify that LAPO's third stage performs BC on a latent action policy. Motivated by IDM's sample efficiency, they propose **LAPO+**, which instead decodes the latent inverse dynamics model (LIDM) into the true action space, then performs IDM labeling. This leverages pretrained latent representations more effectively.
 - **Correlation Analysis:** They analyze the performance gap between IDM labeling and BC in ProcGen in relation to environment dynamics complexity (their own classification) and the measured conditional entropies of learned policies/IDMs, linking back to Claims 1 and 2.
- **Manipulation Benchmarks (Push-T, Libero10):** They test methods on real-world robot manipulation tasks, using the **Unified Video-Action (UVA) architecture** as a testbed. They compare BC, IDM labeling, VM-IDM, and analyze different sampling strategies within the UVA framework (standard policy sampling vs. VM-IDM sampling). They pretrain all models in VM mode on unlabeled data as an initialization for SSIL.

#### 3.4. Experimental Details

- All experiments include detailed descriptions of architectures (linear classifiers, MLPs, CNNs, diffusion models), dataset construction (varying sizes of D_L and D_U), training procedures (optimizers, learning rates, schedules), and evaluation metrics (test accuracy, normalized episodic return, IoU, success rate).
- Multiple random seeds are used to ensure robustness of results.

This comprehensive methodological approach allows the authors to systematically dissect the advantages of IDM-based learning, moving from theoretical justifications to empirical validation on both toy and complex real-world benchmarks, and ultimately to proposing improved algorithms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings that both unify existing understanding and offer novel insights into the efficacy of IDM-based policies in semi-supervised imitation learning.

1. **Unification of IDM-based Policies:**
 - The paper formally demonstrates that, in the limit of infinite unlabeled data and sufficient model capacity, **VM-IDM** and **IDM labeling** methods learn the *same* policy, which they term the "IDM-based policy."
 - This IDM-based policy is shown to be consistent, meaning it recovers the expert policy `π*` when provided with infinite action-labeled data, analogous to Behavior Cloning (BC).

2. **Explanation for IDM Sample Efficiency (The Core Argument):**
 - The paper attributes the superior sample efficiency of IDM learning over BC primarily to two factors:
 - **Claim 1: Reduced Complexity of Ground-Truth IDM:**
 - **Empirical Evidence (Mazes):** Experiments on maze environments strongly support this. The ground-truth IDM `h*` could be perfectly learned by a low-capacity model (e.g., linear classifier or 1-layer CNN), while the expert policy `π*` required significantly higher capacity or more labeled data.
 - **Analytic Form:** For simple mazes, `h*` can be analytically expressed as a linear transformation of `s' - s`, confirming its structural simplicity.
 - **Generalization:** This simplicity allows IDM learning to achieve better generalization, especially in low-data regimes, by enabling a better bias-variance trade-off or leveraging the "simplicity bias" of neural networks.
 - **Claim 2: Reduced Stochasticity of Ground-Truth IDM:**
 - **Empirical Evidence (Grids):** Experiments in a grid world demonstrate that IDM-based policies are much less affected by increased stochasticity in the expert policy compared to BC. While `π*` can be highly stochastic (multiple optimal actions), `h*` often becomes deterministic upon observing `s'`, making it easier to learn.
 - **Theoretical Bound:** The inequality `H(a | s, s') <= H(a | s)` provides a formal basis, suggesting `h*` is generally less uncertain.

3. **Performance on ProcGen Environments:**
 - **IDM Labeling vs. BC:** IDM labeling consistently outperforms BC, often by orders of magnitude, in 9 out of 16 ProcGen environments, particularly in low-data regimes.
 - **Complexity & Stochasticity Correlation:** Analysis of ProcGen results (Figure 5) indicates a positive correlation between the mean return gap `r(IDM) - r(BC)` and environments classified as having "simple" dynamics (where IDM is expected to be simpler). A positive, though imperfect, correlation is also observed with the entropy gap `H(π_BC) - H(h_IDM)`, further supporting the stochasticity claim.
 - **LAPO+ Superiority:** The proposed **LAPO+** algorithm (an improved version of LAPO) significantly outperforms both BC and standard LAPO in low-data regimes across ProcGen tasks. This demonstrates that leveraging the statistical advantages of IDM learning through appropriate decoding strategies can lead to substantial algorithmic gains.

4. **Performance on Robot Manipulation Tasks:**
 - **IDM-based vs. BC:** On Push-T and Libero10 benchmarks, IDM-based policies (VM-IDM and IDM labeling) generally outperform BC, sometimes by a large margin (e.g., on Push-T, where the IDM is particularly simple).
 - **VM-IDM vs. IDM Labeling:** VM-IDM often shows a small but consistent edge over IDM labeling, which the authors speculate might be due to the generalization capabilities of the VM on finite unlabeled data.
 - **Unified Architectures (UVA):** When using the advanced UVA architecture, sampling actions as a **VM-IDM (UVA)** leads to performance equal to or better than sampling as a standard **Policy (UVA)**. This highlights that these unified architectures learn capable IDMs that can be effectively exploited.

In summary, the paper provides compelling evidence, both theoretical and empirical, that the intrinsic properties of the Inverse Dynamics Model—namely its often lower complexity and stochasticity—are key drivers behind its superior sample efficiency compared to Behavior Cloning, leading to better policy learning in semi-supervised settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the fields of imitation learning, semi-supervised learning, and robotics, with several potential impacts:

1. **Deeper Theoretical Understanding:** The most significant contribution is providing a more complete and rigorous theoretical framework for *why* IDM-based methods are more sample-efficient than Behavior Cloning. By formalizing the concepts of IDM-based policies and attributing their success to reduced complexity and stochasticity, the paper moves beyond empirical observations to offer fundamental insights. This understanding can help demystify the "black box" nature of some SSIL successes.

2. **Informed Algorithmic Design:** The identified factors (complexity and stochasticity) provide a valuable framework for designing future SSIL algorithms. Researchers can now better predict when and where IDM learning will yield significant advantages. For instance, in environments where actions are uniquely determined by `(s, s')` (low IDM stochasticity) or where the optimal policy is highly complex but the IDM is simple, methods leveraging IDMs are likely to excel. This can guide the choice of model architectures, training objectives, and regularization strategies to optimize for IDM properties.

3. **Improved Data Efficiency for Robotics:** Robotic learning often faces extreme challenges in collecting labeled data. The insights from this paper directly support the development of more data-efficient robotic policies. By reinforcing the value of IDMs, especially in conjunction with abundant unlabeled video data, the work contributes to solutions for learning complex manipulation skills or navigation behaviors with fewer human demonstrations. This can accelerate the deployment of autonomous systems in real-world scenarios.

4. **Advancements in Latent Action Learning:** The proposed **LAPO+** algorithm is a direct application of the paper's insights, demonstrating how a principled understanding of IDM properties can lead to concrete algorithmic improvements. LAPO+ significantly outperforms its predecessor in low-data regimes, showcasing a more effective way to leverage latent representations for policy learning. This can inspire further innovation in the growing area of latent action models.

5. **Effective Use of Unified Architectures:** The analysis with the UVA model highlights how modern, high-capacity architectures designed for multi-task learning (including video prediction, forward dynamics, and inverse dynamics) can be effectively leveraged through IDM-based inference. Showing that sampling as a VM-IDM can equal or surpass standard policy sampling within these unified models suggests that the learned IDM component is highly capable and can be a preferred route for action inference.

6. **Guidance for Benchmarking and Evaluation:** The paper's systematic experimental approach, including varying environment and goal complexities, and the detailed analysis of factors influencing performance, sets a high standard for evaluating SSIL methods. Future benchmarks might incorporate explicit measures of IDM and policy complexity/stochasticity to provide deeper insights into algorithmic performance.

While the discussion acknowledges limitations such as reliance on curated unlabeled datasets and potential sampling costs, the fundamental insights into IDM's sample efficiency lay a strong foundation for future work addressing these practical considerations, pushing the boundaries of what's achievable in learning from limited supervision.
