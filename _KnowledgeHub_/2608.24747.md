# SkillForge: Evolving Verifiable Skills for Reinforcement Learning Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> SkillForge is a reinforcement-learning framework that keeps a verifiable skill bank evolving alongside an LLM agent's policy. A compact skill catalog sits in the prompt, and the agent invokes skills through explicit tags, so GRPO jointly optimizes environment actions and skill-call decisions. Per-skill success tracking triggers LLM reflexion, while multi-pathway induction adds new skills, beating SkillRL by 6.3% on average across ALFWorld, WebShop and AppWorld.

## Problem

- Most RL-trained LLM agents stay episodic: each episode restarts from scratch, so effective behaviors must be rediscovered repeatedly instead of reused across tasks.
- Memory-based agents store raw trajectories that are long, redundant and noisy, so they recall past episodes rather than yielding compact, reusable decision knowledge.
- Prior skill-based RL treats the skill bank as append-only: skills are injected in bulk, usage is unobservable, credit is unattributable, and stale skills are never revised.

## Method

- **Explicit skill calling** injects a ==title-plus-intent catalog==, and the agent emits a ==skill-call tag== whose full content returns as the next observation, so ==GRPO== optimizes invocation and actions jointly.
- **Evidence-based verification** combines each skill's ==EMA success rate== and usage count into an ==underperformance score== that prioritizes skills for ==LLM reflexion==, which keeps or rewrites ==principle and applicability conditions==.
- **Multi-pathway induction** has a teacher LLM synthesize skills every five steps by ==extraction from successes==, ==refinement from failures==, or ==contrastive success-failure analysis==, then deduplicates lexically and semantically.

## Results

- With Qwen2.5-7B, SkillForge reaches **93.6** ALFWorld success and **89.8/83.0** WebShop score/success, beating plain GRPO by **16.0** and **16.9 points** respectively.
- Against the strongest skill baseline it gains **3.7** on ALFWorld and **10.3** WebShop success, while AppWorld scenario goal completion nearly triples from **5.36 to 14.3**.
- Ablations on Qwen3-4B: dropping explicit calling falls **87.9 to 77.9** on ALFWorld and **44.6 to 33.3** AppWorld TGC; multi-pathway induction and effectiveness tracking each cost several points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Making skill or tool usage an explicit token-level action turns retrieval into a learnable decision and makes per-skill credit assignment possible.
> - Append-only knowledge bases decay; cheap usage statistics plus targeted LLM review preserve quality without auditing every stored entry.
> - Evolved skills transfer across model scales: a 4B model's late-stage bank matched a 30B model's self-evolved bank, so skills encode non-model-specific knowledge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous agents based on large language models (LLMs) is an active area of research, with applications spanning web navigation, deep research, and personal assistance. Reinforcement learning (RL) is a dominant paradigm for training these agents to acquire adaptive behaviors in complex environments. However, a significant limitation of many current RL-trained agents is their episodic nature; they typically restart each task without accumulating or reusing knowledge from past interactions. This necessitates redundant learning and can hinder performance in long-horizon, open-ended tasks.

Existing approaches to address this issue typically fall into two categories:
1. **Memory-based methods:** These approaches equip agents with external memory to store past trajectories for future reference. While some advancements include compressing experience or implementing online updates, these methods primarily focus on recalling past episodes rather than abstracting reusable knowledge. Raw trajectories can be long, redundant, and noisy, making direct reuse challenging.
2. **Skill-based approaches:** A more structured alternative involves distilling experience into "skills," which are compact, reusable decision principles. Recent work, such as SKILLRL, has demonstrated the utility of extracting skills from raw trajectories and integrating them into the agent's policy prompt. These methods have shown improvements over vanilla RL and memory-based techniques.

Despite the promise of skill-based learning, current implementations, including SKILLRL, often treat the skill bank as an append-only repository. Skills are continuously added but rarely evaluated for their ongoing effectiveness or relevance. This design introduces several limitations:
- **Lack of observable usage:** Skills are often injected into prompts in bulk, making it unclear which specific skill, if any, the agent truly relies upon during decision-making.
- **Difficulty in attributing effectiveness:** Without explicit usage signals, it is challenging to determine whether a successful trajectory is a direct consequence of a particular skill or merely co-occurs with its presence.
- **Uncontrolled skill quality:** Incorrect, suboptimal, or outdated skills can persist indefinitely in the skill bank, potentially degrading the agent's overall knowledge base and performance.

SKILLFORGE aims to advance the field by addressing these limitations. It introduces a framework for continuous skill evolution that moves beyond static, append-only skill banks, enabling dynamic verification, refinement, and principled growth of skills through explicit interaction and evidence-based tracking. This work positions itself as a development towards more adaptive, robust, and efficiently learning LLM agents in dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for SKILLFORGE stems from the observed inefficiencies and limitations in current large language model (LLM) agents trained with reinforcement learning (RL). While LLMs facilitate complex decision-making, the episodic nature of most RL training means agents often fail to retain and reuse valuable knowledge across episodes. This results in repetitive learning, where agents must rediscover effective behaviors for similar problems, hindering overall learning efficiency and scalability in open-ended environments.

The authors identify specific shortcomings in existing solutions:
- **Ineffectiveness of raw memory:** Storing and retrieving raw trajectories as external memory, though a common strategy, is inefficient. Raw data is often lengthy, redundant, and noisy, making it difficult to extract actionable, generalizable insights. Even compressed memory often focuses on recall rather than distillation of reusable knowledge.
- **Limitations of existing skill-based methods:** While skill-based approaches represent a step forward by abstracting experience into compact decision principles, current frameworks, exemplified by SKILLRL, exhibit critical flaws in how skills are managed:
 - **Append-only skill banks:** Skills are added without a mechanism for review or removal, leading to an accumulation of potentially irrelevant, redundant, or even detrimental knowledge.
 - **Implicit skill usage:** Skills are typically injected into the agent's prompt as context. This implicit injection makes it impossible to definitively know if the agent actually utilized a specific skill, or if its actions were guided by other prompt elements or inherent model capabilities.
 - **Lack of accountability for skill performance:** Without explicit usage and attribution, it is difficult to assess the effectiveness of individual skills. Consequently, there is no direct signal to identify which skills are genuinely beneficial and which might be leading to failures or inefficiencies.
 - **Absence of quality control:** The inability to verify and refine skills means that incorrect or outdated principles can persist within the knowledge base, gradually polluting it and undermining the agent's performance.

To address these limitations, SKILLFORGE sets forth the following key objectives:
1. **Explicit Skill Invocation:** To make skill usage directly observable and attributable, the framework aims to allow agents to explicitly "call" skills during interaction, treating these invocations as discrete actions. This enables the RL algorithm to optimize not only environmental actions but also the decisions about *when* and *which* skills to invoke.
2. **Evidence-Based Skill Verification:** To ensure the quality and relevance of the skill bank, the framework aims to track per-skill effectiveness metrics (e.g., success rate, usage count) based on explicit invocations. These metrics will form the "evidence" for identifying underperforming skills that require attention.
3. **Continuous Skill Refinement and Growth:** SKILLFORGE aims to incorporate a mechanism for LLM-based "reflexion" to review and revise underperforming skills. Simultaneously, it seeks to implement a multi-pathway skill induction process that synthesizes new skills from successful trajectories, failed attempts, and contrastive analyses, allowing the skill bank to grow dynamically while maintaining its quality.
4. **Improved Generalization and Efficiency:** Ultimately, the overarching objective is to train stronger, more adaptive LLM agents that can effectively accumulate, reuse, and refine knowledge across diverse and challenging tasks, thereby enhancing their generalization capabilities and learning efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SKILLFORGE is presented as a continuous skill evolution framework designed to maintain a verifiable skill bank alongside the agent’s policy during reinforcement learning (RL) training. The methodology is structured into three main stages: Skill Representation & Retrieval, Skill Calling and Policy Optimization, and Skill Evolution.

#### 3.1. Skill Representation and Retrieval

- **Problem Setup:** The framework considers an LLM agent with a policy $\pi_\theta$ interacting with an environment $E$ over multi-step episodes, guided by a task description $d$. The agent's objective is to maximize a binary reward $r(\tau) \in \{0, 1\}$ for a trajectory $\tau$. The agent is augmented with a skill bank $B$, composed of general skills ($B_g$) and task-specific skills ($B_k$) for each task type $k$. The joint objective is to optimize both the policy parameters $\theta$ and the skill bank $B$.
- **Skill Schema:** Each skill $s \in B$ is a structured knowledge unit comprising:
 - **Title:** A unique callable identifier.
 - **Intent:** A brief description of the skill's purpose.
 - **Principle:** The core decision strategy.
 - **Applicability:** Conditions specifying when the skill should be applied.
 - **Category:** A label indicating if the skill is `general` or `task type k`.
 - **Status:** A flag (e.g., `active`, `under revision`) for tracking.
 This design ensures skills are self-contained and allows for progressive disclosure: a compact summary in the catalog and full content upon invocation.
- **Skill Bank Initialization:** The initial skill bank ($B_0$) is created by rolling out an instruction-tuned policy and distilling collected trajectories (both successful and failed) using a teacher LLM ($M_T$), similar to the approach in SKILLRL. Skills are organized into general strategies and task-specific knowledge.
- **Retrieval and Catalog Injection:** At the start of each episode, a subset of skills is retrieved from $B_g \cup B_k$. Retrieval is based on semantic similarity, using embedding-based cosine similarity between the task description $d$ and skill intents $e_s$. The top-K most relevant skills are formatted into a compact catalog (title and one-line intent summary) and appended to the system prompt. The full skill content is only accessed upon explicit invocation.

#### 3.2. Skill Calling and Policy Optimization

- **Rollout with Explicit Skill Calling:** During interaction, the agent's output at each step consists of an environment action $a_{env_t}$ and an optional skill invocation $c_t$. The agent emits a structured `<skill_call>NAME</skill_call>` tag to invoke a skill from the retrieved catalog.
- **Skill Output and Feedback:** If a skill is invoked ($c_t \neq \emptyset$), the framework resolves the called name and provides the full structured skill content (intent, principle, applicability conditions) as part of the next observation $o_{t+1}$. This makes skill usage a discrete, traceable event within the trajectory, ensuring observability and attributability.
- **Policy Update:** Trajectories containing skill calling events are sampled. Each trajectory $\tau^{(i)}$ receives a binary outcome reward $R_i$. The policy is optimized using Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where the advantage $\hat{A}_i = (R_i - \bar{R})/\sigma_R$ is used. The objective function penalizes large policy changes from a reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$) using a KL divergence term. Since the `<skill_call>` tag is part of the generated token sequence, the RL objective jointly optimizes both environmental actions and skill invocation decisions.
- **Trajectory Abstraction:** Each rollout trajectory is summarized by an LLM into a concise abstraction, capturing key decisions, skill calls, and outcomes, while filtering out redundant information. These abstractions are partitioned into successful ($T^+$) and failed ($T^-$) sets and stored in per-task-type buffers for subsequent skill induction.

#### 3.3. Skill Evolution

The skill bank undergoes continuous evolution through two main processes: induction of new skills and verification/refinement of existing ones.

- **Multi-Pathway Skill Induction:** Every $I$ training steps, a teacher LLM ($M_T$) synthesizes new skills using the trajectory abstractions. Three pathways are utilized:
 - **Extraction:** Generalizable strategies are derived from successful trajectories ($T^+$).
 - **Refinement:** Recurring failure patterns from failed trajectories ($T^-$) are used to formulate corrective strategies.
 - **Contrastive Analysis:** Decisive behavioral differences are identified by comparing successful and failed trajectory pairs.
 The teacher LLM generates new skills ($S_{new}$) by considering $T^+$, $T^-$, the current skill bank $B$, and the induction mode ($m$). Generated skills are deduplicated (lexical and semantic) before being added to the bank.
- **Evidence-Based Skill Verification:** Explicit skill calling enables fine-grained tracking of each skill's effectiveness.
 - **Effectiveness Tracking:** For each skill $s$, an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of its success rate ($\hat{p}_s$) and its total usage count ($n_s$) are maintained.
 - **Underperformance Score:** These statistics are combined into a confidence score `conf(s) = (1 - $\hat{p}_s$) * (1 - 0.5^(n_s/h))`, where $h$ is a usage half-life. A higher score indicates stronger evidence of underperformance.
 - **LLM Reflexion:** Skills with high underperformance scores are prioritized for review by an LLM. The LLM examines the skill definition in the context of its recent usage and provides a verdict: `keep` (no change) or `revise` (rewriting its principle and/or applicability conditions). This mechanism aims to ensure the skill bank remains high-quality and free of outdated or incorrect knowledge.

#### 3.4. Training Procedure

The overall training loop (Algorithm 1) consists of alternating phases:
1. **Skill-Augmented Rollout and Policy Update:** At each step, skills are retrieved, the agent performs rollouts with explicit skill calling, and the policy parameters are updated using GRPO. Per-skill usage statistics are updated online.
2. **Skill Bank Maintenance:** Every $I$ steps, the skill bank is updated. Trajectories are abstracted, new skills are induced via multi-pathway analysis and deduplicated, and existing skills are verified through effectiveness tracking and LLM reflexion. The updated skill bank is immediately available for subsequent rollouts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SKILLFORGE across three benchmarks: ALFWorld, WebShop, and AppWorld, utilizing different LLM backbones (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Instruct, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- SKILLFORGE consistently demonstrated improved performance compared to all baseline methods, including closed-source LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro), prompt-based agents (ReAct, Reflexion, Mem0, SimpleMem), and RL-based methods (RLOO, GRPO, Mem0+GRPO, SimpleMem+GRPO).
- Using Qwen2.5-7B, SKILLFORGE achieved a 93.6% success rate on ALFWorld and an 89.8 average score with an 83.0% success rate on WebShop. These results represent improvements over GRPO by +16.0 on ALFWorld and +10.5 score (+16.9 success) on WebShop.
- On AppWorld, SKILLFORGE significantly increased Task Goal Completion (TGC) from 17.9% to 23.8% and Scenario Goal Completion (SGC) from 3.57% to 14.3%, indicating strong gains in multi-step and complex tasks.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Skill-based Methods

- When compared to SKILLRL, a closely related skill-based baseline using the same Qwen2.5-7B backbone, SKILLFORGE showed notable improvements: +3.7% on ALFWorld (93.6% vs. 89.9%), +10.3% in success rate on WebShop (83.0% vs. 72.7%), and substantial increases on AppWorld (TGC from 19.0% to 23.8%, SGC from 5.36% to 14.3%). These improvements suggest that continuous skill refinement is beneficial, particularly for more challenging, multi-step tasks.

#### 4.3. Scaling Across Models

- SKILLFORGE demonstrated benefits across various model scales. The Qwen3-4B-Instruct model, when integrated with SKILLFORGE, achieved strong performance (e.g., 87.9% on ALFWorld, 84.0% success on WebShop), which in some cases approached or exceeded the performance of the larger Qwen2.5-7B model utilizing SKILLRL. Scaling up to Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct further yielded the highest overall results (e.g., 94.3% on ALFWorld, 59.5% TGC on AppWorld).

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- An ablation study on Qwen3-4B models revealed the contributions of individual SKILLFORGE components:
 - Removing explicit skill calling or the entire skill bank led to significant performance drops (e.g., ALFWorld success rate fell from 87.9% to 77.9% and 79.3%, respectively), confirming the importance of both the skills themselves and the explicit invocation mechanism.
 - Multi-pathway skill induction was identified as a critical component, and deduplication also played a role in performance, particularly for AppWorld (TGC dropped from 44.6% to 38.7% without it).
 - Both effectiveness tracking and LLM reflexion within the skill verification process contributed to performance gains, with effectiveness tracking showing a more substantial impact. This indicates that all proposed components offer complementary benefits.

#### 4.5. Skill Bank Evolution and Distribution

- The total number of active skills in the bank was observed to grow steadily during training (e.g., from 44 to 90 on ALFWorld, and 44 to 86 on AppWorld). This growth was controlled, with general skills increasing gradually and task-specific skills expanding to address environment-specific demands. This suggests that the framework induces useful skills and revises unreliable ones.
- t-SNE visualizations of skill intent embeddings showed that newly induced skills gradually expanded into previously unexplored regions of the embedding space as training progressed, indicating increased diversity in learned strategies. This pattern supports the idea that trajectory-driven induction covers new task situations, while reflexion maintains the coherence of existing skills.

#### 4.6. Skill Transferability

- Experiments demonstrated that skills evolved by a smaller model (Qwen3-4B) could be transferred and effectively applied to a larger model (Qwen3-30B-A3B) without further training. Later-stage skills consistently outperformed earlier ones. Notably, skills evolved by Qwen3-4B at step 80 matched or exceeded the performance of Qwen3-30B-A3B's self-evolved skill bank on AppWorld (31.5% vs. 30.4% TGC), suggesting that a smaller model, with sufficient evolution, can produce competitive skills.

#### 4.7. Training Dynamics and Efficiency

- Training curves showed that SKILLFORGE initially lagged a variant without explicit skill calling due to the overhead of learning skill usage. However, it quickly surpassed this variant, achieving faster convergence and higher asymptotic performance, with the gap widening in later stages. This indicates compounding benefits from continuous skill induction and verification.
- Efficiency analysis indicated that the total skill-related computational overhead (skill retrieval, calling, and update) remained below 10% across all benchmarks. SKILLFORGE was found to be comparable or even faster in wall-clock time than the GRPO baseline on ALFWorld and WebShop, suggesting that the benefits of reduced redundant exploration and shorter rollout episodes offset the overhead of skill management.

#### 4.8. Case Study

- A case study of the "Authenticate Before Acting" skill on AppWorld illustrated its evolution. The initial skill (Step 0) provided a basic authentication strategy. Through trajectory feedback and reflexion, it was progressively refined (Step 40, Step 80) to incorporate more explicit credential verification, stronger applicability conditions, and end-to-end authentication checks. This demonstrated the framework's ability to transform simple heuristics into more robust strategies based on environment feedback.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SKILLFORGE presents a significant step towards developing more intelligent and adaptive reinforcement learning agents capable of operating effectively in complex, open-ended environments. The work directly addresses a fundamental limitation of many current RL-trained LLM agents: their inability to accumulate and reuse knowledge systematically across episodic interactions.

The potential impacts of this research are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Agent Capabilities:** By enabling continuous verification and refinement of skills, SKILLFORGE facilitates the development of agents that are not only more successful in task completion but also more robust and reliable. The explicit skill calling mechanism allows for clearer accountability and better understanding of agent decision-making, which is crucial for complex applications.
- **Improved Learning Efficiency:** The ability to distill, store, and dynamically update reusable skills reduces the need for agents to repeatedly learn similar behaviors from scratch. This can lead to more efficient learning processes and faster adaptation to new tasks or variations, which is critical for scaling RL to real-world problems.
- **Dynamic Knowledge Management:** The framework introduces a principled approach to managing an evolving knowledge base. Moving beyond append-only skill repositories, SKILLFORGE ensures that the skill bank remains high-quality, relevant, and compact by actively identifying and revising underperforming skills and deduplicating new ones. This dynamic management is essential for long-term agent development and continual learning.
- **Foundation for Lifelong Learning Agents:** The continuous skill evolution mechanism provides a concrete step towards building truly lifelong learning agents that can autonomously expand and refine their capabilities over extended periods of interaction with their environment, without suffering from knowledge decay or accumulation of irrelevant information.
- **Transferability and Generalization:** The demonstrated transferability of evolved skills across different model scales suggests the potential for developing more generalizable skill sets. This could lead to a paradigm where smaller, efficiently trained models contribute to a shared, high-quality skill bank that larger, more capable models can leverage, or even where skills learned in one domain can be adapted to others.
- **Reduced Reliance on Human Supervision:** While currently relying on a teacher LLM for skill induction and revision, the framework's mechanisms for evidence-based verification and automated refinement reduce the need for constant human intervention to curate and maintain the skill bank, moving towards more autonomous skill development.
- **Broader Applicability:** The techniques introduced in SKILLFORGE could find application beyond the evaluated benchmarks, potentially enhancing the performance of LLM agents in various domains such as robotics, game playing, complex software interaction, and scientific discovery, where cumulative knowledge and adaptive strategies are paramount.

While the framework demonstrates consistent improvements, the authors acknowledge limitations such as the reliance on an external teacher LLM for skill synthesis and revision, potential retrieval overhead with a continuously growing skill bank, and increased inference cost due to explicit skill calling. Future research could explore self-teaching mechanisms for skill evolution, strategies for hierarchical skill organization to manage scale, and optimization of prompt lengths for efficiency. Despite these limitations, SKILLFORGE establishes a robust foundation for building more capable and adaptable reinforcement learning agents.
