# ManiCM: Real-time 3D Diffusion Policy via Consistency Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiCM introduces a real-time 3D diffusion policy for robotic manipulation by adapting consistency models to generate actions in a single inference step. This system achieves a 10-fold acceleration in decision latency, operating at approximately 17 milliseconds per step, while maintaining or improving task success rates across various simulation and real-world manipulation tasks compared to iterative 3D diffusion policies.

## Problem

- Diffusion models for robotic manipulation suffer from high inference latency due to their iterative denoising process, preventing real-time closed-loop control.
- Existing acceleration techniques for diffusion policies often lack generalizability across diverse task domains.
- High-dimensional 3D sensory inputs further exacerbate the computational inefficiency of 3D diffusion policies, leading to latencies of over 170ms per step for state-of-the-art methods.

## Method

- **ManiCM** leverages ==Consistency Models (CMs)== to distill the iterative 3D diffusion process into a single-step action generation.
- It processes sparse point clouds from an RGB-D camera and robot proprioception as conditions, encoding them into a compact 3D representation via an MLP.
- The model is specifically designed to predict the clean action sample directly, rather than noise, optimizing for the low-dimensional action space of manipulation tasks and improving convergence stability.

## Results

- Achieved an average runtime of **17.3** ± **0.1** ms per step across 31 tasks, representing a 10-fold acceleration over the state-of-the-art 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) which took **177.6** ± **1.4** ms.
- Maintained competitive average success rates (**78.5** ± **4.2%**) compared to DP3 (**77.5** ± **3.8%**), with a **3.4%** absolute improvement in Metaworld Very Hard tasks.
- Demonstrated comparable real-world performance on a UR3e robot, achieving **10x** faster inference speeds (e.g., **16.8** ms for 'Transfer Piece') while maintaining success rates (**80%**) compared to DP3 (**70%** at **172.4** ms).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Consistency models can effectively accelerate 3D diffusion policies for robotic manipulation, enabling real-time operation without sacrificing performance.
> - Directly predicting the clean action sample, instead of noise, is more effective for consistency distillation in low-dimensional robotic action spaces, leading to faster convergence and higher success rates.
> - Significant acceleration (up to 10x) can be achieved by distilling the knowledge of an iterative diffusion teacher model into a single-step consistency model, outperforming simple model backbone reduction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has continuously sought to develop more capable and versatile robotic agents. Recent advancements have explored various architectures for end-to-end control, including perceptive models like convolutional networks and transformers, and generative models, particularly diffusion models. Diffusion models have garnered attention in robotics due to their capacity to model complex, high-dimensional robotic trajectories and facilitate nuanced decision-making, leveraging robot observations as conditioning inputs. They have been applied in diverse areas such as high-level procedure planning, low-level motion policies, teacher models, and data synthesis.

However, a significant challenge associated with diffusion-based policies is their computational inefficiency during inference. The iterative denoising process inherent in these models, even with sampling acceleration techniques, requires considerable time to synthesize high-quality actions. This latency becomes particularly problematic in 3D robotic manipulation tasks, where high-dimensional 3D sensory inputs (e.g., point clouds) further increase processing time. Existing state-of-the-art 3D diffusion policies can exhibit decision latencies upwards of 170 milliseconds per step, which prevents their application in real-time closed-loop control scenarios critical for dynamic physical interactions.

Previous efforts to mitigate this inefficiency have included hierarchical sampling strategies, but these approaches often lack generalizability across different task domains, as the optimal hierarchy can vary significantly with task complexity. This limitation restricts their practical utility in diverse robotic manipulation environments.

Parallel to these developments in robotics, the concept of consistency models has emerged in the field of image generation. Consistency models aim to accelerate the sampling speed of diffusion models by training them to produce high-fidelity outputs in a minimal number of inference steps, ideally one. This work bridges the gap between the established success of consistency models in vision tasks and the unaddressed real-time performance requirements of 3D diffusion policies in complex robotic manipulation. By adapting consistency model principles, the paper aims to enable real-time action generation from 3D visual conditions, thereby addressing a fundamental bottleneck in the deployment of advanced diffusion-based control policies in practical robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time 3D diffusion policy for robotic manipulation, referred to as ManiCM. This model aims to generate robot actions with minimal latency while maintaining competitive task success rates, thereby enabling the deployment of diffusion-based control in real-time closed-loop robotic systems.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges in the current landscape of robotic manipulation research:

1. **Runtime Inefficiency of Diffusion Models in Robotics:** While diffusion models have demonstrated strong performance in modeling complex, high-dimensional robotic trajectories and making nuanced decisions, their inherent iterative denoising process is computationally intensive. This leads to substantial inference latency (e.g., hundreds of milliseconds per action), making them unsuitable for real-time control where decisions must be made rapidly to interact with dynamic environments. This is particularly pronounced in 3D manipulation tasks that rely on high-dimensional visual inputs like point clouds.
2. **Limitations of Existing Acceleration Techniques:** Prior attempts to accelerate diffusion model inference, such as hierarchical sampling, often lack generality. The effectiveness of such methods can be highly dependent on specific task domains, making them less practical for diverse robotic manipulation scenarios. A more universally applicable acceleration mechanism is needed.
3. **Emergence of Consistency Models in Generative AI:** Consistency models have recently shown promise in accelerating generative processes in other domains, specifically image generation. These models enable the synthesis of high-quality outputs in a single or very few inference steps by learning a direct mapping from any point on a diffusion trajectory back to the original clean data. This advancement presents a potential solution to the runtime problem in diffusion-based robotic policies.
4. **Unexplored Potential in 3D Robotic Manipulation:** Despite the success of consistency models in image generation, their application to complex robotic manipulation tasks, particularly those involving high-dimensional 3D visual conditions, remains largely unexplored. Integrating these concepts into the robotics domain could unlock new capabilities for real-time control.
5. **Optimizing Prediction Targets for Low-Dimensional Action Spaces:** The paper is also motivated by the need to determine the most effective prediction target within the consistency model framework for robotic actions. While image generation typically predicts noise, the relatively low dimensionality of robot action spaces might benefit more from directly predicting the clean action sample, which could lead to faster convergence and reduced variance.

In summary, the research aims to address the critical gap between the powerful generative capabilities of diffusion models and the strict real-time requirements of practical robotic manipulation by introducing and adapting the concept of consistency models for 3D visuomotor policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of ManiCM involves leveraging the principles of Consistency Models (CMs) to distill the iterative denoising process of a 3D diffusion policy into a single-step inference mechanism. The overall pipeline is depicted in Figure 2.

**Problem Formulation and Consistency Model Preliminaries:**
The task is framed as an imitation learning problem where a policy $\pi$ maps observations $o$ to robot actions $a$. Observations consist of sparse point clouds from an eye-to-hand RGB-D camera and robot proprioception. Actions define changes in end-effector pose and gripper state.
The Consistency Model (CM) aims to learn a function $f(\cdot, \cdot)$ that maps any point $(x_t, t)$ on a Probability Flow Ordinary Differential Equation (PF-ODE) trajectory back to the original data distribution $x_\epsilon$. This function must satisfy the self-consistency property: $f(x_t, t) = f(x_{t'}, t')$ for any $t, t'$ on the same trajectory, and a boundary condition $f(x_\epsilon, \epsilon) = x_\epsilon$. The function $f_\theta$ is parameterized as $f_\theta(x, t) = c_{skip}(t)x + c_{out}(t)F_\theta(x, t)$, where $F_\theta$ is a deep neural network, and $c_{skip}, c_{out}$ are differentiable functions (with $c_{skip}(\epsilon)=1, c_{out}(\epsilon)=0$). CMs are typically trained using consistency distillation, minimizing a loss $L(\theta, \theta^-; \Phi) = E [d(f_\theta(x_{n+1}, t_{n+1}), f_{\theta^-}(\hat{x}_{\Phi n}, t_n))]$, where $\theta$ are parameters of the online network, $\theta^-$ are EMA parameters of a target network, and $\Phi$ is an ODE solver.

**ManiCM: Manipulation Consistency Model Design:**
1. **3D Conditional Generation:**
 - **Input Processing:** Raw depth images from a single RGB-D camera are converted into point clouds. These point clouds are then down-sampled using Farthest Point Sampling to focus on relevant regions (robot arm, targets) and encoded into a compact 3D representation via an MLP encoder.
 - **Conditioning:** This compact 3D representation, along with robot poses (proprioception), serves as conditions for the diffusion process.
 - **Diffusion Process:** The standard diffusion model iteratively denoises a noisy action $a_t$ to produce a cleaner action $a_{t-1}$.
2. **Manipulation Self-Consistency Function:**
 - To enable one-step inference, the standard diffusion process is reformulated to impose a consistency constraint. The model is trained such that the original, clean action $a_0$ can be directly recovered from any noisy action $a_t$ along the ODE trajectory using the function: $a_0 = c_{skip}(t)a_t + c_{out}(t)F_\theta(a_t, t, p, q)$. Here, $F_\theta$ is the manipulation consistency function, and $p, q$ are point cloud and robot pose conditions.
 - **Prediction Target Choice:** A critical design choice for $F_\theta$ is whether to predict the noise ($\epsilon$) or the action sample ($a_0$). The paper argues for predicting the **action sample** directly. This is motivated by the observation that in low-dimensional action spaces (approximately 28 dimensions in manipulation tasks), learning the distribution of samples is less challenging than predicting noise. Additionally, predicting noise can introduce higher variance that becomes amplified in consistency models, hindering convergence. Directly predicting the sample is hypothesized to lead to faster convergence and lower variance.

**Manipulation Consistency Distillation (MCD) for Training:**
ManiCM is trained using a consistency distillation technique, similar to that in image generation, but adapted for robotic actions:
1. **Noise Addition:** A ground-truth expert action sequence $a_0$ is subjected to a forward diffusion process for $n+k$ steps, generating a noisy action $a_{n+k}$.
2. **Teacher Network Role:** The noisy action $a_{n+k}$ is fed into a *frozen, pre-trained teacher network* (e.g., DP3, a 3D diffusion model). This teacher network decodes the action to produce a "clean" action estimate, $\hat{a}^*_0$.
3. **Target Network Input Generation:** The estimated clean action $\hat{a}^*_0$ is then processed by a *k-step ODE solver* (e.g., DDIM) to obtain $\hat{a}_{\Phi n}$, which represents a target for consistency.
4. **Online Network Prediction:** The noisy action $a_{n+k}$ is simultaneously fed into the *online network* $f_\theta$ to predict its own clean action estimate, $\hat{a}_0$.
5. **Consistency Loss:** A *target network* $f_{\theta^-}$ (whose parameters $\theta^-$ are an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the online network's parameters $\theta$) takes $\hat{a}_{\Phi n}$ as input to produce $\hat{a}^-_0$. The consistency distillation loss, $L_{MCD}(\theta, \theta^-) = E[\|f_\theta(a_{n+k}, t_{n+k}) - f_{\theta^-}(\hat{a}_{\Phi n}, t_n)\|^2_2]$, enforces that the outputs of the online and target networks are consistent. This loss encourages the online network to learn the direct mapping from any point on the trajectory to the clean action.
6. **Inference:** Once trained, ManiCM can generate high-quality robot actions in a single step by using only the online network.

**Implementation Details:**
The model uses an AdamW optimizer, cosine decay learning rate scheduler with linear warm-up. The EMA rate is 0.95. DDIM-Solver with a skipping step of k=10 is used for the ODE solver. Point clouds are downsampled to 512 points. Both point clouds and agent poses are encoded using MLP encoders.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of ManiCM across various robotic manipulation tasks.

**A. Comparisons on Runtime Efficiency:**
- **Significant Acceleration:** ManiCM (1-step inference) achieves an average runtime of 17.3 ± 0.1 ms per step across 31 tasks from Adroit and Metaworld benchmarks. This represents an approximate 10-fold acceleration compared to the state-of-the-art 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), which required 177.6 ± 1.4 ms per step.
- **Beyond Model Size Reduction:** The 4-step inference version of ManiCM also improves efficiency by approximately 3 times. Furthermore, ManiCM (1-step) demonstrates significantly faster runtime than "Simple DP3," a lightweight variant of DP3, indicating that reducing the number of inference steps via consistency distillation is more effective for speedup than merely shrinking the model backbone.
- **Competitive with Non-Diffusion Methods:** ManiCM's runtime (16.9 ms) is comparable to or faster than non-diffusion-based methods like BC-GMM+3D (15.9 ms) and BC-RNN+3D (16.6 ms), while offering superior performance in terms of success rates.

**B. Comparisons on Success Rate:**
- **Maintained or Improved Performance:** ManiCM (1-step inference) maintains competitive average success rates (78.5 ± 4.2%) compared to DP3 (77.5 ± 3.8%), and in some cases, surpasses it.
- **Enhanced Performance in Challenging Tasks:** Notably, ManiCM demonstrates a 3.4% absolute improvement over the state-of-the-art method in Metaworld Very Hard tasks.
- **Benefit of Multi-Step Inference:** Increasing ManiCM's sampling steps to 4 yields a slightly higher average success rate (79.0 ± 3.9%), suggesting a trade-off between speed and marginal performance gains.

**C. Multi-Task Performance:**
- On the RLBench multi-task simulation environment, ManiCM achieves competitive success rates compared to the 3D Diffuser Actor baseline while demonstrating a 13.3x acceleration in inference speed.

**D. Real-World Experiments:**
- **Validation in Physical Hardware:** ManiCM was evaluated on three real-world manipulation tasks (Slide Block, Stack Cube, Transfer Piece) using a UR3e robot arm with a DH Robotics AG-95 gripper and a Realsense D435i camera.
- **Consistent Real-World Performance:** In these real-world scenarios, ManiCM achieved approximately 10x faster inference speeds than DP3 while maintaining comparable success rates. For instance, in "Transfer Piece," ManiCM achieved an 80% success rate with 16.8 ms runtime, outperforming DP3's 70% success rate with 172.4 ms runtime.

**E. Ablation Studies:**
- **Sample Prediction vs. Epsilon Prediction:** Directly predicting the action sample led to significantly faster convergence and higher success rates (78.5% average) compared to predicting noise ('epsilon') (24.4% average). This confirms the hypothesis that for low-dimensional action manifolds, sample prediction is more direct and stable, especially when combined with consistency distillation, which amplifies variance issues in noise prediction.
- **Impact of Teacher Model Diffusion Steps:** The study found that task success rates do not monotonically increase with more diffusion sampling steps for either DP3 or ManiCM, suggesting redundancy in many steps. ManiCM consistently distilled competitive performance from teacher models trained with different numbers of diffusion steps (e.g., 100 or 1000 steps), while achieving substantial runtime acceleration (up to ~57x) even when the teacher used more inference steps.

**F. Qualitative Analysis:**
- Qualitative examples from Adroit and Metaworld tasks (e.g., "Adroit Pen" and "Metaworld Soccer") illustrate that ManiCM successfully generates high-quality motions that complete complex tasks in one step. In contrast, the baseline DP3 sometimes failed or produced suboptimal trajectories by merely mimicking expert demonstrations without fully understanding the task goal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the application of diffusion models to robotic manipulation by directly addressing their primary limitation: runtime inefficiency. The proposed ManiCM model introduces the concept of consistency models to 3D robotic manipulation, enabling real-time action generation.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:

- **Enabling Real-Time Deployment:** By accelerating 3D diffusion policies approximately 10-fold (from ~170ms to ~17ms per step), ManiCM makes these powerful generative models suitable for real-time closed-loop control in dynamic physical environments. This removes a critical barrier to their practical deployment in industrial, service, and research robotics.
- **Expanding Diffusion Model Utility:** The work demonstrates that consistency models, previously successful in vision domains like image generation, can be effectively adapted to complex robotic control problems involving high-dimensional 3D observations and low-dimensional action spaces. This expands the known utility and applicability of consistency distillation techniques.
- **Improved Generalizability and Robustness:** The results indicate that the acceleration achieved does not come at the cost of performance; instead, ManiCM maintains or even slightly surpasses the success rates of slower, iterative diffusion policies. This suggests that consistency distillation might also contribute to a more robust and generalized policy, potentially by avoiding overfitting to specific iterative denoising patterns.
- **Guidance for Model Design:** The ablation study on sample versus epsilon prediction provides valuable insights for future model design in robotics and potentially other low-dimensional generative tasks. The finding that directly predicting action samples is more effective for consistency distillation in manipulation tasks offers a principled guideline for parameterizing such models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies the scalability of consistency models to distill large-scale pre-trained diffusion policies as an area for future exploration. This suggests ManiCM serves as a foundational step towards integrating even more powerful, large-scale models into real-time robotic control, potentially paving the way for more general-purpose robot agents.

Overall, ManiCM contributes to making sophisticated, data-driven robot policies more accessible and practical for real-world applications by overcoming a major computational bottleneck, thus moving closer to highly autonomous and responsive robotic systems.
