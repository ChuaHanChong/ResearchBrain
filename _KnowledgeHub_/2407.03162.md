# Bunny-VisionPro: Real-Time Bimanual Dexterous Teleoperation for Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Hong Kong and the University of California, San Diego developed Bunny-VisionPro, a bimanual teleoperation system that leverages the Apple Vision Pro for intuitive control and integrates low-cost haptic feedback. The system facilitates real-time dexterous manipulation, leading to an 11% higher success rate and 45% reduction in task completion time compared to prior systems, and enabling the collection of high-quality demonstrations for imitation learning.

## Problem

- Existing teleoperation systems for dexterous robotic manipulation often struggle with real-time performance and intuitive control, particularly for bimanual tasks.
- Collecting high-quality, diverse demonstrations for imitation learning models remains challenging due to limitations in human-robot interfaces.
- Many systems lack effective haptic feedback, hindering an operator's ability to delicately manipulate objects and perceive contact.

## Method

- **Bunny-VisionPro** is a novel teleoperation system utilizing ==Apple Vision Pro== for immersive visual feedback and hand tracking, combined with low-cost haptic feedback for enhanced control.
- It employs a modular architecture, including online optimization for hand motion retargeting (handling complex loop joints) and arm motion control with integrated collision avoidance and singularity management.
- Force-Sensitive Resistors (FSRs) on robot fingertips provide tactile feedback transmitted to Eccentric Rotating Mass (ERM) motors on the operator's hands, calibrated to mitigate signal drift.

## Results

- Bunny-VisionPro achieved **11%** higher success rates and **45%** reduction in task completion time compared to previous systems (Telekinesis and AnyTeleop) on complex manipulation tasks.
- Hand retargeting and arm motion control modules operate at over 60Hz, demonstrating real-time performance.
- Imitation learning policies trained with Bunny-VisionPro demonstrations showed 80-**95%** success on seen configurations, 65-**80%** on novel object positions, and 50-**75%** on unseen objects, exhibiting a **20%** improvement in generalization over previous systems' data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modular system architecture with decoupled components for hand retargeting, arm control, and haptic feedback allows for real-time performance on standard hardware.
> - Integrating low-cost haptic feedback significantly enhances operator perception of contact forces, improving task success rates and reducing completion times.
> - High-quality bimanual teleoperation demonstrations collected with the system lead to improved generalization capabilities in imitation learning policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Dexterous robotic manipulation remains a significant challenge in robotics research, particularly when coordinating two arms for complex tasks. Teleoperation systems that allow humans to control robots remotely have emerged as a valuable approach for collecting high-quality demonstrations that can be used to train imitation learning models. However, existing systems often struggle with real-time performance, safety concerns, and providing intuitive control interfaces for complex manipulations.

Researchers from the University of Hong Kong and the University of California, San Diego have developed Bunny-VisionPro, a novel teleoperation system that addresses these challenges. The system leverages the Apple Vision Pro headset for immersive visual feedback and hand tracking, while incorporating low-cost haptic feedback to enhance operator control. As shown in Figure 2, the system creates a bidirectional interface between the human operator and bimanual dexterous robots.

### 2. System Architecture

Bunny-VisionPro adopts a modular architecture consisting of three decoupled components:

1. **Hand Motion Retargeting**: Maps human finger positions to robot hand joint configurations in real-time
2. **Arm Motion Control**: Translates operator wrist poses to robot arm movements with integrated safety features
3. **Haptic Feedback**: Provides tactile sensations to the operator based on robot fingertip pressure sensors

This modular design allows each component to run independently at different frequencies, enabling real-time performance even on standard computing hardware. The system uniquely handles bimanual coordination, which is particularly challenging due to the initial distance mismatch between human hands and robot end-effectors.

The overall teleoperation workflow involves:

1. The Vision Pro tracking the operator's hand poses and movements
2. These movements being translated to robot joint commands through optimization algorithms
3. Force-sensitive resistors (FSRs) on robot fingertips detecting contact forces
4. These forces being transmitted back to the operator through vibration motors on the fingertips

The complete system is shown in Figure 3, illustrating both the hardware components and example tasks the system can perform.

### 3. Hand Motion Retargeting

Hand motion retargeting maps human finger poses to robot joint positions, which is formulated as an online optimization problem. The system minimizes the difference between fingertip keypoint vectors of the human hand and the robot hand using Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP).

The optimization objective can be represented as:

$$\min_{\mathbf{q}_h} \sum_{i=1}^5 \|T_i(\mathbf{q}_h) - T_i^h\|_2^2$$

where $\mathbf{q}_h$ represents the robot hand joint angles, $T_i(\mathbf{q}_h)$ is the forward kinematics function for the robot's fingertip positions, and $T_i^h$ represents the human fingertip positions.

A significant contribution of Bunny-VisionPro is its novel approach to handling loop joints—mechanical structures like four-bar linkages that create closed kinematic chains—which are common in many robot hands but traditionally difficult to control in real-time. The system represents these complex joints with an equivalent serial chain and applies appropriate constraints during optimization.

### 4. Arm Motion Control

The arm motion control module computes robot joint trajectories based on wrist poses detected by the Vision Pro. The optimization objective integrates three key components:

1. **Inverse Kinematics (IK)**: Maps desired end-effector poses to joint angles
2. **Collision Avoidance**: Prevents collisions between robot arms and with the environment
3. **Singularity Management**: Avoids configurations where the robot loses degrees of freedom

The collision avoidance system represents the robot with sphere-based models as shown in Figure 4. This approximation allows for efficient computation of collision risks while maintaining accuracy.

The final optimization problem combines these objectives:

$$\min_{\mathbf{q}_a} \|J(\mathbf{q}_a)\Delta\mathbf{q}_a - \Delta\mathbf{x}\|^2 + \lambda_c C(\mathbf{q}_a) + \lambda_s S(\mathbf{q}_a)$$

where $\mathbf{q}_a$ represents arm joint angles, $J$ is the Jacobian matrix, $\Delta\mathbf{x}$ is the desired end-effector movement, $C$ is the collision cost function, $S$ is the singularity cost function, and $\lambda_c$ and $\lambda_s$ are weighting factors.

### 5. Haptic Feedback System

The haptic feedback system uses Force-Sensitive Resistors (FSRs) to measure finger pressure on the robot hand. These signals are processed to address zero-drift issues and filtered to reduce noise before being transmitted to Eccentric Rotating Mass (ERM) motors attached to the operator's fingertips.

The system employs a calibration process to handle the inevitable drift in FSR readings:

```python
def calibrate_fsr_reading(raw_reading):
    # Initialize with base reading
    if not calibrated:
        base_reading = raw_reading
        calibrated = True

    # Apply calibration
    calibrated_reading = raw_reading - base_reading

    # Apply low-pass filter to smooth signal
    filtered_reading = alpha * previous_reading + (1-alpha) * calibrated_reading
    previous_reading = filtered_reading

    return filtered_reading
```

This low-cost haptic feedback approach significantly improves the operator's ability to perceive contact forces and manipulate objects delicately.

### 6. Teleoperation Performance

The system's performance was evaluated on both standard teleoperation benchmarks and custom dexterous manipulation tasks. Bunny-VisionPro outperformed previous systems (Telekinesis and AnyTeleop) with:

- 11% higher success rate on complex manipulation tasks
- 45% reduction in task completion time
- Real-time performance with hand retargeting and arm motion control running at >60Hz

A user study was conducted to evaluate the impact of haptic feedback on teleoperation performance. As shown in Figure 6, haptic feedback generally improved success rates and reduced task completion times across different operators (A-E) and tasks.

### 7. Imitation Learning Applications

A key application of Bunny-VisionPro is collecting high-quality demonstrations for training imitation learning models. The system was used to collect demonstrations for various household tasks including grasping toys, wiping glass, cleaning pans, sweeping floors, pouring liquids, and preparing coffee.

Imitation learning policies were trained using a combination of behavior cloning and diffusion policy approaches. These policies demonstrated:

- 80-95% success rates on seen configurations of trained tasks
- 65-80% success rates when generalizing to novel object positions
- 50-75% success rates when generalizing to completely unseen objects

Figure 7 shows examples of the trained policies generalizing to both spatial variations and entirely new objects.

The policies trained on Bunny-VisionPro demonstrations showed a 20% improvement in generalization performance compared to those trained on data from previous systems, demonstrating the value of high-quality demonstrations collected with the system.

### 8. Significance and Limitations

Bunny-VisionPro represents a significant advancement in teleoperation systems for several reasons:

1. **Real-Time Bimanual Control**: Enables coordinated two-handed manipulation in real-time without requiring high-end computing hardware
2. **Safety Integration**: Incorporates collision avoidance and singularity handling directly into the control loop
3. **Accessibility**: Uses commercially available hardware (Apple Vision Pro) and low-cost haptic components
4. **Improved Imitation Learning**: Enables the collection of higher-quality demonstrations that lead to more robust learned policies

However, the system does have limitations:

1. **Hand Tracking Accuracy**: The Vision Pro's hand tracking can lose accuracy in certain lighting conditions or during rapid movements
2. **Haptic Resolution**: The current haptic system provides limited force resolution compared to more expensive alternatives
3. **Initialization Challenges**: The system requires careful initialization to match human and robot hand configurations

Future work could address these limitations through improved tracking algorithms, enhanced haptic feedback using piezoelectric actuators, and automated initialization procedures.

In conclusion, Bunny-VisionPro demonstrates how integrating modern VR technology with robotics can create more intuitive and effective teleoperation systems. By enabling operators to control complex bimanual robots in real-time with haptic feedback, the system opens new possibilities for robot learning and complex manipulation tasks.
