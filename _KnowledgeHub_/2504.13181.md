# Perception Encoder: The best visual embeddings are not at the output of the network

> [!summary] Summary
> Perception Encoder introduces a family of vision models that achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse vision and vision-language tasks, demonstrating that general, high-quality visual features can be extracted from the intermediate layers of a single, contrastively-trained network. It provides specific alignment tuning methods to make these features accessible for tasks ranging from zero-shot classification to dense spatial prediction and multimodal language understanding.

## Problem

- Computer vision has relied on specialized large-scale pretrained encoders for different tasks, leading to fragmentation and complexity.
- Combining diverse pretraining techniques (e.g., contrastive, captioning, self-supervised spatial) results in complex, difficult-to-scale multi-objective schemes.
- There is a need for a single, simple, and scalable pretraining technique that can learn state-of-the-art features for all downstream vision and vision-language tasks.

## Method

- Developed ==PE_core==, a unified image-and-video encoder, through robust, multi-stage contrastive training using a refined image-text recipe and a novel video data engine.
- Designed =="alignment tuning"== methods to extract and align potent features residing in PE_core's ==intermediate layers== for different task families.
- ==PE_lang== adapts PE_core for MLLMs by projecting from optimal intermediate layers, while ==PE_spatial== uses self-distillation and SAM mask logit distillation to enhance spatial coherence for dense prediction.

## Results

- PE_core_G achieved **86.6%** average zero-shot ImageNet robustness and **76.9%** zero-shot Kinetics-400 accuracy, outperforming specialized models in image and video classification/retrieval.
- PE_lang_G, by aligning intermediate features, individually outperformed other vision encoders for MLLMs, achieving leading results on OCR (**94.6%** DocVQA), VQA, and video understanding (**82.7%** PerceptionTest) benchmarks.
- PE_spatial_G achieved state-of-the-art performance on dense prediction tasks, including **61.5%** J&F on zero-shot tracking and a new COCO state-of-the-art of **66.0** box mAP for detection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust global vision-language contrastive learning alone can produce strong, general embeddings for a wide variety of downstream tasks.
> - Powerful, task-agnostic visual features are often learned and reside within the intermediate layers of contrastively trained vision transformers, even when the final output layer does not adequately express them.
> - Novel "alignment tuning" methods can effectively extract and align these hidden intermediate features to the network's output, making them readily usable for diverse applications without retraining the entire model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has, for the past decade, heavily relied on large-scale pretrained vision encoders as foundational building blocks for a myriad of perception-related applications. Historically, these encoders have been specialized, with various pretraining objectives tailored to specific downstream tasks. For instance:

- **Vision-Language Contrastive Learning (e.g., CLIP, ALIGN):** Excels in learning global image and text embeddings, making them highly effective for zero-shot classification and retrieval, as well as providing strong vision-language alignment for open-world and generative tasks.
- **Captioning Losses (e.g., CoCa, AIM):** Focus on predicting image descriptions using a language decoder, which transfers well to multimodal language model (MLLM) tasks like visual question answering.
- **Spatially Self-Supervised Losses (e.g., DINOv2, MAE):** Learn dense spatial correspondences without explicit language supervision, making them invaluable for tasks requiring precise localization, such as object detection, segmentation, and depth estimation.

The challenge in the current landscape is the increasing complexity of combining these diverse pretraining techniques. Many recent works attempt to fuse two or more objectives, but the combinatorial explosion of strategies makes scaling difficult and often results in models optimized for a subset of tasks rather than general applicability. The community has been actively searching for a *single, simple, and easily scalable* pretraining technique that can learn state-of-the-art features for *all* downstream tasks.

This paper directly addresses this challenge. Instead of proposing a novel complex multi-objective pretraining scheme, the authors make a surprising and impactful claim: **global vision-language contrastive learning alone can produce strong, general embeddings for a wide variety of downstream tasks, including those traditionally dominated by other pretraining methods.** The crucial caveat is that these powerful embeddings are "hidden" within the *intermediate layers* of the network, rather than being naturally exposed at the final output layer. This finding fundamentally shifts the perspective on how to leverage contrastively trained models for broad applicability, suggesting that the problem is not a lack of diverse learned representations but rather an "alignment problem" – how to effectively extract and utilize these rich internal features. The work thus proposes a simplified pathway to foundation models that are broadly capable across both global (classification, retrieval) and dense spatial (detection, segmentation) vision tasks, and multimodal language tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **Perception Encoder (PE)**, a new family of state-of-the-art vision encoders for comprehensive image and video understanding. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Simplification of Pretraining:** To demonstrate that a single, simple, and easily scalable pretraining technique—specifically, contrastive vision-language learning—can produce general, high-quality embeddings for a vast array of downstream vision and vision-language tasks, thus reducing the need for complex multi-objective pretraining.
- **Unlocking Hidden Capabilities:** To reveal and exploit the surprising discovery that powerful, task-agnostic visual features are often learned and reside within the *intermediate layers* of contrastively trained vision transformers, even when the final output layer does not adequately express them for certain tasks.
- **Addressing the "Alignment Problem":** To develop novel "alignment tuning" methods that effectively extract and align these hidden intermediate features to the network's output, making them readily usable for diverse downstream applications without retraining the entire model or requiring complex task-specific decoders.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** To establish new benchmarks across a wide variety of tasks, including zero-shot image/video classification and retrieval, document/image/video Q&A, and dense spatial tasks like detection, tracking, and depth estimation, showcasing the generality and robustness of the PE models.
- **Fostering Open Research:** To contribute to the open science community by releasing the PE models, code, and a novel, synthetically and human-annotated video dataset (PE Video Dataset, PVD), enabling further research and development.

In essence, the paper aims to prove that the "best visual embeddings are not at the output of the network," and by intelligently extracting them, a simpler and more general vision foundation model can be built.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Perception Encoder (PE) is structured around two core ideas: building a powerful, robust vision-language backbone via contrastive learning (**PE_core**), and then developing specialized "alignment tuning" methods to unlock its full potential for various downstream tasks (**PE_lang** and **PE_spatial**).

#### 3.1. Perception Encoder: Core (PE_core) Training

The development of **PE_core**, a unified image-and-video encoder, involves a meticulous, multi-stage training process:

1. **Robust Image Pretraining:** This initial stage focuses on extracting general knowledge from billion-scale image-text data. The authors start with a vanilla CLIP model (OpenCLIP ViT-L/14) and systematically introduce several carefully tuned regularization and efficiency techniques, keeping the training FLOPs fixed:
 - **Progressive Resolution:** Training at gradually increasing resolutions (e.g., 98, 154, 224, 336, 448 px) improves efficiency and model robustness by preventing overfitting to global tokens at early stages.
 - **Increased Batch Size:** Doubling the batch size from 32K to 64K increases the effective number of hard negatives, enhancing contrastive learning.
 - **LAMB Optimizer with High Learning Rate:** LAMB stabilizes large batch training and allows for a higher initial learning rate (2x10^-3), crucial for adapting to different resolutions.
 - **RoPE (Rotary Positional Embedding):** Added to attention layers to improve extrapolation, further enhancing robustness.
 - **Attention Pooling:** Used for constructing the CLIP embedding, with a surprising finding that keeping the class token as input is beneficial for smaller models.
 - **Tuned Data Augmentation:** Heavy random cropping, brightness/saturation jitter, and horizontal flipping are applied. Random cropping encourages using the entire caption, jitter helps with low-light/documents, and horizontal flip, surprisingly, does not hurt OCR but helps general robustness.
 - **Mask Regularization:** Inspired by MaskFeat, a regularization loss is introduced where 1/16th of the batch is duplicated and masked, with masked tokens aligned to their unmasked counterparts via cosine similarity, ensuring disjoint gradients from the main CLIP loss.
 This robust recipe significantly improves zero-shot ImageNet robustness and general classification, demonstrating better scaling behavior than vanilla CLIP across model sizes and training steps.

2. **Bootstrapping a Video Data Engine and Finetuning:** Recognizing the scarcity of high-quality video-text data, the authors developed an innovative "video data engine" to generate well-aligned synthetic captions for 22M videos.
 - **Three-Phase Engine Development:**
 - **Base Video Captioner (PLM):** An early version of Perception Language Model (PLM), a multimodal LLM with an image-only PE encoder and a Llama decoder, trained on a large collection of image and video datasets (64.7M samples).
 - **PLM + Refined Data:** The base PLM is fine-tuned on 265K videos (including 105K from the novel PVD) with human-refined captions, significantly boosting captioning quality.
 - **LLM Summarization:** The refined PLM video captions, image-only frame captions (from Llama 3.2 vision model), and original video metadata (title, description) are synthesized into concise, aligned captions using a Llama 3.3 70B model.
 - **Video Finetuning:** The robust image encoder (from Stage 1) is then fine-tuned on these 22M recaptioned videos. For video encoding, a simple but effective strategy is employed: uniformly sampling 8 frames from video clips, extracting frame-level embeddings with the image encoder, and then average pooling these to obtain a single video embedding for contrastive learning with the synthesized video captions. This short finetuning step dramatically improves performance on both image and video classification and retrieval.

3. **Smaller Model Distillation:** To maximize the performance of smaller PE_core models (B and L scales), PE_core_G (the 2B parameter G-scale model) serves as a teacher. The student model's image-to-text and text-to-image similarity distributions are optimized to match the teacher's, with a crucial finding that a smaller softmax temperature (0.5x student's) for the teacher's distribution significantly enhances knowledge distillation.

#### 3.2. Alignment Tuning

The core insight that optimal features reside in intermediate layers necessitates specific alignment strategies for downstream tasks.

1. **Language Alignment (PE_lang):** This method aims to "lift" the strong language-relevant features from PE_core's intermediate layers to the end of the network for multimodal large language models (MLLMs).
 - **Setup:** PE_core is adapted to a pretrained decoder-only LLM (Llama 3) via a vision projector.
 - **Training Stages:**
 - **Warmup:** Projector-only training with autoregressive next-token prediction loss on 1M image-text samples.
 - **Finetuning:** Training all parameters (projector and LLM) on 70M diverse multimodal data samples (natural images, documents, charts, videos) using next-token prediction.
 - **Key Findings from Ablations:** A 2-layer MLP projector is preferred over a linear layer. Most importantly, projecting from *layer 47* of PE_core_G (instead of the last layer 50) is optimal for MLLM tasks. Encoder regularization (LayerScale, DropPath) further stabilizes and improves training.
 - **Effect:** Language alignment successfully lifts features, making the last layer of PE_lang G the optimal output across various language tasks (OCR Q&A, Visual Q&A, Captioning, Grounding), often exceeding the performance of even the best intermediate layers of the original PE_core G.

2. **Spatial Alignment (PE_spatial):** This method addresses the dichotomy observed in PE_core's spatial features (e.g., optimal layers for detection vs. tracking). The goal is to preserve optimal semantic information while emphasizing local alignment for dense prediction tasks.
 - **Core Feature Analysis:** Visualization of attention maps and feature similarity reveals that "global tokens" emerge around layer 33 in PE_core_G, which are beneficial for semantic understanding but detrimental for pure spatial correspondence tasks like zero-shot tracking (which peaks before their emergence).
 - **Alignment Strategy:**
 - **Retaining Semantics (Self-Distillation):** The model is fine-tuned to minimize cosine similarity between its last layer and the frozen *layer 41 features* of its PE_core_G initialization (chosen for its strong semantic content). Heavy regularization (DropPath, LayerScale, 75% MaskFeat) is applied to the student.
 - **Encouraging Locality (SAM Mask Logit Distillation):** Instead of distilling from SAM's internal features (which also exhibit global tokens), the authors use SAM 2.1-L's *mask prediction logits*. By querying SAM with a 32x32 grid of points and concatenating the resulting HxW mask logits into a HxW x 1024 tensor, a feature map is created that explicitly represents locally well-aligned, smooth, and consistent features without spatial artifacts. The student is then trained with MSE loss to match the pairwise cosine similarity of its tokens to those of the SAM mask logits.
 - **Effect:** Combining both self-distillation (for semantics) and SAM mask logit distillation (for locality) creates PE_spatial_G, which successfully aligns strong, general, and spatially coherent features to its last layer, improving performance across all dense prediction tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper demonstrates the state-of-the-art performance and unique properties of the Perception Encoder family of models across a diverse range of vision and vision-language tasks.

#### 4.1. PE_core: Unified Image and Video Encoder

- **Zero-Shot Image Performance:** PE_core_G achieves an average zero-shot ImageNet robustness of 86.6%, outperforming all other contrastive models, including those trained on proprietary datasets like Google's JFT-3B and WebLI. It sets a new state-of-the-art across general classification, fine-grained classification, and image-text retrieval benchmarks simultaneously.
- **Zero-Shot Video Performance:** Using a simple 8-frame average pooling strategy, PE_core_G reaches 76.9% zero-shot Kinetics-400 video classification accuracy, significantly outperforming native video models that use full temporal attention, and achieving comparable performance on video retrieval benchmarks. This highlights the effectiveness of the proposed video data engine and its ability to create a truly unified image-video encoder with relatively less video data than other specialized video models.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** Ablation studies on image pretraining show that each introduced technique (progressive resolution, data augmentation, mask regularization, etc.) consistently improves robustness and generality across various difficult benchmarks (e.g., ObjectNet, ImageNet Adversarial) without trade-offs. The video data engine further enhances performance on both image and video tasks, emphasizing that training with well-aligned video data benefits both modalities.
- **Frozen Encoder Probing:** PE_core_G's frozen features outperform all existing open encoders (including those with significantly more parameters) on ImageNet-1k when evaluated with k-nearest neighbors, linear probing, and attention probing.

#### 4.2. General Features in Contrastive Disguise (Intermediate Layer Analysis)

- **Surprising Intermediate Layer Performance:** A key finding is that the *intermediate layers* of PE_core_G (a purely contrastively trained model) contain features that are competitive with, and often match or exceed, the performance of state-of-the-art models specifically trained with captioning (AIMv2-3B) or self-supervised spatial objectives (DINOv2-g) for their respective specialized domains. This includes tasks like OCR Q&A, Visual Q&A, Grounding, Object Detection, Depth Estimation, and Zero-Shot Tracking.
- **The "Alignment Problem" Identified:** The analysis reveals that while these powerful features exist, their performance *diminishes rapidly towards the final layer* of PE_core_G. This suggests that the model learns general representations during contrastive pretraining, but the process of creating the global CLIP token in the final layers can "obfuscate" or compress these features, making them less accessible for certain downstream tasks.
- **Pretraining Recipe Impact on Internal Features:** The systematic ablation of the robust image pretraining recipe shows that the improvements are not just at the output but also enhance the quality of intermediate features, particularly for spatial tasks like COCO detection, where the "best layer" performance significantly improves.

#### 4.3. PE_lang: Language Alignment Results

- **State-of-the-Art MLLM Vision Encoder:** PE_lang_G, by aligning PE_core_G's intermediate features, individually outperforms all other popular vision encoders when integrated into an MLLM (Llama 3.1 8B or QwenLM 2.5 7B). It achieves leading results across diverse MLLM benchmarks, including OCR, Chart, Document Q&A (e.g., 94.6% DocVQA with an 8B LLM, 80.9% InfographicVQA), Visual Q&A, Captioning, Video Understanding (e.g., 82.7% PerceptionTest with an 8B LLM), and Grounding.
- **Successful Feature Lifting:** The layerwise analysis confirms that language alignment successfully "lifts" the optimal features, ensuring that the last layer of PE_lang is the best performing layer for all language-related tasks, resolving the hidden feature problem.
- **Transferability:** PE_lang demonstrates strong transferability across different base LLMs (Llama vs. Qwen), further validating the generality of its language-aligned vision features.
- **System-Level Performance:** When integrated into PLM-8B (a full MLLM system), PE_lang_G drives state-of-the-art performance against other leading open-access MLLMs like InternVL3, LLaVA-OneVision, and Qwen2 VL.

#### 4.4. PE_spatial: Spatial Alignment Results

- **Feature Coherence:** Analysis shows that PE_core_G's last layer features lose spatial coherence due to the emergence of "global tokens." The proposed spatial alignment method successfully restores this coherence while retaining semantic richness.
- **Outperforming Specialized Models for Dense Prediction:** PE_spatial_G achieves state-of-the-art performance on various dense prediction tasks:
 - **Frozen Feature Probing:** Outperforms other state-of-the-art spatial models (like DINOv2) on zero-shot tracking (61.5% J&F on DAVIS), semantic segmentation, and depth estimation, with its best features successfully aligned to the last layer.
 - **End-to-End Finetuning (Detection/Segmentation):** In controlled settings, PE_spatial_G is state-of-the-art among vision backbones for object detection and instance segmentation on COCO and LVIS, significantly outperforming other contrastive encoders.
 - **System-Level Detection:** Achieves a new COCO state-of-the-art of 66.0 box mAP using a simpler DETR-style decoder (DETA) and minimal extra detection data (Object365), marking the first time a general, contrastively pretrained model reaches this level without extensive detection-specific pretraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Perception Encoder research holds profound significance for the future of computer vision and multimodal AI, primarily by simplifying the foundation model paradigm while pushing performance boundaries.

1. **Paradigm Shift in Pretraining:** The most significant impact is challenging the prevailing notion that multiple, specialized pretraining objectives are necessary for a universally capable vision encoder. By demonstrating that a robustly implemented *single contrastive objective* can yield rich, general features for diverse tasks (classification, retrieval, language understanding, and dense spatial prediction), PE points towards a simpler and more unified approach to building vision foundation models. This simplification reduces the complexity of model development, training infrastructure, and data curation, making foundation model research more accessible and efficient.
2. **Unlocking Latent Representations:** The discovery and successful exploitation of "hidden" high-quality features within intermediate layers of standard vision transformers is a critical insight. This suggests that even seemingly task-limited models might possess far richer internal representations than their output layers indicate. This opens new avenues for research into feature extraction, representation learning analysis, and lightweight alignment techniques, rather than solely focusing on designing ever more complex pretraining objectives or larger models.
3. **Broad Applicability and Performance:** The consistent state-of-the-art results across an unprecedented variety of benchmarks—from zero-shot image/video understanding to complex MLLM tasks and high-precision spatial predictions—validate the effectiveness of the PE approach. This broad applicability positions PE as a truly versatile foundation model, capable of serving as a powerful backbone for numerous downstream AI applications. Its ability to surpass models trained on proprietary datasets or with more complex pretraining schemes (e.g., JFT-3B, multi-objective methods for detection) is a major achievement.
4. **Efficiency and Scalability:** The robust image pretraining recipe, coupled with the innovative video data engine, demonstrates high data efficiency for video understanding and effective scalability across model sizes. The alignment tuning, requiring significantly fewer samples and FLOPs compared to the initial pretraining, offers an efficient pathway to specialize the general backbone for specific task families without costly full model retraining.
5. **Open Science Contribution:** The release of PE models, code, and the novel PE Video Dataset (PVD) is a substantial contribution to the open-source AI community. This commitment to transparency and reproducibility will accelerate research by providing high-quality resources for other researchers to build upon, compare against, and explore novel applications, fostering collaborative progress in multimodal AI.
6. **Impact on Multimodal AI and Robotics:** A truly general vision encoder like PE is invaluable for multimodal large language models and embodied AI systems (e.g., robotics). Its ability to provide robust visual context for language, and precise spatial understanding for physical interaction, can significantly advance the capabilities of these complex AI agents, leading to more intelligent and versatile human-AI interactions.

In conclusion, Perception Encoder represents a significant step towards the holy grail of a single, highly general, and performant vision foundation model, fundamentally reshaping how the AI community approaches the design and utilization of large-scale visual encoders.
