# Accelerating Diffusion Planners in Offline RL via Reward-Aware Consistency Trajectory Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> RACTD (Reward-Aware Consistency Trajectory Distillation) accelerates diffusion planners in offline reinforcement learning by enabling single-step action trajectory generation through distilling a reward-aware student from a teacher diffusion model. The method achieves up to a 142x speedup in inference time and improves average performance on D4RL Gym-MuJoCo benchmarks to 96.4.

## Problem

- Diffusion models in reinforcement learning suffer from slow inference speeds due to their iterative sampling process, hindering real-time applications.
- Existing consistency-based RL methods struggle to effectively learn from suboptimal and heterogeneous offline datasets, or involve complex and unstable multi-network concurrent training.
- Classifier-guided diffusion methods require training noise-aware reward models, which is complex and can lead to inaccurate guidance from corrupted inputs.

## Method

- **RACTD** employs a decoupled training framework involving a pre-trained ==teacher diffusion planner==, an independent reward model, and a student consistency model.
- The student model is trained with a multi-objective loss function combining consistency trajectory distillation (CTM and DSM loss) with an explicit negative reward objective.
- The reward objective utilizes a frozen, independently trained reward model operating on noise-free inputs to steer the student towards high-reward action modes in a single sampling step.

## Results

- RACTD achieved the highest average score on D4RL Gym-MuJoCo benchmarks (96.4 for offline, 101.5 for online model selection), outperforming existing baselines.
- The method demonstrated substantial inference time speedups, including a **142x** speedup over its EDM teacher model and a **43x** speedup over Diffuser on MuJoCo tasks, all with single-step sampling.
- It surpassed the Diffuser baseline on D4RL Maze2d tasks, achieving an average score of 133.4 compared to Diffuser's 119.5, with significantly fewer sampling steps (up to **256x** reduction).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a reward objective directly into the consistency trajectory distillation process enables a single-step planner to effectively select and generate high-reward behaviors from multimodal distributions learned by a teacher diffusion model.
> - Decoupling the training of the reward model and the student consistency model simplifies the overall framework, avoids the complexity of noise-aware reward modeling, and enhances training stability.
> - Consistency trajectory distillation, when combined with reward guidance, allows the student to retain the teacher's capacity for complex, long-horizon planning while achieving substantial speedups.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models have demonstrated capabilities in various domains, including decision-making tasks within reinforcement learning (RL). They are recognized for their ability to capture multi-modal behavior patterns and exhibit generalization. However, a limitation for practical deployment is their iterative sampling process, which leads to slow inference speeds.

To address this, various acceleration techniques for diffusion models have been developed. These include the use of ordinary or stochastic differential equation (ODE/SDE) solvers, sampling step distillation, and improved noise schedules. Among these, consistency distillation and consistency trajectory models (CTM) have emerged as methods for training a student model to emulate a many-step diffusion teacher model with fewer, or even a single, sampling step, particularly in image generation.

The application of consistency-based distillation to decision-making tasks has faced specific challenges. Current approaches primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Behavior Cloning (BC) approaches:** These methods train consistency models on expert demonstrations. While effective with high-quality data, they often perform suboptimally when confronted with mixed-quality or non-expert datasets.
2. **Actor-Critic (AC) frameworks:** These integrate few-step diffusion-based samplers. However, they necessitate the concurrent training of multiple networks from scratch, which can introduce complexity, instability, sensitive hyperparameter tuning, and computational overhead.
3. **Guided Diffusion Sampling:** This method requires training noise-aware reward models and still relies on multi-step sampling, which can be detrimental for time-sensitive applications.

This work positions itself to overcome these limitations by proposing a novel approach that integrates reward optimization directly into the consistency trajectory distillation process for offline RL. It aims to develop a method that achieves single-step sampling while generating high-reward action trajectories, particularly from suboptimal data, and maintains a simplified, decoupled training procedure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective approach to consistency-based distillation for offline reinforcement learning that addresses two fundamental challenges: the handling of suboptimal datasets and the complexity of concurrent multi-network training. The goal is to enable single-step action trajectory generation while achieving strong performance.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Computational Overhead of Diffusion Models:** Despite their capabilities in decision-making, the iterative sampling process of diffusion models leads to significant computational overhead, making them less suitable for practical, time-sensitive applications such as real-time robotics or autonomous systems.
- **Limitations of Existing Consistency-Based RL Methods:**
 - **Behavior Cloning's Sensitivity to Data Quality:** While behavior cloning with consistency models performs well on expert demonstrations, it often struggles with heterogeneous and suboptimal offline datasets (e.g., D4RL medium-replay buffers) that contain a mix of behaviors with varying reward qualities. Such datasets commonly feature multimodal behavior patterns, where only a subset of these modes leads to high rewards. Rejecting low-reward data is often sample-inefficient.
 - **Complexity of Actor-Critic Frameworks:** Integrating diffusion or consistency models into actor-critic frameworks for RL requires the simultaneous optimization of multiple neural networks from scratch. This concurrent training process typically involves sensitive hyperparameters, can lead to instability, and introduces substantial computational overhead.
 - **Challenges with Guided Diffusion Sampling:** Existing guided diffusion sampling methods require the training of noise-aware reward models and still depend on multi-step sampling. Evaluating rewards from highly corrupted noisy inputs can be challenging, potentially leading to inaccurate guidance that compounds over multiple denoising steps.
- **Need for a Simplified and Efficient Solution:** There is a need for an approach that can leverage the multimodal information captured by diffusion models from suboptimal datasets, guide the learning process towards high-reward behaviors, and enable rapid, single-step inference, all while maintaining a straightforward training procedure.

This paper addresses these motivations by aiming to incorporate reward optimization directly into the consistency trajectory distillation. This approach leverages a pre-trained diffusion planner as a teacher to capture diverse behaviors and uses a standalone reward model to explicitly steer a single-step sampling student planner towards high-reward action modes. This design intends to simplify training by decoupling components and avoid the complexities of noise-aware reward modeling by operating in a clean sample space.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named Reward-Aware Consistency Trajectory Distillation (RACTD), integrates reward optimization into the consistency trajectory distillation process. It involves a decoupled training framework comprising a teacher diffusion planner, a reward model, and a student consistency model.

#### 3.1. Problem Setting and Action Sequence Modeling

The work focuses on offline reinforcement learning, where the objective is to learn a policy $\pi$ to maximize cumulative discounted reward from a static dataset of experiences. Following existing diffusion planner approaches, the model operates as a planner by modeling fixed-length sequences of future actions ($\vec{a}_n$) conditioned on fixed-length sequences of past observed states ($\vec{s}_n$). This formulation, $p(\vec{a}_n | \vec{s}_n)$, promotes coherent action sequences. During execution, either the first predicted action is taken before replanning, or the entire predicted sequence is executed.

#### 3.2. Teacher Model Training

An EDM (Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models) model serves as the teacher. The teacher is trained to reverse a gradual noise corruption process. Its training objective is a denoising score matching (DSM) loss, which aims to predict a scaled version of the clean data (action sequences). The pseudo Huber loss is used as the distance metric. During inference, the teacher model utilizes a 2nd-order Heun solver to reverse the probability flow ODE (PFODE), requiring multiple iterative denoising steps.

#### 3.3. Reward Model Training

A differentiable return-to-go network, denoted $R_\psi$, is independently pre-trained. This reward model takes the current state $s_n$ and the *predicted action* $a_n$ (extracted from the clean action sequence generated by the student) as inputs to estimate the future discounted cumulative reward $\hat{r}_n$. The architecture consists of four ConvBlocks followed by a Linear layer. A key aspect of this approach is the **decoupled training** of the reward model, which operates entirely in the noise-free state-action space, eliminating the need for noise-aware training and associated complexities.

#### 3.4. Student Model Training (RACTD)

The student model, which aims for single-step sampling, is trained via a multi-objective loss function that combines elements of consistency trajectory distillation with an explicit reward objective.
- **Consistency Trajectory Distillation (CTD):** This component is based on Consistency Trajectory Models (CTM) and Denoising Score Matching (DSM).
 - **CTM Loss ($L_{CTM}$):** This loss trains the student model $G_\theta$ to predict across arbitrary time intervals along the diffusion process trajectory. Given three timesteps $0 \le k < u < t \le T$, the student learns to align direct prediction from $t$ to $k$ with a two-stage prediction involving the teacher from $t$ to $u$ and then the student from $u$ to $k$. All predictions are mapped to time 0 for distance calculation.
 - **DSM Loss ($L_{DSM}$):** This loss ensures that the samples generated by the student model are close to the original training data, similar to the teacher's training objective.
- **Reward Objective ($L_{Reward}$):** This is the core novel component. After the student model generates a clean action sequence $\hat{x}^{(T)}_0 = G_\theta(x_T, T, 0)$ in a single step, the action $a_n$ from this sequence, along with the current state $s_n$, is fed into the *frozen* pre-trained reward model $R_\psi$. The reward objective is designed to maximize this estimated discounted cumulative reward: $L_{Reward} = -R_\psi(\vec{s}_n, \hat{a}_n)$. This acts as a mode selection mechanism, steering the student towards high-reward behaviors.
- **Combined Loss:** The final loss function for RACTD is a weighted sum of these three components: $L = \alpha L_{CTM} + \beta L_{DSM} + \sigma L_{Reward}$, where $\alpha, \beta, \sigma$ are hyperparameters.

#### 3.5. Decoupled Training Framework

The framework emphasizes decoupled training: the teacher diffusion planner, the student consistency model, and the reward model are trained independently. This simplifies the overall training process, enhances flexibility by allowing different reward models to be used with the same teacher, and avoids the issues of concurrent optimization found in actor-critic methods. The single-step denoising of the student allows the reward model to operate on noise-free inputs, avoiding the complexities of noise-aware reward models.

#### 3.6. Reward Objective as Mode Selection

The reward objective is utilized to select optimal modes from the multimodal distributions learned by the teacher. In datasets containing behaviors of varying quality, the unconditioned teacher may capture all modes (high and low reward). RACTD uses the reward signal to guide the student to preferentially sample from the high-reward modes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate RACTD's capabilities in terms of performance, sampling efficiency, and the efficacy of its reward-aware distillation mechanism across various offline RL benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on D4RL Gym-MuJoCo Benchmarks

- **Superior Average Performance:** RACTD achieved the highest average score across the D4RL Gym-MuJoCo environments (96.4 for offline model selection, 101.5 for online model selection, Table 1). This represents a substantial improvement compared to other baselines.
- **Consistent High Performance:** RACTD obtained the best or second-best performance on 8 out of 9 tasks within the Gym-MuJoCo suite. The exception was a "medium-expert" dataset, where the benefit of reward guidance is naturally diminished due to the high quality of the baseline data.
- **Efficiency Compared to Baselines:** RACTD is highlighted as the only planning-based method to achieve single-step sampling (1 NFE) while demonstrating strong performance. In contrast, consistency model-based actor-critic methods (e.g., Consistency AC) require 2 NFEs, and other diffusion planners (e.g., Diffuser, Diffusion QL) require 5-20 NFEs.

#### 4.2. Performance on D4RL FrankaKitchen Benchmark

- **Outperformance on Suboptimal Data:** RACTD consistently outperformed other consistency model-based or flow-based actor-critic methods (e.g., Consistency AC, Flow Q-learning) on suboptimal datasets (kitchen-partial and kitchen-mixed) (Table 2).
- **Efficiency with Competitive Performance:** RACTD achieved performance comparable to multi-step diffusion approaches (e.g., Diffusion QL) with a 5x reduction in sampling steps (1 NFE vs. 5 NFEs), validating the efficacy of its reward-aware distillation framework on tasks where reward guidance is crucial.

#### 4.3. Long-Horizon Planning on D4RL Maze2d

- **Enhanced Performance on Complex Tasks:** RACTD surpassed the Diffuser baseline across all Maze2d environments (U-Maze, Medium, Large), achieving an average score of 133.4 compared to Diffuser's 119.5 (Table 3). This indicates its ability to capture complex behavioral patterns and high-dimensional information for long-horizon planning.
- **Single-Step Efficiency in High Dimensions:** Despite the significantly higher planning dimensions (up to 384 for Large Maze) compared to MuJoCo tasks, RACTD maintained its single-step denoising capability (1 NFE). Diffuser, for instance, required up to 256 NFEs for similar tasks, underscoring RACTD's expressiveness and efficiency.

#### 4.4. Inference Time Comparison

- **Significant Speedup on MuJoCo:** On the hopper-medium-replay task, RACTD achieved a 20x reduction in NFEs and a 43x speedup compared to Diffuser. It was also 80x more efficient in NFEs and 142x faster than its EDM teacher model (Table 4).
- **Substantial Speedup on Maze2d:** For Maze2d, RACTD demonstrated speedups of approximately 57x, 92x, and 114x on Umaze, Medium, and Large mazes, respectively, when compared to Diffuser. This was achieved by reducing NFEs by a factor of up to 256 (Table 3).

#### 4.5. Ablation Study: Impact of Reward Objective

- **Optimal Configuration (RACTD):** Comparative analysis (Table 5, Table 12) across configurations (unconditioned teacher/student, reward-aware teacher/unconditioned student, fully reward-aware teacher/student, and RACTD's unconditioned teacher/reward-aware student) indicated that the RACTD framework yielded optimal performance. This suggests that allowing the teacher to capture comprehensive behavior patterns and then using the student to selectively distill high-reward strategies is effective.
- **Benefits of Reward Incorporation:** Incorporating reward objectives at any stage resulted in substantial performance improvements.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study: Reward Objective Weights

- **Sensitivity to Weights:** The reward loss weight acts as a hyperparameter affecting training stability and performance (Figure 4, Table 15). Lower weights (e.g., 0.3, 0.8) generally led to stable training and higher performance. Excessive weights (e.g., 1.5) caused performance fluctuations and instability as the reward loss dominated other terms.

#### 4.7. Ablation Study: DSM Loss Weights

- Performance was found to be sensitive to the DSM weight, with optimal performance observed in a specific range (Table 14).

#### 4.8. Ablation Study: Number of Sampling Steps

- Evaluation of multi-step sampling (2, 3, or 4 denoising steps) with the student model showed minimal performance improvement (Table 16), confirming the effectiveness and efficiency of single-step sampling.

#### 4.9. Ablation Study: Observation and Planning Horizon

- Increasing the number of past observations and the planning horizon was shown to further improve performance on specific tasks (Table 17).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, RACTD, contributes to the field of reinforcement learning and generative modeling with several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Bridging Efficiency and Performance in Diffusion-based RL:** RACTD directly addresses a key limitation of diffusion models in RL—their slow inference speed—by enabling single-step action trajectory generation. This advancement allows diffusion planners to operate at speeds compatible with real-time applications, such as robotics and autonomous systems, where iterative denoising steps are prohibitive. The achievement of up to 142x speedup over diffusion counterparts, while also improving average performance by 9.7% over existing state-of-the-art, signifies a substantial step towards practical deployment.

- **Effective Learning from Suboptimal Data:** The reward-aware distillation mechanism offers a method for effectively leveraging suboptimal and heterogeneous offline datasets. These datasets are common in real-world scenarios but pose challenges for traditional behavior cloning and reward-agnostic generative models. By explicitly guiding the student model to prioritize high-reward modes from the teacher's multimodal distribution, RACTD enhances the ability of models to extract optimal behaviors from mixed-quality data.

- **Simplified and Flexible Training Framework:** The decoupled training of the teacher diffusion model, student consistency model, and reward model significantly reduces the complexity and instability associated with concurrent multi-network optimization found in actor-critic frameworks. This modularity allows for easier integration of different reward models or teacher planners, fostering flexibility and potentially speeding up research and development cycles.

- **Robust Reward Guidance:** By designing the reward model to operate in the noise-free action space, RACTD avoids the complexities and potential inaccuracies of noise-aware reward models required by classifier-guided diffusion. This design choice provides a more stable and effective reward signal for policy optimization.

- **Theoretical Grounding:** The presented connection of RACTD's reward objective to off-policy deterministic policy gradient provides a theoretical basis, linking it to established and well-understood offline RL algorithms. This connection lends credibility to the proposed optimization strategy.

- **Broadening Applications of Diffusion Models:** By making diffusion models more efficient and robust to data quality, RACTD expands their potential application space within RL, particularly in domains that demand both high-quality actions and rapid decision-making. This includes advanced robotics, human-robot interaction, and complex control systems.

While acknowledging the limitation of training three separate networks, the proposed RACTD framework offers a pathway to combine the representational power of diffusion models with the efficiency required for real-world interactive systems, potentially leading to more capable and deployable RL agents.
