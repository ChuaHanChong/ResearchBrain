# Actor-Critic for Continuous Action Chunks: A Reinforcement Learning Framework for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation with Sparse Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> AC3 is an off-policy actor-critic reinforcement learning framework that directly learns continuous action sequences for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. It achieves superior success rates on complex bi-manual and tabletop tasks, demonstrating data efficiency with few expert demonstrations and stability in sparse reward settings.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning struggles with long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks due to expansive state-action spaces and the need for coherent, sequential actions.
- Learning effectively from sparse rewards, where feedback is only given at task completion, makes exploration and credit assignment difficult for agents.
- Integrating continuous action chunking into RL often leads to unstable training and issues like exploding Q-values, while imitation learning methods are limited by demonstration quality and are prone to distributional shift.

## Method

- **AC3** employs an off-policy actor-critic architecture to directly predict continuous ==action chunks== (sequences of actions), fostering coherent motion planning.
- It introduces an intra-chunk ==n-step Temporal Difference== (TD) target for the critic to stabilize value estimation and improve sample efficiency for larger chunk sizes.
- An asymmetric actor update rule trains the policy exclusively on successful trajectories to prevent degradation, complemented by a self-supervised reward shaping module that provides intrinsic rewards at meaningful 'Anchor Points' via a contrastively learned goal network.

## Results

- AC3 achieved superior success rates on 15 bi-manual mobile manipulation tasks (BiGym) and outperformed or matched baselines on 10 tabletop manipulation tasks (RLBench), demonstrating robust online learning and improvement over imitation learning.
- The framework showed high data efficiency, learning effectively from as few as 10 expert demonstrations in BiGym tasks, and maintained computational efficiency with a lightweight actor network enabling rapid inference (**2.9ms** per action chunk).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the asymmetric actor update, the intra-chunk n-step return, and appropriately calibrated self-supervised reward shaping were all critical for AC3's stable and effective performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymmetric actor updates, which restrict policy learning to successful trajectories, are crucial for stable policy improvement in sparse reward and high-dimensional state-action spaces, preventing policy collapse.
> - An intra-chunk n-step return (e.g., 4 steps for a 16-step chunk) effectively balances the bias-variance trade-off for the critic, mitigating Q-value explosion and enabling stable learning for continuous action chunks.
> - Self-supervised reward shaping, by providing intrinsic rewards at meaningful anchor points derived from successful trajectory features, significantly enhances exploration and learning stability in sparse reward environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved notable advancements in continuous-action control. However, existing methods often demonstrate proficiency in short-horizon tasks but encounter significant difficulties when applied to long-horizon robotic manipulation problems. These long-horizon tasks, such as moving plates or sandwich flipping, typically involve multiple sub-tasks, require precise manipulation, and present an expansive state-action exploration space. The challenge is further compounded by the prevalence of sparse rewards, where positive feedback is only provided upon successful task completion, making effective exploration and credit assignment difficult.

Action chunking, a paradigm that involves predicting entire sequences of actions, has emerged as a successful approach for managing temporal dependencies in long-horizon tasks, particularly within Imitation Learning (IL). State-of-the-art IL methods like ACT and Diffusion Policy leverage generative models to clone complex, continuous action sequences from expert demonstrations. While powerful, these IL approaches are inherently bounded by the quality and quantity of expert data and are susceptible to distributional shift, limiting their robustness in unseen states and preventing improvement through online interaction.

Integrating action chunking into an RL framework presents its own set of challenges. The expanded action space resulting from chunking can make exploration and value estimation intractable for standard RL algorithms, often leading to training instability and issues such as exploding Q-values. Prior attempts to combine chunking with RL have explored different avenues. CQN-AS, for instance, discretizes action chunks, which sacrifices precision and flexibility. Other concurrent work has utilized complex distillation pipelines to generate continuous action chunks, but these methods typically require large-scale offline datasets and incur high computational costs, limiting their applicability in data-efficient or real-time control scenarios.

This paper addresses a critical gap by proposing an RL framework that can directly and data-efficiently learn continuous action chunks. The work aims to combine the benefits of temporal abstraction and online self-improvement from RL, particularly in settings with few expert demonstrations and sparse rewards, without the limitations of discretization or complex distillation processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel reinforcement learning framework, named AC3 (Actor-Critic for Continuous Chunks), that can effectively learn to generate high-dimensional, continuous action sequences for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. The framework is specifically designed to operate stably and data-efficiently, even in environments characterized by sparse rewards and limited expert demonstrations.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges prevalent in current robotic manipulation research:
- **Struggles of existing RL methods:** Traditional RL algorithms generally perform poorly on long-horizon tasks that require extended sequences of coherent actions and suffer from an expanded exploration space.
- **Problem of sparse rewards:** In long-horizon tasks, rewards are often sparse, meaning positive feedback is received only at task completion. This makes it difficult for agents to attribute success or failure to specific intermediate actions, hindering efficient autonomous exploration and value learning.
- **Limitations of Imitation Learning (IL):** While action chunking in IL has shown promise for long-horizon tasks, its performance is capped by the provided expert demonstrations. IL methods are prone to distributional shift when encountering states not present in the training data, limiting their robustness and ability to generalize or improve beyond the initial dataset.
- **Difficulties in integrating chunking with RL:** Directly applying action chunking to RL exponentially increases the complexity of the action space, leading to unstable exploration and value estimation, such as exploding Q-values. Existing RL approaches that incorporate chunking often make compromises, such as discretizing the action space (sacrificing precision) or relying on computationally expensive distillation processes and large offline datasets (limiting data efficiency).

To overcome these challenges, AC3 aims to provide a direct, stable, and data-efficient RL framework for continuous action chunking. The central motivations are to enable more flexible and precise robotic control, leverage the potential for online improvement that RL offers, and effectively learn from a small number of expert demonstrations under real-world data constraints and sparse reward structures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AC3 (Actor-Critic for Continuous Chunks) framework is an off-policy actor-critic algorithm, based on the DDPG-style architecture, designed to learn continuous action chunks for long-horizon, sparse-reward robotic manipulation. It operates by predicting sequences of continuous actions (chunks) and incorporates targeted stabilization mechanisms for both the actor and the critic. The overall design comprises three conceptual levels: the AC3 Agent architecture, specific update rules, and a self-supervised reward shaping module.

**Problem Formulation:** The task is modeled as a Semi-Markov Decision Process (SMDP) defined by (S, A, P, r, γ), where S is a high-dimensional state space (including RGB visual and proprioceptive states), A is a continuous action chunk of fixed length C, P is the state transition dynamics, γ is the discount factor, and r is a sparse reward function (r ∈ {0, 1}). The objective is to learn a policy π_θ that maximizes the expected discounted return, leveraging a small offline dataset of N expert demonstrations D_demo.

**AC3 Agent Architecture:**
- **Actor (π_θ): Continuous Action Chunking Policy:** The actor network, parameterized by θ, directly predicts a continuous action chunk A_t = {a_t, a_(t+1)..., a_(t+C-1)} ∈ R^(C×d_a) from the current state s_t. This temporal abstraction aims to align motion planning with task logic, mitigate local sub-optimalities, and enhance control coherence. The policy network employs a simple MLP+GRU architecture to capture temporal dependencies efficiently.
- **Critic (Q_ϕ): Intra-Chunk n-step Q-value Estimation:** The critic network, parameterized by ϕ, estimates the expected return after executing an entire chunk A_t from state s_t. To address the issues of noisy reward estimation and reduced sample efficiency that arise with large chunk sizes (C), the critic's update utilizes an intra-chunk n-step Temporal Difference (TD) target. This target, y_t (Equation 2), combines the sum of observed rewards over 'n' steps (r_(t:t+n-1)) and a bootstrapped value estimate from target networks Q_ϕ' at the landing state s_(t+n). Similar to TD3, two target critics are used with a clipped double-Q estimate (min Q_ϕ'_i) to mitigate over-estimation bias and stabilize learning. The critic network also uses an MLP+GRU architecture.

**Training and Update Rules:**
- **Critic Update:** The critic networks (Q_ϕ1, Q_ϕ2) are updated by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between their current Q-value predictions and the calculated n-step TD target y_t (Equation 3). This aims to learn an accurate value function to guide policy updates.
- **Actor Update: Asymmetric Update Rule:** To ensure reliable policy improvement under sparse rewards and high-dimensional states, the actor's update is constrained to a "trusted region." This is achieved by training the actor exclusively on successful trajectories. A dedicated replay buffer B_succ is constructed by filtering successful online rollouts and expert demonstrations. The actor's loss L_θ (Equation 6) is a weighted combination of:
 - **Behavioral Cloning (BC) loss (L_θ_BC, Equation 4):** Directly imitates expert actions from B_succ, providing initial guidance.
 - **Q-value loss (L_θ_Q, Equation 5):** Maximizes the Q-value predicted by the critic for the actor's generated actions, also sampled from B_succ.
 This asymmetric approach prevents policy degradation that could arise from using inaccurate Q-values from uncertain or unsuccessful state regions, which often occurs in sparse-reward settings.

**Self-Supervised Reward Shaping Module:**
To further alleviate the issue of sparse rewards for the critic's learning, a self-supervised module provides intrinsic rewards.
- **Goal Network (G_ω):** A state goal network G_ω(·) is pre-trained on the offline demonstrations D_demo using a contrastive learning paradigm. It learns a low-dimensional latent space where temporally close states within a trajectory form positive pairs, and distant or cross-trajectory states form negative pairs. A triplet loss (Equation 7) is used to push positive pairs closer and negative pairs further apart.
- **Intrinsic Reward (r_int): Anchor Points:** During online policy learning, intrinsic reward is provided at "Anchor Points," which are set at fixed intervals of K timesteps (e.g., K=C), aligning with action chunks. The intrinsic reward r_int(s_t) (Equation 9) is granted if the current state s_t is an anchor point and its minimum squared latent distance d(s_t) (Equation 8) to any state in the demonstration dataset (as measured by G_ω) is below a margin threshold 'm'. This provides a semi-dense and reliable signal guiding the value function's learning.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Optimizers and Rates:** AdamW optimizer; learning rates of 1.0 × 10^(-4) for actor/critic, 1.0 × 10^(-5) for goal network.
- **RL Parameters:** Target network soft update coefficient μ=0.005, discount factor γ=0.99, exploration noise σ=0.01 with clip threshold c=0.1.
- **Chunking and Returns:** Action chunk size C=16; 4-step returns for both BiGym and RLBench tasks.
- **Reward Shaping:** Anchor interval K=16, margin threshold m=0.5, intrinsic reward value a=0.1.
- **Actor Loss Weights:** λ_BC=1.0, λ_Q=0.1.
- **Observations:** RGB observations (84x84) with 4-frame stack (BiGym) or 8-frame stack (RLBench), concatenated with proprioceptive states.
- **Action Control:** Absolute Joint (BiGym), Delta Joint (RLBench).
- **Auxiliary Techniques:** Temporal ensemble for action smoothing during execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of AC3 was evaluated through extensive experiments on 25 robotic manipulation tasks from two benchmarks: BiGym and RLBench.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **BiGym:** 15 bi-manual mobile manipulation tasks. Each task used 10 human-collected expert demonstrations and featured sparse rewards (1 for success, 0 otherwise). Observations included 84x84 RGB images from head/wrist cameras and proprioceptive states.
 - **RLBench:** 10 tabletop manipulation tasks. Each task used 100 synthetic expert demonstrations, also with sparse rewards. Observations included 84x84 RGB images from multiple cameras and proprioceptive states.
- **Baselines:**
 - **CQN-AS:** A hierarchical discrete Q-learning method for action chunks.
 - **DrQ-v2:** A single-timestep actor-critic algorithm, augmented with an auxiliary Behavioral Cloning (BC) loss to assist in sparse reward settings.
 - **Chunk-wise BC:** An imitation learning baseline using the same actor network architecture as AC3, trained only on offline chunk-based demonstrations.
- **Evaluation:** Performance was measured by the task success rate over 25 episodes at each checkpoint, with results reported as the mean performance and standard deviation over 3 random seeds. Training was conducted for 50k steps per task.

**Performance Comparison:**
- **BiGym Results:** AC3 demonstrated superior success rates on most of the 15 bi-manual mobile manipulation tasks. Compared to the chunk-wise BC baseline, AC3 consistently discovered better strategies and rapidly increased the success rate, often exhibiting a more pronounced upward trend and maintaining a higher performance level (e.g., in "sandwich remove" and "move plate" tasks). This indicates AC3's effectiveness and stability in exploring high-dimensional action spaces and actively improving through online interaction.
- **RLBench Results:** In RLBench tasks, which generally feature cleaner and more demonstrations with smaller action spaces, chunk-wise BC often learned effective policies. Building upon this, AC3 still outperformed the BC baseline on most tasks and achieved performance comparable to CQN-AS, despite using a simpler network architecture. In contrast, DrQ-v2 showed significantly worse performance than the BC baseline on most tasks, underscoring the importance of chunk-based prediction for long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks.

**Ablation Study and Analysis:**
The study included ablations on key design choices, focusing on "move plate" (complex BiGym task) and "open oven" (simpler RLBench task).
- **Effect of Action Chunk Length (C):** For complex tasks like "move plate" (high-dimensional action space), a longer chunk size (C=16) was crucial for maintaining coherent bi-manual movements. A smaller C=4 resulted in ineffective policies. For simpler tasks like "open oven" (fewer DoF), a moderate chunk length (e.g., C=8) struck a better balance between action coherence and exploration efficiency, as overly long chunks increased exploration difficulty.
- **Effectiveness of Intra-Chunk n-step Return (n):**
 - Setting n=C (using full chunk return) led to policy failure in complex tasks, attributed to high variance and the critic's inability to provide fine-grained feedback for intra-chunk actions with limited demonstrations and sparse rewards.
 - Setting n=1 (one-step TD) caused Q-value explosion in higher-dimensional tasks due to the "Unconstrained Action Subspace" problem. Here, the actor could exploit meaningless gradients for the unexecuted portion of the chunk, leading to an unstable feedback loop.
 - Moderate n-step values (e.g., n=4 or n=8, while C=16) provided a balance, allowing for short-term fine-tuning within chunks and reducing exploration noise. This approach helped stabilize Q-values and achieve more efficient policy improvement.
- **Analysis of Self-Supervised Reward Shaping:** The self-supervised reward shaping module, which provided intrinsic rewards at "Anchor Points" along successful trajectory features, significantly improved performance and stability in complex tasks like "move plate" compared to not using reward shaping. This positive effect was also observed in simpler tasks. However, setting the intrinsic reward value too high (e.g., equal to task success reward) or increasing it linearly led to Q-value explosion and policy failure, as the agent learned to exploit the misspecified intrinsic reward.
- **Training Stability and Efficiency (Asymmetric Actor Update):** An ablation study showed that training the actor on *all* experiences (including failures) caused severe policy degradation or complete collapse. This confirmed the necessity of AC3's asymmetric update rule, which restricts actor learning to successful trajectories, thereby shielding it from misleading gradients generated by biased Q-value estimates in unexplored or unsuccessful regions.
- **Efficiency:** AC3 demonstrated high computational efficiency. Its lightweight Actor network (6.56M parameters) allowed for rapid inference (2.9ms per action chunk), which is over three times faster than CQN-AS (9.5ms). This speed is important for real-time control and temporal action smoothing techniques. The pre-training of the goal network was also fast, typically completed in under an hour.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AC3 as a notable advancement in reinforcement learning for complex robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Long-Horizon, Sparse-Reward Challenges:** AC3 provides a robust framework that directly tackles two major challenges in robotic RL: long-horizon tasks requiring sequential actions and learning under sparse reward signals. By enabling stable learning in these difficult settings, it opens pathways for automating more intricate robotic operations.
- **Effective Continuous Action Chunking:** The framework successfully integrates continuous action chunking into an RL setting, offering a more flexible and precise control mechanism than methods relying on discrete chunking or single-timestep actions. This is crucial for manipulation tasks demanding high fidelity and coherence in movement sequences.
- **Data Efficiency:** A significant impact of AC3 is its ability to achieve superior performance using only a small number of expert demonstrations. This data efficiency is highly valuable for real-world robotic applications where collecting extensive expert data is often prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, or impractical.
- **Enhanced Learning Stability:** The proposed stabilization mechanisms—the asymmetric actor update rule and the intra-chunk n-step critic update—are critical innovations. The asymmetric update protects the actor from misleading gradients in unexplored state regions, preventing policy degradation. The intra-chunk n-step return balances the bias-variance trade-off and provides more stable value estimates, mitigating the risk of Q-value explosion, particularly in high-dimensional action spaces. These mechanisms contribute to more reliable and consistent training outcomes.
- **Improved Exploration through Intrinsic Rewards:** The self-supervised reward shaping module, which provides intrinsic rewards at meaningful anchor points, effectively guides the critic's learning in reward-sparse environments. This mechanism channels exploration towards promising regions of the state space that align with successful behaviors, accelerating policy acquisition.
- **Practical Applicability and Efficiency:** AC3’s design, employing a simple model architecture and demonstrating fast inference speeds, makes it a practical solution for real-world deployment. Its low latency allows for integration into high-frequency control loops and can accommodate techniques like temporal ensembling for smoother action execution.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a stable and data-efficient foundation for further research in hierarchical reinforcement learning, skill learning, and generalization across diverse robotic platforms and tasks. The principles of asymmetric learning and targeted intrinsic reward generation could inspire similar solutions for other challenging RL domains.

In conclusion, AC3 contributes a stable, data-efficient, and practical reinforcement learning framework for long-horizon continuous robotic manipulation tasks, effectively leveraging action chunking and addressing the complexities of sparse rewards and limited demonstrations.
