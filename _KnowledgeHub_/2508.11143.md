# Actor-Critic for Continuous Action Chunks: A Reinforcement Learning Framework for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation with Sparse Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> AC3 is an off-policy actor-critic reinforcement learning framework that directly learns continuous action sequences for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. It achieves superior success rates on complex bi-manual and tabletop tasks, demonstrating data efficiency with few expert demonstrations and stability in sparse reward settings.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning struggles with long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks due to expansive state-action spaces and the need for coherent, sequential actions.
- Learning effectively from sparse rewards, where feedback is only given at task completion, makes exploration and credit assignment difficult for agents.
- Integrating continuous action chunking into RL often leads to unstable training and issues like exploding Q-values, while imitation learning methods are limited by demonstration quality and are prone to distributional shift.

## Method

- **AC3** employs an off-policy actor-critic architecture to directly predict continuous ==action chunks== (sequences of actions), fostering coherent motion planning.
- It introduces an intra-chunk ==n-step Temporal Difference== (TD) target for the critic to stabilize value estimation and improve sample efficiency for larger chunk sizes.
- An asymmetric actor update rule trains the policy exclusively on successful trajectories to prevent degradation, complemented by a self-supervised reward shaping module that provides intrinsic rewards at meaningful 'Anchor Points' via a contrastively learned goal network.

## Results

- AC3 achieved superior success rates on 15 bi-manual mobile manipulation tasks (BiGym) and outperformed or matched baselines on 10 tabletop manipulation tasks (RLBench), demonstrating robust online learning and improvement over imitation learning.
- The framework showed high data efficiency, learning effectively from as few as 10 expert demonstrations in BiGym tasks, and maintained computational efficiency with a lightweight actor network enabling rapid inference (**2.9ms** per action chunk).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the asymmetric actor update, the intra-chunk n-step return, and appropriately calibrated self-supervised reward shaping were all critical for AC3's stable and effective performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymmetric actor updates, which restrict policy learning to successful trajectories, are crucial for stable policy improvement in sparse reward and high-dimensional state-action spaces, preventing policy collapse.
> - An intra-chunk n-step return (e.g., 4 steps for a 16-step chunk) effectively balances the bias-variance trade-off for the critic, mitigating Q-value explosion and enabling stable learning for continuous action chunks.
> - Self-supervised reward shaping, by providing intrinsic rewards at meaningful anchor points derived from successful trajectory features, significantly enhances exploration and learning stability in sparse reward environments.
