# Hybrid Feedback Sampling for Sample-Efficient Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> FS-MPC fixes sampling-based MPC's exponential sample complexity on unstable systems by proving the optimal sampling proposal equals sampling from an optimized feedback policy, then mixing local feedback samples with global isotropic samples via a computed ratio. Real-world Unitree H1 tests show it succeeds where standard MPPI fails.

## Problem

- Sampling-based MPC (MPPI) needs exponentially more samples as horizon grows for open-loop unstable systems, since open-loop rollouts diverge before reaching low-cost regions.
- Existing fixes (covariance/filtering tweaks, horizon reduction, offline-learned distributions) improve efficiency but never address the root instability in the open-loop shooting step itself.
- Gradient-based NMPC (iLQR/DDP) handles instability via feedback but fails on high-dimensional, contact-rich, non-differentiable dynamics due to noisy gradients and local minima.

## Method

- **Feedback Sampling MPC (FS-MPC)** samples control sequences by rolling out an optimized feedback controller with injected noise instead of open-loop Gaussian perturbation, proven equivalent to the optimal LTV proposal covariance.
- ==Hybrid feedback sampling mixture distribution== combines a local feedback proposal (weight alpha) with a global isotropic proposal (weight 1-alpha), with alpha set analytically from a sample-complexity bound to guarantee local-minimum discovery.
- Learned feedback extension swaps the iLQR covariance for a ==PPO-trained stochastic policy== as the local proposal, extending the hybrid scheme to non-differentiable contact-rich dynamics.

## Results

- FS-MPC improves cumulative cost over standard MPPI by **43.4%** with a suboptimal feedback controller, and beats iLQR and MPPI across Acrobot, Quadrotor, Quadruped Hill, H1-2 PNP and Allegro tasks (Table I).
- With a PPO feedback policy on humanoid Brax tasks, FS-MPC reaches **-15.65** and **-29.41** cost on running/standup versus -12.50/-27.75 for MPPI and -10.07/-21.14 for feedback-only F-MPPI.
- On real Unitree H1 hardware, MPPI fails to walk (tracking error listed as Failed) while FS-MPC achieves **0.70±0.66 m** tracking error versus iLQR's 0.93±1.00 m, and completes pick-and-place where MPPI crashes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal sampling-proposal design for unstable systems is mathematically equivalent to sampling with a feedback controller, unifying gradient-based and sampling-based MPC under one framework.
> - Feedback-only sampling can underperform even the nominal policy since injected noise plus strong feedback suppresses landscape exploration, so pure local exploitation is not sufficient.
> - Analytically balancing local exploitation against global exploration via a sample-complexity-derived mixing ratio generalizes across differentiable and non-differentiable (RL-policy) feedback sources.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC), particularly sampling-based variants like Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), has gained popularity in robotics for its parallelizability and flexibility in solving real-time optimal control problems. However, existing successful applications of these methods are often restricted to open-loop stable systems, such as autonomous vehicles or quadruped robots operating in less dynamic scenarios. When applied to inherently unstable systems like quadrotors or humanoid robots, especially with long prediction horizons, standard sampling-based MPC algorithms encounter numerical instability, leading to sample divergence and inefficient exploration of the control space. This issue results in a sample complexity that grows exponentially with the horizon.

Prior research has attempted to address sampling efficiency through various strategies:
1. **Improved covariance and filtering designs**: Methods like those in aim to guide the sampler towards stable trajectories.
2. **Horizon reduction techniques**: Approaches such as value function shaping and control sequence interpolation reduce the effective prediction horizon, thereby mitigating the exponential growth issue.
3. **Offline learning of sampling distributions**: Learning-based methods generate better proposal distributions through prior exploration.

While these techniques enhance sampling efficiency, they do not fundamentally resolve the instability inherent in the open-loop rollout process. The paper identifies that the root cause of the problem lies in the core mechanism of open-loop trajectory sampling. This work distinguishes itself by directly addressing the instability during the rollout step, proposing a sampling strategy specifically designed to manage this issue.

Furthermore, the paper situates its approach in contrast to gradient-based Nonlinear MPC (NMPC) methods (e.g., SQP, DDP, iLQR), which are effective for differentiable dynamics but struggle with the high dimensionality and inherent discontinuities of contact dynamics in complex robotic systems. Existing hybrid MPC methods, such as Tube MPPI and Safe-MPPI, primarily focus on robustness and safety, often leading to conservative sampling. In contrast, FS-MPC aims for optimality and improvement over standard sampling-based MPC, utilizing feedback controllers that are not exclusively gradient-based. The proposed method is presented as a meta-algorithm compatible with various feedback controllers, including those derived from iLQR or learned through reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, such as MPPI, when applied to high-dimensional, open-loop unstable dynamical systems with long prediction horizons. The core problem is the inherent numerical instability of the open-loop rollout step in these methods, which causes sampled states to diverge rapidly. This divergence leads to an exponential increase in the number of samples required to discover low-cost trajectories, rendering these approaches sample-inefficient and numerically unstable in complex, dynamic tasks.

The paper formalizes this problem with Theorem 1, demonstrating that for an unstable linear system, the expected cost of samples grows exponentially with the horizon, and the probability of obtaining a low-cost trajectory decreases exponentially. Consequently, the sample complexity required to obtain even one acceptable sample also grows exponentially. This theoretical analysis underscores the necessity for a more effective sampling strategy.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are to:

1. **Investigate the instability of shooting methods in sampling-based MPC**: Understand the root cause of sample inefficiency.
2. **Develop an optimal sampling proposal distribution**: Determine a sampling strategy that mitigates the exponential growth in sample complexity. The paper specifically aims to show that this optimal distribution can be realized by sampling with an optimized feedback policy.
3. **Introduce Feedback Sampling MPC (FS-MPC)**: Formulate a new algorithm based on the insight gained from optimal proposal distribution design.
4. **Incorporate a hybrid sampling design**: Develop a mechanism within FS-MPC that balances local exploitation (guided by feedback) and global exploration (standard stochastic sampling) based on system stability and computational resources. This aims to achieve both sample efficiency and global convergence for nonlinear systems.
5. **Achieve faster convergence and improved optimality**: Theoretically and empirically demonstrate that FS-MPC offers superior performance compared to standard MPPI and also surpasses standalone feedback policies in certain contexts.
6. **Validate the method on challenging tasks**: Empirically evaluate FS-MPC in diverse, high-dimensional, contact-rich, and unstable control tasks, including humanoid loco-manipulation and dexterous manipulation in both simulation and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of FS-MPC is developed through an analysis of sampling inefficiency in standard MPC, leading to the design of an optimized feedback sampling strategy that incorporates a hybrid approach for robustness and exploration.

**A. Addressing Sample Inefficiency of Sampling-based MPC**
The paper begins by formally analyzing the sample inefficiency of standard sampling-based MPC, particularly MPPI. It highlights that the "open-loop shooting" procedure, where control sequences are rolled out without intermediate feedback, is inherently unstable for open-loop unstable systems. Theorem 1 quantifies this instability for controllable unstable linear systems, showing that the expected cost of samples and the required number of samples to find a low-cost trajectory grow exponentially with the prediction horizon $H$. This exponential sample complexity motivates the need for a sampling strategy that can maintain trajectory stability.

**B. Optimal Feedback Sampling for Linear Systems**
To overcome exponential sample complexity, the paper investigates optimizing the proposal distribution $q(\cdot)$ to ensure samples remain near the nominal trajectory.
1. **Optimal Proposal Distribution Design for LTV Systems**: For Linear Time-Variant (LTV) systems with quadratic costs, the objective is formulated as minimizing the expected cost of a single sample by optimizing the proposal covariance matrix $\Sigma^k$ (Equation 3). This is a matrix optimization problem with a known closed-form solution.
2. **Optimized Proposal Distribution Restricts Divergence**: Theorem 2 demonstrates that using this optimized covariance matrix (which accounts for spatiotemporal correlations) significantly alters the scaling behavior. For a fixed-determinant constraint, the expected cost and variance of sampled trajectories scale linearly with the horizon $H$, rather than exponentially. Crucially, the probability of sampling a low-cost trajectory becomes a constant, leading to a logarithmic sample complexity.
3. **Equivalence to Feedback Sampling**: The key theoretical contribution is establishing the equivalence between the optimal proposal covariance matrix and adding feedback during the sampling process. Theorem 3 proves that the distribution of a control sequence generated by an iLQR feedback controller with injected noise is identical to the distribution obtained by sampling directly from the optimal high-dimensional Gaussian distribution with covariance $\Sigma^*_k$. This implies that dynamic programming-like recursive feedback control can generate the same optimal sample distribution as a complex, high-dimensional covariance matrix. This insight forms the foundation of Feedback Sampling MPC.

**C. Hybrid Feedback Sampling for Linearizable Systems**
While the iLQR-based feedback design is optimal for LTV systems, its direct application to nonlinear systems via linearization may lead to convergence to local minima in non-convex landscapes. To address this, the paper proposes a **hybrid feedback sampling strategy**:
1. **Mixed Proposal Distribution**: The algorithm uses a mixture model for its proposal distribution $q_{\alpha}(U^k)$ (Equation 6). This combines a "Local Search" component (sampling from an optimized distribution derived from local linearization, i.e., an iLQR-based feedback policy) and a "Global Search" component (sampling from a standard isotropic Gaussian distribution with larger variance, similar to vanilla MPPI).
2. **Dynamic Mixing Ratio ($\alpha$)**: The scalar $\alpha \in$ controls the balance between local exploitation and global exploration. The value of $\alpha$ is dynamically determined (Equation 8) based on the total sample count $N$ and a calculated minimum number of samples $N^*_{local}$ required to reliably find a local minimum (Equation 7). This scheme prioritizes local exploration when samples are limited and shifts surplus samples to global exploration as $N$ increases, ensuring both local optimality and global convergence.

**D. Hybrid Feedback Sampling for Non-differentiable Systems**
For contact-rich problems where system dynamics are non-differentiable, gradient-based methods like iLQR are not applicable. FS-MPC extends its framework to these scenarios by allowing the "local search" feedback policy to be obtained from pre-trained Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies (e.g., PPO-trained neural networks). The objective (Equation 9) still aims to minimize the expected cost of samples, but the proposal distribution $q(\cdot; U^k, \theta^*)$ is implicitly defined by the stochastic RL policy $\pi_{\theta^*}$ (Equation 10). The hybrid proposal distribution (Equation 11) and dynamic $\alpha$ remain the same, assuming the RL feedback can generate trajectories with bounded cost variance.

**E. Algorithm Realization (FS-MPC)**
The complete Hybrid Feedback Sampling algorithm (Algorithm 2) integrates these components. It iteratively optimizes the control sequence by:
1. Determining the mixing ratio $\alpha$ and splitting samples into $N_{local}$ and $N_{global}$.
2. Optimizing sample parameters (e.g., iLQR gains or RL policy parameters) for the local proposal.
3. Sampling control sequences from both local (feedback-guided) and global (isotropic Gaussian) proposals.
4. Rolling out trajectories and computing costs.
5. Updating the mean control sequence (e.g., using softmax-weighted average in MPPI).
When deployed in a receding horizon fashion, this constitutes Feedback Sampling MPC (FS-MPC).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical validation of FS-MPC across various tasks and systems, both in simulation and real-world deployment. The results demonstrate improvements in sample efficiency, stability, and task performance compared to conventional methods.

**A. FS-MPC for Linearizable Systems**
Experiments were conducted on a suite of tasks, including Acrobot, Quadrotor, Quadruped Hill, H1-2 PNP (humanoid pick-and-place), and Allegro hand manipulation. These tasks span a range from lower-dimensional to higher-dimensional, and from contact-free to contact-rich, featuring inherently unstable dynamics. For these systems, iLQR feedback was used for the local search component.

- **Performance Comparison (Table I)**: FS-MPC consistently demonstrated improved performance compared to standard MPPI across all tested tasks. For instance, on the H1-2 PNP task, FS-MPC achieved a cumulative cost of 1.06 ± 0.10, outperforming MPPI (1.55 ± 0.15) and iLQR (1.72 ± 0.06). For the Allegro task, FS-MPC (6.50 ± 0.38) showed a substantial reduction in cost compared to MPPI (12.22 ± 4.84) and iLQR (16.86 ± 2.40).
- **Benefits over MPPI and iLQR**: The feedback design in FS-MPC was observed to limit the divergence of samples, leading to more stable trajectories (visualized in Figure 4), which is a significant advantage over standard MPPI. Against iLQR, FS-MPC maintained comparable performance on simpler, contact-free tasks (Acrobot, Quadrotor) but demonstrated better performance on long-horizon, contact-rich tasks (H1-2 PNP, Allegro) where iLQR struggled with noisy gradients and local minima. The paper reported a 43.4% improvement in cumulative cost with a suboptimal feedback controller.

**B. FS-MPC for Non-differentiable Systems**
For systems with non-differentiable dynamics, where gradient-based methods are not applicable, FS-MPC was evaluated using a PPO-trained neural network as the feedback policy in the Brax simulator.
- **Performance Comparison (Table II)**: On humanoid running and standing up tasks, FS-MPC (with PPO feedback) achieved lower (more optimal) cumulative costs compared to the nominal PPO controller and standard MPPI. For humanoidrun, FS-MPC achieved -15.65 ± 0.10, outperforming PPO (-12.34 ± 0.01) and MPPI (-12.50 ± 0.00). Similarly for humanoidstandup, FS-MPC obtained -29.41 ± 0.55, better than PPO (-24.76 ± 0.01) and MPPI (-27.75 ± 0.47).
- **Significance of Hybrid Design**: The vanilla feedback MPPI (F-MPPI with PPO) performed worse than the nominal PPO controller due to instability caused by injecting noise without sufficient exploration. FS-MPC's hybrid design, which balances local exploitation and global exploration, was crucial in enabling it to outperform both the nominal PPO and MPPI in these non-differentiable scenarios.

**C. Deployment on Real Robot**
FS-MPC was validated on a full-size Unitree H1 humanoid robot for whole-body locomotion and manipulation tasks, integrating robot proprioception with a Vicon motion capture system.
- **Locomotion (Table III, Figure 5)**: For walking tasks requiring tracking a linear trajectory, standard MPPI failed to stabilize the robot due to high variance in its sampling procedure. FS-MPC, however, successfully stabilized the robot and completed the task. It showed a tracking error of 0.70 ± 0.66 m, which represented an improvement over iLQR (0.93 ± 1.00 m). The paper noted that the marginal improvement over iLQR might be attributed to limitations in the state estimation system (MoCap accuracy and communication delay), leading to high cost variance.
- **Manipulation (Figure 6)**: In tasks like holding a cylinder and placing it into a cart, MPPI generated noisy and unstable behavior, resulting in robot crashes. FS-MPC consistently produced stable and robust behavior, enabling the robot to successfully perform the manipulation tasks due to its feedback-driven sampling design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Feedback Sampling MPC (FS-MPC), an approach that addresses a fundamental challenge in sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC): the sample inefficiency and numerical instability encountered when controlling high-dimensional, open-loop unstable dynamical systems, especially over long prediction horizons.

**Significance:**
1. **Theoretical Foundation**: The work provides a theoretical analysis of MPPI's exponential sample complexity and establishes a key duality: the optimal sampling proposal distribution for Linear Time-Variant (LTV) systems is equivalent to sampling from an optimal feedback policy. This theoretical insight underpins the effectiveness of feedback in sampling-based control.
2. **Hybrid Approach for Robustness and Exploration**: FS-MPC integrates a novel hybrid sampling design that strategically balances local exploitation (guided by optimized feedback) and global exploration (via standard stochastic sampling). This balance is critical for navigating non-convex cost landscapes and handling system uncertainties, allowing the algorithm to achieve both sample efficiency and broader convergence.
3. **Generalizability**: The framework is designed to be a meta-algorithm, capable of incorporating different types of feedback controllers, including gradient-based methods like iLQR for differentiable dynamics and learned policies (e.g., PPO-trained neural networks) for non-differentiable systems. This broadens its applicability across a diverse range of robotic control problems.
4. **Empirical Validation in Challenging Scenarios**: The comprehensive experimental validation across various tasks—from unstable classical control to high-dimensional, contact-rich humanoid loco-manipulation—demonstrates FS-MPC's capability to successfully tackle problems where standard sampling-based methods typically fail. Its deployment on a real-world humanoid robot further attests to its practical viability and robustness.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Expanded Applicability of Sampling-Based MPC**: By resolving fundamental instability issues, FS-MPC can enable sampling-based MPC to be applied effectively to a wider range of complex, unstable, and contact-rich robotic systems (e.g., bipedal humanoids, agile aerial vehicles, dexterous manipulators). This could accelerate advancements in fields such as mobile robotics, human-robot interaction, and advanced manufacturing.
2. **Improved Performance and Efficiency**: The method's demonstrated ability to achieve faster convergence and improved optimality can lead to more precise and robust control in real-time applications. This has implications for enhancing the autonomy and capabilities of next-generation robotic systems.
3. **Foundation for Future Research**: The hybrid sampling paradigm, particularly its integration with learned feedback policies, opens avenues for future work. Integrating learned components such as value functions or model-based estimators into the sampling process could further enhance scalability, computational efficiency, and overall performance in high-dimensional robotic systems, pushing the boundaries of real-time optimal control.
