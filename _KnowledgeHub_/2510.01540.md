# Towards Better Optimization For Listwise Preference in Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Penn State University and TikTok Inc. developed Diffusion-LPO, a framework that extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to directly leverage listwise human preferences for text-to-image diffusion models. This approach consistently enhanced visual aesthetics and prompt alignment, yielding up to a 12% improvement in PickScore win rate over pairwise DPO on general text-to-image generation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) methods for diffusion models, such as DPO, predominantly rely on less informative pairwise human preference data.
- Human preferences are often inherently expressed as richer, more precise ranked lists among multiple options, but current DPO implementations do not fully utilize this structural information.
- Naive decomposition of listwise data into pairwise comparisons can introduce redundancy, treat all pairs equally, and potentially lead to an underestimation of aggregate reward for negative samples.

## Method

- Proposed **Diffusion-LPO**, a novel framework that extends ==Direct Preference Optimization== (DPO) to directly model listwise human preferences for text-to-image diffusion models.
- Leveraged the Plackett-Luce (PL) model to probabilistically define rankings and derived an objective function that ensures a higher-ranked item is preferred over all its lower-ranked alternatives within a given list.
- Adapted the Diffusion-DPO surrogate for tractability, approximating the log-likelihood ratio with a denoising error term, which allows the framework to generalize standard Diffusion-DPO when the list size is two.

## Results

- Diffusion-LPO consistently improved text-to-image alignment and image editing, achieving over a **12%** increase in PickScore win rate compared to pairwise Diffusion-DPO on SD1.5 and further gains on SDXL.
- Demonstrated enhanced personalized preference alignment, increasing win rates from **71.1%** to **72.3%** for held-in users and substantially from **70.3%** to **80.2%** for held-out users, indicating better generalization.
- Ablation studies confirmed Diffusion-LPO's theoretical advantage over Group-Pairwise DPO (GP-DPO), showing significant performance gains across all metrics (e.g., **80.4%** vs. **52.3%** PickScore win rate), validating the importance of direct listwise optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Listwise preference modeling (Diffusion-LPO) offers a theoretically superior optimization signal compared to merely decomposing rankings into pairwise comparisons (Group Pairwise DPO), which understates the effective margin of negatives.
> - Significant listwise preference information can be effectively extracted and utilized from existing pairwise datasets (like Pick-a-Pic) by constructing Directed Acyclic Graphs from user preferences.
> - The listwise optimization approach is generalizable, enhancing performance not only for Diffusion-DPO but also for other DPO-based methods, as demonstrated with DSPO-LPO.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion, Imagen, and DALL-E 2, have demonstrated capabilities in generating high-fidelity and semantically aligned images. While these models are initially pretrained on vast datasets, aligning their outputs more closely with specific human preferences often necessitates further fine-tuning. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as a prominent paradigm for this alignment, drawing inspiration from its success in large language models (LLMs).

Within RLHF, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has gained traction for its computational efficiency, as it bypasses the need for training an explicit reward model. DPO-based methods have been successfully adapted for diffusion models, leading to approaches such as Diffusion-DPO, DSPO, Diffusion-KTO, and MaPO, which have shown superior performance over standard supervised fine-tuning by directly incorporating preference signals.

However, a notable limitation in most existing DPO applications for diffusion models is their reliance on *pairwise* human preference data. These pairwise comparisons, often modeled using the Bradley-Terry model, simplify complex human judgments into binary wins and losses between two options. This work identifies a gap: human preferences are often inherently expressed as *ranked lists* among multiple options, which convey richer and more precise information than isolated pairs. For instance, a user's preference of image A over B, and B over C, implicitly forms a transitive ranking A > B > C. While some prior efforts have explored decomposing such rankings into multiple pairwise comparisons or utilizing auxiliary evaluators to assign reward scores, these approaches introduce computational overhead or potential biases from synthetic reward signals.

This paper addresses this limitation by proposing Diffusion-LPO, a framework designed to directly leverage listwise preference data. It aims to harness the full structural information embedded in ranked lists, presenting a generalization of pairwise DPO that could enhance existing DPO-based methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate Diffusion-LPO, a novel framework for Listwise Preference Optimization tailored for text-to-image diffusion models. This framework aims to align generative models more effectively with human preferences by directly modeling ranked lists of images rather than reducing them to individual pairwise comparisons.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and identified limitations in current preference alignment techniques for diffusion models:

1. **Underutilization of Rich Human Feedback:** While RLHF and DPO have proven effective, their application to diffusion models has predominantly relied on pairwise preference data. Human preferences, particularly in real-world scenarios, are often inherently listwise or can be aggregated into ranked lists, providing a more fine-grained and accurate representation of user judgment. For example, when a user ranks three images as x(a) > x(b) > x(c), this contains more information than just the individual pairs (x(a) > x(b)), (x(a) > x(c)), and (x(b) > x(c)).
2. **Prevalence of Implicit Ranking Information:** The authors observe that even in datasets designed for pairwise comparisons, such as the Pick-a-Pic dataset, a significant portion (56%) of annotations can be grouped into consistent rankings of size greater than two. This indicates that latent listwise preferences are abundant and can be extracted to provide richer supervision signals.
3. **Limitations of Pairwise Decomposition:** A straightforward approach to handle listwise data with pairwise optimization methods is to decompose a ranking of *m* images into *m*(m-1)/2 pairwise comparisons. However, this naive decomposition can introduce redundancy, treat all pairs with equal importance regardless of their position in the overall ranking, and potentially lead to an underestimation of the aggregate reward of negative samples. Furthermore, some existing listwise approaches rely on auxiliary evaluators to assign reward scores, which introduces additional computational overhead and resource burdens, and may not perfectly capture true human preferences.
4. **Enhancing Consistency and Fidelity:** By directly optimizing for listwise preferences, the goal is to enforce consistency across the entire ranking, encouraging each higher-ranked sample to be preferred over *all* its lower-ranked alternatives within the list. This is hypothesized to lead to a more faithful representation of human preferences and, consequently, a better-aligned diffusion model that generates images with higher visual aesthetics and prompt alignment.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Propose Diffusion-LPO as a direct preference optimization framework for listwise human feedback, building upon the Plackett-Luce model.
- Demonstrate that Diffusion-LPO, as a generalization of pairwise DPO, can be integrated into existing DPO-based methods to improve performance.
- Empirically validate the effectiveness of Diffusion-LPO across diverse text-to-image tasks, including general generation, image editing, and personalized preference alignment, showing improved alignment with human preferences over existing pairwise baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of Diffusion-LPO lies in extending the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework to accommodate listwise human preferences, leveraging the Plackett-Luce (PL) model for probabilistic ranking.

#### 3.1. Reward Under Plackett–Luce Model

Given a prompt *c* and a ranked list of *m* candidate images G = {x(1), x(2)..., x(m)} where x(1) > x(2) > ... > x(m), the PL model defines the probability of this specific ranking. This model frames the ranking process as a sequential selection, where at each step, an item is chosen from the remaining set based on its utility (latent reward *r(c, x)*). The probability is formulated as a product of softmax probabilities, ensuring that higher-ranked items are assigned greater preference over all available lower-ranked alternatives.

#### 3.2. Objective for Diffusion-LPO

The authors adapt the standard RLHF objective, which aims to maximize reward while regularizing the policy's divergence from a reference policy. By reparameterizing the latent reward *r(c, x0)* in terms of the log-likelihood ratio between the fine-tuned and reference diffusion models, and substituting this into the Plackett-Luce likelihood, the authors derive the Diffusion-LPO objective (Equation 3). This objective is a summation over the ranked list, where each term involves the difference between the log-likelihood ratio of the current item and the log-sum-exp of the log-likelihood ratios of all remaining lower-ranked items.

To make the objective tractable for diffusion models, the authors employ the Diffusion-DPO surrogate, which approximates the log-likelihood ratio with a term related to the denoising error of the fine-tuned model (ϵθ) compared to the reference model (ϵref). This leads to the final Diffusion-LPO loss (Equation 4), which is an expectation over the dataset, diffusion timesteps, and images. Importantly, when the list size *m* is 2, Diffusion-LPO simplifies to the standard Diffusion-DPO objective, demonstrating its generalized nature. The framework is also shown to be extensible to other DPO-based methods, such as DSPO (resulting in DSPO-LPO).

#### 3.3. Constructing Listwise Groups from Pairwise Data

The Pick-a-Pic dataset, primarily containing pairwise preferences, required restructuring to be suitable for listwise optimization. For each unique user and prompt, the authors aggregated all pairwise preferences (e.g., xA > xB, xB > xC) to construct a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) where nodes represent images and directed edges represent preferences. From these DAGs, consistent listwise rankings (topological paths) were extracted.
Statistical analysis of the reformulated Pick-a-Pic v1 dataset showed that out of 511,840 preference pairs, 53.71% belonged to lists of size greater than 2, making them directly applicable to Diffusion-LPO. Only 2.20% of pairs formed inconsistent (cyclic) groups, which were discarded.

#### 3.4. Advantage of Listwise Preference Modeling over Group Pairwise DPO (GP-DPO)

The paper explicitly compares Diffusion-LPO with a naive extension of pairwise DPO, termed Group Pairwise DPO (GP-DPO), which decomposes a listwise ranking into *m*(m-1)/2 individual pairwise comparisons. The theoretical analysis reveals a key difference:
- **GP-DPO**: By treating each pairwise comparison with equal importance, GP-DPO effectively averages the reward differences when a positive sample is compared against its lower-ranked alternatives (Equation 7). This leads to an *underestimation* of the aggregate reward of negative samples for a given positive sample, thereby inflating the effective margin between the positive and its grouped negatives.
- **Diffusion-LPO**: In contrast, Diffusion-LPO directly normalizes over the *entire negative group* using a softmax-like formulation (Equation 6). This approach more accurately reflects the preference for a higher-ranked item over the collective set of all lower-ranked items. The analysis (Equation 8) demonstrates that the reward assigned to negatives under GP-DPO is upper-bounded by the corresponding term in Diffusion-LPO, confirming that GP-DPO underestimates the difficulty in distinguishing a preferred item from its grouped non-preferred items within a rank.

#### 3.5. Baselines and Evaluation

The study uses Stable Diffusion 1.5 (SD1.5) and SDXL as backbones, fine-tuning them on the constructed listwise Pick-a-Pic v1 dataset. Baselines include:
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on preferred images.
- Pairwise Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024).
- DSPO (Zhu et al., 2025).
- Original pretrained SD1.5 and SDXL.
- Diffusion-DPO\* (Diffusion-DPO trained on the larger Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset for comparison).
Evaluation metrics for text-to-image alignment and image editing include PickScore, HPSV2, CLIP score, Image Reward, and Aesthetic Score. For personalized preference generation, GPT-4o is employed as a judge to rate alignment with user profiles. The authors emphasize that baselines are chosen to avoid requiring additional reward models or evaluators for fair comparison, distinguishing Diffusion-LPO from other listwise methods that do.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Diffusion-LPO across various text-to-image generation tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness compared to pairwise DPO baselines.

#### 4.1. General Text-to-Image Alignment

Table 1 presents quantitative results across three evaluation datasets: Pick-a-Pic, Parti-Prompts, and HPSV2, for both SD1.5 and SDXL models.
- **SD1.5 Performance:** Diffusion-LPO consistently achieved substantial gains over pairwise Diffusion-DPO. For example, on the Pick-a-Pic evaluation set, Diffusion-LPO improved the PickScore win rate by over 12% compared to Diffusion-DPO (80.4% vs. 68.2%). Similar improvements were observed across Parti-Prompts and HPSV2 datasets for various metrics, indicating more reliable alignment with human preferences. SFT showed competitive results on SD1.5, but its performance was inconsistent.
- **SDXL Performance:** Diffusion-LPO generally outperformed all baselines, including Diffusion-DPO\*, which was trained on a larger Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset. For instance, on Parti-Prompts, Diffusion-LPO improved PickScore win rate by 6% and HPS score win rate by 5% compared to Diffusion-DPO. SFT's performance significantly degraded on SDXL, suggesting a mismatch between its training data quality and SDXL's intrinsic generation capabilities.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 3):** Diffusion-LPO images exhibited improved visual aesthetics and finer-grained detail handling compared to other baselines. For example, in the "dolphin mid-jump" prompt, Diffusion-LPO generated smoother curvature and more accurate shapes for the dolphin tails.

#### 4.2. Image Editing

Evaluating on the InstructPix2Pix dataset with SD1.5, Diffusion-LPO demonstrated improved performance in instruction-based image editing.
- Diffusion-LPO achieved notable win-rate gains of 9.1% on PickScore and 4.4% on HPS compared to its pairwise counterpart, Diffusion-DPO. These results suggest that listwise supervision provides stronger alignment signals for adhering to editing instructions.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 8):** Diffusion-LPO produced more faithful and higher-quality edits. In one example, only Diffusion-LPO successfully transformed a paint-like image into a realistic photograph as instructed. In another, while all methods edited a cottage into a castle, Diffusion-LPO's output was visually sharper and more coherent with realistic architectural details.

#### 4.3. Personalized Preference Alignment

The study extended Diffusion-LPO to personalized preference generation, using user embeddings to condition the diffusion model. PPD (Personalized Preference Diffusion), which uses Diffusion-DPO, was compared against PPD-LPO (using Diffusion-LPO).
- PPD-LPO yielded consistent gains: the win rate improved from 71.1% to 72.3% for held-in users and from 70.3% to 80.2% for held-out users (unseen during training). This indicates that listwise optimization not only enhances personalization for known users but also substantially improves generalization to unseen users.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4):** PPD-LPO further enhanced both the visual appeal and fine-grained details of generated images, aligning more closely with specific user profiles.

#### 4.4. Ablation: Rank Enforcement from Diffusion-LPO

An ablation study compared Diffusion-LPO against Group-Pairwise DPO (GP-DPO) on SD1.5. As predicted by the theoretical analysis in Section 4.2, Diffusion-LPO significantly outperformed GP-DPO across all evaluation metrics.
- For example, on the Pick-a-Pic dataset, Diffusion-LPO achieved an 80.4% PickScore win rate compared to GP-DPO's 52.3% (Table 2). This result validates that directly optimizing with the Plackett-Luce model, which properly normalizes over the entire negative group, is more effective than pairwise decomposition, confirming the importance of structural rank information.

#### 4.5. Generalization from Other DPO Frameworks (Appendix E)

The concept of listwise preference optimization was also applied to DSPO, resulting in DSPO-LPO. This extension demonstrated performance improvements over the original pairwise DSPO on SDXL across all metrics (Table 4), indicating that the listwise approach can generalize to and boost other DPO-based methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Diffusion-LPO, a framework for listwise preference optimization in text-to-image diffusion models, addressing a significant limitation of existing pairwise DPO methods. The findings carry several significances and potential impacts:

1. **Advancement in Generative Model Alignment:** By effectively leveraging the richer information contained in ranked lists of human preferences, Diffusion-LPO represents a notable step forward in aligning complex generative models with nuanced human judgments. This moves beyond the coarse binary comparisons that often oversimplify human feedback.
2. **Improved Output Quality and Alignment:** The consistent and substantial improvements demonstrated across various tasks—including general text-to-image generation, image editing, and personalized preference alignment—highlight Diffusion-LPO's ability to produce images with superior visual aesthetics, better adherence to prompts, and more precise instruction following. This can lead to more satisfying and effective user experiences with generative AI tools.
3. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The superior performance on held-out users in personalized preference alignment indicates that listwise optimization can improve the model's ability to generalize to unseen user preferences. This is crucial for deploying personalized generative AI systems at scale.
4. **Computational Efficiency without External Models:** Unlike some other approaches that attempt to incorporate richer ranking signals, Diffusion-LPO achieves its performance gains without the need for training separate reward models or relying on auxiliary evaluators. This maintains the computational efficiency benefits inherent in the DPO framework, making it a practical solution for real-world applications.
5. **Broad Applicability and Extensibility:** As Diffusion-LPO generalizes the pairwise DPO objective, its underlying principles can potentially be applied to enhance a wider family of DPO-based methods. The successful demonstration with DSPO-LPO supports this potential for broader integration.
6. **Better Data Utilization:** The methodology for constructing listwise preferences from existing pairwise datasets (like Pick-a-Pic) demonstrates how to extract and utilize richer, implicit ranking information from readily available data, making existing preference datasets more valuable.

In conclusion, Diffusion-LPO offers a robust and effective method for fine-tuning text-to-image diffusion models with human feedback. Its ability to extract and leverage the full context of ranked preferences contributes to more precise and higher-quality generative outputs, potentially impacting the development of more aligned, user-centric, and versatile AI generation systems. Future work, as noted by the authors, could explore larger list sizes and a broader range of datasets to further validate and extend its applicability.
