# When Visualizing is the First Step to Reasoning: MIRA, a Benchmark for Visual Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> The MIRA benchmark introduces 546 multimodal problems requiring intermediate visual representations for reasoning, revealing that state-of-the-art MLLMs achieve only 16.5% average accuracy without visual guidance. However, providing human-annotated visual chain-of-thought improves performance by a relative 33.7%, indicating a critical need for models capable of integrating visual generation into complex problem-solving.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) primarily rely on text-only chain-of-thought reasoning, which is inefficient and limited for problems with intrinsic visual or spatial components.
- Existing benchmarks for MLLMs mainly evaluate visual perception or superficial reasoning, failing to assess models' ability to generate and utilize intermediate visual representations for complex problem-solving.
- MLLMs lack the human-like cognitive ability to "draw to think," creating visual aids to guide reasoning for visually-intensive tasks.

## Method

- Introduction of ==MIRA==, a benchmark featuring 546 multimodal problems across 20 task types, explicitly designed to require intermediate visual information for successful reasoning.
- Each problem in MIRA is meticulously constructed with ==human-annotated, step-wise visual clues== to facilitate evaluation in a ==Visual Chain-of-Thought (Visual-CoT)== setting.
- A ==three-level diagnostic evaluation protocol== (Direct, Text-CoT, and Simulated Visual-CoT) is established to provide granular insights into MLLM capabilities and the impact of visual guidance.

## Results

- Advanced closed-source MLLMs, including GPT-5, achieve an average accuracy of only **16.5%** on MIRA with direct inputs, highlighting their limitations in visual reasoning.
- Standard Text-CoT proves insufficient for visually-intensive tasks on MIRA, offering minimal gains and sometimes leading to performance degradation (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro saw an **18.3%** drop).
- Providing models with human-annotated intermediate visual cues (Simulated Visual-CoT) consistently improved performance, yielding an average relative gain of **33.7%** across models and tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a fundamental lack of capability for complex visual reasoning that necessitates "drawing to think," struggling significantly on the MIRA benchmark.
> - Text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is largely ineffective for MIRA's visually-intensive problems and can sometimes degrade MLLM performance compared to direct answering.
> - Providing explicit human-annotated intermediate visual cues, as in the Simulated Visual-CoT setting, substantially enhances MLLM reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The MIRA benchmark positions itself at the forefront of multimodal AI research by addressing a critical, yet underexplored, aspect of reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): the ability to leverage and generate *intermediate visual representations* for complex problem-solving. This work significantly advances beyond existing paradigms by explicitly focusing on "visual chain-of-thought" (Visual-CoT).

**Evolution of Reasoning in AI:**

1. **Text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** The concept of Chain-of-Thought prompting, introduced for Large Language Models (LLMs), revolutionized their reasoning capabilities by enabling them to decompose complex problems into sequential, intermediate textual steps. This approach has shown remarkable success in arithmetic, commonsense, and multi-hop question answering tasks. However, as MIRA highlights, these methods are "purely textual," meaning every intermediate step must be verbalized. This is often an "awkward and lossy medium" for problems with inherent visual or spatial components.

2. **Perception-focused MLLM Benchmarks:** Prior MLLM research has largely been driven by benchmarks like Visual Question Answering (VQA), image captioning, and visual grounding. While crucial for evaluating models' ability to *understand* and *describe* visual inputs, these tasks primarily test perception and superficial reasoning (e.g., identifying objects, describing scenes). Even datasets incorporating multi-step reasoning (e.g., ScienceQA) typically maintain text-only intermediate steps and do not necessitate visual generation. Existing efforts that incorporate visual CoT elements often fall short by emphasizing pattern discovery or simple Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs) with limited visual clues.

3. **Integrating Visual Cues (Algorithmic Level):** Some preliminary efforts have explored integrating visual cues at an algorithmic level. Techniques like Visual CoT (augmenting textual rationales with bounding boxes), tool-augmented methods (e.g., Visual ChatGPT, VisProg, ViperGPT leveraging external drawing/vision tools), and frameworks like Visual Sketchpad (executing code to generate diagrams) represent steps towards visual reasoning. However, these are often "fundamentally bounded by the capabilities of the tools they rely on" and lack a systematic benchmark for open-ended reasoning scenarios that intrinsically *require* visual generation.

**MIRA's Contribution and Niche:**

MIRA specifically addresses the critical gap where "natural language sometimes becomes an awkward and lossy medium for expressing intermediate states." It argues that many real-world problems—involving spatial reasoning, geometric manipulation, or physical simulation—are better solved by humans through "drawing to think." MIRA introduces a benchmark that explicitly requires models to generate (or at least utilize) intermediate visual images (sketches, diagrams, path drawings) to guide their reasoning.

By providing 546 multimodal problems with human-annotated intermediate visual images and a three-level evaluation protocol (Direct, Text-CoT, Simulated Visual-CoT), MIRA moves beyond:
- **Text-only CoT:** Demonstrating its inadequacy for visually-intensive tasks.
- **Perception-focused MLLMs:** Exposing their limitations in complex visual reasoning beyond simple understanding.
- **Tool-augmented methods:** Providing a benchmark that tests the *intrinsic* capability for visual reasoning rather than relying on external, pre-programmed tools.

The benchmark aims to push MLLMs toward a "unified multimodal paradigm geared towards 'thinking while drawing'," inspiring the development of models that can seamlessly integrate visual and textual reasoning, much like human cognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the MIRA benchmark is to systematically evaluate and foster the development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that can perform complex reasoning by generating and utilizing intermediate visual representations. This objective is driven by a fundamental motivation: to bridge the gap between how humans intuitively solve visually-intensive problems through "drawing to think" and the current limitations of MLLMs that primarily rely on textual reasoning.

The core motivations and objectives can be broken down as follows:

1. **Exposing the Limitations of Text-only Reasoning:** The paper highlights that while Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting has significantly improved LLMs' reasoning, existing methods are "purely textual." This verbalization proves inherently limiting for problems that are intrinsically visual, involving spatial relationships, geometric manipulation, or physical simulations. MIRA aims to demonstrate that relying solely on textual prompts is often inefficient, awkward, and lossy for such tasks, thus underscoring a critical weakness in current MLLMs.

2. **Establishing a Benchmark for Visual Chain-of-Thought (Visual-CoT):** The primary objective is to create a novel benchmark, MIRA (Multimodal Imagination for Reasoning Assessment), specifically designed for scenarios where "generating intermediate visual images is essential for successful reasoning." This includes tasks requiring sketches, structural diagrams, or path drawings as intermediate steps.

3. **Mirroring Human Problem-Solving:** MIRA is motivated by observing how humans solve complex problems by creating visual aids (e.g., sketching a diagram to understand forces, drawing a path for a rolling die). The benchmark aims to assess MLLMs' capacity to emulate this "drawing to think" cognitive process, moving beyond mere perception to genuine visual reasoning.

4. **Identifying Gaps in Current MLLMs:** By introducing challenging problems across diverse domains (Euclidean Geometry, Physics-Based Reasoning, Abstract Spatial & Logical Puzzles, Causal Transformations), MIRA seeks to expose the "fundamental gap between perception-oriented evaluation and true visual reasoning." The paper aims to quantify how current state-of-the-art MLLMs, even "strongest private models (e.g., GPT-5, o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro)," struggle significantly when such visual imagination is required.

5. **Proposing a Diagnostic Evaluation Protocol:** To gain deeper insights into model capabilities, MIRA proposes a unified three-level evaluation protocol (Direct, Text-CoT, and Simulated Visual-CoT). This protocol allows researchers to decouple the influence of visual information from textual generation abilities, providing a clear path to understand *why* models fail and *how much* visual cues contribute to performance.

6. **Guiding Future MLLM Development:** Ultimately, MIRA's motivation is to serve as a reproducible evaluation platform and metric system to "drive research toward a unified multimodal paradigm geared towards 'thinking while drawing'." It aims to encourage the development of MLLMs that can natively generate and tightly integrate high-quality intermediate visual states throughout their reasoning process, pushing the boundaries of multimodal intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MIRA introduces a comprehensive benchmark and a novel evaluation protocol designed to assess Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in tasks that fundamentally require intermediate visual reasoning.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Data Design and Construction Details

The MIRA benchmark, encompassing 546 multimodal problems across 20 distinct task types, is meticulously designed around three core principles:

1. **Requirement of Intermediate Visual Information:** Each problem is constructed such that its successful resolution necessitates the generation or utilization of intermediate visual cues. These visual-CoT elements are analogous to human "scratchpad diagrams" (e.g., force-body diagrams, traced paths, geometric constructions). This directly contrasts with traditional benchmarks where problems can often be solved through language alone.
2. **Annotated Step-wise Visual Clues:** To enable evaluation in a Visual-CoT setting, every instance is paired with human-annotated, step-wise visual clues. These can be single pivotal images or a sequence of visual trajectories, allowing for the analysis of both static and dynamic visual reasoning. Examples include tracking a die's movement or visualizing overlapping geometric shapes.
3. **Strict Human Annotation and Cross-Validation:** High data quality is ensured through a rigorous construction pipeline (Figure 3).
 - **Prototype Questions & Image Generation:** Problems are initially created manually by graduate-level researchers, drawing inspiration from online visual puzzle communities and brain-teaser websites. These are complemented by programmatically generated problems to control difficulty.
 - **Manual Refinement:** Initial image inputs are refined for clarity and quality using image editing tools (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Flash).
 - **Construction and Validation:** A rigorous quality control process, including cross-review and conflict resolution, ensures each problem has a single, unambiguous ground-truth answer and a reliable visual reasoning trajectory.

The benchmark spans four challenging domains:
- **Euclidean Geometry (EG):** Tasks like Convex Hull, Overlap, Mirror Pattern, Cubes Count.
- **Physics-Based Reasoning (PBR):** Tasks like Billiards, Electric Charge, Mirror Clock, Gear Rotation.
- **Abstract Spatial & Logical Puzzles (ASLP):** Tasks like Unfolded Cube, Defuse A Bomb, Puzzle, Trailer Cubes Count.
- **Causal Transformations (CT):** Tasks like Paper Airplane, Rolling Dice (Top/Sum/Two).

#### 3.2. Evaluation Protocol

MIRA employs a three-level diagnostic evaluation protocol to provide granular insights into MLLM capabilities:

1. **Level 1: Direct Evaluation:** Models receive only the input image (Iq) and question (Tq) and must produce the final answer directly. This assesses the model's baseline, end-to-end problem-solving ability without any explicit reasoning guidance.
2. **Level 2: Text-CoT Reasoning:** Models are prompted to first generate a textual chain of thought (step-by-step rationales) and then provide the final answer. This tests the effectiveness of conventional text-based reasoning on MIRA's visually-intensive tasks. The paper explores two sub-settings:
 - **General Template (Tgen):** A generic CoT prompt applied uniformly.
 - **Specialized Template (Tspec):** Task-specific CoT prompts designed to align more closely with visual reasoning steps, probing the upper bound of text-only guidance.
3. **Level 3: Simulated Visual-CoT Reasoning:** This is a crucial setting where models are provided with manually annotated intermediate visual images (CoT images) alongside the input image and question. The model is prompted to reason based on these visual clues before giving the final answer. This setting is "simulated" because current MLLMs cannot yet reliably *generate* these intermediate visuals themselves, but it helps evaluate their capacity to *utilize* such visual information if provided.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Baseline Models:** A diverse cohort of MLLMs is evaluated, categorized into:
 - **Closed-Source SOTA MLLMs:** Top-tier commercial models representing the state-of-the-art (e.g., GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, Claude 4 Opus/Sonnet, o3, Seed1.5/1.6, Qwen-VL-Max).
 - **Open-Weight MLLMs (Understanding):** Models strong in visual comprehension but lacking general-purpose image generation (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, GLM 4.5 V).
 - **Open-Weight Unified MLLMs (Understanding & Generation):** Emerging models with both comprehension and generation capabilities (e.g., Bagel, Janus-pro), whose performance in Visual-CoT simulates their potential to use self-generated visual aids.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Micro-averaged accuracy is used. A tiered pipeline extracts answers, prioritizing `<answer></answer>` tags, falling back to heuristic regular expressions, and finally using GPT-4o-2024-11-20 as a semantic judge for ambiguous outputs.
- **Probing Upper Bounds:**
 - **Pass@k and Majority Voting:** Models generate *k* different reasoning paths and answers for the same problem. Success is counted if at least one answer is correct (Pass@k), or if the majority of *k* responses are correct. This investigates whether failures are due to accidental errors or fundamental capability gaps.
 - **Task-Specific Text-CoT Prompts:** As mentioned in Level 2, specialized textual prompts are designed to better align with visual reasoning, to see if advanced prompt engineering can compensate for the lack of visual intermediate steps.

This comprehensive methodology allows MIRA to not only benchmark current MLLM performance but also diagnose their strengths and weaknesses in visual reasoning, providing clear directions for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MLLMs on the MIRA benchmark yields several critical insights into the current state of multimodal reasoning, particularly regarding the role of intermediate visual information.

1. **MIRA is Intrinsically Challenging for Current MLLMs:**
 - Even the most advanced closed-source models (e.g., GPT-5) achieve only an average of **16.5% accuracy** with direct inputs (Level 1 evaluation). No model surpasses 20% accuracy in this setting.
 - Specific categories, particularly **Abstract Spatial & Logical Puzzles (ASLP)**, prove exceptionally difficult, with an average accuracy of only **9.5%** for private models, compared to 16.1% on other categories. This highlights that MLLMs struggle significantly with tasks requiring meticulous visual understanding and fine-grained spatial reasoning.

2. **Text-CoT Alone is Insufficient for Visual Reasoning Tasks:**
 - Contrary to its benefits in purely textual reasoning, standard Text-CoT (Level 2) offers minimal to no advantage on MIRA and can even degrade performance for stronger models.
 - For example, Gemini 2.5 Pro and o3 experience performance *degradation* of 18.3% and 14.0% respectively when using Text-CoT compared to direct input.
 - In harder categories like Puzzles and Causal Transformations, Text-CoT negatively impacts proprietary models' accuracy by an average of 4.2% and 2.6% respectively. This suggests that for tasks demanding auxiliary visual processes, textual rationales are an inadequate substitute.

3. **Simulated Visual-CoT Significantly Enhances Performance:**
 - Providing human-annotated intermediate visual cues (Level 3) leads to **substantial and consistent performance improvements** across nearly all models.
 - Models achieve an average relative gain of **33.7%** across all tasks and models with Visual-CoT. For instance, GPT-5-mini improves from 13.7% to 23.2% accuracy.
 - The benefits are particularly pronounced in challenging categories: Physics-Based Reasoning (PBR) tasks see a striking jump from 20.7% to 40.0% accuracy with visual reasoning patterns. Puzzle tasks also show improvements, albeit more modestly.
 - Open-weight models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, GLM 4.5V) also benefit from Visual-CoT (average 9.9% direct vs. 13.0% Visual-CoT), though their gains are more limited, possibly due to smaller parameter scales and less training on interleaved visual-CoT data.
 - Open-weight unified models like Bagel and Janus-Pro, despite their image generation capabilities, show relative gains of 17.3% and 46.9% respectively with Visual-CoT, indicating that these visual aids help them better understand and reason, even if they cannot generate them.

4. **Limited Gains from Expanding Search Space and Prompt Engineering:**
 - Attempts to probe the models' "upper-bound potential" using Pass@k and majority voting show that while performance increases from k=1 to k=4 (average 15.3% improvement), gains nearly converge between k=4 and k=8 (only 3.0% average). Stronger models exhibit even smaller gains from wider search spaces, suggesting their failures are due to a "fundamental lack of capability" rather than mere reasoning errors.
 - Designing specialized Text-CoT prompts that align with Visual-CoT guidance (Tspec) yields only modest average gains of 1.4% for closed-source models and 1.5% for open-weight models, and can even be negative in some cases. This further underscores the "inherent limitation of text-only guidance" when visual information is indispensable.

5. **Case Study Reinforcement:** A case study on a Euclidean Geometry task (Convex Hull) explicitly demonstrates how GPT-5 struggles with abstract textual descriptions, relying on vague terms, while the provision of a simple "Visual-CoT Image" transforms its reasoning into direct, accurate analysis.

In summary, MIRA effectively reveals that current MLLMs lack the "think-while-drawing" capability crucial for complex visual reasoning. While Text-CoT is ineffective, providing explicit visual cues dramatically improves performance, highlighting the critical role of visual imagination and offering a temporary solution until models can generate these visuals autonomously.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MIRA benchmark represents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal artificial intelligence, with profound implications for the development and evaluation of future MLLMs. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Exposing a Fundamental Gap in MLLM Capabilities:** MIRA definitively highlights a critical deficiency in current MLLMs: their inability to perform complex reasoning that requires generating and utilizing intermediate visual representations, akin to humans "drawing to think." This benchmark moves beyond evaluating mere visual perception or text-based reasoning, demonstrating that state-of-the-art models, even "GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and o3," fall short when visual imagination is truly necessary. This finding is crucial for redirecting research efforts.

2. **Driving the Development of "Thinking While Drawing" Paradigms:** By explicitly defining and evaluating "Visual Chain-of-Thought," MIRA provides a concrete target for future MLLM architectures. It underscores the urgent need for a "unified multimodal paradigm geared towards 'thinking while drawing'"—models capable of autonomously generating high-quality intermediate visual states (sketches, diagrams) during their reasoning process and tightly coupling these visuals with subsequent language-based reasoning. This will inspire innovation in architectural design, training methodologies, and data curation.

3. **Providing a Rigorous and Reproducible Evaluation Platform:** MIRA offers a meticulously curated dataset of 546 problems across 20 diverse task types, annotated with ground-truth answers and intermediate visual steps. Coupled with its three-level diagnostic evaluation protocol (Direct, Text-CoT, Simulated Visual-CoT), it provides a standardized, reproducible, and comprehensive platform for benchmarking and comparing new MLLMs. This structured evaluation is vital for tracking progress and identifying specific areas for improvement in visual reasoning.

4. **Informing Dataset Design and Training Strategies:** The benchmark's findings emphasize that current MLLMs are either not adequately trained on interleaved Visual-CoT data or lack the architectural capacity to fully leverage it. This will catalyze the creation of new, larger-scale multimodal datasets that explicitly incorporate visual reasoning trajectories and prompt the development of novel training objectives that reward both visual generation and its integration into the reasoning chain.

5. **Advancing Multimodal General Intelligence:** Bridging the gap between textual and visual reasoning is a crucial step towards developing more human-like general artificial intelligence. Problems requiring "drawing to think" are ubiquitous in fields like engineering, design, science, and mathematics. MIRA's success in stimulating this research will enable AI systems to tackle a broader spectrum of real-world problems that currently remain intractable for text-only or perception-focused models.

6. **Highlighting the Potential for Open-Source Models:** While open-weight models currently lag behind their closed-source counterparts on MIRA, the benchmark provides a clear pathway for their improvement. By offering a standardized evaluation, MIRA encourages the open-source community to develop and release models specifically designed with Visual-CoT capabilities, fostering broader access to advanced multimodal AI.

In conclusion, MIRA is not merely another benchmark; it is a foundational piece of work that redefines the scope of multimodal reasoning. By exposing the limitations of existing MLLMs and providing a clear path forward, it will significantly shape the research agenda for developing AI systems that can truly "imagine" and "reason" in a manner more akin to human cognition.
