# When Visualizing is the First Step to Reasoning: MIRA, a Benchmark for Visual Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> The MIRA benchmark introduces 546 multimodal problems requiring intermediate visual representations for reasoning, revealing that state-of-the-art MLLMs achieve only 16.5% average accuracy without visual guidance. However, providing human-annotated visual chain-of-thought improves performance by a relative 33.7%, indicating a critical need for models capable of integrating visual generation into complex problem-solving.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) primarily rely on text-only chain-of-thought reasoning, which is inefficient and limited for problems with intrinsic visual or spatial components.
- Existing benchmarks for MLLMs mainly evaluate visual perception or superficial reasoning, failing to assess models' ability to generate and utilize intermediate visual representations for complex problem-solving.
- MLLMs lack the human-like cognitive ability to "draw to think," creating visual aids to guide reasoning for visually-intensive tasks.

## Method

- Introduction of ==MIRA==, a benchmark featuring 546 multimodal problems across 20 task types, explicitly designed to require intermediate visual information for successful reasoning.
- Each problem in MIRA is meticulously constructed with ==human-annotated, step-wise visual clues== to facilitate evaluation in a ==Visual Chain-of-Thought (Visual-CoT)== setting.
- A ==three-level diagnostic evaluation protocol== (Direct, Text-CoT, and Simulated Visual-CoT) is established to provide granular insights into MLLM capabilities and the impact of visual guidance.

## Results

- Advanced closed-source MLLMs, including GPT-5, achieve an average accuracy of only **16.5%** on MIRA with direct inputs, highlighting their limitations in visual reasoning.
- Standard Text-CoT proves insufficient for visually-intensive tasks on MIRA, offering minimal gains and sometimes leading to performance degradation (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro saw an **18.3%** drop).
- Providing models with human-annotated intermediate visual cues (Simulated Visual-CoT) consistently improved performance, yielding an average relative gain of **33.7%** across models and tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a fundamental lack of capability for complex visual reasoning that necessitates "drawing to think," struggling significantly on the MIRA benchmark.
> - Text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is largely ineffective for MIRA's visually-intensive problems and can sometimes degrade MLLM performance compared to direct answering.
> - Providing explicit human-annotated intermediate visual cues, as in the Simulated Visual-CoT setting, substantially enhances MLLM reasoning capabilities.
