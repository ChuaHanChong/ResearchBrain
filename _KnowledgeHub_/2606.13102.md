# FTP-1: A Generalist Foundation Tactile Policy Across Tactile Sensors for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new generalist foundation tactile policy, FTP-1, enables robots to learn and transfer tactile manipulation skills across various tactile sensors and robot platforms. It achieved an average success rate improvement of +17.2% on seen sensor setups and +31.6% on unseen tactile sensors in contact-rich tasks, showcasing robust generalization and reactive control.

## Problem

- Generalist robotic policies often lack the fine-grained tactile perception necessary for robustly performing contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- Existing tactile policies are largely sensor-specific, failing to generalize across the wide variety of tactile hardware due to heterogeneous signal formats, resolutions, and morphologies.
- The absence of a unified, transferable tactile policy baseline hinders systematic development and comparison of tactile manipulation research.

## Method

- Introduced **FTP-1**, a multi-expert foundation policy, integrating language, RGB vision, proprioception, and diverse tactile sensor inputs into a unified action space.
- Developed a ==Morphology-Aware Tactile Token Space== (MTTS) with sensor-specific heterogeneous encoders to abstract and standardize diverse tactile signals (image, array, state-type) into common token representations.
- Employed an independent, pretrained 300M-parameter Transformer as a dedicated tactile expert, trained on the new 3,000-hour FTP-1-Dataset, to process and fuse tactile tokens effectively.

## Results

- FTP-1 demonstrated superior performance on seen tactile sensor setups, achieving an average success rate improvement of **+17.2%** in contact-rich manipulation tasks compared to existing baselines.
- The policy exhibited strong transferability to previously unseen tactile sensor setups, showing a **+31.6%** higher average success rate than baselines without FTP-1's pretraining.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the learned transferable tactile knowledge from pretraining was responsible for FTP-1's robust adaptation, leading to more stable actions and improved reactive control during critical manipulation phases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified, morphology-aware token space can effectively abstract and standardize heterogeneous tactile signals, enabling a single policy to interpret data from diverse sensor types and anatomies.
> - Utilizing an independent, pretrained tactile expert within a multi-expert architecture is crucial for learning transferable tactile skills across varied hardware and prevents interference with pre-existing vision-language knowledge.
> - Large-scale pretraining on a diverse, heterogeneous tactile dataset is essential for acquiring generalizable tactile manipulation knowledge, leading to robust adaptation to unseen tactile sensors and contact-rich tasks.
