# A Primer on SO(3) Action Representations in Deep Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper systematically evaluates SO(3) action representations in deep reinforcement learning across three continuous-control algorithms and various robotic tasks. Researchers identified the local delta tangent vector ("s_τ") as the most reliable representation, consistently achieving high performance by avoiding issues like exploration warping and conflicting policy gradients seen with other representations.

## Problem

- Representing 3D rotations (SO(3)) within deep reinforcement learning (DRL) action spaces is geometrically complex, lacking a global, smooth, minimal parameterization.
- Existing SO(3) representations (Euler angles, quaternions, rotation matrices, Lie algebra coordinates) have known trade-offs in supervised learning, but their implications for DRL actions were largely unexamined.
- The impact of SO(3) representations on DRL-specific phenomena like exploration dynamics, entropy regularization, and training stability remained unclear.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical evaluation of four common **SO**(3) action representations (Rotation Matrices, Unit Quaternions, Tangent-Space, Euler Angles) for both global and delta actions.
- Compared representations across three widely used continuous-control DRL algorithms (PPO, SAC, TD3) and both dense and sparse reward settings.
- Evaluated performance in an idealized pure-rotation environment and three realistic robotics benchmarks (drone control, RoboSuite manipulation, Fetch orientation tasks).

## Results

- The local delta tangent vector representation ("s_τ") proved to be the most reliable and generally best-performing choice, consistently achieving top or competitive results across all algorithms, reward settings, and environments.
- Global rotation matrices generally performed well, often ranking second, while quaternions frequently exhibited decreased convergence stability due to conflicting policy gradients arising from their double-cover property.
- Euler angles were largely a poor choice due to severe discontinuities and singularities, demonstrating significantly lower success rates (e.g., **32.3%** vs. **69.8%** for "s_τ" in PickAndPlaceOrient) for tasks requiring broad SO(3) coverage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The choice of SO(3) representation profoundly influences DRL exploration dynamics and training stability, with projections of Euclidean neural network outputs onto SO(3) significantly warping action distributions.
> - Standard Euclidean entropy regularization can be suboptimal for SO(3) actions, particularly for off-policy algorithms like SAC, potentially leading to poor performance or policy collapse.
> - Maintaining local smoothness and ensuring discontinuities or singularities are outside the agent's action space is more critical than global mathematical properties for effective DRL training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a crucial and persistent challenge in machine learning, particularly in robotics and embodied intelligence: the accurate and efficient representation of 3D rotations, specifically within the context of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) actions. The set of all 3D rotations forms the Lie group SO(3), a compact, curved, and non-commutative manifold that inherently lacks a global, smooth, minimal parameterization. This geometric complexity means that common representations like Euler angles, quaternions, rotation matrices, and Lie algebra coordinates each come with distinct trade-offs, such as singularities, discontinuities, or redundancy.

The existing research landscape has extensively studied these trade-offs for *supervised learning* tasks, such as rotation estimation or feature prediction (e.g., Zhou et al., 2019; Peretroukhin et al., 2020; Brégier, 2021; Geist et al., 2024). Geist et al. (2024) specifically offer a comprehensive overview that links mathematical properties of representations to observed performance in supervised settings, providing concrete recommendations.

However, a significant gap remains concerning the implications of these SO(3) representations for *actions* in DRL. While some intuition from supervised learning might transfer, action representations in RL introduce unique challenges. They fundamentally shape the exploration dynamics of stochastic policies, interact with exploration noise, and influence training stability and convergence. Prior DRL works that touched upon SO(3) action representations were often narrow in scope: Alhousani et al. (2023a;b) proposed specific action representations for limited problems, and Schuck et al. (2025a) explored the topic but restricted their investigation to Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients (DDPG) under sparse rewards, a specific algorithm and reward setting.

This paper directly fills this critical gap by providing the first *general, systematic evaluation* of SO(3) action representations across *three widely used continuous-control DRL algorithms* (PPO, SAC, TD3), considering both *dense and sparse reward formulations*. It delves into RL-specific phenomena like exploration warping, entropy regularization interactions, and projection mechanisms, which are often overlooked in supervised learning contexts. By distilling concrete, implementation-ready guidelines, this work moves beyond theoretical discussions to offer practical advice for practitioners, thereby significantly advancing the state-of-the-art in applying DRL to complex robotic control tasks requiring full pose control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the ubiquitous need for robotic control tasks to operate on 3D orientations (e.g., manipulating objects, controlling drone attitudes). The authors recognize that while the geometric challenges of SO(3) are well-known, and representations have been studied in supervised learning, their implications for *actions* within deep reinforcement learning remain largely unexplored and unclear. The goal is to demystify this complex area and provide practical guidance to practitioners.

The key objectives of the paper can be summarized as follows:

1. **Systematic Evaluation Across Standard RL Algorithms:** To conduct a comprehensive evaluation of SO(3) action representations using three widely adopted continuous-control algorithms: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), and Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients (TD3). These algorithms are chosen for their relevance to robotics, where learned policies are frequently deployed on physical hardware.
2. **In-depth Analysis of Representation-Induced Effects:** To investigate *why* different action representations lead to varying training performance. This involves analyzing how the mapping from Euclidean neural network outputs to valid SO(3) rotations influences critical RL mechanisms, including:
 - **Exploration dynamics:** How representations shape the stochasticity and coverage of the action space.
 - **Interaction with entropy regularization:** How the entropy bonus, crucial for algorithms like SAC, is affected by non-Euclidean action spaces and projections.
 - **Training stability:** The impact of representations on convergence and consistency across runs.
 - **Action projections and scaling:** The effects of projecting off-manifold network outputs onto valid rotations and scaling actions to comply with actuation constraints.
3. **Distillation of Practical Guidelines:** To synthesize the empirical findings into clear, implementation-ready guidelines for selecting and utilizing SO(3) action representations in deep reinforcement learning. These guidelines aim to help practitioners make informed decisions and avoid common pitfalls when designing policies for orientation control.

In essence, the authors aim to provide a "primer" that simplifies the process of getting orientation control right in RL, empowering researchers and engineers to build more effective and robust robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper employs a systematic and multi-faceted empirical study, combining controlled idealized environments with realistic robotic benchmarks, to evaluate SO(3) action representations in DRL.

**Algorithms Investigated:**
The study focuses on three leading continuous-control DRL algorithms:
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** A policy gradient method known for its stability and on-policy learning.
- **Soft Actor-Critic (SAC):** An off-policy maximum entropy DRL algorithm known for its sample efficiency and robustness.
- **Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients (TD3):** An off-policy deterministic policy gradient algorithm that addresses function approximation errors.

**SO(3) Action Representations Compared:**
The authors systematically compare four common representations, considering both "global actions" (desired orientation in the global frame) and "delta actions" (intrinsic delta rotation relative to the current state):
- **Rotation Matrices (R):** A 9-dimensional representation, unique and smooth, but over-parameterized and requiring orthonormalization.
- **Unit Quaternions (q):** A 4-dimensional representation (with a unit-norm constraint), smooth and numerically robust, but exhibiting a double-cover of SO(3).
- **Tangent-Space (Lie Algebra Coordinates, `τ`):** A minimal 3-dimensional representation using the exponential map. Evaluated as `E_τ` (global) and `s_τ` (local/delta frame). Locally smooth but globally prone to singularities at large angles.
- **Euler Angles (φ, θ, ψ):** A minimal 3-dimensional representation, intuitive but suffering from order dependence, angle wrapping, and gimbal lock singularities.

**Reward Structures:**
Evaluations are conducted under both dense and sparse reward settings. For sparse rewards with off-policy algorithms (SAC and TD3), Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) is utilized to facilitate learning.

**Evaluation Environments:**
1. **Idealized Pure-Rotation Environment:** A custom-designed, goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a state space of `SO(3) × SO(3)` (current and goal orientations) and a configurable `SO(3)` action space. The environment dynamics steer the agent towards a desired orientation along the shortest geodesic path, with a maximum angular step `α_max`. This environment allows for isolating the effects of action representations on rotational dynamics, separate from other robotic complexities. Rewards are either dense (negative geodesic distance to goal) or sparse (0 for success within tolerance, -1 otherwise). Observations are flattened rotation matrices.
2. **Robotics Benchmarks:** To validate findings in more realistic scenarios, the study uses three different robotic platforms and tasks:
 - **Drone Control (PPO):** Benchmarked on two tasks using vectorized versions of `safe-control-gym` (Yuan et al., 2022; Brunke et al., 2022): figure-8 trajectory tracking and an adapted IROS 2022 Safe Robot Learning Competition drone racing task.
 - **RoboSuite (SAC):** Evaluated on nine manipulation tasks (e.g., block lifting, peg-in-hole) using the `robosuite` framework (Zhu et al., 2020) with operational space control.
 - **Fetch Orientation Environments (TD3 with HER):** Modified versions of the goal-conditioned `Fetch` environments (Andrychowicz et al., 2017) to include pose goals (ReachOrient and PickAndPlaceOrient), using a Franka FR3 robot arm.

**Methodological Details and Specific Analyses:**
- **Projections:** Policies produce Euclidean outputs that must be mapped to valid `SO(3)` elements. For quaternions, this is unit normalization; for matrices, it's an SVD-based projection to the closest rotation. The authors use a practical compromise for stochastic policies: projecting the mean inside the network and then applying the projection again to sampled, off-manifold actions within the environment. Ablations are conducted to assess the impact of projecting sampled actions during training.
- **Unit-Rotation Centering:** For delta quaternion and matrix representations, a custom policy network modification is explored where the network output is effectively centered around the identity rotation, to mitigate issues with initial exploration for zero-centered outputs. Ablation studies quantify its impact.
- **Action Scaling:** For tangent vector representations, the network output norm is limited to `α_max` to prevent reaching singularities and enable fine-grained control. Ablations examine the effects of this scaling.
- **Exploration Dynamics Analysis:** The impact of projections on Gaussian noise samples is visualized (Figure 7) and discussed, showing how they warp Euclidean distributions on the `SO(3)` manifold.
- **Entropy Regularization Analysis:** Detailed ablations investigate how varying entropy coefficients affect action magnitudes and distributions for different representations, especially for SAC, which is prone to issues with off-manifold actions.
- **Conflicting Policy Gradients:** For multi-cover representations (like quaternions), the learned Q-values are analyzed to observe if the critic learns multi-modal distributions, leading to conflicting gradients for the actor.
- **Hyperparameter Optimization:** Hyperparameters are tuned for each algorithm and environment using Bayesian optimization (Bergstra et al., 2011) to ensure fair comparisons.
- **Reproducibility:** The project webpage and code are made publicly available, facilitating verification and further research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides comprehensive insights into the performance and underlying mechanisms of various SO(3) action representations in deep reinforcement learning.

**Overall Best Performing Representation:**
- The **delta tangent vector representation (`s_τ`)** in the local frame emerges as the most reliable and generally best-performing choice. It consistently yields the best final policies or is highly competitive across algorithms (PPO, SAC, TD3) and reward settings (dense, sparse) in both idealized and robotic environments. Its advantages include not requiring projections, avoiding cut-locus singularities for typical angular step sizes, and lower dimensionality.

**Performance of Other Representations:**
- **Global Rotation Matrices (`R`)** generally perform well, often second to `s_τ`, especially with dense rewards. However, they can struggle with SAC under sparse rewards.
- **Quaternions (`q`, `∆q`)** generally perform weaker than matrices, often exhibiting decreased convergence stability due to the double-cover of SO(3), which can lead to conflicting policy gradients (A.1.6).
- **Euler Angles (`(ϕ, θ, ψ)`, `∆(ϕ, θ, ψ)`)** are largely a poor choice due to severe discontinuities, singularities (gimbal lock), and highly non-linear behavior, especially for tasks requiring large angular changes or full SO(3) coverage. They perform reasonably only in specialized domains where rotations are minimal (e.g., stable drone flight).

**Explanations for Performance Differences (Hypotheses Validation):**

1. **Smoothness and Uniqueness (Hypothesis 1):**
 - While smoothness and uniqueness benefit learning, they don't have to hold globally for limited angular step sizes. The key is that discontinuities and singularities are *out of reach* for the agent's action space.
 - Global matrices perform well due to their smoothness and uniqueness.
 - Local tangent vectors (`s_τ`) excel because, within the limited maximum step angle (`α_max`), the tangent space is unique, has no discontinuities, and the exponential map is almost linear, effectively avoiding global singularities.
 - Quaternions struggle due to their double-cover creating multi-modal Q-functions and conflicting gradients (A.1.6).

2. **Exploration Dynamics (Hypothesis 2):**
 - Projections of Euclidean network outputs onto `SO(3)` significantly *warp* the action distribution, negatively impacting exploration (Figure 7).
 - Euler angles and quaternions are most severely affected, leading to concentrated distributions around singularities (Euler angles, Figure 9) or narrow sub-manifolds (quaternions).
 - Matrix and local tangent representations induce a more even spread, which is advantageous for exploration, especially in sparse reward environments.
 - Ablations show that projecting sampled actions (instead of just the mean) during training severely harms PPO's performance (A.1.1).

3. **Entropy Regularization (Hypothesis 3):**
 - Standard entropy regularization in PPO and SAC can be suboptimal for `SO(3)` actions, as maximizing Euclidean entropy might not correspond to diverse rotational actions (A.6).
 - Increased entropy can drive actions towards larger magnitudes in Euclidean space (Figure 14), which, for quaternions and matrices, doesn't necessarily mean more diverse rotations after projection.
 - This is particularly detrimental for SAC with matrix and quaternion representations in sparse reward settings, leading to poor performance or policy collapse, as the entropy bonus incentivizes undesirable action outputs before the critic learns meaningful gradients (Figure 15, A.2.2).
 - Scaling delta tangent actions (see below) mitigates this effect for `s_τ`.

4. **Unit Rotation-Centered Delta Actions (Hypothesis 4):**
 - Centering delta quaternion and matrix actions around the identity rotation improves performance *specifically for PPO* (Figure 21). This helps prevent erratic initial exploration due to zero-centered Euclidean outputs mapping to a wide range of rotations.
 - For unstable robotic systems like drones, unit-centering is crucial for early training stability and faster convergence (Figures 26, 27).
 - Results are inconclusive or show mixed benefits for SAC and TD3. Delta tangent and Euler actions are inherently centered.

5. **Action Scaling for Tangent Vectors (Hypothesis 5):**
 - Scaling tangent vectors to the range of permissible rotations (`α_max`) significantly improves performance and stability (Figures 10, 11, 12). This ensures that the agent learns within the functional action space, avoids the cut locus, and allows for finer control.
 - It is particularly important for SAC, where unscaled tangent actions can lead to failures when combined with entropy maximization (A.1.5).

**Robotics Benchmarks:**
- Findings from the idealized environment generally transfer. Local tangent actions (`s_τ`) consistently outperform or are competitive with other representations across drone control (PPO), RoboSuite manipulation (SAC), and Fetch orientation tasks (TD3+HER).
- For tasks requiring broad SO(3) coverage (e.g., `PickAndPlaceOrient`), the performance differences between representations become more pronounced, with `s_τ` showing a clear advantage (e.g., 69.8% success vs. 32.3% for Euler angles).
- For tasks with limited SO(3) requirements (e.g., stable drone flight), Euler angles can perform surprisingly well, but offer no advantage over `s_τ`.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper makes highly significant contributions to the field of deep reinforcement learning for robotics, offering both fundamental insights and practical, actionable guidelines.

**Practical Impact:**
- **Reduced Trial-and-Error:** The most immediate impact is providing RL practitioners with clear, empirically-backed recommendations for choosing and implementing SO(3) action representations. This will significantly reduce the amount of guesswork and trial-and-error involved in designing policies for orientation control, thereby accelerating research and development in robotics.
- **Improved Performance and Stability:** By identifying the most robust representation (`s_τ`) and detailing the pitfalls of others (e.g., exploration issues, entropy regularization interactions), the work enables practitioners to train more performant, stable, and sample-efficient policies for tasks requiring 3D rotational control. This directly translates to more capable robot manipulators, agile drones, and other embodied agents.
- **Enhanced Understanding:** The systematic analysis of *why* representations behave differently—linking performance to geometric properties, projection mechanisms, and RL-specific phenomena like exploration and entropy—deepens the community's understanding of learning on manifolds within DRL.

**Broader Advancement of the Field:**
- **Fills a Critical Gap:** The paper successfully bridges the gap between theoretical knowledge of SO(3) geometry and practical DRL application, which was previously underexplored for action spaces. It extends existing knowledge from supervised learning to the more complex and dynamic RL setting.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By establishing a robust baseline and highlighting remaining challenges, the paper lays a strong foundation for future research. The identified need for better benchmarks that require full SO(3) manifold control is crucial for pushing the boundaries of what RL agents can achieve in complex 3D environments.
- **Catalyst for Geometric RL:** This work reinforces the growing importance of geometric deep learning approaches in robotics. By demonstrating how representation-induced geometry profoundly influences exploration and optimization, it encourages further development and integration of manifold-aware techniques into RL algorithms.

**Future Directions Identified:**
The authors themselves suggest several avenues for future work, indicating the breadth of their contribution:
- **Observation-based settings:** While the current findings likely transfer, empirical evidence for environments with complex observation spaces incorporating rotations would be valuable.
- **Discrete action spaces:** Exploring discretization schemes and the required density of `SO(3)` covers for discrete RL algorithms opens up new research questions.
- **Diffusion policies:** Investigating `SO(3)` representations for diffusion models, which are gaining traction in robotic imitation learning, could yield different conclusions due to their multi-modality and noise processes.
- **New benchmarks:** The call for specialized benchmarks that truly demand full `SO(3)` manifold control underscores a current limitation in evaluating such capabilities comprehensively.

In conclusion, "A Primer on SO(3) Action Representations in Deep Reinforcement Learning" is a highly valuable contribution that provides much-needed clarity and practical guidance for a pervasive problem in robotics. Its systematic approach, rigorous empirical validation, and actionable recommendations are poised to significantly impact the development and deployment of DRL agents in real-world robotic applications.
