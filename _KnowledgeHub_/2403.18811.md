# Duolando: Follower GPT with Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning for Dance Accompaniment

> [!summary] Summary
> Duolando introduces dance accompaniment: generating a follower's 3D duet motion conditioned on music and a leader's movement. It quantizes body-part motion and leader-follower relative translation with VQ-VAEs, then autoregressively predicts tokens via an interaction-coordinated GPT with look-ahead conditioning, and fine-tunes with off-policy reinforcement learning against step-consistency rewards. Trained on the new 1.9-hour DD100 MoCap dataset, it cuts skating and raises contact frequency to 52.36%.

## Problem

- No public duet dance data exists; prior MoCap and music-to-dance datasets lack strong physical contact, music modality, finger motion, or minute-long clips.
- Solo dance generators ignore a partner entirely, so they cannot position or pose a follower responsively, yielding cross-distance features wildly off from real duets.
- Group dance methods synthesize all agents jointly, retrieving coordinated patterns from training data, which is easier than responding to an externally preconditioned leader.

## Method

- **DD100** dataset: 20-camera optical MoCap at 120 FPS plus inertial gloves, fit to SMPL-X, covering 10 ballroom and ballet genres, 5 dancer pairs, 1.9 hours.
- ==Interaction-coordinated GPT== over five ==VQ-VAE== codebooks (upper, lower, two hands, relative translation), with ==Look-Ahead Transformers== giving 29 future music and leader tokens.
- ==Off-policy reinforcement learning== loss matching the policy probability to a sigmoid-mapped Q value, replayed on self-generated out-of-distribution rollouts with a step-wise foot-velocity consistency reward.

## Results

- Explicit relative-translation prediction drops interactive FID from **7970.19** to **21.68** and lifts contact frequency from **7.76%** to **57.43%**, a sevenfold rise.
- Off-policy RL improves kinetic FID from **106.72** to **25.30** (**76%**), interactive FID to **9.97** (**54%** lower), and cuts skating ratio **1.06%** to **0.33%**.
- Users preferred full Duolando over ablated variants in **80%**, **83%**, and **62%** of pairs, but over ground truth only about **15%** of the time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting inter-agent relative translation explicitly, rather than integrating velocity, is what buys real physical contact in interactive motion generation.
> - Explicit global displacement and locally generated footwork can disagree; a cheap consistency reward on self-generated OOD rollouts repairs the mismatch without labels.
> - Off-policy RL with an explicit probability target lets past rollouts be reused, avoiding the actor-critic failure of endlessly suppressing already-improbable actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

This paper introduces a novel task called "dance accompaniment" and presents Duolando, a GPT-based model that generates responsive dance movements for a follower dancer given a leader's movements and background music. Unlike existing dance generation methods that focus on solo performances or loosely coordinated group dances, this work tackles the challenging problem of generating highly interactive duet dances where two dancers maintain close physical coordination and contact.

The research addresses a significant gap in 3D human motion synthesis by creating both a large-scale dataset of professional duet dances and a sophisticated model capable of generating coordinated follower movements. This work has important implications for virtual reality applications, interactive entertainment, and human-AI interaction systems.

### 2. The Dance Accompaniment Problem

Dance accompaniment represents a fundamentally different challenge from existing motion generation tasks. While previous work has focused on generating solo dances from music or creating group dances with minimal interaction, duet dancing requires:

- **Dynamic responsiveness**: The follower must continuously adapt to the leader's movements
- **Physical coordination**: Maintaining appropriate spatial relationships and contact
- **Musical synchronization**: Both dancers must stay aligned with the musical rhythm
- **Anticipatory behavior**: The follower needs to predict and prepare for the leader's next moves

This task differs from traditional human-human interaction scenarios because it involves sustained, complex, and aesthetically-driven coordination rather than simple interactions like handshakes or conversations. The challenge is compounded by the need to handle out-of-distribution scenarios where the model encounters unfamiliar leader movements or musical styles.

### 3. The DD100 Dataset

A critical contribution of this work is the creation of DD100 (Duet Dance 100), a comprehensive dataset designed specifically for dance accompaniment research. This dataset addresses the lack of suitable training data for interactive dance generation.

#### 3.1. Dataset Characteristics

- **Scale**: 117 minutes of professional dance performances
- **Diversity**: 10 distinct ballroom dance genres (5 Latin, 4 Modern, and Pas de Deux ballet)
- **Quality**: High-precision motion capture using 20 optical cameras at 120 FPS
- **Detail**: Inertial motion capture gloves for accurate hand gesture recording
- **Interaction**: Strong physical interaction including contact and close-proximity movements
- **Duration**: Extended clips (36-107 seconds) enabling long-term choreography analysis

#### 3.2. Data Processing

The raw motion capture data is converted into SMPL-X (Skinned Multi-Person Linear Model-eXpressive) format, providing detailed body poses, hand gestures, and global translations. Each dance sequence is paired with its corresponding background music, creating the multimodal input necessary for the accompaniment task.

### 4. Duolando Model Architecture

The Duolando framework employs a two-stage approach that first quantizes motion into discrete tokens and then uses a transformer-based model to generate follower movements.

#### 4.1. Stage 1: Motion Quantization

The first stage uses Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoders (VQ-VAEs) to convert continuous motion data into discrete tokens:

- **Body Part Decomposition**: Four separate VQ-VAEs encode different body regions (upper body, lower body, left hand, right hand)
- **Relative Translation**: A fifth VQ-VAE models the spatial relationship between leader and follower
- **Token Generation**: Motion sequences are converted into discrete code sequences for transformer processing

#### 4.2. Stage 2: Interaction-Coordinated GPT

The core generative model is a GPT-based transformer that predicts follower motion tokens autoregressively:

```
P(z_follower | m, z_leader, z_follower_prev)
```

Where:
- `z_follower`: Tokenized follower motion sequence
- `m`: Music features
- `z_leader`: Tokenized leader motion sequence
- `z_follower_prev`: Previous follower motion tokens

#### 4.3. Key Architectural Features

**Look-Ahead Mechanism**: The model incorporates future information about music and leader movements using Look-Ahead Transformers (LAT). This allows the follower to anticipate changes and maintain smooth coordination.

**Interaction Coordination**: A specialized attention mechanism ensures coherent interaction between all input modalities while preserving causal order in the generation process.

**Multi-Modal Integration**: The model processes 10 distinct input streams: music, leader's body parts (4), follower's previous motions (4), and relative translation.

### 5. Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning

A key innovation in Duolando is the application of off-policy reinforcement learning to address generalization challenges and motion artifacts.

#### 5.1. The Problem

While supervised training on DD100 provides a strong foundation, GPT models often struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios, leading to issues like "skating" where foot movements don't match global displacement.

#### 5.2. Off-Policy Solution

The authors formulate token prediction as a reinforcement learning problem:

- **Action**: Predicting the next motion token
- **Policy**: The GPT model parameters
- **State**: The current sequence context
- **Reward**: Custom rewards targeting specific motion quality aspects

#### 5.3. Novel Loss Function

The off-policy approach introduces an explicit optimization target:

```
L_off_RL = -log π_θ(a_t|s_t) × σ(Q(s_t, a_t))
```

This formulation allows the model to:
- Reuse past generated data for training efficiency
- Establish clear optimization targets based on expected rewards
- Avoid the inefficiencies of traditional on-policy methods

#### 5.4. Step-wise Rewards

A specialized reward function penalizes skating artifacts by evaluating consistency between predicted global translation and decoded lower body velocity. When inconsistencies exceed a threshold, negative rewards guide the model toward more natural stepping movements.

### 6. Experimental Results

The evaluation framework includes three categories of metrics:

#### 6.1. Motion Quality Metrics

- **FID (Fréchet Inception Distance)**: Measures similarity to real motion
- **Diversity**: Evaluates variety in generated sequences

#### 6.2. Interaction Metrics

- **Cross-Distance FID**: Assesses leader-follower spatial relationships
- **Contact Frequency**: Percentage of frames with physical contact
- **Beat Echo Degree**: Measures rhythmic synchronization between dancers

#### 6.3. Musical Alignment

- **Beat-Align Score**: Evaluates synchronization with musical beats

#### 6.4. Key Findings

The quantitative results demonstrate the effectiveness of each component:

1. **Interaction Coordination**: Improves beat echo degree by 20% compared to non-interactive variants
2. **Relative Translation**: Dramatically improves contact frequency (7-fold increase) and spatial coordination
3. **Off-Policy RL**: Reduces motion quality artifacts by 76% and decreases skating ratio by 69%

#### 6.5. User Study Validation

A user study with 15 participants confirmed subjective preferences for Duolando, with the full model outperforming ablated versions in 62-83% of comparisons. While still challenging to match ground truth performance (15% preference), the results validate the approach's effectiveness.

### 7. Significance and Future Directions

This work makes several important contributions to the field of human motion synthesis:

#### 7.1. Technical Contributions

- **Novel Task Definition**: Establishes dance accompaniment as a distinct research problem
- **Dataset Creation**: DD100 provides a valuable resource for future interactive motion research
- **Model Innovation**: Demonstrates effective application of off-policy RL to generative models
- **Architectural Advances**: Shows how to handle complex multi-modal, multi-agent generation

#### 7.2. Applications

The ability to generate responsive dance partners has immediate applications in:
- **Virtual Reality**: Creating immersive dance experiences with AI companions
- **Entertainment**: Generating content for games, films, and interactive media
- **Training Systems**: Providing virtual practice partners for dance instruction
- **Social VR**: Enabling natural human-AI interaction in virtual environments

#### 7.3. Ethical Considerations

The authors acknowledge potential risks including user addiction to virtual interactions and the possibility of generating realistic but fake human responses. This responsible approach to AI research highlights the importance of considering societal implications.

#### 7.4. Future Research Directions

This work opens several avenues for future research:
- Extending to other forms of interactive human motion
- Improving generalization to diverse dance styles and music genres
- Developing real-time generation capabilities for live interaction
- Exploring applications beyond dance to general human-AI physical interaction

The Duolando framework represents a significant step forward in creating AI systems capable of natural, responsive physical interaction with humans, moving closer to truly interactive virtual companions and immersive digital experiences.
