# FAVLA: A Force-Adaptive Fast-Slow VLA model for Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FAVLA introduces a force-adaptive fast-slow Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that processes semantic information at a low frequency while integrating high-frequency force feedback for reactive control during robotic manipulation. This approach significantly enhances success rates and reduces peak contact forces in contact-rich tasks by dynamically adjusting the action expert's execution based on predicted contact dynamics.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models struggle with contact-rich robotic manipulation due to mismatched sensor frequencies, often downsampling critical high-frequency force data.
- Unified-frequency fusion of modalities leads to delayed and non-reactive responses to abrupt contact events, causing potential damage or task failure.
- Imbalanced modality tokenization in standard VLA models can bias attention away from sparse but critical force cues.

## Method

- Developed a force-injected fast-slow VLA model architecture that decouples a slow Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone for semantic reasoning from a fast Force-Injected Action Expert (AE) for reactive control.
- The AE directly injects high-frequency force information into multiple transformer layers using an additive force adapter, allowing for immediate action adjustments.
- Implemented a force-adaptive inference strategy where a VLM-predicted force variance head dynamically adjusts the AE's execution frequency based on predicted contact dynamics, running more frequently during contact.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of 80.8% across four contact-rich tasks, outperforming vision-only baselines by 38.0 percentage points and the strongest force-aware baseline by 13.8 percentage points.
- Consistently reduced average peak contact forces on sensitive tasks (e.g., 7.7 N for Gear Assembly, 9.9 N for Box Flipping), representing a substantial reduction compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the force-injected action expert, force variance prediction, and force-adaptive inference strategy are each critical for performance improvements, with the adaptive strategy outperforming all static frequency approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling semantic understanding (slow) from reactive contact control (fast) with appropriate sensor processing rates is crucial for robust contact-rich robotic manipulation.
> - Directly injecting high-frequency force feedback into the action expert via an additive adapter significantly enhances reactive control and task performance.
> - An adaptive inference strategy, dynamically adjusting the action expert's frequency based on predicted force volatility, optimizes computational resources while ensuring timely responses to physical interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a crucial challenge at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and contact-rich robotic manipulation, pushing the boundaries of what general-purpose robots can achieve in complex physical interactions.

**The Rise of VLA Models:** In recent years, VLA models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for robotic control. By integrating large vision-language models (VLMs) with continuous control policies, often implemented as "action experts," these models (e.g., π0, RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA) have demonstrated impressive semantic generalization and transferability across various robots and tasks. Through large-scale multi-task pretraining, they enable robots to interpret natural language instructions and visual cues to perform a wide range of tasks, from pick-and-place to more intricate manipulation.

**Challenges in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Despite their success in free-space operations, VLA models often struggle with contact-rich tasks like insertion, assembly, and precise manipulation under varying contact conditions. These tasks demand more than just visual alignment; they require rapid, reactive motion correction in response to dynamic physical interactions, such as impacts, stick-slip phenomena, or jamming. Subtle force changes, often imperceptible through vision alone, are critical indicators for success or failure.

**Integrating Force Feedback – The Current State:** A natural evolutionary step has been to incorporate physical interaction signals, particularly force/torque data, into VLA inputs. Previous works have shown improved performance on contact-rich tasks by adding force feedback to VLA models (Yu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b) or directly to action policies (Xue et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025b). Similarly, tactile-based approaches (Huang et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2025) aim to provide fine-grained contact perception.

**The Gap FAVLA Addresses:** The primary limitation of most existing force-aware VLA approaches, and the core problem FAVLA tackles, is their tendency to fuse all modalities—vision, language, and force—at a *single, unified operating frequency*. This design choice ignores the inherent mismatched sampling rates of real-world sensors (e.g., slow cameras at 15 Hz vs. fast force/torque sensors at 100-1000 Hz). Consequently, high-frequency force data, which contains vital cues for reactive correction, is often downsampled to match the slowest modality. This discarding of critical information, coupled with the common VLM-Action Expert (AE) pipelines that execute action chunks largely open-loop between infrequent VLM updates, leads to significant delays in responding to abrupt contact events, hindering performance and potentially causing damage.

FAVLA directly addresses this critical gap by introducing a novel *force-adaptive fast-slow* architecture. It argues that semantic understanding (vision, language) is inherently "slow," while reactive contact control (force feedback) must be "fast." By explicitly decoupling these timescales and implementing an adaptive inference strategy, FAVLA pushes the research landscape towards more biologically plausible and practically robust robotic control, where sensory information is processed at rates appropriate for its nature and task demands. This work represents a significant step towards enabling VLA models to not just understand and plan, but also *physically interact* with their environment with the necessary agility and precision for complex tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind FAVLA stems from the inherent limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models when applied to contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks. While VLA models excel at semantic generalization and high-level reasoning, their effectiveness diminishes in scenarios requiring precise physical interaction and rapid, reactive adjustments based on contact forces.

The key problems identified by the authors that motivate this work are:
1. **Mismatched Sensor Frequencies and Downsampling:** Real-world robot sensors operate at vastly different sampling rates. Cameras provide low-frequency visual data (e.g., 15 Hz), while force/torque sensors offer high-frequency contact cues (e.g., 100-1000 Hz). Most existing VLA models, when incorporating force, downsample this high-frequency data to match the slowest modality. This discards crucial reactive signals needed for immediate correction during impacts, stick-slip events, or force spikes.
2. **Delayed Responses with Unified-Frequency Fusion:** Fusing all modalities at a single, low operating frequency, combined with VLM-Action Expert (AE) pipelines that often execute action chunks in an open-loop fashion between expensive VLM updates, leads to delayed and non-reactive responses. In contact-rich industrial tasks like high-precision assembly, such delays can result in excessive manipulation forces, component damage, or task failure.
3. **Imbalanced Modality Tokenization:** In standard VLA models, vision often contributes far more tokens than force. This imbalance can bias attention away from sparse but critical force cues, even when those cues are paramount for contact regulation.

Given these challenges, the authors propose FAVLA with the following key objectives:

- **Decouple Slow Semantic Reasoning from Fast Contact-Aware Control:** The central objective is to build a VLA model that inherently respects the different timescales of information processing. This means separating the "slow" task of semantic scene understanding (from vision and language) from the "fast" requirement for reactive control based on immediate physical contact.
- **Integrate High-Frequency Force Feedback Effectively:** Design an architecture that can fully leverage high-frequency force signals for reactive correction without downsampling, ensuring that critical contact cues are not lost.
- **Enable Force-Adaptive Action Expert Execution:** Develop a mechanism to dynamically adjust the action expert's (AE) execution frequency based on the predicted contact dynamics. The AE should run more frequently when contact is imminent or ongoing, and less frequently during free-space motion, optimizing computational resources while ensuring responsiveness.
- **Achieve Closed-Loop Action Chunk Refinement:** Enable the fast action expert to refine action chunks in a closed-loop manner, conditioning on the latest force sequence data, to allow for immediate adjustments during execution.
- **Improve Reactivity, Success Rates, and Safety:** Ultimately, the goal is to significantly enhance the robot's ability to perform contact-rich tasks, achieving higher success rates, greater reactivity to unexpected events, and maintaining lower peak contact forces to prevent damage to objects or the robot itself.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FAVLA addresses the challenges of contact-rich manipulation by proposing a novel force-adaptive fast-slow VLA framework built upon the π0 architecture. It decouples the processing of slow semantic information from fast reactive control signals, while maintaining an end-to-end learning scheme.

#### 3.1. Force-Injected Fast–Slow VLA Model Architecture

FAVLA decomposes the VLA model into two main components operating at different frequencies:

- **Slow VLM Backbone:**
 - **Function:** This component is a large VLM transformer responsible for semantic scene and instruction conditioning. It runs at a fixed, low frequency.
 - **Inputs:** It processes RGB images (from external and wrist cameras) encoded by a SigLIP image encoder, language instructions embedded by a Gemma tokenizer, and a *history force data sequence* (f_t-τ+1:t_).
 - **History Force Encoding:** A lightweight Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) tokenizer encodes the history force data into `z_f` tokens. This TCN uses 1D convolutions, causal dilated temporal blocks, and layer normalization to extract features from the time series.
 - **Processing:** Vision, language, and history force tokens are concatenated as prefix tokens for the VLM backbone, allowing full self-attention across these modalities.
 - **Output:** The VLM backbone outputs final Vision-Language-Force (VLF) tokens (`H_VLF`) and, crucially, caches intermediate-layer key/value (KV) states for these prefix tokens. This KV cache provides the semantic context for the fast component.

- **Fast Force-Injected Action Expert (AE):**
 - **Function:** This is a smaller transformer designed for force-reactive control. It runs at a *higher, variable* frequency, refining action chunks in a closed-loop manner.
 - **Inputs:** It conditions on the latest proprioceptive state (`s_t`), the *latest high-frequency force measurements* (f'_t-τ+1:t_), and the slow-updated KV cache from the VLM.
 - **Force Adapter:** This is a key innovation. Instead of simply concatenating force tokens, the AE uses a force adapter to inject the latest force information directly into *multiple transformer layers*. The latest force sequence (f'_t-τ+1:t_) is processed by another TCN tokenizer to obtain `z'_f` tokens. In each AE layer, after self-attention, the noisy action token `z_a` performs cross-attention with `z'_f` (projected into force keys/values), and the resulting force-conditioned update is *additively injected*. This design allows the AE to directly adjust actions based on immediate contact signals.
 - **Action Generation:** The AE generates action chunks (`A_t` = {a_t..., a_t+H-1_}) using a conditional flow matching model.

- **Force Variance Head:**
 - **Function:** An auxiliary MLP head is added to the VLM backbone. It predicts the *near-future volatility* of the contact force during manipulation. This prediction is crucial for adaptively scheduling the AE's execution frequency.
 - **Input:** It takes a specific VLF token (corresponding to a learnable input token) from `H_VLF`.
 - **Prediction Target:** The head is trained to regress a normalized scalar prediction of future force variance (`˜ν_t`), which is computed during dataset preprocessing as an EMA-filtered and weighted variance of the 6D force/torque signal over the action chunk window.

- **Training Objectives:** The entire FAVLA model (VLM, AE, and force variance head) is optimized jointly. The total loss (`L_total`) is a weighted sum of the action generation loss (`L_action`, from flow matching) and the auxiliary variance prediction loss (`L_var`), with a hyperparameter `λ` to balance them.

#### 3.2. Force-Adaptive Fast–Slow Inference Strategy

This strategy dictates how the VLM and AE interact during real-time execution:

- **Decoupled Operation:** In each "visual cycle" (VLM update), the VLM performs a single forward pass to process slow modalities and update the KV cache. Within this cycle, the AE can run multiple times, conditioned on the *latest* force observations and the (stale but semantically rich) KV cache, to generate high-frequency actions.
- **Adaptive Inference Frequency:**
 - The VLM's predicted force variance (`˜ν_t ∈`) is used to dynamically schedule the number of AE executions (`n_t`) within the next VLM cycle.
 - `n_t = max(1, ⌈˜ν_t ⋅ N_max⌉)`, where `N_max` is the maximum frequency ratio.
 - This means during free-space motion (low `˜ν_t`), the AE runs at a low frequency (e.g., `n_t = 1`). As contact becomes imminent or active (high `˜ν_t`), the AE execution frequency adaptively increases, providing faster, more reactive control.
- **Consistent Action Ensemble:** To mitigate inconsistency arising from multiple AE inferences within a single VLM cycle:
 - The *sampled noise* (ϵ) for the flow matching model is fixed across all AE calls within the same visual cycle, ensuring consistent stochastic sampling.
 - **Temporal Ensemble** is applied to aggregate overlapping action chunk predictions across successive visual cycles, which helps to produce smoother and more stable action execution.

This comprehensive methodology, with its dual-frequency architecture, direct force injection, predictive variance head, and adaptive inference strategy, allows FAVLA to process multimodal sensory data effectively across different timescales, enabling responsive and precise control for contact-rich robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive real-world experiments conducted on contact-rich tasks demonstrate FAVLA's significant superiority over existing VLA models.

- **Overall Performance and Success Rates:**
 - FAVLA achieved an impressive average success rate of **80.8%** across the four diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks: USB Insertion, Gear Assembly, Box Flipping, and Board Wiping.
 - It significantly **outperformed all baselines**. Compared to the vision-only π0, FAVLA showed a **38.0 percentage point improvement** in average success rate. Against the strongest force-aware baseline, ForceVLA, FAVLA still improved success rate by **13.8 percentage points**.
 - Particularly notable improvements were observed in tasks like Gear Assembly (93.3% success) and Board Wiping (70.0% success), which are highly sensitive to force regulation. The authors attribute this to FAVLA's closed-loop force control and reactive action adjustments.

- **Superior Force Control Capabilities:**
 - FAVLA consistently demonstrated the ability to maintain contact forces within safe and effective ranges.
 - On sensitive tasks like Gear Assembly and Box Flipping, FAVLA achieved average peak contact forces of **7.7 N** and **9.9 N**, respectively. This represents a substantial reduction of **4.3 N** and **2.3 N** compared to the vision-only π0 baseline, and also lower than other force-aware baselines.
 - This reduction in peak forces is crucial for preventing damage to delicate objects or the robot itself, highlighting the effectiveness of the adaptive fast-slow inference strategy in achieving precise force regulation.

- **Ablation Studies Validating Component Contributions:**
 - **Force-Injected Action Expert:** Incrementally adding the force-injected action expert to a vision-only baseline significantly improved success rates (e.g., from 50% to 65% on Box Flipping, 10% to 60% on Board Wiping). This confirms that directly integrating force feedback into the AE is highly effective.
 - **Force Variance Prediction:** The inclusion of the force variance prediction module further boosted performance (e.g., Box Flipping from 65% to 70%), indicating that explicitly modeling the uncertainty of future contact forces aids in action generation.
 - **Force-Adaptive Inference Strategy:** The addition of the adaptive frequency mechanism, driven by force variance prediction, provided the final and most substantial performance gains (e.g., Box Flipping from 70% to 80%, Board Wiping from 60% to 70%), validating its crucial role in responsive control.

- **Effectiveness of Adaptive Frequency Adjustment:**
 - An ablation study comparing static execution frequencies (n=1, 2, 4 AE steps per VLM step) with FAVLA's adaptive strategy demonstrated that while higher fixed frequencies improved performance, the **force-adaptive strategy consistently outperformed all static approaches**.
 - For instance, in Gear Assembly, the adaptive strategy achieved 93% success compared to 90% for the best static (n=4) approach. This confirms that dynamically adjusting the AE's frequency based on real-time needs is more effective than a constant high frequency, as it ensures responsiveness during critical contact phases while maintaining efficient execution during non-contact periods.

- **Accurate Prediction of Contact Dynamics (Visualization):**
 - Visualizations of the adaptive frequency prediction clearly showed that the VLM's force variance head accurately forecasts upcoming physical interactions. The model effectively **increased its AE update frequency just before entering phases where contact was expected** (high force variance) and reduced it during stable, non-contact periods (low force variance). This capability allows the system to be precisely reactive when needed without relying on manual rules.

- **Robustness and Generalization:**
 - Qualitative results (visualizations of task execution) illustrated FAVLA's strong generalization ability and robustness. The model successfully adapted to various perturbations, such as angularly rotated sockets, unseen gear colors, different box sizes, and various marker colors, demonstrating its practical applicability in diverse real-world scenarios.

In summary, the experimental results unequivocally validate FAVLA's novel architectural and inference strategies. It not only achieves significantly higher success rates in demanding contact-rich tasks but also performs manipulations with greater safety and precision by drastically reducing peak contact forces, marking a substantial advance in robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of FAVLA represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring nuanced physical interaction. Its core contribution lies in effectively bridging the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level reactive control by proposing a biologically inspired fast-slow processing paradigm. The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:

- **Enabling Robust Contact-Rich Manipulation:** FAVLA directly addresses one of the long-standing challenges in robotics: performing precise manipulation in contact-rich environments. By effectively integrating high-frequency force feedback and adapting its control frequency, FAVLA allows robots to handle impacts, friction changes, and tight tolerances with unprecedented reactivity and lower peak forces. This capability is critical for tasks like industrial assembly, delicate part insertion, and dexterous manipulation, where errors can lead to damage or failure.

- **Improved Safety and Reliability:** The ability to significantly reduce average peak contact forces is a major safety advantage. Excessive forces can damage manipulated objects, the robot's end-effector, or even pose a risk in human-robot collaboration scenarios. FAVLA's compliant and reactive control strategy inherently leads to safer operation, making robots more reliable and trustworthy in physical environments. This will accelerate the deployment of robots in sensitive industrial and service applications.

- **Enhanced Efficiency and Task Success:** By providing timely and appropriate responses to contact dynamics, FAVLA drastically improves task success rates. The adaptive frequency mechanism ensures that computational resources are allocated efficiently—more frequently when contact is critical, and less during free-space motion—optimizing the overall performance without unnecessary overhead. This efficiency translates directly to faster and more reliable task completion, a key metric for industrial automation.

- **A Paradigm Shift in VLA Design:** The "force-adaptive fast-slow" paradigm introduced by FAVLA offers a new blueprint for designing VLA models. It highlights the importance of matching sensor processing rates with the inherent timescales of different modalities, moving beyond a unified-frequency approach. This hierarchical and adaptive control architecture could inspire future research in multi-modal robot learning, especially for systems dealing with inherently multi-rate sensory inputs like touch, audio, and vision.

- **Foundational for General-Purpose Robotics:** For general-purpose robots to become truly versatile, they must not only understand complex instructions and perceive their environment but also interact physically in a nuanced and adaptive manner. FAVLA contributes a crucial piece to this puzzle, making VLA models more robust and capable of handling the complexities of the physical world. It brings us closer to robots that can operate autonomously and proficiently in unstructured and dynamically changing environments.

- **Direct Applications in Industrial Automation:** The immediate impact of FAVLA is evident in industrial settings requiring high-precision assembly, handling fragile components, or performing tasks with variable contact conditions. From electronics manufacturing to custom product assembly, the increased success rates and reduced risks of damage offered by FAVLA could lead to significant cost savings and improved quality in automated processes.

In conclusion, FAVLA is a compelling demonstration of how intelligent architectural design and adaptive control strategies can elevate the performance of VLA models in physically demanding tasks. By allowing robots to "feel" and react to their environment with unparalleled precision and agility, this work paves the way for a new generation of safer, more reliable, and highly capable robotic systems.
