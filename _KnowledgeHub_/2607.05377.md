# Cortex: A Bidirectionally Aligned Embodied Agent Framework for Long-horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Cortex, developed by researchers from Tsinghua University and Shanghai AI Laboratory, introduces a bidirectionally aligned embodied agent framework that addresses limitations in long-horizon manipulation tasks. This framework achieves 95.5% zero-shot success on the LIBERO-Long benchmark and demonstrates up to 65% success in complex real-world multi-stage experiments, outperforming monolithic baselines.

## Problem

- Monolithic VLA models suffer from "Markovian short-sightedness," leading to repetitive actions and compounding errors in long-horizon tasks.
- Hierarchical dual-system paradigms face a "semantic-kinematic domain gap" where high-level plans lack explicit embodied constraints for low-level execution.
- Existing memory approaches often treat memory passively, without grounding it to specific actions or physical states needed to resolve ambiguities during dynamic interactions.

## Method

- A ==bidirectionally aligned== framework couples a ==VLM (cognitive orchestrator)== with a ==VLA (reactive executor)== via a customized ==subtask interface== to ensure both executability and tractability.
- A scalable metadata pipeline standardizes ==32 canonical skill primitives== for subtask formulation and incorporates ==tractability principles== (e.g., reachability constraints) into data generation.
- An ==event-balanced sampling== strategy fine-tunes the VLM to resolve planning ambiguities during subtask transitions, complemented by a robust ==inference harness== for real-world deployment and error recovery. **Cortex** fine-tunes a ==Qwen3-VL-8B== System-2 and a ==π0.5== System-1 executor.

## Results

- Achieved a **95.5%** zero-shot success rate on the LIBERO-Long benchmark, improving by **3.1%** over monolithic baselines like π0.5.
- Demonstrated a **65%** success rate on complex real-world "Chemical Washing" tasks and **55%** on other long-horizon tasks, while end-to-end methods like π0.5 achieved **0%** success.
- Exhibited robust closed-loop physical error recovery, subtask-level feedback, and trial-and-error capabilities in unseen real-world environments, comparable to human-guided policies (**70-75%** success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly aligning high-level plans with low-level execution constraints through a standardized, physically grounded interface is crucial for overcoming the semantic-kinematic gap.
> - Active progress verification and semantic memory updates, particularly during subtask transitions, significantly reduce temporal and semantic ambiguities in long-horizon tasks.
> - A robust deployment harness, incorporating rich task contexts, strict skill constraints, and error recovery mechanisms, is essential for translating planned steps into resilient real-world execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which facilitate the direct mapping of multimodal inputs to continuous motor control. These models have demonstrated capabilities in various short-horizon manipulation tasks, including robust zero-shot generalization. However, as the complexity of tasks increases, particularly in terms of temporal length and sequential dependencies, monolithic VLA architectures encounter limitations. Their Markovian nature, which primarily relies on current observations, leads to a "short-sightedness" where models struggle to track long-term task progress, differentiate intermediate states, and avoid repetitive or erroneous actions, resulting in compounding execution failures.

To address these limitations, hierarchical dual-system paradigms have emerged, aiming to decouple high-level cognitive planning (often managed by a Vision-Language Model, VLM) from low-level reactive execution (handled by a VLA). While these systems offer a structural approach to long-horizon tasks, they commonly suffer from a "semantic-kinematic domain gap." This gap arises because the high-level plans generated by VLMs often lack explicit embodied constraints, making them difficult for downstream VLA executors to physically ground. Consequently, these plans can be kinematically infeasible, and the VLA may not be sufficiently robust or adaptive to the VLM's outputs. Previous efforts, such as those relying on explicit linguistic or code interfaces, sometimes proved too rigid, while latent-coupled systems sacrificed interpretability and explicit progress tracking. Existing approaches to temporal memory in embodied agents largely treat memory as passive storage, lacking an action-oriented or physically grounded state abstraction necessary to resolve ambiguities in dynamic, multi-stage interactions.

Cortex positions itself within this landscape by introducing a framework designed to bridge the semantic-kinematic gap in hierarchical dual-system architectures. It aims to overcome the Markovian limitations of monolithic VLAs by establishing a bidirectionally aligned interface between the VLM and VLA, ensuring that high-level plans are both executable and tractable for low-level control. This work seeks to advance generalist manipulation policies by enabling more reliable and robust performance on complex, long-horizon tasks, including those in real-world, unseen environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Cortex stems from the recognized challenges in enabling embodied agents to perform complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks reliably. Existing VLA models, despite their progress in short-horizon scenarios, are fundamentally limited by their reactive, Markovian operation. This design causes them to frequently lose track of intermediate states, engage in repetitive actions, and accumulate execution errors when faced with tasks requiring sequential reasoning and sustained progress verification. This "Markovian short-sightedness" inhibits their scalability to real-world, multi-stage scenarios.

Furthermore, while hierarchical dual-system methods offer a potential solution by separating planning from execution, they suffer from a critical lack of bidirectional alignment. High-level planning models often generate plans that are semantically rich but kinematically ungrounded, failing to account for the physical capabilities and constraints of the low-level executor. Conversely, reactive executors may not be robust enough to handle the ambiguities or imprecision of high-level plans. This disconnect, termed the semantic-kinematic domain gap, prevents synergistic operation between the cognitive planner and the reactive executor. Previous attempts at incorporating memory often treated it passively, without grounding it to specific actions or physical states, thus failing to resolve ambiguities during dynamic interactions.

Against this background, the key objectives of the Cortex framework are:
1. **To overcome Markovian short-sightedness:** Develop an embodied agent capable of continuous progress verification and temporal reasoning for long-horizon tasks, moving beyond purely reactive policies.
2. **To establish bidirectional alignment:** Create a framework that deeply couples a high-level cognitive orchestrator (VLM) with a low-level reactive executor (VLA) through a bidirectionally aligned interface. This alignment ensures that the VLM's plans are executable and tractable for the VLA, and the VLA's execution provides feedback for the VLM's state updates.
3. **To define a customized planning interface:** Design an interface that standardizes manipulation subtasks into a set of canonical skill primitives. This interface aims to convey precise, physically grounded subtask plans, incorporating principles of tractability like representative object attributes and improved trajectory reachability, directly into the data generation process.
4. **To resolve planning ambiguities:** Implement training strategies, specifically an event-balanced sampling method, to address semantic and temporal ambiguities that arise during subtask transitions, thereby enhancing the model's ability to maintain a coherent task state.
5. **To enable robust closed-loop execution:** Develop a robust inference pipeline, incorporating harness engineering (task contexts, skill constraints, and error recovery mechanisms), to ensure seamless and resilient execution of long-horizon tasks in both simulated and real-world environments.
6. **To demonstrate zero-shot generalization:** Achieve the capability for zero-shot completion of unseen real-world long-horizon tasks by leveraging a generalist VLM in conjunction with a fine-tuned VLA.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Cortex proposes a bidirectionally aligned embodied agent framework comprising a Vision-Language Model (VLM) acting as a cognitive orchestrator and a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for reactive execution. The core of this framework is a customized subtask interface designed to ensure both executability and tractability, thereby aligning the VLM and VLA.

**Framework Overview and Interface Formulation:**
The VLM receives global instructions and image observations, performing subtask planning while maintaining a textual memory log. The VLA, in turn, takes the VLM-generated subtask as input for reactive execution. The subtask ($s_t$) is formulated as an interface that translates open-ended human intentions into high-frequency motor commands. For example, a global instruction like "Wash the beaker" is decomposed into executable skill primitives such as $s_1$: "[Pick] the beaker from the table." To counter execution loops caused by ambiguous visual states, an actively updated semantic memory ($M^{(t)}$) is maintained, which aggregates previously achieved milestones, providing temporal context to connect discrete subtasks.

**Metadata Annotation & Generation:**
To populate this interface, a scalable pipeline for constructing structural metadata is developed.
- **Open-source Data Annotation:** Over 4,000 hours of long-horizon episodes from existing benchmarks (AgibotWorld, Galaxea, BEHAVIOR-1K, RoboCerebra) and new real-world teleoperation data are aggregated. To ensure **executability**, 32 canonical skill primitives (e.g., Pick, Place, Unscrew, Pour) are standardized with strict language templates. For existing datasets, a Qwen3-VL-235B model is used to re-annotate subtasks according to these templates. For real-world demonstrations, an automated boundary inference pipeline is deployed, converting raw trajectories into structured subtask annotations. This pipeline fuses state-action and visual features into a joint representation, then uses dynamic programming to compute optimal monotone boundaries by minimizing frame-to-subtask compatibility cost, regularized by duration priors and low-motion boundary penalties.
- **Simulation Procedural Data Generation:** Simulators like RoboTwin and RMBench are utilized to synthesize novel, optimal sequences. Subtask annotations are generated online during expert demonstration collection, programmatically defining boundaries based on semantic stage entry and low-level primitive completion. To enforce **tractability**, detailed simulation assets are used to determine object categories, derive colors, and disambiguate identical objects via relative spatial identifiers. Reachability constraints, based on robot embodiment descriptions, are explicitly incorporated to ensure kinematically feasible execution paths.

**Training:**
- **Event-balanced Sampling:** This strategy addresses planning ambiguity during subtask transitions, which is a challenge for models fine-tuned with standard uniform sampling. The training trajectories are divided into three distinct temporal phases:
 - **Boundary transition phase:** This phase encompasses a period around ground-truth subtask boundaries ($t_k$), specifically $t \in [t_k - \epsilon_1, t_k + \epsilon_2]$. Here, the model learns to visually verify physical completion, triggering discrete state advancement by updating memory and planning the next subtask. As visual completion often appears slightly after the exact boundary, $\epsilon_2$ is set to be larger than $\epsilon_1$.
 - **Intra-task execution phase:** This covers intervals where the current subtask is ongoing, $t \in (t_{k-1} + \epsilon_2, t_k - \epsilon_1)$. The model learns "semantic patience" by maintaining the active subtask and memory state.
 - **Final tail phase:** This concludes the trajectory, $t \in [t_K - \epsilon_1, t_K]$, where the model outputs a termination token.
 An approximately equal sampling ratio between these phases is targeted, with denser sampling in the scarcer transition and final frames. System-2 VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) is fine-tuned on this dataset to predict subtasks and update memory, followed by fine-tuning System-1 VLA (π0.5) on motor commands conditioned by the VLM's executable subtasks.

**Inference:**
- **Asynchronous Inference:** The VLM (System-2) and VLA (System-1) operate asynchronously. System-2 runs at a lower frequency to monitor progress and update semantic memory, while System-1 executes continuous action chunks at a higher frequency.
- **Harness Engineering:** A deployment harness acts as an arbitration layer, bridging open-ended human intentions with kinematically aligned skills. It constructs prompts for the VLM, providing rich task contexts (global instruction $I$ and updated memory $M^{(t)}$) and enforcing strict skill constraints on the VLM's output space. During post-processing, the harness maps the generated subtask description to the closest canonical primitive in the VLA's skill library using sequence matching. It also incorporates mechanisms for command holding, abnormal-transition filtering, and timeout-driven kinematic resets to ensure robust closed-loop execution and physical error recovery in real-world scenarios. The VLM is augmented with textual history of robot states, quantized into bounded integer tokens, to provide high-frequency progress estimation that may not be reliably recoverable from sparse image observations alone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Cortex involved open-loop VLM assessments, closed-loop simulation benchmarks, and real-world robot experiments, addressing the effectiveness of its interface, ambiguity resolution, and bidirectional alignment.

**Open-loop VLM Evaluation (Interface & Metadata):**
System-2's ability to generate physically grounded plans was assessed using an LLM-as-a-Judge protocol (Qwen-3.5-9B) across three dimensions: Spatial Information, Long-Horizon Task consistency, and Counting Task accuracy. Evaluations were conducted at both step-level (teacher-forced, using ground-truth memory) and episode-level (self-forced, with autoregressive memory input).
Ablation studies on Cortex variants demonstrated that the sequential introduction of components (baseline with event-balanced sampling, addition of interface information, restriction of harness to skills, and full harness) yielded consistent performance improvements. The full harness configuration achieved the highest average total scores: 8.318 at the step-level and 7.810 at the episode-level. These scores surpassed those of state-of-the-art generalist models like GPT-5 and Gemini, indicating the efficacy of the curated structural metadata and multi-granular instruction priors in reducing kinematic hallucinations. An ablation study on the Galaxea dataset further confirmed that event-balanced sampling, by optimizing the ratio of boundary transition to intra-task execution phases, led to improved subtask accuracy, memory retention, and total scores despite using fewer total samples, validating its role in resolving temporal ambiguity.

**Closed-loop Simulation Evaluation (Resolving Ambiguities):**
The complete dual-system framework was evaluated in multi-stage simulation environments, strictly conditioned on raw, high-level global instructions.
- **LIBERO-Long Benchmark:** Cortex achieved a zero-shot success rate of 95.5%, which represented a 3.1% improvement over monolithic baselines such as π0.5 (92.4%). Other leading generalist agents, like Gemini-3.1-Pro (91.0%), exhibited lower performance, suggesting challenges in physically grounding their plans due to their disembodied observation nature.
- **RoboTwin Evaluation:** Cortex demonstrated a success rate of 88.00% on long-horizon task splits (86.8% overall), outperforming monolithic VLAs (e.g., π0.5 at 82.74%) which experienced performance degradation as task horizon increased. This robustness was attributed to Cortex's ambiguity resolution mechanisms:
 - **Semantic Ambiguity:** Fine-grained attribute and spatial grounding, combined with dynamic insertion of intermediate subtasks (e.g., "handover" in `dump bin bigbin`, improving from 92% to 98%), resulted in kinematically favorable task chunking.
 - **Temporal Ambiguity:** Event-balanced sampling allowed System-2 to actively dictate temporal flow, injecting intermediate subtasks (e.g., `press button`) to ensure continuous physical progression.
- **RMBench Evaluation:** Across memory-dependent tasks, Cortex showed strong performance, including 100% success on "Rearrange Blocks" and "Put Back Block," 99% on "Swap Blocks," and 20% on "Press Button," significantly outperforming other baselines. The inclusion of a textual robot state history in the prompt was critical for tasks requiring high-frequency progress estimation, such as counting button presses.

**Closed-loop Real-world Evaluation (Asynchronous Bidirectional Loop):**
Cortex was deployed zero-shot on an ARX ACONE physical platform for complex multi-stage chemistry experiments and kitchen assembly workflows.
- **Results:** Cortex achieved a 65% success rate on "Chemical Washing" and 55% on other long-horizon real-world tasks. This contrasts with end-to-end methods like π0.5 and task-level πmem, which yielded 0% success, often getting stuck in repetitive actions or failing to infer global progress. Cortex's performance was comparable to human-guided policies using a fine-tuned VLA (70-75%).
- **Key Capabilities:**
 - **Zero-shot Completion:** The generalist VLM enabled completion of unseen real-world tasks simply by combining with a fine-tuned VLA, a capability not feasible through VLA fine-tuning alone.
 - **Closed-Loop Physical Error Recovery:** The asynchronous bidirectional loop, augmented by harness engineering, supported fault tolerance. For instance, timeout-driven kinematic resets resolved perception-action desynchronization, allowing Cortex to re-process updated observations and dispatch corrected subtasks, as demonstrated in a 14-step chemistry procedure.
 - **Subtask-level Feedback and Trial-and-Error:** Cortex could maintain real-time feedback, identify deviations, preserve the current subtask, and re-attempt execution until visual evidence confirmed completion (e.g., repeated attempts to grasp a stopper or automatically recognizing a manually opened bottle cap and skipping the open-cap subtask).
 - **Prompt-mode Flexibility:** The system demonstrated adaptability to diverse instruction modalities (coarse goals, detailed procedures, explicit subtask lists), crucial for real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Cortex represents an advancement in the development of embodied AI agents for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks. Its significance lies in its capacity to address fundamental limitations of current VLA models and hierarchical planning systems. By formalizing a *bidirectionally aligned* framework, Cortex effectively mitigates the "Markovian short-sightedness" that hampers monolithic VLAs, as well as the "semantic-kinematic domain gap" prevalent in dual-system approaches. This framework provides a structured and executable interface between high-level cognitive planning (VLM) and low-level reactive execution (VLA), enabling a synergistic operation previously challenging to achieve.

The introduction of standardized subtask primitives and principles of tractability directly into the data generation pipeline contributes to more robust and grounded task planning. The event-balanced sampling strategy for training explicitly addresses ambiguities during subtask transitions, leading to improved task consistency and progress tracking. Furthermore, the meticulously designed harness engineering, including context provision, skill constraints, and robust error recovery mechanisms like timeout-driven kinematic resets, enhances the system's reliability in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments.

The demonstrated ability of Cortex to achieve state-of-the-art performance in complex simulations, combined with its robust zero-shot completion and physical error recovery capabilities in real-world multi-stage tasks (such as chemistry experiments), indicates a potential for broader application in industrial automation, domestic robotics, and scientific research. This work establishes a scalable foundation for long-horizon robot autonomy by enabling agents to reason about and execute complex procedures with a higher degree of independence and resilience to unexpected events.

Despite its contributions, the authors acknowledge limitations that suggest future research directions. Enhancing memory representation to integrate visual memory retrieval and pixel-level grounding could address the loss of spatial coordinates and visual nuances in text-based memory, particularly for large-scale mobile manipulation. Furthermore, improving sensitivity to high-frequency micro-state changes by seamlessly fusing proprioceptive states with vision encoders remains a challenge for rapidly changing and dynamic environments. Addressing these areas could further extend the capabilities and generalizability of the Cortex framework.
