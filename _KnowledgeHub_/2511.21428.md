# From Observation to Action: Latent Action-based Primitive Segmentation for VLA Pre-training in Industrial Settings

> [!summary] Summary
> A fully unsupervised pipeline, LAPS, extracts and segments semantically coherent action primitives from continuous industrial video streams, generating structured datasets for Vision-Language-Action model pre-training. It achieved an F1@5s of 84.75 on an industrial motor assembly dataset and validated the semantic coherence of discovered action clusters using a VLM, providing a scalable solution for data acquisition in manufacturing.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models require large-scale, action-annotated training data, which is costly and difficult to obtain, particularly in industrial environments.
- Existing unsupervised action segmentation methods often rely on pixel-level visual changes, making them susceptible to non-semantic variations and limited in capturing behavioral intent from continuous video streams.
- Prior latent action learning approaches typically assume pre-segmented video clips, failing to address the upstream challenge of discovering and segmenting action primitives from raw, continuous observations.

## Method

- Introduces **LAPS**, a fully ==unsupervised pipeline== transforming continuous industrial video into structured action primitives for VLA pre-training, comprising Motion Tracking, Action Detection & Segmentation, and Semantic Action Clustering.
- Develops a motion tokenizer and a novel "Latent Action Energy" metric operating in an abstract latent action space to detect semantic action boundaries, capturing shifts in behavioral intent.
- Incorporates unsupervised clustering using a frozen Transformer encoder for temporal embedding, followed by k-means, and validates semantic coherence with a Vision-Language Model (VLM).

## Results

- LAPS achieved segmentation performance comparable to state-of-the-art unsupervised methods on public benchmarks (e.g., GTEA F1@**5s** of 73.12) and superior results on industrial datasets (e.g., Exocentric view F1@**5s** of 84.75, F1@**2s** of 81.93), outperforming baselines.
- Unsupervised clustering with a frozen Transformer encoder produced distinct and semantically coherent action clusters, validated by UMAP visualizations and quantitative metrics (Silhouette score of **0.588**, Calinski-Harabasz Index of 3919.2).
- VLM-based Intra-Cluster Semantic Similarity (ICSS) confirmed the high semantic coherence of discovered clusters (Overall ICSS of **0.926**), providing quantitative evidence of a high-quality, structured output dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic action boundaries are more effectively detected by tracking "behavioral intent" in an abstract latent action space (Latent Action Energy) rather than relying on noisy pixel-level visual or motion changes.
> - A specialized motion tokenizer, trained on keypoint tracks, is crucial for effectively capturing the specific dynamics of industrial tasks, outperforming generic visual features.
> - Explicitly modeling temporal dynamics with a frozen Transformer encoder significantly enhances the clustering of action primitives, leading to more semantically coherent groupings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist agents, frequently embodied as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, represents a significant research objective in robotics. These models, such as GR00T and AgiBot GO-1, require extensive pre-training on large-scale, diverse datasets comprising human video recordings and robot trajectories to achieve generalization and reliable instruction-following capabilities. However, a fundamental challenge in deploying these models in real-world settings, particularly in industrial environments, is the scarcity of high-quality, action-annotated training data. Unlike the abundant text and image data available online, obtaining annotated robot action data is typically costly and necessitates methods like teleoperation.

Current VLA pre-training strategies often employ a hierarchical framework: a high-level generalist module encoding action sequences into abstract latent action tokens, and a low-level controller fine-tuned on these token sequences. This approach shifts the data bottleneck to the requirement for large collections of pre-segmented video clips paired with latent token sequences from curated datasets. The broader research landscape has explored latent action representations to mitigate the labeled data bottleneck, learning action priors from video data in an abstract, embodiment-agnostic space. Prior works like LAPO and LAPA demonstrated the effectiveness of latent action learning but often relied on pixel-level objectives (e.g., next-frame prediction or VQ-VAE-based reconstruction) and assumed access to already curated short video clips, rather than addressing the upstream challenge of discovering and segmenting action primitives from continuous video streams. Such pixel-level objectives can also capture action-irrelevant background noise, limiting descriptive capacity.

Unsupervised action segmentation, which involves identifying boundaries between actions, is commonly addressed by local boundary detectors. While fully-supervised and weakly-supervised methods exist, they require labels or transcripts. Existing unsupervised techniques, such as ABD, detect change-points based on visual feature similarity, making them susceptible to non-semantic physical changes like lighting variations. More complex methods like OTAS integrate explicit features such as object detectors and Graph Neural Networks, introducing significant computational complexity.

Robot learning has predominantly focused on home and laboratory settings with varied and unstructured tasks. Industrial environments, characterized by structured, repetitive workflows and a finite set of skilled actions, present a distinct and valuable domain. While some large-scale initiatives have begun to include industrial scenarios, data acquisition largely remains through manual teleoperation.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing a fully unsupervised system designed to extract a core "vocabulary" of actions from passive observation of continuous industrial video streams. It proposes a novel approach that leverages latent action representations for identifying temporal boundaries of action primitives, moving beyond pixel-level or optical flow changes. The system directly tackles the data sourcing bottleneck for industrial VLA latent pre-training by automatically transforming unstructured, hours-long industrial video footage into a structured repository of action primitives. By operating in a completely unsupervised manner and focusing on "behavioral intent change detection" rather than visual change, the presented framework aims to provide a scalable solution for integrating embodied AI in manufacturing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the significant data bottleneck in training generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly for deployment in industrial settings. These models require vast quantities of high-quality, action-annotated data for effective pre-training and generalization, yet such data is expensive and challenging to acquire through traditional methods like teleoperation. Industrial environments, while structured and characterized by repetitive tasks, still rely on manual data annotation for robot learning, hindering scalability.

The key objectives of this work are:

- **To develop a novel unsupervised framework** for unlocking and structuring large volumes of unlabeled human demonstration data from continuous industrial video streams. This aims to automatically generate suitable data for VLA model pre-training.
- **To enable the automatic discovery and segmentation of semantically coherent action primitives** from these continuous video streams. This involves moving beyond low-level visual or motion cues to identify meaningful task phases.
- **To introduce a "Latent Action Energy" metric** derived from an abstract latent action space, which can robustly detect semantic action boundaries by capturing shifts in "behavioral intent" rather than mere physical movement.
- **To create a fully automated, end-to-end pipeline** that can transform raw industrial video footage into a structured dataset comprising segmented video clips and their corresponding latent action sequences, directly amenable to VLA pre-training.
- **To validate the semantic coherence of the discovered action primitives** through unsupervised clustering and a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based assessment, thereby confirming the quality of the generated dataset for downstream learning tasks.
- **To provide a scalable solution** for embodied AI integration in manufacturing by autonomously discovering the complete "action vocabulary" of workstations through passive observation, thereby reducing reliance on costly manual annotation and teleoperation.

Essentially, the research aims to allow robots to learn like humans through continuous, self-directed observation, by providing the structured, high-level action primitive data that is crucial for pre-training generalist VLA models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, named LAPS (Latent Action-based Primitive Segmentation), implements a fully unsupervised pipeline that converts continuous, unlabeled industrial video streams into a structured dataset of action primitives for VLA pre-training. The pipeline consists of three sequential stages: Motion Tracking, Action Detection & Segmentation, and Semantic Action Clustering.

#### 3.1. Motion Tracking

The initial stage utilizes off-the-shelf point trackers, such as CoTracker, to extract dense motion trajectories from raw video streams. These trajectories, represented as motion keypoints, are buffered in a sliding window for subsequent processing.

#### 3.2. Action Detection & Segmentation

This core stage transforms motion keypoints into segmented action primitives.
- **Motion Tokenizer:** A lightweight motion tokenizer, Mθ, based on a Transformer-based autoencoder (derived from AMPLIFY), is trained on a large dataset of short video clips. It comprises an encoder (Eθ) and a decoder (Dθ), along with a Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) layer for discretization. Keypoint tracks (κ ∈ R^(T×N×2)) are used as input. The encoder transforms velocities derived from these tracks into a latent sequence, which is then discretized into tokens z_t ∈ Z. The decoder is trained with a classification objective to predict the relative displacement of each track point, modeling motion dynamics as a categorical distribution. The tokenizer outputs two representations for each sliding window: a sequence of continuous quantized vectors (S_q = {z_q,1..., z_q,T}) from the FSQ codebook and a sequence of discrete code indices (S_d = {c_1..., c_T}). The continuous sequence S_q is crucial for internal operations, preserving geometric and semantic information, while the discrete indices S_d form the final segment-level sequences for VLA pre-training.
- **Latent Action Energy (E_action):** A novel metric defined as the L2 norm of the temporal difference within the continuous quantized latent space: E_action(t) = ||z_q,t - z_q,t-1||_2. This metric remains low during periods of stable latent action (inactivity) and exhibits sustained high activation during continuous, coherent action primitives. Semantic shifts, marking action boundaries, are detected when the energy signal returns to a low state.
- **Unsupervised Action Detector:** This component functions as a causal state machine with hysteresis, processing the one-dimensional E_action(t) signal.
 - **Causal Signal Smoothing:** The raw E_action(t) is smoothed using an exponential moving average (EMA) to reduce high-frequency noise without future data leakage (y_t = αE_action(t) + (1 − α)y_t−1).
 - **Online Boundary Detection with Hysteresis:** A two-state (ON/OFF) controller detects segment boundaries. Activation (OFF → ON) occurs when y_t > θ_on for 'u' consecutive frames. Deactivation (ON → OFF) occurs when y_t < θ_off for 'd' consecutive frames, with θ_off ≤ θ_on. This dual-threshold and debounce scheme ensures stable segmentation in noisy streams.
 - **Primitive and Sequence Extraction:** Upon an OFF transition, the video segment (A_i) corresponding to the active interval is extracted, along with its overlapping discrete FSQ code indices (S_i), preparing data for VLA pre-training.
- **Threshold Calibration:** The primary threshold (θ_on) is determined via an unsupervised offline optimization procedure. A low-level velocity energy signal (temporal difference of keypoint velocities) is used as a proxy. A heuristic auto-thresholding method (Otsu's method) is applied to this proxy signal to generate binary pseudo-labels (y_pseudo). A parameter sweep then finds the θ_on for E_action(t) that maximizes the F1-score against these pseudo-labels. The lower threshold (θ_off) is a fraction of θ_on (θ_off = r ⋅ θ_on).

#### 3.3. Semantic Action Clustering

The final stage involves unsupervised discovery of the finite set of actions intrinsic to the workstation, validating the semantic consistency of segmented actions.
- **Temporal Embedding via Frozen Transformer:** Each segmented primitive A_i is represented by its continuous Latent Action Sequence S_q,i ∈ R^(Ti × dm). A lightweight Transformer encoder, operating in a training-free inference mode with randomly initialized and fixed parameters, processes these variable-length sequences. Input vectors are linearly projected with sinusoidal positional encodings, processed by a stack of L layers with H multi-head self-attention heads, and then aggregated into a single segment-level embedding e_i ∈ R^d using mean pooling (empirically found to be superior in this frozen setting, with d=256, L=4, H=4).
- **Action Clustering via Cosine k-means:** High-dimensional embeddings (d=256) are clustered using k-means. To adapt k-means for cosine distance, embeddings are first standardized to zero mean and unit variance, then L2-normalized. Standard k-means (minimizing Euclidean distance) on these normalized vectors is mathematically equivalent to optimizing cosine similarity. The number of clusters 'k' is set a priori based on domain knowledge of workstation tasks.
- **Semantic Validation via Vision-Language Model (VLM):** To quantitatively assess semantic coherence, an Intra-Cluster Semantic Similarity (ICSS) metric is proposed using a pre-trained VLM (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32).
 - **Segment Embedding:** For each video primitive A_i, a fixed-length embedding v_i is computed by sampling multiple frames, extracting L2-normalized visual features using the VLM’s encoder, and aggregating them via norm-weighted pooling.
 - **Pairwise Similarity:** For each cluster C_k, a set of primitive pairs is sampled.
 - **Metric Calculation:** ICSS_k is the average cosine similarity of all sampled pairs within a cluster. A high ICSS score compared to a random-pair baseline indicates successful grouping of semantically meaningful actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted a series of experiments on public benchmarks (GTEA and Breakfast datasets) and a proprietary Industrial Motor Assembly Dataset to validate the LAPS framework.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of the Latent Motion Energy

Qualitative analysis demonstrated that the Latent Action Energy (E_action) provided a more discriminative signal for semantic boundary detection compared to traditional optical flow-based approaches. E_action exhibited clear, sustained peaks during ongoing actions and sharp drops at ground truth semantic boundaries, indicating action completion. In contrast, optical flow signals were noisy and primarily reflected physical movement rather than semantic task phases, struggling to distinguish meaningful transitions from spurious motion artifacts. This supports the hypothesis that segmentation is more effectively performed within an abstract latent semantic space.

#### 4.2. Unsupervised Action Segmentation

- **Public Benchmarks (GTEA, Breakfast):** On these datasets, LAPS achieved performance comparable to state-of-the-art unsupervised temporal action detection (TAD) baselines such as ABD and OTAS. For instance, on GTEA, LAPS recorded an F1@5s of 73.12 and F1@2s of 63.20. On Breakfast, it achieved an F1@5s of 58.82 and F1@2s of 36.72. These results were obtained with lightweight training (approximately 25 minutes) on raw video, contrasting with baselines that often rely on extensive pre-training of complex visual features (e.g., I3D).
- **Industrial Motor Assembly Dataset:** LAPS demonstrated superior performance on the proprietary industrial dataset. For the top-down view, it achieved F1@5s of 84.26 and F1@2s of 81.27. For the exocentric view, it recorded F1@5s of 84.75 and F1@2s of 81.93. These scores significantly surpassed those of the optical flow baseline (e.g., F1@2s of 43.68 and 42.54), ABD (e.g., F1@2s of 34.08 and 29.86), and OTAS (e.g., F1@2s of 40.69 and 33.38). This indicates LAPS's robustness in industrial settings and its ability to accurately capture semantic transitions in repetitive, countable actions, where conventional methods degrade due to sensitivity to low-level physical motion variations.

#### 4.3. Quality of Action Primitives (Clustering)

- **UMAP Visualization:** A UMAP visualization of action primitive embeddings, colored by k-means cluster ID, revealed distinct and well-separated clusters. Manual inspection confirmed that these clusters corresponded to semantically coherent groupings within the discrete set of workstation tasks, such as 'Move Baffle', 'Replace Motor', and 'Grasp Motor'. This qualitative evidence supports the claim that the pipeline automatically discovers and organizes the inherent action vocabulary of a workstation.
- **Quantitative Clustering Metrics:** Quantitative validation on the Exocentric View dataset (with k=3) showed that the Frozen Transformer encoder significantly outperformed an attention-norm pooling baseline. LAPS achieved a Silhouette score of 0.588 and a Calinski-Harabasz Index of 3919.2, compared to 0.498 and 3523.6, respectively, for the baseline. This suggests that explicitly modeling temporal dynamics is essential for distinguishing subtle action patterns and forming semantically coherent clusters.

#### 4.4. Semantic Coherence Validation via VLM

The Intra-Cluster Semantic Similarity (ICSS) metric, validated using a Vision-Language Model (VLM), confirmed the semantic coherence of the discovered clusters. The mean ICSS within the discovered clusters (Overall ICSS: 0.926 ± 0.033) was substantially higher than the similarity of random pairs sampled from the entire dataset (Baseline: 0.804 ± 0.127). The tight standard deviations of the cluster scores, compared to the baseline's wider variance, provided quantitative evidence that the pipeline successfully grouped semantically similar action primitives. This automated validation supports that the output is a structured, high-quality dataset of "countable" actions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the impact of key design choices:
- **Motion Tokenizer:** Substituting the specialized motion tokenizer (Mθ) with generic CLIP features resulted in a severe degradation in both segmentation and clustering performance, emphasizing the criticality of a domain-specific tokenizer for industrial tasks.
- **Latent Action Energy Computation:** The effectiveness of the E_action metric was contingent on its computation in the quantized space (S_q). Applying it to raw velocities or pre-quantized latent representations yielded poor results, validating the importance of discretization for capturing semantic intent.
- **Clustering Encoder:** The Frozen Transformer encoder consistently outperformed simple mean-pooling in the clustering stage, demonstrating the necessity of explicitly modeling temporal dynamics for discovering coherent action vocabularies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a critical limitation in the development and deployment of generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the scarcity and high cost of acquiring large-scale, action-annotated training data, particularly in industrial settings. The Latent Action-based Primitive Segmentation (LAPS) pipeline offers a scalable and fully automated solution to this problem, transforming vast amounts of unstructured industrial video data into a structured, usable format for VLA pre-training.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enabling Scalable VLA Pre-training:** By automating the discovery and segmentation of semantically coherent action primitives, LAPS provides a mechanism to generate high-quality, structured datasets without manual annotation. This directly addresses the data bottleneck for training high-level generalist modules in hierarchical VLA frameworks, making it feasible to pre-train VLA models on real-world industrial observations at scale.
- **Unlocking Unlabeled Industrial Data:** The framework allows manufacturers to leverage their existing vast repositories of unlabeled video footage from production lines, transforming passive observations into a valuable knowledge base for embodied AI systems. This reduces reliance on expensive and time-consuming teleoperation or human annotation processes.
- **Robust Semantic Understanding:** The novel Latent Action Energy metric, operating in an abstract latent action space, is designed to capture "behavioral intent" rather than just physical movement. This leads to more meaningful action primitive segmentation that is resilient to non-semantic visual noise, a crucial aspect for robot learning in dynamic environments.
- **Automated Action Vocabulary Discovery:** The pipeline's ability to automatically cluster segmented primitives into semantically coherent categories effectively discovers the "action vocabulary" of an industrial workstation. This structured knowledge is essential for robots to understand and execute complex tasks.
- **Accelerated AI Deployment in Manufacturing:** By providing a fully automated, end-to-end system for data preparation, LAPS can accelerate the integration and continuous enhancement of VLA models in real-world manufacturing settings. This supports the development of more adaptable and intelligent robotic systems for tasks like assembly, inspection, and material handling.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** The work represents a step towards allowing robots to learn from continuous, self-directed observation, akin to human learning. It provides a foundation for robots to acquire skills and knowledge autonomously from their environment.

While currently limited to highly repetitive tasks typical of manufacturing domains, the authors propose future work to extend this pipeline to more diverse domains, such as domestic households and hospitals. Additionally, efforts to bridge the gap from high-level task understanding to actual task execution, by correlating discovered latent spaces with teleoperated skills for dual-arm manipulators, indicate a clear path towards transforming this structured latent knowledge into real-world robotic capabilities.
