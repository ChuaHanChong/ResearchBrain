# BORA: Bridging Offline Reinforcement Learning and Online Residual Adaptation for Real-World Dexterous VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> BORA, from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborators, introduces an offline-to-online reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance real-world dexterous manipulation for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This framework achieves an absolute increase of 33% in average success rate under standard settings and up to 43% improvement in unseen object generalization by integrating stable offline intent learning with sample-efficient online residual adaptation.

## Problem

- Credit assignment failure in high-dimensional generative action architectures due to lengthy denoising chains and noisy micro-actions in dexterous manipulation.
- Visual occlusion during dexterous tasks causes traditional decoupled critics to overfit to background features, leading to erroneous value guidance.
- Real-world deployment introduces execution errors and distribution shifts, requiring continuous adaptation of offline-learned VLA policies, which is challenging and risky for large models.

## Method

- An offline RL phase employs a consistency policy for action chunk generation (1-3 denoising steps) and an action-conditioned critic, fusing VLM cognition tokens with action chunks to mitigate gradient vanishing and visual overfitting.
- A lightweight Residual Chunk Actor, operating on top of a frozen VLA base, generates online compensations for real-world execution errors, guided by critic inheritance and a conservative value objective.
- A Human-in-the-Loop (HiL) system integrates human corrections and an asymmetric intervention-driven reward function (penalizing interventions, rewarding recovery) to enable sample-efficient online adaptation via an RLPD pipeline.

## Results

- BORA-Offline improved average success rates by 14 points (to **67%**) and unseen object generalization by 25 points (to **52%**) over a pure imitation learning baseline by effectively learning high-fidelity physical intents.
- The full BORA framework achieved the highest performance, with an overall average success rate of **86%** in standard settings and **70%** under object-unseen configurations.
- Online adaptation demonstrated rapid convergence, requiring only 1-2 human interventions per task (approximately **20%** of online trajectory execution time), indicating high sample efficiency for real-world adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Truncating the generative action's computation graph with a consistency policy allows for stable and efficient gradient flow, enabling the VLM to learn high-level physical intents from high-dimensional, multimodal dexterous actions.
> - Conditioning the critic on VLM cognition tokens and continuous action chunks grounds value estimation in physical interactions, making it robust to severe visual occlusions common in dexterous manipulation.
> - A lightweight residual adaptation mechanism, combined with critic inheritance and sparse human interventions, enables rapid, sample-efficient, and safe online fine-tuning without causing catastrophic feature drift in the pre-trained VLM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is increasingly leveraging Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to enable robots to understand high-level commands and execute complex tasks in real-world environments. These models aim to bridge visual-language understanding with physical robotic manipulation. However, a significant challenge within this paradigm is the application to dexterous manipulation, which involves controlling high-dimensional robotic hands (high degrees of freedom or DoFs). Existing VLA policies, often trained via imitation learning (IL), frequently struggle with the inherent action diversity, complex contact dynamics, and potential for compounding execution errors present in dexterous tasks.

Current VLA post-training schemes, which are designed to adapt pre-trained VLA policies to specific robots and tasks, encounter several limitations in dexterous contexts. These include "credit assignment failure" in action generation, particularly with generative architectures like Diffusion Models that involve many denoising steps, leading to noise accumulation in RL gradients. Additionally, visual occlusion, common in dexterous manipulation, can cause traditional decoupled critics to overfit to background visual artifacts rather than physical interactions. Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) can reuse data but may offer limited gains due to suboptimal demonstrations and distribution shifts. Online RL, while adaptive, is often costly, sample-inefficient, and unstable for high-DoF dexterous hands. Existing offline-to-online RL methods, primarily developed for robot arms or parallel grippers, do not fully address the specific challenges of high-dimensional dexterous actions, severe occlusions, and noisy correction data that can destabilize value estimation and lead to catastrophic feature drift in the pre-trained VLM.

BORA addresses these issues by proposing an offline-to-online RL post-training framework specifically tailored for real-world dexterous VLA models. It aims to improve real-world success rates and generalization by refining the VLA's understanding of physical interaction intents through a two-stage adaptation process. This work builds upon and extends recent advancements in VLA models, consistency policies for efficient action generation, and offline-to-online RL methodologies, adapting them to the intricacies of dexterous manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient post-training framework, named BORA (Bridging Offline Reinforcement Learning and Online Residual Adaptation), for real-world dexterous Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The framework aims to enhance the reliability and success rates of VLA policies when deployed on physical dexterous robots.

The motivation for BORA stems from several core challenges identified in the application of VLA models to dexterous manipulation:

1. **High-Dimensional Hand Control and Action Diversity:** Dexterous manipulation involves a high number of degrees of freedom (DoFs) and diverse action possibilities to achieve the same task. This complexity makes it difficult for traditional imitation learning (IL) to extract generalized physical interaction intents, resulting in policies that are unstable and prone to failure in real-world settings.
2. **Credit Assignment Failure in Generative Action Architectures:** Mainstream generative action models (e.g., Diffusion Models) typically involve many denoising steps. When applied to dexterous actions, which often contain redundant or unintentional micro-actions, backpropagating Reinforcement Learning (RL) gradients through long temporal computation graphs leads to significant noise accumulation. This hinders the model's ability to extract high-level intents and align them with low-level action execution, making effective RL post-training difficult.
3. **Visual Occlusion at the Perceptual Level:** Dexterous manipulation frequently involves the robot's hand occluding the manipulated object or critical task regions. Traditional decoupled RL critics, which primarily rely on visual context, are susceptible to overfitting to background visual artifacts rather than evaluating the actual physical contact consequences of actions. This can lead to erroneous guidance for dexterous VLA models.
4. **Real-World Execution Errors and Distribution Shifts:** Even with robust offline training, real-world deployment introduces discrepancies due to complex friction, contact dynamics, and state deviations, causing action-outcome mismatches. The offline-learned intents must adapt to these online distribution shifts.
5. **Impracticality of Full-Parameter Online Fine-Tuning:** Directly updating all parameters of a VLA model during online fine-tuning is impractical due to fragile dexterous hardware limiting data collection and the noisy nature of online intervention data. Such full-parameter updates can easily trigger catastrophic feature drift and value collapse in pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), undoing prior learning.
6. **Need for Sample-Efficient and Safe Online Adaptation:** High-DoF dexterous hands make online exploration sample-inefficient and potentially unsafe. There is a need for a framework that enables rapid and safe adaptation to the real world without requiring extensive or risky physical interactions, while also preventing catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained VLM representation.

BORA aims to address these motivations by introducing a novel offline-to-online framework that integrates an action-conditioned critic, a consistency policy for efficient action generation, and a lightweight, human-in-the-loop residual adaptation mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BORA proposes a two-stage offline-to-online reinforcement learning framework designed for dexterous VLA models. The core philosophy is to first establish foundational manipulation skills through offline RL and subsequently compensate for real-world execution errors via Human-in-the-Loop (HiL) online residual RL adaptation.

#### 3.1. Offline RL with Action-Conditioned Critic and Consistency Policy

The offline phase of BORA addresses the challenges of credit assignment failure and visual occlusion in high-dimensional dexterous action spaces.

- **Consistency Policy for Action Generation:** To mitigate credit assignment failure caused by long denoising chains in generative action architectures, the actor is parameterized as a consistency policy. This policy generates continuous action chunks (sequences of actions) in 1-3 denoising steps, significantly truncating the computation graph. This truncation facilitates efficient backpropagation of informative gradients into the VLM, promoting stable offline optimization and alignment between high-level task representations and low-level action execution.
- **Action-Conditioned Critic:** To counteract visual overfitting caused by occlusions and to handle high-dimensional chunk-level evaluation, the critic is designed to explicitly fuse continuous action chunks with the VLM’s cognition tokens ($z_t = \Phi_{\text{VLM}}(s_t)$). The critic outputs $k$-dimensional value vectors, $Q_{\phi}(s_t, A_t) = [q_{\phi}(z_t, a_{t+i}, i)]_{i=0}^{k-1} \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and $V_{\psi}(s_t) = [v_{\psi}(z_t, i)]_{i=0}^{k-1} \in \mathbb{R}^k$, where $i$ denotes a relative position encoded by a learnable positional embedding. This design ensures value estimation is grounded in physical interactions rather than solely on visual context.
- **Shifted Value Bootstrap:** To enforce temporal consistency and propagate credit within action chunks, a shifted value bootstrap mechanism is employed. The target value $y_{t,i}$ for a specific step $i$ within a chunk depends on rewards $r_{t+i}$ and the value of the next step within the chunk $v_{\psi}(z_t, i+1)$ or, for the last step, on the value of the next state $v_{\psi}(z_{t+1}, 0)$.
- **Critic Optimization:** The critic parameters $\phi$ and $\psi$ are optimized using a masked Bellman residual loss for the Q-function and an IQL-style expectile value objective for the V-function. The expectile objective is used to extract conservative value targets from potentially suboptimal offline data, enhancing stability.
- **Conservative Policy Improvement:** The actor, parameterized as a consistency-based policy $\pi_{\theta}$ defining a Gaussian action distribution, is optimized using a validity-masked clipped PPO surrogate objective. This objective utilizes an intra-chunk Action-level GAE Recursion ($\hat{A}_{t,i}$) to smooth the optimization signal, representing the instantaneous advantage $\delta_{t,i} = q_{\phi}(z_t, a_{t+i}, i) - v_{\psi}(z_t, i)$. To prevent out-of-distribution deviation and ensure conservatism, the full actor objective combines PPO with Behavior Cloning (BC) regularization on the normalized action chunk. An optional advantage gating mechanism may skip actor updates under noisy offline value estimates.
- **Two-Stage Post-Training:** The offline procedure is divided into a BC Warm-up stage, where only the BC objective is used, and a subsequent BC+PPO Guidance stage, where both objectives are combined, using critic-derived advantages.

#### 3.2. Bridging the Deployment Gap: HiL Residual Chunk Adaptation

The online phase focuses on adapting the offline-trained policy to real-world complexities while preserving the foundation model's priors.

- **Frozen VLA Base and Lightweight Residual Actor:** To prevent catastrophic feature drift, the offline-trained VLA base is frozen. A lightweight Residual Chunk Actor ($\pi_{\text{res}}$), parameterized by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), is introduced to generate compensations at the action chunk level. The final deployed composite chunk ($A_{\text{final}}$) is formulated as $A_{\text{base}} + \lambda_{\text{res}} \cdot \pi_{\text{res}}(s_{\text{prop}}, A_{\text{base}}, z_{\text{VLM}})$, where $\lambda_{\text{res}}$ is a scaling coefficient to restrict intervention magnitude and ensure action manifold smoothness. For subsequent rounds, the base action can also incorporate the prior round's residual actor.
- **Critic Inheritance and Conservative Value Guidance:** The offline-trained critic $Q_{\phi}$ initializes the online value function, preserving a pre-shaped value landscape. To ensure monotonic policy improvement, the residual actor optimizes a conservative value guidance objective (hinge loss) that penalizes residual actions that underperform the frozen VLA base by a margin $\delta$. This mechanism regularizes adaptation within a safe optimization boundary.
- **Intervention-Driven RLPD Pipeline:** Human corrections are integrated via an RLPD (Reinforcement Learning from Physical Demonstrations) protocol. This protocol maintains a 1:1 sampling ratio between the static offline dataset and an online buffer dynamically enriched by human interventions. This joint optimization regularizes the model against non-stationary online trajectories.
- **Human-in-the-Loop (HiL) Intervention System:** Following DexHiL, human operators can take control to recover tasks when the model fails. A linear interpolation mechanism ensures smooth transitions from autonomous execution to human takeover, maintaining temporal consistency of hand poses.
- **Asymmetric Intervention-Driven Reward Function:** To provide core optimization signals for the RLPD pipeline, an asymmetric reward function is engineered. An instant penalty ($r_{\text{int}}$) is imposed if an intervention is triggered (policy drifts out of distribution). Conversely, a positive recovery reward ($r_{\text{rec}}$) is granted upon successful completion of a human corrective action. This mechanism guides value updates, compelling the residual policy to penalize high-risk states and learn from high-quality recovery trajectories with minimal real-world interactions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates BORA across five complex real-world dexterous tasks: Pick the plush toy, Pick and Place, Open the box, Pull the tissue, and Press the button. Experiments are conducted under both standard and object-unseen configurations (novel objects) over 20 trials per task.

#### 4.1. Offline Intent Alignment with Action-Conditioned Value Guidance (RQ1)

- **Limitations of Imitation Learning (IL):** The "CP Base" (consistency policy-based IL without RL fine-tuning) achieved an average success rate of 53.0% under standard settings, which decreased to 27.0% under object-unseen evaluation. This indicates limited generalization capability of pure IL in high-dimensional and multimodal dexterous action spaces. "VITRA (Diffusion)" baseline, using a diffusion action expert, showed similar or slightly lower performance.
- **Efficacy of BORA-Offline:** BORA-Offline, which incorporates the consistency policy and action-conditioned critic, demonstrated improved performance. It achieved average success rates of 67.0% in the standard setting (a 14 percentage point gain over CP Base) and 52.0% under object-unseen evaluation (a 25 percentage point gain over CP Base). This suggests that BORA's truncated consistency policy formulation effectively resolves the credit assignment bottleneck in offline RL by allowing informative gradients to flow into the VLM.
- **Action-Conditioned Critic vs. Decoupled Critic:** The "Decoupled-Critic Baseline," which uses a separately trained critic not jointly conditioned on VLM cognition tokens and action chunks, performed poorly. In the standard setting, it averaged below CP Base. Under object-unseen evaluation, it exhibited unstable value guidance, collapsing on the "Open the Box" task despite some improvements on others. In contrast, BORA-Offline's integrated critic, by conditioning on VLM semantic tokens, bound value updates to the multimodal latent space, demonstrating greater stability and preventing overfitting to spurious visual features. Qualitative support from t-SNE visualizations indicated BORA-Offline maintained the policy’s action-representation structure better, staying closer to the SFT/BC manifold. Saliency maps further showed BORA's critic placing stronger emphasis on the dexterous hand and task-relevant contact regions, unlike the decoupled critic which showed scattered responses on non-contact areas.

#### 4.2. Sample-Efficient Online Physical Adaptation (RQ2)

- **Addressing the Offline-to-Online Gap:** Despite the intent awareness established offline, a performance drop was observed on object-unseen tasks for BORA-Offline. BORA-Full, which adds the online residual chunk adaptation, provided a solution.
- **Rapid Performance Convergence:** BORA-Full exhibited rapid performance convergence within the initial two online reinforcement learning rounds, with minimal further improvement afterward. This indicates sample efficiency, as human operators intervened only 1-2 times per task, representing approximately 20% of the online trajectory execution time.
- **Overall Performance Improvement:** BORA-Full achieved the highest average success rates among all evaluated baselines. In the standard setting, it reached an average of 86.0% (a 33 percentage point absolute increase over CP Base and 19 percentage points over BORA-Offline). Under the object-unseen configuration, it elevated the average success rate from 52.0% (BORA-Offline) to 70.0% (a 43 percentage point increase over CP Base and 18 percentage points over BORA-Offline). This demonstrated effective adaptation while preventing representation drift in the frozen pre-trained VLM foundation.

#### 4.3. Mechanistic Analysis of Critic Inheritance (RQ3)

- **Stable and Responsive Value Estimation:** The inherited offline-trained action-conditioned critic, combined with the online asymmetric intervention-driven reward, provided stable and discriminative value estimation.
- **Discriminative Capability:** The critic maintained high value confidence along successful trajectories and consistently low Q-value estimates along failed trajectories, even in visually similar states with severe hand-object occlusion. This ability to evaluate state-action pairs at a structural semantic level, rather than relying on raw camera pixels, was crucial.
- **Guidance for Residual Policy:** The inherited critic mitigated superficial visual ambiguities, penalized high-risk execution states, and effectively guided the lightweight residual policy ($\pi_{\text{res}}$) to incorporate human corrective priors with minimal physical interactions. Value-function visualizations further supported this, showing distinct value profiles for successful versus failure episodes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The BORA framework presents a significant advancement in the domain of real-world dexterous manipulation using Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance stems from addressing several fundamental challenges that have historically limited the practical deployment of such systems.

Firstly, BORA's **action-conditioned critic and consistency policy** design offers a solution to the "credit assignment failure" inherent in high-dimensional action spaces and the problem of visual occlusion. By grounding value estimation in both VLM cognition tokens and continuous action chunks, the framework ensures that learning focuses on actual physical interactions rather than spurious visual features. This approach provides more stable and reliable value guidance during offline reinforcement learning, a critical step for initial skill acquisition.

Secondly, the **lightweight, Human-in-the-Loop (HiL) online residual adaptation mechanism** is crucial for bridging the offline-to-online gap. By freezing the VLA base and training only a small residual actor, BORA prevents catastrophic feature drift and value collapse that can occur during full-parameter online fine-tuning. This, coupled with critic inheritance and intervention-driven rewards, enables rapid, sample-efficient, and safe adaptation to real-world execution errors and physical variances. The reported 33% absolute increase in average success rate and up to 43% improvement in unseen object generalization underscore its practical effectiveness.

The potential impact of BORA is multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Reliability of Dexterous Robots:** By improving real-world success rates and generalization capabilities, BORA could accelerate the deployment of dexterous robots in complex tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation, such as in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, or domestic assistance.
- **More Efficient Robot Learning:** The sample-efficient online adaptation reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection, making the training process less time-consuming, less costly, and less prone to hardware wear-and-tear. This is particularly important for delicate and expensive dexterous hardware.
- **Foundation for Future Dexterous AI:** The framework provides a robust foundation for building more capable and adaptable dexterous VLA models. It opens avenues for investigating how to integrate additional sensory inputs, such as dense tactile feedback, and for scaling to even more complex, long-horizon operational tasks.
- **Progress in Offline-to-Online RL:** BORA contributes to the broader field of offline-to-online reinforcement learning by demonstrating effective strategies for combining the scalability of offline data with the adaptivity of online interaction, specifically tailored for high-dimensional, contact-rich robotic tasks.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the reliance on visuo-proprioceptive inputs (without dense tactile feedback) and evaluation on a single arm-hand topology, the demonstrated performance indicates BORA's substantial contribution to making dexterous VLA models more robust and applicable in real-world scenarios.
