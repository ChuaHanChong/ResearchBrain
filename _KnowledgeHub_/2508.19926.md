# FARM: Frame-Accelerated Augmentation and Residual Mixture-of-Experts for Physics-Based High-Dynamic Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The FARM framework enhances physics-based humanoid control by combining frame-accelerated augmentation with a residual Mixture-of-Experts architecture, achieving a 42.8% reduction in tracking failure rates and a 14.6% decrease in global mean per-joint position error on high-dynamic motions while preserving performance on low-dynamic actions, alongside introducing the High-Dynamic Humanoid Motion (HDHM) dataset.

## Problem

- Existing physics-based humanoid controllers struggle to generalize robustly to highly dynamic, explosive motions, despite performing well on low-dynamic, everyday movements.
- The lack of a public, standardized benchmark dataset specifically for high-dynamic humanoid motions hinders systematic evaluation and progress in this challenging area.
- Current approaches often require significant engineering overhead for task-specific reward redesign and hyperparameter tuning, limiting large-scale adoption.

## Method

![[2508.19926-method.png]]
*The overall FARM pipeline, including (a) hard sample mining from existing datasets, (b) the training process using PPO, and (c) inference on high-dynamic motion benchmarks.*

- Develops the **FARM** framework, which uses frame-accelerated data augmentation to expose the controller to rapid pose changes during training.
- Incorporates a residual Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module with a ==Speed-Aware Router== and Dynamic Expert-Assignment, enabling adaptive computational resource allocation based on motion intensity.
- Curates and releases the High-Dynamic Humanoid Motion (HDHM) dataset to serve as a public benchmark for high-dynamic control evaluation.

## Results

- FARM significantly improved high-dynamic motion tracking, reducing failure rates by **42.8%** and global mean per-joint position error (MPJPEg) by **14.6%** on the HDHM dataset compared to the FC baseline.
- The framework preserved near-perfect performance on low-dynamic AMASS motions, achieving a **100%** success rate with minimal increase in MPJPEg (from **40.8** mm to **42.1** mm) relative to the FC baseline.
- The residual MoE adaptively allocated computational resources, activating more experts for high-dynamic motions, and learned structured representations correlated with motion complexity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simply accelerating low-dynamic motion data is insufficient; a fundamental architectural adaptation, such as an adaptive Mixture-of-Experts, is required for robust high-dynamic control.
> - A residual Mixture-of-Experts architecture, when combined with a speed-aware router and dynamic expert assignment, can effectively extend the dynamic range of a base controller without degrading performance on low-dynamic tasks.
> - Targeted training on "hard samples" (mined failure cases from accelerated motions) significantly improves training efficiency and performance for high-dynamic control.
