# StellaVLA: In-Context Structured Demonstration for Generalizable Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> StellaVLA is a retrieval-augmented vision-language-action model that adapts at test time by conditioning on one retrieved demonstration converted offline into structured text: task plan, subgoal descriptions, and verbalized 2D/3D motion. A parallel dual-training objective supervises actions plus spatial language; the language expert is dropped at inference behind a KV-cached prefix. It ranks first on VLA-Arena (0.63), with 98.8% on LIBERO and 85.1% on LIBERO-Plus.

## Problem

- VLA policies degrade sharply out of distribution when scene, viewpoint, or object changes, and recovery normally demands fresh data collection plus weight fine-tuning.
- Existing in-context imitation learning supplies only raw observations and continuous actions, encouraging surface-level mimicry; policies treat demonstrations as noise and fall back on pretrained priors.
- Embodied chain-of-thought methods expose reasoning but decode text autoregressively at deployment, adding latency that breaks the timing requirements of high-frequency robotic control.

## Method

- **Offline structured-context extraction**: an off-the-shelf VLM segments trajectories into subgoals, and a ==deterministic verbaliser== renders each segment's 3D workspace displacement plus its 2D camera-plane projection as text, without human labels.
- **Parallel dual-training**: one shared ==Qwen3-VL-4B== backbone feeds an MLP action expert regressing action chunks under L1 loss and a ==spatial-language expert== trained by cross-entropy (weight 0.3) on subtask and verbalized motion.
- **Asymmetric inference with demonstration prefix caching**: the spatial-language expert is stripped at deployment and the immutable retrieved prefix is ==KV-cached== once, leaving the control loop free of autoregressive decoding.

## Results

- Ranks first on VLA-Arena with overall score **0.63** versus **0.44** for the strongest baseline, and per-level means of **0.84**, **0.62**, and **0.43** across L0-L2.
- Reaches **98.8%** average on LIBERO and **85.1%** zero-shot on LIBERO-Plus, beating the matched demonstration-free control by **10.1** points, with **+23.5** under camera-viewpoint perturbation.
- Replacing the correct demonstration with none drops LIBERO average to **62.4%** and a wrong-task demo to **44.9%**; decoding the language output costs **3177ms** versus **88ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured language abstracts demonstrations away from appearance: text-only context nearly matches image plus text, while image-only context wins in distribution but collapses under visual perturbation.
> - Reasoning supervision can be paid for during training and discarded at inference, but more is not better: raising its loss weight improved in-distribution precision while monotonically hurting robustness.
> - Retrieved context transfers procedure, not geometry: gains concentrate on viewpoint and goal ambiguity, vanish under object-layout change, and a fixed prefix cannot re-plan after drift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotic manipulation, translating visual and textual inputs into physical actions. A significant challenge for these models is their performance degradation, often referred to as "collapse," when operating out of distribution (OOD). This occurs when the scene, viewpoint, or objects encountered differ from those present during training, typically necessitating new data collection and fine-tuning.

In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) has emerged as an approach to address OOD performance by adapting policies at test time without requiring weight updates. Existing ICIL frameworks typically condition the policy on retrieved expert trajectories, consisting of raw observations and continuous actions, as a contextual prefix. However, this approach primarily facilitates surface-level imitation, showing "what" an expert did without conveying "why." This limitation can lead to behavioral inertia, hindering the model's ability to generalize to novel OOD tasks. The paper positions StellaVLA as an advancement within the ICIL paradigm, addressing this limitation by integrating explicit reasoning into demonstrations.

Within the broader VLA research, several directions are explored:
- **VLA Model Architectures:** Prior work has focused on various action generation methods (discretized tokens, regression, flow-matching), computational efficiency, and augmenting policy representations with geometric, depth, or world-model priors. StellaVLA retains a standard VLM backbone with a continuous-action expert, focusing on how expert demonstrations are represented and exploited.
- **Language as Action Representation:** Approaches in this area verbalize low-level actions, jointly model language and action tokens, or represent action hierarchies in language. StellaVLA extends this by using language as a shared representation for both retrieved context and training supervision, organizing demonstrations into semantic sub-goals and kinematic rationales. This differs from latent-action approaches that encode behavior into learned latent variables outside the VLM's native semantic space.
- **Test-Time Adaptation:** Policies can adapt by updating model parameters or by conditioning a fixed policy. StellaVLA falls into the latter category, adapting behavior through demonstrations. It distinguishes itself by employing retrieved demonstrations as *explicit task demonstrations* with structured procedural context, rather than merely additional execution history, enabling adaptation without parameter updates.
- **Human and Cross-Embodiment Demonstrations:** Leveraging diverse data sources like human videos and heterogeneous robot data is a recognized strategy to broaden behavioral coverage. Prior work addresses embodiment mismatch through shared latent actions, cross-embodiment motion representations, or retargeting. StellaVLA tackles this at the representation level, converting real-robot, human-hand, and XR-retargeted demonstrations into a common structured representation before retrieval. Crucially, off-embodiment demonstrations serve only as context, while executable actions are always predicted and supervised in the target robot's native control space, enabling procedural guidance without requiring direct action space alignment.

By focusing on augmenting demonstrations with structured reasoning and employing a parallel dual-training design, StellaVLA seeks to overcome the generalization limitations of current VLA models while maintaining real-time control efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for StellaVLA stems from the observed fragility of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models when faced with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. Current VLA models exhibit a notable performance decline when the environment, viewpoint, or objects differ significantly from their training data. Addressing these OOD challenges typically requires extensive data collection and subsequent fine-tuning for each new situation, a process that is resource-intensive and impractical for real-world deployment.

In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) offers a promising alternative by enabling adaptation at test time without altering model weights. However, existing ICIL methods often present retrieved expert trajectories as raw sequences of observations and actions. This formulation primarily facilitates "surface-level imitation," where the policy observes "what" the expert did but lacks insight into "why" those actions were performed. This absence of explicit reasoning often leads to "behavioral inertia," where the policy struggles to generalize to novel OOD tasks and instead defaults to its pre-trained priors, treating the demonstration as noise.

The core objective of StellaVLA is to enhance the generalization capabilities of VLA models by moving beyond mere action imitation to incorporating the underlying reasoning. This objective is motivated by the observation that foundation models tend to generalize more effectively when intermediate reasoning steps are explicitly articulated.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:
- **To address OOD performance degradation:** To enable VLA models to adapt robustly to unseen scenes, viewpoints, or objects without the need for additional data collection or fine-tuning at deployment.
- **To shift from "what" to "why" in demonstrations:** To provide the VLA policy with structured reasoning, allowing it to understand the intent and plan behind expert actions, rather than just mimicking pixel-level trajectories.
- **To create structured demonstrations efficiently:** To develop an automated, offline pipeline that converts raw expert trajectories into structured, reasoning-augmented demonstrations (e.g., task plans, sub-goal descriptions, verbalized 3D motion) without incurring any human annotation costs.
- **To enable cross-embodiment transfer:** To design a framework where structured demonstrations can be transferred across different embodiments, such as real-robot, human-hand, or XR demonstrations, thereby leveraging diverse data sources.
- **To internalize reasoning during training:** To implement a parallel dual-training design that uses a joint action-and-language objective to supervise the policy, enabling it to internalize both "what to do" and "why."
- **To maintain real-time control at inference:** To ensure that the incorporation of structured reasoning does not introduce prohibitive latency during execution, allowing for real-time, high-frequency robotic control by removing the language expert at inference and utilizing demonstration prefix caching.

By achieving these objectives, StellaVLA aims to equip VLA models with a more robust and generalizable "mental model" for robotic manipulation, enhancing their practical applicability in complex and varied environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The StellaVLA framework is designed to improve VLA generalization through structured contextual rationales, extending In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL). The methodology consists of three main components: offline structured context extraction, a parallel dual-training paradigm, and an asymmetric inference strategy.

#### 3.1. Offline Structured Context Extraction

This component automatically converts raw expert trajectories into rationale-augmented segments without human annotation.
- **Raw Trajectory Formulation:** Expert demonstrations τ = {(o_t, a_t)} are collected, comprising sensory inputs (e.g., multi-view RGB, proprioception) and continuous control commands (e.g., end-effector pose, gripper state). These trajectories can originate from diverse sources such as real-robot teleoperation, human hand tracking, or XR-retargeted demonstrations. The raw numerical actions inherently lack explicit semantic rationale.
- **Semantic Segmentation via Causal Deduction:** An automated pipeline leverages an off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen3-VL, to infer the expert's underlying thought process. The VLM decomposes a continuous trajectory into discrete, semantically meaningful segments. For each segment, the VLM analyzes visual changes to identify the high-level sub-goal achieved, thereby deducing the causal decision-making process.
- **Kinematic Verbalization via Language-as-Action:** Following segmentation, a "language-as-action" paradigm is applied to deterministically translate physical actions within each segment into structured text. This capitalizes on the semantic affinity of VLMs for language processing. The output is a **Structured Context (l_k)** for each segment, which includes a hierarchical representation:
 - **Semantic Rationale (Sub-goal Description):** A high-level semantic objective for the segment (e.g., "Reach for the handle of the blue mug"), automatically identified by the VLM.
 - **Kinematic Rationale (Movement Description):** Fine-grained motion verbalized as a 3D movement in the workspace (e.g., "Move the end-effector by Δx, Δy, Δz and close gripper") and a 2D movement, obtained by projecting the 3D trajectory onto the camera plane. A deterministic verbalizer (Φ) maps action spans to these 3D and 2D descriptions. The sub-goal description is attached to every frame (s_t), while l_k encapsulates the segment-level rationale.
- **Rationale-augmented Demonstration Pool:** This process transforms raw trajectories τ into τ_rat = {(o_t, a_t, s_t, l_k)}, where each time step is grounded by its observation, action, subtask label, and the explicit segment-level structured rationale. All available expert demonstrations are processed to construct a comprehensive D_pool = {τ^(i)_rat}.

#### 3.2. Parallel Dual-Training Paradigm

This paradigm optimizes the VLA policy using rationale-augmented demonstrations as both explicit supervision and in-context prompts.
- **Retrieval-augmented Prompt Formulation:** During training, a target trajectory τ_tgt is selected. The system queries D_pool \ {τ_tgt} to retrieve the top-1 rationale-augmented expert trajectory, ranked by cosine similarity between the language embeddings of task instructions. This language-based retrieval mechanism supports cross-embodiment demonstration use. The retrieved trajectory is formatted into a contextual prefix prompt P_demo.
- **VLA Model Input:** The final input to the VLA model at time step t is x_t = [P_demo ⊕ I ⊕ o_t], concatenating the retrieved demonstration, the current task instruction, and the current observation. P_demo acts as an implicit In-Context Learning (ICL) signal.
- **Parallel Experts Architecture:** The VLA policy's core is a unified Vision-Language Model backbone f_θ.
 - The backbone processes x_t into a shared latent representation h_t = f_θ(x_t).
 - **Action Expert:** A lightweight MLP head takes h_t and regresses the continuous action chunk ˆA_t = (ˆa_t..., ˆa_t+H-1) for the next H control steps.
 - **Spatial-Language Expert:** The native autoregressive LM head takes h_t and emits the rationale ˆc_t = (ˆs_t, ˆm_t) for the current step, which includes the subtask ˆs_t and the verbalized 3D and 2D movement ˆm_t of the corresponding action chunk.
 - Crucially, these two experts operate independently and in parallel; the action prediction does not causally depend on the generated text tokens.
- **Spatial Language Supervision:** At each time step, supervision c_t = [s_t, Φ(A_t)] is defined, where s_t is the ground-truth subtask label for o_t, and Φ(A_t) is the verbalized 3D and 2D movement of the ground-truth action chunk A_t.
- **Dual Rationale Objective:** The model is optimized to minimize a joint objective: L = L_act(ˆA_t, A_t) + λL_lang(ˆc_t, c_t). L_act is a regression loss (e.g., L1) for continuous actions, and L_lang is a standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss for structured rationale text generation. λ is a balancing coefficient. This objective encourages h_t to connect demonstrated task structure and current observation with required robot motion.

#### 3.3. Asymmetric Inference and Caching

This strategy ensures that the benefits of structured reasoning do not introduce latency during real-time control.
- **Action-only Execution:** During inference, the autoregressive spatial-language expert is entirely removed. The policy performs a single forward pass through the backbone and the lightweight MLP action expert to directly output the action chunk ˆA_t. This asymmetric approach decouples reasoning acquisition (during training) from action execution, avoiding autoregressive decoding overhead.
- **Demonstration Prefix Caching:** To optimize efficiency, the retrieved rationale-rich demonstration prefix P_demo is static throughout an episode. Its key-value (KV) cache is computed once at the beginning (t=1). For subsequent steps, only the current observation o_t (the "live suffix") is processed, drastically reducing computational cost during high-frequency control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents an extensive evaluation of StellaVLA across simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, VLA-Arena, LIBERO-Plus) and real-world robot manipulation tasks, including analyses of structured demonstrations and inference efficiency.

#### 4.1. Generalization in Simulation

- **In-Distribution Validation in LIBERO:** StellaVLA achieved an average success rate of 98.8% on the standard LIBERO benchmark, demonstrating that structured context does not hinder in-distribution performance and, in fact, slightly improves it. Compared to StarVLA-OFT (a matched control without demonstration or spatial-language supervision), StellaVLA showed small gains on Object tasks (+0.4%) but more substantial improvements on Goal (+3.4%) and Long (+3.0%) tasks, where current observation alone might be insufficient to determine intent or sub-goal order.
- **Task-Level Generalization on VLA-Arena:** StellaVLA ranked first on the VLA-Arena leaderboard (as of Aug 1, 2026), with an overall score of 0.63, significantly outperforming the strongest baseline, π_0.5 (0.44). StellaVLA achieved mean success rates of 0.84 on L0 (in-distribution), 0.62 on L1 (intermediate generalization), and 0.43 on L2 (hardest) difficulty levels. The performance margin over π_0.5 increased with task difficulty, from 0.15 at L0 to 0.24 at L1 and 0.17 at L2, indicating enhanced generalization without parameter updates. Specific suites showed strong performance, such as maintaining 0.80 on Unseen Objects across all levels and improving from 0.64 (L0) to 0.84 (L2) on Task Workflows. However, Long Horizon tasks remained challenging, with success rates near zero for all methods, including StellaVLA, at L1/L2.
- **Zero-Shot Robustness on LIBERO-Plus:** StellaVLA achieved an average success rate of 85.1% on LIBERO-Plus, outperforming StarVLA-OFT by 10.1 points in zero-shot robustness. Notable gains were observed under camera viewpoint (+23.5 points), sensor noise (+19.7 points), robot initial state (+14.7 points), and language perturbations (+8.3 points). These improvements suggest that structured context effectively aids adaptation when the observation or instruction changes but the task procedure remains consistent. Minimal gains were seen for object-layout changes (+0.1 point), indicating that scene-specific spatial relations in demonstrations do not transfer reliably under such perturbations.

#### 4.2. Real-World and Cross-Embodiment Evaluation

- **Real-Robot Performance:** StellaVLA achieved 85.0% success in-distribution and 75.0% on OOD-L1 tasks using an AgileX Piper arm. For OOD-L1 tasks (object attribute perturbations), StellaVLA exhibited a 5-point drop in success rate, significantly less than StarVLA-OFT (25-point drop) and π_0.5 (20-point drop), indicating greater robustness to object attribute changes.
- **Unseen Task (OOD-L2):** While no method fully completed the unseen OOD-L2 drawer task, StellaVLA achieved an average progress score of 1.9 (out of 4), compared to 1.5 for π_0.5 and 1.1 for StarVLA-OFT, suggesting that the retrieved procedure provided partial guidance even for novel tasks.
- **Cross-Source Consistency:** An evaluation showed low mean L1 disagreement (0.0014–0.0016σ) between predicted action chunks when only the retrieved demonstration source (real-robot, human-hand, or XR-retargeted) was changed while keeping the current observation fixed. This supports the consistency of the structured representation across different embodiment sources. Removing the demonstration caused a larger disagreement (0.0041–0.0045σ).

#### 4.3. Understanding Structured Demonstrations (Ablation Studies)

- **Causal Role of Demonstration:** Supplying a correct demonstration resulted in 98.8% average success on LIBERO. Removing the demonstration reduced success to 62.4%, and providing a wrong-task demonstration further lowered it to 44.9%. This demonstrates that StellaVLA actively utilizes the demonstration for task specification rather than treating it as auxiliary visual context, especially critical for goal-oriented tasks.
- **Spatial-Language Components:** Removing 3D movement descriptions from the demonstration resulted in a 97.8% average success rate, while removing 2D gripper path descriptions led to 97.3%. The larger impact of the 2D path is consistent with its role in visual grounding.
- **Demonstration Modality:** At evaluation, text-only demonstrations nearly matched the performance of image+text inputs (98.8% AVG, 84.4% LP vs. 98.8% AVG, 85.1% LP), whereas image-only demonstrations showed a significant performance reduction (92.9% AVG, 75.7% LP). This suggests that much of the transferable task information resides in the structured language representation. When varied during training, image-only demonstrations yielded higher in-distribution performance but lower OOD robustness compared to text-only demonstrations, indicating that structured language encourages reliance on stable task-level and spatial structure rather than appearance-based shortcuts.
- **Context Granularity:** Reducing the number of subgoal keyframes from ten to three in a demonstration resulted in a minimal performance drop of 0.7 points, implying that the high-level subgoal sequence is more critical than dense trajectory replay.

#### 4.4. Spatial-Language Supervision and Deployment

- **Effect of Language-Loss Weight (λ):** In-distribution performance peaked at λ=0.3 (98.8% AVG), while LIBERO-Plus robustness decreased with increasing λ (from 86.9% at λ=0 to 81.9% at λ=1.0). λ=0.3 was selected for the best in-distribution performance.
- **Inference Efficiency:** A forward pass without a demonstration took 64 ms. Adding an image+text demonstration without caching increased latency to 183 ms, but with prefix caching, the steady-state cost was reduced to 91 ms. The action-only inference path (88 ms) was significantly faster than decoding the spatial-language output (3177 ms, ~36x slower), confirming the effectiveness of asymmetric inference in maintaining real-time control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

StellaVLA introduces a framework designed to enhance the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which is a critical area for the broader deployment of robotic systems. The work addresses the common challenge of performance degradation in VLA models when operating in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments.

The potential impact of StellaVLA stems from several key aspects:

- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** StellaVLA demonstrates substantial improvements in robustness to OOD conditions, including shifts in task, environment, visual attributes, and viewpoints. This is evidenced by its leading performance on benchmarks like VLA-Arena, LIBERO, and LIBERO-Plus, where it consistently surpasses strong prior models. This enhanced generalization could enable robots to operate more reliably in varied and unstructured real-world settings.
- **Advancement in In-Context Learning:** By moving In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) beyond raw action imitation to incorporating structured reasoning ("why" actions are performed, not just "what"), StellaVLA provides a more potent and transferable form of in-context guidance. This could lead to VLA models that learn more conceptually from demonstrations, rather than merely memorizing trajectories.
- **Automated and Scalable Demonstration Structuring:** The development of an automated offline pipeline for extracting hierarchical semantic and kinematic rationales from raw trajectories at zero human-annotation cost is a significant practical contribution. This eliminates a major bottleneck in creating high-quality, structured demonstration datasets, making the approach more scalable for large-scale robot learning.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The ability to convert diverse demonstration sources—including real-robot, human-hand, and XR-retargeted trajectories—into a common structured representation allows for leveraging heterogeneous data. This expands the pool of available expert knowledge and could facilitate knowledge transfer from human operators or virtual environments to physical robots, broadening the applicability of VLA models.
- **Real-Time Control with Reasoning:** The asymmetric inference strategy, which removes the spatial-language expert at deployment and utilizes demonstration prefix caching, addresses the critical issue of latency in robotic control. This design enables the policy to benefit from the rich structured reasoning learned during training without incurring the prohibitive overhead of autoregressive language decoding during high-frequency real-time execution, making sophisticated reasoning compatible with practical robotic operation.
- **Potential for More Interpretable Models:** By explicitly representing and supervising sub-goals and movements through language, StellaVLA might contribute to more interpretable VLA models. The learned rationales could provide insights into the robot's decision-making process, aiding debugging and fostering trust in autonomous systems.

While the framework shows strong performance, the authors acknowledge limitations such as continued challenges with extremely long-horizon compositional tasks and the dependence on high-quality retrieval. Future work in these areas, along with exploring more precise kinematic representations, could further extend the impact of StellaVLA. Overall, this research represents a step toward building more intelligent, adaptive, and deployable robotic systems by integrating structured reasoning into in-context learning.
