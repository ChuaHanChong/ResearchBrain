# Tool-Augmented Spatiotemporal Reasoning for Streamlining Video Question Answering Task

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University researchers developed the "STAR" framework, which augments Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with a Video Toolkit and a spatiotemporal reasoning strategy to enhance video question answering. This approach yielded an 8.2% accuracy increase over GPT-4o on the VideoMME dataset and achieved substantial efficiency gains by reducing runtime and processed frames across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with simultaneously discerning spatial relationships within frames and understanding complex temporal dynamics across video frames.
- Existing tool-augmented LLMs for VideoQA often use unidimensional tools, lack balanced tool management, and have insufficient scheduling strategies, leading to inefficiencies and reasoning shortcuts.
- The inability to progressively localize a 3D Region of Interest (RoI) in video content hinders deep understanding and robust, step-by-step reasoning.

## Method

- A comprehensive and extensible ==Video Toolkit== was constructed, comprising approximately **22** plug-and-play tools categorized into temporal, spatial, and general-purpose functionalities, using lightweight specialized CV models and natural language interfaces.
- The ==Spatiotemporal Reasoning Framework (STAR)== was proposed, an agentic and iterative framework built on ReAct principles, which strategically controls tool invocation using GPT-4o as an LLM Planner.
- STAR employs an ==alternating spatiotemporal tool invocation pattern== to progressively narrow down the 3D Region of Interest in the video, guiding the MLLM through deliberate, step-by-step visual reasoning to overcome 'toolchain shortcuts'.

## Results

- STAR achieved a **70.0%** accuracy on VideoMME, an **8.2%** gain over GPT-4o (**61.8%**), with comparable input frames and reducing runtime from several minutes to **15.8** seconds per video.
- On challenging LongVideoBench, STAR improved accuracy by **4.6%** over GPT-4o (**57.2%** vs. **52.6%**) while significantly reducing the number of processed frames and overall runtime for long-form videos.
- Ablation studies confirmed the spatiotemporal-interleaved strategy outperformed 'No Constraints' (**61.2%** accuracy) and 'Spatiotemporal Disentanglement' (**68.6%** accuracy), achieving the highest accuracy (**70.0%**) and frame efficiency (**30.2** frames processed) on VideoMME.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaving spatial and temporal tool invocation is crucial for effective video understanding, facilitating progressive 3D Region of Interest (RoI) localization and preventing reasoning shortcuts by forcing comprehensive analysis.
> - Augmenting MLLMs with specialized perception tools leads to more accurate and robust outputs for specific tasks (e.g., object detection, action localization) compared to relying solely on direct MLLM inference.
> - A training-free, agentic framework that strategically manages a diverse toolkit can significantly boost MLLM performance and efficiency on complex video understanding tasks without requiring core model retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **Video Question Answering (VideoQA)**, which aims to equip artificial intelligence models with the ability to perceive, understand, and reason about dynamic real-world scenarios presented in video format. VideoQA serves as a crucial benchmark for evaluating the holistic capabilities of foundation models, particularly in processing complex spatiotemporal information.

The current landscape of VideoQA approaches, especially with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs), can be broadly categorized into two main paradigms:
1. **Video-centric Large Language Models (Video-LLMs):** These models (e.g., Qwen-VL, InternVL3) are designed to directly process extensive video data, often by consuming a large number of frames. While powerful, they can suffer from redundancy and inefficiency, especially with long videos, due to the sheer volume of data they must ingest.
2. **Tool-augmented Large Language Models (Tool-augmented LLMs):** This approach enhances the reasoning capabilities of LLMs by allowing them to invoke external, specialized tools. Examples include DoraemonGPT, which orchestrates various visual perception tools.

The authors meticulously identify several critical limitations inherent in *existing Tool-augmented LLMs* for VideoQA, which their work directly addresses:
- **Unidimensional Tool Utilization:** Many existing systems focus on either spatial or temporal reasoning tools, failing to simultaneously model intricate spatial relationships *within* frames and the causal dynamics of temporal evolution *across* frames. This limits deep understanding.
- **Unbalanced Tool Quantity and Diversity:** While increasing the number of tools can theoretically compensate for MLLM shortcomings, simply adding more tools without proper management leads to execution issues and inefficiency due to LLM uncertainty in selection.
- **Insufficient Tool Scheduling Strategy:** A lack of an effective mechanism for orchestrating tool invocation can result in disordered toolchains, negatively impacting both efficiency and accuracy, sometimes leading to a "toolchain shortcut" where the LLM opts for an immediate, often suboptimal, answer instead of a step-by-step reasoning process.

This research positions itself as a significant advancement in the tool-augmented LLM paradigm by tackling these identified shortcomings head-on. By proposing a comprehensive **Video Toolkit** that is explicitly designed for *spatiotemporal* analysis and an innovative **Spatiotemporal Reasoning Framework (STAR)** for strategic, interleaved tool scheduling, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of prior work. The core idea is to enable a more human-like, deliberate "visual thinking" process, analogous to "chain-of-thought" prompting in pure language models, but extended to dynamic visual environments. This work pushes the boundaries towards building truly "autonomous and intelligent video analysis assistants" by making MLLMs more effective, efficient, and robust in understanding complex video content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the spatiotemporal reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for the Video Question Answering (VideoQA) task** by equipping them with a structured and intelligently managed set of external tools. This overarching goal is motivated by several critical challenges observed in the current state of MLLM-based VideoQA:

1. **Addressing MLLM Spatiotemporal Reasoning Deficiencies:** MLLMs, despite their impressive general capabilities, inherently struggle with simultaneously discerning subtle spatial relationships within individual video frames and comprehending the complex, causal dynamics of temporal evolution across an entire video. This dual challenge is particularly pronounced in reasoning-intensive VideoQA tasks.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Tool-Augmented LLMs:**
 - **Unidimensionality:** Prior tool-augmented systems often employ tools that are either purely spatial or purely temporal, failing to leverage the synergistic interplay between these two dimensions essential for comprehensive video understanding. The motivation is to enable *simultaneous* and *interdependent* spatiotemporal analysis.
 - **Tool Management Imbalance:** Simply expanding a toolkit with more tools does not guarantee better performance; it can introduce chaos and inefficiency due to the LLM's uncertainty in selecting and sequencing them. The motivation is to create a toolkit that balances the *quantity* and *diversity* of tools with effective management.
 - **Lack of Strategic Tool Scheduling:** Without a principled method to invoke tools, LLMs can adopt "toolchain shortcuts," leading to suboptimal or incorrect answers by bypassing necessary multi-step reasoning. The motivation is to develop a framework that imposes a strategic order on tool invocation, guiding the LLM towards progressive problem decomposition and more robust reasoning.

Based on these motivations, the authors articulate the following key objectives:

- **To construct a comprehensive and extensible Video Toolkit:** This toolkit is designed to cover a wide range of video analysis functionalities, categorized into temporal, spatial, and general-purpose tools. It aims to integrate state-of-the-art computer vision models and basic image/video manipulations through a natural language interface, ensuring that tools are plug-and-play and easily expandable.
- **To propose a novel Spatiotemporal Reasoning Framework (STAR):** This framework's core objective is to strategically control the sequence of tool invocations. STAR is designed to enforce an alternating pattern of temporal and spatial tool use, progressively narrowing down the "3D Region of Interest" (3D RoI) in the video. This strategy aims to mitigate the "toolchain shortcut" problem and encourage step-by-step, deliberate visual reasoning.
- **To demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency:** The ultimate objective is to experimentally validate that equipping MLLMs with the proposed Video Toolkit and STAR framework leads to substantial gains in VideoQA accuracy while simultaneously reducing computational costs (fewer processed frames, faster runtime) across diverse benchmarks, particularly for long-form videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology presented in this paper involves augmenting a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM), specifically GPT-4o, with a comprehensive Video Toolkit and orchestrating its use through a novel Spatiotemporal Reasoning Framework (STAR).

#### 3.1. Video Toolkit Construction

The authors design a comprehensive and extensible Video Toolkit guided by three principles:
1. **Decomposition into Temporal and Spatial Dimensions:** Video processing naturally separates into understanding *when* events occur (temporal) and *where* objects/actions are *within* those events (spatial). This leads to categorizing tools into temporal, spatial, and general-purpose.
2. **Natural Language Interface for CV Results:** The toolkit aims to seamlessly integrate outputs from specialized computer vision models (e.g., bounding boxes) into a natural language format that the MLLM can interpret and utilize for reasoning.
3. **Flexible Temporal Processing:** Support for both segment-level and frame-level analysis is crucial for adapting to different video lengths and question types.

The toolkit comprises approximately 22 plug-and-play tools, with variants based on lightweight specialized models (e.g., YOLO, Grounding DINO, BLIP2, MMAction, Grounded-SAM), simple manipulations (e.g., Patch Zoomer, Video Trimmer), and MLLM-based prompting pipelines (e.g., iterative prompting for action localization).

**Representative Tool Examples:**
- **Temporal Tools:**
 - **Frame Selector:** An LLM-based tool that selects informative frames, discarding irrelevant ones. It has variants inspired by AKeyS and T* which consider task goals, scene transitions, or image grids for MLLM-based selection.
 - **Temporal Grounding/Referring:** Tools based on lightweight Video-LLMs to predict temporal spans for described events or describe content within given spans.
 - **Action Localization:** Organizes video into an image grid for GPT-4o to identify action-related frames.
- **Spatial Tools:**
 - **Object Detector:** Uses YOLO or Grounding DINO to detect objects. Bounding box (bbox) outputs are utilized in three ways: textual descriptions, zooming (Patch Zoomer), or visual marking (Set-of-Mark Prompting) before MLLM input.
 - **Image Captioner/QA:** Uses VLMs (BLIP2, LLaVA, GPT-4o) to generate descriptions or answer questions for individual frames.
 - **Text Detector (OCR):** Recognizes text within frames.
 - **Semantic Segmentation:** Uses Grounded-SAM for detailed object segmentation.
- **Both Temporal and Spatial Tools:** Examples include Google Search (for external knowledge), Object Identifier (preparatory for detection/tracking), Action Recognition (MMAction), Image Grid QA/Multiple Image QA (feeding multiple frames to MLLM), Python Code Generator, and Object Tracker (Ultralytics).
- **General-Purpose Tools:** Text Summarizer (summarizes information in the Visible Frame Dictionary) and Video QA (leverages a Video-LLM for direct video question answering).

All tool functionalities are encapsulated in standardized "tool cards" for easy integration and extensibility.

#### 3.2. Spatiotemporal Reasoning Framework (STAR)

STAR is a training-free, agentic, and iterative framework built upon the ReAct framework, where an LLM Planner (GPT-4o) makes observations from tool results, generates thoughts, and decides on the next action. The core innovation of STAR lies in its strategic scheduling to overcome the "Toolchain Shortcut" problem—where the LLM Planner favors single-step, general tools over progressive problem decomposition.

**STAR Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**
1. **Initialization:** A `Visible Frame Dictionary (V)` is populated with a sparse, uniformly sampled set of video frames. This dictionary stores gathered information for each frame.
2. **Initial Tool Selection:** The LLM Planner selects an initial tool from either the temporal or spatial toolset (not general tools).
3. **Alternating Invocation:** For a specified number of iterations (`I`):
 - If the previous tool was spatial, the Planner must select a temporal tool next.
 - If the previous tool was temporal, the Planner must select a spatial tool next.
 - This alternating pattern ensures a continuous interplay between temporal scope narrowing and spatial detail refinement.
 - Temporal tools update the frame indices in `V` (adding/removing frames).
 - Spatial tools process selected frames and update their corresponding information in `V`.
4. **Termination and General Tools:** After `I` iterations, if temporal and spatial tools are still insufficient to answer the question, the LLM Planner is allowed to invoke general tools as a last resort to derive the final answer.
5. **Autonomy, Adaptivity, Progressiveness:** STAR is autonomous (LLM invokes tools), adaptive (dynamically adjusts strategy based on video/question), and progressive (starts with few frames, expands as needed).

**Why Spatiotemporal Interleaving?**
This interleaving strategy is crucial because it ensures mutual feedback: temporal tools narrow down relevant time segments, then spatial tools refine the spatial focus within those segments. The results from spatial analysis then inform subsequent temporal reasoning, creating a complementary understanding. Ablation studies confirm this interleaving significantly outperforms decoupled (first all temporal, then all spatial) or unconstrained strategies in both accuracy and efficiency.

**Mitigating MLLM Limitations:**
STAR addresses MLLM limitations by:
1. **Leveraging Specialized Tools:** Outsourcing specific perception tasks (e.g., object tracking, re-identification) to specialized tools yields more accurate outputs than direct MLLM inference.
2. **Progressive 3D RoI Localization:** By progressively narrowing down the relevant 3D Region of Interest (spatial and temporal dimensions), STAR reduces noise, minimizes interference, and encourages deliberate, System II-like reasoning, similar to chain-of-thought prompting.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Evaluated on four diverse VideoQA benchmarks: VideoMME, NExT-QA, LongVideoBench, and EgoSchema, covering various video lengths (seconds to hours), domains, and question types.
- **Metrics:** Multiple-choice QA accuracy, number of processed frames, and runtime.
- **Framework Variants:**
 - **STAR:** Full version, uses GPT-4o-2024-08-06 as the LLM Planner and tools based on open-source models up to 3B parameters.
 - **STAR-MINI:** Lightweight version, uses GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 as the LLM Planner and tools smaller than 500M parameters, runnable on personal computers.
- **Initial Sampling:** 16 uniformly sampled frames for videos longer than 16 seconds; 1 frame per second for videos 16 seconds or shorter.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the proposed Video Toolkit and STAR framework significantly enhance MLLM capabilities for VideoQA, achieving notable improvements in accuracy and efficiency across multiple benchmarks.

1. **Overall Performance Boost and Efficiency:**
 - **VideoMME (Table 1):** STAR achieves an accuracy of 70.0%, marking an **8.2% gain over GPT-4o** (61.8%) with a comparable number of input frames. It notably outperforms leading 7B open-source Video-LLMs (e.g., InternVL3-8B at 66.3% by 3.7%) and approaches the performance of 72B-scale open-source Video-LLMs (Qwen2-VL-72B at 71.2%). Crucially, STAR achieves this with remarkable efficiency, reducing runtime to 15.8 seconds, a substantial speedup compared to 6-8 minutes for Qwen2-VL-72B.
 - **LongVideoBench (Table 2):** On challenging long-form videos, STAR (57.2%) achieves a **4.6% improvement over GPT-4o** (52.6%). It significantly outperforms 7B Video-LLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 53.7%, InternVL3-8B at 48.9%), especially in the very long video categories (180s-3600s), demonstrating superior capability in extracting key information from extended content. It also substantially reduces processed frames and runtime. (Note: Proprietary models like Gemini 1.5 Pro and GPT-4o with more frames and subtitle information achieve higher scores, but STAR focuses purely on video content).
 - **NExT-QA (Table 3, 4):** STAR achieves the highest accuracy (82.1%) using the fewest frames, surpassing both 7B Video-LLMs and all (M)LLM-based frame selection methods. It shows strong performance across causal, temporal, and descriptive question types. The lightweight variant, STAR-MINI, outperforms DoraemonGPT by **6.3% in accuracy** on the NExT-QA validation set (62.0% vs. 55.7%), affirming the superiority of the spatiotemporal interleaving strategy even with constrained computational resources.
 - **EgoSchema (Figure 2b):** STAR demonstrates superior scalability, consistently achieving the highest accuracy as the number of input frames increases, outperforming other advanced methods like LLoVi, VideoAgent, VideoTree, and AKeyS.

2. **Efficacy of Spatiotemporal Interleaving (Ablation Studies - Table 5):**
 - The ablation study on toolchain generation strategies clearly highlights the effectiveness of STAR's spatiotemporal-interleaved approach. STAR achieves the highest accuracy (70.0%) and frame efficiency (30.2 frames processed), coupled with longer and more diverse toolchains (8.7 length, 6.3 tools).
 - It significantly outperforms "No Constraints" (61.2% accuracy, 112.6 frames), "Prompting" (60.4% accuracy), "In-Context Learning" (63.2% accuracy), and critically, "Spatiotemporal Disentanglement" (68.6% accuracy, 40.6 frames). This directly validates that interleaving spatial and temporal reasoning is crucial for improved accuracy and efficiency, as it facilitates progressive 3D Region of Interest (RoI) localization.

3. **Positive Contribution of Individual Tools (Appendix D, Table 6):**
 - Ablation studies confirm that removing almost any individual tool from the comprehensive Video Toolkit generally leads to a drop in accuracy and/or an increase in processed frames, indicating that each tool contributes positively to the overall performance of the STAR framework.

4. **Balanced Tool Utilization (Appendix G, Table 9 & 10):**
 - Analysis of tool usage frequency shows that STAR promotes a more balanced and comprehensive utilization of tools across and within categories compared to a "No Constraints" setting. By preventing over-reliance on a few general tools (e.g., Video QA Tool, Image QA Tool), STAR encourages a wider array of specialized tools to be invoked, leading to more robust and detailed reasoning.

5. **Generalizability Across LLM Planners (Appendix F, Table 8):**
 - The STAR framework consistently provides a substantial 7-8% accuracy improvement for various LLM planners, including GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-pro, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and DeepSeek-R1. This demonstrates the broad applicability and robust architecture of the framework, as its benefits are not limited to a single underlying LLM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant step forward in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for video understanding and question answering, with a high potential for broad impact.

1. **Advancement in Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** The most significant contribution is the explicit and effective integration of both spatial and temporal reasoning capabilities into a cohesive framework. By addressing the previous unidimensionality of tool use, STAR enables MLLMs to perceive and understand dynamic video content in a far more comprehensive and human-like manner. The progressive localization of "3D Region of Interest" is a novel and powerful mechanism for focused reasoning.

2. **Enhancement of Foundation Models:** This work provides a practical and highly effective paradigm for augmenting large multimodal foundation models. It demonstrates that strategic tool integration and orchestration can significantly boost MLLM performance on complex tasks without requiring extensive re-training of the core model, thereby making MLLMs more powerful, adaptable, and efficient for real-world applications. The consistent performance gains across different LLM planners underscore its generalizability.

3. **Improved Efficiency for Long-Form Videos:** VideoQA, especially for long videos, is computationally intensive. STAR's ability to achieve higher accuracy while processing fewer frames and reducing runtime is a critical breakthrough. This efficiency makes advanced video analysis more feasible for practical applications with limited computational resources, tackling the "needle-in-a-haystack" problem inherent in long videos.

4. **Robust and Extensible Architecture:** The "plug-and-play" nature of the Video Toolkit ensures high extensibility, allowing new and improved computer vision models or analytical tools to be easily integrated as they emerge. The agentic nature of the STAR framework, with its autonomous and adaptive tool invocation, offers a flexible blueprint for building intelligent systems capable of tackling diverse and evolving tasks.

5. **Paving the Way for Autonomous Video Analysis Assistants:** The authors explicitly state that their work is an "important step towards building autonomous and intelligent video analysis assistants." This vision has profound implications for various domains:
 - **Content Creation and Editing:** Streamlining tasks like scene analysis, summarization, and content generation.
 - **Surveillance and Security:** More intelligent detection and reasoning about events in real-time video feeds.
 - **Education:** Analyzing instructional videos to answer student questions or generate summaries.
 - **Accessibility:** Developing tools that can interpret video content for visually impaired users.
 - **Scientific Research:** Automating analysis of experimental videos in biology, physics, or engineering.

6. **Highlighting the Value of Strategic Reasoning:** The ablation studies emphatically demonstrate that *how* tools are invoked (the tool scheduling strategy) is as crucial, if not more, than the mere availability of tools. This underscores the importance of deliberate, step-by-step reasoning architectures in AI, moving beyond brute-force approaches.

While the current reliance on proprietary GPT models for the planner (and associated API costs) is acknowledged as a limitation, the authors' plan to explore lightweight planners and better integration of multimodal inputs (subtitles, audio) indicates a clear roadmap for future research, promising even more accessible and comprehensive video analysis solutions. Overall, this paper offers a robust, efficient, and highly impactful framework that significantly advances the capabilities of MLLMs in understanding the dynamic visual world.
