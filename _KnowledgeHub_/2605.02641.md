# Mamoda2.5: Enhancing Unified Multimodal Model with DiT-MoE

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance's Mamoda2.5 unifies multimodal understanding, image generation, and video generation/editing within a single Autoregressive–Diffusion framework, leveraging a fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts Diffusion Transformer (DiT-MoE) for computational efficiency. The model demonstrates competitive performance on benchmarks like VBench 2.0 (61.64) and OpenVE-Bench (3.86), while its distilled version achieves up to 95.9 times faster video editing inference.

## Problem

- Unified video generation and editing frameworks face high computational complexity due to the quadratic scaling of visual tokens with spatio-temporal resolution, making large, dense models prohibitively expensive.
- Instruction-guided video editing is hampered by the scarcity of high-quality paired training data and significant computational overhead for encoding source videos.
- Achieving top-tier performance in complex video generation and editing tasks, especially regarding temporal consistency and fine-grained instruction adherence, remains a challenge for existing models.

## Method

- Introduces **Mamoda2.5**, an Autoregressive–Diffusion framework with a fine-grained ==Mixture-of-Experts== (**MoE**) ==Diffusion Transformer== (**DiT**) architecture, activating a sparse subset of parameters per forward pass for efficiency.
- Develops a three-stage synthetic video editing data pipeline that generates high-quality paired video instruction data by sharing early denoising steps between pre-edit and post-edit generations to ensure structural consistency.
- Implements a ==joint few-step distillation== and reinforcement learning framework to compress generative models, significantly accelerating inference for editing tasks from 30 steps to 4 steps.

## Results

- The DiT-MoE backbone (25 billion total parameters, ~3 billion activated) contributes to efficient training and achieves a VBench 2.0 score of 61.64 for text-to-video generation.
- Outperforms evaluated models on OpenVE-Bench (3.86 overall score) and FiVE-Bench (87.41 FiVE-Acc) for instruction-based video editing, demonstrating improved spatial reasoning and detail preservation.
- The 4-step distilled model reduces video editing inference latency to 9.2 seconds (480px, 81 frames), achieving up to a 95.9 times speedup over VInO and 41.7 times over OmniVideo2, while matching or exceeding the 30-step teacher's quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained MoE architectures, combined with dense-to-MoE upcycling, enable scaling visual generative models to 25 billion total parameters while activating only ~3 billion per pass, accelerating training and reducing costs.
> - A carefully designed synthetic data pipeline using shared denoising steps between pre- and post-edit generations effectively generates high-quality paired video editing data, overcoming data scarcity for complex tasks.
> - Joint distillation and reinforcement learning can drastically accelerate model inference (up to 95.9x faster) for complex editing tasks, yielding high-quality, efficient student models that can surpass the teacher's performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of unified vision models is evolving towards integrated systems capable of both understanding and generation, moving beyond single-task specialized models. While considerable progress has been made in combining visual understanding with image generation and editing, unified frameworks for video generation and editing are still in early development. This is primarily due to the inherent complexity of video data and the significant computational resources required.

Existing specialized video generation models, such as HunyuanVideo and WanVideo, have demonstrated that scaling parameters within the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) paradigm can improve video quality and realism. However, video tasks are computationally intensive, with visual token counts increasing quadratically with spatial resolution and temporal duration, leading to escalating training and inference costs. This challenge makes the deployment of high-quality, long-duration video generation with dense architectures prohibitively expensive.

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models have emerged as a scalable solution for large language models, allowing for sparse activation of parameters to increase model capacity without proportional increases in computational cost. While MoE designs have been explored in image generation (e.g., DiT-MoE, Race-DiT, DiffMoE) and to a limited extent in video (e.g., WanVideo 2.2 with coarse-grained MoE), the systematic integration of fine-grained MoE architectures with token-level routing for unified video generation and editing remains an area of active research.

Concurrently, instruction-guided visual editing, particularly for video, presents challenges in acquiring high-quality paired training data and managing the computational overhead of encoding source videos for conditioning. Prior open-source efforts, like VInO and OmniVideo, have explored unified video editing but often employ dense architectures, exhibiting performance limitations in complex scenarios involving large motion or fine-grained instruction adherence. This research positions Mamoda2.5 within this landscape by addressing the computational and data challenges through a fine-grained MoE architecture and a novel data synthesis pipeline, aiming to advance unified multimodal understanding and generation for both images and videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce Mamoda2.5, an advanced unified Autoregressive–Diffusion (AR–Diffusion) framework designed to seamlessly integrate multimodal understanding and generation capabilities within a single architecture. The motivation behind this development is multifaceted, addressing several key limitations in the current research landscape:

1. **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks**: The prohibitive computational cost associated with scaling large video generation models, particularly due to the quadratic complexity of Diffusion Transformers (DiT) on spatio-temporal tokens, necessitates a more efficient architecture. The authors aim to overcome this by employing a fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design, enabling the model to scale to a high total parameter count while activating only a sparse subset of parameters per forward pass, thereby reducing training and inference costs.

2. **Achieving Unified Multimodal Functionality**: Existing models often specialize in either understanding or generation, or are limited to specific modalities (e.g., image-only). A key motivation is to develop a single, monolithic architecture that can simultaneously support a diverse set of visual tasks, including text-to-image generation, text-to-video generation, instruction-based image editing, and instruction-based video editing, in addition to multimodal understanding. This unification aims to eliminate the need for separate, task-specific models and foster deeper interaction between these capabilities.

3. **Enhancing Generation and Editing Quality**: While current models have made progress, achieving top-tier performance in complex video generation and, especially, video editing tasks (e.g., maintaining temporal consistency, handling large motions, precise instruction following) remains challenging. The researchers are motivated to develop a model that can rival or surpass the quality of existing state-of-the-art proprietary and open-source models across these tasks.

4. **Accelerating Inference for Practical Deployment**: High inference latency is a significant barrier to the real-world application of video generation and editing models. A specific objective is to drastically accelerate model inference for editing tasks, particularly through few-step distillation and reinforcement learning, making the model more practical for industrial deployment.

5. **Improving Training Data Quality and Scalability**: The scarcity and complexity of high-quality, paired video editing data are significant obstacles. The work is motivated to engineer a highly scalable synthetic data pipeline capable of mass-producing diverse and high-quality training pairs, which is critical for training robust video editing capabilities.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to create a highly efficient, unified, high-performing, and deployable multimodal model that effectively tackles the computational, architectural, and data challenges prevalent in advanced visual AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mamoda2.5 is built upon an AR–Diffusion paradigm, unifying instruction understanding and visual generation/editing within a single end-to-end pipeline.

**Model Architecture:**
The architecture comprises three main stages:
1. **Autoregressive (AR) Understanding Module**: This module is responsible for semantic modeling of multimodal inputs (e.g., system prompts, visual inputs, editing instructions). It inherits the architecture and pretrained weights from Mamoda 2.0. A novel MetaQueries mechanism is introduced, where learnable query tokens activate generation experts within the AR backbone. The resulting features are combined with other conditional representations and forwarded to the DiT module, bypassing issues associated with autoregressive visual token prediction.
2. **Visual Generation with DiT-MoE**: This is the core generative component, an MoE-based Diffusion Transformer (DiT-MoE) that iteratively denoises latent states. It systematically incorporates a fine-grained MoE architecture into a unified visual generation and editing setting.
 - **Fine-Grained Expert Segmentation**: FFN sublayers in the single-stream DiT are replaced with MoE layers that decompose "large experts" into smaller, specialized sub-experts. Mamoda2.5 uses 128 routed experts with Top-8 activation per token, in addition to one shared expert that is always activated. The routed experts have a narrower intermediate dimension (`d_e = 1,024`) compared to conventional FFNs (`d_ff = 14,336`).
 - **Routing Strategy**: Top-K token-choice routing with sigmoid gating is employed. Sigmoid evaluates each expert's relevance independently, aligning with the fine-grained expert design.
 - **Load Balancing**: Expert Bias, a loss-free mechanism, dynamically adjusts gating scores during Top-K selection to suppress over-selected experts and improve device utilization.
3. **VAE Encoder/Decoder**: This component maps between pixel space and latent space. Mamoda2.5 utilizes a 3D causal VAE from Wan2.2 with a 4×16×16 spatio-temporal compression ratio, which reduces the number of spatial tokens compared to commonly used VAEs (e.g., 4×8×8), thus mitigating DiT computation and memory costs.

**Upcycling: Dense-to-MoE Initialization:**
To mitigate the high cost of training large MoE models from scratch, a three-stage upcycling procedure is proposed to initialize Mamoda2.5 from a pretrained dense model (Wan2.2 5B).
1. **Attention Weight Transfer**: Self-attention and layer normalization parameters are directly copied.
2. **Random Neuron Sampling for Expert FFN**: For each expert, a unique random permutation of the dense model's FFN neurons is generated, and `d_e` neurons are sampled to initialize the expert's FFN weights. This provides initialization diversity without post-hoc perturbation.
3. **Router Initialization**: Router weights are randomly initialized, and Expert Bias terms are set to zero.

**Multi-Task Conditional Generation:**
All visual generation and editing tasks are formulated as conditional generation, with conditioning features injected into the MoE DiT module via in-context conditioning. Multimodal conditioning features are processed by a refiner and concatenated with VAE-encoded latents and noisy latents, allowing global self-attention over the unified input sequence. An auxiliary ByT5 encoder is integrated for character-level text rendering, improving spelling and layout fidelity.

**Data Curation:**
The training data consists of five categories: multimodal understanding (from Honey-1M), text-to-image (from Mamoda2), text-to-video (in-house, real + synthetic), image editing (from Mamoda2), and a specialized video editing (V2V) dataset.
- **Video Editing Data Synthesis Pipeline**: To address the scarcity of high-quality paired V2V data, a three-stage synthetic data pipeline is developed:
 1. LLM-based prompt pair generation.
 2. Paired video synthesis using a strong T2V model, crucially sharing a subset of early denoising steps between pre-edit and post-edit generations to ensure structural consistency.
 3. VLM-based recaptioning, quality filtering, and bidirectional inversion to double the dataset.

**Model Training:**
The training pipeline is multi-stage and progressive:
1. **Pretraining**: Starts with text-to-image pre-training (480px) for spatial fidelity, followed by two stages of text-to-video pre-training with increasing resolution and duration (480px, 12fps, then 720px, 24fps) to learn temporal motion patterns.
2. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Combines T2V, T2I, and multimodal understanding data to align with human preferences and strengthen visual-language comprehension.
3. **Editing-Specific Training**: Initiates with image editing pre-training (480px) for instruction following, then mixed image and video editing training (720px, 24fps) with variable video lengths.
4. **Joint Few-Step Distillation and Reinforcement Learning**: A framework that simultaneously optimizes distribution matching distillation (L_DMD) and DiffusionNFT reinforcement learning (L_NFT). This compresses the 30-step editing model into a 4-step, CFG-free student model, achieving significant inference acceleration while potentially surpassing the teacher's quality. A multi-dimensional reward system (Edit Quality, Visual Quality, Background Consistency, Text Rendering Accuracy) guides RL.

**System Optimization:**
- **Training Optimization**: Leverages FSDP2 for sharded data parallelism, Unified Sequence Parallelism (USP) for long video training, selective recomputation, and asynchronous activation offloading for memory efficiency. MoE-specific optimizations include Grouped GEMM operators and fused MoE Token Permute/Unpermute. An efficient online encoding pipeline combines Encoder DP and Encoder Interleaved with dual-cache to reduce encoding overhead.
- **Inference Acceleration**: Utilizes mixed parallelism, Selective and Sliding Tile Attention (SSTA) for sparse attention, and an adaptive cache strategy to optimize for long video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Mamoda2.5 demonstrates competitive performance and efficiency across a range of multimodal tasks.

**Efficiency and Inference Speed:**
- The DiT-MoE backbone, with 25 billion total parameters, activates approximately 3 billion parameters per forward pass, contributing to reduced training costs and enhanced generation capabilities.
- For video generation (720px, 93 frames, 50 steps), Mamoda2.5 completes inference in 110 seconds on a single xPU. This is reported to be over 12 times faster than Wan2.2 A14B, 5 times faster than HunyuanVideo 1.5, and 18 times faster than LongCat Video.
- For video editing (480px, 81 frames, 30 steps), Mamoda2.5-Edit achieves inference in 69 seconds, representing a 12.8 times speedup over VInO and 5.6 times over OmniVideo2.
- The distilled 4-step model, Mamoda2.5-Edit-Distill, further reduces video editing latency to 9.2 seconds, translating to a 95.9 times speedup over VInO and 41.7 times over OmniVideo2. These speedups are attributed to sparse MoE activation and the high-compression (4x16x16) VAE.

**Text-to-Video Generation:**
- On the VBench 2.0 benchmark, Mamoda2.5 achieves a total score of 61.64, positioning it as a top-tier performer among open-source video generators, while remaining competitive with evaluated proprietary models.

**Instruction-based Video Editing:**
- **OpenVE-Bench**: Mamoda2.5 attains an overall score of 3.86 on the spatially aligned setting, outperforming all evaluated models, including a top-tier proprietary model (3.73) and Kling O1 (3.69). It exhibits gains in tasks such as Replace, Remove, Text, and Creative Edit. Qualitative comparisons suggest improved spatial reasoning, accurate object placement, better detail preservation, minimal collateral changes, and handling of complex edits compared to Kling O1.
- **FiVE-Bench**: Mamoda2.5 outperforms all open-source baselines with an FiVE-Acc score of 87.41, surpassing Omni-Video2 (73.53). This indicates strong performance in complex, motion-rich editing scenarios.
- **Reco-Bench**: Using Gemini-2.5-pro for evaluation, Mamoda2.5 achieves the highest overall scores across Add, Remove, and Replace categories compared to VInO, suggesting effective supervision from its data pipeline.

**Text-to-Image Generation:**
- On the GenEval benchmark, Mamoda2.5 achieves an overall score of 0.83, making it the highest-performing among evaluated video generation models and competitive with dedicated image generation models.

**Instruction-based Image Editing:**
- On ImgEdit and GEdit-EN benchmarks, Mamoda2.5 shows competitive performance among open-source models, achieving an average quality score of 4.22 on ImgEdit and an overall score of 7.05 on GEdit-EN. It ranks first in the Replace category on ImgEdit.

**Multimodal Understanding:**
- Mamoda2.5 closely matches the understanding-only Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct on various multimodal understanding benchmarks (e.g., MMVet, OCRBench, AI2D), and surpasses it on reasoning-intensive benchmarks such as MMMU and MathVista. This suggests that the model maintains core visual-language comprehension despite its focus on generation and editing.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **MoE Architecture**: Ablations on T2I generation show that MoE models converge significantly faster than dense counterparts with the same activated parameter count. Fine-grained expert configurations (e.g., E64A8) yield approximately 2.2 times faster convergence than a dense baseline.
- **Upcycling Initialization**: The random neuron sampling upcycling strategy (Expert Attn) results in the fastest convergence (approximately 5 times faster than from-scratch training) and lowest final training loss. It also leads to substantial improvements on instruction-following benchmarks (e.g., GenEval +12.47 and DPGBench +4.10 over from-scratch).
- **Conditioning Architecture**: The combination of Qwen3-VL-8B and in-context conditioning achieves the best instruction-following performance, with in-context injection demonstrating a greater impact than the choice of encoder.
- **Video Editing Data**: Fine-tuning on the Mamoda2.5-Edit-Data consistently surpasses fine-tuning on the open-source Reco-Data across editing accuracy, video quality, and naturalness on Reco-Bench, validating the effectiveness of the synthetic data pipeline.
- **Joint Distillation and RL**: The 4-step Student model, trained with joint distillation and RL, demonstrates a rapid increase in hybrid reward, surpassing the 30-step Teacher baseline after approximately 100 training steps. Qualitative examples show the FSD model matching or exceeding the 30-step teacher, achieving approximately 15 times faster inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Mamoda2.5 represents an advancement in the development of unified multimodal models, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Computational Efficiency for Scaling**: The fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture is a significant contribution, demonstrating that large-scale visual generation models, particularly for video, can achieve high parameter counts (25 billion) while maintaining efficient computational resource usage (3 billion activated parameters). This approach mitigates the prohibitive costs of dense architectures, making the development and deployment of high-quality, long-duration video generation and editing more practical.

2. **Model Unification and Versatility**: Mamoda2.5 unifies diverse visual tasks—text-to-image, text-to-video, image editing, video editing, and multimodal understanding—within a single, end-to-end architecture. This unification simplifies model development and deployment by eliminating the need for separate, task-specific models, potentially fostering more cohesive and integrated AI systems.

3. **Performance Benchmarking**: The model establishes competitive or state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks, notably surpassing several evaluated open-source and proprietary models (e.g., Kling O1 on OpenVE-Bench) in video editing. This indicates that the architectural and data-centric innovations contribute to a higher quality of visual outputs.

4. **Accelerated Inference for Real-World Applications**: The joint distillation and reinforcement learning framework, which compresses a 30-step editing model into a 4-step, CFG-free version, significantly reduces inference latency (up to 95.9x faster than baselines). This acceleration is critical for real-time or near real-time applications, making advanced video editing capabilities more accessible and usable in industrial settings.

5. **Addressing Data Scarcity in Video Editing**: The proposed synthetic data pipeline for video editing, which leverages strong video generation models and shared denoising steps to ensure structural consistency, addresses a key bottleneck in training robust video editing models. This method could enable the creation of larger and higher-quality datasets, further accelerating research in this domain.

6. **Practical Deployment and Commercial Value**: The successful deployment of Mamoda2.5 in real-world advertising scenarios for content moderation and creative restoration demonstrates its practical utility. Its ability to automate compliance checks, provide fine-grained feedback, and restore problematic video assets can improve advertising delivery efficiency, reduce manual labor costs, and accelerate creative production cycles, signifying its potential for substantial commercial impact.

In summary, Mamoda2.5 contributes to the field by demonstrating a scalable, efficient, and high-performing unified multimodal model, with a clear path towards practical application in computationally demanding visual AI tasks.
