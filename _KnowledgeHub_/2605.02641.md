# Mamoda2.5: Enhancing Unified Multimodal Model with DiT-MoE

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance's Mamoda2.5 unifies multimodal understanding, image generation, and video generation/editing within a single Autoregressive–Diffusion framework, leveraging a fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts Diffusion Transformer (DiT-MoE) for computational efficiency. The model demonstrates competitive performance on benchmarks like VBench 2.0 (61.64) and OpenVE-Bench (3.86), while its distilled version achieves up to 95.9 times faster video editing inference.

## Problem

- Unified video generation and editing frameworks face high computational complexity due to the quadratic scaling of visual tokens with spatio-temporal resolution, making large, dense models prohibitively expensive.
- Instruction-guided video editing is hampered by the scarcity of high-quality paired training data and significant computational overhead for encoding source videos.
- Achieving top-tier performance in complex video generation and editing tasks, especially regarding temporal consistency and fine-grained instruction adherence, remains a challenge for existing models.

## Method

- Introduces **Mamoda2.5**, an Autoregressive–Diffusion framework with a fine-grained ==Mixture-of-Experts== (**MoE**) ==Diffusion Transformer== (**DiT**) architecture, activating a sparse subset of parameters per forward pass for efficiency.
- Develops a three-stage synthetic video editing data pipeline that generates high-quality paired video instruction data by sharing early denoising steps between pre-edit and post-edit generations to ensure structural consistency.
- Implements a ==joint few-step distillation== and reinforcement learning framework to compress generative models, significantly accelerating inference for editing tasks from 30 steps to 4 steps.

## Results

- The DiT-MoE backbone (25 billion total parameters, ~3 billion activated) contributes to efficient training and achieves a VBench 2.0 score of 61.64 for text-to-video generation.
- Outperforms evaluated models on OpenVE-Bench (3.86 overall score) and FiVE-Bench (87.41 FiVE-Acc) for instruction-based video editing, demonstrating improved spatial reasoning and detail preservation.
- The 4-step distilled model reduces video editing inference latency to 9.2 seconds (480px, 81 frames), achieving up to a 95.9 times speedup over VInO and 41.7 times over OmniVideo2, while matching or exceeding the 30-step teacher's quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained MoE architectures, combined with dense-to-MoE upcycling, enable scaling visual generative models to 25 billion total parameters while activating only ~3 billion per pass, accelerating training and reducing costs.
> - A carefully designed synthetic data pipeline using shared denoising steps between pre- and post-edit generations effectively generates high-quality paired video editing data, overcoming data scarcity for complex tasks.
> - Joint distillation and reinforcement learning can drastically accelerate model inference (up to 95.9x faster) for complex editing tasks, yielding high-quality, efficient student models that can surpass the teacher's performance.
