# Physics-Informed Policy Optimization via Analytic Dynamics Regularization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University developed PIPER, a framework that integrates analytic physical constraints into neural policy optimization using a differentiable Lagrangian residual as a regularization term. This approach consistently improved sample efficiency by 20-45%, stability by up to 47%, and precision by up to 79.5% across diverse robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Model-free reinforcement learning algorithms suffer from high sample complexity, as they implicitly re-learn fundamental physics principles through trial and error.
- Policies learned without explicit physical guidance can yield physically inconsistent or unstable actions, often exploiting simulation integration errors.
- Current methods for integrating physics into RL often involve approximations, rigid safety layers, or significant modifications to core RL algorithms.

## Method

- The **PIPER** framework directly integrates a ==differentiable Lagrangian residual== as a ==regularization term into the actor's objective function==.
- An ==Automated Dynamics Oracle (ADO)== queries the simulator's internal physics engine at runtime to extract ==exact ground-truth inertial properties (M, C, G)==.
- A ==Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN)== predicts joint accelerations, which, combined with ADO data, form a ==Lagrangian residual== to quantify and penalize physical inconsistencies in the policy's actions.

## Results

- The framework consistently improved sample efficiency (**20-45%**), stability (up to **47%**), and precision (up to **79.5%**) across all evaluated Gymnasium-Robotics benchmark tasks (FetchReach, FetchPush, FetchSlide, FetchPickAndPlace).
- For kinematic control (FetchReach), **PIPER-SAC** reduced final positioning error by **71.5%** and sample complexity by **45%** compared to baselines.
- In contact-rich tasks like FetchPush and FetchPickAndPlace, **PIPER-TQC** significantly enhanced stability (**47%** and **42%** respectively) and precision (**34.7%** and **51%** respectively), by enforcing work-energy and grasp stability constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating analytical physical laws as a differentiable Lagrangian residual directly into the policy optimization objective effectively acts as a "physics preconditioner," shaping policy updates towards physically consistent solutions.
> - Utilizing an Automated Dynamics Oracle to extract exact, ground-truth inertial properties from the simulator's internal description at runtime provides a superior and precise physical signal compared to data-driven dynamics approximation.
> - Enforcing task-specific physical constraints, such as work-energy for continuous contact or impulse-momentum for impacts, significantly enhances robustness and accuracy in complex robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in robotic control, enabling the learning of complex manipulation and locomotion tasks. Algorithms such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), and Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) have advanced the field by allowing policies to be learned directly from high-dimensional sensor observations. However, a persistent limitation of these data-driven, model-free RL approaches is their high sample complexity and the tendency to produce physically inconsistent actions. This arises because neural policies often attempt to implicitly rediscover complex physical laws from data alone, treating the environment as a "black box" and disregarding readily available, accurate dynamics models within simulators. This "tabula rasa" approach leads to agents spending extensive computational resources relearning fundamental physics principles like inertia and momentum conservation through trial and error, sometimes resulting in control policies that exhibit high-frequency oscillations or exploit simulator integration errors.

In response to these challenges, a line of research focuses on integrating physical priors into learning-based control to enhance stability, safety, and data efficiency. Existing efforts include:
- **Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO)**: Integrates trust-region updates with explicit safety constraints during policy optimization.
- **Action Correction Layers and Residual Reinforcement Learning**: Augment classical controllers with learned residuals or introduce lightweight safety mechanisms.
- **Inductive Biases**: Explore concepts like energy conservation and symmetry-aware representations.
- **Deep Lagrangian Networks**: Embed physics explicitly into model-based learning.
- **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)**: Integrate physical laws into neural models by enforcing governing equations as soft constraints. PINNs have been used in robotics for system identification, learning forward/inverse dynamics, or as surrogate dynamics models within RL to regularize trajectory prediction or state transitions.

This work, PIPER (Physics-Informed Policy Optimization with Analytical Dynamic Regularization), distinguishes itself by proposing a novel framework that bridges the gap between data-driven policy learning and physical laws. It directly integrates *analytic* physical constraints into neural policy optimization using a differentiable Lagrangian residual as a regularization term. Unlike methods that rely on approximating physical laws from data, PIPER leverages an "Automated Dynamics Oracle" to extract ground-truth inertial properties (M, C, G) directly from the simulator's internal physics description. This approach allows for algorithm-agnostic applicability to a range of model-free RL algorithms without requiring rigid safety layers, handcrafted base controllers, computationally expensive rollouts for model-based consistency, or modifying core RL update rules. The PINN in PIPER serves as a training-time "physics coach" to regularize the actor, rather than approximating or learning the dynamics model itself, ensuring the final policy remains lightweight for deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current state-of-the-art model-free reinforcement learning algorithms when applied to robotic control tasks. Despite their empirical successes in learning complex behaviors, these methods often exhibit:
- **High Sample Complexity:** Agents require millions of environmental interactions to learn effective policies, primarily because they must implicitly infer or "rediscover" fundamental physics principles (e.g., inertia, gravity, momentum conservation) solely from trial-and-error experience, even when accurate dynamics models are available within the simulator.
- **Physically Inconsistent Actions:** Policies learned without explicit physical guidance can produce actions that are dynamically implausible or exploit simulation integration errors, leading to "jittery" or high-frequency oscillations rather than smooth, energy-efficient motions. The "black box" nature of standard model-free RL means it discards the rich physical information encoded in modern physics engines.

The central research question addressed is how to integrate the well-defined physical laws governing robotic systems directly into data-driven policy learning. This integration is intended to overcome the aforementioned limitations by providing the policy with a dense, structure-aware gradient that complements the typically sparse reward signal.

The key objectives of the PIPER framework are:
- **Improve Learning Efficiency:** By guiding the policy search towards physically consistent solutions, the framework aims to reduce the number of environmental interactions required for the agent to achieve a high level of performance. This is achieved by pruning the search space of physically impossible or implausible actions.
- **Enhance Stability:** The regularization term encourages policies that adhere to physical laws, leading to more stable and robust control actions, especially in tasks involving continuous contact or hybrid dynamics. This prevents policies from exploiting simulator integration errors.
- **Increase Control Accuracy:** By biasing policy updates towards dynamically consistent solutions, the framework seeks to achieve higher precision in reaching target states or performing manipulation tasks, resulting in smoother and more predictable robot movements.
- **Seamless Integration of Physical Constraints:** Develop a method to incorporate physical constraints directly into neural policy optimization using analytical soft physics constraints without requiring alterations to existing simulators or core RL algorithms.
- **Utilize Simulator's Internal Dynamics:** Leverage the exact Euler-Lagrange terms (M, C, G) derived from the robot's simulator description at runtime to construct a residual vector that quantifies violations of the equations of motion.
- **Algorithm-Agnostic Applicability:** Ensure the proposed framework is compatible with a wide range of existing model-free RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, TD3, SAC, TQC).
- **Robustness in Complex Robotic Tasks:** Validate the approach on a curriculum of robotic tasks that represent increasing physical complexity, including kinematic control, continuous contact, impulse transfer, and hybrid dynamics, to demonstrate its effectiveness in contact-rich regimes and sparse-reward settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PIPER (Physics-Informed Policy Optimization) is a framework designed to regularize policy learning by explicitly enforcing the laws of rigid-body mechanics. Its core mechanism involves integrating a differentiable Lagrangian residual as a regularization term within the actor's objective function.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The methodology is grounded in **Lagrangian Rigid-Body Mechanics**. A manipulator is modeled as a holonomic kinematic chain with generalized coordinates `q` and velocities `q̇`. Its dynamics are derived from the Lagrangian `L = K - P`, where `K` is kinetic energy and `P` is potential energy. Applying the Euler-Lagrange operator yields the explicit structural form of the equations of motion:
`M(q)q̈ + C(q, q̇)q̇ + G(q) = τ + τ_ext`
where `M(q)` is the inertia matrix, `C(q, q̇)q̇` represents Coriolis and centrifugal forces, `G(q)` is gravitational force, `τ` is the generalized control torque, and `τ_ext` represents external forces.
For computational efficiency, the **Generalized Bias Force** `b(q, q̇) = C(q, q̇)q̇ + G(q) - τ_ext` is defined, allowing the system dynamics to be expressed in a compact **Inverse Dynamics** form: `M(q)q̈ + b(s) = τ`.
The framework operates within a **Goal-Conditioned Markov Decision Process (GC-MDP)**, with an action space `A` corresponding to direct joint torque control. To mitigate overestimation bias, Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) are employed.

#### 3.2. Automated Dynamics Oracle (ADO)

To enable the enforcement of the inverse dynamics equation without manual derivation, an **Automated Dynamics Oracle (ADO)** is implemented. This oracle queries the simulator's internal spatial algebra engines at runtime to extract ground-truth physical properties:
- **Inertia Matrix `M(q)`:** Computed via the Composite Rigid Body Algorithm (CRBA).
- **Generalized Bias `b(q, q̇)`:** Extracted using the Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm (RNEA) by creating a "zero-acceleration" virtual state (`q̈ = 0`).
External contact forces (`τ_ext`) are directly queried from the MuJoCo simulator's contact sensors to ensure accuracy in contact-rich scenarios. These quantities provide exact physical terms for regularization, independent of the neural network's learning process.

#### 3.3. Physics-Informed Policy Optimization

The central mechanism of PIPER involves the integration of physics principles into the policy optimization loop:
- **Acceleration Proxy Network (PINN):** Since instantaneous acceleration `q̈` is not directly observable, a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN), denoted `Φ_ϕ`, is introduced to predict joint accelerations `q̈_hat = Φ_ϕ(s_t, a_t)`. This PINN is trained to minimize the prediction error against finite-difference accelerations observed in the environment, with an additional term for physics residual regularization.
- **Lagrangian Residual:** Physical inconsistency is quantified by the **Physics Residual vector `r(s, a)`**. By substituting the PINN's predicted acceleration and the ADO's extracted inertial terms into the inverse dynamics formulation, an estimated physical torque `τ_phy_hat(s, a) = M(q)Φ_ϕ(s, a) + b(s)` is computed. The residual is then defined as `r(s, a) = τ_phy_hat(s, a) - a`, where `a` is the torque proposed by the policy. This ensures the penalty evaluates the consistency of the policy's chosen action.
- **Policy Regularization:** The residual is incorporated into the policy optimization through a weighted penalty term `L_phys`. The augmented actor objective becomes:
 `max_θ J_total = E[RL_Loss] - λ_phys E[||r(s, π_θ(s))||^2]`
 where `RL_Loss` represents the standard RL objective (e.g., PPO's clipped surrogate or SAC's entropy-regularized objective), and `λ_phys` is the physics regularization weight.
- **Gradient Flow and Optimization:** The `L_phys` term provides an auxiliary gradient signal that shapes the policy manifold. Through the chain rule, it creates a "Physics Preconditioner" `H = M ∇_a Φ`, which effectively rotates the standard policy gradient. This ensures that policy updates align with the robot’s inertial properties, penalizing actions requiring physically impossible momentum changes.
- **Energy Conservation Enforcement:** For tasks involving contact or friction, adherence to the First Law of Thermodynamics is enforced. A residual `r_energy` is derived to quantify violations of energy conservation (`dE/dt = q̇^T τ`), which is then incorporated into the PINN loss. This prevents the policy from exploiting simulator integration errors to generate "free energy."

#### 3.4. Task-Specific Constraint Instantiation

The general Lagrangian residual is adapted for different control regimes:
- **Kinematic Control (FetchReach):** For free-space reaching tasks, `L_reach` focuses on internal articulation consistency (`M(q)q̈_hat + b(q, q̇) - τ`) and kinematic feasibility (distance to target).
- **Contact Dynamics (FetchPush):** For pushing tasks, `L_push` augments `L_reach` with an explicit work-energy theorem constraint, including a friction dissipation term, to prevent non-conservative energy spikes.
- **Impulse Transfer (FetchSlide):** For tasks dominated by momentum transfer, `L_slide` enforces the impulse-momentum theorem at impact points and incorporates a sliding friction residual to ensure adherence to Coulomb friction laws.
- **Hybrid Dynamics (FetchPickAndPlace):** For grasping tasks, the framework models a `Combined Mass Matrix` (`M_comb`) to account for discrete changes in inertia when an object is grasped. A grasp stability constraint (`L_grasp`) is enforced to penalize insufficient normal force during lifting, ensuring stable force-closure grasps.

The overall training pipeline, defined as the PINN-RL Training Pipeline, coordinates data collection, dynamics learning (for the PINN proxy), and policy regularization. It is designed to be algorithm-agnostic, supporting both on-policy and off-policy RL methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates PIPER on the Gymnasium-Robotics benchmark suite using a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda arm, covering tasks of increasing physical complexity: FetchReach-v4 (kinematic control), FetchPush-v4 (contact dynamics), FetchSlide-v4 (impulse transfer), and FetchPickAndPlace-v4 (hybrid dynamics). The evaluation uses three metrics: Sample Efficiency (steps to 95% success), Precision (final Euclidean distance to goal), and Stability (standard deviation of success rate over final evaluation rollouts). All experiments were run with 5 independent random seeds.

#### 4.1. Kinematic Control (FetchReach-v4)

In this free-space reaching task, PIPER-SAC reduced sample complexity by 45%, PIPER-PPO by 30%, and PIPER-TD3 by 26%, compared to their respective baselines. PIPER-SAC achieved a 71.5% reduction in final positioning error (from 7.55 mm to 2.15 mm), while PIPER-TD3 showed a 79.5% reduction. Stability improved by 29% for PIPER-SAC, 25.7% for PIPER-PPO, and 34.5% for PIPER-TD3. This indicates that the Lagrangian residual acted as a "Physics Preconditioner," guiding policy updates into the null-space of valid accelerations and effectively suppressing high-frequency control noise, leading to smoother and more precise convergence.

#### 4.2. Contact Dynamics (FetchPush-v4)

For the pushing task involving continuous contact, PIPER-TQC demonstrated a 47% improvement in stability (reducing the standard deviation of success rate from 14.3 to 7.7) and a 34.7% gain in precision (reducing final error from 12.4 mm to 8.1 mm). Sample efficiency improved by 26% (from 420,000 to 310,000 steps). The work-energy constraint prevented "force jittering" and non-conservative energy spikes, leading the agent to apply more consistent pushing forces.

#### 4.3. Impulse Transfer (FetchSlide-v4)

In this momentum-dominated sliding task, PIPER-TQC increased the success rate by 7% (from 85% to 92%) and reduced the final error by 60% (from 50.2 mm to 20.1 mm). Sample efficiency improved by 20% (from 850,000 to 680,000 steps), and stability improved by 39% (reducing standard deviation from 13.6 to 8.3). The impulse-momentum constraint guided the policy to learn a precise striking primitive, rather than relying on high-variance exploration.

#### 4.4. Complex Manipulation (FetchPickAndPlace-v4)

For the hybrid dynamics task of picking and placing an object, PIPER-TQC achieved a 99% peak success rate (a 3% increase) and a 51% reduction in final error (from 8.5 mm to 4.2 mm). Stability improved by 42% (reducing standard deviation from 25.0 to 14.6), and sample efficiency improved by 23% (from 470,000 to 362,000 steps). The grasp stability constraint ensured robust force-closure grasps, preventing object slippage and drops during discrete contact transitions.

#### 4.5. General Observations and Limitations

Overall, PIPER consistently improved sample efficiency (ranging from 20% to 45%), stability (up to 47% reduction in variance), and precision (up to 79.5% reduction in final error) across all tested tasks and RL algorithms. The physics regularization acted as a structure-preserving signal, leading to smoother and more reliable control.
The computational overhead introduced by the exact dynamics extraction and the auxiliary PINN proxy network was approximately 15-20% in wall-clock training time compared to standard baselines. A primary limitation identified by the authors is PIPER's reliance on accurate and introspectable simulation models (e.g., MuJoCo XML). Applying this framework to systems with complex, hard-to-model dynamics (e.g., highly deformable objects) or addressing the Sim2Real gap (where simulated dynamics differ from the real world) remains an open challenge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, PIPER, that integrates analytical physical priors into reinforcement learning, demonstrating their utility in enhancing robotic control policy performance and data efficiency. The work contributes to the scientific understanding of how to bridge the gap between classical control theory, which explicitly leverages underlying physical laws, and modern learning-based approaches that often treat environments as black boxes.

The significance of this work can be summarized as follows:
- **Improved Efficiency and Performance in RL:** The consistent gains in sample efficiency (20-45%), stability (up to 47%), and precision (up to 79.5%) across diverse robotic tasks highlight the practical benefit of explicitly grounding policies in physical laws. This suggests that incorporating domain-specific knowledge can significantly accelerate learning and lead to more accurate and reliable control outcomes compared to purely data-driven methods.
- **Enhancement of Model-Free RL:** By providing a differentiable "physics coach" through Lagrangian regularization, PIPER offers a method to infuse structural knowledge into model-free RL algorithms without altering their core mechanisms or requiring a full dynamics model for inference. This allows existing successful algorithms to benefit from physics awareness.
- **Reduction of Physically Inconsistent Behavior:** The regularization specifically targets and penalizes actions that violate fundamental rigid-body mechanics or energy conservation. This capability can mitigate issues like "jittering" or exploitation of simulator integration errors, which are common in unconstrained RL, leading to more physically plausible and robust robot behaviors.
- **Algorithm-Agnostic and Plug-and-Play Integration:** The design of PIPER, including its Automated Dynamics Oracle, allows it to be applied to various on-policy and off-policy RL algorithms and to extract dynamics from standard simulator descriptions (like MuJoCo XML) without manual derivation. This broad compatibility and ease of integration can facilitate wider adoption.
- **Foundation for Physically Grounded Robotics:** The paper establishes a paradigm where a robot's physical properties are treated as accessible features rather than latent variables to be inferred. This approach offers a path toward more predictable and trustworthy robotic control systems, which is crucial for deployment in real-world applications where safety and reliability are paramount.

The potential impact of this research extends to:
- **Real-World Robotic Applications:** More efficient and precise learning can translate to faster development cycles for robotic systems and more reliable operation in tasks requiring fine manipulation, contact interaction, or dynamic movements. This could benefit applications in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and exploration.
- **Reduced Training Costs:** Decreased sample complexity means fewer simulator interactions, leading to reduced computational resources and time required for training policies.
- **Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While the paper acknowledges the Sim2Real gap as a limitation, providing policies that are inherently more physically consistent in simulation could potentially lead to better transferability to real-world robots, as these policies are less likely to rely on simulator-specific artifacts.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for further research into integrating physics into learning, such as adapting the framework to other physics engines and robot description formats (e.g., URDF), extending it to systems with more complex or deformable dynamics, and explicitly addressing the Sim2Real challenge for physics-informed policies.
