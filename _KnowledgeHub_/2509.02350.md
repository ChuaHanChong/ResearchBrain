# Implicit Reasoning in Large Language Models: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey organizes research on implicit reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) by proposing a new execution-centric taxonomy and consolidating evidence for how multi-step reasoning unfolds internally without generating explicit text. The work highlights critical challenges and future directions to foster more efficient and robust LLM reasoning systems.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often rely on explicit reasoning methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), which are inefficient due to generating lengthy textual intermediate steps, leading to high computational costs and inference latency.
- The rapidly expanding field of implicit reasoning in LLMs, where multi-step computation occurs internally, lacks a unified conceptual framework and systematic categorization of its diverse approaches.
- There is an underexplored need for a mechanism-level understanding of *how* internal reasoning unfolds within LLMs, beyond general discussions of latent representations.

## Method

- The survey introduces a novel, ==execution-centric taxonomy== that categorizes implicit reasoning methods based on *how* and *where* internal computation takes place, rather than solely on representational forms.
- It systematically details three primary execution paradigms: ==Latent Optimization== (manipulating internal representations), ==Signal-Guided Control== (using specialized non-textual tokens), and ==Layer-Recurrent Execution== (iterative re-use of layers).
- The authors synthesize structural, behavioral, and representation-based evidence to empirically demonstrate the presence and dynamics of implicit multi-step reasoning within LLMs.

## Results

- The survey establishes a novel, three-part execution-centric taxonomy for implicit reasoning: Latent Optimization, Signal-Guided Control, and Layer-Recurrent Execution, providing a unified framework for the field.
- It compiles and synthesizes comprehensive mechanistic and behavioral evidence, confirming the existence and operation of implicit reasoning within LLMs.
- The work provides a structured overview of over **70** benchmark datasets and evaluation metrics, highlighting the inconsistencies in current practices and the need for standardized assessment of implicit reasoning methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit reasoning offers advantages over explicit methods, including lower generation costs, faster inference, and a potentially better alignment with the internal computational processes of LLMs.
> - LLMs exhibit verifiable patterns in their internal layers, observable behaviors, and detectable information in hidden states, all indicative of internal multi-step computations and implicit reasoning.
> - Current implicit reasoning methods face significant challenges related to interpretability, control, a performance gap compared to explicit CoT for complex tasks, and a lack of standardized evaluation protocols.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models has witnessed an explosion of capabilities, demonstrating strong generalization across a myriad of tasks. Central to these capabilities is the LLM's capacity for reasoning, which is crucial for solving multi-step problems and complex decision-making. Historically, much of the focus in enhancing LLM reasoning has been on "explicit reasoning," primarily through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. CoT involves LLMs verbalizing their intermediate reasoning steps in natural language, which improves interpretability and accuracy on complex problems.

However, the authors identify a critical drawback of explicit reasoning: inefficiency. The generation of lengthy textual reasoning steps consumes significant computational resources, increases inference latency, and adds to overall cost. This overhead, coupled with the potential for "overthinking" (generating unnecessary or irrelevant steps), has spurred a shift in research attention.

This survey positions itself squarely within this evolving landscape by focusing on "implicit reasoning." Implicit reasoning refers to the paradigm where multi-step reasoning occurs silently, via latent structures within the LLM, without emitting intermediate textual steps. This approach offers several advantages, including lower generation cost, faster inference, and a potentially better alignment with the internal computational processes of LLMs.

While prior surveys have touched upon latent representations and reasoning more broadly, the authors assert that a "dedicated and mechanism-level examination of how reasoning unfolds internally within LLMs remains absent." This survey directly addresses this gap. It differentiates itself from existing literature reviews by shifting the focus from representational forms (e.g., latent representations in general) to computational strategies, introducing a novel taxonomy centered on *execution paradigms*. By providing a systematic organization of fragmented literature, the survey consolidates diverse efforts in latent-state modeling, compact reasoning trajectories, loop-based computation, and test-time control, which were previously without a unified conceptual framework. This foundational work is crucial for guiding future research toward more efficient, controllable, and cognitively grounded reasoning systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is to address the fragmentation and underexplored nature of implicit reasoning within the rapidly expanding field of Large Language Models. While the benefits of implicit reasoning—such as efficiency and better alignment with internal computation—are increasingly recognized, the diverse range of existing approaches lacks a unifying framework.

The key objectives of this survey can be summarized as follows:

1. **Introduce a Functional, Execution-Centric Taxonomy:** The core objective is to move beyond representational forms and establish a novel taxonomy for implicit reasoning methods. This taxonomy is centered on *execution paradigms*, categorizing methods based on "how and where internal computation unfolds." This aims to provide a coherent framework for understanding and organizing the diverse strategies employed in implicit reasoning.
2. **Systematically Characterize Implicit Reasoning Paradigms:** The survey aims to detail the three main execution paradigms identified: latent optimization, signal-guided control, and layer-recurrent execution. It further refines these into sub-types based on reasoning granularity and control mechanisms, offering a structured understanding of each approach.
3. **Investigate and Synthesize Evidence for Implicit Reasoning:** A crucial objective is to empirically ground the discussion by reviewing and synthesizing structural, behavioral, and representation-based evidence that supports the actual presence and dynamics of implicit reasoning in LLMs. This helps to move beyond mere speculation and provide concrete indications of internal computational processes.
4. **Provide a Structured Overview of Evaluation Protocols and Benchmarking Practices:** The authors recognize a significant oversight in prior surveys regarding evaluation. A key objective is to systematically review the metrics and benchmark datasets commonly used to assess the effectiveness and reliability of implicit reasoning methods, highlighting the challenges and lack of standardization in this area.
5. **Identify Challenges and Outline Future Directions:** Beyond consolidation, the survey aims to critically assess the current limitations of implicit reasoning, such as interpretability issues, control challenges, performance gaps, and architectural constraints. By outlining these challenges, the authors intend to stimulate future research toward building more efficient, robust, interpretable, and cognitively aligned reasoning systems.

In essence, the motivation is to bring order to a chaotic but promising sub-field, provide a foundational understanding for researchers, and clearly chart a path for its future development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a **functional perspective** to systematically categorize and analyze existing literature on implicit reasoning in LLMs. This approach emphasizes *how* and *where* latent computations unfold within the model, rather than solely focusing on the forms of latent representations.

The methodological backbone of the survey is a **three-part taxonomy of execution paradigms**:

1. **Latent Optimization:** This paradigm covers methods that directly manipulate and optimize internal representations to improve reasoning without generating intermediate text. It is further broken down by the granularity of the optimized target unit:
 - **Token-Level:** Methods manipulating individual tokens (e.g., inserting semantic concepts, non-interpretable latent tokens, or learning discrete latent codes).
 - **Trajectory-Level:** Methods treating the entire reasoning trajectory as a unit, compressing explicit steps into continuous latent thoughts. This category is subdivided into:
 - **Semantic Anchoring:** Directly aligning latent trajectories with explicit reasoning supervision.
 - **Adaptive Efficiency:** Dynamically adjusting reasoning length or speed during inference.
 - **Progressive Refinement:** Step-by-step internalization or iterative updating of implicit reasoning.
 - **Exploratory Diversification:** Introducing soft or perturbed latent representations to explore multiple reasoning paths.
 - **Internal-State-Level:** Methods taking the model's internal hidden states as the target for reasoning regulation (e.g., distilling explicit CoT into latent embeddings, implicit memory modules).

2. **Signal-Guided Control:** This paradigm includes methods that steer internal reasoning by inserting specialized tokens or signals that modulate computation without producing textual outputs. These are categorized by the nature of the signal:
 - **Single-Type Signal:** A single control mechanism uniformly modulates the reasoning process (e.g., thinking tokens, pause tokens, planning tokens, or token-free latent adjustments).
 - **Multi-Type Signal:** Employing multiple distinct control signals, each governing a specific aspect of the reasoning process (e.g., separate tokens for memory recall and reasoning).

3. **Layer-Recurrent Execution:** This paradigm focuses on architectural modifications where recurrence is introduced into the forward computation of Transformer models, enabling multi-step reasoning through repeated internal computation via shared weights and dynamic depth control.

Beyond this core taxonomy, the survey employs a multi-faceted analytical approach:

- **Evidence Review:** The authors conduct a parallel investigation into three categories of empirical and mechanistic evidence supporting the presence of implicit reasoning:
 - **Layer-wise Structural Evidence:** Analyzing patterns in intermediate model layers, such as linear predictability of final outputs or task-specific subtask execution at different depths.
 - **Behavioral Signatures:** Examining observable behaviors during inference, like grokking phenomena, step-skipping, or reasoning leaps, to infer internal processes.
 - **Representation-Based Analysis:** Utilizing probing methods, activation interventions, or mechanistic reverse-engineering to understand how latent reasoning manifests in the model's hidden states.
- **Evaluation and Benchmarking Overview:** A systematic review of current evaluation practices, including:
 - **Metrics:** Categorizing common metrics into answer correctness (Accuracy, Pass@k, EM, BLEU/ROUGE), resource efficiency (Decoding Latency, Output Length, Computational Usage, ACU), perplexity, and probing accuracy.
 - **Benchmarks:** Organizing widely used datasets into five categories: General Knowledge and Commonsense Reasoning, Mathematical Reasoning and Programming, Language Modeling and Reading Comprehension, Complex Multi-Hop and Multidisciplinary QA, and Multi-modal Reasoning. Each category provides detailed characteristics and typical use cases.
- **Challenges and Future Directions:** A critical discussion of current limitations in implicit reasoning, structured into six key areas (interpretability, control, performance gap, evaluation, architecture, supervision dependence), each accompanied by potential research avenues.

The authors maintain a continuously updated GitHub project (https://github.com/digailab/awesome-llm-implicit-reasoning), reinforcing the dynamic and evolving nature of the field and providing a living resource for the research community. This comprehensive and structured methodology allows for a deep, systematic, and up-to-date analysis of implicit reasoning in LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, the "findings" are primarily the structured categorization of existing research, the synthesis of evidence, and the identification of gaps and future directions, rather than empirical results from new experiments. The main findings and contributions can be summarized as follows:

1. **A Novel Execution-Centric Taxonomy for Implicit Reasoning:** The survey successfully organizes the fragmented literature into a coherent and functional taxonomy based on three primary execution paradigms:
 - **Latent Optimization:** This paradigm encompasses methods that enhance reasoning by directly manipulating the model's internal representations. Key sub-categories include:
 - **Token-Level:** Approaches like CoCoMix, Latent Token, LPC, and Token Assorted manipulate individual special tokens or continuous semantic concepts within the input sequence to guide internal computation.
 - **Trajectory-Level:** Methods such as CCoT, HCoT, CODI, and SynAdapt (Semantic Anchoring), LightThinker, CoT-Valve, CoLaR (Adaptive Efficiency), ICoT-SI, Coconut, Heima, PonderingLM, BoLT (Progressive Refinement), and LaTRO, Soft Thinking, SoftCoT, SoftCoT++, COT2 (Exploratory Diversification) compress or progressively refine multi-step reasoning into continuous latent trajectories, or explore diverse latent paths.
 - **Internal-State-Level:** ICoT-KD, System2 Distillation, ReaRec, Beyond Words, System-1.5 Reasoning, LTMs, and HRPO, which operate on the model's hidden states, often through distillation, memory modules, or dynamic routing, to internalize reasoning.
 - **Signal-Guided Control:** This paradigm uses specialized, non-textual signals to modulate internal computation. This includes:
 - **Single-Type Signals:** Methods like Thinking Tokens, Pause Tokens, Filler Tokens, Planning Tokens, Quiet-STaR, LatentSeek, and DIT, which typically introduce a specific type of control token or mechanism to allocate additional internal computation.
 - **Multi-Type Signals:** Approaches such as Memory & Reasoning and Thinkless, which use distinct control tokens (e.g., `<memory>`, `<reason>`) for finer-grained, structured internal control.
 - **Layer-Recurrent Execution:** This paradigm leverages architectural recurrence, where model layers or blocks are re-used iteratively to progressively refine token representations. Examples include ITT, Looped Transformer, CoTFormer, Huginn, and RELAY, which simulate deeper reasoning chains while maintaining parameter efficiency.

2. **Comprehensive Review of Mechanistic and Behavioral Evidence:** The survey provides a robust argument for the existence of implicit reasoning by compiling evidence from three angles:
 - **Layer-wise Structural Evidence:** Studies like Jump to Conclusions and Internal Chain-of-Thought indicate that intermediate layers can approximate final predictions or sequentially decompose tasks, showing depth-wise computational patterns.
 - **Behavioral Signatures:** Phenomena such as grokking (Wang et al., 2024a), step-skipping (Liu et al., 2024b), and latent multi-hop reasoning (Yang et al., 2024c) demonstrate LLMs' capacity to perform internal computations that lead to generalized or efficient problem-solving.
 - **Representation-Based Analysis:** Techniques like MechanisticProbe (Hou et al., 2023), TTT (Kudo et al., 2024), and CoE (Wang et al., 2024d) show that reasoning-relevant information is encoded in hidden states and attention patterns, which can be probed or influenced by steering vectors.

3. **Standardized Overview of Evaluation and Benchmarking:** The survey highlights the inconsistencies in evaluation and systematically categorizes metrics (Answer Correctness, Resource Efficiency, Perplexity, Probing Accuracy) and over 70 benchmark datasets across five domains (General Knowledge, Math/Programming, Language Modeling/Reading Comprehension, Multi-hop/Multidisciplinary QA, Multimodal Reasoning). This comprehensive list reveals the breadth of tasks used and the need for more standardized evaluation protocols.

4. **Identification of Critical Challenges:** The survey explicitly outlines six key limitations hindering the progress of implicit reasoning:
 - **Limited Interpretability and Latent Opacity:** The inherent "black-box" nature of internal reasoning.
 - **Limited Control and Reliability:** Difficulty in guiding or correcting silent computations.
 - **Performance Gap:** Implicit methods often lag behind explicit CoT in accuracy for complex tasks.
 - **Lack of Standardized Evaluation:** Inconsistent metrics and benchmarks across studies.
 - **Architecture and Generalization Constraints:** Dependence on specific architectures and limited scalability to larger models.
 - **Dependence on Explicit Supervision:** Most methods still rely on explicit CoT traces for training, limiting independence and scalability.

These findings provide a clear roadmap for understanding the current state of implicit reasoning, its underlying mechanisms, and the critical areas that require further research and development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey on implicit reasoning in Large Language Models carries significant implications for both current research and the future development of AI. Its primary significance lies in its ability to **unify and structure a rapidly growing yet fragmented research area**, providing a much-needed conceptual framework for understanding the diverse approaches to internal LLM reasoning.

The **execution-centric taxonomy** is a major contribution, shifting the discussion from *what* latent representations are to *how* and *where* internal computation happens. This structured view is invaluable for:

- **Clarifying the Landscape:** It helps researchers navigate the extensive and varied literature, distinguishing between different strategies (latent optimization, signal-guided control, layer-recurrent execution) and their granularities.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By categorizing methods and highlighting their mechanisms, the survey points to areas where novel combinations or advancements within specific paradigms might be most fruitful. It encourages a deeper exploration of internal computational strategies rather than just representational forms.

The **synthesis of mechanistic and behavioral evidence** is crucial for validating the very premise of implicit reasoning. By demonstrating that LLMs indeed exhibit structural patterns, behavioral signatures, and detectable information in their internal representations that are indicative of multi-step computation, the survey provides empirical grounding for this paradigm. This strengthens the argument for pursuing implicit reasoning as a viable and potentially superior alternative to explicit CoT.

Furthermore, the **systematic review of evaluation metrics and benchmarks** addresses a critical gap in the field. The identified inconsistencies and lack of standardized protocols underscore the urgent need for a unified evaluation framework. By cataloging existing datasets and discussing their applicability, the survey lays the groundwork for:

- **Improved Comparability:** Encouraging researchers to adopt consistent benchmarks and metrics, facilitating fairer comparisons between different implicit reasoning methods.
- **Diagnosing Failure Modes:** Moving beyond simple accuracy to metrics that assess the quality, depth, and robustness of internal reasoning, thereby enabling better diagnosis of why models fail.

The explicit articulation of **challenges and future directions** is another high-impact aspect of this survey. By identifying six critical limitations (interpretability, control, performance gap, evaluation, architectural constraints, and dependence on explicit supervision), the authors not only present a realistic view of the current state but also carve out specific, actionable research agendas. This structured challenge-and-opportunity perspective will likely:

- **Stimulate Innovation:** Motivate researchers to develop novel solutions for enhancing interpretability (e.g., causal intervention analysis), improving control (e.g., confidence-aware execution), closing the performance gap, and creating architecture-agnostic designs.
- **Foster Development of Independent Implicit Reasoning:** The call for supervision signals that operate directly on latent trajectories, moving away from explicit CoT dependence, could lead to more scalable and autonomous implicit reasoning systems.

In sum, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work; it is a foundational piece that redefines the understanding of implicit reasoning in LLMs. By providing a clear framework, synthesizing evidence, critiquing evaluation practices, and charting future directions, it significantly advances the field toward developing LLMs that are not only powerful but also more efficient, robust, and aligned with complex cognitive processes. This could lead to breakthroughs in areas requiring high-speed, low-cost reasoning, such as real-time decision-making, embedded AI, and scalable intelligent agents.
