# Efficient Continual Adaptation of Pretrained Robotic Policy with Online Meta-Learned Adapters

> [!summary] Summary
> Online Meta-Learned Adapters (OMLA) proposes an online meta-learning framework for continuously adapting pretrained robotic policies, using meta-learned adapters to proactively transfer knowledge across tasks. This approach achieves superior forward transfer (e.g., 0.86 average FWT on LIBERO-OBJECT compared to LoRA's 0.71) and prevents catastrophic forgetting in both simulated and real-world robotic control tasks.

## Problem

- Existing continual learning methods in robotics suffer from catastrophic forgetting and conservative learning, hindering efficient adaptation to new tasks.
- Current adapter-based approaches for robotic policy fine-tuning treat each new task's adapter learning independently, limiting knowledge transfer from past experiences.
- Traditional meta-learning frameworks can be computationally intensive for high-dimensional robotic observations and large models, making them impractical for online continual adaptation.

## Method

- OMLA integrates parameter-efficient LoRA adapters with an online meta-learning objective to learn a task-agnostic prior for adapter parameters.
- A two-stage learning paradigm is introduced: an online meta-learning phase updates adapter initialization using past tasks, followed by a fine-tuning phase on the current new task.
- A novel similarity-based sampling strategy is employed for meta-learning, selecting relevant observation-action pairs from past episodes to reduce computational overhead.

## Results

- OMLA achieved consistently higher Forward Transfer (FWT) compared to LoRA and Experience Replay (ER) across LIBERO task suites, for example, 0.86 FWT on LIBERO-OBJECT versus 0.71 for LoRA.
- Both OMLA and LoRA demonstrated a Backward Transfer (BWT) of 0, effectively mitigating catastrophic forgetting, unlike ER which showed negative BWT values.
- The method proved robust across varying dataset sizes (5 to 20 demonstrations) and different policy architectures (ViT and ResNet-18 backbones), and achieved higher success rates in real-world pick-and-place tasks (e.g., 84.0% with 20 demonstrations vs. LoRA's 60.0%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning an informed prior for adapter parameters through online meta-learning enables more efficient and effective adaptation to new tasks by facilitating knowledge transfer.
> - A targeted sampling strategy for meta-learning can significantly reduce computational costs in robotics while retaining sufficient information for effective adaptation.
> - Continually incorporating newly learned task data into the meta-learning pool is crucial for enriching the prior and maintaining high adaptation performance.
