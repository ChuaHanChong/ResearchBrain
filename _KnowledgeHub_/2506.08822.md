# FreqPolicy: Efficient Flow-based Visuomotor Policy via Frequency Consistency

> [!summary] Summary
> FreqPolicy introduces an efficient, one-step visuomotor policy that leverages frequency-domain consistency constraints within a flow matching framework to model the temporal dynamics of robotic actions. The method achieves high-quality action generation with significantly reduced inference latency across various manipulation tasks and observation modalities, including real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Existing generative modeling-based visuomotor policies suffer from high inference latency due to their multi-step sampling processes, hindering real-time robotic deployment.
- Prior acceleration techniques for visuomotor policies often adapt methods from image generation, overlooking the critical time-series nature and temporal coherence requirements of robotic actions.
- There is a need for robust and efficient one-step action generation to enable stable, high-frequency control in dynamic and long-horizon robotic tasks.

## Method

- **FreqPolicy** is a ==conditional flow matching==-based visuomotor policy that learns to map observations to action chunks for imitation learning.
- It introduces novel frequency consistency constraints by projecting action velocity vectors into the frequency domain (using Discrete Cosine Transform) and enforcing consistency between velocities at different timesteps along the flow.
- An adaptive weighting scheme for frequency components is employed to dynamically emphasize informative dynamic variations, accounting for structured temporal changes in manipulation tasks.

## Results

- Achieved NFE=1 (one-step inference) with superior success rates on Robomimic, Adroit, and MetaWorld benchmarks, outperforming prior one-step methods and some multi-step baselines.
- Integrated with the OpenVLA model, OpenVLA-FreqPolicy attained an average success rate of **94.8%** (compared to OpenVLA-DP's **68.1%**) while being 5 times faster than OpenVLA-FlowMatching (10 NFE).
- Demonstrated strong real-world efficacy, achieving success rates comparable to or exceeding multi-step policies (e.g., **70%** at **93.5 Hz** for Fruit Sorting) in long-horizon tasks with significantly reduced computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic actions are inherently time-series data, and explicitly leveraging their temporal dependencies in the frequency domain can enhance action generation effectiveness and promote straighter probability flows.
> - Frequency representations, such as Discrete Cosine Transform coefficients, are effective in capturing non-stationary and oscillatory patterns in fine-grained action dynamics.
> - Adaptively weighting different frequency components based on their discrepancy allows the policy to focus on the most informative dynamic variations, improving robustness across diverse task phases.
