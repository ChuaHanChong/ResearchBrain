# FreqPolicy: Efficient Flow-based Visuomotor Policy via Frequency Consistency

> [!summary] Summary
> FreqPolicy introduces an efficient, one-step visuomotor policy that leverages frequency-domain consistency constraints within a flow matching framework to model the temporal dynamics of robotic actions. The method achieves high-quality action generation with significantly reduced inference latency across various manipulation tasks and observation modalities, including real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Existing generative modeling-based visuomotor policies suffer from high inference latency due to their multi-step sampling processes, hindering real-time robotic deployment.
- Prior acceleration techniques for visuomotor policies often adapt methods from image generation, overlooking the critical time-series nature and temporal coherence requirements of robotic actions.
- There is a need for robust and efficient one-step action generation to enable stable, high-frequency control in dynamic and long-horizon robotic tasks.

## Method

- **FreqPolicy** is a ==conditional flow matching==-based visuomotor policy that learns to map observations to action chunks for imitation learning.
- It introduces novel frequency consistency constraints by projecting action velocity vectors into the frequency domain (using Discrete Cosine Transform) and enforcing consistency between velocities at different timesteps along the flow.
- An adaptive weighting scheme for frequency components is employed to dynamically emphasize informative dynamic variations, accounting for structured temporal changes in manipulation tasks.

## Results

- Achieved NFE=1 (one-step inference) with superior success rates on Robomimic, Adroit, and MetaWorld benchmarks, outperforming prior one-step methods and some multi-step baselines.
- Integrated with the OpenVLA model, OpenVLA-FreqPolicy attained an average success rate of **94.8%** (compared to OpenVLA-DP's **68.1%**) while being 5 times faster than OpenVLA-FlowMatching (10 NFE).
- Demonstrated strong real-world efficacy, achieving success rates comparable to or exceeding multi-step policies (e.g., **70%** at **93.5 Hz** for Fruit Sorting) in long-horizon tasks with significantly reduced computational overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic actions are inherently time-series data, and explicitly leveraging their temporal dependencies in the frequency domain can enhance action generation effectiveness and promote straighter probability flows.
> - Frequency representations, such as Discrete Cosine Transform coefficients, are effective in capturing non-stationary and oscillatory patterns in fine-grained action dynamics.
> - Adaptively weighting different frequency components based on their discrepancy allows the policy to focus on the most informative dynamic variations, improving robustness across diverse task phases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on visuomotor policies derived through imitation learning. A significant development in this area has been the adoption of generative modeling-based policies, such as diffusion models and flow matching, which are recognized for their capability to model complex multimodal action distributions in high-dimensional action spaces. These policies transform Gaussian noise into actions through an iterative sampling process.

However, a primary challenge with these generative models in robotics is their high inference cost, stemming from the multi-step sampling required to generate an action. This latency can impede real-time deployment and stable action execution in dynamic or long-horizon robotic tasks. Previous research efforts to mitigate this issue have largely involved adapting acceleration techniques originally developed for image generation to the robotics domain. These techniques include consistency distillation (e.g., Consistency Policy, ManiCM) and distribution matching (e.g., SDM, OneDP), which aim to reduce the number of sampling steps to achieve one-step or few-step action generation. FlowPolicy is a notable predecessor that applied consistency constraints from Consistency-FM, developed for image generation, to flow-based policies in robotics to enable one-step generation.

A central premise of the current work is that a fundamental distinction exists between image generation and robotic manipulation that has been largely overlooked by these adapted methods. Image generation typically produces independent samples, whereas robotic manipulation requires generating continuous and temporally coherent action trajectories. This paper positions itself by arguing that leveraging these inherent temporal dependencies is crucial for enhancing action generation effectiveness. FreqPolicy introduces a novel approach that specifically imposes frequency consistency constraints on flow-based visuomotor policies, aiming to effectively capture temporal structure and achieve efficient, high-quality one-step action generation by explicitly considering the time-series nature of actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and high-quality one-step visuomotor policy, named FreqPolicy, that addresses the limitations of existing generative modeling-based policies in robotic manipulation. The core motivation stems from the recognition of a bottleneck in current generative policies: their reliance on iterative sampling processes, which leads to high inference latency and restricts their applicability in real-time robotic systems, especially for tasks requiring stable and smooth action execution over extended periods or in dynamic environments.

The detailed motivations and specific objectives are as follows:

- **Addressing High Inference Latency:** Generative models, particularly diffusion and flow-matching approaches, generate actions through multiple sampling steps. This iterative nature, while enabling robust modeling of multimodal action distributions, incurs significant computational overhead, rendering them unsuitable for applications demanding high-frequency control. The research aims to enable one-step action generation to overcome this latency.
- **Leveraging Temporal Dependencies in Robotic Actions:** Existing acceleration methods for visuomotor policies often adapt techniques from image generation. However, image generation typically deals with independent samples, whereas robotic manipulation involves action trajectories that are time-series data with inherent continuity and temporal coherence. The authors hypothesize that explicitly incorporating this temporal knowledge can significantly improve action generation. FreqPolicy is motivated to be the first one-step visuomotor policy to impose temporal knowledge for robotic manipulation.
- **Utilizing Frequency-Domain Information:** Drawing inspiration from time-series forecasting and speech processing, the authors propose that frequency representations can effectively capture non-stationary and oscillatory patterns in temporal dynamics, which time-domain features might miss. For high-frequency sampled action chunks in robotics, frequency features are expected to provide finer discrimination of subtle variations in smooth trajectories. Therefore, a key objective is to enforce consistency of action features in the frequency domain across different timesteps along the flow.
- **Capturing Structured Temporal Variations:** Robotic manipulation sequences often alternate between phases of low-dynamic, smooth movements (e.g., reaching) and high-dynamic, complex movements (e.g., contact-rich interactions). The distribution of frequency components within action chunks thus varies over time. The research aims to design an adaptive mechanism to account for these structural temporal variations, dynamically emphasizing frequency components that exhibit greater discrepancy and are therefore more informative.
- **Facilitating Straight Flow Trajectories:** The proposed frequency consistency constraints are intended to promote the convergence of one-step action generation toward the target distribution by guiding the learning process towards straighter probability flows. This contributes to the goal of accurate one-step inference.
- **Demonstrating Generalizability and Real-World Efficacy:** A practical objective is to validate FreqPolicy across a range of simulation benchmarks (2D and 3D inputs, including integration with Vision-Language-Action models) and in real-world robotic scenarios to demonstrate its superiority over existing one-step action generators and its practical utility in real-time applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FreqPolicy is designed as a flow matching-based visuomotor policy that incorporates frequency-domain consistency constraints to achieve efficient one-step action generation. The methodology builds upon conditional flow matching and introduces novel objectives to leverage temporal characteristics of robotic actions.

**Task Setting:**
The work operates within the imitation learning paradigm for robotic manipulation. The policy, denoted as $\pi_\theta(a_{1:H}|o)$, learns a mapping from observations ($o$) to action chunks ($a_{1:H}$, with chunking size $H$). Observations can include proprioceptive states, RGB images (from eye-in-hand or third-person cameras), and point cloud data. Actions are typically SE(3) motions of the end-effector or joint states. The method is evaluated under both 2D and 3D observation settings.

**Preliminaries: Flow Matching (FM):**
Flow matching aims to learn a time-dependent vector field $v(t, x)$ that transforms a simple prior distribution ($p_0(x)$) into a complex target distribution ($p_1(x)$) via an ordinary differential equation (ODE). Specifically, conditional flow matching minimizes the objective:
$L_\theta = E_{t, p_1(x_1), p_t(x_t|x_1)} \|v_\theta(t, x_t) - u(t, x_t|x_1)\|^2$
where $v_\theta(t, x_t)$ is the learned vector field and $u(t, x_t|x_1)$ is a target vector field, often instantiated as a linear interpolation for straight probability paths: $u(t, x_t|x_1) = \frac{x_1 - x_t}{1 - t}$. This foundational objective allows the policy to regress a vector field from noise to expert actions. While this base policy can generate multimodal actions, it still requires multiple sampling steps for inference.

**FreqPolicy Components:**

1. **Basic Flow Matching Learning Objective ($L_{fm}$):**
 FreqPolicy starts with a standard flow matching objective to learn the vector field. Given an initial noise $a_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and an expert action $a_1 \sim p_{expert}$, the objective is to approximate the conditional velocity field $u(t, a_t) = a_1 - a_0$. The interpolated state is $a_t = (1-t) \cdot a_0 + t \cdot a_1$. The loss is:
 $L_{fm} = E_{t \sim U(0,1), (a_0, a_1) \sim \mathcal{D}} \|v_\theta(t, a_t) - (a_1 - a_0)\|^2$
 This provides the foundational learning for action generation.

2. **Frequency Consistency Constraint Objective ($L_{freq}$):**
 Unlike prior approaches that treat action chunks as static vectors, FreqPolicy treats each action chunk as a temporal signal. To promote straighter flows and enable one-step generation, consistency is enforced in the frequency space between action velocity vectors across different timesteps.
 - **Interpolated States:** For a given pair $(a_0, a_1)$, two arbitrary timesteps $s, r \in$ are selected, and interpolated states $a_r = (1-r) \cdot a_0 + r \cdot a_1$ and $a_s = (1-s) \cdot a_0 + s \cdot a_1$ are constructed.
 - **Consistency Constraints:** The objective includes two parts:
 - Direct consistency of velocities: $\text{Sim}(v_\theta(s, a_s), v_\theta(r, a_r))$
 - Trajectory consistency: enforces that trajectories starting from $a_s$ and $a_r$ converge to the same point at a later time $u$ (where $r < s < u$).
 - **Frequency-Domain Projection:** To leverage temporal characteristics, velocity vectors are projected into the frequency domain using the Type-II Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT):
 $F(v_t)_k = \sum_{n=0}^{H-1} v_t(n) \cdot \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{N} \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right) k\right)$, for $k=0, \ldots, H-1$
 where $F(v_t)_k$ is the k-th spectral coefficient.
 - **Similarity Function:** The $\text{Sim}(\cdot)$ function is defined as the $\ell_2$ norm between the frequency coefficients of the two velocity vectors:
 $\text{Sim}(v_r, v_s) = \|F(v_r) - F(v_s)\|^2$
 This encourages temporal consistency in action chunks and guides the policy towards learning straighter, more stable flow trajectories.

3. **Adaptive Frequency Component Loss:**
 Recognizing that the importance of different frequency components varies throughout a manipulation task (e.g., low-dynamic vs. high-dynamic phases), an adaptive weighting scheme is introduced.
 - **Adaptive Weights:** Inspired by Focal Loss, weights $w_k$ are computed for each frequency band $k$ based on the difference between the corresponding frequency components of two action velocity vectors, using an exponential scaling:
 $w_k = \frac{\exp(\|F(v_r)_k - F(v_s)_k\|^2)}{\sum_{j=0}^{H-1} \exp(\|F(v_r)_j - F(v_s)_j\|^2)}$
 - **Weighted Similarity:** The adaptive frequency component loss then becomes a weighted sum of squared differences of frequency coefficients:
 $\text{Sim}(v_r, v_s) = \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} w_k \cdot \|F(v_r)_k - F(v_s)_k\|^2$
 This adaptively emphasizes informative dynamic variations, strengthening the alignment of temporal structure across timesteps.

**Overall Training Objective:**
The final training objective combines the standard flow matching loss and the frequency-domain consistency loss:
$L_{total} = L_{fm} + L_{freq}$
This unified formulation promotes learning both accurate and temporally consistent flows suitable for one-step inference.

**Model Architecture (Appendix A.1):**
- **Backbone:** A standard 1D CNN-based U-Net architecture is used.
- **Perception Encoder:** For 2D images, ResNet-18 is employed. For 3D point clouds, a lightweight MLP is used.
- **VLA Integration:** FreqPolicy can function as a policy head for existing VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA) by predicting action vector fields from noise-conditioned latent features.

**Inference:**
For action generation, an initial noise vector $a_0$ is sampled, and the learned vector field $v_\theta(t, a_t)$ is deterministically integrated from $t=0$ to $t=1$ using an ODE solver (e.g., Euler or Runge-Kutta), yielding the final action $a_1$ in a single step (NFE=1).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of FreqPolicy was assessed through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, covering various task complexities and observation modalities.

**Simulation Experiments with 2D Inputs (Robomimic Benchmark):**
- FreqPolicy achieved NFE=1 (one-step inference) and demonstrated superior performance compared to prior one-step methods, including Consistency Policy (CP) and IMLE Policy.
- It also surpassed some classical multi-step policies in terms of success rate.
- When compared to a reimplemented Consistency-FM (which uses spatial-domain consistency), FreqPolicy exhibited improved performance, specifically a 6% increase in the Transport task and a 5% increase in the Tool Hang task, supporting the efficacy of the proposed frequency-domain consistency.

**Simulation Experiments with 3D Inputs (Adroit and MetaWorld Benchmarks):**
- Across 53 tasks in Adroit and MetaWorld benchmarks, FreqPolicy consistently yielded improved average success rates. For instance, on the MetaWorld benchmark, the average success rate increased from 74.8% (achieved by SDM) to 78.5%.
- Unlike methods such as SDM, FreqPolicy does not require a pre-trained teacher model.
- Compared to FlowPolicy (which uses direct consistency constraints in the spatial domain), FreqPolicy maintained a 1.3% lead on MetaWorld, with this advantage being more pronounced in the Medium, Hard, and Very Hard splits. This further validated the benefits of frequency consistency supervision for more complex tasks.
- FreqPolicy operated with NFE=1.

**Integration with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models (LIBERO Benchmark):**
- FreqPolicy was integrated into the OpenVLA model (OpenVLA-FreqPolicy) and evaluated on 40 tasks across four LIBERO task suites.
- OpenVLA-FreqPolicy achieved an average success rate of 94.8%, outperforming OpenVLA-DP (68.1%) and OpenVLA-FlowMatching (93.5% at 1 NFE, 93.7% at 10 NFE).
- A notable improvement was observed in the Long task suite, where OpenVLA-FreqPolicy achieved 87.6% success compared to OpenVLA-DP's 11.8%.
- It demonstrated significantly faster inference speed (5 times faster than OpenVLA-FlowMatching with 10 NFE) while achieving a higher success rate (94.8% vs. 93.7%). Its inference speed (6.05 Hz) was comparable to OpenVLA-OFT (6.15 Hz), an improved version of OpenVLA.

**Real-World Experiments (3 Long-Horizon Tasks):**
- FreqPolicy was evaluated on three long-horizon tasks (Fruit Sorting, Toy Organization, Trash Disposal) using Franka and UR robotic arms.
- It achieved success rates comparable to, and in some cases exceeding, multi-step policy models (Diffusion Policy and Flow Matching Policy) while performing single-step inference.
- For example, in the Fruit Sorting task, FreqPolicy achieved a 70% success rate at an inference frequency of 93.5 Hz, surpassing multi-step Diffusion Policy (55% at 19.8 Hz) and Flow Matching Policy (60% at 20.2 Hz).
- These results indicated that FreqPolicy could match or surpass the performance of multi-step policies with reduced computational overhead in physical robot deployments.

**Ablation Study (Robomimic):**
- The study systematically evaluated components contributing to FreqPolicy's performance:
 - A vanilla flow matching baseline (NFE=1) showed initial performance.
 - Adding a general consistency constraint (spatial domain, similar to Consistency-FM) provided a modest improvement.
 - Applying frequency consistency constraints across the full frequency spectrum yielded more substantial gains than restricting to only high or low-frequency bands. This supported the broad utility of frequency-aware consistency.
 - The adaptive frequency component loss further enhanced performance, confirming its benefit in capturing task-dependent temporal variations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FreqPolicy contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a method for efficient and high-quality one-step action generation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Real-time Robotics Challenges:** The work directly tackles the critical problem of high inference latency in generative modeling-based visuomotor policies. By enabling reliable one-step action generation, FreqPolicy enhances the practicality of these powerful models for real-time control in dynamic and long-horizon robotic tasks, where stable and high-frequency action execution is essential. This can accelerate the deployment of advanced AI policies in practical robotic systems.
- **Novel Integration of Temporal Knowledge:** FreqPolicy introduces a novel approach by explicitly incorporating frequency-domain consistency constraints into flow-based visuomotor policies. This is a departure from prior methods that primarily adapted image generation acceleration techniques, which often overlook the time-series nature and temporal coherence requirements of robotic actions. The use of Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) and the emphasis on frequency-domain feature alignment represents a distinct technical contribution.
- **Improved Performance and Efficiency Trade-off:** The empirical results demonstrate that FreqPolicy achieves comparable or superior success rates to multi-step generative policies while operating at significantly higher inference speeds (e.g., 5x faster in VLA settings and up to 93.5 Hz in real-world scenarios). This suggests a more favorable performance-efficiency trade-off than existing one-step generators and some multi-step baselines.
- **Enhanced Generalizability for VLA Models:** The successful integration of FreqPolicy into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (OpenVLA) highlights its potential to improve the efficiency of these complex systems without compromising task performance. This could foster the development of more responsive and capable generalist robots that can execute instructions derived from natural language in real-time.
- **Robustness to Diverse Task Dynamics:** The introduction of an adaptive frequency component loss allows the model to selectively emphasize relevant frequency bands based on the dynamic phases of a manipulation task. This adaptability is critical for handling the varied motion patterns inherent in robotic operations, from smooth reaching to abrupt contact-rich interactions, potentially leading to more robust policies.
- **Future Research Directions:** While primarily validated on flow matching, the core idea of frequency-domain consistency could be extended to diffusion-based visuomotor policies, potentially opening new avenues for acceleration in that domain. The identification of computational demands during training (requiring twice forward passes for two random samples) also points to future work in optimizing training efficiency.

In summary, FreqPolicy contributes to making advanced generative visuomotor policies more viable for real-world robotic applications by providing an efficient, accurate, and temporally aware one-step action generation mechanism, thereby advancing embodied vision-motion planning and VLA models.
