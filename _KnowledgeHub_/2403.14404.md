# Physics-Informed Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Physics-Informed Diffusion Models (PIDMs) introduce a framework that integrates physical laws, such as partial differential equations, directly into the training objective of denoising diffusion models. This approach enables the generation of samples that are not only statistically consistent with data but also rigorously adhere to underlying physical principles, achieving significantly reduced residual errors in benchmarks like Darcy flow and topology optimization.

## Problem

- Traditional data-driven generative models, including denoising diffusion models, often fail to ensure physical consistency in generated samples for scientific problems.
- Generated data from purely data-driven diffusion models frequently violate underlying physical laws, making them unsuitable for precision-critical scientific and engineering applications.
- Existing methods for incorporating physical constraints are often heuristic or post-hoc, failing to fundamentally embed these constraints into the model's core learning process.

## Method

- Develops **PIDMs** (Physics-Informed Diffusion Models) by unifying the data-driven objective of ==denoising diffusion models== with a ==physics-informed loss term==.
- Treats the discrepancy from governing physical equations (==residual function R(x₀)==) as a ==virtual observable==, enforcing R(x₀) = 0 by maximizing its log-likelihood.
- Combines this physics-informed objective with the standard diffusion model's ==data-likelihood objective==, offering two efficient evaluation strategies (==Mean Estimation== and ==Sample Estimation==) during training.

## Results

- PIDMs reduced residual error (R_MAE) by up to **two orders of magnitude** in 2D Darcy flow compared to standard diffusion models, outperforming other physics-guided baselines.
- In topology optimization, PIDMs achieved a median compliance error of **0.06%** on in-distribution test sets, significantly outperforming specialized task-specific frameworks like **TopoDiff-G** and **DOM**.
- The models demonstrated increased robustness to overfitting and preserved generative diversity across both benchmarks, yielding physically consistent and varied solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly embedding physical laws into the diffusion model's training objective through a "virtual observable" framework ensures rigorous adherence to first principles.
> - Enforcing physical constraints during training acts as an effective regularization mechanism, enhancing model robustness against overfitting and improving generalization.
> - The framework is versatile, applicable to various equality and inequality constraints, and maintains the computational efficiency of standard diffusion model inference as the physics-informed loss only affects training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the critical and rapidly expanding intersection of generative artificial intelligence, specifically denoising diffusion models, and scientific machine learning (SciML) or physics-informed machine learning.

Denoising Diffusion Models (DDMs) have recently emerged as a powerful class of generative models, demonstrating unprecedented success in learning intricate data distributions across diverse modalities, including images, videos, graphs, and text. Their ability to generate high-fidelity, diverse samples has led to their increasing adoption in scientific domains. In SciML, DDMs are being leveraged for tasks such as upscaling low-fidelity data to reduce computational costs, designing novel molecules and materials with desired properties, and solving inverse problems by capturing the full distribution of feasible solutions.

However, a fundamental challenge arises when applying purely data-driven generative models like DDMs to scientific problems: the generated samples, while statistically aligned with training data, often fail to rigorously adhere to the underlying physical laws and governing equations (e.g., Partial Differential Equations or PDEs). In scientific and engineering applications, where physical consistency, precision, and adherence to first principles are paramount, samples that do not satisfy these constraints are often unusable or misleading. Current traditional DDM training objectives do not intrinsically enforce such physical constraints.

Prior efforts to address this limitation have explored various avenues, including post-processing generated samples, incorporating guidance mechanisms, or developing task-specific frameworks. While these methods offer some improvements (e.g., ensuring manufacturability or rudimentary physical plausibility), the paper argues that they largely rely on post-hoc corrections or lack a derivation from first principles. Critically, these approaches typically do not embed the physical constraints directly into the model's core learning process, meaning the underlying learned data distribution itself may still contain physically inconsistent samples. The authors specifically point out that a "fundamental framework that rigorously addresses the incorporation of PDE constraints in such generative model settings, along with a robust mechanism to enforce these constraints akin to the well-established physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) has remained elusive."

This paper directly addresses this gap by proposing Physics-Informed Diffusion Models (PIDMs). It aims to unify the proven representation capabilities of DDMs with the rigorous constraint enforcement mechanisms inspired by Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs). By integrating physical laws directly into the generative model's training objective, PIDMs represent a significant step towards creating generative AI systems that are not only capable of synthesizing novel data but also inherently respect the fundamental principles governing the physical world. This positions the work as a crucial advancement in making generative models truly dependable for scientific discovery and engineering design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel and rigorous framework, Physics-Informed Diffusion Models (PIDMs), that seamlessly unifies denoising diffusion models with physics-informed neural networks (PINNs). This framework aims to enable generative models to internalize and strictly adhere to the underlying physical constraints, such as partial differential equations (PDEs), during their training process.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several critical needs and observations within scientific machine learning:

- **Ensuring Physical Consistency and Precision:** In scientific and engineering fields, generated data must not only be statistically plausible but also physically consistent. Traditional data-driven diffusion models, despite their generative power, do not inherently enforce known physical laws. This often leads to samples that, while resembling real data, are fundamentally inconsistent with governing equations, rendering them unsuitable for practical deployment where precision is crucial (e.g., in fluid dynamics, material design, or structural analysis).
- **Bridging the Gap with PINNs:** Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) have demonstrated success in solving forward and inverse problems by embedding physical laws (like PDEs) directly into the neural network's loss function. However, PINNs typically focus on solving specific problem instances and do not operate as generative models capable of learning and sampling from an entire distribution of solutions. The motivation is to port the robust constraint enforcement mechanism of PINNs into the generative capabilities of diffusion models.
- **Limitations of Prior Approaches:** Existing methods for imposing physical constraints on generative models, such as post-processing or external guidance, are often reactive and heuristic. They aim to "correct" samples after generation or steer the sampling process without fundamentally altering the learned distribution's inherent compliance with physics. These approaches may compromise the generative diversity or computational efficiency during inference. The authors are motivated to create a "first-principle-based loss term" that directly embeds constraints, offering a more robust and theoretically grounded solution.
- **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness:** The authors hypothesize that by explicitly informing the model about the underlying physics during training, the model will learn a more robust and meaningful internal representation of the data distribution. This could lead to improved generalization capabilities and increased robustness against overfitting, as the model is compelled to generate samples that are not only statistically accurate but also physically valid.
- **Versatility and Simplicity:** A key motivation is to develop a framework that is simple to implement within existing diffusion model architectures, highly versatile in handling various types of equality and inequality constraints (not just PDEs), and does not negatively impact the efficiency of the inference process.
- **Leveraging Generative Diversity for Inverse Problems:** For inverse problems, diffusion models are powerful because they can capture the full distribution of feasible solutions. By integrating physics, this strength is preserved while ensuring that all generated solutions are physically sound, offering a richer set of compliant designs for engineers and scientists.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Physics-Informed Diffusion Models (PIDMs) revolves around a novel framework that unifies the data-driven objective of denoising diffusion models with a physics-informed loss term, directly embedding physical constraints into the training process.

**Denoising Diffusion Models (DDMs) Background:**
The paper first briefly reviews DDMs, which learn to reverse a fixed forward diffusion process that gradually adds Gaussian noise to data samples (x₀). The reverse process, approximated by a neural network, aims to denoise x_t back to x₀. The standard training objective minimizes the mean squared error between the true clean signal x₀ and the model's estimate ˆx₀.

**Assembly of Governing Equations:**
Physical laws are typically expressed as Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) and boundary conditions. For image-based DDM architectures common in scientific machine learning, the solution field (e.g., pressure, displacement) is represented on a discretized grid (e.g., n × n pixels). The paper defines a residual function, R(x₀), which quantifies the discrepancy between a generated sample x₀ and the governing equations (including boundary conditions). This residual is computed using finite differences or comparable methods operating directly on the discretized solution field.

**Consideration of PDE Constraints – The Physics-Informed Loss:**
1. **Virtual Observables:** The crucial innovation is to treat the requirement R(x₀) = 0 as a "virtual observable" ˆr = 0. This virtual observable is assumed to be sampled from a Gaussian distribution, q_R(ˆr|x₀) = N(ˆr; R(x₀), σ²I). By maximizing the log-likelihood of this virtual observable at ˆr = 0, the model is compelled to generate samples x₀ for which R(x₀) approaches zero, thereby enforcing the physical laws. In the limit of σ² → 0, this strictly enforces the residual equations.
2. **Joint Optimization Objective:** The authors combine this physics-informed objective with the standard data-likelihood objective of diffusion models. The overall goal is to maximize:
 E_{x₀~q(x₀)} [log p_θ(x₀)] + E_{x₀~p_θ(x₀)} [log q_R(ˆr=0|x₀)].
 This joint objective ensures that the model learns to generate samples that are both statistically consistent with the training data and physically compliant.

**Simplification of the Training Objective:**
The joint objective poses a computational challenge because the second term requires sampling from the model's current distribution p_θ(x₀), which is costly for iterative diffusion models. To mitigate this, two strategies are explored for efficiently evaluating the residual R(x*₀) during training:

1. **Mean Estimation (PIDM-ME):** This approach evaluates the residual directly on ˆx₀ = E[x₀|x_t], the model's estimate of the clean signal given the noisy input x_t. While efficient (ˆx₀ is already computed for the data loss), it introduces a "Jensen gap" (R(E[x₀|x_t]) ≠ E[R(x₀|x_t)]). To address this, the variance of the virtual residual likelihood (σ²) is scaled to be larger at noisier timesteps (large t) and tighter (smaller σ²) as t approaches 0, ensuring stricter adherence to constraints for less noisy estimates.
2. **Sample Estimation (PIDM-SE):** To achieve higher consistency, this method attempts to evaluate the residual on an actual sample x₀ ~ p_θ(x₀). This is made feasible by employing Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIMs) with an accelerated, often two-step, sampling process to generate an estimate x*₀ from x_t. This incurs additional computational cost (more forward passes) but offers a better alignment of the two loss terms. Similar to mean estimation, the variance of the residual likelihood is adjusted with time (Σ_t/c), where 'c' is a hyperparameter controlling the relative weight of the physics loss.

The final combined loss function, L_PIDM(θ), incorporates both the standard data loss and the physics-informed loss:
L_PIDM(θ) = E_{t~[1,T],x₀:T~q(x₀:T)} [λ_t ||x₀ - ˆx₀(x_t, t)||² + (1/(2 * Σ_t/c)) ||R(x*₀(x_t, t))||²].
This elegant formulation requires only minor modifications to standard DDM training protocols. Notably, inference remains unaffected by this additional training objective.

**Generalization and Consistency:**
The framework is designed to be versatile, applicable to any differentiable forward surrogate model R(x₀) for equality or inequality constraints, and even auxiliary optimization objectives. The paper also provides theoretical arguments in the appendix, suggesting that, in ideal score-based model settings, this extended objective maintains consistency and continues to recover the true data distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling numerical evidence from two distinct scientific machine learning benchmarks: 2D Darcy flow and structural topology optimization. The results highlight the superior performance of Physics-Informed Diffusion Models (PIDMs) compared to purely data-driven diffusion models and other contemporary physics-aware generative methods.

#### 4.1. Darcy Flow Case Study

- **Dramatic Reduction in Residual Error:** PIDMs demonstrated a remarkable improvement in adhering to the Darcy flow equations. The residual error (R_MAE) of generated samples was reduced by "up to two orders of magnitude" compared to a standard diffusion model. This significant reduction means that PIDM-generated pressure fields are far more physically consistent and accurate.
- **Superiority over Baselines:**
 - **Standard Diffusion Model:** Generated samples had high residual errors, indicating a lack of physical consistency.
 - **Physics-Guided Diffusion (Shu et al., 2023):** This method, which uses residual information as guidance, showed no significant reduction in residual error, suggesting that mere gradient inclusion is insufficient without direct enforcement.
 - **CoCoGen (Jacobsen et al., 2024):** This approach, employing first-order residual corrections during inference, achieved only moderate improvements, failing to bring the residual errors to a level considered physically consistent.
- **Mean vs. Sample Estimation (Darcy Flow):** PIDM with mean estimation (PIDM-ME) slightly outperformed sample estimation (PIDM-SE) in terms of residual error. PIDM-ME took approximately 23% longer to train than the standard model, while PIDM-SE took about 69% longer due to additional forward passes for sampling.
- **Natural Regularization:** Intriguingly, both PIDM-ME and PIDM-SE showed increased robustness to overfitting compared to the vanilla diffusion model. The test data loss trajectories for PIDM, particularly PIDM-SE, closely mirrored or even improved upon the vanilla model. This supports the hypothesis that enforcing physical constraints acts as an effective regularization mechanism, leading to a more robust and generalized internal representation of the data distribution.
- **Preservation of Generative Diversity:** The generated samples (permeability and pressure fields) from PIDMs maintained generative diversity, successfully capturing various physically consistent solutions rather than collapsing to a single, deterministic outcome, as confirmed by visual inspection and the test data loss.

#### 4.2. Topology Optimization Case Study

- **Outperformance in Optimization Objectives:** PIDM significantly "outperforms task-specific frameworks" such as TopoDiff-G (Mazé & Ahmed, 2023) and DOM (Giannone et al., 2023) in terms of compliance minimization (a measure of mechanical stiffness). PIDM achieved a median compliance error of 0.06% and 0.56% on in- and out-of-distribution test sets, respectively, which is considerably better than the 0.74-3.47% range reported by the specialized models. This is particularly notable as PIDM achieves this without requiring auxiliary datasets or surrogate models, simplifying the training process.
- **Reduced Residuals and Improved Physical Adherence:** The PIDM reduced the residual error (R_MAE) by 33% and 35% compared to the standard diffusion model on the in- and out-of-distribution sets, respectively. This signifies closer adherence to mechanical equilibrium equations.
- **Better Volume Fraction Control:** While the standard diffusion model performed well in compliance, it often had an increased volume fraction error. PIDM demonstrated only a slight increase in volume fraction error, making its compliance results more genuinely comparable and superior given the constraint.
- **Comprehensive Output:** PIDM not only generates optimized structural designs (density fields) but also the corresponding displacement fields, which are crucial for detailed mechanical analysis (e.g., stress distribution).
- **Sample Estimation Preferred (Topology Optimization):** For this task, PIDM with sample estimation slightly outperformed mean estimation, indicating the benefit of evaluating residuals on actual samples for certain complex systems.

Overall, the main findings robustly demonstrate that integrating a physics-informed loss term directly into the training of diffusion models leads to significantly enhanced physical consistency, often improved generative performance, and increased robustness to overfitting, all while preserving the models' ability to generate diverse solutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Physics-Informed Diffusion Models (PIDMs) represents a significant advancement in scientific machine learning, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts across various scientific and engineering disciplines.

#### 5.1. A Foundational Framework for Physics-Informed Generative AI

PIDMs establish a rigorous and fundamental framework for unifying data-driven generative modeling with first-principle physics. By moving beyond heuristic corrections or post-processing, this work fundamentally changes how generative models can be trained to produce physically consistent outputs. This paradigm shift will be crucial for developing truly reliable and trustworthy AI systems for scientific discovery and engineering design.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Reliability and Deployability in Scientific Applications

The core significance of PIDMs lies in their ability to generate samples that are not only statistically plausible but also rigorously adhere to underlying physical laws (e.g., PDEs). This dramatically increases the reliability, precision, and trustworthiness of generated data, making diffusion models suitable for critical applications where physical consistency is non-negotiable. This includes:
 - **Material Design:** Generating novel materials with specific, physically consistent properties and microstructures.
 - **Fluid Dynamics:** Reconstructing or generating fluid flow fields that obey Navier-Stokes equations.
 - **Structural Engineering:** Designing optimized structures that satisfy mechanical equilibrium and performance criteria.
 - **Inverse Problems:** Providing a distribution of physically valid solutions for complex inverse design challenges, allowing for robust decision-making.

#### 5.3. Improved Generalization and Model Robustness

The observed regularization effect, where PIDMs are less prone to overfitting and can even achieve better data likelihoods (or at least comparable) than purely data-driven models, is highly significant. This suggests that embedding physical knowledge forces the model to learn a more robust, interpretable, and generalizable internal representation of the data distribution, which inherently respects the underlying physical mechanisms. This can lead to models that perform better on unseen data and conditions.

#### 5.4. Versatility and Broad Applicability

The framework's versatility to incorporate various equality and inequality constraints, as well as auxiliary optimization objectives, extends its utility beyond PDEs in specific physics domains. This means PIDMs could be applied to a wide array of constrained generative problems in fields like chemistry, biology (e.g., molecular design with binding affinity constraints), medicine (e.g., generating medical images with anatomical consistency), and environmental science.

#### 5.5. Efficient Integration and Inference

The method's simplicity of implementation, requiring only minor modifications to existing diffusion model training protocols, lowers the barrier to adoption. Crucially, it leaves the inference process unaffected, preserving the computational efficiency and speed of diffusion model sampling, which is a major advantage over methods that rely on costly runtime corrections or auxiliary models.

#### 5.6. Stimulating Future Research

The authors explicitly hope this work will "stimulate others to extend their generative model training objective." They outline several promising avenues for future research, including:
 - Developing more sophisticated virtual likelihood variance schedulers.
 - Exploring architectures that allow for consistent residual evaluation on non-fixed regular grids (e.g., graph neural networks, implicit neural representations).
 - Investigating the use of automatic differentiation for gradient calculations in PDE evaluation, potentially offering higher precision at increased computational cost.

In conclusion, PIDMs offer a powerful and principled approach to infuse generative AI with the foundational laws of physics. By enabling diffusion models to "internalize" physical constraints during training, this research paves the way for more reliable, accurate, and scientifically impactful applications of generative models across the scientific and engineering landscape.
