# Physics-Informed Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Physics-Informed Diffusion Models (PIDMs) introduce a framework that integrates physical laws, such as partial differential equations, directly into the training objective of denoising diffusion models. This approach enables the generation of samples that are not only statistically consistent with data but also rigorously adhere to underlying physical principles, achieving significantly reduced residual errors in benchmarks like Darcy flow and topology optimization.

## Problem

- Traditional data-driven generative models, including denoising diffusion models, often fail to ensure physical consistency in generated samples for scientific problems.
- Generated data from purely data-driven diffusion models frequently violate underlying physical laws, making them unsuitable for precision-critical scientific and engineering applications.
- Existing methods for incorporating physical constraints are often heuristic or post-hoc, failing to fundamentally embed these constraints into the model's core learning process.

## Method

- Develops **PIDMs** (Physics-Informed Diffusion Models) by unifying the data-driven objective of ==denoising diffusion models== with a ==physics-informed loss term==.
- Treats the discrepancy from governing physical equations (==residual function R(x₀)==) as a ==virtual observable==, enforcing R(x₀) = 0 by maximizing its log-likelihood.
- Combines this physics-informed objective with the standard diffusion model's ==data-likelihood objective==, offering two efficient evaluation strategies (==Mean Estimation== and ==Sample Estimation==) during training.

## Results

- PIDMs reduced residual error (R_MAE) by up to **two orders of magnitude** in 2D Darcy flow compared to standard diffusion models, outperforming other physics-guided baselines.
- In topology optimization, PIDMs achieved a median compliance error of **0.06%** on in-distribution test sets, significantly outperforming specialized task-specific frameworks like **TopoDiff-G** and **DOM**.
- The models demonstrated increased robustness to overfitting and preserved generative diversity across both benchmarks, yielding physically consistent and varied solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly embedding physical laws into the diffusion model's training objective through a "virtual observable" framework ensures rigorous adherence to first principles.
> - Enforcing physical constraints during training acts as an effective regularization mechanism, enhancing model robustness against overfitting and improving generalization.
> - The framework is versatile, applicable to various equality and inequality constraints, and maintains the computational efficiency of standard diffusion model inference as the physics-informed loss only affects training.
