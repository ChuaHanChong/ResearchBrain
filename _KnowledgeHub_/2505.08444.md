# Extracting Visual Plans from Unlabeled Videos via Symbolic Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Vis2Plan, a neuro-symbolic framework, learns to plan complex, multi-stage robot manipulation tasks from unlabeled video demonstrations. It automatically extracts symbolic representations and generates physically consistent visual subgoals, achieving superior task success rates and significantly faster planning speeds compared to existing visual planning methods.

## Problem

- Existing visual planners for long-horizon robot tasks frequently generate physically unrealistic or unreachable intermediate visual subgoals, leading to execution failures (model hallucination).
- Many current visual planning methods, especially those using video generative models, are computationally intensive and too slow for real-time robotic applications.
- Advanced visual planning techniques often depend on extensive human effort to label unstructured demonstration datasets with natural language descriptions or reward annotations, limiting scalability.

## Method

- **Vis2Plan** automatically extracts ==symbolic representations== and ==stable states== from raw, unlabeled play data using vision foundation models and temporal similarity analysis.
- It constructs a high-level ==symbolic transition graph== from extracted symbolic states and employs an ==A* search== for abstract planning toward user-specified goals.
- A symbolic-guided visual planning stage integrates a ==reachability estimator== and a ==beam search== to generate physically feasible visual subgoal sequences, which are then executed by a low-level ==goal-conditioned visuomotor policy==.

## Results

- Vis2Plan achieved an average success rate of **0.71** in real-world robot experiments, outperforming baseline methods by **53%** in aggregate success rate for multi-stage manipulation tasks.
- The framework demonstrated robust performance in simulation, with up to **0.82** success on challenging long-horizon tasks, consistently surpassing end-to-end and other hierarchical planners.
- Visual plan generation with Vis2Plan was significantly faster, averaging **0.03** to **0.05 seconds**, which is up to **35 times** quicker than diffusion video generation models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating symbolic planning and discrete state representations into hierarchical visual planning effectively mitigates model hallucination, ensuring physically consistent and executable visual subgoals.
> - Unsupervised extraction of symbolic states and transitions from unlabeled video data, using off-the-shelf vision foundation models, enables learning complex manipulation skills without explicit human annotation.
> - A learnable reachability estimator is crucial for filtering and validating generated visual subgoal sequences, ensuring they are motorically achievable by a low-level robot controller.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks by learning from uncurated and unlabeled video demonstrations, often referred to as "play data." This area of robot learning is significant because real-world deployment often involves dynamic environments and diverse tasks, making manual labeling of extensive datasets impractical.

Existing approaches to visual planning for long-horizon tasks frequently leverage advancements in video generative models and vision-language models (VLMs). These models typically operate by synthesizing intermediate visual subgoals, often guided by language descriptions or explicit visual targets. However, the paper identifies several limitations inherent in these methods:
- **Model Hallucination:** Planning directly in pixel or dense latent space can lead to the generation of physically unreachable or unrealistic visual subgoals, hindering the low-level controller's ability to execute the plan. This issue is particularly pronounced in long-horizon scenarios where models struggle with reliable reasoning.
- **Computational Cost:** Generating visual plans using complex video generative models is computationally intensive and slow, often taking seconds to produce a plan. This makes them less suitable for real-time robotic applications.
- **Data Labeling Requirements:** Many advanced VLM-based methods still necessitate significant human effort to label unstructured play datasets with natural language descriptions, despite some progress in reducing this workload. Even with powerful VLMs, reliably generating correct task descriptions remains a challenge.

The broader landscape of robot learning from demonstrations includes methods for learning versatile skills through semantic grounding and hierarchical imitation learning, and approaches for discovering abstractions from interactions or demonstrations. While some methods learn predicates or planning operators, they often assume given predicates or low-level skill generators, limiting their applicability to unlabeled multimodal data. Recent work also explores the use of large language models (LLMs) and VLMs for high-level planning, but they face challenges in refining plans into low-level actions, producing reliable image descriptions, and maintaining real-time performance due to their scale.

Vis2Plan positions itself as a neuro-symbolic approach designed to overcome these limitations by integrating symbolic planning into hierarchical visual planning. It aims to provide a framework that is efficient, explainable, and robust by:
1. Automatically extracting symbolic representations from raw, unlabeled play data using vision foundation models (VFMs).
2. Building a high-level symbolic transition graph that allows for multi-goal, multi-stage planning.
3. Generating physically consistent intermediate sub-goal images grounded by symbolic representations, thereby mitigating hallucination.
4. Achieving faster visual plan generation compared to diffusion video generation models.
5. Eliminating the need for human-provided task descriptions or reward annotations.

By doing so, Vis2Plan seeks to bridge the gap between high-level abstract planning and low-level continuous control, offering a method for learning long-horizon skills from uncurated data that is both interpretable and performant in real-world robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient, explainable, and robust framework for visual planning that enables robots to learn versatile, long-horizon manipulation skills from uncurated and unlabeled play data. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific challenges identified in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Visual Planners:**
 - **Model Hallucination:** Current video generative and vision-language models often plan directly in pixel or dense latent spaces, leading to "black-box" forecasting. This can result in the generation of visually plausible but physically unreachable or unrealistic intermediate subgoals, making it difficult for low-level controllers to execute tasks reliably, especially over long horizons. A core motivation is to mitigate these hallucinations by ensuring the physical consistency and photo-realism of generated subgoals.
 - **High Computational Cost:** Generative models typically require significant computational resources and time (seconds) to synthesize visual plans. This slowness renders them impractical for real-time robot operations. The objective is to significantly reduce the inference time for visual plan generation, making it feasible for real-world applications.
 - **Dependency on Labeled Data:** Many advanced visual planning methods demand extensive human effort to label unstructured play datasets with natural language descriptions or reward annotations. This human workload limits scalability. A key motivation is to develop a method that trains solely on raw, unlabeled play data, minimizing human intervention.

2. **Enabling Long-Horizon and Multi-Stage Planning from Unlabeled Data:**
 - Play datasets, composed of long trajectories demonstrating various sub-skills without explicit task labels, contain rich information for complex tasks. However, extracting useful long-horizon visual plans from such data is challenging. The framework aims to:
 - **Discover and Stitch Sub-skills:** Automatically identify and combine fundamental manipulation sub-skills present within unlabeled trajectories to construct complex plans.
 - **Generate Physically Reachable Subgoals:** Ensure that the sequence of intermediate visual subgoals generated for multi-stage tasks is not only visually coherent but also physically achievable by a low-level robot controller.

3. **Enhancing Explainability and Interpretability:**
 - "Black-box" planning methods offer limited insight into their decision-making processes, making debugging and verification difficult. A critical motivation is to introduce interpretability into the planning process. By leveraging symbolic representations, the framework aims to provide "fully inspectable reasoning steps," allowing human operators to understand why a particular plan was chosen.

4. **Decoupling High-Level Planning and Low-Level Control:**
 - The research is motivated by the potential benefits of a hierarchical approach, where high-level planning focuses on abstract task decomposition and subgoal generation, while low-level control handles motor execution. This decoupling can reduce computational costs for action annotation and offer better generalization.

In summary, the key objectives are to develop a hierarchical visual planning system that is efficient, robust, explainable, and can learn complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks purely from unlabeled robot play data, thereby overcoming the critical limitations of current end-to-end or generative model-based approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Vis2Plan is presented as a symbolic-guided visual planning framework designed for multi-stage robotic manipulation from unlabeled play data. The methodology is structured into three main stages: Coarse Symbolic Skill Extraction, Planning Modules Learning, and Symbolic-guided Visual Planning, which is the inference stage.

**Problem Setting:** The framework operates on an unlabeled play dataset, D = {(o, a)}, consisting of observation-action sequences. This data is collected from a teleoperator exploring an environment, without explicit task instructions or reward signals. The goal is to extract a hierarchical planner capable of handling multi-stage, multi-goal tasks, discovering symbolic abstractions of sub-skills, and controllably generating desired plans with minimal human annotation.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Coarse Symbolic Skill Extraction

This stage focuses on reducing the planning horizon and discretizing the high-dimensional observation space by extracting symbolic representations of task states.

- **Stable State Identification:**
 - **Video Preprocessing:** Raw demonstration videos are processed to detect and track task-relevant objects. This involves using an open-vocabulary Vision-Language Model (VLM), Qwen, for initial object detection in the first frame, followed by object tracking using SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2) across subsequent frames. This generates object-centric video sequences, where each video focuses on a single tracked object.
 - **Feature Extraction:** A vision foundation model (VFM), specifically SigLIP2, is used to extract high-dimensional features from each object-centric frame, resulting in K object-centric feature sequences for K task-relevant objects.
 - **Temporal Similarity and Segmentation:** The temporal similarity between consecutive object-centric frames is calculated using the cosine similarity of their VFM features. These similarities are summed across all objects to create a composite similarity score over time. Gaussian smoothing is applied, and non-maximum suppression is used to identify peaks in this score. These peaks correspond to "stable states" where object statuses are static or similar, effectively segmenting long demonstrations into sub-skills (transitions between two stable states).

- **Symbolic Transition Labeling:**
 - **Unsupervised State Discovery:** Agglomerative Clustering, applied to the VFM features of identified stable states with cosine similarity, is used to discover representative visual states for each object in an unsupervised manner. The Silhouette score determines the optimal number of clusters.
 - **Symbolic Labeling:** KNN classifiers are trained to label image frames with these discovered representative states. For each sub-skill identified, its precondition (start) and effect (end) frames are labeled, resulting in a set of symbolic transitions (z_before, z_after). These transitions are then used to construct a directed symbolic graph for high-level planning.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Planning Modules Learning

This stage develops modules that enable closed-loop control and bridge the discrete symbolic plan with continuous visual observations.

- **Next-Symbolic-State Predictor (C_theta):**
 - **Purpose:** To map real-time image observations to symbolic states and predict possible next symbolic subgoals.
 - **Training:** A classifier-like predictor C_theta is trained to predict the z_after symbol of a transition from any intermediate image O_I_i within that transition, using a cross-entropy loss. This allows the system to determine the next relevant symbolic state from a current visual observation.

- **Reachability Estimator (R_psi):**
 - **Purpose:** To ensure that generated visual subgoals are physically feasible and reachable by the low-level policy.
 - **Mechanism:** This module is trained using a modified Contrastive Reinforcement Learning (CRL) approach, specifically adapting the MC-InfoNCE loss for a state-only case. The objective is to estimate the discounted state occupancy measure.
 - **Function:** After training, R_psi(o_i, o_j) quantifies the physical reachability from observation o_i to o_j. Higher values indicate better reachability based on the offline dataset. A reachability threshold δ is established based on observed transitions.

- **Low-Level Goal-Conditioned Visuomotor Policy (pi_g):**
 - **Purpose:** To execute short-horizon actions to move the robot towards a specified visual subgoal.
 - **Training:** Trained using behavior cloning with a hindsight relabeling strategy. Given an unlabeled video demonstration, the goal image (O_t+h) is sampled from a future frame (H steps later) in the same trajectory.
 - **Models:** AdaFlow is used for simulation tasks, and Mimicplay policy is used for real robot tasks as the low-level controller. This policy takes the current observation (O_t) and the next image goal (O_t+h) as input and outputs an h-horizon action chunk.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Symbolic-guided Visual Planning (Inference)

This stage integrates the learned modules to perform closed-loop hierarchical planning and control.

- **Symbolic Planning:**
 - Given the current image observation (O_t), the Next-Symbolic-State Predictor (C_theta) identifies a set of candidate next symbolic states (Z_next).
 - An A* search algorithm is then performed on the symbolic graph (constructed in Stage 1) for each candidate in Z_next to find the shortest symbolic path (τ_z) from the current state to the user-specified task symbolic goal (z_g).

- **Visual Subgoal Generation:**
 - For each symbolic subgoal (z_i) in the planned symbolic path (τ_z), corresponding visual observations (O_i) are sampled from the relabeled dataset.
 - To construct a sequence of physically feasible visual subgoals (τ_O), a beam search is employed. This search aims to maximize the sum of reachability scores (R_psi) between adjacent visual subgoals, ensuring that each transition satisfies the reachability threshold δ. An optimization is implemented where pre-computed reachability features (ϕ(s) and ψ(s)) are sampled directly to reduce computational overhead.

- **Goal-Conditioned Action Chunk Generation:**
 - The low-level policy (π_g) receives the current visual observation (O_t) and the next visual subgoal (O_g) from the generated visual plan (τ_O).
 - It then generates an h-horizon action chunk (u), which the robot executes in the environment.
 - The entire process operates in a closed-loop manner, iteratively updating the current observation and planning towards the next subgoal until the task goal is achieved.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Vis2Plan was conducted in both simulation (LIBERO Mimicplay kitchen environment) and real-world robotic manipulation scenarios, focusing on single-stage (short-horizon) and multi-stage (long-horizon) tasks. The primary metric for evaluation was the task success rate.

#### 4.1. Performance in Simulation (LIBERO Mimicplay)

- **End-to-End Goal-Conditioned Baselines:**
 - GC-RNN and GC-Transformer generally exhibited limited success, primarily on short-horizon tasks (e.g., Task-1: 0.96 for GC-RNN). They performed poorly on multi-stage tasks.
 - GC-Diffusion showed modest performance on some tasks (e.g., 0.81 on Task-2 short-horizon, up to 0.28 on a long-horizon task), indicating some capacity for visual goal conditioning.
 - GC-ACT also struggled, similar to GC-RNN and GC-Transformer.

- **Hierarchical Planners:**
 - GSR (Graph-Search Retrieval) and UVD-graph demonstrated reasonable performance at the subgoal level but experienced a significant drop in success rates for longer, multi-stage tasks due to inaccuracies in high-level subgoal planning.
 - AVDC (video generative diffusion model) showed mixed results, outperforming some baselines but still achieving success rates below Vis2Plan, particularly in multi-goal scenarios.
 - Vis2Plan consistently outperformed all baselines. It achieved high success rates on most single-goal tasks (e.g., 0.94 on Task-2 and Task-3). Crucially, Vis2Plan excelled in challenging long-horizon scenarios, yielding success rates of 0.59 (average over tasks 1-5, Table 1 shows 0.14, 0.24, 0.72, 0.56, 0.82 respectively for different long-horizon tasks, with 0.82 being highest) and 0.82 on Task-5, significantly surpassing other hierarchical and end-to-end methods. The improvement over GC-Diffusion when integrated with hierarchical planners like AVDC and Vis2Plan indicated that meaningful visual subgoal plans enhance task success.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Experiments

- **Context:** These experiments involved a Boston Dynamics Spot robot arm performing kitchen manipulation tasks, using 15 teleoperated human play trajectories for training. Given the challenges faced by GC-Diffusion in real-world settings (likely due to limited training data), Mimicplay policy was adopted as the low-level controller for hierarchical methods, employing a sub-trajectory retrieval strategy.
- **Performance:**
 - GC-Diffuser (end-to-end) failed to complete any tasks in the real world.
 - Vis2Plan demonstrated robust performance, matching or exceeding the best-performing baselines in five out of six tasks. It achieved a perfect success rate (1.0) on Task-1 (short-horizon: Put pot on stove) and Task-6 (long-horizon: Put pot in sink, green onion in cabinet), and outperformed all baselines on Task-5 (long-horizon: Put pot on stove, green onion in cabinet) with a success rate of 0.63.
 - While Vis2Plan's performance on Task-3 (Open microwave) was slightly lower than some baselines (0.45 vs. 0.55 for GSR and UVD-graph), its overall average success rate of 0.71 was significantly higher than all other methods (GSR: 0.47, UVD-graph: 0.30, AVDC: 0.18, GC-Diffusion: 0.0).

#### 4.3. Visual Plan Quality Analysis

- An analysis of high-level plan quality (the ratio of generating "meaningful" visual plans, as determined by the reachability estimator R_psi) revealed specific failure modes for baselines:
 - **GSR:** Achieved perfect scores for single-task plans but struggled with multi-task plans (0.57 success rate), often connecting visually similar but contextually incorrect states.
 - **AVDC:** Suffered from generative inconsistencies and hallucinations (e.g., inventing non-existent objects, missing items), leading to a significant drop in success for multi-task scenarios (0.16 success rate).
 - **UVD-graph:** Showed reasonable success (0.75 single-task, 0.86 multi-task) but frequently generated overly complicated subgoals, resulting in long image sequences that were difficult for low-level policies to follow.
 - **Vis2Plan:** Consistently achieved perfect scores (1.0) across all simulation and real-world tasks (both single and multi-task) for high-level plan quality. This indicates its robustness in producing precise, feasible, and coherent visual plans, which is a critical factor for reliable execution.

#### 4.4. Visual Planning Inference Speed

- Vis2Plan demonstrated superior efficiency in visual plan generation. It generated visual plans in an average of 0.03 seconds for short-horizon simulation tasks and 0.05 seconds for long-horizon real-world tasks.
- This performance is significantly faster than AVDC (1.42 seconds consistently across tasks) and GSR for long-horizon tasks (15.12 seconds in simulation, 10.13 seconds in real-world), and competitive with UVD-graph which is the fastest (1.1x10^-4 s for short real-world) but suffers from plan quality issues. This speed enables near real-time planning.

In summary, the results indicate that Vis2Plan generates physically consistent image goals, offers fully inspectable reasoning steps, and achieves significantly higher aggregate success rates (53% higher in real robot settings) compared to strong diffusion video generation-based visual planners, while generating visual plans substantially faster (35x faster).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Vis2Plan framework presents a notable contribution to the field of robot learning and manipulation, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Advancement in Learning from Unlabeled Data:** The work demonstrates a robust method for learning complex, long-horizon manipulation skills directly from raw, unlabeled human play data. This capability is significant as it reduces the dependency on labor-intensive human annotations (task descriptions, reward signals), making data collection and skill acquisition more scalable and accessible for real-world robotic systems.

2. **Mitigation of Model Hallucination:** By grounding visual subgoals in a discrete symbolic representation and filtering through a learned reachability estimator, Vis2Plan effectively addresses a major challenge in generative visual planning: the generation of physically infeasible or unrealistic subgoals. This ensures that the generated visual plans are not only photo-realistic but also physically achievable by the robot's low-level controller, leading to higher task success rates.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Real-Time Capability:** The framework significantly reduces the computational cost of visual plan generation compared to video generative models, achieving near real-time inference speeds. This efficiency is crucial for the deployment of robot learning solutions in dynamic real-world environments where rapid decision-making and planning are required.

4. **Improved Explainability and Interpretability:** The use of symbolic guidance provides an inherent level of explainability. The symbolic transition graph and the resulting symbolic plans offer inspectable reasoning steps, allowing developers and users to understand the high-level logic behind the robot's actions. This transparency can facilitate debugging, verification, and building trust in autonomous systems.

5. **Robustness in Complex, Multi-Stage Tasks:** The experimental results in both simulation and real-world settings demonstrate Vis2Plan's ability to consistently outperform existing end-to-end and hierarchical baselines, particularly in challenging multi-stage, long-horizon tasks. This robustness suggests a practical path towards deploying intelligent robots in complex environments like kitchens, where tasks involve sequential manipulations of multiple objects.

6. **Integration of Foundation Models and Classical AI:** Vis2Plan effectively combines the representational power of modern vision foundation models (e.g., Qwen, SAM2, SigLIP2) for perception and symbol extraction with the logical reasoning capabilities of classical AI symbolic planning (A* search). This integration leverages the strengths of both paradigms to address the limitations of each when applied in isolation.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Adoption of Robot Learning:** By simplifying the data requirements and improving reliability, Vis2Plan could accelerate the adoption of learned robot manipulation skills in various industries, from manufacturing to service robotics.
- **Development of More Autonomous Systems:** The framework contributes towards building more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems that can infer complex task structures and plan their actions without explicit programming for every scenario.
- **Foundation for Neuro-Symbolic AI in Robotics:** The work contributes to the growing field of neuro-symbolic AI in robotics, demonstrating how symbolic reasoning can provide structure and interpretability to deep learning models, particularly for sequential decision-making.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as noted by authors):**
- The current system requires humans to specify task-related object names in play data.
- The extracted symbolic information lacks the semantic richness of natural language, limiting generalization to out-of-domain settings. Future work could integrate with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to combine Vis2Plan's generalization ability with VLMs' semantic richness, while mitigating VLM hallucination.
- The performance bottleneck for robot control lies with the data-driven low-level goal-conditioned policy, suggesting that improving this component through more training data or task-specific controllers could further enhance performance.

Despite these limitations, Vis2Plan offers a robust, efficient, and interpretable framework, paving the way for scalable and inspectable robot learning from unlabeled video demonstrations.
