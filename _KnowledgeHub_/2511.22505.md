# RealD$^2$iff: Bridging Real-World Gap in Robot Manipulation via Depth Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> RealD²iff inverts diffusion denoising into a clean-to-noisy paradigm: a hierarchical coarse-to-fine depth diffusion model synthesizes real-sensor-like noise onto clean simulated depth. Frequency-Guided Supervision learns global structural distortion while Discrepancy-Guided Optimization refines local perturbations. The enhanced simulation data trains depth-aware manipulation policies that transfer zero-shot to a real Franka arm, restoring near-simulation success rates across four policies and four tasks.

## Problem

- Simulated depth is artifact-free, so policies trained on it break on real sensors whose structured noise and edge artifacts corrupt 3D perception for manipulation.
- Hand-crafted noise augmentation such as Gaussian or Perlin injection cannot reproduce the full complexity of real depth-sensor patterns and stays confined to simulation.
- The opposite fix, denoising real data at deployment, trains policies in an idealized clean environment, hurting robustness to unseen noise and demanding heavy point-cloud preprocessing.

## Method

- **RealD²iff**'s ==clean-to-noisy paradigm==: a ==hierarchical diffusion model== splits the intractable sim-to-real depth mapping into coarse global structural noise plus fine local perturbation, fine-tuning ==Stable Diffusion 2.1==.
- **Frequency-Guided Supervision** high-pass-filters ground-truth residuals so the coarse stage learns mid/low-frequency deformation, and a pooled 8x8 discrepancy map flags unreliable regions.
- **Discrepancy-Guided Optimization** partitions pixels at threshold 0.3 and reweights the fine-stage loss (0.5 satisfactory, 1.5 discrepant); a six-stage pipeline feeds enhanced depth to ==RGB-D policies==.

## Results

- On real Stack-Cube, depth realism and control improve to FID **76.7**, Chamfer L2 **3.62**, success rate **0.92**, versus DepthAnything **100.4**/**4.75**/**0.81** and sim-only **401.3**/**28.26**/**0.25**.
- Zero-shot transfer lifts Diffusion Policy Pick Cube from **8/30** to **30/30** and Can-to-Plate from **1/30** to **19/30**; all four policies approach simulation-level success.
- Ablations show end-to-end training reaches only **53.9** mIoU against **63.7** with FGS plus DGO, while in-domain living-room mIoU hits **66.5** versus ControlNet's **61.7**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative models can be run backwards: learning to synthesize sensor corruption is a better domain bridge than learning to remove it.
> - Decomposing a hard translation into coarse structure then local detail eases optimization and avoids premature convergence, a reusable recipe for noisy-target generation.
> - Depth-realism metrics track downstream control success, but the approach stays untested on transparent, reflective, and articulated objects where depth sensors fail worst.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Policy learning has demonstrated progress in robotic manipulation, enabling robots to acquire skills from demonstrations. However, a primary constraint in deploying these policies in the real world is the "sim2real gap," which refers to discrepancies between simulated and real environments. While simulation offers a scalable method for generating diverse demonstration data, the visual sim2real gap, particularly concerning depth observations, remains a significant challenge for manipulation tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning.

Existing approaches to address the sim2real gap typically fall into several categories:
- **Data Collection Paradigms**: Human video demonstrations offer realism but lack temporally aligned robot actions. Teleoperation provides aligned perception-action pairs but is resource-intensive and not scalable for large datasets.
- **Physics Gap Solutions**: Domain randomization and domain adaptation are common strategies for mitigating discrepancies in dynamics and friction, particularly effective for locomotion tasks.
- **Visual Gap Solutions**: For manipulation, high-fidelity simulation rendering is critical. Some methods augment simulation data with noise or apply post-processing to camera outputs. However, these often struggle to capture the full complexity of real-world sensor noise patterns. Conversely, approaches that denoise real-world data might place policies in idealized training environments, potentially reducing robustness to real-world uncertainties.
- **3D Representations**: Recent works incorporate 3D representations like point clouds or depth data to improve policy generalization, but these still contend with depth sensor noise and often require extensive preprocessing.
- **Generative Models**: Diffusion models have gained attention for their stable training processes and ability to generate high-quality images with fine detail preservation. Depth diffusion models have primarily focused on tasks like depth estimation, super-resolution, or refinement, aiming to produce clean, deterministic outputs.

This research distinguishes itself by inverting the conventional approach of diffusion models. Instead of denoising real-world data or generating clean representations, it proposes a "clean-to-noisy" paradigm. The objective is to synthesize complex, real-world-like noise directly onto clean simulated depth data. This approach specifically targets the "depth gap," which the authors note has received limited attention despite its critical impact on 3D perception in manipulation. By generating realistic noisy depth in simulation, the work aims to bridge this gap, allowing policies trained in simulation to achieve zero-shot transfer to the real world without further fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The fundamental constraint motivating this work is the visual sim2real gap in robot manipulation, specifically concerning depth observations. Real-world depth sensors introduce complex, structured noise patterns and artifacts that are not present in clean simulated depth data. This discrepancy directly impacts the fidelity of 3D perception, which is crucial for robots performing precise manipulation tasks, such as grasping or pouring.

The key motivations and objectives of this research are:

1. **Addressing the Depth Sim2Real Gap**: The primary motivation is to overcome the challenge posed by the visual sim2real gap in depth sensing. While simulators can efficiently generate large-scale demonstration data, the unrealistic cleanliness of simulated depth prevents policies trained in such environments from directly transferring to the noisy conditions of the real world. This gap necessitates robust methods to enable effective deployment of simulated policies.

2. **Developing a Clean-to-Noisy Depth Synthesis Paradigm**: The authors aim to invert the conventional perspective of diffusion models, which typically focus on denoising. Instead, they propose a "clean-to-noisy" paradigm where a model learns to synthesize realistic noise patterns directly from clean simulated depth. This approach enables simulation to produce enhanced depth observations that inherently possess real-world sensor characteristics.

3. **Designing a Hierarchical Coarse-to-Fine Diffusion Framework (RealD²iff)**: A detailed objective is to construct RealD²iff, a novel diffusion framework that can systematically decompose and generate complex depth noise. This involves separating noise into global structural distortions (e.g., bias, scale drift) and fine-grained local perturbations (e.g., jitter, edge artifacts). The hierarchical design, coupled with specialized supervision strategies like Frequency-Guided Supervision (FGS) and Discrepancy-Guided Optimization (DGO), is intended to facilitate progressive learning of these distinct noise components.

4. **Enabling Zero-Shot Sim2Real Robot Manipulation**: A practical objective is to utilize the generated real-world-like depth data to train robotic policies that can transfer directly to physical robots without requiring additional fine-tuning or real-world data collection. This aims to substantially improve real-world policy performance and reduce the high costs associated with traditional sim2real adaptation.

5. **Facilitating Realistic Data Generation**: The work also seeks to provide a method for generating high-fidelity clean–noisy paired datasets synthetically. This eliminates the need for manual, laborious sensor data collection in the real world specifically for noise modeling, offering a scalable solution for data augmentation.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a data-driven solution to the visual sim2real gap, making simulation-driven robot learning more practical and effective for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on RealD²iff, a hierarchical coarse-to-fine diffusion framework designed to synthesize real-world-like depth noise from clean simulated depth data. This framework is integrated into a six-stage pipeline for robotic manipulation.

#### 3.1. RealD²iff: Hierarchical Diffusion Framework

The core idea is to learn a mapping F: D_sim → D_real by decomposing it into two sub-mappings, H1: D_sim → ˆD_coarse and H2: D_sim → ˆD_fine. RealD²iff employs a two-stage diffusion process to progressively add noise, moving from global structural deviations to fine local perturbations.

- **Coarse Stage: Global Structural Initialization**:
 - **Objective**: To generate global structural noise (∆_c) that accounts for mid- and low-frequency components such as depth bias, scale drift, and surface deformation.
 - **Frequency-Guided Supervision (FGS)**: A coarse diffusion model (D_c) is trained to approximate the distribution of structural noise (¯∆*_c = D_real - D_sim after high-pass filtering). This supervision focuses D_c on capturing large-scale geometric deviations while avoiding overfitting to high-frequency stochastic sensor artifacts. The objective function is L_struct, minimizing the difference between the predicted noise and the structural ground truth.
 - **Discrepancy Map Generation**: Alongside ∆_c, the coarse model also predicts a pixel-wise discrepancy proxy (ˆM_0) between the coarse prediction and the real depth distribution. This is then aggregated into a region-level discrepancy map (M) via 8x8 pooling. M serves as a spatial uncertainty signal, indicating areas where the coarse prediction deviates significantly from real depth.
 - **Output**: ˆD_coarse = D_sim + ∆_c, and the discrepancy map M.

- **Fine Stage: Local Perturbative Enhancement**:
 - **Objective**: To refine local perturbation noise (∆_f), capturing high-frequency elements like stochastic jitter, edge misalignment, or reflection artifacts that remain after the coarse stage.
 - **Discrepancy-Guided Optimization (DGO)**: The discrepancy map M from the coarse stage acts as a prior. The depth domain is partitioned into high-discrepancy regions (Ω_dis, where M(p) > τ) and low-discrepancy regions (Ω_sim, where M(p) ≤ τ).
 - **Adaptive Reweighting**: A fine diffusion model (D_f) is trained, conditioned on both ˆD_coarse and M. Its loss function (L_fine) is reweighted, assigning a higher coefficient (λ) to Ω_dis to prioritize refinement in uncertain areas and a lower coefficient (ω) to Ω_sim to softly preserve existing structures.
 - **Output**: ˆD_fine = ˆD_coarse + ∆_f, representing the final real-world-like depth.

#### 3.2. Statistical Justification for Diffusion-Generated Realism

The paper provides an empirical and distribution-level argument for why diffusion-generated residuals can mimic real-world noise. Assuming real observations D_real = D_sim + δ (where δ is real noise) and generated depth ˆD_fine = D_sim + ∆, the goal is for ˆD_fine to match D_real.
- **Assumptions**: The justification relies on three empirically verifiable assumptions: conditional moment stability of real residuals (A1), bounded generative approximation by the diffusion model (A2), and bounded support of simulated depth (A3).
- **Bound on TV Distance**: The Total Variation (TV) distance between the marginal distributions of real and generated depth (TV(p_D_real, p_ˆD_fine)) is shown to be bounded by the expected TV distance between the real and predicted residual distributions (E[TV(p_δ, p_∆)]). By Pinsker's inequality, this bound can be further related to the KL divergence. As the diffusion model minimizes KL divergence during training, the discrepancy between the predicted and real depth distributions asymptotically approaches zero.

#### 3.3. Manipulation with RealD²iff Pipeline

RealD²iff is integrated into a six-stage pipeline to enable zero-shot sim2real transfer for robot manipulation:

1. **Simulation Construction & Alignment**: An object-centric RGB-D environment is built in Isaac Sim 5.0.0. Geometric similarity between simulated and real-world objects is prioritized. Camera extrinsics are estimated using a differentiable-rendering-based calibration method, and slight randomization of camera poses is introduced to enhance robustness to minor misalignments.
2. **Demonstration Generation & Enhancement**: Expert demonstrations are collected in simulation using the MimicGen framework, augmented with a whole-body control scheme for smoother trajectories. Practical enhancements like randomized resets and adaptive velocity modulation are applied to improve data quality and diversity.
3. **Policy Learning & Deployment**: Existing depth-aware robot learning policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, PPT, CAGE, RISE) are adapted. Their visual backbones (e.g., ResNet) are extended with an additional depth stream, and a dedicated depth branch is fused with other visual-proprioceptive embeddings. The observation space includes RGB-D images, robot joint positions, and gripper status.
 - **Deployment Strategy**: During real-world deployment, RealD²iff is applied *offline* to process the clean simulated depth maps from the training data, introducing realistic noise. The policy is then trained on this RealD²iff-enhanced simulated depth. This allows the trained policy to be deployed on physical robots without requiring online correction or depth-domain adaptation, achieving zero-shot sim2real transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments address three main questions: the ability of RealD²iff to generate realistic noisy depth, its effectiveness in bridging the sim2real gap for robot manipulation, and the contributions of its design components.

#### 4.1. Performance of Depth Generation

- **Setup**: RealD²iff was trained on public datasets (ByteCameraDepth, LASA) containing clean-noisy depth pairs, without using real robots or expert trajectories. This training utilized two 24GB Nvidia GeForce RTX 4090 GPUs.
- **Metrics**: Evaluation was based on mIoU, Chamfer L2 (scaled by 1,000, lower is better), and F-score.
- **Comparisons**: Compared against Pix2Pix, Giga-GAN, Stable-Diffusion, and ControlNet, adapted for 1-channel 16-bit depth generation.
- **Settings**: Experiments included an in-domain setting (training and validation on subsets of both datasets) and a zero-shot out-of-domain (OoD) setting (training on one dataset, validating on another).
- **Results (Quantitative - Table 1)**: RealD²iff consistently outperformed all baseline methods in both in-domain and OoD settings across all metrics. For instance, in the "Bedroom" scene for OoD, RealD²iff achieved 51.8 mIoU, 4.63 Chamfer L2, and 22.1 F-score, which was notably superior to ControlNet (48.4 mIoU, 7.31 Chamfer L2, 19.5 F-score). Similar improvements were observed in in-domain tasks. This indicates RealD²iff's capacity to generate depth noise with higher fidelity to real-world characteristics and robust generalization capabilities.
- **Results (Qualitative - Figure 4)**: Visualizations of generated depth maps across diverse scenes (bedroom, office, living room, lounge, market) demonstrated that RealD²iff produces accurate and generalized real-world-like noisy depth.

#### 4.2. Performance of Sim2Real Robot Manipulation

- **Setup**: A Franka Research 3 robot arm with a RealSense D435 camera in the real world, and a corresponding environment in Isaac Sim 5.0.0. 900 demonstration trajectories per task were collected in simulation.
- **Tasks**: Four manipulation tasks of increasing difficulty: Pick Cube, Place Banana, Can-to-Plate, and Stack Cube. Each task was evaluated across 10 different locations with 3 trials per location.
- **Compared Policies**: Diffusion Policy, PPT, CAGE, and RISE were adapted to include a depth channel.
- **Validation Settings**:
 - (a) Simulation performance with clean depth.
 - (b) Real-world performance of policies trained on clean simulated depth (real(w/o)).
 - (c) Real-world performance of policies trained on RealD²iff-enhanced simulated depth (real(w.)).
- **Results (Quantitative - Table 2)**:
 - **Sim2Real Gap Observation**: Policies showed high success rates in simulation with clean depth (e.g., Diffusion Policy: 30/30 for Pick Cube). However, when these policies were directly deployed in the real world with actual noisy depth (real(w/o)), their performance significantly deteriorated (e.g., Diffusion Policy: 8/30 for Pick Cube). PPT exhibited comparatively less degradation but still showed a drop.
 - **RealD²iff Effectiveness**: Policies trained with RealD²iff-enhanced depth (real(w.)) demonstrated substantial improvements in real-world performance, approaching their simulation success rates. For example, Diffusion Policy for Pick Cube improved from 8/30 to 30/30. PPT for Place Banana improved from 11/30 to 30/30. These results indicate that RealD²iff effectively bridges the depth sim2real gap, enabling zero-shot transfer without additional fine-tuning.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness Studies of RealD²iff

- **Impact of Noise Modeling Choices (Table 3)**:
 - **Comparisons**: RealD²iff was compared against analytical noise models (Gaussian, Perlin noise), an advanced domain randomization technique (ActiveDR), and state-of-the-art monocular depth estimation models (UniDepth, DepthAnything) repurposed for noise generation.
 - **Metrics**: Depth realism (FID, Chamfer L2) and real-world robot manipulation success rate (SR on Stack-Cube task).
 - **Results**: RealD²iff achieved the best depth fidelity (lowest FID: 76.7, lowest Chamfer L2: 3.62) and the highest manipulation success rate (SR: 0.92). This suggests that RealD²iff’s generated noise patterns are more effective at mimicking real-world conditions and improving robot robustness than other augmentation methods.
- **Impact of Generative Frameworks (Table 4)**:
 - **Comparison**: Ablation comparing the hierarchical coarse-to-fine framework of RealD²iff against an end-to-end (E2E) single-stage diffusion approach.
 - **Results**: The E2E approach performed notably worse (e.g., mIoU 53.9, Chamfer L2 6.64, F-score 22.8) than the full RealD²iff model (mIoU 63.7, Chamfer L2 3.70, F-score 29.1). This outcome supports the benefits of the hierarchical decomposition in accumulating representational priors and easing optimization at different spatial scales.
- **Impact of Training Strategies (Table 4)**:
 - **Ablation**: Studies assessed the individual contributions of Frequency-Guided Supervision (FGS) and Discrepancy-Guided Optimization (DGO) within the RealD²iff framework.
 - **Results**: Removing FGS or DGO individually led to a reduction in generative performance compared to the full model, as evidenced by lower mIoU and F-scores and higher Chamfer L2. For example, without FGS, mIoU dropped to 60.4, and without DGO, it dropped to 58.3, compared to 63.7 for the full model. This confirms the effectiveness of both FGS and DGO in enhancing the realism and detail of generated depth noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation and sim2real transfer, particularly by addressing the visual depth sim2real gap.

1. **Bridging the Depth Gap**: The work introduces a novel and effective method for synthesizing real-world-like noisy depth from clean simulated data. This directly addresses the "depth gap," a previously underexplored but critical component of the visual sim2real problem that profoundly affects 3D perception accuracy in robotic tasks.

2. **Novel Clean-to-Noisy Paradigm**: By inverting the conventional denoising perspective of diffusion models, RealD²iff proposes a "clean-to-noisy" paradigm. This approach enables the generation of complex, uncertain sensor noise patterns, offering a new direction for enhancing simulation fidelity and generalizability in robot learning. This innovative use of diffusion models extends their applicability beyond traditional image generation and denoising tasks.

3. **Enabling Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer**: A key impact is the demonstration of robust zero-shot sim2real transfer for robotic manipulation. Policies trained exclusively on RealD²iff-enhanced simulated depth achieve performance in the real world comparable to simulation, eliminating the need for costly, time-consuming real-world data collection and fine-tuning. This significantly lowers the barrier to deploying simulation-trained robots.

4. **Scalable Data Generation**: RealD²iff facilitates the synthetic creation of high-fidelity clean-noisy paired depth datasets. This removes the reliance on manual real-world sensor data capture for noise modeling, providing a scalable solution for data augmentation and reducing practical overheads in robot learning research and development.

5. **Robust Policy Learning**: Policies trained with depth data that incorporates realistic noise patterns are inherently more robust to the uncertainties and imperfections of real-world depth sensors. This enhances the practical applicability and reliability of learned policies in uncontrolled environments.

6. **Generalizability of Framework**: The hierarchical coarse-to-fine diffusion framework, coupled with Frequency-Guided Supervision (FGS) and Discrepancy-Guided Optimization (DGO), demonstrates strong generalization capabilities across diverse scenes, objects, and manipulation policies. This indicates a broadly applicable solution for various robotic tasks.

7. **Future Research Directions**: The work lays a foundation for further exploration into challenging manipulation scenarios, such as tasks involving transparent, reflective, or articulated objects, where depth perception is particularly difficult. The integrated pipeline provides a general recipe that could be adapted for other embodied intelligence problems under real-world constraints.

In summary, RealD²iff offers both a practical solution and a conceptual advancement in addressing a fundamental challenge in robot learning, contributing to the broader goal of making simulation-driven robotics more viable and impactful for real-world applications.
