# Learning 3D Dynamic Scene Representations for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Columbia and Stanford University developed a 3D Dynamic Scene Representation (DSR) and the DSR-Net framework, allowing robots to maintain complete, persistent models of objects even when occluded, which significantly improves 3D motion prediction and manipulation planning. The approach achieved a 0.72 voxel IoU in planar pushing tasks, substantially outperforming prior methods which scored around 0.31-0.32 IoU.

## Problem

- Existing visual predictive models often focus on 2D motion or partial 3D scene flow, causing objects to 'fade and vanish' from representation when occluded in dynamic environments.
- Previous 3D scene representations typically assume static environments, limiting their applicability in complex, dynamic robot manipulation scenarios.
- Current interactive perception methods for 3D motion prediction rely on per-pixel flow from partial observations, leading to incomplete or inaccurate scene representations, especially for occluded objects.

## Method

- Introduced a ==3D Dynamic Scene Representation (DSR)== that explicitly models ==object permanence==, ==amodal completeness==, and spatiotemporal continuity.
- Proposed **DSR-Net**, an end-to-end ==3D recurrent neural network== framework to learn DSR by simultaneously discovering, tracking, reconstructing objects, and predicting their dynamics from depth observations and robot actions.
- Integrated DSR with a ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== method for planar pushing tasks, utilizing the complete scene representation to enable more effective action planning.

## Results

- **DSR-Net** achieved superior 3D scene flow prediction with a visible surface Mean Squared Error (MSE) of **5.54 cm** and a full volume MSE of **0.082 cm** in simulation, outperforming several baseline models.
- The model demonstrated robust temporal information aggregation, achieving high unordered IoU scores of **0.772** in simulation for amodal shape completion and maintaining consistent object identity during occlusions.
- In planar pushing tasks, **DSR-Net** integrated with MPC achieved a final voxel IoU of **0.72** between the final and target states, significantly outperforming baselines like SE3-Net (**0.31** IoU) and SE3Pose-Net (**0.32** IoU).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A complete and persistent 3D scene representation, capable of aggregating information over multiple interactions and maintaining object presence despite occlusion, is crucial for robust visual predictive modeling.
> - Spatially aligned history aggregation, achieved through motion prediction and warping, is vital for forming coherent and amodally complete object representations over time.
> - Explicitly modeling object instances, their full geometry, and their motion provides a structured and interpretable representation that enhances high-level reasoning and action planning in robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and comprehensive 3D scene representations is a foundational challenge in robotics, particularly for manipulation tasks. Historically, visual predictive models in robotics have largely focused on predicting 2D motion, such as optical flow, or 3D scene flow of only visible points from partial observations. These approaches encounter limitations in cluttered or dynamic environments, where objects frequently become occluded, leading to data degradation, loss of object representation, and impaired predictive capabilities.

Previous research in computer vision and robotics has explored various scene representations. Passive perception methods, like object detection and segmentation, primarily yield 2D representations, which are often insufficient for 3D robotic applications. Efforts to infer 3D scene representations from partial observations (e.g., single RGB or depth images) have led to explicit representations like 3D volumes or meshes, and more recently, implicit neural representations. However, these methods typically operate under the assumption of static environments, limiting their applicability in dynamic manipulation scenarios.

Active perception systems enhance representation building by updating camera viewpoints for exploration. While these systems can improve 3D geometry estimation, they also predominantly focus on static scenes, lacking mechanisms to model object dynamics or physical interactions.

The field of interactive perception, where perception is facilitated by interaction with the environment, addresses the need for learning predictive and dynamic scene representations conditioned on observations and interactions. Recent advancements in this area include object-centric representations and models for 3D motion of rigid shapes. However, many of these approaches still predict motion based on per-pixel flow, which considers only partial, observable surfaces and does not effectively leverage cumulative historical interactions and observations. Consequently, the resulting scene representations often remain incomplete or inaccurate, particularly concerning occluded objects.

This work addresses the identified gaps by proposing a 3D dynamic scene representation (DSR) that explicitly models crucial properties—permanence, amodal completeness, and spatiotemporal continuity—that have been recognized as fundamental for human scene understanding. By aiming to create a complete and persistent 3D representation that aggregates information over multiple interactions, this research seeks to overcome the limitations of current visual predictive models, thereby enabling more robust and accurate robotic manipulation in complex, dynamic, and cluttered environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the utility of learning a complete and persistent 3D scene representation for visual predictive modeling in robot manipulation. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and desired properties:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Predictive Models:** Current visual predictive models predominantly focus on 2D motion or partial 3D scene flow. This leads to issues such as objects "fading and vanishing" from representation when occluded, which severely limits their performance in cluttered environments where robots frequently reconfigure scenes. The motivation is to develop a representation that maintains object presence despite occlusion.

2. **Capturing Object Permanence:** A core motivation is to ensure that the representation can aggregate visual information into a persistent 3D model. This means that objects, even when disappearing from view due to occlusion, remain represented. This property is considered crucial for predicting object motion accurately, especially when objects are moved by others while occluded or when they reappear in view.

3. **Achieving Amodal Completeness:** The research aims for a representation that can infer the complete 3D occupancy of each object from partial observations, including regions that are not directly visible. This allows for the prediction of the rigid body motion of the entire object rather than just its visible surfaces, which is essential for precise manipulation.

4. **Ensuring Spatiotemporal Continuity:** The objective is for the representation to recognize individual object instances and track their identities consistently over time. This continuity is vital for understanding sequences of interactions and for maintaining a coherent model of the scene.

5. **Enhancing Interpretability:** A further motivation is to develop a representation that explicitly models object instances, their geometry, and their motion. Such an explicit model would facilitate high-level reasoning in robotic applications, making the representation directly usable for task and action planning.

6. **Improving Action Planning in Manipulation Tasks:** Ultimately, the improved scene understanding provided by a complete and persistent 3D representation is intended to enable more accurate and effective action planning for downstream robotic manipulation tasks, such as planar pushing.

To achieve these objectives, the authors articulate three specific contributions:
- Introducing 3D Dynamic Scene Representation (DSR) that encapsulates object permanence, amodal completeness, and continuity.
- Proposing DSR-Net, an end-to-end framework that learns DSR using 3D convolutions and aggregates information across multiple interactions.
- Constructing a new benchmark dataset comprising both simulated and real-world robot interactions for training and evaluating dynamic 3D scene representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose the 3D Dynamic Scene Representation (DSR) and introduce DSR-Net, a 3D recurrent neural network, to learn and utilize this representation. The system is designed to simultaneously discover, track, reconstruct objects, and predict their dynamics.

**DSR-Net Architecture:**
DSR-Net takes a depth observation (encoded as a Truncated Signed Distance Function, TSDF) and a robot action as input at each time step. It processes this information through several interconnected modules to output amodal object instance masks and voxel-wise scene flow.

1. **Scene Encoder:** This module processes the incoming depth observation, represented as a 128x128x48 TSDF volume, and concatenates it with the warped scene representation from the previous time step ($S'_{t-1}$). The combined input passes through 22 3D convolution layers, interleaved with batch normalizations and Leaky ReLUs. Skip connections are utilized between specific layers to facilitate feature propagation. The output is an 8x128x128x48 volumetric scene representation ($S_t$).

2. **Action Encoder:** Robot actions are defined as planar pushes, represented by a tuple (p_x, p_y, d), indicating the start coordinates and one of eight predefined push directions. This action is transformed into an 8x128x128 one-hot action map to maintain spatial alignment with the scene representation. Nine 3D convolution layers encode this action map into two embeddings of sizes 16x32x32 and 8x64x64, providing action context at different spatial scales.

3. **Motion Prediction Network:** This network takes the aggregated scene representation ($S_t$) and the action embeddings as input. It consists of eight 3D convolution layers, followed by a 3D convolution layer with a 4x4x2 kernel, which condenses the feature volume into a 128-length vector. This vector then passes through five fully connected layers to predict *k* SE(3) transformations (rotation R and translation t). Each SE(3) transform corresponds to a potential object instance (k=5, with k-1 transformations for objects and one for static background). These transformations are used in conjunction with the predicted instance masks to compute voxel-level scene flow ($F_t$). The motion loss ($L_{motion}$) is calculated as the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted scene flow and ground truth.

4. **Amodal Instance Mask Prediction:** A 1x1x1 convolution layer and a softmax layer convert the scene representation ($S_t$) into a voxel-wise probability distribution ($M_t$) over the *k* object classes. During training, a permutation matching strategy is employed to establish temporal consistency for object identities across time steps. The optimal matching is determined by minimizing a negative log-likelihood loss, and the final mask loss ($L_{mask}$) is computed using Cross Entropy.

5. **Forward Warping for Spatially Aligned History Aggregation:** To ensure that historical information is effectively aggregated, the current scene representation ($S_t$) is warped using the predicted scene flow ($F_t$) and object masks ($M_t$). This process spatially aligns features from previous observations with the current frame, addressing object motion. Trilinear interpolation is used for 3D warping to minimize artifacts like empty holes. The resulting warped representation ($S'_t$) is then fed back into the scene encoder at the next time step as historical context.

**Loss Function:**
The total loss function is a weighted sum of the motion loss and the mask loss: $L = L_{motion} + \alpha L_{mask}$, where $\alpha=5$ is a weighting factor.

**Training Strategy:**
The model is implemented in PyTorch and optimized using ADAM. Training is structured in three stages to gradually increase complexity: (1) single-step training on a cube dataset, (2) multi-step training on the cube dataset, and (3) multi-step training on a ShapeNet dataset. This progressive finetuning approach improves the model's robustness and generalization.

**Application in Robot Manipulation (Model Predictive Control - MPC):**
DSR is integrated with a shooting-based MPC method for planar pushing tasks. The DSR model predicts object masks and future states. Actions are sampled specifically around the predicted object masks to reduce the search space. The cost function for planning considers both the Mean Squared Error of predicted object positions ($L_{pos}$) and the Intersection over Union (IoU) with a target state. DSR's ability to maintain object permanence enables planning algorithms to utilize full state information, including occluded objects.

**DSR Benchmark Dataset:**
A novel benchmark dataset was created to train and evaluate DSR-Net, encompassing both simulated and real-world robot interactions.
- **Simulation Data:** 8,000 sequences (80,000 interactions) were generated using cubes and 44 ShapeNet objects. A heuristic-based policy encouraged spatial order changes and prevented objects from leaving the workspace. A separate test set of 400 sequences (4,000 interactions) used novel YCB objects.
- **Real-world Data:** A UR5 robot with a pusher tool and two calibrated Intel RealSense D415 RGB-D cameras (front for input, back for ground truth annotation) were used. 90 sequences (900 interaction steps) were collected with YCB objects. A user interface facilitated manual 6DoF pose annotation using point clouds from both cameras. The real-world data was split into 50 finetuning sequences and 100 testing sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DSR-Net focused on three areas: motion prediction accuracy, effectiveness of temporal information aggregation (object permanence and continuity), and performance in a robot manipulation task.

#### 4.1. Motion Prediction

DSR-Net demonstrated a higher accuracy in predicting 3D scene flow compared to various baseline models. The Mean Squared Error (MSE) was used as the evaluation metric, computed separately for visible surfaces and the full object volume.
- **Quantitative Comparison:** In simulation, DSR-Net achieved a visible surface MSE of 5.54 cm and a full volume MSE of 0.082 cm, outperforming SE3-Net (7.84 cm visible), SE3Pose-Net (13.01 cm visible), 3DFlow (7.34 cm visible, 0.093 cm full), and SingleStep (5.94 cm visible, 0.086 cm full). On real-world data, DSR-Net (without finetuning) had visible MSE of 6.51 cm and full MSE of 0.090 cm, which further improved to 3.33 cm (visible) and 0.048 cm (full) after finetuning on real-world data.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visualizations indicated that DSR-Net produced accurate 3D amodal masks for all objects and precise motion predictions, encompassing occluded regions. In contrast, SE3-Net's motion estimation was limited to visible surfaces and only predicted masks for moving objects, while SE3Pose-Net, despite predicting masks for all objects, performed less accurately in motion due to its reliance on object poses rather than detailed geometry.

#### 4.2. Temporal Information Aggregation

This section evaluated DSR-Net's ability to aggregate historical information to capture object permanence and continuity using 3D amodal instance mask Intersection over Union (IoU) scores (unordered and ordered).
- **Amodal Shape Completion:** DSR-Net achieved higher unordered IoU scores (0.772 in simulation, 0.628 in real-world) compared to SingleStep (0.753 sim, 0.613 real) and NoWarp (0.762 sim, 0.625 real). This indicated that aggregating past observations effectively leads to a more accurate inference of an object's complete 3D geometry from partial views.
- **Object Permanence:** DSR-Net effectively inferred the existence and complete shape of objects even when they were fully occluded, handling both static (occluded but not moving) and dynamic (moving then occluded) occlusion cases. The SingleStep model failed in both types of occlusions. The NoWarp model could handle static occlusion due to history information but failed in dynamic occlusion due to the lack of motion information in its history aggregation.
- **Object Continuity:** DSR-Net maintained consistent object identity over time, as evidenced by high ordered IoU scores (0.767 in simulation, 0.628 in real-world). This performance was robust even when objects were visually indistinguishable or their spatial order significantly changed during interactions (e.g., swapped positions). The SingleStep model exhibited a larger discrepancy between unordered and ordered IoUs, indicating a failure to consistently track object identities without history aggregation.
- **Contribution of Motion Prediction to Aggregation:** Comparisons with the NoWarp baseline confirmed that spatially-aligned features, achieved through motion prediction and warping, enhance history aggregation. DSR-Net consistently outperformed NoWarp in both ordered and unordered IoUs. An oracle model (GTWarp) using ground truth motion showed even higher performance, supporting the hypothesis that accurate motion prediction is beneficial for coherent history aggregation.

#### 4.3. Application in Robot Manipulation

The utility of DSR was evaluated in a planar pushing task using Model Predictive Control (MPC). The goal was to align objects with a target configuration over a sequence of actions.
- DSR-Net, integrated with MPC, achieved a voxel IoU of 0.72 between the final and target states. This significantly outperformed the baselines, SE3-Net (0.31 IoU) and SE3Pose-Net (0.32 IoU), over 100 simulated target states.
- This result indicated that DSR-Net's comprehensive scene representation, including information about occluded objects, enabled the planning algorithm to select more effective action sequences, leading to more accurate object placement and state matching.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a 3D Dynamic Scene Representation (DSR) and an associated learning framework, DSR-Net, which addresses fundamental challenges in robot perception and manipulation. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Advancement in 3D Scene Understanding:** The work provides a novel approach to learning comprehensive 3D dynamic scene representations. By explicitly incorporating object permanence, amodal completeness, and spatiotemporal continuity, DSR-Net overcomes limitations of previous models that struggle with occluded objects and partial observations. This advancement contributes to a more human-like understanding of physical scenes by robotic systems.

2. **Improved Robot Manipulation Capabilities:** The enhanced scene understanding directly translates into improved performance for robotic manipulation tasks. The demonstration in planar pushing, where DSR-Net enabled more accurate action planning and target state matching compared to prior methods, indicates its potential to make robots more effective and reliable in interacting with dynamic and cluttered environments.

3. **Robustness in Complex Environments:** By maintaining representations of objects even when occluded and inferring their complete 3D geometry, DSR-Net provides a foundation for robots to operate more robustly in real-world cluttered settings. This capability is critical for applications where objects frequently move out of view or are only partially observable.

4. **Foundation for High-Level Robotic Reasoning:** The explicit modeling of object instances, their full geometry, and their motion provides a structured and interpretable representation. This can serve as a robust foundation for higher-level reasoning, complex task planning, and even learning intuitive physics, moving beyond reactive control towards more cognitive robotic systems.

5. **Creation of a Valuable Benchmark:** The development of a new, comprehensive benchmark dataset, including both simulated and real-world interactions with ground truth object motion, is a significant contribution to the research community. This dataset provides a standardized resource for training, evaluating, and comparing future dynamic 3D scene representation models, accelerating research in this domain.

6. **Bridging Perception and Control:** This research effectively bridges advanced visual perception with robotic control. By providing a predictive model of 3D scene dynamics, it empowers control algorithms like Model Predictive Control to make more informed decisions over time, directly linking sensory input to effective physical action.

7. **Potential for Future Extensions:** The authors identify several avenues for future work, such as extending the model to deformable or articulated objects and incorporating graph neural networks to better model long-range relationships and complex interactions. These directions suggest that the DSR framework can serve as a baseline for developing more sophisticated and versatile robotic perception systems.

In summary, this work represents a step forward in enabling robots to perceive and interact with dynamic 3D environments more intelligently by providing a complete, persistent, and continuous understanding of the scene.
