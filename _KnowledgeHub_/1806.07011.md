# VirtualHome: Simulating Household Activities via Programs

> [!summary] Summary
> VirtualHome is a platform that simulates diverse household activities, offering a 3D interactive environment and two datasets for training autonomous agents. It enables agents to execute complex, multi-step tasks by translating natural language descriptions or video demonstrations into structured programs, achieving a 77.4% Longest Common Subsequence score and 39.8% executability from natural language input.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to perform complex, multi-step household tasks due to the difficulty of explicitly programming every action and the lack of commonsense understanding.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, richly annotated real-world data necessary for training AI models to understand and execute human activities for embodied AI.
- Existing action and activity recognition methods often focus on atomic actions, failing to interpret composite activities or high-level natural language instructions and videos.

## Method

- Developed **VirtualHome**, a ==3D interactive simulation environment== that enables an agent to execute ==atomic actions== and interact with objects in a household setting.
- Created the ActivityPrograms dataset by crowdsourcing natural language descriptions of household tasks and formalizing them into ==executable programs== that include explicit commonsense steps.
- Generated the VirtualHome Activity dataset through automated simulation, producing richly annotated videos with perfect ground truth data for training computer vision models.
- Implemented a ==sequence-to-sequence model==, enhanced with ==reinforcement learning== (==REINFORCE==), to translate natural language descriptions or video demonstrations into ==executable programs==.

## Results

- The ActivityPrograms dataset provides **2,821** crowd-sourced programs for household activities, averaging **11.6 steps** per program, with **64%** deemed complete.
- The VirtualHome Activity dataset comprises **5,193** programs with corresponding natural language descriptions and richly annotated videos generated through diverse simulations.
- The program generation model achieved a mean Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) score of **77.4%** and an executability rate of **39.8%** when translating natural language descriptions into programs.
- From video input, the model attained a **49.5%** mean LCS score and **22.4%** executability, demonstrating the feasibility of visual program synthesis while highlighting challenges in generalization to unseen environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing complex household activities as formal, executable programs effectively bridges the gap between high-level human instructions and low-level robotic actions, providing clarity for agents.
> - Large-scale, richly annotated synthetic data generated via simulation is crucial for training robust AI models for embodied AI and video understanding, mitigating the limitations of real-world data collection.
> - Reinforcement learning, incorporating rewards for both semantic correctness (Longest Common Subsequence) and practical executability in a simulator, substantially improves the quality and validity of generated programs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"VirtualHome: Simulating Household Activities via Programs" addresses several critical gaps and pushes the boundaries in multiple interconnected fields of artificial intelligence and robotics.

**Robotics and Autonomous Agents:** A central challenge in robotics is enabling autonomous agents to perform complex, multi-step tasks in unstructured environments, particularly human-centric spaces like homes. Traditional robotics often relies on explicit, low-level programming for specific tasks. This paper directly tackles this by proposing a high-level, programmatic representation of activities that can "drive" a robot. It moves beyond constrained, simple tasks (e.g., navigation) to a wide variety of everyday household activities, which are inherently more complex due to numerous interaction possibilities and commonsense knowledge requirements. Previous works cited in the paper were often limited to specific domains like cooking or short action sequences, whereas VirtualHome aims for a more exhaustive set of household tasks.

**Action and Activity Recognition/Understanding:** The field of computer vision has made significant strides in recognizing atomic actions and objects. However, understanding *composite activities*—sequences of actions leading to a goal—remains challenging, especially when inferring intent from natural language or video. This work contributes by linking high-level natural language descriptions and visual demonstrations to a formal, executable program representation. This bridges the gap between perception (understanding what is happening or what is requested) and action (executing the necessary steps). It builds upon related work in parsing videos into action plans but expands the scope beyond narrow domains.

**Program Synthesis and Code Generation:** The paper draws inspiration from the growing interest in generating and interpreting source code from natural language. While prior work focused on retrieving code snippets, generating "If-This-Then-That" rules, or specifying game logic, VirtualHome applies this paradigm to generate executable programs for robotic agents from both natural language instructions and video demonstrations. This represents a novel application of program synthesis techniques in the context of embodied AI.

**Simulation for AI Training and Evaluation:** A significant bottleneck in developing intelligent agents is the scarcity of large-scale, richly annotated real-world data, especially for training robots that interact with environments. Collecting real-world data for complex activities is expensive, time-consuming, and often lacks perfect ground truth. VirtualHome directly addresses this by creating a synthetic 3D environment using a game engine (Unity3D). This aligns with a broader trend in AI research (e.g., autonomous driving simulators like CARLA, indoor navigation environments like AI2-THOR or MINOS) to leverage synthetic data for training robust models, particularly for reinforcement learning and embodied AI. VirtualHome distinguishes itself by focusing specifically on simulating a wide range of *human actions and interactions* within a household setting, providing dense, perfect ground-truth information (pose, segmentation, depth, flow) that is impossible to acquire in the real world at scale. It offers a "robot playground" where agents can be trained and tested without real-world constraints or risks.

**Dataset Creation:** The paper's contribution of the ActivityPrograms dataset is crucial. It addresses the fundamental lack of a database that not only describes activities in natural language but also formalizes them into precise, executable programs, including the often-omitted commonsense steps. This provides a unique resource for training models that can translate human instructions into robot-understandable commands. The subsequent VirtualHome Activity dataset, generated via simulation, further provides the necessary visual data for training video understanding models at an unprecedented scale with rich annotations.

In summary, this work is positioned at the vanguard of AI research, aiming to enable more intelligent, adaptable, and user-friendly autonomous agents by integrating multimodal understanding (language, vision), symbolic reasoning (programs), and large-scale data generation through advanced simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and underlying motivations of the VirtualHome project are multifaceted, aiming to bridge the gap between human instruction and robotic execution in complex household environments.

1. **To Model Complex Household Activities as Programs:** The core objective is to move beyond simple, atomic actions and develop a robust representation for complex, multi-step activities that commonly occur in a household. The authors propose using "programs"—sequences of atomic actions and interactions with specific objects—as a high-level, unambiguous, and executable representation for these tasks.
 - **Motivation:** Natural language descriptions of tasks often omit commonsense steps that humans implicitly understand but robots require explicitly. Programs provide a formal, step-by-step instruction set that ensures clarity and executability for an artificial agent. This is essential for building autonomous agents that can reliably perform tasks.

2. **To Create a Comprehensive Knowledge Base of Household Tasks (ActivityPrograms Dataset):** A crucial objective was to collect a novel dataset that formalizes this programmatic understanding of household activities. This dataset comprises natural language descriptions paired with corresponding structured programs.
 - **Motivation:** A significant bottleneck in developing intelligent robots is the lack of large-scale, diverse, and well-annotated datasets that link human-level task descriptions to machine-executable instructions. Existing datasets were often limited in scope or lacked the detailed, programmatic representation needed for robots. This dataset aims to fill that void, capturing the "natural language know-how" and translating it into a robot-centric format.

3. **To Develop a 3D Simulation Environment (VirtualHome Simulator):** The project aimed to implement a realistic and interactive 3D simulation environment where these collected programs could be executed by an artificial agent.
 - **Motivation:** Real-world robot deployment and data collection are costly, time-consuming, dangerous, and hard to scale. A simulator offers a safe, controllable, and reproducible "playground" where robots can be trained, tested, and fine-tuned for a vast array of tasks. It allows for rapid iteration and experimentation without physical constraints.

4. **To Generate a Large-Scale, Richly Annotated Video Dataset (VirtualHome Activity Dataset):** Leveraging the simulator, a key objective was to automatically generate a massive dataset of activity videos, complete with dense, perfect ground-truth annotations.
 - **Motivation:** Training robust computer vision models for video understanding (e.g., action recognition, pose estimation, semantic segmentation) requires enormous amounts of data. Acquiring such data from the real world, especially with perfect per-frame ground truth, is practically impossible. Simulation provides an elegant solution, enabling the creation of diverse videos with ideal annotations (time-stamps, 2D/3D pose, segmentation, depth, optical flow, camera parameters), which are invaluable for supervised learning.

5. **To Learn to Translate Natural Language Descriptions and Videos into Programs:** The ultimate goal is to develop AI models capable of automatically inferring these formal programs from human-understandable inputs (natural language instructions or video demonstrations).
 - **Motivation:** To empower "naive users"—people without programming expertise—to teach robots new tasks intuitively. This democratizes robot programming, allowing a robot to learn by simply being told what to do or shown a demonstration, thereby making robots more accessible and useful in everyday life. This objective directly addresses the challenges of human-robot interaction and communication.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in VirtualHome is a sophisticated blend of crowd-sourcing, 3D simulation development, and deep learning techniques, designed to create a comprehensive ecosystem for understanding and executing household activities.

#### 3.1. Data Collection (ActivityPrograms Dataset)

The creation of the ActivityPrograms dataset involved a two-stage crowdsourcing effort:
- **Natural Language Descriptions:** Amazon Mechanical Turk (AMT) workers were tasked with providing natural language descriptions of common household activities. To ensure diversity, activities were initiated in one of eight randomly selected home scenes (e.g., living room, kitchen, bedroom). These descriptions were intentionally collected as humans would describe them to other humans, meaning they often omitted "commonsense" steps.
- **Program Generation:** In the second stage, *different* AMT workers were shown these natural language descriptions and asked to translate them into formal programs. To overcome the barrier of programming experience, a graphical programming interface inspired by MIT's Scratch project was developed. This interface provided 77 predefined "blocks" (atomic actions like `walk`, `pick-up`, `switch-on`) and allowed annotators to combine them into sequences. A critical instruction to annotators was to include *all* necessary steps for a robot to complete the task, even those not explicitly mentioned in the natural language description. Programs were structured as `[action] <object>(id) ... <object>(id)`, where `id` disambiguates object instances. More qualified Upwork workers were used for quality control.
- **Dataset Statistics:** This process yielded 2,821 programs, averaging 11.6 steps per program, involving 75 atomic actions and 308 objects. An analysis revealed that 64% of programs were complete, 28% missed minor steps, and 8% missed crucial steps.

#### 3.2. VirtualHome Simulator Development

The simulator was built using the Unity3D game engine, leveraging its advanced features for physics, kinematics, and navigation.
- **Environment and Assets:** Six diverse, pre-furnished 3D home models were acquired, each containing an average of 357 object instances. Four rigged humanoid models (agents) were used. To support the wide range of objects mentioned in programs, 3D models for an additional 30 object classes were obtained from 3D Warehouse, with at least three variations per class for visual diversity.
- **Atomic Action Animation:** The simulator implemented the 12 most frequent atomic actions identified from the collected programs (e.g., `walk/run`, `grab`, `switch-on/off`, `open/close`, `sit/standup`). These actions were designed to be object-specific (e.g., opening a fridge vs. a drawer). Unity's NavMesh was used for path planning to enable agents to navigate while avoiding obstacles. RootMotion FinalIK was employed for inverse kinematics to generate realistic agent poses. Objects themselves were also animated to reflect interactions (e.g., shaking a coffee maker, lighting up a TV screen).
- **Scene Preparation and Program Execution:** Before executing a program, the simulator "sets" the scene by placing any missing objects referenced in the program into plausible locations based on a pre-collected knowledge base of object-support relationships. To animate a program, the system maps program-specified objects to actual instances in the 3D environment and computes the agent's interaction position and other action attributes (e.g., hand choice, speed). A backtracking algorithm is used to find a feasible sequence of object assignments and interaction parameters.
- **Camera System:** Multiple static cameras (6-9 per room, 26 per home) were placed. During recording, the system dynamically switches between cameras to ensure the agent is visible and within an optimal distance. Camera parameters (position, angle, field of view) were randomized during video generation to enhance dataset diversity.

#### 3.3. VirtualHome Activity Dataset Generation

To create a large-scale video dataset with rich ground truth, the authors used a semi-synthetic approach:
- **Synthetic Program Generation:** A probabilistic grammar was used to synthesize 5,193 new programs, constrained to only use the 12 atomic actions fully implemented in the simulator.
- **Crowd-sourced Descriptions for Synthetic Programs:** Human annotators were then asked to provide natural language descriptions for these *synthesized* programs, ensuring that the descriptions remained natural and human-like.
- **Automated Video Generation and Ground Truth:** Each of these 5,193 programs was animated in the VirtualHome simulator. The animation process involved extensive randomization (home, agent, camera, object placement, agent start location, action speed, object choice for interaction) to maximize diversity. Crucially, the simulator automatically generated comprehensive ground-truth information for each video, including: time-stamps for each atomic action, 2D and 3D agent pose, class and object instance segmentation, depth maps, optical flow, and camera parameters. This rich annotation is critical for training robust video understanding models.

#### 3.4. Program Generation Model (Seq2Seq with Reinforcement Learning)

The task of translating natural language or video into a program was formulated as a sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) problem.
- **Architecture:** An RNN encoder (LSTM) processes the input sequence (words for text, video features for video) into a hidden vector. An RNN decoder (LSTM) then generates program steps one at a time. Each step is represented by an action and up to two object arguments, each with a unique ID. An attention mechanism is used to focus on relevant parts of the encoded input.
- **Training Phases:**
 1. **Pre-training (Maximum Likelihood Estimation - MLE):** The model was initially trained using a standard cross-entropy loss, with ground-truth steps fed to the decoder at each time step. Word2vec embeddings were used for actions and objects.
 2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** To improve program quality and executability, the model was fine-tuned using policy gradient optimization (REINFORCE).
 - **Rewards:** Two types of rewards were combined:
 - `r_LCS`: Normalized Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) score between the generated program and the ground-truth program, measuring semantic correctness.
 - `r_sim`: A binary reward (1 or 0) indicating whether the generated program was successfully executable in the VirtualHome simulator, measuring practical validity.
 - The total reward was a weighted sum: `r(w_s, g) = r_LCS(w_s, g) + 0.1 * r_sim(w_s)`, balancing semantic accuracy with executability.
- **Input Encoding for Different Modalities:**
 - **Text:** Word2vec embeddings of the words in the natural language description were fed sequentially to the RNN encoder.
 - **Video:** Each video was segmented into 2-second clips. A separate model (DilatedNet for semantic segmentation + Temporal Relation Network for sequence reasoning) predicted an embedding for the instruction (action+object) from each clip, which was then fed to the RNN encoder for program generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings across dataset creation, simulation capabilities, and multimodal program generation.

#### 4.1. Dataset Contributions

- **ActivityPrograms Dataset:** The crowd-sourced dataset provided 2,821 real-world household activity programs, averaging 11.6 steps per program, and covering 75 atomic actions and 308 objects. A quality assessment showed that 64% of the programs were complete and 28% were missing only minor, inferable steps. This dataset is a unique resource for robot-centric instruction.
- **VirtualHome Activity Dataset:** The simulator generated 5,193 programs with corresponding natural language descriptions and richly annotated videos. This dataset, with an average of 9.6 steps per program, provides dense ground-truth information (pose, segmentation, depth, flow) for training advanced video understanding models at scale.

#### 4.2. Video-Based Step Classification

- The model for classifying individual action-object-object steps from 2-second video clips achieved a mean accuracy of 45.42% on seen homes and 19.12% on unseen homes for the combined step prediction (action + objects). This highlights the inherent challenge of visual recognition in dynamic 3D environments and the difficulty in generalizing across different scene layouts or appearances. Despite the challenges, the model significantly outperformed a random retrieval baseline (3.43% mean accuracy).

#### 4.3. Program Generation from Natural Language Descriptions

- The models were evaluated using a normalized Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) metric, which acts like an Intersection over Union (IoU) for programs, measuring the similarity in action sequences. An additional metric, "Simulator (%)", tracked the percentage of generated programs that were executable in VirtualHome.
- **Performance:** The proposed Reinforcement Learning (RL) model, particularly `PG(LCS+Sim)` (using both LCS and simulator executability rewards), consistently outperformed all baselines (random sampling, random retrieval, Skipthoughts, and MLE pre-training).
 - On the **VirtualHome Activity dataset**, `PG(LCS+Sim)` achieved a mean LCS score of 77.4% and an executability rate of 39.8%. This demonstrates the model's ability to generate semantically accurate and frequently executable programs from text.
 - On the more challenging **ActivityPrograms dataset** (real descriptions, but not fully executable in the simulator due to limited implemented actions), `PG(LCS)` achieved a mean LCS score of 44.7%. The executability metric could not be reported here due to simulator limitations.
- **Impact of RL Rewards:** The RL training effectively balanced semantic correctness (`r_LCS`) with executability (`r_sim`). While `PG(LCS)` improved LCS scores, `PG(LCS+Sim)` showed a slightly lower LCS but significantly higher executability, confirming the value of the simulator-based reward in guiding program generation towards practically valid outputs.

#### 4.4. Program Generation from Videos

- This was deemed the most challenging task. The `PG(LCS+Sim)` model achieved a mean LCS score of 49.5% and an executability rate of 22.4%.
- **Generalization Gap:** Similar to step classification, there was a notable drop in performance on unseen homes (38.9% mean LCS) compared to seen homes (64.5% mean LCS), indicating that visual program generation still struggles with generalization to novel environments.
- The RL approach with both LCS and simulator rewards proved beneficial, improving both LCS and executability over an LCS-only RL model, demonstrating its ability to learn from both semantic similarity and practical feedback.

#### 4.5. Human Evaluation of Executed Programs

- A human study involving AMT workers evaluated the quality of activities performed by the agent in VirtualHome, based on programs generated from natural language descriptions.
- **Correlation:** The study found a strong agreement between the model's LCS-based program prediction accuracy and human judgments of execution quality (rated as Failed, Marginal, Adequate, Good, Excellent, Perfect). Programs with higher LCS scores generally received higher human ratings.
- **Simulator Limitations:** Some discrepancies, where high-LCS programs still received lower human scores, were attributed to "imperfect animation," suggesting areas for further improvement in the simulator's realism.

In summary, the results confirm the feasibility of generating structured, executable programs from both natural language and video, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed seq2seq model with RL. The project also highlights the significant challenge of generalization in complex, dynamic 3D environments, particularly when transitioning to unseen scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VirtualHome project presents a substantial contribution to AI and robotics, with far-reaching significance and potential impact across several domains.

1. **Enabling General-Purpose Household Robots:** The most direct and profound impact lies in advancing the capabilities of autonomous agents. By providing a framework for translating human-understandable instructions (natural language or video) into unambiguous, executable programs, VirtualHome moves us closer to a future where robots can perform a wide variety of complex household tasks without requiring expert programming. This empowers "naive users" to interact with and teach robots more intuitively, democratizing robotic control and making robots more useful in everyday life.

2. **Novel Data Resources for AI Research:**
 - **ActivityPrograms Dataset:** This dataset is a unique and invaluable resource for research in natural language processing, program synthesis, and human-robot interaction. It explicitly addresses the critical need for data that bridges high-level human intent with low-level robotic actions, including essential commonsense steps.
 - **VirtualHome Activity Dataset:** The large-scale, richly annotated video dataset generated by the simulator is a groundbreaking contribution for computer vision. It provides an unprecedented volume of ground-truth data (2D/3D pose, segmentation, depth, flow) that is impossible to collect in the real world. This will significantly accelerate research in video understanding, activity recognition, and embodied AI, particularly for training robust deep learning models.

3. **Powerful Simulation Platform for Embodied AI:** VirtualHome establishes a robust "robot playground" for researchers. It allows for safe, reproducible, and scalable experimentation with complex AI agents. This simulation environment will be instrumental for:
 - **Reinforcement Learning:** Training agents to learn optimal policies for interacting with environments and objects.
 - **Curriculum Learning:** Developing incremental learning strategies for complex tasks.
 - **Generalization Studies:** Investigating how well learned policies transfer to novel environments, objects, or task variations.
 - **Benchmarking:** Providing a standardized platform for comparing different AI algorithms for task execution and multimodal understanding.

4. **Advancing Multimodal Understanding and Generation:** The project successfully demonstrates the feasibility of cross-modal translation – from language to program, and from video to program. This pushes the boundaries of AI systems that can interpret diverse human inputs and generate structured outputs that lead to physical actions. The use of a combined reward function in RL, incorporating both semantic correctness (LCS) and practical executability (simulator feedback), is a significant methodological advancement for training agents in such complex tasks.

5. **Bridging Perception and Action:** VirtualHome directly links perception (understanding visual scenes and language instructions) with action (executing a sequence of robot commands). This integration is fundamental for the development of truly intelligent, embodied agents that can perceive their world, understand high-level goals, reason about necessary steps, and act upon that understanding.

6. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Bias:** By leveraging simulation, the project provides a scalable solution to the persistent problem of data scarcity in robotics and activity understanding. It also offers a controlled environment to study and mitigate biases that might arise from real-world data collection.

7. **Future Research Avenues:** The paper concludes by opening numerous exciting avenues for future work, such as training agents to perform tasks purely from visual observation using reinforcement learning, further enhancing simulator realism and action repertoire, and improving program synthesis from more diverse textual corpora. The high performance achieved in text-based animation, in particular, suggests the potential for future crowd-sourcing efforts to directly create animations via natural language, streamlining content creation.

In conclusion, "VirtualHome" is a pioneering effort that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in autonomous agents, multimodal AI, and simulation-driven research. By providing critical datasets, a powerful simulator, and effective learning methodologies, it lays a crucial foundation for building more capable, intuitive, and ultimately, more useful robots in human environments.
