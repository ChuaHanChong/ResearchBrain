# LaDi-WM: A Latent Diffusion-based World Model for Predictive Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Defense Technology, Peking University, and Shenzhen University developed LaDi-WM, a latent diffusion-based world model for predictive manipulation. This model leverages visual foundation models to construct a rich latent space and employs an iterative imagination-guided policy to achieve state-of-the-art performance on robotic tasks, outperforming previous methods by 15.1% in average success rate on the LIBERO-LONG benchmark with limited demonstrations.

## Problem

- Existing world models often struggle to generate accurate, high-quality future visual states, limiting their effectiveness for precise robot manipulation.
- Latent spaces in current world models are typically optimized for image reconstruction and often fail to explicitly capture crucial geometric and semantic information essential for robotic manipulation.
- Direct pixel-level prediction of future states is computationally expensive, prone to errors, and exhibits limited generalizability for dynamic robot-object interactions.

## Method

- **LaDi-WM** predicts future environmental dynamics by modeling them as a ==diffusion process== in a generalizable latent space, derived by concatenating geometric features from ==DINOv2== and semantic features from ==Siglip==.
- A novel ==interactive diffusion process== is proposed, utilizing ==cross-attention== between separate denoising networks for geometric and semantic latent components to model their dynamic correlation.
- An ==Imagination-Guided Diffusion Policy== iteratively refines its action sequences by feeding initial actions to the trained **LaDi-WM** to generate imagined future states, which are then used to produce refined actions.

## Results

- Achieved **68.7%** average success rate on the LIBERO-LONG benchmark with **10** demonstrations, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art by **15.1%**, and reached **90.7%** with full data.
- Demonstrated strong generalizability, improving real-world manipulation by **20%** average success rate over vanilla Behavior Cloning and showing cross-scene transferability to the CALVIN D-D benchmark.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the diffusion architecture, interactive latent space, and iterative refinement mechanism are crucial, with iterative refinement alone boosting performance by **19.9%** in one iteration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging pre-trained Visual Foundation Models (DINOv2 for geometry, Siglip for semantics) creates a richer, manipulation-relevant latent space that is easier for a world model to learn and generalize.
> - An interactive diffusion mechanism, where geometric and semantic latent components influence each other via cross-attention, effectively models complex multimodal dynamics, leading to more aligned and accurate predictions.
> - Iterative refinement of robot actions based on progressively more accurate world model forecasts significantly improves policy precision and consistency at inference time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Embodied AI, robotic manipulation, world models, and generative modeling, particularly diffusion models and visual foundation models.

**Embodied AI and Predictive Manipulation:** The field of Embodied AI seeks to create intelligent agents that can interact with and understand their physical environment. Predictive manipulation is a critical component, enabling robots to foresee the consequences of their actions and plan accordingly. Traditional approaches often struggle with long-horizon tasks and complex physical interactions due to difficulties in accurately predicting future states.

**World Models:** World models are central to predictive control, learning the dynamics of an environment to forecast future observations based on actions.
- **Pixel-space vs. Latent-space World Models:** Earlier world models (e.g.,) often predicted future states directly in pixel space. However, generating high-fidelity pixel-level predictions, especially for dynamic robot-object interactions, is computationally expensive, prone to errors, and exhibits limited generalizability. This led to the rise of latent-space world models (e.g., Dreamer series, IRIS, TDMPC2), which predict compressed representations of states, simplifying the learning problem and improving generalizability.
- **Limitations of Existing Latent Spaces:** A key limitation highlighted by the authors is that while existing latent world models are more generalizable, their latent spaces are typically optimized for image reconstruction. They often fail to explicitly capture crucial geometric and semantic information that is essential for effective robotic manipulation (e.g., object poses, categories, affordances). DINO-WM began to address this by leveraging DINO for geometric features, but this paper pushes further by incorporating both.
- **Diffusion Models in World Models:** More recently, diffusion models have been explored for world modeling (e.g.,), primarily for pixel-level prediction, demonstrating their power in modeling complex distributions and generating high-quality images. However, their computational overhead for pixel-space generation remains a challenge. LaDi-WM bridges this by applying diffusion modeling in a *meaningful latent space*, aiming for both fidelity and efficiency.

**Policy Learning for Robotic Manipulation:** World models are typically used to facilitate policy learning.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Methods like Dreamer and TDMPC2 integrate world models with RL, allowing agents to "imagine" and learn within simulated environments. However, RL-based approaches often require dense rewards and can be sample-inefficient, making them challenging for complex, real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Behavior Cloning (BC) / Direct Action Prediction:** Other methods (e.g., AVDC, ATM) directly predict actions or trajectories based on observations. AVDC uses video prediction to estimate optical flow, while ATM uses instruction-conditioned trajectory prediction. These methods can suffer from robustness issues (e.g., optical flow estimation) or limited generalization due to task-specific prediction or sparse representations. LaDi-WM aims to overcome these limitations by providing rich, task-agnostic future guidance.

**Leveraging Visual Foundation Models (VFMs):** A significant trend in Embodied AI is the use of large, pre-trained VFMs (like DINO, CLIP) to provide robust and generalizable visual representations. These models have learned powerful features from vast datasets, capturing both low-level geometric cues and high-level semantic understanding. LaDi-WM strategically integrates DINO (for geometric features) and Siglip (a CLIP variant, for semantic features) into its latent space, directly addressing the representational shortcomings of previous latent world models.

In essence, LaDi-WM addresses the gap between computationally expensive, high-fidelity pixel-space world models and efficient but information-lacking latent-space world models. It proposes a novel approach that combines the generative power of diffusion models with the rich, semantically and geometrically aware latent spaces derived from VFMs, all within a framework designed to robustly enhance robotic manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the performance and generalizability of robot manipulation policies by developing a novel world model capable of generating accurate, high-quality predictions of future visual states. This is achieved by operating within a carefully constructed latent space.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Improving Predictive Manipulation:** The potential of predictive states to boost robot policy performance, especially for long-term tasks, is widely recognized. However, the existing methods often fall short in generating sufficiently accurate and high-quality future visual states.
- **Addressing Challenges in Pixel-level Prediction:** Directly predicting pixel-level images of future robot-object interactions is notoriously difficult. Such predictions frequently exhibit limited fidelity, accuracy, and generalizability, making them unreliable for precise manipulation tasks.
- **Enhancing Latent Space Representations:** While latent-space world models simplify model learning and improve generalizability compared to pixel-space models, their latent spaces are often optimized for mere image reconstruction. This optimization neglects the crucial geometric and semantic information (e.g., object shape, location, identity, and affordances) that is vital for successful robot manipulation. The authors are motivated to create a latent space that intrinsically captures these key manipulation-relevant features.
- **Leveraging Visual Foundation Models (VFMs):** The recent success of pre-trained VFMs like DINO and CLIP in capturing rich visual features presents an opportunity. The authors are motivated to harness these models to construct a latent space that is inherently aligned with robust geometric and semantic understanding, which they hypothesize will be easier for a world model to learn and generalize.
- **Developing a Robust and Generalizable Policy:** The ultimate goal is to enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks more reliably and generalize to unseen scenarios. This necessitates a policy learning framework that can effectively utilize the high-fidelity, information-rich future predictions from the world model to refine its actions iteratively.

In summary, LaDi-WM aims to overcome the limitations of current world models by predicting the evolution of a richer, VFM-aligned latent space using diffusion modeling, thereby providing superior future guidance for robust and generalizable robotic manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LaDi-WM framework consists of two tightly integrated components: a Latent Diffusion-based World Model (LaDi-WM) for imagining future states, and an Imagination-Guided Diffusion Policy for action planning and iterative refinement.

#### 3.1. Latent Diffusion-based World Model (LaDi-WM)

The core of LaDi-WM is to predict future environmental dynamics by modeling them as a diffusion process in a generalizable latent space.
- **VFM-aligned Latent Space:** Instead of raw pixels or a generic autoencoder latent space, LaDi-WM leverages pre-trained Visual Foundation Models to extract a rich latent representation.
 - **Geometric Features:** DINOv2 (f_dino) is used to extract geometric latent codes (z_D_t), capturing spatial relationships and object structures.
 - **Semantic Features:** Siglip (f_sigl) is used to extract semantic latent codes (z_S_t), providing high-level understanding of object identities and categories.
 - These two distinct representations are concatenated to form the complete latent state: z_t = [z_D_t; z_S_t]. This composite latent space is designed to contain all information crucial for manipulation.
- **Interactive Diffusion Modeling:** Recognizing that DINO and Siglip codes follow different distributions, a novel interactive diffusion process is proposed to model their dynamic correlation without treating them as a single, homogenous vector.
 - **Forward Process:** Gaussian noise is gradually added to both z_D_t+1: and z_S_t+1: independently.
 - **Decomposition Network:** To facilitate interaction, the model first uses decomposition networks (f_θ1, f_θ2) to estimate "clean latent components" (C_D_n, C_S_n) from the noisy DINO and Siglip codes, respectively. These networks are conditioned on historical latent states and action controls.
 - **Interactive Denoising Network:** The key innovation. Two separate denoising networks (f_θ3, f_θ4) are used for DINO and Siglip codes. Crucially, the denoising network for DINO (f_θ3) receives the *clean semantic component (C_S_θ)* as a cross-attention input, while the denoising network for Siglip (f_θ4) receives the *clean geometric component (C_D_θ)*. This cross-attention mechanism allows the geometric and semantic dynamics to interact and influence each other during the denoising process, promoting alignment and richer dynamic modeling.
 - **Architecture:** A Diffusion Transformer architecture is adopted for the world model.
 - **Training Objective:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is used to train the world model to predict the clean latent components and the noise, optimizing for accurate future latent state prediction.

#### 3.2. Predictive Policy Learning

Built upon LaDi-WM, an imagination-guided diffusion policy is designed to solve manipulation tasks through imitation learning.
- **Diffusion Policy Architecture:** A transformer-based diffusion policy is employed, chosen for its ability to handle multimodal action distributions and high-dimensional action spaces, which are common in complex manipulation tasks.
- **Inputs to Policy:** The policy network (π_θ) takes various inputs:
 - Historical latent states (z_t-l:t) from VFMs.
 - Historical end-effector states (e_t-l:t).
 - Task instruction (T).
 - **Imagined Future States (z_hat_t+1:t+k+1):** This is the critical feedback mechanism from LaDi-WM.
- **Iterative Refinement (Inference Time):** This is a unique aspect of the policy.
 1. The policy first generates an *initial* action sequence (a_hat_t:t+k-1).
 2. This initial action sequence is fed into the *trained LaDi-WM*, which then generates corresponding *imagined future states* (z_hat_t+1:t+k).
 3. These imagined future states are then provided as *additional input* to the policy model.
 4. The policy uses these imagined states to produce a *refined* action sequence.
 5. This refinement loop can be iterated multiple times (e.g., 2-4 times), allowing the policy to progressively correct and optimize its actions based on increasingly accurate forecasts from the world model. The authors analyze this convergence, showing it reduces action prediction entropy.
- **Training:** The policy is trained using MSE loss between the denoised action and the ground truth action, effectively learning to leverage the imagined future states for better action prediction.

The combined approach ensures that the world model provides semantically and geometrically rich predictions, and the policy effectively uses these predictions through an iterative refinement process, leading to robust and accurate manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments on both synthetic benchmarks (LIBERO-LONG, CALVIN D-D) and real-world environments demonstrate the significant effectiveness of LaDi-WM.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Improvements

- **LIBERO-LONG Benchmark:** LaDi-WM achieved a remarkable 68.7% average success rate (Avg.SR) with only 10 demonstrations per task, outperforming the behavior cloning (BC) baseline by 27.9% and the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) method, Seer, by 15.1%. With full training data, LaDi-WM reached 90.7% Avg.SR, surpassing Seer by 3%.
- **CALVIN D-D Benchmark:** LaDi-WM showed superiority with an Avg.Len. of 3.63, outperforming vanilla BC by 1.19 and other SOTA methods like ATM and Seer.

#### 4.2. Generalizability

- **Real-World Generalization:** In real-world manipulation tasks with a 7-DOF CR10 robot arm, LaDi-WM improved performance by 20% Avg.SR over vanilla BC.
- **Cross-Scene Generalization:** A world model trained on LIBERO, when transferred to policy learning on CALVIN (distinct scene configurations), achieved an Avg.Len. of 3.05, surpassing vanilla BC and ATM, demonstrating its ability to generalize across different environmental setups.

#### 4.3. Scalability

- **World Model Training Data:** Increasing the training data for the world model from 500 to 4,500 demonstrations progressively reduced test MSE and improved policy learning performance.
- **Policy Learning Training Data:** LaDi-WM consistently outperformed Seer across all data scales (from 10 demonstrations to complete data), indicating robust scalability with varying data availability.
- **Model Size:** Scaling up the policy model architecture led to measurable performance gains on both LIBERO-LONG and CALVIN benchmarks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **World Model Architecture:**
 - The diffusion-based architecture outperformed a transformer-only world model by 7.8% Avg.SR.
 - Including semantic information (Siglip features) improved Avg.SR by 3.4%, particularly benefiting tasks involving distinct semantic objects.
 - The novel interactive diffusion process (cross-attention between geometric and semantic components) was 1.8% better than a simple concatenation approach, fostering better alignment between the two latent distributions (visually confirmed in Figure 4).
 - The use of "clean interaction" (decomposing clean components before interaction) was also shown to be beneficial.
- **Iterative Refinement:** This mechanism proved crucial for performance.
 - Without any imagination, the Avg.SR was 40.8%.
 - 1 iteration of imagination boosted it to 60.7% (+19.9%).
 - 2 iterations further improved it to 68.7% (+27.9%).
 - Performance plateaued after 2 iterations, indicating efficient convergence. Analysis showed iterative refinement progressively reduced the entropy of the predicted action distribution, leading to more precise and consistent actions.
- **Effect of World Model:** Our LaDi-WM guidance significantly outperformed both vanilla BC and a "copy model" (which just uses current observation as future imagination), highlighting the true predictive power of the world model.
- **Latent vs. Pixel Diffusion:** Learning dynamics in the proposed compact, VFM-aligned latent space was significantly better, yielding a 14.7% higher Avg.SR compared to a pixel-diffusion world model.
- **Imagined Future Frames:** Optimal performance was observed with 6 imagined future frames. Too many frames led to a slight drop, attributed to compounding errors in longer-term predictions.
- **Denoising Steps:** The policy network achieved convergent performance with just 2 denoising steps, demonstrating the efficiency of the underlying diffusion algorithm.
- **Robotic States:** Using joint states as policy input outperformed end-effector (EE) states by 9.0% Avg.SR.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LaDi-WM framework represents a substantial step forward in predictive manipulation for embodied AI, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** By achieving significant improvements on both synthetic and real-world benchmarks, LaDi-WM sets a new benchmark for predictive manipulation systems, demonstrating superior accuracy and robustness.
- **Leveraging Visual Foundation Models for World Modeling:** The novel integration of DINO and Siglip to construct a geometrically and semantically rich latent space is a pioneering approach. It effectively addresses the limitations of generic latent spaces, proving that explicitly leveraging pre-trained VFM features can dramatically enhance a world model's ability to capture manipulation-relevant dynamics. This could pave the way for more widespread adoption of VFMs in core robotics control loops.
- **Innovative Interactive Latent Diffusion:** The interactive diffusion process, which allows geometric and semantic latent components to dynamically influence each other through cross-attention during denoising, is a clever solution for modeling complex, multimodal dynamics. This mechanism improves the alignment and fidelity of predictions, leading to faster learning convergence and stronger generalization.
- **Robust and Refinable Policies:** The imagination-guided diffusion policy, particularly with its iterative refinement mechanism, provides a powerful paradigm for action planning. By continuously refining actions based on progressively more accurate imagined states, the system can achieve greater precision and consistency, which is crucial for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks. This iterative refinement process is a practical way to harness the world model's predictive power at inference time.
- **Enhanced Generalizability:** The task-agnostic training of the world model and its demonstrated ability to generalize across different scenes and real-world environments is highly significant. This capability is vital for deploying robots in diverse and unstructured settings without requiring extensive retraining for every new task or environment.
- **Practical Applicability:** The strong performance in real-world experiments validates the practical utility and robustness of LaDi-WM, indicating its potential for real-world robotic applications in areas such as manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics.
- **Future Research Directions:** The limitations section, while acknowledging restricted training data scale and challenges with long-term predictions due to compounding errors, opens up promising avenues for future work. Integrating larger datasets (like Open X-Embodiment, DROID) and developing memory mechanisms to maintain long-horizon temporal consistency are critical next steps, pointing towards the development of even more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

Overall, LaDi-WM pushes the boundaries of how world models can be constructed and utilized in robotics, demonstrating a highly effective method for robust, generalizable, and precise predictive manipulation. Its methodology is likely to inspire future research in combining advanced generative models with rich, VFM-derived representations for complex embodied AI tasks.
