# MAMMOTH: A Multi-Modal End-to-End Policy for Off-Road Mobility Robust to Missing Modality

> [!summary] Summary
> MAMMOTH is an end-to-end multi-modal navigation policy for off-road environments designed to operate reliably across varied lighting conditions, including night-time, and despite missing sensor modalities. The system fuses RGB, thermal, 3D pointcloud, and ego velocity data, achieving robust navigation and generating physically-aware trajectories that minimize collisions and undesirable behaviors in real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Autonomous navigation in unstructured off-road environments is challenged by highly variable terrain, drastic illumination changes (glares, shadows, night), and the potential for sensor degradation.
- Existing multi-modal navigation systems often exhibit rigid dependency on complete and perfect sensor inputs, making them fragile to individual sensor failures or partial sensor suites.
- Many end-to-end policies rely heavily on RGB input, leading to poor performance in visually degraded or varied lighting; even those incorporating LiDAR remain vulnerable to missing modalities.

## Method

- **MAMMOTH**, a unified end-to-end policy, employs a ==sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== architecture with ==per-modality routers== for synergistic ==multi-modal fusion== of RGB, thermal, 3D pointcloud, and ego velocity inputs.
- A ==modality dropout== strategy during training enables zero-shot generalization to missing sensor inputs, making the policy robust to real-world sensor failures and deployable on platforms with incomplete sensor suites.
- It integrates an intrinsic ==traversability heuristic==, derived self-supervisedly from IMU data, into a ==diffusion policy== to generate physically-aware trajectories that prioritize safety and stability over purely geometric paths.

## Results

- MAMMOTH consistently outperformed RGB-only and naive multi-modal baselines, demonstrating robust night-time off-road mobility with a collision rate of **0.8** and undesirable behaviors of **0.75**, surpassing baselines' daytime performance.
- The policy maintained strong performance even with **one missing modality**, validating its resilience to sensor degradation; the 3D pointcloud was crucial for collision avoidance, while thermal and ego velocity enhanced terrain understanding.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of MAMMOTH's architectural choices, showing that end-to-end training of vision encoders, **16 experts** with **TopK=4**, and per-modality routers with Laplace gating were optimal for performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The synergistic multi-modal fusion through a sparse MoE and per-modality routers allows dynamic weighting of sensor contributions and deep cross-modal understanding, improving robustness and computational efficiency.
> - Modality dropout during training is an effective method for achieving robustness to missing modalities, enabling high-fidelity generalization without relying on complex reconstruction objectives.
> - Incorporating a physically-aware traversability heuristic into a diffusion policy facilitates the learning of safer, more stable trajectories with minimal computational overhead, beyond just geometric considerations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation in unstructured off-road environments presents significant challenges due to the variability of terrain, dynamic lighting conditions, and potential for sensor degradation. Historically, classical navigation approaches relied on strong geometric priors and pre-built maps, which limited their scalability and generalization to novel or highly dynamic off-road settings.

More recently, learning-based methods have advanced the field, primarily categorizing into two streams: traversability estimation and end-to-end (E2E) policies. Traversability estimation methods, such as SALON, HDIF, RoadRunner M&M, and IRisPath, focus on generating costmaps by fusing multi-modal sensor data. While these methods have shown success in estimating traversability, they often struggle to generalize to low-light conditions and lack inherent mechanisms to handle acute sensor degradation or failure.

End-to-end policies, including ViNT and NoMaD, establish a direct mapping from raw sensor inputs to control actions or local waypoints, demonstrating reactive behaviors. Many of these policies, however, are heavily reliant on RGB visual input. This reliance renders them susceptible to performance degradation in varied lighting conditions, such as glares, shadows, or low ambient light, which are common in off-road environments. Some more recent E2E approaches, like DTG and MTG, incorporate LiDAR pointclouds, but they remain vulnerable to scenarios where specific modalities are missing due to sensor malfunction.

MAMMOTH addresses these identified limitations by proposing an E2E navigation policy that aims for robustness across varying lighting conditions (including night-time) and resilience to sensor degradation. It seeks to combine the benefits of multi-modal perception with the reactivity of E2E policies, while explicitly tackling the issue of missing modalities, a critical aspect largely unaddressed by prior E2E methods in the context of off-road navigation. The work positions itself as an advancement towards system-level robustness and deployability for autonomous systems operating in challenging, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified end-to-end navigation policy, named MAMMOTH, for robust off-road visual-goal-conditioned navigation and undirected exploration. The motivation stems from several critical limitations observed in existing autonomous navigation systems when applied to unstructured off-road environments:

1. **Extreme Environmental Variability:** Off-road environments are characterized by complex and diverse terrains, including ditches, sharp rocks, and tree roots, coupled with drastic illumination changes (e.g., glares, shadows, low ambient light, and complete darkness at night). Information from any single sensor modality often proves insufficient or unreliable under such conditions.
2. **Sensor Degradation and Failure:** These environments also pose a persistent risk of acute sensor degradation or failure due to factors like high vibrations, adverse weather, or low illumination. Current multi-modal methods typically exhibit a rigid dependency on the presence of near-perfect sensor inputs, rendering them incapable of robustly handling individual modality failures.
3. **Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
 - **Traversability Estimation Methods:** While these methods effectively estimate terrain costs, they generally do not generalize well to low-light conditions and lack mechanisms for graceful degradation in the event of sensor failure.
 - **RGB-only End-to-End Policies:** A strong reliance on RGB inputs makes these policies unreliable in visually degraded or varied lighting conditions, which are endemic to off-road settings.
 - **LiDAR-based E2E Policies:** Even methods incorporating LiDAR data remain vulnerable to scenarios where this crucial modality might be absent or degraded.

To address these motivations, MAMMOTH is designed with three core objectives:

- **Synergistic Multi-Modal Fusion:** To leverage the complementary strengths of different sensor inputs (RGB, Thermal, 3D Pointcloud, Ego Velocity) for comprehensive scene understanding and robust mobility in varying light conditions, including night-time. This aims to enable both selective reliance on specific modalities and deep cross-modal understanding.
- **Flexible and Robust Deployment:** To ensure the system remains operational and generalizes effectively even when certain sensor modalities are degraded or entirely missing. This is crucial for real-world deployability on diverse robotic platforms that may not possess a full sensor suite.
- **Physically-Aware Trajectory Generation:** To move beyond purely geometric planning by integrating an intrinsic traversability heuristic into the trajectory generation process. This objective is to allow the policy to learn to prefer smoother and safer trajectories that account for the physical consequences of traversing varied terrain, without incurring significant computational overhead.

The overarching motivation is to develop an E2E policy that demonstrates system-level robustness and high deployability, enabling autonomous navigation in previously challenging conditions such as complete night-time off-road environments and scenarios with sensor failures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MAMMOTH is presented as a unified end-to-end (E2E) navigation policy for off-road environments, designed for both visual-goal-conditioned navigation and undirected exploration, while being robust to diverse lighting and sensor degradation. The policy, $\pi$, maps a history of $k$ multi-modal observations $O_t$ to a distribution over future actions $A$, which are trajectories $T = \{(x_i, y_i, c_i)\}^N_{i=1}$, where $(x_i, y_i)$ are local 2D coordinates and $c_i$ is an intrinsic traversability heuristic. For long-horizon tasks, it is paired with a topological memory.

**Architectural Overview:**
MAMMOTH's architecture consists of modality-specific encoders, fusion layers for cross-modal integration, and a diffusion-based trajectory head.

**Synergistic Multi-Modal Fusion:**
The policy integrates inputs from four modalities: RGB images, thermal LWIR images, 3D LiDAR pointclouds, and ego velocity information. Each observation, current and $k$ past, is encoded into a latent representation. These representations are then processed by fusion layers, each comprising a Multi-Head Self Attention (MHSA) block and a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer. A per-modality router design with Laplace gating is employed. This configuration allows the model to:
- **Selectively rely** on specific modalities by dynamically weighting their contribution based on perceived environmental conditions.
- Achieve **synergistic fusion** by fostering deep cross-modal understanding among modalities.
- Improve **computational efficiency** by using conditional computation, which decouples the total model parameters from per-inference FLOPs.

**Flexible and Robust Deployment:**
To handle acute sensor degradation and missing modalities, MAMMOTH incorporates a **modality dropout** strategy during training. For each training step, a binary mask is sampled for each modality, randomly dropping all tokens corresponding to that modality or a combination of modalities. This "mask-and-ignore" approach forces the model to learn robust cross-modal representations without over-relying on any single input. This directly translates to zero-shot generalization to real-world sensor failures during inference and allows deployment on robotic platforms with incomplete sensor suites. Unlike other methods, it avoids the computational overhead of reconstruction objectives.

**Physically-Aware Trajectory Generation:**
MAMMOTH moves beyond purely geometric planning by training a diffusion policy to learn the joint conditional probability distribution $p(T|C_t)$ over physically-grounded trajectories $T$. Crucially, each trajectory $T$ is augmented with an intrinsic traversability heuristic $c_i$. This heuristic is derived self-supervisedly from the Power Spectral Density (PSD) of the vertical (Z-axis) acceleration signal obtained from the robot's onboard IMU data. The policy is trained as a conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) to reverse a noising process, predicting the injected noise $\epsilon$ from a noisy sample $x_t$, conditioned on the rich multi-modal sensory and goal context $C_t$. This conditioning enables the model to learn the correlation between a spatial path and its associated physical cost. During inference, this allows for trajectory ranking based on the computed traversability heuristic, prioritizing vehicle safety and stability with minimal computational overhead.

**Loss Function:**
The model is trained end-to-end using a supervised loss function: $L = \alpha \cdot L_{Dist} + (1-\alpha) \cdot \beta \cdot L_{Diff} + (1-\beta) \cdot L_{MoE}$. $L_{Dist}$ is the L2 loss for temporal distance prediction. $L_{Diff}$ is the diffusion loss for trajectory generation. $L_{MoE}$ is an auxiliary load-balancing loss that encourages balanced utilization of all experts within the MoE layers.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Datasets:** Training uses a combination of the public TartanDrive 2.0 dataset and a new in-house off-road dataset, totaling over 9 hours of data. The in-house dataset specifically provides thermal LWIR modality and night-time trajectories, addressing gaps in TartanDrive 2.0. Ground truth trajectories and the traversability heuristic (from Z-axis IMU) are generated from odometry data.
- **Encoders:** RGB and thermal inputs (96x96 images) use separate EfficientNet-B0 encoders. Ego velocity is processed via Fourier features and an MLP. 3D pointclouds (48,000 points, 4-channel) are processed by a PointPillars encoder and distilled into a vector.
- **Configuration:** A context of 4 observations ($k=3$ past, $1$ current) is used, tokenized into 256-dimensional embeddings. These are processed by 4 fusion layers, each with 4 attention heads. The MoE backbone uses 16 experts and 4 per-modality routers.
- **Diffusion Policy:** Generates 8 trajectories with 8 waypoints, using a DDPM with a cosine noise scheduler and 10 denoising timesteps. A 1-D Conditional U-Net serves as the noise prediction network.
- **Modality Dropout:** RGB, 3D pointcloud, and Velocity modalities are masked with a probability of $p=0.5$. The thermal modality uses a lower probability of $p=0.2$ to compensate for its limited presence in TartanDrive 2.0.
- **Training and Deployment:** MAMMOTH is trained for 20 epochs with a batch size of 100 on an NVIDIA H100 GPU, using AdamW optimizer with cosine scheduling. Deployment is on an all-terrain robot (Copernicus) equipped with a Livox LiDAR, IMU, RGB, and LWIR cameras, inferring at 2Hz on a NVIDIA Jetson Orin AGX. Experiments are conducted in five distinct real-world off-road environments under both day and night conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluate MAMMOTH through extensive real-world robot experiments, comparing it against several baselines and conducting ablation studies. Performance is measured using mean Collision Rate (CR), goal-reaching Success Rate (SR), and mean "Undesirable Behaviors" (UB).

**A. Comparison with Baselines (Table I):**
- **RGB-only Baselines (NoMaD, FlowNav):** Both NoMaD and FlowNav, even after fine-tuning (FT), exhibited inferior performance across all metrics. They had high CR (e.g., NoMaD-FT: 2.1) and UB (e.g., NoMaD-FT: 1.7) during daytime exploration, and significantly low SR (e.g., NoMaD-FT: 20%). They were unable to operate in night conditions, highlighting the limitations of RGB-centric methods in complex off-road environments and varied illumination. MAMMOTH, in contrast, demonstrated night-time mobility with better CR and UB values (0.8 and 0.75 respectively) than these baselines achieved in daytime.
- **Multi-modal Ablation Baselines:**
 - **Concat-fusion:** A naive multi-modal baseline using simple concatenation for fusion showed drastically poor performance (CR 1.7, UB 1.6 day; CR 1.7, UB 2.1 night), underscoring the importance of a sophisticated fusion backbone.
 - **Modified-NoMaD:** An adaptation of NoMaD to accept multi-modal inputs, using a dense transformer for fusion, showed minor improvements over concat-fusion but still suffered from high CR and UB (1.6, 1.7 day; 1.5, 1.3 night), suggesting that a vanilla dense transformer is insufficient for deep cross-modal understanding in this domain.
 - **MAMMOTH w/o Heuristic:** This variant of MAMMOTH, lacking the intrinsic traversability heuristic, achieved decent collision avoidance due to multi-modal fusion (CR 1.2 day, 1.3 night) but exhibited high UB (1.9 day, 2.0 night), indicating poor terrain understanding.
- **MAMMOTH (Full):** Outperformed all baselines. During daytime, it achieved a CR of 0.7, UB of 0.6, and an SR of 33%. Its most significant advantage was robust night-time mobility, demonstrating CR of 0.8 and UB of 0.75, which were superior to all other baselines' daytime performance. The inclusion of the traversability heuristic significantly reduced UB and CR, indicating improved terrain awareness with negligible computational overhead. The relatively low overall goal-reaching SR across all methods was hypothesized to be due to the lack of distinct features in off-road environments.

**B. Missing Modality Ablations (Tables II and III):**
- **Single-Modality Dropout (Table II):** MAMMOTH performed optimally when all perception modalities were present (CR 0.7 day, 0.8 night; UB 0.6 day, 0.75 night). It maintained good performance even with one missing modality, particularly at night. The 3D pointcloud modality was critical for collision avoidance (CR rose when missing), while thermal and ego velocity improved terrain understanding, especially during night-time.
- **Modality Contribution Analysis (Table III):** Incrementally adding modalities to an RGB-only suite showed:
 - RGB-only performance was poor (CR 1.5 day, 1.7 night; UB 1.7 day, 1.2 night).
 - Adding thermal reduced UB, indicating its role in terrain understanding.
 - Adding 3D pointcloud drastically reduced CR, confirming its importance for obstacle avoidance.
 - The best performance was achieved when all modalities were available, demonstrating MAMMOTH's ability to utilize complementary information through synergistic fusion.

**C. Ablation Study (Architectural Design Decisions, Table IV):**
- **Pretrained Vision Encoder:** Using a pretrained EfficientNet-B0 yielded inferior performance compared to end-to-end training, suggesting that off-road-specific features were not adequately captured by the pretraining data.
- **Number of Experts:** The chosen 16 experts showed optimal performance. 8 experts resulted in higher UB, suggesting insufficient representational complexity, while 32 experts also showed degraded performance, indicating diminishing returns.
- **TopK Values:** A TopK value of 4 was optimal. Smaller (TopK=2) and larger (TopK=8) values led to degraded performance, suggesting an imbalance in expert contributions.
- **Gating Functions and Router Design:** Softmax gating performed well for terrain understanding but resulted in higher CR. A single, joint router performed significantly worse than the per-modality router design with Laplace gating, validating the efficacy of MAMMOTH's specific MoE configuration for multi-modal data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MAMMOTH research contributes to the field of autonomous navigation by addressing critical challenges in unstructured off-road environments. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a unified end-to-end navigation policy that exhibits **system-level robustness** and **high deployability**.

The work proposes a solution that can operate reliably in conditions previously considered highly challenging for autonomous systems:
- **Varied Illumination:** MAMMOTH demonstrates robust navigation in extreme lighting conditions, including glares, shadows, and, notably, complete night-time off-road environments where RGB-only systems typically fail. This significantly expands the operational envelope for autonomous robots.
- **Sensor Degradation and Missing Modalities:** By incorporating a modality dropout training scheme and a sparse Mixture-of-Experts architecture, MAMMOTH shows high-fidelity generalization to scenarios where individual or multiple sensors are degraded or entirely unavailable. This flexibility enhances the practicality and resilience of autonomous platforms, allowing them to remain operational even with partial sensor suites or in the face of real-world sensor malfunctions.
- **Physically-Aware Navigation:** The integration of an intrinsic traversability heuristic, derived from IMU data, into a diffusion policy enables the generation of physically-grounded trajectories that prioritize safety and stability. This mechanism offers a computationally efficient alternative to external, post-hoc guidance methods for terrain-aware planning.

MAMMOTH's contributions are significant for advancing the capabilities of autonomous ground vehicles, particularly in applications such as search and rescue, environmental monitoring, defense, and exploration in remote or hazardous terrains. By providing a policy that is robust to environmental variability and sensor limitations, the research offers a step towards more reliable and adaptable off-road robotic systems. The planned public release of the code and dataset further increases its potential impact, enabling other researchers to build upon these advancements.
