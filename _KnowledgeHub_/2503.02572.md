# RaceVLA: VLA-based Racing Drone Navigation with Human-like Behaviour

> [!summary] Summary
> RaceVLA applies Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to autonomous racing drones, enabling navigation through natural language commands and visual inputs. The system, developed by researchers at Skoltech, achieved stable flight patterns with an average velocity of 1.04 m/s and demonstrated strong generalization capabilities compared to existing VLA models.

## Problem

- Autonomous drone navigation, especially for high-speed applications like drone racing, is challenging due to dynamic environments and the need for split-second decisions.
- Traditional drone autonomy methods, such as planning, trajectory generation, or reinforcement learning, often lack the adaptability and responsiveness required for high-speed, complex racing scenarios.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while promising for robotics, had limited application in drone technology, leaving a gap in human-like, natural language-driven drone control.

## Method

- **RaceVLA** adapts and fine-tunes the OpenVLA model, based on the LLaMA2 7B large language model, for autonomous racing drones.
- The system takes first-person view (FPV) video frames and natural language instructions as input through a modified VLA architecture.
- It outputs a 4D action vector consisting of three linear velocities (Vx, Vy, Vz) and yaw angular speed (ω) for direct, end-to-end drone control.

## Results

- RaceVLA achieved stable flight patterns with an average velocity of **1.04** m/s and a maximum speed of **2.02** m/s during racing tasks.
- The system successfully executed various tasks, including navigating through single gates, square gates, and multi-gate circular track formations.
- RaceVLA demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, outperforming RT-2-X across all tested axes and showing superior performance over OpenVLA in motion (**75.0%** vs. **60.0%**) and semantic (**45.5%** vs. **36.3%**) generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, previously applied to robotic manipulators, can be effectively adapted for real-time, high-speed drone control.
> - An end-to-end VLA approach allows direct mapping from visual and language inputs to control actions, bypassing the need for intermediate representations like maps or trajectories.
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) can successfully adapt large VLA models like OpenVLA to specialized domains such as drone racing while preserving general capabilities.
