# Reward Model Routing in Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> BayesianRouter introduces a hybrid framework for dynamically selecting reward models during large language model (LLM) alignment, integrating offline-learned priors with online Bayesian Thompson sampling to improve alignment quality and efficiency. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance across instruction-following and reasoning benchmarks while maintaining a constant O(1) computational cost for reward model calls.

## Problem

- Current LLM alignment methods often rely on a single reward model, leading to limited generalizability across diverse tasks, high computational costs for powerful RMs, and risks of overoptimization or "reward hacking."
- Existing reward model (RM) routing approaches, like LASER, suffer from coarse-grained batch-level selection, insufficient exploration in discovering optimal RMs, and inefficient cold-start phases.
- Dynamically choosing the most suitable reward model for each query from a diverse pool remains a challenge for robust, scalable, and efficient LLM alignment.

## Method

- A hybrid reward model (RM) routing framework, **BayesianRouter**, is introduced, consisting of an offline router and an online Bayesian router, for per-query RM selection during DPO training.
- The offline router is trained on static preference datasets to learn relative RM competence and specialization, providing prior knowledge about RM strengths.
- An online Bayesian router uses Thompson sampling to adaptively select RMs for individual preference pairs, balancing exploration and exploitation, with its initial state informed by the offline router's priors to mitigate cold-start issues.

## Results

- BayesianRouter consistently achieved the highest scores across all evaluated instruction-following (e.g., AlpacaEval-2: **63.23**) and reasoning (e.g., GSM8K: **75.66**) benchmarks.
- The framework outperformed individual reward models, ensemble techniques, and prior routing methods like LASER, demonstrating enhanced alignment quality with only O(1) reward model calls per preference pair.
- Ablation studies confirmed the complementary importance of both the offline-learned priors and the online Bayesian Thompson sampling, showing performance degradation when either component was removed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating prior knowledge of reward model (RM) capabilities learned offline with an online adaptive selection mechanism through Bayesian Thompson sampling yields a more robust and efficient routing strategy.
> - Selecting RMs at the instance (preference-pair) level using an uncertainty-aware Bayesian approach offers superior performance compared to batch-level selection or simpler online methods.
> - Encoding the entire preference pair (prompt and both responses) rather than just the prompt provides richer context for the router to make more accurate RM assignments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has led to significant advancements in artificial intelligence. A critical aspect of deploying these models effectively and safely is "alignment," the process of fine-tuning LLMs to adhere to human values and preferences. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its AI-augmented variant (RLAIF) have emerged as the dominant paradigms for achieving this alignment. These methods typically rely on a reward model (RM) to provide a scalar feedback signal for optimizing the policy LLM, often through techniques like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

The prevailing practice in RLHF/RLAIF pipelines involves using a single reward model throughout the training process. However, this approach faces several limitations:
1. **Limited Generalizability**: No single RM has been shown to perform optimally across all tasks or domains, leading to suboptimal alignment when a fixed RM is applied universally. Benchmarks like RewardBench 2 highlight this inconsistency.
2. **High Costs**: Employing powerful, general-purpose LLMs (e.g., GPT-4/5) as RMs can provide high-quality feedback but incurs prohibitive inference costs at scale, making them impractical for extensive RLHF training.
3. **Risk of Overoptimization**: Over-reliance on a single RM can lead to overfitting to its specific biases or noise, causing the policy LLM to exploit the RM's flaws (reward hacking) rather than genuinely aligning with human intent.

To address these challenges, research has explored leveraging ensembles of reward models. While naive ensembling (running multiple RMs in parallel for every query) can combine strengths, it significantly increases computational cost (from O(1) to O(N) RM calls) and can introduce conflicting signals. Consequently, RM routing methods have gained interest. These methods dynamically select the most suitable RM for each input, aiming to retain the benefits of diversity while maintaining O(1) RM calls.

LASER (Nguyen et al., 2024) is a notable prior work that introduced instance-level RM routing in RLHF, framing it as a contextual multi-armed bandit problem using LinUCB. However, LASER exhibits its own limitations:
1. **Coarse-grained Routing**: It selects one RM per *batch* of prompts, which may not be optimal for individual prompts within the same batch that might favor different RMs.
2. **Limited Exploration**: LinUCB, based on point estimates and optimism, can prematurely converge to a suboptimal RM, leading to insufficient exploration of alternatives.
3. **Inefficient Cold-Start**: At the beginning of training, LASER assumes equal RM quality and requires extensive interactions to identify specialized strengths, leading to suboptimal RM usage and reduced sample efficiency in early stages.

This paper positions itself as an advancement in the RM routing landscape by proposing a hybrid framework that addresses the aforementioned limitations of existing methods, particularly LASER, thereby aiming to improve the robustness and scalability of LLM alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and efficient reward model (RM) routing framework, termed BayesianRouter, that enhances the alignment quality of large language models (LLMs) while mitigating the inherent challenges of single-RM and existing routing approaches in RLHF/RLAIF pipelines.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current LLM alignment practices:
1. **Overcoming Single Reward Model Shortcomings**: Current RLHF/RLAIF pipelines frequently rely on a single RM, which is suboptimal due to its limited generalizability across diverse tasks and domains. A single RM cannot consistently excel in all contexts (e.g., conversational helpfulness versus mathematical reasoning), leading to a bottleneck in alignment quality. Moreover, using a single, powerful general-purpose LLM as an RM is prohibitively expensive for large-scale training. Finally, reliance on a single RM increases the risk of "reward hacking," where the policy LLM exploits the RM's specific biases or noise rather than genuinely aligning with human preferences. The research seeks to leverage the complementary strengths of multiple RMs to address these issues.

2. **Improving Upon Existing RM Routing Methods**: While RM routing, which dynamically selects an RM from a pool for each input, offers a promising direction by preserving the benefits of model diversity while maintaining O(1) RM calls, current methods like LASER have their own limitations:
 - **Suboptimal Granularity**: LASER selects an RM at the batch level, meaning prompts within a single batch, despite potentially requiring different RM expertise, are processed by the same chosen RM. This coarse-grained approach can lead to suboptimal choices for individual queries. The motivation is to achieve *instance-level* RM selection for more precise routing.
 - **Inefficient Exploration**: LASER's use of LinUCB for online selection, which relies on point estimates and optimism, can lead to premature exploitation of seemingly good RMs and insufficient exploration of alternatives. This can hinder the discovery of RMs truly optimal for specific query types. A more robust exploration strategy is motivated.
 - **Cold-Start Problem**: At the beginning of training, LASER lacks prior knowledge about RM strengths and must gather extensive interaction data to identify them. This slow initialization phase can result in suboptimal RMs being used early on, impacting sample efficiency and making training outcomes sensitive to initial conditions. The research aims to provide an efficient cold-start mechanism.

To address these motivations, BayesianRouter is designed as a hybrid framework with specific objectives:
- To learn an "offline router" that can pre-estimate the reliability and specialization of RMs for a given query by training on existing preference datasets. This component aims to provide rich prior knowledge.
- To integrate this offline knowledge with an "online Bayesian router" that performs per-query RM selection using Bayesian Thompson sampling. This online component aims to adapt to the evolving policy distribution and balance exploration and exploitation more effectively.
- To demonstrate that this combined approach consistently outperforms individual RMs, RM ensembling, and existing routing methods across various LLM alignment benchmarks, thereby offering a more robust and scalable solution for LLM alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BayesianRouter is a hybrid RM routing framework designed for adaptive RM selection within preference-based alignment pipelines, specifically targeting the DPO family of algorithms. For each input preference pair (prompt and two candidate responses), BayesianRouter selects a single, most appropriate RM from a candidate pool to evaluate the pair, and the resulting preference signal is used to train the policy model. The framework consists of an offline RM router and an online Bayesian RM router, with a principled integration strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Method Overview

The method operates within an online Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) setup. At each training step, a policy model `π_t` generates `k` candidate responses for a batch of prompts. A reward model `R` then evaluates these candidates to form preference pairs `(x_i, y_w_i, y_l_i)`, which are used to update `π_t` via the DPO loss. The core problem is to select the most suitable RM `R_n` from a pool `M = {R_n}^N_{n=1}` for each unlabeled preference pair `(x_i, y_j_i, y_j'_i)`, maintaining O(1) RM calls per step.

#### 3.2. Offline RM Router

The offline router is trained on static preference data to predict which RM is most likely to correctly identify the preferred response for a given pair.

- **Collecting RM Behavior Data**: Each candidate RM `R_n` is run on an existing human-annotated offline preference dataset `ˆD_pref = {(x_i, y_i, y'_i, ℓ_i)}`, where `ℓ_i` indicates the ground truth preferred response. A binary indicator `δ^(n)_i` records whether `R_n` agrees with `ℓ_i`. This creates `D_beh = {(q_i, δ^(n)_i)}`.

- **Preference-Pair Feature Construction**: Unlike prior work that only uses the prompt, BayesianRouter encodes the entire preference pair `(x_i, y_i, y'_i)`. A shared pre-trained encoder `Enc(·; W_e)` generates embeddings `e_i = Enc(x_i ∥ y_i)` and `e'_i = Enc(x_i ∥ y'_i)`. These are then aggregated into a single preference-pair representation `h_i` using an MLP: `h_i = MLP([e_i + e'_i; |e_i - e'_i|]; W_l)`. The SmolLM2-135M-Instruct model is used as the encoder.

- **Multi-Task Objective**: The offline router is trained with two prediction heads:
 - **Bradley-Terry (BT) Head (Primary)**: This head learns relative RM competence. It uses a "disagreement set" `D_bt`, comprising preference pairs where two RMs (`R_n`, `R_n'`) have differing behavior labels (`δ^(n)_i = 1, δ^(n')_i = 0`). An embedding matrix `E_bt ∈ R^(N×d)` is learned, where `E_bt[n]` represents RM `R_n`. BT scores `s^(n)_i = ⟨h_i, E_bt[n]⟩` are computed, and a pairwise logistic (Bradley-Terry) loss `L_bt = -Σ log σ(s^(n)_i - s^(n')_i)` is optimized, encouraging higher scores for RMs that align with ground truth when others do not.
 - **Classification (CLS) Head (Auxiliary)**: This head exploits the full `D_beh` dataset. It learns an embedding matrix `E_cls ∈ R^(N×d)`. For each RM `R_n`, it predicts its behavior label `δ^(n)_i` using `z^(n)_i = ⟨h_i, E_cls[n]⟩` and optimizes a binary cross-entropy loss `L_cls` over `D_beh`. This provides complementary supervision and shared representation learning.

- **Training and Output**: The router minimizes `L_total = L_bt + λL_cls`. After training, the `E_bt` matrix, capturing relative RM strengths, is retained as a prior.

#### 3.3. Bayesian Online RM Router

The online router adaptively selects RMs during online DPO training and updates its posterior distribution based on observed rewards. It addresses distribution shift and cold-start issues.

- **Contextual Bandit Formulation**: The problem is framed as a contextual bandit, where candidate RMs are arms, the preference pair embedding `h_i` is the context, and partial feedback (reward from the selected RM) is observed.
- **Bayesian Thompson Sampling**: This method is adopted instead of LinUCB (used in LASER) to encourage exploration and robustly discover RM specializations. Each RM `R_n` maintains a Gaussian posterior `w_n ~ N(μ_n, Σ_n)` for its latent weight vector `w_n` (modeling `r = w_n^T h_i + ε`).
- **RM Selection**: At each step `t`, for each preference pair `h_i`, a sample `w^(t)_n` is drawn from each RM's posterior. The RM `R_n*` with the highest expected utility (`argmax_n h_i^T w^(t)_n`) is selected.
- **Posterior Update**: After `R_n*` provides a scalar reward `ˆr_i^(n)` for its assigned pairs, its posterior (`μ_n, Σ_n`) is updated using standard Bayesian linear regression formulas. Only the selected RM's posterior is updated.
- **Reward Normalization**: To provide stable learning signals, the raw DPO loss `-L_DPO` is transformed into an advantage-style reward `r_i(t) = ¯ℓ_t - L^(i)(t)` (batch-level baseline centering), which is then further quantile-rescaled to `ˆr_i(t)` using past rewards.

#### 3.4. Offline–Online Integration

The `E_bt` embeddings from the offline router are used to initialize the prior mean (`μ^(0)_n = E_bt[n]`) for each RM's weight vector in the online Bayesian router. This "prior injection" strategy provides initial knowledge about RM strengths, mitigating the cold-start problem and improving early routing accuracy. The online updates then refine these posteriors, allowing adaptation to the evolving policy-induced data distribution while retaining the offline insights as a regularizer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of BayesianRouter involved extensive experiments on instruction-following and reasoning benchmarks, comparing its performance against various baselines.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Policy Model**: LLaMA3-SFT-v2.
- **Reward Model Pool**: Four small, high-performing RMs from the RewardBench 2 leaderboard were selected: Mistral-RM-for-RAFT-GSHF-v0 (RM 0), GRM-Llama3.2-3B-rewardmodel-ft (RM 1), GRM-gemma2-2B-rewardmodel-ft (RM 2), and Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-0.6B (RM 3).
- **Offline Router Encoder**: SmolLM2-135M-Instruct.
- **Offline Preference Datasets**: HelpSteer3 and RM-Bench (50,402 preference pairs combined).
- **Instruction-Following Benchmarks (Test Only)**: AlpacaEval-2, MT-Bench, Chat-Arena-Hard.
- **Reasoning Benchmarks (Train/Test Splits)**: GSM8K and MMLU.
- **Baselines**:
 - **Single RM**: Each individual RM from the pool.
 - **Majority vote**: Ensembling by majority voting across all RMs.
 - **Random router**: Randomly selects an RM for each preference pair.
 - **Uncertainty-Weighted Optimization (UWO)**: Ensembles by down-weighting preference pairs with high RM disagreement.
 - **LASER**: Previous state-of-the-art RM routing method using LinUCB for batch-level selection.
 - **Ablations**: `w/o offline` (BayesianRouter without offline priors) and `w/o online` (BayesianRouter using only the offline router, fixed).

#### 4.2. Main Results

Table 1 presents the primary performance metrics across benchmarks:
1. **Consistent Outperformance**: BayesianRouter consistently achieved the highest scores across all instruction-following benchmarks (AlpacaEval-2: 63.23, MT-Bench: 58.75, Chat-Arena-Hard: 66.20) and reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K: 75.66, MMLU: 57.39). This indicates its adaptability to diverse task distributions.
2. **Superiority over Single RMs**: BayesianRouter outperformed all individual RMs, including the best-performing ones (e.g., RM1 on instruction-following or RM3 on reasoning). This demonstrates the effectiveness of dynamic routing in aggregating complementary strengths without manual selection of a single "best" RM.
3. **Efficiency and Performance vs. Ensembles**: The method significantly surpassed ensemble approaches like Majority Vote and UWO. While ensembles can leverage multiple RMs, they incur O(N) RM calls. BayesianRouter achieves higher performance with only O(1) RM calls, making it more practical for scaling.
4. **Advancement over Existing Routing**: BayesianRouter substantially outperformed other routing baselines, including Random Router and LASER. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed hybrid routing strategy, particularly the per-query Bayesian Thompson sampling over LASER's batch-level LinUCB.
5. **Complementary Contributions of Hybrid Components**: The ablation studies (`w/o offline` and `w/o online`) showed notable performance degradation when either the offline-learned prior or the online Bayesian feedback loop was removed. This highlights the complementary nature and importance of both stages. Notably, the `w/o offline` variant still exceeded LASER, indicating the intrinsic advantage of the per-query Bayesian Thompson sampling approach.

#### 4.3. Additional Analysis of BayesianRouter

- **Effectiveness of Offline Router (Table 2)**: The offline router demonstrated strong performance in routing preference pairs, particularly in the in-distribution (ID) setting (90.31% accuracy on HelpSteer3) and consistent, though smaller, gains in the out-of-distribution (OOD) setting (87.92% on RewardBench 2). The gap to the oracle performance (100%) underscores the generalization challenges of purely offline routing and the importance of online adaptation. The multi-task objective (including the CLS head) was validated as beneficial, and larger encoders offered modest improvements.
- **Controlled Simulation of Online DPO (Table 3)**: A simulation using human-labeled RewardBench 2 data, where the router selected RMs to label pairs with known ground truth, confirmed that BayesianRouter achieved the highest annotation accuracy during "training" (88.23%) and consequently the best downstream alignment performance (e.g., AlpacaEval-2: 57.63). This directly links routing quality to alignment gains, isolating the effect from other factors.
- **Ablation on Integration Strategy (Table 4)**: Comparing BayesianRouter with a `Weighted-score` variant (simple linear combination of offline and online scores) demonstrated that prior injection is a more principled and effective integration mechanism. BayesianRouter consistently outperformed `Weighted-score` across all datasets.
- **Training Efficiency (Figure 2)**: BayesianRouter maintains O(1) RM calls, incurring overhead only for preference pair encoding and lightweight Bayesian posterior updates. These overheads are independent of the number and size of candidate RMs. Experiments on GSM8K with an expanded RM pool showed that BayesianRouter, while slower than the single fastest RM, substantially outperformed Majority Voting and the single slowest RM. It was marginally slower than LASER, primarily due to encoding preference pairs independently versus reusing policy embeddings. This confirms its efficiency and scalability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents BayesianRouter, a novel and robust framework for adaptive reward model (RM) selection that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Alignment Quality**: By dynamically selecting the most appropriate RM for each individual query, BayesianRouter effectively aggregates the complementary strengths of multiple RMs. This leads to superior alignment performance across diverse instruction-following and reasoning tasks compared to relying on a single, fixed RM, which often struggles with generalization across domains. The consistent outperformance against strong baselines, including prior routing methods, suggests a more effective policy optimization signal.

2. **Improved Efficiency and Scalability**: BayesianRouter maintains an O(1) computational cost for RM calls per preference pair, unlike naive RM ensembles that incur O(N) costs, where N is the number of RMs. This efficiency is critical for scaling RLHF/RLAIF pipelines to larger RM pools or more extensive training. The framework's overhead from preference pair encoding and Bayesian updates is lightweight and amortizes well as the candidate RM pool grows, demonstrating practical scalability.

3. **Robustness Against Overoptimization and Cold-Start**: The hybrid architecture addresses two critical issues:
 - **Overoptimization**: By routing among diverse RMs, the policy LLM is less likely to overfit to the idiosyncratic biases of a single RM, mitigating the risk of reward hacking and promoting more generalized alignment.
 - **Cold-Start**: The offline router provides an informed prior for the online Bayesian router, accelerating the learning process from the beginning of training. This eliminates the inefficient exploration phase seen in methods lacking initial knowledge, leading to faster convergence and more stable outcomes.

4. **Principled Exploration-Exploitation Balance**: The adoption of Bayesian Thompson sampling for online RM selection provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for balancing exploration (discovering which RMs are best for which contexts) and exploitation (using the currently best-known RM). This uncertainty-aware approach is shown to be more effective than optimism-based methods like LinUCB, leading to more reliable RM assignments.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work lays a foundation for further research in adaptive RM selection, particularly in jointly optimizing annotation accuracy and RM inference cost. This could involve dynamically routing to smaller, more efficient RMs when appropriate, further reducing computational budgets without sacrificing quality. It also suggests avenues for improving offline data collection and utilization to create even stronger initial priors.

In summary, BayesianRouter offers a practical and effective solution to long-standing challenges in LLM alignment, paving the way for more robust, efficient, and higher-quality AI systems through advanced reward modeling.
