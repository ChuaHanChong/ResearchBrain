# ZeroSearch: Incentivize the Search Capability of LLMs without Searching

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called ZEROSEARCH enables training large language models (LLMs) to leverage search capabilities by replacing costly real-world search engine APIs with a simulated LLM environment. This approach from Alibaba Group's Tongyi Lab achieves superior performance while substantially reducing training costs.

## Problem

- Training large language models (LLMs) to effectively use real-world search engines via reinforcement learning (RL) incurs prohibitively high API costs.
- Real search engines provide uncontrolled document quality, which can lead to unstable and inefficient RL training for search-augmented LLMs.
- LLMs inherently have static knowledge, leading to issues like hallucination and an inability to access real-time information without external tools.

## Method

- Introduces an RL framework that replaces real search engine interactions with a ==lightweight fine-tuned LLM acting as a 'simulation search engine'== during training.
- Employs a ==curriculum learning-based rollout mechanism== that dynamically controls the probability of generating 'noisy' documents, progressing from easier (more useful) to harder (more noisy) training scenarios.
- Utilizes a rule-based ==F1 score for reward calculation== and applies loss masking for document tokens to ensure stable and effective reinforcement learning.

## Results

- ZEROSEARCH consistently outperforms all baseline methods, including models trained with real search engines like Search-R1, achieving higher average Exact Match (EM) scores across diverse question answering datasets.
- The framework significantly reduces training costs, cutting expenses by over **85%** compared to using commercial search APIs (e.g., **~$70.8** vs. **$586.7** for 64,000 search requests over 12 hours of training).
- It demonstrates strong generalizability across different LLM sizes (**3B** to **7B**) and model families, with performance improving as the simulation LLM size increases, and benefits from curriculum rollout for enhanced stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models (LLMs) can effectively simulate real-world search engines, providing a controllable and cost-efficient environment for training search-augmented LLMs.
> - Training LLMs with a simulated search environment yields superior performance compared to using real search engines, largely due to enhanced training stability and controlled document quality.
> - A curriculum learning strategy that gradually increases the proportion of noisy documents during training improves the robustness and reasoning capabilities of search-augmented LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated proficiency across various tasks, including reasoning, question answering, and code generation. However, their internal knowledge is static, derived solely from pre-training data, which renders them susceptible to generating factual inaccuracies or outdated information (hallucinations). To enhance reliability and factual grounding, integrating external information sources is a recognized necessity.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a prominent strategy to address this limitation. Initial RAG efforts relied on prompt-based techniques, guiding LLMs through query generation, decomposition, and multi-turn retrieval. While effective, these methods often require extensive prompt engineering and high reasoning capabilities from the LLM. Subsequent advancements involved supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for smaller LLMs and test-time scaling techniques, such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), which, despite their promise, introduce significant computational overhead.

More recently, reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a method to improve LLM reasoning and decision-making, moving beyond explicit step-by-step supervision. In the context of information retrieval, RL has been applied to train policy models for effective search. Some studies, like DeepResearcher and WebThinker, have explored live interaction with commercial search engines, aiming to train models in environments closely mirroring real-world web search. This approach, however, faces two primary challenges: the unpredictable quality of documents returned by live search engines, which can introduce noise and instability into training, and the high API costs associated with the frequent search requests required for extensive RL training rollouts, limiting scalability.

ZEROSEARCH positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome the limitations of RL-based search training that relies on real search engines. It proposes an alternative framework that maintains the benefits of RL for search capability enhancement while mitigating the issues of uncontrolled document quality and prohibitive API costs through a simulated search environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ZEROSEARCH is to develop a novel reinforcement learning framework that can effectively incentivize the search capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) without requiring interaction with real-world search engines during the training phase. This objective is driven by two significant challenges identified in existing RL-based approaches for training LLMs with search capabilities:

1. **Uncontrolled Document Quality:** When LLMs are trained using live commercial search engines, the quality and relevance of the retrieved documents are inherently unpredictable. This variability can introduce noise into the training data, making it difficult for the policy model to consistently learn optimal search strategies. High-quality documents might lead to rapid but brittle learning, while low-quality or irrelevant documents can hinder progress or lead to unstable training, as the model struggles to discern useful information from noise. The motivation here is to establish a controlled training environment where the quality of retrieved information can be systematically managed to foster more stable and effective learning.

2. **Prohibitively High API Costs:** Reinforcement learning, especially during the rollout phase, often necessitates a substantial number of interactions with the environment to explore different actions and gather sufficient experience. In the context of search-augmented LLMs, this translates to hundreds of thousands of search requests made to commercial search engine APIs. Such extensive API usage incurs considerable financial costs, which can become a major barrier to scalability and broad adoption of these advanced RL training methodologies, particularly for research groups or organizations with limited budgets. The motivation is to significantly reduce or eliminate these recurring API costs, thereby making RL training for search more accessible, scalable, and economically feasible.

By addressing these challenges, ZEROSEARCH aims to provide a robust and cost-efficient method for enhancing LLMs' ability to utilize external search tools, ultimately improving their reasoning and generation capabilities in real-world applications without the practical limitations of direct real-world search engine interaction during training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ZEROSEARCH proposes an RL framework that replaces real search engine interactions during training with a simulated search environment. The core idea leverages an LLM itself to simulate the search engine, generating documents in response to queries.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning Objective

The optimization objective for the policy model ($\pi_\theta$) is formulated to maximize an F1 score-based reward function ($r_\phi$) while maintaining a penalty for divergence from a reference model ($\pi_{ref}$). Crucially, the simulation LLM ($\pi_\psi$) has fixed parameters throughout the training process, meaning the policy model learns to interact with a consistent simulated environment.

$$ \max_{\pi_\theta} E_{x \sim D, y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x;\pi_\psi)} [ r_\phi(x, y) ] - \beta D_{KL} [ \pi_\theta(y | x; \pi_\psi) || \pi_{ref}(y | x; \pi_\psi) ] $$

#### 3.2. Training Template

The policy model interacts with the simulated environment through a multi-turn template that structures its reasoning and retrieval process. This template involves three distinct stages:
- **Reasoning:** The model articulates its internal thought process within `<think>...</think>` tags.
- **Searching:** If external knowledge is required, the model issues a search query within `<search>...</search>` tags.
- **Answering:** Once sufficient information is gathered, the model provides its final answer within `<answer>...</answer>` tags.
This structured approach promotes a transparent and organized decision-making flow.

#### 3.3. Search Simulation Tuning

To enable an LLM to effectively simulate a search engine, a lightweight supervised fine-tuning (SFT) procedure is applied.
1. **Data Collection:** Interaction trajectories are gathered by prompting an LLM to interact with a real search engine until an answer is reached. From these trajectories, query-document pairs are extracted.
2. **Document Labeling:** An LLM acts as a judge to assess whether each retrieved document contains sufficient information to answer its corresponding query. Documents are labeled as either "useful" or "noisy."
3. **SFT for Simulation LLM:** The LLM undergoes SFT to learn to generate both useful and noisy documents in response to queries. The distinction between these output types is controlled by specific keywords ("useful" or "noisy") in the prompt template for the simulation LLM. The input question and its ground-truth answer are also incorporated into the prompt to broaden the simulation LLM's knowledge base. After SFT, this "simulation LLM" is capable of generating documents with controllable quality.

#### 3.4. Rollout with Curriculum Search Simulation

During the RL rollout phase, the policy model generates search queries, which are then fed to the fine-tuned simulation LLM. To gradually increase the training difficulty, a curriculum learning-based rollout mechanism is implemented. The probability of generating noisy documents ($p_i$) increases progressively over training steps, following a function:
$$ p_i = p_s + \frac{b^{i/m} - 1}{b - 1} (p_e - p_s) $$
where $p_s$ and $p_e$ are the initial and final noise probabilities, $i$ is the current training step, $m$ is the total number of training steps, and $b$ is an exponential base (defaulting to 4). This curriculum allows the policy model to first learn basic output structures and task requirements with predominantly useful documents, then progressively adapt to more challenging scenarios with increasing proportions of noisy information.

#### 3.5. Reward Design

A rule-based reward function, focusing solely on answer accuracy, is employed. The F1 score is used as the reward metric:
$$ r_\phi(x, y) = \frac{2 \times IN}{PN + RN} $$
where $IN$ is the number of overlapping words between prediction and ground truth, $PN$ is the number of words in the prediction, and $RN$ is the number of words in the ground truth. This F1-based reward aims to balance precision and recall, mitigating issues like "reward hacking" observed with exact match (EM) where models might generate excessively long answers. Explicit rewards for output format are omitted, as models were observed to produce well-formed responses inherently.

#### 3.6. Training Algorithm

ZEROSEARCH is compatible with various RL algorithms, including REINFORCE, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). A critical component for training stability is a **loss masking mechanism**. Rollout sequences contain both tokens generated by the policy model and document tokens returned by the simulation LLM. Since the document tokens are externally generated and not directly controlled by the policy model, gradients are only computed with respect to the policy model's own outputs. This masking prevents instability that would arise from attempting to optimize over uncontrollable external inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ZEROSEARCH was evaluated on several question answering benchmarks, including single-hop (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA) and multi-hop (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle) datasets, using Exact Match (EM) as the evaluation metric. Experiments were conducted with Qwen-2.5-7B/3B (Base/Instruct) and LLaMA-3.2-3B (Base/Instruct) models.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison

ZEROSEARCH consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to various baselines, including vanilla prompting methods (Direct Answer, CoT, RAG), advanced RAG methods (RAgent, Search-o1), and RL tuning methods (R1, Search-R1). This advantage was observed across both in-domain (NQ, HotpotQA) and out-of-domain datasets, indicating the robustness and generalizability of the method. Notably, ZEROSEARCH outperformed Search-R1, which relies on a real search engine (Google Web Search via SerpAPI), suggesting its effectiveness as an alternative for large-scale RL training. Performance generally improved with larger policy models.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Real Search Engine and Training Dynamics

Reward curves showed that ZEROSEARCH achieved a smoother and more pronounced reward improvement over training steps compared to Search-R1 (using a real search engine). This was attributed to the controlled document quality in ZEROSEARCH, which allows for gradual task difficulty progression, in contrast to the unpredictable nature of real search results. Both base and instruction-tuned models demonstrated consistent reward improvements, with base models eventually reaching performance levels comparable to their instruction-tuned counterparts. An analysis of interaction turns showed an initial sharp drop as the model learned to invoke the search engine correctly, followed by a stabilization where the model efficiently conducted multi-turn searches.

#### 4.3. Choice of Simulation LLMs

The performance of the policy model was significantly influenced by the simulation LLM used:
- **Fine-tuned vs. Prompt-based:** Fine-tuned simulation engines consistently outperformed prompt-based ones, highlighting the importance of SFT for mimicking real search engine behavior and bridging the stylistic gap.
- **Model Size Impact:** A fine-tuned 7B LLM used as the simulation engine achieved performance comparable to Google Search, while a 14B simulation LLM surpassed it. This indicated that larger, well-trained LLMs can effectively substitute real search engines and that performance scales with the simulator's model size and its ability to distinguish useful from noisy documents. Even a 3B LLM proved effective in incentivizing search capabilities.

#### 4.4. Compatibility with RL Algorithms

ZEROSEARCH was found to be compatible with REINFORCE, GRPO, and PPO, with all three algorithms enhancing the model's search capabilities. REINFORCE yielded the best performance, consistent with findings in prior research like Search-R1, and was noted for its advantages in training stability.

#### 4.5. Curriculum Rollout Study

The curriculum rollout strategy, which incrementally degraded document quality, consistently outperformed a random rollout baseline (fixed 0.5 noise probability) across different policy models (Qwen-2.5-3B-Base and LLaMA-3.2-3B-Base). This confirmed the effectiveness of gradually increasing task difficulty, allowing the policy model to first grasp basic search invocation and output formats before adapting to more complex, noisy scenarios.

#### 4.6. Effect of Document Token Loss Masking

Ablation studies demonstrated that applying loss masking to document tokens, preventing gradients from being computed on them, was crucial for training stability and effectiveness. Without masking, a substantial drop in reward and overall model performance was observed.

#### 4.7. Cost Analysis

A cost comparison revealed that ZEROSEARCH significantly reduced expenses compared to using commercial search APIs (e.g., SerpAPI). For approximately 64,000 search requests over 12 hours of training, the GPU cost for a 14B simulation LLM was estimated at $70.8, whereas the API cost for Google Search was $586.7. This highlights the substantial cost efficiency of the simulated approach, especially for large-scale RL training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ZEROSEARCH represents a methodological advancement in training Large Language Models (LLMs) to effectively utilize external search capabilities. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to address critical practical and theoretical challenges in the current research landscape:

1. **Overcoming Training Barriers for RL-based Search:** The framework directly tackles the two main limitations of existing RL-based search training methods: the unpredictable quality of real search engine results and the prohibitive API costs. By introducing a controllable, simulated search environment, ZEROSEARCH provides a stable and consistent training signal, mitigating noise and enhancing learning effectiveness. This eliminates the financial barrier, making advanced RL techniques for search more accessible to researchers and developers who might otherwise be constrained by budget.

2. **Enhanced Scalability and Robustness:** The significant reduction in training costs allows for more extensive and thorough RL training, which is crucial for optimizing complex search strategies. The curriculum-based rollout mechanism, by gradually increasing task difficulty, fosters stronger reasoning capabilities and improves the robustness of the policy model to varying document qualities, moving beyond the simple "easy-to-hard" paradigm to a controlled degradation. This systematic approach can lead to more resilient and adaptive LLMs in real-world search scenarios.

3. **Generalizability Across Models and Algorithms:** The demonstrated compatibility with various LLM architectures (base and instruction-tuned, different parameter sizes) and RL algorithms (REINFORCE, PPO, GRPO) suggests that ZEROSEARCH is a versatile framework. This broad applicability increases its utility across different research and development contexts, allowing flexibility in choosing backbone models and optimization techniques.

4. **Pioneering LLMs as Simulators:** A broader implication of this work is the potential for LLMs to act as sophisticated simulators of real-world environments. By fine-tuning an LLM to mimic the behavior of a search engine, the paper showcases a paradigm where complex external systems can be emulated by other LLMs, opening new avenues for training agents in various domains without direct, costly, or risky real-world interactions. This could extend beyond search to other interactive tasks where simulating the environment is beneficial.

5. **Practical Deployment and Cost Efficiency:** For organizations aiming to integrate search capabilities into their LLMs, ZEROSEARCH offers a significantly more cost-efficient pathway for training compared to reliance on commercial APIs. While it requires initial GPU infrastructure for the simulation LLM, this is a one-time setup cost that is quickly amortized over numerous training runs, especially when the simulation server can be shared. This practical benefit could accelerate the development and deployment of retrieval-augmented LLMs in various applications.

**Limitations:** Despite its advantages, the approach requires the deployment of the simulated search LLM on GPU servers, which introduces an infrastructure cost. While this cost is substantially lower than recurrent commercial API expenses, it is not zero and requires access to computational resources. This implies that while the method is more accessible, it still has a hardware requirement.

Overall, ZEROSEARCH offers a practical and effective solution to a significant bottleneck in RL-based LLM training for search, promising to make such advanced capabilities more scalable, affordable, and robust for future AI development.
