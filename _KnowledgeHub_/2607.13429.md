# Generalizable VLA Finetuning via Representation Anchoring and Language-Action Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and collaborators developed Anchor-Align, a finetuning method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that addresses catastrophic forgetting and language-action misalignment by anchoring pretrained VLM representations and aligning language with actions. This approach led to a 22.6% success rate on challenging out-of-distribution tasks, real-world success rates increasing to 54.2-60.0% for different VLA architectures, and a substantial improvement in language-action alignment to 78.4%.

## Problem

- Standard behavior cloning finetuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often leads to catastrophic forgetting of the pretrained VLM's high-level semantic and spatial representations.
- Existing VLA co-training methods frequently result in language-action misalignment, where the model's internal language predictions contradict its action predictions on the same observation.
- These issues hinder VLA models' generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios and compromise their robustness in real-world deployments.

## Method

- Introduces **Anchor-Align**, a finetuning strategy augmenting ==behavior cloning== with two new objectives: ==Vision-Language Anchoring== and ==Language-Action Alignment==.
- ==Vision-Language Anchoring== distills layer-wise representations from a frozen copy of the pretrained VLM into the trainable VLA backbone, preserving original semantic and spatial priors.
- ==Language-Action Alignment== explicitly aligns language and action predictions by programmatically converting continuous action targets into discrete, language-based ==motion-direction labels== for joint supervision on the same observation.

## Results

- Anchor-Align significantly improved generalization, achieving **22.6%** success on the LIBERO-PRO position-swap task where baselines scored near zero, and demonstrated higher robustness against various perceptual perturbations.
- In real-world xArm7 experiments, the method consistently improved success rates (e.g., from **28.3%** to **54.2%** for VLA-Adapter, and **36.7%** to **60.0%** for StarVLA) and reduced semantic and memorization errors.
- The approach effectively prevented catastrophic forgetting, retaining **70%** of GQA accuracy after **10K** finetuning steps (versus **6%** for standard BC), and increased language-action alignment from **16.8%** to **78.4%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Catastrophic forgetting of pretrained VLM representations can be directly combated by distilling hidden states from a frozen anchor VLM, thereby maintaining high-level semantic knowledge.
> - Explicitly aligning the VLM's internal language predictions with its generated actions using programmatically derived language labels significantly improves policy coherence and performance.
> - Preserving high-level semantic priors and enforcing language-action consistency are complementary goals, leading to enhanced generalization and robustness in VLA policies without complex architectural changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent paradigm for developing robotic manipulation policies. These models typically adapt pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by finetuning them on expert robot demonstrations, primarily using behavior cloning (BC). The underlying assumption is that VLMs, trained on vast internet-scale image-text data, possess robust semantic and spatial understanding that can be transferred to robot control, enabling generalization to novel objects and tasks.

However, the authors identify two persistent issues with this standard BC finetuning approach. First, the finetuning process can lead to "catastrophic forgetting," where the VLM's original, high-level visuolinguistic representations are overwritten. This degradation compromises the model's ability to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, such as those involving novel object positions or semantic variations. While co-training with auxiliary web image-text data has been proposed as a remedy to preserve these representations, the authors demonstrate that this approach is often insufficient.

Second, co-training often supervises the VLA's action head and language head disjointly on separate observations. The action head is trained on robot demonstrations, while the language head is trained on generic image-text tasks. This separate supervision can result in "language-action misalignment," where the model's internal language predictions may contradict its action predictions on the same observation. Standard manipulation benchmarks typically do not expose this misalignment, allowing policies to appear capable while lacking coherent internal reasoning.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a finetuning method, Anchor-Align, designed to explicitly address both catastrophic forgetting of VLM representations and language-action misalignment. It builds upon existing VLA architectures, such as VLA-Adapter and StarVLA, which directly finetune pretrained VLMs, by introducing a modified finetuning recipe that enhances generalization and robustness without requiring additional large-scale robotic pretraining or complex architectural changes. The research positions itself as a method for preserving and aligning pretrained VLM priors, suggesting a path for more effective adaptation of general-purpose foundation models to embodied robotics.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generalizable finetuning method for VLA policies that mitigates the limitations of standard behavior cloning (BC) approaches. Specifically, the authors aim to address two critical failure modes observed during VLA finetuning:

1. **Preventing Catastrophic Forgetting of Pretrained VLM Representations:** Standard BC finetuning often overwrites the rich visuolinguistic and spatial understanding acquired by VLMs during their internet-scale pretraining. This loss of semantic priors impairs the VLA's ability to generalize to novel situations, such as variations in object attributes (e.g., color) or spatial arrangements. The motivation is to preserve these valuable representations, which are crucial for OOD generalization, rather than allowing them to be corrupted during adaptation to robot control tasks. The authors demonstrate this issue with examples such as a VLA trained to pick up a green mug failing to pick up a pink mug when instructed, or reverting to memorized trajectories when object positions are swapped. Existing solutions like co-training with auxiliary image-text data are shown to be inadequate in preventing this representational drift.

2. **Addressing Language-Action Misalignment:** In current co-training paradigms, the action prediction head and the language understanding head of a VLA are supervised on different types of data (robot actions vs. generic image-text pairs), even though they share a common VLM backbone. This can lead to a situation where the model's language output might contradict its motor commands for the same observation, indicating a lack of coherent internal reasoning. The motivation is to ensure that the VLA's language understanding is semantically aligned with its action generation, so that when the policy predicts a movement in a certain direction, its internal language representation also reflects that same direction. This alignment is hypothesized to improve task success and policy robustness.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose "Anchor-Align," a finetuning strategy that integrates two novel components:
- **Vision-Language Anchoring:** Designed to directly prevent catastrophic forgetting by distilling layer-wise representations from a frozen copy of the pretrained VLM into the trainable VLA backbone. This objective aims to maintain the VLM's original semantic and spatial priors.
- **Language-Action Alignment:** Engineered to explicitly align the language and action predictions by supervising both heads on the same robot observation. This is achieved by programmatically converting continuous action targets from expert demonstrations into discrete, language-based motion-direction labels.

The overarching motivation is to demonstrate that preserving the integrity of pretrained VLM representations and actively aligning them with action learning are not conflicting goals, but rather complementary steps that lead to significantly improved generalization, robustness, and long-horizon control for VLA policies in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Anchor-Align augments the standard Behavior Cloning (BC) finetuning process for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with two additional objectives. The approach builds upon existing VLA architectures that utilize a pretrained VLM backbone and a dedicated action head.

#### 3.1. Base VLA Architecture

The core VLA architecture processes visual observations and language instructions through a pretrained VLM backbone, which generates multimodal representations. Robot proprioceptive state is incorporated as a conditioning signal via a lightweight state projector. Task-relevant information is extracted from the vision-language representations using learned action queries, either through joint self-attention within the backbone or cross-attention in the action head. The action head then predicts a fixed-horizon chunk of continuous 7-Degrees of Freedom (DoF) actions (3D translation, 3D rotation offsets, and gripper control). The action head can employ either direct regression (using an L1 loss) or conditional flow matching (using an L2 loss over predicted velocity). The authors utilize VLA-Adapter (with a Prismatic-Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone and a regression head) and StarVLA (with a Qwen2.5-VL backbone and a GR00T FM-DiT flow-matching head) as base architectures for their evaluations.

The overall training objective combines the standard BC loss with the two proposed objectives:
$L_{total} = L_{action} + \lambda_{anchor}L_{anchor} + \lambda_{align}L_{align}$
where $\lambda_{anchor}$ and $\lambda_{align}$ are weighting factors for the anchoring and alignment losses, respectively.

#### 3.2. Vision-Language Anchoring

To combat catastrophic forgetting and preserve the VLM's pretrained representations, Vision-Language Anchoring introduces a distillation loss. A frozen copy of the pretrained VLM, referred to as the "anchor VLM," processes the same input batch (images and text) in parallel with the trainable VLA backbone. The hidden states of the vision and text tokens from the trainable backbone are then regularized to match those of the frozen anchor VLM at every decoder layer.

The per-layer anchoring loss is calculated as the squared Frobenius norm of the difference between the hidden states of the trainable backbone ($H_S^{(u)}$) and the anchor VLM ($H_A^{(u)}$) at decoder layer $u$:
$L_{anchor}^{(u)} = \|H_S^{(u)}[m] - H_A^{(u)}[m]\|^2_F$
where $m$ denotes the set of vision and text token positions. The total anchoring loss $L_{anchor}$ is the average of these per-layer losses across all decoder layers $D$:
$L_{anchor} = \frac{1}{|D|}\sum_{u \in D} L_{anchor}^{(u)}$
This objective directly encourages the finetuned backbone to retain the representational geometry of its pretrained state, acting as a direct form of knowledge preservation without requiring additional labeled data.

#### 3.3. Language-Action Alignment

To ensure that the VLA's language predictions are consistent with its generated actions, Language-Action Alignment introduces a joint supervision objective. This objective converts continuous action targets from expert demonstrations into discrete, language-based motion-direction labels. Both the action head and the VLM backbone's language output are then supervised on the same robot observation using these labels.

The language-action alignment process involves:
1. **Language Label Construction:** For each non-stationary segment of a ground-truth demonstration trajectory, the continuous 3D translational components of the action are averaged over a short chunk. Stationary samples (where the magnitude of the average translation is below a threshold $\tau$) are filtered out. For the remaining segments, the dominant translation axis (X, Y, or Z) and its sign determine one of six discrete direction labels: "up," "down," "left," "right," "forward," or "backward." This process generates language labels ($a_{lang}$) from continuous action data without human annotation.
2. **Language Prediction:** The last-layer hidden state of the last instruction token from the VLM backbone, immediately preceding action prediction, is extracted ($h_{pre}$). This state, summarizing visual and linguistic context, is projected onto the vocabulary through a learned projection ($W_{proj}$) and the frozen pretrained language head ($W_{lm}$). This yields logits over the vocabulary ($\hat{a}_{lang}$).
3. **Alignment Loss:** The alignment loss ($L_{align}$) is then computed as the cross-entropy between these predicted logits ($\hat{a}_{lang}$) and the ground-truth discrete motion-direction label ($a_{lang}$):
 $L_{align} = CE(\hat{a}_{lang}, a_{lang})$
This objective forces the backbone's internal representations to generate language outputs that are consistent with the robot's intended actions on the same observation, thereby grounding action generation in VLM semantics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Anchor-Align was evaluated across various simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, employing two distinct VLA architectures.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmark Comparison

- **LIBERO-PRO (OOD Generalization):** Anchor-Align demonstrated superior performance across all out-of-distribution (OOD) perturbation axes. Notably, on the challenging "position-swap" test, where object positions are rearranged at evaluation (requiring generalization beyond memorized trajectories), Anchor-Align VLA achieved 22.6% success, significantly higher than other baselines that scored 0% or near-zero (e.g., VLA-Adapter at 2.3%). This indicates improved visual grounding and spatial understanding. On "language rephrase" and "object swap" axes, Anchor-Align also showed higher success rates (97.0% and 96.2%, respectively) compared to baselines.
- **LIBERO-Plus (Perceptual Robustness):** The method consistently improved robustness against various real-world deployment variabilities. Anchor-Align VLA outperformed VLA-Adapter on every axis, with the most substantial gains observed in "background texture" (+8.9%), "sensor noise" (+7.4%), "robot initial state" (+6.5%), and "lighting" (+5.8%). The model achieved high success rates under these perturbations, such as 99.6% for background texture and 99.0% for lighting changes.
- **CALVIN ABC→D (Long-Horizon Generalization):** For multi-step, language-conditioned tasks, Anchor-Align VLA consistently achieved higher success rates at all chain lengths (1/5 to 5/5 instructions completed) and resulted in the longest average rollout length (4.5 tasks out of 5) compared to all baselines, including strong action-pretrained VLAs like OpenVLA-OFT. This suggests that preserving VLM representations and aligning them with actions improves the policy's ability to maintain task progress over longer horizons.
- **Standard LIBERO Suites:** Even on in-distribution tasks, Anchor-Align VLA achieved state-of-the-art results across the Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites, further underscoring its general effectiveness.

#### 4.2. Impact of Anchoring and Alignment (Ablation Studies and Controls)

- **Necessity of Both Components:** Ablation studies showed that both Vision-Language Anchoring and Language-Action Alignment individually improved performance over the standard BC baseline on LIBERO-PRO and LIBERO-Plus. Combining both objectives in Anchor-Align VLA yielded the best overall performance, indicating their complementary roles.
- **Alignment is Not Mere Regularization:** Control experiments (Shuffle and Scatter) designed to test if the alignment loss acted as generic regularization demonstrated that corrupting the semantic content of the direction labels caused performance to collapse to baseline levels. This confirmed that the benefits of Language-Action Alignment arise from genuine semantic alignment, not just from adding an auxiliary classification task.

#### 4.3. Real-World Results (xArm7 Robot)

- **Consistent Generalization Across Architectures:** Anchor-Align VLA consistently improved real-world generalization across different perturbation regimes (Spatial Rearrangement, Cluttered Scene, Compositional Object-Layout) and for two distinct VLA architectures: VLA-Adapter (regression head) and StarVLA (flow-matching diffusion head).
 - For VLA-Adapter, mean success increased from 28.3% to 54.2%.
 - For StarVLA, mean success increased from 36.7% to 60.0%.
- **Semantic Perturbation:** On a challenging semantic perturbation test (trained on "green mug," tested on "pink mug"), Anchor-Align VLA picked the correct "pink mug" in 100% of trials, while the standard BC baseline defaulted to its training prior, picking the "green mug" in 90% of trials. This provides strong evidence that Anchor-Align preserves and utilizes pretrained VLM semantic concepts.
- **Failure Mode Analysis:** Inspection of real-world failures indicated that Anchor-Align VLA significantly reduced "semantic errors" (e.g., picking a visually similar distractor), "incorrect object" approaches, and "memorization errors" (replaying training trajectories).
- **Faster Task Completion:** In successful real-world rollouts, Anchor-Align VLA policies completed tasks approximately 1.7 times faster on average than standard BC policies, with tighter completion time distributions. This was attributed to more decisive and higher-magnitude action predictions, particularly during critical grasping phases.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Language Preservation and Alignment

- **VLM Representation Preservation:** Quantifying visual reasoning ability using GQA accuracy during finetuning showed that standard BC lost 94% of its accuracy within 10K steps (catastrophic forgetting), whereas Anchor-Align retained 70%. Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) analysis confirmed that standard BC significantly degraded pretrained text representations layer-wise, while Anchor-Align maintained high CKA scores.
- **Action Decodability:** Linear probing revealed that Anchor-Align VLA achieved the highest action decodability (R^2 = 0.60) while simultaneously maintaining strong language preservation (CKA = 0.91), indicating that it enriches pretrained representations rather than destroying them.
- **Language-Action Alignment Quantification:** A diagnostic tool converting robot actions to language labels was used to measure alignment. Standard BC and co-trained VLAs exhibited low language-action alignment (e.g., VLA-Adapter at 16.8% alignment) and near-zero correlation between alignment and task success. Anchor-Align VLA raised alignment significantly (to 78.4%) and established a strong positive correlation (+0.51) between alignment and task success, confirming that coherent internal representations translate to better performance. This misalignment was observed across multiple diagnostic axes (direction, task completion, grasp, orientation) in other state-of-the-art co-trained VLAs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates a significant advancement in the finetuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot manipulation. The introduction of Anchor-Align addresses two fundamental limitations of standard behavior cloning: the catastrophic forgetting of pretrained VLM representations and the misalignment between the language and action outputs of VLA policies.

The core significance of this work lies in demonstrating that effective VLA finetuning does not necessitate a trade-off between preserving high-level semantic knowledge from large VLMs and acquiring precise control for embodied tasks. Instead, Anchor-Align shows that by explicitly anchoring VLM representations and aligning language with action, policies can achieve both robust generalization and accurate control.

**Key impacts include:**
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The substantial improvements on OOD benchmarks like LIBERO-PRO's position-swap and real-world semantic perturbations highlight the model's ability to reason about novel object configurations and semantic instructions, moving beyond rote memorization of training trajectories. This is crucial for deploying robots in diverse and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Improved Robustness:** Consistent gains on LIBERO-Plus across various perceptual perturbations (e.g., lighting, background changes, sensor noise) indicate that Anchor-Align policies are more resilient to the inherent variability of real-world sensor data.
- **Better Long-Horizon Control:** The performance on CALVIN suggests that the method supports more reliable execution of complex, multi-step instructions, where errors can compound over time.
- **Efficient and Data-Agnostic Improvements:** A notable aspect is that Anchor-Align achieves these gains without requiring additional external data, new architectural components, or substantial increases in training computational overhead compared to co-training methods. The proposed objectives leverage supervision already present within the demonstration data.
- **Diagnostic Tools:** The programmatic framework for converting continuous actions into language labels offers a valuable diagnostic tool for understanding and quantifying language-action misalignment in VLA models, which can inform future research directions.
- **Generalizability Across Architectures:** The demonstration of consistent improvements across different VLM backbones (0.5B vs. 3B parameters) and action head types (regression vs. flow-matching) indicates that Anchor-Align is a broadly applicable finetuning recipe, not tied to specific architectural choices.

The findings suggest a promising paradigm for adapting future vision-language foundation models to embodied tasks, allowing them to leverage their vast web-scale knowledge more effectively and reliably. The principles of anchoring and alignment could also extend beyond VLAs, offering a concrete path for post-training action-conditioned video world models to retain broad spatiotemporal priors while becoming more controllable and semantically grounded, potentially by enforcing action-outcome and instruction-rollout consistency. This research paves the way for more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents.
