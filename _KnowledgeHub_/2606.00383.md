# Behavior Cloning of MPC for 3-DOF Robotic Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Waterloo developed a behavior cloning approach to approximate Model Predictive Control (MPC) policies for a 3-degree-of-freedom robotic manipulator. Their method achieved a three-fold reduction in inference latency, reaching approximately 1 millisecond per control step, and a 25% decrease in CPU utilization compared to the original MPC, with an average final tracking error of 2.9 centimeters.

## Problem

- Model Predictive Control (MPC) faces significant computational burden, making it challenging for real-time control in resource-constrained robotic systems.
- Robotic manipulators require fast, consistent control loop execution for dynamic interaction, which traditional MPC's fluctuating latency often hinders.
- The need for computationally efficient and real-time capable control policies for complex robotic tasks.

## Method

- A ==behavior cloning== framework was employed to train neural networks to mimic an "expert" hierarchical controller, which combined Inverse Kinematics (IK) and Model Predictive Control (**MPC**).
- A dataset of expert demonstrations, including current robot state, target position, and MPC-generated torques, was collected from a MuJoCo simulation.
- Various neural network architectures, including Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) like GRUs, were systematically evaluated for their ability to predict MPC control torques.

## Results

- The optimal `MLP_Deep` architecture achieved a mean inference latency of approximately **1.1** ms per control step, a three-fold reduction compared to the MPC expert.
- It also demonstrated a **25%** decrease in CPU utilization while achieving an **84.98%** success rate under relaxed tolerances (5 cm) and a mean final tracking error of **2.9** cm.
- Offline evaluations showed that deep Multi-Layer Perceptrons (e.g., `MLP_Deep` with MSE around **0.05**) delivered high accuracy and explained variance, significantly outperforming linear models and GRU architectures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) consistently outperformed Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and MLPs augmented with sliding windows, indicating that the immediate robot state provides sufficient information for the control decision.
> - For MLP architectures, increasing network depth was found to be more beneficial than increasing width for accurately capturing the non-linear inverse dynamics of the robot.
> - A precision gap under strict error tolerances (2.9 cm average final error) suggests that while behavior cloning captures the expert's global trajectory, it lacks MPC's continuous gradient-based error correction.
