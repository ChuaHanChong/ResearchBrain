# PanoEnv: Exploring 3D Spatial Intelligence in Panoramic Environments with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce PanoEnv, a framework featuring a synthetic, geometry-grounded benchmark and a reinforcement learning method, which substantially improves Vision-Language Models' 3D spatial reasoning in panoramic images. The PanoEnv-RL model achieves a 132% relative gain in open-ended 3D reasoning, outperforming larger baselines and showing strong sim-to-real transferability.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with 3D spatial reasoning in 360° panoramic (ERP) images due to severe geometric distortions and misaligned 2D learning priors.
- Existing VQA datasets for panoramic images lack precise, objective 3D geometric ground truth, hindering the development and evaluation of true 3D spatial intelligence.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) alone is insufficient for fostering complex, generative reasoning and robust 3D understanding in VLMs.

## Method

- Development of ==PanoEnv-QA==, a large-scale VQA benchmark with over **14.8K** geometry-grounded QA pairs programmatically generated from synthetic 3D environments (TartanAir), providing objective ground truth for depth, segmentation, and 3D bounding boxes.
- Introduction of ==PanoEnv-RL==, a 3D-aware reinforcement learning post-training framework using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== on a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), equipped with a multi-faceted, geometry-aware reward system leveraging PanoEnv-QA's ground truth.
- Implementation of a ==two-stage curriculum strategy== for stable RL training, starting with structured questions and progressing to a balanced mixture of structured and open-ended questions to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and enhance generative spatial reasoning.

## Results

- Benchmarking reveals that state-of-the-art VLMs achieve low performance on PanoEnv-QA, with the best baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) reaching only **49.34%** overall accuracy and a mere **6.39%** on open-ended questions.
- PanoEnv-RL (GRPO-Balanced) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves overall accuracy to **52.93%** and dramatically boosts open-ended question accuracy to **14.83%**, representing a **+132%** relative gain over the baseline.
- The PanoEnv-RL model demonstrates robust zero-shot transferability to real-world datasets like OSR-Bench, outperforming larger baselines (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B) on tasks such as Object Counting and Relative Distance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLMs are largely incapable of robust 3D spatial reasoning in panoramic environments, particularly for open-ended questions requiring precise metric or compositional relations.
> - Leveraging precise 3D geometric ground truth from synthetic environments for reinforcement learning reward signals, combined with a progressive curriculum, enables VLMs to acquire complex 3D spatial intelligence stably and effectively.
> - Training on high-fidelity synthetic data with physically-grounded rewards can instill transferable geometric logic, allowing models to generalize zero-shot to real-world panoramic reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and growing challenge at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and embodied AI: enabling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to robustly understand and reason about 3D spatial relationships within 360-degree panoramic environments.

**Evolution of Visual Question Answering (VQA):** The field of VQA has evolved significantly since early datasets focused on object recognition (e.g., VQA, VQA v2.0). Subsequent efforts shifted towards more complex reasoning, including spatial and logical relationships (e.g., GQA, CLEVR) and commonsense understanding (VCR). More recent benchmarks like What's-Up and VSI-Bench explore spatial understanding and cognitive mapping. However, the vast majority of these advancements have been confined to 2D perspective images, which fundamentally differ from omnidirectional panoramas.

**Challenges of Omnidirectional Vision:** Omnidirectional images (ODIs), or 360° panoramas, offer a holistic view of a scene, making them invaluable for applications like VR/AR, autonomous navigation, and robotics. Unlike pinhole cameras, they capture long-range context and off-axis relations, enabling global awareness. However, when these images are represented using Equirectangular Projection (ERP), they introduce severe geometric distortions (e.g., stretching near the poles, scale and shape variations) and nonlinear mappings of 3D relations to 2D coordinates. These distortions make tasks that are simple in perspective views, such as determining relative positions or object sizes, remarkably challenging for conventional VLMs trained primarily on 2D data. Existing VQA datasets for panoramas, such as VQA 360°, OSR-Bench, and 360-R1, represent important early efforts. However, PanoEnv distinguishes itself by providing a combination of panoramic coverage, dense 3D geometry, and fine-grained, *physics-grounded supervision* derived from synthetic environments, which is crucial for training and evaluating true 3D reasoning.

**Role of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in VLMs:** While supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is standard for VLMs, its ability to foster complex, generative reasoning can be limited. Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly with human feedback (RLHF) or self-play, has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enhancing reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs). Recent works like 3D-R1, Robot-R1, VLN-R1, and Nav-R1 have extended RL to 3D spatial understanding and embodied AI, demonstrating its potential for complex geometric reasoning and physical interaction. In the panoramic VQA domain, 360-R1 also applied RL, but its reward function relied on semantic similarity, potentially introducing biases from LLM judges or human annotations. PanoEnv advances this by deriving its rewards directly from objective, verifiable 3D geometric ground truth from a simulation engine, ensuring the learning signal is anchored in physical reality rather than subjective interpretation or hallucination. This emphasis on physically-grounded rewards for RL in the context of panoramic 3D spatial reasoning is a significant contribution to the broader landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation of this research is to overcome the inherent limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in comprehending and reasoning about the 3D spatial characteristics of scenes presented in 360° Equirectangular Projection (ERP) images. The authors identify two primary challenges: the significant geometric distortion introduced by ERP, which misaligns with VLMs' 2D learning priors, and the scarcity of existing VQA datasets that provide precise 3D ground truth for omnidirectional scenes.

To address these issues, the paper sets forth three key objectives:

1. **Develop a Large-Scale, Geometry-Grounded Panoramic VQA Benchmark:** The first objective is to create "PanoEnv-QA," a novel and extensive Visual Question Answering benchmark specifically designed to test 3D spatial intelligence on ERP images. This benchmark is unique because its questions are programmatically generated from synthetic 3D environments, allowing for pixel-perfect, objective ground truth annotations including depth, segmentation, and 3D bounding boxes. This foundation ensures that questions require genuine 3D reasoning, not just 2D heuristics, and provides verifiable supervision signals for training.

2. **Conduct Comprehensive Benchmarking of State-of-the-Art VLMs:** The second objective is to rigorously evaluate the capabilities of current state-of-the-art VLMs on the PanoEnv-QA benchmark. This evaluation aims to systematically expose their weaknesses in 3D spatial understanding, particularly for complex, open-ended questions that demand metric reasoning or compositional multi-axis relations. The expected outcome is to demonstrate a significant performance gap, thereby motivating the need for specialized training paradigms.

3. **Propose and Validate a 3D-Aware Reinforcement Learning Post-Training Framework:** The third and most ambitious objective is to design and implement a reinforcement learning (RL) framework that can effectively instill 3D spatial intelligence into VLMs. This framework must leverage the precise 3D ground truth of PanoEnv-QA for its reward signals, address the inherent instability of RL training for language generation, and mitigate catastrophic forgetting of previously learned capabilities. The goal is to develop a robust method that can significantly improve VLMs' ability to perform geometry-grounded reasoning in omnidirectional environments.

In essence, the motivation stems from the recognition that while 360° panoramas are vital for holistic scene understanding in many advanced applications, existing AI models are ill-equipped to handle their unique geometric complexities. This research aims to bridge that gap by providing both the necessary diagnostic tools (PanoEnv-QA) and a targeted solution (PanoEnv-RL) to foster true 3D spatial intelligence in VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **PanoEnv**, a comprehensive framework encompassing both a large-scale, geometry-grounded panoramic VQA benchmark (PanoEnv-QA) and a 3D-aware reinforcement learning (RL) post-training methodology (PanoEnv-RL).

#### 3.1. PanoEnv-QA Construction

The PanoEnv-QA benchmark is meticulously constructed from synthetic 3D environments to ensure precise and verifiable ground truth.

- **Data Foundation:** The dataset is built upon **TartanAir**, a synthetic dataset renowned for its accurate 3D ground truth (depth, segmentation, 3D poses). For each scene, six perspective views from a cubemap camera setup are merged into a high-resolution ERP image. This process ensures perfectly aligned RGB, depth, and semantic segmentation modalities, facilitating pixel-level correspondence between appearance, geometry, and semantics. This programmatic generation of ground truth is a cornerstone, enabling objective reward calculation.
- **Object Filtering and Sampling Heuristics:** To ensure high quality and physical validity, a rigorous pipeline filters objects based on 2D bounding box area (≥ 900 pixels), width/height (≥ 25 pixels), aspect ratio (< 5), and semantic exclusion of uninformative background categories (e.g., sky, wall, rubble). For comparative questions, object pairs with significant 2D bounding box overlap (> 0.90) are excluded to prevent ambiguity.
- **Question-Answer Pair Generation:** Over 14.8K QA pairs are automatically generated across five distinct categories, progressively increasing in complexity and requiring deeper 3D understanding:
 - **Camera View Source Identification:** Assesses if the model understands that an ERP image is a composite of multiple views. It identifies which of the six cubemap views (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) an object originates from using spherical coordinates derived from 2D pixel locations and their corresponding 3D unit direction vectors.
 - **Object Distance Estimation:** Evaluates quantitative and qualitative depth reasoning. Ground truth depths are extracted from ERP depth maps. Robust depth profiles are computed using median (p50) and inter-quartile range (IQR) to account for object thickness and partial occlusions. For thick objects, the near-end quantile (p20) is used as the effective depth.
 - **Environment Identification:** Tests high-level scene understanding and contextual reasoning (e.g., indoor/outdoor, urban/nature). It leverages TartanAir's ground-truth environment labels, employing semantically chosen distractors for multiple-choice questions.
 - **Relative Spatial Positioning:** Assesses the ability to reconstruct accurate 3D spatial relationships (e.g., "above," "in front of"). Object 2D centroids and median depths are inversely projected into a unified 3D Cartesian coordinate system. Linguistic labels are derived by comparing vector differences between object centroids against thresholds.
 - **Intrinsic Attribute Comparison:** Probes view-independent physical properties like 3D shape and size. For each object, all pixels within its segmentation mask are projected into 3D space to form a point cloud, from which a tight 3D bounding box and its physical dimensions (length, width, height) are computed. This enables questions about Volume Comparison and Shape Analysis (e.g., "flatter," "more elongated" based on a flatness score).
- **Data Quality Assurance:** A human verification study on a random subset confirmed a 96% accuracy rate for the programmatically generated ground truth, validating its reliability and alignment with human perception.

#### 3.2. 3D-Aware RL Post-Training

To address the limitations of baseline VLMs identified through benchmarking, the authors propose a reinforcement learning post-training framework.

- **Model Backbone and Optimization:** The **Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct** model serves as the backbone. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, a variant of PPO optimized for language generation, is employed. GRPO computes advantages by comparing responses within a group of K candidate responses from the current policy, using the group's mean reward as a baseline. A Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty against the original pre-trained policy (`π_ref`) is included to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- **Multi-faceted Reward System:** This is a core innovation, leveraging the PanoEnv-QA ground truth for precise feedback. The total reward `R(s, a)` is a weighted combination of accuracy (`R_acc`, w_acc = 0.9) and format correctness (`R_fmt`, w_fmt = 0.1).
 - **Format Reward (`R_fmt`):** A binary reward (1.0 or 0.0) for strictly adhering to the `<Reasoning>...</Reasoning><Answer>...</Answer>` output structure.
 - **Accuracy Reward (`R_acc`):** Uses an automatic routing mechanism to apply one of five specialized, geometry-aware strategies based on the question type:
 - **Strategy A (True/False):** Strict, case-insensitive string match.
 - **Strategy B (Multiple Choice):** Smart subject extraction, normalization, and comparison.
 - **Strategy C (Distance):** Numerical parsing, unit conversion to meters, and a scaled reward based on relative error (e.g., 1.0 for ≤10% error, 0.5 for ≤20% error).
 - **Strategy D (Spatial Relationship):** Parses directional keywords across three independent axes. Reward is the fraction of correctly identified dimensions.
 - **Strategy E (Counting):** Requires an exact numerical match (digits or textual numbers).
- **Two-Stage Curriculum Strategy:** To stabilize RL training, especially with heterogeneous reward signals and open-ended generation, a curriculum learning approach is used:
 - **Stage 1: Structured Pretraining (GRPO-Structured):** Initial training is performed solely on structured questions (True/False, Multiple Choice). These low-entropy tasks provide reliable rewards, allowing the policy to quickly learn output formatting and stable discrete decision-making.
 - **Stage 2: Mixed Open-Ended Training (GRPO-Balanced):** The model, initialized from Stage 1, is then fine-tuned on a balanced mixture of structured and open-ended questions. This stage focuses on improving generative open-ended spatial reasoning while mitigating catastrophic forgetting of the structured skills learned in Stage 1.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that the model learns from objective physical reality and progressively builds complex 3D reasoning skills in a stable and efficient manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating both the inadequacy of current VLMs for panoramic 3D spatial reasoning and the effectiveness of the proposed PanoEnv framework.

- **PanoEnv-QA Dataset Characteristics:**
 - The constructed PanoEnv-QA dataset comprises 14,827 high-quality QA pairs across 595 panoramic scenes, derived from 60 diverse virtual environments.
 - Question distribution is balanced, with each of the five core categories (Camera View Source Identification, Object Distance Estimation, Environment Identification, Relative Spatial Positioning, Intrinsic Attribute Comparison) constituting roughly 20% of the dataset.
 - The dataset exhibits high diversity in answer characteristics, including binary, quantitative, object names, and full-sentence descriptions, with an average answer length of 10.9 characters. Importantly, the Yes/No ratio for binary questions is balanced (45.3% vs. 54.7%), preventing models from exploiting statistical shortcuts.
 - Human verification confirmed a high reliability of the programmatically generated ground truth, with a 96% accuracy rate, reinforcing its suitability for objective evaluation and RL supervision.

- **Benchmarking State-of-the-Art VLMs (Baseline Performance):**
 - Evaluation of 14 state-of-the-art VLMs on a 3,040-sample test set revealed generally poor performance in zero-shot 3D spatial reasoning on panoramic images.
 - The best performing baseline, **Qwen2.5-VL-7B**, achieved an overall accuracy of only 49.34%.
 - Performance on **open-ended (OE) questions** was particularly dismal, with the best OE accuracy being a mere 8.36% (achieved by DeepSeek-VL2-Base and Qwen2.5-VL-32B). Qwen2.5-VL-7B managed only 6.39%.
 - These results highlight a significant gap between current VLM capabilities and the demands of true 3D spatial understanding in distorted panoramic environments, especially for generative tasks that require precise metric reasoning or complex compositional relations.

- **RL Post-Training Results (PanoEnv-RL Framework):**
 - Our full **GRPO-Balanced** model, fine-tuned on Qwen2.5-VL-7B using the two-stage curriculum and ground-truth-guided rewards, achieved a new state-of-the-art performance on PanoEnv-QA.
 - It reached an overall accuracy of **52.93%**, representing a notable improvement of 3.59% over the best zero-shot baseline.
 - Crucially, the model demonstrated a dramatic improvement in **open-ended (OE) question accuracy, rising from 6.39% (baseline) to 14.83%**. This represents a remarkable **+132% relative gain** in OE reasoning capabilities.
 - The model also achieved the highest LLM-judge scores for semantic correctness and reasoning quality, with a **Q-Score of 6.24** and a **P-Score of 5.95**, even surpassing much larger 26B and 32B baseline models.
 - Performance on structured tasks (True/False and Multiple Choice) was maintained or slightly improved (from 60.46% to 62.89%), demonstrating that the curriculum successfully mitigated catastrophic forgetting.
 - Training dynamics (Fig. 4) visually confirmed the efficacy of the two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 rapidly improved format adherence and structured decision-making, while Stage 2 built upon this foundation to enhance OE reasoning without sacrificing earlier gains.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Ablation experiments confirmed the critical role of the two-stage curriculum. The **GRPO-Balanced (Struct→Mixed)** strategy consistently outperformed all other variants (one-stage, structured-only, OE-only, reverse-curriculum).
 - Training only on structured tasks (**GRPO-Structured**) yielded good performance on T/F and MCQ but a severe collapse on OE (5.7% accuracy), underscoring the need for OE data.
 - Conversely, training only on open-ended tasks (**GRPO-OE**) moderately improved OE but weakened structured skills.
 - The staged approach proved essential for stable optimization, balancing discrete decision-making and free-form generation.

- **Sim-to-Real Generalization (Supplementary Material):**
 - The PanoEnv-RL (7B) model demonstrated robust zero-shot transferability to the OSR-Bench, a real-world panoramic reasoning benchmark.
 - Despite being trained solely on synthetic TartanAir data, PanoEnv-RL (7B) outperformed both the base 7B model and the significantly larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B on tasks like Object Counting (0.507 vs. 0.498) and Relative Distance (0.371 vs. 0.325).
 - This suggests that the framework successfully instilled transferable 3D spatial reasoning and geometric logic rather than merely memorizing synthetic data, providing strong evidence for the practical utility of learning from physically-grounded synthetic environments.

In summary, the research clearly illustrates that existing VLMs are not inherently equipped for robust 3D spatial reasoning in panoramic contexts. However, with the carefully designed PanoEnv-QA benchmark providing objective supervision and the PanoEnv-RL framework leveraging ground-truth-guided rewards and a curriculum-based approach, even smaller models can achieve substantial gains in 3D spatial intelligence, outperforming much larger baselines and demonstrating strong sim-to-real transferability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PanoEnv research makes several significant contributions that collectively advance the field of Vision-Language Models and their application in omnidirectional perception, with broad potential impact across multiple domains.

**1. A Definitive Benchmark for Panoramic 3D Spatial Intelligence:** PanoEnv-QA fills a crucial void in the VQA landscape. Unlike prior datasets that either focused on 2D perspective images or lacked dense, precise 3D geometric annotations for panoramas, PanoEnv-QA provides programmatically generated questions with objective, verifiable 3D ground truth. This foundation is invaluable for both rigorous evaluation and effective reinforcement learning, moving beyond subjective human annotations or potentially hallucinated LLM-generated rewards. It provides a standardized and challenging testbed for the community to develop and compare models that can genuinely understand 3D space from 360° imagery.

**2. Demonstrating the Gap in Current VLM Capabilities and a Path Forward:** The comprehensive benchmarking of 14 state-of-the-art VLMs conclusively shows their severe limitations in 3D spatial reasoning, especially for open-ended questions in panoramic environments. This empirical evidence underscores that current training paradigms often bias models towards 2D heuristics, failing to build a faithful 3D scene representation. PanoEnv's findings serve as a strong call to action for the research community to explicitly address these 3D geometric challenges. The proposed RL framework then offers a concrete and effective method to bridge this gap.

**3. Advancing RL for Multimodal Reasoning with Ground-Truth Guidance:** The PanoEnv-RL framework represents a significant methodological innovation. By integrating a multi-faceted, ground-truth-guided reward system into Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), the authors provide a powerful paradigm for training VLMs to acquire precise geometric reasoning skills. This approach shifts away from noisy, proxy rewards (e.g., LLM judges) towards signals anchored in physical reality. The two-stage curriculum strategy further stabilizes this complex training process, effectively balancing the acquisition of structured output formats with the development of nuanced open-ended generative reasoning, mitigating catastrophic forgetting. This framework can inspire similar approaches for other complex multimodal tasks requiring verifiable, physically-grounded intelligence.

**4. Enabling Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer of Geometric Logic:** The successful zero-shot transferability of the PanoEnv-RL model to a real-world panoramic dataset (OSR-Bench) is highly significant. It demonstrates that learning from high-fidelity synthetic data with accurate 3D ground truth can instill transferable geometric logic, rather than just memorizing textures or patterns. This finding suggests that synthetic environments, when equipped with precise physical properties, can serve as a potent training ground for developing generalizable 3D spatial intelligence that can bridge the simulation-to-reality gap, a critical step for embodied AI and robotics where real-world 3D annotations are often scarce.

**5. Broadening Impact on AI Applications:** The enhanced 3D spatial intelligence fostered by PanoEnv has profound implications for a wide range of applications:
 - **Virtual/Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** More immersive and responsive experiences driven by AI agents with a deeper understanding of the virtual 3D environment.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Improved environmental perception, safer navigation, and more robust decision-making in complex 3D surroundings for autonomous vehicles and drones.
 - **Robotics:** Enhanced ability for robots to perceive, localize, and interact intelligently with objects in real-world 3D environments, moving towards more capable and physically-aware embodied agents.
 - **General Vision-Language AI:** PanoEnv pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities beyond merely interpreting 2D images, fostering a more fundamental understanding of the 3D world that underlies our visual experiences.

In conclusion, PanoEnv does not just introduce a new dataset or model; it provides a holistic solution—a diagnostic tool, a learning framework, and a compelling demonstration—that effectively addresses a fundamental challenge in achieving true spatial intelligence in AI, paving the way for more capable and context-aware multimodal systems.
