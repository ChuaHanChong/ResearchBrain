# Making two action heads agree: coordination mechanisms and a runtime collapse certificate for flow-matching policies

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-representation flow-matching policy decodes one predicted motion into joint space and end-effector space; the kinematic residual between them is a runtime failure signal that misfires when the two branches pick different valid modes. The paper maps which shared variables make the branches agree, proving auxiliary latents are erased at the CFM optimum while partition tokens reach near-ceiling coordination, and derives a label-free diversity-capacity bound separating coordination from collapse.

## Problem

- Runtime failure detection for diffusion and flow-matching policies relies on observation-side shift scores blind to the action distribution, or on entropy and ensembles that cost extra training and inference.
- Disagreement-based uncertainty breaks at multimodal decision points: two independently sampled branches can pick different valid routes, so the signal fires while neither prediction is wrong.
- Mismatch rate cannot measure whether branches coordinate: low mismatch can come from chance agreement, marginal imbalance, or collapse onto one mode, so prior metrics misreport agreement.

## Method

- ==Dual-branch conditional flow matching==: separate ==joint-space== and ==end-effector velocity fields== over a shared condition encoder, reconciled by ==differentiable forward kinematics== into a ==cross-representation residual==, with ==source-noise coupling== beta interpolating shared to independent noise.
- A ==coordination calculus==: the ==chance-corrected effect Delta== (baseline minus observed mismatch) bounded by a ==modal-diversity capacity== built from each branch's ==Gini-Simpson diversity==, yielding an attainable region and a ==label-free collapse certificate==.
- Mechanism atlas of five shared-variable designs - ==auxiliary latent==, ==amortized posterior==, ==source-noise sharing==, ==endpoint-consistency regularizer== on forward-kinematics-reconciled endpoints, ==data-derived partition tokens== - plus a ==z-blindness lemma== for latents outside the ==CFM construction==.

## Results

- In **117** robot-side runs, partition tokens reach rho = **91.1-100%** of the bound, auxiliary latents stay inside the prespecified **2%** band, and endpoint consistency reaches rho = **50.3%** while valid-pair rate falls **0.77** to **0.53**.
- Shared source noise flips sign with the representation map: interaction range **90.1** percentage points, permutation p = **1e-4**, and an eight-seed robot mean of **-0.5%** (sd **3.4**) with four signs each way.
- On LIBERO-Plus, the coordinated residual adds **1.57** pp false alarms versus unimodal steps and scores AUROC **0.6200** at **100** steps versus **0.5285** for max-z; coordination yields no robust detection gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A shared random input only coordinates generative branches if it enters the training construction; appended latents outside the loss target are provably ignored at the population optimum.
> - Never read agreement off a mismatch rate; report coordination against a diversity capacity, since zero mismatch is equally consistent with perfect coordination and total mode collapse.
> - Sharing initial noise is not map-invariant: it can anti-coordinate when decoder mode basins misalign, so coupling tricks need checking per representation pair, not assuming a benign sign.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot can reach the same goal by taking either of two valid routes around an obstacle. The policies studied here produce one predicted motion in two representations: joint space and end-effector position. Forward kinematics converts the joint prediction so the system can compare the two outputs in physical units. On a multimodal task, however, the two branches can sample different *valid* routes to the same goal, and the resulting residual can be large even when neither branch is wrong. The paper analyzes measurement and mechanism; it does not propose a new policy or monitor. This matters in practice. On the LIBERO-Plus test split, multimodal successful steps raise the coordinated residual's false-alarm rate by 1.57 percentage points relative to unimodal successful steps.

Why two correct branches can disagree. One condition feeds a joint-space head and a Cartesian-position head, and forward kinematics transforms the joint output. The two drawn trajectories take different valid routes to the same goal, and the gap between them represents a benign cross-mode residual. This is a schematic, not measured data; measured decompositions appear elsewhere in the paper.

### 2. Agreement is more than low mismatch

The paper uses four quantities to tell real agreement apart from low mismatch.

- `obs` is the mismatch rate between the branches' mode labels. It is calculated only among pairs where both outputs are valid.
- `BL` is the mismatch expected if each branch independently chose from its own observed mode frequencies.
- Their difference, Δ = BL − obs, is positive when same-mode agreement exceeds that chance baseline.
- Each branch's Gini–Simpson diversity is one minus the sum of squared mode probabilities. The geometric mean of the two diversities, Cₖ, is the capacity bound: |Δ| ≤ Cₖ.

Plotting Δ against Cₖ gives a label-free certificate. Zero mismatch with near-zero capacity can indicate mode collapse rather than useful coordination.

This certificate has limits. It concerns same-label agreement, not all dependence, and at three or more modes Δ = 0 need not mean the branches are independent. The coordination estimates also condition on both outputs being valid, so the valid-pair rate is reported separately.

### 3. A shared draw can disagree

The intuitive expectation is that giving both generators the same random input should make them choose the same mode. The authors distinguish two kinds of shared input.

- **Auxiliary latent.** The latent is merely appended to the CFM velocity-field input and is absent from the interpolation construction and regression target. The population squared-loss optimum is predicted to ignore it. This exact-zero prediction depends on stated assumptions. The finite robot results support equivalence within the prespecified ±2% band, not an exact zero in every finite trained model.
- **Source noise.** Source noise is different because it participates in the flow construction and couples the samplers.

The decisive test is a preregistered testbed comparison across three representation maps. With shared source noise, the normalized effect changes from −23.3% on the null-space map to +66.8% on the polar map. That is a 90.1-percentage-point interaction range, and the interaction permutation test gives *p* = 1 × 10⁻⁴, with ten training seeds per cell. This is evidence that the sign depends on the tested representation map. It does not show that one particular explanation is the only cause, and it is not a prediction for all representations.

Panel (c) plots normalized effect Δ/Cₖ across null-space, orthogonal, and polar maps. Shared source noise changes from −22.7%/−9.2% to +67.8%, and the β=0, λ=3 consistency family changes from −7.3%/+10.9% to +92.4%. Independent/auxiliary-latent and token families stay near their prior values. Plotted values are three-seed, not the registered ten-seed confirmatory estimates.

A discrete partition gives a contrasting route. In BimodalBypass (v2), the K=2 window token is fitted from training windows and drawn uniformly at test time. It reaches Δ = +0.4994 and ρ = 100%, where ρ = Δ/C is a normalized effect, not a success rate. The token is a robust robot-side route but not a necessary one. High coordination does not by itself establish fidelity to the demonstrations' mode frequencies.

### 4. What the monitor shows

On LIBERO-Plus, the thresholds are signal-specific and calibrated to 5%. The coordinated residual's false-alarm rate is 9.75% on multimodal successful steps versus 8.19% on unimodal successful steps, a +1.57-point gap (95% CI [+0.70, +2.46]). For comparison, the observation-side `max |z|` control shows a gap of −0.01 points. This is evidence for a residual false-alarm surcharge in this benchmark, not evidence that every multimodal state is harder.

The preregistered prediction was that coordination would shrink this surcharge, and it did not meet its registered criterion. The coordinated-minus-uncoordinated gap difference is −0.03 percentage points (95% CI [−1.00, +0.86]). This result is inconclusive; it does not prove that coordination cannot help. The authors measured a capacity of 0.0055 afterward. This post hoc value characterizes a regime with little modal variation to coordinate, but it does not identify the sole bottleneck.

The paper's contribution is a way to read cross-representation disagreement: a low residual is interpretable only alongside chance-corrected agreement and modal capacity. Its main boundary is that the monitoring result does not establish benefit in policies that genuinely sample several modes.
