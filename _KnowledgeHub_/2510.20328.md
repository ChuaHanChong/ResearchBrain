# MemER: Scaling Up Memory for Robot Control via Experience Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed MemER, a hierarchical policy framework that equips existing generalist robot policies with long-term visual memory through intelligent experience retrieval. This method allows robots to successfully perform complex, multi-step, partially observable manipulation tasks requiring minutes of memory, outperforming memory-less and short-context baselines on real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Most current robot policies lack long-term memory, struggling with tasks requiring recall of information gathered minutes ago in partially observable environments.
- Naively extending observation context windows for end-to-end policies is computationally prohibitive and can lead to brittle generalization due to irrelevant or redundant information.
- Existing memory-based solutions for robotics often scale only to short horizons or are ill-suited for dynamic manipulation tasks, while proprietary VLMs suffer from high latency and insufficient robot-specific reasoning.

## Method

- A hierarchical policy framework is introduced, comprising a high-level VLM-based policy for subtask generation and keyframe nomination, and a low-level generalist robot policy for action execution.
- An intelligent experience retrieval mechanism, utilizing 1D single-linkage clustering on nominated keyframes, dynamically builds and maintains a compact, task-relevant visual memory over long horizons.
- Open-source Vision-Language Models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) and generalist robot policies (π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​) are finetuned and merged with pretrained weights to optimize for robot-specific memory and task performance.

## Results

- MemER achieved 59 object retrievals (out of 60 possible) in 'Object Search' and only 1 wrong scoop in 'Counting Scoops', significantly outperforming memory-less and short-context baselines on all three complex long-horizon tasks.
- Performance of MemER was on par with a 'Human HL' policy (human-provided subtasks), indicating that the memory mechanism is highly effective and primarily limited by low-level policy execution.
- The approach demonstrated efficient real-world deployment, with the high-level policy operating at ~1Hz and the low-level policy at ~2Hz, enabling practical closed-loop control with minutes of memory.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual memory alone proved to be the most robust representation for long-term robot memory in manipulation tasks, outperforming and showing more resilience than text-based memory or multimodal combinations including text.
> - Proprietary VLMs, without fine-tuning, lack the robot-specific perceptual grounding to identify truly meaningful keyframes and are too slow for real-time closed-loop robot deployment.
> - Linear interpolation (model merging) of finetuned weights with pretrained VLM weights effectively preserves general robustness while adapting to specific robot tasks, and multi-task training enhances cross-task object generalization.
