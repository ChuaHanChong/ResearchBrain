# Learning Whole-Body Humanoid Locomotion via Motion Generation and Motion Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a whole-body humanoid locomotion framework that merges a diffusion-based motion generator with an RL-based motion tracker. The system enables the Unitree G1 robot to adaptively navigate diverse, challenging terrains in real-time by producing perception-aware reference motions and executing them robustly.

## Problem

- Direct deep reinforcement learning (RL) for humanoids often results in limited whole-body coordination due to high degrees of freedom, morphological instability, and difficult reward design.
- Motion imitation via RL provides coordinated skills but lacks adaptability to unstructured environments and real-time perceptual adjustments.
- Generative models can create expressive kinematic trajectories, but their direct deployment on physical robots can cause unstable motion artifacts and real-time control issues due to latency.

## Method

- A ==three-stage pipeline== (Data Collection & Curation, Pre-training, and RL Fine-tuning) integrates the motion generator and tracker.
- A ==diffusion-based motion generator== produces whole-body reference motion sequences over a 0.5-second horizon, conditioned on terrain height scans and past robot states, updated in a ==receding-horizon== manner.
- A single ==whole-body motion tracker== trained with **PPO** learns to imitate these generated references while adhering to physical constraints, effectively filtering them for physically plausible execution.

## Results

- The **Unitree G1** humanoid robot successfully demonstrated whole-body locomotion across diverse terrains, including climbing a **75 cm** box, traversing stairs, and vaulting hurdles, dynamically transitioning between motion styles.
- Quantitative simulation showed that the "Tracker + Gen." system achieved success rate **0.962** on 80 cm box climbing vs. **0.230** for "Tracker Only", and better generalization across obstacle heights and orientations.
- RL fine-tuning consistently improved success rates across all evaluated tasks, particularly for demanding vertical transitions, by enhancing the tracker's robustness to imperfections in online generated motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online, terrain-aware motion generation is critical for enabling broad adaptability to diverse obstacle geometries and dynamic adjustment of locomotion styles beyond fixed reference trajectories.
> - RL fine-tuning of the motion tracker is essential for robust real-world deployment, allowing it to adapt to and filter imperfections and temporal discontinuities in generated references to ensure safe behaviors.
> - Integrating a generative model for scalable motion references with an RL tracker effectively bridges the gap between diverse motion generation and robust, physically plausible execution on hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and adaptable whole-body humanoid locomotion is a central challenge in robotics. Prior research in this area has explored several avenues, each with its own limitations.

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated success in enabling dynamic behaviors for quadrupedal robots. However, extending these capabilities to humanoid robots is more complex due to their higher degrees of freedom, morphological instability, and the difficulty of designing effective reward functions. Direct RL approaches often result in lower-body-dominated locomotion, lacking the coordinated whole-body movements necessary for complex terrain traversal, such as parkour.

To mitigate the complexities of reward shaping, motion imitation via RL has emerged as an alternative. This approach leverages reference motion data to efficiently transfer coordinated skills to robots. While effective at replicating choreographed trajectories, pure motion trackers are typically limited in their adaptability to diverse, unstructured environments and lack mechanisms for real-time perceptual adaptation.

Skill composition is another strategy, involving the distillation of multiple specialized policies or the composition of skills from various reference trajectories. While this can enable transitions between different expert skills, such methods have encountered challenges with scalability to a wide range of skills and robust generalization to novel terrains. They often require intricate distillation procedures, including expert assignment and data distribution design.

Generative models, particularly diffusion models, have shown promise in scalable motion control for both character and robot applications. They can generate a repertoire of motions from large datasets, reducing manual engineering effort. However, directly deploying kinematic trajectories generated by these models on physical humanoid robots can introduce artifacts, such as foot sliding or temporal discontinuities, potentially leading to control instability. Recent work has addressed this by combining diffusion-based motion generation with RL-based motion tracking, allowing the tracker to produce physically plausible actions. Nevertheless, the applicability of these combined frameworks to real humanoid robots in demanding locomotion tasks, particularly those requiring real-time whole-body coordination and robustness to real-world disturbances, has remained underexplored, partly due to inference latency and robustness concerns.

This research addresses these identified gaps by proposing a whole-body humanoid locomotion framework that integrates a diffusion-based motion generator with an RL-based motion tracker. The framework aims to leverage the benefits of learned coordinated human whole-body skills for perceptive humanoid locomotion on real hardware, enabling dynamic adaptation to varied terrains in real-time while mitigating the inherent challenges of prior approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a whole-body humanoid locomotion system capable of real-time, terrain-aware adaptation on a physical humanoid robot. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims to address distinct limitations in existing methodologies:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Direct RL for Humanoids:** Traditional reinforcement learning, solely reliant on reward shaping, often struggles with humanoid robots. The high-dimensional control space, inherent morphological instability, and difficulty in designing effective reward functions lead to inefficient exploration and often result in controllers that exhibit limited whole-body coordination. This work aims to provide structural guidance to the RL process by leveraging reference motion data, thereby facilitating the acquisition of coordinated behaviors.
2. **Enhancing Adaptability of Imitation-Based RL:** While imitation learning can transfer complex whole-body skills, pure motion trackers are generally limited to replaying pre-defined trajectories. They lack the intrinsic mechanisms to adapt these motions dynamically based on real-time perceptual inputs from the environment, which is crucial for operating in unstructured and diverse terrains. A key objective is to enable motion trackers to dynamically adjust locomotion styles in response to varying environmental conditions.
3. **Improving Scalability and Robustness of Skill Composition:** Existing methods for skill composition, such as distilling multiple expert policies, often face challenges in scaling to a diverse set of skills and generalizing robustly to novel terrains. These methods can also require intricate engineering for distillation. This research seeks to provide a more scalable and less labor-intensive approach to skill composition, moving beyond explicit distillation pipelines.
4. **Addressing Motion Artifacts and Deployment Constraints of Generative Models:** While generative models can produce expressive kinematic trajectories, their direct deployment on physical robots can lead to unstable motion artifacts like foot sliding. Furthermore, inference latency can hinder real-time control. This work aims to integrate generative models in a way that generates physically plausible actions for real robots, while also addressing real-time performance and robustness requirements for hardware deployment.
5. **Enabling Perceptive, Whole-Body Locomotion on Real Hardware:** A central motivation is to demonstrate a functional system on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1) that combines coordinated whole-body movements with real-time adaptation to diverse and challenging terrains using onboard perception and computation. This objective moves beyond simulated demonstrations to address the complexities of real-world deployment.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between learning complex, coordinated humanoid motions and enabling their robust, real-time, and adaptive execution on physical robots in varied environments, utilizing a framework that combines motion generation for terrain-aware skill selection with motion tracking for physically plausible execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates motion generation and motion tracking within a three-stage training and deployment pipeline: Data Collection & Curation, Pre-training, and RL Fine-tuning, followed by specific considerations for hardware deployment.

**A. Data Collection & Curation**
The initial dataset comprises approximately five minutes of motion clips, derived from recorded motion videos and public motion datasets (e.g.,). These motions cover key terrain skills such as climbing onto/jumping down from a 50 cm box, vaulting over a 35 cm hurdle, stair ascent/descent on 20 cm steps, and omnidirectional walking.

1. **Initial Dataset Construction:** Human motions from videos are first reconstructed using GVHMR, then retargeted to the humanoid robot using a contact-constrained Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver. Terrain objects are manually aligned with the retargeted motions. To ensure physical plausibility, raw retargeted trajectories are not used directly. Instead, a DeepMimic-style tracking policy is trained on these motions, and the resulting refined, physically feasible robot trajectories form the initial training data.
2. **Motion Augmentation:** To improve generalization, the dataset is expanded through kinematics-based augmentation. This involves varying terrain geometry, such as scaling obstacle heights (e.g., 35-75 cm for boxes, 25-45 cm for hurdles, 15-20 cm for stairs) and inserting small randomized boxes. Motions are then optimized to remain consistent with these modified terrains, using losses for terrain penetration and motion smoothness. Similar to initial construction, these optimized trajectories are tracked by a policy, and the resulting physically feasible motions are added to the dataset, totaling approximately one hour of motion data.

**B. Pre-training Stage**
This stage independently trains the motion generator and motion tracker on the curated dataset, endowing them with basic environmental reactivity and the ability to produce appropriate outputs.

1. **Whole-body Motion Tracker:** A single whole-body motion tracker is trained using a DeepMimic-style RL framework with the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm in IsaacLab. The objective is to imitate all reference motions while adhering to physical constraints. The reward function (`R_pre`) combines imitation rewards (`r_mimic`) and regularization terms (`r_reg`). Tracker observations include reference state (linear/angular velocity, joint position/velocity, key body positions), proprioceptive terms (five frames of base angular velocity, projected gravity, joint position/velocity, previous action), and terrain height scans. The policy outputs 23-dimensional target joint positions.
2. **Diffusion-based Motion Generator:** A diffusion model, based on MDM, is trained to predict whole-body reference motion sequences. It forecasts future motion features (root position/orientation, joint positions, body link positions) over a 0.5-second horizon (25 frames). The model is conditioned on a target heading vector, a terrain height scan, and the past two frames of motion features. Training involves sampling motion sequences, computing heading vectors, and using initial frames as conditions. The loss function includes a reconstruction loss and geometric losses (velocity, joint consistency, terrain penetration). To enhance robustness, height scans and previous-state conditions are perturbed during training.

**C. RL Fine-tuning Stage**
This stage refines the combined system for robust, real-time closed-loop deployment. It addresses limitations arising from imperfect generated references and aims to improve generalization.

The motion tracker is fine-tuned with RL, while the motion generator remains frozen. During fine-tuning, the generator produces reference frames conditioned on past robot states, establishing a closed-loop prediction process. To reduce deployment latency, the motion generator uses only two denoising steps. Noise is also injected into both the generator's inference and the tracker's observations to improve real-world robustness.

A new task reward (`r_task`) for heading tracking is introduced, alongside the imitation and regularization terms, forming `R_post`. This encourages the system to follow desired directions while leveraging pre-trained motion priors. The fine-tuning also incorporates more diverse training terrains, including varied stair heights (15-25 cm), continuous vaulting hurdles (25-55 cm), and multiple climbing boxes/pyramid steps (30-85 cm) with varying widths, yaw, and pitch angles. This stage enables the tracker to act as a motion filter, adapting to the distribution and imperfections of generated references and suppressing unsafe behaviors.

**D. Details for Hardware Deployment**
The entire pipeline is deployed onboard the Unitree G1 robot.
- **Perception:** DLIO uses the onboard Livox MID360 LiDAR and IMU for robot base pose estimation. Elevation Mapping CuPy reconstructs terrain height information.
- **State Estimation Stability:** Head movement from aggressive motions can degrade base pose estimation. To mitigate this, LiDAR IMU and torso IMU measurements are fused by compensating for head pitch variation, improving robustness.
- **Generator Acceleration:** The diffusion-based motion generator is optimized with TensorRT, reducing inference time to approximately 0.02 seconds.
- **Receding-Horizon Control:** Although the generator has a 0.5-second prediction horizon, the reference is updated every 0.25 seconds in a receding-horizon manner during deployment to enhance responsiveness.
- **Computational Hardware:** The motion generator runs on a Jetson Thor, while the motion tracking controller and other modules run on the Unitree G1's integrated Jetson Orin.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both qualitative hardware demonstrations and quantitative simulation analyses to support its claims regarding the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed system.

**A. Hardware Results**
Experiments on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot demonstrate the system's ability to execute whole-body locomotion over diverse and challenging terrains.
- **Versatile Box Traversal:** The robot successfully climbs onto and jumps down from a 75 cm box, exhibiting versatile behaviors such as straight ascent/descent, ascent followed by a 90° turn and side jump-down, and traversing the box from a corner. During ascent, the robot utilizes knees and hands for support, and during descent, hands are used for cushioning, consistent with the learned motion style.
- **Stair and Vaulting Traversal:** The system enables traversal of staircases and sequences of vaulting hurdles of varying heights. For vaulting, the robot typically crosses obstacles directly rather than stepping onto them.
- **Mixed Terrain Navigation:** The controller can dynamically transition between different motion styles (e.g., vaulting, stairs, box climbing) within a single run, even on terrain combinations not explicitly seen during RL fine-tuning, such as stair ascent following a jump-down motion.
- **Local Navigation:** A form of local navigation behavior is observed, where the robot may partially override the generated reference motion to bypass an obstacle if its current state is unsuitable for direct traversal, thus aiming to reach the target position by an alternative path.

**B. Quantitative Results**
Simulation experiments were conducted to quantitatively evaluate the contributions of online motion generation and the RL fine-tuning stage.

1. **Benefit of Online Motion Generation:**
 - **Comparison:** Two settings were compared: "Tracker Only" (fine-tuned motion tracker with a fixed reference trajectory) and "Tracker + Gen." (the proposed full system with online motion generation).
 - **Tasks:** Box climbing, vaulting, and stair ascent, with varied obstacle heights and yaw rotations. Success was measured by reaching a goal position on the terrain.
 - **Findings:** The "Tracker + Gen." system consistently achieved better generalization and higher average success rates across all three tasks, particularly as terrain geometry (height, orientation) deviated significantly from the nominal reference. For instance, in box climbing, "Tracker Only" performance degraded considerably with increased box height (e.g., 0.230 success rate at 80 cm vs. 0.962 for "Tracker + Gen."). While the fine-tuned "Tracker Only" exhibited some limited local generalization, it was insufficient for substantial changes in terrain that require a change in motion timing or style. These results highlight that online, terrain-aware reference motions produced by the generator are crucial for adapting traversal behavior to diverse obstacle geometries.

2. **Impact of Fine-Tuning the Motion Tracker:**
 - **Comparison:** The pretrained tracker was compared against the fine-tuned tracker, both operating with the same frozen motion generator.
 - **Tasks:** Climbing up, climbing down, vaulting, ascending stairs, and descending stairs, with varying obstacle and step heights. Success rate was measured for reaching a goal position behind the obstacle.
 - **Findings:** Fine-tuning the tracker consistently improved success rates across all evaluated tasks. The improvements were most pronounced on more challenging terrain settings and those requiring demanding vertical transitions. For climbing up and down, the success rate improvement became more apparent with increasing box height. Similar advantages for the fine-tuned tracker were observed on larger step heights for stair ascent and descent. The analysis suggests that fine-tuning is not merely a marginal refinement but systematically enhances robustness. This is attributed to the fact that the pretrained tracker is optimized on idealized, smooth offline motions, whereas online generated references can contain temporal discontinuities or artifacts due to conditioning on noisy robot states and terrain observations. Fine-tuning allows the tracker to adapt to these imperfections and effectively filter generated motions to produce safe behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that advances the field of whole-body humanoid locomotion, particularly concerning real-world deployment and adaptability.

The primary significance lies in the successful demonstration of a system that combines the strengths of diffusion-based motion generation and RL-based motion tracking to achieve perceptive, whole-body locomotion on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1). This addresses a critical gap in the literature, which has seen limited real-time demonstrations of such integrated systems on physical hardware for complex tasks.

The work's findings highlight the importance of both online motion generation and subsequent RL fine-tuning of the tracker. Online motion generation provides a scalable mechanism for skill composition and adaptation, allowing the robot to dynamically adjust its locomotion strategy based on real-time terrain perception, overcoming the rigidness of fixed-reference tracking. The fine-tuning stage is shown to be crucial for bridging the reality gap, enabling the tracker to robustly handle the imperfections and distributional shifts inherent in generated reference motions, effectively functioning as a safety-critical motion filter.

The system's ability to traverse diverse terrain types and previously unseen terrain combinations, including multi-obstacle sequences and varied approach angles, signifies progress towards more autonomous and versatile humanoid robots. The observed local navigation behavior further indicates an emergent adaptability beyond direct motion following.

The methodology reduces reliance on extensive, manually engineered distillation strategies, offering a more generalized approach to learning varied skills by leveraging large-scale motion datasets via generative models.

Potential impacts include:
- **Expanded Operational Range:** Enabling humanoid robots to operate reliably in more challenging and unstructured environments, moving beyond laboratory settings.
- **Enhanced Autonomy:** Contributing to the development of humanoids that can autonomously adapt their physical interaction with complex surroundings.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Providing a robust foundation for future work in whole-body control, particularly for tasks integrating locomotion with manipulation (loco-manipulation) and operation in highly dynamic outdoor environments. The identified limitations regarding reliance on high-quality geometric sensing also suggest important directions for future research, such as incorporating neural mapping or belief encoders to improve robustness against sensing failures.

Overall, this research contributes to the practical realization of agile and adaptive whole-body humanoid locomotion systems, moving closer to human-level mobility in complex environments.
