# FreeTraj: Tuning-Free Trajectory Control in Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FreeTraj introduces a tuning-free method for precise trajectory control in video diffusion models by guiding both initial noise construction and attention computation. The approach achieves superior trajectory alignment (mIoU 0.281, CD 0.154) compared to existing tuning-free techniques, while mitigating artifacts and demonstrating capabilities for longer and larger video generation.

## Problem

- Text-to-video diffusion models lack precise geometric and motion control, making it difficult for users to dictate an object's exact path.
- Existing methods for video trajectory control are primarily training-based, requiring significant computational resources and data for fine-tuning pre-trained models.
- Prior tuning-free control methods often introduce visual artifacts, such as 'attention isolation' or 'blocky artifacts,' compromising video quality.

## Method

- A ==tuning-free== framework, **FreeTraj**, guides object trajectories by simultaneously manipulating the ==initial noise construction== and the ==attention computation== during the denoising process.
- Achieves ==noise guidance== by localizing ==initial noise flow== within specified bounding boxes and ==resampling high-frequency noise== components to maintain visual quality.
- Implements ==attention guidance== through ==soft attention masks== in spatial and temporal self-attention layers and modified ==cross-attention scores==, preventing ==attention isolation== artifacts.

## Results

- FreeTraj achieves superior trajectory alignment, with a Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of **0.281** and a Centroid Distance (CD) of **0.154**, outperforming prior tuning-free methods.
- User studies indicate strong subjective performance, with FreeTraj receiving **65.74%** of votes for trajectory alignment, **62.65%** for video-text alignment, and **63.89%** for video quality.
- The method successfully extends controllable trajectory generation to longer videos (e.g., **64 frames**) and higher resolutions, effectively mitigating the common issue of duplicated main objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-frequency components of initial noise in video diffusion models contain spatio-temporal correlations that can be manipulated to guide object motion trajectories.
> - Combining initial noise manipulation with carefully designed 'soft attention masks' in the denoising process is essential for achieving precise trajectory control while avoiding visual artifacts.
> - Mitigating 'attention isolation' through soft masking and selective high-frequency noise resampling is crucial for maintaining visual fidelity in tuning-free video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual content generation, especially text-to-video synthesis, has witnessed rapid advancements due to the powerful modeling capabilities of diffusion models. Foundational models like Imagen Video, SVD, Lumiere, and most recently, Sora, have showcased remarkable abilities to create vivid and dynamic content from text prompts. However, a significant challenge remains: achieving precise geometric and motion control over the generated content. While text prompts allow for flexible content creation, they often fall short in expressing concrete user intentions regarding the exact movement or trajectory of objects within the video.

Traditionally, research addressing this control problem has primarily focused on "training-based" methods. These often involve adapting pre-trained diffusion models by adding conditional structures, such as ControlNet-like architectures (e.g., VideoComposer, Control-A-Video), which require fine-tuning on large datasets of specific conditioning signals (like depth maps, sketches, or motion vectors). Other training-based methods employ reference videos (e.g., Tune-A-Video, MotionDirector) to generalize motions, but still necessitate training for each reference. While highly effective, these approaches are computationally intensive, labor-intensive for data collection, and limit creativity to pre-existing motions or require significant resources for customization.

In response to these limitations, a newer trend of "tuning-free" or "training-free" methods has emerged. These methods aim to leverage the inherent properties of pre-trained diffusion models to enable control without additional training. Prior works in this category, such as Peekaboo (Jain et al., 2023a) and TrailBlazer (Ma et al., 2023a), primarily employ explicit attention control mechanisms to direct object movement based on specified trajectories or bounding boxes. These methods demonstrated the feasibility of training-free control, but often faced challenges related to generating artifacts or maintaining high visual quality and precise adherence to complex trajectories.

FreeTraj positions itself directly within this "tuning-free" paradigm. It builds upon the understanding that diffusion models, despite being trained for open-world generation, possess latent potential for substantial control without needing re-training. The paper distinguishes itself from prior tuning-free methods by proposing a more comprehensive and robust approach that simultaneously imposes guidance on *both* the noise construction (initial latent space) and the attention computation (denoising process). This dual-guidance strategy is a key differentiator, as previous works primarily focused on attention manipulation.

Furthermore, FreeTraj explicitly addresses a critical issue observed in prior attention-based control methods: "attention isolation," where hard masks or significant deviations from the training distribution lead to artifacts. By proposing "soft attention masks" and a tailored scheme for applying operations, FreeTraj aims to mitigate these quality degradations, thereby offering a more reliable and visually coherent tuning-free control solution. The work also extends its utility by demonstrating seamless integration with frameworks for longer and larger video generation, addressing other significant challenges in the field. By improving accuracy, quality, and generalizability in a tuning-free manner, FreeTraj contributes significantly to making controllable video generation more accessible and practical for a wider range of users and applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the FreeTraj research are:

1. **To enable precise trajectory-controllable video generation in diffusion models without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning.** This is the overarching goal, aiming to overcome the computational and data-intensive nature of existing training-based control methods.
2. **To investigate and demonstrate how initial noises influence the motion trajectory of generated content in video diffusion models.** The authors hypothesize that video diffusion models inherently contain information about motion in their low-frequency noise components, and by manipulating these, trajectory control can be achieved.
3. **To develop a novel tuning-free framework, FreeTraj, that achieves trajectory control by imposing guidance on both noise construction and attention computation.** This involves designing specific mechanisms for manipulating the initial latent noise and modifying the attention layers within the denoising U-Net.
4. **To address and mitigate the issue of "attention isolation" and associated artifacts** that arise when introducing alterations to noise or attention mechanisms in a tuning-free manner, especially prominent in prior works.
5. **To extend the proposed trajectory control mechanism to facilitate longer and larger video generation,** thereby enhancing the utility and applicability of the approach beyond standard video lengths and resolutions.

The core motivation behind these objectives stems from two main observations and limitations of current video generation models:

- **Lack of Concrete Geometric Control:** While text-to-video diffusion models excel at generating diverse and vivid content based on text prompts, these prompts often lack the specificity required for concrete geometric control, particularly concerning the precise motion or trajectory of objects. Users cannot easily dictate an object's path through a scene.
- **Resource-Intensive Training for Control:** Existing methods that offer geometric or motion control primarily rely on training-based approaches (e.g., ControlNet-like adapters). These methods demand substantial computing resources for training, are labor-intensive for data collection, and can be restrictive in their customization capabilities. The authors contend that the intrinsic properties of diffusion models might already offer a pathway to control without this overhead.
- **Under-exploration of Initial Noise Influence:** The paper highlights that while many efforts focus on modifying text embeddings or attention mechanisms for control, the influence of initial noise on video motion has been largely under-explored. This presents an opportunity to unlock new tuning-free control paradigms.
- **Artifact Generation in Prior Tuning-Free Methods:** Early tuning-free attempts at control, particularly those manipulating attention (like Peekaboo), were prone to generating visual artifacts due to deviations from the model's training distribution. Mitigating these artifacts is crucial for practical applicability.

By addressing these motivations, FreeTraj aims to provide a flexible, user-friendly, and computationally efficient solution for generating videos with desired motion trajectories, making advanced video generation more accessible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FreeTraj introduces a novel tuning-free framework that achieves trajectory-controllable video generation by leveraging inherent properties of pre-trained video diffusion models. The core of its methodology lies in a dual-guidance strategy: manipulating both the initial noise construction and the attention computation during the denoising process. The chosen base model for implementation is VideoCrafter 2.0, a state-of-the-art open-source latent video diffusion model.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Video Diffusion Models

The paper first briefly reviews the foundation of Video Diffusion Models (VDMs), which involve a forward process of gradually adding noise to 4D video data and a reverse denoising process learned by a noise prediction network (ϵθ). It highlights the use of Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) for efficiency, where a VAE maps videos to a compact latent space. The UNet architecture, equipped with convolutional, spatial, and temporal attention modules, is central to these models.

#### 3.2. Noise Influence and Guidance

The authors hypothesize that low-frequency components of initial noise retain spatio-temporal correlations that influence trajectory.

- **Noise Flow Phenomenon:** Initial experiments demonstrate that by generating initial noise for the first frame and then systematically shifting (flowing) it across subsequent frames (e.g., top-left to bottom-right), the generated video exhibits objects and textures moving in the same direction. This confirms the initial noise's ability to guide motion. However, this direct "noise flow" leads to strong artifacts due to deviation from the independent random distribution.
- **High-Frequency Noise Resampling:** To mitigate artifacts while preserving trajectory information (which is primarily in low-frequency noise), FreeTraj resamples high-frequency noise components. It applies a 3D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to the "flowed" initial noise, replaces its high-frequency part with newly sampled random noise, and then applies an Inverse FFT. An empirical analysis shows that resampling 75% of high-frequency noise strikes a good balance between visual quality and motion control.
- **Trajectory Injection:** To achieve object-level trajectory control, FreeTraj localizes the noise flow. Instead of flowing noise across the entire frame, it samples a `local_noise` and injects it only into the specified bounding box area for each frame along the desired trajectory. This means that within the object's bounding box, the initial noise remains consistent across frames, while outside it, noise is independently sampled. This method aims to guide the main object while preserving the background's natural motion.

#### 3.3. Guidance in Attention

To achieve more precise and robust trajectory control, especially when object movements deviate from the model's prior knowledge, FreeTraj incorporates guidance directly into the attention mechanisms of the UNet. Unlike prior works that use hard masks, FreeTraj employs "soft attention masks" to prevent "attention isolation" (where regions become spatially or temporally disconnected, leading to artifacts). This guidance is applied during the early denoising steps (`t ∈ {T, ..., T-N}`).

- **Cross Attention Guidance:** Spatial cross-attention layers connect text prompts to visual features. To force the model to render the target object within the given bounding box, FreeTraj modifies the attention scores. It enhances the attention weights between foreground pixels and target prompt tokens (`α * g(i,j)`) and suppresses attention between background pixels and target prompt tokens (`-∞`). A Gaussian weight `g(·,·)` is applied for smoother emphasis within the foreground.
- **Self Attention Guidance (Spatial and Temporal):** Self-attention layers model relationships within the visual features. To ensure that foreground information stays within the object's region and interacts minimally with the background, FreeTraj introduces a soft mask. For spatial self-attention, attention between foreground and background pixels is weakly suppressed by a coefficient `β` (e.g., 0.01), maintaining some interaction to prevent attention isolation. Similarly, for temporal self-attention, the mask ensures that pixels within the object's bounding box primarily attend to other pixels within that same object across time, and background pixels attend to other background pixels, again with a soft suppression to prevent temporal artifacts.

#### 3.4. Longer and Larger Video Generation

- **Longer Video Generation:** FreeTraj seamlessly integrates with FreeNoise (Qiu et al., 2023), another tuning-free framework for long video generation. FreeNoise's "Local Window Fusion" (for temporal attention) and "Noise Rescheduling" (for noise initialization) are adapted. FreeTraj's temporal attention guidance applies within these local windows, and its noise guidance mechanisms are applied *after* FreeNoise's noise rescheduling, ensuring compatibility and allowing for controllable trajectories in extended video sequences.
- **Larger Video Generation:** When generating videos at resolutions higher than the model's training resolution, pre-trained models often produce duplicated main objects (a common issue in diffusion models). FreeTraj addresses this by constraining the main object's information to the target trajectory areas, effectively suppressing its signals in other regions, thereby reducing the duplication phenomenon.

The methodology is meticulously designed to balance aggressive control with maintaining visual fidelity, a crucial challenge in tuning-free approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FreeTraj's efficacy is validated through extensive experiments, comparing it against direct inference (Direct) and other state-of-the-art tuning-free trajectory control methods like Peekaboo and TrailBlazer, primarily using VideoCrafter 2.0 as the base model.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Trajectory Control

- **Quantitative Comparison (Table 1):**
 - **Trajectory Alignment (mIoU, CD):** FreeTraj significantly outperforms all baselines in metrics directly related to trajectory control. It achieves the highest Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 0.281 (compared to 0.235 for Peekaboo and 0.179 for TrailBlazer), indicating better alignment of the generated object's bounding box with the desired trajectory. It also yields the lowest Centroid Distance (CD) of 0.154 (compared to 0.179 for Peekaboo and 0.219 for TrailBlazer), signifying that the generated object's center is closer to the target trajectory's center. Direct inference, as expected, performs poorly in these metrics.
 - **Video Quality (FVD, KVD, CLIP-SIM):** While FreeTraj achieves superior trajectory control, its FVD (436.22) and KVD (29.85) are slightly worse than Peekaboo (FVD 403.00, KVD 25.30). The authors explain this by noting that Peekaboo tends to generate objects in the center, a behavior similar to the original VideoCrafter's training distribution, which might lead to better FVD/KVD scores. FreeTraj, by providing more flexible and potentially "unnatural" trajectories (from the perspective of the model's inherent biases), might slightly increase these divergence metrics. CLIP Similarity (CLIP-SIM) is comparable across controlled methods (around 0.95-0.96), indicating consistent text-to-video alignment. TrailBlazer generally performs the worst across all metrics, showing weaker control and quality.
- **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 4):**
 - Visual examples clearly demonstrate FreeTraj's superiority. It successfully drives the target object (e.g., a corgi, a swan) along the given trajectory with vivid and natural motions.
 - Peekaboo, while showing rough control, often generates objects that are not precisely aligned and can introduce artifacts (e.g., an additional black swan with "weird artifacts"). The authors attribute this to Peekaboo's use of "hard attention masks."
 - TrailBlazer exhibits the weakest control, with objects often deviating significantly from the desired path.
- **User Study (Table 2):** A user study involving 27 participants confirms FreeTraj's strong performance from a human subjective perception. FreeTraj received the highest percentage of votes across all three evaluation aspects:
 - **Trajectory Alignment:** 65.74% (significantly higher than "Ours w/o Noise" at 19.75%, Peekaboo at 6.48%, and TrailBlazer at 8.03%).
 - **Video-Text Alignment:** 62.65% (demonstrating that control doesn't compromise semantic consistency).
 - **Video Quality:** 63.89% (indicating visually appealing results despite the FVD/KVD scores). This subjective assessment strongly supports FreeTraj's practical effectiveness.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Figure 5)

- **Ablation of Noise Guidance:** When only attention guidance is used (`Ours w/o Noise`), the model can still control the trajectory, but with some loss of accuracy compared to the full FreeTraj (as also supported by the user study). This highlights the complementary role of noise guidance.
- **Ablation of Attention Isolation:**
 - **No High-Frequency Noise Resampling:** If the high-frequency noise resampling step is omitted during trajectory injection (pure noise flow in the local area), the generated videos suffer from "blocky artifacts" that follow the trajectory. This validates the importance of resampling to maintain distribution fidelity.
 - **Hard Attention Mask:** Using hard attention masks (similar to Peekaboo's approach) also leads to severe "attention isolation" and "blocky artifacts," confirming that the proposed soft attention masks are crucial for artifact mitigation and maintaining visual quality.
These ablations confirm that both noise guidance and the carefully designed soft attention guidance are essential for FreeTraj's robust performance and artifact prevention.

#### 4.3. Longer and Larger Video Generation Results (Figure 6, Figure 7)

- **Longer Videos:** FreeTraj successfully integrates with the FreeNoise framework to generate trajectory-controllable long videos (e.g., 64 frames). The examples show complex, planned paths (e.g., a dark knight riding a horse, a corgi running) are accurately followed, demonstrating the method's scalability for extended temporal sequences.
- **Larger Videos:** FreeTraj effectively mitigates the common problem of duplicated main objects when generating videos at resolutions higher than the training data. By constraining the main object's information to the target trajectory, it suppresses its appearance in other areas, leading to cleaner, more focused larger-resolution outputs.

In summary, FreeTraj demonstrates state-of-the-art tuning-free trajectory control, superior to existing methods in precision and subjective quality, while effectively mitigating common artifacts and extending its capabilities to longer and larger video formats.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FreeTraj presents a significant contribution to the field of generative AI, particularly in making advanced video synthesis more controllable and accessible. Its tuning-free nature has several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Increased Accessibility and Reduced Computational Burden:** By eliminating the need for additional training or fine-tuning, FreeTraj dramatically lowers the computational resources and specialized data collection efforts required for trajectory control. This democratizes access to advanced video generation capabilities, enabling researchers, developers, and creators without massive computational clusters to experiment with and deploy controllable video synthesis. It makes the technology more practical for real-world applications where rapid prototyping and iteration are crucial.

2. **Enhanced User Control and Customization:** FreeTraj empowers users with concrete geometric control over generated videos, a capability largely missing from basic text-to-video models. Users can now explicitly define object paths, opening up new creative possibilities beyond what is achievable with mere text prompts. The flexibility to provide trajectories manually or integrate with LLM (Large Language Model) trajectory planners suggests a future where natural language descriptions can be translated into precise motion plans, bridging the gap between high-level creative intent and low-level geometric execution.

3. **Deeper Understanding of Diffusion Model Dynamics:** The paper's investigation into how initial noises influence motion trajectories offers valuable insights into the internal workings of video diffusion models. Demonstrating that low-frequency noise components retain spatio-temporal correlations capable of guiding motion is a fundamental discovery. This understanding can inspire future research into manipulating other latent properties of diffusion models for novel control mechanisms, potentially leading to more robust and versatile generative architectures.

4. **Mitigation of Common Generative Artifacts:** The careful design of "soft attention masks" and the understanding of "attention isolation" represent a critical advancement for tuning-free methods. By showing how to introduce control signals without causing severe visual artifacts, FreeTraj provides a blueprint for developing more stable and high-quality inference-time manipulations of diffusion models, benefiting a wider range of control and editing tasks.

5. **Scalability to Longer and Larger Videos:** The seamless integration with existing long video generation frameworks (like FreeNoise) and its ability to suppress object duplication in larger video generation significantly expands the practical utility of trajectory control. This addresses major bottlenecks in real-world video production, where consistent object motion across extended durations and high-resolution outputs are often required.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges that "this tuning-free paradigm is still limited by the underlying model, such as the consistency of object appearance that easily changes during large movements." This highlights that while trajectory is controlled, maintaining perfect object identity, texture, or fine-grained details across complex, long movements remains a challenge. This is an inherent limitation of working with pre-trained models without fine-tuning for specific appearances.

**Overall Impact:** FreeTraj represents a significant step towards practical and user-friendly controllable video generation. By offering a robust tuning-free solution that tackles critical challenges like control precision and artifact generation, it broadens the applicability of diffusion models in creative industries, simulation, and various content creation workflows. Furthermore, its foundational insights into noise manipulation within diffusion models could inspire future research towards developing more inherently controllable and interpretable generative architectures.
