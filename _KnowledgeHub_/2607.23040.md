# Actuator-Aware Spatiotemporal Tube Synthesis for Temporal Reach-Avoid-Stay Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework is presented that synthesizes actuator-aware spatiotemporal tubes (STTs) for unknown nonlinear Multi-Input and Multi-Output (MIMO) systems to complete temporal reach-avoid-stay (T-RAS) tasks, ensuring control efforts remain within actuator limits by design. This approach resulted in an approximate 50% reduction in required control effort during simulations compared to existing STT methods.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems need to perform temporal reach-avoid-stay (T-RAS) tasks in complex, uncertain, and constrained environments, often with unknown nonlinear dynamics.
- Existing Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) and funnel-control frameworks typically address actuator saturation *after* tube synthesis, leading to online re-optimization, unbounded control efforts, or transient constraint violations.
- Abrupt tube shape adjustments in prior methods can demand large, potentially infeasible, control efforts that exceed physical actuator limits.

## Method

- A framework is developed for **actuator-aware Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) synthesis**, parameterizing the tube's centerline and width using ==Bernstein polynomial basis functions==.
- A ==nonlinear optimization problem== is formulated to synthesize the STTs, which directly incorporates a ==linear actuator feasibility condition== derived from the system's ==closed-loop error dynamics==.
- An ==approximation-free Prescribed Performance Controller (PPC)== is employed for tracking, where its intrinsic control input limits are embedded into the tube design process itself.

## Results

- An omnidirectional mobile robot simulation demonstrated successful completion of a T-RAS task, navigating obstacles and reaching the goal while its trajectory remained within the synthesized STT.
- The proposed actuator-aware framework successfully kept all control efforts within the specified actuator limits (maximum control effort **0.831 m/s** for u1, **0.803 m/s** for u2, against a **1.0 m/s** limit).
- Compared to an existing STT method, the proposed approach achieved an approximate **50%** reduction in the total squared norm of control effort (**226.824** vs. **539.813**), and avoided exceeding actuator limits, which the comparative method did significantly (**1.273 m/s** for u1, **2.724 m/s** for u2).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating actuator constraints directly into the STT synthesis, rather than post-hoc, generates tubes that are inherently traversable and feasible by the system's actuation capabilities.
> - Bernstein polynomials enable sample-free enforcement of geometric and derivative constraints for STT design and allow for direct calculation of bounds on centerline velocity and tube width contraction.
> - Analyzing worst-case closed-loop error dynamics provides a critical linear relationship between tube properties (centerline velocity, width contraction) and actuator limits, which can be directly used as a constraint in the optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous systems operating in complex, uncertain, and constrained environments require sophisticated planning and control strategies. Tasks such as a mobile robot navigating sandy ground, an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) flying through a forest with wind gusts, or a legged robot traversing rough terrain often involve reaching a target safely while avoiding obstacles, adhering to state and control bounds. This is often termed a temporal reach-avoid-stay (T-RAS) specification. The challenge is amplified when system dynamics are nonlinear, unknown, or partially known, and subject to physical actuator limits.

Traditional path planning algorithms, including graph-based methods like A* and Dijkstra, and sampling-based methods such as Probabilistic Roadmaps (PRM) and Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT), typically generate geometric paths. These methods require a separate downstream tracking controller, often do not inherently provide time-optimal solutions, and lack formal guarantees regarding system constraints or robustness to uncertainties. Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers an optimization-based approach that considers system dynamics and constraints; however, it generally necessitates an accurate dynamic model, which may be unavailable or difficult to obtain for unknown nonlinear systems.

Funnel-based feedback control techniques represent another class of methods that aim to maintain trajectory tracking within guaranteed bounds. While effective for reachability and tracking, their application to complex collision avoidance tasks can be challenging. Integrations of path planning with funnel-based tracking often require prior trajectories from external planners and may not scale efficiently with an increasing number of obstacles.

The Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) framework addresses some of these limitations by constructing a safe, time-varying, goal-reaching tube in the state space for unknown nonlinear control-affine systems, without the need for explicit trajectory planning. The core concept involves defining STT functions in the output space and employing a Prescribed Performance Controller (PPC)-style tracking controller to confine the system's output within this tube. Related formulations include those for Signal Temporal Logic specifications recast as tube constraints and multi-agent coordination with inter-agent collision avoidance. Existing STT formulations often pose requirements as Robust Optimization Problems (ROP) relaxed into Scenario Optimization Problems (SOP) via discrete-time sampling, which can lead to abrupt tube shape adjustments and large control efforts.

A significant challenge in both STT and funnel-control frameworks is the handling of actuator saturation. Previous approaches typically address input constraints *after* the tube synthesis process. This decoupled treatment often involves modifying the controller online in response to saturation events, replacing closed-form control laws with repeatedly solved optimization problems (e.g., Funnel MPC), or allowing controller gains to grow unboundedly, effectively assuming unlimited actuation capacity. These methods can result in transient violations of actuator limits, increased computational load due to online re-optimization, or a reduction in performance due to controller redesigns.

This work contributes to the field by proposing an actuator-aware STT synthesis framework. It distinguishes itself by directly incorporating actuator constraints into the tube synthesis process, aiming to produce tubes that are feasible by construction. This approach seeks to avoid the issues associated with post-synthesis handling of actuator limits, such as online re-optimization or unbounded control efforts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an actuator-aware Spatiotemporal Tube (STT) synthesis framework. This framework is designed to enable an unknown nonlinear Multi-Input and Multi-Output (MIMO) system to successfully accomplish temporal reach-avoid-stay (T-RAS) tasks while operating strictly within predefined actuator constraints.

The motivation for this specific objective stems from several limitations observed in existing STT synthesis and control methodologies:
- **Decoupled Actuator Handling:** Prior STT synthesis methods typically generate the spatiotemporal tubes first and then address actuator saturation as a separate, subsequent problem. This sequential approach often necessitates online re-optimization of the controller or a redesign of the control law to accommodate input limits. Such online adjustments can be computationally intensive, introduce delays, or lead to suboptimal performance.
- **Unbounded Control Efforts:** Many existing STT and funnel-control frameworks implicitly assume unlimited actuation capacity. To guarantee T-RAS performance, their controllers might theoretically demand unbounded gains, which is physically unrealistic and can cause actuator saturation in real-world applications.
- **Abrupt Tube Adjustments:** When existing methods attempt to avoid obstacles or manage task specifications, the resulting modifications to the tube shape can be abrupt. These sudden changes can translate into large, potentially discontinuous, control efforts that exceed the system's physical capabilities.
- **Lack of Proactive Actuator Consideration:** There is a gap in existing literature regarding frameworks that intrinsically consider and enforce actuator limits *during* the initial tube synthesis phase. A proactive approach would ensure that the generated tube itself is inherently traversable by the system given its actuation capabilities, eliminating the need for reactive measures.

Therefore, the key motivation is to integrate actuator constraints directly into the STT design. The aim is to derive an control input-aware tube design that ensures the Prescribed Performance Controller (PPC) used for tracking the STT operates within actuator bounds by construction, thereby eliminating the need for online re-optimization or subsequent controller modifications. This integration is hypothesized to result in smoother trajectories, reduced control effort, and reliable task completion under physical limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves the synthesis of actuator-aware Spatiotemporal Tubes (STTs) for unknown nonlinear MIMO systems to achieve T-RAS tasks, coupled with an approximation-free prescribed performance controller (PPC).

#### 3.1. System Definition and Problem Formulation

The work considers an N-th order control-affine MIMO nonlinear system described by a series of differential equations. The system state `x(t)` and output `y(t)` are defined. The functions `f_i` and `G_i` are unknown but assumed to be uniformly bounded, continuous, and locally Lipschitz, with `G_i` being positive definite. Bounded unknown external disturbances `d_i(t)` are also considered.
The T-RAS task specification requires the system output `y(t)` to start from a specified set `S`, reach a goal set `T` at a prescribed time `t_g`, and continuously avoid a time-varying unsafe region `O`.
The STTs are defined as a time-varying interval `[ρ(t), ρ̄(t)]` around a nominal center trajectory `c(t)` with a half-width `w(t)`. The STTs must satisfy the T-RAS conditions, including initial, goal, and collision avoidance constraints.
The objective is to synthesize a controller-aware smooth STT `T(t)` for the system that reaches the target region safely without reaching actuator saturation.

#### 3.2. STTs Synthesis using Bernstein Polynomials

- **Parameterization:** The centerline `c(t)` and the width `w(t)` of the STT are parameterized using Bernstein polynomial basis functions of degree `d`. This choice leverages properties such as: (i) interpolation between start and goal points, (ii) the convex-hull property, which guarantees that the entire trajectory remains within the convex hull of its control points, and (iii) the ability to compute derivatives directly from the control points, providing closed-form derivative bounds. The STT trajectories and their derivatives are expressed in terms of Bernstein control points `C_i` and `W_i`.
- **Optimization Problem:** The STT synthesis is formulated as a Nonlinear Optimization Problem (NLP) to determine the optimal Bernstein control points `C*` and `W*`.
 - **Objective Function (Equation 7a, 8):** The optimization minimizes the sum of squared differences between consecutive centerline control points, `||C_{i+1} - C_i||^2`. This term is designed to minimize the centerline velocity, which in turn aims to reduce the control effort required for tracking.
 - **Constraints (Equations 7b-7h):**
 - **Tube Width (7b):** `W_i >= w_min`, ensuring a positive minimum tube width, accommodating robot geometry and minimum error bounds.
 - **Velocity Bounds (7c, 7d):** Constraints are placed on the maximum centerline velocity (`c_max`) and the minimum tube width contraction rate (`w_min`). These are expressed in terms of differences between consecutive control points.
 - **Actuator-Aware Constraint (7e):** This is a key constraint: `c_max - w_min <= (α * u_max - β)`. It establishes a linear relationship between the tube's geometric properties (centerline velocity and width contraction rate) and the system's actuator limits (`u_max`). The parameters `α` and `β` are derived from the controller design to ensure control input feasibility.
 - **Terminal Constraints (7f, 7g):** `||C_0 - y(0)|| <= δ` for the initial condition and `||C_d - y(t_c)|| + w(t_c) <= ρ_G` for the goal-reaching condition, ensuring the tube starts and ends in the specified regions.
 - **Obstacle Avoidance (7h, 10):** The STTs must remain outside the obstacle set `O`. This is enforced using a smoothed log-sum-exp formulation for convex obstacles (or unions of convex shapes), which is a common approach to convert inequality constraints into a smooth, differentiable form for optimization.

#### 3.3. Actuator-Aware Controller Design

- **Controller Type:** An approximation-free closed-form control law based on Prescribed Performance Control (PPC) is utilized, employing a back-stepping approach due to the system's lower-triangular structure.
- **Error Dynamics and Normalization:** Tracking errors `z_i` are defined, with `z_1 = x_1 - y_d`. The performance bounds `ϕ_i` are introduced, where `ϕ_1 = w(t)` (the STT width) and `ϕ_i` for `i >= 2` are time-varying bounds. Normalized errors `e_i = z_i / ϕ_i` are used.
- **Control Input Law (Equation 13):** The control inputs `u_i` are designed using a hyperbolic tangent-like function involving the normalized error `e_i` and a positive definite diagonal gain matrix `K_i`. This specific form helps to constrain control inputs, effectively "saturating" them.
- **Actuator Feasibility Condition Derivation (Equations 14-21):** The core of the actuator-aware design lies in analyzing the worst-case closed-loop error dynamics.
 - The derivatives of the control inputs (`dot(u_i)`) and normalized errors (`dot(e_i)`) are derived.
 - Bounds on `dot(e_i)` and `ψ_i = du_i/de_i` are inferred.
 - The worst-case outward motion of the system (`φ_i`) is calculated by considering bounds on system functions `f_i`, `G_i`, disturbances `d_i`, and previous virtual control input derivatives `dot(u_{i-1})`.
 - The performance constraint (`ϕ_i`) is then formulated such that the worst-case uncertainty (`φ_i`) does not exceed the available guaranteed restoring action from the control input `u_i` plus a term related to the performance bound's contraction rate.
 - For the first error `z_1`, this leads to the critical feasibility condition: `φ_1 <= (g_1 + g_1)u_1 + inf_t dot(w(t))`.
 - This condition is rearranged to `dot(c_max) - dot(w_min) <= (g_1 + g_1)u_1 - (f_1 + g_1 p_2 + d_1)`, where `dot(c_max) = sup_t dot(c(t))` and `dot(w_min) = inf_t dot(w(t))`. This directly corresponds to the actuator-aware constraint (7e) in the STT synthesis NLP, with `α = g_1 + g_1` and `β = f_1 + g_1 p_2 + d_1`. By embedding this derived condition, the synthesis process ensures that the generated tube is compatible with the system's actuator limits.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed actuator-aware STT synthesis framework was evaluated through a simulation study involving an omnidirectional mobile robot performing a T-RAS task.

#### 4.1. Omnidirectional Ground Robot Simulation

- **System Model:** The robot's dynamics are described by a 3-DOF model, where `x_1, x_2` represent position, `x_3` represents orientation, and `u_1, u_2, u_3` are control inputs for linear and angular velocities, subject to disturbances `d(t)`.
- **Task Specification:** The robot was tasked to navigate from a start region `S = [-1.1, 1.8] × [0.1, 0.9]` to a goal region `T = [4.6, 5.4] × [4.6, 5.4]` within a prescribed time `t_c = 7.5` seconds, while avoiding static obstacles.
- **STT Synthesis:** A 7th-degree Bernstein polynomial basis function was used for the time-varying STTs. The optimization problem (Equation 7) was solved to obtain the control points `C` (centerline) and `W` (width) of the STT.
- **Qualitative Demonstration (Figure 1):** Figure 1(a) visually presents the robot's executed trajectory, demonstrating that it successfully navigates around obstacles and reaches the goal. Figure 1(b) illustrates the synthesized STTs, indicating that the robot's path remains confined within the generated tube throughout the task.

#### 4.2. Comparative Analysis

The proposed actuator-aware framework was compared against an existing STT construction technique by Das et al.. For a fair comparison of raw control effort demand, the tracking controller was deployed without the `arctan` saturation function used in Equation 13, meaning `u = -K ln((1-e)/(1+e))`. Both STT synthesis frameworks utilized the same tracking controller and gain `K = diag([1.5, 1.5])`. Actuator limits were set at `u_{1,max} = 1.0 m/s` and `u_{2,max} = 1.0 m/s`.

- **Control Effort (Figure 2 and Table 1):**
 - **Proposed Actuator-Aware STTs Design:**
 - Maximum control effort `u_1`: 0.831 m/s.
 - Maximum control effort `u_2`: 0.803 m/s.
 - Both maximum values remained below the prescribed actuator limit of 1.0 m/s.
 - Total squared norm of control effort (`P ||u_1||^2 + P ||u_2||^2`): 75.690 + 151.134 = 226.824.
 - **Das et al. STTs Design:**
 - Maximum control effort `u_1`: 1.273 m/s.
 - Maximum control effort `u_2`: 2.724 m/s.
 - Both maximum values exceeded the prescribed actuator limit of 1.0 m/s.
 - Total squared norm of control effort (`P ||u_1||^2 + P ||u_2||^2`): 139.295 + 400.518 = 539.813.

- **Key Outcome:** The simulation results demonstrate that the proposed actuator-aware STT synthesis framework successfully keeps the required control effort within the prescribed actuator limits throughout the T-RAS task. In contrast, the existing STT design method demanded control efforts that significantly surpassed these limits. Quantitatively, the proposed framework achieved an approximate 50% reduction in the required control effort (sum of squared norms) compared to the method by Das et al. This reduction highlights the benefit of incorporating actuator constraints directly into the tube synthesis process, leading to more efficient and feasible control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an STT synthesis framework that integrates actuator constraints directly into the tube design process, addressing a limitation in existing methodologies for complex autonomous system control.

#### 5.1. Methodological Advancement

The primary significance lies in the novel approach of creating "actuator-aware" spatiotemporal tubes. By parameterizing the STT centerline and width using Bernstein polynomial basis functions, the framework leverages their convex-hull and derivative properties for sample-free enforcement of geometric and derivative constraints. Crucially, the derivation of a linear actuator feasibility condition from the closed-loop error dynamics of an approximation-free prescribed performance controller (PPC) and its translation into linear constraints on Bernstein control points represent a notable methodological contribution. This integration allows for the synthesis of actuator-feasible tubes by construction, eliminating the need for repeated online re-optimization or complex controller redesigns that characterize previous approaches.

#### 5.2. Enhanced System Reliability and Efficiency

The simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework successfully accomplishes T-RAS tasks while rigorously adhering to prescribed actuator limits. This ensures that the control commands generated are physically realizable by the system, preventing actuator saturation, which can lead to performance degradation, instability, or system failure in real-world applications. Furthermore, the demonstrated approximate 50% reduction in required control effort compared to existing methods suggests a significant improvement in energy efficiency and potentially reduced wear on actuators, contributing to extended operational lifetimes for autonomous platforms.

#### 5.3. Broader Applicability and Impact

This work has potential implications for a range of autonomous systems, particularly those operating with tight physical constraints or in demanding environments. Mobile robots, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and robotic manipulators, which often feature nonlinear dynamics, operate with uncertainties, and possess limited actuation capabilities, could benefit from this framework. It enhances the safety and robustness of task execution by guaranteeing that trajectories are not only safe (obstacle-avoiding, goal-reaching) but also dynamically feasible given the system's hardware limitations. The ability to guarantee actuator feasibility *by design* contributes to more predictable and dependable autonomous operation.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The paper outlines several avenues for future work, including formal stability analysis and recursive feasibility guarantees to further strengthen the theoretical foundations of the framework. Extending the formulation to handle dynamic and uncertain environments with moving obstacles would increase its real-world applicability. Finally, experimental validation on physical platforms would be crucial for demonstrating the practical utility and robustness of the proposed actuator-aware STT synthesis framework beyond simulations.
