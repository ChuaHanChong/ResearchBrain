# HelloWorld: Enabling Socially Interactive Characters in Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HelloWorld is a video world model enabling direct, user-controlled social interactions with in-world characters through a self-distillation training pipeline and a training-free temporal cross-attention mask. This approach yields characters that reliably perform prompted actions, direct their gaze towards the viewer (40.2° GazeDev), and execute interactions at specified times (81.7% TimeAcc), while maintaining high video quality.

## Problem

- Existing video world models lack robust support for direct social interaction between users and in-world characters, featuring static or non-responsive behaviors.
- Virtual characters often exhibit limited naturalness and responsiveness, unable to perform specific social gestures in direct response to user input with precise temporal control.
- There is an absence of dedicated benchmarks and metrics to effectively evaluate the quality and effectiveness of character interactions within video world models.

## Method

- A ==self-distillation== pipeline finetunes a pretrained video generation model, using its own generated data, to acquire world model capabilities and support rich social interactions.
- A training-free ==temporal cross-attention mask== is employed during inference to precisely localize character interactions within user-defined temporal windows.
- The **'HelloWorldBench'** benchmark is introduced with novel metrics (Action Accuracy, Timing Accuracy, Gaze Deviation) to specifically evaluate socially interactive world models.

## Results

- HelloWorld achieved an **81.7%** TimeAcc, significantly surpassing baselines (**30.9%** to **52.6%**), and a GazeDev of **40.2°**, demonstrating superior temporal localization and viewer-directed interactions.
- The model maintained high video quality (Aesthetic score **5.27**) and camera controllability (CamCtrl **82.9%**), performing competitively or better than state-of-the-art baselines.
- A user study confirmed a statistically significant preference for HelloWorld over competing methods across criteria like 'Action Naturalness', 'Interaction', and 'Scene Quality'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging a frozen base model to generate interaction-rich training data through self-distillation efficiently enhances social interaction capabilities without requiring external datasets.
> - A simple, training-free temporal cross-attention mask can effectively control the precise timing of complex social interactions in video generation.
> - Using a 'warp video' as explicit geometric guidance for camera motion allows the text prompt to focus its capacity on intricate scene and interaction details.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video world models has demonstrated significant advancements in simulating visual environments and generating future frames in response to dynamic conditions. Recent models have introduced interactivity, allowing users to control camera trajectories or trigger events within these generated worlds. These capabilities have found applications in areas such as game production, world simulation, and film making, enabling virtual environments that respond to user input.

However, a notable limitation in the current landscape of video world models is the absence of robust support for direct social interaction between the user and the characters populating these worlds. Existing models typically feature static characters or characters exhibiting ambient, non-interactive behaviors. While some progress has been made in generating animated characters, their motions are generally not directed towards the user or responsive to user-initiated social cues. Other related works have explored object interactions within worlds or interactions between non-player characters (NPCs) within specific game contexts. Concurrently, research in multimodal social interactions has focused on understanding and generating human communication, including speech, facial expressions, gaze, and body movements, leading to advancements in talking video generation and 3D motion generation. While some "social world models" leverage large language models (LLMs) to simulate social dynamics, they are often limited to text modality, lacking multimodal interactivity.

"HelloWorld" addresses this identified gap by introducing a video world model specifically designed to enable socially interactive characters. It extends the capabilities of existing world models beyond environmental reconstruction and camera control to include direct, viewer-directed social behaviors. This work differentiates itself by being the first video world model to focus on multimodal social interaction between in-world characters and the user, encompassing diverse character types (human, animal, toy) and a broad vocabulary of social behaviors (actions, gestures, facial expressions, and speech).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "HelloWorld" project is to develop an interactive video world model that allows users to actively create and control social interactions with characters situated within the generated virtual environments. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

Firstly, the researchers aim to overcome the current limitation of video world models, which, despite their progress in simulating visual worlds and supporting interactive camera controls, do not facilitate direct social engagement with in-world characters. Existing characters often appear as static elements or perform pre-programmed, non-responsive actions, diminishing the potential for immersive and engaging user experiences.

Secondly, the motivation stems from the desire to enhance the naturalness and responsiveness of virtual characters. The project seeks to enable characters to perform specific social gestures—such as turning towards the viewer, waving, nodding, or delivering a short greeting—in direct response to user input (e.g., a button press). This capability is intended to make interactions feel more intuitive and integrated within the generated world.

Thirdly, the development of "HelloWorld" is motivated by the need for precise temporal control over these interactions. It aims to ensure that social responses occur at user-specified moments, rather than being ambient or randomly timed. This level of control is considered crucial for creating coherent and intentional interactive narratives.

Finally, the project identifies a gap in existing evaluation methodologies for video world models. Current benchmarks primarily focus on aspects like scene fidelity, background consistency, and camera controllability, largely overlooking the quality and effectiveness of character interactions. Therefore, a significant objective is to establish a dedicated benchmark, "HelloWorldBench," equipped with specific metrics to rigorously assess the "what," "when," and "whether" of social interactions, thereby providing a comprehensive framework for evaluating socially interactive world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

"HelloWorld" is structured around a two-component system: a self-distillation pipeline for model finetuning during training and a training-free temporal cross-attention mask for inference. This framework allows the model to generate socially interactive videos conditioned on a first-frame image, a text prompt, a camera trajectory, and an interaction window specified by user input.

**Preliminary Task Formulation:**
The video world model, denoted as G, accepts four inputs: an initial frame (x₀) depicting the scene and characters; a text prompt (y) describing the world and interaction; a camera trajectory (C) specifying camera movement across N frames; and an interaction window (W = [τ_s, τ_e]) defining the temporal segment for social interaction. The model generates a video (V) where the camera adheres to C, and the character interacts with the viewer within W.

A crucial conditioning mechanism is the "warp video" (V_warp), which is a pseudo-video created by lifting the first frame (x₀) into a point cloud using an off-the-shelf 3D reconstructor (Pi3X) and reprojecting it along the target camera trajectory (C). V_warp serves as explicit geometric guidance for camera motion, and its tokens, after discarding invalid observations, are injected as history tokens into the DiT (Diffusion Transformer) model.

**Training with Self-Distillation:**
To imbue a pretrained video generation model with world model capabilities while preserving its capacity for rich social interactions, "HelloWorld" employs a self-distillation training pipeline:
1. **Training Data Preparation:** The process begins by prompting a frozen base video generation model (LTX-2.3) with elaborate text prompts. These prompts are composed of four parts: `y_scene` (scene description), `y_inter` (character interactions), `y_camera` (camera trajectory), and `y_quality` (video quality). This allows the base model to synthesize interaction-rich video clips that inherently include camera motions. Subsequently, Pi3X is applied to these generated videos to recover the first-frame point cloud and the precise per-frame camera trajectory.
2. **Finetuning Process:** For each synthesized clip, a warp video is rendered using the recovered first-frame point cloud and camera trajectory. A visible-token selection module then filters out warp tokens corresponding to occluded or out-of-view regions, retaining only valid geometric information. A lightweight LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) module (rank-32), applied to the self-attention projection matrices within the video branch of the DiT, is finetuned. The model is trained using a flow matching loss, conditioned on the first-frame tokens, noise tokens, and the filtered warp tokens. Notably, the camera-related part of the text prompt (`y_camera`) is deliberately excluded during finetuning. This design ensures that the model learns to follow the specified camera trajectory exclusively through the warp video condition, thereby dedicating the text prompt capacity to scene and interaction details. This self-distillation approach eliminates the need for external data collection or manual annotation.

**Inference with Temporal Cross-Attention Mask:**
During inference, "HelloWorld" incorporates a training-free temporal cross-attention mask to precisely control the timing of character interactions:
1. **Input Translation:** User keyboard inputs are translated into a camera trajectory (C), from which the warp video is constructed. The system then follows the same forward pass procedure as during training.
2. **Interaction Trigger:** When the user presses an "interaction button F" at time τ_s, an interaction window W = [τ_s, τ_e] (e.g., spanning subsequent seconds) is defined.
3. **Cross-Attention Masking:** A temporal mask (M) is applied to the cross-attention layers of the DiT. This mask prevents video and audio query tokens *outside* the interaction window W from attending to text tokens related to the interaction prompt (`y_inter`). Specifically, if a query token's temporal position `i` is outside W and a text token `j` belongs to `y_inter`, the attention weight `M_ij` is set to `-∞`. Otherwise, it is `0`. This mechanism ensures that the character's social response is temporally localized within the specified window, while the character exhibits ambient behavior outside of it. This training-free module incurs negligible computational overhead.

**HelloWorldBench:**
To evaluate socially interactive world models, the authors introduce "HelloWorldBench," a dedicated benchmark.
1. **Dataset Construction:** It comprises 120 high-quality images from Unsplash, featuring diverse subjects (humans, animals, toys, robots) and scenes. For each image, an LLM agent generates 2-4 subject-appropriate interaction descriptions. These are combined with four common camera trajectories (static, scan, dolly-in, orbit) and randomly assigned interaction timings (early, middle, late) to create 400 evaluation samples.
2. **Evaluation Metrics:** Beyond conventional metrics like background consistency (BgCons), aesthetic score (Aesthetic), and camera controllability (CamCtrl), three interaction-specific metrics are proposed:
 - **ActAcc (Action Accuracy) ↑**: Measures if the generated character performs the prompted interaction. A VLM judge (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) answers an 8-way multiple-choice question on the character's action.
 - **TimeAcc (Timing Accuracy) ↑**: Assesses if the interaction occurs at the user-specified moment. The VLM judge selects the correct temporal segment (e.g., 0-3s, 3-6s, 6-9s) for the interaction or "no interaction."
 - **GazeDev (Gaze Deviation) ↓**: For human samples, this metric quantifies whether the interaction is directed towards the viewer. It calculates the mean angular deviation between the estimated gaze direction (using UniGaze) and the camera's optical axis within the interaction window, assigning 90° if no face is detected.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the "HelloWorldBench" demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed model across social interaction, video quality, and camera control metrics, in comparison to various baselines.

**Comparison with Baselines (Table 1):**
"HelloWorld" consistently outperformed existing world models and video generation models, including WorldPlay, Matrix-Game 3.0, LingBot-World, SANA-WM, Warp-as-History, and the base model LTX-2.3.
- **Social Interaction:** "HelloWorld" achieved the highest scores on two of the three interaction metrics and a competitive score on the third.
 - **TimeAcc**: "HelloWorld" recorded 81.7%, significantly higher than all baselines, which ranged from 30.9% to 52.6%. This indicates that the proposed temporal cross-attention mask effectively localizes character interactions to the specified timing window, a capability largely absent in other models.
 - **GazeDev**: "HelloWorld" achieved the lowest gaze deviation of 40.2°, suggesting that its characters are more reliably directed towards the viewer compared to baselines (ranging up to 77.2°).
 - **ActAcc**: While LingBot-World had a slightly higher ActAcc (50.5%), "HelloWorld" was competitive at 41.4%, significantly outperforming most other baselines (e.g., WorldPlay at 10.5%). This demonstrates that "HelloWorld" generates the intended social actions with a high degree of fidelity.
- **Video Quality and Camera Control:** "HelloWorld" maintained competitive or superior performance in conventional metrics.
 - **Aesthetic**: "HelloWorld" scored 5.27, slightly higher than the best baseline LingBot-World (5.21) and Warp-as-History (5.14).
 - **BgCons (Background Consistency)**: At 96.9%, "HelloWorld" demonstrated high scene fidelity, comparable to Warp-as-History (95.3%).
 - **CamCtrl (Camera Controllability)**: "HelloWorld" achieved the highest score of 82.9%, surpassing SANA-WM (70.0%) and Warp-as-History (65.1%), confirming the efficacy of warp-video conditioning for camera control.

**Qualitative Visualizations (Figure 5):**
Visual comparisons with baselines reinforced the quantitative findings. "HelloWorld" consistently generated faithful, viewer-directed interactions, where characters accurately performed prompted actions and maintained eye contact with the camera. In contrast, baseline models frequently failed to produce correct interactions, showing characters not looking at the camera, performing incorrect actions, or exhibiting visual hallucinations (e.g., an extra character appearing to "make a heart").

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Training Data (Table 2, Figure 6a):** Comparing models trained with real videos (Real-video), self-generated human-only interaction videos (Human-only), and full self-generated interaction videos (Full).
 - Training with self-generated interaction data (Human-only and Full) significantly improved ActAcc and GazeDev compared to training on real videos (which lack social interactions). For instance, GazeDev dropped from 51.3° (Real-video) to 40.2° (Full).
 - Other metrics like TimeAcc, video quality, and camera following remained largely unaffected, indicating that self-distillation enhances interaction capabilities without degrading other aspects.
 - Qualitative results showed that models trained without interaction data could perform actions but not direct them toward the viewer, often with hallucinations.
- **Effect of Temporal Cross-Attention Masks (Table 3, Figure 6b):**
 - Applying the mask to the video stream (M_v) significantly improved TimeAcc (from 36.7% to 80.9%), demonstrating its effectiveness in temporal localization.
 - Masking both video and audio streams (M_v + M_a) further improved "SpeechInWin" (accuracy of speech starting within the interaction window) from 62.8% to 69.1%, alongside a slight increase in TimeAcc (to 81.7%).
 - The slight decrease in ActAcc when masks were applied was attributed to the cost of temporal localization, not a loss of interaction ability, as characters would otherwise perform actions throughout the video.
 - Qualitative examples illustrated that without the mask, interactions could occur at arbitrary times despite explicit text prompts, while the mask ensured precise timing.

**Computational Cost (Table 4):**
Generating a 10-second clip with "HelloWorld" at 1280x704 resolution and 24 fps took 60.2 seconds (0.26 s/frame), incurring 9.4x10^15 FLOPs. This represents a modest increase (approximately 20% inference time, 36% FLOPs) compared to the base model (LTX-2.3) due to warp-video conditioning, yet remains competitive with or faster than many baselines.

**User Study (Table 5):**
A user study involving 30 human raters evaluating 41 samples confirmed the perceptual quality of "HelloWorld." Users preferred "HelloWorld" over four competing methods across all three assessed criteria: "Action Naturalness," "Interaction," and "Scene Quality." All confidence intervals for "HelloWorld"'s preference were above 66%, indicating a statistically significant preference. The most substantial margin was observed against the real-video LoRA model in the "Interaction" category, validating the self-distillation strategy's role in enhancing the perceived quality of social interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "HelloWorld" project represents a significant advancement in the field of video world models by introducing socially interactive characters, a capability previously underexplored. The work’s significance stems from several key contributions and its potential implications for various applications.

Firstly, the primary contribution is the development of a video world model that facilitates direct social interaction between users and in-world characters. This fills a critical gap in existing models, which typically support environmental manipulation and camera control but lack direct character responsiveness. By enabling characters to perform diverse, viewer-directed social actions (such as waving, nodding, or speaking) in a timely manner, "HelloWorld" creates a more immersive and engaging user experience within generated virtual worlds.

Secondly, the methodological innovations presented are notable. The self-distillation pipeline allows for the finetuning of a pretrained video generation model into an interactive world model without requiring new, manually collected or annotated datasets. This efficiency in data utilization is a practical advantage, as it leverages the model's own generations to learn specific behaviors while preserving its foundational video generation quality. The introduction of a training-free temporal cross-attention mask provides a novel and efficient mechanism for precise control over the timing of interactions, ensuring character responses align with user-specified moments without additional training overhead.

Thirdly, the creation of "HelloWorldBench" is a substantial contribution to the research community. As the first benchmark specifically designed for evaluating socially interactive world models, it provides a standardized framework with dedicated metrics (ActAcc, TimeAcc, GazeDev) to assess the "what," "when," and "whether" of character interactions. This benchmark facilitates rigorous evaluation and comparison of future research in this emerging domain.

The demonstrated performance of "HelloWorld," which surpasses existing models in social interaction quality while maintaining state-of-the-art video aesthetics and camera controllability, underscores the effectiveness of its design. This comprehensive capability positions "HelloWorld" as a foundational step towards more sophisticated and human-centric generative AI applications.

The potential impact of this work is broad. In **game development**, it could enable more responsive and believable non-player characters (NPCs) that genuinely react to players, enhancing gameplay immersion. For **interactive storytelling** and **virtual reality/augmented reality experiences**, "HelloWorld" could facilitate dynamic narratives where virtual characters engage directly with the audience, responding to their cues. It also has implications for **virtual assistants** and **digital avatars**, making them more personable and interactive. The ability to generate complex, directed social interactions could further advance the state-of-the-art in **generative AI for film making** and **content creation**, allowing for the rapid prototyping of interactive scenes.

Future work outlined by the authors includes transitioning from pre-specified camera trajectories and interaction scripts to **real-time, autoregressive interaction** to enable spontaneous user engagement. Other important directions involve supporting **longer video generation** and ensuring **consistent modeling of individual characters**, including persistent identities and sustained multi-round interactions, which would further enhance the realism and utility of socially interactive world models.
