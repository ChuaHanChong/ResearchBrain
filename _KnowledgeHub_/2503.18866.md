# Reasoning to Learn from Latent Thoughts

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University, the University of Toronto, and the Vector Institute introduced "Reasoning to Learn," a framework that enhances large language model data efficiency by explicitly modeling and leveraging latent thought processes during pretraining. Their Bootstrapping Latent Thought (BoLT) algorithm allows models to iteratively improve their ability to generate and learn from high-quality latent thoughts, achieving 25.38% on MATH and outperforming strong baselines by a large margin.

## Problem

- Data scarcity and the data bottleneck threaten to limit the continued scaling and advancement of large language models.
- Existing synthetic data generation techniques often fail to explicitly capture the underlying human reasoning processes crucial for efficient learning.
- LMs need mechanisms to improve their learning efficiency from observed data without relying on extensive external supervision or task-specific rewards.

## Method

- Developed =="Reasoning to Learn"==, a latent variable approach that trains LMs to decompress observed text (X) by inferring ==natural language latent thoughts (Z)==, modeling the joint distribution p(Z, X).
- Introduced the ==Bootstrapping Latent Thought (BoLT)== algorithm, an ==Expectation-Maximization (EM)== based iterative self-improvement loop. In the E-step, the model generates improved latent thoughts, and in the M-step, it trains on these bootstrapped (X, Z) pairs.
- Utilized a frontier model (GPT-4o-mini) for warmstarting latent thought generation, then allowed the model to self-improve, generating latent thoughts in a dedicated =='latent space'== using special tokens during next-token prediction training.

## Results

- Training with GPT-4o-mini-synthesized latent thoughts achieved **25.38%** on MATH, significantly outperforming baselines like Raw-Repeat (**5.74%**) and WRAP-CoT (**19.36%**) while using **3.3x** fewer raw data epochs.
- The BoLT algorithm demonstrated monotonic self-improvement over several iterations on fixed data, consistently increasing both the ELBO and downstream MATH accuracy.
- Models trained with BoLT using increased Monte Carlo samples (K) for latent generation showed improved ELBO and MATH accuracy, affirming the link between inference compute investment and latent quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling latent thoughts in a separate, dedicated space is crucial for data-efficient learning, outperforming methods that intersperse reasoning directly within the raw text.
> - Language models can iteratively bootstrap their own ability to infer and utilize high-quality latent thoughts through an EM-like self-improvement process, moving beyond initial external supervision.
> - Increased inference compute during the E-step (latent generation) directly correlates with higher-quality synthetic latents and improved downstream model performance, offering a new avenue for scaling data efficiency.
