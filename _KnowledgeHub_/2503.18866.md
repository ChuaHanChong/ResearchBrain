# Reasoning to Learn from Latent Thoughts

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University, the University of Toronto, and the Vector Institute introduced "Reasoning to Learn," a framework that enhances large language model data efficiency by explicitly modeling and leveraging latent thought processes during pretraining. Their Bootstrapping Latent Thought (BoLT) algorithm allows models to iteratively improve their ability to generate and learn from high-quality latent thoughts, achieving 25.38% on MATH and outperforming strong baselines by a large margin.

## Problem

- Data scarcity and the data bottleneck threaten to limit the continued scaling and advancement of large language models.
- Existing synthetic data generation techniques often fail to explicitly capture the underlying human reasoning processes crucial for efficient learning.
- LMs need mechanisms to improve their learning efficiency from observed data without relying on extensive external supervision or task-specific rewards.

## Method

- Developed =="Reasoning to Learn"==, a latent variable approach that trains LMs to decompress observed text (X) by inferring ==natural language latent thoughts (Z)==, modeling the joint distribution p(Z, X).
- Introduced the ==Bootstrapping Latent Thought (BoLT)== algorithm, an ==Expectation-Maximization (EM)== based iterative self-improvement loop. In the E-step, the model generates improved latent thoughts, and in the M-step, it trains on these bootstrapped (X, Z) pairs.
- Utilized a frontier model (GPT-4o-mini) for warmstarting latent thought generation, then allowed the model to self-improve, generating latent thoughts in a dedicated =='latent space'== using special tokens during next-token prediction training.

## Results

- Training with GPT-4o-mini-synthesized latent thoughts achieved **25.38%** on MATH, significantly outperforming baselines like Raw-Repeat (**5.74%**) and WRAP-CoT (**19.36%**) while using **3.3x** fewer raw data epochs.
- The BoLT algorithm demonstrated monotonic self-improvement over several iterations on fixed data, consistently increasing both the ELBO and downstream MATH accuracy.
- Models trained with BoLT using increased Monte Carlo samples (K) for latent generation showed improved ELBO and MATH accuracy, affirming the link between inference compute investment and latent quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling latent thoughts in a separate, dedicated space is crucial for data-efficient learning, outperforming methods that intersperse reasoning directly within the raw text.
> - Language models can iteratively bootstrap their own ability to infer and utilize high-quality latent thoughts through an EM-like self-improvement process, moving beyond initial external supervision.
> - Increased inference compute during the E-step (latent generation) directly correlates with higher-quality synthetic latents and improved downstream model performance, offering a new avenue for scaling data efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a growing concern in the field of large language models (LMs): the potential for **data scarcity to become a bottleneck for continued model scaling**. As computational resources for LM pretraining rapidly increase, the supply of high-quality human-written text data is not keeping pace, threatening to limit future advancements. The paper positions its work as a crucial step towards improving **data efficiency** in this data-constrained regime.

The authors contrast their approach, termed "reasoning to learn," with existing paradigms in several ways:
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** Previous works (e.g., Eldan & Li, Gunasekar et al., Maini et al.) have explored generating synthetic data to augment or replace human-written text. This paper distinguishes itself by taking a **latent variable perspective**, augmenting *each document chunk* with inferred "latent thoughts" (Z) rather than replacing or merely paraphrasing the raw text (X). This augmentation is shown to be crucial for downstream performance, outperforming strong synthetic data generation baselines that embed reasoning directly in the surface text.
- **Learning to Reason vs. Reasoning to Learn:** The paper draws a clear distinction between its approach and "learning to reason" paradigms (e.g., RL-based methods like STar, o1, R1; or supervised finetuning of CoT). These methods typically rely on explicit external supervision, verifiable rewards, or task-specific data to improve LMs' reasoning capabilities. In contrast, "reasoning to learn" focuses on enabling LMs to *improve their learning efficiency from observed data* by inferring the underlying thought processes, operating purely on **task-agnostic pretraining data** through likelihood-based supervision. The authors view these as complementary, suggesting their approach could provide a better starting point for subsequent task-specific reasoning training.
- **Latent Variable Inference for Reasoning:** While some works explore latent variable inference for reasoning traces, these often remain domain-specific or tied to external rewards. This paper extends the latent variable perspective to *pretraining data augmentation* with a focus on general-domain applicability, using the LM itself as a "prior" for generating latent thoughts.
- **Self-Improvement Mechanisms:** The paper contributes to the growing interest in LM self-improvement. Unlike teacher-student distillation settings prevalent in synthetic data research, this work focuses on **bootstrapping the data generator itself**—an LM iteratively improving its ability to synthesize high-quality latent thoughts, which in turn trains more capable models. This self-improvement loop, powered by an Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm, is a novel application in the context of data-efficient pretraining.

In essence, this research introduces a principled, scalable framework for enhancing data efficiency by making the implicit human reasoning process explicit within the LM's learning pipeline, leveraging the model's own capabilities for iterative self-improvement without relying on external task-specific supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the looming **data bottleneck in large language model scaling**. As the computational power for pretraining continues to grow exponentially, the availability of diverse and high-quality human-generated text data is becoming increasingly limited. This imbalance poses a significant challenge to the continued advancement of powerful LMs.

The key objectives are to:
1. **Improve Pretraining Data Efficiency:** Develop a novel approach that allows LMs to extract more capabilities from a fixed or limited amount of raw text data.
2. **Explicitly Model Latent Thoughts:** Introduce a paradigm where LMs are trained to "decompress" the underlying human thought processes and contextual knowledge that precede the generation of observed text. The hypothesis is that these latent thoughts contain critical reasoning steps and contextual information that are highly valuable for data-efficient learning.
3. **Enable LM Self-Improvement:** Design an iterative mechanism that allows an LM to bootstrap its own performance. This means the model should be able to progressively improve its ability to infer and utilize latent thoughts, leading to a virtuous cycle of enhanced capability and data efficiency, without requiring a "strong teacher" or additional external supervision beyond initial warmstarting.
4. **Demonstrate Practical Scalability:** Validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach in a data-constrained continued pretraining setting, particularly for reasoning-intensive tasks, and show how inference compute can be leveraged to further improve data efficiency during the self-improvement process.

The authors draw an analogy to human learning, where individuals "decompress" complex, compressed texts (like research papers) by inferring the author's underlying reasoning. By teaching LMs to emulate this "reasoning to learn" process, the goal is to bridge the gap between human and LM data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, "Reasoning to Learn," is built upon a **latent variable perspective** of language modeling, where observed data (X) is considered the outcome of underlying latent thoughts (Z).

#### 3.1. Latent Thought Models

- **Formalization:** The approach models the human data generation process as a joint distribution `p(Z, X) = p(Z)p(X | Z)`. An LM is trained not just on `p(X)` but to explicitly "decompress" `Z` from `X` (approximating `q(Z | X)`) and to learn from `X` augmented with `Z` (`p(Z, X)`).
- **Natural Language Latents:** A key insight is that latent thoughts (Z) can be encoded in natural language, allowing the LM itself to act as a prior for generating these thoughts via its existing reasoning abilities. This simplifies training and enables weight sharing.
- **Training Setup:** The LM is trained using standard next-token prediction. Given paired data `{(Z_n, X_n)}`, `Z_n` is randomly placed either *before* `X_n` (to train `p(Z_n, X_n)`) or *after* `X_n` (to train `q(Z_n | X_n)`). Special `<StartOfLatent>`, `<EndOfLatent>`, `<Prior>`, and `<Post>` tokens delineate and contextualize the latent thoughts.

#### 3.2. Training with Synthetic Latent Thoughts (Warmstart)

- Initially, human-generated data lacks explicit latent thoughts. Thus, a "surrogate posterior" `~q(Z | X)` is used to synthesize `Z` to augment the raw data `X`.
- **GPT-4o-mini as Teacher:** For warmstarting, a powerful frontier model like GPT-4o-mini is prompted (using a carefully designed prompt, C.1.1) to infer missing reasoning steps or background knowledge for chunks of text from a dataset.
- **Data Chunking:** Documents are chunked into segments (Poisson distribution, mean 8 sentences), and one latent thought is generated for each chunk, conditioned on preceding chunks (context window L=3).

#### 3.3. Prediction with Latent Thoughts

- During evaluation, few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting is applied. Instead of placing CoT reasoning directly in the raw text space (`X`), it is placed within the special latent tokens (`<StartOfLatent><Prior>...<EndOfLatent>`) to leverage the model's trained latent reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.4. Bootstrapping Latent Thought Models (BoLT) - The Self-Improvement Loop

To overcome the limitation of relying on a strong external teacher, BoLT implements an **Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm**:
- **E-step (Synthesizing Better Latents):**
 - For each observed text chunk `X`, `K` latent samples `Z^(k)` are drawn from the current model's approximate posterior `q(Z | X; M_t)`.
 - Importance weights `w^(k) = p(Z^(k),X;M_t) / q(Z^(k)|X;M_t)` are computed. These weights favor latents that are both simple (high `p(Z)`) and predictive of the data (high `p(X|Z)`).
 - One latent `Z_n` is then resampled from the categorical distribution proportional to these importance weights. This procedure is shown to sample from a tighter Importance-Weighted Autoencoder (IWAE) ELBO, serving as a "policy improvement operator" that finds higher-quality latents. Scaling `K` (inference compute) directly improves latent quality.
- **M-step (Training with Bootstrapped Latents):**
 - The model `M_{t+1}` is trained on the augmented dataset `(X_t, Z_t)`, where `Z_t` are the improved latents from the E-step. This training process (next-token prediction with special tokens) reinforces the model's ability to generate and utilize these higher-quality thoughts.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** TinyLlama-1.1B (Zhang et al., 2024), chosen for its suitability for academic budgets and lack of prior mathematical reasoning training.
- **Dataset:** FineMath-4+ (Lozhkov et al., 2024), a reasoning-intensive web corpus, used for data-constrained continued pretraining (CPT).
- **Data Budget:** 480M unique "raw" tokens, with an 8B total training token compute budget.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Raw-Repeat:** Training on raw corpus for many epochs.
 - **Raw-Fresh:** Training on 8B unique raw tokens (anticipated upper bound).
 - **WRAP-Orig:** Rephrasing raw data in diverse styles (easy, hard, Wikipedia, Q&A) using GPT-4o-mini (Maini et al., 2024).
 - **WRAP-CoT:** A strong variant of WRAP that rephrases documents with *interspersed reasoning steps* directly in the raw text space, to test if gains are simply from adding reasoning traces.
- **Evaluation:** MATH, GSM8K, and MMLU-STEM benchmarks, using both synthetic and standard few-shot CoT prompting. Finetuning evaluations are also used to mitigate in-context learning confounds.
- **Bootstrapping Setups:**
 - **Fixed Data:** Iterative self-improvement on a single corpus, retraining from scratch at each M-step.
 - **Continual Bootstrapping:** Iterative improvement across a series of logarithmically increasing corpora, initializing `M_t` from `M_{t-1}` (resembling continual learning).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the effectiveness of "reasoning to learn" and the BoLT algorithm:

1. **Synthetic Latent Thoughts Significantly Improve Data Efficiency:**
 - Training with GPT-4o-mini-synthesized latent thoughts ("Latent Thought") achieved **25.38% on MATH** (using synthetic few-shot CoT), drastically outperforming baselines like Raw-Repeat (5.74%), Raw-Fresh (11.18%), and even the strong WRAP-CoT (19.36%). Similar gains were observed on GSM8K and MMLU-STEM.
 - This performance gain was achieved despite seeing the raw data 3.3x fewer times than baselines, highlighting strong data efficiency.
 - The method showed robustness to prompt variations, unlike some baselines that degraded with standard CoT prompts.

2. **Explicit Latent Space is Crucial:**
 - Ablation studies confirmed that modeling latent thoughts (`Z`) in a space *separate* from the observed text (`X`) is critical. Mixing latents directly into the raw text space during training (like WRAP-CoT) or using CoT in the raw text space during evaluation resulted in notably lower performance. This supports the hypothesis that explicitly disentangling thought processes from final text improves learning.

3. **BoLT Enables Monotonic Self-Improvement:**
 - In the "bootstrapping on fixed data" setup, BoLT consistently improved model performance across multiple iterations (up to at least three, sometimes four) on both likelihood-based metrics (ELBO, NLL) and downstream MATH accuracy. This demonstrates a clear self-improvement loop where a model generates higher-quality latents, which in turn train a more capable model.
 - BoLT-trained models significantly outperformed raw data baselines (both FLOP-matched and token-matched) across iterations, showing the value of self-generated latents for data efficiency.
 - Finetuning evaluations confirmed these gains, suggesting the improvements are robust beyond in-context learning abilities.

4. **Scaling Inference Compute Improves Latent Quality:**
 - Increasing the number of Monte Carlo samples (`K`) during the E-step (latent generation) monotonically improved the ELBO and downstream MATH accuracy of the trained models. This suggests a new avenue for scaling pretraining efficiency by investing more inference compute into generating higher-quality latent thoughts.

5. **BoLT Works in Continual Learning Settings:**
 - In the "continual bootstrapping" setup, iteratively improving the latent thought model led to steeper scaling trends in likelihood metrics and consistently higher downstream MATH performance compared to baselines that fixed the latent generator at earlier, less capable iterations. This indicates that BoLT can effectively integrate into practical continual pretraining scenarios.

6. **Potential Side-Effects Observed:**
 - Few-shot GSM8K performance showed a decline in later BoLT iterations, though this was not seen after task-specific finetuning. This "negative" result is hypothesized to be due to increased optimization on the math-heavy FineMath corpus potentially degrading natural language understanding required for GSM8K word problems (supported by NLL degradation on a general-domain corpus like DCLM).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant contribution to the field of large language models by introducing a novel and effective paradigm, "Reasoning to Learn," to tackle the critical challenge of data scarcity. Its potential impacts are multifaceted:

1. **Addressing Data Bottleneck:** The primary significance lies in offering a promising path to continue scaling LM capabilities even when human-written data becomes a limiting factor. By enabling models to extract more value from existing data, this approach can extend the lifespan of current datasets and reduce the reliance on ever-larger, newly curated corpora.

2. **Complementary to "Learning to Reason":** The paper establishes "reasoning to learn" as a distinct but complementary paradigm to "learning to reason." While the latter focuses on improving task-specific reasoning capabilities, the former enhances the fundamental data efficiency of pretraining. LMs trained with "reasoning to learn" could serve as superior base models for subsequent task-specific fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, potentially transferring general-domain reasoning abilities learned at scale.

3. **Leveraging Asynchronous Compute:** The BoLT algorithm’s E-step involves synthetic data generation, which can be distributed across disparate, asynchronous computing resources. This "shifts a portion of the overall synchronous pretraining compute to an asynchronous workload," potentially inspiring new infrastructure designs for LM pretraining that make more efficient use of heterogeneous and distributed hardware. The finding that increasing Monte Carlo samples improves latent quality provides a concrete axis for scaling this asynchronous compute.

4. **Enabling Self-Improvement Loops:** BoLT demonstrates a powerful mechanism for models to iteratively self-improve their learning efficiency and latent thought generation abilities without requiring continuous external human supervision or a stronger teacher model after an initial warmstart. This self-referential improvement loop could unlock new trajectories for autonomous model development.

5. **Generalizability Beyond Text:** The underlying principle that observed human data is a compressed outcome of latent thought processes is not limited to text. The authors suggest that this approach could be applied to other data modalities (e.g., video data, multimodal data), where the data efficiency problem is often even more pronounced. This opens up exciting avenues for future research in diverse AI domains.

6. **Insights into Model Internals:** By forcing LMs to explicitly model and generate latent thoughts, this work could also offer deeper insights into how models process information and perform reasoning, potentially fostering more interpretable and controllable AI systems.

While the current experiments are limited to a 1.1B parameter model and a math-heavy corpus due to computational constraints, the robust gains and self-improvement observed lay a strong foundation for future work to scale this approach to larger models and general-domain pretraining data, paving the way for more data-efficient and autonomously evolving language intelligence.
