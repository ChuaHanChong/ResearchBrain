# SPIRAL: A Closed-Loop Framework for Self-Improving Action World Models via Reflective Planning Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed SPIRAL, a closed-loop framework for generating controllable, long-horizon videos conditioned on high-level semantic actions. This framework integrates reflective planning agents and a self-improving mechanism, demonstrating enhanced action completeness and temporal consistency in generated videos, particularly for complex and extended tasks.

## Problem

- Existing one-shot, open-loop text-to-video generation models struggle with incomplete action execution and weak semantic grounding for high-level commands over long durations.
- These models suffer from long-horizon temporal incoherence and object/scene drift due to a lack of explicit state representation and memory.
- Open-loop designs lead to error accumulation, where initial failures propagate and amplify over time, degrading video quality and fidelity.

## Method

- **SPIRAL** implements a hierarchical, ==closed-loop "think-act-reflect" paradigm==, comprising a ==PlanAgent== for structured reasoning, an ==Action-Conditioned World Model== for video synthesis, and a ==CriticAgent== for verification and feedback.
- The **PlanAgent** uses ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== reasoning and ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)== to decompose high-level goals into executable action sequences with pre- and post-conditions.
- A VLM-parameterized **CriticAgent** provides dual-level feedback (local refinement and global replanning) and scalar rewards, enabling continuous policy improvement for the World Model via ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== and a ==World Memory== module.

## Results

- The full SPIRAL PlanAgent achieved **58.72%** accuracy on EgoPlan-Bench, outperforming GPT-5.1 by **3.94%** and fine-tuned Video-LLaMA baselines by **6.89%**, with World Memory maintaining stable performance on longer tasks.
- SPIRAL consistently improved generative models (e.g., Wan, Sora, Kling) across ActWM-Bench metrics, showing enhanced action completeness and temporal coherence, especially for "Hard" (long-horizon) tasks where baseline models significantly degraded.
- GRPO training demonstrated steady increases in reward convergence for action adherence and temporal coherence, internalizing policy improvements that resulted in more robust, temporally consistent videos with smoother transitions and fewer physical inconsistencies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A closed-loop "think-act-reflect" framework with explicit planning and critical evaluation significantly improves the controllability, semantic grounding, and temporal consistency of long-horizon video generation.
> - Hierarchical planning, coupled with a memory module and dual-level feedback, is crucial for maintaining coherence and preventing error accumulation across extended procedural tasks.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with stochastic exploration, allows the generative World Model to internalize reflective capabilities, reducing reliance on external inference-time guidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the domain of video generation, specifically focusing on the advanced task of long-horizon action-conditioned video generation. Recent progress in video generation models, particularly Text-to-Image-to-Video (TI2V) systems, has enabled the synthesis of realistic and temporally plausible short video clips. However, these models typically operate in an open-loop, single-shot manner, which presents significant challenges when extended to long-horizon tasks. Such tasks require sustained action execution, causal motion sequences, and consistent object-tool interaction over extended durations. Existing open-loop models often result in incomplete action execution, hallucinated motions, and temporal incoherence, such as object drift or scene inconsistency.

The research aligns with an emerging trend in generative AI towards "agentic, closed-loop systems." This paradigm incorporates iterative planning, reflective feedback, and chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning to enhance controllability, grounded verification, and self-correction in generation processes. Prior work in agentic visual generation (e.g., GEMS, Gen-Searcher, JarvisArt) and agentic video generation (e.g., EditThinker, VideoAgent, CoAgent, A2RD) has explored structured workflows and "thinking while generating" approaches. SPIRAL distinguishes itself by explicitly integrating reflective planning agents with an explicit memory module to ensure sustained action grounding and consistency over extended temporal horizons.

Furthermore, this work contributes to the area of self-evolving generative models. Previous efforts in this space (e.g., VideoAgent, VISTA) leverage internal signals or external feedback for progressive improvement. SPIRAL introduces a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based self-evolution strategy, which utilizes critic-derived signals to continuously enhance the video generator's performance. The paper also connects to video world models, which aim to learn environmental dynamics and predict future observations. While many world models focus on global scenes or short-term changes, SPIRAL specifically focuses on persistent, object-grounded control, where language acts as high-level semantic actions guiding long-horizon behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance long-horizon action-conditioned video generation, enabling the synthesis of temporally coherent videos that precisely follow complex action instructions over extended periods. This task inherently demands procedural ordering, persistent action execution, and scene consistency, aspects that conventional short-clip TI2V models generally do not address adequately.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations identified in existing single-shot, open-loop video generation models:
- **Incomplete Action Execution**: When confronted with multi-stage actions, current models often fail to complete the entire sequence, truncating actions or only partially executing them. This is problematic for generating videos of complex procedures.
- **Hallucinated Motions**: Without explicit planning or corrective feedback, generative models can produce motions that deviate from the intended instructions, leading to visually implausible or incorrect actions.
- **Long-Horizon Temporal Incoherence**: The absence of memory or state tracking mechanisms in open-loop systems frequently results in objects drifting out of place, characters changing appearance, or scenes losing consistency over extended video durations.
- **Lack of Task Decomposition and Verification**: These failures are fundamentally attributed to the single-shot nature of generation, which lacks mechanisms for decomposing a complex task into manageable sub-actions, performing intermediate verification of generated segments, or applying corrective feedback when errors occur. Without such mechanisms, errors can compound and lead to a complete breakdown of the intended action sequence.

To address these challenges, the research sets forth several specific objectives:
- To propose a closed-loop, agentic framework that can augment existing video generators with explicit planning, reflective feedback, and context memory.
- To enable this framework to support self-evolution of the video generator, translating inference-time corrections into permanent model improvements through a reinforcement learning mechanism.
- To establish a dedicated dataset (ActVideoGen-Dataset) and evaluation benchmark (ActVideoGen-Bench) tailored specifically for training and rigorously assessing long-horizon action-conditioned video generation, focusing on action quality and temporal coherence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed solution, SPIRAL (Sequential Planning and Iterative Reflection for Action-conditioned Long-horizon video generation), is a closed-loop agentic framework designed to address the limitations of open-loop video generation. It conceptualizes long-horizon video generation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and operates through a "think-act-reflect" cycle.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework Architecture

SPIRAL consists of three primary agents—PlanAgent, VideoGenerator, and CriticAgent—along with a ContextMemory module, which work in a hierarchical, iterative fashion:
- **PlanAgent (π_plan)**: This agent serves as the high-level policy. It receives a global goal `g` and current context `M` and employs Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to decompose the goal into structured atomic plans `S = {s_1..., s_T}`. Each plan `s_t` is a tuple `(a_t, c_pre_t, c_post_t)`, specifying an action instruction (`a_t`) and its physical pre- and post-conditions (`c_pre_t`, `c_post_t`). This design compels explicit reasoning about causal dependencies. The PlanAgent is instantiated using Qwen3-VL-8B and undergoes two-stage training: Instruction Tuning (IT) on ActVideoGen-Dataset for structured CoT planning, followed by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to align generated plans with physical reality and temporal logic, mitigating logical hallucinations.
- **VideoGenerator (π_gen)**: This component acts as the execution policy, translating each atomic plan `s_t` into a video segment `v_t`. To maintain long-term consistency, `v_t` is conditioned on both the action instruction `a_t` and the historical context stored in `M_t-1`. The framework supports plug-and-play integration of various T2V and I2V backbones (e.g., SVI based on Wan2.1-I2V-14B). It is adapted through a "Streaming Long-Tuning" strategy via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the ActVideoGen-Dataset, including multi-resolution training to improve robustness across spatial formats.
- **CriticAgent (C)**: This agent is responsible for closed-loop verification and feedback. For each generated video segment `v_t`, it assesses its alignment with the plan `s_t` across five dimensions: action adherence, object interaction, goal achievement, temporal coherence, and physical realism. It produces a scalar reward `r_t` and textual feedback `f_t`. The CriticAgent, also instantiated with Qwen3-VL-8B, is trained in two stages: SFT to distill judgments from large VLMs (e.g., Gemini-3-Pro), and Pairwise Reward Modeling (RM) using a Bradley-Terry objective on the GAIA dataset to enhance discriminative accuracy, especially for action-level sensitivity.
- **ContextMemory (M_t)**: This module accumulates successful action-video transitions `(s_t, v_t)` to preserve visual and semantic context, thereby mitigating long-horizon drift.

#### 3.2. Closed-Loop Feedback Mechanism

SPIRAL employs a dual-level feedback mechanism:
- **Inner Loop (Local Refinement)**: If `r_t` falls below a predefined threshold `τ`, indicating minor artifacts or incomplete action execution, `f_t` is used to refine the instruction `a_t` (e.g., emphasizing unmet post-conditions), triggering immediate regeneration of the current step.
- **Outer Loop (Global Replanning)**: If generation fails consecutively after `K` attempts, implying an infeasible plan, the failure propagates to the PlanAgent, which then triggers replanning of the trajectory `S` from step `t` onward, leveraging the ContextMemory.

#### 3.3. Self-Evolving via GRPO Optimization

Beyond inference-time correction, SPIRAL features a self-evolving strategy based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This mechanism enables closed-loop reinforcement learning to continuously optimize the VideoGenerator's intrinsic weights.
- **Process**: The VideoGenerator policy `π_θ` is iteratively refined. For each action step `s_t`, the current policy samples `G` candidate video segments. The CriticAgent evaluates these segments, assigning rewards. Advantages `A_i` are computed using group-wise normalization of these rewards. The VideoGenerator's parameters are updated by maximizing a GRPO objective, which includes importance sampling and KL divergence regularization to prevent excessive deviation from the SFT-initialized policy.
- **Curriculum Learning**: This framework naturally facilitates curriculum learning, where the VideoGenerator progressively evolves from executing short atomic actions to mastering complex, long-horizon procedural tasks by gradually scaling action complexity and temporal horizons.
- **Stochastic Exploration**: Video generation is cast as a Markov Decision Process. Stochastic exploration required for GRPO is achieved through a reverse-time Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) formulation, incorporating a diffusion term to generate diverse trajectories while ensuring shared noise initialization to stabilize gradient estimates.

#### 3.4. ActVideoGen-Dataset and Benchmark Construction

- **ActVideoGen-Dataset**: A task-specific dataset was constructed, comprising 24,616 tasks and 118,156 step-level annotations. Data was aggregated from Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, COIN, and EPIC-KITCHENS. A hierarchical annotation pipeline, combining VLM (GLM-4.5V) for step-wise action annotation and LLM (GPT-5.1) for CoT planning generation, converted raw videos into structured `⟨g, CoT, S, V⟩` records. Quality was ensured via VLM-based filtering (Qwen3-VL-235B) and human verification (93% agreement).
- **ActVideoGen-Bench**: A comprehensive evaluation suite was developed with 300 prompts stratified into simple, medium, and hard difficulty levels. Evaluation metrics include Static Quality and Dynamic Quality (from VBench), but primarily focuses on **Action Quality** (Action Completeness, Action Smoothness, Object Interaction, Physical Fidelity) assessed by a multi-agent evaluator utilizing GPT-5 for reasoning and cross-verified chain-of-query.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SPIRAL was conducted across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating consistent gains in long-horizon action quality and temporal consistency.

#### 4.1. Evaluations on PlanAgent

- **Superiority in Long-Horizon Planning**: On EgoPlan-Bench, SPIRAL’s PlanAgent (Qwen3-VL-8B + Memory + IT + DPO) achieved an overall success rate of 58.72%, surpassing GPT-5.1 (54.78%) and GRPO-CARE (57.00%). The integration of context memory significantly boosted accuracy from 35.81% to 43.63%, highlighting its importance for long-horizon consistency.
- **Impact of Hierarchical Training**: Instruction Tuning (IT) provided a 9.66% gain over a memory-augmented baseline, confirming the necessity of structured supervision. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) further added a 5.43% improvement, serving as a physical regularizer against logical inconsistencies.
- **Robustness to Extended Horizons**: The memory module maintained planning accuracy stability across varying task lengths, sustaining 43.02% accuracy at extreme horizons, whereas performance without memory sharply degraded.

#### 4.2. Evaluations on CriticAgent

- **Superiority in Reward Assessment**: On VideoGen-RewardBench, the CriticAgent (Qwen3-VL-8B + SFT + RM) achieved an overall accuracy of 68.86%, outperforming previous methods like VisionReward (67.59%). This was attributed to the reward modeling optimization, which enhanced sensitivity to text-action alignment.
- **Impact of Training Stages**: While SFT provided a robust initialization, the Pairwise Reward Modeling (RM) phase was critical for discrimination, delivering a 5.7% boost in overall performance and sharpening the distinction between successful and failed executions.

#### 4.3. Evaluations on Closed-Loop Video Generation

- **Superiority in Action Execution**: Integrating SPIRAL with diverse T2V and I2V backbones consistently enhanced Action Quality. For instance, action completeness showed gains of +44.8% for LongLive and +31.0% for Wan2.1. Other action quality metrics (Action Smoothness, Object Interaction, Physical Fidelity) also improved across most backbones.
- **Analysis of Generic Metrics**: Minor fluctuations were observed in generic visual and dynamic metrics, which are typically biased towards short-term fidelity. SPIRAL’s primary gains were in action completeness and procedural correctness, reflecting its focus on long-horizon tasks.
- **Qualitative Results**: Demonstrated coherent multi-step execution for both egocentric interaction and exocentric human kinematics, ensuring temporal ordering and consistency.
- **Difficulty Stratification**: SPIRAL maintained stability across simple, medium, and hard task difficulties, effectively mitigating performance collapse observed in baselines on complex, long-duration tasks (>40s) with extended action dependencies.

#### 4.4. Evaluations on GRPO-based Self-Evolving

- **Curriculum-Driven Training Dynamics**: GRPO reward curves for action adherence and temporal coherence showed steady convergence, validating the efficacy of curriculum learning in progressively scaling plan complexity and temporal horizons.
- **Agent-Driven Optimization**: GRPO optimization resulted in further improvements: action completeness increased by +12% and smoothness by +4% compared to the closed-loop baseline, demonstrating that self-evolving translates inference-time verification into permanent model enhancements. The overall quality saw an additional 4.4% gain beyond the closed-loop framework.
- **Qualitative Analysis**: GRPO-based self-evolving yielded more complete, temporally coherent, and physically plausible multi-step actions compared to open-loop baselines, which often suffered from incomplete motions and physical distortions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **PlanAgent and CriticAgent**: The PlanAgent significantly boosted Action Completeness (+28.1%), confirming the importance of explicit task decomposition. The CriticAgent’s dual-level feedback further enhanced overall quality through local refinement and global replanning. The full SPIRAL framework achieved optimal performance, indicating the complementary nature of planning and closed-loop verification.
- **ContextMemory**: The global ContextMemory module maintained visual consistency across long horizons, preserving object identity and scene layout, which directly enhanced overall Action Quality (e.g., 3.3% gains against local memory).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SPIRAL presents a substantial advancement in the field of long-horizon action-conditioned video generation by introducing a closed-loop "think-act-reflect" framework. This approach systematically addresses critical limitations of current open-loop models, such as incomplete action execution, hallucinated motions, and temporal incoherence, which are prominent challenges in synthesizing complex, multi-step procedures.

The primary significance lies in its novel framework design, which synergistically integrates a PlanAgent for robust task decomposition, a ContextMemory for visual persistence across time, and a CriticAgent for dual-level verification and corrective feedback. This architecture ensures that generated videos adhere to intricate action instructions and maintain temporal and scene consistency over extended durations.

Furthermore, the introduction of a GRPO-based self-evolving strategy is a notable contribution. By leveraging curriculum learning and agent-driven feedback (specifically, critic-derived rewards), SPIRAL translates inference-time corrections into permanent, intrinsic improvements of the video generator. This mechanism fosters the development of more robust and capable generative models that can internalize planning and verification signals, extending their utility beyond mere inference-time problem-solving.

The development of the ActVideoGen-Dataset and ActVideoGen-Bench provides essential resources for the research community. These task-specific datasets and dedicated evaluation protocols are designed to train and rigorously assess long-horizon action-conditioned video generation models, facilitating future research and standardized benchmarking in this complex domain.

The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across diverse T2V and I2V backbones highlight the generalizability and practical applicability of SPIRAL. This research has the potential to impact various applications, including more controllable and accurate content creation, advanced educational simulations, and sophisticated procedural task automation. By encouraging a shift in evaluation paradigms towards action quality, object interaction, and physical consistency, SPIRAL contributes to bridging the gap towards more advanced, reliable action-conditioned video world models. The identified limitations, such as inference cost and action representation granularity, also delineate clear avenues for future research, aiming to further enhance the robustness and efficiency of long-horizon video generation systems.
