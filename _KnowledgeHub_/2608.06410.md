# ADIAS: Automated Design of Interactive Agentic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> ADIAS reframes automated agent design as issue-centric optimization: instead of organizing cross-round experience around candidate agents, it carries repair progress forward as an explicit persistent issue state. An Issue Manager tracks stable issue identities, lifecycle status, evidence and intervention outcomes; issue-guided planning then jointly picks a parent agent and revision plan for a focused full-code patch. Across five interactive benchmarks it beats the strongest baseline by 25.2% on average.

## Problem

- Candidate-centric optimizers scatter repair evidence across candidate records, so each round must reconstruct what is unresolved, yielding broad, redundant or misdirected revisions.
- Whole-candidate evaluation entangles multiple issues, making a single intervention's outcome hard to isolate while complementary partial progress stays stranded across separate branches.
- Recording interventions only at candidate level leaves beneficial and harmful edits mixed, so ineffective or regressive changes propagate into later rounds unnoticed.

## Method

- ==Persistent issue state==: an ==Issue Manager== associates diagnosed failures with stable identities and tracks priority, evidence, per-issue intervention-outcome history, and lifecycle over active, tentatively-fixed, confirmed-fixed and regressed.
- Lifecycle gating plus initialization: a one-shot ==external prior search== and a ==diagnostic agent== seed the state, and an issue is confirmed fixed only after two consecutive absent evaluations.
- ==Issue-guided optimization==: planning prioritizes severe, repeated, active or regressed issues, jointly selects parent agent and revision direction, then emits a focused few-file patch over the unrestricted full-code space.

## Results

- Averaged over Tau-Bench, ALFWorld, TextCraft, WebShop and ScienceWorld, ADIAS scores **78.4** versus **62.6** for the strongest baseline DGM-H, a **25.2%** relative gain, best on all five.
- Cross-backbone Tau-Bench scores reach **81.3**, **90.6**, **84.4** and **87.5** on DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.2, Hy3-Preview and GPT-5.4, averaging **85.95** against **67.98** for the best baseline.
- Ablations drop average score from **88.8** to **52.7** with raw archive-wide synthesis (**-40.7%**), **54.5** with latest-candidate continuation (**-38.6%**), and **72.5** without round-level diagnosis (**-18.4%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Persisting structured history is not enough: the state must actively control parent selection and revision direction, since context-only variants still lost 17 to 39 percent.
> - The two common parent heuristics fail oppositely: best-so-far strands partial repairs across branches, latest-candidate inherits regressions and burns rounds on rollback.
> - Automated design is not a free win: a handcrafted ReAct agent outbeat two automated baselines, and gains here assume accurate, unmeasured failure diagnosis and attribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has led to their increasing deployment in agentic systems, which augment backbone models with capabilities such as planning, memory, and tool use. These systems rely on an "agent harness" to orchestrate interactions with the environment. The design of effective agent harnesses is often task-dependent and resource-intensive when performed manually. Consequently, recent research efforts have shifted towards automated agent design, aiming to generate and improve agent systems through iterative execution feedback.

Existing automated agent design methods typically operate through repetitive cycles of revision, evaluation, and feedback summarization. These approaches are largely characterized as "candidate-centric" optimization. In this paradigm, cross-round experience, including agent code, evaluation scores, and execution traces, is organized primarily around individual candidate agents. The objective of each round is to propose a new, higher-quality candidate agent based on the history of previous candidates.

The authors identify several limitations inherent in this candidate-centric approach, particularly in the context of interactive environments:
1. **Inefficient repair targeting**: Information regarding unresolved issues, necessary modifications, and prior interventions is fragmented across various candidate records. This necessitates the optimizer to reconstruct the repair context in each round, potentially leading to broad, redundant, or misdirected changes.
2. **Fragmented repair-progress consolidation**: Because each candidate agent is evaluated as a whole and may influence multiple issues, isolating the impact of specific interventions becomes challenging. Additionally, incremental progress on a single issue can be distributed across different candidate versions, hindering efficient consolidation.
3. **Regressive intervention propagation**: Recording interventions and their outcomes at the candidate level can entangle beneficial and detrimental changes. This entanglement may lead to the propagation of ineffective interventions into subsequent optimization rounds.

This work positions itself as a response to these limitations by introducing "issue-centric agent optimization." In contrast to organizing experience around candidates, this new paradigm organizes cross-round experience around persistent issues. By maintaining an explicit and persistent issue state, the work aims to guide optimization more directly and efficiently, thereby enabling more focused repairs, better consolidation of partial progress, and the avoidance of previously ineffective interventions. This approach aims to advance automated agent design, especially for complex interactive environments where feedback is sparse and attribution is difficult.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the challenges associated with manually and automatically designing effective agent harnesses for interactive large language model (LLM) agents. Manual design is labor-intensive and highly task-specific, making it difficult to scale. While automated agent design offers a promising alternative, current "candidate-centric" methods exhibit several inefficiencies that limit their effectiveness, particularly in interactive environments characterized by large design spaces, sparse and delayed feedback, and costly evaluations.

The authors explicitly detail the motivations for proposing a new paradigm:
- **Addressing the implicit nature of repair progress in candidate-centric methods**: Current systems organize historical data around agent candidates, which means the progress made in resolving specific issues is not explicitly tracked. This forces the optimizer to "reconstruct" what to repair in every round, often leading to suboptimal or redundant revision efforts.
- **Improving the efficiency of repair targeting**: With scattered evidence regarding unresolved issues, past interventions, and necessary modifications, existing methods struggle to pinpoint the exact aspects requiring repair. This often results in broad, unspecific, or misdirected changes.
- **Facilitating the consolidation of partial repair progress**: When an intervention partially addresses an issue, its effect might be diluted or lost if the overall candidate agent's performance does not immediately improve significantly. This makes it difficult to consolidate incremental improvements across different candidate versions.
- **Preventing the propagation of ineffective interventions**: The entanglement of beneficial and harmful changes at the candidate level means that sub-optimal modifications can persist across rounds, consuming valuable optimization budget on re-exploring or undoing previous errors.

To overcome these limitations, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **Formulate issue-centric agent optimization**: To establish a new conceptual framework where the repair progress is explicitly carried forward as a persistent issue state. This state would directly guide subsequent optimization steps, rather than being re-derived implicitly from the history of candidate agents.
2. **Instantiate the formulation in a practical framework (ADIAS)**: To develop a concrete system called ADIAS (Automated Design of Interactive Agentic Systems) that realizes the principles of issue-centric optimization for automated full-code agent design.
3. **Develop mechanisms for reliable issue-state maintenance**: This objective involves creating components that can transform heterogeneous execution trajectories and sparse outcomes into stable, evidence-grounded issue identities. It also includes reconciling new observations with historical records and accurately tracking the repair progress and lifecycle status of each identified issue across optimization rounds. This addresses the challenge of attributing outcomes when underlying issues may manifest differently or when a single revision has multiple effects.
4. **Enable actionable and attributable revision through issue-guided optimization**: To design a system where the dynamic, multi-issue, and primarily descriptive issue state can be translated into concrete, focused code modifications. This requires jointly determining the target issue(s) and the revision direction, ensuring that the resulting modification is sufficiently focused to allow for clear attribution of its behavioral outcome to the intended repair.
5. **Demonstrate superior performance and robustness**: To empirically validate that ADIAS outperforms existing state-of-the-art candidate-centric methods across various interactive benchmarks and exhibits robustness across different backbone models.
6. **Illustrate the importance of issue-centric components**: Through controlled ablation studies, to show that the persistent issue state and its role in actively guiding optimization are critical for the observed performance gains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ADIAS (Automated Design of Interactive Agentic Systems) is designed to implement issue-centric agent optimization, moving away from candidate-centric methods. It achieves this through two core mechanisms: a persistent issue state for organizing cross-round experience and issue-guided optimization for directing agent revisions. The overall workflow consists of an initialization phase and an iterative optimization loop.

**Task Definition:**
The work focuses on the automated design of an interactive agent for a given task environment, evaluator, and backbone model. Starting with an initial agent A_0, the system iteratively generates and evaluates new candidates (A_t) over T rounds. Each evaluation produces interaction trajectories (T(A, x)) and performance metrics (M_q(A)). A diagnostic process analyzes these outcomes to generate a diagnostic report (D_t). The ultimate objective is to achieve high test performance (M_test) by selecting the best candidate from the generated archive (A_0..., A_T).

**Issue-Centric Optimization Formulation:**
Unlike candidate-centric methods (A_t+1 = arg max J_cand(A) from H_t), issue-centric optimization augments the candidate-organized history (H_t) with a persistent issue state (E_t). The next agent A_t+1 is proposed based on both H_t and E_t, aiming to maximize expected repair progress on unresolved issues (A_t+1 = arg max J_issue(A; E_t)). After evaluation, A_t+1 is added to H_t, and the behavioral evidence is used to update E_t (E_t+1 = UpdateIssueState(E_t, H_t+1)). This ensures repair progress is explicitly maintained and guides optimization.

**ADIAS Framework (Two Stages):**

**Phase 1: Initialization**
This stage establishes the foundational issue state for the subsequent optimization process.
1. **External Prior Search**: Before any agent modification, a one-shot search is performed to gather publicly available task-level information (e.g., benchmark descriptions, common failure modes, useful agent mechanisms). This generates a set of provisional priors (P) treated as hypotheses rather than verified issues.
2. **Initial Agent Evaluation and Diagnosis**: The initial agent (A_0) is evaluated, producing trajectories (T_0) and performance metrics (M_0). A dedicated **Diagnostic Agent** analyzes T_0 to identify evidence-grounded failures, generating an initial diagnostic report (D_0) which includes observed issue categories, supporting evidence, and inferred causes. The diagnostic agent interacts with a structured trajectory database using query tools to inspect failure patterns.
3. **Initialize Issue State (E_0)**: The **Issue Manager** reconciles the task priors (P) with the diagnostic report (D_0) to construct the initial persistent issue state (E_0). External priors are refined or discarded based on observed agent behavior.

**Phase 2: Iterative Optimization Loop**
This is a five-step loop that continuously refines the agent based on the evolving issue state. Each issue state E_t is represented as a set of issue records {e_t_i}, where each record e_t_i includes a stable identity (`id_i`), priority (`q_t_i`), lifecycle status (`s_t_i`), supporting evidence (`B_t_i`), and intervention-outcome history (`U_t_i`).

1. **Propose Repair (Issue-Guided Planning)**:
 - The Issue Manager uses the current issue state (E_t-1) to select a small set of **target issues**. Prioritization considers severity, recurrence, active status, and recent regression.
 - It then jointly determines a **parent agent** (A_p_t) from the archive and a **revision plan** (R_t) for the selected issues. This planning considers the target issues' evidence, lifecycle, and previous intervention outcomes.
2. **Modify (Focused Full-Code Revision)**:
 - Conditioned on A_p_t and R_t, an optimizer generates a **code patch** (δ_t).
 - This patch is applied to A_p_t to produce the new candidate agent (A_t).
 - The optimizer is encouraged to implement a *focused revision* (e.g., modifying a small number of files) while maintaining a *full-code editable space* (e.g., prompts, observation processing, memory, planning, tool-use policies, control flow, verification, recovery). This balance aims to improve outcome attribution without restricting design freedom.
3. **Evaluate**: The new agent (A_t) is evaluated in the task environment, yielding interaction trajectories (T_t) and performance metrics (M_t).
4. **Diagnose**: The Diagnostic Agent analyzes T_t (and task priors P) to produce a diagnostic report (D_t), similar to the initialization phase.
5. **Update Issue State**: The Issue Manager updates the persistent issue state from E_t-1 to E_t based on D_t and M_t. This involves:
 - **Issue Association**: New failures from D_t are either associated with existing issues (based on category, capability, context, evidence) or assigned new stable identities.
 - **Lifecycle Transition**: Each issue's status (`s_t_i`) is updated (e.g., ACTIVE, TENTATIVELY-FIXED, CONFIRMED-FIXED, REGRESSED) based on whether it is observed and its history. A fixed issue becomes CONFIRMED-FIXED after remaining absent for at least α_min (set to 2) consecutive evaluations.
 - **Intervention-Outcome History Update**: The revision applied in round `t` and its observed outcome are recorded in the history (`U_t_i`) of the targeted issue. This helps in attributing specific outcomes to interventions and distilling reusable repair lessons.

After T rounds, the candidate with the highest validation performance from the agent archive (A) is selected for final evaluation on a held-out test set.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ADIAS involved a comprehensive set of experiments across diverse interactive benchmarks, comparisons with multiple baselines, cross-model robustness checks, and detailed ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Main Results

- **Strong Task Performance and Efficiency**: ADIAS demonstrated superior performance across all five interactive agent benchmarks, including Tau-Bench (customer service), ALFWorld (embodied AI), TextCraft (crafting games), WebShop (web navigation), and ScienceWorld (scientific experimentation). On average, ADIAS outperformed the strongest baseline, DGM-Hyperagents (DGM-H), by 25.2% (78.4 score for ADIAS vs. 62.6 for DGM-H). Specifically, ADIAS achieved scores of 81.3 on Tau-Bench, 94.0 on ALFWorld, 91.0 on TextCraft, 69.4 on WebShop, and 56.3 on ScienceWorld, consistently surpassing all baselines. These gains were also reflected in interaction efficiency, with ADIAS achieving the highest score-per-interaction ratio on every benchmark, indicating more effective conversion of environment interactions into task progress.
- **High Optimization Efficiency and Stability**: Analysis of the optimization process on ALFWorld (Figure 3) showed that ADIAS identifies high-performing agent designs within the initial few iterations and maintains strong, consistent validation performance throughout the optimization budget. The cumulative-average score also increased steadily, suggesting that improvements were not based on isolated high-scoring candidates but rather a consistent generation of effective designs.
- **Cross-Model Robustness**: Experiments on Tau-Bench using four different backbone models (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.2, Hy3-Preview, and GPT-5.4) demonstrated that ADIAS consistently achieved the highest task performance and interaction efficiency across all models. This indicates that the issue-centric approach provides robust automated design capabilities independent of specific LLM backbones.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A controlled ablation study investigated the contribution of different components of ADIAS, specifically separating the roles of evidence sources, the representation of issue state, and its active control over optimization.
- **Importance of Evidence for Issue-State Construction**: Removing either the "External Prior" (task-level knowledge) or "Round-Level Diagnosis" (trajectory analysis) led to performance and efficiency drops. For instance, without external priors, TextCraft performance decreased from 91.0 to 61.0. Without round-level diagnosis, ALFWorld dropped from 94.0 to 63.4. These results suggest that both sources are crucial for reducing cold-start uncertainty and grounding the issue state with behavioral evidence.
- **Superiority of Persistent Issue State over Raw Archive**: Replacing the durable issue state with "Archive-Wide Synthesis" (a candidate-centric approach similar to Meta-Harness, where the designer synthesizes the next step from a raw archive of candidates and traces) resulted in a significant average performance drop of 40.7% (from 88.8 to 52.7). This indicates that simply retaining rich historical evidence in a raw format is insufficient for effective revision in sparse-feedback, interactive environments.
- **Issue State Must Control Optimization**: Decoupling the issue state from active optimization control, by using it only as contextual information for parent selection policies like "Best-Candidate Revision" or "Latest-Candidate Continuation," also led to substantial performance degradations. Best-Candidate Revision, while the strongest ablation, still lagged ADIAS by 9.4 to 18.6 points across benchmarks. Latest-Candidate Continuation showed even larger drops. This highlights that the persistent issue state must serve as an operational control signal to ensure optimization is conditioned on active issues and their intervention outcomes, rather than relying on aggregate candidate scores or chronological order alone.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative analysis of optimization traces on the TextCraft benchmark (Figure 4) provided insights into the operational advantages of ADIAS:
- **Candidate-Centric Limitations**: Raw candidate archives (Archive-Wide Synthesis) often led to repeated restarts from high-scoring candidates and imprecise revisions because underlying errors (e.g., "quantity missing error") were not effectively localized across rounds.
- **Slow Consolidation in Best-Candidate Revision**: This policy hindered the consolidation of partial repairs. Useful code changes that lowered the aggregate score were not consistently inherited, requiring designers to repeatedly recover and reimplement insights across different branches.
- **Propagation of Ineffective Revisions in Latest-Candidate Continuation**: This policy led to the accumulation of errors or wasted rounds for rollbacks when ineffective modifications were inherited by subsequent candidates, rather than allowing a return to a more suitable parent.
- **ADIAS's Continuous Issue-Specific Repair**: ADIAS facilitated continuous, issue-specific repair by consistently selecting appropriate starting points and revision directions. It enabled the retention of successful repairs (e.g., command normalization) and allowed the system to focus on remaining issues (e.g., grounding and recovery). This demonstrated a progression from broad planning failures to more specific issue resolution, driven by the active issue, previous interventions, and their observed outcomes.

#### 4.4. Evaluation Robustness and Expanded Distribution Evaluation

Additional experiments confirmed the robustness of ADIAS. Repeated evaluations on Tau-Bench (Table 6) showed that ADIAS achieved identical success rates across two independent rollouts and a substantially higher Pass_2 score (71.9 vs. 56.3 for handcrafted, 37.5 for DGM-H), indicating more consistent and reliable task-solving. Scaling up to the complete Tau-Bench retail distribution (Table 7) further confirmed ADIAS's advantage in both performance and efficiency, demonstrating its ability to generalize to broader and more complex task distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a paradigm shift in automated agent design, moving from candidate-centric to issue-centric optimization. This re-framing positions agent design as a cumulative repair process rather than a sequence of independent candidate generations. The instantiation of this paradigm in ADIAS (Automated Design of Interactive Agentic Systems) provides a concrete framework that leverages a persistent issue state and issue-guided optimization to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of full-code agent development.

**Significance:**
1. **Novel Paradigm for Agent Optimization**: The core contribution is the introduction and validation of issue-centric agent optimization, which explicitly tracks and leverages the state of identified issues across design iterations. This is a departure from conventional methods that implicitly reconstruct repair objectives from candidate history.
2. **Improved Efficiency and Effectiveness**: By directly guiding optimization with a persistent issue state, ADIAS addresses critical limitations of prior work, such as inefficient repair targeting, fragmented consolidation of partial progress, and the propagation of ineffective interventions. This leads to substantial gains in task performance and interaction efficiency across diverse interactive benchmarks.
3. **Enhanced Attributability and Focused Revision**: The framework's ability to jointly select repair targets and revision directions, followed by focused full-code modifications, improves the attribution of behavioral outcomes to specific interventions. This precision is vital for effective iterative design, especially in complex interactive environments with sparse feedback.
4. **Robustness Across Models and Tasks**: The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across various backbone LLMs and diverse interactive tasks (e.g., customer service, embodied AI, web navigation) highlight the general applicability and robustness of the issue-centric approach.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Advancement of LLM Agent Capabilities**: ADIAS can contribute to the development of more capable, robust, and autonomous LLM-based agents by providing a more systematic and efficient method for their design and refinement. This could accelerate progress in domains requiring sophisticated reasoning, planning, and interaction.
2. **Scalable Agent Development**: By automating and optimizing the design process, ADIAS reduces the manual labor and expertise required to create high-performing agent harnesses. This could make advanced agentic systems more accessible and scalable for various applications.
3. **Inspiration for Other System Optimization Problems**: The principle of maintaining explicit "issue state" to guide complex iterative optimization processes could be generalized beyond agent design to other software engineering or system development contexts where debugging, refactoring, and continuous improvement are critical.
4. **Foundational for Self-Improving AI**: By enabling the system to explicitly track and resolve its own shortcomings, ADIAS moves towards more sophisticated forms of self-improving AI, where the learning agent can manage its own evolution more effectively.

**Limitations Noted by the Authors:**
The authors acknowledge two main limitations of the current work:
1. **Reliance on Diagnostic Quality**: ADIAS's effectiveness relies on the accuracy of trajectory diagnosis and issue association. The paper focuses on the issue-centric optimization framework rather than the diagnosis quality itself, and thus, the accuracy of failure labels or issue attributions was not separately reported or varied in comparisons. Future work could explore improvements in diagnostic agents.
2. **Scope of Evaluation**: The evaluation was limited to text-based interactive benchmarks. The application and performance of ADIAS in multimodal environments or tasks requiring substantially longer-horizon planning and interaction remain areas for future investigation.
