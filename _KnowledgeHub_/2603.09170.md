We present ZeroWBC, a teleoperation-free framework that learns humanoid whole-body interaction from human egocentric videos paired with synchronized whole-body motion and text annotations. ZeroWBC adopts a generation-then-tracking formulation to tackle the static scene whole-body interaction control problem. Given an initial egocentric image and a language instruction, a fine-tuned Vision-Language Model generates future human whole-body motion tokens, which are decoded into continuous motions and retargeted to the humanoid. The resulting reference motions, together with root and key body-part trajectories, are then executed by a general interactive motion tracking policy. To improve interaction performance, we introduce an interaction-oriented tracking reward that prioritizes global root and key body-part trajectory alignment while preserving natural whole-body motion. Experiments on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot show that ZeroWBC enables diverse scene-aware behaviors without robot teleoperation demonstrations. These results suggest a scalable paradigm for learning natural humanoid whole-body interaction from human egocentric data.

## Problem

- High cost and limited scalability of robot teleoperation data for training complex whole-body humanoid interactions.
- Existing humanoid control systems are often motion-centric, lacking robust capabilities for adaptive interaction with diverse scene objects and geometries.
- Difficulty in transferring policies from simulation to real robots due to the sim-to-real gap, and limitations of decoupled control in achieving natural whole-body coordination.

## Method

- A hierarchical two-stage framework combining motion generation from multimodal inputs (initial egocentric image and language) with general interactive motion tracking.
- Utilizes a low-cost, scalable human-centric data collection pipeline (egocentric video, motion, text) to train the system, eliminating the need for robot teleoperation.
- An RL-based interaction-oriented tracking policy designed to prioritize global root and key body-part trajectories (e.g., hands, feet) for precise task-critical interaction intent.

## Results

- ZeroWBC consistently outperformed baselines on interactive motion tracking, showing superior performance on interaction-focused metrics (MPIPE and MPIVE) across various datasets.
- Demonstrated high success rates on few-shot tasks with novel conditions on a Unitree G1 robot, including 96% for obstacle avoidance and 84% for sofa sitting, surpassing a general tracker baseline.
- Achieved zero-shot generalization for unseen tasks like chair navigation (90% success) and chair sitting (76% success) across varied chair types and initial conditions in the real world.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human egocentric data provides a scalable and cost-effective supervision source, enabling the learning of diverse whole-body humanoid interactions without costly robot teleoperation.
> - Prioritizing the tracking of global root and key body-part trajectories is crucial for preserving task-critical interaction intent during human-to-humanoid transfer, despite morphological differences.
> - A two-stage fine-tuning approach for Vision-Language Models, combining large-scale general data with domain-specific egocentric data, is effective for achieving both broad semantic understanding and spatially grounded motion generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics aims to enable robots to perform complex, scene-aware interactions in diverse real-world environments. Achieving versatile and natural whole-body control for such interactions remains a significant challenge.

Existing approaches to humanoid control have demonstrated progress in several areas:
- **Motion Tracking and Robustness:** Recent work has improved the fidelity and robustness of humanoids in tracking reference motions for tasks like locomotion, dancing, or athletic skills. Examples include systems capable of high-fidelity tracking of single motions or adaptable general motion tracking.
- **Large-scale Motion Generation:** Advancements in motion generation, particularly with large-scale motion models and generation-to-control pipelines, have expanded the repertoire of possible humanoid behaviors.
- **Visual Humanoid Control:** Integrating perception with control is an area of active research. Some systems leverage visual inputs for navigation or interaction.

However, several limitations persist in these existing paradigms, particularly concerning whole-body *interaction* with the environment:
- **Motion-centric Focus:** Many systems are motion-centric, excelling at predefined movements but lacking robust capabilities for interacting with surrounding objects or scene geometry in an adaptable manner.
- **Data Scarcity and Cost:** Visual humanoid control often relies on costly large-scale robot teleoperation data. Collecting such data for whole-body interaction is expensive, time-consuming, and typically requires multiple operators. Furthermore, teleoperation data often employs decoupled upper and lower-body control, which can limit natural coordination.
- **Task Specificity and Sim-to-Real Gap:** Alternative approaches, such as task-specific reinforcement learning in simulation, suffer from limited generalization to diverse tasks and are vulnerable to discrepancies between simulation and the real world (sim-to-real gap).
- **Decoupled Control:** Many teleoperation-based systems use decoupled control strategies for different body parts, hindering the natural, holistic coordination required for complex whole-body interactions.
- **Limited Egocentric Perception:** While some systems integrate vision, many rely on third-person observations or lack first-person perspective, which is crucial for grounded interaction.

The research presented in "ZeroWBC" aims to address these limitations by proposing a teleoperation-free framework that leverages human egocentric data, which is more scalable and cost-effective to collect. This approach seeks to enable versatile, scene-aware whole-body humanoid interaction by learning directly from human demonstrations, moving beyond purely motion-centric or costly robot-specific data collection methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to achieve versatile and natural whole-body humanoid interaction control in complex real-world environments without relying on expensive robot teleoperation data.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges in current humanoid control research:
1. **High Cost of Robot Teleoperation Data:** Training humanoid robots for complex whole-body interaction tasks typically requires large volumes of teleoperation data. This data is costly to collect, requiring specialized hardware, significant human effort (often multiple operators), and extensive time. For instance, the authors note that 8 hours of teleoperation might yield only about 100 demonstrations, whereas human egocentric data collection is significantly more efficient. This high cost hinders the scalability and diversity of learned behaviors.
2. **Limitations of Existing Control Paradigms:**
 - **Motion-Centric vs. Interaction-Centric:** Many advanced humanoid control systems are designed for locomotion or athletic skills (motion-centric), but lack the inherent capability for natural interaction with varied objects and complex scene geometries (interaction-centric).
 - **Decoupled Control:** Teleoperation systems often resort to decoupled control for different body parts, which can lead to unnatural and uncoordinated whole-body movements.
 - **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Task-specific reinforcement learning in simulation faces challenges when transferring policies to real robots due to discrepancies between simulated and physical environments.
3. **Need for Scalable and Natural Supervision:** There is a demand for alternative supervision sources that are more scalable and can naturally capture complex human whole-body interactions. Human demonstrations, particularly egocentric videos paired with synchronized whole-body motion and text annotations, offer a promising solution. These provide first-person scene observations, dense motion labels, and task-level semantic understanding at a lower cost.

To address these motivations, ZeroWBC defines key objectives:
- **Develop a Teleoperation-Free Learning Paradigm:** Introduce a scalable method for supervising humanoid interaction that avoids the need for costly robot teleoperation, leveraging human egocentric data instead.
- **Propose a Generation-to-Tracking Framework:** Design a two-stage framework that can convert high-level multimodal inputs (initial egocentric image and language instruction) into executable whole-body humanoid behaviors. This framework needs to account for the inference latency of large vision-language models, opting for an open-loop design suitable for static interaction tasks.
- **Design an Interaction-Oriented General Tracking Policy:** Create a low-level control policy capable of accurately executing the generated motions while specifically preserving task-critical interaction intent. This involves tracking not only retargeted joint-space motions but also the global trajectories of key body parts (e.g., root, hands, feet), which is crucial for precise interaction despite morphological differences between humans and humanoids.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ZeroWBC adopts a hierarchical, two-stage framework: (1) motion generation and (2) general interactive motion tracking. This generation-then-tracking formulation is designed for vision-conditioned humanoid whole-body scene-interaction, particularly in mostly static environments, to mitigate the impact of high inference latency from large vision-language models.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

To avoid costly robot teleoperation, a human-centric data collection pipeline is employed. This setup utilizes a chest-mounted GoPro camera synchronized with an inertial or optical motion capture system. This approach is characterized by low cost and high scalability. During data collection, the camera height is aligned with the robot's camera height to reduce the domain gap between human and robot egocentric viewpoints. The self-collected dataset includes approximately 5 hours of egocentric video, whole-body motion, and text annotations for tasks like navigation, box moving, sofa sitting, and obstacle avoidance, with variations in object placements and scene layouts. Public datasets like Nymeria and HumanML3D are also utilized.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Motion Generation

This stage is responsible for converting an initial egocentric image and a language instruction into continuous human whole-body motions.

- **Motion Tokenizer:** A VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder) is trained to compress continuous, high-dimensional SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear Model) human motion sequences into compact discrete motion tokens. The VQ-VAE encoder downsamples motion clips into latent sequences, which are then quantized using a codebook and reconstructed by a decoder. The tokenizer is trained with multiple losses including reconstruction, commitment, velocity, root-rotation, and global-translation losses to ensure high reconstruction quality and physical plausibility.
- **Fine-tuning Vision-Language Model (VLM):** The pre-trained Qwen2.5-VL-3B model serves as the backbone. Its vocabulary is extended with special motion tokens derived from the VQ-VAE codebook. The VLM is then fine-tuned to autoregressively predict a complete future sequence of motion tokens, conditioned on an initial egocentric visual observation and a language instruction. This is formulated as a standard next-token prediction task, enabling open-loop motion planning where the entire motion sequence is generated upfront.
 - **Two-stage Fine-tuning:**
 1. **General Cross-Modal Alignment:** The VLM is initially trained on large-scale public image-text-motion data (from Nymeria and HumanML3D) to establish general cross-modal understanding.
 2. **Domain-Specific Spatial Grounding:** Subsequently, the model is further fine-tuned on the self-collected egocentric dataset, which features higher-quality first-person observations and synchronized motion labels for interaction tasks. This stage enhances the model's ability to generate spatially grounded and physically plausible motions from a robot-aligned egocentric perspective. Only the LM decoder of Qwen-VL is fine-tuned to preserve pre-trained capabilities.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: General Interactive Motion Tracking

The generated human motions are decoded from tokens, retargeted to the Unitree G1 humanoid robot's kinematics, and then executed by a unified, reinforcement learning (RL)-based control policy.

- **Tracking Objectives:** The control policy aims to track a composite target vector `G`, which includes:
 - `g_inter`: Interactive trajectories of key body parts. This consists of the global position and linear velocity of the root, left wrist, right wrist, left foot, and right foot, along with the angular velocity of the root. These are extracted from the original human motion sequence.
 - `m_ref`: Retargeted reference motion. This specifies the joint-level kinematic targets for the robot, comprising reference joint angular vectors and velocities for all 23 joints of the Unitree G1.
- **Reward Function:** The RL reward function is formulated to prioritize the tracking of `g_inter` (interaction trajectories) while applying a weighted penalty for deviations from `m_ref` (joint-space reference motion). This design ensures that the policy emphasizes maintaining task-critical spatial intent (e.g., hand positions for object manipulation, root position for sitting) while still promoting natural whole-body motion. Regularization terms are included to penalize unphysical behaviors, high action rates, joint limit violations, and undesired body contacts (outside feet and hands).
- **Adaptive Motion Scheduling:** During RL training, each motion clip is assigned a dynamic sampling weight based on its current tracking difficulty, estimated from EMA tracking error and success/failure statistics. This prioritizes difficult clips while maintaining coverage.
- **Progressive Difficulty Exposure:** Motion files are categorized into difficulty levels (1-10) based on semantic and kinematic complexity. The training curriculum gradually introduces more complex motions as the policy's performance on easier levels meets predefined thresholds. This strategy enhances stability and robustness for learning diverse motion styles.
- **Temporal Conditioning with Future Motion Encoding:** To enable anticipatory control, the policy's observation space is augmented with a "future motion encoder." The actor input includes proprioceptive states and a multi-scale motion command (current, short-horizon, and long-horizon target poses). A lightweight temporal convolutional encoder processes long-horizon targets to capture global motion trends, improving stability for dynamic actions by allowing the policy to anticipate upcoming movements and contact events.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ZeroWBC framework was evaluated through multimodal motion generation quality, general interactive motion tracking performance, and real-world humanoid interaction tasks on a Unitree G1 robot.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Multimodal Motion Generation

The motion generation module, based on a VQ-VAE tokenizer and a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B, was evaluated using FID (Fréchet Inception Distance), R-Precision, and MM-Dist metrics.
- **Fine-tuning Strategy Effectiveness:** Joint training with Nymeria (egocentric image-text-motion), HumanML3D (text-motion), and the self-collected egocentric dataset resulted in the best performance on both Nymeria and self-collected test sets (e.g., FID of 0.298 and 0.245, respectively). This indicates that large-scale public data provides general motion priors, while fine-tuning on domain-specific egocentric data significantly improves spatial grounding for interaction tasks.
- **Value of Egocentric Visual Context:** ZeroWBC, with its multimodal (image-text) input, demonstrated superior performance (FID of 0.756, R@3 of 0.755) compared to the text-to-motion baseline MotionGPT (FID of 0.869, R@3 of 0.536) under the same Nymeria training. This suggests that egocentric visual context enhances motion generation beyond language-only conditioning.
- **Input Modality Ablations:** Further ablations confirmed that using both image and text inputs achieved the best performance. Image-only generation performed poorly due to insufficient task specification, while text-only generation, though better for semantic quality, lacked direct scene grounding.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on General Interactive Motion Tracking

The general interactive motion tracking policy was evaluated on HumanML3D, self-collected, and generated motion data using joint-level metrics (MPJPE, MPJAE, MPJVE) and interaction-focused metrics (MPIPE, MPIVE).
- **Performance vs. Baselines:** ZeroWBC consistently outperformed GMT across all evaluated metrics and datasets. Compared to the stronger SONIC baseline, ZeroWBC achieved competitive performance on joint-level metrics, and notably, it demonstrated superior performance on interaction-related metrics (MPIPE and MPIVE) across all datasets. For example, on HumanML3D, ZeroWBC achieved MPIPE of 0.1885 and MPIVE of 0.1753, compared to SONIC's 0.2108 and 0.1992. This outcome is attributed to ZeroWBC's reward function, which prioritizes global root and key body-part trajectory alignment over solely joint-space tracking accuracy.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablations confirmed the contribution of individual components of the tracking policy. Removing progressive difficulty exposure (w/o Curr.), adaptive motion scheduling (w/o A.S.), or future motion conditioning (w/o F.C.) consistently led to degraded tracking performance. The 'w/o Interact' ablation showed that removing interaction-oriented targets could slightly improve some joint-level metrics but significantly degraded key body-part tracking accuracy, reinforcing the importance of the interaction-oriented objective.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

ZeroWBC was deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to perform diverse real-world interaction tasks.
- **Few-shot Generalization:** For tasks seen during training but with novel object placements, scene layouts, and robot initial poses, the interactive tracker achieved high success rates. It significantly outperformed a general tracker (based on SONIC) across tasks like obstacle avoidance (96% vs. 80%), ball kicking (78% vs. 60%), sofa sitting (84% vs. 48%), and box moving (64% vs. 16%). This highlights the effectiveness of preserving human motion root and key body-part trajectories for task success.
- **Zero-shot Generalization:** The framework demonstrated capabilities in zero-shot tasks (chair navigation and chair sitting) that were entirely absent from the training data. ZeroWBC achieved success rates of 90.0% for chair navigation and 76.0% for chair sitting across varied chair types and initial conditions. This suggests that the Qwen-VL model provided effective semantic priors for unseen objects and tasks, enabling the tracking policy to execute feasible whole-body actions.
- **Failure Analysis:** Most failures were observed to arise from contact limitations or small spatial misalignments, rather than fundamental motion generation issues. For example, boxes might slip due to limited hand friction, or the robot might slightly miss the target in ball kicking or sofa sitting, despite executing otherwise natural motions. This suggests future work could focus on contact-aware tracking and tactile feedback.
- **Real-time Control Feasibility:** The open-loop generation-then-tracking strategy was adopted due to the observed latency of about 400 ms for VLM inference, decoding, and retargeting, which is too high for high-frequency closed-loop control in dynamic environments. For static scenes, this open-loop approach proved effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ZeroWBC framework presents a scalable approach for advancing whole-body humanoid interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Acquisition:** By leveraging human egocentric videos with synchronized whole-body motion and text annotations, ZeroWBC introduces a teleoperation-free learning paradigm. This method offers a more scalable and significantly less costly alternative to traditional robot teleoperation, which typically requires substantial resources and yields limited data. This paradigm could accelerate the development of more versatile humanoid robots by making data collection more accessible.
- **Enabling Natural and Diverse Whole-Body Interaction:** The framework demonstrates the ability to generate and execute diverse, scene-aware whole-body behaviors for humanoid robots from high-level multimodal inputs (initial image and language instruction). This moves beyond motion-centric control towards interaction-centric capabilities, which are crucial for humanoids operating in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Addressing Key Challenges in Humanoid Control:**
 - **Human-to-Humanoid Transfer:** The interaction-oriented tracking policy effectively addresses the challenge of preserving task-critical interaction intent during transfer from human demonstrations to a robot with different morphology. By prioritizing the global trajectories of key body parts, the robot can achieve precise interactions even when perfect joint-level imitation is difficult.
 - **Generalization Capabilities:** The two-stage fine-tuning of the Vision-Language Model, combining large-scale general data with domain-specific egocentric data, facilitates both broad semantic understanding and spatially grounded motion generation. This is evidenced by the framework's ability to perform few-shot tasks under novel conditions and even zero-shot tasks involving unseen objects.
 - **Practical Deployment:** The generation-then-tracking open-loop design offers a practical approach for current humanoid systems, bypassing the latency challenges of real-time closed-loop control with large vision-language models for applications in static scenes.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** ZeroWBC lays a foundation for future advancements in humanoid control, particularly in areas like dynamic environment interaction and complex contact-rich tasks. The identified limitations (e.g., open-loop nature, lack of force feedback) highlight clear avenues for future research, such as developing faster generation models, model distillation, and integrating advanced sensing modalities for closed-loop, adaptive control.

In summary, ZeroWBC offers a practical and scalable path toward learning natural humanoid whole-body interaction, potentially broadening the range of applications for humanoid robots by enabling them to perform more intuitive and diverse interactions with their environments.
