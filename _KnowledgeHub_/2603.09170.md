# ZeroWBC: Learning Natural Whole-Body Humanoid Interaction from Human Egocentric Data

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called ZeroWBC enables humanoid robots to perform natural, scene-aware whole-body interactions by learning from human egocentric videos and language instructions, bypassing the need for expensive robot teleoperation data. This approach demonstrates strong generalization to unseen tasks and objects, achieving up to 90% success on novel chair navigation and 76% on chair sitting, while significantly outperforming general tracking baselines on various interaction tasks.

## Problem

- Acquiring large-scale, high-fidelity whole-body teleoperation data for humanoid robots is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- Current humanoid control systems are limited in performing versatile, scene-aware whole-body interactions, often relying on task-specific training or motion-centric approaches with poor generalization.
- Direct retargeting of human motion to a robot frequently leads to misalignments of task-critical points, compromising interaction precision due to kinematic and morphological differences.

## Method

- Leverage human egocentric videos, synchronized whole-body motion, and text annotations as a scalable and cost-effective data source for learning interaction behaviors, avoiding robot teleoperation.
- Implement a two-stage "generation-then-tracking" framework (**ZeroWBC**) that first uses a fine-tuned ==Vision-Language Model== (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) to generate future human motion tokens from an egocentric image and language instruction.
- Develop an interaction-oriented general tracking policy that explicitly prioritizes global root and key body-part trajectories (wrists, feet) alongside joint-level reference tracking to preserve task-critical interaction intent.

## Results

- Achieved substantial improvements in success rates on various interactive tasks compared to general tracking baselines, for instance, increasing box moving success from **16%** to **64%** and sofa sitting from **48%** to **84%**.
- Demonstrated zero-shot generalization to novel tasks and object categories, such as navigating (**90%** success) and sitting on various chairs (**76%** success), which were not present in the training data.
- Confirmed that multimodal inputs (egocentric image and language) are essential for robust motion generation and reliable real-world interaction, with single-modality inputs yielding near-zero success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human egocentric video data, paired with motion and language, provides a scalable and cost-effective alternative to robot teleoperation for training complex humanoid interaction skills.
> - Fine-tuning a Vision-Language Model on multimodal inputs (image+text) effectively generates long-horizon, scene-aware human motions, enhancing spatial grounding for robot interaction.
> - Prioritizing interaction-critical body part trajectories (root, wrists, feet) in the tracking policy is crucial for preserving task intent and precision during human-to-humanoid motion transfer.
