# ACoT-VLA: Action Chain-of-Thought for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework introduces Action Chain-of-Thought (ACoT) for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling robots to reason directly in the action space by leveraging both explicit motion trajectories and implicit action priors. This approach enhances task success rates and robustness across diverse robotic manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from a "semantic-kinematic gap," as their reasoning in abstract vision-language spaces provides only indirect guidance for precise physical actions.
- Language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Visual CoT/World Model approaches, while improving generalization, still rely on semantic or visual intermediaries that are not directly optimized for low-level robot control.
- Pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones, primarily designed for understanding and question-answering, lack the granular knowledge of the action space essential for truly grounded robotic policy learning.

## Method

- The **ACoT-VLA** framework introduces ==Action Chain-of-Thought (ACoT)==, formulating the reasoning process as a structured sequence of coarse ==action intents== directly in the action space.
- An ==Explicit Action Reasoner (EAR)== generates kinematically plausible reference action trajectories as explicit guidance, while an ==Implicit Action Reasoner (IAR)== extracts ==latent action priors== from the VLM's internal representations.
- An ==Action-Guided Prediction (AGP) head== integrates these explicit and implicit action guidances to condition the final policy learning, effectively bridging the ==semantic-kinematic gap==.

## Results

- ACoT-VLA achieved a **98.5%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art by **1.6%** and demonstrating enhanced robustness in long-horizon tasks.
- The framework exhibited substantial robustness against distribution shifts on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark (**84.1%** avg success), improving performance by up to **16.3%** under robot initial-state perturbations.
- Real-world deployment on AgiBot G1 and AgileX platforms confirmed consistently higher average success rates (e.g., **66.7%** vs. **61.0%** for π0.5) across various manipulation tasks, validating practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The most effective form of reasoning for generalist robot policies occurs directly in the action space, providing "kinematically coherent guidance" superior to abstract language or visual sub-goals.
> - Combining explicit, coarse-grained action trajectories (from EAR) with implicit, latent action-related semantics (from IAR) synergistically enhances robotic policy learning and robustness.
> - Even VLM backbones, primarily trained for semantic understanding, contain extractable implicit action priors in their internal representations that are valuable for grounding robot actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an area of increasing focus in robotics, aiming to develop generalist robot policies capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks. These models typically leverage pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to encode multimodal inputs (visual observations and language instructions) into a latent representation, which then conditions an action decoder to generate robot actions.

Recent advancements in VLA models have explored the introduction of explicit intermediary reasoning steps to bridge the gap between high-level multimodal inputs and low-level action execution. Two prominent paradigms have emerged:
1. **Language-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** This approach involves predicting sub-tasks or generating linguistic rationales as intermediate reasoning steps. For instance, models might decompose a complex task into a sequence of natural language sub-goals to guide action generation.
2. **Visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) / World Models:** This paradigm utilizes world models to predict future visual states or synthesize goal images. These visual forecasts are then used to guide the action policy, offering a form of visual reasoning about desired outcomes.

Despite the progress demonstrated by these approaches, a fundamental challenge persists: the inherent "semantic-kinematic gap." Existing VLA policies primarily operate by reasoning in the vision-language (input) space. The knowledge encoded in VLM backbones is optimized for linguistic understanding and semantic alignment, derived from web-scale datasets, rather than for the nuances of physical dynamics or precise action execution. Similarly, world models, while predicting future states, provide guidance that remains within the visual domain. Both semantic and visual forms of reasoning offer indirect guidance, which can be suboptimal for generating the granular information required for accurate robotic motor commands. This indirectness limits their capacity to convey the full, kinematically coherent information essential for robust and grounded robotic policy learning.

The work presented in this paper introduces Action Chain-of-Thought (ACoT) as a novel paradigm that seeks to address this semantic-kinematic gap directly. By reformulating the reasoning process as a structured sequence of coarse action intents, ACoT-VLA proposes to provide guidance that is kinematically coherent and operates directly within the action space. This direct action-space reasoning differentiates it from previous methods that rely on language or visual intermediates, positioning ACoT-VLA as a departure from input-space reasoning towards output-space deliberation for robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically the "semantic-kinematic gap." While current VLA models leverage rich semantic and visual representations from pre-trained VLMs, their reasoning processes often remain in the abstract input space (language or vision), providing only indirect guidance for the precise, low-level physical actions required for robotic manipulation. This indirectness leads to several challenges:

1. **Limited Information Transfer:** Pre-trained VLMs are optimized for linguistic understanding and semantic alignment, not for the physical dynamics of robot manipulation. Therefore, the knowledge they encode, when translated directly or indirectly through language/visual intermediates, does not adequately convey the granular, kinematically coherent information needed for accurate action execution.
2. **Heterogeneity Between Perception and Action:** A fundamental disconnect exists between high-level, abstract inputs (e.g., "pick up the red block") and the precise motor commands (e.g., joint angles, end-effector poses) needed to execute them. Current intermediate reasoning (e.g., sub-task prediction, goal image synthesis) fails to fully bridge this gap, as it still operates largely within the perceptual domain.
3. **Suboptimal Guidance:** Both linguistic (e.g., sub-task lists) and visual (e.g., goal images) forms of reasoning, while offering some guidance, are inherently limited in their capacity to provide the direct motion cues essential for physical interaction. They do not explicitly deliberate about the sequence of movements a robot needs to perform.

Given these limitations, the authors posit that the most effective form of reasoning for robotic tasks should occur directly within the action space. This "kinematically coherent" guidance, analogous to learning from physical demonstration, could offer more efficient and veridically grounded policy learning.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Introduce Action Chain-of-Thought (ACoT):** To conceptualize a new paradigm where the deliberative process for robotic policies is formulated as a structured chain of explicit action-space intents, rather than abstract linguistic or visual sub-goals.
2. **Develop Mechanisms for Action-Space Guidance:** To design and implement two complementary mechanisms for synthesizing action-related information:
 - **Explicit Action Reasoner (EAR):** To generate observable motion trajectories (coarse reference actions) as direct, executable guidance.
 - **Implicit Action Reasoner (IAR):** To infer latent action priors from the internal representations of multimodal inputs, capturing contextual action tendencies.
3. **Propose ACoT-VLA Architecture:** To integrate EAR and IAR into a unified framework, ACoT-VLA, that effectively leverages both explicit and implicit action guidance to condition the downstream action head, thereby enabling grounded generalist robot policy learning.
4. **Empirical Validation:** To demonstrate the effectiveness and versatility of ACoT-VLA through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, evaluating its performance, generalization capabilities, and robustness against distribution shifts.

In essence, the research aims to fundamentally shift the "thinking" process of robot policies from perceiving and understanding to directly deliberating in the language of actions, thereby fostering more precise, robust, and generalizable robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on the Action Chain-of-Thought (ACoT) paradigm, which formulates the reasoning process as a structured sequence of coarse action intents. This is materialized in the ACoT-VLA architecture, which incorporates two complementary components: an Explicit Action Reasoner (EAR) and an Implicit Action Reasoner (IAR), both operating on features derived from a shared pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The generalist robot policy $\pi_\theta$ aims to predict action sequences $a_{t:t+H-1}$ given a natural language instruction $l$ and current visual observation $o_t$. This is typically expressed as $a_{t:t+H-1} = \pi_\theta(o_t, l)$. Existing methods introduce guidance signals $g \in \{g_{lang}, g_{vis}\}$ (language-level or vision-level) to enhance prediction. ACoT-VLA extends this by investigating guidance signals $g_{action}$ that operate directly in the action domain, further disentangled into explicit ($g^{ex}_{action}$) and implicit ($g^{im}_{action}$) forms.

#### 3.2. ACoT-VLA Architecture Overview (Figure 2)

The ACoT-VLA framework leverages features from a shared VLM backbone. The VLM encodes $o_t$ and $l$ into a contextual key-value cache $(K^{VLM}, V^{VLM})$. This cache then feeds into the EAR and IAR modules.

#### 3.2.1. Explicit Action Reasoner (EAR)

The EAR is designed to autonomously synthesize coarse-grained reference action sequences, providing explicit guidance ($g^{ex}_{action}$) in the action space.
- **Mechanism:** EAR is implemented as a lightweight transformer network.
 - It takes a noisy action sequence $\tilde{a}_{t:t+H_{ref}-1}$ as input, where $H_{ref}$ is the horizon for reference actions.
 - This noisy sequence is embedded into an initial hidden representation.
 - Within each transformer layer, it performs self-attention to capture temporal dependencies within the action sequence.
 - Crucially, it employs cross-attention with the contextual key-value cache from corresponding VLM layers, injecting multimodal contextual priors.
 - The output is a denoised reference action sequence $a^{ref}_{t:t+H_{ref}-1}$.
- **Output:** The generated reference action sequence is then encoded via an MLP projector to obtain an action embedding $Z^{ex}$, serving as explicit action-space guidance.

#### 3.2.2. Implicit Action Reasoner (IAR)

The IAR aims to extract latent action priors ($g^{im}_{action}$) that are implicitly encoded within the VLM's multimodal latent space, such as visual affordances or action-related semantics.
- **Mechanism:** IAR operates directly on the VLM's key-value cache.
 - For each VLM layer, a learnable query matrix $Q_i$ is initialized.
 - To enhance computational efficiency and reduce redundancy, the VLM's key-value pairs are first downsampled into a lower-dimensional space using learnable linear projectors.
 - Cross-attention is applied between the learnable queries ($Q'_i$) and the downsampled key-value pairs ($K'_i, V'_i$) to extract action-relevant information from each VLM layer.
 - The resulting features from each layer are then integrated via average pooling and transformed through an MLP projector to produce compact representations $z^{im}_i$.
- **Output:** These layer-wise representations are aggregated to form a unified implicit action-related feature $Z^{im}$, which provides implicit action-space guidance.

#### 3.2.3. Action-Guided Prediction (AGP) Head

This component is responsible for synergistically integrating both the explicit ($Z^{ex}$) and implicit ($Z^{im}$) action guidances to condition the final policy prediction.
- **Mechanism:**
 - A noisy action segment $\tilde{a}_{t:t+H-1}$ (for the final action sequence) is encoded into a noisy action embedding, which serves as the action query $Q_{action}$.
 - Dual cross-attention operations are performed:
 - $Q_{action}$ interacts with $Z^{ex}$ to retrieve explicit kinematic priors.
 - $Q_{action}$ interacts with $Z^{im}$ to retrieve implicit behavioral priors.
 - The attended representations from both cross-attention steps are concatenated and fused through a self-attention block to produce a unified representation $\bar{h}$.
- **Output:** This aggregated representation $\bar{h}$ is then fed into the action head $\pi^{head}_\theta$, which predicts the denoised executable action sequence $a_{t:t+H-1}$.

#### 3.3. Training Objectives

The entire ACoT-VLA framework is optimized using a standard flow-matching mean-squared error (MSE) objective. The total loss function ($L_{total}$) is a weighted sum of the flow-matching MSE for the Explicit Action Reasoner ($\mathcal{L}_{\pi^{ref}_\theta}$) and the action head ($\mathcal{L}_{\pi^{head}_\theta}$), with balance factors $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$.

#### 3.4. Teacher Forcing Stabilization

During training, to ensure stable optimization and prevent interference with the action head, the $Z^{ex}$ embedding for the AGP head is computed directly from ground-truth reference trajectories. During inference, however, the model switches to a fully self-conditioned mode, where the EAR autonomously generates the reference actions to guide the action head.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The approach builds upon the $\pi^{0.5}$ framework. It utilizes SigLIP as the visual encoder and Gemma 2B as the LLM backbone. Specific hyper-parameters include action horizons for reference actions ($H_{ref}=15$) and policy output ($H=10$), action shifts, and balance factors ($\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = 0.5$). The EAR employs 18 transformer layers, and the IAR uses learnable query matrices with specific dimensions for downsampling VLM's key-value cache.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental results in both simulation and real-world environments to validate the ACoT-VLA framework.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments: LIBERO Benchmark

- **Performance:** ACoT-VLA achieved state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods across all evaluated robot capabilities: spatial awareness (Spatial), object manipulation (Object), goal completion (Goal), and long-horizon reasoning (Long).
- **Improvement:** The method demonstrated a 1.6% absolute improvement in average success rate compared to the previous state-of-the-art method, $\pi^{0.5}$.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** A notable improvement was observed in the LIBERO-Long suite, which involves tasks requiring extended sequences of actions and strict error control. This is attributed to ACoT's direct operation in the action space, providing structured action guidance that enhances robustness in complex, multi-step manipulations.

#### 4.1.2. LIBERO-Plus Benchmark (Distribution Shift Robustness)

- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** When policies trained on the LIBERO dataset were directly evaluated on LIBERO-Plus (which introduces various perturbations like camera viewpoints, robot initial states, language variations, lighting, background, sensor noise, and object layouts), ACoT-VLA showed significant robustness. It demonstrated pronounced improvements against distribution shifts, particularly in robot initial-state perturbations (+3.2% compared to the baseline) and language variations (+4.2%), areas where existing language- or vision-guided policies experienced degradation.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning:** Under a supervised fine-tuning setting on the LIBERO-Plus dataset, ACoT-VLA achieved an 88.0% average success rate, further confirming its effectiveness in robust policy learning under varied conditions.

#### 4.1.3. VLABench Benchmark (Generalization to Diverse Tasks)

- **Overall Performance:** ACoT-VLA attained the highest Intention Score (IS) of 63.5% and Progress Score (PS) of 47.4% across the benchmark's five public tracks (in-distribution, cross-category, commonsense reasoning, semantic-instruction, and unseen-texture).
- **Robustness to Appearance Shifts:** Substantial gains were observed in the challenging "unseen-texture" track (+12.6% in IS and +7.2% in PS), indicating strong robustness to variations in object appearance.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Systematic ablation experiments on the LIBERO benchmark provided insights into the contributions of each component:
- **Explicit Action Reasoner (EAR):** The inclusion of EAR alone increased the average success rate from 96.9% (baseline, $\pi^{0.5}$) to 98.3%. This suggests that explicit action-space guidance, in the form of reference trajectories, significantly benefits action sequence prediction by providing strong inductive bias and reducing ambiguity.
- **Implicit Action Reasoner (IAR):** The addition of IAR alone raised the average success rate to 98.1%. This indicates that leveraging implicit action distributions and latent motion cues extracted from VLM representations provides effective complementary guidance.
- **Combined EAR + IAR:** Integrating both EAR and IAR achieved the highest average success rate of 98.5%, demonstrating their complementary nature in providing comprehensive action-centric guidance.
- **Reference Action Configurations (EAR):** Experiments with varying action shifts and horizons for the reference actions in EAR consistently showed performance improvements over the baseline. Shorter horizons combined with moderate shifts tended to yield stronger gains.
- **KV-cache Interaction Strategies (IAR):** Different strategies for extracting action-relevant cues from the VLM's key-value cache were evaluated. The "Downsample" strategy, which involves downsampling the VLM's key-value cache before cross-attention, achieved the best performance. This suggests that the original VLM features may contain noise for action prediction, and effective interaction mechanisms are crucial for aligning vision-language and action information.

#### 4.3. Real-World Deployment

ACoT-VLA was deployed on the AgiBot G1 robot and the AgileX robotic platform for three manipulation tasks ("Wipe Stain," "Pour Water," and "Open-set Pick"):
- **Performance:** ACoT-VLA consistently achieved higher average success rates compared to $\pi^{0.5}$ and $\pi^0$ (e.g., 66.7% vs. 61.0% vs. 33.8% for AgiBot G1).
- **Real-World Applicability:** The results demonstrate the framework's effectiveness under real-world sensing conditions, including contact-rich manipulation and fine-grained object handling.
- **Cross-Embodiment Adaptability:** The consistent improvements observed on both AgiBot G1 and AgileX platforms indicate ACoT-VLA's adaptability across different robotic embodiments.

#### 4.4. Sim-to-Real Evaluation (Genie-Sim 3.0)

- **Performance:** ACoT-VLA outperformed the baseline in both simulation (84.2% vs. 75.7%) and real-world transfer (82.9% vs. 77.5%).
- **Minimal Degradation:** The method exhibited minimal performance degradation during sim-to-real transition. This is attributed to the ACoT paradigm's focus on kinematically grounded action guidance, which remains consistent despite visual domain gaps, enhancing sim2real capabilities.

#### 4.5. Latency Analysis

The introduction of the reasoning modules (EAR and IAR) resulted in a slight increase in inference latency (e.g., from 91ms to 112ms for the full ACoT-VLA). However, this marginal overhead was outweighed by the substantial performance improvements, indicating a favorable trade-off between computational cost and efficacy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "ACoT-VLA: Action Chain-of-Thought for Vision-Language-Action Models" introduces a significant conceptual and architectural shift in the development of generalist robot policies, with several implications for the field of embodied AI and robotics.

#### 5.1. Conceptual Paradigm Shift

The most significant contribution is the introduction of the Action Chain-of-Thought (ACoT) paradigm. By arguing that deliberation should occur directly in the kinematically grounded space of actions rather than abstract linguistic or visual spaces, the paper proposes a fundamental rethinking of how robotic policies "reason." This approach moves beyond indirectly guiding action generation through semantic or visual sub-goals, focusing instead on direct motion cues. This shift has the potential to make robot decision-making more intuitive and aligned with the physical reality of manipulation.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Semantic-Kinematic Gap

The ACoT-VLA framework directly addresses the long-standing challenge of the "semantic-kinematic gap," which highlights the disconnect between high-level human instructions (semantics) and low-level robot motor commands (kinematics). By synthesizing and fusing explicit trajectory plans and implicit behavioral priors directly within the action space, ACoT-VLA creates a more direct and information-rich conduit between high-level intent and low-level motor control. This can lead to more precise and less ambiguous action generation.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance and Generalization

Empirically, ACoT-VLA demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple challenging simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, VLABench) and real-world tasks. Its robustness to various distribution shifts (e.g., camera views, robot initial states, language variations, unseen textures) in LIBERO-Plus and strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities on Genie-Sim 3.0 highlight its potential for more generalizable and reliable robot operation in diverse and unstructured environments. The particular gains in long-horizon tasks suggest improved compositional reasoning capabilities directly tied to action sequencing.

#### 5.4. Implications for Future Research

The ACoT paradigm opens several new avenues for research:
- **Structured and Interpretable Agents:** By explicitly formulating reasoning as a sequence of action intents, ACoT could lead to more interpretable robotic policies, where the "thought process" is directly observable in the form of coarse action plans.
- **Action Representation Learning:** The work highlights the importance of effective action representations. Future research could explore enriching action representations with explicit geometric or physical structures (e.g., object-centric coordinates, contact points) to allow ACoT to operate in a more geometrically interpretable 3D space, potentially unleashing its full potential.
- **Efficiency and Resource Constraints:** While the current framework introduces a slight computational overhead, further research could focus on optimizing the reasoning modules for deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- **Learning from Human Demonstration:** The analogy of ACoT to learning from physical demonstration suggests possibilities for more effective integration of human demonstrations, particularly coarse-grained actions, into robot policy learning.

#### 5.5. Potential Societal and Industrial Impact

By advancing the capabilities of generalist robots, ACoT-VLA contributes to the development of more versatile and autonomous robotic systems. This could impact various domains, including manufacturing, logistics, service robotics, and even assistive technologies, where robots need to perform complex manipulation tasks in dynamic and uncertain environments. The ability for robots to "think" in actions moves closer to the vision of truly intelligent embodied agents.
