# ACoT-VLA: Action Chain-of-Thought for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework introduces Action Chain-of-Thought (ACoT) for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling robots to reason directly in the action space by leveraging both explicit motion trajectories and implicit action priors. This approach enhances task success rates and robustness across diverse robotic manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world deployments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from a "semantic-kinematic gap," as their reasoning in abstract vision-language spaces provides only indirect guidance for precise physical actions.
- Language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Visual CoT/World Model approaches, while improving generalization, still rely on semantic or visual intermediaries that are not directly optimized for low-level robot control.
- Pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones, primarily designed for understanding and question-answering, lack the granular knowledge of the action space essential for truly grounded robotic policy learning.

## Method

- The **ACoT-VLA** framework introduces ==Action Chain-of-Thought (ACoT)==, formulating the reasoning process as a structured sequence of coarse ==action intents== directly in the action space.
- An ==Explicit Action Reasoner (EAR)== generates kinematically plausible reference action trajectories as explicit guidance, while an ==Implicit Action Reasoner (IAR)== extracts ==latent action priors== from the VLM's internal representations.
- An ==Action-Guided Prediction (AGP) head== integrates these explicit and implicit action guidances to condition the final policy learning, effectively bridging the ==semantic-kinematic gap==.

## Results

- ACoT-VLA achieved a **98.5%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art by **1.6%** and demonstrating enhanced robustness in long-horizon tasks.
- The framework exhibited substantial robustness against distribution shifts on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark (**84.1%** avg success), improving performance by up to **16.3%** under robot initial-state perturbations.
- Real-world deployment on AgiBot G1 and AgileX platforms confirmed consistently higher average success rates (e.g., **66.7%** vs. **61.0%** for π0.5) across various manipulation tasks, validating practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The most effective form of reasoning for generalist robot policies occurs directly in the action space, providing "kinematically coherent guidance" superior to abstract language or visual sub-goals.
> - Combining explicit, coarse-grained action trajectories (from EAR) with implicit, latent action-related semantics (from IAR) synergistically enhances robotic policy learning and robustness.
> - Even VLM backbones, primarily trained for semantic understanding, contain extractable implicit action priors in their internal representations that are valuable for grounding robot actions.
