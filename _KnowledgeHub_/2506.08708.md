# PhyBlock: A Progressive Benchmark for Physical Understanding and Planning via 3D Block Assembly

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyBlock introduces a progressive benchmark to evaluate Vision-Language Models' physical understanding and multi-step planning capabilities through 3D block assembly tasks, revealing that state-of-the-art models exhibit a steep performance drop with increasing task complexity and struggle with spatial orientation and dependency reasoning. It comprehensively evaluates 23 leading VLMs and establishes a significant gap between current AI and human performance.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) primarily focus on perception or single-step reasoning, failing to assess multi-step, long-horizon planning crucial for complex real-world tasks.
- Many current benchmarks neglect realistic physical interactions (e.g., gravity, stability), leading to an idealized evaluation environment that limits practical applicability.
- State-of-the-art VLMs lack robust understanding of physical interactions, spatial relationships, and object stability necessary for grounded planning in 3D environments.

## Method

- **PhyBlock** introduces a two-branch benchmark built within a high-fidelity physics simulator (Genesis) using interactive 3D blocks to evaluate physical understanding and planning.
- The benchmark features 400 hierarchical 3D block assembly tasks categorized into four ascending difficulty levels, assessing capabilities from basic perception to advanced spatial planning.
- It includes 2,200 rigorously curated Visual Question Answering (VQA) samples across 16 semantic categories, with a focus on dynamic reasoning like counterfactual and predictive questions.

## Results

- All evaluated VLMs showed a steep decline in assembly planning F1 scores, dropping over 50% from Level-1 to Level-4 tasks, with Claude 3.7 Sonnet falling from 76.78% to 41.82%.
- Models demonstrated a pronounced perception-reasoning gap, performing well on basic object VQA but dramatically collapsing on dynamic reasoning queries like counterfactual and causal inference.
- Error analysis revealed pervasive issues across architectures, primarily incorrect spatial orientation and violations of fundamental physical dependencies, accounting for the majority of planning mistakes.
- Human experts achieved approximately 94.7% accuracy, underscoring a considerable gap between current VLM capabilities and human-level physical understanding and planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs exhibit a steep performance drop, often by more than half, from simple to complex assembly tasks, indicating limitations in long-horizon planning and dependency reasoning.
> - A significant perception-reasoning gap exists; models excel at identifying object properties but struggle dramatically with VQA questions requiring dynamic reasoning or causal inference.
> - Universal error modes across all VLM architectures include incorrect spatial orientation and failure to meet fundamental support dependencies during assembly.
> - Enabling 'thinking mode' or chain-of-thought prompting provides minimal to no performance improvement for physical understanding and planning tasks.
