# Gondola: Grounded Vision Language Planning for Generalizable Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Inria and École normale supérieure researchers develop Gondola, a grounded vision-language planning framework that generates fine-grained segmentation masks and leverages multi-view camera inputs for robotic manipulation, achieving 74.8% success rate on novel rigid objects (10.3% improvement over 3D-LOTUS++) and 52.4% on novel articulated objects (10.9% improvement) on the GemBench benchmark through a Vision-Language Model architecture that fine-tunes InternVL-4B with LoRA while integrating SAM2 for multi-view object grounding, trained on three specialized datasets including 15,000 robot grounded planning examples, 58,000 multi-view referring expressions, and pseudo long-horizon tasks that enable history-aware planning, demonstrating that precise mask-based spatial grounding combined with contextual task history significantly outperforms coarser bounding box approaches and single-view methods for generalizable robotic manipulation across unseen objects and environments.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems struggle with generalization to unseen objects, novel environments, and diverse language instructions
- Existing LLM-based planning methods lack precise visual grounding, often producing abstract plans that are not actionable in real physical environments
- Current vision-language approaches use coarse spatial representations (bounding boxes, points) insufficient for fine-grained manipulation tasks
- Single-view camera inputs suffer from occlusions and limited field of view, hindering robust scene understanding
- Multi-step tasks require contextual consistency that existing systems fail to maintain across sequential planning steps

## Method

- ==Design Vision-Language Model== architecture combining frozen InternVL-4B with LoRA fine-tuning and SAM2 for multi-view segmentation
- Generate fine-grained segmentation masks instead of coarse bounding boxes for precise spatial grounding of objects and locations
- Process multi-view camera inputs with special view separation tokens to provide comprehensive 3D scene understanding
- Integrate history plans as textual context to maintain coherent sequential reasoning for long-horizon tasks
- Construct three specialized training datasets: robot grounded planning (15,000 examples), multi-view referring expressions (58,000), and pseudo long-horizon tasks

## Results

- Achieved **87.3%** (L1), **74.8%** (L2), **52.4%** (L3), and **19.0%** (L4) success rates on GemBench benchmark, outperforming 3D-LOTUS++ by **10.3%** on novel rigid objects and **10.9%** on novel articulated objects
- Demonstrated superior grounding performance with **87.9%** IoU compared to **62.8%** for bounding box variants across multiple generalization levels
- Multi-view referring expression dataset improved segmentation quality consistently, while pseudo long-horizon dataset specifically addressed L4 performance challenges
- Real robot experiments showed promising generalization to unseen tasks (**3.7**/10 average) despite limited real-world training data, highlighting the sim-to-real transfer potential

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained segmentation masks provide superior spatial grounding compared to bounding boxes, with significantly higher grounding IoU (87.9% vs 62.8%)
> - Multi-view inputs substantially improve object grounding and action prediction by mitigating occlusions and providing better 3D scene perception
> - History plan integration enables context-aware sequential planning but requires careful dataset construction to avoid distribution shift issues
> - Joint training on specialized datasets (referring expressions, long-horizon tasks) addresses specific generalization challenges more effectively than generic fine-tuning
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large foundation models (InternVL, SAM2) enables robust robotic adaptation without requiring massive robot-specific datasets
