# Gondola: Grounded Vision Language Planning for Generalizable Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Inria and École normale supérieure researchers develop Gondola, a grounded vision-language planning framework that generates fine-grained segmentation masks and leverages multi-view camera inputs for robotic manipulation, achieving 74.8% success rate on novel rigid objects (10.3% improvement over 3D-LOTUS++) and 52.4% on novel articulated objects (10.9% improvement) on the GemBench benchmark through a Vision-Language Model architecture that fine-tunes InternVL-4B with LoRA while integrating SAM2 for multi-view object grounding, trained on three specialized datasets including 15,000 robot grounded planning examples, 58,000 multi-view referring expressions, and pseudo long-horizon tasks that enable history-aware planning, demonstrating that precise mask-based spatial grounding combined with contextual task history significantly outperforms coarser bounding box approaches and single-view methods for generalizable robotic manipulation across unseen objects and environments.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems struggle with generalization to unseen objects, novel environments, and diverse language instructions
- Existing LLM-based planning methods lack precise visual grounding, relying on coarse spatial representations (bounding boxes, points) that produce abstract plans insufficient for fine-grained, actionable manipulation
- Single-view camera inputs suffer from occlusions and limited field of view, and existing systems fail to maintain contextual consistency across sequential planning steps in multi-step tasks

## Method

- ==Design Vision-Language Model== architecture combining frozen InternVL-4B with LoRA fine-tuning and SAM2, generating fine-grained segmentation masks instead of coarse bounding boxes for precise spatial grounding of objects and locations
- Process multi-view camera inputs with special view separation tokens for comprehensive 3D scene understanding, and integrate history plans as textual context to maintain coherent sequential reasoning for long-horizon tasks
- Construct three specialized training datasets: robot grounded planning (15,000 examples), multi-view referring expressions (58,000), and pseudo long-horizon tasks

## Results

- Achieved **87.3%** (L1), **74.8%** (L2), **52.4%** (L3), and **19.0%** (L4) success rates on GemBench benchmark, outperforming 3D-LOTUS++ by **10.3%** on novel rigid objects and **10.9%** on novel articulated objects
- Demonstrated superior grounding performance with **87.9%** IoU vs **62.8%** for bounding-box variants; the multi-view referring expression dataset improved segmentation consistently while the pseudo long-horizon dataset specifically boosted L4 performance
- Real robot experiments showed promising generalization to unseen tasks (**3.7**/10 average) despite limited real-world training data, highlighting the sim-to-real transfer potential

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained segmentation masks provide superior spatial grounding compared to bounding boxes (87.9% vs 62.8% grounding IoU), and multi-view inputs substantially improve object grounding and action prediction by mitigating occlusions and providing better 3D scene perception
> - History plan integration enables context-aware sequential planning but requires careful dataset construction to avoid distribution shift issues
> - Joint training on specialized datasets (referring expressions, long-horizon tasks) addresses specific generalization challenges more effectively than generic fine-tuning, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large foundation models (InternVL, SAM2) enables robust robotic adaptation without requiring massive robot-specific datasets

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, with a long-term goal of enabling robots to perform complex physical tasks in unstructured environments, mimicking human-like intuitive interaction. Historically, robotic manipulation has struggled with **generalization**—the ability to perform tasks on unseen objects, in novel environments, or under diverse language instructions.

Early approaches in robotic manipulation often relied on end-to-end learning-based models, including Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While successful in specific scenarios, these models are heavily reliant on large, diverse real-world robot datasets. The inherent **scarcity and limited diversity of such datasets** (as highlighted by references to Open X-Embodiment, DROID, AgiBot-World Colosseo) severely restrict their generalization capabilities, especially for long-horizon tasks.

To overcome these data limitations and enhance generalization, the research landscape has seen a shift towards **modular frameworks**. These frameworks separate high-level task planning from low-level action execution. This modularity allows the planning component to leverage broader, internet-scale data, which is less coupled to specific robot embodiments.

A significant recent trend involves incorporating **Large Language Models (LLMs)** into task planning due to their impressive reasoning and few-shot planning abilities. LLMs can decompose complex instructions into sub-steps or generate executable code. However, a critical limitation of pure LLM-based planning is their **lack of direct grounding in the physical world**. This "grounding problem" means LLMs often produce plans that are not actionable or reliable in real visual environments.

Prior attempts to address this grounding issue include:
- **Affordance-based methods (e.g., SayCan):** Combine LLMs with visual affordance predictors, but are limited to a predefined set of skills.
- **Image captioning (e.g., ECoT):** Convert visual scenes to text for LLMs, but captions can be inaccurate or miss crucial details, leading to error propagation.
- **Fine-tuning Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Recent works have fine-tuned VLMs to generate visually grounded plans using intermediate representations like points, bounding boxes, or latent hidden states. While promising, these often suffer from:
 - **Coarse representations:** Points and bounding boxes lack the precision needed for fine-grained manipulation in 3D.
 - **Interpretability issues:** Latent representations can be difficult to interpret and may lack conciseness.
 - **Single-view input:** Most methods rely on single-view images, exacerbating challenges due to occlusions and limited fields of view.

**Gondola directly addresses these critical limitations.** It positions itself at the forefront of VLM-based grounded planning by focusing on **multi-view inputs** for enhanced scene understanding and generating **fine-grained segmentation masks** for precise object grounding, rather than coarser bounding boxes or points. By incorporating **history plans** as context, it also tackles the challenge of long-horizon task consistency, building upon the strengths of existing VLMs like InternVL and segmentation models like SAM2. This work aims to push the boundaries of generalizable robotic manipulation by providing a more robust, visually grounded, and context-aware planning solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the Gondola research can be summarized as follows:

- **Improve Generalization in Robotic Manipulation:** The overarching goal is to enable robots to perform tasks effectively even when encountering unseen objects, novel environments, or complex, diverse language instructions. Traditional methods struggle significantly with this, necessitating new approaches.
- **Address the "Grounded Planning" Challenge:** While LLMs excel at high-level reasoning and planning, they lack direct perception of the physical world. This leads to abstract plans that are not sufficiently "grounded" in visual reality to be executable by a robot. Gondola aims to bridge this gap by generating plans that are visually precise and actionable.
- **Overcome Limitations of Existing Grounding Methods:**
 - **Coarse Visual Grounding:** Prior VLM-based methods often use bounding boxes or single points for grounding, which are insufficient for the precise manipulation required in robotics. Gondola's objective is to provide **fine-grained segmentation masks**, offering a more accurate and reliable spatial representation of target objects and locations.
 - **Single-View Input Constraints:** Relying on a single camera view limits the robot's perception due to occlusions and restricted fields of view, hindering robust planning. Gondola seeks to leverage **multi-view images** to provide a more comprehensive 3D understanding of the scene.
 - **Lack of Contextual Consistency:** For multi-step, long-horizon tasks, previous planning systems might lose track of progress or generate inconsistent sub-plans. Gondola aims to integrate **history plans** to maintain contextual awareness and improve planning consistency over time.
- **Facilitate Generalizable Task Execution:** By providing highly grounded and context-aware plans, Gondola intends to enable downstream low-level motion planning policies to execute tasks more reliably across a wider range of scenarios, ultimately enhancing the robot's overall task completion success rate.
- **Develop Comprehensive Training Data for Grounded Planning:** Recognizing the scarcity of suitable datasets, a key objective is to systematically construct **new, diverse training datasets** from a simulated environment (RLBench) that specifically support grounded vision-language planning, multi-view object grounding, and long-horizon task reasoning.

In essence, Gondola is motivated by the need for a robotic manipulation system that combines the powerful reasoning capabilities of LLMs with precise visual understanding and temporal context, thereby achieving superior generalization in complex, real-world robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Gondola proposes a novel grounded vision-language planning model designed to enhance generalization in robotic manipulation. Its methodology encompasses a unique model architecture, specific data construction strategies, and joint training objectives.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The Gondola architecture is built upon a **Vision-Language Model (VLM)** framework, specifically fine-tuning the Sa2VA model. It comprises three main components:

1. **Image Encoder:** A pretrained vision transformer (ViT) **InternVL-300M** is used to generate image patch embeddings from multi-view inputs. While the ViT itself is frozen to retain its powerful pre-learned visual representations, a 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) is trained to adapt these visual features into the language space. Crucially, the same image encoder is applied across all camera views, with a special token `\n` used to denote view separation within the input sequence to the LLM. This allows for concatenation of image tokens from multiple views into a single sequence.

2. **Large Language Model (LLM):** The core reasoning and plan generation component is **InternVL-4B**. The base parameters of this LLM are also kept frozen, with **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)** layers added for parameter-efficient fine-tuning on the robotic manipulation tasks. Gondola extends the vocabulary of Sa2VA by incorporating a dedicated `<seg>` token, which signals the need for mask generation. Paired delimiter tokens `<p>` and `</p>` are used to precisely delineate object and location references that require spatial grounding. Furthermore, to provide contextual awareness for sequential tasks, the textual history of previously generated plans (`H`) is encoded as additional compact text tokens and fed into the LLM as input. The LLM's output consists of interleaved action texts, object/location descriptions, and the `<seg>` tokens.

3. **Segmentation Model:** **SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2)** is employed for multi-view object grounding. When the LLM predicts a `<seg>` token, its corresponding hidden embedding (`h_seg`) is projected through a 2-layer MLP to generate a prompt embedding for SAM2. SAM2 then uses this prompt to decode the corresponding object mask for *each view image separately*, producing a set of binary masks (`M_o` or `M_l`) across all input views for the referred object or location.

#### 3.2. Training Data Construction

To effectively train Gondola, the authors construct three specialized datasets using the **RLBench simulator** and the GemBench training split (31 task variations), ensuring fairness in generalization evaluation against prior work.

1. **Robot Grounded Planning Dataset:**
 - **Process:** Each task trajectory in RLBench is manually decomposed into a sequence of high-level plans, with each step defined as an action, object, and placement location triplet. This manual annotation is done once per task variation.
 - **Automation:** Given these semantic labels, corresponding segmentation masks for objects and locations are automatically extracted from the simulator for multi-view images at each "keystep" (steps with significant motion change).
 - **Scale:** Approximately 15,000 training tuples are generated from 100 episodes per GemBench training task variation, with randomized object placements.

2. **Multi-View Referring Expression Dataset:**
 - **Purpose:** To explicitly strengthen Gondola's multi-view object grounding capabilities.
 - **Process:** An automatic preprocessing pipeline is implemented to standardize and refine RLBench's default semantic labels, which can be noisy or ambiguous (e.g., appending color names). All object instances and their corresponding segmentation masks are automatically extracted at each keystep.
 - **Formulation:** Each example is formulated as a query like "Please segment one of the [object name]," with the output being the multi-view segmentation masks.
 - **Scale:** This yields 15,000 multi-view image examples and 58,000 referring expressions.

3. **Pseudo Long-Horizon Tasks Dataset:**
 - **Purpose:** To enhance planning for complex, multi-step, long-horizon tasks.
 - **Process:** Random pairs of different short training task sequences from GemBench are concatenated to create compositional tasks. An LLM is then used to generate a coherent joint instruction for these combined tasks.
 - **Benefit:** Despite abrupt scene transitions in simulation, this dataset helps the model learn to leverage history plans for task progress tracking and contextual prediction over extended horizons.

#### 3.3. Training Objectives

Gondola is trained to jointly optimize two primary objectives:

1. **Plan Generation:** A standard **cross-entropy loss** (`L_plan`) is used for next token prediction, encompassing all tokens in the generated plan, including action names, object descriptions, and the special segmentation tokens (`<seg>`, `<p>`, `</p>`).
2. **Multi-View Object Grounding:** A combined loss `L_grd = L_bce + L_dice` is used for binary mask prediction across all views.
 - **Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) Loss (`L_bce`):** Measures pixel-wise difference between predicted probability masks and ground-truth binary labels.
 - **Dice Loss (`L_dice`):** Measures the overlap between predicted and ground-truth masks, particularly effective for imbalanced mask sizes.

#### 3.4. Integration with Low-Level Policies

For online task execution, Gondola integrates with a low-level 3D motion planning policy (specifically, the one released by 3D-LOTUS++). Unlike 3D-LOTUS++ which plans once, Gondola operates in an **iterative feedback loop**:
- At each step, Gondola takes multi-view RGB images, the high-level instruction, and previously executed history plans to generate the next action plan (action name, object/location, multi-view masks).
- Aligned depth images are combined with these masks to create a consolidated 3D point cloud of segmented objects.
- This point cloud and the predicted action name are fed into the 3D motion planning policy to generate robot actions.
- The system can either execute the entire action sequence generated by the motion policy ("action chunking") or replan after each single action, allowing for continuous correction.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a sophisticated VLM architecture with meticulously curated multi-modal datasets and an iterative execution loop, forms the backbone of Gondola's approach to generalizable robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Gondola's performance was rigorously evaluated on the **GemBench benchmark**, which assesses generalization across four levels: L1 (new locations), L2 (novel rigid objects), L3 (new articulated objects), and L4 (unseen long-horizon tasks). The evaluation included both offline "grounded planning" (pure model performance) and online "task completion" (integrated with a motion policy).

#### 4.1. Grounded Planning Evaluation

- **Key Metrics:** Action (Act) and Object (Obj) name prediction accuracy (exact text match), and Grounding (Grd) performance (Intersection over Union, IoU, of predicted vs. ground-truth masks across views).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Boxes vs. Masks:** Gondola's mask-based grounding (Table 1, row 4) significantly outperformed a box-based variant (Table 1, row 1) across all metrics and generalization levels. The box-based model frequently suffered from format errors and much lower grounding quality (e.g., L1 Grd: 62.8% for boxes vs. 87.9% for masks). This highlights the superiority of end-to-end mask generation for precise robotic planning.
 - **Multi-view Inputs:** Providing multi-view images (Table 1, row 3) substantially improved action, object, and grounding predictions compared to single-view input (Table 1, row 2), especially for grounding (e.g., L1 Grd: 87.9% vs. 87.8%). Multi-view alleviates occlusions and provides better 3D scene perception.
 - **History Plans:** Including history plans (Table 1, row 4) generally boosted performance on L2 and L3 by enabling more coherent and context-aware planning. However, it initially showed a performance drop on L4. This was attributed to a distribution shift where the model over-relied on textual history without sufficient grounded context for long-horizon tasks.
 - **Fine-tuning Datasets:**
 - The **multi-view referring expression dataset** proved most effective in improving segmentation quality, leading to consistently better grounding performance across all levels (e.g., L1 Grd: 89.1% with RefExp vs. 87.9% without).
 - The **pseudo long-horizon task dataset** specifically addressed the L4 performance drop, mitigating the history plan shift issue and significantly boosting L4 performance (L4 Act: 93.9%, Obj: 51.2%, Grd: 53.8% with Long data, vs. 79.4%, 44.9%, 40.0% without). This dataset trained the model to reason over extended plan histories.

#### 4.2. Task Completion Evaluation (Online Execution)

- **Metric:** Success Rate (SR) across 20 episodes per task variation for 5 runs.
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art:**
 - Gondola (integrated with 3D-LOTUS++'s motion policy) was compared against state-of-the-art methods, including end-to-end policies (e.g., Hiveformer, PolarNet, RVT-2) and LLM-based planning methods (3D-LOTUS++).
 - Methods without LLMs showed strong L1 performance but struggled with generalization (L2-L4). LLM-based methods generally improved L2-L4 generalization, albeit sometimes at a slight cost to L1.
 - **Gondola significantly outperformed the state-of-the-art LLM-based method, 3D-LOTUS++, across all four generalization levels.** Gondola achieved 87.3% (L1), 74.8% (L2), 52.4% (L3), and 19.0% (L4) average success rates, compared to 3D-LOTUS++'s 68.7% (L1), 64.5% (L2), 41.5% (L3), and 17.4% (L4). This represents a notable improvement of **10.3% on L2 (novel rigid objects) and 10.9% on L3 (novel articulated objects)**, and a slight edge on L4.
 - This demonstrates Gondola's superior generalizability, benefiting from multi-view inputs, history-aware planning, segmentation masks, and diverse training data.

- **Action Chunking:** The impact of action chunk size (how many low-level actions the motion policy executes before Gondola replans) varied. Smaller chunks (more frequent replanning) generally helped tasks requiring fine-grained, reactive manipulation (e.g., 'SlideBlock', 'PutInCupboard'). Larger chunks (less frequent replanning) were sometimes more effective for long-horizon tasks, where consistent high-level planning was beneficial and the current model's fine-grained history encoding within subplans was limited.

#### 4.3. Real Robot Experiments

- Gondola was deployed on a real UR5 robotic arm with three RealSense D435 cameras.
- The model was fine-tuned on a joint dataset of RLBench and limited real robot teleoperation demonstrations (20x7 for 7 task variations).
- Performance on seen tasks averaged 5/10, and on unseen tasks averaged 3.7/10.
- **Observation:** Performance was lower than in simulation, primarily due to the limited real robot data. Multi-view consistency was notably lower in the real world, leading to frequent segmentation errors. This highlights a key limitation and a direction for future work: increasing diverse real-world multi-view data. Despite this, Gondola showed successful predictions for some unseen tasks, demonstrating its potential in real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Gondola represents a significant step forward in generalizable robotic manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level physical execution. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** By outperforming state-of-the-art methods on novel objects and long-horizon tasks, Gondola moves robotic manipulation closer to real-world applicability. The ability to generalize to unseen scenarios is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured environments like homes and factories, reducing the need for extensive retraining or manual reprogramming for every new task or object.

2. **Precision through Grounded Planning:** The explicit generation of fine-grained segmentation masks for target objects and locations is a critical advancement. Unlike coarse bounding boxes or abstract latent embeddings, these masks provide precise spatial grounding that directly informs the robot's motion planner. This precision is essential for delicate manipulation tasks and helps reduce errors stemming from misidentification or inaccurate localization of interaction points.

3. **Robustness via Multi-View Perception:** Leveraging multi-view camera inputs is a practical and effective way to mitigate issues like occlusions and limited fields of view inherent in single-camera setups. This comprehensive scene understanding enhances the reliability of object grounding and subsequent planning, leading to more robust task execution.

4. **Context-Aware Planning with History Integration:** The incorporation of history plans allows Gondola to maintain a coherent understanding of the task's progress, enabling more intelligent and consistent multi-step planning. This is particularly impactful for long-horizon tasks, where sequential reasoning and adaptation based on past actions are vital.

5. **Data-Efficient Fine-Tuning:** By building upon powerful pre-trained VLMs (InternVL, SAM2) and using parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques like LoRA, Gondola demonstrates how large foundation models can be adapted for robotics without requiring immense, robot-specific datasets from scratch. This makes the approach more practical and scalable.

6. **Structured Data Generation for Robotics:** The methodology for constructing simulated datasets (robot grounded planning, multi-view referring expression, pseudo long-horizon tasks) is itself a valuable contribution. It provides a blueprint for generating high-quality, structured data that specifically targets the challenges of vision-language grounded planning, addressing a critical bottleneck in robot learning.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Wider Adoption of Robots:** More generalizable robots can perform a broader range of tasks with minimal re-configuration, accelerating their adoption in logistics, manufacturing, and domestic settings.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** More intuitive and reliable language-based control fosters better collaboration between humans and robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Gondola's framework, particularly its emphasis on precise visual grounding and temporal reasoning, sets a new state-of-the-art and provides a strong baseline for future research in embodied AI, multi-modal learning, and robot autonomy.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors):**
- **Data Scarcity:** Current dataset is limited to short-horizon tasks in tabletop environments. Future work needs more diverse simulated and real-world data to fully unleash generalization. The real robot experiments already show this limitation.
- **Visual History Encoding:** More efficient memory mechanisms are needed to incorporate detailed visual history without overwhelming the model, especially with multi-view inputs.
- **Error Anticipation and Correction:** Reliance on imitation learning from successful episodes means the model struggles with anticipating and correcting failures. Incorporating examples of failure and recovery could lead to more robust real-world applications.

Despite these limitations, Gondola represents a significant stride towards making robots more intelligent, adaptable, and capable of understanding and executing complex instructions in visually rich environments.
