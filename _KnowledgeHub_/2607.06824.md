# CaLiSym: Learning Symplectic Dynamics of Real-World Systems through Structured Canonical Lifts

> [!summary] Summary
> CALISYM, a framework developed by researchers at the University of Oxford and University of Sussex, extends exact symplectic learning to real-world, non-conservative robotic systems by embedding their observed dynamics and interaction ports into a higher-dimensional canonical phase space. This approach reduces out-of-distribution autoregressive prediction errors by up to 69.5% on systems like a double pendulum, quadrotor, and quadruped, while using fewer parameters than comparative models.

## Problem

- Existing physics-informed learning methods with strong geometric guarantees are limited to closed, conservative systems and cannot directly model real-world robots with actuation, dissipation, and contact.
- Black-box neural dynamics models often exhibit compounding errors, long-horizon drift, and violate physical conservation laws, especially when encountering out-of-distribution data.
- Real-world robotic systems are inherently open and non-conservative, which fundamentally mismatches the assumptions of traditional geometry-preserving dynamics modeling techniques.

## Method

- The **CaLiSym** framework augments the physical state and external interaction variables (==ports== like actuation and contact) into a higher-dimensional, structured ==canonical phase space==, where the learned dynamics are exactly ==symplectic==.
- An ==autoregressive gauge-fixed lifted rollout== procedure is introduced, which alternates between lifting the observed physical state and ports, evolving the state in the lifted symplectic space using a neural network, and projecting back to the physical state.
- The approach utilizes parameter-efficient symplectic neural network architectures (**GR-SYMPNET** and **GRB-SYMPNET**) that enforce exact symplecticity in the lifted space through structured layers, combining expressivity with structure preservation.

## Results

- CALISYM achieved significantly lower out-of-distribution autoregressive prediction errors across diverse robotic systems, including a **69.5%** reduction on a controlled double pendulum, and superior performance on a real-world quadrotor and quadruped compared to baselines.
- The models consistently demonstrated parameter efficiency, often achieving better or comparable performance with a notably smaller number of parameters across all benchmarks.
- Numerical verification confirmed that the learned lifted dynamics preserved the symplectic form to machine precision (mean epsilon_J_Omega values in the range of **10^-16** to **10^-15**) for all evaluated systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-conservative dynamics in real-world systems can be rigorously re-represented as Hamiltonian dynamics in an appropriately augmented or 'lifted' state space, allowing for the application of exact symplectic learning.
> - Maintaining geometric fidelity in a higher-dimensional, lifted phase space, even when the observed physical dynamics are non-conservative, leads to more stable and accurate long-horizon predictions.
> - The separation of underlying geometric structure from energy/momentum exchange processes through canonical port variables offers a principled way to model open systems while retaining strong physical inductive biases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of dynamics prediction for robotic systems has seen advancements through both classical model-based control and data-driven machine learning approaches. Classical methods, relying on first principles such as rigid-body mechanics, offer high fidelity but often incur significant computational costs, making them challenging for real-time applications like model-predictive control (MPC). Furthermore, they can struggle to accurately model complex, non-conservative effects such as aerodynamic drag, friction, impacts, or actuator limitations, which are prevalent in real-world robotics.

In parallel, data-driven "world models" using neural networks have emerged as alternatives, promising speed and adaptability. These black-box models learn state transitions directly from data. However, without explicit physical constraints, they frequently exhibit compounding errors, long-horizon drift, and violate fundamental physical conservation laws, especially when encountering data distributions unseen during training. This can render them unreliable for safety-critical applications or long-term planning.

Physics-informed learning aims to bridge this gap by integrating known physical structures directly into learning architectures. Approaches such as Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs) and Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs) parameterize energy functions, while Symplectic Neural Networks (SYMPNETs) construct discrete-time updates that preserve the canonical symplectic form. These methods leverage geometric priors to improve data efficiency, extrapolation, and long-horizon stability by constraining the learned dynamics to physically plausible trajectories.

A significant limitation of many existing physics-informed methods, particularly those offering the strongest geometric guarantees like exact symplecticity, is their reliance on the assumption of closed conservative systems. Real-world robotic systems, however, are inherently open; they exchange energy and momentum with their environment through actuation, dissipation, and external constraints (e.g., contacts). This fundamental mismatch prevents direct application of these geometry-preserving techniques to a broad range of practical robotic scenarios.

The current work, CALISYM, positions itself within this landscape by extending the applicability of exact symplectic learning to controlled, dissipative, and contact-rich systems. It draws inspiration from geometric mechanics, which demonstrates that seemingly non-Hamiltonian behavior can often be re-represented as Hamiltonian dynamics on an appropriately augmented or "lifted" state space (e.g., through symplectification or cotangent lifts). By adopting this principle, CALISYM seeks to retain the stability and geometric guarantees of symplectic models while accommodating the complex, non-conservative dynamics characteristic of real-world robotics. Additionally, by proposing GRB-SYMPNET, the work also connects to developments in local function approximation, such as spline-based models and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs), aiming to combine their expressivity with structure preservation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables the application of exact symplectic learning to robotic systems whose observed dynamics are fundamentally non-conservative, encompassing actuation, dissipation, and explicit constraints.

This objective is motivated by several considerations:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Symplectic Learning:** Current symplectic learning methods provide robust long-horizon stability and generalization capabilities due to their preservation of the underlying phase-space geometry. However, these benefits have largely been confined to ideal, closed conservative systems. Real-world robots, being actuated, dissipative, and interacting with their environment (e.g., through contact), do not adhere to these assumptions. This limits the practical utility of strong geometric priors in many robotic applications.
- **Improving Dynamics Prediction Accuracy and Stability:** Traditional black-box neural dynamics models, while flexible, often suffer from compounding errors and drift during long-horizon rollouts due to a lack of physical inductive biases. This compromises their reliability for planning and control. Physics-informed models offer improvements, but the challenge remains in extending these benefits to non-conservative systems without sacrificing the accuracy of prediction.
- **Enabling Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** Deployed robotic systems frequently encounter operating conditions (terrains, payloads, contact modes) that differ from their training environment. Unconstrained models tend to degrade significantly under such distribution shifts. A model that inherently respects physical laws, even when observing non-conservative dynamics, is expected to exhibit greater robustness and extrapolation capabilities.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-time Control:** Many control and planning algorithms, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC), require computationally inexpensive dynamics models that can be queried rapidly and repeatedly. While high-fidelity physics models can be slow, and complex recurrent neural networks can be memory-intensive for training (e.g., Backpropagation Through Time), a Markovian, parameter-efficient model that maintains stability over long horizons is desirable.
- **Facilitating Advanced Control Synthesis:** Explicitly representing energy exchange as canonical variables within a geometry-preserving framework could open pathways for passivity-based and certificate-based control methods. These approaches rely on accurate energy bookkeeping to provide formal guarantees about system behavior.
- **Structured Modeling of Open Systems:** The work aims to offer a principled way to model open systems by separating the underlying geometric structure (which remains Hamiltonian/symplectic in a lifted space) from the physical processes of energy and momentum exchange (which are captured by port variables and projection). This offers a structured approach to modeling complex interactions.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to provide a practical and theoretically grounded framework that combines the stability and data efficiency of geometric learning with the demands of real-world robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CALISYM's methodology centers on extending exact symplectic learning to non-conservative systems by redefining where the geometric prior is applied. Instead of directly enforcing symplecticity on the observed physical state, the framework embeds this state and its interaction variables (ports) into a higher-dimensional, structured canonical phase space, where the learned dynamics are exactly symplectic.

#### 3.1. Structured Lift and Augmented Cotangent-Lifted Space

The core approach involves augmenting the measured physical state `x = (q, p)` (generalized configurations `q` and momenta `p` on the cotangent bundle `T*Q`) with additional variables that represent external interactions. These interactions are collectively termed "physical ports," denoted `e`, which can include actuator torques, contact forces, or other environmental exchange variables.

The physical state `x` and ports `e` are embedded into a lifted canonical state `Z = (Q, P)`, where `Z` is equipped with a canonical symplectic form `Ω`. For a system with control and contact ports, the lifted coordinates are defined as:
- `Q = (q, y, λ_u, λ_c)`
- `P = (r, p, μ_u, π_c)`
Here, `(q, p)` are the physical configuration and momentum. `(r, y)` are auxiliary conjugate fibers associated with the physical state. `(λ_u, μ_u)` represent the actuation port (with `μ_u` corresponding to the actual control input), and `(λ_c, π_c)` represent the contact port (with `λ_c` corresponding to contact forces). This structure ensures that the lifted variables form a canonical cotangent representation. The dynamics in this augmented space are assumed to be generated by a lifted Hamiltonian `H_lift(b, ζ)` that is linear in certain fiber variables, representing a cotangent lift of the physical dynamics augmented with canonical port variables.

#### 3.2. Gauge-Fixed Data Section and Rollout Procedure

In practice, only the physical state and interaction ports are observed. This corresponds to a "gauge-fixed data section" `S_e` within the lifted space `Z`, where auxiliary fibers (`r, y, λ_u, π_c`) are set to zero, and the remaining port variables (`μ_u, λ_c`) are fixed to the measured or commanded values `u_k` and `f_c,k`.

The prediction process, termed "autoregressive gauge-fixed lifted rollout," alternates between the physical section and the full lifted phase space:
1. **Lift:** The current physical state `x_k` and port `e_k` are mapped to the lifted space `Z_k` by the section embedding `σ_e_k(x_k)`.
2. **Evolve:** An exactly symplectic neural network `Φ_∆t_θ` learns and propagates the lifted state from `Z_k` to `¯Z_k+1`.
3. **Project:** The predicted lifted state `¯Z_k+1` is projected back to the physical state `ˆx_k+1` using `Π_X(¯Z_k+1)`.
4. **Re-embed:** For the next step in an autoregressive rollout, the projected physical prediction `ˆx_k+1` is re-embedded into the lifted space `Z_k+1` using the *next* observed or commanded port value `e_k+1`.

This procedure ensures that the learned map `Φ_∆t_θ` is exactly symplectic in the lifted space `Z`, while the induced physical dynamics on `X` are allowed to be non-conservative (forced, dissipative, or constrained).

#### 3.3. Symplectic Predictors

The framework is architecture-agnostic regarding the choice of `Φ_∆t_θ`, provided it implements an exactly symplectic map. The paper employs two types of symplectic neural networks:
- **GR-SYMPNET (Generalized-Ridge SYMPNET):** Each layer of this network is generated by a scalar Hamiltonian `K_ℓ` acting on a low-dimensional "ridge coordinate" `ξ_ℓ = A_ℓP + B_ℓQ`. The condition `A_ℓB_ℓ^T = B_ℓA_ℓ^T` ensures the exact symplecticity of the layer's flow. The full network, being a composition of such layers, remains symplectic. This architecture is designed for parameter efficiency in high-dimensional lifted spaces by localizing nonlinear computations to the low-dimensional ridge.
- **GRB-SYMPNET (Generalized-Ridge B-spline SYMPNET):** This variant of GR-SYMPNET replaces the general scalar function `K_ℓ` with a combination of B-spline basis functions and a smooth tail. This allows for local function approximation within the low-dimensional ridge coordinate, enhancing expressivity without compromising the exact symplectic structure. GRB-SYMPNET is utilized for systems with compact low-dimensional lifted dynamics.

#### 3.4. Training, Normalization, and Verification

- **Loss Function:** Models are trained using a combination of a one-step Teacher-Forcing (TF) loss `L_1` and a multi-step TF loss `L_H` (mean-squared error between predicted and ground-truth physical states). An additional regularization term `L_sec` penalizes the deviation of auxiliary fibers from zero in the lifted space *before* re-embedding, encouraging the learned map to remain close to the data section over a single step.
- **Normalization:** To handle heterogeneous units and scales of lifted variables, a specific normalization scheme is applied to the base-fiber coordinates `(b_i, ζ_i)` that mathematically preserves the canonical symplectic form `db_i ∧ dζ_i`.
- **Verification:** Although GR-SYMPNET and GRB-SYMPNET are structurally symplectic, numerical verification is performed by computing the Jacobian residual `ϵ_J_Ω` to confirm that the trained models maintain symplecticity to numerical precision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CALISYM was conducted across three robotic systems with varying complexities and non-conservative characteristics, consistently comparing its performance against multiple baselines (MLP, Transformer, RWM-TF, DHNN, D-SymODEN). The primary metric was out-of-distribution (OOD) autoregressive mean-squared error (MSE), alongside parameter count, to assess both predictive accuracy and model efficiency.

#### 4.1. Controlled Dissipative Double Pendulum

- **System Type:** Simulated, torque-controlled, damped, low-dimensional system exhibiting chaotic behavior.
- **CALISYM Configuration:** Uses GRB-SYMPNET, embedding joint angles, momenta, and control torques into a 12-dimensional lifted space.
- **Results:** CALISYM achieved an OOD AR MSE of 2.341, marking a 69.5% reduction compared to the strongest baseline (7.688). This was accomplished with approximately 3.7K parameters, significantly fewer than the baselines (~10K). The rollouts demonstrated accurate tracking of joint angles and momenta over extended horizons. The in-distribution multi-horizon rollout analysis also showed stable numerical behavior over 800 steps (8 seconds) with bounded error growth.

#### 4.2. Real-World Quadrotor Dynamics

- **System Type:** Underactuated aerial system with control-dependent dynamics, rotor thrust, and aerodynamic effects (dissipation).
- **CALISYM Configuration:** Uses GR-SYMPNET, lifting vehicle pose, orientation, generalized momenta, and commanded rotor thrusts/body torques into an 18-dimensional lifted space.
- **Results:** CALISYM obtained the lowest OOD AR MSE (0.180), outperforming the next best baseline (0.355). It utilized approximately 24K parameters, which was fewer than the competing methods (~50K-53K). This experiment demonstrated that the lifted formulation successfully transfers to strongly forced aerial dynamics without modification, maintaining stable long-horizon predictions despite significant control inputs and unmodeled dissipation.

#### 4.3. Real-World Quadruped Dynamics

- **System Type:** High-dimensional, contact-rich floating-base system with joint torques and ground contact, from the ANYmal-D robot.
- **CALISYM Configuration:** Employs GR-SYMPNET, embedding floating-base and joint coordinates, their momenta, control torques, and contact forces into a 66-dimensional lifted space.
- **Results:** CALISYM achieved an OOD AR MSE of 2.697, representing a 12.7% reduction relative to the strongest baseline (Transformer at 3.089) and a 46.9% reduction compared to D-SymODEN (5.080). This was achieved with approximately 316K parameters, a smaller count compared to most baselines (400K-720K). The rollouts accurately captured both floating-base motion and leg joint dynamics despite the complexity of intermittent contacts and varied scales of variables.

#### 4.4. Summary of Key Findings

- **Consistent OOD Performance:** Across all three diverse robotic systems, CALISYM consistently delivered lower out-of-distribution autoregressive prediction errors compared to both black-box and other physics-informed baselines.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** CALISYM models demonstrated competitive or superior performance with a notably smaller number of parameters across all benchmarks, suggesting that the gains stem from the geometric inductive bias of the lifted representation rather than increased model capacity.
- **Markovian Nature:** Unlike some baselines that used context windows, CALISYM operates as a fully Markovian predictor, relying only on the current state and port variables, which is efficient for control applications.
- **Exact Symplecticity:** Numerical verification confirmed that the learned lifted dynamics preserved the symplectic form to numerical precision (mean `ϵ_J_Ω` values in the range of `10^-16` to `10^-15`), confirming the structural integrity of the approach.
- **Transferability:** The core lift-evolve-project-re-embed procedure proved transferable across systems with fundamentally different dynamics (pendulum, quadrotor, quadruped), supporting its generality as a mechanism for extending symplectic learning.

These results indicate that by enforcing symplectic structure in a carefully constructed lifted phase space, CALISYM successfully extends the benefits of geometry-preserving dynamics modeling to a wide range of real-world, non-conservative robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CALISYM framework represents a notable advancement in the field of physics-informed machine learning for robotics, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Broadened Applicability of Symplectic Learning:** The work overcomes a fundamental limitation of existing symplectic learning methods by demonstrating how to apply exact symplectic guarantees to non-conservative, real-world robotic systems. This includes systems with continuous actuation, significant dissipation, and intermittent contact, which were previously largely excluded from the benefits of such structure-preserving approaches. This expansion significantly widens the scope for leveraging geometric priors in practical robotics.

- **Enhanced Long-Horizon Prediction Stability and Accuracy:** By maintaining geometric fidelity in a structured lifted phase space, CALISYM consistently achieves improved long-horizon autoregressive prediction accuracy, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios. This stability is crucial for model-based control, planning, and simulation, where compounding errors in dynamics models can lead to catastrophic failures or unreliable predictions over extended time horizons.

- **Increased Data Efficiency and Robustness:** The geometric inductive bias embedded in CALISYM allows it to learn effective dynamics models with fewer parameters and potentially less data compared to black-box models. Furthermore, its ability to generalize to unseen dynamical regimes (out-of-distribution scenarios) suggests enhanced robustness for robotic systems operating in varied and unpredictable real-world environments.

- **Computational Efficiency for Real-time Control:** The models generated by CALISYM are Markovian and computationally lightweight, requiring only a single forward pass for dynamics prediction. This characteristic makes them highly compatible with the demands of real-time control applications like model-predictive control (MPC) and imagination-based reinforcement learning, where fast and stable dynamics evaluations are critical.

- **Enabling Advanced Control Paradigms:** The explicit representation of energy exchange through canonical port variables in the lifted space offers a structured avenue for integration with energy-aware control techniques. This could facilitate the development of passivity-based control and certificate-based control synthesis, which rely on precise energy bookkeeping for formal stability and safety guarantees.

- **Architectural Versatility and Scalability:** The core concept of a structured canonical lift is demonstrated to be architecture-agnostic and scalable across systems of varying dimensionality and interaction complexities (from a low-dimensional pendulum to a high-dimensional quadruped). This versatility suggests that the framework can be applied to a broad array of robotic platforms without requiring extensive redesigns.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research, including the exploration of richer port representations, the integration of uncertainty quantification into lifted dynamics, and direct coupling with model-predictive control and trajectory optimization pipelines. More broadly, it reinforces the principle that identifying and exploiting underlying geometric structures, even when not immediately apparent in the measured physical dynamics, can yield substantial benefits in learning and control for complex physical systems.
