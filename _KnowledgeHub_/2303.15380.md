# Hi4D: 4D Instance Segmentation of Close Human Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> Hi4D is a method and dataset for closely interacting humans. Personalized SNARF-based implicit avatars, fit on contact-free frames, act as priors while pose optimization and shape refinement alternate to split fused multi-view 4D scans into per-person instances. The released dataset covers 20 subject pairs, 100 sequences, over 11K frames with textured scans, SMPL registrations, 2D/3D masks, and vertex-level contact.

## Problem

- Multi-view volumetric capture fuses spatially proximal subjects into a single connected mesh, so per-subject 4D geometry, registration, and contact cannot be recovered.
- Existing interaction datasets give only joint positions, region-level contact on 631 frames, static frames, or lack textured scans and ground-truth parametric registrations.
- Prior disentanglement of interacting people leans on manual annotation, and rigid tracking fails because articulation and contact themselves deform the shapes.

## Method

- Dynamic personalized priors: per-subject ==SNARF== implicit avatars with canonical shape and learned ==LBS== deformation fields, built from contact-free 4D scans with ==SMPL== registered beforehand.
- Alternating optimization: joint multi-subject SMPL pose fitting under a scan-to-mesh surface energy plus pose prior, interleaved N times per frame with shape-network refinement, propagated across frames.
- Shape refinement with a ==collision loss==: occupancy composited as a max over subjects, trained by ==binary cross entropy== against the fused scan while penalizing points inside two subjects.

## Results

- Full method reaches **0.989** IoU, **0.22 cm** Chamfer, **0.23 cm** P2S, **0.945** normal consistency, beating the SMPL+D tracking baseline and both ablated variants.
- Removing alternating optimization drops IoU to **0.938**; the collision loss cuts mean interpenetration volume from **11.62e-4** to **5.82e-4** cubic meters, a **49.91%** reduction.
- Baselines struggle on the benchmark: monocular SMPL estimators reach **87.6-93.0 mm** MPJPE, while monocular reconstruction tops out at **0.780** IoU versus **0.906** for 8-view DeepMultiCap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Person-specific learned priors resolve instance ambiguity that generic templates cannot, since a personalized surface model knows what geometry belongs to whom under occlusion.
> - Alternating rather than concurrent pose and shape updates disentangles the two error sources; joint optimization confuses body parts in contact regions.
> - Contact-induced deformations of cloth and soft tissue cannot be learned from isolated scans, so capture protocols must model them during interaction itself.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "Hi4D" is situated within the broader landscape of computer vision, specifically focusing on 3D human reconstruction, pose and shape estimation, and instance segmentation. This field has witnessed advancements in reconstructing individual humans and groups where subjects are spatially separated. However, a persistent challenge remains in robustly analyzing scenarios involving multiple humans in close physical interaction, particularly under prolonged contact and severe occlusions.

Existing instance segmentation methods primarily operate in the 2D domain or focus on static 3D objects, exhibiting limitations when applied to articulated humans undergoing dynamic interactions. While explicit body models like SMPL are widely used, their fixed topology can restrict the representation of individual features, clothing details, and contact-induced deformations. Recent work has explored implicit neural representations to address some of these limitations, offering higher resolution and greater expressive power for modeling articulated clothed bodies.

In the context of multi-person pose and shape estimation, most approaches are designed for scenarios where individuals are largely separated, with limited attention paid to close-range interactions involving physical contact. Datasets that include human-human interaction, such as ShakeFive2, MuPoTS-3D, ExPI, MultiHuman, and CHI3D, often lack one or more critical modalities for studying close contact. These deficiencies include the absence of high-fidelity dynamic textured geometry, accurately registered parametric body models, comprehensive vertex-level contact information, or full instance segmentation masks. For example, CHI3D, while addressing closer interactions, relies heavily on manual annotations for disentanglement, provides only region-level contact labels, and lacks textured scans. MultiHuman offers textured scans but without ground-truth body model registrations, and ExPI provides dynamic textured meshes but lacks instance masks, body model registrations, and detailed contact information.

This work addresses a recognized gap in both methodology and data for analyzing dynamic, physically close human-human interactions. It aims to overcome the limitations of current multi-view systems, which typically fuse proximal subjects into a single, connected mesh, thereby preventing individual instance-level analysis. By introducing a novel segmentation method and a comprehensive dataset, this research seeks to establish a foundation for developing AI systems capable of interpreting the complexities of close human interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged difficulty of robustly disentangling and reconstructing multiple closely interacting people from multi-view capture data. Current computer vision systems and multi-view reconstruction setups typically fuse the 3D surfaces of spatially proximal subjects into a single, connected mesh due to occlusions and complex, dynamically changing shapes. This fusion prevents the recovery of complete, per-subject 4D surface geometry, accurate parametric body model registrations, and detailed contact information, which are crucial for interpreting complex human behaviors.

Furthermore, existing datasets featuring human-human interactions are often limited in their fidelity, dynamism, and richness of annotations when it comes to prolonged physical contact. Many lack high-quality 4D textured geometry, registered parametric body models, and granular, vertex-level contact information, making them unsuitable for training and evaluating methods specifically designed for close interaction scenarios. The absence of such data hinders the development of AI systems capable of understanding and interpreting the nuances of human physical interaction, which is a significant component of human communication and activity.

In response to these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a Novel Method for 4D Instance Segmentation:** To create a robust method that can automatically track and segment 4D scans of multiple closely interacting people through extended periods of dynamic physical contact. This method aims to leverage emerging neural implicit surface representations to disentangle fused raw scan geometry into individual instances.
2. **Construct a Comprehensive Dataset (Hi4D):** To compile a new, high-quality dataset, named Hi4D, which provides segmented, complete 4D textured geometry of closely interacting humans. This dataset is designed to include rich interaction-centric annotations, such as corresponding multi-view RGB images, accurately registered parametric human models, 2D and 3D instance segmentation masks, and vertex-level contact annotations. The goal is for Hi4D to facilitate research into a wide array of computer vision tasks related to close human interaction.
3. **Establish Experimental Protocols and Baselines:** To define and provide experimental protocols for various computer vision tasks that are enabled by the Hi4D dataset. This includes evaluating state-of-the-art methods for multi-person pose and shape modeling, as well as detailed geometry reconstruction, to establish benchmarks for future research in this challenging domain.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to take a foundational step towards enabling future AI systems to interpret the complex and often occluded interactions of multiple humans under close physical contact.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology in Hi4D centers on addressing the challenge of segmenting and tracking multiple closely interacting humans from fused 4D scans. This is achieved by leveraging personalized neural implicit avatars within an alternating optimization framework. The approach comprises three main components: building dynamic personalized priors, initializing the tracking process, and performing instance segmentation through an alternating optimization of pose and shape.

### 4. Dynamic Personalized Prior

To handle the complexities of articulated human shapes and interactions, the method first establishes personalized avatars for each individual subject.
- **Parametric Body Model Fitting:** For each subject, SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear model) is registered to individual 4D scans captured during dynamic motion. This process involves minimizing an energy function that aligns the SMPL mesh with the scan, incorporates 3D keypoint energies (derived from triangulated 2D keypoints from multi-view RGB images), and includes prior terms to constrain human pose and shape. A subject-specific body shape parameter (β) is pre-estimated from a minimally clothed static scan and kept fixed during subsequent fitting to clothed scans.
- **Human Avatar Learning:** Neural implicit surfaces are employed to create detailed avatars, specifically using SNARF for its generalization capabilities across various poses. Two neural fields are trained in canonical space:
 - **Shape Field ($f_σ$):** Predicts the occupancy probability of any 3D point in canonical space, implicitly defining the canonical shape as its 0.5 level set. SMPL pose parameters are inputs to model pose-dependent deformations.
 - **Deformation Field ($w_ω$):** A person-specific field that transforms the acquired canonical shape to a desired pose through linear blend skinning (LBS).
 - **Correspondence Search:** An iterative root-finding method is used to determine the canonical space correspondence for query points sampled in deformed space.
 - **Training Losses:** The implicit model is trained using binary cross-entropy between predicted occupancy and ground-truth occupancy from points sampled in deformed space.
 - **Mesh Extraction:** Canonical meshes are extracted from the continuous occupancy fields using Multiresolution IsoSurface Extraction (MISE) and then deformed to posed space via LBS with the learned deformation field.

### 5. Instance Segmentation During Interaction

After building individual avatars, the method proceeds to track and segment these avatars through periods of physical contact.
- **Initialization:** For the last frame before physical contact ($t_0$), SMPL models are registered to the still-separated raw scans to obtain initial pose parameters for each subject. The corresponding canonical shapes are extracted using the learned avatars. For subsequent contact frames ($t \in \{t_1, \ldots, t_τ\}$), the shapes are initialized from the previous frame's optimized parameters.
- **Alternating Optimization:** This is a temporal tracking process performed for each contact frame, alternating between pose optimization and shape refinement for N iterations.
 - **Pose Optimization:** The SMPL parameters ($Θ_p_t$) for all interacting subjects are jointly optimized. The objective function minimizes a surface energy term that encourages the union of the posed meshes (deformed canonical shapes) to align with the fused raw input scan ($M_{raw_t}$). A regularization term ensures individual SMPL template alignment and penalizes unrealistic human poses.
 - **Shape Refinement:** Following pose optimization, the shape network weights ($σ_p_t$) of each avatar are refined dynamically. This step aims to capture high-frequency details and model contact-induced deformations (e.g., clothing flattening). Points are sampled from the fused raw scan, and subject-specific canonical correspondences are found. The final occupancy prediction for any sampled point is composited as the union (maximum) of individual avatar predictions. The shape network weights are refined by minimizing a binary cross-entropy loss between this composited occupancy and the raw scan's occupancy.
 - **Collision Loss:** A critical component of shape refinement is the collision loss. This term penalizes interpenetration of surfaces by identifying points predicted to be inside multiple subjects (i.e., with occupancy > 0.5 for more than one avatar). It encourages the shape networks to adjust such that surfaces adhere to contact deformation rather than intersecting.

### 6. Dataset (Hi4D) Collection

The methodology includes the collection of the Hi4D dataset. This involved recruiting 20 unique pairs of participants (40 individuals with diverse body shapes and clothing) to perform 100 interaction sequences, such as hugging, dancing, and sports. The dataset comprises over 11,000 frames, with more than 6,000 frames featuring physical contact. Critical annotations include vertex-level contact information covering over 95% of the parametric human body, in addition to multi-view RGB images, 4D textured scans, instance segmentation masks (2D and 3D), and registered parametric body models.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both methodological evaluations and benchmark results on the newly introduced Hi4D dataset.

### 8. Method Performance and Ablation Studies

The proposed method for 4D instance segmentation demonstrates quantitative performance metrics on the Hi4D dataset: volumetric IoU (mIoU) of 0.989, Chamfer distance (C-L2) of 0.22 cm, point-to-surface distance (P2S) of 0.23 cm, and normal consistency (NC) of 0.945.

- **Ablation Study: Shape Refinement:** The inclusion of the shape refinement stage (Sec. 5.3) was found to be crucial. Without it, the method's performance decreased, with mIoU dropping to 0.982, C-L2 increasing to 0.36 cm, P2S to 0.37 cm, and NC to 0.934. Qualitatively, shape refinement enabled more accurate modeling of clothing details and contact-aware deformations that arise during physical interaction, which are not learned from individual non-contact scans.
- **Ablation Study: Alternating Optimization:** The alternating optimization scheme between pose and shape network refinement (Sec. 5.2, 5.3) was also evaluated. Optimizing these concurrently resulted in a drop in performance (mIoU 0.938, C-L2 0.48 cm, P2S 0.46 cm, NC 0.927). This suggests that the alternating approach helps in disambiguating the effects of pose and shape network updates, particularly in areas of heavy contact.
- **Ablation Study: Collision Loss:** The collision loss term (Eq. 8) was shown to be effective in preventing interpenetration between the segmented meshes. Its removal led to intersecting meshes in contact areas. Quantitatively, the collision loss reduced the average intersection volume between individual segmented meshes by 49.91%, from 11.62 × 10⁻⁴ m³ to 5.82 × 10⁻⁴ m³.
- **Comparison to SMPL+D Baseline:** The proposed method, which leverages personalized implicit avatars, quantitatively outperformed a straightforward SMPL+D tracking baseline across all evaluation metrics. This indicates the advantage of incorporating personalized prior knowledge for resolving instance ambiguities during close interaction.

### 9. Benchmark Baselines on Hi4D

The paper defines several vision benchmarks on the Hi4D dataset and evaluates state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating the dataset's challenging nature.

- **SMPL Estimation:**
 - **Monocular Setting:** State-of-the-art monocular methods (PARE, ROMP, BEV) exhibited relatively high Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Mean Volume Error (MVE), indicating that current approaches struggle with strong human-human occlusion. These methods also showed limitations in providing reasonable spatial arrangements and contact relations, as evidenced by the Contact Distance (CD) metric.
 - **Multi-view Setting:** The multi-view SMPL estimation method MVPose, evaluated with 4 and 8 views, achieved lower MPJPE and MVE compared to monocular methods. However, even with multiple views, issues such as heavy interpenetration and inaccuracies in 3D poses persisted, especially in contact areas.
- **Detailed Geometry Reconstruction:**
 - **Monocular Setting:** Single-person geometry reconstruction methods (PIFuHD, ICON) extended to handle multi-person scenarios showed a significant drop in performance (e.g., mIoU 0.761 for PIFuHD, 0.780 for ICON) compared to the proposed method. Qualitatively, these methods were not robust against human-human occlusions and failed to produce high-quality reconstructions.
 - **Multi-view Setting:** The multi-view method DMC, tested with 4 and 8 views, demonstrated better global geometry reconstruction but still exhibited artifacts, particularly in areas such as hands and feet. Quantitative results also indicated opportunities for improvement in this task.

In summary, the main findings highlight that the proposed method is effective in segmenting and tracking closely interacting humans by integrating personalized implicit avatars and a robust alternating optimization scheme. The baseline experiments underscore that the Hi4D dataset presents a challenging environment for current state-of-the-art methods in both SMPL estimation and detailed geometry reconstruction, indicating a need for further research advancements in close human interaction analysis.

### 10. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer vision through both a novel methodology and a specialized dataset, which collectively address a previously under-served area: the detailed analysis of close human-human physical interaction.

**Methodological Significance:**
The introduction of a method that leverages personalized neural implicit avatars combined with an alternating optimization scheme for pose and shape refinement marks a significant step towards robustly disentangling fused 4D scans of interacting subjects. Current multi-view systems typically struggle with this problem, producing merged geometries where individual instances cannot be separated or analyzed. By providing a mechanism to segment these complex, dynamic, and occluded interactions, the method enables granular, instance-level understanding of human bodies in contact. The inclusion of a collision loss term specifically designed to model contact-aware deformations is a practical advancement for achieving realistic reconstructions in such scenarios. This methodological advancement can serve as a foundational component for developing more sophisticated human interaction analysis systems.

**Dataset Significance:**
The creation of the Hi4D dataset (Humans interacting in 4D) is a substantial contribution to the research community. It fills a critical gap by providing a comprehensive collection of 4D textured scans of closely interacting humans, alongside rich, interaction-centric annotations. Key features of Hi4D that enhance its significance include:
- **High-fidelity dynamic textured geometry:** Essential for detailed surface reconstruction tasks.
- **Accurately registered parametric body models:** Facilitates pose and shape estimation research.
- **2D and 3D instance segmentation masks:** Directly supports instance-level analysis and segmentation tasks.
- **Vertex-level contact annotations:** Provides fine-grained ground truth for studying physical contact, which is often difficult to obtain and is lacking in many existing datasets. The sheer volume of contact frames (>6K frames with vertex-level contact) distinguishes Hi4D.
The diversity of subjects (20 pairs) and interaction types (hugging, dancing, sports) further increases the dataset's utility, ensuring a broad range of challenging scenarios for model development and evaluation.

**Potential Impact:**
The combined methodological and data contributions have several potential impacts:
- **Enabling Next-Generation AI Systems:** Hi4D provides the necessary data and benchmarks to spur the development of next-generation AI systems capable of perceiving, interpreting, and predicting complex human-human interactions in detail. This can unlock research into areas such as social signal processing, non-verbal communication analysis, and human behavior understanding.
- **Advancing Computer Graphics and Animation:** The high-quality 4D textured geometry and detailed deformation models can benefit computer graphics applications, enabling more realistic simulations and animations of human-human interaction, especially for virtual reality, augmented reality, and gaming.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** A deeper understanding of physical human interaction could inform the design of more robust and intuitive human-robot interaction systems, particularly in collaborative robotics where physical contact might occur.
- **Biomechanics and Ergonomics:** The detailed 3D body models and contact information could potentially be used in biomechanical studies or ergonomic assessments to analyze forces and deformations during physical interaction.
- **Benchmarking and Research Direction:** By providing established experimental protocols and baseline results for various tasks (SMPL estimation, detailed geometry reconstruction), Hi4D clearly outlines current limitations of state-of-the-art methods in close interaction scenarios, thereby guiding future research directions.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the current lack of explicit hand/facial expression modeling and computational efficiency, the fundamental contributions of the segmentation method and the Hi4D dataset lay a robust groundwork for subsequent advancements in the challenging domain of close human interaction analysis.
