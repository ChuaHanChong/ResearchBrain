# Aria Everyday Activities Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> The Aria Everyday Activities (AEA) Dataset introduces a comprehensive open-source collection of egocentric, multimodal data captured by Project Aria glasses, designed to advance personalized AI assistants by providing globally aligned 6DoF trajectories, 3D eye gaze, and speech transcriptions for diverse daily activities. This dataset offers 7.3 hours of recordings across five indoor locations, containing over one million image frames and high-quality machine perception outputs for robust contextual understanding.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric datasets lacked the diverse sensor modalities (e.g., spatial audio, precise 3D location) and robust time synchronization required for future AR devices and personalized AI.
- Current multimodal AI assistants primarily rely on public internet data or explicit user prompts, limiting their ability to leverage continuous, rich contextual data from a wearer's everyday environment.
- Developing AI systems capable of persistent space-time context reasoning and understanding wearer intent from cues like eye gaze necessitated a dataset representative of unobtrusive, always-on AR glasses.

## Method

- The Aria Everyday Activities (AEA) dataset was developed, an open, egocentric, and multimodal collection captured by Project Aria glasses in natural, consented indoor environments without specialized infrastructure.
- It provides comprehensive raw sensor data, including high-resolution RGB, monochrome scene cameras, eye-tracking videos, multiple IMUs, seven-channel spatial audio, magnetometers, and barometers.
- The dataset features high-quality Machine Perception Services (MPS) outputs, such as 1 kHz 6DoF closed-loop trajectories (globally aligned across recordings), semi-dense point clouds, 3D eye gaze vectors, and time-aligned speech transcriptions.

## Results

- The dataset comprises 143 daily activity sequences, totaling 7.3 hours of recordings and over one million image frames from five diverse indoor locations.
- Neural scene reconstruction using Gaussian Splatting achieved an average PSNR of 25.12 for single-recording scenarios and 22.35 for multi-recording aggregations, demonstrating robust static scene reconstruction from dynamic egocentric video.
- Exemplar applications successfully leveraged 3D eye gaze to prompt visual segmentation of observed objects and utilized time-aligned speech transcriptions to ground segmentation masks based on spoken content.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating raw multimodal sensor data with refined, globally aligned machine perception outputs (like 6DoF trajectories and 3D eye gaze) creates a robust foundation for 4D scene and wearer intent understanding for egocentric AI.
> - The dataset's design and collected data successfully enable advanced applications such as persistent 3D neural scene reconstruction and multimodal-prompted segmentation, showcasing its utility for complex contextual AI tasks.
> - A scalable methodology for collecting rich egocentric data in natural, diverse indoor environments, coupled with stringent privacy protocols including manual anonymization, is feasible for advancing AR/AI research.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the emerging field of egocentric AI and augmented reality (AR) research, addressing a perceived gap in existing datasets required for developing advanced, personalized AI assistants. The authors contend that the future ubiquity of AR devices and personal wearable AI will necessitate new technologies capable of leveraging continuous, multimodal egocentric data. Such devices, which capture "always-on" data from the wearer's point of view, are expected to enable AI systems to reason about space-time context persistently, understand wearer intent through cues like eye gaze, and integrate long-duration multimodal sensor streams.

Current multimodal AI assistants, such as GPT-4v and Gemini, primarily rely on publicly available internet data or conscious user prompts. The paper argues that these systems lack access to the rich contextual data necessary for truly personalized and contextualized AI.

The authors contrast their work with several categories of existing datasets:

- **Egocentric Datasets (e.g., EpicKitchens, Ego4D):** While foundational for egocentric video understanding, these datasets are primarily captured by traditional video cameras (e.g., GoPro head-mounted rigs). They often lack the diverse sensor modalities (spatial audio, inertial data) expected from future AR glasses, precise 3D location data, and robust time synchronization across modalities or devices. The authors highlight the difficulty in accurately and reliably estimating 3D information from these datasets due to rapid head motion. Some subsets of Ego4D rely on pre-scanned 3D environments or SfM tools, which can be prone to failure in uncurated egocentric scenarios. The Aria Everyday Activities (AEA) dataset aims to overcome these limitations by providing high-frequency 6DoF trajectories and calibrated eye gaze information.
- **Project Aria Datasets (e.g., Aria Pilot Dataset, Aria Digital Twin (ADT), Ego-Exo4D):** AEA is presented as an updated version of the Everyday Activities sequences from the initial Aria Pilot Dataset (APD). It differentiates itself from ADT, which features fully digitized environments with precise object and wearer poses, but requires extensive setup (e.g., motion capture systems, visual artifacts) that limits scalability. AEA, in contrast, is collected in natural, fully consented environments without such requirements, demonstrating a more scalable approach. Compared to Ego-Exo4D, which is a large multi-modal egocentric dataset focusing on procedural activities and constrained spaces, AEA focuses on longitudinal daily activities in home environments, providing multi-person synchronized egocentric recordings and multi-trajectory alignment within a single location.
- **Other 3D Multimodal Datasets (e.g., Assembly101, HoloAssist):** These datasets often use more cumbersome head-mounted devices (e.g., Oculus Quest, HoloLens) and typically focus on specific collaborative tasks or assembly activities. AEA emphasizes natural human activities in indoor settings using an unobtrusive, always-on form factor, which is more representative of future AR glasses.
- **Autonomous Driving Datasets (e.g., Argoverse, KITTI, Waymo Open Dataset):** While providing rich multimodal data, these datasets address different scenarios and sensor modalities, making them less directly applicable to personal AI in daily human environments.

In summary, the AEA dataset positions itself as a crucial resource for developing the next generation of personalized, contextual AI assistants by providing a comprehensive, spatially and temporally aligned multimodal dataset captured in a device form factor representative of future AR glasses, specifically addressing the limitations of existing datasets in terms of sensor richness, 3D context, and natural activity representation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce the Aria Everyday Activities (AEA) Dataset, an open, egocentric, and multimodal dataset designed to foster research in AI and AR technologies. The dataset is specifically tailored to address the requirements of future personalized and contextualized AI assistants that are envisioned to leverage continuous, rich contextual data from their wearers and environments.

The motivation for creating AEA stems from several key observations and perceived needs:

- **Advancing Personalized and Contextualized AI:** The authors believe that future AI assistants will act as an extension of the wearer's mind, necessitating a deep understanding of the user's immediate context. This includes the ability to leverage a comprehensive suite of sensor data (spatial audio, motion, long-duration video), reason about space-time context in a persistent global coordinate system, and infer wearer intent from cues like eye gaze and physical interactions.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Datasets:** Current egocentric datasets are identified as insufficient for this purpose. They often lack the full range of raw sensor data expected from future AR glasses (e.g., spatial audio, inertial data, precise 3D location), robust time synchronization across modalities and devices, and the contextual information needed to infer wearer actions or intentions.
- **Enabling Research with Future AR Device Capabilities:** The AEA dataset is specifically designed to provide data captured by Project Aria glasses, a sensor platform that offers always-on egocentric data in an unobtrusive form factor. This enables researchers to work with data modalities (e.g., high-resolution RGB, low-resolution monochrome for tracking, eye-tracking videos, multiple IMUs, spatial audio, magnetometer, barometer) that are representative of next-generation AR devices.
- **Providing State-of-the-Art Machine Perception Outputs:** A core motivation is to offer high-quality, reliable machine perception data (MPS outputs) as either contextual inputs for models or as pseudo-ground truth. This includes precise 6DoF trajectories, scene point clouds, per-frame 3D eye gaze vectors, and time-aligned speech transcriptions. The authors aim to provide the best possible 3D information through reprocessing with the most recent MPS.
- **Enhancing Data Accessibility and Tooling:** The project seeks to update data formats for consistency with new open-source tooling (Project Aria Tools), making the dataset easier to load, query, and utilize for a broader research community. This includes providing C++ and Python APIs and hands-on examples.
- **Illustrating Research Applications:** By demonstrating exemplar applications such as neural scene reconstruction and multimodal prompted segmentation, the authors intend to showcase the dataset's utility and inspire researchers to explore novel AI capabilities that leverage its rich contextual information.
- **Scalability and Naturalism:** The dataset aims to provide a model for easily scalable egocentric data capture in natural, fully consented indoor environments, focusing on longitudinal daily activities rather than highly controlled or specialized scenarios.

In essence, the key objective is to provide a comprehensive, high-quality, and open dataset that directly supports the development of advanced AI capable of understanding and interacting with users in their daily lives through AR-like devices, thereby accelerating research in multimodal contextual AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in creating the Aria Everyday Activities (AEA) dataset involved a structured approach to data collection, extensive post-processing with advanced machine perception services, and the development of open-source tools for data accessibility and application.

#### 3.1. Dataset Collection Process

- **Device:** Data was collected using Project Aria glasses, specifically utilizing "Profile 9," which includes all device sensors except WiFi, Bluetooth, and GNSS. This configuration aimed to capture a rich suite of sensors suitable for indoor environments.
- **Wearers and Locations:** The dataset features recordings by multiple wearers (1-2 per scenario) across five geographically diverse indoor locations.
- **Activity Design:** Wearers followed five general "guidance scripts" designed to represent longitudinal daily activities. These scripts outlined scenarios such as "A lazy morning before the party," "Catch up and have some fun," and "What do you want for dinner?". The scripts provided high-level prompts, allowing wearers to improvise and act naturally, aiming for open-vocabulary descriptions of egocentric activities (e.g., cooking, cleaning, exercising, dining, reading, playing games, social interaction).
- **Privacy:** A stringent privacy protocol was implemented. All recordings were captured in fully consented indoor environments devoid of personally identifiable information. Manual annotation and blurring of all human faces and licenses were performed across both RGB and monochrome scene camera streams, followed by multiple rounds of quality analysis to ensure anonymization. Recordings failing quality checks were excluded.

#### 3.2. Raw Sensor Data

The Project Aria glasses recorded the following multimodal sensor data:
- **RGB Camera:** Rolling shutter, 110° horizontal field of view (HFoV), 20 frames per second (fps), 1408 × 1408 resolution.
- **Monochrome Scene / SLAM Cameras:** Global shutter, 150° HFoV (left and right), 10 fps, 640 × 480 resolution. Used for visual SLAM and hand tracking.
- **Eye Tracking Cameras:** Two inward-facing monochrome cameras with IR illumination, 80° diagonal field of view (DFoV), 10 fps, 320 × 240 resolution.
- **Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs):** Two units, one on each side. Left IMU: 800 Hz, 4g (accelerometer), 500°/s (gyroscope). Right IMU: 1000 Hz, 8g (accelerometer), 1000°/s (gyroscope).
- **Microphones:** Seven microphones (five front-facing, two side-facing) capturing spatial audio with 7 channels at a 48 kHz sample rate.
- **Other Sensors:** Magnetometer (10 Hz, 0.1T resolution) and Barometer (50 Hz, 0.66Pa, 0.005°C resolution). GPS and WiFi sensors were not activated.

#### 3.3. Machine Perception Data (MPS)

This constitutes a significant update from the Aria Pilot Dataset, utilizing the most recent Project Aria Machine Perception Services:
- **3D Location Data:**
 - **Open-loop trajectory:** Real-time, high-frequency (1 kHz) 6DoF pose estimation for each recording, suitable for real-time applications.
 - **Close-loop trajectory:** Post-processed, high-frequency (1 kHz) 6DoF trajectory. Crucially, these trajectories share a consistent global coordinate system across all recordings within the same physical location, enabling spatial alignment of multi-person and multi-activity data.
 - **Semi-dense Points:** A partial 3D reconstruction of the static environment, generated from tracked feature points, aligned with the global reference frame. These "semi-dense observations" are paired with the closed-loop trajectories.
- **Eye Gaze Tracking:** Provided as a per-frame 3D ray anchored to the Central Pupil Frame, offering a 3D directional vector of the wearer's gaze, an enhancement over previous 2D reprojected eye gaze points.
- **Online Device Calibration:** Per-frame sensor extrinsic and intrinsic calibrations are provided.
- **Time Synchronization:** Multi-person activities were captured with sub-millisecond accuracy using SMPTE LTC timecode, ensuring precise temporal alignment across multiple devices.
- **Speech2text Transcription:** Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) was used to generate time-aligned text transcriptions of speech, each associated with a confidence rating.

#### 3.4. Dataset Tools

The Project Aria Tools were updated to support the AEA dataset. These open-source utilities facilitate:
- Loading and querying multimodal sensory data and paired machine perception data from single or multiple devices based on device timestamps.
- APIs for device and camera calibration, enabling 6DoF pose transformations and 2D/3D point projection/reprojection between sensors.
- A visualization tool for temporal scrubbing and exploring single or multi-person scenarios, displaying synchronized RGB streams, 3D trajectories, global point clouds, eye gaze vectors, projected eye gaze, and transcribed speech in a shared coordinate system.

#### 3.5. Exemplar Applications

To demonstrate the dataset's utility, two research applications were provided:
- **3D Neural Scene Reconstruction (using Gaussian Splatting):**
 - The official Gaussian Splatting implementation was adapted for long, multi-sequence egocentric trajectories.
 - Batch processing was used to handle memory constraints.
 - RGB images were rectified using Project Aria Tools.
 - The reconstruction was initialized using AEA's semi-dense points, and per-frame poses were derived from the high-frequency closed-loop trajectory.
 - For multi-recording reconstructions, frames and point clouds were concatenated.
 - Training ran for 30K iterations (single recording) and 100K iterations (multi-recording), employing consistent hyperparameters for pruning and densification.
- **Prompted Segmentation (using EfficientSAM):**
 - EfficientSAM was employed for segmentation due to its balance of accuracy and real-time performance.
 - **Eye-gazed prompted segmentation:** The 3D eye gaze vector was projected onto the 2D RGB image (assuming a virtual depth of 1.0 meter) to generate a 2D point prompt for EfficientSAM.
 - **Speech grounded segmentation:** GroundingDino was used to align time-aligned speech sentences with image regions, detecting objects mentioned. The resulting bounding box served as a box prompt for EfficientSAM to produce a segmentation mask.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The AEA dataset provides a comprehensive collection of egocentric multimodal data, complemented by machine perception outputs and demonstrated utility through exemplar applications.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics and Statistics

- The dataset comprises 143 daily activity sequences, totaling 7.3 hours of recording across five indoor locations.
- It contains over 1 million image frames, including 545,802 RGB color frames and over 545,802 monochrome scene camera frames.
- Recordings involved one to two wearers, following five pre-defined, high-level scripts that guided improvisation for a diverse range of everyday activities (e.g., cooking, cleaning, social interaction).
- The dataset's collection and processing strictly adhered to privacy commitments, including manual blurring of faces and licenses in all visual streams.

#### 4.2. Machine Perception Data Quality

The updated Machine Perception Services (MPS) significantly improved the derived data compared to previous releases:
- **3D Localization:** The provision of high-frequency (1 kHz) 6DoF closed-loop trajectories, globally aligned within each location, enables robust spatial understanding. These trajectories are paired with semi-dense point clouds, providing a partial 3D reconstruction of the static environment consistently across multiple recordings. The authors manually verified the correctness of these alignments.
- **Eye Gaze:** The output now includes a precise 3D directional vector for eye gaze, anchored to the Central Pupil Frame, offering more detailed information about wearer intention in 3D space than previous 2D projections.
- **Time Synchronization:** Multi-person activities benefit from sub-millisecond accurate time synchronization using SMPTE LTC timecode, ensuring precise temporal alignment for complex interactions.

#### 4.3. Exemplar Application Results: 3D Neural Scene Reconstruction (Gaussian Splatting)

- **Reconstruction Capability:** The Gaussian Splatting method successfully converged for individual recordings, producing good quality reconstructions of static areas, even in the presence of dynamic egocentric motion. Blurry areas in renderings indicated dynamic or unobserved regions.
- **Multi-recording Aggregation:** When applied to aggregated data from all recordings in a shared location, the method effectively reconstructed well-observed living areas. This demonstrated the dataset's utility for persistent scene understanding, with dynamic motions being implicitly handled as outliers, preventing their inclusion in the static scene representation. For instance, Location 2, with 2.3 hours of data and nearly 170K RGB frames, resulted in a reconstruction with approximately 627K Gaussian points.
- **Quantitative Evaluation (PSNR):**
 - For single-recording (SR) scenarios, the average PSNR across all locations was 25.12, with individual locations ranging from 23.70 to 26.27.
 - For multi-recording (MR) scenarios, the average PSNR was 22.35, with individual locations ranging from 21.83 to 24.18. The slight decrease in PSNR for multi-recording setups is expected due to the challenges of aggregating diverse observations with potential dynamic elements, yet the results demonstrate effective scene reconstruction from complex, varied inputs.

#### 4.3.2. Prompted Segmentation

- **Eye-gaze Prompted Segmentation:** This application demonstrated the ability to use the projected 2D eye gaze as a point prompt for EfficientSAM. This effectively highlighted objects the wearer was looking at, showcasing how wearer attention can directly inform visual understanding tasks.
- **Speech Grounded Segmentation:** By integrating time-aligned speech transcription with GroundingDino, the system could detect objects mentioned in speech and use their bounding boxes as prompts for EfficientSAM to generate segmentation masks. This illustrates the potential for multimodal input, specifically speech, to ground visual segmentation in a contextual manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Aria Everyday Activities (AEA) Dataset represents a significant contribution to the fields of augmented reality, egocentric AI, and multimodal machine learning. Its impact is anticipated across several dimensions:

- **Foundation for Next-Generation AR/AI:** The dataset provides a critical resource for developing AI systems that can operate effectively in future AR and personal wearable AI devices. By offering data from an unobtrusive, always-on form factor, it simulates the real-world conditions under which these AI assistants will need to function, fostering research into truly personalized and contextualized intelligence.
- **Enabling Comprehensive Contextual Understanding:** AEA addresses a key limitation of existing datasets by integrating a rich array of raw multimodal sensor data (visual, inertial, auditory) with high-quality, spatially and temporally aligned machine perception outputs (6DoF trajectories, 3D eye gaze, scene point clouds, speech transcription). This comprehensive context is crucial for AI models to reason about the wearer's environment, actions, and intentions in a persistent 4D space.
- **Advancing Egocentric 3D Perception:** The dataset's precise 6DoF trajectories and semi-dense point clouds, aligned across multiple wearers and activities within a shared global coordinate system, provide robust "pseudo ground truth" for challenging egocentric 3D understanding tasks. This is particularly valuable for developing algorithms that can perform robust scene reconstruction, object localization, and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) from highly dynamic and uncurated egocentric viewpoints.
- **Facilitating Multimodal Human-AI Interaction Research:** The inclusion of 3D eye gaze and time-aligned speech transcriptions opens new avenues for research into intuitive human-AI interaction. The exemplar applications demonstrated how these modalities can be used as prompts for visual tasks like segmentation, paving the way for AI assistants that understand user intent not just through explicit commands, but also through implicit cues like gaze and speech context.
- **Scalability for Real-World Scenarios:** Unlike some highly controlled datasets that require specialized environments, AEA was collected in natural indoor settings without additional infrastructure. This approach demonstrates a scalable method for gathering extensive egocentric data, which is essential for training robust AI models that generalize to diverse real-world conditions.
- **Lowering Barriers to Entry:** By providing an open-source dataset and updated Project Aria Tools, the authors aim to make this complex multimodal data more accessible and usable for a wider research community. The included code examples for applications such as neural reconstruction and prompted segmentation serve as practical starting points, encouraging broader experimentation and innovation.
- **Inspiring New Research Directions:** The dataset is poised to inspire novel research in areas such as robust 3D reconstruction from dynamic egocentric videos, multimodal activity understanding, intent prediction from gaze and speech, and the development of foundation models that can integrate diverse sensory inputs for comprehensive environmental and user context.

In summary, the AEA dataset offers a vital, state-of-the-art resource that addresses the fundamental data requirements for building the next generation of intelligent, context-aware AI assistants embedded within future AR and personal wearable devices.
