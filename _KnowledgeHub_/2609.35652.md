# MM-ABC: Towards Generalist Mobile Manipulation via Seeing, Coordinating and Imagining

> [!summary] Summary
> MM-ABC is a mobile manipulation foundation model on a Qwen3-VL-4B backbone. It injects sparse multi-level VLM features into MM-APT, a two-stream action transformer that coordinates arm and base through masked joint attention and clean-action prediction. A training-only branch predicts future VGGT-Omega geometry. After pretraining on 5,000+ hours from 17 embodiments, it reaches 61.2% on RoboCasa365 and 83% real-world success.

## Problem

- Explicit 3D reconstruction and dense future generation improve spatial perception for mobile manipulation, but they add substantial modeling and inference cost at deployment time.
- Recent methods split mobility and manipulation into separate action streams, but splitting alone does not ensure the streams exchange information while keeping their distinct control semantics.
- Flow-based policies default to velocity prediction, which forces a finite-width multi-stream expert to carry and cancel high-dimensional noise instead of mapping directly onto the low-dimensional action manifold.

## Method

- Sparse **DeepStack** conditioning: hidden states from VLM layers 11, 19 and 27 enter the first three expert blocks as zero-initialized gated residual updates on top of the final-layer context.
- **MM-APT**: separate manipulation and body streams over a unified 80D ==masked action space== share ==joint attention==. Near-term tokens cannot see far-future tokens, and the network predicts clean action chunks.
- Training-only **World Expert**: future queries, isolated from action tokens, predict frozen ==VGGT-Omega== geometry features 32 and 64 steps ahead through ==cosine alignment==, and the branch is removed at inference.

## Results

- RoboCasa365 task-weighted average of **61.2%** versus **54.2%** for ABot-M0.5. EBench **44.71%** versus **41.41%** for pi0.5. Best means on all three ManiSkill-HAB suites: **86.4%**, **70.2%** and **65.6%**.
- LIBERO average of **99.1%**. The same LIBERO-only policy reaches **82.8%** on LIBERO-Plus, beating methods trained on perturbed demonstrations, such as OpenVLA-OFT+ at **79.6%**.
- Composite-seen ablations from **32.8%**: velocity prediction drops to **29.2%**, no future supervision to **24.9%**, last-layer conditioning to **25.6%**. Real-world mean is **83%** versus **71%** for pi0.5.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Clean-action and velocity targets are algebraically equivalent but behave differently: clean targets avoid carrying noise through the network, giving 23x lower endpoint variance at high noise.
> - Predicting future geometry can work purely as auxiliary training supervision. A removable branch keeps much of a world model's representational benefit without paying its inference cost.
> - Splitting arm and base into separate streams is not enough. Structured cross-stream attention with one-way near-to-far visibility lets the subsystems coordinate while keeping immediate actions grounded.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

MM-ABC starts from a practical constraint. A fixed-base robot cannot reach beyond its workspace. Adding a mobile base makes reachability controllable, but it also forces the robot to perceive from changing viewpoints and to coordinate large-scale base motion with precise arm actions. In the Birthday Party Setup task, the robot picks up a cake from the table ahead and carries it to a decorated table on the left. There, it passes a candle between its hands and inserts it into the cake.

MM-ABC gives the arm and the base distinct action pathways that communicate with each other. It feeds them visual-language information from several levels of a vision model. It also uses predicted future geometry as a training signal, which is removed before deployment. In real-world evaluation, MM-ABC averages 83% across five tasks, versus 71% for π₀.₅. Each method receives the same 200 demonstrations per task and is evaluated on 20 trials per task. A trial counts only if every task step is completed.

Birthday Party Setup (3×): the robot picks up a cake from the table ahead, carries it to a decorated table on the left, passes a candle between its hands and inserts it into the cake, then moves beside the table and turns on a speaker.

### 2. A workspace the robot can change

Base motion determines reach and viewpoint, while arm motion handles local interaction. The two are coupled despite having different roles. This is manipulation over a workspace the robot changes, not simply navigation followed by manipulation.

MM-ABC has three parts:

- sparse multilevel conditioning from a vision-language model (VLM);
- separate but communicating manipulation and body-action streams;
- a future-geometry prediction branch used only during training.

Future observations are targets for this auxiliary branch, not inputs to the action policy. The branch and its frozen geometric teacher are removed at inference.

Separating arm and base actions is not new. [[2607.00678|ABot-M0.5]] uses distinct mobility and manipulation action subspaces, and [MoPA](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.12081) derives separate perceptual query streams for mobility and manipulation. MM-ABC's stated distinction is a different combination: shared read-only visual-language context, specialized but communicating action streams, and future geometry used as auxiliary training supervision rather than as a deployed world model.

MM-ABC architecture. Current image and language context and sparse VLM features feed MM-APT, alongside a separate training-only future expert and the VGGT-Omega teacher. Manipulation actions (M₁/M₂), body actions (B₁/B₂), and future queries (F₁/F₂) use joint masked attention. Far actions can read near actions, near actions cannot read far actions, and future queries are isolated from action tokens.

### 3. Predicting the action endpoint, supervising the future

Clean-action prediction estimates the final action chunk directly. Velocity prediction estimates the direction of change along the noise-to-action path. The two are algebraically convertible, but the paper argues that they place different representational demands on a finite-width model.

The paper tests both choices in a from-scratch ablation on RoboCasa365's 16 composite-seen tasks. The full model's unweighted mean success rate is 32.8%. Replacing clean-action prediction with velocity prediction, while retaining the future branch, lowers it to 29.2%. Removing future supervision while retaining clean-action prediction lowers it to 24.9%.

These are component comparisons, not pretrained benchmark scores. All variants omit robot-data-mixture pretraining and use 120,000 training steps with batch size 64. They support the value of future supervision in this setup, not a claim that future prediction is necessary for every policy.

The real-world advantage is also not uniform: MM-ABC and π₀.₅ both succeed in 75% of trials on Industrial Parts Organization. The evidence supports the complete design and these particular component contrasts. It does not show that any one element alone explains all benchmark or real-world gains.

### 4. A changing workspace

A mobile base makes new reaches possible, but it also changes the views and geometry that the arm and base must jointly interpret. MM-ABC presents seeing, anticipating through training, and coordinating action as connected parts of mobile manipulation, rather than simply adding a base to a fixed-workspace policy.
