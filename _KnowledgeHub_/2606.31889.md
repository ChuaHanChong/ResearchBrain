# Improving path-tracking performance of an articulated tractor-trailer system using a non-linear kinematic model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at sinc(i), FICH-UNL/CONICET developed a non-linear kinematic model for articulated tractor-trailer systems, which integrates both front-wheel steering and a central articulated joint, and applied it within an NMPC framework to improve the path-tracking accuracy of the towed trailer. This approach significantly reduced trailer lateral deviation to less than 12 cm during headland turns, a substantial improvement over the 3.8-meter deviation observed when only the tractor's position was controlled.

## Problem

- Existing autonomous guidance systems for agricultural vehicles often fail to precisely control the path of towed implements, leading to significant deviations (e.g., gaps and overlaps) during turns.
- Many mathematical models for agricultural machinery either focus on simple front-steering tractors or neglect the complex kinematics of towed implements, especially for articulated vehicles with dual steering mechanisms.
- Traditional control strategies struggle with the highly non-linear dynamics and multiple inputs/outputs inherent in articulated tractor-trailer systems, particularly when ensuring the trailer accurately follows a desired path.

## Method

- A novel ==non-linear kinematic model== for an articulated tractor-trailer system was derived, incorporating both front-wheel steering and a central articulated joint, presenting a generalized representation for various vehicle configurations.
- This comprehensive kinematic model was integrated into a ==Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC)== framework, which explicitly minimizes a ==cost function== to keep the trailer on the desired path while adhering to physical constraints.
- The NMPC controller, utilizing the detailed model, was configured to directly control the trailer's ==xy-position==, rather than just the tractor's, and validated through realistic simulations in Gazebo using ROS.

## Results

- The proposed method, directly controlling the trailer's position with the new kinematic model, reduced the trailer's maximum lateral path deviation during headland turns to less than **12 cm**.
- In contrast, the baseline scenario, where only the tractor's front block was controlled, resulted in a maximum lateral deviation of approximately **3.8 meters** for the trailer during headland turns.
- Both the articulation angle and front steering angle demonstrated coordinated, dynamic adjustments in the proposed scenario, enabling the trailer to follow the desired path with high accuracy on both straight segments and turns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Controlling only the tractor's position leads to substantial lateral deviation of the towed trailer, especially in headland turns, confirming that dedicated trailer control is essential for precision agriculture.
> - A generalized non-linear kinematic model can accurately represent complex articulated tractor-trailer systems with dual steering, offering a versatile foundation for control design.
> - Non-linear Model Predictive Control effectively manages the complex, non-linear dynamics and operational constraints of articulated agricultural vehicles, enabling precise path tracking for the trailer itself.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of precision agriculture (PA), which seeks to integrate advanced technology with agricultural machinery to enhance efficiency and productivity. Historically, agricultural tractors have undergone continuous improvements, leading to reduced human intervention in many operations. However, autonomous guidance remains a significant challenge, particularly for vehicles with towed implements.

Existing autonomous guidance systems commonly focus on controlling the trajectory of the primary vehicle (tractor) to follow a desired path. When an agricultural implement (trailer) is towed, it often deviates from the tractor's path, especially during turns, leading to inaccuracies such as gaps and overlaps in field operations. Several previous works have attempted to address this:
- Some approaches involve independent steering controllers for both the tractor and the implement (e.g., Pickett et al., 2016; Merx and Germann, 2017). These systems often account for implement error as an offset, but may not fully integrate the complex interaction dynamics between the tractor and the implement, potentially leading to residual errors.
- Other systems utilize actuators to enable lateral movement of the implement relative to the tractor (e.g., Kremmer et al., 2020). However, controllers based on simpler algorithms like PID might struggle to adapt to varying road conditions or physical constraints.

Modern control techniques, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC), have emerged as suitable methods for managing the highly non-linear dynamics and multiple inputs/outputs inherent in agricultural vehicles with towed implements (Rawlings et al., 2017). NMPC applications for tractor-implement systems have been explored to control the lateral position of towed implements (Backman et al., 2012) or achieve accurate trajectory tracking under variable soil conditions (Kayacan et al., 2014). However, these studies typically focus on tractors with traditional front-wheel steering mechanisms and do not incorporate the complexities of articulated vehicles.

Furthermore, tractors can employ different steering mechanisms: traditional front-wheel steering or a central articulated joint. Some advanced designs combine both mechanisms. The performance of MPC-based controllers relies significantly on the accuracy of the underlying mathematical model describing the system's behavior. Kinematic models, which deal with speeds and angles while disregarding inertia, are computationally less intensive and have been shown to be sufficiently accurate for vehicles operating at low accelerations (Werner et al., 2012; Kong et al., 2015; Tang et al., 2020).

Despite the body of literature on agricultural machinery modeling, a gap exists:
- Many models focus on simple front-steering tractors, often neglecting the kinematics of towed implements (Farmer, 2008; Zhang and Wei, 2017; Nayl, 2013).
- Models that do consider vehicle-and-implement systems are frequently restricted to front-steering tractors (Kayacan et al., 2016; Yue et al., 2018).
- Models for articulated vehicles exist, but they often do not incorporate the coupling of an implement or front-wheel steering capabilities (Nayl et al., 2012; 2015).

This research aims to bridge this gap by proposing a novel, generalized kinematic model for an articulated tractor-trailer system that includes both front-wheel steering and a central articulated joint. The objective is to demonstrate how this comprehensive model, when integrated into an NMPC framework, can enhance the path-tracking performance, specifically of the towed trailer, in precision agriculture applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a novel non-linear mathematical kinematic model for an articulated tractor-trailer system that incorporates dual steering mechanisms: traditional front-wheel steering and a central articulated joint.

A key motivation behind this objective is the recognized limitation of existing models and control strategies in achieving precise path tracking for the *towed implement* in articulated agricultural vehicles. In precision agriculture tasks such as seeding, spraying, and harvesting, the accurate positioning of the implement is critical to avoid gaps and overlaps, which can lead to inefficient resource use and reduced crop yields. While autonomous guidance systems for tractors exist, they often prioritize the tractor's trajectory, neglecting the complex dynamics of the trailer, especially during turns. The paper highlights that in curves and headland turns, the trailer tends to follow a different path than the tractor, necessitating a control strategy that directly monitors and adjusts the trailer's position.

The specific motivations for pursuing this work include:
- **Addressing the "Trailer Deviation" Problem:** Previous research has shown that controlling only the tractor's position can lead to significant deviations of the trailer in turns, even if the tractor itself follows the path accurately. This work is motivated by the need to mitigate these deviations.
- **Leveraging Dual Steering Mechanisms:** Articulated tractors with both front-wheel and central articulation steering offer enhanced maneuverability, particularly beneficial in small fields or complex headland turns characteristic of precision agriculture. Developing a model that accurately represents these dual mechanisms is crucial for harnessing their full potential.
- **Improving Precision Agriculture Operations:** By enabling more accurate path tracking of the implement, the research aims to reduce waste and improve the effectiveness of operations like seeding and spraying, thereby contributing to the goals of precision agriculture.
- **Utilizing Advanced Control Techniques:** The inherent non-linear dynamics and multiple inputs/outputs of an articulated tractor-trailer system make it suitable for advanced control methods like NMPC. The authors are motivated to demonstrate how NMPC, when coupled with a precise and comprehensive model, can effectively manage these complexities and operational constraints.
- **Generalizing Existing Models:** The proposed model is intended to be a generalization that can encompass simpler articulated or front-steering vehicle models, offering a more versatile tool for researchers and practitioners.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Operating at low speeds, common in many agricultural tasks, allows for the use of kinematic models, which are computationally less demanding than dynamic models, facilitating practical implementation on embedded systems.

Ultimately, the central motivation is to improve the autonomous navigation capabilities of articulated agricultural vehicles with towed implements, specifically by enabling direct and accurate control over the trailer's trajectory through a sophisticated kinematic model integrated within an NMPC framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research methodology involved the derivation of a novel non-linear kinematic model for an articulated tractor-trailer system, its integration into a Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework, and validation through realistic simulations.

**Model Derivation:**
The first step was to develop a mathematical model of an articulated tractor-trailer system featuring both front-wheel steering and a central articulated joint.
- **System Description:** The system was conceptualized with a tractor composed of a rear and front block, connected by an articulation joint, and towing a trailer. Key geometric parameters such as distances between axles and joints ($L_r, L_f, d_1, d_2$), along with various yaw and steering angles ($\theta_t, \theta_r, \gamma, \phi$), were defined.
- **Coordinate Frames:** Five coordinate frames were established: a global world frame (w), and frames aligned with the trailer (t), tractor's rear block (r), tractor's front block (f), and front wheels (s).
- **Geometric Relationships:** Standard rotation matrices were used to express the positions of the trailer's axle center, tractor's rear axle center, and tractor's front axle center in the global frame. Time derivatives of these position vectors were then derived.
- **Non-holonomic Constraints:** The "bicycle model" simplification was applied, assuming no lateral slip and allowing each block (trailer, rear, front) to move only in the direction orthogonal to its axle. This simplification yields expressions relating the linear velocities ($v_t, v_r, v_f$) to the angular rates and orientations.
- **State and Control Input Definitions:** The system's state vector was defined as $x = [x_t, y_t, \theta_r, \theta_t, \gamma, \phi]^T$, encompassing the trailer's Cartesian position, the yaw angles of the tractor's rear block and the trailer, the articulation angle, and the front steering angle. The control input vector was defined as $u = [v_f, \omega_1, \omega_2]^T$, representing the forward speed of the front block, the angular velocity of the articulation joint, and the angular velocity of the front steering angle, respectively.
- **Kinematic Equations:** By combining the geometric relationships, non-holonomic constraints, and definitions of states and control inputs, a set of coupled non-linear differential equations was derived, representing the complete kinematic model in the form $\dot{x} = F(x, u)$. The paper highlights that this model is a generalization of more limited models found in the literature.

**Control Strategy - Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC):**
- **Discretization:** For NMPC implementation, the continuous-time kinematic model was discretized using the collocation method with three collocation points. This approach aims to provide high accuracy at a relatively low computational cost, transforming $\dot{x} = F(x, u)$ into its discrete-time equivalent $x_{k+1} = \hat{F}(x_k, u_k)$.
- **Cost Function:** A quadratic cost function, $J(k)$, was formulated to be minimized over a fixed prediction horizon $N$. The cost function includes three main terms:
 1. A term penalizing the squared error between the predicted trailer's xy-position ($x_{\{x_t,y_t\}_{k+j|k}}$) and the desired reference path ($r_{x_{\{x_t,y_t\}_{k+j|k}}}$), weighted by matrix $Q$.
 2. A term penalizing the magnitude of the control inputs ($u_{k+j|k}$), weighted by matrix $R$, to ensure smooth and feasible control actions.
 3. A terminal cost term at the end of the prediction horizon, penalizing the error between the predicted and reference trailer position ($x_{\{x_t,y_t\}_{k+N|k}} - r_{x_{\{x_t,y_t\}_{k+N|k}}}$), weighted by matrix $P$, which also contributes to stability.
- **Constraints:** To ensure realistic and safe operation, physical constraints were imposed on the system's state variables and control inputs. These included limits on the articulation angle ($\gamma$), front steering angle ($\phi$), front block speed ($v_f$), and their respective rates of change ($\omega_1, \omega_2, \Delta v_f, \Delta \omega_1, \Delta \omega_2$). NMPC inherently handles these constraints during the optimization process.
- **Receding Horizon Principle:** In line with NMPC, only the first computed control input from the optimal sequence is applied to the system. The optimization problem is then re-solved at the next sampling instant with updated information in a receding horizon fashion, allowing for compensation of disturbances and unmodeled dynamics.

**Simulation and Implementation:**
- **Software Tools:** The NMPC controller was implemented using Python, the CasADi symbolic framework for algorithmic differentiation and optimization, and the "Nonlinear Model Predictive Control Tools for CasADi" toolbox, alongside the HSL Mathematical Software Library.
- **Simulation Environment:** The Robot Operating System (ROS) framework was utilized to describe the articulated tractor-trailer system in a machine-readable Unified Robot Description Format (URDF). This URDF model was then integrated with the Gazebo simulator, a realistic physics engine, to perform simulations. Gazebo's capabilities were used to model physical properties such as mass, inertia, and wheel friction.
- **Joint Configuration in Gazebo:** The vehicle's kinematics in Gazebo was configured using:
 - Velocity joints for the wheels to control speed.
 - Position joints for the central articulation and front steering angles, modeled with servomotor-like actuators via hardware interfaces.
- **Experimental Scenarios:** Two scenarios were designed for comparative analysis:
 1. **Baseline Scenario:** The NMPC controller was configured to control the xy-position of the *tractor's front block*, deliberately excluding knowledge of the trailer's kinematics.
 2. **Proposed Scenario:** The NMPC controller incorporated the newly developed articulated tractor-trailer kinematic model, directly controlling the xy-position of the *trailer*.
Both scenarios used a common agricultural path (a 1600 m² field requiring straight paths and headland turns) to evaluate path-tracking performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated the performance of the proposed non-linear kinematic model for an articulated tractor-trailer system in path-tracking tasks, comparing a baseline control strategy (controlling the tractor's front block) with the proposed approach (controlling the trailer's position).

**Kinematic Model Development:**
The research successfully derived a novel non-linear kinematic model for an articulated tractor-trailer system that incorporates both front-wheel steering and a central articulated joint. This model was presented as a generalization of simpler models, capable of describing various vehicle configurations by restricting specific steering mechanisms. The model defines the system's states by the trailer's position ($x_t, y_t$), the yaw angles of the tractor's rear block and the trailer ($\theta_r, \theta_t$), the articulation angle ($\gamma$), and the front steering angle ($\phi$). Control inputs include the front block speed ($v_f$) and the angular velocities of the articulation and front steering angles ($\omega_1, \omega_2$).

**Simulation Results (Comparison of Scenarios):**
The simulations were conducted in a Gazebo environment using ROS, with NMPC controlling the vehicle to follow a predefined path in a simulated agricultural field.

1. **First Example: Controlling Tractor's Front Block Position (Baseline)**
 - When the NMPC controller was configured to track the reference path using the tractor's front block position, both the tractor's front block and the trailer followed straight paths accurately.
 - However, during headland turns, a significant deviation was observed for the trailer. The tractor's front block maintained the desired path, but the trailer described a turn with a notably smaller radius than the reference, indicating a lateral offset.
 - Analysis of the error in the y-axis ($e_y$) showed that while this error was very small along straight paths, it reached a maximum deviation of approximately 3.8 meters during headland turns. The error in the x-axis ($e_x$) varied with acceleration/deceleration but demonstrated that the trailer was correctly aligned with straight paths at both the end and beginning of each infield row, despite the turn deviations.

2. **Second Example: Controlling Trailer's Position (Proposed Model)**
 - When the NMPC controller incorporated the proposed articulated tractor-trailer kinematic model and was set to directly control the trailer's xy-position, a substantial improvement in path tracking was observed.
 - The trailer followed the desired path with high accuracy along both straight segments and headland turns.
 - The y-axis error ($e_y$) was significantly reduced. It remained below 1 cm on straight paths and was less than 12 cm during headland turns. This represents a substantial reduction compared to the 3.8-meter error in the baseline scenario.
 - The x-axis error ($e_x$) showed an initial transient but decreased to below 16 cm during steady-state path following, indicating precise longitudinal tracking.
 - The articulation angle ($\gamma$) and front steering angle ($\phi$) exhibited coordinated movements. Both angles were approximately zero on straight paths, indicating stable forward motion. During turns, they dynamically adjusted to facilitate accurate path following.
 - Control inputs (front block speed $v_f$, angular velocities $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$) demonstrated the NMPC's ability to generate appropriate commands. The speed varied from 0 to 2 m/s, adjusting for turns, while angular velocities showed consistent patterns with angle changes. The paper notes that these control inputs displayed "violent vibration," which might require further smoothing for practical implementations, possibly by integrating them into the state vector as first-order differential equations.

**Computational Aspects:**
The simulations were performed on a standard Intel i7 processor, suggesting that the NMPC implementation with the kinematic model is computationally feasible for research and development purposes. The use of CasADi and MPCTools facilitated the optimization process.

In summary, the main finding is that by embedding the proposed non-linear kinematic model of an articulated tractor-trailer system into an NMPC controller, the path-tracking performance of the *trailer* is substantially improved, particularly in challenging headland turns, significantly reducing lateral deviation errors compared to strategies that only control the tractor's position.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous agricultural machinery and robotic systems, particularly in precision agriculture. The primary significance lies in the development of a comprehensive and generalized non-linear kinematic model for an articulated tractor-trailer system and its effective integration within a Non-linear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Enhanced Precision in Agricultural Operations:** The most direct impact is the substantial improvement in the path-tracking accuracy of towed implements. By enabling the trailer itself to follow a precise desired trajectory, even through challenging headland turns, the system can significantly reduce overlaps and gaps during operations such as seeding, spraying, and harvesting. This leads to more efficient resource utilization (e.g., seeds, fertilizers, pesticides), reduced environmental impact, and potentially higher crop yields.
- **Improved Efficiency and Reduced Waste:** The ability to precisely control the implement's path minimizes redundant passes or missed areas, which translates into lower fuel consumption, less wear and tear on machinery, and optimized use of agricultural inputs.
- **Advancement of Autonomous Agricultural Vehicles:** This work contributes to the broader goal of fully autonomous agricultural operations by addressing a critical challenge: the accurate control of multi-body, non-linear vehicle systems. It provides a robust kinematic modeling approach that can serve as a foundation for future developments in autonomous tractors and specialized agricultural robots.
- **Generalizable Modeling Approach:** The proposed kinematic model is highlighted as a generalization that encompasses simpler tractor-trailer configurations. This adaptability makes it a versatile tool for modeling various articulated agricultural vehicles, potentially simplifying the development of control systems for diverse machinery.
- **Leveraging Advanced Control Techniques:** The successful application of NMPC demonstrates its practical utility for complex, non-linear systems with operational constraints. This showcases NMPC as a viable and effective control strategy for managing the intricate dynamics of articulated agricultural vehicles, promoting its adoption in real-world applications.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays the groundwork for several future research directions, such as incorporating dynamic characteristics, considering the effects of non-flat terrains (pitch and roll angles), and further refining control input smoothing. Addressing these complexities will lead to even more robust and adaptable autonomous systems.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The availability of the code as open-source fosters collaboration and accelerates further research and development within the academic and industrial communities. This can lead to broader adoption and innovation in agricultural automation.
- **Reduced Operator Burden and Enhanced Safety:** By automating precise path tracking, the system reduces the cognitive load and continuous steering adjustments required from human operators. This can improve operator comfort, reduce fatigue, and potentially enhance safety in agricultural environments.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the state-of-the-art in autonomous guidance for articulated agricultural vehicles with towed implements. By providing a novel, precise kinematic model and demonstrating its efficacy with NMPC, the study offers a tangible path toward more efficient, sustainable, and automated precision agriculture practices.
