# VLA-R1: Enhancing Reasoning in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-R1, developed by GigaAI, CASIA, and Tsinghua University, introduces a two-stage training paradigm combining Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data synthesis with Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to enhance reasoning and action execution in Vision-Language-Action models. This approach achieved state-of-the-art results, improving affordance perception by 17.78% and trajectory prediction by 17.25% on the ShareRobot dataset, and demonstrated robust generalization across simulation and real-world robotics tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack explicit, step-by-step reasoning, leading to failures in complex tasks with visual ambiguity or multiple valid options.
- Current VLA post-training methods, primarily supervised fine-tuning, use simplistic reward designs that are insufficient for jointly optimizing both reasoning quality and execution accuracy.
- Models struggle with out-of-distribution conditions and real-world deployments due to limited generalization capacity without explicit reasoning guidance.

## Method

- Developed a VLA-CoT data engine to automatically generate 13,000 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations from a large language model, providing explicit step-by-step reasoning aligned with affordance and trajectory labels.
- Employed a two-stage training paradigm: supervised fine-tuning on the VLA-CoT-13K dataset for structured reasoning, followed by Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- Designed three specialized verifiable rewards for RLVR
					to jointly optimize perception, reasoning, and action: GIoU for affordance, Angle-Length Augmented Fréchet distance for trajectory, and a binary output format reward.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the ShareRobot dataset, with a **17.78%** improvement in affordance perception IoU (36.51) and a **17.25%** reduction in trajectory average distance (91.74) compared to previous baselines.
- Demonstrated robust cross-domain generalization, maintaining superior performance on out-of-domain datasets and achieving average success rates of **55%** for affordance and **70%** for trajectory in simulation.
- Validated practical viability on real-robot platforms, attaining an average success rate of **62.5%** for affordance perception and **75%** for trajectory execution across various tabletop manipulation scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision, derived from synthetically generated data, effectively teaches VLA models to articulate intermediate reasoning steps, improving task decomposition and visual grounding.
> - Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), guided by specific geometric and format-based rewards, significantly refines both the quality of the reasoning chain and the accuracy of the resulting actions.
> - The complementary combination of CoT-based structured reasoning and RLVR-based execution refinement leads to superior generalization and robustness across diverse robotic manipulation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI is rapidly evolving, with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models at its forefront. VLAs aim to create intelligent agents capable of understanding natural language instructions, perceiving their environment, and executing physical actions in the real world. These models leverage large-scale pre-training on internet-scale image-text data to learn robust cross-modal alignments and open-vocabulary semantics, which are then grounded into the action space through multitask manipulation data. This approach has shown remarkable promise in enabling robots to perform tasks with strong cross-task and cross-scene generalization, moving beyond traditional modular pipelines or state-driven policies.

Prior representative works in this area include VoxPoser for zero-shot trajectory planning, ManipLVM-R1, and RoboBrain, which integrate affordance perception and pose estimation. More recently, works like OpenVLA and RoboMamba have explored fine-grained Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for performance gains.

However, a critical limitation across most existing VLA models is their struggle with explicit, step-by-step reasoning. These models often directly generate final actions without transparently considering intermediate logical steps such as affordance constraints (e.g., where to grasp an object), geometric relations, or optimal container selection. This lack of explicit reasoning leads to failures in complex scenarios involving visual ambiguity (e.g., color similarity, duplicate instances) or multiple valid options. Furthermore, the post-training pipelines for VLAs often rely solely on supervised fine-tuning, with reward designs that are typically single-objective and insufficient for systematically optimizing both reasoning quality and execution accuracy, particularly under out-of-distribution conditions or in real-world deployments.

This paper directly addresses these challenges by extending the "R1" framework, a line of research focused on Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to enhance reasoning in various AI models. Previously, Vision-R1, LMM-R1, and VLM-R1 applied RLVR to vision-language models for tasks like mathematical reasoning, multimodal understanding, and visual grounding, respectively, often achieving performance comparable to much larger models. ManipLVM-R1 extended this to robotic manipulation, but VLA-R1 pushes further by integrating a new level of explicit reasoning supervision and a more comprehensive RLVR strategy.

VLA-R1 distinguishes itself by simultaneously advancing data-level Chain-of-Thought supervision and optimization-level reward alignment, thereby bridging the gap between reasoning transparency and successful action execution in embodied AI. It builds upon the successes of prior VLA and RLVR works, specifically ManipLVM-R1, but introduces novel verifiable reward designs and a specialized CoT dataset generation engine to target the explicit reasoning deficiencies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of VLA-R1 is to **enhance the reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**, enabling them to perform complex robotic manipulation tasks with greater robustness, accuracy, and generalization. This overarching goal is driven by two key motivations derived from the limitations of current VLA models:

1. **Addressing the Lack of Explicit Step-by-Step Reasoning:** Current VLA models frequently operate as "black boxes," directly predicting actions without articulating the underlying thought process. This leads to failures when faced with nuanced instructions or visually ambiguous environments (e.g., distinguishing between similar objects, selecting the correct receptacle from multiple options). The motivation here is to enable models to perform explicit inference over crucial factors like affordance constraints, geometric relations, and object attributes, making their decision-making process more interpretable and robust.
2. **Improving Post-Training for Reasoning Quality and Execution Efficacy:** Existing post-training methods, primarily supervised fine-tuning (SFT), often lack targeted reinforcement for reasoning quality. Even when reinforcement learning (RL) is employed, reward designs tend to be simplistic and single-objective, failing to jointly optimize critical aspects such as precise region alignment (for perception) and consistent trajectory generation (for action). This limitation hinders generalization to out-of-distribution data and real-world scenarios. The motivation is to develop a systematic post-training strategy that explicitly reinforces both the quality of the reasoning chain and the accuracy of the resulting actions.

In essence, VLA-R1 aims to create a VLA model that can not only execute instructions but also "explain its thinking," thereby fostering greater reliability and adaptability in embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-R1 proposes a two-stage training paradigm that combines supervised fine-tuning with a novel reinforcement learning framework.

**A. Overall Architecture:**
The VLA-R1 architecture comprises a vision-language backbone and an action decoder. An input image and natural language instruction are processed:
- **Vision Branch:** A visual encoder processes raw images, projecting features into a shared embedding space.
- **Language Branch:** A tokenizer and embedder process the task instruction.
- **Multimodal Decoder:** Fuses visual and textual information, along with temporal history, to generate a structured output. This output includes a `<think>...</think>` reasoning segment (making intermediate steps explicit) and an `<output>...</output>` action prediction.
- **Action De-tokenizer:** Maps discrete predicted tokens into continuous 7D robot actions (∆x, ∆θ, ∆Grip) for direct execution.
The foundation model is initialized with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, utilizing a redesigned Vision Transformer for the visual pathway and the standard Qwen2.5 decoder stack for text.

**B. Data Synthesis (VLA-CoT-13K Dataset):**
To provide explicit supervision for reasoning, the authors developed the **VLA-CoT data engine**. This engine leverages a large language model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, to automatically generate high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations. These CoT annotations provide step-by-step reasoning processes explicitly aligned with affordance (e.g., bounding boxes) and trajectory (e.g., waypoint sequences) labels. This process produced the VLA-CoT-13K dataset, comprising 13,000 such annotations. This dataset is crucial for teaching the model task-consistent reasoning patterns during the initial training phase, addressing the lack of detailed reasoning explanations in existing datasets.

**C. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
In the first training stage, VLA-R1 undergoes supervised fine-tuning on the synthetically generated VLA-CoT-13K dataset. Unlike naive instruction tuning, CoT supervision provides intermediate signals, encouraging explicit task decomposition, stronger visual grounding, and stable credit assignment. This phase supervises both the structured `<think>` segment and the final `<output>` (action) segment, which helps regularize reasoning style, reduce hallucination, and prepare the model for subsequent reinforcement learning.

**D. Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):**
After SFT, VLA-R1 is further optimized using Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), specifically adopting the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm. GRPO is a scalable variant of RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) extended here for multimodal action reasoning. The core innovation lies in the design of three verifiable rewards:

1. **GIoU Affordance Reward (R_GIoU):** This reward uses Generalized Intersection over Union (GIoU) to measure the alignment between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes for affordance perception. Unlike standard IoU, GIoU penalizes misaligned predictions even when there's no overlap, considering the smallest enclosing box. This provides informative gradients for improving spatial grounding robustness, especially in cluttered environments.
 - $R_{GIoU} = \text{GIoU}(\hat{b}, b^*)$
2. **Fréchet Trajectory Reward (R_traj):** This reward measures the alignment of predicted and ground-truth trajectories using the **Angle-Length Augmented Fréchet distance (ALAF)**. ALAF is an improvement over pointwise Euclidean losses as it respects the temporal ordering of curves and incorporates local geometric properties (unit motion direction and local segment length). The reward is designed such that larger values indicate better alignment.
 - $R_{traj} = 1 - \tilde{D}_{ALAF}(\hat{T}, T^*)$
 where $\tilde{D}_{ALAF}$ is the normalized ALAF distance.
3. **Output Format Reward (R_format):** A simple binary reward that enforces structural correctness. It grants a reward of 1 if the model's output adheres to the required format (`<think>...</think>` followed by `<output>...</output>`), and 0 otherwise. This ensures interpretable reasoning traces and prevents degenerate outputs during post-training.
 - $R_{format} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if the output adheres to format,} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$

These rewards, integrated with GRPO, allow VLA-R1 to systematically optimize both its reasoning processes and the accuracy of its generated actions, reducing reliance on manual annotation while improving learning stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and generalization capacity of VLA-R1 were rigorously evaluated across four settings: in-domain datasets, out-of-domain datasets, simulation environments, and real-robot platforms.

**A. Performance on Benchmarks (In-domain and Out-of-Domain):**
- **In-domain (ShareRobot dataset):** VLA-R1-3B achieved state-of-the-art results across all metrics. For affordance perception, it attained an IoU of **36.51**, a **17.78% improvement** over the strong baseline ManipLVM-R1-3B. For trajectory prediction, it significantly reduced errors, achieving an Average distance of **91.74**, which is a **17.25% reduction** compared to ManipLVM-R1-3B (DFD=106.2, HD=97.9, RMSE=71.12). Generic open-source models performed poorly (IoU typically below 10), and even supervised fine-tuning baselines (e.g., RoboBrain-7B, NORA-3B) showed lower performance (IoU generally 5-25 range).
- **Out-of-Domain (UMD Part Affordance, VAIT):** Despite substantial distribution shifts, VLA-R1-3B maintained its superiority. For affordance, IoU increased to **33.96**, and trajectory error metrics (DFD=114.3, HD=98.43, RMSE=68.97, Avg=93.90) remained significantly lower than baselines, including ManipLVM-R1-3B. This demonstrates VLA-R1's strong cross-domain generalization and robustness.

**B. Performance in Simulation (RoboTwin simulator):**
VLA-R1 was tested on two robotic embodiments, Piper and UR5, under randomized tabletop clutter.
- **Affordance Perception:** VLA-R1 achieved an average Success Rate (SR) of **55%** (60% on Piper, 50% on UR5).
- **Trajectory Execution:** Performance improved to an average SR of **70%** (80% on Piper, 60% on UR5).
In contrast, the NORA-3B baseline performed significantly worse, with an average SR of 40% for affordance and a mere 5% for trajectory tasks. These results confirm VLA-R1's robust cross-embodiment stability and superior generalization even under heightened environmental variation.

**C. Performance in the Real World:**
Evaluations were conducted across four canonical tabletop scenarios (Bowl picking, Fruit picking, Kitchen scenario, Mixed scenario) with randomized object placements and poses.
- **Affordance Perception:** VLA-R1 achieved an average SR of **62.5%**.
- **Trajectory Execution:** VLA-R1 attained a higher average SR of **75%**.
The NORA-3B baseline recorded significantly lower success rates (35% for affordance, 47.5% for trajectory). The errors observed for VLA-R1 primarily stemmed from distractors like color similarity and positional variation, yet its predictions remained concentrated near the target, indicating a degree of tolerance and self-correction. This validates its practical viability and stability in complex real-world settings.

**D. Ablation Study:**
An ablation study confirmed the individual and combined impact of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and Reinforcement Learning (RL):
- **Without CoT & RL:** Baseline performance (IoU=23.74, Avg distance=128.38).
- **CoT only:** Improved IoU to 28.37 and reduced average distance to 124.6. This highlights CoT's role in attribute disambiguation and benefit to affordance tasks.
- **CoT + RL:** Achieved substantial gains across all metrics (IoU=36.51, Avg distance=91.74). This underscores the complementarity of CoT (providing structured reasoning) and RLVR (refining execution policies via reward signals), leading to significant end-to-end performance improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLA-R1 represents a significant advancement in Vision-Language-Action models, addressing crucial limitations that have hindered their deployment in complex real-world robotic applications. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning and Interpretability:** By explicitly integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision and designing fine-grained verifiable rewards, VLA-R1 enables models to articulate their step-by-step reasoning. This not only makes the decision-making process more transparent and interpretable but also significantly improves the model's ability to handle ambiguous instructions and complex environmental cues, moving beyond "black-box" action generation. This interpretability is vital for debugging, safety, and trustworthiness in embodied AI systems.
2. **Robust Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** The comprehensive evaluation across in-domain, out-of-domain, simulation, and real-robot platforms convincingly demonstrates VLA-R1's superior generalization capabilities. Its ability to maintain high performance despite distribution shifts and environmental variations is a critical step towards developing truly adaptive and widely applicable robotic agents.
3. **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The paper introduces two key methodological innovations:
 - **VLA-CoT Data Engine:** The automatic generation of high-quality CoT annotations using a large language model offers a scalable solution to the bottleneck of manual data annotation for reasoning-intensive tasks. This approach can inspire similar data synthesis strategies across various AI domains.
 - **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) with GRPO and Specialized Rewards:** The design of GIoU for affordance, Angle-Length Augmented Fréchet distance for trajectory, and a binary format reward provides a systematic and effective framework for jointly optimizing perception, reasoning, and action execution. This detailed reward engineering is a substantial contribution to the field of robotic reinforcement learning.
4. **Practical Viability for Real-World Robotics:** The strong performance on physical robot platforms, even under challenging conditions like clutter and object similarity, validates VLA-R1's practical utility. Its robustness suggests that it can tackle real-world manipulation tasks with higher success rates and reliability than previous approaches. This brings embodied AI closer to practical deployment in diverse settings like manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** VLA-R1 provides a promising foundation for future research in embodied AI. Its current limitation regarding validation on diverse robotic platforms (e.g., bimanual arms, quadruped robots) highlights an important future direction, challenging researchers to extend its generalization to an even broader array of embodiments and interaction paradigms. The "R1" series framework, exemplified by this work, suggests a systematic pathway for endowing AI models with increasingly sophisticated reasoning abilities.

In conclusion, VLA-R1 represents a significant stride in bridging the gap between high-level reasoning and low-level action execution in embodied AI, laying the groundwork for more intelligent, robust, and interpretable robotic systems.
