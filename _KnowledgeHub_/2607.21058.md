# Human-Inspired Framework for Robotic Craniotomy: Integrating Multimodal Fusion and Adaptive Trajectory Adjustment

> [!summary] Summary
> A closed-loop robotic craniotomy framework integrates preoperative trajectory planning, multimodal intraoperative perception using force and acoustic signals, and adaptive trajectory adjustment. This system achieved complete and safe bone flap removal on ex vivo goat skulls, demonstrating 97% breakthrough detection accuracy and an average residual bone layer thickness of 0.428 mm without dural injury.

## Problem

- Manual craniotomy poses high risks of dural or brain injury (50%-70% durotomy rates) and is prone to surgeon fatigue.
- Prior robotic systems are either open-loop, lacking adaptation to intraoperative changes, or closed-loop, often relying on single-signal monitoring that lacks robustness.
- Existing closed-loop systems typically halt at breakthrough, leaving residual bone that requires manual removal, limiting full autonomy.

## Method

- A **dual-contour-fusion-based adaptive spiral trajectory planning** method generates paths that conform to the complex 3D geometries of both outer and inner skull surfaces.
- A **CMA-TCN-Transformer** network with ==Adaptive Bayesian Filtering (ABF)== robustly fuses force and acoustic signals for real-time bone-layer state identification and low-latency breakthrough detection.
- A **breakthrough-triggered in-situ projection-based trajectory adjustment** strategy compensates for axial deviations, enabling autonomous and safe removal of residual bone.

## Results

- The multimodal monitoring system achieved **97%** breakthrough prediction accuracy on bovine ribs with an average detection delay of **0.048 +- 0.097 s**.
- **All four** closed-loop craniotomy experiments on ex vivo goat skulls were successfully completed without dural injury, while an open-loop baseline experiment resulted in dural damage.
- The framework consistently enabled complete bone flap removal with an average residual bone-layer thickness of **0.428 +- 0.015 mm** after closed-loop craniotomy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal fusion of force and acoustic signals, combined with context-aware temporal modeling and adaptive filtering, yields highly accurate and robust bone-layer state monitoring, suppressing transient disturbances.
> - Generating trajectories based on both outer and inner skull contours is critical for adapting to non-uniform bone thickness and complex curvatures, ensuring consistent tool-bone relative pose.
> - Dynamic, real-time trajectory adjustment upon breakthrough allows for safe and complete residual bone removal, overcoming the limitations of static preoperative plans and significantly enhancing surgical autonomy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Craniotomy is a fundamental neurosurgical procedure involving the creation of a bone window. Conventionally, this is a multi-step manual procedure, prone to surgeon fatigue and associated with a significant risk of dural or brain injury. Previous studies have reported unintended durotomy rates of 50%–70% in manual craniotomy, underscoring the need for improved safety and precision.

Robotics has been introduced to address these limitations. Early robot-assisted craniotomy focused on collaborative systems that improved execution stability and dura protection but still required extensive real-time surgeon intervention, offering limited autonomy.

Subsequent research investigated preoperative imaging-based trajectory planning to enable greater autonomy. These open-loop systems generated cutting paths based on CT data. However, a significant limitation of these approaches is their inability to account for intraoperative discrepancies arising from registration errors, structural deformation of the robotic system, or intraoperative skull motion. Furthermore, these methods often focused primarily on optimizing trajectories on the outer cranial surface, neglecting the complex three-dimensional geometries and non-uniform thickness of the inner skull surface, which can increase the risk of dural injury.

To mitigate the risks of open-loop execution, closed-loop craniotomy systems utilizing real-time sensor feedback have been developed. These systems have employed force feedback for breakthrough detection and vertical force control, or acoustic signals for monitoring cutting depth and bone-milling recognition. Machine learning techniques, such as CNN-LSTM networks, BP neural networks, and RBF neural networks, have been integrated to process sensor data for state monitoring and bone recognition. While these sensor-based systems have enabled intraoperative state monitoring and closed-loop control, several challenges persist:
- Many methods rely on single-signal monitoring, which may lack robustness in complex and heterogeneous bone structures.
- Validation is often conducted on simplified specimens or flat bone plates, which do not fully represent the complex curved geometry of the human skull.
- Existing systems typically trigger an immediate stop upon breakthrough detection, often leaving a relatively thick residual bone layer that still requires manual removal, thus limiting full automation.

This work aims to advance the field by addressing these specific challenges through a comprehensive, human-inspired closed-loop framework that integrates sophisticated trajectory planning, multimodal intraoperative perception, and adaptive trajectory adjustment for complete and safe bone flap removal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a human-inspired closed-loop robotic craniotomy framework that intelligently integrates preoperative planning with intraoperative execution to achieve safe, autonomous, and complete bone flap removal.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from the identified limitations of current craniotomy procedures and robotic systems:

- **Risk and Inefficiency of Manual Craniotomy**: Manual craniotomy is a high-risk, skill-dependent procedure associated with surgeon fatigue and a substantial risk of dural or brain injury. The reported durotomy rates highlight the need for technological solutions that enhance safety and precision while reducing reliance on subjective surgeon experience and sustained attention.
- **Deficiencies of Open-Loop Robotic Systems**: Existing open-loop robotic craniotomy systems, which rely solely on preoperative images (e.g., CT scans), cannot compensate for dynamic intraoperative factors such as registration errors, robotic system deformation, or subtle intraoperative skull motion. This fundamental limitation compromises safety, as the actual surgical environment may deviate from the preplanned trajectory, potentially leading to over-penetration and injury.
- **Inadequate Geometric Adaptability in Planning**: The cranial bone exhibits non-uniform thickness and complex three-dimensional geometries on both its inner and outer surfaces. Many previous robotic planning approaches primarily optimized trajectories on the outer surface, neglecting these intricate geometric constraints. This oversight increases the risk of dural injury as the milling tool may not consistently maintain a safe distance from the dura mater. The motivation is to develop a planning method that intrinsically considers both skull contours and continuously adjusts the tool pose.
- **Limitations of Current Closed-Loop Monitoring**: While some closed-loop systems use real-time sensor feedback, most rely on single-signal monitoring (e.g., force or sound). This approach can be insufficient for robust bone-layer state recognition due to heterogeneous bone density and various intraoperative disturbances, leading to potential false positives or delayed breakthrough detection. The motivation is to enhance perception robustness through multimodal sensing.
- **Incomplete Autonomy in Residual Bone Removal**: Current closed-loop systems typically trigger an immediate stop upon breakthrough detection. This often leaves a relatively thick residual bone layer that requires subsequent manual removal, reducing the overall autonomy and efficiency of the robotic system. The motivation is to develop a strategy that allows for safe and automated residual bone removal after breakthrough, ensuring complete bone flap separation.

In summary, the key objectives are:
1. To develop an adaptive spiral trajectory planning method that considers both outer and inner skull contours to generate safe and kinematically feasible trajectories for bulk and residual bone removal.
2. To establish a robust multimodal (force and acoustic) intraoperative monitoring system capable of accurate and low-latency breakthrough detection under varying bone conditions, mitigating transient disturbances.
3. To implement a breakthrough-triggered in-situ trajectory adjustment strategy that dynamically compensates for depth deviations, enabling safe and autonomous removal of residual bone without dural injury.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates three main components: preoperative trajectory planning, intraoperative real-time monitoring, and intraoperative execution with trajectory adjustment.

#### 3.1. A. Dual-Contour-Fusion-Based Adaptive Spiral Trajectory Planning Method

This method is designed to generate trajectories that conform to complex skull geometries while maintaining a consistent tool–bone relative pose.
1. **Skull Model Reconstruction**: A skull mesh model (M) is reconstructed from preoperative CT data.
2. **Volume Approximation**: The target bone-removal volume (V) is approximated as a cylinder defined by a prescribed central axis (with unit normal ˆn), a radius (R), and the skull thickness (d). This simplifies geometric modeling while defining the region of interest.
3. **Contour Extraction and Mapping**: A boolean intersection (I = M ∩ V) is performed to extract the outer (C_outer) and inner (C_inner) closed contours of the skull within the target volume. These contours are then mapped into local cylindrical coordinates (ϕ, ρ, z).
4. **Adaptive Trajectory Generation**:
 - The total number of spiral turns (N_turns) is determined by dividing the maximum distance between C_outer and C_inner by the allowable cutting depth per turn.
 - A periodic phase mapping (˜ϕ_k) aligns the mapped phase within a standard 2π period.
 - A global piecewise adaptive blending function α(ϕ) is introduced. This function smoothly distributes the cutting depth from the outer contour to the inner contour, ensuring a gradual transition.
 - The final Cartesian trajectory point x(ϕ) is synthesized via linear interpolation, weighted by α(ϕ), combining ρ(ϕ) and z(ϕ) based on the blended contours. This generates the main trajectory (T_main) for bulk removal.
 - An auxiliary trajectory (T_aux) is explicitly appended using the inner contour (C_inner) to specifically address residual bone removal.
5. **Tool Posture Optimization**: To improve chip evacuation, the tool axis is assigned an inclination angle (θ) relative to the local path tangent. To avoid robot kinematic singularities, the global directional preference of the Tool Center Point (TCP) frame is maintained. The local X-axis is orthogonalized against the updated Y-axis, and the Z-axis is determined by the right-hand rule. The resulting quaternion sequence ({q_k}) is processed via sign correction, 1D Gaussian filtering, and spherical linear interpolation to ensure continuity and smooth tool orientation. The complete trajectory set T = {(x_k, q_k)} includes both position and orientation.

#### 3.2. B. Intraoperative Bone-Removal State Monitoring Based on CMA-TCN-Transformer with ABF

This human-inspired framework couples force and sound signals to achieve robust online identification of bone-layer states (air cut, outer cortical bone, cancellous bone, inner cortical bone, breakthrough) and low-latency breakthrough monitoring.
1. **Data Preprocessing**: Force and sound signals from a 6-DOF force/torque sensor and a microphone are first filtered and segmented into aligned temporal windows.
2. **Feature Extraction (Three-branch Parallel Architecture)**:
 - **Force Signals**: A Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), comprising cascaded dilated convolutions and residual connections, processes force signals to enlarge the temporal receptive field, yielding force features (H_F).
 - **Sound Signals**: Multilayer 1D convolutions extract local spectral patterns, followed by a Squeeze and Excitation (SE) module that suppresses machining noise via channel-wise dynamic recalibration, producing enhanced sound features (H_S).
 - **Priori Anatomical Information**: A multilayer perceptron (MLP) combined with a trend-aware convolution module generates position features (H_P), embedding bone-removal progress information to mitigate classification ambiguity.
3. **Multimodal Fusion (Two-Stage Cross-Modal Attention Block - CMA)**: A CMA block sequentially performs bidirectional force-sound and force-position coupling. The concatenated fused feature (H_fused) is then refined by a Gated Residual Network (GRN), which adaptively regulates information flow.
4. **Temporal Modeling (Transformer with Enhancement)**: The refined feature sequence is fed into a Transformer network to capture long-range temporal dependencies. To counteract potential over-smoothing of sharp local variations by the Transformer's self-attention mechanism, a temporal-difference convolution module is cascaded post-Transformer as a high-pass enhancement filter, explicitly amplifying local gradient changes indicative of breakthrough.
5. **Adaptive Bayesian Filtering (ABF)**: To suppress transient predictive fluctuations (e.g., from heterogeneous bone density or local disturbances) and prevent premature breakthrough predictions, ABF is applied. It combines a Markovian state-transition model with surgical-progress-aware likelihood modulation. A context-aware dynamic likelihood vector (L_t) is constructed to penalize implausible state transitions while adaptively boosting sensitivity to weak breakthrough signals. The state confidence (b_t) is recursively updated using the prior, the state-transition matrix (A, encoding unidirectional progression), and the modulated likelihood. Once the breakthrough state is identified, the confidence vector is permanently locked to prevent misclassification.

#### 3.3. C. Breakthrough-Triggered in-situ Projection-Based Trajectory Adjustment Strategy

This strategy compensates for axial deviations to enable safe residual bone removal after breakthrough detection.
1. **Initial Registration**: Preplanned trajectories (T_main and T_aux) are transformed into the robot base frame using point-cloud registration and eye-in-hand calibration.
2. **Main Trajectory Execution**: The robot begins milling along T_main. A variable milling feed-speed strategy is employed: a prescribed speed (v_set) for bulk removal (progress ξ < 0.9) and a smoothly reduced safety threshold (v_safe = 1 mm/s) when approaching the estimated inner cortical layer (ξ ≥ 0.9).
3. **Breakthrough Detection and Interruption**: Throughout execution, the CMA-TCN-Transformer with ABF continuously monitors the bone-layer state. Upon identifying breakthrough, the robot controller immediately interrupts T_main execution and latches the actual breakthrough pose (p_breakthrough).
4. **In-situ Trajectory Adjustment**:
 - Acknowledging that p_breakthrough may deviate from the theoretical breakthrough point due to intraoperative factors, p_breakthrough is projected onto the predefined T_aux along the local normal direction (ˆn) to obtain a reference point (p_ref).
 - An axial compensation vector (δ) is calculated as the projection of the difference between p_breakthrough and p_ref onto ˆn.
 - This computed δ is uniformly applied as a translation to all discrete waypoints (x_i) on T_aux, generating a modified auxiliary trajectory (T_mod).
5. **Residual Bone Removal**: The robot then follows T_mod at v_safe to complete the safe removal of residual bone and achieve automated bone-flap separation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed framework was validated through experiments on bovine ribs and ex vivo goat skulls.

#### 4.1. A. Bovine Rib Milling Experiment

Bovine ribs, with their distinct cortical-cancellous-cortical structure, were used to evaluate the monitoring system's accuracy and response latency.
- **Impact of Adaptive Bayesian Filtering (ABF)**: Experiments showed that without ABF, the CMA-TCN-Transformer occasionally produced false breakthrough predictions in the inner cortical bone due to heterogeneous bone density, potentially leading to premature trajectory switching. With ABF, these transient interferences were effectively suppressed, resulting in robust and continuous state monitoring.
- **Multimodal Fusion Performance**:
 - Using sound signals alone, the breakthrough prediction accuracy was 89.3%.
 - Using force signals alone, accuracy increased to 92.7%, indicating improved robustness over sound, though false positives still occurred.
 - **Fusing both force and sound signals improved breakthrough prediction accuracy to 97%**, demonstrating the benefit of multimodal information.
- **Detection Latency and Overshoot**: Across 16 experiments, the breakthrough recognition delay was **0.048 ± 0.097 s**. The maximum overshoot observed was **0.29 mm** at a feed rate of 2 mm/s, which remains within typical dura safety margins. Delays for detecting transitions into outer cortical bone, cancellous bone, and inner cortical bone were 0.17 ± 0.038 s, 0.27 ± 0.078 s, and 0.16 ± 0.065 s, respectively.

#### 4.2. B. Autonomous Robotic Craniotomy Experiments on Ex Vivo Goat Skulls

Experiments on ex vivo goat skulls (chosen for thickness comparable to human skulls) were conducted to verify the overall framework's safety and effectiveness under complex geometries. Five experiments were performed: four using the closed-loop trajectory adjustment strategy and one open-loop baseline. Surgical success was defined by smooth bone flap detachment and intact dura mater.
- **Trajectory Execution and Tool Stability**:
 - The Z-axis coordinate of the end-effector exhibited periodic variations with increasing period, consistent with the adaptive fusion function α(ϕ), demonstrating smooth depth transition from outer to inner skull surfaces.
 - The robot transitioned smoothly from the main trajectory (T_main) to the modified auxiliary trajectory (T_mod) at the predicted breakthrough point, without noticeable overshoot.
 - Quaternion curves showed minimal fluctuations, indicating stable tool orientation.
- **Monitoring Validation**: Force and sound signals during skull milling showed significant fluctuations, reinforcing that simple threshold-based methods would be insufficient for reliable state monitoring. The predicted breakthrough occurred earlier (approximately one spiral revolution) than the CT-based estimate, highlighting that open-loop execution relying solely on CT data is prone to over-cutting. This observation validated the necessity of the proposed in-situ trajectory compensation strategy.
- **Surgical Outcomes**:
 - **All four closed-loop craniotomy experiments were successfully completed without dural injury.**
 - The **open-loop baseline experiment resulted in dural injury**, demonstrating the crucial role of the closed-loop system and trajectory adjustment.
 - **Residual Bone Layer**: The detached bone flaps from closed-loop experiments consistently showed either a partial residual bone layer or no obvious layer, indicating effective and safe depth regulation near the inner cortical boundary. The average residual bone-layer thickness after closed-loop craniotomy was **0.428 ± 0.015 mm**.
- **Robustness**: The framework demonstrated safety, effectiveness, and robustness across various experimental parameters, including different feed rates, cutting depths, flap sizes, and skull thicknesses, under complex skull-surface geometry.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in robotic craniotomy by introducing a comprehensive closed-loop framework that addresses critical limitations of existing approaches.

**Advancements over Previous Work:**
- **Integrated Closed-Loop Control**: The framework uniquely integrates preoperative planning, multimodal intraoperative perception, and adaptive trajectory adjustment into a unified closed-loop system. This holistic approach bridges the gap between static preoperative imaging and dynamic intraoperative conditions, which previous open-loop and simpler closed-loop systems failed to achieve.
- **Enhanced Geometric Adaptability**: The dual-contour-fusion-based trajectory planning method explicitly leverages both outer and inner skull contours, allowing the robot to generate complex spiral trajectories that adapt to the non-uniform thickness and curvature of the skull while maintaining an optimal tool-bone relative pose. This is a notable improvement over methods that simplify skull geometry or primarily focus on the outer surface.
- **Robust Multimodal Perception**: The CMA-TCN-Transformer network with Adaptive Bayesian Filtering demonstrates superior robustness and accuracy in bone-layer state monitoring by fusing force and acoustic signals. The 97% breakthrough detection accuracy and low latency (0.048 s) surpass many single-signal or less sophisticated machine learning approaches, providing a more reliable foundation for intraoperative decision-making.
- **Safe and Automated Residual Bone Removal**: The breakthrough-triggered in-situ projection-based trajectory adjustment strategy is a key innovation. By dynamically compensating for axial deviations at the moment of breakthrough, it enables the robot to safely and autonomously remove residual bone, eliminating the need for manual intervention typically required by systems that only stop upon breakthrough. This feature significantly enhances surgical autonomy and safety by consistently achieving a minimal and safe residual bone thickness (0.428 ± 0.015 mm).
- **Proven Safety and Effectiveness**: The successful completion of all closed-loop ex vivo goat skull craniotomies without dural injury, in contrast to the failure of the open-loop trial, provides empirical evidence of the framework's clinical potential for enhanced patient safety.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Increased Patient Safety**: The primary impact is the significant reduction in the risk of dural and brain injury during craniotomy by minimizing over-penetration and improving precision.
- **Improved Surgical Efficiency and Autonomy**: By automating a critical, high-risk, and skill-dependent procedure, the framework has the potential to reduce surgeon fatigue, streamline surgical workflows, and potentially shorten operative times.
- **Broader Application in Neurosurgery**: The principles of multimodal perception, adaptive trajectory generation, and in-situ compensation could be adapted to other robotic bone-removal procedures in neurosurgery or other orthopedic applications requiring high precision and safety.
- **Foundation for Future Surgical Robotics**: This work lays a robust foundation for the development of more intelligent and fully autonomous surgical robotic systems capable of operating safely and effectively in complex and dynamic biological environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that the current framework has limitations. Preoperative CT data alone may not reliably identify pathological soft-tissue conditions, suggesting that future work should incorporate multimodal preoperative planning, potentially including MRI. Furthermore, the validation was limited to ex vivo specimens. Future studies in in vivo animal models are necessary to assess the robustness of the system under physiological disturbances such as bleeding, pulsation, and soft-tissue motion, which are present in live surgical environments.
