# Interpretable Image Classification with Adaptive Prototype-based Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> ProtoViT, developed by researchers from Dartmouth College, Duke University, and the University of Maine, integrates Vision Transformers with prototype-based reasoning to create an interpretable image classification model. It achieves superior classification accuracy while providing transparent, human-understandable explanations for its decisions by showing which parts of an input image are similar to learned prototypes.

## Problem

- Existing interpretable models, particularly prototype-based ones, often relied on less powerful CNN backbones, limiting their performance compared to modern Vision Transformers (ViTs).
- Many prototype models suffered from limitations like spatially rigid prototypes, lack of semantic coherence in learned features, or failing to provide truly interpretable explanations when combined with ViTs.
- The need for AI systems in high-stakes domains to not only be accurate but also to clearly explain their decision-making process in human-understandable terms.

## Method

- ==ProtoViT== integrates a pre-trained Vision Transformer as a feature encoder with a novel ==Greedy Matching Layer== and an Evidence Layer for classification.
- The Greedy Matching Layer incorporates a ==greedy matching algorithm with adjacency masking== to ensure contiguous and non-redundant sub-prototypes, and an ==adaptive slots mechanism== for flexible prototype complexity.
- A four-stage training process optimizes for classification accuracy, prototype coherence, and sparsity, critically including ==prototype projection== to real training image patches for interpretability.

## Results

- ProtoViT achieved **86.5%** accuracy on the CUB-200-2011 dataset, outperforming previous prototype-based models like ProtoPNet (**84.8%**) and other ViT-based approaches.
- The model demonstrated at least equivalent robustness to CNN-based prototype models against adversarial attacks in the Location Misalignment Benchmark.
- A user study indicated that ProtoViT provided clearer explanations and more coherent prototypical features compared to ProtoPNet and Deformable ProtoPNet, with participants rating its explanations as more understandable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining Vision Transformers with prototype-based reasoning can yield models that are both high-performing and inherently interpretable.
> - Enforcing geometric coherence and allowing adaptive prototype complexity through techniques like greedy matching with adjacency masking and adaptive slots leads to more semantically meaningful and understandable prototypes.
> - A multi-stage training process, including prototype projection to real image patches, is crucial for ensuring that learned prototypes are directly human-inspectable and meaningful.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

In high-stakes domains like healthcare, finance, and autonomous driving, understanding why an AI system makes a specific decision is just as important as the accuracy of that decision. This need has driven the development of interpretable machine learning models that can explain their reasoning in human-understandable terms.

ProtoViT (Prototype-based Vision Transformer) represents a significant advancement in interpretable image classification. Developed by researchers from Dartmouth College, Duke University, and the University of Maine, this model combines the powerful feature extraction capabilities of Vision Transformers (ViTs) with prototype-based reasoning, a methodology that explains decisions by comparing input images to learned prototypical cases.

Unlike black-box models that provide little insight into their decision-making process, ProtoViT explains its classifications by showing which parts of an input image are similar to previously learned prototypes. This case-based reasoning approach makes the model's decision process transparent and understandable to humans.

### 2. Background on Interpretable Models

Machine learning models generally fall into two categories regarding interpretability:

1. **Post-hoc explainable models**: Standard models (like CNNs or ViTs) that are trained normally, with explanations generated after training using techniques like saliency maps or attention visualization.

2. **Inherently interpretable models**: Models designed from the ground up to be interpretable, incorporating explanation mechanisms directly into their architecture.

Prototype-based models belong to the second category. They learn a set of prototypical image parts during training and make predictions by comparing new inputs to these prototypes. Previous prototype models like ProtoPNet introduced this approach but had limitations:

- Most rely on CNN backbones rather than more powerful ViT architectures
- Many use rigid, spatially fixed prototypes that can't adapt to geometric variations
- Some struggle with coherence, producing prototypes that lack clear semantic meaning
- Recent ViT-based prototype models don't provide truly interpretable explanations

ProtoViT addresses these limitations by creating a model that combines ViTs with adaptive, deformable, and semantically coherent prototypes.

### 3. The ProtoViT Architecture

ProtoViT consists of three main components:

1. **Feature Encoder Layer**: A pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone that extracts latent feature representations from input images. These features capture complex patterns and relationships within the image.

2. **Greedy Matching Layer**: The core innovation of ProtoViT, which compares the input image's features to a set of learned prototypes using a novel matching algorithm that ensures coherence and adaptability.

3. **Evidence Layer**: A fully connected layer that aggregates the prototype similarity scores to make the final classification decision.

This architecture allows ProtoViT to leverage the feature representation power of ViTs while maintaining interpretability through clearly defined prototypes that connect directly to specific regions in training images.

### 4. Greedy Matching Layer

The Greedy Matching Layer contains three key innovations that enable ProtoViT to produce meaningful prototypes:

1. **Greedy Matching Algorithm**: This algorithm compares latent feature tokens from an input image to a set of learned prototypes. Crucially, it operates without replacement, meaning once a token has been matched to a sub-prototype, it cannot be matched to another. This prevents redundancy and improves the distinctiveness of the prototypes.

2. **Adjacency Masking**: To ensure that sub-prototypes within a prototype are geometrically contiguous (representing coherent visual concepts rather than disconnected parts), the algorithm uses adjacency masking. This encourages prototypes to represent meaningful, cohesive visual elements.

3. **Adaptive Slots Mechanism**: Different visual concepts may require different levels of complexity to represent adequately. ProtoViT's adaptive slots mechanism allows the model to learn prototypes with varying numbers of sub-prototypes, enabling flexibility in capturing different object features.

The mathematical formulation of the greedy matching can be represented as:

$$s(x, p_j) = \sum_{i=1}^{L} \max_{l \in \{1, 2..., L\}} \max_{k \in \{1, 2..., K_j\}} \frac{z_{l,k,j} \cdot \text{sim}(x_l, p_{j,k})}{K_j}$$

Where $s(x, p_j)$ is the similarity score between input $x$ and prototype $p_j$, $z_{l,k,j}$ indicates whether token $l$ is matched with sub-prototype $k$ of prototype $j$, and $\text{sim}(x_l, p_{j,k})$ measures the similarity between a token and a sub-prototype.

### 5. Training Methodology

ProtoViT employs a four-stage training process designed to ensure both high accuracy and interpretability:

1. **Initial Optimization**: The layers before the last layer are optimized using stochastic gradient descent with a combination of losses:
 - Classification loss for accuracy
 - Cluster loss to ensure prototypes are close to training examples
 - Separation loss to differentiate prototypes between classes
 - Coherence loss to enforce semantic meaningfulness
 - Orthogonality loss to reduce redundancy among prototypes

2. **Prototype Slots Pruning**: Unused or redundant prototype slots are identified and removed to improve efficiency and interpretability.

3. **Prototype Projection**: A critical step for interpretability, where each prototype vector is projected to the closest latent patches in the training data. This ensures that every prototype directly corresponds to a real image part that humans can inspect.

4. **Last Layer Optimization**: The last layer is fine-tuned to introduce sparsity, ensuring that each class is represented by a small number of prototypes, making explanations more concise.

This carefully designed training process balances the goals of classification accuracy and interpretability, ensuring that ProtoViT produces prototypes that are both discriminative and semantically meaningful.

### 6. Experimental Results

ProtoViT was evaluated on multiple datasets, including the CUB-200-2011 dataset for bird species identification and the Stanford Cars Dataset. The experimental results demonstrate several key findings:

1. **Superior Classification Accuracy**: ProtoViT achieves higher accuracy than existing prototype-based models, including both CNN-based approaches like ProtoPNet and Deformable ProtoPNet, and other ViT-based approaches. On the CUB-200-2011 dataset, ProtoViT achieves an accuracy of 86.5%, outperforming ProtoPNet (84.8%) and other competitors.

2. **Robustness**: When tested against the Location Misalignment Benchmark, which measures a model's ability to handle object position variations, ProtoViT showed at least equivalent robustness to CNN-based prototype models against adversarial attacks.

3. **User Study Results**: A user study demonstrated that humans perceive ProtoViT as providing clearer explanations and more coherent prototypical features compared to ProtoPNet and Deformable ProtoPNet. Participants rated ProtoViT's explanations as more understandable and trustworthy.

These results validate that ProtoViT successfully combines the performance advantages of Vision Transformers with the interpretability benefits of prototype-based models.

### 7. Interpretability Analysis

ProtoViT provides interpretability at multiple levels:

1. **Local Interpretability**: For each classification, ProtoViT shows which parts of the input image are similar to which learned prototypes, providing a visual explanation for why a specific decision was made.

2. **Global Interpretability**: Analysis of the learned prototypes across the dataset reveals that ProtoViT captures semantically meaningful and consistent visual concepts. For example, in bird classification, prototypes might represent distinctive features like "red crown," "yellow beak," or "spotted wing pattern."

3. **Coherence Analysis**: The prototypes learned by ProtoViT are visually coherent, representing contiguous regions with clear semantic meaning. This contrasts with some previous models where prototypes could represent scattered, disconnected pixels that don't correspond to meaningful visual concepts.

The interpretability of ProtoViT is particularly valuable in domains where understanding the reasoning behind a classification is critical, such as medical diagnosis where a doctor needs to verify the model's rationale.

### 8. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its advantages, ProtoViT has several limitations that point to areas for future research:

1. **Explicitness of Reasoning**: While ProtoViT shows which prototypes influenced a decision, it doesn't provide explicit textual justifications for its reasoning process. Future work could integrate vision-language models to generate natural language explanations.

2. **Computational Complexity**: The greedy matching algorithm introduces additional computational overhead compared to simpler approaches. Optimizing this process for real-time applications remains a challenge.

3. **Domain Generalization**: The current evaluation focuses on fine-grained image classification tasks. Further investigation is needed to assess ProtoViT's effectiveness in other domains such as medical imaging or satellite imagery.

4. **Location Misalignment**: While ProtoViT handles location variations better than rigid prototype models, there's room for improvement in addressing extreme positional shifts or occlusions.

These limitations present opportunities for future research to build upon the foundations laid by ProtoViT.

### 9. Conclusion

ProtoViT represents a significant advancement in interpretable image classification by successfully integrating Vision Transformers with adaptive, deformable, and coherent prototypes. The model achieves superior classification accuracy while providing transparent, human-understandable explanations for its decisions.

The key innovations of ProtoViT—the greedy matching algorithm, adjacency masking, and adaptive slots mechanism—enable it to capture meaningful visual concepts that directly connect to specific parts of real training images. This approach bridges the gap between high-performance black-box models and the need for transparency in AI systems.

As machine learning continues to be deployed in sensitive domains, models like ProtoViT that provide both accuracy and interpretability will be increasingly valuable. They enable human-AI collaboration, facilitate regulatory compliance, and help build trust in automated decision-making systems. ProtoViT demonstrates that we can have powerful, modern deep learning architectures without sacrificing our ability to understand how they arrive at their decisions.
