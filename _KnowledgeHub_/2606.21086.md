# ReFPO: Reflow Regularization for Flow Matching Policy Gradients

> [!summary] Summary
> ReFPO enhances Flow Matching Policy Gradients (FPO) by incorporating an explicit geometric regularizer, improving the stability of online reinforcement learning and enabling high-fidelity one-step action inference. The approach maintains performance comparable to multi-step methods while significantly reducing latency and mitigating training instabilities across continuous control benchmarks.

## Problem

- Generative models used as reinforcement learning policies often suffer from high inference latency due to iterative sampling, hindering real-time applications like robotic control.
- Existing flow-based online RL methods struggle with training stability, exhibiting volatile proxy ratios, and previous acceleration techniques often introduce complex, computationally expensive multi-stage pipelines.
- Flow Matching Policy Gradients (FPO) implicitly straighten policy flows in an advantage-weighted manner, which can distort the flow field for actions with negative advantages, compromising one-step inference accuracy and overall stability.

## Method

- **ReFPO** augments the FPO objective with an explicit Reflow regularizer (L_Reflow) that directly biases the learned velocity field towards straighter paths.
- The L_Reflow term reuses the Conditional ==Flow Matching== (CFM) loss (ℓ_θ) already computed for the FPO objective, incurring no additional computational overhead during training.
- This regularization is applied without reweighting by advantage, providing a consistent geometric anchor that improves flow consistency for all sampled trajectories, irrespective of their reward.

## Results

- ReFPO achieved higher average rewards (686 ± 139) on MuJoCo tasks compared to FPO (641 ± 140) and Gaussian PPO (622 ± 192), while also suppressing CFM proxy-ratio spikes and achieving a lower "Explosion Rate" (**0.00189** vs. FPO's **0.00365**).
- It significantly reduced the performance gap between 10-step and 1-step inference, with its 1-step reward (690 ± 139) comparable to multi-step performance, unlike FPO's lower 1-step reward (565 ± 160).
- On high-dimensional Humanoid control, ReFPO achieved 17.371ms for one-step action generation, a substantial reduction from 52.169ms for 10-step, demonstrating robust low-latency inference and outperforming FPO, especially under sparse conditioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The authors identified that FPO inherently functions as an "implicit advantage-weighted Reflow process," where policy updates encourage straightening, but without explicit, consistent control over the overall flow geometry.
> - Explicitly regularizing the flow field to be straighter, independent of advantage weighting, directly reduces the local truncation error of one-step ODE solvers, enabling high-fidelity single-step action generation.
> - Consistently promoting straighter velocity fields stabilizes the PPO-style inner optimization loop by reducing volatility in the CFM-based proxy ratio, mitigating training instabilities and proxy-ratio spikes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated at the intersection of generative models, particularly flow matching and diffusion models, and reinforcement learning (RL). Generative models have increasingly been adopted as policy representations in RL due to their capacity to capture complex, multimodal action distributions, a capability often beyond traditional Gaussian policies. This expressivity is particularly relevant for high-dimensional and complex tasks where optimal behavior might not be unimodal.

However, a significant challenge limiting the practical deployment of these flow-based policies is their iterative sampling nature. Generating a single action often necessitates multiple Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) integration steps, which introduces latency and can impede real-time applications, such as robotic control. Prior efforts to address this latency include acceleration techniques like Rectified Flow, Consistency Models, and multi-stage distillation. While these methods have demonstrated efficacy in supervised learning contexts, their direct application to online RL environments often results in complex, multi-stage pipelines with considerable computational overhead. These "force-fitted" combinations are noted to struggle with the non-stationary optimization landscape inherent in online RL.

Within the online RL domain, early generative policy methods such as DDPO, DPPO, Flow-GRPO, and ReinFlow typically conceptualized the iterative denoising process as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP). This approach, however, departs from the continuous-time nature of the underlying flows and incurs high computational costs. Flow Matching Policy Gradients (FPO) emerged as a notable advancement, offering a holistic framework that leverages Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) as a variational lower bound. This allowed for the derivation of a tractable gradient framework for flow-based policies, thereby circumventing the challenges of computing exact action likelihoods, which are generally intractable for these models.

The present work, ReFPO, builds directly upon FPO. It identifies a gap in FPO, specifically that FPO primarily focuses on the directionality of gradients for policy updates but does not explicitly explore or control the evolution of the intrinsic flow geometry across RL iterations. The paper positions itself as addressing this gap by introducing mechanisms to enhance the geometric stability and efficiency of flow-based policies during online optimization, particularly to enable robust one-step inference without incurring the computational or architectural complexities of previous acceleration methods. It also touches upon the challenge of model collapse in recursive straightening procedures, which ReFPO aims to mitigate by grounding path rectification in environmental feedback.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate Reflow-regularized Flow Matching Policy Gradients (ReFPO), an online reinforcement learning method designed to enhance the efficiency and stability of flow-based control by integrating explicit Reflow regularization into the FPO framework.

The motivation for ReFPO stems from a specific insight regarding the behavior of Flow Matching Policy Gradients (FPO). The authors identified a structural property within FPO: the gradient updates in FPO can be interpreted as an implicit advantage-weighted Reflow process. This means that the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective, which serves as a tractable PPO proxy in FPO, inherently induces a self-iterative straightening effect on the policy's self-sampled trajectories when operating within an online RL loop. This perspective suggests that FPO, beyond optimizing the reward-weighted policy objective, concurrently performs a form of path rectification on its own generated data.

However, this implicit Reflow effect within FPO is not explicitly controlled. The authors argue that while advantage-weighted updates tend to straighten flows for actions associated with positive advantages (high rewards), they can simultaneously distort the flow field for actions with negative advantages. This imbalance can lead to several undesirable outcomes:
1. **Increased Local Bending:** The fitted velocity field may exhibit unnecessary curvature.
2. **Amplified Discretization Errors:** One-step or coarse discretization for action generation becomes less reliable due to the uneven flow field, leading to larger local truncation errors.
3. **Volatile Proxy Ratio:** The CFM-based proxy ratio, critical for PPO-style updates in FPO, can become more volatile, leading to training instability, including significant "spikes" in the proxy ratio.

To address these limitations, ReFPO's key objective is to introduce an explicit geometric regularizer that biases the learned flow field towards straighter paths irrespective of the advantage sign. This regularization aims to provide a "geometric anchor" that improves consistency and stability. The method is motivated by the desire to achieve:
- **Reduced CFM Proxy-Ratio Spikes:** To stabilize the PPO-style training process.
- **High-Fidelity One-Step Inference:** To enable generating effective actions with a single ODE integration step, thereby significantly reducing inference latency for real-time applications. This one-step performance is intended to match or exceed multi-step performance.
- **Preservation of Multimodal Policy Expressivity:** The regularization should not lead to mode collapse or diminish the policy's ability to represent diverse behaviors.
- **Minimal Computational Overhead:** The design emphasizes integrating the regularizer with a single line of code change, avoiding additional computational costs or auxiliary distillation stages by reusing already computed CFM loss terms.
- **Scalability and Stability:** To provide a robust approach for generative policies in complex physical simulations, demonstrated across various benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReFPO's methodology is built upon the Flow Matching Policy Gradients (FPO) framework, augmenting it with an explicit geometric regularization term. The approach is structured around identifying an implicit Reflow process in FPO and then making it explicit and controllable.

**Foundation in PPO and FPO:**
The paper first reviews Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which stabilizes policy gradient updates using a clipped likelihood ratio `r(θ)`. For flow-matching policies where exact likelihoods are intractable, FPO replaces `r(θ)` with a computable surrogate `ˆr_FPO(θ) = exp(L_CFM_θ_old - L_CFM_θ)`. This proxy is derived from Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) losses, leveraging their variational interpretation as estimates of the negative Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) of the induced policy distribution. FPO then integrates this proxy into the PPO clipped surrogate objective, preserving the core structure of PPO while enabling flow-based policy training.

**FPO as an Implicit Online Reflow Process:**
A central conceptual contribution is the interpretation of FPO as an implicit online Reflow process. In FPO, the action-noise pairs `(ϵ, a_1)` used to define the CFM objective are generated on-policy by the flow policy itself, creating a self-sampling, closed-loop dynamic. The paper analyzes the unclipped FPO surrogate objective `L(θ) = -E_t[ˆr_t(θ)A_t]`. The gradient of this objective with respect to `θ` is proportional to `-A_t ˆr_t(θ) ∇_θ L_CFM,θ(a_t; o_t)`. Since minimizing the CFM loss `L_CFM,θ(a_t; o_t)` encourages the model's velocity field `v_θ` to align with the straight vector `(a_t - ϵ)` at intermediate points, positive advantages effectively drive the policy to rectify its own induced trajectories. This constitutes an "advantage-weighted, online Reflow process."

**Enhancing Stability via Explicit Reflow Regularization:**
ReFPO explicitly introduces a geometric prior to the FPO objective to improve one-step inference and PPO-style training stability. This is achieved by adding an unweighted CFM regularizer `L_Reflow(θ) = E_τ,ϵ[||v_θ(a_τ,t, τ ; o_t) - (a_t - ϵ)||^2]` to the FPO objective. The full ReFPO objective is defined as `L_ReFPO(θ) = L_FPO(θ) + λ L_Reflow(θ)`, where `λ > 0` is a hyperparameter controlling the regularization strength.

The key aspects of this regularization are:
- **Computational Efficiency:** The `L_Reflow` term reuses the same CFM loss `ℓ_θ` that is already computed for the `L_FPO` objective, requiring no additional flow evaluations or computational overhead during training.
- **Geometric Anchoring:** Unlike the `L_FPO` surrogate, `L_Reflow` is *not* reweighted by the advantage. This ensures that the regularization consistently biases the learned velocity field toward straighter paths for all sampled trajectories, regardless of their immediate reward or advantage. This provides a crucial geometric anchor for the policy.

**Mechanism and Effects:**
The paper elaborates on two complementary effects of this explicit regularization:
1. **Rollout-Time Effect (One-Step Inference Accuracy):** By encouraging the learned velocity field `v_θ` to align with the straight target direction `(a_t - ϵ)`, the Reflow regularizer reduces unnecessary curvature in the field. This directly impacts the local truncation error of one-step ODE solvers for action generation. A straighter field implies smaller terms `||∂_τ v_θ||` and `||J_x v_θ||_op ||v_θ||` in the Taylor expansion of the error, making one-step rollout more accurate and closer to multi-step integration without dedicated distillation.
2. **Fixed-Batch Training Effect (PPO Stability):** The regularizer stabilizes the PPO-style inner optimization loop on a frozen batch of samples. The FPO proxy ratio `ρ(θ)` is an exponential function of the difference in CFM losses. A first-order expansion shows that the volatility of `log ρ(θ)` is influenced by the residual between the fitted velocity field and the straight target direction, as well as its parameter sensitivity. By directly penalizing this residual (without advantage reweighting), ReFPO biases the model towards a simpler, straighter velocity field. This results in a smoother variation of the fixed-batch CFM proxy across parameter updates, which empirically reduces proxy-ratio spikes and the frequency of clipping events, thereby stabilizing training.

**Algorithmic Summary (Algorithm D.1):**
The training procedure integrates the `L_Reflow` term into the standard FPO optimization loop. After collecting trajectories and computing advantages, the algorithm iterates through optimization epochs. In each epoch, for every state-action pair and its corresponding Monte Carlo timestep-noise samples, the CFM loss `ℓ_θ` is computed for both the current and old policies. The `L_FPO` term is then formed using the clipped surrogate and advantage weighting, and the `L_Reflow` term is calculated as the mean of the computed `ℓ_θ` values. The total `L_ReFPO` objective is then used to update the policy parameters via gradient descent. Value function parameters are updated separately, similar to standard PPO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ReFPO was evaluated across three distinct domains: GridWorld, MuJoCo Playground, and high-dimensional Humanoid Control, using various metrics including average reward, one-step performance, path straightness, and training stability diagnostics.

**GridWorld:**
- **Multimodality and Path Geometry:** In a 25x25 GridWorld task with bifurcated sparse rewards, ReFPO successfully preserved the multimodal structure of the policy, allowing trajectories to branch and reach both goal regions. Compared to vanilla FPO, ReFPO produced visually straighter and more spatially consistent trajectories. This indicated that the explicit Reflow regularizer reduced unnecessary curvature in the learned vector field without causing the policy to collapse into a single mode.
- **One-Step Inference:** While FPO could complete this simple task with one Euler step, its one-step action field exhibited local fluctuations. ReFPO mitigated these artifacts, leading to more coherent one-step samples and demonstrating improved discretization robustness.

**MuJoCo Playground:**
Evaluations on 10 continuous-control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite highlighted ReFPO's performance, stability, and one-step inference capabilities over 100M environment steps.
- **Training Stability:** Diagnostic analysis on tasks like PointMass, FingerSpin, and BallInCup showed that FPO experienced large CFM proxy-ratio spikes, which often coincided with reward degradation. ReFPO effectively suppressed these spikes, maintaining smaller proxy ratios and more stable reward curves throughout training. This was quantitatively supported by a lower "Explosion Rate" (fraction of samples with large proxy-ratio changes) for ReFPO (0.00189) compared to FPO (0.00365).
- **Average Performance and One-Step Consistency:** ReFPO achieved a higher average reward (686 ± 139) than FPO (641 ± 140) over 100M steps for 10-step evaluation. Crucially, ReFPO significantly reduced the performance gap between 10-step and 1-step evaluation. Its 1-step reward (690 ± 139) was comparable to, and in some tasks slightly exceeded, its 10-step performance, indicating robust one-step action generation. FPO's 1-step reward was lower (565 ± 160).
- **Flow Diagnostics:** ReFPO exhibited a lower "Straightness Error" (MSE between learned velocity and straight target direction) of 0.0116, compared to FPO's 0.0475, confirming that the regularization led to straighter learned paths.
- **Baseline Comparison:** ReFPO outperformed Gaussian PPO (622 ± 192) on average. It also surpassed post-training flow-matching baselines like ReinFlow and Flow-GRPO, even when these baselines were given an additional 100M steps of continued training. Flow-GRPO showed severe one-step degradation under coarse discretization.
- **Ablation Studies:** An ablation study on the Reflow coefficient `λ` found `λ = 0.04` to provide the optimal balance between reward and stability. An alternative variant applying advantage-weighted straightening performed worse, particularly for one-step evaluation, suggesting that consistent geometric regularization across all trajectories is more effective than selective straightening based on reward.

**Humanoid Control:**
On a high-dimensional MoCap tracking task in Isaac Gym, ReFPO was evaluated for scalability and robust low-latency inference.
- **One-Step Performance and Latency:** ReFPO maintained strong one-step performance in humanoid control. The N=1 results were consistently comparable to, and sometimes better than, N=10 results across different conditioning regimes. This translated to a significant reduction in inference latency, with one-step action generation achieving 17.371ms compared to 52.169ms for 10-step, supporting its utility for real-time control.
- **Expressivity with Sparse Conditioning:** Under full-joint conditioning, Gaussian PPO remained the strongest. However, as conditioning became sparser (root+hands, root-only), flow-based policies (FPO and ReFPO) significantly outperformed Gaussian PPO. In these sparse regimes, ReFPO further improved over FPO, with its one-step variant achieving the best success rate and Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE). This suggests ReFPO is particularly beneficial when incomplete goal information necessitates multimodal action modeling and robust one-step inference.
- **Additional Diagnostics:** ReFPO demonstrated a lower Jacobian Explosion Rate, lower Policy-Ratio Explosion Rate, and smaller Jacobian norms compared to FPO, indicating enhanced geometric stability of the learned velocity fields.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ReFPO offers several contributions to the field of reinforcement learning, particularly concerning the practical application of generative models for policy learning.

One significant aspect is the **enhancement of stability and efficiency** in online reinforcement learning with flow-based policies. By explicitly regularizing the intrinsic geometry of the learned flow field, ReFPO addresses the inherent instability and suboptimal one-step inference performance that can arise from uncontrolled, advantage-weighted rectification in methods like FPO. This leads to more stable training dynamics, characterized by reduced proxy-ratio spikes and smoother learning curves.

A major practical impact lies in enabling **high-fidelity one-step inference with minimal latency**. By actively promoting straighter probability flows during training, ReFPO allows for accurate action generation using only a single integration step. This significantly reduces the computational cost and inference time typically associated with iterative generative models, making flow-based policies more viable for real-time applications such as robotics, where low latency is critical. This is achieved without introducing additional complex distillation stages or computational overhead, as the regularizer reuses components already present in the FPO pipeline.

The work also provides a **novel geometric interpretation of FPO**, identifying it as an "implicit advantage-weighted Reflow process." This deeper understanding of the underlying dynamics of flow-based policy gradients could inform future research directions and inspire new algorithms that leverage or control these geometric properties more effectively.

Furthermore, ReFPO demonstrates **improved robustness in complex and high-dimensional control tasks**, particularly when dealing with multimodal action distributions or sparse conditioning. Its superior performance over FPO in these challenging scenarios, especially in Humanoid Control with limited observation, highlights its capacity to learn and maintain expressive policies while ensuring efficient action generation. This broadens the applicability of flow-based policies to more challenging real-world problems.

The reusability of the CFM residual as a regularizer suggests **potential generalizability** to a wider family of CFM-based online RL or fine-tuning methods beyond FPO, such as ORW-CFM-W2, with minimal algorithmic changes. This could extend the benefits of geometric regularization to other emerging flow-matching techniques.

In summary, ReFPO contributes to bridging the gap between the theoretical promise of expressive generative policies and the practical demands of online reinforcement learning by offering a method that improves stability, efficiency, and real-time applicability through principled geometric regularization. The insights gained may also foster further research into the intrinsic dynamics of flow-based generative models in control.
