# RawVLA: Embodied Neural Image Signal Processor For Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RawVLA is a lightweight streaming neural image signal processor that renders RAW camera data for frozen VLA policies. Decoupled luminance and chroma GRU states predict structured exposure, white-balance, color-correction, and monotonic tone-curve controls, trained only through the policy's native action loss. On the new RawVLA-Bench, it lifts LIBERO average success from 43.01% to 68.82% and real low-light success from 0% to 66.5%.

## Problem

- VLA research treats camera-rendered RGB as given, abstracting perception as RGB-to-action and leaving the fixed in-camera ISP outside learning and evaluation loops.
- Fixed ISPs tuned for human viewing clip highlights, amplify noise, or compress tone under low light and overexposure, discarding visual signals that manipulation policies need.
- Task-oriented and neural ISP studies target static vision tasks with frame-wise designs, so the effect of ISP choices on closed-loop manipulation success remains unmeasured.

## Method

- ISP sensitivity analysis: a factorized ==pseudo-RAW pipeline== varies exposure, sensor noise, chromatic response, tonal response, and bit depth one at a time across VLA and world-action-model policies.
- **RawVLA** ==streaming neural ISP==: burst-denoised RAW feeds decoupled luminance and scale-invariant chroma ==GRU== states, decoding exposure, zero-sum white balance, residual color matrix, and a monotonic ==Bernstein tone curve==.
- Action-driven optimization trains the ISP through the frozen policy's native regression or ==diffusion action loss== plus light brightness and chroma-range regularizers; **RawVLA-Bench** replays demonstrations under five illumination regimes.

## Results

- On LIBERO across PI-0, PI-0.5, and Qwen3-OFT, RawVLA averages **68.82%** success versus **43.01%** for the Default ISP and **42.26%** for RAWild, preserving normal-light performance.
- On RoboTwin 2.0, RawVLA reaches **54.98%** average success versus **43.91%** for the Default ISP and **29.59%** for the best neural ISP, ranking first in every adverse illumination regime.
- Real dual-arm low light: **66.5%** success versus **0%** Default ISP and **11%** DarkISP at **168 FPS**; removing the spatial luminance feature drops LIBERO average from **62.24%** to **36.35%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The camera ISP is not task-neutral: exposure dominates VLA sensitivity while bit depth matters little, so imaging pipelines belong inside robot learning and evaluation loops.
> - Video-predictive world-action models showed narrower stable ranges than VLA policies under exposure, noise, and tone shifts, a caution for pixel-predictive backbones in variable lighting.
> - A small, physically structured, factorized front-end trained through a frozen policy's action loss can recover robustness without retraining the backbone or adding extra sensors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A camera has to turn sensor readings into images before a robot policy can act on them. That conversion is done by the camera's image signal processor (ISP), which turns RAW sensor measurements into RGB. A VLA (vision-language-action model) typically receives only that rendered RGB and treats the rendering as fixed. Lighting can change what survives that conversion, so the same frozen policy may receive an image that no longer supports the task.

RawVLA sits between camera and policy. It adaptively renders RAW observations for a **frozen** VLA rather than modifying the VLA itself. The researchers tested it on four real-world dual-arm manipulation tasks with a PI-0.5 policy fine-tuned under normal illumination. Under low light, the average task-success rate was **66.50% with RawVLA, 0.00% with the fixed Default ISP, and 11.00% with DarkISP**. Under normal illumination, RawVLA averaged 75.00%, close to Default ISP's 76.50%.

### 2. The camera is part of the policy's input

The paper first ran a controlled sensitivity study. It changed one imaging dimension at a time while holding the underlying pseudo-RAW observation fixed. The tested dimensions were exposure, sensor noise, white point, relative color response, tonal response, and bit depth.

Exposure is especially influential. Severe under- or overexposure can collapse task success, and different policies respond differently. World-action models (WAMs) generally have a narrower stable range. Bit depth is less influential than the other tested dimensions for the tested VLAs.

Radar-style plots of task success on LIBERO (left) and RoboTwin 2.0 (right), with spokes for exposure, tonal response, noise, white point, color relation, and bit depth. LIBERO shows six VLA/WAM policies; RoboTwin shows Qwen3-OFT and FastWAM in Easy and Hard settings.

### 3. Render for action, continuously

RawVLA works causally: its output at each moment uses the current and past frames, not future observations. It starts with a causal burst of RAW frames. Motion-aware frequency-domain fusion denoises the burst without simply averaging away moving arms and objects.

Separate luminance and chroma descriptors feed separate recurrent states. Luminance summarizes spatial structure and intensity distribution. Chroma uses scale-invariant color statistics, so absolute brightness does not leak into color estimation. The states also receive the previous ISP settings. From these states, RawVLA predicts structured controls (exposure, white balance, a color-correction matrix, and a shared monotonic tone curve), which render RGB for the frozen VLA.

The proposed RawVLA architecture: RAW burst and denoising, luminance and chroma paths, separate recurrent states, explicit exposure, white-balance, color-correction, and tone controls, rendered RGB, frozen VLA, and action chunk.

Training follows the same division of labor. The VLA's native action loss is differentiated through the rendered image into RawVLA, while the VLA parameters stay fixed. The main benchmark training uses no paired pixel-reconstruction target. Brightness and chroma regularizers stabilize this optimization, especially under extreme illumination.

A training-objective ablation tests whether the gains come from action-aligned processing rather than generic brightening or color constraints. On RoboTwin 2.0 with PI-0.5, full RawVLA averaged 57.12% success across five illumination regimes. Removing the action loss while retaining the brightness and chroma auxiliaries yielded 47.94%, and using action loss alone yielded 33.70%. This favors combining action supervision with the auxiliary constraints. It does not show that action loss alone suffices.

### 4. Where the results hold

The real-world averages cover pick and place, drug drawer, pick flower, and stack and cover, with 50 trials per task. A single PI-0.5 checkpoint was used throughout. The same learned RawVLA ISP served under both normal and low light.

The results also have clear limits. The simulated benchmarks use three-channel pseudo-RAW, not camera-native Bayer measurements. The real-world evaluation covers a small set of indoor tasks and lighting conditions. For the two tested WAM backbones, the authors report no substantial, consistent robustness gain.

RawVLA is not the first task-oriented ISP. Earlier work such as [Dirty Pixels](https://doi.org/10.1145/3446918) and [RAW-Adapter](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.17027) also tied RAW processing to downstream vision tasks. The authors describe their work as extending task-oriented ISP to sequential VLA behavior and manipulation success. RawVLA makes camera processing an explicit part of the action system. The evidence supports it for the evaluated frozen VLA policies and conditions, not for all embodied policies or camera pipelines.
