# Bi-Adapt: Few-shot Bimanual Adaptation for Novel Categories of 3D Objects via Semantic Correspondence

> [!summary] Summary
> Bi-Adapt trains bimanual point-level action proposal/scoring networks on a supporting set of categories, then transfers affordance to novel categories via foundation-model semantic correspondence (DIFT), followed by few-shot fine-tuning on limited real interactions. Achieves 59-70% success rate on unseen instances across 5 tasks, far outperforming DualAfford and heuristic baselines.

## Problem

- Existing bimanual manipulation methods rely on costly large-scale demonstration collection and training, generalizing poorly to unseen objects in novel categories.
- Data-driven imitation-learning approaches require labor-intensive real-world interaction data and time-consuming training on huge datasets to generalize.
- Prior affordance-learning and RL-based dual-arm stabilization methods struggle to predict precise affordances on unseen objects and are costly, requiring many policy rollouts.

## Method

- **Perception Module** with two coupled submodules, First and Second Gripper Modules, trained via ==reversed dataflow== (M2 first, then M1) to learn collaborative point-level bimanual actions on a supporting set.
- **Affordance Transfer** using ==DIFT diffusion features== for semantic correspondence, mapping successful contact points from supporting-set source images to novel-category target objects via cosine similarity.
- Few-shot adaptation procedure that executes proposed actions on mapped contact-point candidates and fine-tunes the ==Action Proposal and Scoring Networks== using online interaction outcomes on limited novel-category instances.

## Results

- Bi-Adapt achieves **59.00-70.00%** sample success rate on unseen novel-category instances across Unfolding, Opening, Uncapping, Closing, and Capping, versus **17.30-35.00%** for DualAfford and M-Where2Act.
- Ablation without affordance transfer drops novel-unseen performance to **31.23-41.30%**, confirming semantic correspondence is essential for cross-category generalization.
- With only **50** interaction demonstrations, Bi-Adapt outperforms DualAfford at every budget level, with Closing task showing over **30%** success rate gain after 3 fine-tuning budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-the-shelf vision foundation models can bridge affordance knowledge across object categories via semantic correspondence, avoiding costly per-category affordance relearning.
> - Cross-category transfer alone is insufficient; a lightweight few-shot adaptation step is needed to filter negative contact points and correct action directions for physical differences.
> - Decoupling bimanual action into sequentially conditioned gripper modules trained with reversed dataflow enables tractable learning over an otherwise large joint action space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual manipulation, involving the coordinated use of two robotic arms, is a crucial capability for robots operating in complex, human-centric environments. However, developing robust and generalizable bimanual manipulation strategies presents significant challenges. Existing methods often grapple with issues such as high data collection costs, extensive training times, and limited generalization capabilities, particularly when confronted with novel objects or categories outside their training distribution.

Prior research in bimanual manipulation has explored various approaches. Traditional control methods, while providing precision, often require complex models and suffer from long computation times. Learning-based approaches, including those leveraging imitation learning (e.g., ScrewMimic), have shown promise but typically demand large-scale datasets of expert demonstrations, which are laborious to collect, especially in real-world settings. A persistent challenge for these methods is the "sim-to-real" gap and low sample efficiency, hindering deployment in diverse, unstructured environments. Furthermore, many approaches have focused on simpler tasks or limited sets of in-category objects, failing to generalize to geometrically and physically varied objects in novel categories.

Affordance learning, which aims to identify possible actions on objects, has been employed to suggest action possibilities and accelerate policy generation. However, these methods often struggle to predict precise affordance maps for objects not seen during training.

More recently, the advent of foundation models in computer vision and natural language processing has opened new avenues for robotic manipulation. These models possess strong common-sense knowledge and generalization capabilities. Some works integrate these models to enhance robotic tasks, for example, by combining 3D geometry with 2D semantic features from foundation models to enable few-shot language-guided manipulation across object variations. Other studies have leveraged the zero-shot generalization of distilled features from pre-trained foundation models for cross-category affordance transfer via semantic correspondence.

Despite these advancements, directly applying off-the-shelf foundation models for bimanual manipulation has limitations. Their ability to extract suitable affordances is bounded by factors such as the foundation model's inherent capabilities, image quality, and the accuracy of selected points. Crucially, they often lack mechanisms to adapt for coordinated bimanual actions, which involve not just contact points but also precise manipulation orientations and collaborative strategies, leading to potential failures on unseen categories without specific adaptation.

Within this landscape, Bi-Adapt positions itself as an approach that seeks to bridge these gaps. It aims to efficiently generalize bimanual manipulation to novel object categories by combining the semantic correspondence capabilities of vision foundation models with a data-efficient few-shot adaptation strategy. This addresses the limitations of both purely data-driven learning methods (which struggle with generalization and data efficiency) and direct application of foundation models (which lack specific adaptation for bimanual coordination).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for efficient generalization of bimanual manipulation to novel categories of 3D objects. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified challenges and limitations in existing robotic manipulation systems:

1. **High Data and Training Costs for Bimanual Tasks:** Complex bimanual tasks inherently require coordinated movements between two arms, making data collection and training for such tasks exceptionally costly and time-consuming. Existing data-driven methods, particularly those based on imitation learning, necessitate extensive expert demonstrations, which are labor-intensive to gather in real-world scenarios. The research aims to mitigate this reliance on vast datasets.

2. **Lack of Generalization to Novel Object Categories:** A significant hurdle for current bimanual manipulation systems is their inability to generalize effectively to objects in novel categories—i.e., objects that differ substantially in geometry or physical properties from those encountered during training. Many approaches perform well only on limited sets of in-category objects and exhibit poor performance on unseen objects from new categories. The motivation is to enable robots to interact with a broader and more diverse set of objects.

3. **Limitations of Direct Foundation Model Application:** While vision foundation models offer powerful common-sense knowledge and generalization capabilities, their direct application for bimanual affordance prediction is not sufficient. The quality of affordance extraction is constrained by the foundation model's inherent capacity, image quality, and the accuracy of point selection. Moreover, they do not inherently provide or adapt for the precise, coordinated manipulation orientations crucial for successful bimanual tasks. A key motivation is to enhance the utility of foundation models for practical bimanual robotics.

4. **Inefficiency of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Generalization:** Reinforcement learning approaches, often used for learning dual-arm stabilization policies, are typically costly and inefficient for achieving large-scale generalization. They frequently require numerous policy rollouts and extensive training, which is time-consuming for complex bimanual tasks. The paper seeks a more efficient learning paradigm.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the Bi-Adapt framework are:

- To accumulate prior knowledge regarding bimanual manipulation skills from a constrained "supporting set" of known object categories.
- To leverage the semantic correspondence capabilities of vision foundation models to transfer actionable affordances from these known categories to novel object categories. This involves mapping contact points that are semantically similar across different object types.
- To introduce a few-shot adaptation strategy. This strategy will enable fine-tuning of the manipulation policy using a limited number of interactions on instances from novel categories, thereby improving the accuracy of contact point selection and adjustment of action directions for optimal bimanual collaboration.
- To demonstrate that, with limited robot demonstrations on novel categories, the fine-tuned model can achieve superior zero-shot performance on additional, unseen instances within those novel categories.
- To validate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed approach through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments across diverse object categories and challenging bimanual tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Bi-Adapt framework is structured into three main phases: Action Learning on a Supporting Set, Affordance Transfer via Semantic Correspondence, and Few-shot Adaptation. This sequential pipeline is designed to enable efficient generalization of bimanual manipulation.

**A. Action Learning for Building Supporting Set**
The initial phase involves acquiring foundational bimanual manipulation knowledge on a set of known object categories. This knowledge forms a "supporting set" that serves as prior information for subsequent generalization.
The core component is a **Perception Module** designed to learn collaborative visual actionable affordances and interaction policies for dual-gripper tasks. To manage the complexity of the two-gripper action space, composite actions are disentangled into two sequentially conditioned actions. This module comprises two coupled submodules: the First Gripper Module (M1) and the Second Gripper Module (M2). M1 proposes the first gripper's action (u1), and M2 proposes the second gripper's action (u2) conditioned on u1.
During **inference**, the process is straightforward: M1 proposes u1, and M2 proposes u2 conditioned on u1. However, for **training**, a reversed dataflow strategy is adopted to ensure optimal collaboration. M2 is trained first to generate suitable u2 given diverse u1 samples from the dataset. Once M2 can effectively collaborate with various u1, M1 is trained to propose u1 that facilitate successful collaboration with M2.
Each gripper module consists of two networks: an **Action Proposal Network (A)**, which predicts the gripper orientation given a contact point, and an **Action Scoring Network (C)**, which evaluates the suitability of the proposed action for manipulation.

**B. Affordance Transfer**
Inspired by human ability to adapt skills to unfamiliar objects based on prior experience and similar objects, this phase leverages semantic correspondence to transfer affordance from the supporting set to novel categories.
**Affordance Representation and Collection:** Affordance is defined as the specific 3D contact points (p1, p2) on an object where interaction initiates. During data collection on training categories, RGBD images are captured before interaction, and successful contact points are recorded. Only contact points leading to successful outcomes are used for affordance transfer.
**Contact Points Mapping via Semantic Correspondence:** When faced with an unseen object and task, source objects and their associated successful contact points are retrieved from the supporting set. The core of this step is using a **vision foundation model** to map 2D affordances from source objects to target objects. Specifically, diffusion features (DIFT) are extracted from both the source image (Is) and the target image (It). By calculating the cosine similarity between the diffusion features corresponding to a source contact point (p2Ds) and all pixels in the target image (p2Dt,j), the pixel in It with the highest similarity is identified as the corresponding point (p2Dt,i). This 2D pixel is then back-projected to obtain a 3D contact point (p3Dt,i) using the target object's depth map. Recognizing that mapped points might not always be ideal, multiple source images and their successful contact point pairs are used to generate several candidate contact-point pairs for further selection.

**C. Few-shot Adaptation**
Mapped contact points and actions proposed by the pre-trained Perception Module may not be optimal for novel categories due to variations in physical properties and geometries. This phase introduces an efficient few-shot adaptation procedure to refine the manipulation strategy.
The process involves executing proposed actions based on the sampled contact-point pair candidates. The pre-trained Perception Module predicts gripper orientations. Actions with the highest likelihood of success are executed, and the outcomes (success or failure) are collected as online interaction data. This data is then used to fine-tune the Perception Module's networks. This adaptation allows the module to better evaluate proposed actions and adjust them to fit the specific characteristics of the novel categories. Through a limited number of interactions on a few instances within a novel category, the Perception Module learns to explore semantically significant areas identified by the foundation model and refine its understanding of geometric and physical differences. After this adaptation, the refined module is designed to generalize and manipulate other unseen objects from the same novel category without requiring additional interactions.

**D. Network Architecture and Training Strategy**
- **Backbone Feature Extractors:** The Perception Module uses a segmentation-version PointNet++ to extract per-point features from the input point cloud (O). Three MLP networks are used to encode contact points (p) and gripper orientations (R) into respective feature vectors.
- **Action Scoring Loss:** For the Action Scoring Networks (C1, C2), a standard binary cross-entropy loss is employed, supervised by ground-truth interaction results (r=1 for success, r=0 for failure). To ensure critical scoring, the training data is balanced with equal proportions of successful and failed cases.
- **Action Proposal Loss:** The Action Proposal Networks (A1, A2) are implemented as conditional Variational Autoencoders (cVAE). The loss function combines a geodesic distance loss to measure the error between reconstructed and ground-truth gripper orientations, and a KL Divergence term to measure the difference between latent distributions. Crucially, these networks are trained only using data corresponding to successful manipulations.
- **Training Data Flow:** Offline interaction data is generated for training on the supporting (known) categories. For few-shot adaptation on novel categories, affordances are sampled, actions are proposed and executed online, and the real-time interaction results are used to fine-tune the networks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Bi-Adapt through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, addressing four key questions.

**Q1: Overall Performance and Baseline Comparison (Table I)**
Bi-Adapt demonstrates superior performance compared to existing baselines (Heuristic, M-Where2Act, DualAfford) on unseen instances of novel categories across five bimanual manipulation tasks (Unfolding, Opening, Uncapping, Closing, Capping).
- Bi-Adapt achieved success rates ranging from 59.00% (Capping) to 70.00% (Unfolding).
- In contrast, the Heuristic approach showed low success rates (19.82% to 43.20%) due to the difficulty of hand-engineering rules for varied geometries.
- M-Where2Act, a dual-gripper adaptation of Where2Act, struggled significantly (17.30% to 32.40%) on tasks requiring strong coordination.
- DualAfford, a method for learning collaborative visual affordance, exhibited better performance on training categories but saw a substantial drop on novel categories (19.60% to 35.00%), highlighting its generalization limitations without explicit cross-category mechanisms.
The results indicate that Bi-Adapt's integrated approach of leveraging foundation models and few-shot adaptation leads to more robust generalization capabilities for complex bimanual tasks.

**Q2: Cross-category Affordance Generalization (Table II, Figure 5)**
The effectiveness of transferring affordance via semantic correspondence (AT) was investigated through an ablation study ("Ours w/o AT").
- "Ours w/o AT," which lacks foundation-model-guided affordance transfer, exhibited significantly lower success rates on novel categories compared to the full Bi-Adapt framework. For instance, on Unfolding, "Ours w/o AT" achieved 37.76% on unseen instances, while the full Bi-Adapt achieved 70.00%. Similar drops were observed across all tasks.
- Visualizations (Figure 5) illustrate that the affordance maps generated by "Ours w/o AT" for novel categories were less accurate than the contact-point-pair candidates derived using the foundation model in Bi-Adapt.
These findings confirm that the semantic correspondence provided by the foundation model is an essential guidance mechanism for cross-category affordance generalization, substantially improving overall performance.

**Q3: Effectiveness of Adaptation (Table II, Figure 6)**
The impact of the few-shot adaptation (FA) strategy was assessed through another ablation ("Ours w/o FA").
- "Ours w/o FA," which uses the pre-trained Perception Module directly with mapped contact points without any fine-tuning, showed lower success rates than the full Bi-Adapt framework on novel categories. For example, on Unfolding, "Ours w/o FA" achieved 61.90% success on unseen instances, compared to 70.00% for Bi-Adapt.
- Visualizations (Figure 6) demonstrate that after adaptation, the selected contact points and the proposed gripper orientations are more suitable for manipulation on novel categories. The adaptation procedure helps filter unsuitable candidates and refines action directions.
This indicates that the few-shot adaptation strategy is effective in refining the initial proposals from the foundation model and pre-trained networks, adjusting them to the specific geometric and physical properties of new object categories.

**Q4: Efficiency of Few-shot Adaptation (Figure 7)**
The efficiency of Bi-Adapt's few-shot learning for novel categories was evaluated by varying the number of interaction budgets (each budget representing 50 interactions) and comparing it with DualAfford.
- Bi-Adapt demonstrated a higher initial success rate at zero interaction budget (achieved solely through semantic correspondence) compared to DualAfford.
- As the number of interaction budgets increased, Bi-Adapt's success rate improved faster and achieved higher overall performance than DualAfford. For example, on the Closing task, 3 budgets of demonstrations led to a significant increase (over 30%) in success rate compared to no fine-tuning.
The results highlight Bi-Adapt's data efficiency, showing that it can achieve substantial performance gains on unseen instances of novel categories with a limited amount of interaction data.

**Real-World Experiments (Figure 8, Figure 9)**
The framework's capabilities were validated in real-world scenarios using two UFactory xArm6 robots and a RealSense D435 camera.
- The system successfully executed bimanual tasks such as Unfolding, Opening, and Uncapping on various physical objects.
- The real-world experiments further demonstrated the framework's generalization ability, showing successful manipulation of varied unseen instances across different categories (e.g., Laptop, Tool Cabinet, Cardboard Box for the unfolding task).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Bi-Adapt framework introduces a methodology that addresses key challenges in robotic bimanual manipulation, specifically regarding efficient generalization to novel object categories. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Generalization for Bimanual Manipulation:** The core contribution is enabling robots to perform complex bimanual tasks on objects from novel categories, a long-standing challenge for existing methods. By combining prior knowledge with cross-category affordance transfer, Bi-Adapt significantly expands the operational scope of bimanual robotic systems beyond their training data.

2. **Data Efficiency and Reduced Training Costs:** The few-shot adaptation strategy is a critical element, allowing for substantial performance improvements on novel objects with a limited number of interactions. This data efficiency mitigates the costly and time-consuming data collection and training processes typically associated with complex robotic tasks, making the deployment of bimanual robots in new environments more practical and economically feasible.

3. **Effective Integration of Foundation Models:** The work demonstrates a practical and effective way to leverage the emergent semantic correspondence capabilities of vision foundation models for active robotic manipulation. By using these models for cross-category affordance mapping, the framework bridges the gap between high-level semantic understanding and specific action planning, extending the utility of foundation models into the realm of dexterous bimanual control.

4. **Improved Robustness and Adaptability:** The adaptation phase, which refines contact points and action orientations, enhances the robustness of the manipulation strategy to physical and geometric variations inherent in novel object categories. This leads to more reliable task execution even when faced with objects that were not explicitly part of the initial training data.

5. **Foundation for Future Robotic Autonomy:** Bi-Adapt contributes a foundational framework that could pave the way for more autonomous and versatile robotic systems. By reducing the need for extensive retraining for every new object, it facilitates the development of robots that can learn and adapt on the fly, a crucial step towards general-purpose manipulation. This work also sets the stage for future research into more complex, long-horizon tasks involving multiple objects and sequential actions, which currently remain a limitation.

In summary, Bi-Adapt offers a practical and efficient solution for enabling bimanual robots to generalize effectively to novel objects, thereby advancing the capabilities of robotic systems in diverse, human-centric environments.
