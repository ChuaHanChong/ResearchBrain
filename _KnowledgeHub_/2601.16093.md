# SAMTok: Representing Any Mask with Two Words

> [!summary] Summary
> Purdue University, ByteDance, and NUS researchers developed SAMTok, a framework that converts any image segmentation mask into two discrete language tokens, allowing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to achieve pixel-wise understanding and generation through standard next-token prediction. This non-intrusive approach enhances MLLMs like QwenVL with state-of-the-art capabilities in various vision-language tasks, including interleaved text-mask generation and multi-round interactive segmentation, while simplifying their training process.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs struggle with fine-grained pixel-wise understanding and generation due to complex, fragmented architectures, since integrating mask capabilities requires co-training with disparate losses and intricate forward pipelines, making it hard to scale.
- Applying discrete reinforcement learning to mask generation is challenging because current segmentation outputs are continuous.
- Previous mask representations (e.g., special image tokens, RLE encoding) incur high inference costs, often requiring many tokens per mask.

## Method

- **SAMTok** uses a mask Variational Autoencoder with ==residual quantization== to represent any 2D mask as two discrete, special language tokens, effectively creating a 'new language' for masks.
- It integrates SAMTok into base MLLMs by treating mask tokens as part of the MLLM's vocabulary, allowing fine-tuning with only standard next-token prediction loss, without architectural modifications.
- The framework applies Reinforcement Learning using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a novel, purely textual answer-matching reward function for mask generation, leveraging its discrete token representation.

## Results

- SAMTok achieves high-fidelity mask reconstruction, preserving fine structures, and ablation studies confirm the superiority of Residual Quantization.
- The integrated MLLM (QwenVL-SAMTok) achieves new state-of-the-art performance on interleaved text-mask generation (GCG) and multi-round interactive segmentation (MR-RefCOCO/+/g, MR-PACO), plus strong results on text-to-mask (GRES, RefCOCO/+/g, GroundingSuite) and mask-to-text (DLC-Bench, MDVP-Bench, VideoRefer-D) tasks, often matching specialized models.
- Reinforcement Learning with the textual answer-matching reward significantly boosts mask generation performance (e.g., +**6.8%** gIoU and +**18.9%** N-acc on GRES), marking the first successful optimization of mask generation using only textual rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing complex 2D segmentation masks as just two discrete tokens, via Residual Quantization for high-fidelity compression and reconstruction, provides a compact and efficient 'language' - preserving fine details in a compact codebook - for MLLMs to process pixel-wise information.
> - MLLMs can acquire robust pixel-wise capabilities non-intrusively by unifying mask understanding and generation into a next-token prediction problem.
> - Discrete mask tokenization enables the successful application of reinforcement learning with a purely textual reward, simplifying optimization for mask generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "SAMTok" addresses challenges in the rapidly evolving field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), particularly concerning their ability to interact with and understand visual information at a pixel-wise level. MLLMs have demonstrated significant advancements in general visual-language understanding; however, extending these capabilities to fine-grained pixel-level interactions, such as segmentation and grounding, remains an active area of research.

Current pixel-wise MLLMs face several limitations. First, there is a lack of a unified design to handle both mask-in (understanding a given region) and mask-out (generating a region) inputs efficiently. Existing approaches often rely on complex region-level feature pooling or specialized segmentation decoders, which introduce architectural complexity. While bounding boxes or points can offer alternative representations for region input/output, they frequently compromise precision and introduce ambiguity compared to detailed masks. Second, integrating reinforcement learning (RL) for mask generation is challenging because many state-of-the-art pixel-wise MLLMs use continuous embeddings to connect MLLMs with segmentation heads, which are not directly compatible with the discrete nature often required for effective RL. Third, the necessity of co-training MLLMs with specialized modules and distinct loss functions for mask understanding and generation adds substantial complexity to the training pipeline, making it difficult to scale with diverse multimodal and purely textual data. Finally, prior attempts to represent masks purely as text (e.g., Run-Length Encoding or polygons) typically result in verbose representations, requiring dozens or hundreds of tokens per mask, which significantly increases inference costs and token budgets.

SAMTok aims to circumvent these limitations by proposing a discrete mask tokenizer that converts any region mask into a compact, information-rich representation of only two special tokens. By treating masks as a new language that MLLMs can understand and generate through standard next-token prediction, SAMTok seeks to simplify the process of endowing MLLMs with pixel-wise capabilities. This approach positions SAMTok as a method for non-intrusive integration of fine-grained visual understanding and generation, aiming to make it as straightforward as training for vision-question answering (VQA) or pure text data, without requiring extensive architectural modifications or specialized loss designs in the base MLLM.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a non-intrusive method to equip base multimodal large language models (MLLMs) with robust pixel-wise understanding and generation capabilities. This is driven by the desire to streamline the learning process, making it comparable in simplicity to standard next-token prediction tasks used in vision-question answering (VQA) or pure text scenarios, thereby eliminating the need for architectural modifications or specialized loss functions within the core MLLM.

The key motivations and specific objectives contributing to this overarching goal are:

1. **Unified and Bidirectional Mask Representation**: The primary motivation is to overcome the fragmentation in existing pixel-wise MLLMs, which often struggle to handle both mask-in (understanding an existing region) and mask-out (generating a new region) tasks within a single, coherent framework. The objective is to design a discrete mask tokenizer, named SAMTok, capable of accurately converting any 2D region mask into a compact textual representation (specifically, two special tokens) and faithfully reconstructing the mask from these tokens. This bidirectional capability is crucial for versatile interaction.
2. **Compact and Efficient Mask Encoding**: Current text-based mask representations (e.g., RLE or polygon strings) are often prohibitively long, consuming significant token budgets and increasing inference latency. A key objective is to achieve a highly efficient and compact mask representation using a minimal number of tokens (two), while preserving the fine-grained information necessary for high-fidelity mask reconstruction. This efficiency is vital for practical scalability and reduced computational overhead.
3. **MLLM Integration without Architectural Modification**: A core motivation is to simplify the integration of pixel-wise capabilities into existing MLLMs. Many current approaches require adding complex region-level encoders, specialized segmentation decoders, and integrating these modules with customized training objectives. The research aims to enable MLLMs to acquire mask understanding and generation abilities solely by treating the tokenized masks as an extended vocabulary, allowing standard next-token prediction loss for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) without altering the MLLM's internal architecture or introducing task-specific losses.
4. **Facilitating Reinforcement Learning for Mask Generation**: A significant challenge in pixel-wise MLLMs is the effective application of reinforcement learning (RL) to improve mask generation. This is often hampered by the continuous nature of segmentation embeddings and the difficulty of defining language-aligned rewards. The motivation here is to leverage the discrete nature of SAMTok's representation to enable direct application of established RL algorithms. Furthermore, a specific objective is to design a purely textual answer-matching reward function for generated masks, better aligned with language-domain RL mechanisms, thereby allowing MLLMs to benefit from RL in mask generation without relying on external mask decoders or complex IoU-based reward computations.
5. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance across Diverse Tasks**: Ultimately, the research aims to validate the effectiveness and generality of the SAMTok paradigm by demonstrating competitive or state-of-the-art performance across a broad spectrum of region-level tasks. These include mask understanding tasks (region captioning, region VQA), mask generation tasks (grounded conversation generation, referring segmentation, scene graph parsing), and interleaved understanding-generation tasks (multi-round interactive segmentation).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on SAMTok, a discrete mask tokenizer designed to bridge continuous 2D region masks with discrete textual tokens, thereby enabling MLLMs to process masks as a new language.

#### 3.1. SAMTok Architecture

SAMTok is conceptualized as a mask Variational Autoencoder (VAE) integrated with vector quantization, leveraging properties from different model types: VAE for bidirectional conversion, SAM for accurate segmentation and compact embedding, and vector quantization for discretizing continuous latents. Its architecture comprises an encoder, a quantizer, and a decoder. Both the encoder and decoder are instantiated from the SAM (Segment Anything Model) framework, specifically SAM2.

- **SAMTok Encoder (f_enc)**: This component is built upon a SAM model, with its final mask prediction head removed. It processes an input image `I` and a 2D region mask `M`. The SAM prompt encoder (`f_prm`) encodes the input mask `M` into dense prompt embeddings. These embeddings are then combined with image features, extracted by SAM's image backbone (`f_img`), and fed into the SAM mask decoder (`f_msk`). The output of this process is a `d`-dimensional continuous mask embedding `z`.
- **Quantizer**: A residual quantization (RQ) scheme is employed to discretize the continuous mask embedding `z`. This scheme is chosen for its ability to achieve high-fidelity quantization using a compact codebook. The quantization process involves two steps:
 1. The continuous embedding `z` undergoes a nearest-neighbor lookup in a predefined codebook `C` to identify the first latent embedding, `e_1`.
 2. A residual embedding, `r_1 = z - e_1`, is computed. This residual then undergoes a second nearest-neighbor lookup in the same codebook `C` (or a separate one) to obtain `e_2`.
 3. The final discrete representation of the mask is a pair of latent embeddings, `q = [e_1, e_2]`.
- **SAMTok Decoder (f_dec)**: The decoder is a full SAM model. It receives the original image `I` and the discrete mask embeddings `[e_1, e_2]`. These discrete embeddings are treated as special language prompt embeddings. Within SAM's prompt encoder (`f_prm`), these embeddings are summed to form a sparse prompt embedding. This sparse prompt interacts with the image features and a pre-initialized mask embedding within SAM's mask decoder (`f_msk`), allowing it to recover features corresponding to the continuous mask embedding `z`. Finally, the mask prediction head of the SAM decoder reconstructs the 2D region mask, `M_hat`.

#### 3.2. Training of SAMTok

SAMTok is trained for the mask reconstruction task on a large-scale dataset of 209 million diverse masks. This dataset is compiled from various open-source segmentation datasets, covering a range of scenarios (indoor, outdoor, UI) and granularities (part-level, object-level, entity-level, semantic-level). The training objective combines a reconstruction loss (`L_recon`) and a commitment loss (`L_commit`). The reconstruction loss is the sum of cross-entropy loss and Dice loss between the original mask `M` and the reconstructed mask `M_hat`. The commitment loss `L_commit` ensures that the continuous embeddings are close to their quantized versions. The encoder and decoder of SAMTok are initialized with pretrained weights from SAM2.1 (Large). During training, the SAM image encoder and prompt encoder parameters are frozen, while the SAM decoder is trainable. The default configuration uses a codebook size of 256 and two quantization steps, with two non-shared codebooks.

#### 3.3. Unified Mask-Token Interface for MLLMs

SAMTok transforms region masks into two discrete tokens, enabling MLLMs to process them as new language elements.
- **Token Integration**: Special mask tokens (e.g., `<|mt_0000|>` to `<|mt_0511|>`) are added to the MLLM's vocabulary. The first 256 tokens correspond to `e_1`, and the next 256 to `e_2`. Special start (`<|mt_start|>`) and end (`<|mt_end|>`) tokens are also introduced.
- **Mask Understanding**: For tasks requiring mask input (e.g., region captioning), the SAMTok encoder tokenizes the given mask into its corresponding special tokens, which are then inserted into the textual prompt of the MLLM.
- **Mask Generation**: For tasks requiring mask output (e.g., referring segmentation), the MLLM predicts the sequence of special mask tokens. During inference, the SAMTok decoder converts these predicted tokens back into 2D segmentation masks.
- **Co-training MLLM**: A large-scale dataset of approximately 5 million interleaved mask-text samples is collected. This dataset covers various tasks, including mask generation (grounding, referring segmentation, GCG, instance segmentation, scene parsing) and region understanding (region captioning, region-based VQA), as well as collaborative tasks (multi-turn interactive reasoning segmentation). All masks in these samples are pre-tokenized by SAMTok and reformatted into dialogue-style text sequences. The MLLM (QwenVL series) is then fine-tuned using a standard next-token prediction loss, without any task-specific losses or architectural modifications to the base MLLM. The MLLM image encoder is frozen, and only the projection layer and LLM are fine-tuned.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning for Mask Generation

SAMTok's discrete mask representation enables the direct application of RL for mask generation.
- **RL Algorithm**: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is adopted.
- **Textual Answer-Matching Reward**: A novel reward function `R_mask = N_TP / max(N_pred, N_gt)` is proposed. `N_TP` is the count of true positive mask words in the model's response that match ground truth. `N_pred` is the total predicted masks (without deduplication to penalize repetition), and `N_gt` is the ground truth mask count. This reward is purely textual, eliminating the need for mask de-tokenization or external tools for correctness verification during RL training.
- **RL Data**: A mix of 26k cold-start samples (generated with chain-of-thought prompting from Qwen3-VL-235B), 8k challenging samples from GRES, and 41k from GCG are used for GRPO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental results demonstrating the capabilities of SAMTok across a variety of pixel-wise understanding and generation tasks.

**SAMTok Reconstruction Quality**:
The ablation studies on SAMTok's region-mask reconstruction capability, evaluated on the EntitySeg validation set, show that SAMTok, particularly with Residual Quantization (RQ), achieves high fidelity. Using a 1024x2 codebook size (two residual codebooks of 1024 each), SAMTok achieves an r-Acc (reconstruction accuracy, measured by mask IoU) of 0.70. This performance is competitive with or superior to other quantization strategies like VQ and FSQ, especially given the compact codebook size. The decoupling of SAMTok from the MLLM ensures that its reconstruction quality remains stable, unlike methods requiring joint training that can lead to degraded mask quality.

**Interleaved Text-Mask Generation Tasks (GCG)**:
On the Grounded Conversation Generation (GCG) benchmark, QwenVL-SAMTok achieves state-of-the-art performance. Compared to previous best methods, it demonstrates improvements across both captioning metrics (METEOR, CIDEr) and mask metrics (AP50, mIoU, Recall) on both validation and test sets. For instance, on the validation set, it shows gains of +1.3% METEOR, +5.5% CIDEr, +5.3% AP50, +5.2% mIoU, and +4.7% Recall, indicating a more precise text-mask alignment.

**Multi-round Interactive Segmentation Tasks (MR-RefCOCO/+/g, MR-PACO)**:
SAMTok facilitates significant advancements in multi-round interactive segmentation, achieving new state-of-the-art results. On MR-RefCOCO/+/g, QwenVL-SAMTok surpasses the previous best by an average of +7.7% cIoU across all interaction rounds. On the MR-PACO benchmark, it yields an even larger average improvement of +10.7%. These results suggest enhanced capabilities in fine-grained spatial reasoning, compositional mask generation, and maintaining long-term visual-linguistic consistency across complex multi-turn interactions, including hierarchical part-whole relationships.

**Text-to-Mask Task (GRES, RefCOCO/+/g, GroundingSuite)**:
For text-to-mask tasks, SAMTok-integrated MLLMs, despite being trained solely with next-token prediction loss, demonstrate strong performance. On GRES, the approach outperforms existing methods that rely on task-specific losses, achieving +1.5% in average gIoU and a substantial +4.3% in average N-acc across all three splits. On RefCOCO/+/g, it shows competitive performance, setting a new state-of-the-art among models with fewer than 4 billion parameters. Furthermore, in zero-shot evaluation on GroundingSuite, SAMTok exhibits superior generalization (67.8 gIoU compared to 62.6 for other region-level MLLMs), indicating that explicit mask supervision is not strictly necessary for effective text-to-mask reasoning.

**Mask-to-Text Task (DLC-Bench, MDVP-Bench, VideoRefer-D)**:
In mask-to-text tasks like region captioning, QwenVL-SAMTok achieves performance comparable to expert models such as DAM on DLC-Bench (65.6 vs. 67.3), significantly outperforming general MLLMs like Qwen2.5VL-7B (41.2). This highlights SAMTok's ability to provide unambiguous region grounding for text generation. In zero-shot evaluations, the method surpasses the expert model DAM in three out of four metrics on MDVP-Bench and achieves competitive performance on the VideoRefer-D video region captioning benchmark, demonstrating strong generalization to diverse visual scenes and modalities.

**Visual Grounding (REC)**:
Comparing SAMTok's mask-token interface with native text-box interfaces for visual grounding, experiments on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg show substantial accuracy improvements. SAMTok yields gains (e.g., +3.6% for RefCOCO val with the 3B model) while preserving natural-language interaction, suggesting that the mask-token representation is more effective than bounding box or point formats for precise visual grounding.

**Reinforcement Learning Results (GRES and GCG)**:
The application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) using the proposed purely textual answer-matching reward function leads to significant performance improvements. On GRES, RL yields an average gain of +6.8% gIoU, +4.9% cIoU, and +18.9% N-acc. On GCG, the mask metrics improve by +4.5% AP50, +2.0% mIoU, and +6.6% Recall on average (though caption quality metrics decreased as they were not optimized). This marks a successful attempt to optimize mask generation performance using only text-based reward signals, demonstrating SAMTok's capability for tighter text-mask alignment under language-based reinforcement.

**SAMTok Integration Across MLLMs**:
Experiments confirm that SAMTok and MLLM are decoupled, allowing SAMTok to be deployed effectively with different MLLM architectures, including models with tile-based visual encoders (e.g., PerceptionLM) and adaptive-resolution encoders (e.g., Qwen-VL series).

**Qualitative Visualizations**:
Visual examples across tasks such as panoptic scene graph generation, generalized referring expression segmentation, region captioning, and grounded conversation generation qualitatively support the quantitative results. They show SAMTok's effectiveness in joint generation of structured relations and pixel-level masks, robust handling of complex referential language, accurate region-level descriptions, and precise localization of multiple phrases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SAMTok represents a significant development in the field of multimodal large language models, particularly for integrating pixel-wise capabilities. Its contributions carry implications for future research and the practical application of intelligent systems.

**Novel Paradigm for Pixel-wise MLLMs**: SAMTok proposes a new paradigm by treating region masks as a new language, tokenized into discrete, compact special words. This approach fundamentally rethinks how MLLMs handle fine-grained visual information, enabling them to learn mask understanding and generation using standard language model training objectives (next-token prediction) and existing MLLM architectures. This eliminates the need for complex, task-specific segmentation decoders, specialized architectural modifications, or additional loss designs that often complicate the development and scaling of pixel-wise MLLMs.

**Enhanced Scalability and Unification**: By simplifying the integration of pixel-wise capabilities, SAMTok offers a more scalable and unified interface. It allows MLLMs to process diverse mask-related tasks (understanding, generation, interactive scenarios) within a consistent, text-based framework. This unification can streamline the training process, making it as straightforward as training for vision-question answering or general language tasks, thereby fostering the development of more versatile and capable multimodal systems. The decoupling of SAMTok from the MLLM also means it can be readily integrated with various MLLM backbones, enhancing its generalizability.

**Advancements in Reinforcement Learning for Mask Generation**: A notable impact of SAMTok is its enablement of reinforcement learning (RL) for mask generation using purely textual reward signals. This is a crucial breakthrough, as previous pixel-wise MLLMs often struggled with RL due to continuous feature spaces or the complexity of integrating external mask decoders for reward computation. SAMTok's discrete mask tokens facilitate direct application of RL algorithms and allow for language-aligned reward functions, which can lead to more stable and effective optimization of mask generation performance. This opens new avenues for leveraging RL to improve the reasoning and interactive capabilities of pixel-wise MLLMs.

**Broad Performance Improvements**: The experimental results demonstrate that SAMTok-integrated MLLMs achieve state-of-the-art or comparable performance across a wide array of benchmarks, including interleaved text-mask generation, multi-round interactive segmentation, text-to-mask, and mask-to-text tasks. This indicates the robustness and practical utility of the proposed method in diverse real-world scenarios requiring precise pixel-level understanding and interaction. The improvements in zero-shot generalization also highlight its potential for handling unseen or novel visual concepts.

**Future Research Directions**: The paper outlines several promising avenues for future work, including extending SAMTok to reconstruct video region masks and represent other visual entities beyond masks, such as points, lines, and bounding boxes. Such extensions could lead to more flexible and comprehensive visual-language interactions. Further applications in general VQA, image generation, and editing tasks are also suggested, indicating SAMTok's potential as a foundational component for advanced multimodal AI systems.

In summary, SAMTok offers a significant contribution by simplifying and unifying the integration of pixel-wise capabilities into MLLMs, making these models more scalable, easier to train, and more amenable to advanced learning paradigms like reinforcement learning. This work points towards a more language-native and efficient future for pixel-level visual reasoning within multimodal AI.
