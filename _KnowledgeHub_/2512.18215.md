# Stable and Efficient Single-Rollout RL for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent AI Lab and university collaborators developed Multimodal Stabilized Single-Rollout (MSSR), an efficient and stable Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). It employs entropy-based advantage shaping to prevent training collapse, leading to stable learning and improved generalization on multimodal reasoning benchmarks by up to 2.3% while significantly enhancing training compute efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing group-based RLVR methods for MLLMs are computationally inefficient, requiring multiple rollouts per input and incurring high costs from redundant forward passes through expensive encoders.
- Group-based approaches suffer from inefficient rollout utilization when all responses in a group yield identical outcomes, resulting in a 'zero advantage' and wasted learning signals.
- Direct application of efficient single-rollout RLVR strategies to multimodal contexts leads to severe training instability and collapse, largely due to high input variance and complex cross-modal credit assignment.

## Method

- The Multimodal Stabilized ==Single-Rollout== (**MSSR**) framework extends vanilla single-rollout RLVR to multimodal settings, generating only one response per input to maximize expected verifiable rewards.
- An adaptive baseline estimation is employed using a Beta distribution, where its discount factor is dynamically adjusted based on the KL divergence between successive policy updates.
- A key innovation, entropy-based advantage shaping, is introduced to the advantage calculation; this mechanism encourages exploration and maintains sufficient policy entropy, preventing mode collapse and stabilizing training.

## Results

- MSSR successfully prevents entropy collapse and achieves stable training dynamics, unlike the unstable Multimodal Vanilla Single-Rollout (MVSR) baseline, leading to consistent accuracy improvements.
- MSSR demonstrates superior training compute efficiency, achieving comparable validation accuracy to group-based GRPO with half the total training steps and significantly fewer redundant forward passes.
- The framework consistently outperforms group-based and other single-rollout baselines across five diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, boosting average accuracy by up to **2.3%** for the Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal single-rollout RLVR is inherently unstable and prone to entropy collapse without explicit stabilization, which is exacerbated by the high dimensionality and complexity of multimodal inputs.
> - Entropy-based advantage shaping is crucial for stabilizing single-rollout RL training in complex multimodal environments, allowing policies to maintain exploration and avoid premature convergence to suboptimal modes.
> - Dynamically adapting the baseline discount factor based on policy change provides a robust mechanism to tune the learning rate, enhancing stability and convergence of the policy updates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of large multimodal models, reinforcement learning, and efficient training methodologies, addressing a critical bottleneck in the advancement of AI reasoning capabilities.

The broader landscape of AI has seen a significant shift towards Large Language Models (LLMs) and, more recently, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as foundational models for a wide array of tasks. A crucial step in deploying these models effectively is fine-tuning them to align with specific objectives or human preferences. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) was an early and successful paradigm for this, but it is resource-intensive due to the reliance on human annotators. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) emerged as a more scalable alternative, replacing subjective human feedback with objective, automatically verifiable correctness signals, particularly useful for tasks like mathematical reasoning where answers are unequivocally right or wrong.

While RLVR has shown promise, especially in multimodal contexts where MLLMs learn to reason over both text and images, existing methods face significant challenges. Prevalent group-based RLVR algorithms, such such as GRPO, require generating multiple "rollouts" or responses for each input. This multi-rollout sampling incurs substantial computational costs, especially for large MLLMs, as it necessitates repeated forward passes through both computationally expensive vision and language encoders. Moreover, these methods can suffer from inefficiencies when all rollouts within a group yield identical outcomes, leading to a "zero advantage" and a wasted learning signal.

In response to these inefficiencies, single-rollout RLVR methods have been explored in text-only settings, demonstrating improved computational efficiency. However, a significant gap remained: extending these efficient single-rollout approaches to the multimodal domain proved highly challenging. The inherent complexity of multimodal inputs, characterized by high-dimensional visual data and the intricate problem of cross-modal credit assignment (attributing success or failure across different modalities), introduced severe instability issues, often leading to training collapse for naive single-rollout strategies.

This paper directly tackles this critical "training efficiency-stability trade-off" in multimodal RLVR. It aims to bridge the gap between efficient text-only single-rollout methods and the demands of complex multimodal reasoning, which hitherto had been dominated by less efficient group-based approaches. By demonstrating a stable and efficient single-rollout solution for MLLMs, the authors contribute to the broader goal of scaling multimodal AI, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and less computationally prohibitive. The work not only builds upon the foundation of RLVR but also carves out a new path for its application in resource-constrained or high-throughput scenarios involving MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that is both **computationally efficient** and **training stable**, without compromising, and ideally improving, multimodal reasoning performance.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Computational Inefficiency of Group-Based RLVR:** Prevalent multimodal RLVR methods, such as GRPO, require generating a "group" of multiple responses (rollouts) for each input to calculate relative advantages. This multi-rollout sampling is extremely costly for MLLMs, as it involves numerous redundant forward passes through expensive vision and language encoders. The authors are motivated to reduce this computational burden significantly.
2. **Overcoming Rollout Utilization Inefficiency:** A related issue with group-based methods is that when all rollouts in a group yield identical outcomes (e.g., all correct or all incorrect), the relative advantage collapses to zero. This leads to a lack of a learning signal and inefficient utilization of generated rollouts, hindering effective policy updates.
3. **Solving Instability of Naive Single-Rollout Approaches in Multimodal Settings:** While single-rollout RLVR variants have been successfully explored in text-only settings for their efficiency, the authors discovered that directly applying these methods to multimodal contexts results in severe instability and frequent training collapse. This instability is attributed to the increased input variance from dense, high-dimensional visual inputs and the complexities of cross-modal credit assignment. There was a clear need for a method that could harness the efficiency of single-rollout without succumbing to this instability in the multimodal domain.
4. **Improving Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** Ultimately, the motivation is to enhance the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. By developing a more stable and efficient training paradigm, the goal is to enable models to learn more effectively from verifiable rewards, leading to improved generalization and higher accuracy across diverse multimodal reasoning tasks.

In essence, the paper aims to resolve the fundamental trade-off between training compute efficiency (desiring single-rollout for speed) and training stability (a challenge for single-rollout in multimodality), a critical bottleneck for scaling RLVR to large multimodal models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, **MSSR (Multimodal Stabilized Single-Rollout)**, is a group-free RLVR framework designed to achieve stable optimization and effective multimodal reasoning with high computational efficiency. It builds upon a vanilla single-rollout formulation and introduces a crucial entropy-based advantage shaping mechanism to address stability issues.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task involves a multimodal input `x = (x_text, x_image)` and a policy model `π_θ(o|x)` that generates a single output `o`. A binary verifiable reward function `r(x, o) ∈ {0, 1}` evaluates the correctness. The objective is to maximize the expected verifiable reward.

#### 3.2. Multimodal Vanilla Single-Rollout (MVSR)

As a foundational step, the authors first generalize text-only single-rollout RLVR to the multimodal domain, termed MVSR.
- **Single Rollout:** For each multimodal input, only one response `o` is generated.
- **Bernoulli Reward Model:** Since the reward `r(x, o)` is binary, it is modeled as a Bernoulli random variable.
- **Beta Distribution for Baseline Estimation:** The expected reward (baseline value) `v_hat(x)` is estimated using a Beta distribution `B(α(x), β(x))`, which is a conjugate prior for the Bernoulli distribution. The baseline is `v_hat(x) = α(x) / (α(x) + β(x))`.
- **Adaptive Parameter Update:** After observing a reward, the Beta parameters are updated: `α(x) ← η * α(x) + r(x, o)` and `β(x) ← η * β(x) + (1 - r(x, o))`. Here, `η` is an adaptive discount factor (`η ∈ [η_min, η_max]`) that varies during training.
- **Adaptive Discount Factor (η) Adjustment:** `η` is adjusted based on the KL divergence between consecutive policy updates over a sliding window `N`. If KL divergence is high (rapid policy change), `η` decreases (faster forgetting); if low (stable updates), `η` increases (slower forgetting). This mechanism helps in adapting to policy evolution.
- **Advantage Calculation and Normalization:** The advantage `A(x, o)` is calculated as `r(x, o) - v_hat^(-1)(x)` (using the baseline from the previous step to avoid bias). These advantages are then normalized across the batch to reduce variance.
- **Initialization:** The baseline `v_hat^0(x)` is initialized with rewards from an initial policy `π_0`, and Beta parameters `α_0(x), β_0(x)` are set accordingly.

#### 3.3. Multimodal Stabilized Single-Rollout (MSSR)

The core innovation of MSSR addresses the instability observed in MVSR due to high stochasticity and variance from single binary rewards, amplified by multimodal inputs, leading to entropy collapse.
- **Entropy-Based Advantage Shaping:** MSSR incorporates an entropy bonus `ψ_t` directly into the advantage calculation. The shaped advantage is `A_hat_t = A_t + ψ_t`.
- **Entropy Bonus Definition:** `ψ_t = min(|A_t| / γ, λ * stopgrad(H_t))`, where `H_t` is the token entropy of the policy's output distribution, and `γ, λ` are scaling factors. `stopgrad` prevents gradients from flowing through `H_t` to avoid unintended policy changes.
- **Intuition:** This mechanism encourages policies to assign higher effective advantage to responses generated under higher uncertainty, even if their reward is momentarily low. This promotes exploration, softens penalties for "near-miss" low-reward responses, and maintains sufficient policy entropy, preventing mode collapse and stabilizing training.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 7B, trained to produce structured outputs with reasoning (`<think></think>`) and final answers (`\boxed{}`).
- **Dataset:** Vision-R1-RL, a multimodal math reasoning dataset (10K samples with real-world images) where rewards are binary and verifiable by exact match.
- **Training Details:** 120 steps on 8 GPUs, AdamW optimizer (learning rate `1e-6`, weight decay `0.01`). Hyperparameters for entropy shaping: `γ = 0.4`, `λ = 2.0`. Discount factor range for Beta: `η_min = 0.875`, `η_max = 0.96`. Sliding window `N = 20`, target KL `KL_target = 0.01` for `η` adjustment. KL regularization to a reference policy with `0.01` coefficient.
- **Baselines:**
 - Group-based: GRPO, RLOO (batch size `256 prompts * 8 rollouts = 2048`).
 - Group-free: REINFORCE++ (single-rollout, batch size `2048 prompts * 1 rollout = 2048`, for fair comparison of total rollouts).
 - Prior SFT+RL and Zero-RL 7B models (for broader context).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Five diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks for generalization: MathVerse, MathVista, MMK12, R1-Onevision-Bench, and HallusionBench. Accuracy measured by extracting answers using Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and matching ground truth.
- **Ablation Studies:** Explored alternatives to entropy shaping: KL regularization (alone), cross-modal regularization (parallel text-only branch as an anchor), and entropy loss term in the policy gradient.
- **Sensitivity Analysis:** Investigated the impact of `N` (sliding window size) and `KL_target` (target KL divergence) on validation accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly validate MSSR's effectiveness in achieving both stability and efficiency in multimodal RLVR, surpassing existing baselines.

- **Training Stability and Entropy Preservation:** The Multimodal Vanilla Single-Rollout (MVSR) approach, while aiming for efficiency, exhibits severe instability. Training and validation accuracy degrade over time, a phenomenon strongly correlated with **entropy collapse** (Figure 3). In stark contrast, MSSR, by integrating entropy-based advantage shaping, effectively **prevents entropy collapse** and achieves stable training dynamics, leading to consistent improvements in both training and validation accuracy (Figures 1a, 1b).
- **Superior Training Compute Efficiency:** MSSR demonstrates significantly higher rollout utilization efficiency compared to the strong group-based GRPO baseline. It achieves similar final validation accuracy to GRPO with **half the total training steps** (Figure 1). Despite introducing a minimal overhead per training step for baseline estimation (e.g., 6.9 vs. 6.1 mins/step for 7B models, Table 3 in Appendix), its reduced total training steps translate to substantial compute savings.
- **Enhanced Generalization Performance:** When trained for comparable computational budgets (equivalent total number of rollouts per step), MSSR consistently **outperforms** group-based baselines (GRPO, RLOO) and other group-free variants (REINFORCE++) across five diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVista, MMK12, R1-Onevision-Bench, HallusionBench). For the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, MSSR boosts average accuracy by 2.1% over GRPO, and for the 7B model, it improves by 2.3% (Table 1). The Qwen2.5-VL-7B + MSSR configuration achieves the strongest overall mean performance among all evaluated methods.
- **Ablation Studies Confirm Necessity of Entropy Shaping:** Extensive ablation studies reveal that alternative stabilization strategies are insufficient for multimodal single-rollout RLVR:
 - **KL Regularization** with a reference policy, while applied, is insufficient on its own to prevent entropy collapse (as seen in MVSR's behavior).
 - **Cross-modal regularization** (using a parallel text-only branch as an anchor) provides only partial stabilization; training accuracy improves, but validation accuracy still degrades, and overall performance remains below MSSR (Figures 4a, 4b, 4c).
 - Adding an **entropy loss term** partially preserves entropy but fails to prevent its collapse entirely, resulting in degraded validation performance (Figures 4a, 4b, 4c). These findings underscore that entropy-based advantage shaping is "essential" for stabilizing single-rollout training in the complex multimodal setting.
- **Reasoning Granularity Improvement:** MSSR-trained policies produce more fine-grained and detailed reasoning steps, as evidenced by a higher average number of key steps (3.3 for MSSR vs. 1.9 for GRPO and 3.1 for the base model) and longer average response lengths (Table 2). This suggests that MSSR encourages a more robust, structured, and less collapsed reasoning process (Figure 5 shows an illustrative example where MSSR provides a correct, detailed breakdown while GRPO fails).
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Analysis of the sliding window size `N` and target KL divergence `KL_target` (for adaptive `η` adjustment) shows that `N = 20` and `KL_target = 0.01` yield the highest validation accuracy towards the end of training (Figures 6a, 6b).

In summary, the results demonstrate that MSSR successfully overcomes the stability challenges of single-rollout RLVR in multimodal contexts while delivering significant improvements in training efficiency and generalization performance, making it a robust and superior approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of multimodal AI and reinforcement learning, with several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Resolving a Critical Trade-off for MLLMs:** The primary significance lies in successfully addressing the fundamental trade-off between training compute efficiency and stability in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). By enabling stable single-rollout RLVR, MSSR removes a major bottleneck that previously limited the scalability and practical applicability of RL-based fine-tuning for complex MLLMs. This breakthrough allows for efficient learning from objective rewards without succumbing to the instability often associated with sparse or high-variance feedback in multimodal settings.
2. **Accelerating MLLM Development and Deployment:** The increased compute efficiency means that MLLMs can be fine-tuned faster and with fewer computational resources. This will accelerate the development cycle for new MLLM architectures and applications. For industry, this translates into lower operational costs and faster iterations on model improvements, making advanced multimodal reasoning more accessible and economical for real-world products and services.
3. **Advancing Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities:** The consistent improvements in generalization performance across diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVista, MMK12, etc.) demonstrate that MSSR not only makes training more efficient but also leads to more capable and robust MLLMs. This could foster the development of MLLMs that exhibit more sophisticated and reliable reasoning over both visual and textual inputs, crucial for applications in scientific discovery, education, intelligent assistants, and complex decision-making systems.
4. **Methodological Insight into RL Stability:** The paper's systematic study and ablation analyses pinpoint entropy-based advantage shaping as an "essential" mechanism for stabilizing single-rollout RLVR in the multimodal context. This finding is a significant methodological contribution, offering a specific, empirically validated technique that can guide future research in RL for high-dimensional, noisy, or complex action spaces. It highlights that the unique challenges of multimodality demand specialized stabilization strategies beyond those effective in text-only settings.
5. **Encouraging Further Research in Multimodal RL:** By successfully demonstrating a stable single-rollout approach, the work opens new avenues for research into other aspects of multimodal RL, such as novel reward design, more sophisticated baselines, and advanced exploration strategies tailored for cross-modal interactions. It underscores the unique properties of multimodal data and signals the need for multimodal-specific solutions in RL.
6. **Broader Impact on AI Systems:** Ultimately, more efficient and stable training of MLLMs with enhanced reasoning capabilities will contribute to the development of more intelligent and versatile AI systems. These systems could better understand and interact with the world through multiple sensory modalities, leading to breakthroughs in fields such as autonomous systems, medical diagnosis, content creation, and personalized learning experiences.

In conclusion, MSSR represents a crucial step forward in making advanced multimodal reasoning with reinforcement learning both practical and high-performing, paving the way for the next generation of intelligent agents capable of complex understanding and problem-solving across diverse data types.
