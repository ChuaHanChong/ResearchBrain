# Inner Monologue: Embodied Reasoning through Planning with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Robotics at Google developed the Inner Monologue framework, which enables Large Language Models to robustly plan and adapt robotic tasks by incorporating real-time, closed-loop natural language feedback from the environment. This approach achieved up to 100% success in real-world tabletop tasks and maintained high performance in mobile manipulation even with adversarial disturbances, unlike prior open-loop methods.

## Problem

- Prior Large Language Model (LLM) based robotic planning systems were largely open-loop, generating plans without reacting to real-time action outcomes or environmental changes.
- This open-loop nature made robotic systems fragile and prone to failure in dynamic, uncertain real-world environments where actions can fail or perceptions are noisy.
- Traditional robotic control methods often struggle with semantic understanding, generalization, and adaptation to novel or unprogrammed scenarios.

## Method

- Developed a closed-loop framework that continuously feeds textual natural language feedback from the robot's environment into the ==Large Language Model=='s (LLM) planning prompt.
- The LLM, acting as a planner, adapts its plan in real-time based on this feedback, without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning of the LLM itself, relying on few-shot prompting.
- Integrates multiple types of natural language feedback, including success/failure detection, object recognition, task progress, and active queries for human input.

## Results

- Inner Monologue consistently outperformed open-loop baselines (e.g., CLIPort, SayCan) across simulated and real-world tabletop and mobile manipulation tasks, especially under disturbances.
- In a real-world 3-block stacking task, the system's success rate increased from **20%** (with only object feedback) to **100%** (with object and success feedback).
- When faced with adversarial disturbances in mobile manipulation, Inner Monologue achieved a **75.0%** success rate, significantly higher than SayCan's **0.0%** due to its ability to recover from failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time, closed-loop natural language feedback from the environment enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform robust planning and self-correction in dynamic robotic tasks.
> - A combination of object detection, action success detection, and task progress tracking feedback is crucial for LLMs to effectively manage complex states and recover from errors.
> - Integrating such feedback unlocks emergent capabilities in LLMs, including adapting to new instructions, proposing alternative goals when faced with infeasibility, and interacting in multiple languages, without fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Inner Monologue: Embodied Reasoning through Planning with Language Models" introduces a novel approach to enhance the reasoning and planning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) for embodied agents, particularly robots, by integrating closed-loop natural language feedback from the environment. This work posits that enabling LLMs to form an "inner monologue" by leveraging real-time observations allows them to robustly plan and adapt in dynamic robotic control scenarios, addressing a critical limitation of prior open-loop LLM planning methods.

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: robotic control, task and motion planning, and the application of large language models (LLMs) to embodied AI.

Traditionally, robotic intelligence has relied on approaches like Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) or Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL). TAMP involves separating high-level discrete task planning from low-level continuous motion planning, often through symbolic reasoning or optimization. HRL, on the other hand, structures learning into different levels of abstraction. While effective, these methods often struggle with semantic understanding, generalization, and adaptation to novel, unprogrammed scenarios.

The emergence of LLMs has dramatically shifted the landscape. These models, trained on vast quantities of text data, have demonstrated not only remarkable fluency in language generation but also surprising capabilities in reasoning, deduction, and world knowledge inference when appropriately prompted. This has led researchers to explore their potential beyond natural language processing, particularly in embodied contexts. Prior work, such as "Language Models as Zero-Shot Planners" and "SayCan" (both with significant author overlap with the current paper), showed that LLMs could be prompted to decompose abstract high-level instructions into sequences of low-level, executable robot skills. These works highlighted the potential of LLMs as high-level planners, leveraging their semantic understanding to interpret human commands and generate task plans.

However, a critical limitation of these early LLM-as-planner approaches was their largely "open-loop" nature. The LLM would propose a sequence of actions, often assuming each step would succeed. This made them fragile in dynamic, real-world environments where actions can fail, perceptions can be noisy, or the environment might change unexpectedly.

"Inner Monologue" directly addresses this gap. It positions itself as the "critical link" that moves beyond simply planning with language to *informing embodied feedback with language*. Inspired by human cognitive processes, where an "inner monologue" allows for self-correction and adaptation based on observations, this work enables the LLM to process natural language feedback from its environment. This allows for replanning, error recovery, and more robust task completion. It differentiates itself from related works like Socratic Models by focusing specifically on the LLM's capacity to *reason over* this language-based environmental feedback for robust planning, rather than just using language as a common interface for multimodal reasoning or conditioning policies. By providing a framework for closed-loop interaction, this paper advances the field toward more intelligent, adaptive, and human-like robotic agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate the extent to which Large Language Models (LLMs), when applied in embodied contexts such as robotic control, can effectively reason over various sources of feedback provided through natural language, *without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning* of the LLM itself. The goal is to achieve this solely through clever prompting and integration with external perception and control modules.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Open-Loop Planning:** Prior LLM-based planning systems for robotics often operate in an open-loop fashion, generating a plan without explicitly considering or reacting to the outcomes of executed actions. Real-world robotic tasks are inherently stochastic and dynamic; actions can fail, objects might be occluded, or the environment might change. Robots need to be able to detect failures, understand the new environmental state, and adapt their plans accordingly.
2. **Mimicking Human Problem-Solving:** Humans frequently engage in an "inner monologue" when solving complex tasks. This internal dialogue involves proposing actions, observing their immediate outcomes, evaluating success or failure, and then making corrective choices. The authors are inspired by this cognitive process, hypothesizing that integrating a similar feedback loop into LLM-based planning could enhance robotic reasoning.
3. **Leveraging LLMs' Inherent Capabilities:** LLMs have demonstrated a remarkable ability to understand, generate, and reason with natural language, along with possessing rich internalized world knowledge. The motivation is to tap into these pre-existing capabilities for embodied reasoning, rather than requiring extensive, domain-specific training. This also aligns with the paradigm of "foundation models" – highly capable models that can be adapted to new tasks with minimal additional effort.
4. **Natural Language as a Universal Interface:** The paper posits that natural language provides a universal and interpretable interface for grounding communication between different modules (LLM, perception models, human users) within a robotic system. This allows for a flexible and intuitive way to convey environmental state and task progress to the LLM.
5. **Enabling Robustness and New Capabilities:** The ultimate motivation is to develop robotic systems that are more robust to errors and disturbances, capable of handling long-horizon tasks, and potentially exhibiting emergent behaviors such as self-correction, adaptation to new instructions, and interactive scene understanding, all while being more amenable to human interaction.

By focusing on how LLMs can *process and plan more richly* in response to language-based feedback, the authors aim to push embodied AI towards greater autonomy and intelligence in unpredictable real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of "Inner Monologue" revolves around a closed-loop framework that continuously injects textual feedback from the robot's environment into the Large Language Model's (LLM) planning prompt. This allows the LLM to "think" and adapt its plans in real-time, much like a human's inner monologue.

#### 3.1. Inner Monologue Formulation

The system operates on a high-level natural language instruction *i*. The robotic agent has access to a library of pre-trained, short-horizon skills ($\pi_k \in \Pi$) each with a corresponding language description ($l_k$). The LLM, acting as the "planner," attempts to find a sequence of these skills to accomplish *i*. Crucially, the planner receives textual feedback *o* from the environment. This feedback can be automatically provided or actively requested by the LLM. The core idea is to continuously append this textual feedback to the LLM's prompt, allowing it to adapt its plan based on the environment's state and the outcomes of its actions. This "grounded environment feedback" promotes improved LLM reasoning in complex, long-horizon settings, even before any external affordance-based grounding methods are applied. The system leverages pre-trained LLMs without finetuning, relying entirely on few-shot prompting.

#### 3.2. Sources of Feedback

The paper investigates various types of environment feedback, all of which are translated into natural language and fed back to the LLM:

- **Success Detection:** This is binary feedback indicating whether the most recent low-level skill execution was successful. In simulation, this can be based on ground-truth state; in real-world settings, it relies on learned or heuristic-based detectors. The output is linguistic (e.g., "Success: True" or "Action was not successful.").
- **Passive Scene Description:** This type of feedback is consistently provided and follows a structured format, without being actively queried by the LLM.
 - **Object Feedback:** Provides a list of objects currently present in the scene, obtained via object recognition models (e.g., "I see: coke, water, chocolate bar.").
 - **Scene Feedback (Task-Progress):** Describes the semantic sub-goals inferred by the LLM towards completing the high-level instruction that have been achieved so far. This helps the LLM track overall task progress.
- **Active Scene Description (Human Feedback):** This is a proactive form of feedback where the LLM can directly ask questions about the scene, the task, or even user preferences. A human (or a VQA model, though humans are used in this work) provides an unstructured natural language response. This allows the LLM to gather ambiguous or missing information (e.g., "Do you want water or coke?").

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The authors implement and evaluate Inner Monologue across three diverse robotic manipulation settings:

1. **Simulated Tabletop Rearrangement (Ravens-based):**
 - **LLM:** InstructGPT-1.3B.
 - **Low-level Policies:** A pre-trained language-conditioned pick-and-place primitive, similar to CLIPort/Transporter Nets.
 - **Feedback:** Scripted modules for Object (list of objects), Success (binary outcome), and Scene (achieved sub-goals). Chain-of-thought prompting is used with Scene feedback.
 - **Tasks:** Rearranging blocks and bowls in desired configurations (e.g., "putting blocks in matching bowls"). Includes both seen and unseen tasks.
 - **Disturbances:** Gaussian noise injected at multiple levels (pixel observation, policy primitive, place locations) to simulate real-world stochasticity.
 - **Baselines:** A multi-task CLIPort policy trained directly on long-horizon task instructions (with and without oracle termination).

2. **Real-World Tabletop Rearrangement:**
 - **Robot:** UR5e arm with wrist-mounted Intel RealSense RGB-D camera.
 - **LLM:** InstructGPT-1.3B.
 - **Low-level Policies:** A zero-shot pick-and-place policy using an LLM to parse target objects, then executing scripted suction-based primitives at bounding box centers.
 - **Feedback:** Pretrained open-vocabulary object recognition (MDETR) for Object feedback; heuristics on bounding box predictions for Success Detection.
 - **Tasks:** 3-block stacking and sorting food/condiments into different plates.
 - **Disturbances:** Artificially injected Gaussian noise into policy actions to stress-test recovery.
 - **Baselines:** Variants of Inner Monologue with different feedback combinations (open-loop, Object only, Success only, Object + Success).

3. **Real-World Mobile Manipulation in a Kitchen Setting:**
 - **Robot:** Everyday Robots mobile manipulator.
 - **LLM:** PALM-540B.
 - **Low-level Policies:** Value functions from pre-trained control policies for affordance grounding (similar to SayCan), including learned manipulation (e.g., counter picking, drawer ops) and scripted navigation.
 - **Feedback:** Learned visual classification model for Success Detection; human-provided object recognition for Object feedback; and human-provided unstructured responses for Active Scene Description (when LLM asks questions).
 - **Tasks:** Manipulation, dexterous manipulation with drawers, and long-horizon combined manipulation/navigation tasks (e.g., "throw away soda on the table").
 - **Disturbances:** Adversarial disturbances added during control policy executions to force failures and test recovery.
 - **Baselines:** SayCan (which grounds actions but does not provide closed-loop feedback to the LLM).

This multi-domain, multi-feedback, and multi-LLM experimental design provides a comprehensive case study on the value and versatility of the Inner Monologue formulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments across diverse robotic domains consistently demonstrate that providing LLMs with closed-loop natural language feedback significantly enhances their ability to reason, plan, and execute complex tasks, particularly in the presence of uncertainties and failures.

#### 4.1. Performance Across Domains

- **Simulated Tabletop Rearrangement:**
 - All variants of Inner Monologue (IM) consistently outperformed the CLIPort baseline, especially on unseen long-horizon tasks and under test-time disturbances. For instance, on the "Put the blocks on mismatched bowls" unseen task, IM with Object + Scene achieved 86.0% success, while CLIPort + oracle had 0.0%.
 - The combination of "Object + Scene" feedback yielded the best performance. This indicates that tracking task progress (Scene feedback) in addition to visible objects (Object feedback) is crucial for the LLM to effectively manage complex combinatorial states and overall task completion.
 - The results highlight that LLM-informed feedback enables the planner to retry actions or replan in the face of failures, a capability that non-hierarchical, open-loop systems like CLIPort lack.

- **Real-World Tabletop Rearrangement:**
 - Inner Monologue (Object + Success) achieved dramatically higher success rates compared to baselines. For "Finish 3-block stacking," performance jumped from 20% (Object only) to 100% (Object + Success). For "Sort fruits from bottles," it increased from 20% to 80%.
 - This domain explicitly showed the complementary nature of object recognition and success detection feedback. Without closed-loop scene description, initially occluded objects might not be part of the LLM's plan. Without success detection, the system could not recover from failed pick-and-place actions caused by policy noise. The combined feedback allows for robust recovery from both perception and execution errors.

- **Real-World Mobile Manipulation in a Kitchen Setting:**
 - Even without adversarial disturbances, Inner Monologue improved upon the SayCan baseline (e.g., from 50.0% to 75.0% for Manipulation tasks).
 - The most striking improvements were observed in scenarios with *adversarial disturbances*. SayCan's success rate plummeted close to 0% in such cases, as it lacks an explicit high-level retry behavior. In contrast, Inner Monologue, particularly with "Object + Success" feedback, maintained significantly higher success rates (e.g., 75.0% for Mobile Manipulation tasks, compared to 0.0% for SayCan).
 - This demonstrates IM's superior robustness and ability to invoke appropriate recovery modes, from simply retrying a failed action to completely replanning the sequence of skills when faced with systematic infeasibility (e.g., an object being too heavy).

#### 4.2. Emergent Capabilities

Beyond quantitative performance improvements, the Inner Monologue framework unlocked several surprising emergent capabilities in the LLM, often without explicit examples in the few-shot prompts:

- **Continued Adaptation to New Instructions:** The LLM could seamlessly incorporate mid-task goal changes from human users, even handling complex instructions like reverting to a "previous task."
- **Self-Proposing Goals under Infeasibility:** When faced with an impossible sub-goal (e.g., attempting to pick up an intentionally "too heavy" object), the LLM could re-reason, identify the infeasibility, and propose an alternative, achievable sub-goal ("I need to find a lighter block.").
- **Multilingual Interaction:** Leveraging the LLM's inherent multilingual understanding, the system could interpret and act upon instructions given in different natural languages (e.g., Chinese) and even symbols or emojis, translating them into English commands for execution.
- **Interactive Scene Understanding:** After a task, the LLM could answer open-ended questions about the current scene, demonstrating an understanding derived from its past actions and the feedback it received (e.g., identifying objects in a bowl or their locations).
- **Robustness to Feedback Order and Typos:** The LLM showed a surprising resilience to variations in the input prompt structure and common typographical errors in human instructions.

While the consistency of these emergent capabilities varied, their mere presence highlights the potential of LLM-driven embodied reasoning when augmented with closed-loop feedback.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Inner Monologue" framework represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI and robotics, particularly in harnessing the power of Large Language Models for complex real-world tasks.

**Enhanced Robustness and Resilience:** The most immediate and profound impact is on the robustness of robotic systems. By enabling LLMs to receive and reason over real-time feedback, robots can now actively detect and recover from failures, adapt to dynamic environmental changes, and handle unforeseen circumstances. This capability is paramount for deploying robots outside controlled laboratory settings and into real-world applications (e.g., homes, factories, public spaces) where unpredictable events are commonplace. This work moves LLM-driven robotics from fragile, open-loop planning to resilient, closed-loop adaptation.

**Efficiency and Scalability:** A key strength of this approach is its efficiency. The significant performance improvements are achieved *without any additional training or fine-tuning* of the LLM itself. Instead, it relies on few-shot prompting and the inherent reasoning and world knowledge already encoded within the pre-trained LLM. This makes the system highly scalable, as new tasks or environmental variations can be addressed by simply modifying the prompts and integrating appropriate feedback modules, rather than undergoing costly and time-consuming model retraining.

**Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** The "inner monologue" paradigm naturally fosters more intuitive and flexible human-robot collaboration. Robots can engage in more sophisticated dialogues, ask clarifying questions, adapt to mid-task human interventions, and provide explanations of their actions or observations. This shift towards a more "thoughtful" and interactive robot makes them more approachable and useful partners for humans.

**Advancing Foundation Models in Robotics:** This research further solidifies the role of LLMs as powerful "foundation models" for robotics. It demonstrates how these versatile models can serve as central cognitive engines, integrating perception, planning, and control through a unified language interface. This could lead to a future where general-purpose AI models form the backbone of highly capable and adaptable robotic systems, reducing the need for extensive task-specific engineering.

**Future Research Directions:** The paper also opens up several exciting avenues for future research:
- **Automated Feedback Generation:** While some feedback in this work relies on humans or scripted modules, fully automating the generation of rich, accurate textual scene descriptions and success detections from learned vision-language models (e.g., advanced image/video captioning or Visual Question Answering systems) would unlock full autonomy.
- **Uncertainty and Ethical Considerations:** Developing methods for LLMs to handle and communicate uncertainty in feedback modules, or to incorporate safety and ethical constraints into their planning, is crucial for responsible deployment.
- **Low-Level Policy Development:** The performance of LLM reasoning is still bottlenecked by the capabilities of the underlying low-level control policies. Future work needs to continue improving these basic skills to fully leverage the LLM's high-level intelligence.
- **Consistency of Emergent Capabilities:** While impressive, the emergent behaviors were not always perfectly consistent. Further investigation into prompt engineering, LLM architectures, or fine-tuning techniques specifically for robust embodied reasoning could make these capabilities more reliable.

In conclusion, "Inner Monologue" provides a compelling demonstration of how integrating closed-loop natural language feedback empowers LLMs to become more robust, adaptive, and interactive planners for embodied robotic agents. It marks a significant step towards building truly intelligent and resilient robots capable of operating effectively in the complexities of the real world.
