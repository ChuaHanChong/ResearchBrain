# Inner Monologue: Embodied Reasoning through Planning with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Robotics at Google developed the Inner Monologue framework, which enables Large Language Models to robustly plan and adapt robotic tasks by incorporating real-time, closed-loop natural language feedback from the environment. This approach achieved up to 100% success in real-world tabletop tasks and maintained high performance in mobile manipulation even with adversarial disturbances, unlike prior open-loop methods.

## Problem

- Prior Large Language Model (LLM) based robotic planning systems were largely open-loop, generating plans without reacting to real-time action outcomes or environmental changes.
- This open-loop nature made robotic systems fragile and prone to failure in dynamic, uncertain real-world environments where actions can fail or perceptions are noisy.
- Traditional robotic control methods often struggle with semantic understanding, generalization, and adaptation to novel or unprogrammed scenarios.

## Method

- Developed a closed-loop framework that continuously feeds textual natural language feedback from the robot's environment into the ==Large Language Model=='s (LLM) planning prompt.
- The LLM, acting as a planner, adapts its plan in real-time based on this feedback, without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning of the LLM itself, relying on few-shot prompting.
- Integrates multiple types of natural language feedback, including success/failure detection, object recognition, task progress, and active queries for human input.

## Results

- Inner Monologue consistently outperformed open-loop baselines (e.g., CLIPort, SayCan) across simulated and real-world tabletop and mobile manipulation tasks, especially under disturbances.
- In a real-world 3-block stacking task, the system's success rate increased from **20%** (with only object feedback) to **100%** (with object and success feedback).
- When faced with adversarial disturbances in mobile manipulation, Inner Monologue achieved a **75.0%** success rate, significantly higher than SayCan's **0.0%** due to its ability to recover from failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time, closed-loop natural language feedback from the environment enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform robust planning and self-correction in dynamic robotic tasks.
> - A combination of object detection, action success detection, and task progress tracking feedback is crucial for LLMs to effectively manage complex states and recover from errors.
> - Integrating such feedback unlocks emergent capabilities in LLMs, including adapting to new instructions, proposing alternative goals when faced with infeasibility, and interacting in multiple languages, without fine-tuning.
