# When Robots Should Say "I Don't Know": Benchmarking Abstention in Embodied Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces AbstainEQA, the first human-annotated benchmark designed to evaluate embodied agents' ability to recognize and abstain from answering ambiguous or unanswerable questions. It demonstrates that current Vision-Language Models significantly underperform in abstention, often relying on linguistic shortcuts rather than genuine visual understanding, which impacts safety and navigation efficiency in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) benchmarks implicitly assume all questions are answerable, forcing agents to respond even with insufficient information.
- Forcing embodied agents to answer ambiguous queries can lead to "hallucinations" (fabricated or incorrect answers) with tangible safety risks in physical environments.
- There is a lack of systematic evaluation for embodied agents' uncertainty awareness and their ability to withhold responses when appropriate, hindering trustworthy human-robot interaction.

## Method

- Conducted a human study confirming that 32.4% of naturally posed EQA queries are ambiguous, and established a ==five-category abstention taxonomy== based on cognitive limitations.
- Developed **AbstainEQA**, the first ==human-annotated benchmark== with 3,272 QA pairs (augmented to 16,360) specifically designed to evaluate abstention in embodied environments (ScanNet and HM3D).
- Evaluated state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and adaptation strategies (prompting, reasoning, supervised fine-tuning) on their abstention capabilities and impact on embodied navigation using an ==LLM-as-a-Judge== framework.

## Results

- The best frontier VLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieved only **42.79%** abstention recall, significantly below human performance of **91.17%**, struggling most with "Referential Underspecification" and "Preference Dependence" categories.
- Ambiguous queries reduced Active EQA navigation success rates from **77.17%** to **61.41%** and increased exploration effort, indicating inefficient and uncalibrated behavior under uncertainty.
- Supervised fine-tuning yielded illusory gains; SFT models' high performance persisted even with randomized or absent visual inputs, demonstrating an over-reliance on linguistic patterns rather than visual evidence for abstention decisions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial proportion of human questions naturally contain ambiguity, highlighting the practical necessity for embodied agents to possess abstention capabilities.
> - Current frontier VLMs and standard adaptation techniques often fail to achieve robust abstention, frequently relying on superficial textual cues rather than true multimodal understanding and uncertainty grounding.
> - Genuine uncertainty awareness is a fundamental prerequisite for safe and efficient embodied navigation and is critical for building trustworthy human-robot interaction systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and under-explored gap in the field of Embodied Question Answering (EQA). EQA tasks traditionally require an agent to interpret natural language, perceive a 3D environment, navigate within it, and provide an answer to a query. Significant progress has been made, particularly with the advent of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which have enhanced embodied reasoning, semantic grounding, and exploration capabilities (e.g., OpenEQA, 3D-MEM). However, a pervasive implicit assumption in most existing EQA benchmarks (e.g., EQA-v1, IQUAD, MP3D-EQA, OpenEQA) is that *every* question posed to the agent is answerable and *must* be answered.

In contrast, real-world human-robot interaction is fraught with ambiguity, underspecification, and incomplete information. Forcing an embodied agent to respond in such situations often leads to "hallucinations" – fabricated or incorrect answers – which are particularly problematic in physical environments. Unlike linguistic hallucinations, these can translate into unsafe actions, damaged property, or even physical harm to humans. Previous work has explored hallucination in LLMs (Huang et al., 2025) and how to manage uncertainty in text and static image/video domains (Min et al., 2020; Kirichenko et al., 2025; Gurari et al., 2018). There has also been work on clarification in embodied agents, where agents learn to ask follow-up questions to resolve ambiguity (Dogan et al., 2024; Ramrakhya et al., 2025).

This paper, "When Robots Should Say 'I Don't Know'," positions abstention as a fundamental prerequisite for effective clarification and safe human-robot interaction. It argues that before an agent can clarify, it must first recognize *when* it lacks sufficient information or when a query is fundamentally unanswerable. This work builds upon the foundational EQA frameworks (like OpenEQA, which provides answerable queries and a VLM-driven approach) by explicitly introducing the challenge of unanswerable or ambiguous queries. By developing AbstainEQA, the authors provide the first human-annotated benchmark for evaluating an agent's ability to withhold answers, thereby bridging the gap between current idealized EQA setups and the messy reality of human communication. This moves the field towards more robust, safe, and trustworthy embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:
1. **Quantify the need for abstention:** Determine how frequently humans naturally pose questions that embodied agents should abstain from answering in real-world scenarios.
2. **Establish a systematic taxonomy of abstention types:** Categorize the diverse forms of ambiguity and insufficient evidence that necessitate abstention in embodied settings, drawing from cognitive theories of human communication errors.
3. **Develop a novel, large-scale benchmark (AbstainEQA):** Create the first human-annotated dataset specifically designed to evaluate embodied agents' ability to recognize and abstain from ambiguous queries, alongside their ability to provide correct responses to answerable ones. This benchmark aims to measure genuine environmental perception rather than mere textual pattern matching.
4. **Benchmark state-of-the-art EQA agents:** Conduct a comprehensive evaluation of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and adaptation strategies (scaling, prompting, fine-tuning) on abstention capabilities to reveal their limitations.
5. **Analyze the impact of abstention on embodied navigation:** Investigate how ambiguous queries affect an agent's exploration strategies and navigational efficiency in active EQA settings.
6. **Provide insights for future research:** Through detailed error analyses, identify core challenges and suggest directions for developing more uncertainty-aware and reliable embodied agents.

The core motivation stems from the critical safety and reliability concerns associated with embodied agents that are forced to answer every question. Fabricated or incorrect answers in physical environments can lead to tangible risks, such as an elderly user slipping due to a robot falsely claiming a floor is dry, or inefficient navigation due to ambiguous instructions. The authors argue that knowing "when to say 'I don't know'" is a fundamental prerequisite for building trustworthy and effective human-robot collaborative systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a multi-faceted approach combining human studies, dataset curation, and extensive benchmarking:

**A. Quantifying the Need for Abstention:**
- A large-scale questionnaire survey was conducted with 50 non-expert participants, who were shown egocentric videos from HM3D and ScanNet environments.
- Participants were asked to generate free-form QA pairs for randomly sampled videos.
- Manual review of 500 collected QA pairs revealed that 32.4% of human queries were ambiguous and required abstention.

**B. Taxonomy of Abstention Types:**
- Drawing on Norman's cognitive framework of human error, the authors categorized ambiguous queries into five types based on agents' cognitive limitations and human subjective instructions:
 - **Actionability Limitation (AL):** Questions requiring physical interaction (e.g., opening a box) beyond the agent's passive visual perception.
 - **Referential Underspecification (RU):** Ambiguous references when multiple entities match a description (e.g., "the white cabinet" when there are many).
 - **Information Unavailability (IU):** Lack of essential spatial (e.g., area of a room) or temporal (e.g., "who put the vase here?") information from observations.
 - **False Presupposition (FP):** Queries based on assumptions contradicting observed evidence (e.g., "What material is the teddy bear on the bed?" when no teddy bear is present).
 - **Preference Dependence (PD):** Questions relying on subjective judgment or aesthetic evaluation (e.g., "Is the painting beautiful?").

**C. Dataset Curation (AbstainEQA):**
- **Data Annotation:** Human annotators viewed videos from ScanNet and HM3D and generated two QA pairs for each of the five abstention types, ensuring balanced coverage. For Active EQA (A-EQA), 184 questions from 3D-MEM were used, preserving original target objects to focus on navigation effects.
- **Dataset Size:** 1,636 abstention cases were annotated and combined with 1,636 answerable cases from OpenEQA, totaling 3,272 unique QA pairs.
- **Data Augmentation:** Questions were paraphrased into five semantically equivalent variants using an LLM, resulting in 16,360 QA pairs for increased diversity.
- **Frame-Level Annotations:** For clear queries, "Answer Frames" (sufficient visual evidence) were marked. For ambiguous queries, "Cause Frames" (visual evidence for abstention reason) and brief textual explanations were provided. This explicitly links visual context to both answers and abstention decisions.
- **Quality Control:** All annotations were reviewed by two additional annotators for correctness and consistency.

**D. Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Response Accuracy:** GPT-4o was used as an "LLM-as-a-Judge" (LLM-Match protocol) to quantify semantic alignment between agent predictions and ground-truth answers on a 1-5 scale, normalized to 0-100%.
- **Abstention Recognition:** GPT-4o also determined if an agent's output constituted a valid abstention, based on a structured evaluation prompt. Metrics included Recall, Precision, Accuracy, and F1-score for abstention detection.
- **Embodied Navigation Evaluation (for A-EQA):** Standard metrics were used: Success Rate (SR), Total Frames (TF), Total Snapshots (TS), and Path Length (PL).
- **Human Validation:** A random subset of 300 samples was human-validated against LLM evaluation, yielding a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.88, confirming the reliability of the LLM-based evaluation framework.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
- **Models Evaluated:** State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) including proprietary models like Claude-Sonnet-4.5, GPT-5, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and open-source models from the Qwen family (Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B).
- **Model Scaling:** Performance was analyzed across different model sizes within the Qwen family and proprietary models.
- **Adaptation Strategies:**
 - **Explicit Prompting:** Coarse-grained (minimal guidance) and fine-grained (explicit taxonomy and rules) prompts were tested to influence abstention behavior.
 - **Explicit Reasoning ("Thinking"):** Models were prompted to generate reasoning chains before answering/abstaining.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** SFT variants were trained, including models with randomized visual inputs or text-only inputs, to assess reliance on visual versus linguistic cues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several critical findings:

1. **Prevalence of Ambiguous Queries:** The initial human study confirmed that 32.4% of naturally posed questions to embodied agents are ambiguous or lack sufficient context, underscoring the real-world necessity for abstention capabilities.

2. **Significant Performance Gap in Abstention:** Even the best frontier VLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieved only 42.79% abstention recall, a stark contrast to human performance of 91.17%. Most other VLMs scored below 33%. Models performed relatively better on "Information Unavailability" due to clear absence of visual evidence but struggled with "Referential Underspecification" and "Preference Dependence," which demand pragmatic disambiguation or subjective judgment.

3. **Navigation Inefficiency Under Ambiguity:** In Active EQA, ambiguous queries significantly reduced the success rate (from 77.17% to 61.41%) and increased exploration effort (Total Frames, Total Snapshots increased). Path lengths exhibited unstable behavior, oscillating between premature termination (e.g., due to semantic ambiguity) and over-exploration (e.g., trying to find unavailable information), indicating a lack of calibrated exploration policies under uncertainty.

4. **Inconsistent Impact of Model Scaling:** While larger proprietary models generally showed higher abstention rates, these improvements were inconsistent across abstention categories. Within specific model families (e.g., Qwen), scaling improved abstention performance, but this did not generalize across different architectures. This suggests that abstention is influenced more by model design, cross-modal alignment, and training objectives than by parameter count alone.

5. **Prompting Increases Recall at the Cost of Precision:** Both coarse- and fine-grained prompts substantially boosted abstention recall (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 28.61% to 75.86% with coarse prompt), but significantly reduced precision, leading to over-abstention even when evidence was sufficient. This indicates that prompting modulates a model's tendency to abstain but doesn't fundamentally improve its ability to genuinely assess task resolvability.

6. **Reasoning Prompts Offer Marginal or Negative Gains:** Explicit "thinking" prompts, designed to encourage step-by-step reasoning, did not substantially improve abstention reliability. Smaller models showed marginal gains, while larger models often experienced declines in both recall and F1-score, suggesting that reasoning often led to verbose explanations rather than calibrated uncertainty assessment.

7. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Overfits to Textual Cues:** SFT models achieved high recall and F1-scores, but these gains were found to be illusory. Their performance barely changed when visual inputs were randomized or completely removed, and text-only baselines performed comparably. Error analysis and word-frequency distributions revealed that SFT models relied on specific linguistic patterns (e.g., "who," "design") in queries rather than grounding their abstention decisions in visual evidence. The same query could elicit the same (wrong) abstention response across visually different scenes where one was answerable and the other was not.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a highly significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and human-robot interaction by fundamentally shifting the focus from merely *answering* questions to *knowing when to abstain*.

**Significance:**
- **Addresses a Core Limitation:** It exposes a critical and previously under-benchmarked limitation of current EQA systems: their inability to recognize uncertainty and withhold unreliable answers. This moves beyond the "hallucination" problem in a purely linguistic sense to its dangerous implications in physical environments.
- **Pioneering Benchmark:** AbstainEQA is the first human-annotated benchmark specifically designed for abstention in embodied settings, providing a crucial tool for future research. Its detailed taxonomy, paired clear/ambiguous queries, and frame-level annotations offer a rich resource for developing and evaluating uncertainty-aware agents.
- **Challenges Current Paradigms:** The findings challenge the efficacy of common strategies like model scaling, prompting, and standard fine-tuning for instilling genuine uncertainty awareness. It demonstrates that these methods often lead to superficial gains, overfitting to textual cues rather than robust multimodal reasoning.
- **Prerequisite for Trust and Safety:** By highlighting abstention as a prerequisite for clarification and safe interaction, the paper provides a clear research direction towards building more trustworthy and reliable embodied agents that can effectively collaborate with humans without causing harm or frustration.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Safer Embodied AI:** Future domestic robots, assistive robots for the elderly, or industrial robots equipped with abstention capabilities will be safer, avoiding actions based on fabricated information.
- **More Trustworthy HRI:** Users will have greater trust in robots that can explicitly communicate their limitations ("I don't know" or "I need more information") rather than fabricating answers, leading to more transparent and effective human-robot collaborations.
- **Foundation for Advanced Clarification:** This work lays the groundwork for more sophisticated clarification strategies, where agents first identify the *need* for clarification (abstention) before deciding *how* to clarify (e.g., asking specific follow-up questions or initiating further exploration).
- **New Research Directions:** The paper identifies clear challenges for future work, emphasizing the need for training paradigms that explicitly couple model outputs with visual evidence, develop uncertainty-aware navigation policies, and construct datasets specifically designed to mitigate textual shortcut biases. This will drive innovation in grounded reasoning, uncertainty quantification, and adaptive exploration in embodied AI.

In conclusion, "When Robots Should Say 'I Don't Know'" is a landmark paper that systematically defines, quantifies, and benchmarks a critical missing capability in embodied AI, setting a new standard for evaluating the robustness and safety of intelligent agents in the real world.
