# When Robots Should Say "I Don't Know": Benchmarking Abstention in Embodied Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces AbstainEQA, the first human-annotated benchmark designed to evaluate embodied agents' ability to recognize and abstain from answering ambiguous or unanswerable questions. It demonstrates that current Vision-Language Models significantly underperform in abstention, often relying on linguistic shortcuts rather than genuine visual understanding, which impacts safety and navigation efficiency in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) benchmarks implicitly assume all questions are answerable, forcing agents to respond even with insufficient information.
- Forcing embodied agents to answer ambiguous queries can lead to "hallucinations" (fabricated or incorrect answers) with tangible safety risks in physical environments.
- There is a lack of systematic evaluation for embodied agents' uncertainty awareness and their ability to withhold responses when appropriate, hindering trustworthy human-robot interaction.

## Method

- Conducted a human study confirming that 32.4% of naturally posed EQA queries are ambiguous, and established a ==five-category abstention taxonomy== based on cognitive limitations.
- Developed **AbstainEQA**, the first ==human-annotated benchmark== with 3,272 QA pairs (augmented to 16,360) specifically designed to evaluate abstention in embodied environments (ScanNet and HM3D).
- Evaluated state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and adaptation strategies (prompting, reasoning, supervised fine-tuning) on their abstention capabilities and impact on embodied navigation using an ==LLM-as-a-Judge== framework.

## Results

- The best frontier VLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieved only **42.79%** abstention recall, significantly below human performance of **91.17%**, struggling most with "Referential Underspecification" and "Preference Dependence" categories.
- Ambiguous queries reduced Active EQA navigation success rates from **77.17%** to **61.41%** and increased exploration effort, indicating inefficient and uncalibrated behavior under uncertainty.
- Supervised fine-tuning yielded illusory gains; SFT models' high performance persisted even with randomized or absent visual inputs, demonstrating an over-reliance on linguistic patterns rather than visual evidence for abstention decisions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial proportion of human questions naturally contain ambiguity, highlighting the practical necessity for embodied agents to possess abstention capabilities.
> - Current frontier VLMs and standard adaptation techniques often fail to achieve robust abstention, frequently relying on superficial textual cues rather than true multimodal understanding and uncertainty grounding.
> - Genuine uncertainty awareness is a fundamental prerequisite for safe and efficient embodied navigation and is critical for building trustworthy human-robot interaction systems.
