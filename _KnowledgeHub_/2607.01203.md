# GPU-Parallel Linearization Error Bounds for Real-Time Robust Optimal Control of Nonlinear and Neural Network Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces GPUSLS-LEO, a robust optimal control framework that computes tight, differentiable linearization error bounds (LEBs) for both analytic and neural network dynamics in real-time. This framework reduces conservatism by 20-45% and achieves 100% robust constraint satisfaction for complex systems, demonstrating real-time performance up to 67 Hz on GPUs.

## Problem

- Existing robust nonlinear optimal control (RNOCP) methods struggle to accurately bound the 'linearization error' between true nonlinear dynamics and their linear approximations.
- Current linearization error bounding (LEB) methods are often too conservative, computationally expensive for real-time use, or lack differentiability, which prevents their integration into gradient-based optimization.
- Robust control for neural network (NN) dynamics presents unique challenges due to the difficulty in computing analytical properties for NNs, and existing System-Level Synthesis (SLS) frameworks are limited in handling non-zero-centered interval bounds.

## Method

- Developed novel ==path-based Hessian (PBH)== bounds for analytic dynamics and ==CROWN-based affine relaxation== bounds with Jacobian correction for neural network dynamics, providing tight and differentiable ==linearization error bounds (LEBs)==.
- Integrated these LEBs into an extended ==System-Level Synthesis (SLS)== framework, **GPUSLS-LEO**, which uses ==exact zonotopic propagation== for error terms and a novel reformulation for efficient ==Riccati-based== controller optimization with right-invertible disturbance matrices.
- Implemented the entire framework on GPUs using ==JAX==, enabling real-time computation of LEBs and robust control policies through ==automatic differentiation== and parallelization.

## Results

- The Path-Based Hessian method produced the tightest LEBs for analytic systems (e.g., Satellite, Quadrotor) compared to other methods, running in under **1 ms** and improving error interval widths through gradient-based optimization.
- GPUSLS-LEO achieved **100%** robust constraint satisfaction in simulations for diverse systems, including a **168D** coupled quadrotor system and a **5D** neural network-controlled T-pusher, with reduced conservatism (e.g., **20-45%** smaller tubes than baselines).
- The framework demonstrated real-time performance, with control rates up to **67 Hz**, and scalability to high-dimensional systems and long control horizons (**7500** steps) while maintaining well-bounded robust tubes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Path-based Hessian methods can yield significantly tighter local linearization error bounds for analytic dynamics by evaluating Hessian entries over incrementally smaller 'prefix sets.'
> - Existing neural network verification tools like CROWN can be adapted and combined with local Jacobian corrections to derive formally certified and differentiable linearization error bounds for NN dynamics.
> - The differentiability of LEBs allows gradient-based optimization to actively minimize linearization error, leading to tighter reachable tubes and more optimal nominal trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robust nonlinear optimal control (RNOCP) is critical for ensuring the safe and reliable operation of complex autonomous systems such as robots, drones, and spacecraft, particularly when operating under uncertainty. A central challenge in RNOCP is the intractable nature of exact reachability analysis for nonlinear dynamics in real-time. To address this, many contemporary methods approximate nonlinear system dynamics with linear time-varying (LTV) models, which enable more efficient computation of reachable sets and robust control policies. System-Level Synthesis (SLS) is one such LTV-based framework that has demonstrated real-time performance on high-dimensional systems over long horizons.

However, a fundamental limitation of LTV-based approaches is that the guarantees of robust constraint satisfaction are conditional on accurately bounding the "linearization error." This error represents the deviation between the true nonlinear dynamics and their LTV approximation. Existing methods for handling linearization error often face trade-offs:
- **Conservatism:** Global Lipschitz estimates or interval arithmetic over the full state space can lead to overly conservative (loose) reachable tubes, restricting the system's operational envelope.
- **Computational Cost:** Many linearization error bounding (LEB) methods are computationally intensive, suitable only for offline computation, or too slow for real-time planning.
- **Differentiability:** Lack of differentiability in LEBs prevents their direct integration into gradient-based optimization algorithms used by RNOCP solvers, limiting the ability to adapt nominal trajectories to minimize linearization error.
- **Formal Guarantees:** Due to these challenges, some LTV-based RNOCP solvers omit formal consideration of linearization error, compromising formal safety guarantees for computational performance.

This research aims to bridge these gaps by developing LEBs that are simultaneously tight, real-time computable, and differentiable. Furthermore, it extends these capabilities to neural network (NN) dynamics, a rapidly growing area in system modeling where traditional analytical tools for bounding dynamics are often less applicable. By providing formally verified, tight, and efficient LEBs, this work seeks to enable robust optimal control solutions that retain formal guarantees without sacrificing real-time performance or introducing excessive conservatism, thereby advancing the state of the art in safe autonomous system operation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable real-time robust optimal control for uncertain nonlinear systems, including those modeled by neural networks, by providing sound and efficient linearization error bounds. The motivation for this work stems from several limitations in existing robust control methodologies:

1. **Addressing Conservatism in Reachability Analysis:** Current LTV-based robust control methods, while efficient, often rely on global linearization error bounds (e.g., global Lipschitz constants or interval arithmetic over the entire domain). These global bounds can be overly conservative when applied to local trajectories, leading to larger-than-necessary reachable tubes and constraining system performance. A key motivation is to develop *tighter, local* linearization error bounds specific to the trajectory of interest.

2. **Enabling Real-Time Performance:** Many existing methods for computing LEBs are computationally expensive, suitable primarily for offline analysis. For robust model predictive control (RMPC) and other real-time planning paradigms, LEBs must be computable rapidly, ideally within a typical control loop's time budget. This necessitates the development of LEB methods that are amenable to high-performance computing architectures.

3. **Facilitating Gradient-Based Optimization:** For an RNOCP solver to leverage LEBs effectively, these bounds must be differentiable. Differentiability allows the optimizer to use gradients to adjust the nominal trajectory, actively minimizing linearization error and further tightening reachable tubes, leading to more optimal and safer control policies. Current methods often lack this property, forcing solvers to treat LEBs as fixed parameters or to ignore them.

4. **Extending Robust Control to Neural Network Dynamics:** Neural networks are increasingly used to model complex, nonlinear system dynamics, particularly in data-driven settings. However, robust control of NN dynamics presents unique challenges, as computing analytical properties like Hessians for NNs can be difficult. A specific objective is to derive certified LEBs for systems described by neural networks, leveraging advancements in NN verification.

5. **Improving Compatibility with System-Level Synthesis (SLS):** Existing SLS-based robust control solvers often assume zero-centered disturbance sets or employ ellipsoidal overapproximations that introduce further conservatism when dealing with general, non-zero-centered interval bounds, as are typically generated by LEB methods. A motivation is to extend SLS to natively accommodate non-zero-centered disturbance sets and to integrate new LEB formulations using precise zonotopic propagation, avoiding unnecessary overapproximations.

6. **Providing Formal Guarantees:** Many high-performance LTV-based robust control solvers forgo formal linearization error analysis to achieve real-time speeds, thereby sacrificing formal guarantees of safety. The work is motivated by the need to develop a methodology that maintains formal robust constraint satisfaction guarantees without compromising real-time performance.

To achieve these objectives, the paper outlines several specific contributions:
- Developing path-based interval bounds for analytic systems, which are proven to be tighter than standard interval Hessian methods.
- Deriving NN verifier-based bounds for neural network dynamics, using affine relaxations and local Jacobian corrections.
- Proposing a novel nonlinear SLS formulation that precisely integrates these LEBs through zonotopic propagation, addressing limitations related to right-invertible disturbance matrices and non-zero-centered disturbance sets.
- Implementing a GPU-accelerated solver, GPUSLS-LEO, using JAX for differentiable and parallel computation of LEBs and robust control policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the problem of real-time robust optimal control (RNOCP) for uncertain nonlinear systems by focusing on the robust satisfaction of constraints under worst-case disturbances. It decomposes the solution into two core problems: first, finding a tight, differentiable linearization error bound (LEB), and second, integrating this bound efficiently into an RNOCP solver.

**Problem Statement and System Overview:**
The paper considers uncertain nonlinear dynamics described by $x_{k+1} = f(x_k, u_k) + E(x_k)w_k$, where $f$ is the dynamics function, $E(x_k)$ is a state-dependent disturbance scaling, and $w_k$ is a bounded disturbance. The RNOCP aims to minimize a cost function while ensuring state and input constraints are satisfied for all possible disturbances over a finite horizon. This intractable problem is approximated by computing a nominal trajectory and a tracking controller around it, typically using an LTV approximation of the dynamics. The core challenge is bounding the residual error $r_k(x_k, u_k, w_k)$ between the true nonlinear dynamics and its first-order Taylor approximation (linearization) along with the disturbance error from a state-dependent $E(x_k)$.

**Linearization Error Bounds (LEBs):**
The methodology focuses on computing a sound component-wise interval enclosure $R \in I^m$ of the linearization error $r(\alpha; \tilde{\alpha}) := p(\alpha) - p(\tilde{\alpha}) - J(\tilde{\alpha})(\alpha - \tilde{\alpha})$, where $p(\alpha)$ is a vector-valued function (e.g., $f(x,u)$) and $\tilde{\alpha}$ is the nominal point.

1. **For Analytic Dynamics:**
 - **Traditional Methods:** Review of classic interval arithmetic methods, which evaluate the Hessian matrix entries over the entire local set $A$ (Eq. 12, 13). These methods can suffer from conservativeness due to dependency and wrapping effects when performing interval products.
 - **Path-Based Hessian (PBH) Bound:** The paper introduces a novel path-based approach (Theorem 1, Eq. 15). Instead of evaluating Hessian entries over the full input set $A$, it uses a coordinate-wise path from the nominal point $\tilde{\alpha}$ to any point $\alpha \in A$. This allows evaluating each Hessian entry over incrementally smaller "prefix sets" $A^{(k)}(\tilde{\alpha})$, which are subsets of $A$. This strategy is proven to yield never looser bounds than the expanded interval Hessian method (Corollary 1) because the interval enclosures of Hessian entries are taken over smaller domains. The method exploits the quadratic form of the Taylor remainder.

2. **For Neural Network (NN) Dynamics:**
 - **Challenge:** Directly computing tight interval Hessians for NNs is generally difficult.
 - **CROWN-based Bounds:** The method adapts existing NN verification tools, specifically CROWN (Convex Relaxation Based Optimization for Neural Networks), to derive affine lower and upper bounds ($W\alpha + b \le p(\alpha) \le \bar{W}\alpha + \bar{b}$) for an NN $p(\alpha)$ over a box input set $A$ (Theorem 2).
 - **Jacobian Correction:** To obtain a linearization error bound, these CROWN bounds are reconciled with the local Jacobian $J(\tilde{\alpha})$ at the nominal point (Theorem 3, Eq. 19-21). This involves expressing the linearization error as $r(\alpha; \tilde{\alpha}) \ge (W - J(\tilde{\alpha}))\alpha + J(\tilde{\alpha})\tilde{\alpha} - p(\tilde{\alpha}) + b$ and similarly for the upper bound. Since the bounds $r(\alpha)$ and $\bar{r}(\alpha)$ are affine in $\alpha$, finding their minimum and maximum over the box set $A$ is computationally efficient.

3. **Zonotope Bounds of Total Error:**
 - The linearization error $r_{lin_k}$ (from PBH or CROWN) and the disturbance error $r_{dist_k}$ (from state-dependent $E(x_k)$) are combined. Both are bounded by component-wise intervals. These intervals are then converted into zonotope bounds $\bar{R}_k E_{n_x} \oplus \bar{c}_k$, where $\bar{c}_k$ is the center and $\bar{R}_k$ is the generator matrix. This provides a compact, geometrically precise representation of the error.

**Integration into GPUSLS (Nonlinear SLS Formulation):**
The core of the methodology is GPUSLS-LEO, which extends the GPUSLS framework to incorporate these new LEBs.

1. **Modified RNOCP Formulation:** The original RNOCP (Eq. 2) is transformed into an iterative optimization problem (Eq. 24). This problem optimizes a nominal trajectory $(z, v)$ and a robust feedback controller $\Phi$ while explicitly considering disturbance and linearization errors.
 - The linearization errors ($\bar{R}_k$, $\bar{c}_k$) are calculated using the computed reachable tube center $\mu_k$ and widths $\sigma_k$ (Eq. 25), ensuring that the LEBs are valid over the predicted closed-loop reachable set.
 - These errors are combined with the exogenous disturbance to form a total error zonotope whose center is $\bar{c}_k$ and generator matrix is $\bar{E}_k = [E(z_k) \ \bar{R}_k]$.
 - Robust constraint satisfaction is enforced by tightening constraints using the reachable tube overapproximation (Eq. 26). This involves calculating tube centers $h_{\mu_k}$ and widths $h_{\sigma_k}$ for both states and inputs (Eq. 27-28), and incorporating these into constraint tightenings $h_k$, $h_f$ (Eq. 29-30). A surrogate cost $\tilde{H}_0$ (Eq. 31) penalizes uncertainty.

2. **Iterative Solution Strategy:** The problem (Eq. 24) is solved iteratively using a sequential quadratic programming (SQP) approach, alternating between two main steps:
 - **(A) Nominal Trajectory Optimization:** A local QP approximation of a constraint-tightened nominal optimal control problem (Eq. 32) is solved to update $(z, v)$. The differentiability of the LEBs allows for an optional penalty term (Eq. 35) to be added to the nominal objective, which guides the optimizer to regions of low propagated residual error.
 - **(B) Robust Controller Optimization:** A robust feedback controller $\Phi$ is computed by optimizing a cost function related to the propagated uncertainty (Eq. 33). A key methodological advancement is the recognition that the problem structure (with $\bar{E}_j$ factors in the cost) is not directly solvable by standard Riccati-based SLS methods. The authors prove (Proposition 1) that if $\bar{E}_j$ is right-invertible, the optimal controller $\Phi$ can be found by solving a reformulated LQR-like problem (Eq. 34) that is compatible with efficient SLS solvers. This is achieved by ensuring $\bar{R}_k$ (and thus $\bar{E}_j$) is always invertible via a small positive diagonal padding.
 - **(C) Tightening Update:** The computed $\Phi$ is then used to update the constraint tightening terms and tube widths for the next iteration of nominal trajectory optimization.

3. **Formulation Novelty:**
 - **Exact Zonotopic Propagation:** Unlike prior ellipsoidal methods that introduce conservativeness through overapproximations of Minkowski sums, this work uses zonotopes for exact tightening computation by concatenating generators for exogenous disturbance and linearization error.
 - **Right-Invertible Disturbance Matrices:** Addresses the incompatibility of these zonotopes with existing SLS solvers by a novel reformulation (Prop. 1) that allows the use of efficient Riccati-based methods.
 - **Non-Zero-Centered Disturbances:** Extends SLS to directly handle non-zero-centered interval bounds from the LEBs without incurring additional conservativeness from artificial zero-centering.

**GPU-Accelerated Implementation (GPUSLS-LEO):**
The entire framework is implemented in JAX, leveraging its capabilities for automatic differentiation and GPU parallelization. This allows for real-time computation of the differentiable LEBs (using `immrax` for interval arithmetic and `jax_verify` for CROWN-based bounds) and efficient solution of the robust control problem on GPUs. Theorem 4 formally guarantees that the computed reachable tubes overapproximate the true closed-loop reachable sets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings regarding the tightness and computational efficiency of the proposed linearization error bounds (LEBs) and the overall performance of the GPUSLS-LEO robust control framework.

**Linearization Error Bound (LEB) Performance:**
1. **Tightness:**
 - For analytic dynamics (Satellite (7D) and Quadrotor (12D) systems), the Path-Based Hessian (PBH) method consistently yielded the tightest over-approximations of linearization error compared to baselines such as Global Sampling, Interval Hessian (IH) classic, and IH-classic-CORA (Fig. 2). PBH remained closer to the random-sampling lower bound as the perturbation radius $\epsilon$ increased. Global sampling proved loose at small $\epsilon$, indicating its inability to capture local dynamics, while standard interval Hessian methods became overly conservative at larger $\epsilon$.
 - For neural network dynamics (Neural T-pusher), the CROWN-based bounds were successfully integrated.
 - The use of nonzero-centered zonotopes, a novel aspect of the formulation, contributed to a 25% reduction in tube sizes compared to using zero-centered zonotopes (Fig. 7a).

2. **Differentiability Benefit:**
 - The differentiability of the PBH method allowed for gradient-based optimization of the nominal point within a local neighborhood using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD). PGD consistently (100% of test cases) yielded smaller error interval widths compared to random sampling for the same computational budget, demonstrating the value of differentiability for finding nominal points that produce tighter LEBs (Table II).

3. **Runtime:**
 - The PBH method ran in under 1 ms (0.361 ms for Satellite, 0.936 ms for Quadrotor), enabling real-time computation. This was significantly faster than dense random sampling and orders of magnitude faster than CORA IH (e.g., 227200 ms for Quadrotor) (Table I). While slower than the initial computation of global bounds (which require extensive offline pre-computation), PBH is efficient for online local updates.

**Robust Optimal Control (RNOCP) Performance with GPUSLS-LEO:**
1. **Reduced Conservatism and Guaranteed Containment:**
 - **Satellite (7D):** GPUSLS-LEO achieved tube widths that were 20% smaller on average than NL-SLS (a baseline relying on global maximum Hessian bounds) and 45% smaller than GPUSLS-E (ellipsoidal propagation). This indicated reduced conservativeness. The method introduced only a 4% increase in tube widths compared to GPUSLS (which omits linearization error), demonstrating that formal LEB inclusion does not significantly impact conservativeness. All simulated rollouts (20 random, 64 adversarial) remained within the computed robust tubes, achieving 100% containment (Fig. 3).
 - **Planar Quadrotor (6D):** GPUSLS-LEO showed 91% smaller tube area for position states compared to Control Contraction Metrics (CCM), which certifies safety over the entire state space. This allowed for more direct and task-optimal trajectories. The method also significantly outperformed an interval-based reachability analysis tool (immrax) whose tubes diverged rapidly (Fig. 4).
 - **Neural T-pusher (5D):** While GPUSLS-LEO tubes were 99% larger than GPUSLS (which ignores linearization error), it achieved 100% containment of rollouts, whereas GPUSLS rollouts frequently exited the computed tubes (Fig. 7b, 7c). This validated the necessity of formally accounting for linearization error. Including a penalty on linearization error gradients reduced tube widths by 8% (Fig. 7a).

2. **Scalability to High Dimensions and Long Horizons:**
 - **Coupled 3D Quadrotor (168D):** The method successfully computed tight tubes and maintained 100% containment for a system of 14 coupled 3D quadrotors over a horizon of 25 steps, demonstrating scalability to high-dimensional state spaces (Fig. 6).
 - **Long Horizon Planar Quadrotor:** GPUSLS-LEO was applied to a planar quadrotor problem with a horizon of 7500 steps, involving approximately $1.95 \times 10^5$ decision variables. Despite the long horizon, the robust tubes remained well-bounded and finite, illustrating the method's scalability for extended operational periods (Fig. 5).

3. **Real-Time Performance:**
 - **Runtime Breakdown:** The per-iteration runtime analysis (Table III) showed that for most systems, the main computational load remained within the core GPUSLS component, indicating that the proposed LEB calculations and modifications did not drastically increase overall solve times. Total runtimes were consistently below the discretization time step (DT), confirming the viability of GPUSLS-LEO for real-time iterative MPC.
 - **Scaling with Dimension:** For increasing numbers of coupled quadrotors, GPUSLS-LEO maintained control rates up to 67 Hz. The overall GPUSLS component scaled approximately logarithmically with state and control dimension, while the PBH component, though faster at lower dimensions, exhibited slower scaling in higher dimensions due to Hessian computation costs (Fig. 8).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework that addresses a critical gap in real-time robust optimal control by providing tight, differentiable, and formally sound linearization error bounds (LEBs) for both analytic and neural network dynamics. The work's significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability of Autonomous Systems:** By formally accounting for linearization error, GPUSLS-LEO ensures robust constraint satisfaction, guaranteeing that the true nonlinear system remains within specified safe operating regions despite model approximations and external disturbances. This is crucial for safety-critical applications in robotics, aerospace, and defense, where system failures due to unmodeled errors can have severe consequences.

2. **Reduced Conservatism in Robust Control:** The development of path-based Hessian bounds for analytic dynamics and CROWN-based bounds for neural network dynamics offers tighter over-approximations of reachable sets compared to prior methods. This reduction in conservatism can lead to more aggressive control policies, improved task performance (e.g., faster maneuvers, more direct paths), and a wider operational envelope for autonomous systems, without compromising safety guarantees. The direct integration of non-zero-centered zonotopic bounds further contributes to this by avoiding unnecessary overapproximations.

3. **Real-Time Capabilities for Complex Systems:** The GPU-parallel implementation in JAX (GPUSLS-LEO) allows for the online computation of LEBs and robust control policies at rates up to 67 Hz. This real-time performance is vital for applications requiring rapid decision-making, such as high-speed robotics or dynamic obstacle avoidance, and enables the use of these robust methods in iterative model predictive control schemes. The demonstrated scalability to systems with up to 168 state dimensions and long control horizons (e.g., 7500 steps) indicates broad applicability.

4. **Advancements in Robust Control of Learned Dynamics:** The ability to compute certified LEBs for neural network dynamics is particularly impactful. As learned models increasingly describe complex system behavior, providing formal safety guarantees for controllers relying on these models is paramount. This work contributes to the development of robust and verifiable control strategies for AI-driven autonomous systems.

5. **Improved Optimization and Trajectory Planning:** The differentiability of the proposed LEBs allows RNOCP solvers to use gradient information to actively minimize linearization error. This enables the optimizer to reshape nominal trajectories towards regions of lower model uncertainty, leading to both tighter reachable tubes and potentially more efficient or safer control solutions.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays a foundation for future extensions of robust optimal control. The authors specifically mention potential applications in contact-rich planning (where nonlinearities are significant), nonlinear output-feedback control, and the integration of learning errors, which are relevant for real-world scenarios involving complex interactions and partial observability.

In summary, this research significantly advances the field of robust optimal control by providing practical, rigorous, and computationally efficient tools for handling linearization error. Its impact lies in enabling safer, more reliable, and higher-performing autonomous systems across a wide range of applications.
