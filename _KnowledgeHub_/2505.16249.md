# Manipulating Elasto-Plastic Objects With 3D Occupancy and Learning-Based Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong and collaborating institutions developed a framework for robotic manipulation of elasto-plastic objects, leveraging a dense 3D occupancy representation and a hybrid 3D CNN-GNN dynamics model. This approach, enhanced by a shape-based action initialization for predictive control, achieved robust shaping of complex 3D forms, with deformation predictions exhibiting significantly lower error rates (e.g., EMD 22.8e-3) compared to prior methods and results comparable to human manipulation in real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Deformable objects exhibit severe self-occlusion during manipulation, making it challenging for robots to infer their full 3D state.
- Representing deformable objects is difficult due to their high degrees of freedom and complex, non-linear deformation patterns, where existing sparse representations often lack sufficient detail.
- Modeling the complicated, irreversible dynamics of elasto-plastic objects under quasi-static motion assumptions accurately poses a significant challenge for robotic control.

## Method

- A ==3D occupancy-based state representation== is inferred from multi-view RGB images to provide dense, fine-grained, and volumetric information about the deformable object, addressing self-occlusion.
- A learning-based dynamics model combines ==3D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)== for multi-scale feature extraction and ==Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)== for capturing inter-voxel relationships to predict complex elasto-plastic deformations.
- A ==learning-based predictive control (MPC)== framework integrates the learned dynamics model, enhanced by a novel ==shape-based action initialization== module to guide initial gripper actions based on the geometric relationship between current and goal shapes.

## Results

- The 3D occupancy-based state representation achieved lower errors (**EMD: 28.6e-3**, **DCD: 34.5e-4**) than patch-based and crop-based baselines in simulation for object state inference.
- The proposed 3D CNN-GNN dynamics model consistently outperformed prior dynamics models (e.g., RoboCraft, RoboCook), demonstrating more accurate deformation predictions (**EMD: 22.8e-3**, **DCD: 25.3e-4**).
- The framework successfully manipulated elasto-plastic objects into various complex 3D goal shapes in both simulation and real-world scenarios, exhibiting generalization across different initial shapes and materials, with performance comparable to human manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense 3D occupancy provides a superior and inherently volumetric state representation for deformable objects, effectively capturing both internal and external geometry despite occlusions.
> - A hybrid 3D CNN-GNN architecture effectively models the complex and irreversible dynamics of elasto-plastic materials by integrating multi-scale spatial features with graph-based relational learning.
> - Geometrically informed action initialization significantly improves the efficiency and consistency of model predictive control, particularly for tasks involving irreversible plastic deformations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deformable Object Manipulation (DOM) is a critical area within robotics, with applications spanning household tasks, industrial processes, and healthcare. Within DOM, objects are broadly categorized into thin-shell structural objects (e.g., clothes, ropes) and volumetric objects (e.g., plasticine, dough). This paper specifically addresses the manipulation of elasto-plastic volumetric objects, which exhibit both elastic deformation under small stress and permanent plastic deformation under larger stress, operating under a quasi-static motion assumption.

Traditional approaches to representing rigid objects often rely on 6D poses, which are insufficient for deformable objects due to their high degrees of freedom, complex deformation patterns, and significant self-occlusion. Prior work in DOM has explored various state representations and dynamics models. For thin-shell objects, methods like mesh embedding, latent space features, and keypoint embedding have been utilized. For volumetric objects, particle-based and point cloud-based representations are common, with studies employing deep neural networks to process point clouds or particle dynamics for learning. However, these 3D spatial representations often suffer from sparsity and lack semantic information compared to raw RGB images, or they introduce challenges in cross-modality feature fusion. Furthermore, many dynamics models for deformable objects have limitations: some rely on 2D image-space representations, which struggle with volumetric deformations and occlusions; others are designed for specific materials (e.g., granular) or assume globally linearizable dynamics, which may not hold for complex elasto-plastic behaviors. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have shown promise for learning dynamics from low-dimensional features, but often depend on accurate RGB-D-based mesh reconstruction or operate solely on surface-level information, thereby neglecting crucial internal structural cues and potentially degrading learned dynamics performance due to spatial information sparsity.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a dense, fine-grained, and volumetric representation using 3D occupancy. This approach aims to provide comprehensive spatial and semantic information, enabling more accurate dynamics modeling, especially for elasto-plastic objects where internal structure is critical for predicting irreversible deformations. By integrating a 3D occupancy prediction network, a learned dynamics model leveraging 3D CNNs and GNNs, and a learning-based predictive control with a novel action initialization strategy, the paper seeks to advance the capability of robots to shape complex deformable materials that have historically posed significant challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for manipulating elasto-plastic volumetric objects, such as plasticine, into desired 3D goal shapes using a robot gripper. This overarching goal is motivated by several inherent challenges in deformable object manipulation (DOM):

1. **Severe Self-Occlusion:** Deformable objects frequently occlude parts of themselves during manipulation, making it difficult for standard vision systems to infer their full 3D state. Current methods, often relying on surface-level point clouds or RGB-D images, may struggle to capture complete object geometry, especially internal structures.
2. **Difficulties of Representation:** Unlike rigid objects with compact 6D pose representations, deformable objects possess high degrees of freedom. Existing representations, such as sparse point clouds or particle systems, can lack the density and semantic richness required to effectively capture complex, volumetric deformations and provide sufficient context for dynamics modeling.
3. **Complicated Dynamics:** Elasto-plastic objects exhibit intricate, irreversible deformations, where their state transitions are non-linear and challenging to model accurately. Many prior dynamics models either simplify these complexities or are tailored to materials with different properties, limiting their applicability to elasto-plastic materials. Furthermore, the quasi-static nature of the deformations adds another layer of modeling complexity.

To address these motivations, the authors set out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Develop a novel state representation:** Propose and validate the use of dense voxel-grid 3D occupancy as a predictable, fine-grained, semantic-rich, and inherently volumetric representation for deformable objects, particularly elasto-plastic ones. This representation is intended to provide detailed spatial information, including internal structures, even in the presence of occlusion.
- **Infer 3D occupancy from multi-view observations:** Design and train a 3D occupancy prediction network that can infer the object's 3D occupancy from multi-view RGB images, thus overcoming issues related to self-occlusion and providing a dense input for dynamics modeling.
- **Learn complex deformation dynamics:** Create a learned dynamics model that can predict the complex, irreversible deformations of elasto-plastic objects. This model leverages the 3D occupancy representation and integrates both 3D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for multi-scale feature extraction and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for capturing inter-voxel relationships.
- **Implement an effective control strategy:** Integrate the learned dynamics model into a learning-based predictive control (Model Predictive Control - MPC) algorithm to plan robot actions.
- **Enhance control efficiency with action initialization:** Introduce a novel shape-based action initialization module specifically designed to improve the efficiency and consistency of the MPC planner by guiding the initial action choices based on the geometric relationship between the current and goal shapes.
- **Create a comprehensive data collection and ground truth generation pipeline:** Build a novel, low-cost data collection platform with full spatial information capture capabilities (including a transparent operating plane and multi-view cameras) and develop a robust pipeline for generating fine-grained, dense 3D occupancy ground truth from multi-view RGB-D data to supervise the occupancy prediction network.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to establish a framework capable of effectively shaping elasto-plastic objects into arbitrary desired forms, thereby expanding the capabilities of robotic manipulation in real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for manipulating elasto-plastic objects integrates 3D occupancy-based state representation, a learned dynamics model, and a learning-based predictive control algorithm.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Formulation

The task is defined as manipulating an elasto-plastic object (plasticine) into a given goal shape, $s_{\text{goal}}$, using a parallel 2-finger gripper. Observations are obtained from multi-view cameras $I = \{I_1, I_2..., I_n\}$. The object's state, $s$, is represented by a neural network, $N(I)$, that infers 3D occupancy from images. A sequence of actions $A = \{a_0..., a_{t-1}\}$ is applied, transitioning the object from an initial state $s_0$ to a final state $s_t$, where $s_t \approx s_{\text{goal}}$. The state transition is approximated by a learned neural network $G(s_{t-1}, a_{t-1})$. The manipulation task is formulated as a Model Predictive Control (MPC) problem, where the action sequence $A^*$ minimizes a cost function $L(G(s_0, A), s_{\text{goal}})$.

#### 3.2. B. 3D Occupancy-based State Representation for Deformable Objects

The core state representation uses a voxel-grid 3D occupancy. This dense, fine-grained, and volumetric representation provides detailed spatial and semantic information. A 3D occupancy prediction network, $N$, is trained to convert multi-view RGB images $I_t$ into 3D occupancy $O_t$. The network employs a ResNet-50 backbone for multi-scale feature extraction from images, followed by a 2D-3D spatial attention module and multi-scale occupancy prediction modules. The output $O_t$ is a probability map for voxel occupancy. To create a sparse, graph-compatible state $s_t$, the 3D occupancy $O_t$ is down-sampled using the Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) algorithm into a sparse voxel set $K = \{v_0..., v_k\}$.

#### 3.3. C. Learning-based Dynamics Model

The dynamics model predicts the next state of the plasticine given its current state and a gripper action. It is built upon a deep neural network combining 3D CNNs and GNNs.
1. **Voxel-based State Graph Construction:** From the down-sampled sparse voxel set $K$, a state graph $S = (V, E)$ is constructed. Vertices $V$ are the voxels in $K$, and edges $E$ connect voxels within a predefined distance, representing their dynamic spatial relationships.
2. **Features Aggregation for Graph Nodes:** For each voxel $v_i$, multi-scale semantic features are extracted. A sparse 3D CNN backbone with 4 levels (feature strides {1, 2, 4, 8}) extracts features $F_1, F_2, F_3, F_4$. A voxel set abstraction module projects down-sampled voxels into the voxel grids to collect features from $F_4, F_3, F_2$. Additionally, a bird's-eye-view (BEV) feature $f^{bev}_{v_i}$ is obtained by projecting the voxel's 3D coordinate to 2D and using bilinear interpolation. These features are concatenated to form the multi-scale semantic feature $f_{v_i}$.
3. **Overall Dynamics Network Structure:** The overall network (Fig. 4) comprises:
 - **3D Occupancy-Based State Encoder:** Takes the down-sampled 3D occupancy (from current and previous three steps) to construct a voxel-based state graph, encoding node features.
 - **Multi-Scale Semantic and Spatial Feature Extraction:** A 3D sparse CNN extracts multi-scale features from the current 3D occupancy, which are then aggregated for each graph node via a voxel set abstraction module.
 - **GNN Encoder:** During training, the aggregated node features are concatenated with the encoded node features and fed into a GNN for dynamics model training. For evaluation, voxel-wise features are only used at the initial step.
4. **Loss Functions:** The dynamics model is trained using a weighted sum of three distance functions:
 - **Earth Mover's Distance (EMD):** Measures the minimum cost to transform one point distribution into another.
 - **Chamfer Distance (CD):** Calculates the average squared distance from each point in one set to its closest point in the other set.
 - **Density-aware Chamfer Distance (DCD):** An extension of CD that considers the density of points in the neighborhood, improving robustness to uneven point distributions.

#### 3.4. D. Learning-based Predictive Control

After the dynamics model is trained, MPC is employed for control.
1. **Action Space and Goal-Conditioned MPC:** The gripper action space is defined by its center position $(x, y, z)$, rotation about the z-axis ($r_z$), and the distance between fingers ($l$). MPC optimizes an action sequence $A_{0 \to t-1}$ to minimize $L(S_t, S_g)$ using the L-BFGS gradient-based optimization algorithm.
2. **Shape-based Action Initialization:** To improve MPC efficiency and consistency, a novel action initialization module is proposed (Fig. 5). It involves:
 - Aligning the current object state ($s_p$) with the goal state ($s_{gt}$).
 - Filtering out points in $s_p$ that are already close to $s_{gt}$ to identify areas needing deformation ($s_r$).
 - Segmenting $s_r$ into regions along x and y axes.
 - Calculating a "cost" (Euclidean distance to $s_{gt}$) for moving points in each region.
 - Selecting two regions with the highest cost. The initial gripper rotation ($r_z$) is defined by the line connecting the centers of $s_r$ in these two regions. The gripper's initial $(x,y,z)$ position is derived from the midpoint of the line connecting $s_{gt}$ centers in these regions, projected onto the $s_r$ connection line. This provides a geometrically informed initial action for the MPC optimizer.

#### 3.5. E. 3D Occupancy Ground Truth for Deformable Object Manipulation

A critical aspect of the methodology is the generation of high-quality 3D occupancy ground truth, as standard RGB-D often yields insufficient density for network supervision.
1. **Data Collection Platform:** A novel, low-cost platform was built (Fig. 6). It features a transparent acrylic operating plane, allowing for the installation of six RGB-D cameras (4 top-view, 2 bottom-view) to capture full spatial information and address occlusion.
2. **Data Collection and Processing:** Multi-view RGB-D images are collected and converted into dense, registered point clouds $P_{raw}$. Points outside the platform's physical boundaries are removed. The region of interest (ROI) containing the deformable object ($P_{do}$) and gripper ($P_g$) is cropped. Color-based filtering and DBSCAN are used to segment $P_{do}$ and the two gripper primitives $\{P^1_g, P^2_g\}$.
3. **Refinement with Geometry Prior:** Missed points belonging to the gripper primitives (due to occlusion/reflection) that incorrectly appear in $P_{do}$ are identified and removed using the known geometry (3D position, radius, length) of the gripper primitives.
4. **Surface Reconstruction:** Poisson Surface Reconstruction is applied to create watertight meshes for the entire scene ($M$), the plasticine ($M_p$), and the gripper primitives ($\{M^1_g, M^2_g\}$) from their respective point clouds.
5. **Dense Occupancy Generation and Semantic Labeling:** The reconstructed mesh $M$ is converted into a dense voxel set $V_d$ (Fig. 7(d)). This involves determining the bounding box of $M$, voxelizing the space, computing the Signed Distance Function (SDF) for each voxel, and removing voxels outside $M$. Finally, voxels are semantically labeled (plasticine, gripper, operating plane) by using the individual meshes $M_p$ and $\{M^1_g, M^2_g\}$, and the operating plane is modeled as a thin cube. Voxel colors are derived from the average RGB values of projected mesh surface points. This pipeline enables the generation of dense volumetric occupancy with fine semantic labels for supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both a custom simulation environment (PlasticineLab) and a real-world setup involving a UR5e robotic arm and Real-sense D453i cameras.

#### 4.1. A. 3D Occupancy-based State Representation

The quality of the 3D occupancy-based state representation was evaluated against two baseline methods: patch-based and crop-based methods, common in prior work like RoboCraft. The comparison was performed on a simulation dataset using Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD), Density-aware Chamfer Distance (DCD), and Chamfer Distance (CD) over 160 frames (four pinches).
- **Result:** The 3D occupancy-based prediction method yielded significantly lower EMD (28.6 ± 1.23 × 1e-3), DCD (34.5 ± 1.03 × 1e-4), and CD (36.0 ± 0.667 × 1e-3) compared to patch-based (EMD: 32.7 ± 3.86, DCD: 39.4 ± 2.05, CD: 38.6 ± 2.12) and crop-based methods (EMD: 30.5 ± 1.62, DCD: 36.7 ± 1.26, CD: 37.1 ± 0.953). The performance of the 3D occupancy-based prediction was very close to the ideal ground truth (EMD: 27.9 ± 1.14, DCD: 34.2 ± 0.998, CD: 35.8 ± 0.676).
- **Finding:** This demonstrates that the proposed 3D occupancy-based method provides a more precise and dense representation of the deformable object's state.

#### 4.2. B. 3D CNN-GNN Dynamics Model Training

The learned dynamics model, which combines 3D CNNs and GNNs and uses the 3D occupancy-based state representation, was compared against two strong baselines: RoboCraft (crop-based state + GNN) and RoboCook (surface-based state + GNN). Models were trained with specific hyper-parameters for the loss functions (EMD, DCD, CD).
- **Result:** The proposed model consistently outperformed the baselines across all metrics. It achieved EMD of 22.8 ± 2.30 × 1e-3, DCD of 25.3 ± 1.52 × 1e-4, and CD of 33.5 ± 0.951 × 1e-3. In contrast, RoboCraft showed EMD of 24.3 ± 2.82, DCD of 27.3 ± 2.30, and CD of 34.7 ± 1.64. RoboCook performed with EMD of 25.5 ± 2.92, DCD of 33.2 ± 2.02, and CD of 36.3 ± 1.45.
- **Finding:** The dense 3D features derived from the occupancy representation, combined with the multi-scale spatial context captured by the 3D CNN and GNN, enable more accurate predictions of complex deformable dynamics. The inclusion of internal nodes in the 3D occupancy provides richer structural understanding compared to surface-only or sparse crop-based methods.

#### 4.3. C. Manipulation Results

Extensive experiments were conducted in both simulation and the real world to evaluate the end-to-end manipulation performance.
- **Simulation:** Qualitative and quantitative comparisons in simulation (shaping letters "X", "T", "K") showed the proposed method achieving superior performance over RoboCraft, indicated by lower average CD (45.61 ± 0.0355 vs 48.89 ± 0.089), DCD (47.97 ± 0.0364 vs 51.33 ± 0.8650), and EMD (39.76 ± 0.0692 vs 44.53 ± 0.0261).
- **Real-world Single-Layer Objects:** The framework successfully shaped plasticine into various goal shapes ("X", "T", "Y", "I", "M") with varying levels of symmetry and difficulty (Fig. 9), demonstrating its ability to create shapes closely resembling the targets.
- **Real-world Multi-Layer Objects:** The method was validated on multi-layer stamping tasks, successfully pinching out two-layer stamps with height ratios close to the desired values (Fig. 10).
- **Generalization:** The method demonstrated generalization across different initial shapes (circle, triangle, irregular polygon), colors (pink, yellow), and materials (Play-Doh, foam clay), consistently achieving the target "X" shape (Fig. 11).
- **Comparison with Human Manipulation:** In real-world comparisons, the proposed method achieved shaping results comparable to human demonstrations and outperformed RoboCraft (Fig. 12).
- **Finding:** The framework can successfully manipulate elasto-plastic objects into complex 3D goal shapes, exhibiting robustness, generalization, and performance competitive with human capabilities in certain tasks.

#### 4.4. D. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were performed to quantify the contribution of specific components:
- **3D CNN Module:** Removing the 3D CNN module from the dynamics model, relying solely on encoded sparse voxel features, resulted in worse performance across all metrics (EMD: 24.5 ± 5.07; DCD: 27.2 ± 2.25; CD: 34.3 ± 2.60) compared to the full model.
 - **Finding:** The 3D CNN module is critical for effectively preserving and leveraging multi-scale 3D spatial information, leading to improved dynamics modeling.
- **Shape-based Action Initialization:** Without this module, actions were randomly initialized. This led to unreasonable and irreversible deformations (Fig. 13a) and significantly higher evaluation losses after the first action (EMD: 46.9 ± 4.54 vs 38.9 ± 0.734; DCD: 51.1 ± 2.54 vs 45.6 ± 1.25; CD: 50.7 ± 4.40 vs 43.4 ± 1.10).
 - **Finding:** Shape-based action initialization is crucial for guiding the MPC planner towards effective and consistent deformations, particularly given the irreversible nature of plastic deformation.
- **DCD Loss:** Training without the DCD loss function resulted in slightly degraded performance (EMD: 23.4 ± 3.94; DCD: 26.7 ± 3.96; CD: 34.2 ± 2.36) compared to the full model.
 - **Finding:** The DCD loss contributes to the overall performance of the dynamics model, likely by providing a more comprehensive metric for point cloud similarity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a comprehensive framework that addresses several significant challenges in the robotic manipulation of elasto-plastic volumetric objects. The core contribution lies in the adoption and effective utilization of 3D occupancy as a dense, fine-grained, and semantically rich state representation. This approach intrinsically handles issues such as self-occlusion and the sparse nature of traditional point cloud representations, providing a more complete understanding of the object's internal and external geometry.

The development of a novel data collection platform with a transparent operating plane is a significant methodological advancement, enabling the capture of full spatial information (including bottom views) necessary for generating accurate 3D occupancy ground truth. This addresses a common limitation in real-world deformable object datasets where occluded regions are often missed. The accompanying pipeline for generating dense 3D occupancy labels from multi-view RGB-D images further enhances the quality of data available for training robust perception models.

The learned dynamics model, integrating both 3D CNNs and GNNs, demonstrates an improved ability to predict the complex and irreversible deformations characteristic of elasto-plastic materials. This capability is crucial for effective manipulation planning, as it allows the system to foresee the consequences of its actions over multiple steps. The ablation studies confirm the importance of the 3D CNN component in capturing multi-scale spatial information, validating the architectural choices.

Furthermore, the introduction of a shape-based action initialization module significantly improves the efficiency and consistency of the learning-based predictive control (MPC) algorithm. For irreversible deformation tasks, an informed initial action guess can prevent suboptimal or irreversible errors, thereby enhancing the overall manipulation success rate and reducing computation time during optimization.

The successful manipulation results, demonstrated across various complex 3D shapes, multi-layer structures, and different initial conditions, colors, and materials, showcase the robustness and generalization capabilities of the proposed framework. The performance achieved, which is comparable to human manipulation and superior to existing baselines like RoboCraft, highlights its practical applicability.

**Potential Impact:**
This work has the potential to impact various fields:
- **Manufacturing and Assembly:** Automating tasks involving the shaping of soft goods, compounds, or raw materials (e.g., in food processing, molding, or construction).
- **Healthcare:** Development of advanced surgical robots capable of precisely manipulating soft tissues, particularly in reconstructive surgery or delicate procedures.
- **Art and Design:** Enabling robots to perform creative tasks like sculpting or material forming.
- **Domestic Robotics:** Improving robotic capabilities for handling diverse deformable household items.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. **Dependency on Full-Space Observations:** The framework currently relies on a transparent operating plane for full-space observations. Without it, occlusions significantly degrade the quality of mesh reconstruction and 3D occupancy generation. Future work may explore methods to infer full 3D occupancy from partial observations or leverage advanced sensing modalities.
2. **Topological Changes:** The employed Poisson surface reconstruction struggles to capture fine topological changes (e.g., splits and merges) due to the limited quality of point clouds from RGB-D cameras. Addressing these intricate deformations remains an open challenge.
3. **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Disparities in material properties between simulation and the real world necessitate fine-tuning on real data, indicating an ongoing sim-to-real gap challenge. Further research into robust sim-to-real transfer learning or material property estimation could mitigate this.
4. **Computational Demands:** The method requires GPU acceleration, limiting its deployment on CPU-only systems and resource-constrained applications. Optimizing the computational efficiency of the 3D occupancy prediction and dynamics model is a relevant direction for broader applicability.

Despite these limitations, the paper lays a foundation for future advancements in robot manipulation of complex deformable objects by demonstrating the power of dense, volumetric representations combined with learning-based dynamics and control.
