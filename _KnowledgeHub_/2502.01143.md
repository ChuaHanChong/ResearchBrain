# ASAP: Aligning Simulation and Real-World Physics for Learning Agile Humanoid Whole-Body Skills

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and NVIDIA developed ASAP, a two-stage framework that aligns simulation and real-world physics for learning agile humanoid whole-body skills. The approach utilizes a learned "delta action model" to compensate for dynamics discrepancies, enabling a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to perform complex, human-like motions and reducing tracking errors by up to 52.7% in real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to perform agile, whole-body human-like skills due to the inherent "sim-to-real gap," where policies trained in idealized simulations fail on physical robots.
- Existing sim-to-real transfer methods like System Identification (SysID) are labor-intensive, limited by predefined parameter spaces, and cannot account for all unmodeled real-world phenomena.
- Domain Randomization (DR), while robust, often leads to overly conservative policies that sacrifice the agility and fine coordination required for dynamic whole-body skills in humanoids.

## Method

- A two-stage framework that first pre-trains motion tracking policies in a high-fidelity simulator (IsaacGym) using retargeted human video data.
- Introduced a novel "delta action model" trained on real-world robot data to learn a residual correction to the nominal simulated actions, effectively aligning the simulator's dynamics with real-world physics.
- Fine-tuned the pre-trained motion tracking policy within the 'aligned' simulator (where the delta action model perturbs the dynamics) to adapt the policy to the more accurate, real-world-representative dynamics.

## Results

- ASAP achieved superior dynamics matching in open-loop replay experiments, consistently outperforming SysID and Delta Dynamics baselines by accurately reproducing real-world trajectories in simulation.
- Closed-loop policy evaluations demonstrated that ASAP significantly reduced motion tracking errors (e.g., E_g-mpjpe, E_mpjpe) and maintained a **100%** success rate across all difficulty levels in sim-to-sim transfer.
- Successfully deployed on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, enabling agile motions and reducing tracking errors for challenging movements, with E_g-mpjpe for a kick motion decreasing from 61.2mm to 50.2mm and for a "Silencer" motion from 159mm to 112mm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly learning a residual (delta) action model from real-world data effectively compensates for the simulator-to-real dynamics mismatch in complex humanoid robots.
> - The dynamics mismatch between simulation and reality is often structured and non-uniform across robot joints (e.g., larger discrepancies in lower-body motors), which a learned delta model can specifically correct.
> - Fine-tuning motion tracking policies within a simulator that has been dynamically aligned with real-world physics enables superior agility and motion performance on physical humanoids compared to traditional robustness methods or direct policy transfer.
