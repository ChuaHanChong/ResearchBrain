# VT-WAM: Visual-Tactile World Action Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The VT-WAM framework introduces a visual-tactile world action model that jointly predicts future visual observations, tactile deformations, and actions within a flow matching framework. This approach achieved an average success rate of 71.67% across six real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, outperforming existing vision-only and tactile-augmented baselines.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation tasks involving physical contact require precise reactions to local interaction states (e.g., deformation, pressure), which are often invisible, spatially limited, or temporally sparse in visual observations.
- Traditional vision-centric policies and many existing visual-tactile policies underutilize critical tactile information due to the temporal imbalance between dense visual signals and sparse, contact-dependent tactile cues.
- Neural networks tend to predominantly rely on visual evidence during multimodal training, leading to a 'visual-dominance bias' where sparse tactile signals, crucial for contact-rich tasks, are overshadowed.

## Method

- **VT-WAM** is a unified ==visual-tactile World Action Model== that concurrently learns future visual prediction, tactile deformation prediction, and action prediction within a single ==flow matching== framework.
- An ==Asymmetric Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Attention== mechanism efficiently bridges visual and tactile information, allowing action prediction to leverage full temporal tactile dynamics for contact evolution while using only a ==first-frame visual anchor== for global scene context.
- ==Contact-gated Action-Visual-Tactile Attention Guidance (AVTAG)== is an auxiliary training mechanism (a ==hinge ranking loss==) that mitigates ==visual-dominance bias== by explicitly encouraging action queries to prioritize tactile evidence during phases of physical contact.

## Results

- VT-WAM achieved an average success rate of **71.67%** across six real-world contact-rich tasks, a **26.67%** improvement over the Fast-WAM baseline (**45.00%**) and **35.84%** over OmniVTLA (**35.83%**).
- The model demonstrated accurate visual and tactile prediction, with the tactile expert achieving lower l2 deformation magnitude error (**0.077**) and higher directional consistency (**0.749**) compared to baseline tactile models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that modeling temporal tactile dynamics significantly improves success rates (e.g., from **40%** to **70%** for 'wipe vase'), and the Contact-Gated AVTAG further enhanced performance by guiding attention to tactile signals during contact phases (e.g., from **70%** to **85%** for 'wipe vase').

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly coupling action prediction with explicit tactile deformation dynamics enables policies to effectively leverage local contact information, which is decisive for successful contact-rich manipulation.
> - An asymmetric attention design, utilizing a visual anchor and full tactile sequences, allows for efficient inference by predicting tactile evolution without the computational overhead of future visual frame prediction.
> - A contact-gated auxiliary loss can successfully guide an action expert to increase reliance on critical tactile evidence during training, mitigating visual bias and improving performance in contact-dependent scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly contact-rich tasks, represents a significant challenge in robotics. These tasks, such as wiping surfaces or inserting components, require precise reactions to local interaction states like deformation, pressure, slip, and friction. Traditional vision-centric policies often prove inadequate because these crucial tactile cues are frequently invisible, spatially limited, or temporally sparse within visual observations.

The broader research landscape for contact-rich manipulation has seen several approaches. One line of work focuses on integrating tactile sensing into action prediction, exemplified by diffusion policies augmented with tactile conditioning (e.g., FARM, TacDiffusion, PolyTouch, KineDex) or reactive policies that use tactile or force feedback for online correction (e.g., RDP, Force Policy, M2-ResPolicy). More recently, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have begun incorporating tactile feedback into large-scale architectures (e.g., BiTLA, OmniVTLA, VTLA, Tactile-VLA). While these methods demonstrate the value of tactile data, they often treat tactile observations as direct policy inputs or use predicted tactile states indirectly for planning or downstream modules. Some visual-tactile world models (e.g., VT-WM, OmniVTA) explicitly predict contact evolution, but the tactile predictions may still not be directly coupled with action generation.

Another relevant area is World Action Models (WAMs), which enhance action prediction by incorporating future-state prediction. Existing WAMs typically focus on modeling visual dynamics for action prediction, either through cascaded approaches that first synthesize future visual states or plans, or joint architectures that learn future dynamics and action prediction within a shared generative objective (e.g., Fast-WAM, DreamZero, Motus). However, these WAMs primarily operate within the visual domain, overlooking the distinct dynamics of tactile interaction.

This work, VT-WAM, positions itself by addressing a recognized limitation: the underutilization of tactile information in existing visual-tactile policies due to the temporal imbalance between dense visual information and sparse, contact-dependent tactile signals. It extends the WAM paradigm by explicitly integrating tactile deformation dynamics alongside visual dynamics, directly coupling tactile evolution with action prediction within a unified flow matching framework. This approach aims to provide policies with a more direct and effective mechanism to leverage local contact information, particularly during critical contact phases, thereby overcoming the visual-dominance bias often observed in multimodal learning for manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent challenges encountered in contact-rich robotic manipulation. These tasks fundamentally rely on local interaction cues such as deformation, pressure, and friction. However, these tactile signals are often weak, transient, or completely occluded from typical visual observations. This characteristic makes vision-centric policies unreliable when fine adjustments based on physical contact are necessary.

Existing visual-tactile policies, while incorporating tactile sensing, often do not fully exploit the information tactile sensors provide. A key reason identified is the temporal imbalance between visual and tactile data. Visual observations generally offer dense, scene-level information across most frames, whereas tactile deformation signals are local, only becoming informative during brief contact phases. This disparity can lead neural networks to predominantly rely on visual evidence during joint training, resulting in the underutilization of tactile signals that are critical for specific interaction dynamics.

Based on this analysis, the core insight driving this work is the necessity to couple action prediction directly with tactile evolution. This coupling enables the manipulation policy to specifically leverage changes in tactile feedback during contact phases, which are decisive for task success.

The key objectives of this research are detailed as follows:

1. **Formulate a Unified World Action Model:** To develop a Visual-Tactile World Action Model (VT-WAM) that concurrently learns future visual prediction, tactile deformation prediction, and action prediction within a single flow matching framework. This objective aims to create a cohesive model that processes multimodal sensory information and action generation in an integrated manner.
2. **Develop Asymmetric MoT Attention:** To design an attention mechanism, termed Asymmetric Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Attention, capable of bridging distinct information streams effectively. Specifically, this mechanism is intended to connect a first-frame visual anchor (providing global scene context) with the temporal dynamics of the full tactile sequence (providing contact evolution information). This design also aims to facilitate an efficient visual-cache inference mode, allowing the model to utilize tactile dynamics during deployment without the computational overhead of predicting future visual frames.
3. **Implement Contact-Gated Attention Guidance:** To introduce Contact-gated Action-Visual-Tactile Attention Guidance (AVTAG), an auxiliary training mechanism. The objective of AVTAG is to mitigate the visual-dominance bias by explicitly encouraging action queries to prioritize and attend more strongly to tactile evidence during phases of physical contact. This guidance is designed to be active only during training, ensuring that the model learns to rely on tactile information when it is physically informative, without altering the inference-time architecture.
4. **Validate Performance on Real-World Tasks:** To empirically evaluate the VT-WAM framework on a set of six real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, covering both surface-interaction and constrained insertion regimes. This objective includes comparing VT-WAM's performance against established baselines to demonstrate its efficacy and robustness.
5. **Conduct Ablation Studies:** To perform detailed ablation studies that quantify the individual contributions of tactile dynamics modeling and the contact-gated attention guidance mechanism, thereby providing insights into the importance of the core design elements for success in contact-rich tasks.

These objectives collectively aim to improve robotic manipulation capabilities by enabling more sophisticated and responsive interaction with the physical environment, particularly in scenarios where tactile feedback is paramount.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VT-WAM is presented as a visual-tactile World Action Model designed for contact-rich manipulation. Its methodology is based on jointly learning future visual prediction, tactile deformation prediction, and action prediction within a unified flow matching framework.

**A. Architecture of VT-WAM:**
The overall architecture comprises a visual-tactile-action expert backbone (Fig. 2a).
1. **Input Modality Tokenization:**
 - **Visual:** Wrist camera observations ($O_v \in \mathbb{R}^{T_v \times 3 \times H \times W}$) are encoded by a Wan2.2 video VAE and then patchified into visual tokens ($X_v \in \mathbb{R}^{N_v \times d}$).
 - **Tactile:** Tactile deformation sequences ($O_t \in \mathbb{R}^{T_t \times 6 \times H_t \times W_t}$) from two tactile surfaces are encoded by a pretrained tactile VAE into tactile tokens ($X_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N_t \times d}$). These represent 3D deformation fields.
 - **Action:** Each action chunk ($A \in \mathbb{R}^{S_a \times D_a}$) is linearly projected into action tokens ($X_a \in \mathbb{R}^{S_a \times d}$).
 - **Auxiliary Inputs:** Language instructions and proprioceptive states are provided to each expert via cross-attention.
2. **Asymmetric Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Attention:**
 - **Mechanism:** At each layer, query, key, and value tensors are computed independently for visual, tactile, and action experts. These are then concatenated (Q, K, V) and processed by masked attention. The attention mask ($M$) dictates information flow between modalities.
 - **Cross-Modal Masking Rules (Fig. 2b):**
 - **Visual Expert (Query):** Masks out tactile and action keys, preventing local contact deformation or future action tokens from altering the visual representation.
 - **Tactile Expert (Query):** Attends only to the first-frame visual anchor (masking future visual tokens) for global context, and masks out action keys.
 - **Action Expert (Query):** Attends to both the first-frame visual anchor and the *entire* tactile sequence. This aligns with the visual-cache inference mode.
 - **Purpose:** This asymmetric design allows action prediction to leverage temporal tactile dynamics for contact evolution while maintaining scene context from a visual anchor, without incurring the computational cost of future visual prediction during inference.
3. **Output Heads:** After the MoT Attention layers, modality-specific projection heads predict velocity fields for visual, tactile, and action tokens, which are used in the flow matching objective. The action head is responsible for producing the action chunk for control.

**B. Contact-Gated Action-Visual-Tactile Attention Guidance (AVTAG):**
This component (Fig. 2c) addresses the visual-dominance bias during training, where dense visual signals might overshadow sparse but critical tactile cues.
1. **Auxiliary Attention Distribution:** AVTAG computes an auxiliary attention distribution from action queries ($Q_a$) to visual ($K_v$) and tactile ($K_t$) keys. Stop-gradient is applied to $K_v$ and $K_t$ to ensure gradients from AVTAG only guide action queries, leaving key representations optimized by the main flow matching objective.
2. **Relative Attention Weights:** For each action token, the auxiliary attention assigned to visual keys ($\alpha_v$) and tactile keys ($\alpha_t$) is summed. These are then normalized into relative visual ($p_v$) and tactile ($p_t$) attention weights.
3. **Contact-Gated Hinge Ranking Loss:** An auxiliary loss ($L_{AVTAG} = E_{r \in C}[\max(0, p_v(r) - p_t(r))])$ is applied. This loss is computed *only* for action tokens within identified contact phases ($C$) where tactile deformation is pronounced. It penalizes instances where relative visual attention ($p_v$) is higher than relative tactile attention ($p_t$), encouraging the action expert to increase reliance on tactile evidence during critical contact. This guidance is training-only.

**C. Training Objective and Efficient Inference:**
1. **Flow Matching Training Objective:** VT-WAM is trained with a joint flow matching objective, $L_{Flow} = \lambda_v L_v + \lambda_t L_t + \lambda_a L_a$. Each $L_x$ is the expected squared error between the predicted velocity field ($\hat{f}_x$) and the flow matching target ($f_x^*$) for modality $x$. The full training objective is $L_{Train} = L_{Flow} + \lambda_{AVTAG} L_{AVTAG}$.
2. **Efficient Visual-Cache Inference:** For real-world control, VT-WAM operates in visual-cache inference mode. The current visual observation acts as a first-frame anchor, and future visual prediction is disabled. The model denoies only tactile and action latents. The tactile expert predicts future tactile deformation (contact evolution), and the action expert predicts the action chunk by attending to both the visual anchor and the denoised tactile sequence. This minimizes latency by avoiding future visual token prediction.

**D. Experimental Setup:**
- **Platform:** A 7-DoF xArm7 robot with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, a wrist camera (128x128 RGB, 30 Hz), and two Xense tactile sensors (35x20 3D deformation fields, 30 Hz) on the gripper fingers.
- **Data:** 100 kinesthetic expert demonstrations per task, synchronized and resampled at 30 Hz.
- **Implementation:** Pretrained Wan2.2-5B for visual, 1B-scale DiT for tactile and action experts. Loss weights: $\lambda_v = \lambda_t = \lambda_a = 1$, $\lambda_{AVTAG} = 0.05$. AdamW optimizer, learning rate $1 \times 10^{-4}$, bf16 precision, gradient clipping, cosine decay. Trained on NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Inference uses 10 denoising steps.
- **Baselines:** DP + Tactile, RDP, $\pi_{0.5}$, OmniVTLA, Fast-WAM. All trained on the same data and platform.
- **Tasks:** Six real-world contact-rich tasks: "wipe board," "wipe vase," "peel cucumber" (surface-interaction) and "insert plug," "swipe card," "insert tube" (constrained insertion).
- **Metrics:** Success rate. For surface-interaction, 0, 0.5, 1 scores are used; for constrained insertion, 0 or 1.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VT-WAM involved assessing its performance on various contact-rich manipulation tasks, analyzing its predictive capabilities, and conducting ablation studies to isolate the contributions of its core components.

**A. Task Performance:**
VT-WAM achieved an average success rate of 71.67% across the six real-world contact-rich tasks (Table I). This represents an increase of 26.67% over the Fast-WAM baseline (45.00%) and 35.84% over OmniVTLA (35.83%).

- **Surface-Interaction Tasks:** VT-WAM obtained an average success rate of 81.67% on tasks such as "wipe board," "wipe vase," and "peel cucumber." In comparison, vision-only policies ($\pi_{0.5}$) and existing tactile-augmented VLA models (OmniVTLA) achieved lower success rates (33.33% to 36.67%), suggesting that simply providing tactile observations as input is insufficient to model necessary interaction dynamics. Fast-WAM, which models visual dynamics, achieved 56.67%, demonstrating the benefit of dynamics modeling. VT-WAM's further improvement is attributed to its explicit modeling of tactile deformation as interaction dynamics.
- **Constrained Insertion Tasks:** For tasks like "insert plug," "swipe card," and "insert tube," VT-WAM achieved a 61.67% average success rate. This outperformed OmniVTLA (38.33%) and Fast-WAM (33.33%). The improvements were particularly notable for tasks like "insert tube," where visual cues can be unreliable due to object transparency, and success relies on precise, contact-informed adjustments. These results indicate that tactile dynamics modeling is beneficial for correcting small pose errors under tight geometric constraints.

**B. Visual-Tactile Prediction Results:**
An analysis of VT-WAM's predictive capabilities, operating in joint inference mode (Fig. 5), demonstrated its ability to predict temporally coherent wrist camera observations and tactile deformation fields. The predicted tactile deformation accurately captured local contact patterns, including pressure concentration and contact migration. Quantitatively (Table II), VT-WAM achieved a lower l2 deformation magnitude error (0.077) and higher directional consistency (0.749) compared to baseline models exUMI (0.091, 0.618) and UVA (0.083, 0.667). This indicates that the tactile expert within VT-WAM successfully learned meaningful contact deformation dynamics.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies on "wipe vase" and "insert tube" tasks (Table III) evaluated the contributions of tactile dynamics modeling and attention guidance.

- **Tactile Dynamics Modeling:**
 - Adding tactile sequence prediction with symmetric MoT attention (+ Sym. (T Seq.)) to Fast-WAM improved success rates (e.g., Wipe Vase: 55% to 65%; Insert Tube: 25% to 40%), confirming the value of tactile dynamics beyond visual information.
 - The importance of temporal tactile information was highlighted by comparing variants of Asymmetric MoT Attention. When action queries were restricted to only the *first* tactile frame (+ Asym. (T$_0$)), performance was lower (Wipe Vase: 40%; Insert Tube: 30%). Allowing action queries to attend to the *full* tactile sequence (+ Asym. (T Seq.)) significantly increased success rates (Wipe Vase: 70%; Insert Tube: 50%), demonstrating that action prediction benefits from understanding tactile evolution over time.
- **Effectiveness of Contact-Gated AVTAG:**
 - The introduction of contact-gated AVTAG to the model using Asymmetric MoT Attention with full tactile sequence (M$_3$ + AVTAG, which is VT-WAM) further improved success rates (Wipe Vase: 70% to 85%; Insert Tube: 50% to 55%). This indicates that AVTAG effectively guides the action expert to prioritize tactile evidence during critical contact phases.
 - An illustrative example (Fig. 6) demonstrated AVTAG's role in contact recovery during vase wiping. When contact was disturbed, a policy without AVTAG showed static, visual-dominant attention and failed to respond. With AVTAG, the action expert increased tactile attention during the contact phase, enabling the policy to re-establish contact and complete the task. This suggests AVTAG mitigates visual bias and promotes effective tactile-informed decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of VT-WAM represents an advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks that necessitate close physical interaction with the environment. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several aspects:

- **Enhanced Performance in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** VT-WAM consistently demonstrated superior performance across a range of real-world contact-rich tasks, outperforming both visual-only and existing tactile-augmented baselines. This indicates a more robust and effective approach to tasks requiring precise force control, sustained interaction, or fine alignment under contact. This capability is critical for deploying robots in unstructured and complex environments where such interactions are common.
- **Improved Utilization of Tactile Information:** The research moves beyond treating tactile observations merely as auxiliary inputs. By integrating tactile deformation dynamics directly into a world action model and coupling it with action prediction, VT-WAM enables a more profound and effective exploitation of tactile sensor data. This is particularly impactful because tactile cues, though sparse, are often decisive for successful contact-rich tasks.
- **Mitigation of Visual-Dominance Bias:** The introduction of Contact-gated Action-Visual-Tactile Attention Guidance (AVTAG) directly addresses a known challenge in multimodal learning: the tendency for models to over-rely on dense visual information at the expense of sparse but critical tactile signals. By explicitly encouraging tactile attention during contact phases, VT-WAM trains a more balanced and context-aware policy, leading to more responsive and physically intelligent robot behaviors.
- **Advancement in Multimodal World Models:** VT-WAM extends the concept of World Action Models (WAMs) to a visual-tactile domain. By jointly modeling visual and tactile dynamics alongside action prediction within a unified flow matching framework, it contributes to the development of more comprehensive and adaptable embodied AI systems that can predict and act upon complex physical interactions. The Asymmetric MoT Attention, designed for efficient inference while retaining temporal tactile dynamics, showcases an innovative way to manage multimodal information flow.
- **Foundational for Future Robotic Applications:** The improved capabilities offered by VT-WAM could have implications for various robotic applications, including dexterous manipulation, assembly, quality inspection, and human-robot interaction where physical contact is integral. The methodologies presented could serve as a foundation for developing policies that are more sensitive to haptic feedback, leading to more sophisticated and nuanced robotic skills.
- **Direction for Further Research:** The work explicitly identifies avenues for future research, such as scaling VT-WAM to multi-task training and investigating scaling laws. This suggests that the foundational principles established in this paper have the potential for broader generalization and application across a wider spectrum of robotic challenges, further extending its impact on the field.
