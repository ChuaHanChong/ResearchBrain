# $Ψ_0$: An Open Foundation Model Towards Universal Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Ψ0 introduces an open foundation model for humanoid loco-manipulation, employing a decoupled learning strategy that pre-trains on human egocentric videos for generalizable visual-action representations and post-trains on significantly less robot data for precise joint control. This approach achieves over 40% higher success rates on complex, long-horizon tasks compared to state-of-the-art baselines, demonstrating improved data efficiency.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in complex, dexterous loco-manipulation due to the difficulty of acquiring large-scale, high-quality teleoperation data.
- Existing large action models often rely on extensive real robot data, which is resource-intensive and challenging to scale for humanoids.
- Directly transferring knowledge from human videos to robots is complicated by a significant 'embodiment gap' (kinematic and dynamic differences), leading to suboptimal performance in co-training approaches.

## Method

- **Ψ0** proposes a triple-system architecture combining a ==Qwen3-VL== VLM backbone (System-2), a flow-based ==Multi-modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT)== action expert (System-1), and an off-the-shelf RL lower-body controller (System-0).
- A three-stage training recipe decouples learning: pre-training the VLM on human egocentric videos for general visual-action representations, post-training the MM-DiT action expert on cross-task real humanoid data, and fine-tuning on task-specific robot data.
- ==Real-Time Action Chunking (RTC)== is introduced to mitigate inference latency of large VLA models, ensuring smooth and stable real-world execution, along with a tailored teleoperation pipeline for high-quality robot data collection.

## Results

- Ψ0 achieves an average overall success rate over **4**0%** higher** than the second-best baseline (GR00T N1.6) across eight diverse, long-horizon dexterous loco-manipulation tasks.
- The model demonstrates high data efficiency, using approximately **800 hours** of human video and only **30 hours** of real-world robot data for post-training and fine-tuning, significantly less than compared baselines.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of VLM pre-training on human video, the superior performance of the MM-DiT expert over naive DiT, and that Real-Time Action Chunking mitigates physical collisions and increases rollout throughput without performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning process, by first leveraging human egocentric video for general priors and then specialized robot data for precise control, effectively bridges the embodiment gap and maximizes data utility.
> - Large-scale human video pre-training is crucial for learning meaningful visual representations and task semantics, even with a different action representation from downstream robot tasks.
> - The MM-DiT action expert's dual-modulation design and joint attention mechanisms are effective for fusing vision-language features with action representations, improving control performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of complex loco-manipulation tasks represents a significant challenge in robotics. While advances in whole-body motion control for humanoids have been observed, manipulation capabilities, particularly dexterous loco-manipulation, have received less attention and encountered greater difficulties.

The broader research landscape has been significantly influenced by the success of large language models (LLMs), inspiring exploration of scaling laws for physically embodied agents. Recent works such as RT 1-2, OpenVLA, Gemini Robotics, GR00T, and Physical Intelligence's π0 and π0.5 have pursued training large action models using extensive real robot data. These approaches have indicated that the reasoning and planning abilities inherent in large models can enhance generalization in robotic manipulation. However, a limitation of these methods is their reliance on large-scale teleoperation data, which is resource-intensive and challenging to acquire, particularly for complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.

Human egocentric videos have emerged as a scalable alternative, offering abundant natural motion patterns and behavior-level information without the high cost of robot teleoperation. Nonetheless, directly transferring knowledge from human videos to humanoid control is complicated by the substantial "embodiment gap," which includes differences in action frequency, motion dynamics, and degrees of freedom between humans and robots. Prior efforts, such as those adopting unified human-centric state-action representations or employing domain adaptation and co-training strategies (e.g., EgoVLA, In-n-On, H-RDT), have attempted to bridge this gap. However, these methods often involve a single monolithic policy that attempts to model two fundamentally distinct action distributions (human and robot), which the authors argue is inherently suboptimal. As a consequence, such policies have shown limitations in controlling humanoids for complex, long-horizon tasks.

This work, Ψ0, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the fundamental question of how to effectively distill motion priors and world knowledge from human egocentric videos to enable robust whole-body control for humanoid robots, specifically by proposing a decoupled learning process that aims to maximize the utility of heterogeneous data sources, thereby addressing the data efficiency and performance limitations of prior co-training approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Ψ0 (Psi-Zero), an open foundation model designed to address challenging humanoid loco-manipulation tasks. The authors identify that existing strategies, which often involve co-training on large and diverse human and humanoid data, are suboptimal due to the fundamental kinematic and motion disparities between humans and humanoid robots. This suboptimality leads to unsatisfactory data efficiency and model performance despite considerable data volumes.

To address this identified challenge, the core motivation of Ψ0 is to decouple the learning process. This decoupling aims to maximize the utility of heterogeneous data sources by structuring learning into distinct stages with specific objectives:

1. **Pre-training for generalized visual-action representations:** The initial stage focuses on autoregressively pre-training a Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone. The motivation here is to leverage large-scale egocentric human videos to enable the model to acquire generalizable visual-action representations and task-level motion priors across diverse human activities. This stage aims to learn visual representations that are aligned with downstream robotic tasks. The authors emphasize the quality of human data, specifically high-quality egocentric human manipulation data, as a critical recipe ingredient.
2. **Post-training for precise robot joint control:** Following the VLM pre-training, a separate flow-based action expert is post-trained using high-quality humanoid robot data. The motivation for this stage is to learn precise robot joint control, thereby capturing embodiment-specific dynamics that are unique to the humanoid platform. This stage includes both task-agnostic training on cross-task humanoid data and task-specific fine-tuning on in-domain teleoperated demonstrations, requiring a comparatively smaller amount of real-robot data. The authors contrast this approach with strategies that scale with noisy internet clips or heterogeneous cross-embodiment robot datasets, asserting that their recipe yields superior performance.

Beyond the core training paradigm, the research is motivated by several practical considerations for developing effective and deployable humanoid VLAs:

- **Improving teleoperation for data quality:** The authors aim to optimize a manipulation-oriented teleoperation pipeline to enhance lower-body stability during whole-body manipulation, recognizing that high-quality in-domain data is critical for fine-tuning.
- **Mitigating inference latency:** Humanoid robots require smooth and reactive control. Large VLA models often introduce inference latency, leading to "stop-and-think" behavior and motion jitter. A motivation is to introduce training-time real-time action chunking to ensure smooth execution in the real world at inference time.
- **Open-sourcing the ecosystem:** A broader objective is to open-source the entire ecosystem, including data processing and training pipelines, the humanoid foundation model, and a real-time action inference engine, to facilitate community research and development in humanoid robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Ψ0 framework is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for humanoid dexterous loco-manipulation, designed with a triple-system architecture. Given a natural language instruction and current observation (head camera image and proprioceptive state), the model predicts a whole-body action chunk.

**A. Model Architecture:**
Ψ0 comprises three components:
- **System-2 (VLM Backbone):** Implemented using Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct, a state-of-the-art vision-language foundation model. This component processes visual observations and language instructions.
- **System-1 (Multi-modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) Action Expert):** A flow-based MM-DiT, approximately 500 million parameters, inspired by Stable Diffusion 3. This expert is conditioned on hidden features from the VLM backbone and predicts future whole-body action chunks. The MM-DiT architecture is designed to efficiently fuse action and vision-language features through joint global attention between action tokens and VL tokens, utilizing a dual-modulation design with time-conditioning features.
- **System-0 (Lower-Body Controller):** An off-the-shelf RL-based tracking policy, AMO, maps 8-DoF lower-body actions (torso roll/pitch/yaw, base height, linear/angular velocities, target yaw rotation) to 15-DoF lower-body joint angles.

The action `a ∈ R^36` is defined by `{q_hand, q_arm, torso_rpy, h_b, v_x, v_y, v_yaw, p_yaw}`, where `q_hand ∈ R^14` and `q_arm ∈ R^14` are joint states for hands and arms, `torso_rpy ∈ R^3` for torso orientation, `h_b ∈ R` for base height, `v_x, v_y ∈ R` for horizontal linear velocities, `v_yaw ∈ R` for angular velocity, and `p_yaw ∈ R` for target yaw rotation. Combined with the lower-body controller, this results in 43-DoF actions for whole-body control.

**B. Training Recipe:**
The training procedure is structured in three stages:

1. **Pre-Training on Egocentric Human Video:**
 - **Data:** Primarily EgoDex (approximately 829 hours of human egocentric video data capturing dexterous manipulation) and Humanoid Everyday (31 hours of humanoid data covering diverse tasks) to mitigate the visual gap.
 - **Action Representation:** A shared 48-DoF task-space action representation `{T_wrist, P_thumb, P_index, P_middle, P_ring, P_pinky}` (wrist pose + 3D fingertip positions) is used for both human and robot end-effectors, enabling joint training.
 - **Action Tokenization:** Continuous actions are tokenized into discrete tokens (N ≈ 20) using a custom-trained FAST tokenizer to improve pre-training efficiency and reconstruction loss.
 - **Objective:** The VLM is trained autoregressively to predict a single next-step action `a_t` (not chunks) to learn task semantics and visual representations, thereby reducing computational cost. The loss maximizes `p_θ(a) = Prod_{t=1 to N} p_θ(a_t | a_<t, ℓ, o_t)`.
 - **Details:** Training involved 64 A100 GPUs for 10 days, 230k steps, with images resized to 360 × 240.

2. **Post-Training on Cross-Task Real Humanoid Data:**
 - **Data:** Humanoid Everyday dataset (approximately 3 million frames of real-world teleoperated data). Action representations for different embodiments (G1 with Dex3-1 and H1 with Inspire Hand) are aligned and padded to 36-DoF joint-space vectors.
 - **Objective:** The VLM backbone parameters are frozen, and the action expert (MM-DiT) is trained from scratch using a flow-matching objective. It predicts future whole-body action chunks `a_t:t+H` conditioned on the hidden features `z_t` from the VLM and a uniformly sampled flow timestep `τ`. The loss is `L_fm = E[||v_flow_ρ(z_t, a_τ_t, τ) - (ϵ - a_t)||]`.
 - **Details:** Training involved 32 A100 GPUs for 30 hours, 30k steps, with images down-scaled to 320 × 240.

3. **Fine-Tuning on In-domain Teleoperation Data:**
 - **Data:** Small amounts of task-specific real-robot data (80 teleoperated trajectories per task).
 - **Objective:** Only the action expert is fine-tuned end-to-end to rapidly adapt to new, long-horizon, dexterous loco-manipulation tasks.
 - **Details:** 40,000 steps per task using a cosine learning rate scheduler.

**C. Real-Time Action Chunking (RTC):**
To address inference latency and ensure smooth control (Ψ0's forward pass takes ~160ms), training-time real-time chunking is employed. This method randomly removes diffusion noise from the first `d` (up to `d_max=6`) action tokens during training, masking them from loss computation. The model is trained to predict future actions consistent with previously executed actions. During inference, unexecuted steps are treated as clean tokens, allowing for continuous action generation. The deployment system uses asynchronous client-server threads, with a 30Hz control loop and an asynchronously triggered inference loop, to ensure seamless chunk transitions.

**D. Tailoring Teleoperation for Loco-Manipulation:**
A specialized teleoperation framework was developed to balance expressiveness, stability, and operational simplicity:
- **Upper-Body Control:** A PICO headset and wrist trackers capture upper-body poses, used in a multi-target inverse kinematics solver to compute humanoid arm joint positions, torso orientation, and pelvis height.
- **Dexterous Manipulation:** MANUS gloves provide fine-grained finger motions, which are retargeted to the G1 humanoid's three-finger dexterous hands, offering precise control without relying on vision-based VR hand tracking.
- **Locomotion:** High-level commands (translational and angular velocities) are inferred from waist and foot trackers and provided to an RL-based policy (AMO) for stable lower-body control, avoiding direct retargeting of full-body human motion which can lead to instability.

**E. Hardware Platform:**
All real-world experiments were conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid platform, equipped with 29 degrees of freedom for whole-body control and two 7-DoF Dex3-1 dexterous hands. Visual observations are captured by a head-mounted Intel RealSense D435i camera.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive real-world experiments demonstrating the performance of Ψ0 across a range of challenging humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.

**A. Overall Performance and Data Efficiency:**
Ψ0 exhibits robust performance across eight diverse long-horizon dexterous loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., removing a lid and turning a faucet, pouring liquids, pushing carts, placing objects, squatting). Compared to several recent open-source baselines (π0.5, GR00T N1.6, InternVLA-M1, EgoVLA, H-RDT, Diffusion Policy, ACT), Ψ0 achieves an average overall success rate that is over 40% higher than the second-best baseline, GR00T-N1.6.

A notable finding is that Ψ0 achieves this performance using a comparatively small amount of real-world robotic data: approximately 800 hours of human video data for pre-training and only 30 hours of real-world robot data for post-training and fine-tuning. This contrasts with baselines that often utilize 10 times as much data, suggesting that the "effective scaling requires scaling the right data in the right way" hypothesis is supported.

**B. Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies were conducted on a dual-arm long-horizon task involving picking, placing, and carrying, as well as on other tasks, to investigate the contributions of different components:

- **Role of Pre-Training and Post-Training:**
 - Freezing the Qwen3-VL backbone (not pre-trained on EgoDex) and fine-tuning only the action head resulted in an overall success rate of 0.2 (poorest performance), indicating the necessity of VLM pre-training.
 - Pre-training the VLM backbone on EgoDex for task-space next-action prediction significantly improved performance, even though the VLM was trained on a different action representation than the downstream action head. This suggests that pre-training effectively learned meaningful visual representations and task semantics.
 - Post-training the action expert on high-quality robot data (Humanoid Everyday) further enhanced overall performance, achieving better embodiment-specific control.
 - Ablations further show that pre-training on only 10% of EgoDex data led to significantly worse performance, demonstrating the value of large-scale human video. Similarly, pre-training solely on Humanoid Everyday performed well on less precise tasks but lagged behind Ψ0 on tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation.

- **MM-DiT versus Naive DiT:**
 - The proposed MM-DiT action expert consistently outperformed a naive DiT variant. This improvement is attributed to MM-DiT’s dual-modulation design and joint attention mechanism, which facilitates more effective integration of vision-language features from the VLM backbone with action representations.

- **Real-Time Chunking (RTC) Behaviors:**
 - Training-time real-time chunking (RTC) was implemented to address the inference latency of large VLA models, which can cause "stop-and-think" behavior and motion jitter.
 - RTC was observed to mitigate physical collisions during policy execution and increase policy rollout throughput without performance degradation. For GR00T, implementing RTC showed comparable performance, indicating its role in improving stability and execution rather than raw task success rate in all contexts.

- **Multi-Task Fine-Tuning:**
 - Multi-task fine-tuning across several tasks resulted in a performance drop for individual tasks compared to single-task fine-tuning. This suggests that multi-task training might disperse the model's learning objective, leading to underfitting in the current setup.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Ψ0: An Open Foundation Model Towards Universal Humanoid Loco-Manipulation" offers several significant contributions and has the potential for notable impact on the field of robotics.

**A. Strategic Data Utilization:**
The central significance lies in the demonstration of an effective paradigm for training humanoid models that challenges the notion of simply scaling data volume. By decoupling the learning process into distinct stages, Ψ0 leverages large-scale, affordable, high-quality human egocentric videos for acquiring generalizable visual-action representations and motion priors. This is complemented by post-training on a comparatively smaller volume of high-quality, domain-specific real-world humanoid data for precise robot joint control. This approach provides an alternative to expensive large-scale teleoperation data collection and addresses the suboptimality of co-training heterogeneous data, thereby offering a more resource-efficient pathway to advanced humanoid capabilities.

**B. Methodological Innovations:**
The paper introduces and empirically validates several technical innovations that enhance the effectiveness and practicality of humanoid VLAs:
- **Staged Training Paradigm:** The decoupled learning process, with pre-training on human video and post-training on robot data, represents a structured approach to bridge the embodiment gap.
- **MM-DiT Action Expert:** The development of a Multi-modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) action expert designed for efficient fusion of visual-language and action features demonstrates an architectural improvement for VLA models.
- **Training-Time Real-Time Action Chunking (RTC):** The implementation of RTC addresses a critical deployment challenge by mitigating inference latency and ensuring smoother, more stable real-world execution, which is crucial for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- **Tailored Teleoperation Pipeline:** The proposed teleoperation framework, which separates upper-body pose tracking, dexterous manipulation, and locomotion commands using a combination of VR headsets, wrist trackers, and MANUS gloves, improves data quality and operational stability for collecting in-domain robot data.

**C. Advancement in Humanoid Loco-Manipulation:**
By achieving a substantial increase in overall success rate compared to existing state-of-the-art baselines on diverse, complex, and long-horizon real-world tasks, Ψ0 demonstrates progress in universal humanoid loco-manipulation. The ability to perform tasks requiring precise arm coordination, long-distance navigation, and dexterous manipulation with reduced real-world robot data requirements suggests a lower barrier to entry for developing and deploying advanced humanoid skills.

**D. Open-Source Contribution:**
The commitment to open-source the entire ecosystem, including the data processing pipeline, training infrastructure, pre-trained model weights, and real-time action inference engine, is a significant contribution to the robotics community. This open access can accelerate future research, enable reproducibility, and foster collaborative development of humanoid learning, potentially leading to faster progress in the field.

**E. Pathway to Generalist Humanoids:**
Ultimately, this work lays a foundation for the development of generalist humanoid robots capable of assisting with everyday tasks. By providing an effective strategy for distilling human motion priors and interaction knowledge into robot control, Ψ0 contributes towards the vision of humanoids that can robustly interact with and adapt to diverse human-centric environments.
