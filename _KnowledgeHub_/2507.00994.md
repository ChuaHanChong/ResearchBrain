# Should We Still Pretrain Encoders with Masked Language Modeling?

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a controlled study comparing Masked Language Modeling (MLM) and Causal Language Modeling (CLM) as pretraining objectives for text encoders across various model sizes and a fixed data budget. The research finds that while MLM generally yields stronger representations for downstream tasks, a two-stage CLM then MLM approach or continuing pretraining with MLM from a CLM checkpoint delivers optimal performance and improved fine-tuning stability.

## Problem

- The observed superior performance of CLM-pretrained decoder models, when repurposed as encoders, might stem from their typically larger scale rather than an inherent advantage of the CLM objective, creating ambiguity in optimal pretraining strategies.
- A lack of controlled studies makes it difficult to disentangle the specific contributions of Masked Language Modeling (MLM) and Causal Language Modeling (CLM) for learning text representations.
- There is a need to identify more efficient and effective pretraining strategies for encoder models, particularly by exploring hybrid approaches or leveraging existing large language model checkpoints.

## Method

- Conducted a large-scale controlled study (**38** models, **110,000 GPU hours**) using identical encoder architectures and sizes (**210M**, **610M**, **1B** parameters) trained on a fixed **100 billion** unique tokens.
- Investigated various pretraining objectives: ==Masked Language Modeling (MLM)== with different masking ratios, ==Causal Language Modeling (CLM)==, and a ==two-stage sequential CLM followed by MLM==.
- Explored two pretraining scenarios: training from scratch (PFS) and Continued PreTraining (CPT) from existing CLM or MLM checkpoints, followed by extensive fine-tuning across four categories of NLP tasks (SC, TC, QA, IR).

## Results

- MLM generally outperformed CLM for text representation tasks (e.g., Sequence Classification, Question Answering) when models were pretrained exclusively from scratch.
- A two-stage pretraining protocol (CLM followed by MLM) consistently achieved better downstream performance compared to MLM-only training under fixed computational budgets.
- Applying MLM Continued Pretraining (CPT) to a CLM-pretrained model resulted in superior downstream performance compared to CPT on an already MLM-pretrained model, demonstrating an efficient pathway to strong encoders.
- CLM-pretrained models showed better data efficiency in early training stages and improved fine-tuning stability, exhibiting lower sensitivity to fine-tuning learning rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - CLM offers benefits beyond raw performance, such as better data efficiency in early training stages and improved fine-tuning stability.
> - Combining CLM and MLM, either through a two-stage protocol or by adapting existing CLM models with MLM, can leverage the strengths of both objectives for superior encoder performance.
> - Existing large CLM-pretrained decoder models can serve as computationally efficient starting points for developing strong encoder models via MLM continued pretraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Natural Language Processing (NLP) extensively relies on high-quality text representations for a wide array of tasks, including sequence classification, named entity recognition, question answering, and information retrieval. Traditionally, encoder models, exemplified by BERT, have been pretrained using Masked Language Modeling (MLM). This approach involves training a model to predict masked tokens within a sequence, leveraging a bidirectional attention mechanism to capture contextual information from both sides of a word.

More recently, decoder-only models, primarily designed for Causal Language Modeling (CLM) (i.e., next-token prediction), have demonstrated unexpected effectiveness when repurposed as encoders. These CLM-trained models have achieved strong performance on text representation benchmarks such as MTEB. However, the observed success of these CLM-based encoders has often been associated with significantly larger model sizes and training data volumes compared to conventional MLM-based encoders. This scale difference introduces a confounding factor, making it unclear whether the superior performance of these adapted decoders stems from an inherent advantage of the CLM pretraining objective itself or is merely a consequence of increased computational resources and data.

This study directly addresses this ambiguity by conducting a controlled comparison between MLM and CLM pretraining strategies. It aims to isolate the impact of the pretraining objective on text representation quality, disentangling it from confounding factors like model and data scale. By systematically evaluating both traditional and sequential pretraining approaches under controlled conditions, the work seeks to clarify the role of causal language modeling in learning effective text representations for encoder-style tasks, thereby informing future directions in encoder design and pretraining methodology. It also explores hybrid strategies that combine the strengths of both paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a controlled investigation into the effectiveness of Masked Language Modeling (MLM) versus Causal Language Modeling (CLM) for pretraining text encoders. The study aims to determine whether the recent success of CLM-trained decoder models when adapted as encoders reflects an intrinsic advantage of the CLM objective or is primarily attributable to larger model and data scales often associated with their training.

The motivation for this detailed inquiry is multi-faceted:
- **Clarifying the Role of CLM:** Recent advancements have shown large decoder models, pretrained with CLM, achieving state-of-the-art results on text embedding benchmarks like MTEB. These models are then repurposed as encoders, challenging the traditional dominance of MLM for encoder pretraining. However, a critical question remains: Is CLM inherently better for learning representations, or do these gains arise from the significantly larger scale (parameters and data) at which these decoders are typically trained? This paper seeks to provide empirical evidence to answer this question by eliminating scale as a confounding variable.
- **Optimizing Encoder Pretraining Strategies:** Understanding the comparative strengths and weaknesses of MLM and CLM can lead to more effective and efficient pretraining methodologies for encoders. If CLM offers specific benefits, it could be integrated into encoder training pipelines, potentially improving performance or reducing computational costs.
- **Addressing Data and Compute Efficiency:** Traditional MLM pretraining can be computationally intensive and may require substantial data to achieve optimal performance. If CLM demonstrates superior data efficiency or fine-tuning stability, it could offer a more accessible pathway to high-quality text representations, particularly for scenarios with limited data or computational resources, such as low-resource languages.
- **Exploring Hybrid Approaches:** The study also aims to investigate whether combining CLM and MLM in a sequential manner can synergize their respective advantages, leading to superior text representations than either method alone, especially within a fixed computational budget. This includes examining a "continued pretraining" scenario where existing CLM-pretrained models are further trained with MLM.
- **Providing Reproducible Benchmarks:** By releasing all project artifacts, including pretrained model checkpoints and codebases, the authors aim to foster further research and ensure the reproducibility of their large-scale findings, contributing to a more robust understanding of pretraining dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study employed a large-scale, controlled experimental design involving the training and evaluation of 38 models and over 15,000 fine-tuning and evaluation runs. This amounted to approximately 110,000 MI250X GPU hours.

#### 3.1. Model Architectures

Models followed the EuroBERT architecture, with three distinct sizes: 210 million, 610 million, and 1 billion parameters. All models utilized a maximum context length of 2,048 tokens and a RoPE θ value of 10,000.

#### 3.2. Pretraining Data

All models were pretrained on unique English tokens derived from the FineWeb-Edu dataset. A critical aspect of the controlled setup was ensuring that all models were exposed to the exact same sequence of samples during training to enable fair comparisons. The default pretraining setup involved 100 billion tokens, which is five times the optimal data budget suggested for 1B-parameter decoder models.

#### 3.3. Pretraining Paradigms

Three primary pretraining approaches were investigated:
1. **Causal Language Modeling (CLM):** Models were trained with a next-token prediction objective using a causal attention mask. The objective was to minimize the negative log-likelihood of predicting the current token given preceding tokens.
2. **Masked Language Modeling (MLM):** Models were trained to reconstruct randomly masked tokens using a bidirectional attention mask. Tokens were masked uniformly with probabilities ($p_{mask}$) of 20%, 30%, 40%, and 50% to explore the impact of masking ratio.
3. **CLM+MLM (Sequential):** This two-stage approach involved initial pretraining with CLM, followed by continued pretraining with MLM.

#### 3.4. Pretraining Hyperparameters

- **Batch Size:** A per-device batch size of 12 samples was used across 192 GPUs, resulting in an effective batch size of 2,373,120 tokens. Input sequences varied in length up to 2,048 tokens.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** A Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) schedule was employed: 2,000 warmup steps, 38,000 steps at a constant learning rate of 5 × 10⁻⁴, and a final 2,000-step linear decay, totaling 42,000 training steps.

#### 3.5. Pretraining Setups

Two distinct pretraining setups were explored:
1. **Pretraining From Scratch (PFS):** Models were initialized randomly and trained for a fixed number of steps (12,000, 22,000, or 42,000) using either CLM, MLM, or sequential CLM+MLM. For sequential training, CLM pretraining was performed first, and then MLM was resumed from CLM checkpoints that had not undergone learning rate decay.
2. **Continued PreTraining (CPT):** Training began from models already pretrained with either CLM or MLM (which had completed their initial training and learning rate decay). These models were then subjected to further training using MLM for varying lengths (2,000, 12,000, and 22,000 steps). This setup aimed to simulate adapting existing models.

#### 3.6. Fine-tuning Tasks and Datasets

Models were evaluated across four categories of text representation tasks:
- **Sequence Classification (SC):** SST-2, MNLI, QQP.
- **Token Classification (TC):** CoNLL (English), OntoNotes (English), UNER (English).
- **Question Answering (QA):** SQuAD, SQuAD-v2, ReCoRD.
- **Information Retrieval (IR):** MS MARCO, NQ, MLDR (English).

#### 3.7. Fine-tuning Protocol

To ensure fair comparison and address fine-tuning instability:
- Each model was fine-tuned for up to 1,000 steps or one full epoch, with a batch size of 32.
- A grid search over six learning rates (1×10⁻⁵ to 5×10⁻⁴) was performed for each model-dataset pair, with 10% warmup and linear decay.
- Five random seeds were used for each run to account for variance.
- Task-specific loss functions were applied (cross-entropy for SC, TC, QA; InfoNCE for IR with in-batch negatives). Mean-pooled token embeddings were used for SC and IR.
- For NQ and MLDR, training was performed on MS MARCO due to lack of specific training data.

#### 3.8. Evaluation Metrics

- **SC:** Accuracy.
- **TC, QA:** F1 score.
- **IR:** NDCG@10.
Results were reported as averages across seeds, along with 95% confidence intervals.

#### 3.9. Infrastructure and Experimental Scale

All experiments were conducted on MI250X GPUs via the Adastra supercomputer using the AMD-optimized EuroBERT codebase. Pretraining involved 192 GPUs, while fine-tuning used single GPUs. The substantial compute budget allowed for robust statistical evaluation, especially crucial given the known variance in representation learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding the efficacy of MLM and CLM pretraining for learning text representations across different setups and scales.

#### 4.1. Pretraining with CLM or MLM

- **MLM generally outperforms CLM on text representation tasks:** Models trained exclusively with MLM tended to achieve better downstream performance, particularly on Sequence Classification (SC) and Question Answering (QA) tasks. This performance gap was consistent across model sizes, with QA showing the highest sensitivity to the absence of bidirectional attention.
- **No universally optimal masking ratio for MLM:** The optimal masking ratio varied depending on model size and downstream task. Larger models generally benefited from higher masking ratios, aligning with previous research. Information Retrieval (IR) tasks consistently favored higher ratios, while token-level tasks like Token Classification (TC) and QA showed better performance with lower ratios for smaller models. Larger models (610M and 1B) exhibited a U-shaped performance curve for token-level tasks, suggesting benefits at both low and high masking rates. A 40% masking ratio for 610M models was selected as a balanced compromise for subsequent experiments.
- **CLM models can be competitive and efficient:** While CLM models often underperformed MLM on SC and QA, they achieved strong results on IR, with the performance gap narrowing at larger model sizes. For TC, CLM models consistently matched or outperformed MLM models, suggesting that causal pretraining can yield robust token-level representations.
- **CLM demonstrates higher data efficiency in early stages:** In the initial phases of training (up to 10,000-20,000 steps), CLM models consistently exhibited superior downstream performance compared to MLM. However, MLM models typically caught up and surpassed CLM as training progressed, while CLM showed diminishing returns in later stages. This indicates CLM's effectiveness for efficient representation learning with fewer training steps or in data-scarce environments.
- **CLM-based pretraining improves fine-tuning stability:** CLM-pretrained models showed reduced sensitivity to the choice of fine-tuning learning rate compared to MLM-pretrained models. This suggests that CLM provides a more stable initialization, potentially simplifying hyperparameter tuning and leading to more reliable performance during adaptation.

#### 4.2. Two-Stage CLM+MLM Pretraining (PFS Setting)

- **Sequential CLM then MLM offers optimal performance under fixed compute:** When pretraining models from scratch with a fixed computational budget, a two-stage strategy combining CLM followed by MLM consistently improved downstream performance over MLM-only training. Specifically, a 25% CLM / 75% MLM split reliably surpassed the pure MLM baseline, and even a 75% CLM allocation maintained comparable results. This indicates that CLM's strengths (e.g., data efficiency, token-level representations) complement MLM effectively.
- **CLM pretraining reduces sensitivity to masking ratio:** Models that underwent initial CLM pretraining demonstrated lower variability in performance across different masking ratios during the subsequent MLM phase compared to models trained solely with MLM. This suggests a stabilizing effect of CLM pretraining on the model's robustness to masking ratio choices.

#### 4.3. Continued Pretraining from CLM and MLM Models (CPT Setting)

- **MLM CPT on a CLM-pretrained model outperforms MLM-only CPT:** Continuing training with MLM on an already CLM-pretrained model consistently resulted in superior downstream performance compared to continuing MLM training on an already MLM-pretrained model. For TC, CLM-adapted models maintained their strong performance. For QA and IR, the performance gap to MLM was effectively closed, and for SC, the MLM-adapted CLM model notably surpassed the MLM-only CPT model.
- **Fewer CPT steps yield strong performance with CLM initialization:** It was observed that a relatively smaller number of MLM CPT steps (e.g., 12,000 steps) on a CLM-pretrained model could achieve performance comparable to or better than longer MLM-only CPT durations. This indicates an efficient pathway to enhance CLM-based models into competitive encoders. Moreover, the improvement curve for CLM-adapted models appeared steeper towards the end of CPT, whereas MLM-only training tended to plateau.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study provides a controlled and comprehensive empirical analysis that challenges the conventional understanding of optimal pretraining strategies for text encoders. Its findings hold several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Re-evaluation of Encoder Pretraining Paradigms:** The research directly addresses the question of whether Masked Language Modeling (MLM) remains the universally optimal approach for encoder pretraining. By demonstrating that Causal Language Modeling (CLM) offers distinct advantages in data efficiency, training stability, and performance on specific tasks (particularly token-level and, with adaptation, retrieval tasks), the study encourages a re-evaluation of current practices.
- **Development of More Efficient Encoder Training:** The finding that sequential CLM followed by MLM pretraining can achieve optimal performance under fixed computational budgets suggests a more efficient training curriculum for high-performing encoder models. This has direct implications for reducing the computational resources and energy consumption associated with large-scale NLP model development.
- **Leveraging Existing Pretrained Models:** The strong performance of continuing MLM pretraining on existing CLM-pretrained models indicates a practical and cost-effective strategy. Given the increasing availability of large, publicly accessible CLM models, this approach offers a pathway to develop robust encoders without incurring the substantial cost of training them from scratch using traditional MLM methods. This could accelerate research and development in areas where specialized encoders are needed.
- **Benefits for Resource-Constrained Scenarios:** CLM's demonstrated data efficiency and improved fine-tuning stability make it a particularly appealing option for settings with limited training data or computational resources, such as pretraining models for low-resource languages or applications with tight budget constraints.
- **Improved Understanding of Pretraining Objectives:** The detailed ablation studies provide a clearer understanding of how different learning objectives (MLM vs. CLM) and attention mechanisms influence the quality of learned representations for various downstream tasks. This nuanced understanding can guide future architectural and training innovations in NLP.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By meticulously controlling variables and releasing all project artifacts, the study provides a robust benchmark and foundation for subsequent research into hybrid pretraining strategies, optimal masking ratios, alternative training curricula, and extensions to multilingual or multi-modal settings. This includes exploring contrastive post-training in conjunction with these pretraining methods.
- **Environmental and Practical Benefits:** Reduced computational resources for model training translate into lower energy consumption and a smaller environmental footprint. Furthermore, more efficient encoder models might reduce the reliance on larger, more compute-intensive generative models for tasks where specialized encoders are sufficient, contributing to more sustainable AI development.
