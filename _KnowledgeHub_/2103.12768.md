# DA4Event: towards bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Event Cameras using Domain Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> DA4Event reframes the event-camera sim-to-real gap as a feature-level domain-shift problem, applying unsupervised domain adaptation (GRL, MMD, AFN, Rotation, Entropy) to simulated-vs-real event data. Its MV-DA4E architecture regroups multi-channel event representations into 3-channel views processed by a shared ImageNet-pretrained ResNet and fused by multi-view pooling, reaching 89.24% on N-Caltech101, near the 90.09% supervised bound.

## Problem

- Event cameras lack large labeled datasets, so training relies on simulators, but simulated events mismatch real sensor statistics and degrade downstream classification accuracy.
- Prior sim-to-real fixes act only at the input level, tuning or sampling the simulator contrast threshold, leaving no mechanism to exploit unlabeled real event recordings during training.
- Standard pipelines replace the first convolutional block to accept k-channel event volumes, discarding pretrained low-level filters that are exactly the layers most vulnerable to domain shift.

## Method

- **DA4E** casts simulated-to-real events as unsupervised domain adaptation, inserting a ==DABlock== that aligns source and target features via ==gradient reversal (GRL)==, ==MMD==, ==adaptive feature norm (AFN)==, ==rotation pretext==, or ==entropy minimization==.
- **MV-DA4E** groups the multi-channel event volume into three-channel views, feeds each through a shared ImageNet-pretrained ==ResNet==, and merges them late via ==multi-view pooling== before classification.
- The **RGB-E** benchmark extends the ROD robotics dataset and its synthetic counterpart SynROD with a simulated event modality, enabling single- and multi-modal synth-to-real evaluation.

## Results

- On N-Caltech101, adaptation beats source-only by up to **6%** on voxel grid, **11%** on HATS, **6%** on EST, **4%** on MatrixLSTM; best MV-DA4E hits **89.24%** versus **90.09%** supervised.
- Across three contrast-threshold settings (0.06, 0.15, uniform sampling), feature-level adaptation consistently outperforms threshold-tuning baselines, e.g. **88.38%** versus **83.12%** at C=0.06.
- On SynROD-to-ROD, events transfer far better than depth without adaptation (**39.43%** versus **7.56%**), and RGB+Event averages **63.46%** against **62.86%** for RGB+Depth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distribution mismatch from an imperfect simulator can be absorbed at the feature level with unlabeled target data, rather than only by hand-tuning simulator parameters.
> - Reshaping multi-channel sensor data into pretrained-compatible three-channel views preserves transferable low-level filters and helps both cross-domain and in-domain accuracy.
> - Representations that discard temporal resolution suffer most under domain shift, and geometry-based pretext tasks can backfire when the modality already encodes motion cues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Event cameras represent a distinct paradigm in visual sensing, diverging from traditional frame-based cameras by asynchronously capturing pixel-level intensity changes as "events." This operational principle offers advantages such as high dynamic range, high temporal resolution, low latency, and robustness to motion blur and poor lighting conditions. These characteristics position event cameras as potentially valuable sensors in challenging computer vision and robotics applications, particularly those involving high-speed motion.

Despite their potential, the novelty of event cameras results in a scarcity of large-scale, labeled datasets necessary for training modern deep learning models effectively. To mitigate this data limitation, researchers commonly employ event camera simulators, which can generate synthetic event data. However, this approach introduces a "sim-to-real gap," where models trained on simulated data may not generalize effectively to real-world event streams due to discrepancies between simulated and actual sensor characteristics (e.g., event thresholds, refractory periods, noise patterns).

Previous efforts to address this sim-to-real gap in event cameras have primarily focused on modifying the data generation process. For instance, some research has explored sampling simulator parameters (like the contrast threshold, C) during training or estimating real camera parameters to better match simulated data to real data at the input level.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing an alternative strategy: applying advanced Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) techniques at the feature level to bridge the sim-to-real gap. UDA, a well-established field in traditional computer vision, aims to enable a model trained on a labeled source domain (e.g., simulated data) to perform well on an unlabeled target domain (e.g., real data) by aligning the feature distributions between the two domains. By adapting these techniques to event-based data, this work seeks to learn more transferable representations, thereby improving the generalization performance of deep networks trained with simulated events. It also extends the analysis to the "Synth-to-Real" gap (between synthetic rendered RGB images and real RGB images), and the combination of simulated events with these scenarios, addressing a more comprehensive view of domain shifts in multimodal data.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two interconnected challenges in event-based computer vision:
1. **Data Scarcity for Event Cameras:** Event cameras are new, and obtaining large, finely annotated real-world datasets for training deep learning models is expensive and time-consuming. This limits the full exploitation of their capabilities.
2. **Sim-to-Real Gap:** While simulated event data can alleviate data scarcity, models trained exclusively on synthetic data often exhibit performance degradation when deployed on real event streams. This domain shift arises from discrepancies between idealized simulation models and the non-idealities of physical sensors, such as varying contrast thresholds (C) or refractory periods. Previous approaches focused on adjusting simulator parameters at the input level, but the effectiveness of adapting at the feature level remained underexplored for event cameras.

Against this background, the paper articulates several key objectives:
- **Bridge the Sim-to-Real Gap using Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) at the Feature Level:** The central objective is to demonstrate that UDA techniques, commonly used in traditional computer vision, can effectively reduce the performance disparity between models trained on simulated event data and evaluated on real event data. This involves treating the sim-to-real problem as a domain shift issue solvable by aligning feature distributions.
- **Learn Transferable Representations:** By employing UDA, the goal is to enable deep networks to learn representations that are robust and generalize well across the simulated and real event domains, even when only unlabeled real event data is available for adaptation.
- **Develop a Novel Multi-View Architecture (MV-DA4E) for Event Representations:** The authors aim to design a network architecture that better exploits the inherent temporal information in frame-based event representations. The motivation is to overcome limitations of standard CNN initialization practices (e.g., replacing the first convolutional layer for non-3-channel inputs) by retaining ImageNet pre-trained weights and fusing information from multiple "views" of the event data at later layers, which are hypothesized to be more domain-invariant.
- **Evaluate UDA Effectiveness across Different Event Representations:** The research seeks to analyze how various event representations (e.g., Voxel Grids, HATS, EST, MatrixLSTM) are affected by the sim-to-real gap and to what extent different UDA approaches can mitigate these issues.
- **Validate the Approach in Real-World Scenarios and Multimodal Contexts:** The paper intends to demonstrate the utility of simulated event data, even when generated from traditional RGB images, by applying UDA in a multimodal setting (RGB-E) on a popular robotics dataset (ROD). This aims to show that simulated events can complement other modalities without significant performance hindrance due to domain shift, thereby unlocking novel uses for the event modality in scenarios where real event data acquisition might be challenging or impossible.
- **Extend Existing Datasets:** A concrete objective is to enhance the popular RGB-D Object Dataset (ROD) and its synthetic counterpart (SynROD) with simulated event data, creating a new RGB-E benchmark for object classification.

In summary, the motivation is to unlock the full potential of event cameras by providing a robust methodology for training deep learning models despite data scarcity and domain shifts, leveraging the ease of simulation alongside unlabeled real event sequences.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology proposed in this paper involves leveraging Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) techniques and a novel multi-view architecture to address the sim-to-real gap for event cameras.

**A. Problem Formulation (UDA for Event Data)**
The core idea is to frame the sim-to-real gap as a domain adaptation problem. A source domain $S = \{(x^s_i, y^s_i)\}^{N_s}_{i=1}$ consists of $N_s$ labeled samples (simulated events) with a known label space $Y_s$. A target domain $T = \{x^t_i\}^{N_t}_{i=1}$ comprises $N_t$ unlabeled samples (real events) with an unknown label space $Y_t$. The assumptions are that the two domains have different distributions ($D_s \neq D_t$) but share the same label space ($Y_s = Y_t$). The objective is to train a model on the source domain that performs well on the target domain by making the source and target feature distributions similar.

**B. Event Representations**
The paper focuses on converting sparse event streams into dense, frame-like representations that can be processed by standard Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Four representations are investigated:
- **Voxel Grids (Event Volume):** Divides time into a fixed number of bins ($B$) and aggregates events by interpolating polarity values over time. This results in a $B$-channel representation where each event's contribution is weighted by its temporal occurrence within a bin.
- **Histograms of Time Surfaces (HATS):** A two-channel representation that combines hand-crafted features with a memory mechanism. It extracts local 2D surfaces from event neighborhoods using an exponential kernel, aggregates them into histograms per polarity, and rearranges them. Temporal resolution is lost as the entire window is condensed into a single frame.
- **Event Spike Tensor (EST):** An end-to-end trainable representation. Similar to voxel grids, it uses timestamp as a pixel feature, but the kernel for weighting event contributions is learned by an MLP. Events are grouped by polarity to produce a two-channel representation per bin.
- **MatrixLSTM:** Also trainable and similar to EST, but pixel features are computed using a matrix of LSTM cells with shared parameters, processing temporal-ordered event sequences per pixel. The number of features and bins can be customized.

**C. MV-DA4Event: A Multi-View Approach**
Recognizing that standard CNNs are often pre-trained on 3-channel RGB images (like ImageNet), and that the initial layers are most susceptible to domain shifts, the paper proposes a multi-view approach:
- **View Creation:** Multi-channel event representations $R_E \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times F}$ are aggregated into multiple 3-channel "views," denoted as $\tilde{R}_E \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times \lceil F/3 \rceil \times 3}$. This allows the initial layers of a pre-trained CNN (typically expecting 3 channels) to be used without modification.
- **Multi-View Network (Fig. 3):** Each 3-channel view is fed separately into a shared feature extractor $F$ (a pre-trained ResNet). The resulting set of features from all views is then combined using a "Multi-View Pooling (MVP)" module, which performs average pooling spatially and across views. This late-fusion strategy aims to leverage robust low-level features from pre-trained layers while allowing for better generalization by fusing information at later stages.

**D. Network Architecture (Fig. 3)**
The overall architecture integrates the event representation, multi-view processing, and UDA components:
1. **Event Generation/Acquisition:** Simulated events are obtained via the ESIM simulator from RGB images (source domain), while real events are directly acquired from event cameras (target domain).
2. **Event Representation:** Events are converted into multi-channel representations (e.g., Voxel Grids, EST) and split into multiple temporal bins.
3. **Multi-View Processing:** For MV-DA4E, the multi-channel representation is grouped into 3-channel views.
4. **Shared Feature Extractor (F):** A ResNet (ResNet34 for N-Caltech101, ResNet18 for ROD) pre-trained on ImageNet processes each view in parallel.
5. **Multi-View Pooling (MVP):** Features from different views are pooled to produce a single feature vector $R^{F}_{out}$ for each domain. If MV-DA4E is not used, the full multi-channel representation is fed to F directly (replacing the first convolutional layer), and MVP is skipped.
6. **Main Classifier (G):** This module takes the source domain feature vector and performs the final prediction for the main classification task.
7. **Domain Adaptation Block (DABlock):** Both source and target feature vectors are fed into the DABlock, where UDA algorithms are applied to align their distributions. Training uses unpaired random batches of source and target samples.

**E. UDA Algorithms**
The DABlock incorporates several UDA techniques:
- **Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL):** This adversarial-based method uses a domain classifier to distinguish between source and target features. The GRL then reverses the gradient during backpropagation to fool the domain classifier, encouraging the feature extractor to produce domain-invariant features.
- **Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD):** A discrepancy-based method that directly minimizes a distance metric between the empirical feature distributions of the source and target domains in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS).
- **Adaptive Feature Norm (AFN):** Addresses domain shift by aligning the $L_2$-norms of deep embeddings between source and target domains, based on the observation that target features often have smaller norms.
- **Rotation (ROT):** A self-supervised pretext task where the network predicts the absolute rotation (e.g., $0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ$) of input images from both domains. Solving this auxiliary task helps learn more generalizable features.
- **Entropy Minimization (ENT):** A regularization technique that minimizes the entropy of predictions on the target domain, promoting high-confidence predictions and implicitly aligning distributions.

**F. Experimental Details**
- **Datasets:**
 - **N-Caltech101:** Sim-to-Real scenario using a simulated version (Sim-N-Caltech101) as source and the real N-Caltech101 as target for object classification.
 - **RGB-D Object Dataset (ROD):** Extended to RGB-E (RGB+Events) and used with its synthetic counterpart (SynROD) for multimodal Syn-to-Real experiments. Events were simulated from RGB images using ESIM.
- **Evaluation:** Top-1 accuracy on the target test set.
- **Implementation:** PyTorch, ResNet34/18 pre-trained on ImageNet. Event representations are generally un-normalized, while RGB images are normalized. Hyperparameters (e.g., DA loss weights) were fine-tuned for each method and representation. Voxel grids and EST used 9 bins, MatrixLSTM 3 bins (producing 3-channel output), HATS produced 2 channels without bins.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of UDA and the proposed MV-DA4E approach in bridging the sim-to-real gap for event cameras.

**A. Sim → Real Adaptation on N-Caltech101**
Experiments on N-Caltech101 (Simulated events as source, Real events as target) demonstrated the following:

- **Effectiveness of UDA Methods:** For almost all event representations, UDA methods consistently outperformed the "Source Only" baseline (trained on simulated data, tested on real data without adaptation). Performance improvements ranged up to 6% for VoxelGrid, 11% for HATS, 6% for EST, and 4% for MatrixLSTM. This indicates that adapting at the feature level is a viable strategy for reducing the sim-to-real gap in event cameras.
- **Varying Sensitivity of Representations:** Not all event representations suffered equally from the domain shift. HATS, which loses temporal resolution by condensing the event stream into a single frame, exhibited the most significant performance drop (up to 16%) in the Source Only scenario and generally benefited more from UDA. This suggests that representations retaining temporal information (like VoxelGrid, EST, MatrixLSTM) are inherently more robust to domain shifts.
- **Limited Target Data:** The approach demonstrated scalability when only a percentage of target data was available during training. Even with 25% of target data, an improvement of up to 4% over the Source Only baseline was observed, highlighting the utility of UDA even with scarce unlabeled real data.
- **Qualitative Results:** t-SNE visualizations showed better alignment of source and target feature distributions when UDA was applied. Grad-CAM visualizations indicated that MV-DA4E training resulted in the network focusing on discriminative regions of the input event representation.

**B. Effectiveness of MV-DA4E (Multi-View Approach)**

- **Consistent Performance Improvement:** The proposed MV-DA4E approach consistently and significantly improved performance over the standard DA4E configuration (where multi-channel representations are fed directly without view grouping) across all UDA methods and event representations (where applicable, HATS being an exception due to its 2-channel output without temporal bins). This supports the hypothesis that retaining pre-trained layers and fusing information from multiple 3-channel views at later stages is beneficial.
- **Intra-Domain Benefits:** MV-DA4E not only improved performance in the cross-domain (Sim-to-Real) scenario but also in the intra-domain supervised setting (Supervised row in Table I). This suggests that the multi-view approach could be a general method for handling event representations, irrespective of domain adaptation tasks.

**C. Comparison with Input-Level Adaptation (Contrast Threshold C)**

- **Superiority of Feature-Level DA:** The research compared its UDA approach (using VoxelGrid representation) against baselines that exclusively adjusted the simulator's contrast threshold (C). The results in Table II indicated that the proposed feature-level UDA methods consistently and substantially outperformed C-only based methods, regardless of the C-value used (fixed at 0.06, estimated at 0.15, or uniformly sampled). This highlights that adapting at the feature level is more effective than, or at least highly complementary to, solely adjusting input generation parameters.
- **Reduced Sensitivity to C-variations:** Furthermore, multi-view approaches (MV-DA4E) demonstrated reduced sensitivity to variations in C, indicating that they provide a more robust solution across different simulation conditions.

**D. SynROD → RealROD (Multimodal Adaptation)**

- **Robustness of Event Modality:** In the Syn-to-Real scenario for object recognition on ROD (using events simulated from RGB-D images), the event modality demonstrated greater robustness to domain changes compared to the depth modality. The performance drop without DA was 7.56% for events versus 39.43% for depth in single-modal settings.
- **Complementarity with RGB:** When combined with the RGB modality (RGB+E), event data slightly improved model accuracy and showed better adaptation performance on average compared to RGB+D. This indicates that even simulated event data can be a valuable complementary modality, aiding adaptation in scenarios where texture and appearance information (RGB) are highly susceptible to domain shifts.
- **UDA Effectiveness in Multimodal Settings:** UDA techniques were effective in this multimodal setting, with average UDA performance on RGB-E exceeding that of RGB-D.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes several contributions with implications for the development and application of event cameras:

- **Novel Approach to Sim-to-Real Gap:** The paper introduces Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) as a feature-level solution to the sim-to-real gap for event cameras. This contrasts with previous input-level approaches (e.g., adjusting simulator parameters) and demonstrates UDA's effectiveness, potentially opening a new direction for research in event-based vision. By reframing the problem as a domain shift, it leverages a mature field of computer vision.
- **Enabling Data Scarcity Mitigation:** By effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap, this work enhances the utility of simulated event data. This is significant because generating large, finely annotated real event datasets is challenging and costly. The ability to train models on readily available simulated data and adapt them to real-world scenarios can accelerate research and development for event-based applications.
- **Introduction of MV-DA4E:** The proposed Multi-View DA4E architecture provides a method for handling multi-channel event representations while retaining the benefits of ImageNet pre-training for CNN backbones. Its consistent performance improvements, even in supervised settings, suggest it could become a general strategy for processing event data, regardless of the specific task or whether domain adaptation is explicitly required.
- **New Benchmarks and Modality Expansion:** The extension of the RGB-D Object Dataset (ROD) to include a simulated event modality (RGB-E) creates a new benchmark for multimodal object classification. This facilitates further research into the synergistic use of different sensor modalities, particularly how simulated event data can complement traditional vision data.
- **Unlocking Event Camera Potential in Robotics:** The validation in a robotics context (ROD dataset) demonstrates the practical applicability of simulated event data. The finding that event modality is more robust to domain shifts than depth and can improve RGB classification, even when simulated, suggests that event-based data can enhance robustness in real-world robotic applications, especially where synthetic data is critical for training.
- **Igniting Future Research:** The paper explicitly states its hope to ignite future work on the opportunity of using UDA methods for event data. This could lead to further exploration of more sophisticated UDA techniques tailored to the unique characteristics of event streams and a deeper understanding of domain shifts in neuromorphic vision.
