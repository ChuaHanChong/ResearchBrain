# GoT-R1: Unleashing Reasoning Capability of MLLM for Visual Generation with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from HKU MMLab, CUHK MMLab, Sensetime, and Beihang University develop GoT-R1, a framework that applies reinforcement learning to Generation Chain-of-Thought reasoning for visual generation, enabling multimodal large language models to autonomously discover effective reasoning strategies beyond predefined templates while achieving state-of-the-art performance on T2I-CompBench through a dual-stage training process that combines supervised fine-tuning with Group Relative Policy Optimization and a comprehensive multi-dimensional reward system evaluating both reasoning chains and generated images.

## Problem

- Existing Generation Chain-of-Thought (GoT) methods rely on supervised fine-tuning with human-defined templates, limiting models' ability to discover more effective reasoning strategies autonomously
- Text-to-image models struggle with compositional fidelity when generating images from complex prompts requiring precise spatial relationships and attribute binding
- Designing appropriate reward mechanisms for visual generation tasks that must assess semantic alignment, spatial accuracy, and visual quality simultaneously

## Method

- ==GoT-R1== framework combining ==Generation Chain-of-Thought== with reinforcement learning using a unified MLLM architecture that jointly models text and image tokens
- Dual-stage training process: supervised fine-tuning on annotated reasoning chains followed by reinforcement learning with ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==
- Multi-dimensional reward framework leveraging MLLMs as reward models to evaluate prompt-to-reasoning alignment, reasoning-to-image alignment, and overall compositional accuracy

## Results

- Achieves **state-of-the-art** performance on T2I-CompBench benchmark, surpassing existing methods including Stable Diffusion, DALLE·3, and LayoutGPT
- Demonstrates significant improvements in compositional tasks involving precise spatial relationships and attribute binding
- Shows that models can autonomously discover more effective reasoning strategies, leading to improved controllability and fidelity in text-to-image generation

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning enables visual generation models to discover reasoning strategies beyond predefined patterns, similar to advances seen in language model reasoning
> - MLLMs serve effectively as both generators and reward models due to their ability to make nuanced judgments about text-image correspondence
> - Comprehensive reward systems that evaluate both intermediate reasoning processes and final outputs are crucial for successful RL training in visual generation

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual generation has seen advancements in creating realistic images from text prompts, primarily driven by diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DALL-E) and autoregressive architectures (e.g., VQGAN-based models). While these models can produce high-fidelity visuals, a recognized limitation pertains to their performance on complex and compositional prompts. Such prompts often specify multiple objects with precise spatial relationships, specific attributes, and numerical constraints (e.g., "a red ball on the left of a blue cube"). Conventional models frequently struggle to explicitly reason about the semantic content and spatial layout required to fulfill these intricate instructions, often resulting in misaligned compositions or incorrect attribute binding.

The Generation Chain-of-Thought (GoT) framework was introduced to address this by incorporating an intermediate semantic-spatial reasoning process. GoT decomposes complex prompts into explicit object descriptions, including location coordinates, before image generation. While GoT improved compositional fidelity, its reasoning capabilities were constrained by supervised fine-tuning on human-defined templates, limiting the model's ability to discover diverse or more effective reasoning strategies autonomously.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated success in enhancing reasoning capabilities in large language models (LLMs), enabling them to self-improve and discover sophisticated reasoning strategies beyond predefined patterns. This work leverages these RL advancements, particularly for autoregressive architectures, which naturally lend themselves to sequential reasoning due to their token-by-token generation process. GoT-R1 positions itself by extending the GoT paradigm to autoregressive visual generation models and integrating RL to overcome the template-bound limitations, aiming to transfer sophisticated reasoning from language models to the visual domain. It also seeks to overcome the challenges of designing comprehensive reward signals for multimodal RL, which must account for semantic fidelity, spatial accuracy, attribute binding, and overall visual quality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the semantic-spatial reasoning capabilities of autoregressive visual generation models when responding to complex and compositional text prompts. This enhancement is sought through the application of reinforcement learning.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the existing visual generation landscape:
1. **Limited Reasoning in Current Models:** Despite generating realistic images, current visual generation models (both diffusion-based and autoregressive) often struggle with complex prompts that require explicit reasoning about object attributes, precise spatial relationships, and numerical counts. They typically map text embeddings directly to visual features without an intermediate, interpretable reasoning step.
2. **Constraint of Predefined Templates in GoT:** While the Generation Chain-of-Thought (GoT) framework introduced an intermediate semantic-spatial reasoning process, its reliance on supervised fine-tuning with human-defined templates limits the model's capacity for autonomous discovery of novel and potentially more effective reasoning strategies. This can lead to reasoning chains that are template-compliant but unfaithful to the prompt.
3. **Untapped Potential of Autoregressive Architectures for Reasoning:** Autoregressive models generate outputs sequentially, which aligns naturally with sequential reasoning tasks. The authors aim to leverage this inherent advantage to develop more sophisticated reasoning for visual generation.
4. **Challenges in Multimodal Reinforcement Learning:** Extending RL to visual generation presents unique challenges, specifically in designing a reward system that can comprehensively evaluate different dimensions of visual output. This includes assessing semantic fidelity, spatial arrangement, attribute binding, coherence, and aesthetic quality, not only in the final image but also in the intermediate reasoning process.
5. **Need for Process Supervision:** Optimizing solely based on final image quality can lead to unfaithful or inconsistent intermediate reasoning chains. Explicit supervision of the reasoning process itself is necessary to ensure alignment between the prompt, the generated reasoning chain, and the final image.

To address these, GoT-R1 proposes a framework that enables autoregressive visual generation models to discover effective reasoning strategies autonomously, moving beyond the constraints of predefined templates. It aims to develop a comprehensive reward system, grounded in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), that evaluates both the reasoning process and the final visual output across multiple dimensions, thereby providing effective supervision throughout the entire generation pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GoT-R1 is a framework designed to enhance semantic-spatial reasoning in autoregressive visual generation models using reinforcement learning. It builds upon the Generation Chain-of-Thought (GoT) paradigm.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture

The base model utilized is a unified Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) architecture that jointly models text and image tokens. This architecture allows for the end-to-end generation of both textual reasoning chains and visual outputs (as sequences of image tokens) from a single text prompt. Examples mentioned include Janus-Pro, which handles both visual understanding and generation.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy

The training process consists of two stages:
1. **Pre-training (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT):** The pre-trained unified MLLM is initially fine-tuned on GoT datasets (LAHR-GoT, JourneyDB-GoT, FLUX-GoT) that contain annotated reasoning chains and corresponding generated images. This SFT stage provides the model with a foundational capability to generate templated reasoning chains before producing image tokens, establishing a strong initial state.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Following SFT, reinforcement learning is applied to guide the model toward exploring more diverse and effective, free-style reasoning chains.
 - For each input prompt, the model samples a group of N (e.g., 16) different reasoning chains and their corresponding images.
 - These samples are evaluated using a comprehensive multi-dimensional reward function.
 - The model parameters are updated using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO is an efficient RL algorithm that computes advantages for each sample by normalizing rewards within its sampled group, eliminating the need for a separate critic model. This approach incentivizes high-reward reasoning strategies and generated images while discouraging low-reward ones.
 - Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used for efficient model updates during GRPO.

#### 3.3. MLLM-Based Dual-Stage Multi-Dimensional Reward System

A core component of GoT-R1 is its dual-stage multi-dimensional reward framework, designed to supervise both the intermediate reasoning process and the final visual output. The total reward (R_total) is calculated as the product of individual reward components, scaled to a range of. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are leveraged as reward models due to their cross-modal understanding and fine-grained evaluation capabilities. The four categories of rewards are:

1. **Prompt-Image Reward (R_PI):** This assesses the overall alignment between the input prompt and the final generated image. An MLLM evaluates the image for compositional accuracy (objects, attributes, layout) against the prompt, assigning a discrete score from 0 to 10.
2. **Prompt-Reasoning Semantic Reward (R_sem):** This evaluates the semantic consistency between the input prompt and the generated GoT reasoning chain. An MLLM analyzes the reasoning chain for:
 - **Completeness:** Inclusion of all concepts from the prompt.
 - **Faithfulness:** Absence of contradictions with the prompt.
 - **Consistency:** Logical alignment with the described scene.
 - **Clarity:** Coherence and proper formatting of the reasoning chain.
 The MLLM assigns a score from 0 to 10 across these dimensions.
3. **Prompt-Reasoning Spatial Reward (R_spa):** This assesses the correctness of the spatial planning within the reasoning chain relative to the prompt's instructions (e.g., "left of," "top"). To enhance the MLLM's spatial comprehension, object bounding box coordinates from the GoT reasoning are converted into a visual format by rendering them on a blank canvas. The MLLM then evaluates this visualized layout, providing a more reliable spatial correctness score than processing raw textual coordinates.
 - R_PR (Prompt-Reasoning Reward) is computed as the average of R_sem and R_spa: (R_sem + R_spa) / 2.
4. **Reasoning-Image Reward (R_RI):** This measures how faithfully the generated image reflects the planned GoT reasoning. An MLLM is used to identify and ground each object specified in the GoT reasoning within the generated image, yielding grounded bounding boxes. The reward is calculated as the average Intersection over Union (IoU) between the planned bounding boxes from GoT and their grounded counterparts in the generated image.
5. **HPS v2.1 Score (R_HPS):** This component integrates HPS v2.1, an existing human preference score, to further improve the general aesthetic quality and human alignment of the generated images.

The total reward is calculated as: R_total = R_PI * R_PR * R_RI * R_HPS. This multiplicative structure ensures that all stages of the generation pipeline (prompt-to-reasoning, reasoning-to-image, and prompt-to-image) and general quality are aligned.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GoT-R1 demonstrates improvements in compositional text-to-image generation, particularly in tasks requiring precise semantic-spatial reasoning.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **T2I-CompBench Results:** GoT-R1-7B achieved the highest scores in five out of six evaluation categories (Color, Shape, Texture, 2D-Spatial, Complex) on the T2I-CompBench benchmark. This includes an improvement of up to 15% after 1000 GRPO fine-tuning steps compared to its base GoT-finetuned model. Notably, the model showed strong performance in the "Complex compositions" category. The smaller GoT-R1-1B model also outperformed larger models like Janus-Pro-7B in several categories, indicating the efficacy of the RL approach across different model sizes.
- **GenEval Results:** On the GenEval benchmark, GoT-R1-7B established a new state-of-the-art with an overall score of 0.75. This represents improvements over the base Janus-Pro-GoT-7B model, with notable gains in compositional abilities: two-object generation improved from 0.69 to 0.94, and attribute binding advanced from 0.43 to 0.68.
- **General Image Quality Results (COCO 2014):** Evaluation on the COCO 2014 validation set showed that GoT-R1-7B achieved improvements in standard image quality metrics, with a CLIP Score of 31.83 (up from 29.97 for Janus-Pro-GoT-7B) and an Aesthetic Score of 5.41 (up from 5.19). Human evaluation indicated a strong preference (77%) for GoT-R1-7B over baseline models, suggesting enhanced overall generation quality in addition to compositional accuracy.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5) illustrate that GoT-R1-7B generates images with stronger prompt alignment, accurately reflecting complex compositional prompts involving multiple attributes, relative spatial relationships, and object numeracy. This contrasts with the GoT-finetuned model, which, while improved over the base model, still struggled with intricate compositional elements. GoT-R1-7B also produced images described as detailed and aesthetically appealing.

#### 4.3. Analysis on Self-Explored Generation Chain-of-Thought

A comparison of GoT-R1-7B's self-explored reasoning chains against Janus-Pro-7B-GoT's predefined GoT, evaluated by GPT-4o, indicated a strong preference for GoT-R1-7B's reasoning. GoT-R1-7B was overwhelmingly preferred across categories (Color, Spatial, Complex, LAION-5B), with preferences ranging from 69% to 84%. This suggests that the RL framework enables the model to autonomously discover and generate superior reasoning paths, which contributes to its enhanced compositional generation capabilities.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Reward Design

The ablation studies on the MLLM-based dual-stage multi-dimensional reward framework revealed the individual contributions of each reward component:
- Training with only R_PI showed the best performance among single-reward variants, but it still underperformed the full GoT-R1-1B, indicating the importance of explicit reasoning process supervision.
- Relying solely on R_PR (prompt-to-reasoning) or R_RI (reasoning-to-image) alone led to poorer outcomes, with R_RI alone being potentially detrimental if the reasoning process is flawed.
- Removing any individual component (R_RI, R_PI, or R_PR's sub-components R_sem and R_spa) from the full reward set resulted in performance degradation. R_sem primarily enhanced attribute binding, while R_spa improved spatial consistency.
- These findings collectively support the necessity of the comprehensive reward design, which aligns all stages of the generation process.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study on Training Details

Further ablation on training details showed:
- Using a product of individual rewards (as in GoT-R1) outperformed summing the rewards.
- The novel MLLM layout evaluation approach, which converts textual coordinates to images for MLLM assessment of R_spa, was more effective than direct MLLM evaluation of textual coordinates.
- Replacing MLLM-based rewards with conventional metrics (CLIP similarity for prompt-image, Grounding DINO for reasoning-image) resulted in degraded compositional performance, even if HPSv2 scores improved (indicating reward hacking without multi-dimensional alignment). This confirmed the efficacy of MLLM-based rewards in providing nuanced feedback.

#### 4.6. Additional Quantitative Analysis (Appendix)

- **MLLM Reward Model Reliability:** The chosen Qwen2.5VL-7B MLLM reward model showed a higher correlation with human judgment (Kendall's tau: 0.3147, Spearman's rho: 0.3428) on prompt-image pairs compared to CLIP Score.
- **Training Stability:** Three independent training runs demonstrated consistent performance across T2I-CompBench categories, with variance less than ±0.02, confirming the robustness of the training process.
- **Multi-dimensional Reward Design:** A single reward signal (HPSv2 only) led to "reward hacking," where HPSv2 scores improved, but compositional performance degraded, validating the importance of the multi-dimensional reward design in preventing optimization pathologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GoT-R1 introduces a framework that advances the state of autoregressive visual generation by enhancing semantic-spatial reasoning capabilities through reinforcement learning. This work addresses a persistent challenge in text-to-image models: their difficulty in accurately interpreting and generating images from complex, compositional prompts.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Autonomous Reasoning Strategy Discovery:** By moving beyond supervised fine-tuning with predefined templates, GoT-R1 enables models to autonomously discover and optimize reasoning strategies. This capability can lead to more flexible and robust generation, adapting to a wider range of prompt complexities and potentially uncovering novel approaches to visual composition.
- **Comprehensive Multimodal Reward System:** The proposed MLLM-based dual-stage multi-dimensional reward framework is a key contribution. It provides fine-grained supervision across the entire generation pipeline—from prompt interpretation to intermediate reasoning chain generation and final image synthesis. This integrated evaluation, encompassing semantic alignment, spatial accuracy, and visual quality, effectively addresses the challenges of applying RL to complex multimodal tasks.
- **Improved Compositional Fidelity:** The experimental results demonstrate significant improvements on established benchmarks like T2I-CompBench and GenEval, particularly in tasks requiring precise attribute binding and spatial relationships. This directly translates to more accurate and controllable image generation for complex scenes, which has broad implications for various applications.
- **Advancement in Autoregressive Models:** The work highlights the natural affinity between autoregressive architectures and sequential reasoning, demonstrating how RL can effectively leverage this for visual generation. It contributes to bridging the gap between sophisticated reasoning in language models and high-quality visual synthesis.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Creative Tools:** The improved compositional control offered by GoT-R1 could empower designers, artists, and content creators with more precise and flexible tools for generating specific visual content, reducing the need for extensive manual editing.
- **Foundational for Multi-modal AI:** The methodology for integrating RL with comprehensive MLLM-based rewards for multimodal reasoning and generation could serve as a foundation for future research in unified AI systems that seamlessly understand, reason, and generate across different modalities.
- **Real-world Applications:** More accurate generation of complex scenes could impact fields such as virtual reality, game development, advertising, and scientific visualization, where precise object placement and attribute control are critical.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors include:**
- **Position Prediction in Object-Heavy Scenes:** The model's ability to generate accurate bounding boxes for more than ten distinct objects in a prompt can decrease, impacting fidelity in very complex scenes.
- **Inference Latency:** The explicit generation of the GoT reasoning chain introduces additional inference latency, increasing total generation time by approximately 30% compared to direct generation.
- **Reliance on Reward Model Capability:** The framework's performance is inherently dependent on the capabilities of the underlying MLLM used as a reward function. Future advancements in MLLMs could further unlock performance gains for GoT-R1.

Despite these limitations, GoT-R1 represents a step forward in endowing visual generation models with more sophisticated and autonomously discovered reasoning capabilities, contributing to the development of more intelligent and controllable generative AI systems.
