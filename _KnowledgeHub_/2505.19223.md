# LLaDA 1.5: Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization for Large Language Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaDA 1.5 systematically improves the alignment of Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) with human preferences by introducing Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization (VRPO). This framework, developed by researchers from Renmin University of China and collaborators, effectively mitigates high variance in ELBO-based likelihood estimates, leading to consistent performance gains, including a +4.7 increase in GSM8K accuracy.

## Problem

- Systematic human preference alignment for Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) remained an underexplored area compared to autoregressive models.
- MDMs rely on approximating log-likelihoods via Evidence Lower Bounds (ELBOs), which must be estimated using computationally intensive Monte Carlo methods.
- These Monte Carlo ELBO estimates introduce high variance into preference optimization processes, hindering stable and effective alignment.

## Method

- Adapted ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)== for MDMs by substituting exact log-likelihoods with Monte Carlo estimated ==Evidence Lower Bounds (ELBOs)==.
- Proposed ==Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization (VRPO)==, a framework integrating three unbiased variance reduction strategies to mitigate high variance.
- VRPO strategies include increasing the total sampling budget, optimally allocating the sampling budget across timesteps, and employing ==antithetic sampling== for paired ELBOs.

## Results

- LLaDA 1.5, utilizing VRPO, consistently outperformed its LLaDA 8B Instruct (SFT-only) predecessor and a baseline LLaDA with naive DPO across diverse benchmarks.
- Achieved notable quantitative improvements, including a **+4.7** increase in GSM8K accuracy, **+3.0** on HumanEval, and **+4.0** on IFEval.
- VRPO led to significantly smoother training loss curves and lower variability during optimization, demonstrating enhanced stability and efficiency in aligning MDMs with human preferences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The bias and variance of the estimated DPO loss and its gradient are directly governed by the variance of the underlying preference score estimator.
> - For a fixed sampling budget, optimal allocation for ELBOs prioritizes sampling more distinct timesteps with fewer masked samples per timestep to minimize variance.
> - Antithetic sampling effectively reduces the variance of differences between correlated ELBO estimates without incurring additional computational cost, enhancing optimization stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of two rapidly advancing fields: Large Language Models (LLMs) and Diffusion Models. Historically, autoregressive models (ARMs), such as the LLaMA series, have dominated the LLM landscape, demonstrating remarkable capabilities across various language tasks. However, Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) have recently emerged as a promising alternative, showing competitive or even superior performance to ARMs at smaller scales and exhibiting excellent scalability in preliminary explorations (Nie et al., 2024; Lou et al., 2023; Ou et al., 2024).

A critical development in the ARM ecosystem has been the successful alignment of these models with human preferences, typically achieved through techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). These alignment methods significantly enhance a model's helpfulness, harmlessness, and honesty, making them more user-friendly and reliable.

However, the application of these alignment methodologies to MDMs remains an underexplored area. While some recent concurrent works have begun to adapt existing alignment frameworks to MDMs, they often lack rigorous theoretical analysis of the unique challenges posed by diffusion models and tend to focus on specialized tasks such as reasoning or code generation (Zekri & Boullé, 2025; Borso et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Gong et al., 2025; Tang et al., 2025).

This paper, "LLADA 1.5," directly addresses this gap. It systematically investigates the alignment of MDMs with human preferences, specifically through the lens of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Its core contribution to the broader landscape is providing both a principled theoretical analysis and comprehensive empirical validation on *general* language tasks, moving beyond specialized applications. By identifying and mitigating the high variance inherent in the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)-based likelihood estimates that are crucial for MDM-DPO, this work establishes a robust foundation for future development of aligned diffusion language models, positioning MDMs as increasingly viable and competitive alternatives to ARMs for a wide range of applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically study and improve the alignment of Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) with human preferences, moving beyond ad-hoc adaptations and specialized tasks. The authors aim to bridge the gap between the proven success of alignment techniques in autoregressive models (ARMs) and the emerging potential of MDMs in language generation.

The key motivation stems from several observations:
1. **Promising MDM Performance:** Masked diffusion models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in language modeling, showing scalability and competitive performance against autoregressive models, thus warranting further development.
2. **Necessity of Alignment:** Human preference alignment has been transformative for ARMs, significantly improving their utility and safety. Applying similar rigorous alignment to MDMs is crucial for their broader adoption and development.
3. **Unique Challenges in MDM Alignment:** Unlike ARMs, where exact log-likelihoods are readily computable, MDMs rely on approximating log-likelihoods via their Evidence Lower Bounds (ELBOs). These ELBOs, being derived from nested expectations over diffusion time and masked data, must be estimated using Monte Carlo methods.
4. **High Variance in ELBO Estimates:** The central problem identified by the authors is that these Monte Carlo ELBO estimates introduce "high variance" into the preference optimization process. This high variance, in turn, propagates as "additional bias and variance into the preference optimization loss and gradient," hindering stable and effective alignment.

Therefore, the specific objectives of this paper are:
- To formally analyze the bias and variance introduced by ELBO-based likelihood estimates within a preference optimization framework (specifically DPO) for MDMs.
- To theoretically demonstrate that these biases and variances are governed by the variance of the underlying preference score estimator.
- To propose and empirically validate a framework, Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization (VRPO), that integrates principled, unbiased variance reduction strategies to mitigate these issues.
- To demonstrate the effectiveness of VRPO on a large-scale MDM (LLaDA) across a comprehensive suite of general language tasks, including mathematics, code generation, and various alignment benchmarks.

By addressing the fundamental statistical challenges of ELBO estimation in MDM alignment, the authors seek to lay a robust groundwork for the future development of high-performing and well-aligned diffusion language models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around adapting Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) and, crucially, developing strategies to mitigate the high variance inherent in MDM's likelihood estimations.

#### 3.1. Adaptation of DPO for MDMs

- The authors begin by adopting the DPO framework, known for its simplicity and strong empirical performance, as the basis for preference optimization.
- A key challenge for MDMs is the intractability of exact log-likelihoods, log $\pi(y|x)$. The standard DPO objective requires these exact likelihoods.
- **ELBO Substitution:** To overcome this, the authors propose substituting the exact log-likelihoods with their Evidence Lower Bounds (ELBOs), denoted as $B_{\pi}(y|x)$. This yields an ELBO-based preference score as a linear combination of four ELBO terms.
- **Monte Carlo Estimation:** In practice, computing $B_{\pi}(y|x)$ exactly is still intractable due to nested expectations (over diffusion time and masked data). Thus, a doubly Monte Carlo method is employed, involving sampling `n_t` timesteps and `n_y_t` masked data samples per timestep, resulting in `n = n_t * n_y_t` total masked data points. This estimation introduces an estimated DPO loss, $\hat{\ell}_{\text{DPO-E}}$.

#### 3.2. Identification of the Core Problem: High Variance

- The authors' central insight is that this Monte Carlo estimation introduces "additional bias and variance" into the preference optimization loss and its gradient.
- **Theoretical Analysis (Theorem 1 and Appendix B.4.1):** They formally analyze this issue, proving that the bias and variance of the estimated DPO loss (and its gradient) are directly bounded by the variance of the preference score estimator, $\hat{s}_\theta(y_w, y_l)$. This score estimator itself is shown to be unbiased (Proposition 3), but its non-linear transformation through the sigmoid function in the DPO loss introduces bias. Figure 2 visually confirms that both bias and variance of $\log \sigma(X)$ increase monotonically with $V[X]$. This fundamental finding motivates focusing on reducing the variance of the score estimator.

#### 3.3. Proposed Solution: Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization (VRPO)

VRPO is a framework that integrates three unbiased variance reduction strategies, all designed to decrease the variance of the preference score estimator:

- **I. Reducing Individual ELBO Variance (Proposition 1):**
 - **1. Increasing Sampling Budget:** The simplest strategy is to increase the total number of masked samples `n = n_t * n_y_t` used for each ELBO estimation. Theoretically, $V[\hat{B}_{\pi}(y)] = \Theta(1/n)$, meaning variance decreases proportionally with increasing `n`. This increases computational cost but directly improves estimation accuracy.
 - **2. Optimal Budget Allocation:** For a fixed total budget `n`, the variance of the ELBO estimator is minimized when the entire budget is allocated to timesteps, meaning `n_t = n` and `n_y_t = 1`. This implies that sampling multiple distinct timesteps with a single masked sample per timestep is more effective than sampling fewer timesteps with multiple masked samples.

- **II. Increasing Correlation Between Paired ELBOs (Proposition 2):**
 - **3. Antithetic Sampling:** This technique involves sharing the same sampled timesteps and masked data between the ELBO estimates of the current policy ($\pi_\theta$) and the reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$) when evaluating the same response ($y_w$ or $y_l$). By inducing a positive correlation between these two estimates (which is generally expected since they are related), the variance of their difference is reduced. This method is computationally "free" as it reuses samples.

#### 3.4. Implementation and Validation

- VRPO is applied to **LLaDA 8B Instruct**, a pre-trained masked diffusion language model, using 350K preference pairs.
- Training involves one epoch with specific optimizer and learning rate settings, combining DPO loss with a small weighted SFT loss for stability.
- Extensive evaluation is performed across mathematics, code, and general alignment benchmarks, comparing LLaDA 1.5 (with VRPO) against LLaDA Instruct and LLaDA with a naive DPO implementation.
- Ablation studies are conducted to analyze the impact of each VRPO component on estimator variance and downstream task performance, both under scalable and fixed computational budgets.
- The authors also discuss the potential generalization of VRPO techniques to other RL-based alignment algorithms like PPO and GRPO, highlighting the broad applicability of their variance reduction insights.

This systematic approach, combining theoretical grounding with practical, efficient variance reduction strategies, forms the backbone of LLaDA 1.5's success.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of LLaDA 1.5, the model aligned using Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization (VRPO), reveals consistent and significant improvements across a wide array of benchmarks, validating the effectiveness of the proposed methodology.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvement of LLaDA 1.5

- LLaDA 1.5 consistently outperforms its predecessor, LLaDA 8B Instruct (SFT-only), and a baseline LLaDA model trained with naive DPO (n_t=1, n_y_t=1, without antithetic sampling). This indicates that the VRPO framework effectively leverages human preferences to enhance MDM capabilities.

#### 4.2. Benchmark-Specific Gains (Table 1)

- **Mathematics & Science:** LLaDA 1.5 shows notable improvements, with GSM8K accuracy increasing by **+4.7**, Math by **+0.4**, and GPQA by **+3.6**. Critically, LLaDA 1.5 achieves the "highest four-shot score on GSM8K and the highest zero-shot score on Math" among comparable MDMs and ARMs (e.g., DeepSeek 7B, Dream 7B, LLaMA 3 8B), demonstrating its highly competitive mathematical reasoning performance (Figure 1, right panel).
- **Code Generation:** Performance on coding benchmarks also significantly improves: HumanEval sees a **+3.0** increase, and MBPP improves by **+1.8**.
- **Alignment Tasks:** LLaDA 1.5 shows strong gains in alignment metrics, with IFEval improving by **+4.0**, Arena-Hard by **+4.3**, AlignBench by **+0.5**, and MTBench by **+0.1**. These results confirm that VRPO effectively aligns MDMs with human preferences across diverse instruction-following and safety scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on VRPO Components (Table 2)

The ablation experiments rigorously evaluate each component of VRPO, providing strong empirical support for the theoretical analyses:
- **Correlation between Variance and Performance:** A clear trend is observed: configurations leading to lower variance in the preference score estimator ($V_{\hat{s}_\theta}$) also result in lower variances in the loss and gradient, and generally, improved task performance. This empirically validates the paper's core theoretical insight that controlling $V_{\hat{s}_\theta}$ is crucial.
- **Impact of Sampling Budget:** Increasing the total sampling budget `n` (e.g., from `n=1` to `n=8` by setting `n_t=8, n_y_t=1`) drastically reduces $V_{\hat{s}_\theta}$ (from 44.0 to 1.0) and translates into significant performance gains (e.g., GSM8K from 80.1 to 83.3). This confirms Proposition 1 (i).
- **Effectiveness of Optimal Allocation:** For a fixed sampling budget, allocating samples optimally across timesteps (e.g., `n_t=4, n_y_t=1` vs. `n_t=1, n_y_t=4` for `n=4`) yields lower estimator variance and better benchmark results. This supports Proposition 1 (ii).
- **Power of Antithetic Sampling:** Antithetic sampling demonstrably reduces variance (e.g., $V_{\hat{s}_\theta}$ is 2183.7 without antithetic sampling, and 1.0 with it, for `n_t=8, n_y_t=1` setting). While the performance gains on downstream benchmarks might not always be proportionally large, this technique significantly stabilizes the optimization process, as evidenced by smoother loss curves.
- **Training Stability (Figure 5):** The loss curves for VRPO configurations exhibit significantly smoother trajectories and lower variability compared to baselines, indicating improved optimization stability and efficiency due to reduced gradient noise.
- **Robustness of Results (Table 5):** Retraining LLaDA 1.5 with different random seeds shows consistent performance gains over LLaDA Instruct, with small standard deviations and confidence intervals, highlighting the robustness of VRPO's effectiveness.

#### 4.4. Computational Cost

- The default sampling budget of `n=8` for VRPO increases computation by roughly 8 times compared to `n=1` methods. However, this added overhead is deemed "modest—less than 0.5% of pre-training"—making it practically acceptable for large-scale models. The "free lunch" aspects of optimal allocation and antithetic sampling further enhance efficiency without additional cost.

In summary, LLaDA 1.5 showcases that a theoretically grounded approach to variance reduction can unlock the full potential of DPO for MDMs, leading to superior and more stable alignment performance across a broad range of challenging tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "LLADA 1.5: Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization for Large Language Diffusion Models" holds significant implications for the advancement of language models and AI research in general.

**1. Foundational Framework for MDM Alignment:** This paper provides a much-needed systematic and principled framework for aligning Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) with human preferences. Prior efforts in MDM alignment were often ad-hoc or focused on specific tasks without deep theoretical underpinnings regarding the unique challenges of diffusion models. LLaDA 1.5 establishes a robust theoretical and empirical blueprint for future MDM alignment research, filling a critical gap in the field.

**2. Practical Enhancement of MDMs:** The resulting model, LLaDA 1.5, demonstrates substantial and consistent performance improvements across a diverse set of challenging benchmarks, including mathematical reasoning, code generation, and various instruction-following and safety tasks. These gains position MDMs as increasingly viable and competitive alternatives to autoregressive models (ARMs), particularly given LLaDA 1.5's competitive mathematical performance. This directly contributes to making MDMs more useful and reliable in real-world applications.

**3. Deep Theoretical Insights into ELBO-based Optimization:** The paper's formal analysis of how the variance of ELBO-based likelihood estimates propagates into the preference optimization loss and gradient is a key theoretical contribution. Identifying the preference score estimator's variance as a central governing factor provides transferable insights beyond DPO and even beyond language modeling, relevant to any deep learning setup that relies on noisy, lower-bound estimates for optimization. This work systematically explains why instability might arise and how to theoretically address it.

**4. Efficient and Unbiased Variance Reduction Techniques:** The proposed Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization (VRPO) integrates three practical and theoretically sound variance reduction strategies: increasing sampling budget, optimal budget allocation, and antithetic sampling. The fact that optimal allocation and antithetic sampling offer "free lunch" variance reduction (without additional computational cost) is particularly impactful, making the approach highly efficient and scalable for large models. These techniques are proven to be unbiased, ensuring that the optimization converges to the correct objective while being more stable.

**5. Generalizability Across Alignment Algorithms:** The authors explicitly highlight that their variance reduction techniques and analysis are not confined to DPO but can extend naturally to other RL-based alignment algorithms like PPO and GRPO, which also involve estimating ELBOs or differences of correlated ELBOs. This broad applicability means the insights gained here can accelerate the development of aligned MDMs regardless of the specific RL paradigm chosen.

**6. Future Research Directions:** This work lays strong groundwork for several future research avenues. It encourages further exploration into:
 - Applying VRPO to other MDM architectures and tasks.
 - Developing even more advanced variance reduction techniques for nested Monte Carlo estimations in complex deep learning settings.
 - Deepening the theoretical understanding of MDM dynamics during preference optimization.
 - Investigating the long-term impacts of reduced variance on model safety, robustness, and generalization capabilities.

In conclusion, "LLADA 1.5" significantly advances the state-of-the-art in MDM alignment by offering a robust, theoretically grounded, and empirically validated framework. Its contributions are poised to drive the continued development of high-performing, well-aligned diffusion language models, expanding their utility and impact in the broader AI landscape.
