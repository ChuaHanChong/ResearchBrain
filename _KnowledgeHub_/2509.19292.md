# SOE: Sample-Efficient Robot Policy Self-Improvement via On-Manifold Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> The SOE framework facilitates robot policy self-improvement through on-manifold exploration, leveraging a learned compact latent representation to generate diverse, safe, and temporally consistent actions. This method yields substantial gains in task success rates and sample efficiency across both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming baseline approaches.

## Problem

- Robot learning from human demonstrations suffers from high data acquisition costs and distributional bias, limiting scalability and generalization.
- Imitation-learned policies often exhibit 'action mode collapse,' resulting in limited action diversity and an inability to discover improved behaviors.
- Unstructured exploration strategies like random noise are inefficient and unsafe for robots operating in high-dimensional action spaces.

## Method

- **SOE** learns a compact, disentangled latent representation of task-relevant information using a ==Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB)== objective, enabling exploration on a ==manifold of valid actions==.
- Exploration is performed by ==perturbing this latent space==, which is then decoded to generate diverse, yet temporally consistent and physically plausible ==action chunks==.
- The framework is implemented as a ==dual-path plug-in module==, allowing seamless integration with existing base policies (e.g., ==Diffusion Policy==) without degrading their core performance, and supports user-guided steering via interpretable latent dimensions.

## Results

- SOE achieved superior policy self-improvement, with an average relative improvement of **50.8%** in task success rates and reduced average jerk across real-world manipulation tasks, while requiring fewer rollouts than baselines.
- In multi-round simulation experiments, SOE consistently improved policy performance over **multiple iterations**, unlike Diffusion Policy and SIME, which showed unstable or degrading performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the VIB's KL term for learning a compact, disentangled latent space, and demonstrated that SOE is robust to latent dimension variations while identifying task-specific intrinsic dimensions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining exploration to a learned task-specific manifold, rather than the raw action space, is crucial for achieving safe, smooth, and effective robot policy self-improvement.
> - A Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) objective effectively disentangles latent dimensions, allowing for structured exploration where high signal-to-noise ratio dimensions correspond to meaningful action variations.
> - A modular, dual-path architecture allows exploration capabilities to be added to and jointly optimized with existing imitation learning policies, enhancing diversity without compromising stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning, particularly for manipulation tasks, has witnessed rapid advancements, largely driven by data-intensive approaches like imitation learning. These methods, leveraging neural networks and large datasets, enable robots to learn complex visuomotor behaviors from human demonstrations, often achieving impressive performance. However, this success comes with significant challenges that form the backdrop for SOE's contributions.

Firstly, a major hurdle is the heavy reliance on human teleoperation for data acquisition. This process is inherently costly, requiring skilled operators and specialized equipment, thereby limiting scalability. More critically, teleoperated data often suffers from distributional bias, failing to cover the diverse scenarios a robot might encounter in the real world. Robots may also internalize human habits rather than truly task-relevant behaviors, leading to limited generalization and robustness. This highlights a need to move beyond mere imitation towards autonomous skill acquisition and refinement.

Secondly, the concept of "policy self-improvement" has emerged as a promising paradigm. Instead of passively imitating, robots actively explore their environments, collect diverse experience, and use this data to refine their policies. This enables robots to discover novel behaviors beyond human demonstrations and develop a deeper understanding of action variability, leading to more robust policies. SOE positions itself directly within this self-improvement paradigm, aiming to make it more effective and practical.

A critical component of effective self-improvement is efficient and safe exploration. Existing imitation-learned policies often suffer from "action mode collapse," overfiting demonstrations and failing to produce diverse behaviors. Unstructured exploration strategies, such as injecting random noise, are generally ineffective in high-dimensional action spaces and pose significant safety risks in real-world deployment (e.g., causing hardware damage). This necessitates a more structured approach to exploration that ensures safety and effectiveness without sacrificing diversity.

SOE specifically addresses this gap by proposing "on-manifold exploration." It builds upon modern robot learning techniques, particularly diffusion policies, which have shown great promise in generating multimodal actions. The paper directly references and compares itself to contemporary methods like Diffusion Policy (DP) and SIME, which also aim to enhance exploration. SIME, for instance, perturbs diffusion policy conditions to induce modal-level exploration. SOE extends this idea by explicitly constraining exploration to the "task manifold," ensuring generated actions are not just diverse but also valid and temporally coherent. This contrasts with "off-manifold" exploration, which can lead to unstable and erratic behaviors.

By focusing on learning a compact, disentangled latent representation of task-relevant factors, SOE aligns with broader trends in machine learning towards more interpretable and controllable latent spaces. Its plug-in module design also promotes modularity and generalizability, allowing it to enhance existing imitation learning pipelines. Overall, SOE advances the state-of-the-art in autonomous robot learning by providing a principled, safe, and sample-efficient method for policy self-improvement, moving closer to the vision of truly adaptive and self-discovering AI agents as articulated by Richard Sutton's "The Bitter Lesson."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the SOE framework stems from the inherent limitations of current data-driven robot learning approaches, particularly their heavy reliance on costly and often biased human teleoperated demonstrations. While imitation learning has achieved impressive results, scaling it up faces significant challenges:
- **High Cost of Data Acquisition:** Teleoperation requires skilled operators and specialized equipment, making large-scale data collection economically prohibitive.
- **Distributional Bias and Limited Coverage:** Human demonstrations, even when abundant, cannot cover every possible scenario a robot might encounter, leading to policies that may fail in novel situations. Robots might also mimic human habits rather than truly optimal or diverse task-relevant behaviors.
- **Action Mode Collapse:** Imitation-learned policies often overfit the expert data, resulting in single-modal motions and a lack of diverse behaviors, which limits their ability to discover improved solutions through self-practice.

To overcome these limitations, the paper champions the paradigm of **robot policy self-improvement**, where robots actively explore their environment to collect diverse experiences and leverage these to refine their capabilities. However, effective self-improvement critically hinges on effective exploration. This leads to the central problem SOE aims to solve: how to enable exploration that is both **safe and effective** in high-dimensional, continuous action spaces, especially when dealing with temporally grouped "action chunks."

The key objectives of the SOE framework are therefore:
1. **Enhance Policy Exploration Capability:** Develop a mechanism that allows robot policies to generate diverse behaviors beyond what is present in initial demonstrations.
2. **Ensure Safety and Smoothness:** Guarantee that exploration actions remain within the realm of valid and physically plausible movements, preventing unsafe, erratic, or potentially damaging behaviors. This is crucial for real-world deployment.
3. **Improve Sample Efficiency:** Minimize the number of interactions required for the robot to discover successful behaviors and improve its policy, addressing the high cost of real-world robot trials.
4. **Preserve Base Policy Performance:** Design the exploration mechanism as a plug-in module that can be seamlessly integrated with arbitrary policy models without degrading the performance of the base policy.
5. **Enable Controllable and Interpretable Exploration:** Leverage the structure of the learned representation to allow for human guidance, steering the exploration towards preferred or promising directions, thereby further boosting efficiency.

In essence, SOE aims to provide a principled, structured approach to exploration that overcomes the shortcomings of random or naive noise injection, enabling robots to autonomously and efficiently refine their skills in complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SOE, or Self-Improvement via On-Manifold Exploration, introduces a novel framework that addresses the challenges of safe, efficient, and diverse exploration for robot policy self-improvement. The methodology is built upon three core components: On-Manifold Exploration, a Plug-in Architecture, and User-Guided Steering.

#### 3.1. On-Manifold Exploration

The central idea of SOE is to constrain exploration to the "manifold of valid actions" to ensure safety and effectiveness. This is achieved by learning a compact latent representation that captures only task-essential information, discarding irrelevant details from observations.
- **Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB):** SOE employs a VIB objective to learn this latent representation. Given an observation $O$ and an action $A$, a latent variable $Z$ is defined via an encoder $p_{\theta}(z|o)$. The objective maximizes the mutual information between $Z$ and $A$ while minimizing the mutual information between $Z$ and $O$ (controlled by a hyperparameter $\beta$). This forces $Z$ to retain only action-relevant features, resulting in a structured and well-shaped latent space.
- **Latent Space Perturbation:** Instead of perturbing actions directly in the high-dimensional action space, exploration is performed in this compact latent space. The encoder $p_{\theta}$ predicts a mean $\mu_t$ and standard deviation $\sigma_t$ for the latent variable $Z$ given an observation $o_t$. A noisy latent variable $z_t$ is then sampled from $N(\mu_t, (\alpha\sigma_t)^2)$, where $\alpha$ is an exploration scale hyperparameter. This $z_t$ is then decoded by $q_{\phi}(A|Z=z_t)$ to generate an action. This "sampling on the manifold" ensures that generated actions are diverse yet temporally consistent and physically plausible, avoiding erratic behaviors often seen with random noise injection.

#### 3.2. Exploration as a Plug-in

To ensure general applicability and avoid degrading the base policy's performance, SOE is designed as a plug-in module with a dual-path architecture:
- **Dual-Path Architecture:** The observation embedding from the base policy's encoder $E(o_t)$ is processed through two parallel paths:
 - **Base Path:** The original embedding $c_t = E(o_t)$ directly conditions the base policy's action decoder $D$ to generate action chunks $a_{t:t+H}$. This path maintains the stable, learned behavior of the base policy.
 - **Exploration Path:** The same observation embedding $c_t$ is fed into the latent encoder $p_{\theta}$ to obtain $\mu_t, \sigma_t$. A perturbed latent variable $z_t$ is sampled and then decoded by $q_{\phi}$ into a modified embedding $\tilde{c}_t$, which then conditions the action decoder $D$ to generate diverse action proposals $\tilde{a}_{t:t+H}$.
- **Joint Optimization:** Both paths are optimized jointly but with distinct loss functions. The base path uses a standard imitation loss ($L_{IL}$), typically a diffusion loss if the base policy is a diffusion model. The exploration path uses the variational information bottleneck loss ($L_{IB}$), which combines action reconstruction (also via the base policy's diffusion decoder) with KL regularization. Crucially, $L_{IB}$ updates only $p_{\theta}$ and $q_{\phi}$, while $L_{IL}$ updates only $E$ and $D$. This separation ensures that the exploration mechanism enhances diversity without compromising the base policy's core performance. The paper instantiates the base policy as Diffusion Policy.

#### 3.3. User-Guided Steering

The learned latent representation $Z$ also provides a mechanism for controllable exploration:
- **Disentanglement:** The VIB objective encourages disentanglement within the latent space, meaning different dimensions of $Z$ tend to correspond to distinct task-relevant factors (e.g., horizontal vs. vertical movement).
- **Effective Dimensions Identification:** To focus user guidance, SOE identifies the most informative latent dimensions using a Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) criterion ($Var(\mu_i) / E[\sigma_i^2]$). High SNR indicates reliable, task-relevant information.
- **Interactive Steering:** For these "effective dimensions," a batch of action proposals is generated by perturbing specific latent dimensions. Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is then applied to select a diverse subset of these proposals. Users are presented with these diverse actions via an interactive interface, allowing them to intuitively select preferred behaviors and directly steer the robot's exploration, further boosting sample efficiency beyond autonomous self-improvement.

This comprehensive methodology allows SOE to achieve structured, safe, and efficient exploration, making it a powerful tool for policy self-improvement in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and real-world settings demonstrate the significant advantages of SOE over existing methods in terms of effectiveness, safety, and sample efficiency for robot policy self-improvement.

#### 4.1. Real-World Task Evaluation (Mug Hang, Toaster Load, Lamp Cap)

- **Superior Performance:** SOE consistently outperforms Diffusion Policy (DP) and SIME across all three real-world tasks. It achieves higher Pass@5 rates (indicating more effective exploration), lower average jerk values (signifying smoother and safer motions), and requires fewer rollouts (demonstrating superior sample efficiency).
- **Significant Policy Improvement:** After just one round of policy self-improvement, SOE yields substantial gains in task success rate. For instance, it achieved an average relative improvement of 50.8% across the real-world tasks. In contrast, DP often showed performance degradation, and SIME offered weaker improvements despite broader exploration, largely due to its erratic behaviors.
- **Qualitative Difference in Exploration:** Visualizations confirm that SOE generates purposeful and structured action proposals, avoiding the repetitive failures of DP and the erratic, temporally inconsistent motions produced by SIME (Fig. 4). This validates the benefits of on-manifold exploration.
- **Effectiveness of User-Guided Steering:** The integration of human-in-the-loop steering further enhances SOE's performance and sample efficiency, leading to even greater success rates with fewer required rollouts (e.g., 62% relative improvement for Lamp Cap).
- **Latent Space Disentanglement:** Visualization of action proposals from different latent dimensions (Fig. 5) confirms that the learned latent space is indeed disentangled. High-SNR dimensions correspond to meaningful and distinct patterns of action variation (e.g., left/right, up/down, straight/curved movements), while low-SNR dimensions yield negligible changes. This empirically validates the design principles and the potential for structured, interpretable exploration.

#### 4.2. Simulation Benchmark Evaluation (Lift, Can, Square, Transport from RoboMimic)

- **Consistent Multi-Round Improvement:** In multi-round self-improvement experiments across four simulation tasks, SOE is the only method that consistently improves policy performance over multiple iterations (Fig. 6). Both DP and SIME exhibit unstable performance, with success rates fluctuating or even declining in certain tasks, highlighting the robustness of SOE's approach.
- **Sustained Sample Efficiency:** While SOE may require a similar number of rollouts as baselines in the initial round, it quickly reduces interaction costs in subsequent rounds (Fig. 7). This indicates that SOE effectively leverages collected experience, avoids redundant exploration, and efficiently discovers successful behaviors over time.
- **Higher Exploration Success Rate:** On average, SOE achieves higher Pass@5 rates, indicating more effective exploration and successful behavior discovery across rounds.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (on Can Task)

- **Importance of KL Term:** Removing the KL regularization term from the VIB objective reduces the final success rate, underscoring its critical role in enforcing a compact, informative, and disentangled latent representation necessary for effective on-manifold exploration.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The noise scale $\alpha$ and KL weight $\beta$ are crucial for balancing exploration diversity and stability. Too small an $\alpha$ leads to conservative exploration, while too large an $\alpha$ can induce aggressive and unstable strategies. Similarly, suboptimal $\beta$ values can lead to entangled latent representations or model collapse. The study identifies effective ranges for these parameters.
- **Robustness to Latent Dimension:** SOE shows robustness to variations in the nominal latent dimension $d$ (e.g., from 16 to 64), with little effect on the final success rate. This suggests that the VIB objective effectively constrains the latent space to capture only task-relevant information, irrespective of its declared dimensionality.
- **Intrinsic Dimension Correlation:** The number of "effective dimensions" (identified by SNR) is shown to be largely invariant for a given task, correlating with task complexity (e.g., 8 for Lift/Can, 16 for Transport, as shown in Table II). This suggests the existence of a task-specific intrinsic dimension that SOE successfully identifies and exploits for structured exploration.

Collectively, these findings strongly support SOE's efficacy as a principled and robust framework for sample-efficient robot policy self-improvement, demonstrating its practical utility in diverse real-world and simulated manipulation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SOE framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly in addressing the critical challenges of data efficiency, safety, and robustness for autonomous robot skill acquisition. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that **on-manifold exploration via a structured latent representation is a principled and highly effective approach to sample-efficient robot policy self-improvement.**

The potential impacts of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability in Real-World Robotics:** By constraining exploration to the manifold of valid actions, SOE drastically reduces the risk of generating unsafe, erratic, or damaging robot behaviors. This is paramount for deploying autonomous robots in real-world settings, where hardware damage or unpredictable movements can be costly and dangerous. The measured reduction in "average jerk" is a direct testament to this, fostering greater trust and practical viability.

2. **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition and Deployment:** The superior sample efficiency of SOE means robots can learn and refine complex manipulation skills with significantly fewer interactions with the environment. This directly translates to reduced training time and resource consumption, making it more feasible to train robots for a wider array of tasks, moving beyond the limitations of expensive human teleoperation. This is particularly crucial for tasks requiring high precision or fine-grained dexterity.

3. **Greater Autonomy and Self-Sufficiency for Robots:** SOE empowers robots to actively discover and correct their own behaviors, rather than passively relying solely on human-provided demonstrations. This aligns with the vision of self-improving AI agents, moving towards robots that can adapt and evolve their capabilities continually, even after initial deployment. This capability is fundamental for developing truly general-purpose robots.

4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** By enabling diverse and structured exploration, SOE helps policies to cover a broader range of task variations and encounter novel scenarios. This intrinsically leads to policies that are less prone to distributional shift errors and more robust to environmental uncertainties, ultimately enhancing their generalization capabilities.

5. **Interpretability and Controllability of Exploration:** The disentangled latent space, combined with user-guided steering, offers an intuitive way for human operators to understand and influence the robot's exploration process. This human-in-the-loop capability can dramatically boost efficiency in scenarios where targeted exploration is beneficial, while also offering a pathway for easier debugging and refinement of robot behaviors.

6. **Modularity and Broad Applicability:** The design of SOE as a plug-in module, seamlessly integrable with existing imitation learning pipelines (like Diffusion Policy), enhances its practical utility. It means that advancements in base policy architectures can directly benefit from SOE's exploration capabilities without requiring major architectural overhauls.

7. **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work opens new avenues for research into structured exploration mechanisms, the learning of intrinsic task dimensions, and the interplay between latent representations and policy refinement. The empirical observation of task-specific intrinsic dimensions, for example, could inspire further theoretical and practical investigations into the minimal degrees of freedom required for various robotic tasks.

In conclusion, SOE provides a robust, safe, and efficient solution to a fundamental challenge in robot learning. By enabling robots to discover capabilities through intelligent, on-manifold exploration, it contributes significantly to the development of more autonomous, adaptive, and deployable robotic systems, pushing the boundaries towards truly intelligent robotic agents that can continually refine their understanding of the world.
