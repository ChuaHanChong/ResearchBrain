# MetaRSI / RSI2: A Meta-Recursive Self-Improving System for Recursive Self-Improving Systems Themselves

> [!summary] Summary
> MetaRSI-v1 is a recursive self-improvement framework in which one loop kernel drives three typed operators with disjoint write surfaces: Data-RSI writes verified training records, Harness-RSI edits a five-slot execution scaffold, and Model-RSI updates weights. A two-axis scheduler decides operator order and rewrites their proposal policies, with a meta layer revising the schedule. Running with no external teacher, it gains 10.9 average points over a frozen 35B target.

## Problem

- Of 45 surveyed self-improvement systems, 69% close their loop against machine-checkable targets, certifying benchmark-bound competence inside a pre-specified slice rather than general capability in a discipline.
- Prior systems mutate a single surface only: within-episode output refinement, scaffold or workflow search, or self-generated finetuning data, leaving the other surfaces fixed and their interaction unexamined.
- Each single-surface loop pays a cost it cannot see: scaffold edits recur at every inference, data amplification cannot reach unexhibited capability, and training can regress held behaviour.

## Method

- One shared loop kernel instantiates three operators over disjoint write surfaces: **Data-RSI** on the corpus, **Harness-RSI** on five scaffold slots (prompt, memory, built-in tools, skills, MCP), **Model-RSI** on ==LoRA== weights.
- **Transition Agent** adapters pass the learning signal and outputs between operators; only five of six orderings are admissible, since harness-then-model is ill-posed because the dataset predates the scaffold change.
- A scheduling agent jointly optimizes two axes, horizontal operator order and vertical rewriting of each operator's proposal policy, while a meta agent revises the scheduling policy once per improvement term.

## Results

- On Qwen3.5-35B-A3B across four sealed splits, the full system gains **+10.9** average points over the frozen system, beating the best single operator (**+6.6**) and both fixed-order compositions (**+7.3**).
- SWE-bench Pro resolve rate nearly doubles, **10.3** to **19.5**, and six frontier models reached only through interfaces gain **+5.6** to **+9.2** on Terminal-Bench 2.1 via the harness route, mean **+7.3**.
- Over five terms the meta-updated improver reaches **+26.2** cumulative versus **+20.7**, carries **68%** of policy edits forward against **38%**, and halves scheduler regret from **16.5%** to **8.2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verification format, not subject matter, is the binding constraint on where improvement loops close; cheap machine-checkable feedback decides feasibility more than domain difficulty does.
> - Who decides operator order is load-bearing, not bookkeeping: identical operators over identical evidence differ by 3.6 points between learned scheduling and the best fixed order.
> - Self-improvement on a fixed substrate decays; improving the improver delays the plateau and raises the ceiling, but does not eliminate diminishing per-term returns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) explores methods for a system to learn from its own failures and improve its model-building processes, leading to cumulative gains in subsequent models. Prior work in RSI has primarily focused on tasks with machine-checkable verification, such as coding, formal science question answering, and mathematical problem sets. A survey of 45 recent systems cited in the paper indicates that 69% close their improvement loops against targets verifiable by machine. This concentration implies a "format bound" rather than a "subject bound," meaning current RSI systems are limited by the tractability of formal evaluation, not necessarily by the intrinsic domain of the problem.

The paper frames this work within the context of the evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs) and artificial intelligence. It identifies four historical engineering regimes that have driven LLM capabilities:
1. **Scale:** Leveraging the Transformer architecture and empirical scaling laws to achieve few-shot competence.
2. **Alignment:** Employing instruction tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback to make models follow instructions.
3. **Inference-time Computation:** Utilizing techniques like chain-of-thought prompting to improve accuracy at fixed weights.
4. **External Scaffolding:** Mediating model capabilities through retrieval, tools, memory, and long-horizon agent loops.

This historical progression illustrates a shift in the locus of effective capability from internal model parameters to external execution structures. Existing RSI approaches often concentrate on modifying only one of these aspects (e.g., output, scaffold, or data) in isolation, leading to limitations such as misattribution of failures, non-composability of improvements, and measurement confined to easily verifiable regimes. For instance, self-refinement techniques revise answers within an episode but leave no lasting improvement, while scaffold-based approaches enhance execution but incur recurring inference costs. Data-centric methods generate finetuning data but may not address underlying model deficiencies.

The present work aims to transcend these limitations by integrating improvements across the data, model parameters, and execution scaffold within a unified framework. It seeks to enable RSI in domains where verification is complex, open-ended, and requires argument, replication, or measurement, addressing the "standing loss" identified in domains with high model capability but low verifiability (Figure 5). This positions MetaRSI-v1 as an effort to build machinery that constructs self-improvement loops across diverse, real-world scientific and engineering practices, moving beyond benchmark-bound capability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a meta-recursive self-improving system, MetaRSI-v1, capable of operating across diverse scientific, engineering, and meta-scientific domains where formal, machine-checkable verification is often unavailable or incomplete. The authors argue that current RSI systems are constrained by their reliance on easily verifiable benchmarks (e.g., coding, closed-form science QA), which limits improvements to a "machine-checkable slice" rather than fostering general capability.

The key motivations driving this work include:

- **Addressing the "Format Bound":** The paper asserts that the current limitation of RSI systems is a "format bound" (restricted to machine-checkable targets) rather than a "subject bound." The goal is to extend RSI to domains where correctness is determined by argument, replication, or measurement, necessitating a framework that can handle open-ended questions and complex verification processes.
- **Unified Paradigm for System Components:** A deployed system's behavior is a joint product of its data, model weights, and execution scaffold (harness). Existing single-surface improvement methods often incur costs they cannot account for (e.g., scaffold extensions increase inference cost, training may regress existing behavior, data amplification cannot generate new capabilities). The paper aims to develop a unified paradigm that allows composable changes across all three surfaces, acknowledging their interdependencies and optimizing across them.
- **Meta-Level Control and Attributable Gains:** Current systems often employ fixed improvement operators or hard-wired sequences, which may be inefficient or ill-posed. A central motivation is to introduce a meta-level policy that dynamically schedules and optimizes the application of different improvement operators based on evidence. This meta-control aims to ensure that improvements are attributable to the self-improvement mechanism itself, rather than to external teachers or stronger models, by having the target model play every role in its own loop (closed-loop self-evolution).
- **Opening New Improvement Paths:** The framework seeks to open complementary routes for self-improvement: one that internalizes capability into model parameters through training (Model-RSI) and another that extends capability via the execution scaffold without touching weights (Harness-RSI). Data-RSI is redefined as a shared substrate that feeds both, converting execution experience into verified records and marking the boundary of existing competence.
- **Formulating Refutable Laws:** The research aims to contribute to a deeper understanding of self-improvement by proposing five refutable structural laws. These laws concern where improvement loops can exist, how operators should compose, and the practical implications of external supervision, providing a theoretical foundation for future RSI development.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to shift the focus of progress from merely building stronger models to effectively closing and optimizing self-improvement loops in diverse, real-world contexts, particularly where human labor is typically required for complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MetaRSI-v1 is designed as a meta-recursive self-improving system that integrates three typed operators sharing a common loop kernel. The methodology is structured across three nested improvement levels, each with distinct agents and objects of improvement.

#### 3.1. The Loop Kernel: One Paradigm for Heterogeneous Operators

The foundation of MetaRSI-v1 is a unified loop kernel, which all self-improvement operators must instantiate. This kernel defines an operator by three conditions:
1. Its sole input is a compiled learning signal (σ) derived from the system's own attempts, not direct environmental observation.
2. Its policy for proposing changes (π) is mutable.
3. The adjudication of whether a change is beneficial is performed by a fixed, external component, outside the operator's write surface.

This kernel refines the self-improvement process into seven stages: Observe → Diagnose → Propose → Validate → Execute → Select → Export. Crucially, the "Diagnose" and "Propose" stages are model-driven, where the target model identifies failures and suggests modifications. The remaining five stages are deterministic code, ensuring that the decision of "what to change" is semantic and model-driven, while "whether the change was good" is objective and auditable.

The learning signal (σ) is a deterministic compilation of an evidence set (E) from the system's attempts. It comprises failure signatures, which are structured into terminal cause, causal role, and reusable mechanism (Equation 10). This signal is perishable, meaning any capability-altering step necessitates its recompilation. The framework emphasizes a strict "authority boundary": neither the signal compiler (Σ) nor the verifier (v) is part of any operator's mutable surface, preventing the system from altering its own definition of success. A sealed evaluator (Q⋆) and held-out task set (T⋆) remain outside all write surfaces.

#### 3.2. Three Base Operators

MetaRSI-v1 instantiates the loop kernel with three operators, each with a disjoint write surface over the system's three core components: data (D), model parameters (θ), and harness (H).

1. **Data-RSI (Amplifier):**
 - **Writes:** The data state (D).
 - **Function:** Synthesizes verified training records from the system's execution experience, pressing out existing capabilities and marking their boundaries.
 - **Mechanism:** Extracts four-dimensional learning signatures (κ) – knowledge, reasoning, verification, and distractor deficits (Equation 11) – from episodes. These signatures guide a four-stage synthesis pipeline: experience extraction, directive generation, adversarial generation, and verification. Adversarial generation involves an "Operator" role authoring records and an "Anchor" role blindly re-deriving them, ensuring self-consistency. Records are further verified by contract and semantic checks before being added to a dataset.
 - **Purpose:** Produces the `Dataset` artifact consumed by other operators, focusing external supervision only past the boundary of observed competence.

2. **Harness-RSI (Scaffold):**
 - **Writes:** The harness state (H).
 - **Function:** Edits the execution scaffold to add capability without modifying model weights. These changes take immediate effect without training costs.
 - **Mechanism:** The harness is a "genome" comprising five slots: system prompt, memory, built-in tools, skills, and Model Context Protocol (MCP)-mounted tools (Equation 15). Changes are made via typed `HarnessPatch` objects, which include a repair hypothesis, mutation types, expected effects, and risk statements. Deterministic validation ensures changes are within declared slots. Promotion requires strict improvement over the historical best on the adaptation set, constrained by a complexity budget.
 - **Purpose:** Offers a cheap, immediate way to add capability, paid as context at every inference.

3. **Model-RSI (Internalization):**
 - **Writes:** The model state (θ).
 - **Function:** Internalizes capabilities into the model parameters through bounded training recipes, converting recurring context costs into a one-time training cost.
 - **Mechanism:** Consumes `Dataset` artifacts from Data-RSI. Proposals specify a bounded training recipe, covering architectural choices (adapter placement), adaptation family (e.g., LoRA rank), optimizer settings, sequence length, and checkpointing (Equation 18). Training proceeds from a fixed base checkpoint over a cumulative dataset. Promotion follows strict improvement criteria on an independent evaluation layer.
 - **Purpose:** Provides durable, inference-cost-free capabilities, persistent across scaffolds.

#### 3.3. Operator Composition: Five Admissible Transitions

The framework defines operator composition based on a "signal freshness" principle (Principle 4.4). A step is "capability-altering" if it changes the deployed system's behavior (Harness-RSI and Model-RSI are, Data-RSI is not). An artifact is "fresh" if its underlying signal was compiled no earlier than the most recent capability-altering step. This leads to five admissible cross-operator transitions (D→H, D→M, H→D, M→D, M→H) and two self-transitions (D→D, H→H), while forbidding H→M and M→M because they would train on stale data (Figure 8). Each admissible transition is managed by a `Transition Agent-v1` adapter that converts the producer's output to the consumer's input, performing type checking and rejecting stale artifacts.

#### 3.4. Two-Axis Scheduling

The `RSI^2 Agent-v1` manages the scheduling of operators across two axes:
1. **Horizontal Orchestration:** Extends the sequence of operators to be applied (e.g., D→H→D→M). It draws an improvement program specifying operator order, purpose, justification, and budget allocation (Equation 25).
2. **Vertical Optimization:** Improves an operator's internal proposal policy (π) itself, rather than just invoking it. This involves issuing a directive to a dedicated `RSI^2 Sub-Agent-v1` for the target operator, which then revises its policy. Policy changes are governed by an explicit "optimization contract" (Equation 27) that specifies mutable, action, and protected surfaces, ensuring changes are declared and auditable.

#### 3.5. MetaRSI: Improving the Scheduler

The outermost loop is managed by the `MetaRSI^2 Agent-v1`, which revises the `RSI^2 Agent-v1`'s scheduling policy (ϕ) once an entire improvement "term" is completed (Equation 28). This meta-agent learns from past terms which sequences and vertical directives produced gains that survived the release gate, correcting systematic misallocations or misdiagnoses. This creates three nested improvement levels with disjoint write surfaces (Table 2, Equation 29), ensuring clear attribution of gains and preventing agents from altering their own definitions of success. The framework also supports "human-in-the-loop" operation, allowing human experts to occupy any of the agent roles while adhering to the same contracts and auditability.

#### 3.6. Overall Operation: The Lifecycle of an Improvement Term

Algorithm 1 describes the lifecycle of an improvement term. It begins by compiling an initial learning signal. The `RSI^2 Agent-v1` then iteratively chooses between horizontal (applying operators in sequence) and vertical (improving an operator's policy) actions, with the learning signal recompiled after every capability-altering step. The term concludes with the `MetaRSI^2 Agent-v1` updating the scheduler's policy for subsequent terms. This iterative process, exemplified by a concrete sequence of steps addressing a "missing_validation" failure, highlights how the system compounds improvements and learns to allocate resources effectively.

#### 3.7. What the Framework Protects

To ensure trust and prevent metric inflation, the framework enforces strict separations: proposal is distinct from validation, and candidate generation from release. The sealed measurement (Q⋆), its task set (T⋆), release rule, and resource ledger are strictly outside every write surface at all levels (Equation 30). This design makes improvements auditable and ensures reported gains reflect actual capability rather than changes to the success criteria.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of MetaRSI-v1 involved experiments on two target lines: a self-hosted open-weight model (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B) where all three operators could act, and several frontier models (e.g., GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5) accessible only via API, thus limited to the harness route (Data-RSI and Harness-RSI). All experiments employed a closed-loop self-evolution setup, meaning the target model itself played every model-driven role, ensuring gains were attributable to the framework and not an external teacher. Benchmarks included executable coding (Terminal-Bench 2.1, SWE-bench Pro) and closed-form scientific/mathematical reasoning (GPQA-D-hard100, AIME).

#### 4.1. Main Results (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B)

Experiments on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, under identical budgets, demonstrated the following:

- **Overall Performance:** MetaRSI-v1 achieved the highest average score improvement across all four benchmarks, increasing the average score by +10.9 points over the frozen initial system (Table 3).
- **Domain-Specific Gains:**
 - AIME (mathematics): +13.3 points
 - GPQA-D-hard100 (science QA): +12.6 points
 - SWE-bench Pro (software engineering): +9.2 points (nearly doubling the resolve rate from 10.3 to 19.5)
 - Terminal-Bench 2.1 (coding): +8.3 points
- **Comparison to Single Operators:** Each single operator (Data-RSI, Harness-RSI, Model-RSI) improved the frozen system, with Harness-RSI being the strongest single operator (+6.6 average points). However, MetaRSI-v1's scheduled composition added a further +4.3 points on top of Harness-RSI's performance. The operator ranking was not stable across domains, indicating their complementary nature (Figure 9b).
- **Comparison to Composition Baselines:** MetaRSI-v1 surpassed both a hand-fixed D→M→H pipeline and a static router (both at +7.3 points average) by +3.6 points. This suggests that the dynamic scheduling policy is a load-bearing component, providing significant value beyond merely combining operators.

#### 4.2. Frontier Models Under the Harness Route

The harness route (Data-RSI and Harness-RSI) was evaluated on six leading frontier models on Terminal-Bench 2.1.
- **Consistent Improvement:** All six frontier models demonstrated self-improvement, with gains ranging from +5.6 to +9.2 points, averaging +7.3 points (Figure 10).
- **Effectiveness for Capable Models:** These gains were measured against already high baselines (e.g., GPT-5.6 Sol at 83.4%, Claude Opus 5 at 84.5%), indicating that the harness route is effective even for highly capable models and addresses deficiencies not specific to smaller models.

#### 4.3. Improving the Improver

Multi-term experiments explored the impact of the meta-level update on the `RSI^2 Agent-v1`'s scheduling policy.
- **Meta-Update Advantage:** In Term 2, the meta-updated improver achieved an average gain of +7.2 points, compared to +4.9 points for the original improver, demonstrating a +2.3 point advantage attributable to the meta-level learning (Figure 11c).
- **Cumulative and Sustained Gains:** Over five terms, the meta-updated path achieved a cumulative gain of +26.2 points, compared to +20.7 points for the path without meta-updates, with the gap widening to +5.5 points by Term 5. The meta-path also sustained higher per-term gains and reached a performance plateau later (Table 4, Figure 11a,b).
- **Harness Route Compounding:** The harness route also compounded on frontier models, with mean cumulative gains of +11.3 points over three terms. The rate of retention in subsequent terms was inversely correlated with initial model headroom (Figure 12).

These results collectively indicate that MetaRSI-v1 provides a robust framework for recursive self-improvement, capable of achieving substantial gains across diverse domains, on both open-weight and API-accessed models, and demonstrating the value of meta-level learning in enhancing the self-improvement process itself.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of MetaRSI-v1 lies in its departure from single-surface, benchmark-specific self-improvement systems, offering a framework designed for broader scientific and engineering applications. Its potential impact extends across several dimensions:

#### 5.1. What Composition Buys

The framework addresses limitations of prior RSI:
- **Combating Misattribution:** By using a shared failure signature vocabulary (Equation 10) grounded in evidence before operator intervention, MetaRSI-v1 ensures that diagnoses (e.g., missing procedural habit vs. absent knowledge) are objectively routed to the appropriate operator, rather than being biased by an operator's limited write access.
- **Enabling Composability:** The concept of typed operators and admissible transitions via `Transition Agent-v1` adapters (Figure 8) formalizes the common engineering practice of iteratively adding, internalizing, and then retiring scaffold rules. This makes complex, multi-stage improvement sequences expressible and auditable.
- **Trusted Measurement with Weak Verifiers:** The strict separation of proposal from validation, and candidate generation from release (Section 4.7), ensures that improvements are truly in capability rather than in the definition of success. This is crucial for domains with partial or expensive verifiers, allowing trust in the loop's output.

#### 5.2. An Amplifier Is Not a Source

The MetaRSI-v1 system is characterized as a "closed amplifier." It excels at redistributing and internalizing competence that the model already implicitly holds or exhibits in rollouts. It cannot, however, bootstrap entirely new knowledge that the model itself lacks. The Data-RSI operator's ability to mark the boundary of existing competence (Equation 11) is significant here; it identifies specific deficits where external information (e.g., human supervision, curated corpus, instrument readings) is genuinely required, thus optimizing the expenditure of such external resources. This transforms an amplifier into a system with an "open boundary," capable of integrating external knowledge in a measured way.

#### 5.3. Two Routes Through the Loop

The framework provides two complementary routes for improvement emanating from Data-RSI, decided by what components of the system can be modified (Table 5):
- **Scaffold Route (D ↔ H):** Applicable to models accessible only via API, this route modifies the execution scaffold. It offers immediate, cheap gains at the cost of recurring inference-time context consumption. Its declarative "genome" (Equation 15) is small, inspectable, and highly shareable (e.g., via RSI-Harness), fostering community contributions.
- **Model Route (D → M):** For open-weight models, this route internalizes capability into parameters via training. It incurs a one-time training cost but results in inference-cost-free, persistent capabilities. The framework's design mitigates risks like slow training and regression on existing capabilities.
- **Composed Routes:** The `M → H` edge, "Redundancy Reconciliation," is a key interaction. After Model-RSI internalizes a behavior, Harness-RSI can prune redundant scaffold entries, returning context budget. This prevents unbounded scaffold growth and demonstrates how composing operators can yield benefits beyond what individual operators achieve.

#### 5.4. Loops Across Domains

The generalizability of MetaRSI-v1 is a core claim, achieved by defining its units as pipeline stages rather than task-specific solutions. To instantiate the framework in a new domain, three inputs are required: a task family, a verifier of *any* fidelity (not necessarily machine-checkable), and a seed scaffold. The rest of the framework (learning signatures, scheduling, protected separations) is domain-independent.

- **Verifier Ladder (Table 6):** The framework's utility shifts across a "verifier ladder" (rungs 1-5). At lower rungs (executable tests, simulation), Data-RSI directs synthesis. At higher rungs (protocol execution with instruments, expert rubrics), Data-RSI's most valuable output is its boundary statement, converting an unbounded request for expert supervision into a bounded, measured allocation.
- **Decomposition of Scientific Production Lines:** The framework supports breaking down complex scientific programs (e.g., materials science, computational biology) into pipeline stages. A first-order loop can be closed at a single stage, producing typed artifacts that can then feed adjacent stages, allowing local loops to grow into pipeline-level ones.
- **Compounding Improvements:** The typed artifacts (learning signatures, genomes, verified records) accumulate and carry lineage, allowing loops closed in one domain to inform those in adjacent domains. The framework also optimizes its own costs, such as probing capabilities and adapter reuse, leading to accelerated improvement over time.

#### 5.5. Toward Loops That Touch the Physical World

The paper projects the framework's applicability to physical domains (e.g., robotic manipulation, wet-lab protocols) where verifiers are instruments and actions have irreversible physical consequences.
- **Carry-over:** The learning signal, budget ledger, and protected separations remain relevant. Data-RSI's boundary statement becomes an "experiment allocator" in instrument-limited settings.
- **Failure Modes & Additions:** Challenges include irreversibility (physical actions cannot be sandboxed) and loop latency. Required additions include staged fidelity (simulation to physical instrument), an irreversibility-aware action space, and human authorization as a protected surface for irreversible actions.
- **Environment Model:** The deeper problem of unmodeled situations in physical environments necessitates a fourth operator whose write surface is the environment model itself, invalidating prior diagnostic artifacts and changing the core metric to the rate of converting unmodeled situations into verified ones.

#### 5.6. The Reusable Artifact

The execution harness and genome format (Equation 15) are open-sourced as `RSI-Harness`. This component provides a public library of narrow, well-tested scaffolds, fostering community contribution and serving as an empirical testbed for the cross-domain generalization claim.

#### 5.7. Five Laws of Recursive Self-Improvement

The paper proposes five refutable laws that govern self-improvement loops:
1. **Law 1 (Verification, not capability, sets the frontier):** The ability to close loops correlates strongly with verifier checkability, not merely model competence (Figure 5).
2. **Law 2 (Self-knowledge expires, so re-description is the rate limit):** Changes in a system invalidate prior descriptions, making re-describing the system a binding cost (Principle 4.4).
3. **Law 3 (Competence is substrate-free; its cost is not):** The same competence can reside in different substrates (scaffold vs. weights) with different cost implications; a loop should move competence to the cheapest substrate (M→H edge).
4. **Law 4 (Trust is measured by what cannot be written):** A gain is trustworthy capability only if the definition of success (sealed evaluator, release rule) is outside every writable surface.
5. **Law 5 (No loop creates capability; every gain is imported):** Loops redistribute and internalize existing competence; genuine new capability must be imported, and Data-RSI measures where that import is needed.

#### 5.8. Position: The Unit of Progress Is the Loop, Not the Model

The paper's concluding position is that for the next stage of AI progress, the critical factor is not model capability, but the number and nature of closed self-improvement loops. The "unit of progress" is the loop, and the key question is how cheaply new loops can be closed where none existed before. This suggests that efforts should prioritize building verifiers, instantiating local loops at pipeline stages, and utilizing the harness route, especially where weights are inaccessible, rather than solely pursuing stronger foundational models. This position is supported by the empirical observation that a significant "standing loss" exists in domains where models are competent but verification is difficult.
