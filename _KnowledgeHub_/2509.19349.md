# ShinkaEvolve: Towards Open-Ended And Sample-Efficient Program Evolution

> [!summary] Summary
> ShinkaEvolve combines large language models with advanced evolutionary computation to create an open-source framework for efficient, open-ended program discovery. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks while dramatically reducing the number of computational evaluations required.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-driven program evolution methods are sample-inefficient, demanding thousands of evaluations and incurring substantial computational costs and execution times.
- Many leading LLM-based code evolution systems remain closed-source, which hinders reproducibility, limits community adoption, and restricts scientific collaboration and extensions.
- Prior evolutionary methods lack any mechanism to filter semantically redundant program proposals before costly evaluation, wasting compute on near-duplicate solutions.

## Method

- An ==adaptive parent and LLM sampling strategy== balances exploration and exploitation, leveraging an archive of evaluated programs and an island model for diversity.
- ==Code Novelty Rejection Sampling== uses embeddings and an LLM-as-judge to prevent redundant evaluations of semantically similar program proposals, improving efficiency.
- A ==bandit-based adaptive LLM ensemble== dynamically prioritizes models based on their relative improvement contributions, complemented by a ==meta-scratchpad== for online refinement and knowledge diffusion.

## Results

- Achieved a new state-of-the-art solution for the 26-circle packing problem using only **150** program evaluations, an improvement of orders of magnitude over prior methods.
- Evolved agent scaffolds for AIME math reasoning that outperformed hand-designed baselines, and improved **10** ALE-Bench C++ solutions by **2.3%** on average, with one ranking second on AtCoder.
- Discovered a novel load balancing loss function for Mixture-of-Experts LLMs that yielded lower perplexity and improved downstream task performance on a **2.7B**-parameter model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically balancing exploration and exploitation through weighted sampling and adaptive LLM prioritization is crucial for achieving high sample efficiency in evolutionary search.
> - The iterative composition of "stepping stones"—suboptimal intermediate solutions—is a vital mechanism for reaching complex, high-quality solutions in program evolution.
> - Integrating robust novelty assessment, such as code embedding-based rejection, dramatically improves efficiency by filtering out redundant program explorations before evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is strategically positioned at the forefront of modern artificial intelligence, integrating large language models (LLMs) with advanced evolutionary computation techniques to accelerate automated scientific discovery. The paper highlights that recent breakthroughs in LLMs have paved the way for "agentic systems" capable of autonomously conducting experiments and testing hypotheses. Within these systems, LLMs are increasingly deployed as sophisticated "mutation operators" that guide the iterative refinement of candidate solutions, typically in the form of code or algorithmic designs. This paradigm has shown significant promise across diverse domains, including competitive programming, mathematical optimization, and the automated design of AI agents.

ShinkaEvolve critically assesses the existing landscape, identifying two pervasive limitations that hinder the full potential of current LLM-driven program evolution methods:
1. **Sample Inefficiency:** Prevailing approaches, such as AlphaEvolve, OpenEvolve, and LLM4AD (which are explicitly referenced as closely related works), are characterized by their "sample inefficiency." They often necessitate thousands of program evaluations to converge on high-quality solutions, incurring substantial computational costs and prolonged execution times. This inefficiency largely stems from suboptimal exploration strategies that fail to effectively leverage the accumulated knowledge throughout the evolutionary process.
2. **Closed-Source Implementations:** A significant portion of leading LLM-based code evolution systems remain proprietary. This "closed-source" nature creates barriers to reproducibility, impedes widespread adoption, and restricts community-driven improvements and extensions, thereby limiting collective scientific advancement.

ShinkaEvolve directly addresses these limitations, positioning itself as a transformative open-source framework designed to achieve "state-of-the-art performance with unprecedented efficiency." By explicitly tackling sample inefficiency through novel algorithmic innovations and promoting open access, ShinkaEvolve aims to make LLM-guided program evolution more practical, powerful, and universally accessible. The work also contributes to the broader field of "Open-Ended Agentic Discovery," where LLMs, through their learned representations, facilitate continuous innovation beyond traditional explicit diversity metrics. ShinkaEvolve's emphasis on systematically combining "stepping stones"—suboptimal intermediate solutions that serve as crucial building blocks for breakthrough innovations—further solidifies its contribution to developing more intelligent and autonomous discovery agents, pushing the boundaries of what AI can achieve in generating novel solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind the development of ShinkaEvolve is rooted in a critical appraisal of the current state of LLM-driven program evolution. While these methods have demonstrated immense potential for automating scientific discovery, their practical utility and widespread adoption are significantly hampered by two core deficiencies:

1. **Computational Burden Due to Sample Inefficiency:** A primary challenge is the "sample inefficiency" of existing approaches. They typically demand thousands of program evaluations to identify effective solutions, making them computationally expensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive. This inefficiency is often attributed to naive exploration strategies that fail to adequately leverage the knowledge garnered from previous generations of solutions, leading to redundant computations and slower convergence.
2. **Barriers to Adoption and Progress Due to Closed-Source Nature:** Many of the most advanced and successful LLM-based code evolution systems are proprietary and remain "closed-source." This lack of transparency directly impedes reproducibility, limits community engagement, and hinders extensions or modifications by external researchers. Consequently, it creates a significant barrier to entry, stifling broader scientific collaboration and progress in this rapidly evolving field.

In response to these critical limitations, ShinkaEvolve was conceived with several key objectives:

- **To Introduce an Open-Source Framework:** A fundamental objective is to provide a fully transparent and accessible platform for LLM-driven program evolution. By releasing ShinkaEvolve as open-source software under the Apache 2.0 license, the project aims to dismantle the barriers imposed by proprietary systems, fostering reproducibility, community contributions, and broader scientific collaboration.
- **To Achieve Unprecedented Sample Efficiency:** Central to ShinkaEvolve's mission is to dramatically reduce the number of evaluations required to discover high-quality solutions. This is pursued through the integration of novel algorithmic innovations specifically designed to optimize the search process, minimize redundant efforts, and accelerate convergence.
- **To Deliver State-of-the-Art Performance:** Beyond mere efficiency, ShinkaEvolve aims to demonstrate its capability to discover solutions that not only rival but also surpass the performance of existing methods across a diverse array of complex computational problems, thereby validating its efficacy and superiority.
- **To Advance Generalized Scientific Discovery:** By synergistically combining the powerful generative capabilities of LLMs with advanced evolutionary search principles, the framework seeks to push the frontiers of automated discovery, enabling the exploration of novel algorithms and solutions across various scientific and engineering domains.
- **To Democratize Open-Ended Discovery:** By making the framework both cost-efficient and openly available, ShinkaEvolve aspires to lower the technical and economic barriers for a global community of researchers and practitioners, thereby enabling broader participation in advanced evolutionary optimization and open-ended innovation.

In essence, ShinkaEvolve is motivated by the desire to transform LLM-powered program evolution into a more practical, potent, and universally accessible tool, thereby accelerating the pace of scientific and algorithmic innovation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ShinkaEvolve's methodology integrates sophisticated evolutionary algorithms with the generative power of Large Language Models (LLMs) through three core algorithmic innovations aimed at achieving superior sample efficiency and solution quality. The framework's operational flow is structured into three main interactive phases: parent and inspiration sampling, program mutation and novelty assessment, and execution with world feedback.

#### 3.1. Parent and Inspiration Sampling

The foundation of ShinkaEvolve's search strategy is an **archive of evaluated programs**, which stores fitness scores and metadata. To foster diversity and prevent premature convergence, the system employs an **island model approach**, where independent subpopulations (islands) evolve in parallel. While occasional "migration" of programs between islands diffuses knowledge, the highest-performing program on each island is safeguarded to preserve unique evolutionary trajectories.

A crucial innovation lies in its **adaptive parent and LLM sampling strategy**, which judiciously balances exploration of novel search regions with exploitation of known high-quality areas:
- **Mutation Context Construction:** Each mutation operation is informed by a "mutation context." This context comprises a primary parent program and several "inspiration programs" selected from both top-performing solutions and random samples within the archive. This diverse set of exemplars guides the LLM in generating creative and relevant mutations.
- **Parent Program Selection:** Two main strategies are used for selecting the primary parent from an island's subpopulation:
 - **Power Law Sampling:** Programs are ranked by their fitness, and the probability of selection follows a power law distribution ($p_i \propto r_i^{-\alpha}$), where the parameter $\alpha$ controls the intensity of exploitation.
 - **Weighted Sampling:** This more advanced technique combines a performance metric (sigmoid-scaled fitness relative to the median) with a novelty component that prioritizes programs with fewer offspring. This balanced approach encourages both the refinement of successful solutions and the exploration of less-traveled, potentially innovative paths.

#### 3.2. Program Mutation and Novelty Assessment

To generate new program proposals, ShinkaEvolve first selects a specific LLM and associated sampling parameters (e.g., temperature) from a pre-defined pool that supports leading models like GPT, Gemini, Claude, and DeepSeek. The framework supports three distinct mutation approaches to ensure broad generative diversity:
- **Diff-Based Edits:** LLMs are prompted to make targeted modifications within `SEARCH/REPLACE` blocks, a method similar to that seen in AlphaEvolve.
- **Full Rewrites:** This allows for more extensive program transformations, with integrated mechanisms to ensure that designated "immutable" code blocks remain unchanged during the rewrite process.
- **Crossover Mutation:** Leveraging LLMs, this approach combines code snippets from a primary parent program with an additional program drawn from the archive, mimicking genetic crossover to produce novel hybrids.
Robustness is ensured by using `EVOLVE-BLOCK-START/END` markers to delineate immutable code sections, and invalid patches are automatically resampled with parsing feedback, inspired by Reflexion.

A critical innovation for efficiency is **Code Novelty Rejection Sampling**:
- Mutable sections of proposed code are transformed into embeddings using a foundation model (e.g., `text-embedding-3-small`).
- The cosine similarity of these new embeddings is then computed against all existing programs in the relevant island subpopulation.
- If the maximum similarity score exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., $\eta = 0.95$), an additional LLM is queried to act as a "novelty judge." This LLM performs a semantic assessment to determine if the proposed program is *meaningfully* distinct. Proposals deemed redundant are rejected, preventing the waste of computational resources on evaluating near-duplicates.

#### 3.3. Execution and World Feedback

Once a program proposal is generated and passes the novelty assessment, it is executed and subjected to a multi-objective assessment. This yields a scalar fitness value, a set of "public metrics," and textual feedback. All this information is meticulously stored in the population archive, providing rich contextual data for subsequent LLM mutations via a standardized prompting format.

Key feedback mechanisms in this phase include:
- **Adaptive LLM Sampling Evolution (Bandit-Based):** Recognizing that the performance of different LLMs can vary across tasks and evolutionary stages, ShinkaEvolve dynamically adjusts the sampling probabilities for each LLM using a modified UCB1 algorithm. Instead of absolute fitness, the update mechanism for an LLM's probability is based on the *relative improvement* achieved by its generated mutation (e.g., $r_{u_i} = \exp(\max(r_i - r_{b_i}, 0)) - 1$, where $r_{b_i}$ is a baseline reward). This approach strategically promotes LLMs capable of producing "bold, high-risk, high-reward" mutations, encouraging significant breakthroughs.
- **Meta-Scratchpad & Online Refinement:** To foster continuous learning and accelerate discovery, a "meta-agent" periodically (e.g., every $T$ generations) analyzes successful solutions in the archive. It identifies common optimization strategies, recurring design principles, and synthesizes these insights into actionable recommendations. These recommendations are then appended to the mutation prompts for forthcoming generations, providing high-level guidance derived from the collective evolutionary experience and enabling knowledge diffusion within the system.

In essence, ShinkaEvolve's methodology is a tightly integrated system that orchestrates intelligent sampling, rigorous novelty filtering, diverse LLM-guided mutations, and adaptive learning from execution feedback to achieve highly efficient, robust, and effective open-ended program evolution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ShinkaEvolve was rigorously evaluated across four distinct and challenging computational domains, consistently demonstrating significant advancements in both sample efficiency and the quality of discovered solutions.

#### 4.1. Circle Packing: Reproducing & Improving AlphaEvolve Results

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** ShinkaEvolve achieved a new state-of-the-art solution for the classic 26-circle packing problem, maximizing the sum of radii within a unit square. Remarkably, this was accomplished using only 150 program evaluations, which represents orders of magnitude fewer samples than previously reported methods like AlphaEvolve.
- **Discovery Dynamics:** The evolutionary process unfolded in three phases: an initial rapid improvement, followed by sustained exploration yielding incremental gains, and finally, a convergence phase for solution refinement. The program evolution tree highlighted the iterative composition of "stepping stones"—suboptimal solutions that served as crucial intermediate building blocks—leading to the optimal solution path.
- **Discovered Solution:** The evolved algorithm integrated three key innovations: a sophisticated initialization strategy placing circles in a golden-angle spiral pattern with strategic corner/edge positioning; a hybrid optimization approach combining gradient-based SLSQP refinement with global exploration via simulated annealing; and intelligent perturbation mechanisms alternating between local circle movements and global ring rotations, complemented by adaptive temperature scheduling with reheating to prevent premature convergence.

#### 4.2. AIME: Evolving Agent Scaffolds for Math Reasoning

- **Superior Agent Design:** ShinkaEvolve successfully evolved agent scaffold designs for AIME 2024 mathematical reasoning problems (30 competition-level questions). These evolved agents significantly outperformed hand-designed baselines, including sophisticated majority-voting approaches.
- **Efficiency-Performance Pareto Frontier:** The system discovered an optimal trade-off point, demonstrating that maximum performance could be achieved with just 7 LLM queries per problem, well within the 10-query computational constraint.
- **Generalization and Robustness:** The discovered scaffolds exhibited strong generalization capabilities to unseen AIME 2025 problems, with greater performance gains observed compared to 2023 problems (suggesting less susceptibility to potential training data contamination). Furthermore, the architectures proved robust across various underlying LLMs (gpt-4.1-mini, gpt-4.1, o4-mini), indicating the evolution of generalizable reasoning strategies rather than model-specific optimizations.
- **Discovered Solution:** The optimal agent features a three-stage architecture: (1) multiple expert personas (meticulous, intuitive, algorithmic) generate diverse solutions at `temperature=0.7`; (2) critical peer review by a skeptical reviewer (at `temperature=0.1`) validates reasoning, identifies flaws, and provides corrections; and (3) a final synthesis stage where an "editor-in-chief" (at `temperature=0.0`) analyzes all inputs to construct a canonical solution, supported by robust fallback mechanisms.

#### 4.3. ALE-Bench: Evolving Programs for Combinatorial Optimization

- **Algorithmic Improvements:** ShinkaEvolve successfully improved high-performing C++ solutions for 10 competitive programming tasks from the ALE-Bench LITE benchmark (initially generated by ALE-Agent) by approximately 2.3% on average.
- **Competitive Standing:** For one specific task, `ahc039`, the ShinkaEvolve-enhanced solution would have ranked second on the AtCoder leaderboard, demonstrating a substantial improvement from the initial fifth-place standing.
- **Nature of Improvements:** The enhancements were typically detailed implementation-level changes. For `ahc039`, improvements included caching validation processes and introducing a "targeted edge move" neighborhood operator for simulated annealing. For `ahc025`, it involved refining fallback weight estimation and replacing simulated annealing with a more focused combination of greedy moves and targeted local search.
- **Observation:** While impactful, the evolved solutions often remained algorithmically close to the initial ALE-Agent solutions, hinting at a potential for overfitting to the initialization or indicating that more profound algorithmic shifts might require further research into mutation strategies.

#### 4.4. LLM Training: Evolving Losses for Balanced and Effective Experts

- **Novel Loss Function Discovery:** ShinkaEvolve successfully discovered a novel load balancing loss (LBL) function for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Large Language Model architectures. This new LBL significantly outperformed the widely adopted "global-batch LBL," which was used as a starting point for the evolutionary search.
- **Impact on Large-Scale MoE Training:** When applied to train a much larger 2.7B-parameter MoE model on approximately 30B Fineweb tokens, ShinkaEvolve's LBL consistently yielded lower perplexity and improved downstream task performance across seven standard benchmarks, particularly when higher regularization coefficients were used.
- **Discovered Solution:** The evolved LBL extends the global-batch LBL with an additional regularization term designed to target and assist individual under-specialized experts. This term utilizes a normalized complement to routing entropy and a minimum usage threshold ($\tau$) to softly penalize experts that are allocated a fraction of tokens below $\tau$. This acts as an adaptive "safety net," becoming stronger when routing entropy is low and focusing on fewer dominating experts, effectively addressing a potential blind spot of the global-batch LBL.

#### 4.5. Ablations & Analysis

Ablation studies conducted on the Circle Packing task provided empirical validation for the efficacy of ShinkaEvolve's individual components:
- **Parent Selection Strategies:** Weighted sampling, which balances performance and novelty, consistently outperformed both random search (pure exploration) and hill climbing (pure exploitation).
- **LLM Ensembling and Prioritization:** The bandit-based adaptive LLM ensemble strategy, which dynamically prioritizes LLMs based on their contribution to fitness improvements, significantly outperformed both a single LLM baseline and a fixed uniform ensemble.
- **Code Embedding-Based Rejection Sampling:** This mechanism, which uses text embeddings and a similarity threshold (e.g., $\eta = 0.95$) to reject redundant program proposals, provided substantial performance gains. While an additional LLM-as-a-novelty-judge offered marginal further improvements, it indicated that embedding similarity is already a highly effective proxy for novelty.

These comprehensive results collectively underscore ShinkaEvolve's robust capacity for efficient, high-quality, and generalizable algorithmic discovery across a broad spectrum of complex computational problems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ShinkaEvolve marks a pivotal development in the landscape of LLM-driven scientific and algorithmic discovery. Its primary significance stems from effectively tackling two critical impediments prevalent in existing methods: profound sample inefficiency and the pervasive closed-source nature of leading systems.

The **significance** of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Transformative Sample Efficiency:** By demonstrating unprecedented sample efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art results with merely 150 evaluations for complex tasks like circle packing (an improvement of orders of magnitude), ShinkaEvolve dramatically lowers the computational cost, time, and resource demands associated with automated discovery. This makes sophisticated evolutionary search accessible and practical for a much broader array of problems and research groups.
- **Robust Algorithmic Innovation:** The strength of ShinkaEvolve lies in its synergistic integration of several sophisticated algorithmic innovations: a weighted parent sampling strategy that intelligently balances exploration and exploitation, code novelty rejection-sampling to prune redundant search paths, an adaptive bandit-based LLM ensemble selection for dynamic model prioritization, and an online meta-scratchpad for knowledge diffusion. The rigorous ablation studies confirm the substantial individual and collective contributions of these components to the framework's efficiency and solution quality.
- **Demonstrated Broad Applicability and Superior Performance:** The successful application across a diverse range of challenging domains—from mathematical optimization (circle packing) and agentic AI design (AIME reasoning scaffolds) to competitive programming (ALE-Bench) and fundamental machine learning architecture (Mixture-of-Experts load balancing losses)—underscores ShinkaEvolve's versatility and efficacy as a generalized discovery engine. Its capacity to uncover human-competitive or even superior solutions highlights its potent problem-solving capabilities.
- **Crucial Open-Source Contribution:** The decision to release ShinkaEvolve as an open-source framework under the Apache 2.0 license is a critical contribution to the scientific community. It directly addresses the transparency and accessibility issues of proprietary systems, fostering reproducibility, encouraging community-driven enhancements, and lowering the barrier to entry for researchers globally.

The **potential impact** of ShinkaEvolve is profound and far-reaching:
- **Democratization of Advanced AI Research:** By making powerful evolutionary optimization tools both accessible and economically feasible, ShinkaEvolve empowers a wider global community of researchers, educators, and developers—including those in resource-constrained environments—to engage in cutting-edge AI innovation, thereby accelerating the collective pace of scientific and algorithmic discovery.
- **Accelerated Scientific and Algorithmic Progress:** The ability to rapidly and efficiently explore vast solution spaces for novel algorithms, agent designs, and foundational machine learning components promises to accelerate scientific and technological advancements across numerous disciplines. This could lead to breakthroughs in areas currently constrained by the limits of human intuition or prohibitive computational bottlenecks.
- **Advancing Autonomous AI Systems:** ShinkaEvolve contributes significantly to the long-term vision of "true open-endedness" in AI, where intelligent systems can autonomously generate their own objectives, continuously refine their strategies (self-referential refinement), and engage in perpetual online meta-learning. This moves closer to the grand challenge of developing AI capable of independent scientific discovery.
- **Enhancing Cost-Efficiency in AI Development:** The discovery of more effective Mixture-of-Experts load balancing losses, for instance, has direct implications for the efficiency and cost-effectiveness of training massive LLMs. Even minor gains in efficiency can translate into substantial economic benefits and resource savings in large-scale AI development.

**Broader Impact & Ethical Considerations:**
While the overall impact of ShinkaEvolve is overwhelmingly positive, particularly in its contributions to democratization and efficiency, the authors prudently acknowledge a potential ethical consideration. The framework's reliance on large-scale LLM usage, despite its own internal efficiencies, still incurs significant API costs. These costs could inadvertently create new economic barriers for researchers without substantial funding, potentially undermining the broader goal of complete democratization. Addressing this might involve future research into more efficient, self-hosted LLMs or exploring federated learning paradigms. Nevertheless, ShinkaEvolve represents a pivotal step towards establishing AI as a more effective, accessible, and indispensable partner in humanity's scientific endeavors.
