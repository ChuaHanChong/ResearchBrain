# TOSC: Task-Oriented Shape Completion for Open-World Dexterous Grasp Generation from Partial Point Clouds

> [!summary] Summary
> TOSC (Task-Oriented Shape Completion) enables robots to generate stable and semantically correct dexterous grasps for objects from severely partial point clouds by focusing completion efforts on task-relevant contact regions. This method improves grasp displacement by 16.17% and reduces geometric reconstruction error (CD-ℓ2\ell_2ℓ2​) by 55.26% compared to prior state-of-the-art techniques.

## Problem

- Existing state-of-the-art task-oriented dexterous grasping methods often assume complete object geometry, failing when objects are severely partially observed due to occlusion or sensor limitations.
- Decoupled approaches, where generic 3D shape completion is followed by grasp generation, frequently hallucinate incorrect geometry in critical manipulation regions, leading to unstable or task-incompatible grasps.
- Robots struggle with robustly manipulating open-world objects and performing specific tasks from incomplete perceptual data in unstructured environments.

## Method

- Task-Oriented Shape Completion (TOSC): Instead of full object completion, the method focuses on reconstructing only the "potential contact regions" crucial for a specified manipulation task, guided by language instructions.
- TOSC Candidate Generation: Leverages pre-trained vision-language models and a 3D generative model to synthesize diverse, plausible task-oriented shape completion candidates from partial point clouds.
- TOSC Selection and Restoration: Employs a 3D Discriminative Autoencoder (DAE) trained on plausible/implausible shapes to select the most suitable candidate and refine its geometry for task compatibility.
- FlowGrasp: Utilizes a constraint-aware conditional flow-matching model to efficiently generate dexterous grasps, incorporating geometric and semantic constraints via a single-step gradient correction.

## Results

- TOSC achieved state-of-the-art performance in task-oriented dexterous grasping, improving Grasp Displacement by 16.17% and achieving an LLM score of 98.30% over previous baselines.
- The task-oriented shape completion component significantly outperformed generic point cloud completion methods, reducing Chamfer Distance (CD-ℓ2\ell_2ℓ2​) by 55.26% and achieving the highest F1-score (0.860).
- The system demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization across 9 novel object categories and various novel language instructions, maintaining high performance metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - For robotic manipulation from partial observations, completing only task-relevant regions is more effective and robust than generic full-object shape completion, as it avoids ambiguous hallucinations.
> - Pre-trained foundation models (vision-language, 3D generative) can be effectively integrated into a pipeline to achieve zero-shot generalization for both open-world objects and novel manipulation tasks.
> - Implicitly enforcing constraints through gradient correction in flow-matching models offers an efficient way to generate task-oriented grasps without complex multi-loss optimization.
