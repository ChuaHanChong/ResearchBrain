# TOSC: Task-Oriented Shape Completion for Open-World Dexterous Grasp Generation from Partial Point Clouds

> [!summary] Summary
> TOSC (Task-Oriented Shape Completion) enables robots to generate stable and semantically correct dexterous grasps for objects from severely partial point clouds by focusing completion efforts on task-relevant contact regions. This method improves grasp displacement by 16.17% and reduces geometric reconstruction error (CD-ℓ2) by 55.26% compared to prior state-of-the-art techniques.

## Problem

- Existing state-of-the-art task-oriented dexterous grasping methods often assume complete object geometry, failing when objects are severely partially observed due to occlusion or sensor limitations.
- Decoupled approaches, where generic 3D shape completion is followed by grasp generation, frequently hallucinate incorrect geometry in critical manipulation regions, leading to unstable or task-incompatible grasps.
- Robots struggle with robustly manipulating open-world objects and performing specific tasks from incomplete perceptual data in unstructured environments.

## Method

- Task-Oriented Shape Completion (TOSC): Instead of full object completion, the method focuses on reconstructing only the "potential contact regions" crucial for a specified manipulation task, guided by language instructions.
- TOSC Candidate Generation: Leverages pre-trained vision-language models and a 3D generative model to synthesize diverse, plausible task-oriented shape completion candidates from partial point clouds.
- TOSC Selection and Restoration: Employs a 3D Discriminative Autoencoder (DAE) trained on plausible/implausible shapes to select the most suitable candidate and refine its geometry for task compatibility.
- FlowGrasp: Utilizes a constraint-aware conditional flow-matching model to efficiently generate dexterous grasps, incorporating geometric and semantic constraints via a single-step gradient correction.

## Results

- TOSC achieved state-of-the-art performance in task-oriented dexterous grasping, improving Grasp Displacement by 16.17% and achieving an LLM score of 98.30% over previous baselines.
- The task-oriented shape completion component significantly outperformed generic point cloud completion methods, reducing Chamfer Distance (CD-ℓ2) by 55.26% and achieving the highest F1-score (0.860).
- The system demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization across 9 novel object categories and various novel language instructions, maintaining high performance metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - For robotic manipulation from partial observations, completing only task-relevant regions is more effective and robust than generic full-object shape completion, as it avoids ambiguous hallucinations.
> - Pre-trained foundation models (vision-language, 3D generative) can be effectively integrated into a pipeline to achieve zero-shot generalization for both open-world objects and novel manipulation tasks.
> - Implicitly enforcing constraints through gradient correction in flow-matching models offers an efficient way to generate task-oriented grasps without complex multi-loss optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of stable and task-oriented dexterous grasps for robots has long been a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation. Recent advancements, particularly with the advent of large foundation models, have pushed the boundaries, enabling "zero-shot" task-oriented dexterous grasping for a wide array of objects (e.g., DexTOG, DexGraspVLA). However, a significant bottleneck remains: these methods often assume complete object geometry. In real-world environments, robots frequently encounter objects under severe partial observation due to occlusion, clutter, and sensor limitations. This critical gap severely limits the practical applicability of current state-of-the-art grasping systems.

Traditional approaches to address partial observation typically involve a two-step process: first, perform generic 3D shape completion on the partial point cloud, and then, generate grasps based on the reconstructed full shape. However, as the authors highlight, this decoupled approach suffers from inherent ambiguities arising from missing data. Generic shape completion, by attempting to reconstruct the entire object, may hallucinate incorrect geometry, especially in regions critical for manipulation. Consequently, grasps generated from these flawed completions often fail.

This paper fundamentally shifts the paradigm by introducing **Task-Oriented Shape Completion (TOSC)**. Instead of completing the entire object, TOSC focuses exclusively on reconstructing the "potential contact regions" crucial for the specified downstream manipulation task. This approach is a direct response to the limitations of generic shape completion in the context of grasping. By explicitly guiding the completion process with task awareness, the method aims to produce more functionally relevant and accurate geometric reconstructions, even when other parts of the object remain ambiguous or incomplete. This makes the work a significant contribution to both point cloud completion and task-oriented grasping, bridging the gap between perception and action in a more intelligent and efficient manner. It leverages the power of pre-trained vision-language models and 3D generative models, common components in modern AI research, but integrates them into a novel, task-specific pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust and effective **task-oriented dexterous grasping for open-world objects under severe partial observation**. The authors identify the core problem as the failure of existing methods when confronted with significant missing data, which invalidates generic shape completion and subsequent grasp generation.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing the "Partial Observation" Challenge:** Real-world robotic manipulation inherently involves partial views of objects. Current state-of-the-art task-oriented grasping methods, while powerful, often assume complete object geometry, leading to performance degradation in practical settings.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Decoupled Approaches:** Simply completing the full object shape and then generating grasps is suboptimal. Generic shape completion can introduce errors or "hallucinations" in critical contact regions due to data ambiguity, leading to unstable or task-incompatible grasps.
- **Introducing "Task-Oriented Shape Completion":** The central motivation is to define and solve a new problem where shape completion is explicitly guided by the manipulation task. This new task aims to prioritize the reconstruction of only the task-relevant contact regions, making the completion process more focused, accurate, and robust for the downstream grasping task.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models for Open-World Capabilities:** To handle diverse and novel objects (open-world scenarios) and tasks, the authors seek to harness the zero-shot capabilities of large pre-trained foundation models for object functional understanding and 3D generation.
- **Developing a Plausibility-Aware and Constraint-Aware System:** The method aims not only to generate completion candidates but also to evaluate their plausibility and optimize them, ensuring that the final completed shape supports a physically and semantically correct grasp. Furthermore, the grasp generation itself should efficiently incorporate task and physical constraints.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for more practical and intelligent robots that can effectively manipulate objects in unstructured environments, by providing them with a task-aware perceptual capability that focuses computational effort where it matters most for the given manipulation goal.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, TOSC, consists of three main stages: Task-Oriented Shape Completion (TOSC) Candidate Generation, TOSC Selection and Restoration, and FlowGrasp for task-oriented dexterous grasp generation.

#### 3.1. TOSC Candidate Generation

This stage leverages pre-trained foundation models to synthesize multiple plausible task-oriented shape completion candidates. Given a partial point cloud, object category, and language instruction:
1. **Depth Map Rendering:** The input partial point cloud ($P_{in}$) is rendered into a depth image ($I_{depth}$). A viewpoint estimation strategy (Hidden-Point-Removal) maximizes visible points for rendering.
2. **RGB Image Synthesis:** The depth image ($I_{depth}$) is fed into ControlNet (Zhang et al. 2023) as a condition to synthesize multiple RGB images. The object's category serves as a prompt. Critically, different "control scales" ($\lambda$) are used in ControlNet to balance strict geometric adherence with semantic completion, thereby generating diverse, plausible candidates to account for ambiguity.
3. **3D Shape Generation:** For each synthesized RGB image, a pre-trained 3D shape generation network (Zhao et al. 2025), specifically Hunyuan3D-DiT-v2-mini-Fast, estimates a 3D mesh, from which a point set is sampled.
4. **Task-Relevant Region Detection and Fusion:** To focus on task-relevant regions, the method uses SAM (Kirillov et al. 2023) to segment RGB images and a multi-modal large model (GPT-4o, Achiam et al. 2023) to detect task-relevant regions in image space. These regions are projected onto both the input and generated point clouds. An Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm, modified with a Chamfer Distance (CD) loss weighted towards task-relevant regions, aligns and fuses the generated shapes with the input partial point cloud. This produces a set of "TOSC candidates" ($P_{can}$), each representing a possible task-oriented completion.

#### 3.2. TOSC Selection and Restoration

This stage addresses the potential imperfections (hallucinations, incorrect estimations) in the generated candidates by proposing a 3D Discriminative Autoencoder (DAE).
1. **Training Data Generation:** To train the DAE, a dataset is curated with both "plausible" and "implausible" shapes relative to manipulation tasks. Plausible data comes from combining objects from six datasets (e.g., ModelNet40, ShapeNetCore, DexGraspNet). Implausible data is generated by deliberately sabotaging plausible shapes: identifying task-relevant segments (using a language model and Part-Slip), then randomly removing these segments, adding noise, and perturbing local patches.
2. **Network Architecture:** The DAE comprises an encoder ($\varepsilon$) and a decoder ($\zeta$).
 - **Encoder ($\varepsilon$):** Tokenizes the input point cloud into local patches (using FPS and KNN) and processes them with Transformer blocks. During training, it randomly masks patch tokens, but importantly, *not* those in task-relevant segments for plausible data. The encoder outputs a latent vector and estimates the mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$) of a Gaussian distribution for plausibility estimation.
 - **Decoder ($\zeta$):** Also uses Transformer blocks to aggregate features from tokens and reconstructs the point cloud.
3. **Training Scheme:** The DAE is trained with a multi-component loss:
 - **KL Divergence Loss ($L_{KL}$):** Optimizes plausible shapes' latent distributions to approximate a standard normal distribution ($N(0,1)$) and implausible shapes' distributions to approximate a different distribution ($N(1,1)$). This enables discriminative plausibility assessment.
 - **Chamfer Distance Loss ($L_{restore}$):** Reconstructs the original plausible point cloud.
 - **Masking Loss ($L_{mask}$):** Penalizes differences in patch token features when some are masked versus unmasked, enhancing robustness to occlusion (used only for plausible data).
4. **Network Inference:** During inference, the encoder computes a latent vector and a Gaussian distribution for each candidate. Plausibility ($s_{can}$) is estimated using the KL divergence between the candidate's distribution and the learned distributions for plausible/implausible shapes. The candidate with the highest plausibility score is selected, and its geometry is further restored by the decoder.

#### 3.3. FlowGrasp

This is a conditional flow-matching model designed to generate task-oriented dexterous grasps from the optimized 3D shape, incorporating constraints efficiently.
1. **Condition Vector:** A condition vector ($l_{con}$) is formed by concatenating PointNet++ features of the restored 3D shape with CLIP embeddings of the language task description.
2. **Constraint-Aware Flow Matching:** Unlike standard flow-matching, FlowGrasp applies a "single-step, input-side gradient correction" to the instantaneous velocity ($u_t$) during training. This correction, formulated as $u_t^*(x_t) = u_t(x_t) - \alpha(t)\nabla(\sum_i w_i^{con} g_i(x_t))$, implicitly enforces geometric and semantic constraints ($g_i$) without explicit additional losses or inference-time overhead.
3. **Training:** The network $\theta$ is trained to regress this corrected velocity $u_t^*(x_t)$ by minimizing an MSE loss.
4. **Inference:** An initial noise vector is integrated through the learned Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) from $t=0$ to $t=1$ to recover the final task-oriented dexterous grasp.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments to evaluate TOSC's performance in task-oriented dexterous grasping and task-oriented shape completion, primarily on a newly created dataset called OakInk-PartialIPC (derived from OakInk) and CapGrasp for language instructions.

#### 4.1. Task-Oriented Dexterous Grasping Performance

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Achievement:** TOSC significantly outperforms existing baselines (GraspCVAE, GraspTTA, SceneDiffuser, DexTOG, DexGYSGrasp) in key metrics.
- **Grasp Displacement:** TOSC improves Grasp Displacement by 16.17% over the state-of-the-art, indicating superior grasp stability under external forces.
- **Perceptual Scores and LLM Score:** Achieves the highest LLM score (98.30%) and perceptual scores for Semantic Consistency (SC), Physical Plausibility (PP), and Interaction Stability (IS). This demonstrates its ability to generate high-quality, semantically correct grasps that fulfill language instructions and are perceived as excellent by human evaluators.
- **Penetration:** Maintains competitive penetration volume and depth compared to baselines, balancing penetration avoidance with grasp stability, contrasting with more conservative (and less stable) approaches like SceneDiffuser.
- **Contact Ratio:** Achieves a contact ratio of 3.11, demonstrating effective hand-object interaction.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 6):** Visual comparisons show that TOSC generates more plausible and stable grasps, especially for objects under partial observation, where baselines often produce unnatural or failing grasps.

#### 4.2. Task-Oriented Shape Completion Performance

- **SOTA Achievement:** TOSC significantly outperforms generic point cloud completion baselines (PointAttn, SVDFormer, SymmCompletion) in all metrics.
- **Chamfer Distance (CD):** Improves CD-$\ell_2$ by 55.26% over the SOTA, achieving 1.66 x 10$^{-4}$, which is a substantial reduction in geometric reconstruction error.
- **F1-score@1 and Density-aware CD (DCD):** Achieves the highest F1-score (0.860) and lowest DCD (0.488), further confirming its superior completion accuracy, especially in task-relevant regions.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 7):** Visual comparisons clearly show that TOSC produces more accurate and task-relevant shape completions from partial inputs compared to generic methods, which often lead to incomplete or erroneous reconstructions in critical areas.

#### 4.3. Zero-shot Generality

- Experiments on 9 novel object categories and several novel language instructions demonstrated TOSC's robust performance across all metrics (Table 4). This highlights its strong generalization capabilities to unseen objects and untrained tasks, attributed to the effective integration of pre-trained foundation models.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

- **TOSC Candidate Generation (TCG):** Removing TCG significantly degraded performance, confirming its crucial role in providing diverse and plausible initial shape candidates.
- **TOSC Selection and Restoration (TSR):** Directly selecting candidates without the DAE's selection and restoration process led to inferior performance, validating the importance of plausibility evaluation and global geometry optimization.
- **Task-Oriented Shape Completion (TOSC):** Replacing task-oriented completion with generic shape completion resulted in a performance drop, underscoring the benefits of task-aware completion.
- **Token Masking:** Removing the token masking scheme from the 3D DAE reduced robustness, confirming its effectiveness in handling occlusions.
- **Gradient Guidance:** Replacing the novel gradient guidance in FlowGrasp with a direct loss-based optimization led to underperformance, demonstrating the superiority and efficiency of the proposed constraint-aware flow-matching approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "TOSC: Task-Oriented Shape Completion" paper introduces a highly significant contribution to the fields of robotics, computer vision, and artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of dexterous manipulation. Its primary impact lies in redefining the problem of shape completion for robotic grasping, moving away from generic reconstruction towards a **task-aware and functionally relevant approach**.

The potential impacts are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Robust Robotic Manipulation in Real-World Environments:** By effectively handling severe partial observations, TOSC significantly enhances the capability of robots to operate in unstructured and cluttered environments, such as homes, warehouses, and industrial settings. This is crucial for applications like household service robots, assistive robotics, and advanced industrial automation, where objects are rarely perfectly presented or fully visible.
- **Advancing Task-Oriented Grasping:** The method directly addresses a major bottleneck in task-oriented grasping, allowing robots to not only generate stable grasps but also to perform them in a way that facilitates specific downstream tasks (e.g., "grip the mug to fill it with hot water without burning my hands"). This elevates the intelligence and utility of robotic systems beyond mere object retrieval.
- **Novelty in Shape Completion:** The introduction of "task-oriented shape completion" as a distinct problem offers a new direction for research in 3D perception. It suggests that for many robotic tasks, a full, perfect 3D reconstruction is not necessary, and focusing on functional regions can be more efficient and robust. This could lead to a new class of specialized 3D perception algorithms.
- **Effective Integration of Foundation Models:** The work demonstrates a powerful paradigm for integrating large pre-trained vision-language models (ControlNet, GPT-4o, CLIP) and 3D generative models (Hunyuan3D) to achieve zero-shot capabilities for novel objects and tasks. This shows a promising path for leveraging the vast knowledge embedded in these models for practical robotic applications, reducing the need for extensive task-specific data collection.
- **Methodological Innovations:** The development of the 3D Discriminative Autoencoder for plausibility evaluation and restoration, coupled with its robust training data generation scheme, provides a generalizable framework for handling ambiguous perceptual inputs. Furthermore, the constraint-aware conditional flow-matching model (FlowGrasp) offers an elegant and efficient solution for incorporating complex constraints into generative models without incurring high computational overhead, a valuable contribution to generative modeling for robotics.
- **Broadening Research Horizons:** This work opens new avenues for research at the intersection of perception, language understanding, and robot control. Future work could explore more complex manipulation tasks, dynamic partial observations, or even learning the optimal "task-relevant regions" directly from interaction.

In conclusion, TOSC represents a significant step forward in making robots more capable and intelligent in interacting with the physical world. By intelligently completing only what is necessary for a given task, it provides a more practical and performant solution for future robotic systems.
