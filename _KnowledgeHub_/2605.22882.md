# GEM-4D: Geometry-Enhanced Video World Models for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GEM-4D introduces a geometry-enhanced video world model for robot manipulation that improves the physical consistency of generated future videos by distilling features from 4D geometry foundation models into a Video DiT during training. This method leads to higher manipulation success rates, such as increasing from 58% to 75% on the Droid AUTOLab dataset, while maintaining efficient inference.

## Problem

- Current video world models for robot manipulation prioritize visual realism but often lack physical grounding, leading to geometric inconsistencies (e.g., rigid objects deforming, contacts drifting, implausible depth changes) in generated videos.
- These geometric inconsistencies render video rollouts unreliable for extracting precise actions required for robot control, as standard generation losses do not explicitly constrain critical factors like camera motion, scene depth, and object motion.
- Existing methods that incorporate geometric structure typically require large-scale geometric annotations, modify the video model's output space, or limit the expressive capacity of pre-trained backbones, increasing complexity and inference costs.

## Method

- **GEM-4D** employs a geometry-enhanced velocity alignment mechanism, distilling dense 4D correspondence supervision from a frozen 4D geometry foundation model (like PAGE-4D) into the intermediate features of a ==Video DiT== generative backbone during training.
- A novel joint training objective is used, where a parallel Geometry DiT, conditioned solely on the Video DiT's intermediate features, is trained with its own flow matching loss to predict the velocity field of a geometry latent.
- An Adaptive Inverse Dynamic System (AIDS) converts the geometrically consistent video rollouts into executable 6-DoF robot end-effector trajectories, incorporating 3D scene grounding, a dual-criterion confidence-gated tracker, and a geometry-kinematics pose fallback.

## Results

- GEM-4D achieved superior performance in 4D scene prediction, with the lowest Frechet Video Distance (FVD) and highest SSIM/PSNR for RGB appearance, lowest AbsRel and highest 
		delta_1/
		delta_2 for depth reconstruction, and lowest Chamfer Distance with highest 
		delta_{vis_{avg}} for 3D reconstruction and correspondence on both real-world and synthetic datasets.
- The model substantially improved robot manipulation task success rates, increasing from **58%** to **75%** on the Droid AUTOLab dataset, **65%** to **83%** on CLVR, and **59%** to **87%** on RAIL compared to the TesserAct baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the geometry flow branch, showing that GEM-4D, particularly with PAGE-4D features, generally outperformed variants with explicit depth supervision or different geometry models like VGGT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern 4D geometry foundation models implicitly encode the complete inter-frame correspondence structure (depth, camera pose, object motion) within their internal feature representations, making them effective "correspondence teachers."
> - Distilling these geometry-aware representations into a video generative backbone at the feature level, rather than modifying the output space or requiring explicit geometric annotations, can effectively enhance geometric consistency without increasing inference cost.
> - A robust, adaptive inverse dynamics system is crucial for translating geometrically consistent, yet still imperfect, generated video rollouts into reliable and executable robot control actions, especially in real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

General-purpose robotic manipulation policies aim to generalize across various scenes, objects, tasks, and robot embodiments. Current approaches often involve generalist vision-language-action policies, which directly map observations and instructions to actions. However, these methods frequently require extensive robot demonstration data and embodiment-specific training or fine-tuning.

Video world models offer an alternative paradigm by predicting future observations conditioned on tasks and current scenes, providing a visual representation of potential interactions. This approach could reduce the dependence on explicit action supervision. Despite their potential, existing video generation models, such as standard pixel- or latent-space diffusion models, primarily prioritize visual realism. While they can produce photorealistic videos, they often lack the physical grounding necessary for robot planning. Specifically, these models may fail to track the same physical points consistently across time, leading to geometric inconsistencies where rigid objects might deform non-rigidly, contacts drift, or depth varies implausibly between frames. These subtle visual errors can fundamentally undermine the ability to extract reliable actions. The limitation arises because standard generation losses do not explicitly constrain factors such as camera motion, scene depth, and object motion, which are critical for maintaining inter-frame correspondence.

Previous efforts to address this issue include methods that incorporate explicit geometric structure. Approaches like TesserACT and 3DFlowAction augment the video model's output to predict geometric quantities such as depth, surface normals, or 3D flow fields. While these methods introduce geometric constraints, they typically necessitate large-scale geometric annotations and modify the output space of the video model, potentially limiting the expressive capacity of pre-trained video backbones. Additionally, they may not provide a unified correspondence signal that comprehensively accounts for camera motion, scene depth, and object motion simultaneously.

This work positions itself within this landscape by recognizing that the geometric factors governing inter-frame correspondence are implicitly encoded within modern 4D geometry foundation models, such as PAGE-4D, Depth Anything V3, VGGT, and DUSt3R-family models. These models are designed to estimate dense geometry and camera motion from video. The authors propose to distill these geometry-aware representations into a video generative backbone during training, rather than adding explicit geometric predictions to the output or requiring additional explicit geometric supervision. This method aims to achieve geometric consistency without altering the video model's output space or incurring additional inference costs.

The work relates to existing video world models for embodied control (e.g., UniPi, Uni-Sim, Genie) by sharing the goal of enabling embodied decision-making through future prediction, but it diverges by explicitly focusing on geometric consistency. It differentiates from geometry-incorporating models (e.g., TesserACT, 3DFlowAction, RoboTransfer) by using feature-level distillation instead of output-space modification, thereby avoiding the need for extensive geometric annotations during the generative model's training and preserving a single-stream architecture during inference. The paper also leverages advancements in feed-forward 3D and 4D geometry models (e.g., DUSt3R, PAGE-4D), not as standalone perception modules, but as "correspondence teachers" whose learned internal representations provide a supervisory signal for the video world model. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first work to utilize 4D geometry foundation models as training-time regularizers for video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind GEM-4D is to address a fundamental limitation of current video world models when applied to robotic manipulation: their inability to consistently track physical points across time. While these models can generate visually plausible future videos from instructions, the lack of geometric consistency means that the generated videos do not accurately represent physically realizable motion. This deficiency renders them unreliable for deriving executable robot actions, which necessitate precise recovery of object and end-effector motions.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Enhance Geometric Consistency:** To develop a video world model capable of generating future frames that maintain reliable inter-frame correspondences, ensuring that pixels corresponding to the same 3D surface point follow physically consistent trajectories over time. This is crucial for accurate action extraction.
2. **Leverage Geometry Foundation Models:** To utilize the implicit geometric understanding embedded within pre-trained 4D geometry foundation models. These models are observed to already encode the essential factors governing inter-frame correspondence (depth, camera motion, object motion) in their internal representations.
3. **Implement Feature-Level Distillation:** To introduce a mechanism for injecting dense 4D correspondence supervision into the video generative backbone. This is achieved by distilling geometry-aware representations from a frozen geometry foundation model into the intermediate features of the video model during training.
4. **Maintain Inference Efficiency:** To ensure that the geometric grounding is achieved without modifying the video model's output space or introducing additional parameters that would increase inference cost, thus preserving a single-stream architecture for efficient generation.
5. **Enable Action Execution:** To integrate a robust inverse dynamics module that can convert these correspondence-consistent video rollouts into executable 6-DoF end-effector trajectories, facilitating direct deployment in both real-world and simulated robot manipulation tasks.
6. **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Performance:** To achieve improved performance in both video prediction quality (visual realism and temporal consistency) and geometric consistency metrics across simulated and realistic scenarios.
7. **Improve Robot Manipulation Success Rates:** To validate that the geometry-enhanced world model and the subsequent action extraction lead to tangible improvements in the success rates of real-world robot manipulation tasks.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to bridge the gap between visually realistic video generation and the geometrically accurate predictions required for practical robot control, moving towards more reliable and generalizable embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GEM-4D methodology is structured around two main components: a geometry-grounded video world model that incorporates geometry-enhanced velocity alignment during training, and an adaptive inverse dynamic system for extracting executable robot actions from generated video rollouts.

#### 3.1. Geometry-Enhanced Velocity Alignment

The core principle involves distilling correspondence-consistent geometric structure from a pre-trained 4D geometry foundation model into the video generative backbone.

- **Inter-Frame Correspondence Formulation:** The paper begins by formalizing inter-frame correspondence, defining how a scene point $X_t$ observed at pixel $p_t$ in frame $t$ projects to $p_{t+1}$ in frame $t+1$ under relative camera motion $(R_{t \rightarrow t+1}, T_{t \rightarrow t+1})$ and scene flow $\Delta X_t$. This equation highlights that reliable correspondence depends on per-frame depth $D$, camera pose $(R, T)$, and object motion $\Delta X$. The authors argue that pixel-level reconstruction losses are insufficient because multiple geometric configurations can produce visually identical frames, meaning a model can achieve zero pixel loss while underlying correspondences are incorrect.
- **Geometry Foundation Models as Correspondence Encoders:** Modern 4D geometry foundation models (e.g., PAGE-4D, Depth Anything V3, VGGT) are trained on dense depth estimation and camera pose regression. These models, by design, implicitly capture depth, camera viewpoint changes, and how depth evolves over time (encoding object motion). Therefore, their learned feature representations are considered to encode the complete correspondence structure. GEM-4D leverages these models as "correspondence teachers" to supervise the video backbone's internal representations, effectively enforcing correspondence consistency without requiring an explicit correspondence loss.
- **Flow Matching for Latent Video Generation:** The generative backbone is a Video DiT (Diffusion Transformer) trained using flow matching. This approach learns a velocity field $v_{vid_\theta}$ that transports a noised video latent $z_1$ (from a normal distribution) to a VAE-encoded video latent $z_0$, conditioned on a language instruction $c$. The training objective, $L_{vid_{FM}}$, involves regressing against an analytically derived target velocity. The Video DiT consists of a backbone $E_{vid_\theta}$ and an output head $U_{vid_\theta}$, where $m_t$ denotes the intermediate features from a mid-level layer of the backbone.
- **Correspondence Distillation via Geometry Flow:**
 - A pre-trained, frozen geometry model $G$ extracts a dense geometric representation $g_0$ from the input video sequence. This $g_0$ serves as the target correspondence representation.
 - A parallel Geometry DiT is introduced. This Geometry DiT is designed to predict the velocity field of a geometry latent $g_t$ (interpolated between $g_0$ and noise $g_1$).
 - Critically, the Geometry DiT receives the intermediate features $m_t$ from the Video DiT backbone as its *only* scene-level conditioning signal. It does not have direct access to raw pixels, camera parameters, or depth maps.
 - The Geometry DiT is trained with its own flow matching loss, $L_{geo_{FM}}$.
- **Joint Objective and Geometric Grounding:** The total training objective is a weighted sum: $L = L_{vid_{FM}} + \alpha L_{geo_{FM}}$. Because both losses share the intermediate representation $m_t$, the gradient with respect to the video backbone parameters $\theta$ includes both an appearance supervision term and a geometry-induced term. This structure compels $m_t$ to encode sufficient information about depth, camera pose, and scene flow to satisfy both the appearance generation and the geometry prediction objectives. This process ensures that the video backbone's internal representations are shaped to be geometrically consistent.
- **Inference Efficiency:** During inference, the geometry branch is entirely discarded. The Video DiT, having internalized the geometric consistency through training, operates as a single-stream generator without any additional computational cost.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Inverse Dynamic System (AIDS)

AIDS converts the geometrically consistent video rollouts generated by GEM-4D into executable 6-DoF robot end-effector trajectories, addressing potential artifacts in generated videos without task-specific training.

- **3D Scene Grounding:** This initial step localizes the target object and the robot end-effector (EE) in 3D. Given an instruction, depth map, and camera intrinsics, vision-language models like Qwen3.5-VL and segmentation models like SAM-2 are used to generate masks for the object and EE. These masks facilitate the extraction of point clouds, and FoundationPose is then used to align the EE CAD model to its point cloud, recovering the initial EE pose $(R_{0_{ee}}, T_{0_{ee}})$.
- **Dual-Criterion Confidence-Gated Tracker:** To track the EE reliably through the generated video, CoTracker3 propagates dense keypoints sampled from the initial EE mask. The system monitors two metrics: the anchor retention ratio $s_t$ (fraction of reliably tracked initial keypoints) and its frame-to-frame change $\Delta s_t$. These metrics differentiate between gradual drift and abrupt collapse. For gradual drift ($s_t$ falls below a threshold $\tau$), the tracker re-anchors keypoints from the most recent reliable mask. For abrupt collapse ($\Delta s_t$ drops sharply below a threshold $-\delta$), the EE mask itself is re-grounded using vision-language semantics from Qwen3.5-VL.
- **Geometry–Kinematics Pose Fallback:** FoundationPose predicts the EE pose $(R_{t_{ee}}, T_{t_{ee}})$ and a confidence score $\kappa_t$ for each frame. If $\kappa_t$ is below a threshold $\kappa^*$ or if the estimated translation/rotation jumps exceed thresholds (checked against previous frame), the FoundationPose estimate is rejected. For rejected frames, the EE translation is recovered by back-projecting valid depth pixels within the EE mask and computing their 3D centroid. The missing rotation is then estimated via spherical linear interpolation between the nearest accepted poses. This provides a robust, temporally consistent EE trajectory.
- **Grasp Insertion and Action Synthesis:** From the recovered EE trajectory, a reference pose is selected near the target object. GraspGen then predicts a set of grasp candidates on the target object's point cloud. These candidates are ranked by a weighted deviation from the reference pose (combining translation and rotation errors), and the best-scoring grasp is selected. This final grasp pose is inserted into the EE trajectory as an intermediate target. The entire trajectory is then smoothed via interpolation. Finally, inverse kinematics converts the smoothed 6-DoF EE trajectory into an executable action sequence for controlling the robot arm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate two primary aspects: the improvement in 4D scene prediction (appearance, depth, and correspondence consistency) and the impact on downstream robot manipulation task success.

#### 4.1. 4D Scene Prediction

- **Quantitative Metrics (Table 1):** GEM-4D consistently demonstrated superior performance across various metrics on both real-world (Droid) and synthetic (RLBench) datasets.
 - **RGB Appearance:** GEM-4D achieved the lowest Frechet Video Distance (FVD) and highest Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) and Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) on the Droid dataset, indicating higher visual quality and temporal consistency compared to baselines like CogVideoX, WAN 2.2-14B, TesserAct, and Geometry-Forcing. Similar trends were observed on the synthetic dataset, where GEM-4D again showed the lowest FVD and highest PSNR.
 - **Depth Reconstruction:** When evaluated for geometric quality using Depth Anything V3 for depth estimation, GEM-4D significantly outperformed baselines. It achieved the lowest Absolute Relative difference (AbsRel) and highest $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ accuracy metrics on both real and synthetic datasets. This indicates that GEM-4D generates frames with more accurate and coherent 3D scene geometry.
 - **3D Reconstruction and Correspondence:** GEM-4D produced the lowest Chamfer Distance, indicating better 3D point cloud reconstruction quality, and the highest $\delta_{vis_{avg}}$ (fraction of visible points tracked), signifying superior correspondence quality. These results suggest that the geometry-aware training effectively improves both visual realism and the consistency of underlying 3D structure.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 5):** Visual comparisons showed that GEM-4D generated more structurally coherent scene evolutions. In contrast, the TesserAct baseline, while producing plausible RGB frames, exhibited inconsistencies such as warping on the manipulator and noisy depth data. GEM-4D's outputs demonstrated cleaner depth maps and tighter object boundaries, preserving geometric structure across frames more effectively.

#### 4.2. Embodied Action Planning

- **Task Success Rates (Table 2):**
 - **Real-world Droid Dataset (Human Study):** On challenging real-world Droid manipulation tasks, GEM-4D consistently improved success rates:
 - AUTOLab: Increased from 58% (TesserAct) to 75%.
 - CLVR: Increased from 65% (TesserAct) to 83%.
 - RAIL: Increased from 59% (TesserAct) to 87%.
 - **Simulated RLBench Dataset (Execution Replay):** In simulation, GEM-4D achieved higher planning success rates across most tasks (ranging from 63% to 82%). For comparison, TesserAct's generated videos, processed by the same AIDS module, showed much lower success rates (ranging from 0% to 49%), indicating the superior quality of GEM-4D's generated videos for action planning. CogVideoX's generated videos were often too inconsistent for reliable processing by the AIDS module, preventing task success rate reporting for that baseline.
- **Qualitative Real-Robot Experiment (Fig. 4, Fig. 6):** Experiments with a UF robot arm in real-world scenarios demonstrated that GEM-4D could generate realistic visual results and reasonable geometry even under unseen backgrounds. This capability supports transfer to real-robot manipulation, with the generated rollouts enabling successful execution of tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Geometry Flow Branch (Table 3):**
 - Baselines (CogVideoX, WAN 2.2-14B) without explicit geometry guidance showed lower performance in both appearance and geometry metrics compared to GEM-4D variants.
 - **GEM-4D (Dep) (explicit depth supervision):** Training with direct depth velocity prediction yielded competitive performance, indicating that direct depth constraints are effective for geometric guidance. However, GEM-4D, which uses the more abstract PAGE-4D features, still generally outperformed this variant, especially in depth accuracy.
 - **GEM-4D (VGGT) (using VGGT features):** Directly incorporating VGGT features slightly degraded performance compared to using PAGE-4D features. This is attributed to VGGT being primarily trained for static or quasi-static scenes, making PAGE-4D's disentanglement of static and dynamic components more suitable for dynamic robotic manipulation scenarios.

These results collectively indicate that the proposed geometry-enhanced velocity alignment, particularly with PAGE-4D as the geometry foundation model, significantly improves both the visual quality and geometric consistency of generated videos. This, in turn, translates to a substantial improvement in the reliability and success rate of downstream robot manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of GEM-4D represents an advancement in the field of video world models for embodied AI, particularly in robot manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Reliability of World Models:** By explicitly addressing the issue of geometric inconsistency in video generation, GEM-4D moves beyond models that produce only visually plausible outputs. It demonstrates that integrating dense 4D correspondence supervision leads to physically grounded predictions, which are crucial for any system that interacts with the real world. This contributes to making generative world models more dependable for downstream tasks.
- **Improved Robot Manipulation Capabilities:** The core impact is evident in the substantial improvement in robot manipulation success rates across both simulated and real-world benchmarks. The ability to generate geometrically consistent video rollouts directly translates into more accurate and executable robot trajectories, facilitating the development of robust policies for complex tasks requiring precise interaction with objects.
- **Efficient Geometric Grounding:** The methodology of feature-level distillation is notable for achieving geometric awareness without incurring additional inference costs or altering the video model's output space. This asymmetric coupling allows the geometry branch to be discarded during inference, making the resulting single-stream generator efficient and practical for real-time robotic applications.
- **Reduced Data Annotation Requirements:** By leveraging pre-trained 4D geometry foundation models as "correspondence teachers," GEM-4D reduces the need for large-scale, explicit geometric annotations specific to the video generation task. This approach can simplify the data acquisition and training processes for developing embodied AI systems, making it more accessible to researchers and practitioners.
- **Advancement in Generalizable Robotic Policies:** The ability of GEM-4D to generate coherent future scenarios, combined with its adaptive inverse dynamics system, contributes to the development of more generalizable robot policies. Robots equipped with such world models could potentially plan and execute tasks in novel environments and with unfamiliar objects by generating predictions that respect physical laws.
- **Foundation for Future Embodied AI Research:** This work establishes a precedent for integrating complex geometric priors into generative models through representation alignment. It opens avenues for future research into incorporating other physical principles (e.g., contact dynamics, material properties, fluid dynamics) into world models, leading to more sophisticated and capable embodied AI agents that possess a deeper understanding of the physical world. The success of the Adaptive Inverse Dynamic System also highlights the importance of robust action extraction modules that can compensate for imperfections in generated trajectories.

In conclusion, GEM-4D contributes a method for creating more reliable and physically aware video world models, which directly enhances their utility for robot manipulation. This work represents a step toward building embodied AI systems that can not only predict future visual states but also effectively reason about and interact with the geometric and dynamic properties of their environment.
