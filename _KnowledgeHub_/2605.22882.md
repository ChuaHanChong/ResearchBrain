# GEM-4D: Geometry-Enhanced Video World Models for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GEM-4D introduces a geometry-enhanced video world model for robot manipulation that improves the physical consistency of generated future videos by distilling features from 4D geometry foundation models into a Video DiT during training. This method leads to higher manipulation success rates, such as increasing from 58% to 75% on the Droid AUTOLab dataset, while maintaining efficient inference.

## Problem

- Current video world models for robot manipulation prioritize visual realism but often lack physical grounding, leading to geometric inconsistencies (e.g., rigid objects deforming, contacts drifting, implausible depth changes) in generated videos.
- These geometric inconsistencies render video rollouts unreliable for extracting precise actions required for robot control, as standard generation losses do not explicitly constrain critical factors like camera motion, scene depth, and object motion.
- Existing methods that incorporate geometric structure typically require large-scale geometric annotations, modify the video model's output space, or limit the expressive capacity of pre-trained backbones, increasing complexity and inference costs.

## Method

- **GEM-4D** employs a geometry-enhanced velocity alignment mechanism, distilling dense 4D correspondence supervision from a frozen 4D geometry foundation model (like PAGE-4D) into the intermediate features of a ==Video DiT== generative backbone during training.
- A novel joint training objective is used, where a parallel Geometry DiT, conditioned solely on the Video DiT's intermediate features, is trained with its own flow matching loss to predict the velocity field of a geometry latent.
- An Adaptive Inverse Dynamic System (AIDS) converts the geometrically consistent video rollouts into executable 6-DoF robot end-effector trajectories, incorporating 3D scene grounding, a dual-criterion confidence-gated tracker, and a geometry-kinematics pose fallback.

## Results

- GEM-4D achieved superior performance in 4D scene prediction, with the lowest Frechet Video Distance (FVD) and highest SSIM/PSNR for RGB appearance, lowest AbsRel and highest 
		delta_1/
		delta_2 for depth reconstruction, and lowest Chamfer Distance with highest 
		delta_{vis_{avg}} for 3D reconstruction and correspondence on both real-world and synthetic datasets.
- The model substantially improved robot manipulation task success rates, increasing from **58%** to **75%** on the Droid AUTOLab dataset, **65%** to **83%** on CLVR, and **59%** to **87%** on RAIL compared to the TesserAct baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the geometry flow branch, showing that GEM-4D, particularly with PAGE-4D features, generally outperformed variants with explicit depth supervision or different geometry models like VGGT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern 4D geometry foundation models implicitly encode the complete inter-frame correspondence structure (depth, camera pose, object motion) within their internal feature representations, making them effective "correspondence teachers."
> - Distilling these geometry-aware representations into a video generative backbone at the feature level, rather than modifying the output space or requiring explicit geometric annotations, can effectively enhance geometric consistency without increasing inference cost.
> - A robust, adaptive inverse dynamics system is crucial for translating geometrically consistent, yet still imperfect, generated video rollouts into reliable and executable robot control actions, especially in real-world scenarios.
