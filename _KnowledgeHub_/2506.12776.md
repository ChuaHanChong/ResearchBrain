# Native Visual Understanding: Resolving Resolution Dilemmas in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces RC-Bench, a resolution-centric benchmark, and NativeRes-LLaVA, an open-source training framework, to enable Vision-Language Models to process images at their native resolutions and aspect ratios. The NativeRes-LLaVA framework demonstrates improved accuracy and robustness on tasks requiring fine-grained visual details, particularly for images with extreme resolutions or aspect ratios.

## Problem

- Most Vision-Language Models (VLMs) process images at a fixed, often low resolution, leading to a loss of fine-grained visual details crucial for tasks like OCR, chart interpretation, or medical image analysis.
- Existing multimodal benchmarks are 'Semantic-Centric' and do not adequately evaluate VLM performance under varying resolution and aspect ratio conditions, hindering the development of robust visual encoding strategies.
- The open-source ecosystem for native-resolution VLMs is fragmented, lacking unified frameworks that allow for systematic research and comparison of dynamic visual encoding approaches.

## Method

- Introduction of ==RC-Bench==, a new Resolution-Centric benchmark specifically designed to evaluate VLMs' visual encoding capabilities under diverse resolutions and aspect ratios, using a balanced distribution of image areas and aspect ratios with human-annotated QA pairs.
- Development of ==NativeRes-LLaVA==, an open-source, end-to-end training framework featuring a ==Native Resolution Vision Encoder (ViT)==, a compression module, and a two-layer MLP connecting to an LLM backbone (Qwen2-7B-Instruct).
- Implementation of ==Multimodal Sequence Packing ('Patch n' Pack')== to efficiently handle variable-length visual token sequences generated by the native resolution encoder, using Variable Length Flash Attention for parallel processing without redundant padding.

## Results

- NativeRes-LLaVA, trained on **1.34M** samples, achieved top results on several resolution-centric benchmarks, including TextVQA (**74.0%**), ChartQA (**79.0%**), DocVQA (**89.7%**), and OCRBench (**705**), outperforming many models trained on larger datasets.
- On the newly introduced RC-Bench, the native resolution strategy consistently showed higher average accuracy and greater robustness (lower Coefficient of Variation for Area Accuracy and Ratio Accuracy) compared to cropping-based and fixed-resolution methods.
- The native resolution method demonstrated a significant advantage in processing images with extreme aspect ratios (e.g., very high or very wide) or ultra-large areas, highlighting its superior generalization to diverse visual data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Native resolution visual encoding provides inherent advantages for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in handling diverse image resolutions and aspect ratios, leading to improved accuracy on fine-grained visual tasks.
> - Existing VLM benchmarks often lack sufficient resolution and aspect ratio diversity, and a resolution-centric benchmark is crucial for accurately evaluating a VLM's ability to process varied visual inputs.
> - The proposed NativeRes-LLaVA framework's open-source nature and modular design, along with its multimodal sequence packing, offer an efficient and accessible pathway for researchers to develop and experiment with native resolution VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is rapidly evolving, driven by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their integration with visual understanding. VLMs like LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and InternVL have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in tasks requiring both visual and linguistic comprehension. However, a significant practical hurdle for these models is handling the immense diversity of real-world image data, particularly concerning resolution and aspect ratio. Most existing VLMs are designed to process images at a fixed, often low, resolution (e.g., 336x336 pixels), leading to a "Resolution Dilemma." This limitation causes loss of fine-grained visual details crucial for tasks like OCR, chart interpretation, or medical image analysis, and struggles with extreme aspect ratios (e.g., panoramas, long documents).

Current approaches to address this "Resolution Dilemma" include:
1. **Upsampling Methods:** Resizing images to a higher resolution before feeding them into a fixed-size encoder.
2. **Cropping-based Methods:** Splitting high-resolution images into smaller, overlapping tiles, processing each tile separately, and then aggregating features. LLaVA-NeXT is a prominent example.
3. **Hybrid Vision Encoders:** Combining multiple encoders, one for low-resolution global context and others for high-resolution details.
4. **Native Resolution Encoding:** Directly processing images at their original resolution and aspect ratio, often using Vision Transformers (ViTs) designed to handle variable input sizes. Qwen2-VL and Kimi-VL are recent examples that have demonstrated superior performance with this strategy.

Despite the emergence of native resolution models, the authors identify two critical gaps in the existing landscape:
- **Fragmented Open-Source Ecosystem:** While some native-resolution VLMs exist, their training frameworks are often proprietary or provide limited interfaces, hindering open research and systematic comparisons.
- **Inadequate Benchmarking:** Current multimodal benchmarks primarily focus on content understanding and often overlook or inadequately evaluate VLM performance under varied resolution and aspect ratio conditions. They lack systematic analysis of how specific image properties impact accuracy.

This paper directly addresses these gaps by introducing both a novel benchmark and an open-source framework. By proposing **RC-Bench**, a resolution-centric benchmark, the authors aim to provide a more rigorous and systematic evaluation tool that accounts for resolution and aspect ratio variations, guiding the development of more robust VLMs. Simultaneously, **NativeRes-LLaVA** offers a modular, open-source training framework that enables researchers to experiment with and deploy native resolution visual encoding effectively. This dual contribution positions the work as a crucial step towards standardizing evaluation and democratizing access to advanced VLM capabilities for diverse visual inputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the identified "Resolution Dilemma" in VLMs, which manifests in two primary forms:
1. **Model Design Limitations:** Most VLMs default to fixed, low-resolution inputs, leading to a loss of critical fine-grained visual information when processing diverse, real-world images with varying resolutions and extreme aspect ratios. While dynamic resolution strategies exist, a unified and open-source framework for native resolution processing is lacking, making systematic research and development challenging.
2. **Benchmark Limitations:** Existing multimodal benchmarks are primarily "Semantic-Centric" (SC), focusing on general content understanding, and are not designed to rigorously evaluate a VLM's ability to handle resolution and aspect ratio variations. This oversight means current evaluations do not adequately reflect real-world visual complexities and fail to guide the design of dynamic visual encoding strategies effectively.

To resolve these dilemmas, the paper sets out the following key objectives:
- **Systematically Analyze Existing Benchmarks:** Investigate the data distribution (resolution and aspect ratio) of current benchmarks to identify their limitations regarding resolution sensitivity.
- **Propose a Resolution-Centric Benchmark (RC-Bench):** Develop a novel benchmark specifically designed to thoroughly evaluate VLMs' visual encoding and resolution processing capabilities under diverse and extreme visual conditions, emphasizing resolution and aspect ratio. This benchmark aims to guide the design of new dynamic visual encoding strategies.
- **Introduce an Open-Source Native Resolution Training Framework (NativeRes-LLaVA):** Develop and open-source a modular, end-to-end training system that enables VLMs to effectively process images at their native resolutions and aspect ratios, preserving maximum image detail. This framework aims to foster open research and provide a convenient platform for experimentation.
- **Conduct Comprehensive Experimental Validation:** Perform extensive experiments on existing visual encoding strategies using both RC-Bench and other resolution-centric benchmarks to empirically validate the superiority of native resolution visual encoding and demonstrate NativeRes-LLaVA's effectiveness.
- **Provide Detailed Analysis of VLM Behavior:** Offer insights into how different visual encoding strategies impact VLM performance under various visual conditions, contributing to a deeper understanding of VLM robustness and flexibility.

In essence, the authors aim to bridge the gap between advanced VLM capabilities and real-world visual data diversity by providing both the tools for better evaluation and a more effective, accessible architectural paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a two-pronged approach to address the resolution dilemmas: establishing a new benchmark (RC-Bench) and introducing an open-source training framework (NativeRes-LLaVA).

#### 3.1. RC-Bench: Resolution-Centric Benchmark

- **Motivation:** Based on an analysis revealing that most existing benchmarks are "Semantic-Centric" (SC) and lack images with diverse resolutions and aspect ratios, RC-Bench is designed as a "Resolution-Centric" (RC) benchmark. RC tasks require fine-grained details (e.g., reading small text), unlike SC tasks (e.g., object recognition) that are less resolution-dependent.
- **Dataset Construction:**
 - **Image Source Selection:** Combines existing public datasets (e.g., OCRBench, DocVQA, ChartQA) with proprietary data, focusing on visual complexity and readability.
 - **Balanced Distribution:** Images are manually balanced across seven predefined resolution levels (Area A-G, based on 384x384 pixel units) and five aspect ratio categories (NM, AW, BW, AH, BH). Image padding and resizing are used to augment underrepresented distributions, ensuring consistency between training and evaluation. This yields 1750 high-quality images.
 - **QA Pair Generation:** A hybrid strategy using GPT-4o for initial candidate QA generation, followed by rigorous human annotation and refinement. Questions primarily demand recognition of textual content (invoices, charts, documents, posters, screenshots) and fine-grained visual details. Answer types include numbers, dates, names, phrases, and complete sentences.
- **Evaluation Methodology:**
 - **Metrics:** Exact Match (EM) for concise answers (numbers, dates, codes), and Average Normalized Levenshtein Similarity (ANLS) for more complex answers (addresses, sentences) to allow for partial matching.
 - **Normalization:** Multiple answer normalization strategies are applied, including handling unit symbols and variations in unit ordering, to enhance robustness.
 - **Detailed Analysis:** Provides model scores across resolution and aspect ratio dimensions, along with the Coefficient of Variation (CV) for Area Accuracy (ACV) and Ratio Accuracy (RCV). CV measures relative dispersion (standard deviation/mean), allowing for a more fine-grained understanding of model performance stability and robustness under varying resolution conditions.

#### 3.2. NativeRes-LLaVA: Native Visual Encoding Training Framework

- **Model Architecture:** Comprises four main modules (Figure 5):
 1. **Native Resolution Vision Encoder:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) initialized from Qwen2-VL-2B. It natively supports dynamic image resolutions and employs 2D Rotary Position Embedding (2D RoPE) for positional encoding, enabling flexible adaptation to various image sizes and aspect ratios without requiring fixed-size inputs.
 2. **Compression Module:** Applies average pooling over adjacent 2x2 feature patches to further compress visual tokens from the vision encoder, improving computational efficiency. For example, a 336x336 image is converted to 576 tokens by ViT, then compressed to 144 tokens by a 4x patch merger.
 3. **Two-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP):** A visual projector connecting the vision encoder's output to the LLM's embedding space.
 4. **Advanced LLM:** Qwen2-7B-Instruct serves as the backbone Large Language Model.
- **Native-Resolution Visual Encoding Mechanism:** Directly processes images at their original resolution and aspect ratio, preserving maximum image detail. This contrasts with fixed-resolution approaches that downsample or crop.
- **Multimodal Sequence Packing (Patch n' Pack):** To handle variable-length patch sequences generated by the native resolution encoder and enable efficient GPU parallel computing (which traditionally prefers fixed lengths), the framework adopts a strategy similar to text processing. Multiple patch sequences from different images within a batch are packed into a single, concatenated sequence. Variable Length Flash Attention is then used to perform attention computation, ensuring that attention calculations for each image's patch sequence are isolated, despite being packed together. This avoids redundant padding and improves efficiency.
- **Training Strategy:**
 - **Stage 1: VLM Pre-training:** Fine-tunes only the Visual Projector (MLP) parameters. The LLM and Vision Encoder are frozen. Uses LLaVA-Pretrain dataset (558K image-text pairs) for 1 epoch. AdamW optimizer with cosine learning rate scheduler.
 - **Stage 2: Visual Instruction Tuning:** Two configurations are explored:
 - **Visual SFT 1:** Uses LLaVA-mix665k dataset (665K samples). Freezes Vision Encoder, fine-tunes MLP Projector and LLM.
 - **Visual SFT 2:** Uses LLaVA-NeXT-Data (779K samples, including OCR datasets). Fine-tunes *all* components (Vision Encoder, MLP Projector, LLM) with a lower learning rate for the Vision Encoder. This configuration is specifically designed to enhance "Resolution-Centric" capabilities due to its richer OCR-focused data.
 - **Computational Setup:** All experiments conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100-80G GPUs, maintaining consistent configurations with LLaVA-NeXT and LLaVA for fair comparison. Max tokens per image are capped to prevent out-of-memory errors.

This systematic approach, combining a tailored benchmark with a flexible, open-source model framework, allows for rigorous evaluation and advancements in VLM capabilities for real-world diverse visual data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across 13 general benchmarks and the newly introduced RC-Bench yielded several key findings, demonstrating the effectiveness and superiority of the Native Resolution Visual Encoding strategy implemented in NativeRes-LLaVA.

#### 4.1. Main Results on General Benchmarks

The authors compare NativeRes-LLaVA with various existing VLMs, categorizing them by training data scale and resolution strategy.

- **Superior Performance without Specialized Data (Table 3):** When trained on a standard LLaVA-mix665k dataset (1.22M samples) which lacks explicit RC-type examples, NativeRes-LLaVA (using Qwen2-7B-Instruct LLM and Native resolution strategy) achieved the best results on TextVQA (72.4%) and MM-Vet (46.6%). This indicates an inherent advantage of native resolution encoding in handling dynamic resolutions, even without extensive specialized training data.
- **Dramatic Improvements with Minimal Specialized Data (Table 4):** With a slightly larger dataset (1.34M samples, including RC-type examples from LLaVA-NeXT-Data), NativeRes-LLaVA significantly outperformed many models trained on much larger or comparable datasets.
 - It surpassed Cambrian-1 (Hybrid, 9.5M data) on OCRBench (705 vs. 624).
 - It outperformed multiple LLaVA-NeXT variants (Cropping-based, 1.34M data) on DocVQA (89.7% vs. 78.2%).
 - It exceeded TextHawk (Cropping-based, 115M data) on InfographicVQA (61.0% vs. 50.6%).
 - It achieved best results across TextVQA (74.0%), ChartQA (79.0%), HR-Bench (61.3%), and MM-Vet (44.5% with 1.34M data, compared to 46.6% with 1.22M, indicating slight trade-offs or data distribution differences).
- **Maintained Competitive Performance on SC-type tasks:** Across both tables, NativeRes-LLaVA demonstrated competitive or superior performance on Semantic-Centric benchmarks (e.g., AI2D, MathVista, SEED-Bench, MME, MMBench, POPE), which are less sensitive to image resolution. This indicates that the native resolution strategy does not sacrifice general semantic understanding while significantly enhancing fine-grained visual capabilities.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study: Results on General Benchmarks (Table 5)

- **Contribution of Native Resolution Strategy:** Replacing LLaVA-NeXT's SigLip-384 ViT with Qwen2-VL-ViT (while still using cropping at fixed resolution) improved performance, but still fell short of NativeRes-LLaVA's native resolution strategy, especially on RC-type tasks (DocVQA, InfoVQA, HR-Bench). This validates that the performance gains are primarily due to the native resolution *strategy* rather than just a more powerful underlying ViT.
- **Impact of Maximum Resolution (MaxRes):** Restricting NativeRes-LLaVA's input to a lower MaxRes (e.g., 378x378 tokens) significantly degraded performance on RC-type tasks, while SC-type task performance remained relatively unaffected or even slightly improved. This confirms the hypothesis that higher resolution is crucial for RC tasks and reinforces the distinction between RC and SC benchmarks.

#### 4.2.2. Results on RC-Bench (Table 6 & 7)

- **Native Resolution's Superiority in Accuracy and Robustness (Table 6):** On RC-Bench, the native resolution strategy consistently outperformed cropping-based and fixed-resolution approaches. It not only achieved higher average accuracy but also demonstrated greater robustness, as indicated by lower Coefficients of Variation for Area Accuracy (ACV) and Ratio Accuracy (RCV). A lower CV signifies more stable performance across varying resolutions and aspect ratios.
- **Comparison with Open-Source Models (Table 7):** Evaluating NativeRes-LLaVA against other open-source models (Cambrian-1, LLaVA-OneVision, Intern-VL, DeepSeek-VL2, Qwen2-VL, Kimi-VL, Seed1.5-VL, GPT-4o) using different strategies confirmed that models employing the Native strategy generally exhibit superior accuracy and robustness on RC-Bench, especially when substantial pre-training data is involved (e.g., Qwen2-VL, Kimi-VL). NativeRes-LLaVA, with its comparatively smaller training data (1.34M), achieved competitive robustness (low ACV/RCV) compared to much larger models.

#### 4.2.3. Cropping-based Vs. Native Resolution Visual Encoding (Figure 6 & Table 8)

A detailed heatmap analysis on RC-Bench (using a unified Qwen2-VL-ViT backbone and same MaxRes 728x728 for both) showed:
- **Specific Performance Dip:** The Native Resolution method showed a slight performance drop in one specific configuration (NM, B: small images with normal aspect ratio). This is attributed to the cropping-based method's "sweet spot" where small images align perfectly with the vision encoder's pre-training resolution, circumventing down-sampling.
- **On-Par Performance in Common Scenarios:** For most other common resolution and aspect ratio combinations, both methods performed similarly.
- **Superior Robustness in Extreme Scenarios:** Crucially, the Native Resolution method demonstrated a clear and significant advantage in processing images with extreme aspect ratios (Very High (BH) and Very Wide (BW) rows) or extreme areas (Ultra-large (G) column), as evidenced by larger positive accuracy differences. This highlights the native method's superior generalization and robustness to diverse, unconventional visual data.

#### 4.2.4. Model Performance at Different LLM Scales (Table 9)

- NativeRes-LLaVA showed significant performance improvement as the LLM scale increased (from 0.5B to 7B).
- Notably, the 2B version of NativeRes-LLaVA already outperformed the 7B LLaVA-NeXT on resolution-sensitive benchmarks (TextVQA, OCRBench, DocVQA, InfographicVQA, RC-Bench), further validating the efficiency and effectiveness of the Native Resolution strategy.

#### 4.2.5. Model Performance at Different LLMs (Table 10)

- The Native Resolution strategy proved compatible and highly effective with different LLM backbones. Even when replacing Qwen2-7B-Instruct with the less capable Vicuna-7B, NativeRes-LLaVA still significantly outperformed LLaVA-NeXT (using Qwen2-7B-Instruct) on resolution-sensitive benchmarks. This suggests that the visual encoding strategy's strength is a primary driver of performance on these tasks, transcending the specific LLM's language capabilities.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that native resolution visual encoding, as implemented in NativeRes-LLaVA, offers significant advantages in accuracy and robustness for Vision-Language Models, especially when dealing with the diverse resolutions and aspect ratios found in real-world visual data. The proposed RC-Bench is shown to be a valuable tool for revealing these distinctions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the field of Vision-Language Models and holds substantial potential for future impact:

- **Addressing Critical Gaps in VLM Research:** The paper directly tackles the "Resolution Dilemma," a fundamental challenge in VLM development. By identifying the limitations of existing benchmarks and the fragmentation of open-source native resolution frameworks, it fills crucial voids that have hindered systematic progress in handling diverse visual inputs.
- **Establishing a New Standard for Evaluation (RC-Bench):** RC-Bench is the first benchmark specifically designed to systematically evaluate visual encoding strategies under extreme visual conditions. Its focus on resolution, aspect ratio, and the use of metrics like Coefficient of Variation (CV) provides a more nuanced and comprehensive assessment of VLM robustness. This will guide researchers in developing and comparing dynamic visual encoding strategies more effectively, accelerating progress in models capable of fine-grained visual understanding.
- **Democratizing Advanced VLM Capabilities (NativeRes-LLaVA):** By open-sourcing NativeRes-LLaVA, the authors provide a modular, end-to-end training framework for native-resolution visual encoding. This democratizes access to state-of-the-art capabilities, allowing a broader academic and open-source community to build upon and experiment with models that preserve crucial image details. This will likely foster innovation and collaboration, reducing the barrier to entry for developing powerful VLMs.
- **Advancing Real-World Application Potential:** The demonstrated superiority of native resolution visual encoding on resolution-centric tasks (like OCR, document analysis, chart interpretation) means VLMs can become significantly more reliable and accurate in real-world scenarios where images often vary wildly in resolution and aspect ratio (e.g., processing scanned documents, analyzing medical imagery, understanding social media content, or interpreting drone footage). This work brings VLMs closer to achieving human-level visual perception by enabling them to "see" and "understand" fine details.
- **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The comprehensive ablation studies not only validate the native resolution strategy but also offer valuable insights into the impact of maximum resolution and LLM scale/type. This provides clear guidance for future research on VLM architecture design, training methodologies, and data curation, particularly for tasks requiring high-fidelity visual input. The identification of the "sweet spot" for cropping-based methods also informs a more nuanced understanding of hybrid approaches.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point towards exciting avenues for future research:
1. **Computational Resources and SOTA Performance:** Current computational constraints prevented training a ViT from scratch at native resolution and conducting larger-scale training to reach state-of-the-art performance comparable to models like Qwen-VL-Series or Seed1.5-VL. Future work will focus on extensive training with larger datasets and potentially training ViTs tailored for native resolution from scratch.
2. **Training Data and Resolution Generalization:** The existing multimodal datasets predominantly feature images with limited resolution and aspect ratio diversity. While NativeRes-LLaVA supports high resolutions (up to 1792x1792), the current training data prevents fully leveraging this capability. Future research will meticulously analyze the impact of training data distribution on resolution generalization and improve data curation strategies.
3. **Computational Bottleneck in High-Resolution Processing:** The quadratic scaling of self-attention (O(N^2)) in native resolution ViTs poses a significant computational and memory burden, especially during the prefill stage. Future work will focus on optimizing this process, potentially by adapting techniques like window attention (as seen in Qwen2.5-VL) to mitigate the computational costs without compromising performance.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust framework and a critical benchmark that collectively resolve key "resolution dilemmas" facing Vision-Language Models. By championing native resolution visual understanding, the authors pave the way for more capable, robust, and universally applicable VLMs that can truly interpret the diverse visual complexities of the real world.
