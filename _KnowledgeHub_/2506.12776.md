# Native Visual Understanding: Resolving Resolution Dilemmas in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces RC-Bench, a resolution-centric benchmark, and NativeRes-LLaVA, an open-source training framework, to enable Vision-Language Models to process images at their native resolutions and aspect ratios. The NativeRes-LLaVA framework demonstrates improved accuracy and robustness on tasks requiring fine-grained visual details, particularly for images with extreme resolutions or aspect ratios.

## Problem

- Most Vision-Language Models (VLMs) process images at a fixed, often low resolution, leading to a loss of fine-grained visual details crucial for tasks like OCR, chart interpretation, or medical image analysis.
- Existing multimodal benchmarks are 'Semantic-Centric' and do not adequately evaluate VLM performance under varying resolution and aspect ratio conditions, hindering the development of robust visual encoding strategies.
- The open-source ecosystem for native-resolution VLMs is fragmented, lacking unified frameworks that allow for systematic research and comparison of dynamic visual encoding approaches.

## Method

- Introduction of ==RC-Bench==, a new Resolution-Centric benchmark specifically designed to evaluate VLMs' visual encoding capabilities under diverse resolutions and aspect ratios, using a balanced distribution of image areas and aspect ratios with human-annotated QA pairs.
- Development of ==NativeRes-LLaVA==, an open-source, end-to-end training framework featuring a ==Native Resolution Vision Encoder (ViT)==, a compression module, and a two-layer MLP connecting to an LLM backbone (Qwen2-7B-Instruct).
- Implementation of ==Multimodal Sequence Packing ('Patch n' Pack')== to efficiently handle variable-length visual token sequences generated by the native resolution encoder, using Variable Length Flash Attention for parallel processing without redundant padding.

## Results

- NativeRes-LLaVA, trained on **1.34M** samples, achieved top results on several resolution-centric benchmarks, including TextVQA (**74.0%**), ChartQA (**79.0%**), DocVQA (**89.7%**), and OCRBench (**705**), outperforming many models trained on larger datasets.
- On the newly introduced RC-Bench, the native resolution strategy consistently showed higher average accuracy and greater robustness (lower Coefficient of Variation for Area Accuracy and Ratio Accuracy) compared to cropping-based and fixed-resolution methods.
- The native resolution method demonstrated a significant advantage in processing images with extreme aspect ratios (e.g., very high or very wide) or ultra-large areas, highlighting its superior generalization to diverse visual data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Native resolution visual encoding provides inherent advantages for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in handling diverse image resolutions and aspect ratios, leading to improved accuracy on fine-grained visual tasks.
> - Existing VLM benchmarks often lack sufficient resolution and aspect ratio diversity, and a resolution-centric benchmark is crucial for accurately evaluating a VLM's ability to process varied visual inputs.
> - The proposed NativeRes-LLaVA framework's open-source nature and modular design, along with its multimodal sequence packing, offer an efficient and accessible pathway for researchers to develop and experiment with native resolution VLMs.
