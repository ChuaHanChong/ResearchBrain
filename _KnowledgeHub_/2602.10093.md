# UniVTAC: A Unified Simulation Platform for Visuo-Tactile Manipulation Data Generation, Learning, and Benchmarking

> [!summary] Summary
> UniVTAC is a simulation platform built on TacEx and Isaac Sim that synthesizes visuo-tactile manipulation data for three commercial sensors using tactile-reactive closed-loop grasping. From 205,826 synthetic samples it pretrains the UniVTAC Encoder via shape, contact, and pose reconstruction pathways, and ships an eight-task benchmark. The encoder lifts ACT success from 30.9% to 48.0% in simulation and 43.3% to 68.3% on a real arm.

## Problem

- Real-world tactile data collection is costly and unstandardized across sensor hardware, starving tactile-centric representation learning and leaving contact-dependent manipulation policies undertrained.
- Existing tactile pretraining either optimizes pixel fidelity while ignoring contact physics, or uses contrastive vision-tactile alignment that yields task-specific representations needing retraining per task.
- Prior simulation data generators target rigid-object manipulation with heuristic gripper commands and binary success checks, producing sensor-destructive contacts and trajectories lacking informative tactile variation.

## Method

- UniVTAC Platform extends TacEx with GelSight Mini, ViTai GF225, and Xense WS models plus atomic Grasp/Move/Place/Probe/Rotate APIs, where a depth-feedback velocity law throttles gripper closure to prevent non-physical penetration.
- UniVTAC Encoder, a shared ResNet-18 trained on three pathways: dual-view shape reconstruction of marked and marker-free images, contact supervision on gelpad depth maps plus 2D marker projections, and 7-D relative-pose regression; decoders are discarded at deployment.
- UniVTAC Benchmark synthesizes expert trajectories with injected randomized failures and contact-feedback corrective retries, and scores trials with physics-based validity criteria rejecting excessive penetration depth or gelpad slip.

## Results

- On eight benchmark tasks (50 demos, 100 rollouts each), ACT plus the encoder averages 48.0% versus 30.9% vision-only ACT and 40.5% for the contrastive VITaL baseline.
- Pathway ablation: from-scratch 34.0%, contact-only 41.9%, shape-only 41.6%, contact+shape 43.9%, full model with pose 48.0%, showing pose supervision drives the final gain on tasks like Lift Bottle.
- Pretraining data scaling from 0 to 200K samples raises average success 34.2% to 48.0%; real-world transfer without finetuning gives 68.3% versus 43.3%, with Insert Tube 55%-to-85% and Bottle Upright 60%-to-95%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reconstruction targets grounded in privileged simulator physics, depth and marker displacement, transfer better than global contrastive alignment, which optimizes agreement rather than actionable contact mechanics.
> - Closing the loop on gripper aperture during synthetic data collection matters as much as the renderer: uncontrolled closure produces penetration artifacts outside any realistic deformation manifold.
> - Pose supervision is the non-obvious ingredient; without metric spatial grounding, shape and contact pathways together plateau, and tactile gains appear even on non-insertion tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has made significant strides, particularly with vision-language-action (VLA) policies enabling complex tasks. However, the paper identifies a critical bottleneck: the robust execution of contact-rich manipulation tasks, such as insertion or precise alignment, where vision alone proves insufficient due to occlusions, limited depth accuracy at close ranges, and the inability to directly perceive contact interactions. This is where visuo-tactile perception becomes indispensable, offering complementary information about localized contact geometry, force distribution, and relative motion.

UniVTAC positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving research areas:

**Addressing the Data Scarcity in Tactile Perception:**
A major challenge in advancing tactile robotics is the scarcity of large-scale, high-fidelity tactile data. Real-world data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and lacks standardization across different sensor designs. This limits the ability to train robust, generalizable tactile-centric representation models and policies. UniVTAC directly tackles this by proposing a simulation-based platform for scalable data synthesis.

**Advancing Visuo-Tactile Sensor Simulation:**
The paper acknowledges the progress in high-fidelity simulators for visuo-tactile sensors, driven by physics-based simulation and differentiable rendering. It references prominent frameworks like Incremental Potential Contact (IPC) methods (e.g., SapienIPC, Taccel, TacEx) and Finite Element Method (FEM) (e.g., TacFlex), and Material Point Method (MPM) (e.g., DiffTactile, Tacchi). UniVTAC builds upon the robust TacEx framework, leveraging its physical accuracy and integration with NVIDIA Isaac Sim. Crucially, UniVTAC extends these capabilities by introducing support for *multiple mainstream visuo-tactile sensors* (GelSight Mini, ViTai GF225, Xense WS) and developing *automated manipulation APIs* with tactile-reactive adaptive control. This differentiates it from existing simulators by offering a more unified and controlled environment for varied sensor configurations and manipulation interactions.

**Evolving Visuo-Tactile Representation Learning:**
Current approaches to learning tactile representations fall into three categories: reconstruction-based (e.g., Marker-Embedded GAN, UniT), explicit geometric supervision (e.g., RDP, PointNet-based methods), and multi-modal alignment (e.g., VITaL, CLIP-like objectives). The paper points out limitations in these: reconstruction methods can miss underlying physics, geometric methods might sacrifice rich textures, and contrastive methods often yield task-specific representations. UniVTAC introduces a novel multi-pathway representation learning framework that combines elements of these, specifically designing supervision objectives (shape reconstruction, contact deformation prediction, pose estimation) to learn *structured, physically-grounded, tactile-centric representations* that disentangle global geometry, local dynamics, and spatial pose. This aims to create more generalizable and actionable features.

**Improving Simulation for Robotic Data Generation:**
Many simulation platforms (e.g., RoboTwin, RoboGen, MimicGen, DexMimicGen) exist for generating large-scale robot manipulation data, often using expert code or teleoperation. However, these largely focus on rigid or articulated object manipulation and typically lack explicit modeling of complex tactile interactions. Their open-loop or weakly constrained execution can produce trajectories that are unrealistic or unsafe for real tactile sensors (e.g., excessive forces, unstable contacts). UniVTAC addresses this by explicitly incorporating *tactile awareness* into its data generation process through closed-loop control and runtime validity checks, ensuring the synthesized trajectories capture rich, physically meaningful contact responses without sensor-destructive behaviors. This is a significant leap towards generating truly *tactile-aware* synthetic data.

**Establishing Standardized Benchmarks:**
The absence of unified and comprehensive benchmarks for visuo-tactile manipulation hinders systematic evaluation and comparison of tactile-driven strategies. UniVTAC fills this gap by introducing the UniVTAC Benchmark, a simulation-based platform comprising eight diverse visuo-tactile tasks. This benchmark not only allows for automated data synthesis but also provides *unified policy evaluation* with tactile-specific, physics-based success criteria, offering a reproducible environment for fair comparison.

In essence, UniVTAC provides a comprehensive and integrated framework that simultaneously addresses the data scarcity, simulation fidelity, representation learning efficacy, and benchmarking needs specific to contact-rich, visuo-tactile robotic manipulation. It stands out by its holistic approach, combining a high-fidelity, multi-sensor simulation platform with an intelligent data generation pipeline and a robust representation learning scheme, all validated by a systematic benchmark and real-world transferability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind UniVTAC stems from the recognition that while vision-language-action (VLA) policies have propelled robotic manipulation forward, they fall short in contact-rich tasks where precise interaction and force feedback are paramount. Vision alone often fails in these scenarios due to issues like end-effector occlusion, limited depth perception at close range, and the inability to observe internal contact forces or slippage. Visuo-tactile perception, combining visual appearance with tactile deformation cues, offers a crucial solution by providing dense, spatially resolved information about contact phenomena.

However, the authors identify two significant challenges that impede the progress of visuo-tactile manipulation:

1. **Scarcity and Cost of Real-World Tactile Data:** Acquiring large-scale, reliable tactile data in the physical world is prohibitively costly and challenging. Real-world tactile sensors are diverse, lack standardization, are expensive to manufacture and deploy, and their data collection processes are tedious. This severely limits the ability to train data-hungry deep learning models for tactile representation learning and policy optimization.
2. **Lack of a Unified Evaluation Platform:** The absence of standardized and comprehensive benchmarks for visuo-tactile manipulation makes it difficult to systematically evaluate and compare different policies and algorithms, hindering iterative improvement and scientific progress.

To address these critical limitations, the research paper sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Objective 1: Develop a Unified Simulation Platform for Visuo-Tactile Data Generation.** The primary goal is to create "UniVTAC," a high-fidelity, simulation-based platform capable of synthesizing large-scale and controllable visuo-tactile interaction data. This platform is designed to support three commonly used visuo-tactile sensors and generate informative contact interactions, including pressure patterns, marker deformations, and tangential force cues, to overcome the real-world data scarcity.

- **Objective 2: Learn Tactile-Centric Representations with the UniVTAC Encoder.** Leveraging the scalable synthetic data generated by the platform, the authors aim to develop the "UniVTAC Encoder." This encoder will be trained using carefully designed multi-task supervisory signals derived from simulation-privileged ground truth. The objective is to learn robust, tactile-centric visuo-tactile representations that capture fine-grained contact boundaries, object pose, and interaction dynamics, suitable for downstream manipulation tasks.

- **Objective 3: Establish a Comprehensive Visuo-Tactile Manipulation Benchmark.** To enable systematic evaluation and comparison of tactile-driven policies, the paper aims to introduce the "UniVTAC Benchmark." This benchmark will consist of eight representative visuo-tactile manipulation tasks, built upon the UniVTAC platform, supporting automated data synthesis and unified policy evaluation to foster reproducible research.

- **Objective 4: Demonstrate Significance through Comprehensive Evaluation and Real-World Validation.** Finally, the work aims to rigorously evaluate the effectiveness of the UniVTAC Encoder and the data generation platform in simulation. Crucially, it seeks to demonstrate the applicability and generalization capabilities of the learned representations through real-world robotic experiments, thereby validating the sim-to-real transfer potential of the proposed framework.

In summary, the overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between the theoretical promise of visuo-tactile perception and its practical application in robust robotic manipulation by providing a scalable, standardized, and physically grounded data and evaluation infrastructure.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UniVTAC framework is designed as an end-to-end, simulation-driven solution for visuo-tactile manipulation, comprising a platform for data synthesis, an encoder for representation learning, and a benchmark for systematic evaluation.

#### 3.1. UniVTAC Platform: Simulation-Based Data Synthesis

The UniVTAC platform extends the TacEx framework, enhancing its soft-body simulation capabilities to support diverse sensor types and complex manipulation tasks.

- **Sensor Configuration and Heterogeneity:** UniVTAC is unique in its support for three mainstream visuo-tactile sensors: GelSight Mini, ViTai GF225, and Xense WS. It models their distinct optical and mechanical properties, allowing for unified algorithm validation across different hardware within a single simulation environment.
- **Automated Manipulation APIs:** To facilitate large-scale, high-fidelity data collection, UniVTAC provides a library of atomic manipulation primitives: Grasp, Move, Place, Probe, and Rotate. A critical innovation here is the *tactile-reactive adaptive control mechanism* embedded within Grasp and Probe. This closed-loop control (Equation 1) dynamically adjusts gripper velocity based on real-time minimum depth from tactile sensors, preventing non-physical penetration and ensuring captured tactile imprints remain within a realistic deformation manifold. This guarantees high-quality, physically consistent training data.
- **Tactile Representation Prerequisites:** The platform is designed to capture ground-truth data aligned with three perception pathways crucial for robust tactile representation:
 - **Shape Perception:** Targets global geometry, disentangling object appearance from sensor artifacts.
 - **Contact Perception:** Focuses on local dynamics, modeling surface deformation and shear-induced marker displacement.
 - **Pose Perception:** Provides spatial grounding, anchoring tactile signals in external metric space for consistent spatial reasoning.
 The pipeline records raw tactile images (`I_marked`) alongside simulation-privileged ground truth: pure contact images (`I_pure`), depth maps (`D`), 2D projections of fiducial markers (`M`), and the object's pose in the gelpad center local frame (`p`).
- **Contact-Rich Data Generation:** The platform curates a geometrically diverse dataset using 14 distinct geometric primitives (indenters) mounted on a prism base. For each episode, the gripper stochastically varies grasping tightness (via a reaching depth threshold `δ_th`) and executes randomized small-scale rotations. This induces rich shear force patterns and marker displacements, generating diverse contact data ranging from light touches to deep indentations. This automated pipeline generates approximately 14,000 interaction frames per shape, totaling 205,826 samples.

#### 3.2. UniVTAC Encoder: Multi-Pathway Representation Learning

The UniVTAC Encoder is formulated as a multi-pathway framework that embeds structured physical priors into tactile observations.

- **Architecture:** It employs a ResNet-18 backbone as a shared encoder to map visuo-tactile observations into a compact latent representation. This latent vector is then fed to multiple pathway-specific decoder heads during training, which are discarded at deployment to ensure no additional inference-time overhead.
- **Supervision Objectives:** The encoder is trained end-to-end using a multi-task objective (Equation 5), incorporating the three perceptual pathways:
 - **Shape Reconstruction Loss (`L_shape`):** Encourages disentanglement of intrinsic object geometry from sensor-specific marker artifacts by jointly reconstructing both raw tactile images with markers (`I_marked`) and counterfactual marker-free images (`I_pure`) using transposed convolutional decoders.
 - **Contact Deformation Loss (`L_contact`):** Captures local deformation dynamics by predicting a dense surface depth map (`D`) and the 2D projections of `N` fiducial markers (`M`) using a similar architecture for depth and a lightweight multilayer perceptron for markers.
 - **Pose Regression Loss (`L_pose`):** Grounds the latent representation in metric space by regressing the relative object pose (`p`, 3D translation + quaternion) using a compact MLP.
- **Loss Function:** All pathways use Mean Squared Error (MSE), with a total loss being a weighted sum of `L_shape`, `L_contact`, and `L_pose` (with empirical weights `λ_s=1.0, λ_c=0.5, λ_p=0.5`).

#### 3.3. UniVTAC Benchmark: Systematic Evaluation

The UniVTAC Benchmark provides a standardized environment for evaluating tactile-driven policies, built upon the UniVTAC platform.

- **Expert-Driven Data Synthesis with Stochasticity:** Unlike conventional expert trajectory generation that often yields "perfect" but tactically uninformative trajectories, UniVTAC introduces *stochasticity and corrective behaviors*. For contact-rich tasks like insertion, intentional randomized failures are introduced, prompting the expert controller (guided by ground-truth physical states in simulation) to perform iterative corrective actions. This generates trajectories rich in diverse contact patterns and corrective interactions, crucial for learning robust tactile-dependent behaviors.
- **Tactile-Specific Success Criteria:** The benchmark moves beyond simple target coordinate checks. A trial is deemed invalid if maximum penetration depth exceeds a safety threshold or if significant relative slip occurs between the gelpad and the object surface. These physics-based constraints ensure that evaluation reflects physically meaningful manipulation behaviors.
- **Representative Tasks:** The benchmark comprises eight visuo-tactile manipulation tasks spanning three perceptual dimensions (Figure 3, Appendix A):
 - **Pose Reasoning:** Lift Bottle, Lift Can, Put Bottle in Shelf.
 - **Shape Perception:** Grasp Classify.
 - **Contact-Rich Interaction:** Insert Hole, Insert HDMI, Insert Tube, Pull Out Key.
 These tasks are designed to systematically evaluate policies under varying degrees of tactile dependency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with UniVTAC yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the platform's utility, the UniVTAC Encoder's efficacy, and the importance of tactile perception in robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on the UniVTAC Benchmark

- **Improved Performance with Tactile Feedback:** Policies augmented with the UniVTAC Encoder, integrated into an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) framework, achieved a substantial average success rate of **48.0%** across the eight benchmark tasks. This is a notable **17.1% improvement** over the vision-only ACT baseline (30.9% average success rate). The gains were consistent across contact-sensitive tasks such as insertion and pull-out scenarios, highlighting the effectiveness of tactile feedback provided by UniVTAC.
- **Superiority over Contrastive Learning Baseline:** The UniVTAC Encoder also outperformed VITaL, a strong baseline that utilizes visuo-tactile pretraining based on contrastive learning, which achieved an average success rate of 40.5%. This suggests that UniVTAC's reconstruction-based pretraining, incorporating physically grounded signals, captures more actionable contact information compared to global feature alignment emphasized by contrastive methods.
- **Benchmark's Discriminative Power:** The benchmark successfully differentiated policies, demonstrating that some tasks are more visually solvable, while others fundamentally rely on tactile feedback for robust execution, especially in contact-rich and correction-intensive settings.

#### 4.2. Efficacy of the UniVTAC Encoder (Ablation Studies)

- **Full Model's Optimal Performance:** The complete UniVTAC Encoder, which integrates geometric shape, contact force, and relative pose perception through its multi-pathway pretraining, achieved the best average success rate of **48.0%**. This confirms the synergistic benefits of combining these distinct physical priors.
- **Individual Pathway Contributions:** Ablation studies (Table II) showed that even individual tactile pathways yielded significant performance gains over training from scratch (no pretraining, 34.0% average success). The "Contact-only" variant improved average success by +7.9% (to 41.9%), and "Shape-only" by +7.6% (to 41.6%). This validates the effectiveness of each design component.
- **Importance of Pose Perception:** The "Contact+Shape" variant (43.9% average success) still underperformed the full model. This indicates that the explicit pose perception pathway is essential, particularly for tasks requiring awareness of object orientation and spatial relationship (e.g., Lift Bottle), enabling more spatially consistent reasoning.

#### 4.3. Tactile Data Scaling for Encoder Pretraining

- **Monotonic Performance Improvement:** A data-scaling study (Table III, Figure 4) demonstrated a clear and consistent trend: the performance of the downstream policy (integrated with the UniVTAC Encoder) improved monotonically as the amount of simulation-synthesized tactile data used for pretraining increased.
- **Benefits of Large-Scale Data:** This finding underscores the value of scalable data generation in simulation, confirming that expanding the diversity and volume of tactile manipulation data directly enhances the effectiveness of the pretrained encoder and its contribution to downstream policy performance.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The UniVTAC Encoder, trained *exclusively on simulation-synthesized data*, exhibited strong generalization capabilities to real-world robotic systems. When transferred to a Tianji Robotics Marvin manipulator equipped with ViTai GF225 sensors, it significantly enhanced performance on three challenging dexterous manipulation tasks (Insert Tube, Insert USB, Bottle Upright).
- **25% Average Success Rate Improvement:** Integrating the UniVTAC Encoder led to an average success rate increase of **25%** across real-world tasks (from 43.3% for vision-only to 68.3% with UniVTAC).
- **Task-Specific Gains:**
 - **Insert Tube:** Performance improved from 55% to 85% (+30%).
 - **Insert USB:** Performance improved from 15% to 25% (+10%).
 - **Bottle Upright:** Performance improved from 60% to 95% (+35%).
- **Qualitative Evidence of Enhanced Dexterity:** Qualitative observations (Figure 7) confirmed that tactile-enabled policies performed smoother, more adaptive insertions, with better alignment correction and improved grip stability, significantly reducing collisions, excessive forces, and destructive interactions compared to vision-only baselines. This highlights the crucial role of tactile feedback in precise alignment and stability during manipulation.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that UniVTAC provides a powerful framework for generating high-quality tactile data, learning robust representations, and systematically evaluating visuo-tactile manipulation policies, with strong empirical validation in both simulation and the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniVTAC framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring intricate physical interaction and contact reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing the Core Data Challenge:** UniVTAC's most immediate and profound impact is in overcoming the long-standing challenge of tactile data scarcity. By providing a scalable, controllable, and high-fidelity simulation platform for generating visuo-tactile interaction data, it democratizes access to the vast datasets needed for training modern data-driven robotic policies. This eliminates the prohibitive cost and logistical complexities associated with real-world tactile data collection, accelerating research and development in tactile robotics.

- **Enabling Robust Representation Learning:** The UniVTAC Encoder, trained with physically grounded multi-pathway supervision, offers a novel approach to learning tactile-centric representations. These representations are shown to be more effective than existing methods, capturing fine-grained contact boundaries, object shape, and pose information. This will lead to more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems capable of understanding and responding to complex physical interactions. The ability to disentangle object geometry from sensor artifacts and model local deformation dynamics is crucial for generalizable tactile perception.

- **Establishing a Standardized Evaluation Benchmark:** The UniVTAC Benchmark provides a much-needed standardized platform for evaluating visuo-tactile manipulation strategies. Its eight representative tasks, combined with tactile-specific, physics-based success criteria, ensure fair, reproducible, and meaningful comparisons across different algorithms and policies. This structured evaluation environment will foster healthy competition, benchmark progress, and guide the development of future tactile-driven control schemes.

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The successful real-world deployment of the UniVTAC Encoder, trained *solely in simulation*, is a testament to the high fidelity of the UniVTAC platform and the generalizability of its learned representations. This strong sim-to-real transfer capability is a critical milestone, demonstrating that simulation can be a reliable and cost-effective tool for developing real-world dexterous manipulation skills, reducing the reliance on extensive and risky physical experimentation.

- **Enhancing Robotic Dexterity and Reliability:** The demonstrated improvements in success rates on contact-rich tasks (e.g., insertion, alignment, stable grasping) directly translate to more dexterous, reliable, and safe robotic manipulation in practical applications. Robots equipped with UniVTAC-trained perception systems will be better positioned to handle delicate objects, perform assembly tasks with tight clearances, and adapt to unforeseen contact conditions, mimicking human-like tactile intelligence.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** UniVTAC lays a robust and extensible foundation for future research in several directions. Its modular design, supporting diverse sensor types, can be extended to new sensor modalities (e.g., force-torque, thermal, array-based tactile sensors). The platform can facilitate research into more dynamic interactions, adaptive control schemes, and learning in open-world manipulation scenarios where tactile feedback is indispensable for robustness and safety. It can also serve as a foundational component for developing multi-modal foundation models for robotics that seamlessly integrate vision, language, and touch.

In conclusion, UniVTAC represents a comprehensive and practical solution that addresses critical bottlenecks in data-driven tactile robotics. By providing a unified platform for data generation, representation learning, and benchmarking, coupled with proven sim-to-real transfer capabilities, it is poised to significantly accelerate progress in developing highly capable and intelligent robotic systems for contact-rich manipulation.
