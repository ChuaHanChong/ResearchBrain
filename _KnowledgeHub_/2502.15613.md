# Pick-and-place Manipulation Across Grippers Without Retraining: A Learning-optimization Diffusion Policy Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> A learning-optimization hybrid framework enables robotic pick-and-place policies to adapt to diverse gripper configurations without requiring retraining. This Gripper-Aware Diffusion Policy (GADP) achieved an average task success rate of 93.3% for seen objects and 70.0% for unseen objects across six novel grippers, significantly outperforming baselines, while preventing collisions.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation policies incur high retraining costs and exhibit limited generalizability when gripper configurations are changed, necessitating new data collection for each end-effector.
- Variations in gripper morphology, particularly Tool-Center-Point (TCP) offsets, cause pre-trained policies to generate inaccurate trajectories, leading to collisions or missed grasps when applied to novel grippers.
- Existing multi-embodiment learning methods often require substantial embodiment-specific data or computationally intensive fine-tuning, restricting their practical deployment for behavior cloning algorithms like diffusion policies.

## Method

- ==The Gripper-Aware Diffusion Policy (GADP)== integrates a learning-optimization hybrid strategy into a diffusion policy, designed for zero-shot adaptation to novel grippers.
- A gripper-invariant grasping probability map (G*_prob), derived from a pre-trained GG-CNN and stabilized through filtering and masking, provides robust, object-centric grasp affordances decoupled from gripper appearance.
- Differentiable gripper mapping functions are used to project novel gripper parameters (TCP height and grasping width) to the reference gripper's basis, while a safety-constrained trajectory projection layer integrates quadratic programming into the final denoising steps of DDIM inference to ensure collision-free and temporally consistent trajectories.

## Results

- The GADP achieved an average pick-and-place success rate of **93.3%** for a seen object and **70.0%** for an unseen object across six distinct gripper configurations, outperforming baseline Diffusion Policy (DP) and Diffusion Policy 3D (DP 3D) by over 60 percentage points on average.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that both the gripper-invariant grasping probability map (G*_prob) and the safety-constrained trajectory projection module are critical components for the policy's robust generalization, especially to unseen objects and visually distinct grippers.
- Safety analysis confirmed that the proposed policy effectively prevented collisions with the table and ensured accurate object interaction for grippers with varying lengths and TCP offsets, dynamically adapting motion to maintain a safe distance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stable, gripper-agnostic grasping knowledge, effectively captured by a processed grasping probability map, is crucial for maintaining consistent approach trajectories despite variations in gripper morphology and viewpoint.
> - Explicitly modeling gripper physical characteristics and mapping them to a reference frame allows a single learned policy to generalize across diverse end-effectors without explicit retraining.
> - Integrating online, hard safety constraints through an iterative projection method within the diffusion denoising process ensures task completion and collision avoidance, enhancing the robustness of data-driven policies to hardware changes.
