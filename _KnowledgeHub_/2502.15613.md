# Pick-and-place Manipulation Across Grippers Without Retraining: A Learning-optimization Diffusion Policy Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> A learning-optimization hybrid framework enables robotic pick-and-place policies to adapt to diverse gripper configurations without requiring retraining. This Gripper-Aware Diffusion Policy (GADP) achieved an average task success rate of 93.3% for seen objects and 70.0% for unseen objects across six novel grippers, significantly outperforming baselines, while preventing collisions.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation policies incur high retraining costs and exhibit limited generalizability when gripper configurations are changed, necessitating new data collection for each end-effector.
- Variations in gripper morphology, particularly Tool-Center-Point (TCP) offsets, cause pre-trained policies to generate inaccurate trajectories, leading to collisions or missed grasps when applied to novel grippers.
- Existing multi-embodiment learning methods often require substantial embodiment-specific data or computationally intensive fine-tuning, restricting their practical deployment for behavior cloning algorithms like diffusion policies.

## Method

- ==The Gripper-Aware Diffusion Policy (GADP)== integrates a learning-optimization hybrid strategy into a diffusion policy, designed for zero-shot adaptation to novel grippers.
- A gripper-invariant grasping probability map (G*_prob), derived from a pre-trained GG-CNN and stabilized through filtering and masking, provides robust, object-centric grasp affordances decoupled from gripper appearance.
- Differentiable gripper mapping functions are used to project novel gripper parameters (TCP height and grasping width) to the reference gripper's basis, while a safety-constrained trajectory projection layer integrates quadratic programming into the final denoising steps of DDIM inference to ensure collision-free and temporally consistent trajectories.

## Results

- The GADP achieved an average pick-and-place success rate of **93.3%** for a seen object and **70.0%** for an unseen object across six distinct gripper configurations, outperforming baseline Diffusion Policy (DP) and Diffusion Policy 3D (DP 3D) by over 60 percentage points on average.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that both the gripper-invariant grasping probability map (G*_prob) and the safety-constrained trajectory projection module are critical components for the policy's robust generalization, especially to unseen objects and visually distinct grippers.
- Safety analysis confirmed that the proposed policy effectively prevented collisions with the table and ensured accurate object interaction for grippers with varying lengths and TCP offsets, dynamically adapting motion to maintain a safe distance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stable, gripper-agnostic grasping knowledge, effectively captured by a processed grasping probability map, is crucial for maintaining consistent approach trajectories despite variations in gripper morphology and viewpoint.
> - Explicitly modeling gripper physical characteristics and mapping them to a reference frame allows a single learned policy to generalize across diverse end-effectors without explicit retraining.
> - Integrating online, hard safety constraints through an iterative projection method within the diffusion denoising process ensures task completion and collision avoidance, enhancing the robustness of data-driven policies to hardware changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic pick-and-place manipulation is a foundational capability for automation across various sectors, from industrial manufacturing to household applications. A wide array of methods, including reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and motion planning, have been developed to enable robots to perform these tasks, often involving diverse objects and dynamic environments. However, a significant limitation within much of the existing research is the assumption of a fixed end-effector morphology (gripper) during both the training and deployment phases. This constraint severely limits the transferability of learned skills across different grippers and necessitates costly retraining or fine-tuning when a new end-effector is introduced, thereby compromising the overall generalizability and flexibility of robotic systems.

Recent research has attempted to address the challenge of multi-embodiment learning. Approaches like the Heterogeneous Pre-trained Transformer (HPT) aim to train policies on large-scale datasets encompassing diverse robot embodiments. Other methods employ latent space alignment techniques to facilitate skill transfer between different robot manipulators. While these strategies have shown promise, they often still require substantial embodiment-specific data collection or computationally intensive policy fine-tuning to adapt to new gripper configurations. This overhead restricts their practical deployment, particularly for behavior cloning algorithms such as diffusion policies, which are sensitive to shifts in observation-action distributions caused by changes in hardware. Specifically, a simple swap from a training gripper to one with different dimensions, such as a varied tool-center-point (TCP) offset, can lead to collisions or missed grasps if a pre-trained diffusion policy is applied without modification.

The current paper positions itself as a development aimed at bridging this gap. It integrates diffusion policies with a hybrid learning-optimization framework. This approach differentiates itself from prior transfer learning or domain adaptation techniques by seeking to enable zero-shot adaptation to novel grippers without requiring any policy retraining or fine-tuning. It builds upon the established foundation of denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) and their application to robotic control (Diffusion Policy, Diffusion Policy 3D), which generate action sequences iteratively. While existing diffusion models for decision-making can incorporate constraints (e.g., through residual loss, classifier-free guidance, or post-processing optimization), these methods often have limitations such as strict consistency requirements, lack of guaranteed constraint satisfaction, or potential deviation from the data distribution. This work introduces an iterative projection method into the denoising process, specifically tailored to mitigate the time consumption and seamlessly adapt to new grippers without altering the underlying robot dynamics model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic pick-and-place policy that can generalize to novel gripper configurations without requiring additional data collection for retraining or fine-tuning the policy itself. This aims to enable zero-shot adaptation across diverse end-effectors, addressing a significant bottleneck in the flexibility and deployment of robotic systems.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key challenges and limitations in current robotic manipulation policies:

- **High Retraining Costs:** Traditional imitation learning and reinforcement learning policies are typically trained for a specific robot and gripper setup. Any change in the end-effector (e.g., different gripper dimensions, shape, or type) necessitates expensive and time-consuming retraining or fine-tuning. This process involves collecting new datasets for each gripper, which is impractical for real-world applications where gripper interchangeability is often desired.
- **Limited Generalizability:** The assumption of fixed hardware severely restricts the generalizability of learned manipulation skills. Robots cannot easily adapt to new tasks that require different grippers or integrate new gripper designs without substantial effort. This limits their versatility in dynamic environments or flexible manufacturing lines.
- **Tool-Center-Point (TCP) Offset Problem:** When grippers are swapped, the physical dimensions and the location of the Tool-Center-Point (TCP) relative to the robot's end-effector flange often change. A pre-trained policy, unaware of this shift, will generate trajectories based on the original gripper's kinematics, leading to inaccuracies. These inaccuracies can manifest as dangerous collisions with the environment (e.g., the tabletop) or failures to properly grasp the target object due to misaligned approach trajectories (Fig. 1a).
- **Ensuring Task Completion and Safety:** Beyond simply adapting to new grippers, the policy must also guarantee successful task completion (e.g., grasping and placing an object) and ensure safety by avoiding collisions. A naive application of existing policies to new grippers often fails to meet these criteria.
- **Leveraging Diffusion Models while Addressing Their Weaknesses:** Diffusion policies offer advantages in generating coherent action sequences from demonstrations. However, their data-driven nature makes them susceptible to out-of-distribution inputs caused by gripper changes. The motivation is to retain the benefits of diffusion-based learning (e.g., sample efficiency) while introducing a mechanism to make them robust to hardware variations without compromising their core learning.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for robotic manipulation systems that are inherently flexible and adaptable to varying hardware configurations, particularly different grippers, without incurring the prohibitive costs associated with extensive re-training or fine-tuning, while simultaneously guaranteeing task success and operational safety.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces a learning-optimization hybrid strategy into a diffusion-based policy to achieve zero-shot adaptation to novel grippers. The framework, termed Gripper-Aware Diffusion Policy (GADP), consists of offline training and an online adaptation process during inference.

**A. Problem Statement Formalization**
The problem is formalized by defining key components:
- **Gripper Configuration (G):** A `d_g`-dimensional vector representing gripper morphology, maximum width `w_max`, and Tool-Center-Point (TCP) `z`.
- **Observation Domain (O):** Comprises `S_sce` (3D point clouds of the scene) and `S_rob` (robot state: end-effector pose `x_ee` and gripper width `g`). `x_ee` is gripper-agnostic, while `g` is gripper-specific.
- **Action Space (A):** `d_a`-dimensional space of end-effector displacements `Δx_ee` and gripper commands.
The policy `π_θ` is trained on demonstrations collected with a reference gripper `G_0`. The core challenge is that deploying `π_θ` with a novel gripper `G_i ≠ G_0` leads to observation-action distribution shifts, resulting in trajectory divergence (e.g., collisions, missed grasps). The goal is to develop `π*θ` that maintains performance under gripper variation without retraining.

**B. Learning Gripper-agnostic Grasping Knowledge**
Visuomotor policies rely heavily on visual observations. Changes in gripper morphology during online execution alter visual inputs (RGB, point clouds), potentially causing out-of-distribution trajectory generations. To mitigate this, the method introduces a **gripper-invariant grasping probability map (G*_prob)** as an additional observation component. This map focuses on object-centric grasp affordances, decoupling them from gripper appearance.

- **G*_prob Synthesis:** The Generative Grasping CNN (GG-CNN), pre-trained on the Cornell Grasping Dataset, is used to generate an initial grasping probability map (`G_prob`) from depth images.
- **Addressing Real-World Instability:** To counter instability in GG-CNN outputs caused by hand-eye camera movement (scale variations) and lighting changes, a stabilization process is applied:
 1. **Threshold Filtering:** Pixels with `G_prob < 0.7` are discarded.
 2. **Centroid Computation:** The centroid `O` of the remaining high-probability pixels is calculated.
 3. **Region Masking:** A circular mask (radius `r = 30` pixels) is applied around `O` to generate `G*_prob`. This spatial filtering removes outliers and noise.
- **Final Observation:** The policy's multimodal observation `O*` consists of `G*_prob`, `S_sce` (scene point clouds), and `S_rot` (robot state, adjusted for rotation, though rotational compensation is noted as future work).

**C. Optimizing Trajectory Generation**
The policy `π_θ` uses a Diffusion Policy (DP) backbone, specifically the Simple DP3 implementation, trained with `O*` and Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss. During inference, Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) sampling with 10 denoising iterations is employed. The key innovations for cross-gripper adaptation are:

1. **Gripper Mapping:**
 - **Problem:** Gripper morphological variations lead to discrepancies in the end-effector's pose (TCP height offset `Δh`) and grasping width (`g`).
 - **Solution:** Differentiable mapping functions `M_h(·)` and `M_g(·)` are defined to project the parameters of a novel gripper `G_i` to the basis of the reference gripper `G_0`:
 - `z'^(i) = M_h(z^(i)) = z^(i) + Δh^(i)` (adjusts vertical TCP height).
 - `g'^(i) = M_g(g^(i)) = α^(i)g^(i)` (scales gripper width).
 - **Calibration:** The mapping parameters `{Δh^(i), α^(i)}` are obtained through offline mechanical measurement of gripper dimensions.
 - **Integration:** The transformed robot pose `S'_rob = (x, y, z'^(i), g'^(i))` is fed into `π_θ` instead of the raw `S_rob`, maintaining observation-space consistency.

2. **Safety-Constrained Trajectory Projection:**
 - **Problem:** Even with gripper mapping, subtle visual perception differences from gripper morphology can still lead to unsafe or task-failing trajectories.
 - **Solution:** A projection layer is integrated into the DDIM denoising process. This projection is activated only in the final few denoising steps (`k ≤ 2`) for efficiency.
 - **a) Constraint-aware Denoising (Quadratic Programming):** At each relevant denoising step `k`, a quadratic program (QP) problem is solved to find the minimal corrective offset `ν_k*t`:
 `min ||ν_k*t||^2_2`
 `s.t. S'_rob(z)_t + Φ(a_k*t) + ν_k*t ≥ ε_safe`
 Where `Φ(·)` maps latent actions to Cartesian displacement (denormalization) and `ε_safe` is a defined safety margin (0.01 m). This ensures the end-effector maintains a minimum safe distance from the table.
 - **b) Temporal Consistency Enforcement:** To ensure safety across the entire `T_a`-step action horizon, cumulative constraints are applied:
 `S'_rob(z)_t + Σ_r=0^j Φ(a_k*t+r) + ν_k*t+j ≥ ε_safe`
 This guarantees that the entire generated trajectory remains safe, and `P(∩_{j=0}^{T_a-1} {S'_rob(z)_t+j ≥ ε_safe}) = 1`.
 - **Execution:** The projected actions `a_k*t` are then used to update the trajectory.

**D. Experiment Setup**
The system uses a Franka Emika Panda robot with various grippers (custom 3D-printed, flexible, Robotiq 2F-85). Perception is handled by an egocentric Azure Kinect DK and a fixed Intel RealSense D455 camera. Control and data synchronization are managed via ROS Noetic on an Ubuntu 20.04 workstation.

**E. Data Collection and Training**
- **Data Collection:** 59 expert demonstrations of pick-and-place tasks were collected using the base gripper `G_0` and a pink block. Multimodal observations included proprioceptive states (6-DoF end-effector pose, gripper width), 3D point clouds, and the `G*_prob` map. Kinesthetic demonstrations provided relative Cartesian displacements and gripper actions.
- **Training:** The Simple DP3 backbone was adapted to Simple DP3 MM (Multi-Modal Simple Diffusion Policy 3D). This architecture concatenates encoded proprioceptive states, point cloud features, and `G*_prob` (encoded via a ResNet-18). The model was trained for 8,000 epochs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation was structured to assess the stability of the grasping probability maps, the generalization capability of the policy across different grippers and objects, and the safety of the generated trajectories.

**A. Evaluation of Grasping Probability Map**
The effectiveness of the gripper-agnostic grasping probability map (`G*_prob`) was assessed under variations in graspable objects, gripper morphology, and viewpoint changes (robot height). Visualizations (Fig. 4) indicated that raw GG-CNN outputs (`G_prob`) exhibited multimodal distributions and instability under these perturbations. In contrast, the processed `G*_prob` consistently showed unimodal, concentrated distributions. Quantitative analysis using KL divergence (Table I) confirmed this, demonstrating that `G*_prob` provided significantly more stable grasping information (KL divergence values of 0.000 across all compared conditions for `G*_prob`, compared to values ranging from 0.009 to 2.271 for `G_prob`). This stability in grasp cues was deemed critical for generating consistent approach trajectories despite sensory variations.

**B. Policy Performance Comparison across Grippers**
The policies were evaluated on pick-and-place tasks using a Franka Panda robot with six different gripper configurations (`G_0` to `G_5`, including 3D-printed and Robotiq 2F-85 grippers), manipulating both a seen object (block) and an unseen object (banana). Training was exclusively performed with `G_0` and the block.

- **Seen Object (Block):**
 - **Our Full Method:** Achieved an average task success rate of 93.3% across all six gripper configurations, demonstrating robust generalization. It achieved 100% success for `G_0`, `G_2`, `G_4`, `G_5`, and 80% for `G_1`, `G_3`.
 - **Baselines (Diffusion Policy (DP), Diffusion Policy 3D (DP 3D)):** Showed significantly lower average success rates of 26.7% and 23.3%, respectively. Even with the training gripper `G_0`, these baselines only achieved 20% success, often failing due to imprecise motion leading to collisions with the table.
 - **Ablation (Ours w/o Projection):** Achieved an average success rate of 33.3%. While successful with `G_0` (100%), it largely failed (0% success) for grippers (`G_1`, `G_4`, `G_5`) that deviated significantly from the training gripper, highlighting the critical role of the safety-constrained trajectory projection.
 - **Ablation (Ours w/o G*_prob):** Achieved an average success rate of 53.3%. Its performance degraded for grippers (`G_1`, `G_5`) that appeared visually distinct from `G_0` to the Kinect camera (success rates of 0% and 20%), underscoring the importance of gripper-agnostic grasping knowledge.
- **Unseen Object (Banana):**
 - **Our Full Method:** Demonstrated robust generalization to unseen object geometries, achieving an average success rate of 70.0% across grippers. It achieved 100% success for `G_2`, 80% for `G_0`, and 60% for `G_1`, `G_3`, `G_4`, `G_5`.
 - **Baselines (DP, DP 3D):** Exhibited low success rates, averaging 30.0% and 20.0%, respectively. Their performance completely collapsed (0% success) when object positions were randomized, indicating a strong reliance on position-specific motion primitives.
 - **Ablation (Ours w/o Projection):** Achieved an average success rate of 30.0%, failing for `G_2`, `G_4`, `G_5`.
 - **Ablation (Ours w/o G*_prob):** Achieved an average success rate of 33.3%.
 These results indicate that both the projection module and the gripper-agnostic grasping knowledge (`G*_prob`) are crucial for robust generalization, especially for unseen objects and varying gripper geometries.

**C. Safety Analysis**
The safety analysis focused on how gripper mapping and safety-constrained trajectory projection ensured safe trajectories and task completion.
- **Baselines on `G_0`:** DP and DP 3D frequently resulted in collisions with the table (TCP height falling below the safety line), leading to task failure (Fig. 6a).
- **Baselines with Projection on `G_0`:** Introducing the safety-constrained trajectory projection during inference enabled both DP and DP 3D to successfully complete the task without collisions, by dynamically adjusting the TCP height to remain above the safety line.
- **Longer Gripper `G_1` (+3.5cm TCP shift):** DP, DP 3D, and the "Ours w/o Projection" ablation consistently caused table collisions. The full proposed policy, however, successfully adapted the motion to the extended gripper length, maintaining collision-free execution.
- **Shorter Gripper `G_4` (-4cm TCP shift):** The "Ours w/o Projection" ablation failed to adapt, resulting in the gripper attempting to grasp the object too high, leading to failure (ungrasped). The full proposed policy generated adaptive motions that accounted for the shorter gripper length, ensuring accurate and stable object grasping.
These results demonstrate that the combination of gripper mapping and safety-constrained trajectory projection is effective in preventing collisions and ensuring accurate object interaction across different gripper geometries.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a notable challenge in robotic manipulation: adapting pick-and-place skills across diverse grippers without requiring policy retraining or fine-tuning. This capability has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Reduced Operational Costs and Increased Flexibility:** By enabling zero-shot adaptation to new grippers, the method eliminates the need for extensive data collection and computational resources typically associated with retraining policies for each new end-effector. This directly reduces development and deployment costs, allowing robots to be more easily integrated into flexible manufacturing lines or research environments where gripper changes are frequent. It facilitates rapid prototyping and deployment with varied tools.

- **Enhanced Robot Generalizability and Versatility:** The ability to transfer manipulation knowledge without re-learning means robots can quickly adapt to a wider range of tasks and objects that might necessitate different gripper types. This enhances the versatility of robotic systems, allowing them to handle diverse items in logistics, assembly, or service robotics applications without significant downtime for reconfiguration.

- **Improved Robustness to Hardware Variations:** The hybrid learning-optimization approach inherently makes the policy more robust to physical changes in robot hardware. The dynamic optimization at inference time directly addresses the problem of Tool-Center-Point (TCP) shifts and gripper width variations, which are common sources of failure for purely data-driven policies when hardware is modified. This adds a layer of reliability to robotic systems.

- **Advancement in Diffusion-based Policies:** The work extends the applicability and robustness of diffusion models for robotic control. By integrating hard constraints through an online optimization process during denoising, it demonstrates how to ensure task completion and safety, moving beyond purely generative capabilities to physically grounded and guaranteed behaviors. This could inspire further research into constraint-aware diffusion models in robotics and other domains.

- **Facilitation of Custom End-Effector Design:** With the reduced barrier to integrating new grippers, researchers and engineers can more freely design and implement custom or 3D-printed end-effectors tailored to specific tasks, fostering innovation in robot hardware without being constrained by the need for extensive software adaptation.

- **Potential for Broader Application:** While demonstrated on pick-and-place tasks, the core principle of a learning-optimization hybrid for hardware-agnostic skill transfer could be extended to other robotic manipulation primitives, leading to more adaptable and general-purpose robots.

Future work outlined in the paper, such as describing gripper configurations with free-form language instructions and extending to SE(6)-space and three-finger grippers, indicates further potential to enhance the flexibility and applicability of the approach, moving towards more intuitively configurable and versatile robotic systems.
