# MOCHI: Motion Enhancement of Collaborative Human-object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> MOCHI introduces a two-stage framework to enhance multi-human object interaction (MHOI) data, refining full-body motions and synthesizing plausible finger-level hand grasps from noisy input. The method reduces motion artifacts such as foot skate from 0.56 to 0.17 and jitter from 71.25 to 19.89 on the CORE4D-Original dataset, and improves contact plausibility (F1 score up to 0.91) while demonstrating capabilities for data augmentation and creation.

## Problem

- Existing multi-human object interaction (MHOI) datasets often lack detailed, fine-grained finger-level articulation due to capture complexity.
- Full-body motions in captured MHOI data frequently contain unnatural artifacts, jitter, and inconsistencies arising from occlusions and retargeting issues.
- Current MHOI data collection methods are costly, hardware-intensive, and struggle with scalability and variability, limiting the development of robust models.

## Method

- **MOCHI** proposes a two-stage framework: first, hand-object interaction generation, then full-body motion refinement, to decompose the complex MHOI enhancement problem.
- Stage 1 synthesizes plausible grasping hand motions using an optimization pipeline that minimizes ==force closure==, object interaction, hand plausibility, and a novel regularization loss that preserves input body motion semantics via cylindrical bounds.
- Stage 2 refines full-body motions using a diffusion-based motion prior, guided by a noise optimization approach with objective functions enforcing pose preservation, contact consistency (with Stage 1 grasps), interaction graph dynamics, collision avoidance, and energy minimization.

## Results

- On the CORE4D-Original dataset, MOCHI reduced foot skate from **0.56** to **0.17** and jitter from **71.25** to **19.89**, with similar improvements for synthetically noisy and HMR-processed CORE4D data (e.g., contact F1 scores increased from **0.44** to **0.66** for HMR).
- The method successfully generates plausible hand-object interactions, increasing contact coverage from **6.72** to **17.13** and decreasing penetration from **0.53** to **0.48** when compared to original CORE4D hand mocap.
- MOCHI effectively post-processes outputs from generative models, augments MHOI data with varying object geometries, creates natural interactions from sparse keyframes, and extends to scenarios with more than two participants or mixed static/dynamic interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the MHOI enhancement problem into separate hand and body stages allows leveraging more abundant single-person motion and hand-object interaction priors.
> - A regularization loss with cylindrical bounds during grasp optimization is effective in ensuring semantic consistency between optimized hand poses and the input body motion.
> - Diffusion noise optimization, combined with specific loss terms (pose, contact, interaction-graph, collision, energy), enables robust refinement of noisy full-body motions while preserving interaction semantics and generating physically plausible results.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the challenges of acquiring and enhancing high-quality data for collaborative multi-human object interaction (MHOI) scenarios. Such scenarios involve dynamic and complex movements requiring mutual anticipation and continuous adjustment between participants and a shared object. Understanding and modeling MHOI is foundational for applications in human-robot collaboration, humanoid manipulation, character animation, and immersive virtual reality.

Current methodologies for MHOI data collection present significant difficulties. Traditional marker-based motion capture systems are limited to constrained environments, susceptible to occlusion-induced tracking failures, and require costly, complex hardware. More scalable solutions, such as monocular camera-based motion capture, often exhibit reduced accuracy and artifacts due to depth ambiguities and single-view constraints. Consequently, existing MHOI data frequently suffers from issues such as contact misalignment between hands and objects, motion jitter, temporal inconsistencies, and missing or incomplete finger-level articulation. Scaling up data collection with these methods remains challenging due to their inherent limitations.

Within the broader landscape of interaction motion synthesis, substantial progress has been made in single-person human-object interaction (HOI), with methods leveraging deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and diffusion models to synthesize natural human motions. Examples include OMOMO and InterDiff for object interaction, and PhysHOI and Skillmimic-V2 for physics-based character control. However, MHOI scenarios, where multiple humans collaboratively manipulate a shared object, remain less explored. Recent works like TeamHOI and CooHOI have extended physics-based character control to MHOI, but their demonstrations are limited to specific objects and tasks. Uni-Inter has introduced a unified model for various interaction contexts.

Datasets for human-object interaction have also advanced, with BEHAVE and FORCE capturing full-body interactions in controlled environments, though they still face occlusion issues. CIRCLE and TRUMANS use VR for diverse interactions but focus on single participants. HOI-M3 captures multiple participants but lacks collaborative interactions. CORE4D specifically targets multi-human collaborative interactions using a hybrid inertial-optical system, yet it still contends with scalability, motion artifacts, and occlusion-induced tracking errors.

Research into human-human interaction synthesis is also active, employing motion patches, DRL, and deep generative models for diverse behaviors. Similarly, hand-object interaction generation has been extensively studied through optimization-based methods and deep neural networks, often for single-person scenarios, some combining body and hand motions. Notably, methods like Wu et al. and HOIDiNi [Ron et al. 2025] employ optimization and generative models for single-person HOI synthesis from high-level controls or text, which differs from the present work's focus on enhancing noisy motion as input.

Motion priors, particularly diffusion models, have been utilized for various motion tasks, including refining noisy motion (RoHM, StableMotion) or reconstructing motion from sparse signals. However, these methods are generally not designed to address the target-specific artifacts inherent in complex interaction scenarios.

This work, MOCHI, distinguishes itself by being the first approach specifically designed for MHOI enhancement that explicitly addresses the simultaneous coexistence of human-object and human-human interactions. It aims to generate physically plausible and natural finger-level and full-body interaction motions from imperfect MHOI data. By decomposing the complex problem, MOCHI leverages more readily available single-person motion priors and hand-object interaction priors, thus complementing existing data collection efforts by providing a pipeline for refining noisy or implausible motions and supporting data augmentation and creation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general method for enhancing the quality of imperfectly captured collaborative multi-human object interaction (MHOI) motion sequences. This method is intended to be applicable to various types of input data, including noisy capture results and erroneous outputs from generative models.

The motivation for MOCHI stems from several identified shortcomings in existing MHOI data and capture methods:

1. **Missing Detailed Hand Motions:** In human-object manipulation scenarios, which constitute a significant category of human interaction, fine-grained finger-level articulation details are frequently absent. This is attributed to the inherent complexity of finger movements and the technical challenges associated with capturing such detailed hand motions reliably.
2. **Unnatural Full-Body Motion Artifacts:** MHOI scenarios are difficult to capture accurately, often leading to full-body motions exhibiting unnatural artifacts. These artifacts primarily arise from challenges such as frequent occlusions between participants and with the interacting object, which can lead to tracking failures, as well as inconsistencies introduced during the retargeting of raw motion capture data.
3. **Limited Data Variability and Scalability:** The complexity and diversity of real-world MHOI scenarios exceed the representational capabilities of current MHOI datasets. Existing data collection methods are often costly and hardware-intensive, hindering the ability to scale up data acquisition to cover a broader range of interactions. This limitation restricts the development of robust models for understanding and synthesizing MHOI.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a two-stage framework that decomposes the complex MHOI data enhancement task. This decomposition strategy is motivated by the observation that high-quality data and priors for single-person motion and hand-object interactions are relatively abundant and feasible to collect, whereas MHOI-specific data remains scarce and challenging to acquire. By breaking down the problem, MOCHI aims to leverage these existing, more accessible priors to improve the quality of MHOI data, while preserving the original interaction semantics embedded in the noisy input. The ultimate goal is to generate visually and physically plausible MHOI sequences that include both refined body and synthesized hand motions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MOCHI is a two-stage framework designed to enhance noisy multi-human object interaction (MHOI) data. The input to the system is an MHOI motion sequence, potentially containing artifacts, defined by the motion of multiple persons (`Mi`, `Mj`), the object pose trajectory (`O`), and its geometry (`G_obj`). The output is an enhanced sequence, including refined body motions (`^Mi`, `^Mj`), a refined object trajectory (`^O`), the original object geometry, and synthesized hand motions (`Hi`, `Hj`).

#### 3.1. Data Representation and Preprocessing

Human body poses are represented as 3D joint positions or root translation/rotation combined with local joint rotations. Hand poses (`h_t`) are reparameterized as a 51-dimensional vector, comprising wrist orientation and translation in object space, and local joint rotations for all finger joints. Before enhancement, the input object trajectory is preprocessed to remove jitter by identifying and interpolating noisy object poses based on abrupt acceleration changes.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Hand-Object Interaction Generation

This stage focuses on synthesizing plausible hand grasping motions. It operates under the premise that grasping hand poses and their spatial relationship to the object remain consistent during contact.
- **Identifying Contact Phase:** Contact labels are initially generated by a distance threshold (8cm) between the wrist and the object. A sliding window convolution (6 frames) refines these labels to identify consecutive contact frames. The contact phases are further refined based on object motion: for collaborative manipulation, contact phases align with object movement; for handovers, a brief temporal overlap and union with object movement define the phases.
- **Grasping Hand Pose Generation:** An optimization pipeline determines an optimal grasping hand pose (`h*`) for each hand during its contact phase. To account for noisy input, a batched optimization approach is used, constructing an initial solution batch from lower arm positions across all contact frames. These initial solutions are augmented with random wrist orientation perturbations for broader exploration.
 The optimization objective (Equation 1) minimizes a combination of four loss terms:
 - `L_fc` (Force Closure): Encourages stable grasps by minimizing the wrench on the object (Equation 2). Contact points are sampled from the hand surface using a Metropolis–Hastings algorithm.
 - `L_obj` (Object Interaction): Consists of `L_dist` (point-to-mesh distance between hand contact points and object surface) and `L_pen` (penetration of object vertices inside the hand mesh), both utilizing signed distance functions (SDF) (Equation 3).
 - `L_hp` (Hand Plausibility): Promotes natural hand shapes and plausible poses, including `L_spen` (self-penetration among hand vertices) and `L_prior` (encourages hand pose parameters to conform to a pretrained multivariate Gaussian distribution) (Equation 4).
 - `L_reg` (Regularization): A novel term designed to preserve input body motion semantics. A cylindrical bound (`C`) is constructed around the lower arm, derived from the averaged wrist and elbow positions across contact frames (Equation 5). This `L_reg` enforces the optimized hand geometry to remain within this cylinder (Equation 6), ensuring semantic consistency.
 Initialization for optimization sets the wrist at the cylinder's upper base, with orientation aligned to the cylinder axis. During optimization, the object's 6DoF pose is perturbed with annealing noise to prevent local minima. The optimal `h*` is selected from all candidates based on the lowest loss value.
- **Hand Motion Generation for Contact Transitions:** This step generates hand motions for pre-contact and post-contact phases to ensure smooth transitions between rest and grasp poses. A conditional diffusion model, trained on hand-object interaction datasets (e.g., GRAB), learns interaction priors. It takes a hand-object proximity feature (`Π`), derived from wrist 6DoF trajectory and hand mesh distances to the object, and the optimized `h*` as input to generate the full finger motion sequence.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Full Body Motion Refinement

This stage refines the full-body motions of all participants, ensuring consistency with the generated hand grasps from Stage 1, while preserving original interaction semantics and removing artifacts. This is framed as a conditional generation process guided by a diffusion-based motion prior. A key aspect is the use of a motion prior trained exclusively on single-person motion data, addressing the scarcity of high-quality MHOI-specific training data.
- **Diffusion Model Basics:** The system utilizes Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs). A forward process incrementally adds Gaussian noise to clean data, while a reverse process reconstructs the original data from noise using a neural network (`m_θ`) conditioned on auxiliary information.
- **Diffusion Noise Optimization:** A noise optimization approach is adopted, where the latent noise (`x_τ`) is iteratively optimized. This involves denoising `x_τ` to obtain `x_0`, computing an objective function `L` on `x_0`, and backpropagating gradients to update `x_τ`. DDIM is used for the reverse process, with gradient normalization and random perturbations to `x_τ` for exploration. The process is applied incrementally for each human subject.
 The per-frame objective (`l_t`) for optimization (Equation 11) combines five loss terms:
 - `L_pose` (Pose Objective): Penalizes deviations from the input motion, comprising `L_body` (joint position difference with added Gaussian noise to prevent overfitting to erroneous input), `L_foot` (penalizes foot penetration or hovering), and `L_fs` (foot slip loss, penalizing foot velocity during ground contact, applied in later iterations) (Equation 12).
 - `L_contact` (Contact Objective): Enforces alignment between generated wrist positions and target wrist positions from Stage 1 grasps during the contact phase (Equation 13). If input body is far from the object, `L_pose` switches to root-relative measurements to prioritize contact.
 - `L_IG` (Interaction-Graph Objective): Preserves coordinated movements and dynamics between participants (Equation 14). An interaction graph (`Γ`) with self-edges (within subject: shoulder, hip, knee) and cross-edges (between subjects: shoulder, hip, head, neck) is constructed. It includes directional alignment (`L_dir`) and velocity alignment (`L_vel`) terms. Positional error is excluded from `L_IG` due to noise sensitivity, as it is addressed by `L_pose`.
 - `L_col` (Collision Objective): Prevents inter-character collisions (using spherical bounding volumes at head, shoulder, collar joints, and optionally human-object collisions). This objective is activated in later optimization steps and for subsequent human subjects.
 - `L_em` (Energy Minimization Objective): Reduces excessively large movements by penalizing joint accelerations, with higher weights for joints closer to the pelvis (Equation 15).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of MOCHI was demonstrated through qualitative and quantitative evaluations across various types of noisy MHOI data and diverse application scenarios.

#### 4.1. Improving Existing MHOI Datasets

The method was evaluated using three variants of the CORE4D dataset: CORE4D-Original (IMU-based mocap), CORE4D-Noisy (synthetically degraded), and CORE4D-HMR (processed with monocular video-based motion capture).
- **Body Refinement:** Quantitative results (Table 1, top) showed significant improvements. For CORE4D-Original, MOCHI reduced foot skate from 0.56 to 0.17 and jitter from 71.25 to 19.89. For CORE4D-Noisy and CORE4D-HMR, the method consistently improved contact plausibility (F1 scores increased from 0.79 to 0.91 for Noisy, and 0.44 to 0.66 for HMR) and motion naturalness (foot skate and jitter were substantially reduced). Qualitative visualizations (Figures 6, 7, 8) confirmed that artifacts such as foot hovering/slipping, unnatural body poses, and inconsistent contacts were effectively corrected.
- **Hand-Object Interaction Generation:** Comparing synthesized hand motions (from CORE4D-Original body input) against the original CORE4D hand mocap data (Table 2, Figure 9), MOCHI outperformed the baseline. Contact coverage increased from 6.72 to 17.13, and penetration decreased from 0.53 to 0.48. This indicated that MOCHI successfully generated plausible and stable grasps, addressing issues of distorted fingers and floating hands present in the original captured data.

#### 4.2. Post-processing Pipeline for Generative Models

MOCHI was applied as a post-processing step to outputs from a modified OMOMO generative model (trained for two-person interactions). Quantitative (Table 1, bottom) and qualitative (Figure 10) results indicated that MOCHI refined the generated body motions, producing more consistent contact, fewer artifacts, and synthesizing plausible hand motions even when only body motion was provided by the generative model.

#### 4.3. Augmentation to Object Variations

The pipeline's ability to augment MHOI data by replacing objects with different geometries was tested using chair-related interactions from CORE4D-Original. Figure 11 showed that the penetration metric for augmented objects remained within a similar range to the original. Visual results (Figure 12) demonstrated successful generation of plausible hand motions adapted to new object geometries while preserving original interaction semantics.

#### 4.4. MHOI Creation with Keyframing

MOCHI was used to create MHOI data from sparse keyframes and objects generated by text-to-3D models. The pipeline effectively refined unnatural skating artifacts and coordination issues introduced by initial interpolation, transforming them into natural interactions (Figures 13, 14). This indicated the method's generalization capabilities to arbitrary objects and its utility as a data creation tool.

#### 4.5. Extensions to Diverse MHOI Scenarios

- **Multi-Person Collaboration (>2 People):** For scenarios with three or four participants, an additional step involving repulsion vectors during arm cylinder construction in Stage 1 was introduced to prevent hand overlaps. Visual results (Figure 15) demonstrated that this effectively prevented inter-person hand collisions during grasping.
- **Mixed Static and Dynamic Object Interactions:** The method was extended to include both static (e.g., sitting on a chair) and dynamic interactions. By modifying the contact objective for static cases (using the hip joint for sitting), MOCHI successfully corrected hovering artifacts for seated interactions and refined dynamic object manipulation (Figure 16).

#### 4.6. Extensions to Single-Person HOI

With minor modifications (disabling human-human interaction terms), MOCHI was applied to single-person HOI. Using HOIDiNi's body motion as input, MOCHI's generated hand-object interactions showed higher contact coverage (24.54 vs 22.40) and lower penetration (0.22 vs 0.25) compared to HOIDiNi's joint body and hand output (Table 3, Figure 17), suggesting improved interaction quality.

#### 4.7. Ablation Studies

- **Cylindrical Bounds for Grasp Optimization:** An ablation study (Figure 18) showed that without cylindrical bounds and random hand initialization, grasps could be physically plausible but semantically inconsistent with the input body motion, demonstrating the importance of the regularization loss.
- **Contact:** Removing Stage 1 and the `L_contact` objective led to lower contact scores (F1 0.81 vs 0.94) and visibly inferior contact quality, highlighting the criticality of hand generation and explicit contact enforcement (Table 4, Figure 19, row 1).
- **Diffusion Optimization Objectives:** Evaluation of individual objectives in Stage 2 (Table 5, Figure 19) revealed:
 - `L_pose` was essential for preserving original motion semantics (its removal resulted in high MPJPE and Root PE).
 - `L_IG` was crucial for maintaining interaction semantics, preventing unnatural poses (e.g., twisted torsos) when relative orientations and velocities were not constrained.
 - `L_col` effectively improved collision avoidance, preventing inter-person head collisions observed in its absence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MOCHI presents a significant contribution by being the first framework to directly address the enhancement of multi-human object interaction (MHOI) data, specifically considering the simultaneous presence of both human-object and human-human interactions. This approach fills a gap in the current research landscape, where existing methods often focus on single-person interactions or generalized multi-person scenarios without explicit object manipulation.

The work's impact stems from several key aspects:
- **Enhanced Data Quality:** MOCHI demonstrates the ability to transform noisy and imperfect MHOI data into physically plausible and natural sequences, including detailed finger-level articulation and refined full-body motions. This addresses a critical need for high-quality MHOI data, which is challenging to acquire directly due to inherent complexities and limitations of current motion capture technologies.
- **Leveraging Existing Priors:** By decomposing the MHOI enhancement problem into separate stages—hand-object interaction generation and full-body motion refinement—MOCHI efficiently leverages abundant and high-quality single-person motion priors and hand-object interaction priors. This strategy mitigates the scarcity of MHOI-specific training data, which is a major bottleneck for advancement in this domain.
- **Versatility and Robustness:** The framework has demonstrated robustness across diverse MHOI data sources, including existing motion capture systems and outputs from generative models. Its applicability extends to varying numbers of participants and different interaction types, encompassing both static and dynamic scenarios.
- **Facilitating Data Creation and Augmentation:** MOCHI serves as a pipeline for data augmentation by allowing the modification of interacting object geometries while preserving interaction semantics. Furthermore, its ability to generate high-quality MHOI data from sparse keyframes opens avenues for more efficient and user-assisted content creation, potentially democratizing the production of complex interaction animations for various applications.

The potential impact of MOCHI is broad:
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** High-quality MHOI data is fundamental for training and validating algorithms for robots designed to collaborate with humans in shared tasks, leading to more natural and effective human-robot teams.
- **Humanoid Manipulation and Animation:** The enhanced data can serve as a rich source of reference motions for humanoid character animation and manipulation tasks in virtual environments, improving realism and interaction fidelity.
- **Virtual Reality and Immersive Experiences:** By enabling the creation of more believable and natural multi-character interactions with objects, MOCHI can enhance the realism and immersion of VR/AR applications.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The refined MHOI data and the underlying enhancement techniques can serve as strong kinematic priors for developing next-generation physics-based character control methods and advanced humanoid robot control systems. Exploring the retargeting of these enhanced motions to diverse robotic platforms represents a promising future direction.

Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as potential failure with excessively noisy input where interaction semantics are unidentifiable, challenges with extreme speed variations, and difficulties in handling continuously changing contact patterns. Addressing these limitations in future work could further broaden the applicability and robustness of this research.
