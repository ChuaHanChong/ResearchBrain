# MOCHI: Motion Enhancement of Collaborative Human-object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> MOCHI introduces a two-stage framework to enhance multi-human object interaction (MHOI) data, refining full-body motions and synthesizing plausible finger-level hand grasps from noisy input. The method reduces motion artifacts such as foot skate from 0.56 to 0.17 and jitter from 71.25 to 19.89 on the CORE4D-Original dataset, and improves contact plausibility (F1 score up to 0.91) while demonstrating capabilities for data augmentation and creation.

## Problem

- Existing multi-human object interaction (MHOI) datasets often lack detailed, fine-grained finger-level articulation due to capture complexity.
- Full-body motions in captured MHOI data frequently contain unnatural artifacts, jitter, and inconsistencies arising from occlusions and retargeting issues.
- Current MHOI data collection methods are costly, hardware-intensive, and struggle with scalability and variability, limiting the development of robust models.

## Method

- **MOCHI** proposes a two-stage framework: first, hand-object interaction generation, then full-body motion refinement, to decompose the complex MHOI enhancement problem.
- Stage 1 synthesizes plausible grasping hand motions using an optimization pipeline that minimizes ==force closure==, object interaction, hand plausibility, and a novel regularization loss that preserves input body motion semantics via cylindrical bounds.
- Stage 2 refines full-body motions using a diffusion-based motion prior, guided by a noise optimization approach with objective functions enforcing pose preservation, contact consistency (with Stage 1 grasps), interaction graph dynamics, collision avoidance, and energy minimization.

## Results

- On the CORE4D-Original dataset, MOCHI reduced foot skate from **0.56** to **0.17** and jitter from **71.25** to **19.89**, with similar improvements for synthetically noisy and HMR-processed CORE4D data (e.g., contact F1 scores increased from **0.44** to **0.66** for HMR).
- The method successfully generates plausible hand-object interactions, increasing contact coverage from **6.72** to **17.13** and decreasing penetration from **0.53** to **0.48** when compared to original CORE4D hand mocap.
- MOCHI effectively post-processes outputs from generative models, augments MHOI data with varying object geometries, creates natural interactions from sparse keyframes, and extends to scenarios with more than two participants or mixed static/dynamic interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the MHOI enhancement problem into separate hand and body stages allows leveraging more abundant single-person motion and hand-object interaction priors.
> - A regularization loss with cylindrical bounds during grasp optimization is effective in ensuring semantic consistency between optimized hand poses and the input body motion.
> - Diffusion noise optimization, combined with specific loss terms (pose, contact, interaction-graph, collision, energy), enables robust refinement of noisy full-body motions while preserving interaction semantics and generating physically plausible results.
