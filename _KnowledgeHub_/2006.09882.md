# Unsupervised Learning of Visual Features by Contrasting Cluster Assignments

> [!summary] Summary
> SwAV introduces an unsupervised learning method that generates visual features by contrasting cluster assignments between different augmented views of an image. This approach achieves 75.3% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet linear evaluation with a ResNet-50 and consistently outperforms supervised pretraining on various downstream transfer tasks like object detection and scene recognition, showcasing improved efficiency and scalability.

## Problem

- Contrastive learning methods (e.g., SimCLR, MoCo) require very large batch sizes or memory banks to gather enough negative samples, leading to high computational and memory costs.
- Traditional clustering-based self-supervised methods (e.g., DeepCluster) are 'offline,' needing periodic full passes over the dataset to re-cluster features, which limits their scalability to large or continuously growing datasets.
- Existing data augmentation strategies often rely on only two augmented views per image, potentially missing benefits from more diverse or multi-scale information.

## Method

- Introduces **SwAV** (Swapping Assignments between multiple Views), an online self-supervised learning method that computes ==cluster assignments== ('codes') for features extracted from augmented views of an image.
- Employs a =='swapped prediction' objective== where the representation from one augmented view is used to predict the cluster assignment of another augmented view of the same image.
- Incorporates a =='multi-crop' data augmentation== strategy that generates two standard full-resolution views and multiple additional low-resolution crops to efficiently capture global and local image details.

## Results

- Achieved **75.3%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet linear classification with a ResNet-50 backbone, reducing the gap to supervised pretraining to **1.2%**.
- Outperformed ImageNet supervised pretraining on various transfer tasks, including linear classification (e.g., Places205, VOC07) and object detection (VOC07+12, COCO), demonstrating superior generalizability.
- Showcased improved efficiency and faster convergence, achieving competitive performance with smaller batch sizes and significantly less training time compared to other state-of-the-art methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comparing cluster assignments of different views (swapped prediction) effectively replaces explicit pairwise feature comparisons in self-supervised learning, improving efficiency.
> - Online clustering with soft assignments, leveraging the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm and an equipartition constraint, enables scalable and robust pseudo-label generation within minibatches.
> - A multi-crop data augmentation strategy, combining global and local views, is highly effective and generalizable for learning richer visual representations across diverse scales.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

SwAV operates within the rapidly evolving field of **unsupervised visual representation learning**, also known as **self-supervised learning**. The overarching goal of this field is to learn powerful visual features from large, unlabeled datasets, thereby reducing the reliance on costly and time-consuming manual data annotation. This area has seen tremendous progress, significantly narrowing the performance gap with features learned through supervised pretraining on massive datasets like ImageNet.

The self-supervised learning landscape is broadly characterized by two dominant paradigms:

- **Instance Discrimination / Contrastive Learning:** This paradigm, exemplified by methods like **NPID (Non-Parametric Instance Discrimination)**, **MoCo (Momentum Contrast)**, and **SimCLR (A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations)**, defines a "positive pair" as different augmented views of the same image and "negative pairs" as views from different images. The learning objective is to pull positive pairs closer in the feature space while pushing negative pairs apart.
 - **Strengths:** Highly effective in learning discriminative features.
 - **Challenges:** These methods typically require a large number of negative samples per batch or a large memory bank (MoCo) to be effective, which can lead to high computational costs, significant memory consumption, or the need for extremely large batch sizes (SimCLR). Explicit pairwise feature comparisons are computationally intensive.

- **Clustering-based Methods:** Pioneers in this category include **DeepCluster** and **SeLa (Self-Labelling via Simultaneous Clustering and Representation Learning)**. These methods generally alternate between two phases: clustering the entire dataset's features to generate pseudo-labels, and then training the neural network to predict these pseudo-labels in a supervised fashion.
 - **Strengths:** Can learn meaningful features by grouping similar images.
 - **Challenges:** Historically "offline," meaning they require multiple passes over the entire dataset to compute and update cluster assignments, making them computationally expensive and difficult to scale to truly massive, unlimited datasets. They also often struggle with "trivial solutions" where all samples collapse into a single cluster, requiring specific regularization.

**SwAV's unique contribution** lies in its ability to bridge these two paradigms, leveraging the strengths of both while mitigating their weaknesses. It introduces an **online clustering-based approach** that draws inspiration from the consistency objectives of contrastive learning. Unlike traditional clustering methods, SwAV computes cluster assignments ("codes") *online* within minibatches, making it scalable to unlimited data. Unlike pure contrastive methods, it avoids explicit pairwise feature comparisons, instead comparing *cluster assignments* derived from different views of the same image. This "swapped prediction" mechanism is central to its innovation, allowing it to achieve strong performance without requiring large memory banks or extremely large batch sizes, which are bottlenecks for many existing contrastive methods. Furthermore, the introduction of the "multi-crop" data augmentation strategy is a general improvement applicable across various self-supervised methods, reinforcing SwAV's position as a significant advancement in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the SwAV research is to develop an **unsupervised visual representation learning algorithm** that can learn high-quality, generalizable visual features that not only reduce but potentially *eliminate* the performance gap with features learned from supervised pretraining, particularly on downstream tasks. The authors are motivated by several key challenges and opportunities in the self-supervised learning landscape:

1. **Addressing Computational and Memory Bottlenecks of Contrastive Learning:** State-of-the-art contrastive learning methods (e.g., SimCLR, MoCo) are highly effective but come with significant computational burdens. They either require extremely large batch sizes (SimCLR) to gather enough negative samples within a single batch or rely on large memory banks (MoCo) and momentum encoders to store and access a vast number of negative samples. Both approaches are memory-intensive and computationally demanding, especially at scale. SwAV aims to develop an alternative that achieves comparable or superior performance *without* these memory and compute overheads associated with explicit pairwise feature comparisons or large external queues.

2. **Overcoming Scalability Limitations of Traditional Clustering Methods:** Previous clustering-based self-supervised learning methods (e.g., DeepCluster, SeLa) have shown promise but typically operate in an "offline" fashion. They require periodic full passes over the entire dataset to re-cluster image features and generate pseudo-labels, making them impractical for online learning or extremely large, potentially continuously expanding, datasets. The motivation here is to enable an *online* clustering mechanism that can scale gracefully to unlimited amounts of data, processing information as it arrives without needing to store or re-process the entire dataset.

3. **Improving Data Augmentation Strategies:** Data augmentation is a crucial component of modern self-supervised learning, enabling the model to learn invariances to various transformations. Most methods rely on generating two augmented "views" per image. The authors recognize that comparing more views could be beneficial but quadratically increases computational costs. They are motivated to develop a more efficient **multi-crop** augmentation strategy that provides diverse views (including local details) without significantly increasing memory or compute requirements. This strategy aims to improve the robustness and richness of the learned representations.

4. **Demonstrating Superior Transferability:** Ultimately, the quality of unsupervised features is judged by their performance on downstream tasks (e.g., classification, object detection). The goal is to develop a method whose learned features not only perform well on standard linear evaluation benchmarks (like ImageNet) but also consistently *outperform* supervised pretraining on a diverse set of transfer tasks, confirming their generalizability and utility in real-world applications.

In essence, SwAV seeks to combine the strengths of contrastive and clustering approaches into a single, efficient, and scalable framework that pushes the boundaries of unsupervised representation learning towards true parity, and even superiority, with supervised methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SwAV introduces a novel approach to self-supervised visual feature learning by combining elements of clustering and contrastive learning in an online, efficient manner. The core of its methodology revolves around a "swapped prediction problem" and an innovative multi-crop data augmentation strategy.

#### 3.1. Swapped Prediction Problem and Online Clustering

The central idea behind SwAV is to avoid explicit pairwise feature comparisons, as seen in contrastive learning, by instead comparing **cluster assignments (codes)** of different augmented views of the same image.

- **Feature Extraction:** For an input image $x$, two (or more) augmented "views" ($x_t$ and $x_s$) are generated by applying different random transformations. Each view is then passed through a convolutional neural network (ConvNet) $f_\theta$ (e.g., ResNet-50) to produce a feature vector, which is then projected onto the unit sphere (normalized). Let these features be $z_t$ and $z_s$.
- **Code Generation:** Instead of directly comparing $z_t$ and $z_s$, SwAV first computes "codes" or soft cluster assignments ($q_t$ and $q_s$) for each feature. These codes are obtained by mapping the features to a set of $K$ trainable **prototype vectors** $C = \{c_1, \dots, c_K\}$.
- **Swapped Prediction Loss:** The loss function is defined as a "swapped" prediction problem: $L(z_t, z_s) = \ell(z_t, q_s) + \ell(z_s, q_t)$. This means the representation $z_t$ is used to predict the code $q_s$ of the other view, and vice-versa. The function $\ell(z, q)$ is a cross-entropy loss that measures the fit between feature $z$ and code $q$, essentially predicting the prototype assignment $q$ from feature $z$ using a softmax over dot products with prototypes. This encourages consistency between the learned features of different views of the same image, as they should ideally map to similar cluster assignments.
- **Online Code Computation:** To make the method online and scalable, the codes $q$ are computed dynamically for features within the current mini-batch, rather than requiring a pass over the entire dataset. This is achieved by formulating the code computation as an **optimal transport problem**. Given a batch of $B$ features $Z = [z_1, \dots, z_B]$, the goal is to find a mapping matrix $Q$ (where $Q_{kb}$ represents the assignment of feature $z_b$ to prototype $c_k$) that maximizes the similarity between features and prototypes, subject to an **equipartition constraint**. This constraint ensures that, on average, each prototype is selected equally often across the batch, preventing all samples from collapsing into a single or few prototypes.
- **Sinkhorn-Knopp Algorithm:** The optimal transport problem for $Q$ is solved efficiently using the **Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm**. This iterative algorithm finds a continuous (soft) solution $Q^*$ to the problem. Crucially, SwAV uses these soft assignments, finding that rounding them to discrete (hard) codes, as done in some previous clustering methods, leads to worse performance in the online setting.
- **Handling Small Batches:** When the batch size $B$ is small compared to the number of prototypes $K$, it's difficult to enforce the equipartition constraint effectively. SwAV addresses this by using a small **queue of features from previous batches** to augment the effective batch size used for computing codes. However, only the codes of the *current* batch's features are used in the training loss, distinguishing it from large memory banks in contrastive methods.

#### 3.2. Multi-Crop Data Augmentation Strategy

Beyond the core swapped prediction mechanism, SwAV introduces an innovative data augmentation strategy called "multi-crop" to further enhance representation learning without increasing computational or memory requirements quadratically.

- **Diverse Resolutions:** Instead of just two high-resolution views, multi-crop generates:
 - **Two standard full-resolution crops** (e.g., 224x224 pixels).
 - **V additional low-resolution crops** (e.g., 96x96 pixels) that capture smaller, local parts of the image.
- **Generalization of Loss:** The swapped prediction loss is generalized to compare any two views from the generated set of $V+2$ views.
- **Efficiency:** Using smaller-sized crops for the additional views ensures that the increase in computational cost is minimal. This allows the model to learn invariances across different scales and capture both global context and fine-grained details.
- **Code Generation for Global Views Only:** Importantly, codes are computed *only* for the full-resolution crops. The authors found that computing codes for all crops, including partial small ones, degrades assignment quality and transfer performance.

By combining this online, soft-assignment clustering with a novel multi-scale data augmentation, SwAV creates a robust and efficient self-supervised learning framework. The training process jointly minimizes the swapped prediction loss with respect to both the ConvNet parameters $\theta$ and the prototype vectors $C$ using standard stochastic gradient descent (SGD).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SwAV demonstrates highly impressive results across various benchmarks, consistently setting new state-of-the-art in self-supervised learning and, in several critical aspects, surpassing supervised pretraining.

1. **ImageNet Linear Classification (Frozen Features):**
 - With a standard ResNet-50 backbone, SwAV achieved **75.3% top-1 accuracy** on ImageNet when training a linear classifier on frozen features. This significantly outperformed previous state-of-the-art self-supervised methods like SimCLR (70.0%) and MoCov2 (71.1%), effectively closing the gap with a fully supervised ResNet-50 (76.5%) to just 1.2%.
 - When applied to wider ResNet models (e.g., ResNet-50 with x5 width multiplier), SwAV reached **78.5% top-1 accuracy**, further shrinking the gap to only 0.6% compared to supervised training with similar architectures.

2. **Semi-Supervised Learning on ImageNet:**
 - When finetuning the SwAV-pretrained ResNet-50 with limited labeled data (1% and 10% of ImageNet labels), it achieved **53.9% (1%) and 70.2% (10%) top-1 accuracy**, respectively. This performance surpassed other self-supervised methods and even competed favorably with specialized semi-supervised learning techniques not primarily designed for representation learning.

3. **Transfer Learning to Downstream Tasks (Outperforming Supervised Pretraining):**
 - **Linear Classification on Other Datasets:** SwAV features consistently outperformed ImageNet supervised pretraining on multiple linear classification benchmarks with frozen features:
 - **Places205 (Scene Recognition):** SwAV achieved 56.7% vs. 53.2% for supervised.
 - **VOC07 (Object Classification):** SwAV achieved 88.9% mAP vs. 87.5% for supervised.
 - **iNaturalist2018 (Fine-grained Classification):** SwAV achieved 48.6% vs. 46.7% for supervised.
 - This marked the **first time** a self-supervised method demonstrably surpassed ImageNet supervised features on these diverse classification tasks.
 - **Object Detection:** When finetuning the pretrained models for object detection:
 - **VOC07+12 (Faster R-CNN):** SwAV achieved 82.6% AP50 vs. 81.3% for supervised.
 - **COCO (Mask R-CNN, DETR):** SwAV achieved 41.6% AP (Mask R-CNN) and 42.1% AP (DETR), both outperforming supervised (39.7% and 40.8% respectively).
 - These results further solidify SwAV's generalizability and practical utility, indicating that its learned features are superior for transfer across a wider range of vision tasks.

4. **Efficiency and Scalability:**
 - **Small Batch Performance:** SwAV maintained strong performance (e.g., 72.7% top-1 after 200 epochs) even with small batch sizes (256 images), outperforming MoCov2 in similar low-batch settings while using a significantly smaller feature queue (3,840 vs. 65,536). This highlights its memory efficiency and reduced reliance on large external components.
 - **Faster Convergence:** SwAV demonstrated faster training times to reach competitive performance. For instance, it achieved 72.1% top-1 accuracy in just 100 epochs (approx. 6h15 on 64 V100 GPUs), whereas MoCov2 required 800 epochs to achieve a slightly lower 71.1%.
 - **Uncurated Large-Scale Pretraining:** SwAV successfully scaled to pretraining on a massive 1-billion uncurated Instagram image dataset. The learned features significantly improved over training from scratch on ImageNet and largely benefited from increased model capacity, demonstrating its robustness and applicability to truly "wild" data without any human supervision or filtering.

5. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Clustering vs. Contrastive:** Ablations suggested that clustering-based methods (SwAV, DeepCluster-v2) generally have a higher learning potential, outperforming SimCLR in fair comparisons.
 - **Multi-crop Effectiveness:** The multi-crop strategy consistently improved the performance of various self-supervised methods (including SimCLR, DeepCluster, SeLa) by 2-4% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, proving its generality and significant impact.
 - **Soft vs. Hard Assignments:** Using soft assignments from the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm yielded better results than hard assignments, indicating the benefit of a smoother optimization landscape.
 - **Prototypes:** Learning the prototypes was beneficial, and the exact number of prototypes had little impact as long as "enough" were used, suggesting their role is more about providing diverse comparison targets rather than precise semantic clusters.

Overall, the findings conclusively position SwAV as a new benchmark in self-supervised learning, particularly highlighting its strong performance, efficiency, and unprecedented ability to surpass supervised pretraining across multiple critical computer vision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SwAV represents a pivotal advancement in unsupervised visual representation learning, offering significant implications for both fundamental research and practical applications in computer vision:

1. **Redefining State-of-the-Art in Self-Supervised Learning:** SwAV's achievement of 75.3% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet linear evaluation with ResNet-50 and its ability to shrink the gap to supervised methods to negligible levels for wider models significantly pushes the frontier of self-supervised learning performance. This demonstrates that unsupervised methods are not just catching up but are now leading in certain key metrics.

2. **Surpassing Supervised Pretraining on Transfer Tasks:** Perhaps the most impactful finding is SwAV's consistent outperformance of ImageNet supervised pretraining on a diverse range of downstream tasks, including linear classification on Places205, VOC07, and iNaturalist2018, as well as object detection on VOC07+12 and COCO. This marks a critical milestone, as it shows that features learned without manual labels can be *more* generalizable and robust than those learned with explicit supervision, particularly when finetuning for new tasks. This undermines the long-held assumption that supervised pretraining is the gold standard for transfer learning.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** By proposing an online clustering method that avoids large memory banks or extremely large batch sizes, SwAV addresses crucial computational and memory bottlenecks prevalent in other leading self-supervised methods (e.g., MoCo, SimCLR). Its ability to work effectively with smaller batch sizes and to learn high-quality representations faster (e.g., 6 hours vs. 40 hours for comparable SimCLR performance) makes self-supervised learning more accessible and practical for researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources. The successful pretraining on 1 billion uncurated Instagram images further validates its robustness and scalability to truly massive, "in-the-wild" datasets.

4. **Novel and Generalizable Data Augmentation:** The multi-crop strategy is a simple yet highly effective contribution. Its demonstrated ability to boost performance across various self-supervised methods (SimCLR, DeepCluster, SeLa) by 2-4% highlights its general applicability and potential to become a standard data augmentation technique in the field. This signifies that strategic combination of different views (e.g., global and local) is crucial for learning rich representations.

5. **Shifting Research Paradigms:**
 - **Reduced Reliance on Labeled Data:** SwAV's success further reinforces the paradigm shift towards exploiting the inherent structure within unlabeled data. This is crucial for applications where labeled data is scarce, expensive, or privacy-sensitive (e.g., medical imaging, specialized industrial vision).
 - **Rethinking Network Architecture Design:** As unsupervised features become more performant, the traditional pipeline of designing architectures for supervised tasks and then adapting them might be challenged. SwAV's discussion on exploring architectures without supervision suggests a future where model design could be driven by self-supervised objectives from the outset.
 - **New Benchmarks for Research:** The release of the SwAV-pretrained model provides a strong new baseline for researchers, enabling them to replace traditionally supervised backbones with an equally or more performant unsupervised alternative for various downstream tasks.

In summary, SwAV not only delivers state-of-the-art performance but also introduces methodological innovations that address key limitations of prior work. Its demonstrated ability to learn superior transferable features without supervision marks a significant step towards truly autonomous and efficient visual representation learning, with profound implications for the future development and deployment of computer vision systems.
