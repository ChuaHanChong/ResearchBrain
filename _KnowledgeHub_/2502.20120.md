# Rethinking Multimodal Learning from the Perspective of Mitigating Classification Ability Disproportion

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel multimodal learning framework is presented to mitigate modality imbalance by dynamically enhancing the classification ability of weaker modalities through a sustained boosting algorithm and adaptive classifier assignment. This approach demonstrates superior performance on six multimodal datasets (CREMAD, KSounds, VGGSound, Twitter, Sarcasm, NVGesture), consistently outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods and maintaining robustness when modalities are missing.

## Problem

- Multimodal learning (MML) often underperforms unimodal baselines due to 'modality imbalance,' where different modalities converge at varying speeds.
- This imbalance results in a disproportion of classification ability between 'strong' and 'weak' modalities, leading to suboptimal overall MML performance.
- Existing solutions for modality imbalance primarily focus on rebalancing learning processes or enhancing inter-modal interaction, neglecting direct enhancement of the weak classifier's intrinsic ability.

## Method

- A ==sustained boosting== algorithm is introduced, which simultaneously optimizes multiple classifiers for a target (weak) modality by minimizing residual errors while preserving previously learned classifiers.
- An adaptive classifier assignment (ACA) strategy dynamically monitors each modality's classification ability using confident scores and assigns new classifiers to the weaker modality at fixed intervals.
- The framework is integrated into a deep multimodal learning setup, with theoretical guarantees provided for the convergence of the cross-modal gap function.

## Results

- The method consistently achieved state-of-the-art results in accuracy, MAP, and MacroF1 scores across six diverse multimodal datasets (CREMAD, KSounds, VGGSound, Twitter, Sarcasm, NVGesture).
- It successfully overcame instances where initial multimodal learning underperformed the best unimodal approach, significantly improving joint learning outcomes.
- The approach demonstrates robustness to hyperparameter changes, maintains competitive training and inference times, and shows stable performance even with missing modalities at test time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly enhancing the classification ability of weaker modalities through a boosting mechanism is a more fundamental approach to mitigate modality imbalance than solely rebalancing learning dynamics.
> - Continuously improving a modality's discriminative power by simultaneously learning multiple classifiers on residual errors can effectively narrow performance gaps.
> - Dynamically adjusting model capacity for each modality based on real-time performance is crucial for adapting to evolving modality strengths and weaknesses during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal learning (MML) aims to integrate information from multiple heterogeneous sources (e.g., audio, video, text) to achieve more robust and comprehensive understanding than what any single modality can provide. Theoretically, MML should outperform unimodal approaches by leveraging complementary information. However, the paper highlights a significant practical challenge: MML often surprisingly underperforms unimodal baselines in certain scenarios.

The core issue identified by the research community is **modality imbalance**. This arises because different modalities, when trained jointly, tend to converge at varying speeds. A "strong modality" converges faster and achieves higher performance, while a "weak modality" lags behind. This disproportion in learning progress and classification ability leads to suboptimal overall multimodal performance.

Existing research has tackled modality imbalance from several perspectives:
- **Rebalancing Learning Processes:** Many approaches focus on manually intervening in the learning dynamics of different modalities. This includes techniques like gradient modulation or adaptive learning rate adjustments to synchronize the learning progress of weak and strong modalities.
- **Bridging Information Gaps and Enhancing Interaction:** Another line of work aims to improve the interaction and information flow between modalities during training. Examples include alternating training algorithms, leveraging gradient boosting to capture complementary information, or flat gradient modification strategies.

This paper identifies a critical oversight in these existing methods: while they focus on balancing the *learning process*, they do not explicitly address or enhance the *inherent classification ability disproportion* between modalities. Stronger modalities naturally yield more robust classifiers due to richer or more easily extractable information. The authors argue that directly improving the performance of the *weak classifier* is a more fundamental approach to mitigate modality imbalance. This perspective leverages the established machine learning technique of **boosting**, which is designed to enhance weak learners into strong ones, thus providing a novel angle in the MML landscape. It deviates from merely adjusting learning dynamics to directly augmenting the discriminative power of underperforming modal components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The **key objective** of this research is to propose a novel multimodal learning approach that dynamically balances the classification ability between weak and strong modalities, thereby mitigating the pervasive issue of modality imbalance and improving overall MML performance.

The **motivation** for this work stems from two core observations:
1. **Prevalence and Impact of Modality Imbalance:** As discussed, despite the promise of MML, modality imbalance frequently leads to suboptimal performance, where the joint model performs worse than its best unimodal counterpart. This indicates a fundamental flaw in how current MML systems handle heterogeneous information.
2. **Limitations of Existing Solutions:** While prior work attempts to rebalance MML by adjusting learning processes or enhancing inter-modal interaction, they often overlook the underlying disproportion in the *classification ability* of individual modality classifiers. Strong modalities intrinsically produce more capable classifiers than weak ones.
3. **Potential of Boosting Algorithms:** The authors hypothesize that directly enhancing the classification ability of the weak modality classifier could effectively narrow the performance gap and improve overall MML. They draw inspiration from boosting algorithms, which are ensemble techniques specifically designed to improve weak learners.
4. **Empirical Evidence (Toy Experiment):** To validate this hypothesis, the authors conduct a compelling toy experiment on the CREMAD dataset. They show that applying gradient boosting to improve only the *weak* (video) modality's classifier, while keeping the *strong* (audio) modality fixed, significantly reduces the performance gap between modalities and boosts the overall multimodal accuracy. This demonstration visually confirms the feasibility and effectiveness of a boosting-based strategy for addressing classification ability disproportion. This experiment serves as the foundational empirical motivation for their proposed method.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a novel multimodal learning framework centered on two main components: a **sustained boosting algorithm** and an **adaptive classifier assignment strategy**, complemented by theoretical guarantees.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Learning Foundation

The framework begins with a standard deep multimodal learning setup. For *K* categories and *N* data points, each modality (e.g., audio 'a', video 'v') has an encoder $\phi^o(\cdot)$ with parameters $\theta^o$ to extract features $u^o$. These features are then fed into a classifier $\psi^o(\cdot)$ with parameters $\Theta^o$ to produce predictions $p^o$. The objective is typically to minimize the cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$ for each modality.

#### 3.2. Sustained Boosting Algorithm

Instead of traditional gradient boosting which learns sequentially and freezes previous models, the "sustained boosting" algorithm aims to continuously enhance the classification performance of a target (weak) modality by simultaneously optimizing multiple classifiers and their residual errors.
1. **Multiple Classifiers:** For a modality targeted for improvement (say, modality 'o'), *n* classifiers are trained. All classifiers share the same encoder $\phi^o$, but each has its own classification head $\psi^o_t$. This configurable classifier module (e.g., two fully-connected layers with ReLU) allows for flexible adjustment of classification ability.
2. **Residual Error Minimization:** When learning the $t$-th classifier, the objective is to fit the *residual labels* ($\hat{y}^o_{it}$), which represent the error from the combined predictions of the previous $t-1$ classifiers, softened by a factor $\lambda$. This is captured by $\epsilon(x^o_i, y_i, t)$ (Equation 2).
3. **Overall and Previous Classifier Preservation:** To prevent degradation of already learned classifiers and ensure the shared encoder is updated effectively, two additional loss components are introduced:
 - $\epsilon_{all}(x^o_i, y_i, t)$: Ensures that the combined prediction of all *t* classifiers is accurate (Equation 3).
 - $\epsilon_{pre}(x^o_i, y_i, t)$: Ensures that the previous $t-1$ classifiers remain well-trained (Equation 4).
4. **Combined Objective:** The overall objective function for the sustained boosting of modality 'o' at iteration *t* is the sum of these three components: $\mathcal{L}(x^o_i, y_i, t) = \epsilon + \epsilon_{all} + \epsilon_{pre}$ (Equation 5), which is then aggregated over the dataset (Equation 6). This differs from traditional boosting by simultaneously optimizing the current classifier for residual error and ensuring the robustness of the cumulative and preceding classifiers, thereby "sustaining" the boosting effect within a single training loop.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Classifier Assignment (ACA) Strategy

The learning status and relative strength of modalities evolve dynamically. To adapt to this, the ACA strategy monitors the modalities' performance and dynamically assigns additional classifiers to the weaker one.
1. **Confident Score:** A "confident score" $s^o_t$ is calculated for each modality 'o' at iteration *t*. This score reflects the average probability assigned to the ground-truth labels by the modality's current set of classifiers, serving as a proxy for its classification ability.
2. **Dynamic Assignment Rule:** At fixed intervals ($t_N$ iterations), the difference in confident scores between modalities (e.g., $s^a_t - \sigma s^v_t$) is evaluated against a tolerance threshold $\tau$. If the difference indicates one modality is significantly weaker (e.g., audio is weaker than video, or vice-versa), a new configurable classifier is added to that specific modality, increasing its capacity to learn. This mechanism allows the model to rebalance classification abilities on the fly.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the convergence property of the "cross-modal gap function" $G(\Phi) = \mathcal{L}_a(\Phi_a) - \mathcal{L}_v(\Phi_v)$, where $\mathcal{L}_a$ and $\mathcal{L}_v$ are the overall losses for audio and video modalities. Under assumptions of strong convexity, smoothness, and effectiveness of the weak classifier boosting, Theorem 1 (informal statement provided) states that the gap function $G(\Phi(T))$ converges at a rate of $O(1/T)$. This theoretical result ensures that the proposed boosting scheme effectively reduces the performance disparity between modalities over time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of the proposed method (Ours) demonstrates its superiority over various state-of-the-art multimodal learning baselines across diverse datasets and metrics.

#### 4.1. Classification Performance Comparison (Table 1)

- **Overall Superiority:** "Ours" consistently achieves the best accuracy, mean average precision (MAP), and MacroF1 scores across all six widely used multimodal datasets (CREMAD, KSounds, VGGSound, Twitter, Sarcasm, NVGesture).
- **Outperforming SOTA:** The method significantly outperforms both traditional multimodal fusion methods (Concat, Affine, ML-LSTM, Sum, Weight) and advanced rebalanced multimodal learning approaches (MSES, G-blend, MSLR, OGM, PMR, AGM, MMParato, SMV, MLA, DI-MML, LFM, ReconBoost).
- **Handling Multiple Modalities:** The strong performance on the NVGesture dataset (which has three modalities: RGB, optical flow, Depth) confirms the method's extensibility beyond two-modality scenarios.
- **Addressing Underperformance:** Importantly, the method effectively overcomes instances where multimodal learning initially underperforms the best unimodal approach (indicated by gray backgrounds in Table 1), demonstrating its ability to truly enhance joint learning.

#### 4.2. Sensitivity to Hyperparameters (Figure 2)

- The method shows robustness to changes in the threshold $\sigma$ (controlling the confident score difference) and the smoothing factor $\lambda$ (for residual labels) within reasonable ranges on the CREMAD dataset, suggesting ease of hyperparameter tuning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 2 & Figure 3)

- **Contribution of Objective Components:** The ablation study reveals that all three components of the sustained boosting objective ($\epsilon$, $\epsilon_{all}$, $\epsilon_{pre}$) contribute to boosting multimodal performance. The full objective leads to the most balanced classification performance across modalities.
- **Effectiveness of Residual Learning:** Figure 3 illustrates that the "Full Prediction" (using all *t* classifiers) consistently outperforms "Prediction of *t-1* CLS" (excluding the latest classifier), confirming that the residual learning effectively enhances the weak modality's performance.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis

- **Impact of Adaptive Classifier Assignment (Table 3):** The adaptive strategy outperforms a fixed number of weak classifiers, highlighting the importance of dynamically adjusting modality capacity based on real-time learning status to address evolving imbalance.
- **Visualization Results (Figure 4):** t-SNE visualizations on CREMAD show that "Ours" learns more discriminative multimodal features compared to naive MML and even ReconBoost. Specifically, it significantly improves the clustering of different categories for the weak (video) modality, visually confirming the enhanced classification ability.
- **Training Overhead (Figure 5 & Table IV):** Despite introducing additional computational components, the method maintains competitive training and inference times compared to other SOTA baselines. The increase in parameters is modest (around 4% for 10 additional classifiers) for a significant performance boost (nearly 20% on CREMAD compared to naive MML with the same backbone).
- **Convergence of Gap Loss (Figure 6 & 7):** Experimental validation on CREMAD confirms the theoretical analysis, showing that the cross-modal gap function gradually converges during training, along with a corresponding improvement in overall accuracy.
- **Impact of Model Capacity (Table 4):** The performance gains are not merely due to increased model parameters. "Ours" (ResNet18 backbone with 12.8M video parameters) significantly outperforms a naive MML baseline using a larger ResNet34 backbone (23.3M video parameters), indicating that the boosting *strategy* itself is the key driver of improvement, not just more capacity.
- **Stability under Modality Missing (Table 5):** The method demonstrates robust performance even when modalities are missing at test time, consistently achieving the best results across different missing rates compared to baselines, including those specifically designed for missing modalities (e.g., MLA).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of multimodal learning by offering a novel and effective solution to the long-standing problem of modality imbalance. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Addressing Modality Imbalance:** By shifting the focus from merely balancing learning processes to directly enhancing the *classification ability* of weak modalities through a sustained boosting mechanism, the paper introduces a fresh perspective. This approach is more fundamental and targets the root cause of performance disparity.
- **Superior Performance:** The consistent achievement of state-of-the-art results across a diverse range of datasets and multimodal tasks underscores the practical effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed method. This can lead to more reliable and accurate multimodal AI systems in real-world applications.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The theoretical analysis demonstrating the convergence of the cross-modal gap function provides a strong scientific basis for the approach, enhancing confidence in its stability and effectiveness.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:** The adaptive classifier assignment strategy ensures the method can dynamically respond to evolving modality strengths during training. Its demonstrated stability under modality missing scenarios further enhances its utility in practical, imperfect data environments.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Achieving significant performance gains with only a modest increase in computational overhead (both parameters and inference time) compared to baselines makes the method practically viable for deployment.
- **Broad Applicability:** The method's successful application across various multimodal combinations (audio-video, image-text, RGB-OF-Depth) suggests its potential for widespread use in different domains requiring multimodal intelligence, such as emotion recognition, action recognition, sentiment analysis, and sarcasm detection.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors):**
The authors acknowledge that the current method primarily focuses on the classifier level. Future work could explore extending this approach to balance classification abilities in early fusion MML setups. Additionally, while the theoretical analysis covers the boosting algorithm's effect on the loss gap, a more comprehensive investigation into the overall convergence behavior of the full iterative framework is warranted. These limitations highlight avenues for future research to further refine and broaden the applicability of this promising approach.
