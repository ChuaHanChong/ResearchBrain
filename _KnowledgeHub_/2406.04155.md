# Improving Physics-Augmented Continuum Neural Radiance Field-Based Geometry-Agnostic System Identification with Lagrangian Particle Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Takuhiro Kaneko from NTT Corporation developed Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO) to improve geometry-agnostic system identification for dynamic continuum materials from sparse-view video. This method refines both geometric reconstruction and physical property inference by enabling continuous geometric correction in Lagrangian space, consistent with physical constraints, demonstrating enhanced accuracy over prior techniques like PAC-NeRF.

## Problem

- Inferring both geometry and physical properties of dynamic continuum materials from visual observations is an ill-posed problem, especially with limited data.
- Existing methods, like PAC-NeRF, fix initial geometry learned from a single first frame, making them sensitive to sparse-view conditions and preventing geometry refinement using temporal information.
- Directly optimizing Eulerian grid representations for geometry over time struggles to effectively integrate explicit physical constraints from Lagrangian particle simulations, potentially leading to physically inconsistent geometric modifications.

## Method

- The paper introduces **LPO** (==Lagrangian Particle Optimization==) to directly optimize both the positions and features (volume density, color) of ==Lagrangian particles== across the entire video sequence.
- **LPO** enables ==geometry correction in Lagrangian space==, leveraging explicit physical constraints from the ==Material Point Method (MPM)== to ensure physically consistent geometric refinements.
- An ==iterative geometry-physics optimization== framework is proposed, where LPO and physical property identification are performed in cycles, using synthesized views from unobserved viewpoints to augment initial geometry training in subsequent iterations.

## Results

- LPO consistently improved geometric reconstruction (higher PSNR, SSIM; lower LPIPS) across nine diverse continuum material scenes, outperforming PAC-NeRF baselines in sparse-view conditions.
- The iterative application of LPO significantly enhanced the accuracy of physical property identification compared to existing methods and ablated models.
- The proposed method maintained its effectiveness and robustness across different selections of sparse training views and when the number of training views was increased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing geometric structure directly in Lagrangian particle space, under physical constraints, is more effective for geometry-agnostic system identification than optimizing Eulerian grid fields without such explicit physical guidance.
> - The inherent interdependence between geometry and physics identification can be leveraged through an iterative refinement process, where improvements in one aspect contribute to better estimation of the other.
> - Allowing continuous geometric refinement over the entire video sequence, rather than fixing initial geometry, is crucial for robust identification of both geometry and physics in sparse-view settings.
