# Improving Physics-Augmented Continuum Neural Radiance Field-Based Geometry-Agnostic System Identification with Lagrangian Particle Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Takuhiro Kaneko from NTT Corporation developed Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO) to improve geometry-agnostic system identification for dynamic continuum materials from sparse-view video. This method refines both geometric reconstruction and physical property inference by enabling continuous geometric correction in Lagrangian space, consistent with physical constraints, demonstrating enhanced accuracy over prior techniques like PAC-NeRF.

## Problem

- Inferring both geometry and physical properties of dynamic continuum materials from visual observations is an ill-posed problem, especially with limited data.
- Existing methods, like PAC-NeRF, fix initial geometry learned from a single first frame, making them sensitive to sparse-view conditions and preventing geometry refinement using temporal information.
- Directly optimizing Eulerian grid representations for geometry over time struggles to effectively integrate explicit physical constraints from Lagrangian particle simulations, potentially leading to physically inconsistent geometric modifications.

## Method

- The paper introduces **LPO** (==Lagrangian Particle Optimization==) to directly optimize both the positions and features (volume density, color) of ==Lagrangian particles== across the entire video sequence.
- **LPO** enables ==geometry correction in Lagrangian space==, leveraging explicit physical constraints from the ==Material Point Method (MPM)== to ensure physically consistent geometric refinements.
- An ==iterative geometry-physics optimization== framework is proposed, where LPO and physical property identification are performed in cycles, using synthesized views from unobserved viewpoints to augment initial geometry training in subsequent iterations.

## Results

- LPO consistently improved geometric reconstruction (higher PSNR, SSIM; lower LPIPS) across nine diverse continuum material scenes, outperforming PAC-NeRF baselines in sparse-view conditions.
- The iterative application of LPO significantly enhanced the accuracy of physical property identification compared to existing methods and ablated models.
- The proposed method maintained its effectiveness and robustness across different selections of sparse training views and when the number of training views was increased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing geometric structure directly in Lagrangian particle space, under physical constraints, is more effective for geometry-agnostic system identification than optimizing Eulerian grid fields without such explicit physical guidance.
> - The inherent interdependence between geometry and physics identification can be leveraged through an iterative refinement process, where improvements in one aspect contribute to better estimation of the other.
> - Allowing continuous geometric refinement over the entire video sequence, rather than fixing initial geometry, is crucial for robust identification of both geometry and physics in sparse-view settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the problem of system identification, a technique used to infer the geometry and physical properties of an object from visual observations. This field is significant in computer vision and physics due to its applications in 3D reconstruction, environmental understanding, and content creation. Traditionally, system identification has been challenged by its ill-posed nature, particularly when both geometric structure and physical properties need to be determined simultaneously.

Recent advancements in neural fields, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for geometric representation and neural rendering for 2D-3D connections, alongside differentiable physical simulators, have provided new avenues for solving this problem. Earlier studies in this area often assumed prior knowledge of the geometric structure of a scene, limiting their practical applicability.

This work specifically targets **geometry-agnostic system identification**, a more challenging variant where no initial geometric assumptions are made. The paper builds upon Physics-Augmented Continuum Neural Radiance Fields (PAC-NeRF), a pioneering model that extends NeRF to incorporate continuum mechanics. PAC-NeRF achieves this by employing a hybrid Eulerian–Lagrangian representation: geometry is represented by Eulerian grid fields (from NeRF), physics is simulated using the Material Point Method (MPM) on Lagrangian particles, and these two representations are connected via particle-grid interconversions. The entire system is differentiable, allowing end-to-end training from multi-view video sequences.

However, PAC-NeRF exhibits a limitation in its two-step optimization process. It first optimizes Eulerian grid representations using only the initial frames of video sequences, then proceeds to optimize physical properties across the full video sequence with these initial geometric representations fixed. This approach makes PAC-NeRF sensitive to the quality of initial geometry learning, which can be particularly problematic in challenging scenarios such as few-shot or sparse-view settings where initial visual information is limited. The present work positions itself as an advancement to overcome this specific limitation of PAC-NeRF, making geometry-agnostic system identification more robust in data-constrained environments. It contributes to the sub-area of physics-informed dynamic NeRFs, particularly those designed for sparse-view settings, by introducing a novel optimization strategy in Lagrangian space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and robustness of geometry-agnostic system identification, particularly in sparse-view settings. This involves improving both the accuracy of geometric reconstruction and the identification of physical properties of dynamic continuum materials from video observations.

The motivation for this work stems directly from a recognized limitation of the PAC-NeRF model: its reliance on a two-step optimization process.
1. **Initial Geometry Optimization from Limited Data:** PAC-NeRF first optimizes the Eulerian grid representations (volume density and color fields) using only the initial frames (t=0) of the video sequences. This step is crucial as it establishes the initial geometric structure.
2. **Fixed Geometry During Physics Optimization:** Subsequently, physical properties are optimized across the entire video sequence, but the initial geometric representations from step 1 are kept fixed.

This two-step approach introduces several problems, particularly in scenarios with sparse visual data (e.g., few-shot or sparse-view settings):
- **Sensitivity to Initial Learning:** If the initial geometry learning from the first frames is inaccurate due to limited views, this error cannot be rectified later in the optimization process. This can lead to persistent geometric inaccuracies.
- **Inability to Leverage Temporal Information for Geometry:** Dynamic scenes contain rich temporal information that could aid in reconstructing geometry. However, PAC-NeRF's fixed initial geometry prevents it from using this temporal consistency and physical evolution to refine the geometric structure over time.
- **Ill-Posed Nature Amplified:** The problem of simultaneously identifying geometry and physics is inherently ill-posed. An inaccurate initial geometry can propagate errors and hinder accurate physical property estimation, creating a "chicken-and-egg" problem where accurate geometry is needed for accurate physics, and vice-versa.

The paper considers a potential alternative: training the Eulerian grid representations using all video sequences rather than just the first frame. However, it identifies a critical drawback with this approach. Eulerian grids are optimized in world (Eulerian) space, making it difficult to directly incorporate explicit physical constraints that manifest in Lagrangian (material or particle) space. Specifically, gradients calculated for particle positions (which are central to physical simulation via MPM) cannot effectively propagate back to the Eulerian grid representations, potentially leading to geometric modifications that violate physical laws.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes **Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO)**. The core motivation for LPO is to enable the optimization of geometric structures across the entire video sequence while directly reflecting the explicit physical constraints imposed by the Material Point Method (MPM). By optimizing the positions and features of Lagrangian particles, the method aims to achieve geometric correction that is consistent with the underlying physics, thereby improving both geometry and physical property identification, especially in challenging sparse-view conditions. A secondary motivation is to leverage this improved geometry to further refine physical property estimates through an iterative optimization scheme, acknowledging the coupled nature of geometry and physics identification.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology builds upon the existing PAC-NeRF framework and introduces Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO) as a key enhancement, which is then integrated into an iterative optimization scheme.

#### 3.1. Problem Statement

The objective is to reconstruct the dynamic geometric representations (appearance and shape) of continuum objects and identify their physical properties from a set of multi-view video sequences. The input consists of ground truth color observations `Ĉ(r, t)` for camera rays `r` and time `t`. A specific challenge addressed is the sparse-view setting, where the set of input rays `R̂` is limited.

#### 3.2. Preliminary: PAC-NeRF Review

The proposed method extends PAC-NeRF, which combines three core components:

- **Continuum NeRF:** This extends a standard static NeRF to represent dynamic continuum materials. A dynamic NeRF defines volume density `σ(x, t)` and color `c(x, d, t)` fields that vary with time. Volume rendering is used to calculate pixel colors `C(r, t)`, with a pixel-wise loss `L_pixel`. To represent continuum materials, conservation laws for velocity, volume density, and color fields (`Dσ/Dt = 0`, `Dc/Dt = 0`) are imposed. The velocity field `v` follows momentum conservation (`ρ Dv/Dt = ∇⋅T + ρg`), solved using Differentiable Material Point Method (DiffMPM).
- **Particle-Grid Interconverters:** PAC-NeRF uses a hybrid Eulerian–Lagrangian approach. Neural rendering operates in Eulerian grid space (discretized voxel-based NeRF), while physical simulation runs in Lagrangian particle space (DiffMPM). Grid-to-Particle (G2P) and Particle-to-Grid (P2G) conversions bridge these spaces, using trilinear shape functions to transfer field values (e.g., volume density or color).
- **Lagrangian Field and PAC-NeRF Optimization:** In PAC-NeRF, an initial Eulerian voxel field `F_G'(t_0)` is optimized using only the first frames of video sequences. This field is then converted to a Lagrangian particle field `F_P(t_0)` via G2P, where particles are randomly sampled, and those with low alpha values (volume density) are removed to represent the object's shape. For subsequent time steps (`t_1`, `t_2`, etc.), `F_P(t_i)` is calculated from `F_P(t_i-1)` using DiffMPM. This `F_P(t_i)` is then converted back to an Eulerian voxel field `F_G(t_i)` via P2G, which is used for rendering. Critically, the initial `F_G'(t_0)` is frozen during the optimization of physical properties across later time steps.

#### 3.3. Geometric Correction with Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO)

The core innovation is LPO, designed to address PAC-NeRF's limitation of fixed initial geometry. Instead of optimizing the Eulerian voxel fields `F_G'(t_0)` for the first frame and then fixing them, LPO optimizes the geometric structure directly in Lagrangian particle space.
- **Optimization Targets:** LPO optimizes not only the volume density (`σ_P`) and color (`c_P`) fields of the particles but also their positions (`x_P`). This contrasts with Eulerian grid optimization, where only grid features are typically optimized, and positions are fixed.
- **Physical Constraint Integration:** Because `x_P` (particle positions) are defined and optimized in Lagrangian space, they can directly reflect the physical constraints imposed by the DiffMPM. This allows gradients calculated for particle positions to be propagated, ensuring that geometric corrections are physically consistent.
- **Leveraging Temporal Information:** By optimizing particle features and positions across the entire video sequence under physical constraints, LPO enables the geometric structure to be refined over time, leveraging the dynamic observations to compensate for limited initial views.

#### 3.4. Physical Identification with Iterative Geometry–Physics Optimization

Recognizing the interdependence between accurate geometry and accurate physical property identification, the paper proposes an iterative geometry–physics optimization (Algorithm 1). This algorithm aims to gradually converge to optimal geometric and physical states.
The iterative process (`R` iterations) is as follows:
1. **(Re)optimize Eulerian Static Voxel Grid:** For the first iteration (i=1), the initial voxel grids are optimized using `Ĉ(ˆr, t_0)` (original first-frame observations). For subsequent iterations (i>1), the optimization also incorporates `˘C(˘r, t_0)` (synthesized first-frame images from unobserved viewpoints generated in the previous iteration).
2. **Physical Property Optimization:** The physical properties are optimized across all observed video frames `Ĉ(ˆr, t)`.
3. **Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO):** The proposed LPO is applied to optimize particle positions and features using all observed video frames `Ĉ(ˆr, t)`.
4. **Color Prediction for Unobserved Views:** After LPO, the updated particle states are used to render new images `˘C(˘r, t_0)` for unobserved viewpoints (`˘R`) in the first frame. These synthesized views are then used to augment the training data for the next iteration's Step 1.

The rationale for using synthesized views `˘C(˘r, t_0)` only for the initial voxel grid optimization (Step 1) and not for physics/particle optimization (Steps 2 & 3) is to mitigate potential negative effects from incomplete geometry correction, especially in early iterations. The choice to re-calculate from scratch using synthesized `t_0` views, rather than directly updating voxel grids via P2G from corrected particles, is to avoid cumulative artifacts known to arise from repeated G2P/P2G conversions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO) and its iterative application across a benchmark dataset of nine diverse continuum material scenes (Newtonian/non-Newtonian fluids, elastic materials, plasticine, granular media). Experiments are conducted primarily in a sparse-view setting, using three views for training and eight for testing, with additional robustness tests for different view selections and counts.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Geometric Correction

- **LPO vs. Baselines (PAC-NeRF-3v/3v†):** LPO consistently improved the geometric reconstruction performance of both the standard sparse-view PAC-NeRF (PAC-NeRF-3v) and an improved sparse-view variant (PAC-NeRF-3v†), as measured by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics. This suggests that LPO's ability to correct geometry across the entire video sequence complements existing methods for robust initial geometry estimation.
- **LPO vs. Ablated Variants (+LPO-F/+LPO-P):** LPO, which jointly optimizes particle features and positions, generally outperformed or was comparable to its ablated versions that optimized only features (+LPO-F) or only positions (+LPO-P). Specifically, +LPO-P (position-only) often showed better performance than +LPO-F (feature-only), indicating that correcting the shape within physical constraints is critical. Feature optimization alone risked over-correction of appearance without fundamental shape improvement.
- **LPO vs. Eulerian Grid Optimization (+GO):** LPO consistently outperformed +GO, which attempts to optimize Eulerian voxel grids across the entire video sequence. This finding supports the hypothesis that optimizing in Lagrangian particle space, with its direct physical constraints, is more effective for geometry correction than optimizing in Eulerian grid space without such explicit physical guidance.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Physical Identification

- **Iterative LPO (+LPO⁴) vs. Baselines (PAC-NeRF-3v/3v†):** The iterative application of LPO (denoted +LPO⁴ for four iterations) improved the accuracy of physical property identification (measured by the absolute difference from ground truth) compared to PAC-NeRF-3v and PAC-NeRF-3v† in most cases. This demonstrates that geometry correction via LPO contributes to more accurate physical parameter estimation.
- **+LPO⁴ vs. Ablated/Comparative Models (+LPO-F⁴/+LPO-P⁴/+GO⁴):** +LPO⁴ exhibited greater stability and generally superior performance compared to its ablated variants and +GO⁴. The ablated models occasionally encountered significant difficulties in estimating certain physical properties, highlighting the importance of joint feature and position optimization in Lagrangian space for robust physical identification.
- **+LPO⁴ vs. Iteration without Correction (+None⁴):** +None⁴, which performed iterative updates without geometric correction, often worsened performance. This indicates that simple iterative re-estimation of physics is insufficient without a mechanism for geometry correction, underscoring the critical role of LPO's geometric refinement.

#### 4.3. Applications: Geometric Recorrection

The study also investigated whether the improved physical properties, obtained through iterative optimization, could further enhance geometric identification. Results showed that accurate physical identification indeed contributed to better geometric recorrection performance (higher PSNR/SSIM, lower LPIPS), reinforcing the "chicken-and-egg" relationship between geometry and physics.

#### 4.4. Robustness Studies (Appendices)

Additional experiments demonstrated the robustness of LPO:
- Its effectiveness was maintained across different selections of three training views.
- Its benefits were also observed when the number of training views was increased to six, suggesting generalizability beyond extreme sparse-view conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in geometry-agnostic system identification, particularly for dynamic continuum materials under sparse-view conditions. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Addressing a Critical Limitation:** The work directly addresses and mitigates a significant limitation of the pioneering PAC-NeRF model, namely its sensitivity to initial geometry learning in data-scarce environments. By enabling continuous geometric refinement throughout the video sequence, it enhances the reliability of physics-augmented neural rendering.
- **Novel Optimization Paradigm:** The introduction of Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO) as a mechanism for geometry correction is a novel contribution. Optimizing particle positions and features directly in Lagrangian space, while explicitly reflecting physical constraints from MPM, represents a distinct approach compared to prior methods that often rely on external regularization or pre-trained generalizable models for few-shot NeRFs. This physically informed optimization paradigm can lead to more plausible and consistent geometric reconstructions.
- **Improved Accuracy in Challenging Settings:** The demonstrated improvements in both geometric correction and physical property identification in sparse-view settings indicate that the method can extend the applicability of physics-augmented neural rendering to more realistic and data-constrained scenarios where obtaining dense multi-view data is impractical.
- **Iterative Refinement Framework:** The proposed iterative geometry–physics optimization framework acknowledges and leverages the inherent interdependence between geometry and physics. This iterative refinement strategy offers a robust mechanism for gradually seeking more accurate representations of both aspects.
- **Broader Applicability:** While developed within the PAC-NeRF framework, the core concept of optimizing Lagrangian particle states to integrate physical constraints into geometric learning is potentially applicable to a wider range of physics-informed models that utilize particle-based representations. This could include other dynamic NeRF variants or differentiable simulators for various material types beyond those explored in this paper, opening avenues for future research and development.
- **Foundation for Downstream Tasks:** More accurate identification of geometry and physical properties can serve as a stronger foundation for various downstream applications, such as realistic 3D content creation, inverse design, robotic manipulation of deformable objects, and environmental understanding where material properties are critical.

The identified limitations, such as increased training time and sensitivity to the initial state before LPO, represent areas for future research, but they do not diminish the conceptual and empirical advancements presented in this work. The work provides a robust method for improving the fidelity and physical consistency of learned representations in complex dynamic scenes.
