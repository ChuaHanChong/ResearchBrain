# MatAnyone 2: Scaling Video Matting via a Learned Quality Evaluator

> [!summary] Summary
> The MatAnyone 2 framework advances video matting by introducing a learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) which facilitates the creation of a large-scale, real-world dataset, VMReal (28K clips, 2.4M frames), and provides online quality guidance. This approach achieves leading performance on synthetic and real-world benchmarks, notably reducing the Grad metric by 27.1% on VideoMatte (512x288) and lowering the boundary unreliable proportion (BER) from 20.23% to 15.19%.

## Problem

- Existing video matting datasets are limited in scale, quality, and realism, hindering model generalization to diverse real-world scenarios.
- Many methods produce "segmentation-like" mattes, failing to preserve intricate boundary details due to insufficient or weak supervision in these regions.
- Propagation-based methods struggle with large appearance variations in long video sequences, as their limited temporal context leads to failures in matting newly appearing regions.

## Method

- A learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) is introduced to assess pixel-wise matting quality without ground-truth alpha labels, providing both online supervision and offline data curation capabilities.
- An automated dual-branch annotation pipeline, leveraging the MQE, creates VMReal, a large-scale (28K clips, 2.4M frames) real-world video matting dataset by fusing complementary video and image matting models.
- A reference-frame training strategy incorporates long-range reference frames with random dropout augmentation to efficiently extend temporal context and handle significant appearance changes in long videos.

## Results

- MatAnyone 2 achieved leading performance on synthetic benchmarks (VideoMatte, YoutubeMatte) and real-world CRGNN dataset across metrics like MAD, MSE, Grad, Conn, and dtSSD, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods.
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrated enhanced semantic robustness and finer details in real-world mattes, mitigating blurry boundaries and 'segmentation-like' edges, with MQE-based metrics showing BER reduced from **20.23%** to **15.19%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of online MQE guidance, training with the VMReal dataset, and the reference-frame strategy, each improving semantic accuracy, boundary fidelity, and temporal consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned pixel-wise quality evaluator (MQE) can accurately assess matting quality for both semantic accuracy and boundary details in a ground-truth-free manner, enabling scalable data annotation and improved online supervision.
> - Combining temporally consistent video matting with detail-preserving image matting, guided by a quality evaluator, creates a superior large-scale, high-quality real-world dataset.
> - Efficiently extending temporal context through a reference-frame strategy with dropout augmentation is crucial for models to adapt to large appearance variations in long video sequences without prohibitive memory overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video matting is a fundamental task in computer vision with extensive applications in visual effects, video editing, and virtual production. Despite recent advancements in the field, existing video matting methods frequently encounter challenges such as blurry boundaries, missing regions, and temporal instability in tracking objects. A primary contributing factor to these limitations is the constrained scale, quality, and realism of currently available video matting datasets. Previous datasets, such as VM800, are significantly smaller than those used in related fields like video object segmentation (VOS) and often consist of synthetic composites, leading to a domain gap when models are applied to real-world videos.

To address these data deficiencies, prior research has explored two main strategies: (1) **Pretraining**, where models are initially trained on segmentation data and then fine-tuned on limited matting datasets. This often results in the degradation of learned segmentation capabilities during fine-tuning due to the scarcity of high-quality matting examples. (2) **Joint-training**, which combines segmentation and matting supervision within a unified framework. While this approach can improve semantic and tracking accuracy, segmentation data primarily provides reliable supervision for non-boundary regions (alpha values of 0 or 1). For intricate boundary regions, methods like MatAnyone rely on unsupervised losses based on assumptions that can lead to weak supervision, often resulting in "segmentation-like" mattes that lack fine details. Such methods struggle to simultaneously enhance semantic accuracy and boundary fidelity.

The emergence of promptable segmentation models like SAM 2 and video diffusion models has inspired new directions in mask-guided and generation-based video matting. While diffusion models like GVM leverage rich priors for robustness, they can still produce blurry alpha mattes. Similarly, large-scale image quality assessment (IQA) models have been used for data curation in other vision tasks, but video matting requires pixel-level and temporally consistent quality evaluation.

This work positions itself within this landscape by directly confronting the data limitation and quality degradation issues. It introduces a novel framework, MatAnyone 2, that aims to scale video matting by developing a learned, pixel-wise quality evaluation mechanism. This mechanism facilitates the creation of a large-scale, real-world video matting dataset and provides improved supervision during training, thereby addressing the long-standing challenges of achieving both high semantic accuracy and fine boundary detail in real-world video matting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to advance video matting capabilities by overcoming the limitations imposed by existing datasets and supervision strategies, thereby achieving high semantic accuracy, fine boundary fidelity, and robust performance in real-world, long video sequences.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges in the video matting field:

1. **Limited Scale and Realism of Existing Video Matting Datasets:** Current datasets are characterized by their small size, primarily synthetic nature, and lack of diverse real-world scenarios. This limitation hinders the generalization ability of video matting models to complex, unconstrained environments. There is a recognized need for a large-scale, real-world video matting dataset with high-quality, pixel-accurate alpha annotations, which are prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to create manually.
2. **Degradation of Boundary Details in Matting:** Many existing methods, particularly those that integrate segmentation priors, often produce mattes that resemble segmentation masks. This "segmentation-like" output arises because segmentation data primarily offers strong supervision for core foreground and background regions but provides insufficient or weak supervision for intricate boundary details (e.g., hair strands). Consequently, models struggle to accurately delineate fine structures, leading to a compromise between semantic stability and boundary fidelity. The motivation is to introduce a more effective and fine-grained supervision mechanism that can guide models to preserve delicate boundary details without requiring ground-truth alpha mattes.
3. **Handling Large Appearance Variations in Long Videos:** Propagation-based video matting methods typically rely on information from a local temporal window to maintain consistency. However, in long video sequences, subjects may undergo significant appearance changes (e.g., new clothing, body parts entering the frame) that are not captured within a short local window. Extending the training window to encompass these variations incurs substantial memory overhead, while not doing so leads to failures in matting newly appearing regions. A motivated objective is to devise an efficient strategy to incorporate long-range temporal context to enable models to adapt to these significant appearance changes without excessive computational demands.

To address these motivations, the authors propose three primary contributions:

- **Introduction of a learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE):** This evaluator is designed to assess both semantic accuracy and boundary detail of alpha mattes at a pixel-wise level without requiring ground-truth supervision. Its dual function is to provide online quality feedback during model training, suppressing erroneous regions, and to serve as an offline selection module for curating high-quality data.
- **Construction of VMReal, a large-scale real-world video matting dataset:** Leveraging the MQE, an automated dual-branch annotation pipeline is developed to combine the strengths of complementary video and image matting models, thereby generating high-quality alpha annotations for a diverse collection of real-world video clips.
- **Development of a reference-frame training strategy:** This strategy aims to efficiently extend the temporal context available to the model during training, allowing it to handle large appearance variations over long video sequences without a proportional increase in memory consumption.

Collectively, these objectives and their proposed solutions aim to establish MatAnyone 2 as a robust and high-performing video matting framework capable of generalized application in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MatAnyone 2 framework introduces several methodological advancements building upon prior work like MatAnyone, primarily through a learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) and an enhanced training strategy.

#### 3.1. Quality Evaluation Model (MQE)

The core of the proposed methodology is the Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE), a network designed to assess the quality of predicted alpha mattes at a pixel-wise level without ground-truth alpha labels.

- **Input and Output:** The MQE receives an input tuple `I_eval_in:= <I_rgb, ˆα, M_seg>`, comprising a video frame, its predicted alpha matte (ˆα), and a corresponding segmentation mask (`M_seg`). It outputs a binary evaluation map `M_eval ∈ {0, 1}^(H×W)`, where '1' denotes a reliable region and '0' signifies an erroneous region.
- **Mechanism:** The segmentation mask `M_seg` is crucial; it allows the MQE to inherit semantic cues for non-boundary regions, while guiding its attention to fine-detail matting quality in boundary areas. The network leverages a DINOv3-based encoder for rich feature extraction, enhancing its ability to perceive and identify inaccurate alpha predictions.
- **Training and Data for MQE:** MQE learning is formulated as a binary segmentation task.
 - **Pseudo Ground Truth Generation:** Since ground-truth evaluation maps (`M_eval_gt`) are not naturally available, they are constructed using the P3M-10k image matting dataset, which provides human-annotated `α_gt` for real-world images. For each image, patches (e.g., 7x7 non-overlapping) are evaluated using a discrepancy measure `D(·) = 0.9 · MAD + 0.1 · Grad`, a weighted combination of standard matting metrics that captures both semantic and detail deviations. These values are min-max normalized to. `M_eval_gt` is then defined as `I(D(α_gt, ˆα) < δ)`, where `I(·)` is an indicator function and `δ` is an empirically set threshold (0.2). This process emphasizes boundary regions where errors frequently occur.
 - **Loss Functions:** To address class imbalance (reliable pixels dominate erroneous ones), focal loss (with `γ=2`, `α=0.25`) is employed to emphasize hard erroneous regions, combined with Dice loss.
 - **Implementation:** The MQE utilizes a pretrained DINOv3 encoder for feature extraction and a DPT decoder for evaluation map prediction. It is trained on 8 A800-80G GPUs for 80K iterations.

#### 3.2. Online Matting-Quality Guidance

The learned MQE provides dynamic, online supervision during the MatAnyone 2 training process.

- **Mechanism:** For each input frame, the MQE generates a pixel-wise error probability map `P^(0)_eval` indicating the likelihood of errors in the alpha prediction, without requiring ground-truth alpha mattes. This map serves as a penalty mask.
- **Online Guidance Loss (`L_eval`):** The loss is defined as `L_eval = ||P^(0)_eval||_1`, which encourages lower error probabilities and, consequently, more accurate alpha predictions. This provides a more effective and stable learning signal for boundary regions compared to previous weak unsupervised losses, enabling large-scale training with unlabeled videos.

#### 3.3. Offline Pixel-wise Selection for Data Curation (VMReal Dataset)

Beyond online guidance, the MQE is instrumental in the automated construction of a large-scale, high-quality, real-world video matting dataset called VMReal.

- **Automated Dual-branch Annotation Pipeline:**
 - **Branches:** Two complementary matting methods form the branches:
 - **Video Matting Branch (B_V):** Typically MatAnyone, which ensures temporal consistency and robust overall structure but may lack fine boundary details. It uses a first-frame segmentation mask (from SAM 2) for target subject propagation.
 - **Image Matting Branch (B_I):** Typically MattePro, guided by per-frame segmentation masks (from SAM 2). It produces sharper boundary details but struggles with temporal stability and semantic consistency across frames.
 - **MQE as Quality Arbiter:** The MQE evaluates the alpha mattes (`α_V`, `α_I`) from both branches, generating their respective evaluation maps (`M_eval_V`, `M_eval_I`).
 - **Fusion Process:** The stable predictions from `B_V` are taken as the base. The MQE selectively integrates high-quality boundary details from `B_I` in regions where `B_V` is deemed unreliable (`M_eval_V = 0`) but `B_I` is reliable (`M_eval_I = 1`). A fusion mask `M_fuse = M_eval_I ⊙ (1 − M_eval_V)` is created. To prevent artifacts, `M_fuse` is smoothed with a Gaussian blur (kernel size 9x9, `σ=5.0`). The final fused alpha matte is `α = α_V ⊙ (1 − M_fuse) + α_I ⊙ M_fuse`. The fused evaluation map becomes `M_eval = M_eval_V ∪ M_eval_I`.
- **VMReal Dataset:** This pipeline constructed VMReal, containing approximately 28K clips and 2.4M frames. A high-quality subset of 4.5K 1080p clips is from video footage websites and YouTube, while the remainder are human-centric subsets from the SA-V dataset (720p). Data preprocessing for SA-V includes a two-stage filtering and merging pipeline using OWL-SAM to obtain complete human masks.
- **Training with VMReal:** With VMReal, joint training with separate matting and segmentation losses is avoided. Instead, all samples are triplets of `⟨I_rgb, α, M_eval⟩`. The matting loss `L_M_mat` (comprising masked L1, Laplacian, and temporal coherence losses) is computed *only* over reliable regions (`M_eval = 1`), ensuring supervision focuses on high-confidence areas. The overall video matting training loss is `L_total_mat = L_M_mat + 0.1 L_eval`.

#### 3.4. Reference-Frame Strategy for Long Videos

To address large appearance variations in long videos, which propagation-based methods struggle with, a reference-frame strategy is introduced.

- **Mechanism:** This strategy incorporates long-range reference frames into the model's memory beyond the local training window. This expands the temporal context without the significant memory overhead associated with simply extending the local window length.
- **Augmentation:** A random dropout augmentation is applied to reference frames, randomly masking patches in both RGB and alpha maps (0-3 boundary patches, 0-1 non-boundary patches, sized). This simulates large appearance changes and mitigates over-reliance on historical memory, enhancing robustness to unseen regions.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

MatAnyone 2 utilizes a matting backbone based on MatAnyone. Training is performed on 8 A800-80G GPUs with a batch size of 16, using 480x480 cropped clips of 8 frames.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate MatAnyone 2 involved quantitative comparisons on synthetic and real-world benchmarks, qualitative assessments, evaluation of MQE's performance, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluations

- **Synthetic Benchmarks (VideoMatte, YoutubeMatte):** MatAnyone 2 consistently achieved leading performance across all tested metrics (MAD, MSE for semantic accuracy; Grad for detail fidelity; Conn for perceptual quality; dtSSD for temporal coherence) on both low (512x288) and high (1920x1080) resolution benchmarks.
 - Compared to MatAnyone, MatAnyone 2 showed significant improvements, for example, reducing Grad by 27.1% and Conn by 22.4% on VideoMatte (512x288).
 - It outperformed diffusion-based GVM and mask-guided MaGGIe, despite being a CNN-based model and requiring only first-frame masks (unlike MaGGIe's per-frame requirement).
- **Real-World Benchmark (CRGNN Dataset):** On the CRGNN dataset, which contains real-world videos with ground-truth alpha mattes annotated every 10 frames, MatAnyone 2 again demonstrated leading performance across all metrics (MAD, MSE, Grad, dtSSD). This indicated enhanced semantic accuracy, finer details, and superior temporal consistency, even surpassing GVM.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluations

Visual comparisons on real-world videos (e.g., Fig. 1, 6, 11, 12) illustrated that MatAnyone 2 generated alpha mattes with improved semantic robustness and finer details compared to other state-of-the-art methods.
- It addressed the blurry mattes and unnatural transitions often produced by diffusion-based methods like GVM around hair.
- It mitigated the "segmentation-like" edges observed in mask-guided methods such as MatAnyone, demonstrating superior detail preservation in challenging conditions like wind-blown hair and complex lighting.
- The method also exhibited robustness in long video sequences, successfully matting newly appearing body parts or handheld objects that other models failed to recognize (Fig. 7, 12).

#### 4.3. MQE Performance Evaluations

- **Accuracy in Identifying Errors:** The MQE successfully identified common failure cases such as low-quality matting details along object boundaries and semantic mispredictions in core regions (Fig. 4), without requiring ground-truth alpha mattes.
- **Correlation Analysis:** A correlation analysis revealed a strong positive correlation (Pearson r = 0.87) between MQE predictions (mean class 0 probability) and ground-truth discrepancy scores (D(·)), indicating the MQE's reliability in reflecting patch-level error extent (Fig. 10). The MQE exhibited a conservative tendency to flag more pixels as erroneous in highly unreliable regions, ensuring severe errors were not overlooked.
- **Evaluator-based Metrics:** To assess quality in real-world videos without ground truth, new metrics were introduced based on the MQE's evaluation map: ERR (overall unreliable proportion), MER (foreground unreliable proportion), and BER (boundary band unreliable proportion). MatAnyone 2 consistently achieved lower values for these metrics compared to other methods (e.g., BER reduced from 20.23% for MatAnyone to 15.19% for MatAnyone 2), complementing reference-based metrics and indicating improved real-world matting quality.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies quantified the contribution of each component:
- **Online Guidance (`L_eval`):** Incorporating `L_eval` as an additional online quality-guidance loss resulted in improvements in semantic accuracy (MAD, MSE) and detail fidelity (Grad), demonstrating that MQE provides a more comprehensive and stable learning signal for boundary regions than previous weak boundary supervision.
- **Training with VMReal Dataset:** Training MatAnyone 2 with the VMReal dataset led to notable improvements in MAD, Grad, and dtSSD, indicating gains in semantic robustness, boundary fidelity, and temporal consistency. Further, training another model, RVM, on VMReal also yielded consistent improvements, validating VMReal as a generalizable supervisory signal for diverse video matting models.
- **Reference-Frame Strategy:** The introduction of the reference-frame strategy with random dropout augmentation further improved performance, particularly in semantic accuracy (MAD and MSE). This demonstrated enhanced robustness in handling large appearance variations across long videos without increasing memory overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MatAnyone 2, a video matting framework that addresses several long-standing limitations in the field. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Quality Issues:** The primary contribution is the development of a learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) that enables scalable, high-quality data annotation without manual ground-truth alpha labels. This capability directly tackles the critical challenge of limited scale, quality, and realism in existing video matting datasets.
- **Creation of VMReal Dataset:** Leveraging the MQE, the authors constructed VMReal, the first large-scale (28K clips, 2.4M frames), scene-diverse, and real-world video matting dataset with high-quality alpha annotations. This dataset is a substantial resource that can accelerate future research and development in video matting by providing robust and generalized training data. Its existence can foster advancements across various video matting models, as evidenced by the improved performance of RVM when trained on VMReal.
- **Enhanced Matting Performance:** MatAnyone 2 demonstrates leading performance on both synthetic and real-world benchmarks, consistently surpassing prior state-of-the-art methods across metrics for semantic accuracy, detail fidelity, and temporal consistency. This indicates that the proposed online guidance from MQE and training on VMReal effectively mitigate the problem of "segmentation-like" mattes and improve fine detail extraction.
- **Improved Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** The reference-frame training strategy enhances the model's ability to handle large appearance variations in long videos, such as newly appearing clothing or body parts, without incurring prohibitive memory costs. This increases the practical applicability of video matting in dynamic, real-world environments.
- **Methodological Innovation in Quality Assessment:** The MQE itself represents a methodological advancement, providing a pixel-wise, ground-truth-free evaluation of matting quality. This can be broadly impactful beyond this specific application, potentially informing automated quality control and data curation in other image and video generation tasks.
- **Broadening Applications:** The improved quality and robustness of video matting achieved by MatAnyone 2 can significantly benefit a wide array of applications, including professional visual effects, amateur video editing, virtual production, teleconferencing, and augmented reality, where precise foreground extraction is crucial.

The research also acknowledges a potential future impact through an "iterative refinement" process, where progressively improved matting models could continuously refine alpha annotations, creating a virtuous cycle of data and model enhancement. This suggests a long-term vision for sustainable progress in the field.
