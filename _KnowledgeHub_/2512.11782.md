# MatAnyone 2: Scaling Video Matting via a Learned Quality Evaluator

> [!summary] Summary
> The MatAnyone 2 framework advances video matting by introducing a learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) which facilitates the creation of a large-scale, real-world dataset, VMReal (28K clips, 2.4M frames), and provides online quality guidance. This approach achieves leading performance on synthetic and real-world benchmarks, notably reducing the Grad metric by 27.1% on VideoMatte (512x288) and lowering the boundary unreliable proportion (BER) from 20.23% to 15.19%.

## Problem

- Existing video matting datasets are limited in scale, quality, and realism, hindering model generalization to diverse real-world scenarios.
- Many methods produce "segmentation-like" mattes, failing to preserve intricate boundary details due to insufficient or weak supervision in these regions.
- Propagation-based methods struggle with large appearance variations in long video sequences, as their limited temporal context leads to failures in matting newly appearing regions.

## Method

- A learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) is introduced to assess pixel-wise matting quality without ground-truth alpha labels, providing both online supervision and offline data curation capabilities.
- An automated dual-branch annotation pipeline, leveraging the MQE, creates VMReal, a large-scale (28K clips, 2.4M frames) real-world video matting dataset by fusing complementary video and image matting models.
- A reference-frame training strategy incorporates long-range reference frames with random dropout augmentation to efficiently extend temporal context and handle significant appearance changes in long videos.

## Results

- MatAnyone 2 achieved leading performance on synthetic benchmarks (VideoMatte, YoutubeMatte) and real-world CRGNN dataset across metrics like MAD, MSE, Grad, Conn, and dtSSD, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods.
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrated enhanced semantic robustness and finer details in real-world mattes, mitigating blurry boundaries and 'segmentation-like' edges, with MQE-based metrics showing BER reduced from **20.23%** to **15.19%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of online MQE guidance, training with the VMReal dataset, and the reference-frame strategy, each improving semantic accuracy, boundary fidelity, and temporal consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned pixel-wise quality evaluator (MQE) can accurately assess matting quality for both semantic accuracy and boundary details in a ground-truth-free manner, enabling scalable data annotation and improved online supervision.
> - Combining temporally consistent video matting with detail-preserving image matting, guided by a quality evaluator, creates a superior large-scale, high-quality real-world dataset.
> - Efficiently extending temporal context through a reference-frame strategy with dropout augmentation is crucial for models to adapt to large appearance variations in long video sequences without prohibitive memory overhead.
