# DriveDreamer4D: World Models Are Effective Data Machines for 4D Driving Scene Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> DriveDreamer4D enhances 4D driving scene representation by utilizing autonomous driving world models to generate diverse synthetic training data for 4D Gaussian Splatting. The system improves visual quality and spatiotemporal coherence for renderings from novel and complex ego-vehicle trajectories, making closed-loop simulation more robust.

## Problem

- Existing 4D driving scene representation methods (e.g., NeRF, 3DGS) struggle to accurately render complex vehicle maneuvers or from out-of-distribution viewpoints due to training data bias towards simple forward-driving scenarios.
- Autonomous driving 'world models' are effective at generating diverse 2D driving videos but lack the 3D/4D spatiotemporal coherence required for high-fidelity physical simulation and reconstruction.
- A critical need exists for robust and realistic closed-loop simulation capabilities to evaluate end-to-end autonomous driving systems, which current sensor simulation methods cannot adequately provide.

## Method

- A Novel Trajectory Generation Module (**NTGM**) synthesizes diverse ego-vehicle trajectories and corresponding 2D video frames using an autonomous driving world model, ensuring physical plausibility and consistency with scene elements.
- A Cousin Data Training Strategy (**CDTS**) combines real-world and generated synthetic data in temporally-aligned pairs to optimize the 4D ==Gaussian Splatting== (4DGS) model.
- The 4DGS optimization includes a specific loss function that excludes depth supervision for synthetic novel trajectory data and incorporates a perception regularization loss to ensure perceptual coherence between real and synthetic views.

## Results

- DriveDreamer4D significantly improves spatiotemporal coherence, achieving average relative gains of 22.6% in Novel Trajectory Agent IoU (NTA-IoU) and 1.8% in Novel Trajectory Lane IoU (NTL-IoU) against PVG baseline.
- Visual quality is substantially enhanced, with a 32.1% relative reduction in FID score compared to PVG for lane change scenarios, indicating fewer artifacts and higher fidelity.
- User studies consistently show strong preference for DriveDreamer4D, with over 80% win rates against baselines, validating the perceived quality and realism of its renderings under complex maneuvers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous driving world models can serve as effective 'data machines' to synthesize diverse, high-fidelity training data for 4D scene representation models, overcoming data scarcity for complex maneuvers.
> - Excluding depth supervision for synthetic novel views is crucial for 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) optimization, as projected LiDAR depth from novel views is inherently incomplete and can degrade performance.
> - The Cousin Data Training Strategy (CDTS), which pairs real and synthetic data with perceptual regularization, effectively merges these data sources to enhance both visual quality and spatiotemporal consistency in 4DGS.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses a critical bottleneck in the advancement of end-to-end autonomous driving systems: the need for robust and realistic closed-loop simulation. Current sensor simulation methods, primarily based on Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), excel at reconstructing scenes under conditions closely resembling their training data, which typically consists of straightforward, forward-driving scenarios. However, these methods severely falter when tasked with rendering complex vehicle maneuvers such as lane changes, acceleration, or deceleration, or from novel, out-of-distribution viewpoints. This limitation restricts their utility for comprehensive closed-loop evaluation, where evaluating an autonomous vehicle's response to diverse and challenging scenarios is paramount.

The broader research landscape has seen efforts to mitigate these limitations. Some approaches (e.g., SGD, GGS) attempt to extend training viewpoints using generative models, but these primarily focus on supplementing sparse image data or static background elements, failing to capture the intricate dynamics and spatiotemporal coherence required for interactive driving environments. Concurrently, significant strides have been made in autonomous driving "world models" (e.g., Sora, DriveDreamer, VISTA, GAIA-1), which demonstrate impressive capabilities in generating diverse, command-aligned driving videos. Yet, these world models are largely confined to 2D video generation, inherently lacking the full 3D/4D spatiotemporal coherence necessary for accurate physical simulation and reconstruction.

DriveDreamer4D bridges this gap by proposing a novel synergy between these two distinct research fronts. It leverages the strengths of autonomous driving world models – their ability to generate diverse, controllable 2D videos – to address the data scarcity and out-of-distribution limitations of 4D scene representation techniques like 4DGS. By using world models as "data machines," DriveDreamer4D essentially "synthesizes" the missing complex maneuver data, thereby enriching the training data for 4DGS. This approach pioneers the integration of video generation models for improving 4D reconstruction in dynamic driving scenarios. It shifts the paradigm from merely *reconstructing* observed scenes to *generating* data that expands the representational capabilities of 4D models, moving towards a more comprehensive and robust simulation environment for autonomous vehicles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind DriveDreamer4D stems from the critical need for advanced closed-loop simulation in the development and evaluation of end-to-end autonomous driving systems. Current open-loop evaluations are deemed insufficient for accurately assessing planning algorithms, necessitating a shift towards more realistic, closed-loop scenarios. This, in turn, demands the ability to reconstruct complex, dynamic driving environments and retrieve sensor data from arbitrary, user-specified viewpoints.

However, existing 4D driving scene representation techniques, such as those based on NeRF and 3DGS, face fundamental limitations. They are highly dependent on the distribution of their training data, which is predominantly biased towards simple, forward-driving conditions. Consequently, these methods struggle to accurately render scenes during complex maneuvers like lane changes, accelerations, or decelerations, and often produce artifacts or inconsistencies when queried from novel viewpoints not present in the original dataset. Autonomous driving world models, while capable of generating diverse 2D driving videos, lack the crucial 3D/4D spatiotemporal coherence required for high-fidelity scene reconstruction.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of DriveDreamer4D are:

1. **To enhance 4D driving scene representation:** The primary goal is to improve the fidelity and robustness of 4D models (specifically 4DGS) to represent dynamic driving environments, especially under complex and novel ego-vehicle trajectories.
2. **To leverage world model priors as a "data machine":** To address the scarcity of diverse training data, the project aims to utilize autonomous driving world models to synthesize novel trajectory videos. These synthetic videos will provide the necessary diverse viewpoints and complex maneuver data to augment real-world datasets.
3. **To ensure spatial-temporal consistency of generated traffic elements:** The synthesized data must maintain high spatiotemporal coherence, accurately reflecting the motion dynamics of both foreground (e.g., other vehicles) and background elements within the driving scene, even for complex maneuvers.
4. **To develop a robust data integration strategy:** Introduce a "Cousin Data Training Strategy (CDTS)" to effectively merge the temporally-aligned real and synthetic data for optimizing the 4DGS model, ensuring perceptual coherence between the two data sources.
5. **To achieve superior rendering quality under novel trajectory views:** Quantitatively and qualitatively demonstrate significant improvements in image fidelity and spatiotemporal coherence for renderings from out-of-distribution viewpoints (e.g., during lane changes, acceleration, and deceleration), compared to existing 4DGS baselines.

In essence, DriveDreamer4D seeks to break the "data distribution" constraint of 4D reconstruction by intelligently generating the data it needs, thereby unlocking the full potential of closed-loop simulation for autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DriveDreamer4D proposes a novel framework that enhances 4D driving scene representation by integrating priors from autonomous driving world models. The core idea is to generate diverse synthetic data (novel trajectory videos) using a world model and then strategically incorporate this data to train a 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) model. The approach is structured into two main components: the Novel Trajectory Generation Module (NTGM) and the Cousin Data Training Strategy (CDTS).

#### 3.1. Novel Trajectory Video Generation (Novel Trajectory Generation Module - NTGM)

The main challenge for 4DGS methods is their reliance on training data primarily from straightforward forward-driving scenarios, which limits their performance in complex maneuvers. NTGM addresses this by generating a rich variety of novel trajectory videos.

- **Novel Trajectory Proposal:**
 - The module first defines new ego-vehicle trajectories that deviate from the original, observed path. This can be achieved either by:
 - **Text-to-trajectory generation:** Using a model (e.g., from) to automatically create diverse complex trajectories based on text prompts.
 - **Custom-designed trajectories:** Manually crafting specific maneuvers like lane changes, accelerations, or decelerations to meet specific simulation needs.
 - The original trajectory (in world coordinates) is transformed into the ego-vehicle's first-frame coordinate system. Changes in velocity and direction are then applied as adjustments along the x-axis (for speed) and y-axis (for lateral movement, e.g., lane change).
 - **Trajectory Safety Assessment:** Crucially, each proposed novel trajectory undergoes a safety check to ensure it remains within drivable areas (B_road) and avoids collisions with other agents (vehicles, pedestrians) by maintaining a minimum distance (d_min). This step ensures the generated scenarios are physically plausible and realistic.

- **Structured Information Projection and Video Synthesis:**
 - Once a safe novel trajectory is defined, the existing road structure (HDMap) and 3D bounding boxes of other traffic participants from the original scene are geometrically projected onto the camera view *from the perspective of the new trajectory*. This generates consistent structured information (new 3D bounding box sequences, HDMap sequences) relative to the modified ego-motion.
 - This "structured information" (3D BBoxes, HDMap), along with the initial frame of the original video and potentially textual prompts, is then fed into a controllable autonomous driving world model (specifically, the authors leverage a model similar to DriveDreamer-2). This world model, based on a video diffusion architecture, synthesizes a sequence of 2D video frames corresponding to the novel trajectory, ensuring consistency with the provided structured conditions. The world model itself is trained on a large dataset (Waymo) to generate high-resolution (960x640) front-view videos.

#### 3.2. Cousin Data Training Strategy (CDTS)

CDTS is designed to effectively merge the real (original trajectory) and synthetic (novel trajectory) data to optimize the 4DGS model.

- **Temporal-Aligned Cousin Pair:** The core of CDTS is to construct training batches comprising temporally-aligned "cousin pairs": the ground truth video from the original trajectory and the synthesized video from the novel trajectory. This ensures that the model learns consistency across different viewpoints for the same temporal instance of the scene.

- **Loss Functions for 4DGS Optimization:**
 - **Original Data Loss (L_ori):** For the real, original trajectory data, the standard 4DGS optimization loss is applied. This includes L1 loss for RGB color, L1 loss for rendered depth (supervised by ground truth LiDAR depth maps), and SSIM loss for structural similarity. This ensures the 4DGS model accurately reconstructs the observed scene.
 - **Novel Data Loss (L_novel):** For the generated novel trajectory videos, an L1 loss for RGB color and SSIM loss are used. Crucially, **depth maps are explicitly excluded** from the loss for novel trajectory data. This is a key insight: LiDAR point clouds are only available for the original trajectory, and simply projecting them to a novel view would result in incomplete and potentially inaccurate depth maps (due to occlusions or previously unseen areas). Including such incomplete depth supervision would hinder, rather than help, the 4DGS optimization for novel views.
 - **Regularization Loss (L_reg):** To further enhance perceptual coherence between the real and synthetic data, a regularization loss is introduced. This loss calculates the L1 difference between the perception features (extracted by a pre-trained perception model F_p, e.g., a CLIP-based model) of the *rendered* original trajectory image and the *rendered* novel trajectory image. This encourages the 4DGS model to produce perceptually consistent representations across different views, even when one view is synthesized.
 - **Overall Loss (L):** The total loss function is a weighted sum of these three components: L = L_ori + λ_novel * L_novel + λ_reg * L_reg, where λ_novel and λ_reg are hyperparameters to balance the contributions of synthetic data and regularization.

By integrating these modules, DriveDreamer4D systematically addresses the data diversity challenge, allowing 4DGS models to learn more robust and generalizable representations for dynamic driving scenes under complex and novel viewpoints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DriveDreamer4D demonstrates significant improvements in 4D driving scene representation, particularly for novel trajectory viewpoints and complex maneuvers, both quantitatively and qualitatively.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

The evaluation metrics used are tailored to assess performance in novel trajectory view synthesis:
- **Novel Trajectory Agent IoU (NTA-IoU):** Measures the spatiotemporal coherence of foreground agents (vehicles). It calculates IoU between YOLO-detected 2D bounding boxes in rendered images and geometrically projected 3D ground truth bounding boxes.
- **Novel Trajectory Lane IoU (NTL-IoU):** Assesses the spatiotemporal coherence of background lanes, calculated as mean IoU between TwinLiteNet-extracted 2D lanes from rendered images and projected ground truth lanes.
- **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID):** Quantifies visual quality by comparing feature distributions of rendered novel trajectory images to original trajectory images, effectively detecting artifacts like flying points and ghosting.
- **User Study:** Gathers subjective feedback on overall video quality and foreground object realism.

The results consistently show DriveDreamer4D outperforming baseline 4DGS methods (PVG, S³Gaussian, Deformable-GS) across various complex maneuvers (lane change, acceleration, deceleration):

- **Spatiotemporal Coherence (NTA-IoU & NTL-IoU):**
 - **NTA-IoU:** DriveDreamer4D significantly enhances foreground agent coherence. Average relative improvements in NTA-IoU are 22.6% (vs. PVG), 43.5% (vs. S³Gaussian), and 15.6% (vs. Deformable-GS). This means vehicles in novel views are more accurately positioned and rendered.
 - **NTL-IoU:** DriveDreamer4D also improves background lane coherence, with average relative increases of 1.8% (vs. PVG), 3.7% (vs. S³Gaussian), and 1.6% (vs. Deformable-GS).
- **Visual Quality (FID):**
 - For lane change scenarios (where FID is most discriminative), DriveDreamer4D achieves substantial FID improvements (lower scores): 32.1% (vs. PVG), 46.4% (vs. S³Gaussian), and 16.3% (vs. Deformable-GS) relative reduction. This indicates significantly fewer artifacts (like ghosting and speckles) and higher fidelity.
- **User Study:**
 - DriveDreamer4D consistently receives overwhelming user preference, with average win rates exceeding 80% across all baselines and maneuver types. This strongly validates the perceived quality and realism of its renderings.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons (Fig. 3 and Fig. 5) strongly support the quantitative findings:
- Baseline algorithms often show foreground vehicles incorrectly changing lanes with the camera, incomplete renderings, and significant background artifacts (speckles, ghosting, blurring).
- DriveDreamer4D-enhanced versions consistently produce sharper vehicle contours, correctly aligned foreground objects, and substantially reduced background artifacts, leading to a more coherent and realistic scene representation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies, conducted with PVG as the base 4DGS model, provide insights into the contributions of the proposed components:
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Optimal performance achieved with `λ_novel = 1` and `λ_reg = 1e-3`.
- **Exclusion of Depth Loss for Novel Views:** Confirming the hypothesis, removing the depth loss for novel trajectory data significantly improves both FID and NTA-IoU. This validates the design choice, as LiDAR depth maps are inherently incomplete for new perspectives due to occlusions.
- **Cousin Data Training Strategy (CDTS):**
 - Using temporal-aligned cousin pairs alone provides noticeable improvements (e.g., FID of 76.20 vs. 79.54 without CDTS).
 - Adding the perception regularization loss further enhances performance, reducing FID to 71.52 and increasing NTA-IoU to 0.428. This demonstrates the effectiveness of CDTS in improving both visual fidelity and spatiotemporal coherence by merging real and synthetic data with perceptual consistency.

In summary, DriveDreamer4D delivers robust, high-quality 4D scene representations for autonomous driving, especially excelling in complex and novel maneuver scenarios where traditional methods fall short. Its strength lies in intelligently leveraging generative world models to synthesize critical training data, demonstrating a viable path towards more realistic and comprehensive closed-loop simulations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DriveDreamer4D marks a significant advancement in the field of autonomous driving simulation and 4D scene representation, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering Integration of World Models for 4D Reconstruction:** This work is a first-of-its-kind endeavor to harness the power of autonomous driving world models (which primarily generate 2D videos) to directly enhance the quality and robustness of 4D scene reconstruction models. This establishes a novel paradigm where generative models act as "data machines" to augment and enrich training datasets for complex 3D/4D tasks, moving beyond simple image generation.

2. **Unlocking Realistic Closed-Loop Simulation:** By enabling high-fidelity rendering of complex maneuvers (lane changes, acceleration, deceleration) and novel viewpoints, DriveDreamer4D directly addresses a critical limitation of current sensor simulators. This is paramount for developing and rigorously evaluating end-to-end autonomous driving systems in closed-loop settings. The ability to simulate diverse, challenging scenarios that are otherwise difficult or dangerous to collect in the real world is crucial for improving safety and performance.

3. **Enhancing Data Diversity and Mitigating Data Scarcity:** The framework effectively tackles the problem of data distribution bias in real-world driving datasets, which are often dominated by straightforward driving. By synthesizing data for out-of-distribution scenarios, DriveDreamer4D significantly expands the "experience" of the 4DGS models, leading to more generalized and robust scene representations. This reduces the heavy reliance on expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection for specific corner cases.

4. **Improved Spatiotemporal Coherence:** The quantitative and qualitative results, particularly the higher NTA-IoU and NTL-IoU scores, demonstrate that DriveDreamer4D not only improves visual quality but also ensures better spatiotemporal consistency of dynamic objects and static background elements. This is vital for accurate physical simulation and for perception models that rely on coherent scene understanding over time.

5. **Practical Industry Relevance:** The strong involvement of Li Auto Inc. underscores the immediate practical applicability and importance of this research for the automotive industry. Enabling more effective closed-loop simulation directly translates to faster iteration cycles for autonomous vehicle software development, more thorough testing, and ultimately, safer and more capable self-driving cars.

6. **Broader Impact on Generative AI and 3D/4D Vision:** Beyond autonomous driving, the methodology of using generative models to create structured, domain-specific synthetic data could inspire similar approaches in other areas requiring high-fidelity 3D/4D reconstruction or simulation, such as robotics, virtual reality, urban planning, or even scientific visualization. It highlights the potential for "generative data augmentation" to drive progress in data-intensive tasks.

In conclusion, DriveDreamer4D represents a significant step towards building truly comprehensive and robust digital twins of dynamic real-world environments. Its innovative approach of turning world models into "effective data machines" positions it to have a lasting impact on how autonomous driving systems are developed, tested, and ultimately deployed.
