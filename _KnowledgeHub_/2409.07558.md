# Unsupervised Point Cloud Registration with Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> A self-distillation framework, DiReg, enables deep learning models to perform unsupervised rigid point cloud registration, achieving performance comparable to supervised methods on both RGB-D and automotive radar datasets. The approach simplifies training by eliminating the need for ground truth labels, hand-crafted features, and pseudo-label verifiers, enhancing adaptability across different sensor modalities.

## Problem

- Rigid point cloud registration, essential for 3D applications, traditionally relies on expensive and difficult-to-acquire ground truth transformations for supervised training.
- The high cost and scarcity of labeled data hinder the scalability of deep learning models for diverse, large-scale real-world scenarios, particularly in autonomous driving.
- Prior unsupervised registration methods often depend on complex components like pseudo label verifiers or specific data structures, limiting their generalizability across different sensor modalities.

## Method

- A self-distillation framework, DiReg, is introduced, employing a student-teacher architecture where both networks share a feature extractor based on sparse convolutions.
- The teacher network, updated via an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student's parameters, generates pseudo-labels by applying a robust solver (RANSAC, optionally ICP) to correspondences derived from unaugmented point clouds.
- The student network is trained using a hardest-contrastive loss on augmented point clouds, guided by the teacher's pseudo-labels, deliberately omitting a pseudo label verifier and hand-crafted features for bootstrapping.

## Results

- On the 3DMatch benchmark for RGB-D data, DiReg achieved a Registration Recall (RR) of **91.6%**, closely matching the supervised FCGF's **92.0%** and outperforming other unsupervised methods.
- For challenging automotive radar data, DiReg demonstrated a Registration Recall (RR) of **95.8%** with a Relative Translation Error of **0.413** cm and Relative Rotation Error of **0.156**°, performing comparably to supervised approaches.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the teacher's unaugmented input and the removal of the pseudo label verifier were critical for DiReg's robust performance and generalizability across diverse sensor modalities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing the teacher network with unaugmented point cloud inputs significantly improves the robustness and speed of the bootstrapping phase, especially with randomly initialized networks.
> - Removing the pseudo label verifier, a component found in prior unsupervised methods, can prevent the discarding of valuable, challenging samples, leading to better overall performance.
> - Reliance on hand-crafted features for bootstrapping (e.g., FPFH) is not generalizable across all sensor modalities and can be ineffective or detrimental for sparse and noisy data like automotive radar.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Rigid point cloud registration is a foundational task in various 3D applications, including 3D reconstruction, augmented reality, and autonomous navigation. Traditionally, this problem has been addressed by two main categories of methods: learning-free heuristics, such as Iterative Closest Point (ICP) and Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC), and supervised deep learning approaches. While effective, supervised methods often require extensive ground truth poses for training, which can be costly and difficult to acquire on a large scale, particularly in automotive contexts where diverse scenarios and large volumes of data from consumer-grade vehicles are prevalent.

The challenge of ground truth data scarcity has led to an emerging focus on unsupervised point cloud registration methods. This work contributes to this evolving landscape by proposing a self-distillation approach. Prior unsupervised methods, such as Self-supervised Geometric Perception (SGP) and Extend Your Own Correspondences (EYOC), have explored student-teacher architectures and self-supervision techniques. SGP, for example, uses a learning-free robust solver as a teacher to generate pseudo labels for a trainable student feature matcher, with new labels generated periodically. EYOC extends this concept to automotive LiDAR data, incorporating a momentum encoder and progressive learning from consecutive frames.

This research aims to advance unsupervised point cloud registration by simplifying the training framework of existing self-supervised methods. It draws inspiration from recent advancements in self-distillation techniques in the image domain, where student and teacher networks, both parameterized feature extractors, are updated continuously. The proposed method specifically addresses limitations observed in SGP and EYOC, such as the reliance on pseudo label verifiers, hand-crafted bootstrap features, and assumptions about data structure like progressive datasets or consecutive frames. By removing these components and proposing a refined self-distillation strategy, this work seeks to enhance the adaptability and generalizability of unsupervised registration across different sensor modalities, including RGB-D and automotive radar, where previous methods might encounter difficulties due to modality-specific feature requirements or augmentation strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Develop an unsupervised point cloud registration method:** The core goal is to enable deep learning models to learn rigid point cloud registration without requiring ground truth transformations during training. This addresses the significant challenge of data scarcity and the high cost associated with obtaining labeled datasets, particularly in real-world applications like autonomous driving.
2. **Simplify the training procedure:** The authors aim to streamline the unsupervised learning process compared to existing methods. Specifically, this involves eliminating the need for components such as a pseudo label verifier, hand-crafted features for bootstrapping, and progressive datasets. These components often require careful tuning or may not generalize well across different sensor types and scenarios.
3. **Enhance adaptability and generalizability across modalities:** A key objective is to demonstrate that the proposed method can perform effectively on diverse types of point cloud data, beyond the typical RGB-D datasets. The study specifically targets generalization to automotive radar point clouds, a challenging modality where conventional hand-crafted features often fail due to the sparse and noisy nature of the data.
4. **Achieve performance comparable to supervised methods:** The underlying motivation is to bridge the performance gap between unsupervised and supervised registration approaches, enabling the use of large-scale, cost-effective crowdsourced data without sacrificing alignment quality.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several practical considerations:
- **Cost and Scalability of Ground Truth Data:** Collecting accurate ground truth poses for point cloud registration is expensive and time-consuming, especially in dynamic environments or for large-scale datasets. This limits the scalability of supervised learning paradigms.
- **Availability of Unlabeled Data:** Modern applications, particularly in autonomous driving, can generate vast amounts of unlabeled point cloud data from mass-produced vehicles (crowdsourced data). Unsupervised methods can leverage this data to learn robust registration models at a lower cost.
- **Limitations of Existing Unsupervised Approaches:** Previous unsupervised methods often rely on specific heuristics or additional data structures (e.g., consecutive frames for progressive learning) that limit their applicability or require modality-specific adjustments. For instance, hand-crafted features like FPFH, used for bootstrapping in some methods, may not be discriminative enough for certain sensor types like radar. Data augmentation strategies in some existing works can also hinder robust initial training.

By addressing these motivations and objectives, the research aims to provide a more robust, generalized, and practical framework for point cloud registration, capable of utilizing large-scale unlabeled data in diverse real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed DiReg (Self-distillation for Registration), employs a student-teacher architecture to learn point cloud registration in an unsupervised manner. This approach is characterized by its continuous generation of pseudo labels by the teacher network to train the student, with the teacher's parameters being updated via an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student's.

**Overall Architecture:**
The framework consists of a student network and a teacher network. Both networks share the same architecture, typically based on a feature extractor.
- **Teacher Network:** Receives the original, unaugmented point cloud pair (A, B). Its feature extractor is not trained directly via backpropagation but updated through EMA of the student's parameters. It is coupled with a learning-free robust solver.
- **Student Network:** Receives augmented views of the point cloud pair (e.g., Ã, B̃). Its feature extractor is trained directly using a contrastive loss, guided by the pseudo labels generated by the teacher.

**Feature Matching:**
The feature matcher within both student and teacher networks utilizes the Fully Convolutional Geometric Features (FCGF) feature extractor, which is a 3D variant of a ResUNet with sparse convolutions. This choice maintains comparability with existing unsupervised approaches.
1. **Feature Extraction:** For a given point cloud pair (A, B) or its augmented views (Ã, B̃), the feature extractor predicts latent features (F_P ∈ R^ℓ) for all points in each cloud (F_A, F_B or F_Ã, F_B̃). The teacher's features are denoted F_t_P and the student's F_s_P.
2. **Raw Correspondence Generation:** For the teacher, initial correspondences (C_raw) are generated by performing a nearest neighbor search in the feature space. For each point in point cloud A, the corresponding point in B is found by identifying the point in B whose feature vector has the minimal distance to A's feature vector (C_raw = NN(F_t_A, F_t_B)).

**Pseudo Label Generation:**
The raw correspondences (C_raw) from the untrained teacher network are typically noisy. To improve their quality and generate reliable pseudo labels, a robust solver is incorporated:
1. **Transformation Estimation:** RANSAC, optionally followed by ICP, is applied to the C_raw correspondences. This solver estimates a rigid transformation (T̂ = {R̂, t̂}) that aligns the two point clouds. The solver optimizes for the unsupervised inlier ratio (IR), which measures the proportion of correspondences that fall within a predefined distance threshold (τ1) after transformation.
2. **Refined Correspondence Generation:** After estimating the transformation T̂, a new set of refined correspondences (C_ref) is generated. This involves transforming points from A using T̂ and then performing a nearest neighbor search in the coordinate space between the transformed A and B. Only correspondences with a distance below a second threshold (τ2) are retained. These C_ref constitute the pseudo labels used to supervise the student.
3. **Removal of Pseudo Label Verifier:** Unlike some prior methods like SGP, DiReg explicitly omits a pseudo label verifier. The authors observed that this verifier, which typically discards samples with low inlier ratios, can negatively impact performance by removing challenging but potentially informative samples.

**Unsupervised Training:**
The student network is trained using the refined pseudo labels (C_ref) from the teacher.
1. **Loss Function:** A hardest-contrastive loss function (L) is employed. This loss encourages features of positive pairs (from C_ref) to be close in feature space while pushing features of "hardest negative" pairs to be distant. Crucially, no gradients are backpropagated through the teacher network, indicated by a stop-gradient operator.
2. **Update Strategy:** The teacher's parameters (θ_t) are updated at each training step using an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student's parameters (θ_s). The momentum term (α) for the EMA follows a cosine schedule, gradually increasing from 0.9 to 1. This continuous update mechanism ensures that the teacher's pseudo labels progressively improve as the student learns.

**Data Augmentation and Bootstrapping:**
Data augmentation plays a critical role, particularly during the initial "bootstrap" phase when the networks are randomly initialized.
1. **Standard Augmentation:** Both point clouds are randomly rotated to encourage rotation invariance in the network, as is common for FCGF.
2. **Teacher Input Augmentation Strategy:** A key design choice in DiReg is to apply data augmentation only to the student's input (Ã, B̃), while the teacher receives the unaugmented input (A, B). This strategy is motivated by the observation that an untrained teacher, especially if randomly initialized, struggles to perform robust registration on augmented data. By providing the teacher with unaugmented data, the initial registration task is simplified, accelerating the bootstrap phase and leading to more stable training. This differentiates DiReg from methods like EYOC, which augment both student and teacher inputs.
3. **Bootstrapping without Hand-crafted Features:** Unlike SGP, which relies on hand-crafted FPFH features for initial bootstrapping, DiReg initiates training with a randomly initialized teacher network. The improved augmentation strategy for the teacher's input is cited as making FPFH features counterproductive. This eliminates a dependency on modality-specific features, enhancing generalizability.

This methodology combines robust self-distillation principles with careful consideration of data augmentation and robust solvers to enable unsupervised learning of point cloud registration without reliance on external labels or complex pre-processing steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated DiReg on two distinct datasets: the 3DMatch benchmark for RGB-D data and a proprietary automotive radar dataset. The performance was compared against supervised FCGF, SGP, and EYOC.

#### 4.1. Performance on 3DMatch (RGB-D Data)

- **DiReg Performance:** DiReg achieved the highest performance among all unsupervised methods. It recorded a Feature Match Recall (FMR) of 92.7%, a Registration Recall (RR) of 91.6%, and an Inlier Ratio (IR) of 24.1%. These metrics indicate its ability to find accurate correspondences and successfully align point clouds.
- **Comparison to Supervised:** DiReg’s performance was close to that of the supervised FCGF model (FMR 93.5%, RR 92.0%, IR 24.3%), demonstrating near-supervised performance in an unsupervised setting.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **SGP:** Achieved comparable results but with a marginal decline (FMR 91.3%, RR 90.8%, IR 22.4%).
 - **EYOC:** Showed significantly lower performance (FMR 62.5%, RR 76.8%, IR 10.6%). The authors attributed this to EYOC’s data augmentation strategy, which applies augmentation to both student and teacher inputs, potentially hindering robust bootstrapping, and its specific design for distant point clouds.
- **Ablation (Shared Parameters):** A variant of DiReg where student and teacher share the same parameters (θ_t = θ_s, i.e., no momentum teacher) also performed strongly (FMR 92.3%, RR 91.1%, IR 22.4%), ranking third overall and offering a memory-efficient alternative.

#### 4.2. Performance on Automotive Radar Data

- **DiReg Performance:** DiReg demonstrated strong performance on the challenging automotive radar dataset. It achieved an RR of 95.8%, a Relative Translation Error (RTE) of 0.413 cm, and a Relative Rotation Error (RRE) of 0.156°.
- **Comparison to Supervised:** DiReg’s performance was again close to the supervised FCGF model (RR 96.6%, RTE 0.355 cm, RRE 0.160°).
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **EYOC:** Performed reasonably well (RR 91.7%, RTE 0.487 cm, RRE 0.166°), better than SGP. This was noted to be consistent with the authors' observation that data augmentation strategy is more critical for 3DMatch, suggesting EYOC's approach might be less detrimental on radar or that its distant-point-cloud focus translates better.
 - **SGP:** Exhibited suboptimal results (RR 90.2%, RTE 0.596 cm, RRE 0.219°). This was later attributed in ablations to its reliance on learning-free FPFH features and its verifier, which are less suitable for radar data.
- **Ablation (Shared Parameters):** The variant of DiReg with shared parameters (θ_t = θ_s) performed very similarly (RR 96.1%, RTE 0.390 cm, RRE 0.181°), indicating that the momentum teacher's impact was less pronounced on this specific dataset.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Data Augmentation for Teacher:** Experiments showed that removing data augmentation from the teacher’s input significantly enhanced the robustness of the training process and accelerated bootstrapping. When augmentation was applied to the teacher, training either prolonged or failed to converge. This validated the design choice of providing unaugmented data to the teacher initially.
- **Importance of Individual Components (Radar Subset):**
 - **ICP:** Including ICP after RANSAC did not improve performance on the radar dataset (DiReg with ICP: RR 95.3%, RTE 0.289 cm, RRE 0.093°; DiReg without ICP: RR 95.7%, RTE 0.308 cm, RRE 0.093°). Consequently, ICP was omitted for radar experiments.
 - **Pseudo Label Verifier:** The verifier, adopted from SGP, resulted in suboptimal performance (RR 81.6%, RTE 0.935 cm, RRE 0.141°). This was attributed to its tendency to remove challenging samples and its potential need for specific adjustments for new datasets.
 - **FPFH + RANSAC:** A direct application of learning-free FPFH features combined with RANSAC failed to work on radar data (RR 3.7%, RTE 52.405 cm, RRE 62.909°). This highlights the limitation of features solely based on 3D coordinates for modalities with additional unique characteristics like radar cross-section. These ablation findings directly explain the suboptimal performance of SGP on the radar dataset.

In summary, the main findings indicate that DiReg is capable of unsupervised point cloud registration with performance levels approaching supervised methods. Its simplified architecture and refined data augmentation strategy for bootstrapping contribute to its robustness and generalizability across diverse modalities, including those where traditional or other unsupervised methods may struggle.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study presents a significant contribution to the field of point cloud registration, particularly in the domain of unsupervised learning. The proposed self-distillation framework, DiReg, offers several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Reduced Reliance on Ground Truth Data:** The most immediate impact is the substantial reduction in the need for expensive and labor-intensive ground truth poses. By achieving performance comparable to supervised methods without labeled data, DiReg enables the utilization of vast amounts of unlabeled, crowdsourced data. This democratizes the development of registration models, making advanced capabilities accessible to applications with limited annotation budgets.
2. **Simplified Training Procedure:** The work simplifies the unsupervised training pipeline by eliminating components like pseudo label verifiers, hand-crafted bootstrap features, and progressive datasets, which were present in earlier unsupervised methods. This simplification reduces the complexity of model development, hyperparameter tuning, and adaptation to new datasets or sensor modalities, making the approach more practical for real-world deployment.
3. **Enhanced Generalizability Across Modalities:** Demonstrating strong performance on both RGB-D and challenging automotive radar point clouds highlights the method's robustness and generalizability. Unlike previous methods that might struggle with radar due to their reliance on specific 3D geometric features (like FPFH), DiReg's learned features and self-distillation approach are shown to adapt effectively. This is particularly impactful for autonomous driving, where diverse sensor suites (LiDAR, radar, camera) are common, and robust multi-sensor registration is critical.
4. **Improved Bootstrapping Strategy:** The identification and resolution of issues related to data augmentation for the teacher network during bootstrapping represent a key methodological insight. By providing unaugmented input to the teacher, the method ensures a more stable and efficient initial learning phase, preventing convergence failures observed in alternative augmentation strategies. This contributes to the foundational understanding of self-supervised learning for geometric tasks.
5. **Accelerated Development and Deployment in Robotics and Autonomous Driving:** In applications such as autonomous navigation, 3D reconstruction, and robotic manipulation, precise point cloud registration is fundamental. By enabling the training of high-quality registration models from large-scale unlabeled data, DiReg can accelerate the development, testing, and deployment of these systems. It offers a pathway to leverage the continuous stream of data generated by deployed vehicles to improve their perception capabilities without constant manual annotation.

**Potential Future Research Directions:** The paper also suggests avenues for future work, such as exploring non-contrastive loss functions to eliminate the reliance on estimated negative pairs, and integrating differentiable versions of RANSAC for end-to-end differentiable training pipelines. These directions could further refine the unsupervised learning process and potentially lead to even more robust and performant models.

In conclusion, this research presents a practical and effective unsupervised point cloud registration framework that addresses key limitations of existing methods. Its ability to achieve near-supervised performance across diverse modalities with a simplified training regimen positions it to have a substantial impact on the development of scalable and robust 3D perception systems.
