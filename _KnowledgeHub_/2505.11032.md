# DexGarmentLab: Dexterous Garment Manipulation Environment with Generalizable Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and UC Berkeley developed DexGarmentLab, a simulation environment, automated data collection pipeline, and a generalizable policy (HALO) to advance dexterous bimanual manipulation of garments. The HALO policy consistently achieved higher success rates in 14 diverse simulation tasks and demonstrated strong real-world performance, while the automated pipeline efficiently generated demonstration data.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with dexterous bimanual garment manipulation due to the infinite state space, non-linear dynamics, and diverse categories of deformable objects.
- Existing simulation environments lack the necessary physical realism and support for multi-finger dexterous hands interacting with deformable materials.
- Policy learning for deformable objects is highly data-intensive, and current methods require extensive, labor-intensive data collection and struggle with generalization to varied garment shapes and deformation states.

## Method

- Introduced **DexGarmentLab**, a simulation environment for dexterous ==bimanual garment manipulation== featuring refined physical simulation parameters and 15 diverse tasks.
- Proposed an automated data collection pipeline that generates diverse manipulation demonstrations from a single expert example by exploiting structural correspondence across garment categories.
- Developed HALO (Hierarchical gArment-manipuLation pOlicy), which combines a Garment Affordance Model (GAM) for precise manipulation point detection and a Structure-Aware Diffusion Policy (SADP) for generalizable trajectory generation.

## Results

- The HALO policy consistently achieved superior performance across 14 dexterous garment manipulation tasks in simulation, with an **0.82** ± **0.06** success rate for 'Fling Dress' compared to **0.59** ± **0.05** for Diffusion Policy.
- The automated data collection pipeline efficiently generated 100 demonstrations per task within 30-80 seconds in simulation, producing data that yielded comparable policy performance to manually collected data.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated HALO's effectiveness on tasks like 'Fold Tops' (13/15 successful trials), and showed that adding a small amount of real-world data (15 demonstrations) significantly improved sim-to-real transfer from 8/15 to 13/15 for 'Hang Trousers'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate physical simulation, particularly with refined adhesion and friction parameters, is critical for realistic and stable robot-garment interactions in dexterous manipulation.
> - Leveraging structural correspondence across garment instances allows for efficient, automated generation of diverse training data, significantly reducing the manual effort typically required for deformable object manipulation tasks.
> - A hierarchical approach, first localizing manipulation affordances and then generating structure-aware trajectories, enables policies to generalize effectively to novel garment shapes, sizes, and deformation states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects, especially garments, remains a grand challenge in robotics, significantly lagging behind advancements in rigid and articulated object manipulation. This paper positions itself at the forefront of addressing this gap, particularly by focusing on *dexterous bimanual garment manipulation*.

Historically, robotic manipulation research has progressed from simple pick-and-place of rigid objects to more complex tasks involving articulated objects. However, deformable objects introduce immense complexity due to their infinite state space, non-linear dynamics, and self-occlusions. Previous attempts at deformable object manipulation often focused on simpler objects like square cloths, ropes, or bags, or relied on methods that lacked realistic physical interactions or comprehensive task diversity. Existing simulators like Softgym and Fluidlab have limitations in object types, physical realism, or support for dexterous multi-finger hands. GarmentLab, a recent precursor, improved garment simulation but still lacked support for dexterous bimanual manipulation and automated data collection.

This work specifically tackles the unmet need for **dexterous (especially bimanual) manipulation of garments**. While there has been growing interest in dexterous manipulation for tasks like in-hand manipulation and articulated object interaction, most research has been single-handed or limited to rigid-body dynamics. Bimanual dexterity, crucial for complex human-like garment handling actions (e.g., cradling, pinching, knotting), has been largely underexplored in the context of deformable materials.

Furthermore, the paper addresses critical bottlenecks in **robot learning for deformable objects**. Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) often requires intricate, task-specific reward engineering, while imitation learning (IL) demands extensive and labor-intensive demonstrations, typically collected through teleoperation. The difficulty in generalizing policies to novel garment shapes, sizes, and deformation states is another pervasive issue. By proposing an automated data collection pipeline and a hierarchical generalizable policy (HALO), DexGarmentLab directly contributes to overcoming these data and generalization hurdles, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with learning-based approaches for deformable object robotics. This work aims to establish a new benchmark and environment that can accelerate research in this complex domain, similar to how environments like OpenAI Gym or Isaac Gym have driven progress in other areas of robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DexGarmentLab stems from the glaring disparity between human capability and robotic proficiency in handling garments. Humans effortlessly perform a multitude of garment manipulation tasks using dexterous hands, thanks to superior adaptability and coordinated multi-finger control. In contrast, robots struggle due to the inherent complexity of garments, characterized by diverse categories, geometries, and intricate deformations. The overarching goal is to equip robots with similar dexterous capabilities for garment manipulation, which is critical for real-world applications in domestic assistance, industrial automation, and healthcare.

The authors identify three core challenges that hinder progress in dexterous garment manipulation:

1. **Data Challenge:** The high-dimensional action space of dexterous hands and the complex, variable states of garments make policy learning extremely data-intensive. Collecting real-world data is costly and time-consuming, while existing simulation-based data collection methods (teleoperation or training expert RL policies) are labor-intensive and inefficient.
2. **Environment Challenge:** Current simulation environments lack the necessary realism and physical accuracy for complex interactions, especially involving dexterous hands and deformable garments. Effective garment manipulation requires realistic interactions not only with the garments themselves but also with other rigid and articulated objects (e.g., hangers, human bodies).
3. **Algorithm Challenge:** Existing RL and imitation learning (IL) approaches struggle with the diverse geometries, complex states, and difficult goals of garment manipulation. They often require intricate task-specific reward designs or extensive, high-quality demonstrations, limiting their scalability and generalization to unseen garment instances or scene configurations.

To address these challenges, the paper sets out the following key objectives, also stated as its main contributions:

- **To introduce DexGarmentLab Environment:** The first simulation environment specifically designed for dexterous (especially bimanual) garment manipulation, featuring a wide range of task scenarios, high-quality 3D assets, and refined simulation techniques for realistic physical interactions.
- **To propose an Automated Data Collection Pipeline:** A method that leverages garment structural correspondence to automatically generate diverse demonstrations from a single expert example, significantly reducing manual intervention and enabling efficient, scalable data generation.
- **To propose Hierarchical gArment-manipuLation pOlicy (HALO):** A novel hierarchical framework that combines affordance prediction and a diffusion-based method to achieve generalizable manipulation of diverse garments, addressing the limitations of existing IL algorithms in handling varied garment shapes and deformations.
- **To demonstrate Effectiveness:** Through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings, to show that the proposed approach is data-efficient and exhibits superior generalization ability compared to baseline methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces DexGarmentLab as a holistic solution, encompassing a simulation environment, an automated data collection pipeline, and a generalizable policy.

#### 3.1. DexGarmentLab Environment

Built upon NVIDIA Isaac Sim 4.5.0, DexGarmentLab is tailored for dexterous (specifically bimanual) garment manipulation.
- **Observation Space:** Includes robot proprioceptive data (joint positions, end-effector 6D poses, kinematics) and visual data (point clouds from depth cameras, RGB images). Point clouds are processed (cropped, down-sampled) for efficiency.
- **Action Space:** A 60-dimensional vector, comprising 6 DoF for each UR10e arm (controlled by IK or PD) and 24 DoF for each Shadow Hand (controlled by PD). This high-dimensional action space is crucial for dexterity.
- **Physical Simulation:** Employs Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) for large, highly deformable garments (tops, dresses) and the Finite Element Method (FEM) for smaller, elastic items (gloves, hats). Crucially, the authors refine PBD by introducing **adhesion, friction, particle-adhesion-scale, and particle-friction-scale parameters**. These parameters significantly enhance realism by enabling hands to grasp and lift garments without penetration artifacts and by stabilizing internal garment dynamics, preventing excessive self-collisions and maintaining structural coherence, an improvement over prior "attach block" methods.
- **Assets and Tasks:** Leverages over 2,500 garment models from the ClothesNet dataset across 8 categories. It features 15 diverse task scenarios, categorized into garment-self-interaction (e.g., Fling, Fold) and garment-environment-interaction (e.g., Hang, Wear, Store), all designed to highlight bimanual dexterity. Controlled randomness in garment and environment asset placement enhances policy generalization.

#### 3.2. Automated Data Collection Pipeline

Addressing the data bottleneck, this pipeline reduces manual effort by generating diverse demonstrations from a single expert example.
- **Core Idea:** Exploit structural correspondence across category-level garments.
- **Garment Affordance Model (GAM):** This model is central to identifying transferable manipulation points.
 - **Training:** Built upon UniGarmentManip, GAM uses a Skeleton Merger to establish skeleton point correspondences between flat garments. Point tracing in simulation then links flat garments to their deformed versions. A PointNet++ network is trained with InfoNCE loss to learn dense visual correspondences, pulling features of positive corresponding pairs closer and pushing negative pairs apart. Input point clouds are normalized for translation and scale invariance, and diverse rotations are included for rotational invariance.
 - **Inference:** Given a demo garment point cloud, its grasp points, and a novel garment point cloud, the pretrained GAM identifies corresponding target grasp points on the novel garment by matching features based on similarity scores.
- **Pipeline Execution:** With the identified target grasp points, demo task sequences, and demo hand grasp poses (initially generated via LeapMotion teleoperation), the robotic arms (via IK) and dexterous hands (via PD) execute the task. Task trajectories are adaptively adjusted based on garment shape, length, and scene configurations (e.g., lifting height for folding, placement for hanging). The process records comprehensive data (images, point clouds, robot states) for policy training.

#### 3.3. Generalizable Policy (HALO)

HALO (Hierarchical gArment-manipuLation pOlicy) is designed to improve generalization across diverse garment shapes and complex deformation states. It decomposes the task into two stages:
- **Stage 1: Generalizable Affordance Points:** The already-described GAM is utilized to accurately locate the manipulation areas on the garment. This step addresses the problem of imitation learning policies failing to grasp the correct position on novel garments.
- **Stage 2: Generalizable Trajectories via Structure-Aware Diffusion Policy (SADP):** This policy builds on the Diffusion Policy framework but incorporates a richer observation representation to achieve better generalization.
 - **Observation (`s`) Representation:**
 - Garment point cloud (operated) and left/right target point affordances (from GAM) are concatenated and processed by PointNet++ to extract `F_garment`.
 - Interaction-object point cloud (if applicable) is processed by an MLP to extract `F_object`.
 - `F_garment` and `F_object` are combined into `F_scene`.
 - The full environment point cloud and robot state are encoded using MLPs and fused with `F_scene` to form the final denoising condition `s` for SADP.
 - **Functionality:** SADP generates joint actions (60 DoF) that adjust trajectories based on the garment's current state (position, shape, structure) and interaction-object state, ensuring robust execution even with unseen variations. This addresses the limitation of previous IL methods in generating suitable trajectories given garment variability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in the paper rigorously evaluate DexGarmentLab's components and the HALO policy's performance across diverse tasks in both simulation and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results in Simulation

- **Superior Performance:** HALO consistently achieves significantly higher success rates across 14 dexterous garment manipulation tasks compared to state-of-the-art diffusion-based baselines like Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3). For example, in tasks like "Fling Dress," HALO achieved 0.82 ± 0.06 success rate, while DP and DP3 scored 0.59 ± 0.05 and 0.51 ± 0.03, respectively. Similar improvements were observed across all tasks, with HALO often achieving success rates in the 0.8-0.9 range.
- **Comparison with Additional Baselines:** HALO also outperformed other imitation learning (ACT) and vision-language action (pi0, RDT) baselines, highlighting its specialized capabilities for deformable object manipulation. RL+VLM methods like Eureka showed very low success rates due to current simulator limitations for parallelization and complex reward functions.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation study effectively demonstrates the critical contributions of GAM and SADP within HALO.
- **Impact of GAM:** Removing GAM (`Ours w/o GAM`) led to a marked decrease in success rates across all tasks (e.g., Fling Dress dropped to 0.66 ± 0.02 from HALO's 0.82 ± 0.06). This quantitatively confirms GAM's role in accurately locating precise manipulation areas, preventing issues like "Fail to Catch Garment" or "Catch Bad Position" as visually illustrated.
- **Impact of SADP:** Excluding SADP (`Ours w/o SADP`) also resulted in a significant performance drop (e.g., Fling Dress dropped to 0.68 ± 0.09). This validates SADP's ability to adjust trajectories dynamically based on garment shapes and structures, preventing failures such as "Move Beyond Hanger" or "Fold Uncompletely" due to misjudged movements.

#### 4.3. Automated Data Collection Efficiency

- The automated data collection pipeline proved highly efficient, acquiring 100 demonstrations per task within 30-80 seconds each in simulation, and 50 demonstrations in real-world for ~36-75 seconds each. This is significantly faster than manual teleoperation, addressing a key challenge.
- Policies trained on autonomously collected data achieved comparable performance to those trained on teleoperation data, validating the quality of the automated pipeline while drastically reducing human effort.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

- **Effectiveness in Reality (Way 1 - Real-world data collection & training):** HALO demonstrated excellent performance on four real-world tasks (Fold Tops, Hang Tops, Wear Scarf, Wear Hat) using a RealMan RM75-6F arm with Psibot G0-R dexterous hands. It achieved 13/15 successful trials in Fold Tops, Hang Tops, and Wear Hat, and 11/15 in Wear Scarf, outperforming DP and DP3 baselines. Visual results underscored GAM's accurate affordance point generation and SADP's adaptive trajectory generation for diverse real garments and interactions.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (Way 2 - Sim-trained policy to real world):**
 - Initially, policies trained *solely* on simulation data showed a performance drop when directly transferred to the real world (e.g., 8/15 for Hang Trousers, 9/15 for Wear Hat). This highlights the persistent sim-to-real gap, even with advanced simulation.
 - However, incorporating a *small amount* of real-world data (15 demonstrations) into the training process significantly improved sim-to-real performance (13/15 for both tasks), demonstrating that sim-to-real performance can be effectively boosted with targeted real-world fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexGarmentLab research marks a significant advancement in the field of robot manipulation, particularly for deformable objects. Its contributions extend across simulation, data generation, and policy learning, with profound implications for future robotics applications.

**1. Pioneering Simulation Environment:** DexGarmentLab is the *first dedicated simulation environment for dexterous (especially bimanual) garment manipulation*. This fills a critical void, providing a robust, high-fidelity platform for researchers to develop and test algorithms that were previously difficult or impossible to evaluate due to the lack of realistic simulation of multi-finger hands interacting with complex deformable materials. The refined physical simulation parameters (adhesion, friction, particle scales) enhance realism, which is crucial for reducing the notorious sim-to-real gap.

**2. Addressing the Data Bottleneck:** The proposed automated data collection pipeline is a game-changer. By leveraging structural correspondence and a single expert demonstration, it drastically reduces the labor and time typically required for collecting large, diverse datasets for robot learning. This data efficiency makes complex, high-dimensional manipulation tasks more tractable and scalable, accelerating research and development in a domain often crippled by data scarcity.

**3. Generalizable and Robust Policy Learning:** HALO's hierarchical design, integrating a Garment Affordance Model (GAM) for precise manipulation area localization and a Structure-Aware Diffusion Policy (SADP) for adaptive trajectory generation, represents a substantial leap in generalizable control for deformable objects. Its ability to generalize to unseen garment shapes and deformation states, even with diverse scene configurations, is a key step towards deploying robots in unstructured real-world environments. This moves beyond task-specific solutions towards more versatile and intelligent robot behavior.

**4. Advancing Dexterous Manipulation:** By emphasizing bimanual dexterous manipulation, DexGarmentLab pushes the boundaries of robotic dexterity. This is essential for tackling highly complex and human-centric tasks that require coordinated multi-finger control, which are beyond the capabilities of simpler grippers.

**5. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** While acknowledging the persistent challenges, the research provides valuable insights into sim-to-real transfer. The finding that a small amount of real-world data can significantly improve policies trained primarily in simulation offers a practical strategy for deploying complex manipulation skills from virtual environments to physical robots.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Domestic Robotics:** The ability to handle garments is fundamental for home-assistant robots capable of tasks like laundry, folding, and tidying, significantly improving quality of life and autonomy for individuals.
- **Industrial Automation:** In textile manufacturing, logistics, and retail, automating garment handling tasks could lead to increased efficiency, reduced labor costs, and improved safety.
- **Healthcare and Assisted Living:** Robots capable of assisting with dressing or manipulating clothing for patients could provide crucial support in hospitals, care homes, and for individuals with limited mobility.
- **Research Catalyst:** DexGarmentLab provides a powerful new tool and benchmark for the entire robotics community, stimulating further research into deformable object perception, control, and learning algorithms, paving the way for even more sophisticated robotic capabilities.

In conclusion, DexGarmentLab is a foundational contribution that addresses long-standing challenges in deformable object manipulation. By providing an advanced simulation environment, an efficient data collection method, and a highly generalizable policy, it significantly accelerates the path towards truly dexterous and intelligent robots capable of interacting with the complex, soft world around us.
