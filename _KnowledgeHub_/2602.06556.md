# LIBERO-X: Robustness Litmus for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LIBERO-X presents a new benchmark for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, featuring a hierarchical evaluation protocol and a high-diversity training dataset to comprehensively assess robustness and generalization. Experiments with LIBERO-X reveal that current VLA models exhibit persistent limitations in spatial generalization, structural invariance, and multi-step reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) benchmarks produce optimistic results due to limited evaluation protocols, often focusing on isolated perturbations and lacking progressive difficulty.
- Current benchmarks suffer from unfaithful data distributions, characterized by insufficient training data diversity and narrow train-test gaps, which encourage models to memorize rather than generalize.
- Models trained on previous benchmarks tend to overfit to specific configurations, showing sharp performance drops under minor distribution shifts.

## Method

- **LIBERO-X** introduces a ==high-diversity training dataset==, collected through ==human teleoperation==, featuring 600 tasks across 100 scenes, each supporting multiple ==fine-grained manipulation objectives==.
- It proposes a ==multi-level evaluation protocol== with five progressively challenging levels that systematically perturb ==spatial layouts==, ==object properties==, and ==instruction semantics==.
- A ==multi-label evaluation strategy== is integrated for ==fine-grained diagnostics==, categorizing tasks by ==interaction type==, ==subtask count==, ==spatial relation==, and ==object attribute==.

## Results

- VLA models demonstrated an average success rate of only **39.4%** at the lowest difficulty level (Level 1), indicating the benchmark's rigor compared to existing evaluations.
- A significant average performance decline of **31.2%** was observed across models from Level 1 to Level 5, with the most substantial drop (**12.7%**) occurring at Level 3, which involved scene topology reconstruction.
- Models showed near-zero success rates for tasks requiring **3+** sequential steps, and performance plateaued when time limits were relaxed beyond **1.3x** human average, suggesting intrinsic limitations rather than just temporal issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLA models struggle with spatial generalization, overfitting to specific configurations and degrading significantly when objects are placed in out-of-distribution positions.
> - Models lack structural invariance, showing substantial performance drops when scene topology is reconstructed, even if semantic relationships are maintained.
> - Long-horizon tasks critically limit VLA model performance, with success rates plummeting to near zero for tasks requiring three or more sequential steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a significant paradigm shift in robotic manipulation, moving towards integrating perception, language understanding, and control in an end-to-end manner. These models promise robust performance across complex tasks, but their systematic and reliable evaluation remains a central challenge. This paper, LIBERO-X, directly addresses this critical need by proposing a new benchmark that aims to provide a more faithful assessment of VLA model capabilities.

Existing VLA benchmarks, such as LIBERO and SimpleEnv, have played a crucial role in advancing the field by offering controlled environments and standardized evaluation protocols. However, the authors argue that these benchmarks often lead to "overly optimistic or even misleading conclusions" due to fundamental limitations in both their evaluation protocols and data distributions.

The shortcomings of previous benchmarks, which LIBERO-X aims to overcome, can be categorized as follows:

1. **Limited Evaluation Protocols:**
 - **Focus on Individual Perturbations:** Many extensions of LIBERO (e.g., SafeLIBERO, LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-Plus) introduce additional perturbation factors like random obstacles or varied initial states. However, these perturbations are often modeled independently, failing to capture the complex, multi-source distribution shifts inherent in real-world environments.
 - **Lack of Progressive Difficulty:** Existing benchmarks typically lack a systematic progression of difficulty. This makes it challenging to characterize how model performance degrades as environmental and task complexity incrementally increase, hindering fine-grained analysis of robustness.
 - **Over-reliance on Memorization:** The paper notes that high nominal success rates on existing benchmarks often decline sharply under even minor perturbations, suggesting that models may be "memorizing" training data rather than acquiring truly generalized skills.

2. **Unfaithful Data Distribution (Train-Test Gap):**
 - **Limited Training Data Diversity:** Existing datasets often exhibit insufficient diversity in tasks, scenes, and trajectories. A single scene might be associated with only a few tasks, and demonstrations for the same task often follow highly homogeneous action sequences, leading to models that learn "template-like strategies."
 - **Narrow Train-Test Gap:** Many benchmarks adopt test configurations that closely resemble training settings, with only minor perturbations. This narrow gap can result in near-saturated success rates that don't reveal a model's true generalization ability or its limitations when faced with out-of-distribution scenarios. This can lead to "action overfitting," where models mechanically replay training actions.

LIBERO-X positions itself as a significant advancement in this landscape by jointly tackling these two critical issues. It introduces a benchmark that:
- Combines a **hierarchical evaluation protocol** with progressively challenging levels, designed to systematically perturb spatial layouts, object properties, and instruction semantics. This allows for a comprehensive assessment of robustness under multi-dimensional distribution shifts.
- Provides a **high-diversity training dataset** collected via human teleoperation, where each scene supports multiple fine-grained manipulation objectives. This rich data foundation is intended to mitigate overfitting and encourage the learning of generalizable manipulation strategies, bridging the train-evaluation distribution gap.

By addressing these core limitations, LIBERO-X aims to establish a more rigorous and reliable foundation for evaluating VLA models, effectively guiding the research community towards developing models that are truly robust and transferable to complex, dynamic real-world robotic deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **systematically rethink Vision–Language–Action (VLA) model benchmarking from both evaluation and data perspectives** to provide a more reliable and comprehensive assessment of their capabilities. The authors explicitly state that reliable benchmarking is critical for advancing VLA models, as it is essential for revealing their generalization, robustness, and the effective alignment of perception with language-driven manipulation tasks.

The core motivations for introducing LIBERO-X stem from two fundamental limitations observed in existing VLA benchmarks:

1. **Inadequate Evaluation Protocols:**
 - **Limited Real-World Perturbations:** Current benchmarks predominantly focus on isolated perturbation types (e.g., only spatial shifts). This fails to reflect the compounded, multi-source distribution shifts that are commonplace in realistic robotic deployment scenarios. Real environments present simultaneous variations in object positions, visual appearances, and task instructions.
 - **Absence of Progressive Difficulty:** Most existing evaluations lack a structured progression of difficulty. This makes it difficult to conduct a fine-grained analysis of how model performance degrades as environmental and task complexity incrementally rise. Without such a progression, it's challenging to pinpoint specific failure modes and understand the boundaries of a model's capabilities. The motivation here is to move beyond "pass/fail" assessments to a more diagnostic understanding of *why* models succeed or fail.

2. **Unfaithful Data Distribution and Overfitting:**
 - **Insufficient Training Data Diversity:** Existing training datasets often suffer from limited diversity in scenes, tasks, and action trajectories. This homogeneity leads to models learning "template-like" behaviors or memorizing specific solutions, rather than acquiring truly generalizable manipulation skills. The consequence is that models may achieve high success rates on test sets that closely resemble training data, but fail significantly when faced with even minor out-of-distribution variations.
 - **Narrow Train-Test Gap:** Many benchmarks feature test configurations that are too similar to their training counterparts. This narrow gap prevents a true assessment of a model's generalization ability, as high performance might simply indicate overfitting rather than robust skill acquisition. The motivation is to create a substantial and realistic train-test divergence to truly "litmus test" the models.

In response to these motivations, the **key objectives** of LIBERO-X are:

- **To introduce a hierarchical evaluation protocol** that systematically increases difficulty across three core capabilities: spatial generalization, object recognition, and task instruction understanding. This protocol aims to enable fine-grained analysis of performance degradation under escalating environmental and task complexity.
- **To construct a high-diversity training dataset** via human teleoperation. This dataset is designed to expose models to a broader distribution of tasks and scenes by associating multiple fine-grained manipulation objectives with each scene, thereby mitigating overfitting and fostering the learning of truly generalizable manipulation strategies.
- **To provide a more reliable foundation for assessing and advancing VLA development** by revealing persistent limitations in scene comprehension and instruction grounding that current benchmarks often mask.

By achieving these objectives, LIBERO-X aims to serve as a comprehensive benchmark that effectively guides VLA development toward more reliable and robust deployment in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LIBERO-X fundamentally rethinks VLA benchmarking by integrating two core methodological components: a **hierarchical evaluation protocol** and a **high-diversity training dataset**.

#### 3.1. High-Diversity Training Dataset

To address the limitations of narrow data distributions and overfitting prevalent in existing benchmarks, LIBERO-X introduces a new training dataset collected via human teleoperation in MuJoCo, utilizing VR technology (Meta Quest 3) and the LeRobot framework. This dataset comprises **2,520 demonstration trajectories, covering 600 tasks across 100 scenes.** The design principles behind this dataset aim to fundamentally increase scene and task diversity:

- **Multi-Task Scene Design:** Unlike previous benchmarks where scenes often correspond to a single task, LIBERO-X significantly increases task density, averaging **6 tasks per scene**. This forces models to learn versatile manipulation skills and adapt strategies under identical visual and physical conditions, rather than relying on fixed scene-task mappings.
- **Attribute-Conditioned Manipulation:** Tasks are explicitly conditioned on fine-grained object properties like size, color, and texture, moving beyond broad category recognition. Scenes include distractor objects with similar attributes, compelling models to develop strong perceptual grounding and discriminative abilities.
- **Spatial Relationship Reasoning:** Tasks go beyond simple target localization, requiring an understanding of complex spatial relationships among objects (e.g., left/right, front/back, near/far). This encourages models to learn composable semantic correspondences between objects and their language descriptions.

The data collection process involved recording state-action sequences at 20 Hz, capturing 6D Cartesian poses and gripper status, along with multi-view images (third-person and hand-eye). Strict protocols were followed to ensure high-quality, smooth, and successful demonstrations, with non-optimal trajectories discarded. The resulting dataset, as visualized by heatmaps of trajectory distributions, exhibits a broader spread and higher density compared to LIBERO, indicating its enhanced diversity. The authors employ the Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) to describe scenes and tasks, providing a structured representation.

#### 3.2. Multi-Level, Multi-Label Evaluation Framework

The LIBERO-X test set features a two-dimensional assessment design: a multi-level framework for progressive difficulty and a multi-label system for fine-grained analysis.

#### 3.2.1. Multi-level Evaluation Protocol

This protocol adopts a "reuse–adjust–reconstruct" strategy over training scenes to build a five-level hierarchy of fine-grained manipulation tasks, progressively escalating complexity across spatial reasoning, object recognition, and instruction understanding. Higher levels cumulatively add new perturbation dimensions.

- **Level 1 - Local Spatial Perturbation:** Introduces minor variations in object positions, assessing model stability under small-scale spatial changes.
- **Level 2 - Extended Spatial Perturbation:** Amplifies spatial variations by dynamically adjusting the sampling radius for object positions, pushing models to cope with larger-scale positional changes.
- **Level 3 - Scene Topology Reconstruction:** Alters the relative positions of objects (e.g., swapping targets with distractors, adding irrelevant objects) to test task execution under restructured object relationships and modified scene configurations, challenging structural invariance.
- **Level 4 - Visual Attribute Variation:** Incorporates variations in object texture, color, and size; introduces unseen object categories; and includes visually similar confounders. This level tests discriminative ability, robustness to attribute changes, and generalization to novel objects.
- **Level 5 - Semantic-Equivalent Reformulation:** Rephrases task instructions while preserving semantics. This includes synonym replacement (L5-1), word compression (L5-2), word order adjustment (L5-3), voice conversion (L5-4), and redundant descriptions (L5-5). It measures the model's ability to generalize across diverse linguistic formulations of the same task.

#### 3.2.2. Multi-label Evaluation Strategy

Complementing the hierarchical levels, a fine-grained multi-label system is applied to each task, enabling in-depth diagnostics across distinct manipulation dimensions:

- **Interaction Type:** Categorizes tasks by the core actions involved, such as Pick-and-Place, State Change (e.g., opening drawers), and Composite Actions (integrating multiple types).
- **Subtask Count:** Classifies tasks by the number of sequential action steps required (Single Action, Two Actions, Three or More Actions), assessing long-horizon planning and sequential reasoning.
- **Spatial Relation:** Describes relative positions and spatial arrangements (Surface, Container, Left–Right Relation, Ordinal Region, Relative Distance), evaluating spatial reasoning and environmental perception.
- **Object Attribute:** Characterizes visual object properties (Color, Size, Pattern, Category), assessing precise visual perception and ability to ground instructions to specific attributes.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that LIBERO-X not only provides a challenging benchmark but also offers the tools for researchers to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of VLA models, driving targeted improvements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on LIBERO-X involved fine-tuning five representative VLA models: OpenVLA-OFT, X-VLA, GR00T-N1.5, π
0, and π
0.5
. All models were fine-tuned using a behavior cloning approach on the custom LIBERO-X training dataset. Each task was evaluated over 10 trials, with minor spatial perturbations applied, and a dynamic time limit set to 1.1 times the average human completion time.

#### 4.1. Multi-level Evaluation Findings (Table II, Figure 5)

The multi-level evaluation revealed significant performance drops across all models, contrasting sharply with the near-saturated success rates often reported on existing benchmarks (e.g., 90% in original LIBERO). Even at Level 1 (minor spatial perturbations), the average success rate was only 39.4%, with the best model (π
0.5
) achieving 65.2%. This underscores the challenging nature of LIBERO-X and its ability to differentiate model capabilities.

- **Overall Performance Degradation:** All models showed a clear downward trend in success rates as task complexity increased from Level 1 to Level 5, with an average performance decline of 31.2%. This confirms the progressive difficulty of the benchmark.
- **Finding 1: VLA models struggle with spatial extrapolation beyond training distributions.**
 - Transitioning from Level 1 to Level 2 (larger spatial shifts) resulted in a notable **9.8% drop** in average success rate. This indicates that current VLM-based policies tend to overfit to specific spatial configurations, relying on memorized pixel-level patterns rather than robust spatial understanding, leading to significant degradation when objects are placed in out-of-distribution positions.
- **Finding 2: Structural invariance is lacking when facing topological perturbations.**
 - Level 3 (altering relative object arrangements) witnessed the **most substantial average performance degradation of 12.7%**. This highlights models' inability to adapt to new scene layouts where semantic relationships remain but topological organization is reconstructed, revealing a failure in reasoning about relative spatial relations.
- **Finding 3: Data diversity enables emergent generalization, though semantic grounding for novel objects remains a bottleneck.**
 - While models maintained non-zero success rates on out-of-distribution tasks, suggesting that the diverse LIBERO-X dataset helps mitigate overfitting, a performance gap was observed in Level 4 (Visual Attribute Variation). Success rates on **confounding objects** were notably higher than those on **unseen objects** (Table III). This indicates that while models gain robustness against visual distractions, they still struggle to ground instructions to entirely novel visual concepts, implying imperfect alignment between pre-trained representations and the control policy for unfamiliar features.
- **Finding 4: Language instruction variations can influence model performance.**
 - Level 5 (Semantic-Equivalent Reformulation) showed a modest **3.26% average drop** from Level 4. Analysis of sub-categories (Table IV) revealed that **voice conversion (L5-4) was the least disruptive variation**, achieving the highest success rate among rephrasing methods. This suggests models maintain better alignment under syntactic voice shifts compared to other linguistic transformations like synonym replacement or word compression.

#### 4.2. Multi-label Evaluation Findings (Figure 6, Tables V-IX)

The fine-grained multi-label evaluation provided deeper insights into specific failure modes.

- **Consistent Performance Patterns:** Within each level, models exhibited synchronized variations, meaning dimensions challenging for one model were generally difficult for others, reflecting inherent task difficulty. Across levels, the relative performance hierarchy remained stable, with degradation manifesting as proportional contraction.
- **Finding 5: Task horizon length critically limits performance.**
 - Models performed best on single-step tasks, but performance degraded sharply as the number of subtasks increased, plummeting to near zero for tasks requiring three or more steps (Figure 6, "Subtask Count" category). This non-linear drop-off points to a fundamental deficiency in long-horizon reasoning and temporal consistency, where compounding errors prevent robust multi-step manipulation.

#### 4.3. Time Limit Threshold Differences Evaluation Findings (Figure 7, Tables V-IX)

Analyzing the impact of time limits relative to human completion time revealed:

- **Finding 6: Relaxed time limits buffer execution imperfections, though benefits diminish beyond a saturation point.**
 - Stringent time constraints (0.8x human average) severely penalized performance, likely due to small inference or control errors. Relaxing limits yielded substantial gains, particularly between 1.0x and 1.1x (the chosen default). However, performance plateaued beyond 1.3x, suggesting that remaining failures stem from fundamental capability deficits (e.g., perceptual hallucinations, logic errors) rather than temporal insufficiency. This indicates that extending time cannot compensate for intrinsic limitations of the models.

In summary, the experiments with LIBERO-X unveil persistent limitations in VLA models regarding spatial generalization, structural invariance, semantic grounding for novel objects, and especially long-horizon reasoning. The benchmark successfully reveals performance disparities and critical bottlenecks that were often masked by less rigorous evaluation protocols.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LIBERO-X represents a significant step forward in the evaluation and development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **More Reliable and Rigorous Assessment:** By systematically rethinking VLA benchmarking, LIBERO-X offers a framework that moves beyond superficial success rates. The observed significant performance drops even for state-of-the-art models, even at the lowest difficulty level, indicate that it provides a far more truthful reflection of model capabilities and limitations compared to existing benchmarks that often lead to "overly optimistic" conclusions. This rigorousness is crucial for accurately gauging progress in the field.
2. **Guiding Future VLA Development:** The specific findings derived from LIBERO-X—such as struggles with spatial extrapolation, lack of structural invariance, bottlenecks in semantic grounding for novel objects, and critical limitations in long-horizon reasoning—provide concrete, actionable insights for researchers. These identified weaknesses can now serve as targeted areas for future model design and algorithmic improvements. For instance, the findings emphasize the need for:
 - Architectures that learn truly robust spatial understanding rather than memorizing configurations.
 - Better mechanisms for compositional reasoning about object relationships and scene topology.
 - Improved alignment between visual representations and language instructions, especially for unseen object attributes.
 - More sophisticated planning and error-mitigation strategies for multi-step tasks.
3. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The benchmark's emphasis on capturing "real-world distribution shifts" and evaluating robustness under "compounded distribution shifts" directly addresses the challenges of deploying robots outside controlled laboratory settings. By exposing VLA models to extreme variations in training data and diverse, progressively difficult test conditions, LIBERO-X pushes models toward acquiring more transferable and robust skills, thus helping to bridge the notorious sim-to-real gap. Meituan's involvement, as a company with real-world robotics interests, further underscores this practical orientation.
4. **Diagnostic Capabilities through Fine-grained Evaluation:** The multi-level and multi-label evaluation system is not just about difficulty but also about diagnosis. Researchers can pinpoint specific failure modes related to interaction types, subtask counts, spatial relations, and object attributes. This granular feedback is invaluable for understanding *why* a model fails, enabling more informed iterative development and refinement of VLA architectures.
5. **Promoting Generalizable Skill Acquisition:** The high-diversity training dataset, collected via human teleoperation and designed with multi-task scenes, attribute-conditioned manipulation, and spatial relationship reasoning, fundamentally aims to mitigate overfitting. By encouraging models to learn generalizable manipulation strategies rather than template-like behaviors, LIBERO-X fosters the development of truly intelligent and adaptable robotic agents.
6. **Establishing a New Baseline:** LIBERO-X sets a new, more challenging baseline for evaluating VLA models. Success on this benchmark will signify a higher degree of robustness and generalization, encouraging the community to pursue more ambitious research goals and fostering a competitive environment for innovation.

In conclusion, LIBERO-X serves as a comprehensive "robustness litmus test" that accurately diagnoses the current limitations of VLA models. By providing a more faithful assessment environment and a richer data foundation, it is poised to become a cornerstone benchmark that effectively guides the development of VLA models toward robust, reliable, and ultimately deployable robotic manipulation capabilities in complex, dynamic real-world environments.
