# Atlas: Orchestrating Heterogeneous Models and Tools for Multi-Domain Complex Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> ATLAS introduces a dual-path framework to dynamically orchestrate diverse large language models and external tools, enabling superior complex reasoning across multiple domains. This system achieved an average accuracy of 63.5% on in-distribution tasks, outperforming RouterDC by 10.1%, and demonstrated robust generalization on out-of-distribution tasks with a 13.1% improvement over baselines through learned model-tool synergies.

## Problem

- Existing LLM tool usage relies on rigid, pre-configured invocation logic, limiting adaptability to diverse tasks and model capabilities.
- Current LLM routing methods select models in isolation, overlooking the performance benefits from synergistic integration with external tools.
- Reinforcement Learning applications for LLMs often optimize individual components rather than jointly leveraging the combined strengths of multiple models and tools.

## Method

- Developed ATLAS, a ==dual-path framework== balancing efficiency for domain-specific tasks with generalization for open-domain reasoning.
- Implemented a ==training-free cluster-based routing mechanism== for known domains, which uses query embeddings and empirical statistics to select optimal model-tool pairs.
- Introduced an ==RL-driven multi-step routing approach== for open-domain scenarios, modeling reasoning as a sequential decision task with an augmented action space and optimizing policy using PPO with a composite reward function.

## Results

- Achieved an average accuracy of **63.5%** on in-distribution tasks, surpassing the strongest baseline, RouterDC, by **10.1%**.
- Demonstrated robust generalization on out-of-distribution tasks, with ATLAS(RL) reaching an average accuracy of **59.4%**, which was **13.1%** higher than RouterDC.
- Attained an average accuracy of **68.9%** across five multi-modal datasets, outperforming the strongest single-tool baseline by **4.3%**, by effectively integrating multi-modal tools.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strategic orchestration of heterogeneous LLMs and external tools can enable smaller open-source models to rival or exceed larger proprietary systems in complex reasoning.
> - A dual-path architecture, combining cluster-based routing for efficiency in known domains and RL-driven routing for generalization in open domains, is effective.
> - Reinforcement learning can teach transferable collaborative decision principles for model and tool selection, leading to robust performance on out-of-distribution tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of large language models (LLMs) with external tools has broadened the capabilities of AI agents, enabling them to move beyond static problem-solving to more collaborative reasoning. This evolving LLM-tool ecosystem necessitates identifying optimal model-tool combinations to maximize performance. Existing research has explored various aspects of this domain but often in isolation.

Current tool usage frameworks generally enhance performance through task planning but frequently rely on fixed logic. This prevents dynamic adaptation to varying model capabilities or task requirements. Concurrently, LLM routing methods aim to optimize model selection for balancing performance and computational efficiency. These methods, such as ZOOTER and RouterDC, often treat LLMs as isolated execution units, thereby overlooking the potential performance gains from integrating external tools. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has also been applied to optimize LLM reasoning capabilities, including recent applications to tool usage. However, these RL approaches typically focus on optimizing individual components (either a single model or a single tool) rather than harnessing the combined strengths of both.

The paper identifies three key limitations in the existing landscape:
1. **Failure to leverage model-tool synergies:** LLM routing methods primarily focus on model selection, neglecting the integration of external tools.
2. **Rigid invocation and limited flexibility:** Current tool usage methods rely on fixed, pre-configured invocation logic, which restricts adaptability and scalability in open-domain tasks.
3. **Isolated optimization of RL:** Advanced RL approaches frequently optimize individual components in isolation, missing opportunities for joint model-tool synergy in complex reasoning.

This research aims to address these identified gaps by proposing a framework that dynamically orchestrates heterogeneous LLMs and tools, thereby seeking to unify and optimize their combined functionalities for complex, multi-domain reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the limitations observed in current AI agent architectures, particularly as the diversity and complexity of both LLMs and external tools continue to grow. Selecting the most effective model-tool combination for a given task is framed as a high-dimensional optimization problem, which existing systems, often reliant on a single model or rigid tool-calling logic, are ill-equipped to handle.

The key objectives of the ATLAS (Adaptive Tool-LLM Alignment and Synergistic Invocation) framework are:

1. **To develop a generalizable framework for dynamic orchestration:** The central objective is to explicitly optimize the heterogeneous synergies between diverse LLMs and tools, enabling adaptive and dynamic tool invocation for complex reasoning tasks. This aims to overcome the current limitations of isolated optimization and fixed logic in existing methods.
2. **To bridge the gap between empirical knowledge and open-domain reasoning through a dual-path design:** The framework seeks to manage both domain-specific and open-domain tasks effectively.
 - For domain-specific tasks where empirical priors can be established, the objective is to implement a **training-free cluster-based routing** mechanism. This aims for efficient selection of model-tool pairs by leveraging domain expertise within a semantic embedding space, exploiting historical performance patterns for rapid and accurate routing.
 - For generalized scenarios lacking explicit priors, the objective is to employ an **RL-driven multi-step routing** approach. This path is designed to iteratively explore model-tool combinations and autonomous trajectories to achieve superior execution paths in unfamiliar or out-of-distribution tasks, thereby ensuring generalization and robustness.
3. **To demonstrate superior performance and adaptability across diverse reasoning tasks:** The research aims to show that this integrated, dual-path approach can outperform both top-performing closed-source LLMs (like GPT-4o) and existing routing methods. This includes evaluating performance on multi-domain tasks and verifying robust adaptability in multi-modal scenarios.
4. **To address scalability challenges:** By employing the dual-path design, the framework aims to efficiently resolve scalability issues inherent in high-dimensional search spaces created by increasing numbers of models and tools, while concurrently ensuring robust performance.

In essence, the work is motivated by the need for an AI agent capable of intelligent, dynamic decision-making regarding which LLM and which tool (or combination thereof) to use at each step of a complex reasoning process, moving beyond static configurations to a more adaptive, synergistic, and generalized approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ATLAS employs a dual-path framework designed to handle both domain-specific, low-latency decision-making and complex, multi-round open-domain reasoning. The framework integrates two primary strategies: Training-free Cluster-based Routing and RL-driven Multi-step Routing.

#### 3.1. Training-Free Cluster-Based Routing

This strategy is based on the hypothesis that the optimal model-tool combination is query-dependent and exhibits semantic locality. It aims to approximate an optimal routing function by leveraging historical metadata.

1. **Search Space Definition:** The search space `S` is defined as the Cartesian product of candidate LLMs `M = {m_1..., m_M}` and available tools `T = {t_1..., t_T}`.
2. **Query Representation:** Each training query `q_i` from a set `Q_train` is mapped into a D-dimensional latent manifold using a pre-trained encoder, resulting in a vector `v_i = E(q_i)`.
3. **Semantic Clustering:** The embedding space is partitioned into `K` disjoint clusters `{C_k}`. This is achieved by minimizing inertia using a clustering algorithm (e.g., K-means) to group queries with similar reasoning requirements and tool affinities around semantic centroids `μ_k`.
4. **Empirical Statistics Collection:** For each model-tool pair `(m, t)` within a cluster `C_k`, empirical statistics are derived from training observations.
 - **Accuracy:** The empirical accuracy `Acc_k(m,t)` is calculated as the success rate of the pair `(m,t)` on queries within `C_k`.
 - **Cost:** The operational cost `Cost_k(m,t)` is modeled based on the average input and output token throughput observed during a profiling phase, accounting for unit prices.
5. **Utility Score Calculation:** A cluster-level utility score `U_k(m,t)` is defined to facilitate a flexible trade-off between reasoning performance and inference cost: `U_k(m,t) = (1-α) * Acc_k(m,t) - α * Cost_k(m,t)`, where `α` is a hyperparameter for balancing.
6. **Inference Time Routing:** For a new query `q_j`:
 - It is projected into the latent manifold `v_j = E(q_j)`.
 - The query is assigned to the nearest cluster `k*` based on proximity to centroids (`k* = arg min_k ||v_j - μ_k||`).
 - The optimal model-tool pair `(m*, t*)` is retrieved from `S` by maximizing the utility score within `k*`.
This approach aims for real-time, cost-aware tool invocation with constant-time complexity relative to the number of clusters.

#### 3.2. RL-Driven Multi-Step Routing

This strategy addresses complex tasks requiring multi-round reasoning and iterative model-tool interactions, instantiating the router as an autonomous agent.

1. **Sequential Decision Task:** The process is modeled as a sequential decision task over a maximum horizon `T_max`. For a query `q_j`, the agent maintains an evolving state `s_t = {q_j, C_t}`, where `C_t` is the accumulated context.
2. **Augmented Action Space:** At each step `t`, a policy `π_θ` samples an action `a_t` from an augmented action space `A`, which includes two types of operations:
 - **Internal Reasoning (think):** The agent performs local chain-of-thought processing to decompose queries or synthesize intermediate results.
 - **Dynamic Routing (route):** The agent selects a specific model-tool pair `(m, t)` from the routing pool `S` to gather external observations `o_t`.
 This iterative loop allows adaptive refinement of the search space based on real-time feedback.
3. **Policy Optimization:** The policy `π` is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to maximize a regularized objective. A reference policy `π_ref` is used for stability, and `β` is a KL-regularization coefficient.
4. **Composite Reward Function:** A finely-tuned rule-based composite reward function `r_ϕ = R_fmt + γR_out + ξR_sel` is designed:
 - **Format Reward (`R_fmt`):** Enforces structural integrity by penalizing trajectories that deviate from predefined formats and tool-invocation syntax (e.g., correct XML-style tags, invocation syntax, mandatory reasoning, uniqueness of response, execution consistency).
 - **Outcome Reward (`R_out`):** A binary signal directly aligning the policy with task correctness (1 for correct answer, 0 otherwise).
 - **Model Selection Reward (`R_sel`):** A penalty-based signal that guides the agent toward efficiency by penalizing the selection of sub-optimal models, with optimal models pre-determined for specific task types (e.g., Qwen2.5-Coder for MBPP, GPT-4o-judged for Calculator/NQ).
This framework aims to facilitate autonomous orchestration by teaching the model to assess its internal state before invoking external resources and to internalize alignment between domains and tool utilization.

#### 3.3. Experimental Settings

- **Models:** Six heterogeneous open-source LLMs (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, InternLM3-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, Qwen3-8B-VL) are used.
- **Tools:**
 - **Foundation Tools:** Code Interpreter, Web Search, Calculator, Process Reward Model (PRM).
 - **Multi-modal Tools:** Qwen3-Chart, Qwen3-Counting, Qwen3-Geo, Hunyuan-OCR.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations are conducted on 15 benchmarks across diverse tasks:
 - Mathematical reasoning (AIME2024, AIME2025, AMC)
 - Code generation (HumanEval, MBPP)
 - Arithmetic reasoning (Calculator)
 - Commonsense reasoning (NQ, WebQ)
 - Logical reasoning (LogiQA2)
 - Scientific reasoning (GPQA)
 - Multi-modal reasoning (ChartQA, Geometry3K, TallyQA, CountBench, TableVQA)
- **Baselines:** Zero-shot/Few-shot Router, Random Router, RouterDC, MLPRouter, BertRouter. Closed-source models (Gemini2.5-Pro, GPT-5, GPT-4.1, GPT-4o) are also included for comparison.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Accuracy is the primary metric. Experiments include in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings, where OOD models are trained on a subset of datasets and evaluated on unseen domains.
- **Implementation:** RL policy model is Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, optimized with PPO for 250 steps, with specific learning rates and reward weights.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across 15 benchmarks demonstrate the performance characteristics of the ATLAS framework.

#### 4.1. In-Distribution Performance

- **Superior Accuracy:** ATLAS(cluster) achieved an average accuracy of 63.5%, which surpassed the strongest baseline, RouterDC, by 10.1%.
- **Domain-Specific Gains:** This advantage was particularly evident in mathematical reasoning tasks. For example, ATLAS(cluster) scored 40.0% on AIME25 and 82.5% on AMC, representing increases of 16.7% and 20.0% respectively over RouterDC.
- **Comparison to Closed-Source Models:** ATLAS(cluster) exceeded the performance of GPT-4o (53.1%) and approached that of GPT-4.1 (63.0%), indicating that the strategic orchestration of smaller-scale models and tools can rival larger proprietary systems in specific contexts.
- **Mechanism:** This performance is attributed to the framework's ability to leverage empirical priors through semantic embedding, mapping queries into structured clusters, and utilizing cached historical performance patterns for near-optimal task-configuration alignment. In contrast, supervised routers experienced limitations with non-linear decision boundaries and failed to capture nuanced domain-specific synergies.

#### 4.2. Generalization Scenarios (Out-of-Distribution - OOD)

- **Degradation of Cluster-based Approach:** In OOD settings, ATLAS(cluster) exhibited significant performance degradation (e.g., AIME25 accuracy dropped from 40.0% to 3.3%), similar to other baselines.
- **Robustness of RL-based Approach:** ATLAS(RL) maintained an average accuracy of 59.4%, which was 10.2% higher than ATLAS(cluster) (49.2%) and 13.1% higher than RouterDC (46.3%) in OOD scenarios.
- **Substantial Gains in Math Reasoning:** The performance gap was most pronounced in mathematical reasoning, where ATLAS(RL) achieved 43.3% on AIME24 and 33.3% on AIME25, significantly outperforming the clustering method (13.3% and 3.3% respectively).
- **Mechanism:** ATLAS(RL) is reported to autonomously explore effective trajectories via multi-faceted reward signals, learning generalizable patterns of model-tool synergies. This enables robust transfer to unfamiliar tasks, maintaining competitive performance on benchmarks like AIME24 (43.3%) and GPQA (42.0%), and in some cases exceeding GPT-4o performance despite using smaller base models. The RL path learns transferable decision principles rather than task-specific mappings.

#### 4.3. Multi-modal Tool Orchestration

- **Overall Performance:** On multi-modal tasks, ATLAS achieved an average accuracy of 68.9%, surpassing the strongest single-tool baseline by 4.3%.
- **Task-Specific Outperformance:** ATLAS consistently outperformed all individual tools across various task categories. For instance, it exceeded Qwen3-Chart (83.0%) on ChartQA and overcame the limitations of single tools on tasks like Geometry3K (where Qwen3-Chart achieved only 50.2%).
- **Mechanism:** This demonstrates that adaptive model-tool routing effectively integrates internal reasoning with external tool augmentation, providing effectiveness in complex multi-modal contexts.

#### 4.4. Generalization Toward Dynamic Model-Tool Synergy (Pool Extensions)

- **Adaptability:** When the routing pool was expanded with new domain-specialized models (Llama-3.1-8B-UltraMedical, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct) and an Outcome Reward Model, without retraining the policy, ATLAS(RL) improved from 59.4% to 61.7% (+2.3%).
- **Specific Gains:** Gains were most significant on mathematical benchmarks (e.g., AIME24 and AIME25 both increased by +6.7%).
- **Baseline Limitations:** In contrast, baseline methods exhibited limited or inconsistent responses, with classifier-based routers struggling due to fixed decision boundaries becoming misaligned with new candidates.
- **Mechanism:** ATLAS(RL)'s ability to learn transferable routing principles through RL exploration facilitated seamless integration of new components without requiring retraining.

#### 4.5. Discussion Points

- **Reasoning Capacity Boundary:** RL training led to a +23.0% absolute improvement in pass@1 accuracy, demonstrating optimized exploration efficiency. At pass@16, performance reached 63.1% (+3.7%), indicating that ATLAS(RL) operates near its reasoning capacity ceiling.
- **LLM API Call Count:** ATLAS exhibited task-adaptive invocation patterns, with more API calls for complex reasoning tasks (e.g., AIME25, GPQA) and minimal calls for straightforward retrieval tasks (e.g., WebQ, NQ), balancing performance and cost.
- **RL Training Convergence:** Incorporating the model selection reward (`R_sel`) resulted in faster convergence to a higher reward plateau and a sharper reduction in entropy, indicating a successful transition from stochastic exploration to deterministic decision-making.
- **Statistical Significance:** Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirmed the statistical significance of ATLAS's superiority over the strongest baseline (RouterDC) in both in-distribution (p = 9.7 × 10^-4) and out-of-distribution settings (p < 0.05).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ATLAS framework introduces a generalizable approach for dynamically orchestrating heterogeneous large language models and external tools, addressing critical limitations in current AI agent designs. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By synergistically combining diverse LLMs and tools, ATLAS enables superior reasoning performance across complex, multi-domain tasks. The results demonstrate that smaller-scale models, when strategically coordinated, can rival or exceed the capabilities of larger, proprietary closed-source models like GPT-4o, suggesting a shift from model-centric scaling to ecosystem-centric orchestration.
2. **Improved Generalization:** The dual-path architecture, particularly the RL-driven multi-step routing, provides robust generalization capabilities. This allows the system to adapt effectively to out-of-distribution tasks and dynamically evolving model-tool pools without requiring extensive retraining, a crucial feature for real-world deployments where new models and tools are constantly introduced.
3. **Adaptive Resource Utilization:** The framework exhibits task-adaptive invocation patterns, intelligently allocating computational resources (e.g., LLM API calls) based on task complexity. This balance between reasoning performance and inference cost contributes to efficiency, preventing redundant invocations for simpler tasks.
4. **Foundation for Next-Generation Autonomous Agents:** ATLAS represents a step towards building more sophisticated autonomous agents. Its ability to perform adaptive model-tool alignment and multi-step reasoning is fundamental for agents designed to address complex real-world challenges that require interleaving internal deliberation with external resource utilization.
5. **Multimodal Reasoning Advancements:** The framework's successful application to multimodal visual reasoning tasks demonstrates its potential to integrate diverse perceptual abilities with complex reasoning, opening avenues for more capable multimodal AI systems.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- The current evaluation primarily focuses on text-based and visual reasoning, leaving other modalities (audio, video) unexplored.
- The framework assumes reliable API access, and real-world issues like network latency or service unavailability could impact performance. Future work plans to investigate lightweight policy architectures and robust fallback mechanisms.

Future research directions include integrating noise-aware retrieval strategies, incorporating structured template guidance for RL policies, evaluating performance in long-horizon interactive settings, and exploring speculative parallelism techniques to reduce inference latency. Extending the framework to richer perceptual abilities through unified visual agents is also highlighted as an important direction for truly general-purpose orchestration systems.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The authors state that all datasets, models, and tools used are publicly available and properly cited, without involving private or sensitive information. The framework itself does not modify underlying models or tools, meaning it inherits their existing biases, safety limitations, and ethical concerns. ATLAS does not introduce new harmful capabilities beyond those already present in its constituent components. Practitioners are advised to evaluate all candidate models and tools for ethical compliance and apply appropriate safety measures.
