# SimpleTIR: End-to-End Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces SimpleTIR, an end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach that stabilizes multi-turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) in Large Language Models (LLMs) under the Zero RL setting. SimpleTIR resolves training instability and gradient explosions by filtering problematic 'void turns,' achieving state-of-the-art performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and fostering diverse, emergent reasoning patterns.

## Problem

- Multi-turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) suffers from training instability and performance collapse.
- External tool feedback causes distributional drift, leading to the generation of low-probability tokens and catastrophic gradient explosions during RL training.
- Existing "Zero RL" approaches for TIR struggle with this instability, often relying on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) which can constrain emergent reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- The interaction process is formally modeled as a ==Hierarchical Markov Decision Process (H-MDP)==, with a single policy trained using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- Introduces =="void turn" filtering==, where entire trajectories are discarded if any turn contains neither executable code nor a final answer, preventing large gradients.
- Applies ==feedback masking==, calculating policy loss only for the LLM's generated tokens, to ensure correct credit assignment and avoid penalizing environmental observations.

## Results

- Achieved remarkable training stability by completely resolving gradient explosions and performance collapse, a common issue in multi-turn RL for LLMs.
- Set new state-of-the-art performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AIME24 score improved from **22.1** to **50.5** with Qwen2.5-7B), outperforming all Zero RL baselines and some SFT-based methods.
- Demonstrated the emergence of diverse and sophisticated reasoning patterns (e.g., higher frequencies of progressive reasoning and error correction) compared to SFT-based models, validating the benefits of the Zero RL approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-turn TIR instability stems from distributional drift caused by out-of-distribution (OOD) external tool feedback, leading to low-probability token generation and catastrophic gradient explosions in policy gradient methods.
> - A simple filtering mechanism for "void turns" effectively prevents high-magnitude gradients and corrects misaligned credit assignment, leading to stable RL training for TIR.
> - Stable Zero RL training, by avoiding SFT, encourages the emergence of diverse and sophisticated reasoning patterns such as cross-validation, progressive reasoning, and error correction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR). The broader research landscape is characterized by an accelerating effort to enhance LLMs' capabilities beyond mere text generation, particularly in complex problem-solving domains like mathematical reasoning.

**The Rise of Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR):** LLMs, despite their impressive linguistic abilities, inherently struggle with tasks requiring precise computation, up-to-date factual knowledge, or complex logical deduction. TIR addresses these limitations by enabling LLMs to interact with external tools (e.g., Python interpreters, search engines). This paradigm has shown significant promise in improving LLMs' reasoning accuracy and breadth, moving them towards more "agentic" behaviors.

**Challenges in Multi-Turn TIR with RL:** While the potential of TIR is clear, extending it to *multi-turn* scenarios – where an LLM iteratively reasons, generates code, executes it, and uses the output for subsequent steps – presents significant challenges. The most prominent among these is **training instability and performance collapse** when using Reinforcement Learning. Prior work (Wang et al., 2025; Mai et al., 2025; Baronio et al., 2025; Moonshot AI, 2025) has consistently highlighted these issues, often attributed to the complex feedback loops and long horizons of multi-turn interactions.

**The "Zero RL" Paradigm:** A crucial aspect of SimpleTIR's context is its adherence to the "Zero RL" paradigm. This approach, pioneered by works like DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI Team, 2025), aims to train LLMs using RL directly from *unaligned base models* (i.e., models not pre-trained with human preference data or instruction fine-tuning). This contrasts with methods that "cold start" with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on carefully curated datasets (e.g., ReTool, Feng et al., 2025). While SFT can provide initial stability, it often constrains the model's ability to discover novel and diverse reasoning strategies – a core benefit of pure RL training. SimpleTIR explicitly seeks to overcome the instability of Zero RL in the multi-turn TIR setting, thus unlocking its full potential for emergent reasoning.

**Addressing the Core Instability:** SimpleTIR identifies a specific, fundamental cause for this instability: **distributional drift from external tool feedback**. When tool outputs, which can be out-of-distribution (OOD) relative to the LLM's pre-training data, are fed back into the model, they cause the model to generate increasingly low-probability tokens. This issue compounds over turns, leading to catastrophic gradient explosions during RL training. This analysis directly tackles a known, persistent problem in the field.

**Relationship to Other RL for LLM Stability Efforts:** The paper situates SimpleTIR against other methods for stabilizing RL training, such as entropy-based methods (Cui et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025a), importance sampling ratio control (Yao et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025b), and data/trajectory filtering (Shrivastava et al., 2025). SimpleTIR's contribution is distinguished by its focus on the *root cause specific to TIR* – the distribution shift induced by tool outputs – and its *orthogonality* to many of these general RL stabilization techniques. It complements existing Zero RL for TIR methods like ZeroTIR (Mai et al., 2025) by offering a unique and effective solution to a critical problem.

In summary, SimpleTIR provides a vital piece to the puzzle of building truly autonomous and capable LLM agents. By stabilizing multi-turn TIR training under the Zero RL paradigm, it paves the way for LLMs that can learn to reason robustly and creatively, directly from interaction with their environment and tools, without relying on human-curated demonstrations that may limit their emergent abilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **stabilize and enhance end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) for multi-turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) in Large Language Models (LLMs) under the Zero RL setting.**

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical challenges and desired outcomes:

1. **Overcoming Training Instability in Multi-Turn TIR:** The authors identify that multi-turn TIR, while highly promising, is plagued by training instability and performance collapse when using RL. This instability primarily manifests as catastrophic gradient norm explosions, derailing the learning process. The key motivation is to pinpoint and address the root cause of this instability to enable successful multi-turn TIR training.

2. **Addressing Distributional Drift from Tool Feedback:** The core diagnostic insight of the paper is that external tool feedback, being out-of-distribution (OOD) relative to the LLM's pre-trained data, causes a "distributional drift." This drift leads the LLM to generate low-probability tokens, which then compound over successive turns. A key motivation is to directly mitigate the detrimental effects of this OOD feedback and the resulting low-probability token generation.

3. **Enabling True "Zero RL" for TIR:** Many existing approaches to multi-turn TIR rely on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as a "cold start" to achieve stability. While practical, SFT can inadvertently constrain the model's exploratory behavior and its ability to discover novel, emergent reasoning patterns. A central motivation for SimpleTIR is to achieve stable multi-turn TIR training *without* such an SFT phase, fully embracing the "Zero RL" paradigm. This allows the model to learn and develop its reasoning strategies organically.

4. **Fostering Diverse and Sophisticated Reasoning Patterns:** By avoiding the constraints of SFT, the authors are motivated to enable LLMs to discover a wider array of problem-solving strategies through RL. This includes complex behaviors like self-correction, cross-validation, and progressive reasoning, which are crucial for advanced agentic capabilities. The objective is not just to perform well, but to learn *how* to reason effectively and adaptively in multi-turn interactive environments.

5. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance on Challenging Tasks:** Ultimately, the research aims to demonstrate that a stabilized Zero RL approach to multi-turn TIR can not only be stable but also achieve competitive, if not superior, performance on challenging reasoning benchmarks, particularly in mathematics.

In essence, SimpleTIR is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of end-to-end Reinforcement Learning for sophisticated, adaptive, and truly emergent multi-turn tool-integrated reasoning in LLMs, by systematically addressing its most significant training bottleneck.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimpleTIR employs a multi-faceted methodology that begins with a clear problem formulation, delves into a rigorous diagnosis of the core issue, and culminates in a novel, yet straightforward, solution.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical MDP Formulation for Multi-Turn TIR

The interaction process is formally modeled as a Hierarchical Markov Decision Process (H-MDP), separating decision-making into two levels:
- **High-level MDP:** Governs the sequence of conversational turns, where a state is the complete conversation history and an action is the generation of an entire response for a turn. Rewards are sparse and terminal, based on overall trajectory success.
- **Low-level MDP:** Operates at the token level within each turn, where states are partial token sequences and actions are individual token selections. This level receives no intrinsic reward, serving to execute the high-level action.
A single, unified policy $\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)$ is trained to implicitly solve this two-level problem, with a discount factor $\gamma=1$ for both levels.

#### 3.2. Joint Policy Optimization and Feedback Masking

- **Optimization Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is chosen, which is a value-function-free RL algorithm. GRPO computes advantage based on the relative performance of trajectories within a group sampled from the same prompt, eliminating the need for a learned value function.
- **TIR-Specific Adaptation (Feedback Masking):** To ensure correct credit assignment in the TIR setting, feedback token masking is applied. The policy loss is calculated only for the LLM's generated response tokens ($l_k$), explicitly excluding the external tool feedback tokens ($f_k$) from gradient computation. This prevents the model from being rewarded or penalized for environmental observations it did not generate.

#### 3.3. Diagnosis: The Emergence of Low-Probability Tokens and Gradient Explosion

The core of SimpleTIR's methodology lies in its diagnosis of the multi-turn instability:
- **Empirical Observation:** Comparison between single-turn and multi-turn TIR reveals that multi-turn training is highly unstable, characterized by performance collapse and recurring gradient spikes (Figure 2).
- **Root Cause Identification:** The key difference is the feedback loop. External tool feedback ($f_k$) is out-of-distribution (OOD) with respect to the LLM's learned data. When this OOD input conditions subsequent generations, the model drifts from its pre-trained distribution, leading to highly stochastic outputs and unnaturally low probabilities assigned to sampled tokens (Figure 3). This effect compounds over multiple turns.
- **Theoretical Analysis (Proposition 3.1):** The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the policy gradient's L2 norm with respect to pre-softmax logits. This analysis formally demonstrates that the gradient norm is highly sensitive to:
 - **Unclipped Importance Ratio ($\rho_{i,t}(\theta)$):** For negatively rewarded trajectories, if a token was generated with very low probability by the old policy, even a small update to the new policy's probability can cause the importance ratio to explode, leading to gradient spikes.
 - **Probability-Dependent Term:** A term involving the token probabilities ($\sqrt{1 - 2P(c) + \sum_j P(j)^2}$) sustains large gradients when low-probability tokens are sampled, further contributing to instability.
- **Misaligned Credit Assignment:** Beyond gradient instability, low-probability tokens (especially prevalent in later, failing turns) create a severe credit assignment problem. A single negative terminal reward penalizes the entire trajectory, unfairly punishing potentially good reasoning in early turns if a later turn collapses due to OOD feedback. This incentivizes simpler, single-turn behaviors.

#### 3.4. SimpleTIR: Stabilizing Training by Filtering Void Turns

Based on the diagnosis, SimpleTIR proposes a simple yet effective trajectory filtering algorithm:
- **Definition of a "Void Turn":** An LLM response that contains *neither* a complete, executable code block *nor* a final answer. These are identified as symptoms of severe distributional drift and high generation stochasticity (e.g., premature end-of-sequence tokens, repetitive text).
- **Filtering Mechanism:** If any turn within a sampled trajectory is identified as a "void turn," the *entire trajectory* is filtered out. It is removed from the current batch, and its policy loss is masked, preventing it from contributing to the GRPO update.
- **Benefits:** This filtering strategy simultaneously:
 - **Blocks High-Magnitude Gradients:** By removing problematic trajectories associated with low-probability tokens and OOD behavior, it prevents their large gradients from propagating through the network.
 - **Corrects Misaligned Credit Assignment:** Ensures that successful early turns are not unfairly penalized by a later collapse in a void turn, as the entire problematic trajectory is simply discarded for policy updates.
- **Plug-and-Play Nature:** The method is designed to be general and easily integrated into existing RL training frameworks without significant modifications.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details for Enhanced Stability and Efficiency

To further support the core filtering mechanism, SimpleTIR incorporates practical implementation choices:
- **No Chat Templates for Base Models:** Uses a simple prefix "Code Execution Result:" to avoid OOD special tokens with unaligned base models.
- **`final_answer` Shortcut:** Prepends a `final_answer` function to every LLM-generated code block, allowing quick task termination for simpler problems.
- **Strict Tool Interaction Control:** LLM generation is strictly stopped after a complete code block, and the *true*, external tool feedback is always appended before the next turn, preventing hallucination.

This comprehensive methodology, from problem modeling and root cause analysis to a practical, targeted solution, forms the backbone of SimpleTIR's effectiveness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SimpleTIR presents compelling findings that underscore its effectiveness in stabilizing multi-turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) and enhancing reasoning capabilities, particularly within the Zero RL paradigm.

1. **Resolved Training Instability and Gradient Explosion:**
 - SimpleTIR demonstrates remarkably stable training dynamics, as evidenced by its consistently well-behaved gradient norms (Figure 1). In stark contrast, naive multi-turn training (using DAPO baseline) suffers from severe instability and catastrophic gradient explosions, leading to performance collapse.
 - Ablation studies confirm that void turn filtering is the critical component for this stability. Alternative filtering methods based on high importance ratios or low token probabilities failed to resolve gradient explosions, highlighting the specificity and effectiveness of SimpleTIR's heuristic (Figure 5, bottom; Table 2).

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Mathematical Reasoning:**
 - SimpleTIR achieves significant performance improvements across a suite of challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including AIME24, AIME25, MATH500, Olympiad, AMC23, and Hmmt Feb 25 (Table 1).
 - Starting from the Qwen2.5-7B base model, SimpleTIR elevates the AIME24 score from a text-only baseline of 22.1 to an impressive 50.5.
 - It consistently outperforms all Zero RL baselines, both those without TIR (e.g., SimpleRL-Zoo, DAPO) and those with TIR (e.g., ZeroTIR). This clearly demonstrates the advantage of robustly integrated tools.
 - SimpleTIR even surpasses some methods that rely on "cold start" supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or specialized math-instruct models (e.g., ToRL and Effective TIR on the Qwen2.5-7B series), showcasing the power of effective Zero RL.
 - The performance scales effectively with model size, with SimpleTIR-32B achieving 59.9 on AIME24 and 92.9 on Math500.

3. **Emergence of Diverse and Sophisticated Reasoning Patterns:**
 - A key advantage of SimpleTIR's Zero RL approach is its ability to encourage the model to discover novel and diverse reasoning patterns, rather than being constrained by pre-defined patterns in SFT datasets.
 - The paper qualitatively and quantitatively identifies three main emergent reasoning patterns: **Cross-Validation**, **Progressive Reasoning**, and **Error Correction Loop** (Figure 6).
 - Quantitative analysis (using Claude-3.7-Sonnet to identify patterns) shows that SimpleTIR-32B exhibits higher frequencies of progressive reasoning (46.5% vs. 18.9% for ReTool) and error correction (38.0% vs. 25.8% for ReTool), while both models demonstrate high cross-verification tendencies (Table 3). This confirms that Zero RL indeed preserves more diversity in reasoning patterns.

4. **Benefits of Scaling Interaction Turns:**
 - Training curves show that SimpleTIR benefits from scaling the maximum number of interaction turns, with average response length and Math500 scores increasing from 1 to 5 to 10 turns (Figure 5, top). This indicates that for some tasks, more iterative reasoning and tool use are beneficial.

In summary, SimpleTIR provides strong empirical evidence that the identified problem of distributional drift and gradient explosion in multi-turn TIR can be effectively mitigated, leading to stable training and state-of-the-art reasoning performance, while simultaneously promoting the emergence of advanced, flexible problem-solving strategies in LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SimpleTIR makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the development of intelligent Large Language Models (LLMs) and autonomous agents.

1. **Enabling Robust Zero RL for Multi-Turn TIR:** This is perhaps the most critical impact. Before SimpleTIR, training multi-turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) directly from base models ("Zero RL") was notoriously unstable. By identifying and effectively mitigating the root cause of this instability (distributional drift and gradient explosions from external tool feedback), SimpleTIR makes robust Zero RL for multi-turn TIR a reality. This unlocks the full potential of RL to imbue LLMs with complex, adaptive reasoning capabilities without the need for extensive and potentially constraining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).

2. **Advancing LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** SimpleTIR's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks, particularly by elevating scores like AIME24 from baseline, demonstrates a substantial step forward in LLM problem-solving. It confirms that integrating tools in a multi-turn, iteratively refined manner is highly effective, and that RL can be a powerful driver for these capabilities when stabilized.

3. **Fostering Emergent and Diverse Reasoning Strategies:** A significant impact lies in SimpleTIR's promotion of emergent reasoning patterns. Unlike SFT-based methods that might reinforce predefined, human-designed strategies, SimpleTIR's Zero RL approach encourages the model to discover sophisticated, agentic behaviors such as self-correction, cross-validation, and progressive reasoning. This leads to more flexible, robust, and potentially novel problem-solving approaches, which is vital for building truly intelligent and adaptable AI agents.

4. **Practical and Generalizable Solution:** The "void turn" filtering mechanism is remarkably simple, intuitive, and "plug-and-play." Its low computational cost and ease of integration into existing RL frameworks make it a highly practical solution for researchers and developers working on LLM agents. This simplicity and generality mean its impact could extend beyond mathematical reasoning to other domains requiring iterative tool interaction.

5. **Theoretical and Empirical Insight into RL for LLMs:** The paper provides valuable theoretical analysis (Proposition 3.1) of why low-probability tokens lead to gradient explosions in policy gradient methods, offering a deeper understanding of a pervasive issue in RL for LLMs. This theoretical grounding, combined with strong empirical validation, strengthens the scientific basis for future RL-driven LLM research.

6. **Path to More Autonomous Agents:** The ability to train LLMs end-to-end for multi-turn, tool-integrated reasoning, directly from base models, is a crucial step towards developing more autonomous and capable LLM agents. These agents can learn to interact with complex environments, utilize diverse tools, and refine their strategies over extended interactions, paving the way for advanced applications in various fields, from scientific discovery to complex decision-making.

In conclusion, SimpleTIR addresses a fundamental bottleneck in the RL training of multi-turn tool-integrated LLMs. By providing a stable and effective solution, it not only pushes the boundaries of LLM performance on challenging reasoning tasks but also fosters the development of more diverse, emergent, and ultimately more intelligent agentic behaviors, marking a significant contribution to the field of AI.
