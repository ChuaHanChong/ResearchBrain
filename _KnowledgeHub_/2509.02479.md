# SimpleTIR: End-to-End Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces SimpleTIR, an end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach that stabilizes multi-turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) in Large Language Models (LLMs) under the Zero RL setting. SimpleTIR resolves training instability and gradient explosions by filtering problematic 'void turns,' achieving state-of-the-art performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and fostering diverse, emergent reasoning patterns.

## Problem

- Multi-turn Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) suffers from training instability and performance collapse.
- External tool feedback causes distributional drift, leading to the generation of low-probability tokens and catastrophic gradient explosions during RL training.
- Existing "Zero RL" approaches for TIR struggle with this instability, often relying on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) which can constrain emergent reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- The interaction process is formally modeled as a ==Hierarchical Markov Decision Process (H-MDP)==, with a single policy trained using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- Introduces =="void turn" filtering==, where entire trajectories are discarded if any turn contains neither executable code nor a final answer, preventing large gradients.
- Applies ==feedback masking==, calculating policy loss only for the LLM's generated tokens, to ensure correct credit assignment and avoid penalizing environmental observations.

## Results

- Achieved remarkable training stability by completely resolving gradient explosions and performance collapse, a common issue in multi-turn RL for LLMs.
- Set new state-of-the-art performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AIME24 score improved from **22.1** to **50.5** with Qwen2.5-7B), outperforming all Zero RL baselines and some SFT-based methods.
- Demonstrated the emergence of diverse and sophisticated reasoning patterns (e.g., higher frequencies of progressive reasoning and error correction) compared to SFT-based models, validating the benefits of the Zero RL approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-turn TIR instability stems from distributional drift caused by out-of-distribution (OOD) external tool feedback, leading to low-probability token generation and catastrophic gradient explosions in policy gradient methods.
> - A simple filtering mechanism for "void turns" effectively prevents high-magnitude gradients and corrects misaligned credit assignment, leading to stable RL training for TIR.
> - Stable Zero RL training, by avoiding SFT, encourages the emergence of diverse and sophisticated reasoning patterns such as cross-validation, progressive reasoning, and error correction.
