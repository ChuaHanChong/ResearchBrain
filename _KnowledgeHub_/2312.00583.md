# DeformGS: Scene Flow in Highly Deformable Scenes for Deformable Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DeformGS, a system from Carnegie Mellon University, Stanford University, National University of Singapore, Technical University of Munich, and NVIDIA, recovers dense 3D scene flow for highly deformable objects by extending 3D Gaussian Splatting with a continuous deformation function and physics-inspired regularization. It achieves a mean Median Trajectory Error (MTE) of 27.754 mm on synthetic datasets, representing a 76.0% improvement over DynaGS and 55.8% over 4D-GS, while also demonstrating robust qualitative performance in real-world deformable manipulation scenarios.

## Problem

- Manipulating deformable objects (e.g., cloth, ropes) is challenging for robots due to their high-dimensional state spaces, complex dynamics, and frequent self-occlusions.
- Robust dense 3D tracking (3D scene flow) is a critical perception primitive for deformable object manipulation, similar to 6D pose estimation for rigid objects.
- Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods struggle to maintain accurate tracking in highly deformable scenes with large deformations, strong shadows, and occlusions, often converging to local optima.

## Method

- **DeformGS** extends ==3D Gaussian Splatting== by learning a continuous deformation function that maps canonical Gaussians to world space over time, utilizing a neural-voxel encoding for complex deformations.
- It introduces physics-inspired regularization terms, including local isometry and conservation of momentum, to ensure physically plausible and smooth 3D trajectories.
- A per-Gaussian masking mechanism is incorporated to selectively apply regularization to dynamic objects and manage computational complexity, guided by noisy 2D masks.

## Results

- Achieved a mean Median Trajectory Error (MTE) of **27.754** mm across six synthetic scenes, significantly outperforming DynaGS (**115.859** mm, 76.0% lower MTE) and 4D-GS (**62.778** mm, 55.8% lower MTE).
- Demonstrated higher mean position accuracy (**0.688**) and a perfect survival rate (**1.0**) on synthetic data compared to baseline methods.
- Qualitatively inferred smooth and coherent 3D trajectories and point clouds for real-world human manipulation of deformable objects (e.g., duvet, cloth), showcasing potential for digital twin creation and robust task-relevant keypoint tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a continuous deformation function with a neural voxel encoding provides sufficient capacity to capture complex and large-scale deformations across space and time.
> - Physics-inspired regularization (local isometry and momentum conservation) is essential for constraining the solution space and ensuring smooth, accurate 3D trajectories, particularly in challenging scenarios like texture-less regions or self-occlusions.
> - Learning per-Gaussian masks enables efficient and targeted application of regularization to only dynamic elements, preventing unintended background deformation and improving overall system robustness.
