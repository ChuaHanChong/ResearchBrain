# DeformGS: Scene Flow in Highly Deformable Scenes for Deformable Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DeformGS, a system from Carnegie Mellon University, Stanford University, National University of Singapore, Technical University of Munich, and NVIDIA, recovers dense 3D scene flow for highly deformable objects by extending 3D Gaussian Splatting with a continuous deformation function and physics-inspired regularization. It achieves a mean Median Trajectory Error (MTE) of 27.754 mm on synthetic datasets, representing a 76.0% improvement over DynaGS and 55.8% over 4D-GS, while also demonstrating robust qualitative performance in real-world deformable manipulation scenarios.

## Problem

- Manipulating deformable objects (e.g., cloth, ropes) is challenging for robots due to their high-dimensional state spaces, complex dynamics, and frequent self-occlusions.
- Robust dense 3D tracking (3D scene flow) is a critical perception primitive for deformable object manipulation, similar to 6D pose estimation for rigid objects.
- Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods struggle to maintain accurate tracking in highly deformable scenes with large deformations, strong shadows, and occlusions, often converging to local optima.

## Method

- **DeformGS** extends ==3D Gaussian Splatting== by learning a continuous deformation function that maps canonical Gaussians to world space over time, utilizing a neural-voxel encoding for complex deformations.
- It introduces physics-inspired regularization terms, including local isometry and conservation of momentum, to ensure physically plausible and smooth 3D trajectories.
- A per-Gaussian masking mechanism is incorporated to selectively apply regularization to dynamic objects and manage computational complexity, guided by noisy 2D masks.

## Results

- Achieved a mean Median Trajectory Error (MTE) of **27.754** mm across six synthetic scenes, significantly outperforming DynaGS (**115.859** mm, 76.0% lower MTE) and 4D-GS (**62.778** mm, 55.8% lower MTE).
- Demonstrated higher mean position accuracy (**0.688**) and a perfect survival rate (**1.0**) on synthetic data compared to baseline methods.
- Qualitatively inferred smooth and coherent 3D trajectories and point clouds for real-world human manipulation of deformable objects (e.g., duvet, cloth), showcasing potential for digital twin creation and robust task-relevant keypoint tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a continuous deformation function with a neural voxel encoding provides sufficient capacity to capture complex and large-scale deformations across space and time.
> - Physics-inspired regularization (local isometry and momentum conservation) is essential for constraining the solution space and ensuring smooth, accurate 3D trajectories, particularly in challenging scenarios like texture-less regions or self-occlusions.
> - Learning per-Gaussian masks enables efficient and targeted application of regularization to only dynamic elements, preventing unintended background deformation and improving overall system robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects, such as cloth, ropes, or soft materials, represents a significant challenge in robotics and computer vision. While substantial progress has been made in manipulating rigid objects—often relying on advancements in 6D object pose estimation and tracking—deformable objects present unique difficulties. These challenges include their high-dimensional or infinite-dimensional state spaces, complex non-linear dynamics, and frequent self-occlusions, as the objects change shape and obscure parts of themselves.

Dense 3D tracking, also known as 3D scene flow, is identified as a crucial intermediate representation for robust deformable object manipulation. Analogous to how 6D pose estimation aids rigid manipulation, 3D scene flow can provide robots with a comprehensive understanding of how a deformable object's surface points move over time. This information can be leveraged for various robot learning paradigms, including imitation learning, learning transition models, identifying and tracking task-relevant key points, and facilitating real-to-simulation (real2sim) transfer for creating digital twins.

Prior work in point tracking has seen advancements, particularly in 2D tracking across video frames using methods trained on large synthetic datasets or focused on optical flow. However, effectively lifting these 2D tracking capabilities to robust 3D tracking for highly deformable scenes, especially under conditions like self-occlusions, shadows, and varying textures, remains an open problem.

This research builds upon the recent development of 3D Gaussian Splatting, a novel-view synthesis technique that offers efficient and high-quality rendering by representing scenes as a collection of 3D Gaussians. Extensions of this technique, such as Dynamic 3D Gaussians and 4D Gaussian Splatting, have adapted it for dynamic scenes by either explicitly optimizing Gaussian properties over time or learning a deformation field. DeformGS positions itself within this lineage, aiming to improve upon these dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods. The paper addresses the limitations of existing dynamic Gaussian Splatting approaches, which may exhibit degraded performance with large deformations, strong shadows, and occlusions, potentially converging to local optima due to their explicit optimization or simpler deformation models. By introducing a continuous deformation function with physics-inspired regularization and a mechanism for learning per-Gaussian masks, DeformGS seeks to achieve more accurate and robust 3D scene flow for highly deformable objects, thereby bridging a gap in perception for advanced robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an approach, DeformGS, capable of recovering dense 3D scene flow (i.e., the trajectory of any point) in highly deformable scenes. This is achieved by simultaneously capturing video from multiple calibrated cameras. A secondary objective is to perform high-quality novel-view synthesis as a means to achieve photometric consistency, which serves as a supervision signal for learning the deformation.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges and opportunities in robotics:

- **Enabling Deformable Object Manipulation:** Current robotic systems excel at manipulating rigid objects, but extending these capabilities to deformable objects (e.g., folding laundry, draping fabrics, repositioning soft materials) is critical for a wide range of automation applications. The inherent complexities of deformable objects, such as their infinite-dimensional state spaces, complex dynamics, and frequent self-occlusions, make their manipulation challenging for robots.
- **Providing a Robust 3D Representation:** Just as 6D object pose estimation is foundational for rigid manipulation, dense 3D tracking (scene flow) is posited as a crucial perception primitive for deformable object manipulation. This 3D representation can serve as an input to imitation learning policies, aid in learning transition models, facilitate the identification and tracking of task-relevant key points, and enable the creation of digital twins through real2sim transfer, thereby improving the robustness and sample efficiency of robot learning.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Dynamic Scene Reconstruction:** While recent advancements in neural rendering, particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting and its dynamic extensions (e.g., Dynamic 3D Gaussians, 4D Gaussian Splatting), have shown promise for novel-view synthesis in dynamic scenes, they encounter difficulties in highly challenging scenarios. These include large deformations, strong shadows, and significant occlusions, where explicitly optimizing Gaussian properties over time or using simpler deformation fields can lead to sub-optimal tracking performance and convergence to local optima. DeformGS aims to overcome these limitations by introducing a more robust deformation function and physics-inspired regularization.
- **Leveraging Multi-Camera Data:** The use of time-synchronized multi-camera captures provides rich visual information, which is particularly beneficial for inferring 3D motion and mitigating ambiguities caused by occlusions that are common in deformable object manipulation.

By achieving accurate 3D tracking and novel-view synthesis in complex deformable scenes, DeformGS aims to provide a fundamental perception capability that can unlock advanced robotic manipulation of previously intractable object categories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeformGS is designed to recover scene flow and perform novel-view synthesis in highly deformable scenes using time-synchronized image sequences from a calibrated multi-camera setup. It builds upon 3D Gaussian Splatting by introducing a continuous deformation function and physics-inspired regularization.

#### 3.1. Gaussian Splatting Foundation

The method utilizes 3D Gaussian Splatting, which represents a scene as a collection of 3D Gaussians. Each Gaussian is defined by its mean position ($\mu$), covariance matrix ($\Sigma$), color ($c$), and opacity ($\sigma$). The covariance matrix is decomposed into a rotation ($R$) and scale ($S$) for optimization ($\Sigma = RSS^T R^T$). A fast, differentiable rasterizer is employed to project these 3D Gaussians into image space, where their projected 2D covariances ($\Sigma'$) and colors are blended to render pixels. This process generates gradients for optimizing the Gaussian properties to match input images.

#### 3.2. 4D Gaussian Splatting for Dynamic Scenes

DeformGS extends this to dynamic scenes by learning a deformation function ($F$) that maps a set of canonical Gaussians into world space at any given time ($t$). Unlike some prior dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods that explicitly optimize Gaussian properties at each timestep or for opacity/scale over time, DeformGS focuses on deforming only position and rotation to maintain tracking continuity.

- **Canonical Neural Voxel Encoding:** To provide sufficient capacity for capturing complex deformations, DeformGS employs a neural-voxel encoding, specifically leveraging HexPlanes. This encoding represents features in various 2D planes within a 4D space (e.g., XY, XZ, YZ, XT, YT, ZT). Each plane has a multi-resolution structure, allowing for efficient querying via bilinear interpolation to obtain a feature vector.
- **Deformation MLP:** This multi-layer perceptron (MLP) takes the feature vector from the neural-voxel encoding and infers three outputs for each Gaussian:
 1. **Position:** The deformed 3D mean position ($\mu'$).
 2. **Rotation:** The deformed rotation ($R'$).
 3. **Shadow Scalar:** A scalar ($s \in$) that modulates the RGB color of the Gaussian, allowing the model to account for changes in illumination, particularly shadows. This scalar is obtained by passing the MLP output through a sigmoid activation function.

Crucially, DeformGS's deformation function *does not* infer opacity or scale changes over time. This design choice is intended to prevent Gaussians from disappearing or appearing, which would hinder accurate 3D point tracking. Instead, it maintains consistent Gaussians whose positions and orientations evolve.

#### 3.3. 3D Tracking using Physics-Inspired Regularization

To ensure that the learned deformations are physically plausible and to constrain the solution space, especially in texture-less regions, DeformGS introduces several regularization terms:

- **Local Isometry Loss ($L_{iso}$):** This term encourages the relative spatial arrangement of k-nearest neighboring Gaussians to remain consistent with their initial configuration at $t=0$. While large-scale deformations will break global isometry, local isometry helps prevent unrealistic local distortions and drift. The loss is computed as the squared difference between the squared distances of neighbors at $t=0$ and at time $t$, weighted by an exponential term based on their initial distance.
- **Conservation of Momentum Loss ($L_{momentum}$):** This term incentivizes smooth trajectories by promoting constant velocity. It penalizes sudden changes in direction or magnitude, effectively acting as a low-pass filter on the 3D trajectories. It is calculated as the L1 norm of the difference between the Gaussian's position at $t+1$ and the sum of its position at $t-1$ and twice its position at $t$, i.e., $||\mu_{i,t+1} + \mu_{i,t-1} - 2\mu_{i,t}||_1$. This encourages linear motion in successive frames.

These regularization terms are applied at every iteration, considering three adjacent time steps ($t-1, t, t+1$) to compute the Gaussian positions in world space.

#### 3.4. Learning 3D Masks

To manage computational complexity and apply regularization effectively in scenes with both static and dynamic elements, DeformGS incorporates a masking mechanism:

- **Noisy Training Masks:** The method leverages existing foundation models, specifically Segment Anything Model (SAM) for the initial frame and XMem for propagating masks to subsequent frames, to generate *noisy* 2D masks of dynamic objects (e.g., cloth).
- **Per-Gaussian Mask Property:** A per-Gaussian property, $m_i \in$, is learned. A rendered mask ($M$) is then constructed by blending these $m_i$ values with Gaussian opacities.
- **Mask Regularization:** A regularization term is added to the overall loss function to ensure that the learned per-Gaussian masks best reconstruct the noisy input masks.
- **Selective Regularization:** The physics-inspired regularization terms (isometry and momentum) are applied *only* to Gaussians identified as dynamic by their learned $m_i$ values. This prevents unintended deformation of static background elements and focuses computational effort.

The entire system is trained end-to-end, optimizing both the canonical Gaussian properties and the parameters of the deformation function using photometric loss from novel-view synthesis as the primary supervision signal, augmented by the described regularization terms and mask learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The performance of DeformGS was evaluated using both synthetic and real-world datasets.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Dataset:** Six distinct Blender-generated synthetic scenes depicting highly deformable cloth objects with varying visual and physical properties, large deformations, shadows, and occlusions (e.g., cloth falling over balls, constrained draping). Each scene provided 100 camera views and 40 consecutive time steps, totaling 4,000 images, along with ground-truth trajectories of mesh vertices.
- **Baselines:**
 - **2D Tracking Oracles (with privileged information):** RAFT, RAFT Oracle (best view chosen using ground-truth trajectories), and OmniMotion Oracle (best view chosen using ground-truth trajectories). These models also had access to ground truth depth information.
 - **Gaussian Splatting Baselines:** Dynamic 3D Gaussians (DynaGS), which explicitly models Gaussian positions and rotations per timestep, and 4D-GS, which uses a deformation field similar to DeformGS but without its specific architectural modifications, shadow scalar, and physics-inspired regularization.
- **Metrics:** Median Trajectory Error (MTE) in millimeters (lower is better), position accuracy ($\delta_{avg}$, proportion of points with error below a threshold, higher is better), and survival rate (proportion of points tracked within 0.5m error, higher is better).

**Quantitative Results (Table 1):**
- **Superior Tracking Accuracy:** DeformGS achieved a mean MTE of 27.754 mm across the six synthetic scenes. This represents a significant improvement over DynaGS (115.859 mm, 76.0% lower MTE) and 4D-GS (62.778 mm, 55.8% lower MTE).
- **Improved Position Accuracy and Survival Rate:** DeformGS also demonstrated higher mean position accuracy ($\delta_{avg}$ = 0.688) and a perfect survival rate (1.0) compared to DynaGS ($\delta_{avg}$ = 0.4118, survival rate = 0.907) and 4D-GS ($\delta_{avg}$ = 0.5667, survival rate = 0.967).
- **Competitive with Oracle Models:** While DeformGS did not utilize privileged information (ground-truth depth or optimal camera view), its performance was competitive with the 2D tracking oracle models that did. For instance, RAFT Oracle had a mean MTE of 18.152 mm, and OmniMotion Oracle had 10.677 mm.
- **Impact of Texture:** The results indicated that scenes with less texture (e.g., Scene 3) yielded significantly higher tracking errors for all methods, including DeformGS, compared to richly textured scenes. For example, DeformGS's MTE in Scene 3 was 88.369 mm, compared to 3.373 mm in Scene 1.
- **Effectiveness of Architectural Changes:** The improved performance of DeformGS over 4D-GS suggests that the proposed deformation function architecture, per-Gaussian masks, and physics-inspired regularization terms contributed to enhanced tracking capabilities.

**Qualitative Results (Figure 4):**
- Visualizations of inferred trajectories in Scene 5 showed that DeformGS produced fewer errors and more accurate 3D tracking, particularly as the cloth draped, compared to DynaGS and 4D-GS. The baseline methods tended to introduce larger errors in such complex deformation scenarios.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Dataset:** Robo360 dataset, featuring human manipulation of deformable objects (duvet, smaller cloth) captured by 86 calibrated cameras. Two scenes were selected for evaluation: a human folding a duvet (17 training views) and a human folding a smaller cloth (20 training views).
- **Evaluation Method:** Qualitative assessment, as ground truth trajectories are unavailable for real-world scenarios.

**Qualitative Results:**
- **Real2Sim for Digital Twins (Figure 5):** DeformGS successfully inferred smooth and coherent 3D trajectories and point clouds for a person manipulating a duvet. The generated point clouds are suggested as a basis for creating digital twins, which could then be used to generate dense supervision for imitation learning.
- **Task-Relevant Keypoint Tracking (Figure 6):** Comparison with 4D-GS on both duvet and cloth scenes indicated that DeformGS generated smoother and more physically plausible 3D trajectories for keypoints. In contrast, 4D-GS produced more cluttered and less coherent trajectories, which would likely be less suitable for robotic applications requiring precise keypoint tracking.

Overall, the findings indicate that DeformGS provides a robust and accurate solution for 3D dense tracking in highly deformable scenes, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods in synthetic benchmarks and demonstrating qualitative effectiveness in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DeformGS research addresses a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation and computer vision: the accurate 3D tracking of highly deformable objects. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Advancing Deformable Object Manipulation:** DeformGS represents a step towards enabling robots to interact with and manipulate deformable objects like cloth, ropes, or soft materials with a higher degree of precision and robustness. This capability is critical for automating tasks in various industries, including manufacturing, healthcare, and domestic robotics (e.g., laundry folding, surgical assistance, assistive dressing).
- **State-of-the-Art 3D Tracking:** The method demonstrates superior performance in simultaneous high-quality dynamic scene reconstruction and accurate 3D metric tracking, significantly outperforming previous state-of-the-art baselines in synthetic environments. This establishes a new benchmark for dense 3D scene flow estimation in complex, deformable scenes with occlusions and shadows.
- **Robust Intermediate Representation for Robot Learning:** By providing a dense 3D tracking capability, DeformGS offers a powerful intermediate representation for various robot learning paradigms.
 - **Imitation Learning:** Accurate 3D trajectories of deformable objects can serve as rich supervisory signals, allowing robots to learn complex manipulation skills from human demonstrations more effectively.
 - **Real2Sim Transfer and Digital Twins:** The ability to reconstruct dynamic 3D geometry and trajectories can facilitate the creation of high-fidelity digital twins of real-world deformable objects and environments. This enables seamless transfer of learned policies from simulation to reality, reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection and improving simulation accuracy.
 - **Task-Relevant Keypoint Tracking:** The method allows for the robust tracking of arbitrary points on deformable objects, which can be critical for defining and executing manipulation tasks (e.g., grasping a specific corner of a cloth, tracking the edge of a garment).
- **Leveraging Gaussian Splatting for Tracking:** The work effectively adapts and enhances the Gaussian Splatting paradigm, known for its novel-view synthesis capabilities, to the challenging task of continuous 3D tracking. The introduction of a dedicated deformation function, physics-inspired regularization (isometry and momentum), and learnable per-Gaussian masks specifically tailored for deformable object dynamics contributes to this success.
- **New Research Resources:** The provision of six synthetic scenes with ground-truth data, along with the source code, is a valuable contribution to the research community. These resources can facilitate future research and fair comparisons in the domain of deformable object tracking and manipulation.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also point to future research directions:
- **Hardware Requirements:** DeformGS, like many dynamic novel-view reconstruction methods, requires a setup of multiple synchronized and calibrated cameras. This entails significant engineering effort for real-world deployment.
- **Real-time Performance:** The current approach is not real-time, which limits its direct applicability in time-critical robotic applications. Innovation in computational efficiency will be necessary.
- **Robustness and Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Occasional instances of Gaussians drifting off their trajectories are observed. The algorithm's sensitivity to regularization hyperparameters suggests a need for more adaptive or robust parameter tuning, or possibly incorporating additional supervision.
- **Integration with 2D Tracking:** A promising future direction involves integrating supervision from state-of-the-art 2D point-tracking algorithms (e.g., RAFT, OmniMotion) to enhance the robustness and accuracy of 3D tracking, particularly in challenging scenarios or for mitigating Gaussian drift.

Despite these limitations, DeformGS represents a notable advancement in perception for deformable object manipulation, offering a foundation for future developments in robotics and computer vision.
