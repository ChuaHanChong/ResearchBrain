# Machine Learning-based Feedback Linearization Control of Quadrotor Subject to Unmodeled Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Turku developed an online adaptive machine learning-based feedback linearization controller for quadrotors to compensate for unmodeled dynamics. This controller, leveraging a Gaussian Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural network and Lyapunov stability theory, demonstrated improved trajectory tracking accuracy and error convergence in both simulations and real-flight experiments on a Crazyflie 2.1.

## Problem

- Quadrotors are inherently underactuated, highly nonlinear systems that encounter unknown or unmodeled dynamics (e.g., air drag, actuator dynamics) and external disturbances in real-world scenarios.
- Classical control methods (PID, LQR) offer limited stability and performance when the system deviates significantly from operating points, while robust controllers (SMC) can cause undesirable chattering.
- Purely data-driven approaches (RL, NN) typically require extensive offline training and often lack formal stability guarantees, making them challenging for real-time, safety-critical applications.

## Method

- A **machine learning-based feedback linearization (FBL)** control framework is developed, integrating an online adaptive ==Gaussian Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural network==.
- The RBF network models and compensates for unknown or unmodeled dynamics in real-time, leveraging its ==universal approximation== capabilities without requiring prior offline training.
- ==Lyapunov stability theory== is applied to derive the control law and the online adaptation rule for the RBF network's weights, providing theoretical guarantees of closed-loop stability and asymptotic convergence.

## Results

- Numerical simulations showed the intelligent controller reduced position RMSE by approximately **87-89%** and yaw orientation RMSE by **54%** compared to a baseline FBL controller.
- Gazebo simulation experiments demonstrated up to a **34.8%** improvement in mean position-norm RMSE over the FBL baseline with optimized learning rates and projection bounds.
- Real-flight experiments on a Crazyflie 2.1 achieved a **7.13%** improvement in mean position-norm RMSE and a **49.27%** reduction in yaw orientation RMSE compared to the baseline FBL, confirming effectiveness in a physical environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online adaptive RBF networks can effectively estimate and compensate for complex unmodeled dynamics in real-time, leading to improved control performance for quadrotors.
> - Integrating a machine learning component (RBF network) with a Lyapunov-based control design successfully combines adaptive capabilities with theoretical assurances of stability and convergence.
> - The proposed methodology enables robust and precise trajectory tracking even in the presence of significant system uncertainties and external disturbances, validated across simulations and real-world experiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The control of quadrotor Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in complex and uncertain environments is an active area of research. Quadrotors are inherently underactuated systems with six degrees of freedom controlled by only four inputs, exhibiting strong nonlinear coupling between their translational and rotational dynamics. This necessitates sophisticated control strategies, especially when dealing with partially known system dynamics or significant nonlinearities.

Classical control methods, such as Proportional Integral Derivative (PID) and Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR), are often adopted for their simplicity. However, their reliance on linearization around specific operating points, typically the hover state, limits their stability and performance when the system deviates significantly from these conditions. Sliding Mode Control (SMC) offers improved robustness against model inaccuracies and external disturbances, but is prone to chattering effects. These high-frequency oscillations can excite unmodeled dynamics and lead to mechanical fatigue. Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides a mechanism for explicit handling of state and input constraints. Nevertheless, many MPC implementations decouple translational and rotational dynamics, neglecting critical cross-axis dependencies, and are characterized by high computational complexity, which can impede real-time application.

More recently, data-driven approaches like Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Neural Network (NN)-based controllers have demonstrated successes in domains such as high-speed drone racing. These methods leverage domain randomization to develop policies resilient to system uncertainties. However, they typically require extensive offline training in simulation, and their black-box nature can complicate stability assurances during online tracking of continuous trajectories.

This research addresses a persistent challenge in quadrotor control: achieving agile and precise trajectory tracking in the presence of unknown or unmodeled dynamics and external disturbances, without relying on extensive offline training. It positions itself by integrating the theoretical guarantees of Lyapunov-based control with the adaptive capabilities of machine learning. Specifically, it seeks to overcome the limitations of classical methods regarding robustness to uncertainties and the challenges of data-driven approaches concerning online stability and prior training requirements. By introducing an online adaptive neural network within a feedback linearization framework, the work aims to enhance real-time performance and robustness while maintaining theoretical stability assurances, thereby contributing to the development of more reliable and versatile autonomous quadrotor systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a novel machine learning-based feedback linearization control framework designed for agile quadrotors operating in dynamic and uncertain environments, particularly when the complete system dynamics are either partially known or highly nonlinear.

The detailed objectives include:
1. **Modeling and Compensation of Unmodeled Dynamics:** To employ a Gaussian Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural network to model and compensate for unknown or unmodeled dynamics in real time. These unmodeled dynamics encompass factors such as air drag, actuator dynamics, and other external disturbances that are challenging to precisely characterize.
2. **Leveraging Universal Approximation:** To utilize the universal approximation capability of RBF networks to effectively model complex nonlinearities and uncertainties inherent in quadrotor systems.
3. **Online Adaptive Learning:** To implement an online adaptation mechanism for the RBF neural network, enabling the network's weights to be updated in real-time based on system performance and environmental interaction, without requiring prior offline training.
4. **Ensuring Closed-Loop Stability:** To derive the control law using Lyapunov stability theory, thereby providing a theoretical guarantee of closed-loop stability and asymptotic convergence for trajectory tracking tasks.
5. **Performance Improvement:** To demonstrate, through simulation and real-flight experiments, that the proposed controller improves trajectory tracking accuracy, achieves rapid convergence, and reduces tracking errors (specifically position-norm and yaw orientation Root Mean Square Error, RMSE) compared to a baseline feedback linearization controller.

The motivation for this research stems from several limitations observed in current quadrotor control strategies:
- **Incomplete System Knowledge:** Real-world quadrotor operation frequently encounters situations where a complete and accurate dynamic model is unavailable due to complex aerodynamics, varying payloads, or environmental disturbances. Traditional model-based controllers struggle under such conditions.
- **Limitations of Classical Controllers:** Simpler controllers like PID lack the robustness for agile maneuvers in uncertain conditions. Robust controllers like SMC, while effective, can introduce undesirable chattering. MPC, despite its capabilities, often faces computational bottlenecks for real-time applications and can simplify dynamics by decoupling subsystems.
- **Challenges with Purely Data-Driven Methods:** While reinforcement learning and neural network controllers show promise, their reliance on extensive offline training limits their adaptability to unforeseen real-time changes. Furthermore, their black-box nature can make formal stability guarantees difficult to establish, which is critical for safety-critical applications.
- **Need for Real-time Adaptation with Stability:** There is a need for control strategies that can adapt to changing conditions and unmodeled disturbances in real-time, while still providing robust performance and theoretical assurances of stability and convergence, which is crucial for reliable autonomous operation. This work aims to bridge the gap between adaptive learning capabilities and rigorous stability analysis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves developing a machine learning-based feedback linearization control framework, integrating adaptive neural networks with a Lyapunov-based stability analysis for quadrotor trajectory tracking.

**Quadrotor Modeling:**
The quadrotor is modeled as a rigid body in 3D space. Its dynamics are derived using the Lagrangian approach, resulting in the equation $M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q) + \tau_d = F$. Here, $M(q)$ is the inertia matrix, $C(q, \dot{q})$ is the Coriolis and centrifugal matrix, $G(q)$ is the gravity term, $F$ is the generalized force vector (translational force and torques), and $\tau_d$ represents all unmodeled dynamics including air drag, actuator dynamics, and external disturbances. The model is decoupled into translational and rotational dynamics.

**Feedback Linearization (FBL) Control Law:**
A standard feedback linearization control law is proposed as $F = M(\ddot{q}_r - 2\Lambda \dot{\tilde{q}} - \Lambda^2 \tilde{q} - \hat{d}) + C\dot{q} + G$. In this formulation, $\ddot{q}_r$ is the derivative of the reference velocity, $\tilde{q} = q - q_r$ is the tracking error, $\Lambda$ is a diagonal matrix of positive control gains, and $\hat{d}$ is the estimate of the unmodeled dynamics $\tau_d$ (denoted as $d$). Applying this control law to the quadrotor dynamics yields an error dynamic equation: $\ddot{\tilde{q}} + 2\Lambda \dot{\tilde{q}} + \Lambda^2 \tilde{q} = d - \hat{d}$. A combined error term, $s = \dot{\tilde{q}} + \Lambda\tilde{q}$, inspired by sliding mode control, is introduced to simplify the error dynamics to $\dot{s} + \Lambda s = d - \hat{d}$.

**Adaptive Neural Network for Unmodeled Dynamics Estimation:**
To estimate the unknown unmodeled dynamics $d$, a Gaussian Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural network is employed. The RBF network's universal approximation property is leveraged to model nonlinearities and uncertainties. The estimate $\hat{d}$ is given by $\hat{d}_i = w_i^\top \phi_i(s_i)$, where $w_i$ is the weight vector and $\phi_i(s_i)$ is the vector of activation functions. The activation functions are Gaussian radial basis functions, $\phi_{i,j} = \exp\left[-\frac{(s_i - c_{i,j})^2}{2\sigma_{i,j}^2}\right]$, where $c_{i,j}$ are the centers and $\sigma_{i,j}$ are the widths of the Gaussian functions.

**Lyapunov Stability Analysis and Online Adaptation:**
The control law and the online adaptation of the RBF network weights are derived using Lyapunov stability theory to guarantee closed-loop stability and asymptotic convergence of trajectory tracking errors. A Lyapunov function $V_i = \frac{1}{2} s_i^2 + \frac{1}{2\eta_i} \tilde{w}_i^\top \tilde{w}_i$ is used, where $\tilde{w}_i = w_i - w_i^*$ is the weight estimation error and $\eta_i$ is the learning rate. By selecting the weight adaptation rule $\dot{w}_i = \eta_i s_i \phi_i$, the time derivative of the Lyapunov function becomes $\dot{V}_i = -s_i(\lambda_i s_i - \epsilon_i)$. This indicates that $\dot{V}_i$ is negative definite when $|s_i| > \frac{\epsilon_i}{\lambda_i}$, ensuring boundedness of the error. To further ensure that the weights $w_i$ remain bounded within a convex region, a projection algorithm is applied to the weight update rule. This guarantees that the tracking error $\tilde{q}$ and its derivative $\dot{\tilde{q}}$ exponentially converge to a defined closed region.

**Geometric Control Formulation:**
To address the inherent coupling between translational and rotational dynamics, a geometric control formulation on the Special Orthogonal Group SO(3) is adopted. This approach derives the desired attitude directly from the translational force vector, ensuring that orientation and angular velocity error terms evolve naturally on the tangent space of the configuration manifold, thus maintaining global stability without the singularities associated with Euler angles.

**Experimental Setup:**
The proposed control scheme is evaluated through both numerical simulations and real-flight experiments using the Bitcraze Crazyflie 2.1 quadrotor.
- **Simulation Environment:** Robot Operating System (ROS)2 Humble with Gazebo Harmonic simulation. Unmodeled external disturbances (air drag) were introduced in simulations as $d = -\zeta \dot{q}$.
- **Hardware:** Bitcraze Crazyflie 2.1 quadrotor, weighing approximately 29g. Communication with a ground station computer is established via Crazyradio PA and CrazySwarm2, providing ROS2 integration.
- **Trajectory:** A spiral trajectory is used as the reference path for tracking.
- **State Estimation:** In simulation, odometry from Gazebo is used; for real flights, odometry from the onboard IMU is utilized.
- **Control Parameters:** Control gains ($\Lambda$), learning rates ($\eta_i$), RBF network centers ($c_{i,j}$) and widths ($\sigma_{i,j}$), and projection bounds ($\mu_i$) for weights are carefully tuned for each experimental setting (numeric, Gazebo, real flight). The baseline controller is implemented by setting the learning rate $\eta_i = 0$, effectively disabling the adaptive RBF network.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both theoretical assurances and empirical evidence demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed machine learning-based feedback linearization controller.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
- The derivation of the control law using Lyapunov stability theory formally guarantees closed-loop stability.
- It also ensures the asymptotic convergence of the trajectory tracking error to a bounded region, with the bounds depending on the approximation error of the RBF network and control gains. This theoretical foundation is a key aspect of the work.

**Numerical Simulations:**
- Comparison with a pure feedback linearization (FBL) controller (i.e., $\eta_i=0$) showed significant improvements.
- For a spiral trajectory, the intelligent controller (IC) reduced position RMSE values (e.g., RMSE$_x$ and RMSE$_y$) from approximately 0.07m-0.076m (FBL) to 0.0086m-0.0098m (IC).
- Yaw orientation RMSE was reduced from 0.0026 rad (FBL) to 0.0012 rad (IC). Angular velocity RMSE remained low for both controllers.
- These results indicated that the adaptive RBF network effectively estimated and compensated for the introduced unmodeled air drag, leading to improved tracking precision.

**Gazebo Simulation Experiments:**
- Two different parameter settings for the intelligent controller (IC) were tested against the FBL baseline.
- The baseline FBL controller exhibited position RMSE values ranging from 0.0344m to 0.1331m across axes.
- IC (Case 1) showed marginal improvements over the FBL baseline, with slightly reduced position RMSEs (e.g., RMSE$_x$ from 0.1193m to 0.1144m).
- IC (Case 2), with optimized learning rates and projection bounds, yielded more substantial improvements: a mean position-norm RMSE improvement of approximately 34.8% relative to the baseline. For instance, RMSE$_x$ decreased to 0.0828m, RMSE$_y$ to 0.0838m, and RMSE$_z$ to 0.0138m.
- A trade-off was observed in Case 2: while translational performance improved, rotational aggressiveness (higher RMSE in yaw orientation and angular velocity) increased. This suggests a need for careful tuning to balance different performance objectives in simulation.
- The intelligent estimates ($\hat{d}$) for position and orientation components demonstrated real-time adaptation and compensation for unmodeled dynamics.

**Real Flight Experiments (Crazyflie 2.1):**
- Real-flight tests confirmed the effectiveness of the intelligent controller in a physical environment, which is subject to inherent unmodeled dynamics and state estimation drift.
- Compared to the baseline FBL controller, the intelligent controller (IC, Case 3) achieved a mean RMSE improvement of the position-norm by approximately 7.13%. For example, RMSE$_x$ reduced from 0.0745m to 0.0725m, and RMSE$_y$ from 0.0694m to 0.0664m.
- A more substantial improvement was observed in yaw orientation, with RMSE reducing by 49.27% (from 0.0376 rad to 0.0191 rad).
- Roll and pitch stabilization were observed in the real flight.
- The intelligent estimates ($\hat{d}$) for both position and orientation components dynamically adjusted to compensate for real-world disturbances, indicating successful online adaptation.
- The results for real flight experiments, based on five runs per setting, showed consistent steady-state tracking.

In summary, the main findings consistently demonstrated that the proposed machine learning-based feedback linearization controller, with its online adaptive RBF network, effectively models and compensates for unmodeled dynamics and disturbances. This resulted in improved trajectory tracking accuracy and faster error convergence across numerical, Gazebo, and real-flight experiments, while retaining theoretical stability guarantees.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of aerial robotics and intelligent control by offering a robust and adaptable solution for quadrotor navigation in challenging environments. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robustness to Uncertainties:** The primary significance lies in its ability to effectively handle unmodeled dynamics, such as air drag, actuator dynamics, and external disturbances, in real-time. This is a critical factor in deploying quadrotors in real-world scenarios where precise system models are often unavailable or subject to change. The online adaptation mechanism of the RBF network allows the controller to continuously learn and compensate for these uncertainties.
- **Real-time Adaptability Without Prior Training:** The online adaptation of RBF network weights without prior offline training is a notable advantage. This characteristic reduces the engineering effort required for system identification and tuning, making the controller more suitable for rapid deployment and adaptable to evolving operational conditions or unforeseen environmental changes.
- **Theoretical Guarantees for Data-Driven Control:** Integrating a machine learning component (RBF network) with a Lyapunov-based control design provides theoretical guarantees of closed-loop stability and asymptotic convergence. This addresses a common limitation of purely data-driven control approaches, where formal stability analysis can be challenging. The combination of learning with stability guarantees enhances the trustworthiness and reliability of the control system.
- **Improved Trajectory Tracking Performance:** The demonstrated improvements in trajectory tracking accuracy and error convergence, particularly in real-flight experiments, indicate a tangible enhancement in quadrotor performance. This is crucial for applications requiring high precision, such as inspection, manipulation, or close-proximity operations.
- **Practical Applicability:** The validation of the controller on a real Crazyflie 2.1 quadrotor underscores its practical feasibility and relevance for lightweight, agile UAV platforms. The open-source code release further promotes reproducibility and future research in this area.
- **Foundation for Advanced Autonomous Systems:** The work lays a foundation for future developments in autonomous aerial robotics. Its robustness to unmodeled dynamics could be extended to more complex tasks, including multi-robot coordination, where inter-robot forces or complex environmental interactions introduce further uncertainties. The prospect of integrating reinforcement learning for parameter tuning and obstacle-aware navigation suggests a pathway towards highly intelligent and autonomous drone systems capable of operating in highly dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Advancement in Adaptive Control:** This research advances the field of adaptive control by demonstrating a successful integration of neural network approximation capabilities within a feedback linearization framework to address a complex control problem with practical implications.

In summary, the proposed controller offers a method for achieving precise and robust quadrotor control under realistic conditions of uncertainty, bridging the gap between theoretical stability and practical adaptive learning. This has implications for increasing the autonomy and reliability of UAVs across diverse applications, from environmental monitoring to search-and-rescue operations.
