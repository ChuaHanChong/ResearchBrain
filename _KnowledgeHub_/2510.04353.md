# Stability-Aware Retargeting for Humanoid Multi-Contact Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> A teleoperation system for humanoid robots, developed by researchers at IHMC and Ocado Technology, proactively enhances stability during multi-contact tasks by dynamically adjusting posture and contact points. This method achieved an average stability margin increase of 27% in simulation and 7% on hardware, alongside a 12.6% improvement in joint torque margins.

## Problem

- Teleoperating humanoids in complex, multi-contact tasks is difficult due to maintaining balance and respecting actuation limits, often leading to motor torque saturation or loss of stability.
- Existing teleoperation frameworks primarily reactively constrain the Center of Mass (CoM) to ensure stability, which can interfere with the operator's high-level task goals.
- Balancing intuitive operator control with autonomous robot stability maintenance is a fundamental challenge in shared control for multi-contact scenarios.

## Method

- A shared control framework employs an actuation-aware 2D centroidal stability model to dynamically adjust the robot's posture and contact points based on stability margin gradients.
- The system utilizes an efficient analytical gradient calculation method, derived from ==Linear Program== sensitivity analysis, to determine how changes in robot configuration affect stability in real-time.
- Operator commands are retargeted by activating pre-contact heuristics for optimal hand placement and post-contact postural adjustments, guided by stability sensitivity, to proactively grow the stability region.

## Results

- In simulation, combining posture and contact retargeting increased the average stability margin by **27%** compared to nominal teleoperation, and a strong correlation (R

= **0.69**) was found between higher stability margins and greater impulse resilience.
- Hardware demonstrations showed an average stability margin increase of **7%** and an average maximum stability margin increase of **121%** during critical task phases, along with a **12.6%** average improvement in joint torque margin.
- The system achieved real-time performance on hardware, with incremental stability region updates completing in 79 µs and stability margin gradient computations in 334 µs, enabling responsive teleoperation at kHz rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proactively adjusting a robot's posture and contact points based on stability gradients can actively expand its stability region, offering a less intrusive way to maintain balance during teleoperation compared to reactive CoM constraints.
> - Integrating actuation limits into the stability model is critical, as postural adjustments become particularly effective for stability enhancement when the robot is constrained by its motor torque capabilities.
> - Real-time computation of complex stability models and their gradients is feasible through efficient analytical methods, enabling dynamic and responsive adjustments for teleoperated systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Stability-Aware Retargeting for Humanoid Multi-Contact Teleoperation" addresses a critical challenge in humanoid robotics: enabling robust teleoperation for complex, contact-rich tasks. This work operates at the intersection of several key research areas: humanoid control, teleoperation, multi-contact locomotion/manipulation, and real-time stability analysis.

**Humanoid Control and Stability:** The foundation of this work lies in the robust control of humanoid robots. A significant body of research focuses on ensuring stability, especially in dynamic and multi-contact scenarios. Traditional methods often revolve around the Zero-Moment Point (ZMP) for bipedal locomotion, but more advanced techniques, like the 2D "support region" introduced by Bretl and Lall, account for non-coplanar contacts and actuation limits. This paper directly builds on the "actuation-aware extension of the support region", which explicitly integrates joint torque bounds into the stability analysis. This represents a move from purely geometric or kinematic stability criteria to models that incorporate the robot's physical capabilities and limitations. Other related work includes extending stability to 3D polytopes for dynamic motions, dynamic push recovery, and optimizing robustness over a horizon, which this paper acknowledges as more computationally intensive.

**Multi-Contact Locomotion and Manipulation:** Humanoids performing tasks like climbing, crawling, or bracing against surfaces heavily rely on multi-contact interactions. Research in this area often focuses on planning feasible contact sequences, and optimizing centroidal trajectories under contact wrench constraints. This paper extends these concepts by focusing on the *online adjustment* of contact points and posture during teleoperation, rather than just pre-planned sequences. It also implicitly touches upon the Contact Wrench Cone (CWC) by analyzing contact wrench feasibility within the LP formulation.

**Teleoperation Systems:** Teleoperation is a powerful paradigm for leveraging human intelligence and dexterity to control robots. However, many existing teleoperation systems for humanoids are designed for relatively simple tasks or terrain. When it comes to complex, multi-contact scenarios, stability becomes a major hurdle. Some notable exceptions include bilateral feedback teleoperation, retargeting into feasible robot dynamics, and learning-based approaches that track learned setpoints. This paper differentiates itself by explicitly incorporating a sophisticated, *proactive* stability model into the teleoperation loop, which aims to improve the robot's inherent stability rather than just reactively constraining its motion. It adopts a *shared control* approach, balancing direct operator command with autonomous robot interventions for safety and performance.

**Real-time Optimization and Sensitivity Analysis:** A key technical challenge in these areas is performing complex calculations in real-time. The paper's contribution of an efficient analytical gradient calculation for the stability margin, derived from Linear Program sensitivity analysis, is crucial for its real-time applicability. This allows for dynamic adjustments at high rates (kHz), which is essential for responsive teleoperation.

**Unique Contribution:** The unique position of this work in the landscape is its focus on *actively growing the robot's stability region* by modifying its posture and contact points, rather than primarily constraining the Center of Mass (CoM) within a static stability boundary. Most prior approaches use stability as a *hard constraint* on CoM motion, which can interfere with the operator's high-level task. This paper, instead, uses the stability margin gradient to *optimize* the robot's lower-level configuration (posture, contact placement) to *preemptively enhance stability*, allowing the CoM to follow the operator more freely. This shared control philosophy aims to provide a more intuitive and less restrictive teleoperation experience for challenging multi-contact tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and intuitive teleoperation framework for humanoid robots that enables them to perform complex, multi-contact tasks with enhanced stability. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Teleoperation Challenges in Multi-Contact Scenarios:** Teleoperation is a powerful tool for generating reference motions for humanoids. However, it becomes exceedingly difficult when robots engage in multi-contact tasks, such as using hands for bracing on non-coplanar surfaces. In these scenarios, maintaining balance is paramount, and operator commands can easily lead to motor torque saturation (actuation limits) or loss of stability through slipping (friction limits). The paper highlights that many existing teleoperation frameworks do not adequately support these complex, contact-rich interactions.

2. **Balancing Operator Control with Robot Stability:** A fundamental conflict arises in teleoperating multi-contact tasks: the need to accurately track the operator's desired motion setpoints while simultaneously ensuring the robot's balance and stability. The motivation is to resolve these competing objectives through a shared control scheme. This scheme should allow the operator extensive control when the robot is stable, but autonomously intervene to improve stability when the robot approaches its limits.

3. **Proactive Stability Enhancement over Reactive Constraint:** The authors are motivated to move beyond merely enforcing stability as a hard constraint on the robot's Center of Mass (CoM), which can restrict the operator's intended motion. Instead, they aim for a proactive approach where the robot's configuration (posture, contact points) is dynamically adjusted to *improve* or *grow* its inherent stability region. This strategy aims to prevent stability issues before they become critical, thereby minimizing interference with the primary manipulation task.

4. **Leveraging Actuation-Aware Stability Models:** The research is motivated by the availability of state-of-the-art stability models, specifically the actuation-aware 2D centroidal stability region. This model integrates not only unilateral and friction constraints but also joint torque limits, making it highly relevant for humanoids operating near their physical capabilities. The motivation is to apply these advanced models to teleoperation in a computationally efficient manner.

5. **Enabling Real-time Performance:** For teleoperation to be practical, any stability-enhancing mechanism must operate in real-time. This motivates the development of efficient algorithms, such as the analytical calculation of the stability margin gradient, which can run at high frequencies (kHz rates) to provide immediate feedback and control adjustments.

6. **Improving Robot Robustness and Safety:** Ultimately, the motivation is to make humanoid teleoperation safer and more reliable. By dynamically adjusting posture and contact to increase stability margins, the robot becomes more resilient to external disturbances (impulses) and less prone to exceeding joint torque limits, thus preventing failures and improving overall task success rates. This is especially crucial for generating high-quality teleoperation data for learning-based approaches, where stable and successful demonstrations are key.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a stability-aware retargeting scheme integrated into a real-time teleoperation framework. It leverages an actuation-aware centroidal stability model and efficient gradient computations to dynamically adjust the robot's posture and contact points, thereby enhancing stability during multi-contact tasks.

#### 3.1. Stability Model and Margin Calculation

The framework utilizes a quasi-static 2D centroidal stability region, an extension of Bretl and Lall's work, that incorporates actuation constraints as described in Orsolino et al.. This region is computed by solving a Linear Program (LP) (Eq. 3), which determines the feasible CoM positions (c_xy) given contact forces (f_k), friction cones (K_k), and critically, joint torque limits (τ⁺/⁻). The *stability margin (m)* is defined as the minimum distance from the robot's current CoM to the boundary of this 2D stability region, serving as a measure of impulse resilience.

#### 3.2. Efficient Analytical Gradient Calculation

A central contribution is the ability to efficiently compute the gradient of the stability region with respect to key robot parameters. This is achieved by applying a general property of Linear Program sensitivity analysis (Eq. 5), which calculates the derivative of the optimal LP solution (a*i, representing a vertex of the stability region) with respect to a parameter (θ).
- **Parameters for Gradient:** The method computes gradients with respect to two quantities:
 1. **Contact point position (p_k):** How shifting a contact point affects the stability region.
 2. **Whole-body configuration (q):** How changes in joint angles and posture affect the stability region.
- **Real-time Feasibility:** By making a time-linearization assumption for the constraint matrix (A) and assuming the constraint vector (b) is approximately constant with respect to configuration changes (validated in the Appendix), the gradient (∇a*i) can be computed through a series of evaluations of a simplified derivative equation (Eq. 7). This process is sufficiently fast for real-time application (kHz rates).

#### 3.3. Shared Control Teleoperation Framework

The overall teleoperation system involves:
- **Operator Input:** A Valve Index VR headset and handheld controllers provide desired hand poses, CoM position (via joystick), and hand actions (load/unload contact, open/close grasp).
- **Inverse Kinematics (IK) Module:** An IK solver continuously computes a desired whole-body velocity (v_d) and configuration (q_d) by minimizing the error to operator-commanded tasks (Eq. 13). This IK also includes a hard constraint (A_xy v_d ≤ h(m_min)) to ensure the CoM stays within the stability region with a minimum tolerance (m_min = 4cm).
- **Whole-Body Controller:** Tracks the desired configuration from the IK module.

#### 3.4. Retargeting Strategy

The core innovation is *retargeting* operator motions to actively improve stability, focusing on growing the stability region rather than just constraining the CoM.

- **Contact Retargeting (Pre-contact):**
 - This is a heuristic approach performed *before* a hand contact is fully established.
 - When the robot's hand approaches a potential bracing surface (within 1 meter), the system previews the stability region assuming contact at the current hand position.
 - The desired hand position (p_h) is then adjusted along the plane of the surface based on the gradient of the stability region's *area* (∇A(p_k)) (Eq. 14). This aims to place the hand in a position that maximizes the potential stability region once contact is made. A maximum adjustment limit is enforced.

- **Posture Retargeting (Post-contact):**
 - This is performed *after* a hand contact has been established.
 - It modifies lower-priority postural objectives: bracing hand orientation, chest/pelvis orientation, and CoM height.
 - The retargeting is dynamically activated based on two conditions:
 1. **High Postural Sensitivity (s_q):** The postural sensitivity (s_q = ||N_h ∇a*i(q)||₂, where N_h is the null-space projector of high-priority tasks) measures how much posture changes can affect stability. Retargeting is activated only when s_q exceeds a threshold (s⁺_q = 0.01), indicating that posture can meaningfully improve stability (i.e., the robot is actuation-constrained).
 2. **Low Stability Margin (m):** Retargeting is performed only when the current stability margin (m) falls below a higher threshold (m⁺ = 15cm) (optional) or if s_q > s⁺_q. If the margin is high or sensitivity is low, the user regains full control of posture.
 - The desired postural setpoints (t_i) are adjusted using the stability margin gradient (∇m(q)) (Eq. 15b). This moves the robot's posture to increase the stability margin.
 - Safeguards include accounting for joint limits in gradient calculation (ignoring contributions that drive joints into limits) and imposing a maximum deviation from the nominal setpoint.

#### 3.5. Real-Time Controller Integration

To enable hardware deployment, several optimizations were made:
- **Custom LP Solver:** An allocation-free Java Linear Program solver was implemented.
- **Incremental Stability Region Updates:** Instead of recomputing the entire stability region from scratch (as in iterative projection methods), the system uses a set of fixed, equally-spaced query directions. It performs one full LP solve per tick and maintains an accurate estimate of the minimum-margin edge with faster, fixed-basis updates (Eq. 16). This significantly reduces computation time (Table II).
- **Perception Integration:** Intel Realsense D455 and ZED 2 Stereo cameras provide real-time colored point cloud data and planar region detection for identifying candidate bracing surfaces (Fig. 9).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validates the proposed stability-aware retargeting framework through extensive simulation studies and hardware demonstrations, focusing on increased stability margins, impulse resilience, and joint torque margins.

#### 4.1. Simulation Validation

The framework was tested on three distinct multi-contact manipulation scenarios (pitched wall in front, ceiling above, wall behind with uneven footholds) where the robot used a hand for bracing to extend its workspace and retrieve an object (Fig. 7).
- **Increased Stability Margin:** An ablation study demonstrated that combining both posture and contact retargeting led to an average **27% increase** in the stability margin compared to nominal teleoperation.
 - **Posture-Only Retargeting:** Significantly increased stability by 20% on average, confirming its effectiveness in scenarios where the robot is actuation-constrained (Fig. 6).
 - **Contact-Only Retargeting:** Surprisingly, this approach *decreased* stability by 3% on average. This suggests that while contact adjustment is intended to grow the preview region, its full benefit is realized when coupled with posture adjustments that can exploit the newly available stability space, particularly by relaxing actuation constraints.
- **Impulse Resilience Correlation:** A simulation study quantitatively linked the computed stability margin (m) to the robot's ability to withstand impulsive disturbances. By applying impulses to the robot's pelvis towards the minimum-margin edge, a regression analysis (R² = 0.69) validated a strong correlation between a higher stability margin and a greater maximum sustainable impulse (Fig. 8). This confirms the stability margin as a reliable indicator of the robot's robustness to external pushes.
- **Task Success:** In all simulated scenarios, the robot successfully retrieved the canister, indicating that the retargeting scheme did not significantly impede the high-level manipulation task.

#### 4.2. Hardware Demonstrations

The framework was deployed on a humanoid robot with integrated real-time control and perception systems. Three canister retrieval tasks were teleoperated with varying wall orientations and canister positions.
- **Increased Stability Margin (Hardware):** Hardware experiments showed an average stability margin increase of **7%** and, notably, an average *maximum* stability margin increase of **121%** during critical phases of the teleoperation (Fig. 10). This highlights the framework's ability to significantly boost stability when the robot is operating near its limits.
- **Improved Joint Torque Margin:** The retargeting scheme led to an average increase of **12.6%** in the joint torque margin (m_τ), with a specific example showing a 10% increase (Fig. 11). This is a direct benefit of the actuation-aware stability model and postural adjustments, which aim to reduce the load on the robot's electric (lower torque capacity) upper body joints. This ensures the robot operates further from its torque limits, reducing the risk of motor saturation and enhancing overall robustness.
- **Real-time Performance:** The efficient LP solver and incremental stability region updates enabled the system to run in real-time, allowing for responsive teleoperation with dynamic stability adjustments.

#### 4.3. Computational Efficiency

Benchmarking data demonstrated the efficiency of the real-time implementation (Table II). The incremental update rule for the stability region (79 µs) was significantly faster than full updates (698 µs) or iterative projection (1731 µs), with negligible loss of accuracy. The stability margin gradient computation (∇m(q)) was also fast enough for real-time operation (334 µs).

In summary, the results consistently show that the proposed stability-aware retargeting method effectively enhances the stability of humanoid multi-contact teleoperation, both in simulation and on hardware. It leads to higher stability margins, improved impulse resilience, and increased joint torque margins, making teleoperation safer and more robust for complex tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid teleoperation, with broad implications for both practical applications and future robotics research.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Robustness and Safety in Teleoperation

The most immediate impact is the creation of a more robust and safer teleoperation experience for humanoid robots. By proactively identifying and improving the robot's stability margin through dynamic adjustments of posture and contact points, the system mitigates critical risks such as motor torque saturation and loss of balance due to slipping. This capability is crucial for deploying humanoids in complex, unstructured, or hazardous environments (e.g., disaster response, industrial maintenance, space exploration) where human intervention is necessary but direct physical presence is impossible or unsafe. The empirical demonstration of increased impulse resilience and joint torque margins directly translates to a more reliable and resilient robot.

#### 5.2. Paradigm Shift in Stability Management

The work introduces a conceptual shift from primarily *constraining* the robot's CoM to *actively optimizing* its configuration (posture, contact placement) to *expand* its feasible stability region. This "shared control" paradigm allows operators to retain more natural control over the high-level manipulation task, as the robot intelligently manages its lower-level stability in the background. This approach is less intrusive than purely reactive CoM-based constraints, leading to a more intuitive and effective teleoperation interface.

#### 5.3. Real-time Capabilities for Complex Models

The development of an efficient analytical gradient for the actuation-aware centroidal stability region and an incremental update rule for real-time computation (kHz rates) is a significant technical achievement. It demonstrates that sophisticated, physics-based stability models can be integrated into high-frequency control loops, opening doors for the online application of other complex optimization and sensitivity analyses in robotics.

#### 5.4. Improving Data for Learning-Based Robotics

As noted in the paper, learning-based approaches are increasingly leveraging teleoperation data for training robot policies. The ability to generate stable, robust, and task-successful teleoperated motions directly improves the quality and utility of this training data. By ensuring the robot operates within its stable limits, the collected demonstrations will be more consistent and less prone to failures, leading to the development of more robust and generalizable learned behaviors. This could accelerate the progress of autonomous humanoid capabilities.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Research and Development

The paper acknowledges several limitations (quasi-static model, snapshot actuation, simplified joint limit handling, heuristic CoM-task relationship), which provide clear avenues for future research.
- **Dynamic Stability Models:** Extending the sensitivity analysis to dynamic stability models (e.g., incorporating reference accelerations) could further enhance robustness for highly dynamic tasks.
- **Advanced Kinematic Limit Integration:** More sophisticated handling of kinematic limits in robustness models could refine posture adjustments.
- **Task-Aware Stability Optimization:** Research into how centroidal motion impacts task performance could lead to more intelligent retargeting strategies that prioritize task success while maintaining stability.
- **Generalization to Other Robot Morphologies:** The underlying principles of gradient-based stability optimization could be adapted to other complex robotic systems beyond humanoids (e.g., legged robots with manipulators).

In conclusion, this research makes a compelling case for the integration of advanced, proactive stability management into humanoid teleoperation. By providing a framework that is both computationally efficient and demonstrably effective on hardware, it pushes the frontier of what humanoids can achieve under human guidance, paving the way for more capable, versatile, and reliable robotic assistants in diverse and challenging real-world scenarios.
