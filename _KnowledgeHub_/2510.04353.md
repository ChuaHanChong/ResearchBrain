# Stability-Aware Retargeting for Humanoid Multi-Contact Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> A teleoperation system for humanoid robots, developed by researchers at IHMC and Ocado Technology, proactively enhances stability during multi-contact tasks by dynamically adjusting posture and contact points. This method achieved an average stability margin increase of 27% in simulation and 7% on hardware, alongside a 12.6% improvement in joint torque margins.

## Problem

- Teleoperating humanoids in complex, multi-contact tasks is difficult due to maintaining balance and respecting actuation limits, often leading to motor torque saturation or loss of stability.
- Existing teleoperation frameworks primarily reactively constrain the Center of Mass (CoM) to ensure stability, which can interfere with the operator's high-level task goals.
- Balancing intuitive operator control with autonomous robot stability maintenance is a fundamental challenge in shared control for multi-contact scenarios.

## Method

- A shared control framework employs an actuation-aware 2D centroidal stability model to dynamically adjust the robot's posture and contact points based on stability margin gradients.
- The system utilizes an efficient analytical gradient calculation method, derived from ==Linear Program== sensitivity analysis, to determine how changes in robot configuration affect stability in real-time.
- Operator commands are retargeted by activating pre-contact heuristics for optimal hand placement and post-contact postural adjustments, guided by stability sensitivity, to proactively grow the stability region.

## Results

- In simulation, combining posture and contact retargeting increased the average stability margin by **27%** compared to nominal teleoperation, and a strong correlation (R

= **0.69**) was found between higher stability margins and greater impulse resilience.
- Hardware demonstrations showed an average stability margin increase of **7%** and an average maximum stability margin increase of **121%** during critical task phases, along with a **12.6%** average improvement in joint torque margin.
- The system achieved real-time performance on hardware, with incremental stability region updates completing in 79 µs and stability margin gradient computations in 334 µs, enabling responsive teleoperation at kHz rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proactively adjusting a robot's posture and contact points based on stability gradients can actively expand its stability region, offering a less intrusive way to maintain balance during teleoperation compared to reactive CoM constraints.
> - Integrating actuation limits into the stability model is critical, as postural adjustments become particularly effective for stability enhancement when the robot is constrained by its motor torque capabilities.
> - Real-time computation of complex stability models and their gradients is feasible through efficient analytical methods, enabling dynamic and responsive adjustments for teleoperated systems.
