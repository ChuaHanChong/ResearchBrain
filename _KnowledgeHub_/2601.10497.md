# MERGETUNE: Continued fine-tuning of vision-language models

> [!summary] Summary
> MERGETUNE presents a novel "Continued Fine-Tuning" paradigm to recover pre-trained knowledge from Vision-Language Models after task-specific fine-tuning, employing a replay-free, learning-based merging strategy anchored by linear mode connectivity. This approach consistently improves generalization and robustness across diverse benchmarks, for instance, boosting CoOp's harmonic mean by 5.58% in base-to-novel scenarios.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for downstream tasks often leads to catastrophic forgetting of valuable generalizable pre-trained knowledge.
- Existing Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods incompletely preserve pre-trained knowledge and can rely on complex modules, while robust ensembling strategies incur high inference costs or unstable performance.
- Direct model merging techniques struggle to integrate knowledge when zero-shot and fine-tuned models are far apart in weight space.

## Method

- Introduces =="Continued Fine-Tuning (CFT)"==, a post-hoc, learning-based model merging paradigm that actively recovers forgotten knowledge from a fine-tuned VLM.
- Formulates a ==replay-free objective== by using a second-order Taylor expansion surrogate for pretraining data constraints, enabling knowledge preservation without needing original pretraining data.
- Guides the learning process by explicitly enforcing ==linear mode connectivity (LMC)== to find a new model state that maintains low-loss paths to both the original zero-shot VLM and the task-adapted VLM.

## Results

- Consistently improved base-to-novel generalization, increasing CoOp's harmonic mean by **+5.58%** (from **71.66%** to **77.24%**) and showing similar gains across other baselines, outperforming existing training-free merging methods.
- Achieved new state-of-the-art ID-OOD generalization in many-shot settings, with MERGETUNE-enhanced linear probing improving average domain accuracy by **+2.27%** (from **57.39%** to **59.66%**) over the base model, surpassing SOTA ensembling methods like VRF.
- Demonstrated model-agnostic applicability and robustness across different VLM backbones (CLIP-L/14, Siglip2-B/16, Siglip2-L/16) and hyperparameter choices, while maintaining a single model for efficient inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Knowledge lost during VLM fine-tuning can be effectively recovered post-hoc through a dedicated continued fine-tuning process, rather than solely relying on mitigation strategies during initial adaptation.
> - Linear mode connectivity provides a principled, learning-based approach to integrate knowledge from disparate model states (e.g., zero-shot and fine-tuned) even when they are geometrically distant in the parameter space.
> - A replay-free surrogate loss can effectively approximate the preservation of pre-trained knowledge, overcoming the major challenge of requiring access to massive pretraining datasets for knowledge recovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Foundational Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exemplified by CLIP, acquire robust zero-shot generalization capabilities through pretraining on extensive image-text datasets. However, adapting these models to specific downstream tasks via fine-tuning commonly results in catastrophic forgetting of the knowledge acquired during pretraining. This forgetting can diminish the model's ability to generalize to novel or out-of-distribution data.

The existing research landscape for VLM adaptation broadly addresses this challenge through two main avenues:
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods:** These strategies, such as prompt learning (e.g., CoOp, CoCoOp) and adapter-based approaches (e.g., Clip-Adapter, MMA), aim to mitigate forgetting by limiting parameter updates to lightweight modules. While effective in few-shot settings and evaluated for base-to-novel generalization, their success in cross-domain and cross-dataset generalization remains partial, and they do not always completely preserve pretrained knowledge, sometimes underperforming the zero-shot VLM on certain benchmarks. Furthermore, these methods often rely on increasingly complex architectural modules to balance adaptation and generalization.
- **Robust Fine-Tuning approaches:** Primarily evaluated in many-shot settings for in-distribution (ID) performance and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, these methods frequently involve combining pretrained and fine-tuned models. Techniques include weight ensembling (e.g., linear interpolation of weights, Model Soups) or prediction-space ensembling (e.g., VRF). While these approaches can alleviate forgetting, they may not fully reconcile pretrained knowledge with task-specific adaptation and can incur increased inference costs or exhibit unstable performance.

A related area of research involves **model merging**, which focuses on combining independently trained models to integrate complementary knowledge. This field is underpinned by the concept of **mode connectivity**, which postulates that distinct optima in a neural network's loss landscape can be connected by continuous paths of low loss. While model merging techniques like TIES-Merging and DARE aim to combine models effectively, applying them to merge a zero-shot VLM with a fine-tuned version can be challenging due to potentially large disparities in their weight spaces, which can disrupt mode connectivity.

This work introduces "continued fine-tuning" (CFT) as a paradigm designed to **recover forgotten pretrained knowledge after a VLM has already undergone adaptation**. Unlike methods that seek to mitigate forgetting *during* the initial fine-tuning process, MERGETUNE operates post hoc, drawing inspiration from model merging and mode connectivity to explicitly integrate knowledge from both the original zero-shot VLM and its task-adapted counterpart. It aims to address the limitations of existing fine-tuning and ensembling methods by providing a more direct and geometrically guided approach to knowledge restoration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for **recovering pretrained knowledge in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) after they have been fine-tuned for downstream tasks.** This recovery is specifically aimed at addressing the catastrophic forgetting that often occurs during the adaptation process, which can lead to diminished generalization capabilities.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several observations within the VLM research landscape:

1. **Incomplete Knowledge Preservation in Existing Fine-Tuning:** While various Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods have been developed to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during adaptation, they often achieve this incompletely. Experimental evidence indicates that no single prior PEFT method consistently outperforms the original zero-shot VLM (e.g., CLIP) across a wide range of evaluation datasets without relying on external knowledge. This suggests that even with careful adaptation strategies, a significant portion of the valuable pretrained knowledge can remain unpreserved or forgotten.
2. **Limitations of Robust Fine-Tuning Ensembles:** Robust fine-tuning approaches, which often involve ensembling the pretrained and fine-tuned models, also exhibit limitations. These methods may not fully reconcile the broad pretrained knowledge with the specific downstream adaptation, and their performance can sometimes be unstable. Furthermore, ensemble-based solutions typically introduce additional inference costs or require complex mechanisms, such as constructing "zero-shot failure sets" or computing per-sample distances, which are not ideal for practical deployment.
3. **Ineffectiveness of Direct Model Merging:** The concept of model merging, which leverages mode connectivity to combine complementary knowledge from different models, holds theoretical appeal for integrating zero-shot and fine-tuned VLM capabilities. However, existing training-free model merging methods are often found to be ineffective in this specific context. The substantial "weight-space gap" that can emerge between a zero-shot model and its fine-tuned version can break the mode connectivity required for successful merging, leading to performance degradation rather than improvement.
4. **Need for a Post hoc and Replay-Free Solution:** Current fine-tuning methods focus on mitigating forgetting *during* adaptation. There is a need for a strategy that can act *after* a model has already been fine-tuned, serving as a general enhancement. Additionally, any method aiming to leverage pretrained knowledge must contend with the inaccessibility or computational impracticality of replaying the original web-scale pretraining datasets (e.g., CLIP's corpus). Therefore, a replay-free approach is motivated by practical constraints.

In response to these motivations, the research proposes a novel paradigm of "continued fine-tuning" with the specific goal of actively restoring lost pretrained knowledge, thereby enhancing the generalization and robustness of already adapted VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces **Continued Fine-Tuning (CFT)**, a novel paradigm to recover forgotten knowledge from vision-language models (VLMs) after they have been fine-tuned. The core strategy, named **MERGETUNE**, is model-agnostic and applies post hoc to existing fine-tuned VLMs without requiring architectural changes. It is guided by the principle of **Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC)** to integrate knowledge from a zero-shot VLM and a fine-tuned VLM.

#### 3.1. Linear Mode Connectivity as an Objective

MERGETUNE's fundamental premise is that if a model (`w`) can be linearly connected to two other solutions (`ˆw1` and `ˆw2`) through consistently low-loss paths, it can effectively inherit and preserve knowledge from both. Given a zero-shot checkpoint (`ˆw1`, e.g., CLIP) and a downstream fine-tuned checkpoint (`ˆw2`, e.g., CoOp), the objective is to find a continued solution `w` that maintains low-loss linear interpolations to both `ˆw1` and `ˆw2`. This is formalized by the following objective function:

`w = arg min_w E_{α∼U} [L1(ˆw1 + α(w − ˆw1)) + L2(ˆw2 + α(w − ˆw2))]`

Here, `L1` and `L2` represent the pretraining and downstream training losses for the zero-shot and fine-tuned models, respectively, and `α` is a uniformly sampled interpolation coefficient between 0 and 1. This formulation leverages LMC as a learning objective to merge knowledge.

#### 3.2. Addressing the Challenge: Replay-Free Pretraining Loss Approximation

A direct implementation of the LMC objective is challenging because `L1` depends on the original pretraining data (e.g., CLIP's web-scale corpus), which is often inaccessible or computationally prohibitive to replay. To overcome this, MERGETUNE introduces a second-order surrogate loss to approximate the `L1` term in a replay-free manner.

**Second-order Surrogate:** The term `L1(ˆw1 + α(w − ˆw1))` is approximated using a second-order Taylor expansion around `ˆw1`:

`L1(ˆw1 + α(w − ˆw1)) ≈ L1(ˆw1) + α∇L1(ˆw1)^T (w − ˆw1) + (α^2/2) (w − ˆw1)^T H1(w − ˆw1)`

where `H1` is the Hessian matrix `∇^2L1(ˆw1)`.
Two assumptions are applied for tractability:
1. `∇L1(ˆw1) ≈ 0`: Assuming `ˆw1` is near a local optimum for Task 1.
2. `H1 ≈ μI`: Assuming isotropic curvature.

Under these assumptions, the `L1` term simplifies to `L1(ˆw1) + (μα^2/2) ∥w − ˆw1∥^2`. Since `L1(ˆw1)` is a constant, the term `(μα^2/2)` can be absorbed into a hyperparameter `λ`, resulting in a replay-free surrogate regularizer:

`R_Task1 = λ∥w − ˆw1∥^2`

#### 3.3. Final Replay-Free Objective

Combining this Task 1 surrogate with the Task 2 objectives yields the final continued fine-tuning loss:

`L(w) = L2(w) + λ∥w − ˆw1∥^2 + β E_{α∼U[0,1)} L2(ˆw2 + α(w − ˆw2))`

In this objective:
- `L2(w)` is the standard downstream task loss, evaluated on the continued model `w`. This term is decoupled from the expectation to ensure strong endpoint supervision for base-class specialization.
- `λ∥w − ˆw1∥^2` is the surrogate loss, encouraging `w` to remain close to the zero-shot model `ˆw1`, thereby preserving pretrained knowledge.
- `β E_{α∼U[0,1)} L2(ˆw2 + α(w − ˆw2))` is the LMC loss for Task 2, enforcing low-loss connectivity between `w` and the fine-tuned model `ˆw2`. The expectation is approximated by evaluating `L2` at a small number of evenly spaced `α` values (e.g., five or ten).

During training, `w` (representing the trainable parameters) is optimized to minimize this combined loss. After training, the resulting `w` is used for inference.

#### 3.4. Application to CLIP Fine-Tuning Methods

MERGETUNE's flexibility allows it to be applied to various VLM fine-tuning strategies by optimizing the relevant trainable parameters (`w`):
- **Prompt-based methods (e.g., CoOp, KgCoOp):** `w` corresponds to the classifier weights derived from learnable prompts.
- **Adapter-based methods (e.g., MMA):** `w` includes fixed prompts and trainable multimodal adapters.
- **Many-shot regimes (e.g., linear probing, end-to-end fine-tuning):** `w` can represent the linear classification head or the entire model parameters.

This allows MERGETUNE to function as a general post hoc enhancement for diverse CLIP adaptation strategies.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Base Models:** Experiments build upon CoOp, KgCoOp, MMA, and PromptKD for few-shot learning, and linear-head and full-model fine-tuning for many-shot settings. CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone is primarily used, with additional evaluations on CLIP ViT-B/32, CLIP-L/14, Siglip2-B/16, and Siglip2-L/16.
- **Training Configuration:** MERGETUNE reuses the same training configurations (optimizer, learning rate, batch size, epochs, data augmentation) as the respective base fine-tuning method. For instance, CoOp+MERGETUNE is trained for 50 epochs after the initial 100 CoOp epochs.
- **Datasets:** Evaluation is conducted on a range of benchmarks:
 - **Few-shot:** 11 diverse recognition datasets (ImageNet, Caltech-101, OxfordPets, StanfordCars, Flowers-102, Food-101, FGVCAircraft, SUN-397, UCF-101, DTD, EuroSAT) for base-to-novel and cross-dataset generalization. ImageNet-1K and its four variants (ImageNet-V2, -Sketch, -A, -R) for domain generalization.
 - **Many-shot:** ImageNet and five distribution-shift variants (-V2, -R, -Sketch, -A, ObjectNet) for ID-OOD generalization.
- **Competitors:** Comparison is made against training-free model merging techniques (TIES, DARE) and representative ensembling approaches (Wise-FT, VRF).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results demonstrating the efficacy of MERGETUNE across various VLM adaptation benchmarks and protocols.

#### 4.1. Few-shot Base-to-Novel Generalization

On 11 diverse datasets, MERGETUNE consistently improved the harmonic mean (HM) accuracy for base-to-novel generalization. For instance, MERGETUNE increased CoOp's HM by +5.58% (from 71.66% to 77.24%) and KgCoOp's HM by +0.97% (from 77.01% to 77.98%). The magnitude of improvement exhibited an inverse correlation with the baseline's initial performance, suggesting MERGETUNE provided more substantial gains to methods that struggled more with knowledge retention. In contrast, training-free model merging methods like TIES and DARE generally degraded performance (e.g., CoOp+TIES reduced HM by 5.33%), indicating their ineffectiveness in directly interpolating between zero-shot and fine-tuned checkpoints in this context.

#### 4.2. Few-shot Cross-Dataset Generalization

When models were trained on ImageNet and evaluated on 10 other datasets without further adaptation, MERGETUNE consistently delivered performance gains. It achieved average HM improvements of +1.92% on CoOp, +1.02% on KgCoOp, +0.89% on MMA, and +0.35% on PromptKD. Notably, MMA+MERGETUNE surpassed the zero-shot CLIP model on all evaluated datasets. Training-free merging methods, TIES and DARE, typically resulted in performance degradation in this setting.

#### 4.3. Few-shot Domain Generalization

For models few-shot-tuned on ImageNet and evaluated on four ImageNet distribution variants, MERGETUNE enhanced robustness to domain shifts. It yielded positive average domain (Avg-D) gains of +0.87% for CoOp, +0.35% for KgCoOp, +0.42% for MMA, and +0.30% for PromptKD. Training-free merging methods generally led to negative performance gains, particularly on datasets with more significant shifts like ImageNet-Sketch and ImageNet-Adversarial.

#### 4.4. ID-OOD Generalization

In the robust fine-tuning evaluation on ImageNet and five distribution-shift datasets, MERGETUNE demonstrated competitive performance. It outperformed state-of-the-art ensembling methods like VRF, achieving higher OOD generalization while using a single model for inference, thereby avoiding the additional costs associated with ensemble-based approaches. When combined with weight-space ensembling (interpolating between the zero-shot and LMC-tuned checkpoint), MERGETUNE achieved the highest average OOD accuracies (60.23% for Linear Probing and 62.90% for E2E-FT). The LMC-tuned model from MERGETUNE served as a superior replacement for standard fine-tuned checkpoints in ensemble schemes. These results indicate an improved ID-OOD trade-off without complex designs, and MERGETUNE-enhanced models consistently outperformed CLIP in all evaluated cases. Training-free merging methods showed unstable performance and significant degradation under challenging shifts.

#### 4.5. Further Analysis

- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** MERGETUNE exhibited robustness to the choice of surrogate loss weight `λ` and Task2 LMC loss weight `β`. Optimal performance was observed when `λ` was in the range `` and `β` was in `[0.1, 0.5]`. The method was also robust to the initialization of the continued model, performing optimally when the initialization blended the zero-shot and fine-tuned weights in a balanced manner (τ ∈ `[0.3, 0.6]`).
- **Interpolation Number (N_α):** Performance improved consistently as the number of interpolation points `N_α` increased from 1 to 10, after which it saturated. `N_α = 5` was identified as a balanced choice considering accuracy gains and computational overhead.
- **Loss Component Contributions:** Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both loss components. Removing the surrogate loss (`λ = 0`) led to catastrophic drops in novel class accuracy, indicating severe overfitting to base classes and a loss of pretrained generalization. Removing the LMC constraint (`β = 0`) reduced the method to the baseline fine-tuning (e.g., KgCoOp), confirming the LMC term's role in knowledge integration.
- **Performance of Interpolated Models:** Analysis of interpolated models between the MERGETUNE-derived model and both the zero-shot CLIP and fine-tuned KgCoOp models revealed smooth, high-accuracy paths. This geometrically confirmed that MERGETUNE successfully established linear mode connectivity with both endpoints, effectively integrating their knowledge.
- **Robustness to Over-merging:** Extended training evaluations (10 to 100 epochs) showed that MERGETUNE did not exhibit over-merging. Performance either remained stable or continuously improved across all datasets, suggesting that the dual-anchored LMC objective effectively regularized the training process against performance degradation.
- **Generalization to Other Backbones:** Experiments with CLIP ViT-B/32, CLIP-L/14, Siglip2-B/16, and Siglip2-L/16 backbones demonstrated that MERGETUNE consistently improved performance across different model scales and architectures, indicating its scalability and model-agnostic effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces "Continued Fine-Tuning (CFT)" as a novel paradigm for Vision-Language Model (VLM) adaptation, which represents a shift in focus from mitigating forgetting *during* fine-tuning to *recovering* lost pretrained knowledge *after* initial adaptation. This approach addresses a critical challenge in VLM deployment: the trade-off between task-specific performance and the preservation of broad generalization capabilities.

The significance and potential impact of MERGETUNE are multifaceted:

1. **Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting Post-Adaptation:** MERGETUNE provides a mechanism to restore valuable pretrained knowledge that is often forgotten during standard fine-tuning processes. This post hoc knowledge recovery complements existing fine-tuning strategies, offering a way to enhance models that have already undergone adaptation.
2. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The method consistently demonstrates improvements across diverse generalization benchmarks, including base-to-novel, cross-dataset, and domain generalization, as well as ID-OOD robustness. This suggests that MERGETUNE can lead to more robust and broadly applicable VLMs, which are less susceptible to performance drops on novel tasks or out-of-distribution data.
3. **Model-Agnostic and Post Hoc Enhancement:** MERGETUNE's design allows it to be applied to a variety of existing fine-tuned VLMs (e.g., prompt-based, adapter-based, linear probing, end-to-end fine-tuning) without requiring architectural modifications. This flexibility makes it a general enhancement strategy that can be seamlessly integrated into current and future VLM adaptation workflows.
4. **Efficient Knowledge Integration via Linear Mode Connectivity:** By leveraging Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) as a learning objective, MERGETUNE offers a geometrically guided approach to integrating knowledge from distinct model states (zero-shot and fine-tuned). The introduction of a second-order surrogate loss for the pretraining task enables this integration without computationally prohibitive data replay, making the method practical.
5. **Competitive Performance with Reduced Inference Cost:** In robust fine-tuning evaluations, MERGETUNE not only surpasses the performance of several state-of-the-art ensemble methods but does so with a single model for inference. This eliminates the computational overhead and complexity associated with multi-model ensembles, making the enhanced models more efficient for real-world applications.
6. **Establishing a Promising Research Direction:** The success of continued fine-tuning, guided by mode connectivity, establishes a new avenue for advancing VLM adaptation research. Future work could explore more sophisticated LMC approximations, alternative geometric merging strategies, or the application of this paradigm to other types of foundational models.

In summary, MERGETUNE offers a principled and effective method for recovering forgotten knowledge in fine-tuned VLMs, leading to enhanced generalization and robustness. Its model-agnostic and replay-free nature, coupled with its performance advantages, positions it as a promising advancement in the field of VLM adaptation.
