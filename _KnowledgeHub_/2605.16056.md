# Health-Conditioned Vision-Language-Action Models for Malfunction-Aware Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Hacettepe University developed a health-conditioned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that adapts robot control to physical joint degradations. The model integrates a lightweight "Health Projector" module into a pretrained VLA architecture, enabling significant performance recovery on critically degraded joints in LIBERO Spatial tasks with minimal additional parameters.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models assume ideal robot hardware, leading to task failures when robots experience physical degradations like sensor drift or actuator malfunctions.
- Current VLA fault tolerance methods do not directly address adaptation to physical degradations of robot joints, focusing instead on semantic task failures or implicit adaptation.
- Without mechanisms to account for physical health, VLA models lack the robustness required for reliable deployment in real-world, unpredictable environments.

## Method

- A **malfunction-aware VLA** model was developed by integrating a ==7D health vector==, which quantifies the operational status of each robot joint, into a pretrained ==VLA-Adapter Pro== architecture.
- A lightweight **Health Projector** module (a two-layer MLP with ~900K parameters) was introduced to project the health vector into the VLA's latent space, added element-wise to ==proprioceptive tokens==.
- A data collection framework was established in ==LIBERO simulation==, allowing teleoperation of robots with varying levels of joint degradation to gather expert demonstrations for fine-tuning the model.

## Results

- The health-conditioned model achieved a **99.0%** healthy success rate and demonstrated considerable performance recovery on critically degraded joints; for example, the J1 (shoulder) success rate improved from **45% to 89%** at a 0.3 weakness level.
- The model exhibited adaptive capabilities, achieving non-zero success rates (e.g., J1 at 0.7 weakness improved from **0% to 2%**) even in scenarios where baseline models completely failed due to severe joint limitations.
- These improvements were realized with a lightweight addition of approximately **900K trainable parameters**, fine-tuned on a compact dataset of **178 episodes**, underscoring its parameter and data efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly conditioning VLA models with a health vector allows them to adapt action predictions to physical joint degradations, facilitating compensatory strategies for task completion.
> - Degradation in kinematically critical joints (e.g., shoulder, elbow) disproportionately affects VLA performance, highlighting the need for targeted adaptation mechanisms.
> - A parameter-efficient approach, utilizing a zero-initialized Health Projector, can preserve nominal performance while providing substantial recovery for degraded states with a relatively small training dataset.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models has experienced significant growth, positioning them as a promising avenue for generalist robot control. These models integrate Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with end-to-end action prediction architectures, enabling robots to interpret visual and linguistic inputs to perform tasks. Notable developments in this area include models such as π0, Project GR00T, and VLA-Adapter, which have demonstrated high accuracy across various simulated and real-world tasks, including operation in cross-embodiment setups and diverse scenarios. Furthermore, advancements have been made to adapt VLAs for deployment on edge devices with limited computational resources.

Despite these advancements, a fundamental assumption within current VLA approaches is that the robot hardware or simulation operates under perfect physical conditions. In practical applications, however, robots are frequently subject to various physical degradations, such as sensor drift, partial actuator failures, or loss of motor torque. Existing VLA models, typically trained on data from healthy robot embodiments, lack the intrinsic mechanisms to adapt to such compromised physical states. This limitation leads to task failures when robots encounter real-world physical deficiencies.

While some VLA research has addressed fault tolerance by focusing on detecting, preventing, and recovering from task failures at a semantic level, or through implicit methods like domain randomization, these efforts do not directly tackle adaptation to physical degradations of the robot's joints or other kinematic components. This paper identifies this as a gap, noting that unlike classical control systems that might explicitly model robot dynamics and failures, VLAs rely on learned representations from ideal conditions. The presented work aims to bridge this gap by introducing a mechanism for VLA models to explicitly account for and adapt to physical joint degradation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the disparity between the idealized conditions assumed by current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and the physical realities of robot operation. Existing VLA models are predominantly trained on demonstrations from robots in perfect working order. Consequently, when a robot experiences common physical degradations—such as joint wear, actuator malfunction, or reduced gripper strength—these models are unable to adapt their control strategies, leading to an inevitable decline in task performance or complete task failure. This constitutes a critical limitation for the deployment of generalist robots in unpredictable, real-world environments. The paper posits that without a mechanism to account for and react to such physical health conditions, the robustness and applicability of VLA models remain constrained.

To address this fundamental gap, the research establishes the following key objectives:

- **Develop Malfunction-Aware VLA:** The overarching objective is to introduce a "malfunction-aware" or "health-conditioned" VLA model capable of adapting its action predictions when faced with degraded robot joints. This involves incorporating explicit information about the robot's physical health into the VLA architecture.
- **Integrate Health Vector with Parameter Efficiency:** Specifically, the goal is to integrate a "health vector," which quantifies the operational status of each robot joint, into a pretrained VLA model. This integration must be lightweight, achieved with a minimal number of additional parameters to ensure computational efficiency and facilitate fine-tuning with limited resources. The paper defines this integration as the "Health Projector" module and aims to formally formulate the malfunction-aware VLA problem.
- **Establish a Data Collection Framework for Degraded States:** To enable the training and evaluation of such a model, a practical framework for collecting teleoperated data in a simulated environment (LIBERO) is required. This framework must allow for the simulation of varying levels of joint degradation and the recording of corresponding expert demonstrations under these compromised conditions.
- **Define Systematic Evaluation Metrics:** The research also aims to introduce systematic evaluation metrics to quantitatively assess the performance of the health-conditioned VLA model. These metrics are designed to measure the model's capability to succeed in tasks under different configurations and levels of joint degradation, particularly within the LIBERO Spatial task suite.

By achieving these objectives, the researchers aim to enhance the resilience and adaptability of VLA-controlled robots, moving closer to systems that can operate reliably despite real-world physical wear and tear.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a health-conditioned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed to adapt robot control to physical joint degradations. This is achieved by extending an existing VLA architecture with a specialized module that processes robot health information.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Formulation

The system considers a 7-Degrees of Freedom (DOF) manipulator, where each joint $j$ can experience degradation. The health status of each joint is represented by a scalar value $h_j \in$, where $h_j = 1$ signifies full health and $h_j = 0$ indicates a completely locked joint. Intermediate values reduce both the actuator gain and the reachable angle range of the joint by a factor of $(1 - w)$, where $w = 1 - h_j$. The overall physical state of the robot is characterized by a 7D health vector $h = [h_0, h_1, \ldots, h_6]$.

A standard VLA policy $\pi_\theta$ typically maps visual observations $o_t$ and a language instruction $l$ to a chunk of $C$ future actions, $\hat{a}_{t:t+C} = \pi_\theta(o_t, l)$. However, policies trained on data from healthy robots cannot effectively adapt to degraded joints. The proposed approach introduces a health-conditioned policy: $\hat{a}_{t:t+C} = \pi_\theta(o_t, l, h)$. In this formulation, the health vector $h$ is projected into the model's latent space using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and integrated with the action prediction head, enabling the model to adjust its motion plan according to the robot's current joint capabilities.

#### 3.2. B. Architecture

The core of the proposed architecture utilizes the VLA-Adapter Pro model as a base. This base model comprises a frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B language model for language processing and a fused DINOv2–SigLIP vision encoder for visual processing. The action prediction is handled by an action head trained with L1 regression.

To incorporate health information, the researchers add a lightweight "Health Projector" module and modify the action head's attention mechanism.

- **Base VLA-Adapter Pro:** The action head is structured as a 24-block residual MLP network. Each block includes an 8-head attention layer that processes three types of tokens: self tokens (representing the evolving action latent state), adapter tokens (learnable action queries concatenated with proprioceptive features), and task tokens (vision–language features derived from the VLM backbone). Key–value projections are distinct for each source, employing rotary position embeddings (RoPE), and a learned gating mechanism controls the influence of task attention. The head predicts a chunk of $C=8$ future 7-DOF end-effector delta actions.

- **Health Projector:** This module consists of a two-layer MLP designed to map the 7D health vector $h$ into the backbone LLM embedding space ($\mathbb{R}^{896}$). The transformation is given by: $f_h = W_2 \text{GELU}(W_1 h + b_1) + b_2$. Crucially, the final layer parameters ($W_2, b_2$) are zero-initialized. This initialization strategy ensures that $f_h = 0$ at the commencement of training, thus preventing any initial disruption to the pretrained action head. The generated health features ($f_h$) are then added element-wise to the proprioceptive token before it enters the attention blocks of the action head. This additive design allows the adapter tokens to carry both proprioceptive and health information without altering the total token count, preserving the pretrained attention pattern at initialization.

- **Parameter Budget:** The Health Projector contributes approximately 810K parameters, while the Action Queries add 57K parameters. In total, the trainable parameters sum to around 900K, representing a lightweight addition that facilitates fine-tuning with limited computational resources and does not substantially increase inference overhead.

#### 3.3. C. Data Collection

A specific dataset was created for LIBERO Spatial tasks, leveraging the pretrained weights of the VLA-Adapter. These tasks involve a 7-DOF manipulator performing pick-and-place operations (e.g., moving a black bowl).

- **Malfunction Simulation:** Joint malfunctions were simulated by constraining the actuator gain and the operational angle range of specific joints, scaled by a factor of $(1-w)$, where $w$ denotes the weakness level (ranging from 0 for healthy to 1 for fully locked).
- **Teleoperation Interface:** An interface was developed to teleoperate the robot in the LIBERO simulation. This allowed control of the robot body via mouse (Open Space Control, OSC) and the wrist, hand, and gripper via keyboard inputs.
- **Dataset Composition:** Teleoperated data was collected under conditions where the baseline VLA-Adapter model typically failed due to joint degradation. Malfunction levels (weakness $w$) were set at 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, and 0.9. For each level configuration, 2 episodes were collected. To maintain data consistency and improve robustness, the dataset was augmented with episodes from the VLA-Adapter's non-degraded successful runs (full healthy vector) and its successful runs under degradation.
- **Recorded Data:** Each episode captured dual-camera RGB observations (256x256 pixels), 7D end-effector delta actions, an 8D proprioceptive state (comprising end-effector pose and gripper state), and the 7D health vector.
- **Total Data:** The dataset comprised 128 malfunction episodes across various joint-malfunction configurations and 50 healthy episodes collected using the pretrained VLA-Adapter Libero Spatial Pro model.

#### 3.4. D. Training Setup

The health-conditioned VLA architecture was fine-tuned using a single NVIDIA L40S GPU.

- **Frozen Components:** The VLM backbone, vision encoder, and Bridge Attention Adapter from the base VLA-Adapter-Pro were kept frozen during training.
- **Trainable Components:** Only the Health Projector and Action Queries were trainable, totaling approximately 900K parameters.
- **Optimizer and Learning Rate:** The AdamW optimizer was employed with an initial learning rate of 2e-4.
- **Training Schedule:** Training proceeded for 15K steps, utilizing a batch size of 8 and a gradient accumulator over 2 steps. Gradient clipping was applied at a norm of 1.0. The learning rate underwent a linear warm-up phase over 500 steps and subsequently decayed by a factor of 10x at 10K steps.
- **Action Normalization and Loss:** Actions were normalized to the range [-1, 1] using BOUNDS Q99 statistics to match the pretrained action head's output space, adhering to the RLDS convention. L1 regression was used as the loss function for predicting the 8-action chunk.
- **Data Augmentation:** Various image augmentations were applied to enhance data robustness across different tasks and objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments primarily focused on evaluating the performance of the health-conditioned VLA model against the baseline VLA-Adapter Pro on LIBERO Spatial tasks under various joint degradation scenarios.

#### 4.1. A. Evaluation Protocol

- **Tasks:** All experiments were conducted on LIBERO Spatial tasks, which involve picking up a black bowl from different locations and placing it on a plate, primarily assessing spatial understanding and manipulation.
- **Baselines:** The pretrained VLA-Adapter without the Health Projector was run for baseline comparison. Each unique combination of task, joint, and malfunction level (7 joints × 4 weakness levels: 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9) was evaluated with 4 episodes. This included a completely healthy setup.
- **Proposed Model Evaluation:** The proposed health-conditioned model was evaluated with 10 episodes for each task, joint, and condition pair.
- **Metric:** The primary performance metric was the binary task success rate.

#### 4.2. B. Baseline Vulnerability Analysis

Table I demonstrates the performance of the pretrained VLA-Adapter LIBERO-Spatial-Pro model when exposed to single-joint degradation without health conditioning.

- **Nominal Performance:** The baseline achieved a high healthy success rate of 97.5%, indicating strong performance under ideal conditions.
- **Vulnerability to Degradation:** Joint degradation significantly impacted performance, particularly for specific joints:
 - **J1 (Shoulder):** Success rate dropped sharply from 97.5% (healthy) to 45% at a weakness level ($w$) of 0.3, and further to 0% for $w \geq 0.7$.
 - **J3 (Elbow):** Exhibited similar vulnerability, falling to 42.5% at $w=0.3$ and to 0% for $w \geq 0.5$.
 - **Kinematic Load:** The paper notes that J1 and J3 are proximal joints bearing substantial kinematic load for pick-and-place motions. Even minor restrictions on these joints led to trajectory requirements that the pretrained policy, unaware of the degradation, could not compensate for.
- **Resilient Joints:** Some joints demonstrated greater resilience:
 - **J4 (Forearm):** Maintained a 97.5% success rate across all weakness levels.
 - **J5 (Wrist) and J6 (Hand):** Remained above 65% even at $w=0.9$.
 - **J0 (Base) and J2 (Upperarm):** Were largely unaffected, with average success rates of 99.4% and 88.1% respectively.
- **Conclusion:** This analysis highlighted a significant vulnerability of standard VLA models to physical degradation, underscoring the necessity for a mechanism to adapt to compromised kinematics.

#### 4.3. C. Health-Conditioned Results

Table II presents a comparison between the baseline and the health-conditioned model across various joints and weakness levels.

- **Healthy Performance:** The health-conditioned model achieved a 99.0% healthy success rate, comparable to the baseline's 97.5%. This confirms that the zero-initialized Health Projector successfully preserved nominal performance when the health vector indicated full health ($h=1$).
- **Improvements on Critical Joints:** The health-conditioned model showed considerable improvements on the joints where the baseline performance collapsed:
 - **J1 (Shoulder):** Success rate increased from 45% to 89% at $w=0.3$, and from 15% to 22% at $w=0.5$. Notably, it achieved a non-zero 2% success rate at $w=0.7$, where the baseline scored 0%.
 - **J3 (Elbow):** Improved from 0% to 34% at $w=0.5$ and gained 3% at $w=0.7$.
- **Performance on Other Joints:** For joints that were already performing relatively well, the proposed model generally maintained competitive performance or showed further improvements:
 - **J2 (Upperarm):** Improved from 72.5% to 83% at $w=0.9$.
 - **J5 (Wrist):** Showed improvements across high-weakness levels, from 65% to 82% at $w=0.7$ and from 67.5% to 79% at $w=0.9$.
 - **J6 (Hand):** Experienced a notable increase from 72.5% to 82% at $w=0.9$.
- **Trade-offs:** A slight reduction in performance was observed on joints where the baseline already performed exceptionally well (J0 and J4). J0 dropped from near-perfect to 71–91%, and J4 from 97.5% to 89–97%. This is attributed to the limited coverage of the custom-collected dataset, which predominantly focused on scenarios where the VLA-Adapter failed, potentially leading to inconsistencies due to a lack of pretrained healthy episodes for these specific joints.

#### 4.4. D. Per-Task Breakdown (Table III)

Table III provides a detailed per-task success rate breakdown, illustrating where the performance gains were concentrated.

- **Compensatory Strategies:** On tasks where the baseline model completely failed, the health-conditioned model demonstrated the ability to learn compensatory strategies. Examples include Task 5, Joint 1, $w=0.3$ (0% baseline to 30% ours), and Task 8, Joint 3, $w=0.5$ (0% baseline to 80% ours). This suggests the model adapted to task-specific alternative trajectories to circumvent the degraded joint's limitations.
- **Physical Limits:** Some task–joint combinations remained at 0% success for both models (e.g., J1 and J3 at $w=0.9$), indicating physical limits beyond which even adaptive action planning cannot overcome due to drastic shrinkage of the reachable workspace.

#### 4.5. E. Analysis

- **Kinematic Criticality:** The results confirmed that degradation in joints critical for main reaching and lifting motions (like J1 and J3) poses the greatest challenge. High weakness levels for these joints can make tasks impossible by severely limiting the robot's reachable workspace.
- **Adaptive Strategies:** The health conditioning mechanism enabled the VLA model to discover and execute adaptive strategies, such as alternative trajectories, to complete tasks despite joint limitations.
- **Cost of Generalization and Data Efficiency:** The research noted that the model's dataset, collected through teleoperation and focused on failure cases of the baseline, was limited in its coverage of all possible malfunction-level and joint combinations. This limitation potentially contributed to the observed slight performance dips on less critical joints. However, the use of only 178 episodes (128 malfunction + 50 healthy) to achieve meaningful recovery on severely degraded joints highlights the parameter and data efficiency of the explicit health conditioning approach via the Health Projector.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel approach to enhance the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by explicitly conditioning them on the physical health status of a robot. The findings demonstrate that VLA models can be made aware of physical degradations through a lightweight and parameter-efficient mechanism, which represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic control.

The **significance** of this work lies in several key aspects:

- **Addressing a Critical Gap:** The study directly addresses a fundamental limitation in current VLA research, which often assumes ideal robot hardware conditions. By enabling VLA models to adapt to physical degradations, the research extends their applicability from controlled environments to more realistic and challenging scenarios where mechanical wear and tear are common.
- **Parameter-Efficient Adaptation:** The introduction of a "Health Projector" module, which adds less than 1 million trainable parameters to a pretrained VLA model, demonstrates that robust adaptation to physical malfunctions can be achieved without necessitating substantial architectural changes or computational overhead. The zero-initialization strategy further ensures that the model's nominal performance is preserved.
- **Empirical Validation of Health Conditioning:** The results empirically validate that explicit health conditioning allows the pretrained VLA-Adapter to adapt its action predictions effectively, leading to significant performance recovery on critically degraded joints where the baseline model entirely fails. This shows that informing the model about its physical state can unlock adaptive compensatory strategies.
- **Data Efficiency:** The ability to achieve meaningful recovery using a relatively small dataset of 178 episodes (128 malfunction + 50 healthy) suggests that the explicit health conditioning mechanism is data-efficient, which is beneficial for practical deployment where extensive data collection for every possible degradation scenario may be infeasible.

The **potential impact** of this research is substantial for the development of more resilient and reliable robotic systems:

- **Increased Robot Robustness:** Robots equipped with health-conditioned VLA models could operate more reliably in real-world environments, where unforeseen physical degradations are inevitable. This could lead to fewer task failures and reduce the need for constant human intervention or costly repairs.
- **Broader Applicability of Generalist Robots:** By enabling adaptation to physical malfunctions, this work pushes the boundaries for generalist VLA models, allowing them to perform complex tasks not just under ideal circumstances but also when facing operational challenges.
- **Foundation for Fault-Tolerant Manipulation:** This research lays a foundation for future advancements in fault-tolerant manipulation and control for learning-based robot systems. It proposes a clear pathway for integrating physical health awareness into high-level cognitive models.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future investigations, including the development of methods for handling multi-joint degradations, exploring implicit health estimation (where the robot learns its own health state without explicit input, leading to zero-shot adaptation), and scaling the methodology to diverse and real-world robot embodiments beyond simulation. These directions could further enhance the autonomy and adaptability of robots.
