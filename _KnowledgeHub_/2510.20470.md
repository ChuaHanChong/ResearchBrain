# Conan: Progressive Learning to Reason Like a Detective over Multi-Scale Visual Evidence

> [!summary] Summary
> The Conan framework equips multimodal large language models with detective-like abilities for multi-step video reasoning by enabling explicit evidence localization, logical deduction over cross-frame clues, and adaptive action decisions. It achieves state-of-the-art results, surpassing closed-source MLLMs like GPT-4o on multiple benchmarks and demonstrating an average accuracy improvement of over 10% on multi-step reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle with multi-step video reasoning, often producing ungrounded textual reasoning chains not explicitly tied to visual evidence.
- Existing approaches for video reasoning frequently suffer from imprecise visual evidence localization or implicit retrieval, hindering reliable deduction from complex video content.
- A key challenge is the absence of large-scale datasets that explicitly capture evidence localization, multi-step deduction, and adaptive action decisions within an interleaved video-text format.

## Method

- Constructed **Conan**-91K, a large-scale dataset with automatically generated, interleaved video-text reasoning traces that explicitly label evidence, context, and distractor frames.
- Implemented a two-phase training curriculum: a multi-stage progressive cold-start (SFT) to incrementally build reasoning skills from textual to vision-centric, followed by a joint Identification-Reasoning-Action (AIR) RLVR framework.
- The AIR RLVR leverages specific reward functions for format, outcome, evidence identification, and retrieval to optimize the model's ability to seek, deduce, and act based on visual evidence.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across six multi-step reasoning benchmarks, improving average accuracy by over **10%** compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**-Instruct.
- Outperformed advanced closed-source MLLMs, including GPT-4o, on several multi-step reasoning benchmarks such as Video-Holmes, VRBench, Human-P&C, and LongVideoReason.
- Demonstrated robust generalization with an average improvement of **6.9%** on four long video understanding benchmarks and proved to be a superior evidence retriever.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding multi-step reasoning in multi-scale visual evidence, distinguishing context from direct evidence, is more effective than ungrounded textual reasoning or implicit frame retrieval.
> - A progressive training curriculum, advancing from textual reasoning to multimodal alignment and then vision-centric deduction, is essential for incrementally building robust multi-hop visual reasoning skills.
> - Automated generation of large-scale, evidence-localized reasoning traces, coupled with difficulty-aware sampling, can create high-quality datasets vital for training MLLMs on complex visual deduction tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Conan" addresses a critical and evolving challenge within artificial intelligence: **video reasoning**. While multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have made remarkable progress in standard video understanding tasks such as question answering, temporal grounding, and captioning, truly complex video reasoning remains a significant hurdle. Unlike perceptual tasks, reasoning requires multi-step deduction, accumulation of visual information across temporal spans, and logical inference to arrive at grounded conclusions.

Historically, approaches to enhance reasoning in MLLMs have largely fallen into two categories:
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based methods (e.g., Video-R1, VideoChat-R1):** These methods have shown promise in incentivizing step-by-step reasoning. However, they often rely on purely textual chain-of-thought (CoT) processes. This lack of explicit grounding in visual evidence can lead to "hallucinated" or unverified reasoning chains that are not genuinely reflective of the video content. The model might generate plausible text but fail to connect it accurately to the visual events.
- **Frame-retrieval approaches (e.g., Video-MTR, FrameThinker, Rewatch-R1):** These methods attempt to integrate visual evidence by dynamically retrieving relevant frames or clips during the reasoning process, boosting performance on long video understanding. While introducing visual grounding, they frequently suffer from imprecise evidence localization or implicit retrieval mechanisms. This means the model might retrieve frames, but not necessarily the *most relevant* ones, or struggle to pinpoint *why* a particular frame is crucial, leading to unreliable reasoning paths. Furthermore, some of these methods may partially rely on benchmark-specific training data, making it difficult to ascertain generalizable reasoning improvements versus overfitting to particular datasets.

"Conan" positions itself as a direct solution to these identified limitations. Inspired by the meticulous deduction of a detective, the framework aims to combine the strengths of both paradigms while mitigating their weaknesses. It seeks to provide **evidence-grounded multi-step video reasoning** by introducing **explicit evidence localization** (identifying context and evidence frames), fostering **logical deduction over cross-frame clues**, and enabling **adaptive action decisions** (when to conclude or explore further). By explicitly grounding the reasoning process in multi-scale visual evidence and employing a sophisticated training curriculum, Conan aims to achieve more reliable, verifiable, and accurate video reasoning, pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve in complex dynamic visual environments. This work is a significant step towards developing MLLMs that not only "see" and "understand" but also "reason" about video content in a human-like, investigative manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind Conan is to address the prevalent limitations of existing MLLMs in performing **multi-step, evidence-grounded video reasoning**. The authors highlight that current models often generate ungrounded textual reasoning or struggle with accurate visual evidence localization, preventing truly robust video comprehension.

To overcome these challenges, the key objectives of the Conan framework are twofold:

1. **To equip MLLMs with the ability to reason like a detective:** This involves enabling models to actively identify relevant visual evidence at multiple scales (context and evidence frames), logically deduce conclusions from cross-frame clues, and adaptively decide whether enough evidence has been gathered to answer a question or if further exploration of the video is necessary. This "seek, deduce, and act" paradigm is central to the framework's design.

2. **To tackle two core technical challenges impeding this goal:**
 - **Challenge 1: Dataset Construction:** How to automatically create a high-quality, large-scale dataset that explicitly captures evidence localization, multi-step deductive reasoning, and confident action decisions within an interleaved video-text format. This is crucial for training models to perform complex reasoning steps that are grounded in visual facts.
 - **Challenge 2: Training Curriculum Design:** How to devise an effective training curriculum that allows MLLMs to progressively acquire visual reasoning skills across multi-scale evidence. This requires a strategy to incrementally build reasoning capabilities, starting from simpler tasks and advancing to more complex, vision-centric deductions.

By achieving these objectives, Conan aims to move beyond superficial video understanding to enable MLLMs to perform deep, verifiable, and adaptive reasoning over dynamic visual information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Conan framework is meticulously designed with a two-pronged approach: constructing a novel dataset and implementing a multi-phase training curriculum.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction: Conan-91K

The authors address the first challenge by introducing **Conan-91K**, a large-scale dataset specifically tailored for Conan-style evidence reasoning. This dataset comprises Conan-CoT-60k (for SFT) and Conan-RLVR-31k (for RL).

- **Data Collection & Processing:** The base data is sourced from GenS-Video-150K, which provides dense frame descriptions, QA pairs, and crucial frame-level relevance scores. These scores are leveraged to categorize video frames into three types:
 - **Evidence frames:** Directly relevant to answering the question.
 - **Context frames:** Offer auxiliary hints that support reasoning.
 - **Distractor frames:** Irrelevant to the question.
 This multi-scale categorization forms the bedrock for explicit evidence localization.

- **Reasoning Trace Construction:** An automated pipeline, guided by the advanced LLM Kimi K2, is employed to generate interleaved video-text reasoning traces. The process operates in a loop:
 1. **Initial Sampling:** 16 frames are uniformly sampled, retaining their type labels.
 2. **Action Decision Loop:**
 - If all sampled frames are distractors, the action **Random Frames Sampling** is chosen, sampling 8 new frames to continue exploration.
 - If some evidence/context frames are present but insufficient to confidently answer (based on a dynamic answering threshold determined by K2), the action **Specific Frames Retrieval** is chosen. This retrieves 8 frames uniformly within relevant clips to expand evidence.
 - If the proportion of evidence frames exceeds the threshold, the loop terminates with the action **Confident Question Answering**.
 3. **K2 Prompting:** In each loop iteration, K2 is prompted with the chosen action, frame types, QA pair, dense frame descriptions, and timestamps. It then generates a coherent textual reasoning trace, performing:
 - **Frame Identification:** Explicitly distinguishing frame types.
 - **Evidence Reasoning:** Textual analysis over the question and accumulated visual clues.
 - **Action Decision:** Justifying the chosen action (e.g., retrieving more frames or answering).

- **Evidence Difficulty-Aware Sampling:** To facilitate progressive training, an **Evidence Difficulty Index (EDI)** is introduced. EDI quantifies reasoning complexity based on the proportion (P) and temporal dispersion (Var) of evidence frames (EDI = (1 − P) * Var). Higher EDI values indicate greater difficulty.
 - **Conan-CoT-60k (SFT):** Focuses on lower-EDI samples with up to three reasoning rounds (25k one-round, 25k two-round, 10k three-round) to build foundational reasoning.
 - **Conan-RLVR-31k (RL):** Employs higher-EDI samples without round constraints, for refining multi-hop reasoning.

#### 3.2. Two-Phase Training Curriculum

Building upon the Conan-91K dataset, a two-phase training curriculum is designed to progressively cultivate multi-step visual reasoning.

- **Phase 1: Multi-Stage Progressive Cold-Start (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT)**
 This phase incrementally activates the model's reasoning abilities using Conan-CoT-60k, starting from simpler reasoning patterns and progressing to vision-centric deduction.
 1. **Textual Reasoning Stage:** Trains on 10k low-EDI, one-round samples where frames are represented by textual descriptions and timestamps. This establishes a structured reasoning foundation (temporal and causal inference).
 2. **Multimodal Alignment Reasoning Stage:** Expands to 25k one-round and 10k low-EDI two-round samples. Timestamps and textual descriptions are inserted *before* visual frames, promoting stable adaptation from textual to multimodal reasoning and enabling initial evidence collection.
 3. **Vision-Centric Reasoning Stage:** Uses the complete Conan-CoT-60k (including additional two- and three-round samples). The model performs deep multi-step reasoning directly over visual frames with interleaved timestamps, enhancing perceptual grounding.

- **Phase 2: Joint Identification-Reasoning-Action (AIR) RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards)**
 This phase refines the SFT-trained model (Conan-SFT) to optimize its Conan-style reasoning using Conan-RLVR-31k. AIR RLVR focuses on optimizing the exploration of effective reasoning trajectories through a set of carefully designed reward functions:
 1. **Format Reward ($R_{fmt}$):** Ensures structural consistency (e.g., correct tag usage, single action per step).
 2. **Outcome Rewards ($R_o$):**
 - **Multi-choice Reward ($R_{mc}$):** Binary (1 or 0) for exact match with ground truth.
 - **Free-form Reward ($R_{free}$):** Average of ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L scores.
 3. **Identification Reward ($R_{ide}$):** Measures the average accuracy of identified evidence/context frames across reasoning rounds.
 4. **Retrieval Reward ($R_{ret}$):** Evaluates the average ratio of evidence/context frames among all retrieved frames, promoting efficient retrieval.
 5. **Joint Reward ($R_J$):** Combines these rewards: $R_J = R_{fmt} + R_o + R_{ide} + R_{ret}$ (if $R_o > 0$), else $R_J = R_{fmt} + R_o$. This encourages structurally valid, evidence-grounded, and accurate reasoning.
 The training employs the **GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization)** algorithm to stabilize training and refine the reasoning policy based on these rewards.

By integrating these components, Conan progressively learns to "seek, deduce, and act" across visual clues, leading to more reliable and accurate multi-step video reasoning. The base model used is Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on six multi-step reasoning benchmarks and four long video understanding benchmarks demonstrate the superior performance and robustness of Conan.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance in Multi-step Reasoning:**
 - Conan significantly surpasses its base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, achieving an **average accuracy improvement of over 10%** across the six multi-step reasoning benchmarks (MMR-V, Video-Holmes, VRBench, VCRBench, Human-P&C, LongVideoReason).
 - Remarkably, Conan **outperforms advanced closed-source MLLMs like GPT-4o** on most of these benchmarks (Video-Holmes, VRBench, Human-P&C, and LongVideoReason), indicating its cutting-edge capabilities in evidence-grounded reasoning.
 - It consistently **outperforms both text-CoT models** (e.g., Video-R1, VideoChat-R1), highlighting the crucial advantage of visual evidence identification and retrieval over pure textual reasoning.
 - Conan also **surpasses concurrent video-CoT approaches** (e.g., Video-MTR, Rewatch-R1), which themselves incorporate frame retrieval. This suggests that Conan's mechanisms for more precise evidence localization and effective frame retrieval are superior.

- **Strong Generalization to Long Video Understanding:**
 - Conan demonstrates robust scalability, showing an **average improvement of 6.9%** on four long video understanding benchmarks (LongVideoBench, MLVU, LVBench, Video-MME). This validates its ability to process and reason over extended video contexts effectively.

- **Superior Evidence Retrieval Capability:**
 - When evaluated specifically as an evidence retriever (selecting key frames for a base QA model), Conan yields the highest performance. This confirms its **superior ability to identify informative visual evidence**, which is a core component of its "detective-like" reasoning.

- **Comprehensive Ablation Study Validation:**
 - **Dataset Design:** The studies confirm that **multi-scale evidence categorization** (distinguishing evidence, context, and distractor frames) and **evidence difficulty-aware sampling** are critical for providing richer contextual cues and facilitating progressive skill acquisition, respectively.
 - **Progressive Cold-Start:** All stages of the progressive cold-start strategy (textual reasoning, multimodal alignment reasoning, and vision-centric reasoning) are shown to be **crucial for gradually activating and solidifying the model's multi-hop reasoning capabilities**. Bypassing any stage or the entire cold-start process leads to significant performance drops.
 - **AIR RLVR Framework:** Both the **identification reward** and **retrieval reward** within the AIR RLVR framework prove effective in refining evidence localization accuracy and frame retrieval efficiency, respectively. The results also validate that the **Conan-style reasoning paradigm with visual grounding is superior to a pure text Chain-of-Thought approach**.

- **Qualitative Evaluation Insights:**
 - Qualitative examples showcase Conan's ability to perform **multi-step reasoning with accurate evidence grounding and efficient frame retrieval**. It identifies causal cues, retrieves relevant frames, and integrates visual evidence to infer correct answers, contrasting with baselines that exhibit hallucinated reasoning or inaccurate evidence localization.
 - Error analysis reveals current limitations in **distinguishing plausible narrative causality from repetitive visual patterns** and in **capturing fine-grained emotional and social dynamics**, suggesting areas for future improvement.

In summary, Conan not only pushes the boundaries of multi-step video reasoning but also offers a scalable and robust framework for developing more intelligent and interpretable MLLMs capable of complex visual deduction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Conan" framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and video understanding, with considerable significance and potential impact:

- **Pioneering Evidence-Grounded Multi-Step Video Reasoning:** Conan directly addresses the critical limitations of existing MLLMs that either rely on ungrounded textual reasoning or struggle with precise visual evidence localization. By explicitly grounding multi-step reasoning in multi-scale visual evidence, Conan moves MLLMs closer to human-like deductive capabilities, fostering more reliable and verifiable conclusions. This shifts the paradigm from merely describing video content to truly reasoning about it.

- **Novel Dataset for Complex Reasoning:** The introduction of **Conan-91K** (Conan-CoT-60k and Conan-RLVR-31k) is a major contribution. This large-scale dataset, with its automatically generated reasoning traces that include frame identification, evidence reasoning, and action decisions, provides an invaluable resource for the research community. It sets a new standard for training MLLMs on complex, interactive reasoning tasks that demand explicit visual grounding, likely inspiring future dataset creation efforts.

- **Robust and Progressive Training Framework:** The proposed **multi-stage progressive cold-start strategy** and the **joint Identification-Reasoning-Action (AIR) RLVR framework** offer a highly effective methodology for cultivating sophisticated reasoning skills. This curriculum-based approach, incrementally building capabilities from textual to vision-centric reasoning and then refining them with targeted rewards, provides a blueprint for training MLLMs on intricate cognitive tasks, potentially applicable beyond video reasoning.

- **Enhanced MLLM Capabilities and Generalizability:** Conan’s demonstrated superior performance across both multi-step reasoning benchmarks and long video understanding tasks highlights its strong scalability and robustness. The ability to generalize effectively to longer videos and show improvements across different model sizes (3B and 8B) and architectures underscores the broad applicability and extensibility of the framework. This suggests that the principles behind Conan can be leveraged to empower a wider range of MLLMs.

- **Potential for More Trustworthy AI Systems:** By emphasizing evidence grounding and verifiable reasoning paths, Conan contributes to the development of more transparent and trustworthy AI systems. Models that can explicitly show *why* they reached a certain conclusion, by pointing to specific visual evidence, are more interpretable and less prone to generating unsubstantiated "hallucinations." This is crucial for deploying AI in sensitive applications.

- **Impact on Real-world Applications:** The enhanced video reasoning capabilities of Conan have significant implications for various domains:
 - **Surveillance and Security:** More accurate event detection and causal analysis in long-form video footage.
 - **Content Moderation:** Better understanding of complex or nuanced harmful content in videos.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Improved situational awareness and decision-making by reasoning about dynamic environments.
 - **Education and Training:** AI assistants capable of analyzing instructional videos and explaining processes.
 - **Scientific Research:** Automated analysis of experimental video data in fields like biology or physics.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper concludes by hinting at "chain-of-frame reasoning" and dynamic frame generation, which could enable models to hallucinate or synthesize visual evidence beyond existing frames. This opens exciting avenues for more imaginative and interactive reasoning, potentially leading to AI systems that can infer unseen events or propose counterfactual scenarios, further blurring the lines between perception, reasoning, and creativity.

In conclusion, Conan pushes the frontier of MLLMs by equipping them with sophisticated, evidence-grounded deductive reasoning skills over visual information. Its contributions in dataset creation, training methodology, and empirical performance set a new benchmark and lay a robust foundation for future research in advanced video AI.
