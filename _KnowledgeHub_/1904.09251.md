# Contact-Aided Invariant Extended Kalman Filtering for Robot State Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> A Contact-Aided Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) for legged robots is presented, leveraging Lie group theory to achieve superior convergence and consistency in state estimation. The method demonstrated improved robustness on a Cassie biped, achieving low position error and enabling accurate local LiDAR mapping.

## Problem

- Legged robots require highly accurate pose and velocity estimates for stable operation, motion planning, and autonomous navigation.
- Existing vision-based state estimation methods are susceptible to environmental factors like lighting and occlusions, limiting robustness.
- Standard Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs) suffer from local stability, state-dependent linearization inaccuracies, and potentially spurious observability.

## Method

- Developed a **Contact-Aided** ==Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF)== that models the robot's state (pose, velocity, contact points) as an element of an extended matrix Lie group.
- Formulated the system dynamics and measurement models to satisfy the "group affine" property, enabling exactly log-linear error dynamics independent of the state trajectory.
- Integrated IMU bias augmentation, provided precise procedures for adding and removing contact points, and utilized exact analytical time-discretizations for practical, real-time implementation.

## Results

- Simulations and hardware experiments on a Cassie biped showed the InEKF converging significantly faster and more reliably than a state-of-the-art quaternion-based EKF, even with substantial initial errors.
- Motion capture validation on a physical robot confirmed the RIEKF's accuracy, with less than **5%** final position error over a 15m path and observable states consistently within their 3σ covariance bounds.
- The InEKF provided reliable long-term odometry over a 200m outdoor walk, enabling effective LiDAR-based local mapping and demonstrating its potential for sustained autonomous operation without external sensors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By leveraging Lie group symmetries, the InEKF achieves exactly log-linear error dynamics, resulting in linearized error propagation and observation models that are independent of the current state estimate.
> - This framework provides superior convergence properties and consistency compared to standard EKFs, particularly for orientation and pose estimation, and offers an intrinsically consistent observability analysis.
> - The InEKF models state uncertainty as a "concentrated Gaussian on a Lie group," which allows for a more geometrically accurate representation of complex, non-Gaussian uncertainty distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate state estimation, encompassing pose and velocity, is fundamental for the stable operation, motion planning, and autonomous navigation of legged robots. Existing state estimation solutions often fall into two primary categories: those relying on vision-based data and those fusing proprioceptive sensor data (kinematics, contact) with inertial measurement units (IMUs).

Vision-based methods, while powerful, are susceptible to environmental factors such as lighting conditions and visual occlusions, limiting their robustness in diverse operational settings. The alternative, widely adopted in legged robotics, involves sensor fusion techniques, prominently the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) and its variants, such as the quaternion-based EKF (QEKF). These EKF-based approaches are computationally efficient and have been successfully implemented on various legged platforms to fuse IMU measurements with leg odometry.

However, standard EKF formulations for nonlinear systems, including robot state estimation, possess inherent limitations. They typically offer only local stability, meaning their convergence is highly dependent on the accuracy of the initial state estimate. The linearization process, performed around the current state estimate, can introduce inaccuracies, and the linearized error dynamics or observation models often depend on the current state estimate, potentially leading to suboptimal performance or even divergence with poor initialization. Furthermore, traditional EKFs can spuriously treat unobservable states as observable, which necessitates additional techniques like observability-constrained EKFs.

This research addresses these limitations by leveraging the theory of Lie groups and invariant observer design to develop an Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF). The InEKF framework is a relatively recent development in nonlinear filtering, building upon the recognition that many robot state estimation problems can be framed on matrix Lie groups. It exploits system symmetries to achieve superior convergence and consistency properties compared to standard EKFs, particularly for problems involving orientation and pose, which naturally reside on Lie groups. Prior work has applied InEKF to attitude estimation, inertial navigation, and SLAM. This paper extends this advanced filtering paradigm to the specific challenges of contact-aided inertial navigation for legged robots, aiming to provide a more robust and accurate state estimator using only proprioceptive sensors, thereby reducing reliance on external sensing modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a contact-aided invariant extended Kalman filter (InEKF) for robust and accurate robot state estimation, specifically tailored for legged robots. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enhancing Legged Robot Autonomy and Control:** Legged robots require precise knowledge of their pose (position and orientation) and velocity to maintain stability, execute complex walking gaits, and navigate diverse terrains. Current state estimation solutions often fall short in robustness or convergence, hindering the development of advanced motion planning and feedback control algorithms necessary for sophisticated tasks in logistics, search and rescue, or domestic assistance.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing State Estimators:**
 - **Vulnerability of Vision-Based Systems:** While visual-inertial odometry (VIO) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) have seen significant advancements, their reliance on visual data makes them susceptible to environmental challenges such as rapid lighting changes, featureless environments, or occlusions. This work seeks to develop a robust low-level state estimator that primarily uses proprioceptive sensors (IMU, joint encoders, contact sensors), which are less affected by external environmental conditions.
 - **Inherent Flaws of Standard EKFs:** Standard extended Kalman filters (EKFs) are widely used due to their computational efficiency. However, their reliance on linearizing highly nonlinear system dynamics around the current state estimate leads to several issues:
 - **Local Stability:** EKFs are at best locally stable, meaning they may diverge if the initial state estimate is inaccurate or if the system operates far from the linearization point.
 - **State-Dependent Linearization:** The linearized error dynamics and observation models in standard EKFs often depend on the current state estimate. Inaccurate estimates can therefore propagate errors in the linearization, reducing filter accuracy and consistency.
 - **Inconsistent Observability:** Standard EKFs can sometimes numerically overstate the observability of certain states, requiring complex modifications like observability-constrained EKFs to ensure consistency with the underlying nonlinear system.

3. **Exploiting System Symmetries with Lie Group Theory:** The core motivation for employing an InEKF stems from the theoretical advantages offered by invariant observer design based on Lie groups. For systems whose states evolve on a Lie group and whose dynamics satisfy a "group affine" property, the estimation error dynamics can be shown to be "log-linear" and autonomous (independent of the system's trajectory). This log-linear property has several important consequences that motivate its application:
 - **Trajectory-Independent Convergence:** The observable state variables can achieve convergence with a domain of attraction that is independent of the system's trajectory.
 - **Estimate-Independent Linearization:** Unlike standard EKFs, neither the linearized error dynamics nor the linearized observation model depend on the current state estimate, which intrinsically leads to improved convergence properties and consistency.
 - **Consistent Observability:** The local observability matrix derived from the InEKF framework is inherently consistent with the underlying nonlinear system, avoiding the issues seen in standard EKFs.

By developing a contact-aided InEKF, the authors aim to provide a state estimator that offers superior convergence and consistency, thereby enabling more robust and reliable autonomous operation for legged robots, even under challenging conditions or with poor initializations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves developing a contact-aided Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) rooted in Lie group theory. This approach addresses the limitations of standard EKFs by exploiting symmetries inherent in the robot's dynamics and measurement models.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Background

The foundation is laid on matrix Lie groups (G) and their associated Lie algebras (g). Key concepts include:
- **Hat operator ($\cdot^{\wedge}$):** A linear map from $\mathbb{R}^{\text{dim}\mathfrak{g}}$ to $\mathfrak{g}$ (matrix representation of the Lie algebra).
- **Exponential map (exp):** Maps elements from the Lie algebra to the Lie group.
- **Invariant Error:** Defined as either right-invariant ($\eta_r = \bar{X}_t X_t^{-1}$) or left-invariant ($\eta_l = X_t^{-1} \bar{X}_t$), where $X_t$ is the true state and $\bar{X}_t$ is its estimate. This definition is crucial for separating the error dynamics from the state trajectory.
- **Group Affine Dynamics:** If the system dynamics $f_u(X_t)$ satisfy a specific "group affine" property, then the invariant error dynamics are autonomous and trajectory-independent.
- **Log-Linear Property:** Under the group affine condition, the invariant error $\eta_t$ can be exactly recovered from a time-varying linear differential equation on the Lie algebra, even if the trajectories are far apart. This property is central to the InEKF's improved performance, as it implies the linearization for error propagation is exact in the deterministic case.
- **Invariant Observations:** If measurements take a specific form (e.g., $Y_t = X_t b + V_t$ or $Y_t = X_t^{-1} b + V_t$), the linearized observation model and innovation also become autonomous.
- **Adjoint Map (Ad):** A linear map that captures the non-commutative structure of a Lie group, used for transformations between error definitions and frames.

#### 3.2. State Representation

The robot's state is modeled as an element of the matrix Lie group $SE_{N+2}(3)$, which is an extension of $SE(3)$ (Special Euclidean Group for 3D pose). For a single contact point, the state matrix $X_t$ includes:
- Orientation of the IMU in the world frame ($R_{WB}(t)$)
- Velocity of the IMU in the world frame ($^W v_B(t)$)
- Position of the IMU in the world frame ($^W p_{WB}(t)$)
- Position of the contact point in the world frame ($^W p_{WC}(t)$)

This representation allows the entire state to be treated as a single Lie group element, crucial for applying invariant filtering theory.

#### 3.3. Continuous-Time System Dynamics

- **IMU Measurements:** Angular velocity ($\tilde{\omega}_t$) and linear acceleration ($\tilde{a}_t$) from the IMU are modeled as true values corrupted by additive Gaussian white noise.
- **Contact Dynamics:** When a contact sensor indicates ground contact, the contact point's world-frame velocity is assumed to be zero, with additive Gaussian white noise to account for potential slippage.
- **Overall Dynamics:** The state dynamics are formulated in a matrix form, $d/dt X_t = f_{u_t}(X_t) - X_t w_t^{\wedge}$. It is shown that the deterministic part, $f_{u_t}(X_t)$, satisfies the "group affine" property.
- **Right-Invariant Error Dynamics:** The right-invariant error dynamics ($\eta_r$) are derived. The log-linear property ensures that the error dynamics on the Lie algebra, $d/dt \xi_t = A_t \xi_t + \bar{w}_t$, can be linearized accurately. The specific matrix $A_t$ for covariance propagation is derived, demonstrating that it is independent of the state estimate. The noise covariance matrix $\bar{Q}_t$ is also defined using the Adjoint map.

#### 3.4. Right-Invariant Forward Kinematic Measurement Model

- **Encoder Measurements:** Joint encoder measurements ($\tilde{\alpha}_t$) are modeled with additive white Gaussian noise.
- **Forward Kinematics:** Forward kinematics are used to compute the relative position of the contact point with respect to the body frame ($^B p_{BC}(\tilde{\alpha}_t)$).
- **Invariant Observation Form:** This measurement is shown to conform to the right-invariant observation form ($Y_t = X_t^{-1} b + V_t$), which allows the innovation to depend solely on the invariant error.
- **Correction Step:** The InEKF correction equations for the state estimate and covariance are derived using standard Kalman filter theory, where the gain $K_t$ and measurement matrix $H_t$ (also independent of the state estimate) are explicitly provided.

#### 3.5. IMU Bias Augmentation (Imperfect InEKF)

- **Bias Model:** Gyroscope and accelerometer biases ($b_g, b_a$) are included as additional states, modeled as slowly varying signals (Brownian motion).
- **Augmented State:** The state becomes a tuple $(X_t, \theta_t) \in G \times \mathbb{R}^6$, where $\theta_t = [b_g, b_a]^T$. The right-invariant error is similarly augmented.
- **Modified Dynamics:** The deterministic system dynamics and the linearized error dynamics matrix $A_t$ are re-derived to account for the biases. While the theoretical log-linear property no longer holds exactly with biases, the formulation is shown to be beneficial in practice.

#### 3.6. Addition and Removal of Contact Points

- **Removal (Marginalization):** Contact points are removed by linear transformation of the covariance matrix, effectively marginalizing the corresponding state variables.
- **Addition (Augmentation):** New contact points are added by initializing their mean position via forward kinematics and augmenting the covariance matrix with a derived linear map that relates the new contact position error to existing errors and measurement noise.

#### 3.7. Alternative Formulations and Discretization

- **Left-Invariant Formulation:** An alternative derivation for a left-invariant error form is presented, along with the dynamics and noise matrices. The relationship between left- and right-invariant errors via the adjoint map allows for flexible switching between formulations.
- **Robo-centric Estimator:** A "robot-centric" version is derived where states are estimated in the robot's body frame. It is shown that the left/right-invariant error dynamics are effectively swapped compared to the world-centric version.
- **Discretization:** Analytical integration methods are used to discretize the continuous-time dynamics for practical implementation, assuming a zero-order hold on IMU measurements. This is critical for preserving the log-linear error dynamics property in discrete time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical derivations and empirical evaluations, highlighting the advantages of the proposed Contact-Aided Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF).

#### 4.1. Theoretical Contributions

- **Invariant EKF Derivation:** A continuous-time right-invariant EKF is derived for a system incorporating IMU and contact process models, with corrections from a right-invariant forward kinematic measurement model.
- **Log-Linear Error Dynamics:** The deterministic system is shown to satisfy the "group affine" property, leading to exactly log-linear error dynamics. This implies that, in the absence of noise, the nonlinear estimation error can be exactly recovered from a linear differential equation, and the linearized error dynamics are independent of the current state estimate.
- **Consistent Observability Analysis:** A linear observability analysis is performed, which, due to the log-linear error dynamics, is inherently consistent with the underlying nonlinear system. This analysis confirms that absolute position and yaw are unobservable without external references, aligning with prior nonlinear observability results, but without the risk of spurious observability seen in standard EKFs.
- **IMU Bias Integration:** A method for augmenting the state with IMU biases is presented, leading to an "imperfect InEKF." Although some theoretical guarantees of perfect log-linearity are lost, empirical results demonstrate superior performance compared to standard EKFs.
- **Flexible Architectures:** Detailed derivations for alternative left-invariant and robo-centric estimator formulations are provided, alongside mechanisms for adding/removing contact points and exact analytical time-discretizations. An open-source C++ library implementing these features is also provided.

#### 4.2. Simulation Results

- **Improved Convergence:** In simulations involving a Cassie-series bipedal robot executing a walking gait, the proposed Right-Invariant EKF (RIEKF) demonstrates considerably faster convergence for observable states (pitch, roll, and body-frame velocities) compared to a state-of-the-art quaternion-based EKF (QEKF). This is observed even when both filters are initialized with significant random errors.
- **Accuracy of Linearized Dynamics:** The deterministic error dynamics of the InEKF are shown to be *exactly* log-linear. In simulations without sensor noise, the difference between the true and propagated error for the InEKF remains zero, regardless of the initial error magnitude. In contrast, the QEKF's approximated error dynamics exhibit increasing error with larger initial state deviations. With sensor noise, while the InEKF's error is no longer exactly zero, it remains more accurate and less sensitive to initial state errors than the QEKF.
- **Covariance Consistency:** The InEKF, by modeling state uncertainty as a "concentrated Gaussian on a Lie group," provides a more geometrically consistent representation of uncertainty. Simulations illustrate that the InEKF can capture complex, non-Gaussian uncertainty distributions (e.g., curved position uncertainties arising from unobservable yaw drift), which a standard QEKF's Euclidean Gaussian ellipse cannot accurately represent. This is particularly evident when dealing with large or complete initial yaw uncertainty, where the InEKF accurately models the resulting ring-shaped position uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Experimental Results on Cassie Robot

- **Real-World Convergence:** Experiments on a physical Cassie biped robot confirm the simulation findings. The RIEKF consistently converges faster and more reliably than the QEKF across 100 runs with varied initial conditions, reinforcing its robustness to initialization errors in a practical setting.
- **Motion Capture Validation:** A motion capture experiment using 18 Qualisys cameras demonstrates the RIEKF's accuracy. Over a 60-second, 15m path, the final position error is less than 5% of the distance traveled. Observable states (orientation, velocity) remain within their 3$\sigma$ covariance bounds, confirming filter consistency. The expected drift in unobservable absolute position and yaw (represented by growing covariance) is also observed.
- **Long-Term Odometry:** A long outdoor walk (200m over 7 minutes 45 seconds) showcases the InEKF's ability to provide reliable odometry. The estimated trajectory maintains sufficient accuracy to keep the robot localized on a sidewalk, with final position error within a few meters and imperceptible yaw drift, despite the unobservable nature of these states. This highlights its potential for sustained autonomous operation without external sensors.
- **LiDAR Mapping Application:** The InEKF's high-frequency pose estimates are effectively utilized for LiDAR-based terrain mapping. By projecting point cloud data into the world frame using the filter's state estimate, the robot successfully builds local maps of the environment, demonstrating the practical utility of the accurate odometry for higher-level perception tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of robot state estimation, particularly for legged robots, with implications for enhanced autonomy and robustness.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Performance and Robustness in Legged Robot State Estimation

The core significance lies in the development and validation of a Contact-Aided Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) that demonstrates superior performance compared to traditional EKF approaches. By exploiting the geometric symmetries inherent in the robot's state (pose, velocity, and contact points on a Lie group), the InEKF achieves:
- **Improved Convergence:** Both simulation and hardware experiments consistently show that the InEKF converges significantly faster and more reliably, especially from poor initial state estimates. This wider basin of attraction enhances the practical deployability of legged robots, as robust initialization becomes less critical.
- **Increased Accuracy and Consistency:** The log-linear property of the InEKF's error dynamics ensures that the filter's linearization is more accurate, even when the state estimate deviates from the true state. This leads to better accuracy and consistency in the state and covariance estimates, crucial for maintaining stability and precise control in dynamic locomotion.
- **Intrinsic Observability Consistency:** The InEKF inherently addresses the issue of spurious observability that can plague standard EKFs. Its observability analysis directly reflects the true unobservable states (absolute position and yaw), avoiding misleading filter behavior and the need for complex constraining mechanisms.

#### 5.2. Advancement of Invariant Filtering Theory in Robotics

This work extends the relatively new theory of invariant observer design and InEKF to a complex, real-world robotic system. By carefully modeling the legged robot state as a matrix Lie group and demonstrating that its contact-inertial dynamics satisfy the "group affine" property, the paper provides a concrete and successful application of advanced geometric control theory to a practical robotics problem. This could encourage further adoption and development of invariant filtering for other robotic applications where states reside on Lie groups.

#### 5.3. Practical Utility and Flexibility for Legged Robot Platforms

The developed InEKF is highly practical for modern legged robots:
- **Proprioceptive-Only Operation:** It relies solely on proprioceptive sensors (IMU, joint encoders, contact sensors), making it robust to challenging environmental and lighting conditions where vision-based systems might fail. This is critical for reliable autonomy in diverse scenarios like search and rescue or outdoor navigation.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The filter is computationally efficient enough to run at high frequencies (>2000 Hz) on embedded hardware, enabling real-time feedback for control.
- **Comprehensive Features:** The inclusion of IMU bias augmentation, detailed procedures for adding and removing contact points, and the formulation of both world-centric and robo-centric estimators, alongside exact analytical discretizations, makes the filter a versatile and complete solution.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of an open-source C++ library significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers to utilize and build upon this advanced state estimation framework.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Higher-Level Autonomy and Mapping

The accurate and consistent odometry provided by the InEKF serves as a robust foundation for higher-level robotic tasks:
- **Local Mapping:** As demonstrated with LiDAR mapping experiments, the high-quality pose estimates enable the construction of accurate local environmental maps, which are vital for navigation, obstacle avoidance, and gait adaptation on uneven terrain.
- **Long-Term Navigation:** The demonstrated low drift over extended periods suggests its suitability as a robust odometry source for various navigation and SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) systems, even if absolute position and yaw remain locally unobservable.
- **Future Research Directions:** This work lays the groundwork for several future advancements, including the development of invariant smoothers for improved accuracy, online estimation of kinematic parameters to compensate for modeling errors, and the integration of additional sensor modalities (e.g., vision, prior maps) within the invariant filtering framework to achieve globally consistent, drift-free localization.

In summary, this research delivers a theoretically sound and empirically validated state estimation solution that enhances the reliability and performance of legged robots, pushing closer to their potential for robust autonomous operation in complex environments.
