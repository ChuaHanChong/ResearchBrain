# Contact-Aided Invariant Extended Kalman Filtering for Robot State Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> A Contact-Aided Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) for legged robots is presented, leveraging Lie group theory to achieve superior convergence and consistency in state estimation. The method demonstrated improved robustness on a Cassie biped, achieving low position error and enabling accurate local LiDAR mapping.

## Problem

- Legged robots require highly accurate pose and velocity estimates for stable operation, motion planning, and autonomous navigation.
- Existing vision-based state estimation methods are susceptible to environmental factors like lighting and occlusions, limiting robustness.
- Standard Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs) suffer from local stability, state-dependent linearization inaccuracies, and potentially spurious observability.

## Method

- Developed a **Contact-Aided** ==Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF)== that models the robot's state (pose, velocity, contact points) as an element of an extended matrix Lie group.
- Formulated the system dynamics and measurement models to satisfy the "group affine" property, enabling exactly log-linear error dynamics independent of the state trajectory.
- Integrated IMU bias augmentation, provided precise procedures for adding and removing contact points, and utilized exact analytical time-discretizations for practical, real-time implementation.

## Results

- Simulations and hardware experiments on a Cassie biped showed the InEKF converging significantly faster and more reliably than a state-of-the-art quaternion-based EKF, even with substantial initial errors.
- Motion capture validation on a physical robot confirmed the RIEKF's accuracy, with less than **5%** final position error over a 15m path and observable states consistently within their 3σ covariance bounds.
- The InEKF provided reliable long-term odometry over a 200m outdoor walk, enabling effective LiDAR-based local mapping and demonstrating its potential for sustained autonomous operation without external sensors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By leveraging Lie group symmetries, the InEKF achieves exactly log-linear error dynamics, resulting in linearized error propagation and observation models that are independent of the current state estimate.
> - This framework provides superior convergence properties and consistency compared to standard EKFs, particularly for orientation and pose estimation, and offers an intrinsically consistent observability analysis.
> - The InEKF models state uncertainty as a "concentrated Gaussian on a Lie group," which allows for a more geometrically accurate representation of complex, non-Gaussian uncertainty distributions.
