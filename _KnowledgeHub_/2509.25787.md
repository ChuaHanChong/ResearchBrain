# Self-Evolving Vision-Language Models for Image Quality Assessment via Voting and Ranking

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoQuality is a self-supervised framework that enables Vision-Language Models to improve their image quality assessment capabilities by iteratively generating supervisory signals through pairwise majority voting and learning-to-rank policy optimization. It achieved weighted average increases of 31.8% in PLCC and 33.7% in SRCC over its baseline, outperforming state-of-the-art supervised models on diverse out-of-distribution datasets.

## Problem

- Obtaining large-scale, human-annotated ground-truth labels for Image Quality Assessment (IQA) is expensive and time-consuming, limiting dataset diversity and model generalization.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can exhibit inconsistencies, requiring resource-intensive human-supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning for post-training alignment.
- Existing self-supervised approaches struggle with subjective and continuous perceptual tasks like IQA, where absolute "correct" scores are not readily available.

## Method

- The framework employs an offline stage to generate high-confidence ==pairwise pseudo-labels== through majority voting from K self-consistency responses of the current VLM policy.
- An online stage fine-tunes the VLM by sampling K reasoning trajectories and scores, computing a fidelity reward based on predicted comparative probabilities (Thurstone model), and updating the policy using regularized Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- This two-stage process is iterative, allowing the model to progressively refine its image quality perception without relying on human annotations.

## Results

- EvoQuality consistently enhanced the base VLM, achieving weighted average increases of **31.8%** in PLCC and **33.7%** in SRCC across eight IQA benchmarks over the baseline after two iterations.
- It surpassed leading supervised VLM-based IQA models on a weighted average across diverse, unseen datasets, demonstrating superior generalization to authentic, synthetic, and AI-generated content without human labels.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the ranking-based EvoQuality approach significantly outperformed a direct regression variant (EvoEstimate) and that increasing the number of voting candidates (up to K=32) yielded more stable and robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-consistency, when adapted through pairwise majority voting, can generate robust supervisory signals for subjective perceptual tasks like IQA, effectively replacing human annotations.
> - Learning-to-rank with fidelity rewards provides a more stable and evolvable signal for continuous and subjective IQA compared to direct absolute score regression, especially for handling complex image artifacts.
> - An iterative self-evolution process can progressively broaden a VLM's capacity to understand and assess image quality across diverse distortion types, leading to improved generalization and reduced overfitting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across a range of tasks, including both high-level applications such as image captioning and visual reasoning, and low-level tasks like image quality assessment (IQA). However, VLMs often exhibit inconsistencies, necessitating post-training methods to enhance their reliability for specific downstream applications. Historically, post-training has primarily relied on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL), both of which require extensive, costly human-annotated data. For instance, recent advancements in RL, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), combined with task-specific verifiable rewards, have been applied to improve the reasoning abilities of large language models (LLMs) and VLMs.

Concurrently, a growing area of research, particularly with the increasing scale of foundation models, focuses on self-supervised post-training strategies. These methods exploit internal model outputs, reducing or eliminating the dependency on ground-truth annotations. Self-consistency, a notable example, leverages multiple diverse reasoning traces from the model itself, aggregating outputs (e.g., via majority voting) to arrive at a more robust conclusion. This approach has proven effective in enhancing reasoning capabilities in LLMs (e.g., TTRL for mathematical reasoning) and VLMs (e.g., MM-UPT for geometric problem-solving). These developments underscore the potential of combining online RL with self-rewarding mechanisms for autonomous model refinement.

Within the domain of IQA, the estimation of perceptual image quality aligned with human judgment is critical. However, obtaining reliable ground-truth labels for IQA is challenging and expensive, typically requiring large-scale subjective studies. This limitation results in IQA datasets being restricted in scale and diversity, consequently hindering the generalization capabilities of supervised IQA models. While some previous approaches have generated pseudo-labels using full-reference metrics, these are confined to synthetic datasets and their performance is bounded by the metric's limitations. The inherent subjectivity and continuous nature of IQA, where relative comparisons are more indicative of human perception than absolute scores, present unique challenges for adapting self-supervised learning. Traditional self-consistency methods designed for discrete reasoning tasks are not directly applicable.

This work, EvoQuality, positions itself at the intersection of these fields. It aims to bridge the gap by extending self-supervised, self-evolving methodologies, particularly the principle of self-consistency and ranking-based learning, to the perceptual domain of IQA. By adapting these techniques to the relative nature of image quality, EvoQuality seeks to enable VLMs to autonomously refine their IQA capabilities, thereby overcoming the critical dependency on expensive human annotations and improving generalization across diverse image distortion types. This represents a significant shift from supervised or pseudo-labeling methods that rely on external metrics, towards a fully self-supervised paradigm for IQA.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, termed EvoQuality, that enables a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to autonomously refine its image quality perception capabilities without requiring any human-annotated ground-truth labels. This framework is designed to facilitate the self-evolution of VLMs in the context of Image Quality Assessment (IQA).

The motivation behind pursuing this objective is multifaceted:

- **Cost and Scarcity of Ground-Truth Labels for IQA:** Traditional IQA methods, especially those involving learning-based models, heavily rely on subjective quality scores (e.g., Mean Opinion Scores, MOS) obtained through extensive and costly human experiments. These subjective studies are time-consuming, resource-intensive, and difficult to scale, leading to limited sizes and diversity of existing IQA datasets. This scarcity of labeled data is a significant bottleneck for developing robust and generalizable IQA models.
- **Limitations of Supervised Post-Training for VLMs:** While supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) are effective for improving VLM performance on downstream tasks, they inherently require verifiable rewards or labels. For IQA, these rewards are typically derived from human annotations, perpetuating the dependency on expensive data.
- **VLM Instability and Inconsistency:** Even with advanced architectures and decoding strategies, VLMs can exhibit instability and inconsistency in their outputs for identical inputs. Post-training is therefore crucial to enhance their reliability and accuracy, but current methods are bottlenecked by data requirements.
- **Under-exploration of Self-Supervision in Perceptual Domains:** Self-supervised techniques, particularly those leveraging self-consistency, have demonstrated success in enhancing VLM reasoning capabilities for discrete tasks like mathematics or visual reasoning. However, their application to continuous and subjective perceptual domains, such as IQA, where there is no absolute "correct" score and evaluation is more reliant on relative comparisons, remains largely unexplored.
- **IQA's Inherent Ranking Nature:** Human perception of image quality is often more consistent and robust when expressed as relative comparisons rather than absolute scores. Many recent IQA methods have adopted learning-to-rank strategies, demonstrating strong performance. The motivation is to adapt the self-consistency paradigm to align with this intrinsic ranking characteristic of IQA, designing self-supervision signals that are appropriate for relative quality judgments.
- **Enhancing Generalization:** The limited scale and diversity of existing IQA datasets often lead to supervised models having restricted generalization abilities to unseen distortion types or content. A self-supervised approach that can learn from a broader, unlabeled corpus has the potential to overcome this overfitting and improve robustness across diverse real-world images, including novel distortions like AI-generated content.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to develop a scalable, label-agnostic, and robust method for improving VLM-based IQA, specifically by leveraging the internal consistency of VLMs to generate meaningful self-supervision in a ranking-based framework, thereby addressing the challenges posed by costly human annotations and the subjective nature of quality perception.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvoQuality is designed as an iterative, two-stage framework that enables a VLM to autonomously refine its image quality assessment capabilities. The core idea is to leverage the principle of self-consistency and adapt it to the ranking-based nature of IQA, allowing the model to generate its own supervisory signals.

**Overall Iterative Process:**
The framework operates in a loop comprising two main stages:
1. **Offline Stage:** The VLM generates pseudo-ranking labels through pairwise majority voting on unlabeled image pairs.
2. **Online Stage:** The VLM's policy is updated using these self-generated pseudo-labels as a fidelity reward signal, guided by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
This iterative process allows the VLM to progressively improve its perceptual judgment.

#### 3.1. Offline Pseudo-Label Generation via Pairwise Majority Voting

- **Input Data:** A batch of unlabeled images {x\_1, x\_2..., x\_N}.
- **Pairwise Comparisons:** The current VLM policy (π\_θ) is used to perform pairwise comparisons on a set of randomly selected image pairs, P = {(x\_i, x\_j)...}.
- **Comparative Prompt:** A structured comparative prompt (`c_compare`) is used to query the VLM. This prompt instructs the VLM to compare two images and strictly state which one has better perceptual quality (e.g., "0" for the first image, "1" for the second). To mitigate positional bias, the order of image pairs is randomly permuted.
- **Self-Consistency and Voting:** For each image pair (x\_i, x\_j), the policy π\_θ(⋅|c\_compare, x\_i, x\_j) is queried *K* times to generate multiple responses.
- **Majority Voting:** The *K* responses are aggregated through majority voting to determine a high-confidence pseudo preference, p\*(x\_i, x\_j). If image x\_i receives more votes, p\*(x\_i, x\_j) = 1. If x\_j receives more votes, p\*(x\_i, x\_j) = 0. In case of a tie, p\*(x\_i, x\_j) = 0.5.
- **Outcome:** This process yields a set of definitive pairwise pseudo-labels, which replace the need for external human Mean Opinion Score (MOS) labels and serve as "ground truths" for the subsequent online training.

#### 3.2. Online Policy Evolution with Fidelity Rewards

- **Quality Score Generation:** For each image x\_i from the offline comparison, the VLM generates *K* reasoning trajectories and corresponding quality scores {o\_k, q\_k(x\_i)}\_K\^k=1. This is done using a direct estimation prompt (`c_score`), which asks the VLM for an overall quality rating on a float scale (e.g., 1 to 5, rounded to two decimal places). Both `c_compare` and `c_score` include a "think" suffix for internal monologue.
- **Predicted Comparative Probability:** Inspired by the Thurstone model, the model computes its predicted comparative probability p\_k(x\_i, x\_j) for each pair (x\_i, x\_j) that was evaluated in the offline stage. This probability is calculated using the standard Gaussian cumulative distribution function (Φ) on the difference between the current image's score (q\_k(x\_i)) and the mean score of the compared image (μ(q(x\_j))), normalized by a term involving the variances of both images' scores (σ\^2(q(x\_i)) + σ\^2(q(x\_j)) + γ).
- **Fidelity Reward Function:** A reward function r\_k(x\_i) is defined based on a fidelity measure, adapting it by substituting the ground-truth preference with the pseudo-label preference p\*(x\_i, x\_j) obtained from Eq. 1. The reward is calculated as the average over all images x\_j paired with x\_i, using a square-root fidelity term: √[p\*(x\_i, x\_j)p\_k(x\_i, x\_j)] + √[(1 - p\*(x\_i, x\_j))(1 - p\_k(x\_i, x\_j))]. This reward signal quantifies how well the model's current predicted probabilities align with its own aggregated judgments from the offline stage.
- **Relative Advantage Computation:** To stabilize training, the *K* fidelity rewards for x\_i are collected into a vector, and a relative advantage a\_k(x\_i) is computed by standardizing rewards within the group (i.e., subtracting the mean reward and dividing by the standard deviation of rewards for x\_i).
- **Policy Update (GRPO):** The VLM policy π\_θ(⋅|c\_score, x\_i) is updated using a regularized Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective function. This objective maximizes the reward-weighted likelihood of the generated trajectories while incorporating a clipping threshold (ϵ) to prevent unstable updates and a KL divergence term (D\_KL) to constrain the new policy from diverging excessively from a fixed reference policy (π\_ref) or the policy from the previous iteration (π\_θ\_old).

**Experimental Setup:**

- **Training Data:** EvoQuality is trained exclusively on the KONIQ dataset, augmented with synthetically generated distortions. All ground truth information (quality scores, distortion types/severities) is deliberately discarded to enforce a fully self-supervised setting.
- **Evaluation:** Zero-shot testing is performed on seven diverse IQA benchmarks, covering authentic, AI-generated, and synthetic distortions (SPAQ, LIVEW, AGIQA, CSIQ, TID2013, KADID, PIPAL).
- **Iterations:** The framework is conducted in two iterations (T=2). Round1 uses only authentic image pairs from KONIQ; Round2 incorporates synthetic images.
- **Hyperparameters:** 20,000 image pairs randomly constructed for offline stage. *K*=32 responses for voting/score generation. AdamW optimizer with learning rate 3 × 10\^-7, linear decay, β=0.05. Training on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating the effectiveness and robustness of the EvoQuality framework:

- **Significant Improvement Over Baseline:** EvoQuality consistently improved the zero-shot performance of the base VLM, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, across all eight IQA benchmarks tested. After a single evolutionary iteration (EvoQuality@Round1), the weighted average PLCC (Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient) and SRCC (Spearman Rank Correlation Coefficient) increased by 27.4% and 29.6%, respectively, over the baseline. A second iteration (EvoQuality@Round2) further boosted these weighted average improvements to 31.8% for PLCC and 33.7% for SRCC.

- **Superiority Over Supervised IQA Models in Zero-Shot Generalization:** Despite being entirely self-supervised and trained only on unlabeled images from the KONIQ dataset, EvoQuality@Round2 achieved performance competitive with, or superior to, state-of-the-art supervised VLM-based IQA models on unseen datasets.
 - On a weighted average across all benchmarks, EvoQuality@Round2 surpassed the leading supervised model, Q-Insight, by more than 0.05 in both PLCC and SRCC.
 - EvoQuality outperformed supervised VLM-based models on 5 out of 7 IQA benchmarks.
 - It demonstrated particularly strong performance on the challenging TID2013 dataset, achieving a PLCC score over 0.15 higher than the next-best performer (NIQE), and on the AGIQA dataset (containing AI-generated content), where it achieved substantially superior performance compared to prior supervised models that struggled to generalize to such novel content.
 - The framework also outperformed VisualQuality-R1, a supervised method that utilizes reinforcement learning with pairwise fidelity rewards from ground truths. This cross-dataset performance suggests that EvoQuality's self-evolving paradigm reduces overfitting and enhances generalization compared to supervised counterparts, even if some supervised models might achieve higher scores on their specific in-domain training data (KONIQ).

- **Progressive Refinement with More Iterations:** The iterative nature of EvoQuality was validated, with the second iteration (EvoQuality@Round2), which incorporated synthetic images during training, further boosting overall performance. The largest gains in the second round were observed on datasets featuring synthetic distortions, such as TID2013 (PLCC improved from +64.2% to +77.4%) and PIPAL (PLCC improved from +38.1% to +46.2%). Crucially, this improvement did not result in catastrophic forgetting, as performance on authentic datasets was maintained.

- **Enhanced Sensitivity to Synthetic Distortions:** EvoQuality showed particularly strong performance on datasets with complex synthetic distortions, outperforming other VLM-based models in these categories. This indicates that the method's self-evolution via pairwise voting and ranking captures a more robust and generalizable model of perceptual quality, especially for well-controlled distortion types.

- **Augmenting Pre-trained IQA Models for Unseen Generalization:** EvoQuality demonstrated flexibility by showing that it can be applied not only to base VLMs but also to augment existing pre-trained, supervised IQA models. When applied to VisualQuality-R1 as the base model, EvoQuality further boosted its out-of-distribution performance in a multi-dataset co-training setting, highlighting its additive capability and practical applicability.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Ranking vs. Direct Regression:** A comparison with `EvoEstimate`, a variant designed for direct absolute quality score prediction, confirmed the superiority of the ranking-based approach. While `EvoEstimate` was competitive on some authentic datasets, `EvoQuality` (ranking-based) showed significantly stronger performance on synthetic distortion datasets and demonstrated progressive improvement over iterations, whereas `EvoEstimate` did not. This suggests that learning from relative rankings provides a more robust signal for complex image artifacts and enables further evolution.
 - **Effect of Candidate Responses (K):** An ablation on the number of candidate responses (K) for voting revealed that introducing a voting process (K > 1) significantly improved performance over K=1 (no consensus). A larger K generally led to better outcomes, with K=32 demonstrating the most stable and robust performance across benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of EvoQuality represents a significant advancement in the field of Image Quality Assessment (IQA) and the broader application of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with several key areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Overcoming Annotation Dependency:** The most direct impact of EvoQuality is its ability to eliminate the reliance on costly and time-consuming human-annotated ground-truth labels for IQA model training. This directly addresses a critical bottleneck in the development and scaling of IQA systems, making it possible to train robust models without extensive subjective studies. This could democratize access to advanced IQA capabilities for researchers and practitioners in resource-constrained environments.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By leveraging a self-supervised, ranking-based approach, EvoQuality demonstrates superior generalization performance across diverse and unseen IQA benchmarks, including authentic, synthetic, and notably, AI-generated distortions. This addresses a common limitation of supervised IQA models, which often overfit to their training data and struggle with out-of-distribution content. The ability to generalize to emerging content types, such as AI-generated images, is particularly impactful for future applications.

- **Scalability for Foundation Models:** The framework provides a scalable paradigm for self-improving VLMs. As foundation models continue to grow in size and capability, self-evolving mechanisms like EvoQuality become increasingly vital for fine-tuning and adapting them to specific tasks without incurring prohibitive labeling costs. This paves the way for more efficient and continuous model improvement cycles.

- **Flexibility and Practical Applicability:** EvoQuality's demonstrated ability to not only enhance a base VLM but also to augment existing pre-trained supervised IQA models highlights its practical utility. This additive capability means that existing investments in supervised models can be further leveraged and improved for better generalization to unseen domains, making it a versatile tool for real-world deployment scenarios.

- **New Paradigm for Subjective Perceptual Tasks:** By successfully adapting self-consistency, voting, and ranking to the subjective and continuous nature of IQA, EvoQuality establishes a new paradigm for self-supervised learning in perceptual domains. This has broad implications for other subjective tasks where relative judgments are more suitable than absolute scores, such as assessing image aesthetics, evaluating the realism of generative adversarial networks (GANs), or ranking user-generated videos for quality. This could unlock autonomous progress in a wider array of perception-related AI applications.

- **Foundational for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the dependency on an in-domain pool of unlabeled images and the computational overhead, suggest clear directions for future research. Investigations into data efficiency, learning from generalized out-of-domain image collections, and developing more efficient voting schemes (e.g., active sampling) could further enhance EvoQuality's practicality and broaden its applicability.

In summary, EvoQuality offers a novel and effective solution to critical challenges in IQA, moving towards a future where sophisticated perceptual AI systems can autonomously refine their understanding of quality, significantly reducing the human effort and data costs currently associated with their development and deployment.
