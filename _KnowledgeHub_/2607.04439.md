# ResearchStudio-Idea: An Evidence-Grounded Research-Ideation Skill Suite from ML Conference Outcomes

> [!summary] Summary
> ResearchStudio-Idea introduces an evidence-grounded skill suite, IdeaSpark, that extracts 15 operational ideation patterns from ML conference outcomes to guide large language models in generating high-quality research proposals. In an automated-judge study, IdeaSpark-generated ideas achieved a mean quality score of 3.87/4, ranking first in 88% of cases and maintaining competitive novelty compared to baseline LLMs.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) can generate research ideas, but often produce proposals that are "novel-but-empty," lacking feasibility or substantive depth.
- A gap exists in systematically grounding LLM-generated ideas in existing literature, identifying genuine bottlenecks, and differentiating from prior work.
- The early stage of research ideation lacks a structured, outcome-grounded framework to guide LLMs in developing defensible research directions.

## Method

- A multi-stage empirical pipeline extracts ==15 high-level ideation patterns== and 31 sub-patterns from a corpus of ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS papers (2021–2025), using a ==two-stage innovation-signature extraction==, ==text-embedding-3-large embeddings==, and ==UMAP + HDBSCAN== clustering.
- The **ResearchStudio-Idea** skill suite, with its core **IdeaSpark** component, integrates these patterns into a ==five-phase workflow==: literature grounding, bottleneck identification, pattern-guided ideation, quality gauntlet, and expansion/validation.
- The system uses ==contrastive evidence from rejected papers== to explicitly identify failure modes, allowing for a rigorous audit process (backed by ==retrieval gates and deterministic validators==) that checks for prior-art collision and evaluates proposals against known weaknesses.

## Results

- IdeaSpark-generated ideas achieved the highest quality scores (mean **3.87/4**) and ranked first in **88%** of cases in a blind automated-judge study against hold-out problem seeds, outperforming generic auto-authored skills and bare LLMs.
- The system maintained competitive novelty (mean **2.92**) while achieving high quality, effectively addressing the "novel-but-empty" problem prevalent in some bare LLM baselines.
- A two-stage abstraction process was critical for inducing stable, strategy-focused clusters from the paper corpus, preventing the collapse into topic-based clusters when embedding raw text.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ML conference outcomes, including rejected submissions, reveal a compact and operational set of 15 recurring ideation patterns that can systematically guide research ideation.
> - Research ideas frequently combine multiple ideation patterns, with a modal composition of two patterns per paper, indicating a compositional nature of research strategies.
> - The quality of execution at the sub-pattern level, rather than the choice of a high-level ideation pattern, often differentiates accepted from rejected work, while community adoption favors usable-infrastructure contributions over some program committee-favored structural-insight moves.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the evolving landscape of research ideation, increasingly influenced by the capabilities of large language models (LLMs). Contemporary LLM-based systems have demonstrated proficiency in tasks such as literature retrieval, hypothesis generation, experiment planning, code writing, and report drafting. This progress has led to the development of several categories of AI-assisted research tools:
- **End-to-end "AI scientist" systems** aim to automate the full research lifecycle, from drafting ideas to producing reports and code. Examples include AI Scientist, AI-Researcher, Agent Laboratory, and Google AI co-scientist.
- **Multi-agent and search-based ideation systems** focus on exploring large spaces of potential research proposals, often by coordinating specialist agents or employing search algorithms over scientific concept networks.
- **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods** ground LLM-generated hypotheses in existing literature or knowledge graphs to enhance relevance and plausibility.
- **Novelty assessment, evaluation, and benchmarking tools** provide frameworks and datasets to check for prior-art collision, assess idea quality, and predict future impact.
- **Pattern induction methods** mine accepted papers from top conferences or large literature corpora to identify recurring innovation patterns.

Despite these advancements, a gap exists between the generation of plausible proposals and the development of genuinely defensible research directions. Previous studies have indicated that LLM-generated ideas, while sometimes appearing novel, can lack feasibility or fail to align with future impactful work. This suggests that surface-level novelty or fluency alone are insufficient proxies for useful ideation.

"ResearchStudio-Idea" positions itself to fill this gap by focusing on the "first mile" of research ideation. It seeks to integrate and operationalize components of existing systems – specifically pattern induction, retrieval-augmented generation, and novelty checking – into a structured, executable skill suite. A distinguishing feature of this work is its use of not only accepted and highly-cited papers but also *rejected submissions* to derive explicit failure modes and provide a contrastive understanding of successful research strategies. This approach aims to equip LLMs with a more robust mechanism for grounding ideas in evidence, identifying meaningful bottlenecks, differentiating from prior work, and evaluating risks before significant investment in implementation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were twofold:
1. **To ascertain if machine learning (ML) conference outcomes can be systematically analyzed to extract a compact yet operationally useful map of recurring ideation patterns.** This objective was motivated by the hypothesis that public records of research practice, particularly those from high-stakes conference reviews, contain discernible signals about the characteristics of impactful research, as well as common pitfalls leading to rejection. The project aimed to move beyond mere descriptive taxonomies to a framework that could actively guide the ideation process.
2. **To investigate whether such an induced map of ideation patterns could be packaged as a model-agnostic skill suite capable of generating and auditing coherent research proposals.** This objective was driven by the recognition that while large language models (LLMs) have made generating candidate research directions increasingly accessible, effective idea development necessitates more than just proposal fluency. Researchers require mechanisms to ground a problem in existing literature, identify genuine bottlenecks, clearly differentiate novel contributions from prior art, and assess potential risks before committing resources to implementation.

The specific motivations underpinning these objectives included:
- **Addressing the "Novel-but-Empty" Problem:** Existing LLM-based ideation methods can produce ideas that appear novel due to their vagueness but lack depth or feasibility upon closer inspection. The motivation was to develop a system that could generate ideas with both competitive novelty and substantive quality.
- **Operationalizing Outcome-Grounded Guidance:** The authors posited that analyzing the outcomes of major ML conferences (ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS) offers a unique empirical basis for ideation guidance.
 - **Accepted (Oral) papers** reveal strategies that program committees deem valuable.
 - **High-cited papers** indicate strategies adopted and reused by the broader scientific community.
 - **Rejected submissions** provide critical "failure signals," detailing how similar attempts by other researchers failed, offering insights into common weaknesses and overlooked aspects. This contrastive evidence was deemed crucial for developing practical, robust ideation skills.
- **Structuring the Early-Stage Research Workflow:** The research aimed to provide a structured workflow for the "first mile" of research ideation. This involved components for systematic literature grounding, precise bottleneck diagnosis, pattern-guided candidate construction, rigorous prior-art collision detection, and an outcome-informed audit process. The intention was to transform raw evidence and identified gaps into auditable research proposals rather than relying on unstructured brainstorming.
- **Developing Reusable, Interoperable Skills:** The project sought to engineer reusable skill components (e.g., literature search, prior-art checking) that could function both as standalone tools and as integrated parts of a larger end-to-end ideation workflow, thereby enhancing modularity and reusability within LLM-based research assistants.

The work specifically scoped its claim to the *idea stage*, emphasizing the production of a defensible research proposal rather than predicting implementation success, human peer review outcomes, or automating the full research lifecycle.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed a multi-stage empirical pipeline to induce ideation patterns from a corpus of ML conference papers, culminating in the design of the "ResearchStudio-Idea" skill suite.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

- **Corpus Collection:** Papers from ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS (2021–2025) were collected using the OpenReview API and Semantic Scholar citation metadata. The corpus excluded ICLR 2020 due to differing review data schema.
- **Paper Labeling:** Each of the 1,947 unique papers was assigned one or more labels:
 - **Oral (1,014 papers):** Papers designated "Oral" by the venue.
 - **High-Cited (HC) (260 papers):** Top-30 most-cited papers per venue-year (top-10 for 2025). These overlapped with Oral papers in 49 instances.
 - **Reject (722 papers):** Papers with an explicit "Reject" decision and available review sets.
- **Metadata Extraction:** For each paper, title, abstract, author list, OpenReview ID, decision string, available review fields (rating, confidence, soundness, contribution, summary, strengths, weaknesses, questions), and meta-review were retained. Introductions were extracted from PDFs when recoverable.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Innovation-Signature Extraction

To prepare papers for strategy-level clustering, a two-stage extraction process using Claude Sonnet 4.6 was applied.
- **Stage 1: Eight Base Fields:** From the paper's title, abstract, introduction, reviews, and meta-review, eight structured fields were extracted: "innovation approach," "key step," "why non obvious," "trigger condition," "reviewer praise," "reviewer concern," "acceptance signal," and "contribution type."
- **Stage 2: Four Domain-Agnostic Rewrites:** The first four (strategy-bearing) fields were then rewritten into domain-agnostic equivalents (e.g., `abstract strategy`) by replacing domain-specific nouns with generic placeholders and adopting imperative phrasing. This step aimed to prevent clustering based on topic rather than underlying research strategy.

#### 3.3. Unsupervised Pattern Discovery

- **Embedding:** The four concatenated, abstracted strategy fields from 1,891 papers were embedded using OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large` (3,072 dimensions) and L2-normalized.
- **Dimensionality Reduction:** UMAP reduced the embeddings to 10 dimensions.
- **Clustering:** HDBSCAN was applied to the reduced space, identifying 31 distinct "ideation sub-patterns." A `min_cluster_size` of 10 was selected, which resulted in 47.7% of papers remaining "unclustered" (indicating they fell between density peaks, not outside the strategy space).
- **Cluster Labeling:** Opus 4.7 automatically generated 2-6 word labels and one-sentence descriptions for each cluster, along with detailed "disambiguation cards" covering tactical patterns, step-by-step recipes, differentiation, application conditions, tactical failure modes, and paper-agnostic lessons.

#### 3.4. Ideation-Pattern Induction

- **Level-1 Taxonomy:** Opus 4.7 was used to induce 15 higher-level "ideation patterns" from the 31 sub-patterns. Each pattern was defined with an operational signature and "when-to-apply" clause.
- **Operational Cards:** A second Opus 4.7 pass generated comprehensive operational cards for each of the 15 ideation patterns. These cards integrated insights from Oral, High-Cited, and Rejected papers, detailing "success conditions," "failure modes," "Oral vs. Reject gap," "Oral vs. HC gap," "reviewer expectations," "cognitive barriers," and "representative examples." This enabled the explicit integration of failure signals.
- **Paper-Level Multi-Label Assignment:** A separate Sonnet 4.6 pass assigned one or more of the 15 ideation patterns to every paper in the corpus, including those unclustered by HDBSCAN, to capture the compositional nature of research strategies.

#### 3.5. IdeaSpark Design

The "IdeaSpark" system was designed as a two-tier skill.
- **Runtime Tier:** Contains lean skill specifications, phase prompts, schemas, retrieval hooks, rendering scripts, and deterministic validators.
- **Evidence Tier:** Houses the 15 ideation-pattern cards, 31 sub-pattern cards, a domain-by-pattern matrix, saturation records, and the corpus-derived failure-mode inventory.

The workflow comprises five phases:
- **Phase 0: Literature Grounding:** Conducts multi-source literature searches across arXiv, OpenReview, OpenAlex, and Semantic Scholar based on user input, creating a broad evidence bundle and a full-text cache.
- **Phase 1: Bottleneck Identification:** Diagnoses a literature-grounded structural gap, identifies closest adjacent papers, and summarizes pattern usage in the retrieved literature. It constructs a method-lineage tree to expose additive/subtractive gaps and prevent regression.
- **Phase 2: Pattern-Guided Ideation:** Selects 1-3 ideation patterns based on the bottleneck's structure and instantiates a candidate research direction (core mechanism, rationale, differentiation, compute budget, falsification prediction) guided by sub-pattern tactical recipes.
- **Phase 3: Quality Gauntlet:** Conducts a focused literature search for mechanism-level collision. An audit then evaluates the candidate against corpus-derived reject lessons, anti-patterns, and specific prior-art threats, issuing an "advance," "revise," or "abandon" verdict.
- **Phase 4: Expansion, Implementability Audit, Rendering, and Validation:** Expands the candidate into a final idea-card bundle, performs an implementability audit, renders structured artifacts (Markdown, LaTeX, PDF), and executes deterministic validators to ensure fidelity to core commitments (e.g., falsification prediction) and structural completeness.

A key design principle is **faithfulness**, where every load-bearing claim either traces to a retrieved record or is explicitly marked as non-grounded, enforced by retrieval gates, deterministic validators, and provenance discipline to resist hallucination. The "Kill-switch" for falsification predictions is explicitly preserved byte-identically through the workflow.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical analysis yielded several findings regarding the structure of ML research ideation and the performance of the IdeaSpark system:

- **Compact and Shared Ideation Pattern Space:** The analysis produced 15 high-level ideation patterns from 31 fine-grained sub-patterns. This pattern set was found to be compact and operational. Crucially, re-clustering the rejected papers in isolation confirmed that they utilized the same 15-pattern vocabulary as accepted papers, rather than a distinct set of "negative" strategies. This suggests that the distinction between accepted and rejected work often lies in the quality of execution and specific failure modes rather than the choice of overarching strategy.
- **Prevalence of Multi-Pattern Composition:** Paper-level multi-label assignments revealed that research papers frequently combine multiple ideation patterns. Across all acceptance categories (Oral, High-Cited, Reject), the modal composition size was found to be two patterns per paper, with a significant tail of 33.6% of papers employing three or more. The mean number of patterns per paper was approximately 2.3, indicating that ideation is often a compositional process.
- **Acceptance and Impact Profile Differences:**
 - **Main Pattern Choice vs. Execution Quality:** At the high-level ideation pattern granularity, the difference in frequency between Oral and Rejected papers (∆OR) was small (±2.9 percentage points), suggesting that the choice of a main pattern alone has limited discriminatory power for acceptance. Instead, execution at the sub-pattern level, as captured in the success conditions and failure modes, appeared more critical.
 - **PC Preference vs. Community Adoption:** A stronger differentiating signal emerged when comparing program committee (PC) preferences (Oral papers) against community adoption (High-Cited papers, ∆OH). PC committees tended to favor structural-insight moves, while the community showed a stronger inclination towards usable-infrastructure contributions. For example, "Audit and Pivot an Assumption" was heavily PC-favored, while "Unify Heterogeneous Inputs into One Space" was notably community-favored. "Reframe as a Solvable Object" showed consistent positive signals for both PC acceptance and community adoption.
- **Domain Breadth and Conditional Effectiveness:** Most ideation patterns demonstrated broad applicability across many of the 28 induced research domains. However, the success rate (Oral acceptance rate) of a given pattern could vary significantly across different domains. "Characterize a Limit, Then Surpass It" emerged as a consistently strong performer across diverse domains, particularly in areas like Trustworthy & Responsible ML and Learning Theory. This indicates that while patterns are generally domain-agnostic, their specific impact is context-dependent.
- **Temporal Trends and Conference Signatures:**
 - **Rising Patterns:** "Decompose and Delegate to Solvers" showed the fastest growth, aligning with the emergence of LLM-driven multi-agent systems. "Design a Confound-Isolating Diagnostic" and "Unify Heterogeneous Inputs into One Space" also increased in prevalence.
 - **Falling Patterns:** "Decompose for Differentiated Treatment," "Audit and Pivot an Assumption," and "Liberate a Fixed Generative Component" experienced a decrease in relative share.
 - **Venue Preferences:** Distinct preferences for certain ideation patterns were observed across ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS, reflecting their respective emphasis (e.g., ICML for diagnostic/theoretical work, ICLR for architectural construction, NeurIPS for systems-level composition and engineered supervision).
- **Ablation Study Results:**
 - **Embedding Model:** OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large` outperformed SPECTER2 in creating strategy-based clusters, yielding a higher silhouette score and more clusters, suggesting better capture of strategic similarity over topical similarity.
 - **Abstraction Stage:** The two-stage abstraction process (rewriting fields to be domain-agnostic before embedding) was crucial for maintaining stable, strategy-focused clusters. Direct embedding of raw, domain-specific text led to a collapse into degenerate topic-based clusters at coarser granularities, underscoring the importance of separating strategic operations from topic vocabulary.
- **Automated-Judge Evaluation (IdeaSpark Performance):**
 - **Quality:** In a blind automated-judge study across 100 held-out ICLR 2026 problem seeds, IdeaSpark-generated ideas achieved the highest quality scores (mean 3.87/4) and ranked first in 88% of cases. This significantly outperformed a generic auto-authored skill (Opus-self-gen, 2.57) and bare LLMs (Opus-4.8 bare: 2.56, GPT-5.5 bare: 1.00). This suggests that IdeaSpark's corpus-grounded pattern cards and multi-phase audited workflow contribute to higher idea quality.
 - **Novelty vs. Quality:** The evaluation revealed a "novel-but-empty" failure mode, particularly with the bare GPT-5.5 baseline, which scored highest on novelty but lowest on quality. Its vagueness allowed it to avoid collision with prior art. IdeaSpark, in contrast, maintained competitive novelty (mean 2.92) while achieving high quality, indicating its capacity to generate genuinely differentiated ideas.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ResearchStudio-Idea" skill suite, particularly the IdeaSpark component, represents an advancement in the application of large language models to scientific research ideation. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Enhancing the Quality and Feasibility of LLM-Generated Research Ideas:** By integrating outcome-grounded ideation patterns, explicit failure modes, and a rigorous multi-phase audit workflow, IdeaSpark aims to address the recognized "novel-but-empty" problem in LLM-generated proposals. The automated-judge evaluation provides initial evidence that the system can consistently produce ideas of higher quality and competitive novelty compared to generic LLM approaches, suggesting a path towards more substantive AI-assisted ideation.
- **Operationalizing Tacit Research Knowledge:** The work systematically mines and operationalizes tacit knowledge regarding successful research strategies and common pitfalls from a large corpus of ML conference outcomes. The 15 ideation patterns and their detailed operational cards, derived from accepted and rejected submissions, translate abstract concepts of innovation into actionable guidance. This can make the process of identifying viable research directions more structured and accessible, potentially benefiting early-career researchers or those exploring new subfields.
- **Providing a Comprehensive, Auditable Ideation Framework:** The ResearchStudio-Idea suite offers a modular set of tools (Paper-Search, Scoop-Check, IdeaSpark) that integrate literature grounding, pattern-guided generation, and prior-art collision checking. The explicit, evidence-traced audit process enhances the transparency and trustworthiness of LLM-generated ideas, allowing researchers to understand the rationale behind a proposal and its potential weaknesses before investing significant resources. This moves LLM ideation beyond a "black box" approach.
- **Leveraging Contrastive Evidence for Robustness:** The inclusion and analysis of rejected papers to extract "failure modes" is a distinctive contribution. This contrastive evidence provides critical insights into why certain strategies fail in practice, allowing IdeaSpark to proactively audit proposals against known weaknesses. This approach can help researchers avoid common errors and develop more robust research designs.
- **Contributing to the Science of Science:** The empirical analysis of ideation patterns, their compositional nature, and their correlation with acceptance, citation impact, and domain-specificity provides valuable data-driven insights into the dynamics of innovation within the ML community. This quantitative understanding can inform future studies on research methodology and the evolution of scientific fields.
- **Enabling Responsible Use of AI in Research:** The explicit design choices to mitigate hallucination (e.g., retrieval gates, deterministic validators, falsification predictions) and the careful handling of sensitive data (e.g., using aggregate failure patterns from rejected papers without profiling individuals) demonstrate a commitment to responsible AI development. The system is positioned as a research scaffold, empowering human researchers rather than replacing their critical judgment.

While the current evaluation relies on automated judges, with human validation as a future step, the work lays a foundation for more structured, evidence-grounded, and auditable LLM-assisted research ideation, with the potential to improve efficiency and quality in the scientific discovery process.
