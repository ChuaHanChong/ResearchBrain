# TEDi: Temporally-Entangled Diffusion for Long-Term Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> The TEDi framework extends Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models to generate arbitrarily long, realistic character motion sequences by temporally entangling the diffusion process with the motion's progression. It produces diverse and stable animations, avoiding common stitching artifacts and motion collapse, and supports interactive control.

## Problem

- Difficulty in synthesizing arbitrarily long, coherent, and diverse motion sequences without degradation, repetition, or artifacts.
- Limitations of current motion diffusion models which produce fixed-length outputs, necessitating complex chaining that leads to visible stitching issues.
- Auto-regressive RNNs struggle with error accumulation and motion collapse over extended generation periods, resulting in unstable or repetitive patterns.

## Method

- ==Introduces Temporally-Entangled Diffusion== (**TEDi**), which adapts the DDPM framework by aligning the diffusion process's gradual denoising with the motion's temporal axis, allowing for temporally-varying noise levels.
- Employs a recursive "typewriting-like" generation mechanism with a stationary motion buffer to continuously output clean frames, enabling arbitrary length motion synthesis.
- Utilizes a training objective combining a standard diffusion loss with a positional loss for kinematic accuracy and a foot contact loss to prevent foot sliding artifacts.

## Results

- Successfully synthesized arbitrarily long motion sequences (e.g., up to 33 seconds) that maintained plausible quality, diversity, and robustness against motion collapse.
- Demonstrated effective guided generation and trajectory control without requiring additional training, allowing for interactive influence over character movements.
- Outperformed baselines like ACRNN in stability and MDM (with outpainting) in avoiding stitching artifacts, and was preferred for diversity and quality in a perceptual study by a majority of respondents.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The gradual denoising nature of Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models can be effectively adapted to the temporal dimension of sequential data, enabling continuous generation.
> - A stationary motion buffer, combined with recursive frame output, allows for arbitrary long-term motion synthesis while inherently resolving stitching artifacts.
> - Training with a blend of random and monotonic noise schedules is crucial for the model to learn both individual pose denoising and temporal coherence, leading to diverse and stable long-range motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of long-term, realistic, and non-repetitive motion sequences is a recognized challenge in character animation, with implications for computer animation, motion control, and human-computer interaction. Recent advancements in generative modeling, particularly Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs), have demonstrated high-quality results in image synthesis and have seen initial adaptations for motion generation.

Existing adaptations of DDPMs for motion synthesis typically produce fixed-length motion sequences. This fixed-length output presents limitations for long-term generation, as chaining multiple short sequences often introduces stitching artifacts, and the multi-step denoising process of DDPMs can limit interactive control. In the broader context of deep motion synthesis, various approaches have been explored, including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Phase-Functioned Neural Networks (PFNNs), and generative adversarial networks (GANs). RNN-based methods have historically been utilized for long-term motion synthesis due to their auto-regressive capabilities, but they often encounter issues such as error accumulation and motion collapse over extended sequences.

This work addresses the limitations of current motion diffusion models by proposing a method that moves beyond fixed-length outputs and aims to resolve stitching issues. It also seeks to overcome the stability and memory erosion problems commonly associated with RNN-based long-term generation. By introducing a novel mechanism that adapts the gradual nature of the diffusion process to the temporal axis of motion, TEDi aims to provide a framework for continuous, high-quality, and controllable long-term motion synthesis, thereby integrating the strengths of diffusion models with the necessity for extended temporal generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework capable of synthesizing arbitrarily long motion sequences while maintaining realism, preventing repetition, and avoiding degenerate outputs such as frozen motion or stitching artifacts.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors:
- **Challenges in Long-Term Motion Generation:** Generating extended, coherent, and diverse motion sequences remains a difficult problem in computer animation. Existing methods often struggle with maintaining quality and avoiding degradation over long durations.
- **Limitations of Current Diffusion Models for Motion:** While Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) have achieved notable success in generating high-quality static images, their direct application to motion synthesis typically produces fixed-length sequences. This inherent limitation necessitates post-processing steps, such as chaining and blending, which can introduce visual discontinuities or "stitching artifacts."
- **Inefficiency and Lack of Control in Standard DDPM Inference:** A typical diffusion process requires numerous denoising iterations to produce a clean sample. For fixed-length motion sequences, this translates to a high computational cost per generation and limits real-time interactive control.
- **Problems with Auto-Regressive RNNs:** Traditional auto-regressive models, often built on RNNs, have been used for long-term generation but frequently suffer from error accumulation and a gradual erosion of memory over time, leading to motion collapse or repetitive patterns in very long sequences.
- **Inspiration from Diffusion's Gradual Nature:** The authors observed that the gradual nature of the diffusion process, where samples are synthesized incrementally along a "diffusion time-axis," could potentially be adapted to the "temporal-axis" of a motion sequence. This conceptual entanglement forms the core motivation for the proposed approach.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives of TEDi are to:
- **Adapt Diffusion to the Motion Temporal-Axis:** Propose a method that extends the DDPM framework to allow for temporally varying noise levels, thereby entangling the diffusion time-axis with the motion's temporal axis.
- **Enable Continuous Motion Generation:** Develop a mechanism that produces a continuous stream of clean motion frames during each step of the diffusion process, facilitating auto-regressive generation of arbitrary length.
- **Maintain Motion Coherence and Quality:** Design a system that prevents stitching problems and maintains framewise consistency throughout long sequences.
- **Facilitate Direct Control and Planning:** Introduce methods for influencing and guiding the generated motion through manipulation of an internal motion buffer, allowing for features like guided generation and trajectory control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Temporally-Entangled Diffusion (TEDi), extends the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) framework to support the synthesis of arbitrarily long motion sequences by entangling the temporal axis of the motion with the time axis of the diffusion process.

#### 3.1. Motion Representation

A motion sequence of length $K$ is represented by a set of poses. Each pose includes:
- Root joint displacements ($O_{xz} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times 2}$) with respect to the xz-plane.
- Root joint height ($O_y \in \mathbb{R}^K$).
- Joint rotations ($R \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times JQ}$), where $J$ is the number of joints and $Q=6$ represents 6D rotation features, defined in the parent's coordinate frame.
- Foot contact labels ($L \in \{0,1\}^{K \times C}$), where $C=4$ for left/right heels and toes, used to mitigate foot sliding.
All these features are concatenated along the channel axis to form a full motion representation $M \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times (JQ+C+3)}$.

#### 3.2. Diffusion Model Extension (Temporally-Entangled Diffusion)

The core idea is to adapt the gradual denoising mechanism of DDPMs to operate along the temporal axis of the motion sequence itself, rather than treating the entire sequence as a static "image" to be denoised uniformly over diffusion time. This is achieved by allowing temporally-varying noise levels within a single motion sequence during training. The authors effectively equate the total diffusion time steps $T$ with the motion sequence length $K$.

Two noise injection schemes are proposed for training:
- **Random Schedule:** For each frame $f_i$ in a sequence, a random diffusion time step $t_i \sim U(0,T)$ is sampled, and noise is applied according to the variance schedule $\beta_{t_i}$. This teaches the model to denoise individual poses regardless of their position in the sequence.
- **Monotonic Schedule:** Noise levels are monotonically increasing across the frames, where $t_i = i$. This introduces temporal coherence and helps ensure smooth transitions between frames during inference.
During training, a mix of these two schedules (e.g., 2/3 random, 1/3 monotonic) is used. The network receives a noisy motion sequence $[\tilde{f}_1, \ldots, \tilde{f}_K]$ as input and is trained to predict the clean motion sequence $[f_1, \ldots, f_K]$ directly.

#### 3.3. Loss Functions

The total training loss ($L$) comprises three components:
- **Diffusion Loss ($L_{diff}$):** This is the standard DDPM objective, which minimizes the squared error between the predicted clean motion and the ground truth clean motion.
- **Positional Loss ($L_{pos}$):** To account for error accumulation along the kinematic chain, a forward kinematics operator ($FK_S$) is used to compute joint positions. This loss penalizes the squared difference between the predicted and ground truth joint positions.
- **Foot Contact Loss ($L_{contact}$):** This loss penalizes high foot velocity when foot contact labels indicate the foot should be on the ground, ensuring self-consistency and reducing foot sliding artifacts. It uses a sigmoid-weighted squared difference of joint positions between consecutive frames.

The diffusion network architecture is inspired by the U-Net, adapted for 1D signals using 1D convolutions, 1D attention blocks, and skip connections to capture long-term frame correlations.

#### 3.4. Inference Procedure (Recursive Generation)

TEDi employs a "typewriting-like" recursive generation mechanism (Figure 3) to produce arbitrarily long motion sequences:
1. **Initialization:** A motion buffer of $K$ frames is initialized with a "primer" motion sequence, which is noised with monotonically increasing variance.
2. **Denoising Step:** The model processes all frames in the motion buffer in parallel, producing a progressively denoised sequence.
3. **Frame Output:** The first frame in the denoised sequence is now considered clean and is "popped" from the buffer, added to the output stream.
4. **Buffer Update:** All remaining frames in the buffer are shifted forward (effectively becoming one step cleaner). A new frame drawn from a standard Gaussian distribution (pure noise) is "pushed" to the end of the buffer.
This process is repeated indefinitely. A key distinction from typical DDPM inference is that a new clean frame is output after only one forward pass of the model, enabling faster auto-regressive generation, while each individual frame still undergoes a full $T$ (or $K$) denoising steps as it traverses the buffer.

#### 3.5. Guided Generation and Trajectory Control

The framework supports interactive control during inference:
- **Guided Generation:** Predefined "motion guides" (sequences of clean frames) can be injected into the motion buffer at specified future time points. The model recursively replaces portions of the noisy buffer with a noised version of the guide. A small buffer region (e.g., 5 frames) before the guide is left for the network to smooth transitions.
- **Trajectory Control:** Similar to guided generation, desired root trajectory information (xz-plane displacement and height) can be directly written into the motion buffer, allowing the model to generate motions that adhere to a specified path.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of TEDi focused on its ability to generate long-term motions, produce diverse outputs, support guided generation and trajectory control, and outperform existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Long-Term Generation and Diversity

- TEDi demonstrated the capability to synthesize arbitrarily long motion sequences, exemplified by sequences up to 33 seconds, while maintaining plausible motion quality throughout.
- The method showed robustness against motion collapse, a common issue in extended generation, where motions become static or degenerate.
- Due to the stochastic nature of diffusion models, TEDi was able to generate diverse motion sequences (e.g., boxing, shuffling, hand-gestures) and variations from the same initial primer, illustrating its generative capacity.

#### 4.2. Guided Generation and Trajectory Control

- **Guided Generation:** The framework successfully integrated predefined motion guides into long generated sequences. The model was observed to "prepare and plan" for upcoming guides, producing natural transitions between interactively generated frames and the specified guide motions.
- **Trajectory Control:** Without additional training, TEDi could adhere to given trajectory information by overwriting the relevant data in the motion buffer, demonstrating effective control over the character's global movement.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Baselines

The authors compared TEDi against two other long-term motion generation methods: ACRNN [Zhou et al. 2018] and the Human Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) [Tevet et al. 2022], with MDM adapted for long-term generation via an inpainting-based "outpainting" scheme.
- **ACRNN:** When trained on a large and diverse dataset (CMU dataset), ACRNN exhibited instability, producing motions that quickly collapsed or showed artifacts like foot levitation and penetration, indicating a lack of robustness for very long sequences in varied settings.
- **MDM (with inpainting):** While MDM generally produces high-quality short sequences, the inpainting-based long-term generation technique resulted in visible "stitching artifacts" or discontinuities at the boundaries where generated and in-painted segments met. TEDi's recursive generation mechanism inherently avoids such stitching problems.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the training scheme highlighted the importance of temporally varying noise levels. A version of the model trained without the random noise schedule (i.e., only with a monotonic noise schedule or temporally invariant noise) showed diminished diversity and reduced stability in long-range motion generation, indicating that the random schedule is crucial for preventing motion collapse.

#### 4.5. Perceptual Study

A perceptual study involving 55 respondents compared TEDi with MDM, ACRNN, and Motion VAE [Ling et al. 2020] based on diversity and quality of generated motions.
- **Diversity:** TEDi was chosen as having the most diverse motions by 34 out of 55 respondents, significantly outperforming MDM (12), ACRNN (8), and Motion VAE (1).
- **Quality:** For motion quality, TEDi was selected by 33 respondents as equivalent to or better than MDM (17), and substantially superior to ACRNN (5). (Motion VAE was not included in the quality comparison).
These results suggest that TEDi produces motions of comparable or higher quality than contemporary diffusion models, while offering greater diversity and stability for long-term generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TEDi framework represents a significant advancement in the field of long-term motion synthesis, particularly within the context of generative diffusion models.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Technical Contribution

The core technical contribution is the concept of "temporally-entangled diffusion," where the gradual denoising nature of DDPMs is aligned with the temporal progression of a motion sequence. This approach, combined with the "stationary motion buffer" and recursive generation mechanism, offers a novel paradigm for generating continuous data streams from a diffusion model. By enabling the model to continually produce clean frames without incrementing the global diffusion time-axis, TEDi addresses a fundamental limitation of traditional DDPMs when applied to sequential data.

#### 5.2. Addressing Key Limitations

- **Long-Term Generation:** TEDi effectively overcomes the fixed-length output limitation of standard motion diffusion models, enabling the synthesis of arbitrarily long motion sequences.
- **Stitching Artifacts:** The auto-regressive and continuous nature of the generation process inherently avoids the stitching artifacts that arise when concatenating or inpainting fixed-length segments from other methods.
- **Motion Stability:** Unlike RNN-based methods that can suffer from error accumulation and motion collapse over long durations, TEDi maintains motion plausibility and robustness, demonstrating stable generation for extended periods.
- **Control and Planning:** The ability to manipulate the motion buffer through guided generation and trajectory control introduces a new level of interactive influence and planning capabilities, which is valuable for character animation workflows.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact and Applications

- **Character Animation:** The capability to generate long, diverse, and controllable character motions will directly benefit animation production, game development, and virtual reality applications by reducing manual labor and enhancing realism.
- **Motion Control:** The framework's ability to integrate guides and trajectories could lead to more intuitive and effective systems for controlling virtual characters in real-time.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** More natural and varied human motion synthesis could improve the fidelity of virtual avatars and intelligent agents in interactive environments.
- **Broader Generative AI:** The "temporally-entangled diffusion" concept and the stationary motion buffer are proposed as potentially applicable to other sequential data modalities beyond motion, such as audio, video, or even for spatially ordered data like patches within images. This suggests a more generalizable framework for continuous generation with diffusion models.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The authors identify areas for future work, including leveraging techniques like DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) to achieve lower inference latency, exploring long-term text-conditioned motion generation for higher-level control, and investigating the interplay between high-level textual prompts and low-level user guidance. These directions point towards further enhancing the practicality, efficiency, and controllability of diffusion-based sequential data generation.
