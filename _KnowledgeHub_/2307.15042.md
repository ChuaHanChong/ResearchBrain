# TEDi: Temporally-Entangled Diffusion for Long-Term Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> The TEDi framework extends Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models to generate arbitrarily long, realistic character motion sequences by temporally entangling the diffusion process with the motion's progression. It produces diverse and stable animations, avoiding common stitching artifacts and motion collapse, and supports interactive control.

## Problem

- Difficulty in synthesizing arbitrarily long, coherent, and diverse motion sequences without degradation, repetition, or artifacts.
- Limitations of current motion diffusion models which produce fixed-length outputs, necessitating complex chaining that leads to visible stitching issues.
- Auto-regressive RNNs struggle with error accumulation and motion collapse over extended generation periods, resulting in unstable or repetitive patterns.

## Method

- ==Introduces Temporally-Entangled Diffusion== (**TEDi**), which adapts the DDPM framework by aligning the diffusion process's gradual denoising with the motion's temporal axis, allowing for temporally-varying noise levels.
- Employs a recursive "typewriting-like" generation mechanism with a stationary motion buffer to continuously output clean frames, enabling arbitrary length motion synthesis.
- Utilizes a training objective combining a standard diffusion loss with a positional loss for kinematic accuracy and a foot contact loss to prevent foot sliding artifacts.

## Results

- Successfully synthesized arbitrarily long motion sequences (e.g., up to 33 seconds) that maintained plausible quality, diversity, and robustness against motion collapse.
- Demonstrated effective guided generation and trajectory control without requiring additional training, allowing for interactive influence over character movements.
- Outperformed baselines like ACRNN in stability and MDM (with outpainting) in avoiding stitching artifacts, and was preferred for diversity and quality in a perceptual study by a majority of respondents.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The gradual denoising nature of Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models can be effectively adapted to the temporal dimension of sequential data, enabling continuous generation.
> - A stationary motion buffer, combined with recursive frame output, allows for arbitrary long-term motion synthesis while inherently resolving stitching artifacts.
> - Training with a blend of random and monotonic noise schedules is crucial for the model to learn both individual pose denoising and temporal coherence, leading to diverse and stable long-range motions.
