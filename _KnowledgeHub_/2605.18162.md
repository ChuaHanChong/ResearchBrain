# Self-Evolving Spatial Reasoning in Vision Language Models via Geometric Logic Consistency

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Lab and collaborators developed SAGE, a self-evolving framework that instills robust spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models by enforcing geometric and linguistic duality consistency. The method improved performance across multiple benchmarks, achieving gains such as +15.0 on MindCube, while using less training data than prior methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit fragile spatial reasoning abilities, often struggling with accurate representations of spatial relationships and object positions, leading to 'spatial hallucinations.'
- Existing VLMs may provide correct answers for specific instances but fail when presented with geometrically or linguistically transformed versions of the same input, indicating a lack of genuine logical understanding ('pseudo-understanding').
- Current reinforcement learning objectives for VLM reasoning do not directly enforce logical consistency across semantically equivalent, paired formulations of spatial relations.

## Method

- Introduced Spatial Alignment via Geometric Evolution (**SAGE**), a model-agnostic and data-efficient post-training framework for VLMs.
- Formalized 'duality consistency' as a training signal, enforcing logical coherence across original-dual input pairs generated through visual (e.g., horizontal reflection, rotation) and linguistic (e.g., logical negation, option permutation) transformations.
- Developed a self-evolving operation pool that dynamically probes the model for inconsistencies, identifies challenging duality operations, and adaptively prioritizes them for active training via a consistency-augmented Group Relative Policy Optimization (==GRPO==).

## Results

- SAGE consistently improved performance across six video understanding benchmarks (e.g., +8.4 on MVBench, +7.6 on VideoMME) and seven spatial reasoning benchmarks (e.g., +15.0 on MindCube, +14.6 on OmniSpatial) using Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- The method achieved superior or competitive performance against larger state-of-the-art models like InternVL-2.5-8B and SpatialLadder-3B, while utilizing less than **37%** of the training data compared to some prior GRPO approaches.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both visual and linguistic duality components contribute to performance gains, with visual duality showing a stronger impact on spatially grounded tasks, and the self-evolving pool efficiently prioritized challenging operations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enforcing logical consistency through geometric and linguistic duality operations can mitigate 'pseudo-understanding' in VLMs, fostering a more robust and generalizable spatial reasoning capability.
> - A self-evolving mechanism for managing duality operations is effective, allowing the training process to dynamically adapt and target specific reasoning vulnerabilities of the model.
> - Integrating duality consistency rewards into a reinforcement learning framework like GRPO provides a powerful inductive bias that improves spatial reasoning and generalization across diverse tasks and transformations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated substantial progress in multimodal reasoning, extending from basic perception to more complex world understanding. This progress has led to their application in domains such as embodied navigation and autonomous driving. However, a persistent challenge in the field of multimodal intelligence is the fragility of VLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities. Existing models often struggle with accurately representing spatial relationships, object positions, motion directions, and relative distances, frequently exhibiting "spatial hallucinations" that compromise their reliability in real-world applications.

Current research efforts to improve VLM reasoning often involve Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). These methods typically design reward signals to encourage specific desirable behaviors, such as providing correct answers or improving temporal and geometric modeling. However, the authors identify a critical gap: existing RL objectives do not directly enforce logical consistency across semantically equivalent, paired formulations of the same spatial relation. For instance, a model might correctly answer a question about an object's position but fail when the same image is horizontally flipped, even though the correct answer should predictably change (e.g., "left" becomes "right"). This discrepancy indicates that VLMs may rely on surface-level statistical patterns rather than possessing a robust understanding of underlying spatial logic, leading to "pseudo-understanding."

Previous works have explored transformations or augmentations, but these are generally predefined or task-specific and do not adapt to the model's evolving weaknesses. While some methods introduce self-correction or evolving environments, they do not specifically enforce transformation-level duality consistency. This research positions itself to address this particular limitation by introducing a self-evolving framework that directly enforces logical consistency through geometric and linguistic duality operations, aiming to move beyond instance-level correctness towards more robust and generalizable spatial reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that despite significant advancements, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit fragile spatial reasoning abilities. While VLMs may correctly answer specific questions about object positions or relations, their performance often degrades unpredictably when presented with geometrically or linguistically transformed versions of the same input, where the correct answer should be predictably mapped. This fragility reveals a fundamental gap: models demonstrate instance-level correctness without possessing robust spatial reasoning or genuine logical invariance. This "pseudo-understanding" limits their reliability in safety-critical applications like embodied navigation and autonomous driving, which necessitate consistent reasoning about spatial arrangements and changes under various transformations.

To address this limitation, the authors propose a framework, Spatial Alignment via Geometric Evolution (SAGE), with the following key objectives:

1. **Introduce Duality Consistency as a Training Signal:** The core objective is to formalize and integrate "duality consistency" as a general training signal for VLM spatial reasoning. This signal aims to enforce logical coherence across pairs of inputs—an original input and its dual counterpart—where the answer to the dual input is deterministically related to the answer of the original input through a known transformation rule. This includes both visual duality operations (e.g., geometric manipulations of images) and linguistic duality operations (e.g., structural perturbations of queries like logical negation or option permutation). The goal is to encourage models to predict answers that are logically consistent under these known transformations, thereby fostering genuine spatial understanding rather than mere pattern matching.

2. **Develop a Self-Evolving Post-Training Framework (SAGE):** The research aims to design SAGE as a model-agnostic and data-efficient post-training framework that can be applied to any existing VLM. A critical objective is to make this framework self-evolving. Instead of relying on a fixed set of transformations, SAGE is designed to maintain a dynamic pool of duality operations. This pool continuously probes the model for inconsistencies, identifies specific problem types or transformations that the model struggles with (i.e., where it appears correct on the original but fails on the dual), and adaptively prioritizes these challenging operations for active training. This adaptive prioritization ensures that training focuses on the most informative signals, addressing the model's current reasoning vulnerabilities efficiently.

3. **Demonstrate Improved Performance and Generalization:** A practical objective is to experimentally validate that SAGE consistently improves the spatial reasoning abilities of VLMs. This involves evaluating the framework across a broad range of video understanding and spatial reasoning benchmarks. The authors intend to show that enforcing duality consistency leads to enhanced generalization, not just to the specific transformations used during training, but also to unseen data and more complex spatial reasoning tasks. Furthermore, they aim to demonstrate that SAGE achieves competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines while being data-efficient relative to prior GRPO-based methods.

In summary, the motivation is to resolve the fragile spatial reasoning of VLMs by instilling a deeper, logically coherent understanding of space through dynamic, consistency-enforcing duality operations, rather than relying on superficial correlations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Spatial Alignment via Geometric Evolution (SAGE) framework is a self-evolving post-training method designed to enhance VLM spatial reasoning by enforcing logical consistency through geometric and linguistic duality operations. It operates in three main stages: Duality Probing, Self-Evolving Operation Pool, and Consistency-Augmented GRPO training.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Duality Probing

This stage identifies reasoning inconsistencies in VLMs by evaluating their predictions on original-dual input pairs. The core concept is the "duality complement."

- **Duality Operation Definition:** A duality operation `T = (T, ϕ, S)` is defined as a triple where:
 - `T: V × Q → V × Q` is an input transformation function (visual input `v`, query `q`).
 - `ϕ: A → A` is the induced answer mapping.
 - `S ⊆ V × Q` is the applicability domain.
 - Crucially, for any `(v, q) ∈ S`, the ground truth answer for the transformed input `T(v, q)` is deterministically `ϕ(a*(v, q))`, where `a*(v, q)` is the ground truth for the original input. This means the correct answers for an original input and its dual are logically linked.
- **Duality Consistency:** The duality consistency `C_T(θ)` of a VLM `M_θ` with respect to an operation `T` is the probability that the model's predicted answer for the transformed input `T(v, q)` matches the expected mapped answer `ϕ(a_hat_θ(v, q))`, where `a_hat_θ(v, q)` is the model's prediction for the original input. A low `C_T(θ)` indicates "pseudo-understanding."
- **Families of Operations:**
 - **Visual Duality:** These operations apply geometric transformations to the visual input `v`, with `q` remaining constant. Examples include:
 - **Horizontal reflection (σ_h):** Swaps "left" ↔ "right" in spatial answers.
 - **Vertical reflection (σ_v):** Swaps "top" ↔ "bottom."
 - **Rotation (ρ_π, 180°):** Inverts all spatial references.
 - **Appearance perturbations (color inversion, grayscale):** `ϕ` is identity for non-color queries, testing reliance on content rather than appearance.
 The set of valid operations is closed under composition, guiding candidate discovery.
 - **Linguistic Duality:** These operations perturb the query `q`, with `v` remaining constant. Examples include:
 - **Option permutation (π):** Reorders multiple-choice options, requiring the model to track content, not position. `ϕ_π(a) = π⁻¹(a)`.
 - **Logical negation (¬):** Inserts negation into the query (e.g., "Is X on the left?" becomes "Is X NOT on the left?"). `ϕ_¬` maps to the complementary answer, testing genuine logical inference.
 - **Semantic paraphrase:** Rephrases the query while preserving meaning, with `ϕ = id`.
- **Efficient Batch Probing:** To measure `C_T(θ)` at scale, `N` examples are sampled, and `T` is applied. Both original and dual inputs are processed in a single batched forward pass through `M_θ`, minimizing overhead.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Self-Evolving Operation Pool

This stage dynamically manages a pool `P` of `M` duality operations, ensuring that training focuses on the most informative signals.

- **Operation States and Transitions:** Each operation `T_i ∈ P` can be in one of three states:
 - **CANDIDATE:** Not yet active in training.
 - **ACTIVE:** Currently being used to generate consistency rewards.
 - **MASTERED:** Model has learned this invariance (consistency `C_T_i` is above a mastery threshold `τ`).
 Every `E` steps, `C_T_i(θ_t)` is evaluated for all operations.
 - **Retire:** `ACTIVE` → `MASTERED` if `C_T_i ≥ τ`.
 - **Forget:** `MASTERED` → `CANDIDATE` if `C_T_i < 0.8τ` (anti-forgetting detection).
 - **Promote:** `CANDIDATE` → `ACTIVE` to fill an open slot (up to `K` active operations), prioritizing operations with low consistency `(1 - C_T_i)` and a novelty bonus.
- **Anti-forgetting:** With probability `p_f` at each step, a random `MASTERED` operation is temporarily included in the `ACTIVE` set to re-expose it to training signals and prevent consistency degradation.
- **Efficiency:** The number of active operations is bounded by `K`, limiting the per-step computational overhead. The total pool size `M` is also fixed.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Consistency-Augmented GRPO

This stage integrates the duality consistency signal into the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.

- **Generation:** For a given sample `(v, q, a*)`, the VLM `M_θ` generates `G` completions for the original input. If an active duality operation `T` is applicable, `G/2` dual completions are also generated for `T(v, q)`.
- **Reward Computation:** The total reward `r(o_i)` for a completion `o_i` is a sum of three components:
 - `r_acc(o_i)`: Accuracy reward (standard task-specific reward).
 - `r_fmt(o_i)`: Format reward (ensuring correct output format).
 - `r_cons(o_i)`: **Consistency reward**, which measures the logical coherence between the generated answer `ans(o_i)` and the answers from dual completions `{o'_j}`. Specifically, `r_cons(o_i) = λ * (1/G/2) * Σ_j=1^G/2 1[ϕ(ans(o_i)) = ans(o'_j)]`, where `λ` is the consistency weight. If no active operation is applicable, `r_cons` is zero.
- **Policy Update:** Following GRPO, group-relative advantages `A_hat_i` are computed based on the total rewards. The model parameters `θ` are then updated by maximizing an objective function `J(θ)` that incorporates these advantages and a KL divergence penalty against a frozen reference model `M_θ_ref` to ensure stable updates.

The overall approach (Algorithm 1) outlines this iterative process of sampling data, generating responses, computing rewards including consistency, updating the policy, and periodically evaluating and evolving the operation pool.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SAGE across six video understanding benchmarks and seven spatial reasoning benchmarks, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the base model. The training dataset comprised approximately 28K samples focused on spatial reasoning, representing less than 37% of the data used by some prior GRPO methods.

#### 4.1. Video Understanding Benchmarks

SAGE demonstrates consistent performance improvements over both proprietary (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash) and open-source (e.g., Video-R1-7B, ViSS-R1-7B) state-of-the-art models on a majority of the evaluated video understanding benchmarks (Table 1).

- **Overall Improvements:** SAGE (when applied after SFT initialization, referred to as `+SFT+SAGE`) yielded gains across most benchmarks, including VSI-Bench (+4.2), VideoMMMU (+5.6), MMVU (+6.1), MVBench (+8.4), TempCmps (+2.6), and VideoMME (+7.6) relative to the `Qwen2.5-VL-7B` base model.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** Particularly strong improvements were observed on benchmarks with higher spatial-temporal reasoning demands, such as VideoMME, TempCmps, and VSI-Bench.
- **SFT Impact:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) alone sometimes led to performance degradation on several benchmarks (e.g., VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, TempCmps), while SAGE consistently improved grounding and spatial consistency. The combination of SFT and SAGE did not always outperform SAGE alone, suggesting that SFT might introduce language-biased representation shifts that could partially conflict with SAGE's spatial duality constraints.

#### 4.2. Spatial Reasoning Benchmarks

SAGE consistently enhanced the spatial reasoning capabilities of the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model across all seven dedicated spatial reasoning benchmarks (Table 2).

- **Significant Gains:** The method achieved substantial improvements on challenging tasks such as SPAR (+12.6), MindCube (+15.0), and OmniSpatial (+14.6) relative to the reproduced base model. These benchmarks require fine-grained geometric understanding and cross-view consistency.
- **Competitive Performance:** SAGE achieved overall superior or competitive performance against strong baselines like InternVL-2.5-8B and SpatialLadder-3B across most benchmarks, despite using a relatively lightweight backbone model. This suggests that SAGE imparts a generalizable inductive bias for spatial reasoning rather than overfitting to specific distributions.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics and Ablation Studies

- **Duality Consistency Evolution (Figure 3):** The duality consistency initially showed a mild V-shaped trend, with a slight early drop followed by a gradual recovery and stabilization. This indicates that initial exposure to diverse spatial patterns from the training data can temporarily perturb pre-trained alignments, but the self-evolving duality mechanism adapts and strengthens consistency over time. SAGE's average consistency was reported to be higher and more stable than Video-R1. Accuracy reward and total reward remained stable, and KL divergence remained bounded, confirming stable optimization.
- **Ablation Study (Table 3):**
 - Both visual-only (`+GRPO (Visual only)`) and linguistic-only (`+GRPO (Linguistic only)`) GRPO variants consistently improved over the pretrained model, indicating the individual contributions of each modality to video understanding.
 - The visual duality branch showed stronger gains on spatially grounded benchmarks (e.g., VSI-Bench, VideoMME), while the linguistic branch provided more balanced but generally smaller improvements. This suggests that spatial reasoning benefits more directly from visual duality constraints.
 - The full SFT+SAGE model achieved the best overall performance on most benchmarks, demonstrating the complementarity of supervised initialization and duality-driven optimization, though the gains were not strictly additive, consistent with the hypothesis of potential interference between SFT's language bias and SAGE's spatial objectives.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis

- **Operation Lifecycle Dynamics (Figure 4):** The self-evolving operation pool demonstrated a clear progression. Easier geometric operations (e.g., spatial flip, vertical flip, 180° rotation) were quickly mastered and retired, while more challenging linguistic transformations (e.g., option permutation, question negation) remained active for longer. The anti-forgetting mechanism periodically reactivated previously mastered operations, helping to maintain pool stability.
- **Impact of Duality Weight:** A duality weight (λ) of 0.3 was found to provide a balance between consistency gains and primary task performance. Higher values (e.g., above 0.5) led to increased consistency but at the cost of accuracy degradation on non-spatial questions.
- **Case Studies (Appendix E):** The paper provides visual examples of failure cases (e.g., inconsistent speed predictions after horizontal flip or 180° rotation, complete recognition failure after color inversion for a "black bear," and selecting option position instead of content after option permutation or ignoring logical negation). These examples illustrate the specific reasoning inconsistencies that SAGE aims to address.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SAGE, a framework designed to enhance the robustness of spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by enforcing geometric and linguistic duality consistency. The work addresses a notable limitation in current VLMs, where models may provide correct answers at an instance level but lack a deeper, generalizable understanding of spatial relationships, leading to fragile performance under predictable transformations. This "pseudo-understanding" is a critical issue for VLM deployment in real-world applications requiring consistent spatial awareness.

The primary significance of SAGE lies in its novel approach to training VLMs:

1. **Enforcing Logical Coherence:** By formalizing duality consistency as a training signal, SAGE moves beyond traditional accuracy metrics to directly encourage a logically coherent understanding of spatial concepts. This fundamental shift aims to instil genuine spatial reasoning, rather than merely fitting surface-level statistical patterns in data.
2. **Self-Evolving Adaptability:** The framework's self-evolving operation pool is a key innovation. It dynamically identifies and prioritizes the specific transformations or types of inconsistencies that a model currently struggles with. This adaptive mechanism allows training to focus on the most informative signals, making the learning process more efficient and targeted compared to static data augmentation or predefined perturbation sets.
3. **Model-Agnostic and Data-Efficient:** SAGE is designed as a lightweight post-training stage that can be applied to any existing VLM. Its ability to achieve consistent performance improvements using substantially less training data (less than 37% of some prior GRPO methods) contributes to the efficiency and accessibility of advanced spatial reasoning capabilities.
4. **Improved Generalization:** The consistent performance gains observed across a diverse array of video understanding and spatial reasoning benchmarks, including challenging tasks requiring fine-grained geometric understanding and cross-view consistency, indicate that SAGE improves generalization. This suggests that models trained with SAGE are better equipped to handle out-of-distribution visual arrangements and new spatial scenarios.

The potential impact of this work extends to various domains:

- **Embodied AI:** Enhanced spatial reasoning is crucial for embodied agents in robotics and virtual environments, enabling more reliable navigation, object manipulation, and interaction with the physical world.
- **Autonomous Systems:** For applications like autonomous driving, consistent understanding of relative positions, distances, and motion under varying viewpoints and conditions is paramount for safety and effectiveness.
- **General Multimodal Intelligence:** The principle of enforcing logical consistency through duality operations could be extended to other forms of reasoning invariance beyond spatial dynamics, potentially leading to more robust and generalizable multimodal foundation models.

However, the authors also acknowledge a limitation: while duality consistency encourages logical coherence, it does not strictly guarantee correctness in cases where both the original and dual predictions are consistently wrong. This highlights that consistency is a necessary but not always sufficient condition for absolute accuracy. Future work may explore automated discovery of more reliable dual operations, cross-architecture generalization, and integration of 3D multi-view consistency, further expanding the impact of this foundational research.
