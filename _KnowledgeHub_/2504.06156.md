# ViTaMIn: Learning Contact-Rich Tasks Through Robot-Free Visuo-Tactile Manipulation Interface

> [!summary] Summary
> ViTaMIn introduces a portable, robot-free visuo-tactile manipulation interface paired with a multimodal pre-training strategy that leverages masked visual and tactile inputs to learn robust representations. This system achieves success rates up to 100% on challenging contact-rich robotic tasks and substantially improves data and training efficiency.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with dexterous, contact-rich manipulation tasks due to the lack of effective tactile information integration.
- Traditional robot demonstration data collection methods are inefficient, expensive, and not scalable, while existing portable interfaces often only capture visual data.
- Multimodal data from vision and touch is often underutilized, with simple feature concatenation being suboptimal for learning robust representations.

## Method

- Developed **ViTaMIn**, a portable, robot-free visuo-tactile manipulation interface by integrating a GoPro camera with compliant, omnidirectional ==AllTact== fingers for robust data collection.
- Introduced a ==multimodal contrastive pre-training== framework that aligns combined masked visual and full tactile observations with future full visual observations in the ==CLIP== embedding space.
- Trained ==Diffusion Policy== on SLAM-filtered demonstration data using the learned visuo-tactile representations for robot control.

## Results

- ViTaMIn achieved superior success rates, reaching up to **100%** on contact-rich tasks like Orange Placement and Dual-Arm Knife Pulling, significantly outperforming vision-only baselines (e.g., **90%** vs **40%** for Test Tube Reorientation).
- Multimodal pre-training substantially improved data efficiency, allowing policies to achieve high performance with as little as **25%** of the total demonstration data.
- The system demonstrated enhanced generalization, maintaining robust performance on novel objects and under varying lighting conditions, showcasing improved adaptability for real-world deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating compliant, omnidirectional tactile sensing into a portable, robot-free interface enables scalable collection of rich multimodal data crucial for contact-rich tasks.
> - Masked contrastive pre-training effectively fuses disparate visuo-tactile modalities by forcing the tactile encoder to learn complementary information from touch to predict future visual context.
> - Leveraging 'action-free' datasets for pre-training significantly boosts data efficiency and generalization capabilities for downstream imitation learning policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

ViTaMIn addresses several critical and evolving trends within the broader robotics and artificial intelligence research landscape, particularly concerning robot manipulation and learning from demonstrations (LfD).

Firstly, the paper is deeply rooted in the paradigm of **Learning from Demonstrations (LfD)**, also known as imitation learning. LfD has emerged as a powerful approach to teach robots complex skills by observing human operators, offering an intuitive alternative to traditional programming or pure reinforcement learning from scratch. Recent progress in LfD has shown immense potential for developing general-purpose robots capable of acquiring a diverse range of complex skills.

Secondly, a significant bottleneck in scaling LfD approaches is the **efficient and high-quality data collection**. Historically, demonstration data has been collected using teleoperation systems, which involve a human operator controlling a physical robot in real-time. These systems (e.g., joint-mapped devices, exoskeletons, or vision-based teleoperation) are often expensive, time-consuming to set up, require a physical robot during collection, and limit flexibility and scalability. The research landscape has therefore seen a shift towards **portable, "embodiment-free" data collection devices**. These devices, such as UMI (which ViTaMIn explicitly builds upon), offer advantages like lower cost, independence from a specific robot, and greater ease of use, making data collection more scalable and user-friendly. However, a limitation of most portable devices has been their primary focus on *vision-only* data, restricting their applicability to tasks that require precise physical interaction.

Thirdly, ViTaMIn tackles the growing recognition of the **crucial role of multimodal sensing, specifically visuo-tactile integration**, in advanced robotic manipulation. Just as humans rely on both sight and touch for dexterous tasks like inserting a plug or tightening a screw, robots need both for contact-rich control, tasks involving visual occlusions, or in-hand manipulation. While early works combined RGB cameras with low-dimensional force/torque sensors, more recent advancements in vision-based tactile sensors have offered higher-resolution contact information. However, many of these sensors still face challenges regarding their rigid design, which can limit end-effector compliance. ViTaMIn's use of a **compliant, omnidirectional Fin Ray gripper with integrated tactile sensing (AllTact)** represents an advancement in physical sensor design, allowing for more natural and robust contact detection from multiple directions, enhancing safety and adaptability.

Finally, the work contributes to the rapidly developing area of **multimodal representation learning and pre-training for robotics**. Leveraging large-scale, often self-supervised pre-training, has dramatically improved performance and generalization in visual tasks (e.g., CLIP, MAE). The challenge is extending this success to multimodal data, particularly for aligning disparate sensory modalities like vision and touch, which have different data structures and characteristics. ViTaMIn proposes a novel masked contrastive pre-training strategy that aligns tactile and masked visual information with future visual observations. This innovative approach to pre-training addresses the data efficiency and generalization challenges inherent in learning from limited expert demonstrations.

In summary, ViTaMIn strategically positions itself at the intersection of these critical trends: it enhances the scalability of robot-free data collection by introducing crucial visuo-tactile capabilities for contact-rich tasks, and further amplifies this by developing an effective multimodal representation learning framework. By doing so, it pushes the boundaries of efficient, robust, and generalizable robot skill acquisition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ViTaMIn stems from the recognized gap between human dexterity, which inherently integrates vision and touch, and the current capabilities of robots, particularly in learning complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks. The authors identify several specific challenges:

- **The critical need for tactile information:** For humans, tactile feedback is indispensable for tasks requiring precise force control, understanding object properties through contact, or manipulating objects in-hand, especially when visual information is limited or ambiguous. Current robot learning systems, especially those relying on scalable data collection methods, often neglect this crucial modality.
- **Inefficiency and inflexibility of traditional data collection:** Conventional methods for collecting robot demonstration data, such as teleoperation, demand significant time, effort, and specific robotic hardware. This limits the scalability and flexibility of creating diverse and large-scale datasets necessary for modern robot learning paradigms.
- **Limitations of existing portable data collection devices:** While portable and embodiment-free systems (like UMI) have emerged as a scalable and cost-effective alternative to teleoperation, they predominantly focus on visual data. This makes them unsuitable for teaching robots dexterous, contact-rich manipulation tasks where tactile feedback is paramount.
- **Suboptimal utilization of multimodal data:** Simply concatenating visual and tactile features often does not fully leverage the complementary nature of these modalities. There is a need for more sophisticated multimodal representation learning strategies to extract meaningful features that enhance data efficiency, robustness, and generalizability.

Given these motivations, ViTaMIn sets forth the following key objectives:

1. **Develop a portable and embodiment-free visuo-tactile manipulation interface:** The primary objective is to create a hand-held device that integrates both visual and tactile sensing, enabling efficient and flexible collection of high-quality multimodal demonstration data without the need for real-time teleoperation of a physical robot.
2. **Integrate advanced tactile sensing:** The system aims to employ a compliant, omnidirectional tactile gripper (Fin Ray with AllTact) to capture rich contact information from all directions, allowing operators to perceive force feedback intuitively and providing expressive tactile signals for robot manipulation.
3. **Propose an effective multimodal representation learning strategy:** The goal is to develop a pre-training approach that can fuse visual and tactile information effectively, learning robust and generalizable representations. This strategy should leverage collected action-free data to improve data efficiency and policy performance for downstream tasks.
4. **Demonstrate superior performance on contact-rich manipulation tasks:** A key objective is to empirically validate that the ViTaMIn system, combined with its multimodal pre-training, significantly outperforms vision-only baselines on a diverse set of challenging real-world tasks that inherently require contact-rich interactions.
5. **Enhance data efficiency, robustness, and generalization:** The system should demonstrably improve how quickly policies learn, their resilience to variations, and their ability to perform well on unseen objects and environmental conditions.

By achieving these objectives, ViTaMIn aims to bridge the gap in robot learning, enabling robots to acquire a broader spectrum of dexterous skills with greater efficiency and robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of ViTaMIn is structured around three main components: a novel hardware system for data collection, a robust data processing pipeline, and an innovative visuo-tactile policy learning framework.

#### 3.1. Hardware System (ViTaMIn Device)

The core of ViTaMIn is a handheld, "embodiment-free" gripper designed for intuitive human operation without requiring direct control of a robot. This portable device integrates several sensors:

- **Image Observation:** A GoPro Hero10 camera with a 155° field-of-view fisheye lens is mounted at the end-effector. It captures visual information at 60 FPS with a resolution of 2704x2028 pixels, ensuring comprehensive coverage of the manipulation workspace.
- **Tactile Observation:** Unlike previous portable grippers, ViTaMIn employs two **AllTact fingers**. These are compliant Fin Ray grippers equipped with omnidirectional tactile sensing capabilities. The embedded cameras within the AllTact fingers capture both global finger deformation and local contact surface deformation as a single image at 30 FPS (640x480 pixels). This compliant design allows for expressive tactile signals crucial for contact-rich tasks.
- **Other Observations:** An additional low-cost synchronization camera connected to a Raspberry Pi 5 (which also houses the battery for data recording) is used for temporal alignment of sensor data. IMU data from the GoPro enhances SLAM robustness, and ArUco markers on the gripper fingers allow for precise gripper width computation from visual observations.

#### 3.2. Data Processing

The collected raw sensor data undergoes a crucial processing pipeline:

- **Sensor Synchronization:** To align the varying frame rates and timestamps of the GoPro and tactile sensors, the synchronization camera (naturally aligned with tactile sensors) and the GoPro simultaneously record ArUco markers displayed on a screen. By matching identical ArUco IDs in both video streams, corresponding timestamps are identified, allowing for temporal alignment with an error below 0.05 seconds.
- **Data Collection and Filtering:** Similar to the UMI system, ViTaMIn utilizes Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) to capture the 6-DOF end-effector trajectories (kinesthetic demonstrations). While SLAM can fail in low-texture environments, it achieves approximately 80% success in the authors' tasks. Only data with successful SLAM tracking is used for imitation learning, ensuring high-quality action labels.

#### 3.3. Visuo-Tactile Policy Learning

The paper's innovative contribution lies in its strategy for learning robust policies from the collected visuo-tactile data:

- **Visuo-Tactile Representation Learning (Pre-training):**
 - **Challenge:** Standard pre-trained visual encoders (like CLIP) are not suitable for tactile images, and simple concatenation of features is suboptimal.
 - **Approach:** A multimodal contrastive learning framework is introduced, leveraging "action-free" datasets (which do not depend on successful SLAM tracking) for pre-training. The objective is to train a tactile encoder (ϕ_T) to capture crucial contact properties and in-hand object pose.
 - **Mechanism (Figure 3):** Given a *current masked RGB image* (˜I_k^V) and a *current full tactile observation* (I_k^T), the system aims to align their combined, fused representation (F_k) with a *future full image observation* (I_{k+1}^V) in the CLIP embedding space.
 - **Intuition:** By masking parts of the current visual input, the tactile encoder is forced to learn complementary information from touch to "predict" or reconstruct the missing visual context for the *next* time step. This drives the tactile encoder to focus on task-relevant contact signals.
 - **Implementation:** The image CLIP encoder (ϕ_V) is frozen. The tactile encoder (ϕ_T) generates tactile embeddings (T_k). These are concatenated with masked visual embeddings (V_k) and passed through a projection layer to create a fused feature (F_k) in the 512-dimensional CLIP space. A standard CLIP loss is then applied between F_k and V_{k+1}. This differs from prior work by integrating a masked image and not requiring proprioceptive states during pre-training.
- **Policy Training:** After the multimodal pre-training, a **Diffusion Policy** is trained on the SLAM-filtered demonstration data. A U-Net serves as the noise prediction network, and DDIM is used for accelerated inference during action prediction.
- **Policy Deployment:** The learned policy is deployed on a **Rokae xMate ER3PRO 7-DOF robotic arm** equipped with a PGI-140-80-W-S parallel gripper (outfitted with the AllTact fingers). The system runs on ROS Noetic, with a 10Hz control loop and latency compensation strategies (predictive buffering, timestamp-based synchronization).

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The methodology is evaluated on five diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks: Orange Placement, Dynamic Peg Insertion, Test Tube Reorientation, Scissor Hanging, and Dual-Arm Knife Pulling. These tasks are designed to test robust pick-and-place, dexterous in-hand manipulation, dynamic actions, and precise contact control. Baselines include a vision-only policy (similar to UMI's original approach with pre-trained CLIP) and ViTaMIn without the multimodal pre-training. Performance is measured by success rates over 20 trials with randomized initial conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with ViTaMIn yield several compelling results, strongly supporting the efficacy of the proposed visuo-tactile interface and multimodal pre-training strategy.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance Across Contact-Rich Tasks

The core finding, summarized in Table II, demonstrates that ViTaMIn consistently and significantly outperforms baseline methods across all five challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks:
- **Vision-only policies** (using only visual observation encoded by pre-trained CLIP) exhibit the lowest success rates, particularly struggling with tasks demanding precise contact or tactile feedback (e.g., Test Tube Reorientation: 40%, Scissor Hanging: 10%, Dynamic Peg Insertion: 45%). This highlights the inherent limitations of unimodal vision for such tasks.
- **ViTaMIn w/o Pre-training** (which concatenates separately encoded visual and tactile observations) shows substantial improvements over vision-only (e.g., Test Tube Reorientation: 70%, Scissor Hanging: 45%). This result underscores the immediate value of incorporating tactile information itself.
- **ViTaMIn (Ours with multimodal pre-training)** achieves the highest success rates, reaching 100% for Orange Placement, 90% for Dynamic Peg Insertion, 90% for Test Tube Reorientation, 70% for Scissor Hanging, and 90% for Dual-Arm Knife Pulling. The consistent gains demonstrate the synergistic effect of both multimodal sensing and the proposed representation learning strategy.

#### 4.2. Failure Analysis Reinforces Need for Tactile Feedback

The analysis of failure cases further elucidates why tactile sensing is critical. Vision-only methods frequently fail due to:
- Imprecise localization and alignment (e.g., Dynamic Peg Insertion).
- Unstable placement or collisions (e.g., Orange Placement).
- Inability to accurately perceive and correct for contact interactions (e.g., Test Tube Reorientation, Scissor Hanging, where issues like rack collisions, over-lifting, misalignment, and incorrect releases are common).
- Poor coordination and weak grasps in dual-arm tasks (e.g., Knife Pulling).
These observations solidify the argument that tactile feedback is indispensable for robust performance in dexterous and contact-rich scenarios.

#### 4.3. Demonstrated Compliance in Force-Sensitive Tasks

Beyond task success rates, ViTaMIn's compliant tactile fingers enable more nuanced control. In a compliant-controlled articulated object manipulation task (flipping a switch while minimizing axial forces), ViTaMIn achieved significantly lower average forces compared to a pure vision input policy. This highlights the system's ability to perform force-sensitive operations smoothly and safely, showcasing the advantage of the compliant Fin Ray gripper with tactile feedback.

#### 4.4. Improved Data and Training Efficiency

Ablation studies reveal the significant impact of the multimodal pre-training:
- **Data Efficiency (Figure 8):** Policies trained with pre-trained tactile representations achieve consistently higher success rates across varying amounts of demonstration data (25%, 50%, 100%). Notably, for tasks like Test Tube Reorientation, the pre-trained policy can master the task even with only 25% of the total demonstration data, demonstrating a remarkable improvement in data efficiency.
- **Training Efficiency (Figure 9):** Pre-training also accelerates learning. Policies leveraging pre-trained representations are able to complete the initial stages of tasks at a remarkably early point in training (e.g., within 10 epochs). The overall success rates increase more rapidly, indicating faster convergence and more efficient use of computational resources.

#### 4.5. Enhanced Generalization Capability

ViTaMIn's ability to generalize to unseen objects and varying environmental conditions is also superior (Table III, Figure 7):
- For tasks like Orange Placement and Scissor Hanging, the pre-trained multimodal policy maintains robust performance when introduced to novel objects and different lighting conditions (e.g., increased brightness, colored disco ball lighting).
- Compared to baselines, ViTaMIn shows significantly higher success rates in these generalization settings (e.g., for Orange Placement with novel objects: Vision 70% vs. Ours 100%; with different lighting: Vision 55% vs. Ours 85%). This indicates that the learned visuo-tactile representations are more robust and less susceptible to changes in visual appearance or environment, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

In conclusion, the main findings robustly demonstrate that integrating a portable visuo-tactile interface with a sophisticated multimodal representation learning strategy dramatically enhances a robot's ability to learn and execute complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks with greater success, efficiency, and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViTaMIn represents a significant leap forward in the field of robot learning and manipulation, with profound implications for future research and practical applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Democratizing High-Quality Data Collection:** By introducing a portable, "robot-free" visuo-tactile manipulation interface, ViTaMIn drastically lowers the barrier to entry for collecting rich, multimodal demonstration data. Unlike traditional teleoperation systems that are expensive and robot-specific, ViTaMIn offers a cost-effective, flexible, and scalable solution. This could accelerate research by making advanced data collection accessible to a broader range of labs and researchers, fostering larger and more diverse datasets for robot learning.

2. **Unlocking Dexterous and Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The ability to effectively capture and utilize tactile information through a compliant, omnidirectional sensor is a game-changer for tasks requiring fine motor control, precise force application, or interactions where vision is insufficient (e.g., pushing a button with appropriate force, reorienting objects in-hand, dealing with transparent or occluded items). ViTaMIn enables robots to learn skills that more closely approximate human-level dexterity in complex physical interactions.

3. **Advancing Multimodal Representation Learning:** The proposed multimodal contrastive pre-training strategy is a key technical contribution. By learning to align masked visual information with tactile signals to predict future visual states, the system creates more robust and expressive tactile representations. This approach improves data efficiency, meaning robots can learn complex skills from fewer human demonstrations, and accelerates training, making the development cycle faster and less computationally intensive. This methodology could inspire new strategies for multimodal sensor fusion in robotics and beyond.

4. **Enhancing Robustness and Generalization for Real-World Deployment:** A major hurdle in deploying robots outside controlled lab environments is their lack of robustness to novelty and variations. ViTaMIn's demonstrated ability to generalize well to novel objects and different lighting conditions is critical. This translates to robots that are more reliable and adaptable in unstructured, dynamic real-world settings, reducing the need for extensive retraining for every new object or slight environmental change.

5. **Foundation for Future General-Purpose Robots:** This work contributes directly to the long-term vision of developing general-purpose robots capable of performing a vast array of tasks in human environments. By providing a scalable way to collect diverse, multimodal data for complex skills and a robust method to learn from it, ViTaMIn paves the way for robots that can adapt and learn new skills continuously, moving closer to true intelligent autonomy.

6. **Extensibility and New Research Avenues:** The paper concludes by acknowledging that while it focuses on fixed-base single-arm and dual-arm tasks with parallel-jaw grippers, future work could extend this approach to more complex robotic hands (dexterous hands), enabling an even richer repertoire of manipulation skills. Moreover, the integration principles could be expanded to other modalities like audio or more granular proprioception, leading to even more comprehensive sensory understanding for robots. The concept of compliant, sensorized grippers in a portable data collection context is likely to be widely adopted and refined.

In essence, ViTaMIn is not just an incremental improvement but a foundational step towards building more capable, adaptable, and human-like robots by addressing critical challenges in data collection, sensor fusion, and skill learning for contact-rich manipulation.
