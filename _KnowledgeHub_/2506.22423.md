# ARMOR: Robust Reinforcement Learning-based Control for UAVs under Physical Attacks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of British Columbia and UC Berkeley developed ARMOR, an adaptive robust reinforcement learning controller for UAVs that maintains safe operation and completes missions despite adversarial sensor manipulation. It achieves this by learning robust latent state representations through a two-stage training framework, resulting in an 88% mission success rate under various physical attacks and superior zero-shot generalization to unseen attack types.

## Problem

- Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) rely on onboard sensors for control, but these sensors are vulnerable to physical attacks (e.g., GPS spoofing, gyroscope tampering).
- Corrupted sensor data leads to inaccurate state estimates, causing unsafe control actions, trajectory deviations, and potential UAV crashes.
- Existing safe RL methods are ineffective when state perception itself is manipulated, and adversarial training incurs high costs and poor generalization to novel attack patterns.

## Method

- **ARMOR** proposes a model-free ==reinforcement learning== controller that explicitly designs robust latent state representations to withstand physical attacks on UAV sensors.
- It employs a two-stage offline training framework: a 'teacher encoder' leverages privileged attack information in simulation to learn robust latent states, and a 'student encoder' then approximates these states using only historical sensor data for real-world deployment.
- The same RL control policy is reused across both stages, accepting the robust latent representation as input, which enhances robustness and training efficiency.

## Results

- ARMOR achieved an average mission success rate of **88%** and 0 crashes across five types of physical attacks (GPS, gyroscope, accelerometer, magnetometer, optical flow), consistently outperforming the HRP baseline.
- The framework demonstrated significantly faster training convergence (approximately **2x** faster than RARL) under adversarial conditions while achieving comparable or slightly superior performance.
- ARMOR exhibited strong zero-shot generalization to unseen single and multi-sensor attack types, achieving up to **70%** success rates and reducing state drift by up to 10 times compared to RARL, which often failed (**0%** success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning of robust latent state representations from the control policy enables greater resilience against physical sensor attacks.
> - Leveraging privileged information during an initial teacher training phase allows for efficient learning of robust control policies that can be transferred to a student model for practical deployment.
> - Utilizing historical sensor data within the student encoder allows it to implicitly infer and mitigate the effects of adversarial disturbances without direct knowledge of the attack.
