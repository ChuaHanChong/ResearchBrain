# Imitation Learning based Alternative Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization for Well-Formed Swarm-Oriented Pursuit Avoidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Imitation learning based Alternative Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (IA-MAPPO) is presented as a hierarchical framework for decentralized multi-robot systems to perform adaptive pursuit avoidance tasks with well-formed swarms. This method significantly reduces communication overheads by up to 65.9% compared to centralized approaches while maintaining comparable pursuit avoidance performance, achieved by integrating imitation learning with an alternative training phase to mitigate performance degradation.

## Problem

- Large-scale Multi-Robot Systems (MRS) face high communication overheads in centralized control schemes, limiting scalability for complex tasks like Formation, Monitoring, and pursuit Avoidance (FMA).
- Decentralized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methods struggle with vast action spaces and sparse rewards, making it difficult for agents to spontaneously achieve consensus for flexible formation control.
- Imitation learning, while useful for decentralization, often leads to performance degradation due to cumulative errors between imitated and practical execution trajectories.

## Method

- A hierarchical control framework, IA-MAPPO, is developed, combining MAPPO for specific formation control, policy distillation for a mixed-formation policy, imitation learning for division control, and an alternative training phase.
- A single mixed-formation 'student' policy is learned via policy distillation from multiple 'teacher' policies, enabling flexible switching between formation patterns with reduced memory footprint.
- A decentralized division control policy is trained using imitation learning to emulate a centralized expert's decisions for adaptive formation switching based on local observations and limited neighbor communication.
- An 'Alternative Training' phase fine-tunes the mixed-formation policy with task-oriented rewards to rectify behaviors and compensate for performance degradation caused by imitation learning.

## Results

- IA-MAPPO achieved a substantial reduction in communication overheads, averaging **5.15** KB per episode compared to **13.14** KB for centralized (CE-MAPPO) and **10.18** KB for leader-follower (LF-MAPPO) approaches.
- The algorithm demonstrated pursuit avoidance performance (average reward) comparable to centralized solutions, indicating that the alternative training effectively mitigated performance degradation from imitation learning.
- IA-MAPPO exhibited improved robustness with fewer collisions with adversaries and fewer communication disconnections between agents compared to purely imitation-learned or random division baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical control effectively manages the complexity of simultaneous formation control and adaptive switching in large-scale MRS, separating high-level decision-making from low-level execution.
> - Policy distillation provides an efficient mechanism to consolidate knowledge from multiple specialized policies into a single generalist policy, improving agent efficiency and adaptability.
> - An alternative training phase is crucial for compensating for the performance loss inherent in imitation learning, allowing decentralized policies to achieve performance levels comparable to centralized baselines.
> - Limited, local communication for confidence aggregation enables decentralized agents to achieve collective consensus on formation changes, drastically reducing global communication overheads.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses cooperative control in Multi-Robot Systems (MRS), a field experiencing increased interest due to the potential of robotic swarms in various applications. Specifically, it focuses on the complex task of Formation, Monitoring, and pursuit Avoidance (FMA) in decentralized large-scale MRS. This task demands high levels of coordination and adaptability from individual agents.

Previous research in MRS cooperative control has explored various approaches:
- **Flocking Control Systems:** Some studies, such as and, have presented systems for static obstacle avoidance or bypassing predefined predator trajectories. However, these often exhibit limited flexibility (e.g., invariant formations) and operate in oversimplified scenarios, making them less suitable for dynamic, unpredictable environments.
- **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL):** MARL algorithms like Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) have been applied to formation control. Approaches such as combining MAPPO with policy distillation for multi-formation assignment, or incorporating attention mechanisms for stability in communication-limited scenarios, have demonstrated adaptability. However, these methods often struggle with unpredictable factors like malicious predator actions, as large-scale MRS agents may not spontaneously achieve policy consensus in a distributed manner.
- **Centralized Control Schemes:** These offer stability and synchronism but impose substantial communication overheads, limiting their scalability in large-scale MRS.
- **Decentralized MARL:** While aiming for distributed decision-making, conventional MARL algorithms face challenges due to the immense joint action space and the sparsity of meaningful environmental rewards, making it difficult for agents to discover effective cooperative behaviors.
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** IL can aid in policy convergence by emulating centralized policy-driven trajectories for decentralized execution. However, direct application of IL often leads to performance degradation due to cumulative errors between the imitated samples and practical execution trajectories.

This paper contributes to the landscape by attempting to bridge the gap between the robustness of centralized control and the scalability of decentralized systems. It specifically aims to provide a solution that achieves flexible formation control in complex pursuit avoidance tasks while significantly reducing communication overheads typical of centralized systems, and simultaneously mitigating the performance degradation commonly associated with decentralized imitation learning approaches. The work seeks to provide a more practical and communication-efficient solution for large-scale MRS deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a flexible and communication-economic solution for executing pursuit avoidance tasks within well-formed swarms in decentralized large-scale Multi-Robot Systems (MRS). This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Enhance Formation Control Flexibility:** Existing FMA (Formation, Monitoring, and Avoidance) work, such as those described in and, often implement invariant formations, which lack the adaptability required for dynamic and unpredictable scenarios. A key objective is to introduce a mechanism that allows for swift and adaptive switching between multiple predefined formation patterns in response to environmental cues, such as the proximity of an adversary.

2. **Reduce Communication Overheads in Decentralized Control:** While decentralized control is crucial for scalability in large-scale MRS, achieving consensus-oriented formation control has been challenging. Centralized schemes, while stable, are communication-intensive. Current MARL approaches, when attempting distributed policy consensus, often face issues with sparse rewards and high-dimensional action spaces. The authors aim to devise a communication-efficient decentralized solution that addresses these practical limitations.

3. **Mitigate Performance Degradation from Imitation Learning:** Imitation Learning (IL) offers a pathway to decentralized execution by learning from centralized policies. However, IL-induced cumulative errors can lead to a noticeable drop in performance compared to the original centralized policy. A core objective is to compensate for this performance degradation, ensuring that the decentralized system maintains a high level of task efficacy.

4. **Develop a Hierarchical Control Framework:** To manage the complexity of simultaneous formation control and adaptive switching, the research aims to establish a hierarchical control framework. This involves a high-level policy for determining when to switch formations (division control) and a low-level policy for executing the specific formation patterns (mixed-formation policy).

5. **Achieve Competitive Performance with Reduced Costs:** Ultimately, the motivation is to develop an algorithm that yields performance comparable to or close to a centralized solution, but with significantly lower communication overheads, thereby offering a more practical and scalable approach for real-world MRS applications. This includes improving resilience against adversary actions and maintaining swarm integrity within a target area.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithm, **Imitation learning based Alternative Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (IA-MAPPO)**, is designed as a hierarchical proactive formation control framework. It integrates MAPPO, policy distillation, imitation learning, and alternative training to address the FMA task in a communication-efficient and decentralized manner.

**A. MAPPO-based Specific Formation Control:**
The foundation involves training individual policies for various fixed formation patterns. For each formation $F_c$, a policy $\pi_{c,i}$ is learned for agent $i$ using the conventional MAPPO algorithm. MAPPO employs a Centralized Training Decentralized Execution (CTDE) architecture and importance sampling. All agents are assumed homogeneous, sharing the same policy parameter $\theta$.
- **Objective:** The parameters $\theta$ (actor network) and $\psi$ (critic network) are updated periodically to maximize the policy objective $J_{\pi_i}(\theta)$ (based on clipped importance ratios) and minimize the value function loss $J_V(\psi)$ (MSE between predicted and actual value with advantage estimation $\hat{A}(t)$).
- **Reward Function:** A global reward $R(t)$ is defined, comprising:
 - **Formation reward $R_f(t)$:** Punishes deviations from the target formation using Hausdorff Distance (HD).
 - **Target area reward $R_a(t)$:** Penalizes agents for straying outside a circular target area.
 - **Evasion reward $R_e(t)$:** Punishes collisions between agents and the adversary.

**B. Mixed-Formation Policy (Policy Distillation):**
To reduce the memory footprint of agents, a single "student" mixed-formation policy $\pi_s$ is learned to encapsulate the capabilities of all specific formation policies.
- **Teacher Models:** The individually trained policies $\{\pi_{c,i}\}$ for each $F_c$ act as teacher models.
- **Data Collection:** A replay buffer $B$ is populated with input-output pairs $\langle(o_c, a_c)\rangle$ from these teacher models, where $o_c$ is the observation and $a_c$ is the action taken by the teacher for formation $F_c$. Observations are zero-padded to align dimensions.
- **Training:** The student policy $\pi_s$ (with parameters $\theta_s$) is trained by minimizing the Mean-Squared-Error (MSE) loss between its outputs and the teacher models' outputs for the same observations, effectively distilling the knowledge from multiple teachers into a single student policy.

**C. Division Control:**
This high-level policy $\pi_f$ is responsible for adaptively deciding when and how to switch between formation patterns. It comprises three sub-procedures:
- **1. Policy Imitation:**
 - **Centralized Policy (${\pi_f}^*$):** An "expert" centralized policy determines the optimal formation $F_c$ for agent $i$ (represented as a C-length one-hot vector $z_i(t)$) based on global state $s(t)$ and predefined safety distance intervals $\delta_{c,\text{safe}}$ relative to the adversary's position.
 - **Decentralized Policy ($\pi_f$):** A decentralized policy network $\pi_f$ (parameterized by $\theta_f$, shared among agents) takes local observation $o_i(t)$ as input and outputs a C-dimension formation confidence vector $\hat{z}_i(t)$.
 - **Imitation Learning:** $\theta_f$ is updated by minimizing the MSE loss between $\hat{z}_i(t)$ and $z_i(t)$ (the labels generated by the centralized policy), allowing the decentralized policy to emulate the expert's decisions.
- **2. Confidence Communication:** After calculating its $\hat{z}_i(t)$, agent $i$ broadcasts this confidence vector to its neighbors within a communication range $\delta_{com}$. It also receives confidence vectors $\{\hat{z}_j(t)\}$ from its neighbors $j \in \xi_i(t)$, forming an aggregated confidence matrix $\hat{Z}_i^+(t)$.
- **3. Masking Operation:** This procedure uses the aggregated confidence to determine the next formation and reshape observations for the low-level policy.
 - **Information Aggregation:** The received confidence vectors in $\hat{Z}_i^+(t)$ are aggregated (column-summed) to produce a C-length importance vector $\phi_i(t)$, indicating the collective preference for each formation pattern.
 - **Formation Pattern Determination:** The formation pattern $c^\sharp$ for the next time-step is chosen by selecting the index corresponding to the maximum value in $\phi_i(t)$.
 - **Observation Reshape:** Based on $c^\sharp$, agent $i$ retrieves the list of its teammates $m_{c^\sharp,i}$ from a pre-stored teammate matrix $M_i^C$. Its raw observation $o_i(t)$ is then reshaped into $h'_i(t)$, including its own raw information and that of its designated teammates, which serves as input to the mixed-formation policy $\pi_s$.

**D. Alternative Training:**
To address the performance degradation caused by cumulative errors inherent in imitation learning, an Alternative Training (AT) phase is introduced to fine-tune the mixed-formation policy $\pi_s$.
- **Interaction and Trajectory Collection:** After the division policy $\pi_f$ is learned, the agents interact with the environment using the joint actions (division instructions $\hat{z}_i(t)$ from $\pi_f$ and acceleration actions $a_i(t)$ from $\pi_s$). Trajectories are collected.
- **Task-Oriented Reward:** A specific alternative training reward $R_{at}(o_t)$ is designed to evaluate the state $o_t$. This reward primarily penalizes the average distance between different sub-groups of agents, encouraging unification and preventing fragmentation.
- **Fine-tuning:** The collected trajectories, along with $R_{at}$, are then used to fine-tune the parameters $\theta$ of the mixed-formation policy $\pi_s$ using the MAPPO objective function (6). This aims to rectify undesired behaviors that might arise from the IL-induced deviations in the division policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in a multi-agent particle environment focusing on an FMA task involving transformation between an 8-agent formation ($F_8$) (low velocity, unite) and a 4-agent formation ($F_4$) (high velocity, split) to avoid an adversary while staying within a target area. The performance was quantified by the total reward $R(t)$ defined in the methodology.

The IA-MAPPO algorithm was compared against several baselines:
- **RAN-MAPPO:** Mixed-formation policy with randomly generated division instructions.
- **CE-MAPPO:** Centralized division policy ($\pi_f^*$) with mixed-formation policy ($\pi_s$).
- **AT-MAPPO:** CE-MAPPO with $\pi_s$ further fine-tuned via Alternative Training (AT).
- **LF-MAPPO:** Leader-follower division policy with mixed-formation policy.
- **IL-MAPPO:** IA-MAPPO without the Alternative Training (AT) component.

The key findings are presented as follows:

1. **Communication Overheads:**
 - The study quantified communication costs in terms of data volume per episode for division control, assuming specific bit lengths for position, velocity, and instruction data.
 - **Centralized (CE/RAN-MAPPO) and Leader-Follower (LF-MAPPO) systems exhibited substantially higher communication overheads.** For instance, CE-MAPPO incurred approximately 13.14 KB, and LF-MAPPO incurred 10.18 KB.
 - **Decentralized systems (IL-MAPPO and IA-MAPPO) demonstrated significant reductions in communication overheads.** IL-MAPPO averaged 4.48 KB, and IA-MAPPO averaged 5.15 KB.
 - Quantitatively, IL-MAPPO and IA-MAPPO reduced communication costs to approximately 34.1%/39.2% of CE-MAPPO and 44.0%/50.6% of LF-MAPPO, respectively. This aligns with the problem formulation's objective of communication efficiency.

2. **Pursuit Avoidance Performance (Average Reward $R(t)$):**
 - **RAN-MAPPO exhibited the worst performance**, indicating the ineffectiveness of random division instructions, leading to frequent communication disconnections and collisions with the adversary.
 - **CE-MAPPO yielded comparatively better performance than IL-MAPPO.** This observation highlights the initial performance degradation when shifting from a centralized division policy to a decentralized one learned solely through imitation learning, as discussed in Section III-D.
 - **IL-MAPPO, while communication-efficient, showed reduced performance** in maintaining connections and ensuring safety (more disconnections and collisions) compared to centralized solutions. This confirms the theoretical performance degradation due to cumulative errors from imitation learning.
 - **IA-MAPPO, by incorporating Alternative Training (AT), achieved performance comparable to CE-MAPPO.** The average reward of IA-MAPPO closely matched that of CE-MAPPO. This indicates that AT effectively compensated for the performance loss incurred by the decentralization through imitation learning.
 - **Analysis of Events (Collisions, Disconnections):** IA-MAPPO, similar to AT-MAPPO (centralized with AT), showed fewer collisions with the adversary and fewer communication disconnections between agents. This improvement contributes to its higher average reward.
 - **Effectiveness of Alternative Training:** The improvement in performance due to AT was attributed to the refined mixed-formation policy ($\pi_s$) and the positive impact of the task-oriented alternative training reward ($R_{at}$), which encouraged group cohesion and rectified undesired group dynamics. The variation of $R_{at}(t)$ during fine-tuning visually supported this improvement.

In summary, the results validate that IA-MAPPO provides a communication-efficient decentralized solution for the FMA task, achieving performance levels comparable to centralized approaches, largely due to the mitigating effects of alternative training on imitation learning's performance degradation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Multi-Robot Systems (MRS) and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), particularly for complex cooperative tasks like Formation, Monitoring, and pursuit Avoidance (FMA). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addresses the Centralized vs. Decentralized Trade-off:** The work offers a method to overcome the limitations of both centralized (high communication cost, low scalability) and purely decentralized (performance degradation, difficulty with sparse rewards) control schemes. By combining policy distillation, imitation learning, and alternative training within a hierarchical framework, IA-MAPPO provides a solution that is both scalable and robust.

2. **Communication Efficiency for Large-Scale MRS:** The significant reduction in communication overheads achieved by IA-MAPPO is critical for the deployment of large-scale MRS in real-world scenarios. In environments where communication bandwidth, energy, or latency are constrained, a communication-economic solution like IA-MAPPO can enable more practical and efficient swarm operations. This is particularly relevant for applications like environmental monitoring, disaster response, and autonomous surveillance.

3. **Enhanced Flexibility in Swarm Behavior:** The ability of agents to swiftly and adaptively switch between multiple formation patterns, enabled by the mixed-formation policy and division control, adds a crucial layer of flexibility. This allows swarms to respond dynamically to changing environmental conditions, such as the unpredictable actions of an adversary, making them more resilient and effective in complex tasks.

4. **Mitigation of Imitation Learning Limitations:** The introduction of Alternative Training (AT) to compensate for the performance loss typically incurred by imitation learning is a notable methodological advancement. This technique enhances the practicality of using imitation learning to decentralize complex control policies, ensuring that the benefits of decentralization do not come at an unacceptable cost to task performance.

5. **Foundation for Future Research and Applications:** The IA-MAPPO framework provides a robust foundation for future research. The authors explicitly mention plans to enlarge swarm scales, enrich formation patterns, and incorporate more practical factors such as communication delay. This suggests the potential for developing even more sophisticated and realistic MRS capabilities based on this work, pushing towards more autonomous and intelligent robotic swarms.

In essence, this research moves MRS closer to practical, large-scale deployments by addressing key challenges in decentralized control, communication efficiency, and adaptive behavior, with potential impacts across various civilian and military applications requiring complex cooperative control.
