# DreamControl-v2: Simpler and Scalable Autonomous Humanoid Skills via Trainable Guided Diffusion Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamControl-v2 presents a framework for autonomous humanoid skills, advancing its predecessor by training a guided diffusion model directly in the robot's motion space. This method, achieved through pre-retargeting diverse human and robot datasets, eliminates manual prompt calibration, scales the acquisition of complex loco-manipulation tasks, and yields more robust policies validated on a Unitree-G1 robot.

## Problem

- Prior methods like DreamControl faced scalability issues due to a 'generate-then-retarget' pipeline, requiring manual, trial-and-error adjustments of spatial conditions in human body space to fit robot embodiments.
- The repertoire of learnable skills was limited by generative priors trained on constrained human motion data, necessitating task-specific workarounds for novel or complex interactions.
- Manual interventions were required for filtering generated trajectories to ensure physical plausibility and task relevance, hindering the automation and efficiency of the skill acquisition process.

## Method

- A ==guided diffusion model== is trained directly in the target **Unitree-G1** robot's motion space, bypassing the 'generate-then-retarget' problem and enabling direct ==spatial guidance== for trajectory generation.
- Diverse human (**AMASS**, **HumanML3D**, **GRAB**, **Nymeria**) and robot (**OmniRetarget**) motion datasets are aggregated and ==pre-retargeted== into a unified G1 robot embodiment space before diffusion model training.
- Automated post-processing and filtering steps are implemented using error-based heuristics to ensure generated trajectories are physically plausible and task-feasible, followed by ==RL policy training== for ==physics-based motion imitation==.

## Results

- The 'Full-Mix' diffusion model achieved superior trajectory quality with a FID score of **0.265** (compared to 0.981 for zero-shot baseline) and demonstrated improved generalization to non-AMASS tasks.
- RL policies trained with trajectories from the improved diffusion models showed substantially higher success ratios, achieving **0.925** for complex Group-1 tasks (e.g., Drawer Open) compared to 0.101 for the zero-shot baseline.
- The pre-retargeting approach yielded a significantly higher valid trajectory rate (**68%**) compared to inference-time prompt calibration (**8%**), effectively eliminating labor-intensive manual tuning.
- The framework successfully enabled the acquisition and real-world deployment of 8 diverse loco-manipulation skills on a physical **Unitree-G1** humanoid robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a diffusion model directly in the robot's motion space, rather than human space, significantly enhances the scalability and automation of generating high-quality reference trajectories by eliminating manual prompt calibration.
> - Aggregating and normalizing a 'Full-Mix' of diverse human and robot motion data into a unified robot representation broadens the learnable skill repertoire and improves the generalization capabilities of the generative prior.
> - Scaling the quantity and diversity of high-quality diffusion-generated reference trajectories directly correlates with the acquisition of more robust and generalizable downstream RL policies for complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust autonomous loco-manipulation skills for humanoid robots presents a significant challenge in robotics. While Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved success in legged locomotion, its application to complex, interaction-rich manipulation tasks is more difficult due to the long-horizon planning requirements.

Existing research has explored several avenues to address this challenge. One approach involves teleoperating humanoid robots to collect data, which is effective for specific tasks but can be expensive and difficult to scale. Another prominent trend utilizes generative priors, such as diffusion models, to synthesize plausible robot trajectories, often conditioned on textual descriptions. These generated trajectories then serve as targets for RL policies, aiming to produce human-like motions that facilitate sim-to-real transfer.

DreamControl, a recent framework, exemplifies this latter approach. It employs a two-stage pipeline where a guided diffusion model generates human-centric motion data, which is then retargeted to a specific robot form factor (e.g., Unitree-G1). An RL policy subsequently learns to track these "dreamed" trajectories while performing a task, guided by both text and spatio-temporal constraints. This enables autonomous environmental interactions.

However, DreamControl has exhibited limitations in scalability and general utility. A primary bottleneck stems from its "generate-then-retarget" pipeline. The diffusion model, trained on human motion capture (mocap) data (e.g., AMASS) and operating in human body space, generates trajectories that, after retargeting, may not accurately preserve the original spatial constraints in the robot's embodiment. This often necessitates manual, trial-and-error adjustments of input spatial conditions in human space, a labor-intensive process that hinders scalability, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks. Furthermore, existing human motion models like OmniControl are primarily designed for AMASS-style actions, which may not be suitable for diverse robotics tasks. DreamControl addressed these limitations with task-specific workarounds, such as Inverse Kinematics (IK) for unrepresented tasks and manual filtering of generated trajectories, further restricting its generalizability.

DreamControl-v2 positions itself as an advancement addressing these specific scalability and utility issues. It proposes an improved framework that trains a guided diffusion model *directly* in the humanoid robot's motion space. This is achieved by pre-retargeting a diverse range of human and robot datasets into a unified robot embodiment space, prior to diffusion model training. This strategy aims to create a more automated and scalable pipeline by removing manual interventions and expanding the range of learnable skills. The work relates to broader efforts in motion synthesis for robots, physics-based motion imitation, and the utilization of diverse data sources for humanoid loco-manipulation, specifically by reformulating motion tracking as a reward signal to enable autonomous execution and leveraging large human motion datasets through a robot-centric representation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a more scalable and automated framework for acquiring robust autonomous loco-manipulation skills for humanoid robots, building upon and addressing the limitations identified in prior approaches like DreamControl.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Eliminate Manual Prompt Calibration:** The original DreamControl framework required manual, trial-and-error adjustments of spatial conditions in human body space. This was necessary because human-generated motions, when retargeted to a robot, would often not satisfy the intended robot-space spatial constraints. This process was labor-intensive, particularly for long-horizon and high-dimensional tasks, and represented a significant bottleneck to scalability. DreamControl-v2 aims to remove this requirement by enabling direct spatial guidance within the robot's state space, thereby simplifying the prompting process.

2. **Broaden the Repertoire of Learnable Skills:** Previous methods were often constrained by the distribution of human motion data available to their generative priors (e.g., AMASS-style actions). This necessitated task-specific workarounds, such as using Inverse Kinematics for tasks not natively supported by the human motion model (e.g., "Drawer Open"). A key objective is to expand the range of motions and interactions the system can generate and learn, enabling the acquisition of diverse, complex, and interaction-rich loco-manipulation tasks without specialized fixes.

3. **Automate Trajectory Generation and Filtering:** The original DreamControl pipeline involved manual filtering of generated trajectories to ensure physical plausibility and task relevance. This human intervention limited the automation and scalability of the system. DreamControl-v2 seeks to establish a more automated pipeline by training a native humanoid diffusion prior that inherently generates cleaner, task-ready trajectories and by implementing automated filtering mechanisms.

4. **Improve Scalability of Reference Trajectory Generation:** The cumulative effect of manual prompt tuning, reliance on limited data distributions, and manual filtering in DreamControl impeded its scalability. The motivation for DreamControl-v2 is to create a streamlined pipeline that can generate large numbers of high-quality reference trajectories more efficiently, which is considered important for achieving robust downstream RL policies.

5. **Enhance Robustness of Downstream RL Policies:** The quality, diversity, and task-relevance of the reference trajectories provided to the RL policy directly influence the performance and robustness of the learned robot skills. By improving the generative prior through direct robot-space training and expanded data sources, DreamControl-v2 aims to yield stronger and more generalizable RL policies.

In essence, DreamControl-v2 is motivated by the need for a simpler, more automated, and more scalable method for autonomous humanoid skill acquisition, moving away from human-centric generation and manual interventions towards a robot-centric, data-driven approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DreamControl-v2 framework maintains the two-stage structure of its predecessor (trajectory generation followed by RL policy training) but introduces significant modifications to the first stage, focusing on training a diffusion prior directly in the target robot's motion space. The methodology can be segmented into four main stages: data aggregation and retargeting, diffusion prior training, RL policy training, and sim-to-real deployment.

**A. Massive Human Data to Robot-Space**
The initial step involves constructing a large-scale dataset suitable for robot motion generation. This is achieved by unifying diverse human and robot motion data into the kinematic space of a specific humanoid robot, the Unitree-G1.

1. **Data Sources:** The data pool integrates several large-scale human motion datasets: AMASS/HumanML3D (locomotion, everyday activities), GRAB (human-object interactions), and Nymeria (multimodal egocentric daily motion). Additionally, robot trajectories from OmniRetarget, which are derived from human data refined with RL and physics-based methods, are included to provide bimanual manipulation motions. This mixture aims to cover a broad range of whole-body interaction behaviors.
2. **Human-to-Robot Retargeting:** All human motion trajectories are retargeted to the G1 humanoid. This process identifies 12 key points common to both human and robot (hips, knees, ankles, shoulders, elbows, wrists). An optimization problem (Eq. 1) is solved to determine the G1’s joint angles (q_robot) and global poses (T_robot) at each timestep, minimizing discrepancies in relative joint angles, relative keypoint positions, foot slip, and accounting for limb length differences. The retargeting system is built using `pyroki` with JAX-based solvers, enhanced for efficiency through single compilation, caching, and multi-thread processing.
3. **Handling Non-SMPL Parameterizations:** For datasets like Nymeria that use alternative kinematic skeletons (e.g., Xsens) instead of the SMPL model, an additional optimization (Eq. 2) recovers SMPL parameters (joint angles θ, global pose T_pose) that best fit the Xsens keypoint observations. This ensures consistency across diverse human data sources.
4. **Filtering and Pre-processing:** Raw trajectories undergo filtering. Feasibility filters from PHC are applied to remove motions infeasible without environment context. Keyword-based retrieval is used to extract task-specific segments from Nymeria (e.g., for "drawer" or "cabinet" interactions). GRAB and OmniRetarget sequences are used without additional filtering.

**B. Diffusion Prior Training**
A guided diffusion model is trained on the robot-space dataset to generate reference trajectories.

1. **Trajectory Parameterization:** The trajectories are represented using a canonicalized SMPL format, which defines 22 keypoints within a local, root-centered coordinate frame. G1 trajectories are aligned to this format. Each trajectory is resampled to 20 FPS, cropped/padded to a maximum of 10 seconds, and canonicalized so the root at t=0 faces the Z+ direction. Each pose is encoded as a 263-dimensional vector comprising root velocities, root height, joint positions, velocities, 6D rotations, and foot contact features.
2. **Training Strategy:** The OmniControl architecture, initialized with MDM weights, is adopted for training. The diffusion model learns to denoise perturbed trajectories. For controllable generation, a realism-guidance module (similar to ControlNet) modulates transformer features based on open-vocabulary text instructions and spatio-temporal control signals (3D positions for specific joints at specific times). This module learns to enforce spatial constraints, physical plausibility, and reduce artifacts.
3. **Sampling:** During inference, OmniControl's spatial guidance (Eq. 3) and Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) are directly applied. An MSE loss guides the generation process by shifting the trajectory towards desired target positions.
4. **Post-Processing and Filtering:** Generated trajectories are converted into executable robot motions (joint angles and global poses) via a post-optimization module. Automated filtering steps employ error-based heuristics (e.g., L2 distance between hand and drawer handle, root height thresholds, foot stability, obstacle avoidance) to ensure physical plausibility and task feasibility, discarding unsuitable trajectories.

**C. RL for Physics-Based Motion Imitation**
The generated, physics-free whole-body trajectories serve as references for an RL policy, which is trained to track them while accomplishing tasks.

1. **Reference Trajectory Parameterization for RL:** The RL policy receives a set of multiple reference trajectories, each including the post-processed diffusion output and binary hand open/closed states inferred from the spatio-temporal conditioning.
2. **Action Space:** The robot’s action space comprises 27 target joint angles for the G1 humanoid (with waist pitch and roll locked) and two binary commands for the Inspire hands.
3. **Observation Space:** Observations include real-robot proprioceptive signals and object poses expressed in the robot’s local root frame, facilitating sim-to-real transfer. Reward signals are designed for both task completion and trajectory tracking.

**D. Sim2Real Deployment**
The learned policies are evaluated extensively in simulation and subsequently deployed on a real Unitree-G1 robot. The real robot uses its onboard IMU for state estimation. The trained skill library encompasses 8 diverse tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings based on extensive experiments in simulation and real-world validation on a Unitree-G1 humanoid robot. The evaluation addresses three key questions regarding trajectory quality, scalability benefits, and design choices.

**A. Scaling Data Improves Diffusion Models**
To assess the impact of training data on trajectory quality, three diffusion model variants were trained: AMASS, AMASS + Nymeria, and Full-Mix (AMASS + Nymeria + GRAB + OmniRetarget).

- **Fidelity and Quality:** Table I indicates that expanding the training distribution consistently improved trajectory quality. FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) scores decreased across all mixtures, signifying enhanced fidelity of generated motions. All trained variants demonstrated substantial improvements compared to the zero-shot OmniControl baseline. The Full-Mix model achieved the most stable performance across metrics, including lower FID (0.265 vs. 0.981 for zero-shot), lower Control L2 error (0.066 vs. 0.112), and lower Skating Ratio (0.096 vs. 0.101).
- **Generalization:** Table II shows that the Full-Mix model performed comparably to the AMASS-only variant on the AMASS test set, suggesting that integrating additional datasets did not compromise performance on the original domain. Importantly, the Full-Mix model substantially outperformed the AMASS-only variant on a non-AMASS test set (FID 0.316 vs. 2.720, Control L2 0.042 vs. 0.065, Skating Ratio 0.032 vs. 0.539), indicating improved generalization to new behaviors. This implies the normalization pipeline effectively unifies heterogeneous datasets.

**B. Improved Diffusion Models Yield Stronger RL Policies**
The impact of diffusion model quality and trajectory scaling on downstream RL performance was investigated.

- **Skill Acquisition:** Policies trained with trajectories from increasingly diverse diffusion models exhibited higher success ratios in simulation (Table I). For Group-1 tasks (complex, interaction-heavy, like Drawer Open and Deep Squat & Pick, not well-represented in AMASS), the Full-Mix model-trained policy achieved a 0.925 success ratio, a substantial improvement over the zero-shot baseline (0.101) and AMASS-only variant (0.030). For simpler Group-2 tasks, DreamControl-v2 maintained performance comparable to DreamControl. This demonstrates DreamControl-v2's ability to handle complex, novel tasks through its automated pipeline.
- **Scaling Diffusion Trajectories:** Figure 3 illustrates that increasing the number of diffusion-generated trajectories provided during RL training consistently improved policy success rates across different tasks (e.g., Pick, Drawer Open, Deep Squat & Pick). The success ratio eventually approached saturation, indicating that a larger and more diverse set of reference trajectories contributed to more robust and generalizable RL policies for varying object placements.

**C. Performance Analysis**
Further analysis detailed the overall performance and the benefits of specific design choices.

- **Overall Simulation Performance:** Table III presents the task-wise performance across 8 skills. DreamControl-v2 achieved high success rates (ranging from 0.91 to 1.00) with low control errors (0.02 to 0.08m) across diverse loco-manipulation tasks, confirming its robust execution capabilities.
- **Inference-Time Prompt Calibration vs. Pre-retargeting:** Table IV compares DreamControl-v2’s pre-retargeting approach with an inference-time prompt calibration baseline (which attempts to tune human-space prompts). The prompt calibration method resulted in higher FID and larger control errors. More critically, it exhibited very low sample efficiency (8% for AMASS, 4% for Full-Mix) due to rejected candidates, indicating a labor-intensive and inefficient process. In contrast, DreamControl-v2’s pre-retargeting achieved superior metrics and a much higher valid trajectory rate (68%). Qualitative results (Fig. 4) further showed that prompt calibration led to inaccurate samples and unstable poses. This suggests that pre-retargeting effectively eliminates trial-and-error prompting and improves fidelity.
- **Network Initialization:** Table V shows an ablation study on network initialization. Fine-tuning the diffusion model from MDM pre-trained weights significantly improved fidelity (FID 0.265 vs. 0.741), R-Precision, Control L2, and physical plausibility (Skating Ratio 0.096 vs. 0.112) compared to training from scratch. This indicates that human-motion priors provide a beneficial initialization that adapts effectively to the robot embodiment.
- **Real-world Validation:** The approach was validated on a real Unitree-G1 robot, demonstrating practical deployment of diverse skills as illustrated in Figure 1 and the accompanying video.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamControl-v2 contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by presenting a framework that enhances the scalability, automation, and capability of acquiring complex loco-manipulation skills. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Scalability and Automation:** The core contribution of DreamControl-v2 is the shift from a generate-then-retarget paradigm with manual tuning and filtering to a pre-retargeting approach that trains a diffusion model directly in the robot's action space. This change eliminates labor-intensive, trial-and-error prompt calibration and manual trajectory filtering, creating a significantly more automated and scalable pipeline for generating reference trajectories. This is crucial for developing general-purpose humanoids that require a vast and diverse skill set.

2. **Broadened Skill Repertoire:** By aggregating and training on a diverse mixture of human and robot motion datasets (Full-Mix), DreamControl-v2 enables the acquisition of a wider array of complex, interaction-rich skills, such as opening drawers or deep squatting with object manipulation, which were previously challenging for diffusion models trained solely on limited human motion data. This expands the practical utility of humanoid robots in varied environments and tasks.

3. **Improved Robustness of Learned Policies:** The research demonstrates that scaling the diversity and quantity of generated reference trajectories leads to more robust and generalizable RL policies. This is a critical factor for successful deployment in real-world scenarios where environmental variations and task conditions necessitate adaptive and resilient robot behaviors. The ability to generate a larger pool of high-quality training data directly correlates with higher policy success rates.

4. **Streamlined Development Workflow:** The ability to directly specify spatial and temporal constraints in the robot's state space, without the need for complex, manual human-space adjustments, simplifies the workflow for roboticists and researchers. This reduces the expertise and time required to develop and implement new skills, potentially accelerating progress in humanoid control.

5. **Effective Transfer of Human Expertise:** The methodology effectively leverages large volumes of human motion data by transforming it into a robot-centric representation. This approach allows for the systematic and scalable transfer of human motor intelligence to humanoid robots, offering a valuable pathway for learning complex, natural-looking movements and interactions.

6. **Real-World Applicability:** The validation of learned skills on a physical Unitree-G1 robot demonstrates the practical feasibility and robustness of the DreamControl-v2 framework. This validation is an important step towards transitioning research findings into deployable robotic systems, highlighting the potential for these autonomous skills to be utilized in practical applications.

In conclusion, DreamControl-v2 represents an advance in autonomous humanoid control by addressing key limitations of prior generative prior-based approaches, offering a more robust, scalable, and automated methodology for skill acquisition.
