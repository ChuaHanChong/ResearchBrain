# Animate Anyone: Consistent and Controllable Image-to-Video Synthesis for Character Animation

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents "Animate Anyone," a new method for consistent and controllable image-to-video synthesis that animates arbitrary characters from single still images, guided by desired pose sequences. The approach demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on various human video synthesis benchmarks, significantly improving temporal consistency and appearance preservation compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing image-to-video synthesis methods struggle to maintain fine-grained appearance consistency and prevent temporal variations (jitter/flickering) in generated character animations.
- Prior approaches often lack robust controllability, making it difficult to precisely guide character movements using desired pose sequences.
- Many character animation techniques exhibit limited generalizability, often requiring per-sample finetuning or failing to animate arbitrary characters robustly across different styles.

## Method

- Extends the Stable Diffusion UNet architecture with a **ReferenceNet** for appearance consistency, a lightweight Pose Guider for controllability, and temporal layers for continuity.
- **ReferenceNet** uses a UNet-like structure and a novel spatial-==attention mechanism== to integrate high-fidelity details from the reference image, while the Pose Guider adds processed pose information to the noise latent.
- Employs a two-stage training strategy: first training for image-level quality (excluding temporal layers), then introducing and training temporal layers for video-level smoothness.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art quantitative results on fashion video, human dance, and talking gesture generation benchmarks (e.g., FVD 81.6 on UBC Fashion Video Dataset, outperforming SD-I2V at 175.4).
- Successfully animates a wide range of arbitrary characters (full-body humans, cartoons, etc.) with high definition and realistic detail, demonstrating strong generalizability.
- Generates videos with impressive spatial and temporal consistency, maintaining character appearance and smooth motion even during substantial movements, effectively addressing inter-frame jitter.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ReferenceNet, designed with an SD-pretrained UNet-like structure and a spatial-attention mechanism, effectively preserves fine-grained details from a non-aligned reference image, outperforming CLIP or ControlNet for appearance consistency.
> - A two-stage training strategy, separating image-level detail learning from video-level temporal coherence, is crucial for achieving high-quality frame generation before refining temporal dynamics.
> - The strategic integration of temporal layers after spatial-attention and cross-attention components within the UNet allows the model to maintain temporal continuity and smoothness while leveraging pre-trained image generation capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Character animation, the process of bringing static character images to life through video, has long been a challenging yet highly sought-after capability in computer vision and graphics. This work emerges from and significantly contributes to the rapidly evolving field of generative AI, particularly within image-to-video synthesis using diffusion models.

Historically, character animation from still images relied on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and related techniques for image animation and pose transfer. While these methods marked important milestones, they frequently suffered from persistent issues such as local distortion, blurred details, semantic inconsistency, and, critically, temporal instability (jitter or flickering) in the generated videos. These limitations severely hindered their widespread application in areas demanding high visual fidelity, such as online retail or professional content creation.

The advent of diffusion models marked a paradigm shift in visual generation. Initially, these models demonstrated unparalleled superiority in generating high-quality and diverse static images (text-to-image synthesis). Researchers then extended these successes to video generation, primarily through text-to-video (T2V) models. These T2V approaches often adapted existing text-to-image architectures by incorporating temporal layers to model inter-frame dependencies, leveraging pre-trained image generation capabilities.

The transition from T2V to image-to-video (I2V) presented new hurdles, particularly for specific tasks like character animation. While general I2V methods could generate videos from images, they often struggled to capture intricate, fine-grained details from the source image accurately and consistently. This led to a "loss of precision" when applied to character-specific appearance features, resulting in temporal variations and a lack of fidelity to the reference character. Moreover, these methods often faltered when faced with substantial character movements, failing to maintain a consistently stable and continuous process.

Within the more niche area of human image animation, several diffusion-based methods have emerged. DreamPose, for instance, focused on fashion video synthesis but often required finetuning on individual input samples, compromising efficiency. DisCo aimed at human dance generation but showed deficiencies in preserving character details and suffered from inter-frame jitter. A common thread among these specialized methods was a limited generalization capability, often excelling on specific benchmarks but failing to animate arbitrary characters robustly.

"Animate Anyone" directly addresses these critical limitations. It positions itself as a foundational solution that simultaneously tackles the two most significant unresolved challenges in character animation: achieving both **generalizability** (animating any character image) and **consistency** (maintaining appearance details and temporal stability throughout the video). By building upon the robust framework of Stable Diffusion and introducing novel components specifically designed for these challenges, the authors aim to push the boundaries of what is possible in controllable and high-fidelity character animation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Animate Anyone" is to **generate realistic, consistent, and controllable animated videos of characters from single still images, guided by desired pose sequences.** This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims to overcome critical shortcomings in existing image-to-video and character animation techniques:

1. **Ensuring Appearance Consistency:** A major challenge in image-to-video synthesis, especially for characters, is to faithfully preserve the intricate and fine-grained appearance details (e.g., clothing textures, facial features, unique patterns) from the single reference image throughout the entire generated video. Existing methods, particularly those relying solely on high-level semantic features (like CLIP embeddings), often fail to maintain this fidelity, leading to "temporal variations in the fine-grained details of the character’s appearance." The motivation here is to design a mechanism that can deeply integrate and consistently propagate these low-level details.

2. **Achieving Controllability via Pose:** The ability to precisely dictate the character's motion is crucial for practical applications. The objective is to enable "desired posture sequences" to effectively drive the character's movements in the generated video. This requires an efficient and robust mechanism for integrating pose information into the generative process without significantly increasing computational complexity.

3. **Guaranteeing Temporal Continuity and Stability:** Generated videos frequently suffer from "inter-frame jitter issues," flickering, or a general lack of smoothness, especially during substantial character movements. A key objective is to ensure that the transitions between video frames are continuous and smooth, maintaining overall visual coherence and high-resolution details throughout the animation.

4. **Promoting Generalizability to Arbitrary Characters:** Many previous character animation methods are tailored to specific domains or require per-sample finetuning, limiting their broader applicability. The authors explicitly state that "currently, there is no observed character animation method that simultaneously achieves generalizability and consistency." A core motivation is to develop an approach capable of animating "arbitrary characters" (e.g., full-body humans, half-length portraits, cartoons, humanoid figures) without being constrained by specific domains or requiring extensive, specialized training for each new character.

The motivation for pursuing these objectives stems from the high demand for realistic and controllable character animation in diverse fields such as online retail (virtual try-on, product demonstrations), entertainment (games, virtual idols, film production), artistic creation, and the development of virtual characters. By addressing the limitations of prior work—namely, the lack of detail consistency, robust controllability, temporal stability, and broad generalizability—"Animate Anyone" aims to provide a robust and versatile solution that can unlock new possibilities in these application areas.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

"Animate Anyone" builds upon the robust architecture of Stable Diffusion (SD), a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM), and introduces several novel components and a two-stage training strategy to achieve its objectives of consistent and controllable character animation.

#### 3.1. Foundational Framework: Stable Diffusion Extension

The core of the method extends the denoising UNet architecture of Stable Diffusion. Stable Diffusion operates in a latent space, using an autoencoder (encoder `E` and decoder `D`) to map images to a compressed latent representation. The denoising UNet is trained to predict noise added to these latent representations, progressively refining a noisy input into a clear image. "Animate Anyone" adapts this UNet to accommodate multi-frame inputs for video generation and inherits its pretrained weights for efficient initialization.

#### 3.2. Key Architectural Components

- **ReferenceNet for Appearance Consistency:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Standard approaches using CLIP image encoders for image conditions often lose fine-grained detail because CLIP operates on low-resolution inputs and focuses on high-level semantic matching.
 - **Design:** ReferenceNet is a dedicated feature extraction network for the reference image. Crucially, it adopts an identical UNet-like framework to the denoising UNet (excluding temporal layers) and also inherits pretrained weights from the original Stable Diffusion. This structural similarity is key to effective feature integration.
 - **Integration:** Instead of traditional self-attention, the denoising UNet incorporates features from ReferenceNet via a novel **Spatial-Attention** mechanism. For each layer, the feature map from the denoising UNet (multi-frame, `t x h x w x c`) is concatenated with `t` copies of the static feature map from ReferenceNet (`h x w x c`) along the width dimension. Standard self-attention is then applied, and only the first half of the resulting feature (corresponding to the denoising UNet's original features) is extracted. This design allows the denoising UNet to "selectively learn features from ReferenceNet that are correlated in the same feature space."
 - **Semantic Features:** Additionally, the CLIP image encoder is still employed for cross-attention, providing high-level semantic features of the reference image, which serves as a beneficial initialization.
 - **Distinction from ControlNet:** The authors explicitly differentiate ReferenceNet from ControlNet, noting that ControlNet is designed for spatially *aligned* control signals (like depth or edges), whereas the reference image and target animation are spatially *related* but not directly aligned. ReferenceNet's design addresses this non-alignment.

- **Pose Guider for Controllability:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Integrating pose control efficiently without significantly increasing computational complexity or requiring extensive finetuning of the denoising UNet itself.
 - **Design:** A lightweight network comprising four convolution layers (with increasing channels) is used. It processes the pose image to align its resolution with the noise latent.
 - **Integration:** The processed pose image is directly added to the noise latent before it is fed into the denoising UNet. The Pose Guider is initialized with Gaussian weights, with a crucial final projection layer using zero convolution (common for adding new control signals without disrupting initial pretrained weights).

- **Temporal Layer for Temporal Continuity:**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Ensuring smooth motion and appearance consistency across video frames, preventing jitter or flickering.
 - **Design:** Inspired by AnimateDiff, temporal layers are inserted after the spatial-attention and cross-attention components within the Res-Trans blocks of the denoising UNet.
 - **Integration:** For a given feature map (batch x frames x height x width x channels), it is reshaped to group spatial dimensions, allowing self-attention to be performed along the temporal (frame) dimension. A residual connection integrates the temporal attention output back into the original feature. This approach facilitates the transfer of pretrained image generation capabilities while enabling temporal modeling.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Strategy

The training process is meticulously divided into two stages to optimize for both image quality and temporal coherence:

- **Stage 1 (Image-Level Training):**
 - **Input:** Individual video frames (768x768 resolution) and a randomly selected reference image from the corresponding video clip.
 - **Network State:** The temporal layer within the denoising UNet is temporarily excluded.
 - **Training Focus:** The denoising UNet, ReferenceNet, and Pose Guider are all trained. The denoising UNet and ReferenceNet are initialized with pretrained Stable Diffusion weights. The Pose Guider uses Gaussian weights with zero convolution for its final layer. VAE encoder/decoder and CLIP image encoder weights are frozen.
 - **Objective:** To enable the model to generate high-quality animated *images* conditioned on a given reference image and target pose.

- **Stage 2 (Video-Level Training):**
 - **Input:** 24-frame video clips.
 - **Network State:** The temporal layer is reintroduced into the previously trained model and initialized using pretrained weights from AnimateDiff.
 - **Training Focus:** Only the temporal layer's weights are trained, while the weights of the rest of the network (denoising UNet, ReferenceNet, Pose Guider, VAE, CLIP) are fixed.
 - **Objective:** To ensure temporal smoothness and continuity of the generated video, leveraging the high-quality single-frame generation capabilities learned in Stage 1.

This two-stage approach strategically separates the learning of appearance details and static pose control from the learning of temporal dynamics, leading to more robust and higher-quality video generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The findings presented in "Animate Anyone" strongly demonstrate its efficacy in achieving consistent, controllable, and high-quality character animation.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

The visual examples provided in the paper and on the project website showcase the model's impressive capabilities:
- **Arbitrary Character Animation:** The model successfully animates a wide range of characters, including full-body human figures, half-length portraits, diverse cartoon characters, and humanoid figures. This highlights its strong generalizability across different character types and styles.
- **High-Definition and Realistic Details:** The generated videos consistently exhibit clear, high-definition, and realistic details for the characters. This suggests the ReferenceNet effectively captures and preserves intricate appearance features from the source image.
- **Spatial and Temporal Consistency:** A critical achievement is the model's ability to maintain consistency with the reference image's appearance details both spatially (within frames) and temporally (across frames). This consistency holds even when the character undergoes substantial movements, addressing a major limitation of previous methods.
- **Temporal Continuity:** The generated animations are remarkably smooth, showing continuous motion without noticeable temporal jitter or flickering, which is attributed to the effective temporal modeling.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparisons (State-of-the-Art Performance)

The authors conduct extensive quantitative evaluations on three standard human video synthesis benchmarks (UBC fashion video, TikTok, and Ted-talk datasets), demonstrating state-of-the-art (SOTA) results across multiple metrics. Key metrics used include SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure), PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio), LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity) for image quality, and FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) for video quality and temporal coherence.

- **Fashion Video Synthesis (UBC Fashion Video Dataset):**
 - "Animate Anyone" achieves significantly superior results compared to baselines like MRAA, TPSMM, BDMM, DreamPose (with and without sample finetuning), and SD-I2V (a strong baseline combining Stable Diffusion, ControlNet, IP-Adapter, and AnimateDiff).
 - Notably, its FVD score of 81.6 is dramatically lower (better) than its closest competitors (BDMM at 148.3, SD-I2V at 175.4), indicating exceptional temporal quality. Its SSIM (0.931) and LPIPS (0.044) also lead, signifying excellent image fidelity and detail preservation, crucial for clothing details.

- **Human Dance Generation (TikTok Dataset):**
 - The method again achieves the best quantitative results (FVD 171.9), outperforming FOMM, MRAA, TPSMM, DisCo, and SD-I2V.
 - This is particularly impressive given that DisCo incorporates human attribute pre-training on larger datasets, whereas "Animate Anyone" was trained exclusively on the TikTok dataset for this comparison, showcasing its efficiency and robust design. Qualitatively, it shows better visual continuity and robustness during complex dance sequences.

- **Talking Gesture Generation (Ted-talk Dataset):**
 - "Animate Anyone" demonstrates strong performance, outperforming DisCo and SD-I2V. While MRAA and TPSMM achieve comparable FVD scores, the authors highlight that those methods use Ground Truth (GT) images as driving signals (essentially video reconstruction), whereas "Animate Anyone" achieves its results using only pose information, indicating a more challenging and practical generative task.

- **Comparison with General Image-to-Video Methods (AnimateDiff, Gen-2):**
 - While these methods possess strong generative capabilities for general video, they struggle significantly when asked to generate substantial character movements or maintain long-term appearance consistency specific to a reference character. This reinforces "Animate Anyone"'s unique strength in specialized character animation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies provide strong evidence for the necessity and effectiveness of each proposed component:

- **Image Condition Modeling:**
 - Demonstrates that ReferenceNet is superior to alternatives like relying solely on CLIP features, using ControlNet, or combining CLIP with ControlNet.
 - CLIP alone fails to transfer full details. ControlNet is ineffective because its features are for spatially *aligned* conditions, not image *references*. This validates the spatial-attention design of ReferenceNet for detail preservation.

- **Details of ReferenceNet Design:**
 - Using Stable Diffusion's pretrained weights for ReferenceNet is crucial; substituting with ImageNet-pretrained ResNet weights significantly degrades performance. This confirms that aligning the feature space of ReferenceNet with the denoising UNet (both from SD) is vital for effective integration.
 - Replacing spatial-attention with simple feature concatenation also leads to worse results, emphasizing that spatial-attention is necessary for the denoising UNet to effectively integrate detailed image features.

- **Temporal Modeling:**
 - The absence of the temporal layer drastically increases FVD (from 81.6 to 176.7), confirming that it's essential for avoiding texture sticking and inter-frame jitter.
 - Skipping the two-stage training (i.e., direct end-to-end training) leads to a decline in image quality metrics. This supports the two-stage strategy, which ensures initial high-quality frame generation before refining temporal coherence.

Overall, the findings consistently validate that "Animate Anyone" represents a significant leap forward in controllable and consistent character animation, addressing key limitations of prior art and establishing new state-of-the-art performance on relevant benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Animate Anyone" represents a substantial advancement in the field of generative AI for character animation, with far-reaching significance and potential impact across various industries and research directions.

#### 5.1. Technical Significance

- **Pioneering Consistency and Generalizability:** The paper successfully tackles the long-standing twin challenges of maintaining fine-grained appearance consistency and achieving generalizability to arbitrary characters, which prior methods struggled to do simultaneously. This makes it a more robust and versatile solution.
- **Innovative ReferenceNet Design:** The introduction of ReferenceNet, with its UNet-like structure leveraging pre-trained Stable Diffusion weights and a novel spatial-attention integration, is a key technical innovation. It provides a more effective way to infuse high-fidelity detail from a single reference image into the generated video, moving beyond the limitations of high-level semantic encoders like CLIP or misaligned control networks like ControlNet.
- **Effective Multi-Component Integration:** The seamless integration of ReferenceNet, a lightweight Pose Guider, and a temporal layer within a Stable Diffusion framework, combined with a strategic two-stage training strategy, demonstrates a sophisticated understanding of how to balance different objectives (detail preservation, pose control, temporal smoothness) in a complex generative model.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Consistently outperforming leading methods on multiple diverse benchmarks underscores its technical prowess and establishes a new benchmark for image-to-video character animation.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact and Applications

The capabilities demonstrated by "Animate Anyone" open up numerous possibilities:

- **Online Retail and E-commerce:** Revolutionize virtual try-on experiences for clothing, accessories, and even virtual models, allowing customers to see products dynamically on customizable avatars without costly photoshoots. It can enable engaging virtual product demonstrations.
- **Entertainment and Media Production:** Streamline the animation pipeline for games, movies, and TV shows by generating realistic character movements from static concept art or character designs. It could facilitate the creation of virtual idols, digital doubles, and animated content for social media and advertising with unprecedented ease and consistency.
- **Artistic Creation and Content Generation:** Empower artists and content creators to quickly animate their illustrations, paintings, or character designs, transforming static art into dynamic videos. This lowers the barrier to entry for video content creation.
- **Virtual Characters and Metaverse:** Accelerate the development of lifelike and controllable virtual characters for metaverse applications, virtual assistants, and interactive digital experiences, enhancing immersion and user engagement.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Enable more realistic and expressive human avatars for telepresence, virtual meetings, and educational tools.

#### 5.3. Societal Implications and Limitations

The authors responsibly acknowledge potential limitations and societal implications:
- **Challenges with Unseen Parts:** The inherent ill-posed problem of generating unseen parts of a character during complex movements (e.g., rotating 360 degrees from a single frontal image) can still lead to instability. This points to a need for future research into multi-view synthesis or more robust 3D-aware generative models.
- **Hand Movement Distortions:** The model may still struggle with complex hand movements, sometimes producing distortions or motion blur. This suggests fine-grained articulation control remains a challenge.
- **Computational Efficiency:** As a diffusion-model-based method using DDPM for inference, it exhibits lower operational efficiency compared to non-diffusion-based methods. Future work could explore faster sampling techniques or distilled models.
- **Ethical Concerns (Deepfakes):** Acknowledging that the model "may be used to produce fake videos of individuals," the authors briefly mention that such creations "can be detected using some face anti-spoofing techniques." This highlights the broader ethical responsibility in developing powerful generative AI and the need for concurrent research in detection and mitigation strategies for misuse.

In conclusion, "Animate Anyone" marks a significant step towards general-purpose, high-fidelity character animation. By establishing a robust framework that simultaneously achieves consistency, controllability, and generalizability, it serves as a foundational solution with the potential to profoundly impact creative industries and human-computer interaction, while also underscoring the ongoing need for responsible AI development and deployment.
