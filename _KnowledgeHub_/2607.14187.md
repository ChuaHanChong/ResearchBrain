# RxBrain: Embodied Cognition Foundation Model with Joint Language-Visual Reasoning and Imagination

> [!summary] Summary
> RxBrain introduces an embodied cognition foundation model capable of joint language-visual reasoning and imagination for complex planning. It demonstrates strong capabilities in embodied understanding, world state prediction, and joint subgoal planning, and enables competitive real-world robot manipulation without extensive action-specific pretraining.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI models either excel at abstract language-based reasoning but lack visual imagination, or vice versa, creating a gap between abstract plans and concrete physical states.
- Prior approaches often treat reasoning ('what should be done') and visual imagination ('what might happen') as distinct capabilities rather than as a coupled cognitive process for planning.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale, explicitly coupled language-visual reasoning and physical state change data needed to train comprehensive embodied models.

## Method

- The **RxBrain** model utilizes a unified multimodal ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture capable of jointly processing and generating language, image, and video tokens.
- An automatic pipeline was developed to convert raw embodied videos into structured, joint text-visual planning supervision, addressing the data scarcity challenge.
- RxBrain employs a multimodal generation formulation that supports text, multi-frame, and ==interleaved text-image generation==, using a ==flow-matching== objective for visual synthesis and an ==inference-aware state construction== strategy.

## Results

- RxBrain achieves a GenEval score of **82.4** for text-to-image generation and leads on several embodied and spatial understanding benchmarks, including CV-Bench (**88.59**) and 3DRSBench (**54.1**).
- It significantly outperforms baselines in world state prediction (MLLM-judge score **0.62**) and joint subgoal planning (MLLM-judge score **0.68**), showing strong language reasoning but identifying visual imagination fidelity as a bottleneck.
- Extended to real-world robotic manipulation, RxBrain achieves an **87%** average success rate on multi-stage household tasks, surpassing strong baselines (e.g., 0 **68%**, 0.5 **82%**) without large-scale action-data pretraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying language-based abstract reasoning with visual imagination in a complementary relationship within a single planning sequence is crucial for effective embodied cognition.
> - A modality-aware Mixture-of-Transformers architecture can maintain strong general multimodal capabilities while also excelling in specialized embodied tasks like world state prediction and joint planning.
> - Multimodal world knowledge and state prediction priors learned during pretraining can serve as a robust foundation for continuous robot action generation, reducing the need for extensive action-specific pretraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) has progressed significantly in two primary directions: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Generative World Models. VLMs typically excel at interpreting visual observations, understanding language instructions, grounding objects, decomposing tasks, and facilitating high-level textual decision-making. However, their cognitive processes often conclude with language-based outputs, leaving desired physical outcomes implicitly defined without explicit visual imagination of goal states. Conversely, generative world models specialize in predicting future observations, simulating state transitions, and synthesizing goal-conditioned visual outcomes, making them suitable for visual planning. Yet, these models often exhibit limitations in explicit causal reasoning, abstract task understanding, and constraint-aware decision-making.

Previous approaches frequently separate these two aspects of embodied cognition: VLMs determine "what should be done," while world models imagine "what might happen." More recent efforts have attempted to unify these capabilities within single architectures. For instance, models like Cosmos 3 aim to integrate understanding, generation, simulation, and action within an omnimodal framework. However, even these integrated models often treat reasoning and generation as distinct capabilities rather than as a coupled cognitive process coordinated towards a shared planning objective. Other works, such as π0.7 and BAGEL, explore combining VLMs with image generation for joint language-visual subgoal generation or interleaved text-image generation, primarily focusing on representing similar semantic content across modalities.

RxBrain positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenge of unifying textual reasoning and visual imagination into a *complementary* relationship within a single planning sequence. It aims to move beyond simply integrating separate capabilities or representing the same semantic content, instead focusing on a joint formulation where language specifies the abstract structure of a plan (task decomposition, primitives, constraints, logic), and visual imagination grounds this structure by predicting world states and subgoals (intermediate and final physical states). This approach contributes to the development of foundation models for embodied cognition by explicitly linking abstract reasoning with concrete visual outcomes within a unified framework, facilitating a more coherent and executable form of embodied planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce RxBrain, an embodied cognition foundation model designed for joint language-visual reasoning and imagination. The core motivation stems from the observed limitations in existing embodied AI models:

- **Bridging the Gap between Abstract Reasoning and Physical States:** Embodied cognition requires agents to not only determine abstract actions but also to anticipate the resulting physical world states. Current VLMs are proficient in abstract task understanding and textual decision-making but often lack an explicit mechanism for imagining the visual consequences of these decisions. Conversely, generative world models can predict future visual states but typically struggle with abstract causal reasoning and complex task decomposition. RxBrain is motivated by the need to bridge this gap, integrating both forms of reasoning within a single, coherent framework.
- **Developing Complementary Modality Roles in Planning:** The research posits that in complex planning tasks, neither language nor visual imagination alone can fully specify an executable intention. Language provides the structural and logical components of a plan (e.g., task decomposition, temporal order, constraints), while visual imagination grounds this structure by depicting the desired physical configurations and object interactions. The motivation is to establish a complementary relationship between these modalities, where language informs visual imagination, and visual imagination concretizes language-based plans.
- **Unified Multimodal Architecture for Understanding and Generation:** To support this joint reasoning, a key objective is to develop a unified multimodal Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture that can simultaneously handle language, image, and video understanding and generation. This architecture is designed to allow perception and imagination to interact within a shared visual processing pipeline while maintaining modality-specific computation.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity for Joint Supervision:** Training such a model requires large-scale data that explicitly couples language-visual reasoning with observed physical state changes. Manually annotating such data is resource-intensive. Therefore, a significant objective is to develop an automatic pipeline to convert raw embodied videos into structured, joint text-visual planning supervision, which aligns textual action descriptions with the visual state transitions they produce.
- **Establishing a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** Existing benchmarks often evaluate embodied understanding or world state prediction in isolation. A further objective is to introduce RxBrain-Bench, a new benchmark specifically designed to evaluate a model's ability to jointly use language and visual imagination for task understanding, goal state prediction, and coherent embodied plan construction across diverse scenarios.
- **Demonstrating Real-World Action Generation:** Finally, the work aims to extend RxBrain to continuous robot action generation to investigate whether the acquired multimodal world knowledge and state prediction priors can provide a robust foundation for action prediction, potentially reducing the reliance on extensive action-specific pretraining for real-robot deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RxBrain integrates joint language-visual reasoning and imagination through a unified multimodal architecture, supported by an automated data construction pipeline and a structured training approach.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

RxBrain is built upon a **modality-aware Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)** architecture.
- **Unified Backbone:** The model uses a single backbone that processes tokens from different modalities (language, understanding visual tokens, generated visual tokens). These tokens are routed to specialized Transformer pathways and interact through cross-modal attention.
- **Visual Representation:** It employs **HY-ViT 2.0** as a single visual encoder, which is pre-trained to extract both semantic features and VAE-aligned fine-grained features. For visual generation, a pre-trained VAE model from FLUX is used as the encoder and decoder, operating in the latent space.
- **Modality-Specific Experts:** The architecture exposes two modality-specialized attention branches (language and vision) and three modality-specific feed-forward network (FFN) experts for text, visual understanding, and visual generation. Visual understanding and generation tokens share the same vision Transformer backbone and attention projection layers, but are routed to separate FFN experts, with the visual-generation FFN being wider (12,288 vs 6,144 intermediate dimension).
- **Hybrid Attention:** Causal attention is applied for autoregressive text generation, while frame-wise bidirectional attention is used for visual tokens in both understanding and flow-matching-based generation.
- **Model Size:** The base RxBrain model contains 6.2 billion parameters, with text and visual-understanding branches each around 1.5 billion, and the visual-generation expert contributing approximately 2.4 billion.

#### 3.2. Multimodal Generation Formulation

RxBrain supports three generation modes:
- **Text Generation:** Standard autoregressive generation, producing text tokens sequentially based on multimodal context (language instructions and visual observations) using causal attention.
- **Multi Frames Generation:** For world state prediction, RxBrain generates four future image frames in the VAE latent space using a flow-matching objective. Frames are denoised in parallel, maintaining causal temporal dependencies through attention masks. Generated frames can attend to their own tokens and all preceding context.
- **Interleave Generation:** For joint subgoal planning, RxBrain autoregressively produces interleaved reasoning text and visual state predictions. After generating reasoning text ($r_i$), a `<Image>` token triggers the synthesis of a corresponding visual latent span ($z_{i,1:N}$) via flow matching, conditioned on the preceding multimodal context. This synthesized frame is then re-encoded by the ViT visual encoder and appended to the context for subsequent reasoning steps. An **inference-aware visual state construction strategy** is employed during training, where previous visual states are obtained from ground-truth images through the same reconstruction and encoding pipeline as inference, mitigating train-inference discrepancy.

#### 3.3. Joint Planning Data Construction

An **automatic pipeline** is developed to convert embodied videos into joint text-visual planning supervision, addressing data scarcity.
- **Corpus:** The corpus comprises 50,177.2 hours of video (21.5 million trainable segments) from four categories: Real-Robot Data, UMI Data, Simulation Data, and Egocentric Human Data.
- **Temporal Segment Annotation:** This stage uses a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM, denoted Φ) to process videos.
 - **Keyframe Sampling:** Visually informative keyframes are sampled based on visual difference, smoothed, pruned, and capped to fit a budget.
 - **Candidate Step Proposal:** Φ assesses clip annotatability and, for suitable clips, proposes a summary, high-level goal, camera viewpoint, and an ordered list of candidate planning steps, each with a short name, detailed description, and coarse start/end keyframe indices.
 - **Boundary Refinement:** Coarse segment boundaries are locally refined around the estimated start and end points using dense frame windows. Φ selects the precise start and end frames where the physical state change is visible, allowing adjacent segments to overlap.
- **Quality Verification:** An MLLM-based verifier checks each localized segment for semantic quality, visual grounding (e.g., hand presence, object visibility), and consistency between text and visual transition. Descriptions can be corrected, and approximately 75.18% of candidate segments are retained.
- **Segment Structuring:** Retained segments are organized into a hierarchical structure of four post-training tasks (L0-L3) to supervise different granularities of planning:
 - **L0 Segment:** Models intra-step visual changes (`[I_start, text] -> I_end`).
 - **L1 Step-level Planning:** Connects consecutive atomic steps, predicting a sequence of future planning steps and their visual outcomes (`[Visual Context, Goal] -> [Text_1, I_end_1, ..., Text_k, I_end_k]`).
 - **L2 Subgoal Planning:** Groups adjacent steps into higher-level subgoals, predicting subgoal descriptions and corresponding boundary frames.
 - **L3 Final-state Imagination:** Predicts the final frame (`I_end_T`) given the initial frame (`I_start_0`) and a concise task goal (`g_task`).

#### 3.4. Training

RxBrain employs a **two-stage curriculum**:
- **Stage 1: Joint Vision-Language Pretraining:** Focuses on establishing unified vision-language generation capabilities. All modules are trained using large-scale image-text pairs and multimodal instruction-following data. This stage emphasizes the flow-matching generation loss, connecting language understanding, visual perception, and visual generation in a shared representation space.
- **Stage 2: Embodied Supervised Fine-tuning:** Adapts RxBrain to embodied scenarios. The training mixture combines general multimodal understanding data with embodied data (instruction-following, multi-frame prediction, interleaved planning trajectories). Losses are balanced between text and vision generation. Interleaved planning data is transformed into multiple text-image generation samples, aligning visual states with language context, and incorporating the inference-aware visual state construction strategy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RxBrain covers four axes: text-to-image generation, embodied and spatial understanding, world state prediction, and joint subgoal planning, supplemented by real-robot action generation.

#### 4.1. Text-to-Image Generation

- RxBrain achieves a GenEval score of 82.4, performing comparably to the generation-specialist model Bagel (82) and surpassing Cosmos-3-Nano (71.68). This indicates that RxBrain maintains strong general image generation capabilities despite its focus on embodied cognition.

#### 4.2. Embodied and Spatial Understanding

- On standard embodied and spatial reasoning benchmarks, RxBrain leads on CV-Bench (88.59), EmbSpatial (82.3), and DA-2k (83.4).
- It demonstrates an advantage in multi-view and 3D understanding, achieving the highest scores on 3DRSBench (54.1), MMSI-Bench (35.8), MindCube (47.5), and SITE-Bench-Image (54.9).
- For robot affordance, trajectory, and placement, it shows the best trajectory prediction on Share-Robot-Trajectory (51.13) and leads RoboSpatial-Home configuration (86.28). However, fine-grained pointing and affordance tasks (e.g., RefSpatial, Where2Place, Share-Robot-Affordance) reveal a gap compared to specialized models like RoboBrain2.5.

#### 4.3. RxBrain-Bench-EVQA (Embodied VQA)

- RxBrain achieves an overall accuracy of 72.7% across 1,123 multiple-choice questions and 258 simulation-planning samples.
- Its strengths are in outcome-aware reasoning and multi-round planning, with best results in Success Detection (85.1%) and Simulation Planning (51.6%). This suggests an ability to update decisions based on intermediate simulated states.
- Areas for improvement include fine-grained spatial grounding, trajectory and temporal reasoning, and error recovery, where its performance is below models like Qwen3.5-4B (78.1%) and Cosmos3-Nano (76.7%).

#### 4.4. World State Prediction (RxBrain-Bench-WorldPred)

- RxBrain achieves a weighted MLLM-judge score ($S_{gen}$) of 0.62.
- It performs well in observation continuity (0.67) and action correctness (0.65). Temporal and physical plausibility (0.53) are identified as the weakest aspects.
- Compared to baselines, RxBrain substantially outperforms the general-purpose video model Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (0.429) and marginally exceeds the specialized embodied world model Cosmos3-Nano (0.591). This suggests a single unified model can match dedicated world models in future prediction.

#### 4.5. Joint Subgoal Planning (RxBrain-Bench-JointPlan)

- RxBrain achieves a weighted MLLM-judge score ($S_{plan}$) of 0.68.
- Language reasoning is a strong capability, with high scores in observation understanding (0.83) and subtask planning (0.78).
- Visual imagination, specifically goal-image correctness (0.52), is identified as the primary bottleneck, indicating room for improvement in visual synthesis fidelity.
- Performance gradually decreases with increasing planning horizon, from 0.69 at two steps to 0.55 at eight steps, reflecting the impact of accumulated autoregressive errors.
- RxBrain significantly outperforms modular (Qwen-Agent, 0.431) and unified (Cosmos3-Nano agent, 0.521; BAGEL-7B-MoT, 0.503) baselines across all judge criteria, particularly in subtask planning and chain completion.

#### 4.6. Extending RxBrain to Action Generation

- By extending RxBrain with a modality-specialized action branch and employing Gated Expert Fusion to reuse pretrained generation expert knowledge, the model is applied to real-world robotic manipulation.
- On three multi-stage household manipulation tasks (Set the Table, Fold and Store Glasses, Pick Trash), RxBrain achieves an average success rate of 87%.
- This performance surpasses that of strong vision-language-action baselines, π0 (68%) and π0.5 (82%), without requiring large-scale action-data pretraining. This suggests that the world knowledge and state prediction priors from multimodal pretraining provide a strong foundation for action prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on RxBrain marks a notable step in the development of embodied cognition foundation models by integrating joint language-visual reasoning and imagination. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified Embodied Planning:** RxBrain offers a framework where abstract language-based planning and concrete visual imagination are not treated as separate components but as complementary parts of a single planning sequence. This unification moves beyond simply connecting perception and action to explicitly linking "what to do" with "what the world should look like," which is fundamental for complex, long-horizon embodied tasks.
- **Leveraging Multimodal Foundation Models:** The research demonstrates that a single, unified Mixture-of-Transformers architecture can simultaneously maintain general multimodal capabilities (e.g., strong text-to-image generation) while excelling in specialized embodied tasks. This suggests a pathway for building generalist AI agents capable of diverse interactions with the physical world.
- **Automatic Data Generation and Benchmarking:** The development of an automatic data construction pipeline for joint text-visual planning supervision directly addresses the challenge of data scarcity in embodied AI. This pipeline converts raw embodied videos into structured training data, making it more feasible to scale embodied cognition research. Furthermore, RxBrain-Bench provides a dedicated evaluation tool for assessing truly interleaved text-visual planning, which was previously lacking in the research landscape.
- **Improved Action Generation with Reduced Data Dependency:** The extension of RxBrain to continuous robot action generation, achieving competitive performance on real-world tasks without extensive action-specific pretraining, implies that the rich world knowledge and state prediction capabilities learned during multimodal pretraining can effectively transfer to low-level control. This could lead to more data-efficient training paradigms for robotic policies and accelerate real-world robot deployment.
- **Foundation for More Agentic AI:** By enabling models to generate detailed plans that combine symbolic reasoning with concrete visual subgoals, RxBrain contributes to the development of more autonomous and capable embodied agents. Such agents could better decompose open-ended tasks, monitor progress, revise plans based on imagined outcomes, and interact with environments in a more intelligent and self-directed manner.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability of RxBrain to produce plans with coupled textual reasoning and visual imagination could facilitate more intuitive and natural human-robot interaction, allowing users to specify tasks and receive feedback that aligns with both abstract goals and observable physical changes.

In summary, RxBrain's contribution lies in its integrated approach to embodied cognition, bridging abstract reasoning and visual imagination through a unified architecture, novel data generation, and a specialized evaluation framework. This work provides a foundational step towards building more comprehensive and capable foundation models for embodied AI.
