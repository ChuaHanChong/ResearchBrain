# CORAL: Towards Autonomous Multi-Agent Evolution for Open-Ended Discovery

> [!summary] Summary
> The CORAL framework introduces an autonomous multi-agent evolutionary system, empowering Large Language Model agents to control their discovery process and collaborate indirectly through shared persistent memory. This approach achieved new state-of-the-art results on 8 out of 11 mathematical and systems optimization tasks, exhibiting a 3-10x higher improvement rate and an 18.3% cycle reduction in kernel engineering.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-powered evolutionary search relies on fixed heuristics and external rules, limiting agent autonomy and adaptability in complex, open-ended problem spaces where optimal search strategies are unknown.
- Current multi-agent LLM systems often use 'vertical scaling' with human-defined roles and rigid communication, which is restrictive for truly open-ended discovery where optimal task decomposition and interaction patterns are not known in advance.
- Open-ended scientific and engineering problems often lack a known ground truth, requiring continuous iterative search and refinement rather than a single-shot optimization against a static target.

## Method

- **CORAL** implements ==autonomous multi-agent evolution== by empowering LLM agents with control over the retrieve, propose, evaluate, and update stages of the ==evolutionary loop==, rather than relying on fixed external rules.
- A ==shared persistent memory==, structured as a file system, enables ==asynchronous multi-agent organization== where agents explore solution spaces in parallel and indirectly exchange discoveries, notes, and reusable skills.
- ==Heartbeat-based interventions== (e.g., periodic consolidation, stagnation redirection) are incorporated to prevent agents from getting stuck, encourage knowledge contribution, and dynamically reorient search strategies.

## Results

- A single **CORAL** agent outperformed fixed evolutionary search baselines on all 11 mathematical and systems optimization tasks, achieving new state-of-the-art results on 8 tasks and demonstrating a **3-10x** higher improvement rate.
- Multi-agent CORAL (4 agents) extended search performance on challenging stress-test problems, achieving an **18.3%** cycle reduction in kernel engineering (a new SOTA) and a **5.0%** score increase in polyominoes packing.
- Analysis revealed that multi-agent gains stem from cross-agent information transfer (e.g., **36%** of attempts on Kernel Engineering used cross-agent parents with higher improvement rates) and increased exploration diversity (low Jaccard similarity of strategies across agents).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Granting LLM agents autonomy over evolutionary decisions and facilitating local verification steps leads to substantially more efficient search and higher improvement rates compared to fixed evolutionary algorithms.
> - Asynchronous multi-agent organization with shared persistent memory fosters emergent collaboration and diverse exploration, extending the search frontier beyond what single autonomous agents can achieve.
> - The systematic accumulation and sharing of knowledge, via agent-generated notes and reusable skills, is critical for driving successful and sustained discovery, especially in complex open-ended tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), agent systems, and evolutionary computation for open-ended discovery.

Recent advances have demonstrated the effectiveness of LLM-powered agents in open-ended problems that require iterative search, testing, and refinement, where optimal solutions are initially unknown. Systems such as FunSearch and AlphaEvolve integrate LLMs into evaluator-guided evolutionary search loops. In these approaches, LLMs generate candidate programs based on high-scoring solutions, external evaluators score the candidates, and a predetermined evolutionary algorithm manages parent selection and population updates. While effective, these methods typically employ fixed evolutionary search strategies, where critical decisions—such as which parent solutions to inspect, when to conduct intermediate tests, and what knowledge to externalize for future use—are governed by rigid, hard-coded heuristics external to the LLM agent itself. This design limits the autonomy and adaptive capacity of the agents.

Furthermore, existing multi-agent LLM systems often rely on "vertical scaling," where human designers decompose tasks, assign specialized roles, and define fixed communication structures (e.g., MetaGPT, AI Scientist). This approach assumes prior knowledge of optimal task decomposition and interaction topologies, which may not hold for complex, open-ended discovery problems.

CORAL proposes a shift towards a paradigm of "autonomous evolution," initially for single agents and then extending to multi-agent systems. This involves delegating a greater portion of the evolutionary algorithm's decision-making to the agents, fostering "horizontal parallelism" where multiple agents explore concurrently, exchange discoveries, and build upon each other's progress. This work aims to integrate the strengths of autonomous LLM agents (capable of planning, implementing, and debugging) into a sustained evolutionary loop, and to enable emergent collaboration among agents without predefined communication protocols.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research arises from the limitations of current LLM-based discovery systems in tackling open-ended scientific and engineering problems. Many significant problems lack a known ground truth and necessitate continuous iteration, proposal generation, testing, and revision to discover strong solutions.

The primary motivations are:
- **Rigidity of current LLM-based evolution**: Existing methods rely on fixed, external rules for orchestrating evolutionary search (e.g., context selection, evaluation timing, knowledge storage). This rigidity limits the LLM agents' ability to adapt their search strategies in response to evolving problem states or insights. The authors hypothesize that granting agents greater autonomy over these decisions could lead to more effective and efficient discovery.
- **Limitations of existing multi-agent collaboration**: While multi-agent systems exist, they often mandate pre-defined roles and communication structures, which are restrictive for open-ended problems where optimal interaction patterns are unknown. The researchers propose that horizontal parallelism, where autonomous agents explore diverse paths and coordinate indirectly, could be a more scalable and adaptable approach for open-ended discovery.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the CORAL project are:
1. **Formulate a new paradigm**: To formally define autonomous evolution as a distinct approach for open-ended discovery, differentiating between autonomous single-agent and multi-agent evolution from conventional fixed evolutionary search.
2. **Develop a framework**: To introduce CORAL as a concrete infrastructure that implements this autonomous multi-agent evolution paradigm. This involves designing mechanisms for shared persistent memory, asynchronous multi-agent organization, and heartbeat-based interventions to support long-horizon search.
3. **Empirical validation**: To empirically demonstrate that CORAL can achieve superior performance compared to fixed evolutionary search baselines across a diverse set of mathematical, algorithmic, and systems optimization tasks. This includes setting new state-of-the-art results on challenging problems.
4. **Mechanistic analysis**: To conduct analyses and ablation studies to identify the specific contributions of agent autonomy (e.g., local verification, knowledge accumulation) and multi-agent collaboration (e.g., information transfer, exploration diversity) to the observed performance gains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CORAL is presented as a framework for autonomous multi-agent evolution, shifting decision-making responsibility from fixed algorithms to the LLM agents themselves.

**Problem Formulation:**
The framework addresses open-ended discovery tasks, characterized by an unknown optimal solution and the need for iterative search guided by an evaluator `E(x, y) = (s, f)` (score and feedback). Each improvement step involves four conceptual stages:
1. **RETRIEVE**: Constructing a working context `M̂t` from shared persistent memory `Mt`.
2. **PROPOSE**: Generating a candidate solution `yt+1`.
3. **EVALUATE**: Obtaining score and feedback `(st+1, ft+1)`.
4. **UPDATE**: Incorporating new information into `Mt`.

**Paradigm Shift:**
- **Fixed Evolutionary Search (Prior Work)**: The four stages are instantiated by external rules. The LLM primarily handles PROPOSE. The agent lacks control over search decisions.
- **Autonomous Single-Agent Evolution**: A single agent controls all four stages, deciding what to retrieve, when to test, when to evaluate, and what knowledge to persist.
- **Autonomous Multi-Agent Evolution (CORAL)**: Multiple agents operate asynchronously, coordinating through shared persistent memory. Agents interact indirectly, sharing discoveries, enabling emergent behaviors without predefined protocols.

**Core Mechanisms of CORAL:**
1. **Shared Persistent Memory as File System**:
 - The collective knowledge `M` is structured as a file system, with symbolic links to each agent's isolated workspace.
 - Agents can `retrieve` and `contribute` to this shared space using a CORAL CLI tool or standard Bash commands.
 - Three root folders provide structure:
 - `attempts/`: Stores historical evaluation outcomes and corresponding solutions, allowing agents to inspect successful approaches.
 - `notes/`: Contains markdown files documenting observations, learnings, and reflections from all agents, fostering knowledge sharing.
 - `skills/`: Houses reusable procedures, tools, scripts, and implementation patterns, comprising natural-language descriptions and executable artifacts.
2. **Multi-Agent Organization**:
 - `N` agents run in parallel, each maintaining its own local context and operating within an isolated workspace.
 - Agents interact indirectly by reading from and writing to the shared persistent memory (`M`). When an agent generates an artifact (attempt, note, skill), it writes to `M`, making it available for other agents to retrieve and incorporate into their own contexts.
 - This design promotes exploration diversity by allowing agents to pursue distinct lines of inquiry while benefiting from collective knowledge accumulation.
3. **Heartbeat-Based Interventions**:
 - To counteract local minima, drift, and ensure knowledge contribution, CORAL implements a "heartbeat" mechanism.
 - Periodically, based on triggers (e.g., number of iterations, time elapsed, score stagnation), the system prompts agents to reflect, consolidate knowledge, or redirect their search.
 - Three types of heartbeats are implemented:
 - **Per-iteration reflection**: Encourages agents to record useful notes during ongoing work.
 - **Periodic consolidation**: Prompts agents to review progress, refine notes, and distill reusable procedures into skills after a fixed number of attempts.
 - **Stagnation-triggered redirection**: Activates when an agent shows no improvement, prompting a reassessment of the current strategy and a potential pivot to a new search direction.

**Implementation Details (from Appendix):**
- **Agent Instruction Prompts**: Agents receive a structured `CORAL.md` document at startup, outlining the task, workflow (plan → edit → eval → repeat), rules, and system interfaces. Multi-agent variants emphasize collaboration.
- **CLI and System Interfaces**: CORAL provides a Command-Line Interface (CLI) with 17 commands for agents (e.g., `coral eval`, `coral log`, `coral show`, `coral notes`, `coral skills`) and for human operators (e.g., `coral start`, `coral stop`).
- **Evaluation Pipeline**: The `coral eval` command automates a sequence: staging/committing changes, dynamically loading a hidden grader, executing the grader with a timeout, determining status (improved, regressed, crashed, timeout), recording the attempt, checkpointing shared memory, and updating evaluation counters.
- **User Interface**: A web-based dashboard provides real-time monitoring of leaderboards, agent logs, shared notes/skills, and run status.
- **Workspace Management**: Each agent operates in an isolated Git worktree, preventing direct code interference. Symbolic links connect agents' workspaces to the centralized shared persistent memory (`.coral/public/`).
- **Concurrency**: Unique filenames (e.g., attempt hashes) minimize write conflicts in shared memory.
- **Agent Lifecycle and Session Persistence**: An `AgentManager` handles agent creation, heartbeat configuration, spawning agent runtime processes, monitoring for new attempts/triggers, and resuming agents with prior context or restarting dead agents.
- **Execution Safeguards**: Include evaluator isolation (grader code hidden from agents), workspace guards (`.gitignore` prevents accidental commits of shared memory), process management for graceful shutdown, and evaluation timeouts.
- **Task Interface**: A unified interface for tasks, defined by a `task.yaml` configuration file and a Python `eval/grader.py` implementation, ensuring modularity.
- **Evaluator Corrections**: The authors identified and corrected several bugs in benchmark evaluators (e.g., PRISM, Transaction Scheduling, EPLB, LLM-SQL) to ensure accurate scoring and reproducibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate CORAL's performance improvements across various tasks and provide insights into the mechanisms underlying its effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Autonomous Evolution Outperforms Fixed Evolutionary Search (Single-Agent)

- **State-of-the-Art**: CORAL (single agent) achieved the best final score on all 11 mathematical and systems optimization tasks evaluated against baselines (OpenEvolve, ShinkaEvolve, EvoX). It established new state-of-the-art results on 8 of these tasks.
- **Efficiency and Improvement Rate**: CORAL exhibited a 3–10× higher improvement rate (fraction of evaluations yielding a better score) compared to fixed evolutionary search baselines. It typically converged to its best score within 5–20 evaluations, significantly fewer than the 60–100 evaluations often required by baselines. This suggests a more efficient use of evaluation resources.
- **Attribution**: The authors attribute this superior performance to CORAL's autonomous design, where agents make search decisions (what to explore, how to modify solutions, when to pivot, when to evaluate) based on their analysis of prior attempts and feedback, rather than adhering to fixed, predefined heuristics.

#### 4.2. Multi-Agent Evolution Extends the Search Frontier

- **Enhanced Performance**: Beyond single-agent autonomy, 4-agent co-evolution in CORAL further improved performance, particularly on challenging stress-test problems where single-agent runs tended to plateau earlier.
 - **Kernel Engineering**: Four co-evolving agents reduced the execution cycles from the previous best known of 1363 to 1103 cycles, representing a 20% gain. This is an 18.3% cycle reduction compared to CORAL's single-agent performance (1350 cycles). This result sets a new state-of-the-art for the task without web search.
 - **Polyominoes Packing**: A 5.0% score increase was observed for 4-agent co-evolution over single-agent CORAL. With web search, CORAL achieved 89.4% coverage, surpassing the previous SOTA of 87%.
- **Generalization to Open-Source Models**: The benefits of multi-agent co-evolution were also observed when using an entirely open-source stack (MiniMax M2.5 + OpenCode) on the math and systems suites, indicating that the organizational advantages are generalizable and not limited to proprietary LLMs.
- **Mechanism**: These gains are attributed not solely to increased compute, but to the multi-agent co-evolution mechanism, which enables diverse exploration through asynchronous operation and shared persistent memory. This allows useful techniques and discoveries to diffuse among agents organically.

#### 4.3. Mechanistic Analyses

- **Local Verification**: Agents frequently performed local code execution and testing before external evaluation (e.g., 61% on Transaction, 57% on Kernel Engineering). This behavior was strongly associated with improvement: attempts with local execution improved more often (e.g., 47% on Kernel Engineering vs. 43% overall). This suggests local validation catches errors and refines candidates efficiently.
- **Knowledge Accumulation**:
 - On standard tasks, agents generated few knowledge artifacts (0.05 per attempt), with marginal gains from knowledge access.
 - On advanced tasks (Kernel Engineering, Polyominoes), agents created over 10× more artifacts (0.55-0.68 per attempt), and knowledge access was strongly correlated with improvement (55% on Kernel Engineering). Notes on advanced tasks contained deeper insights (e.g., architectural bottlenecks, failed strategies to avoid).
- **Cross-Agent Information Transfer**:
 - On Kernel Engineering, 36% of attempts were built upon another agent's commit, resulting in a higher improvement rate (17% vs. 9% overall). Most new records (66%) originated from cross-agent parents.
 - On Polyominoes, while direct code transfer was less frequent (12%), it was highly effective (50% improvement rate). Information transfer predominantly occurred through shared notes and skills (87% of rounds referenced knowledge from other agents).
- **Exploration Diversity**: Analysis of strategy keywords indicated that multi-agent populations explored a substantially broader search space than any single agent, with more than half of each agent's strategy vocabulary being unique.
- **Contribution Balance**: On Kernel Engineering, contributions were relatively balanced across agents. On Polyominoes, one agent contributed disproportionately to new records.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Knowledge Accumulation**: Disabling the creation of notes and skills led to degraded final scores across all three stress-test tasks. For example, Kernel Engineering performance regressed from 1350 to 1601 cycles (an 18.6% drop), confirming the causal role of knowledge artifacts.
- **Co-evolution vs. Independent Runs**: Comparing 4-agent co-evolution against the best performance from four independent single-agent runs showed that co-evolution consistently achieved better results on all three stress-test tasks. This indicates that the gains from multi-agent evolution are not merely a result of increased computational resources but stem from the coordination and knowledge sharing mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CORAL framework presents a significant step towards realizing autonomous open-ended discovery with LLM agents. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Paradigm Shift in LLM-based Discovery**: By delegating crucial evolutionary search decisions to autonomous agents and enabling asynchronous multi-agent coordination through shared persistent memory, CORAL introduces a new paradigm that moves beyond rigid, fixed evolutionary search algorithms. This approach suggests a more flexible and adaptive method for complex problem-solving.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency**: The empirical results demonstrate that CORAL significantly outperforms existing fixed evolutionary search baselines across a diverse range of tasks, achieving new state-of-the-art results on 10 tasks. The higher improvement rates and lower evaluation counts indicate a more efficient use of computational resources, which is particularly relevant for costly LLM API calls.
- **Scalability and Robustness**: The ability of multiple autonomous agents to collectively explore and discover solutions that single agents cannot find, even with equivalent computational resources, highlights the power of horizontal parallelism. The generalization of these gains to open-source LLMs further suggests the robustness and broad applicability of CORAL's architectural principles.
- **Mechanistic Insights into Agent Intelligence**: The detailed analyses provide a clearer understanding of *why* autonomous and multi-agent approaches are effective. Highlighting the importance of local verification, structured knowledge accumulation (notes and skills), and cross-agent information transfer offers actionable insights for future agent system design.
- **Acceleration of Scientific and Engineering Discovery**: CORAL's success in tasks like kernel engineering and mathematical optimization points to its potential to accelerate discovery in scientific research and complex engineering challenges where optimal solutions are elusive. By automating and enhancing the iterative discovery process, it could enable breakthroughs that are difficult or time-consuming for human researchers alone.
- **Foundational Infrastructure**: CORAL is presented as a strong baseline framework and an extensible infrastructure, encouraging further research into autonomous discovery systems. Its modular design and open-source nature can facilitate community engagement and future developments.
- **Future Directions**: The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the reliance on powerful foundation models, the lack of bootstrapped agent heterogeneity, and the assumption of well-specified evaluators. These open avenues for future research, including training specialized small models, exploring diverse agent personalities, and co-evolving evaluators.

Overall, CORAL contributes to the growing body of work suggesting that autonomous, collaborative AI agents can fundamentally change how complex, open-ended problems are tackled, marking a potential turning point in AI's capability for sustained, knowledge-accumulating discovery.
