# MaxSup: Overcoming Representation Collapse in Label Smoothing

> [!summary] Summary
> MaxSup, a regularization technique developed by researchers including those from the University of Mannheim and the University of Washington, mitigates the representation collapse and misclassification overconfidence issues of Label Smoothing (LS). By modifying LS's loss to remove a problematic "error amplification" term, MaxSup achieves higher top-1 accuracy across classification benchmarks, such as a 0.41% improvement for DeiT-Small on ImageNet-1K, and produces more transferable feature representations.

## Problem

- Standard multi-class classification with one-hot labels can lead to overconfident models, as real-world classes often share features.
- While Label Smoothing (LS) helps reduce overconfidence, it paradoxically increases overconfidence in misclassified samples.
- LS also leads to an over-compression of feature representations, diminishing intra-class diversity and impairing feature transferability to downstream tasks.

## Method

- The authors analytically decomposed Label Smoothing's loss, identifying a specific "==error amplification term==" responsible for overconfidence in misclassified samples and feature collapse.
- Max Suppression (**MaxSup**) is proposed, which modifies the regularization by penalizing the model's *top-1 logit* (zmaxz_{max}zmax​) instead of the ground-truth logit (zgtz_{gt}zgt​).
- This approach consistently suppresses the most confident prediction, preventing the error amplification seen in LS when the model misclassifies, and maintaining the ground-truth logit's value.

## Results

- MaxSup consistently yielded higher top-1 accuracy across diverse image classification benchmarks, including a **0.41%** improvement for DeiT-Small over LS on ImageNet-**1K**, and performed best in fine-grained and long-tailed classification.
- MaxSup preserved intra-class variation and improved inter-class separability in feature spaces, leading to more robust and transferable features evidenced by enhanced linear-probe transfer accuracy and higher mIoU (**42.8%** vs. **42.4%** for LS) in semantic segmentation on ADE20K.
- MaxSup demonstrated improved calibration in out-of-distribution settings, enhanced model interpretability via Grad-CAM, and achieved these benefits with negligible computational overhead and robustness to hyperparameter changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Label Smoothing's (LS) overall benefits stem solely from its regularization component, while a distinct "error amplification term" causes its detrimental effects, such as overconfidence in misclassifications.
> - The "error amplification term" arises when the ground-truth logit is not the maximum, inadvertently reinforcing incorrect top-1 logits by penalizing the actual ground-truth logit.
> - By consistently penalizing the *top-1 logit* rather than the ground-truth logit, MaxSup prevents this error amplification and maintains richer, more discriminative feature representations.
