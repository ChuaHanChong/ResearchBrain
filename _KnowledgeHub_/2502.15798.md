# MaxSup: Overcoming Representation Collapse in Label Smoothing

> [!summary] Summary
> MaxSup, a regularization technique developed by researchers including those from the University of Mannheim and the University of Washington, mitigates the representation collapse and misclassification overconfidence issues of Label Smoothing (LS). By modifying LS's loss to remove a problematic "error amplification" term, MaxSup achieves higher top-1 accuracy across classification benchmarks, such as a 0.41% improvement for DeiT-Small on ImageNet-1K, and produces more transferable feature representations.

## Problem

- Standard multi-class classification with one-hot labels can lead to overconfident models, as real-world classes often share features.
- While Label Smoothing (LS) helps reduce overconfidence, it paradoxically increases overconfidence in misclassified samples.
- LS also leads to an over-compression of feature representations, diminishing intra-class diversity and impairing feature transferability to downstream tasks.

## Method

- The authors analytically decomposed Label Smoothing's loss, identifying a specific "==error amplification term==" responsible for overconfidence in misclassified samples and feature collapse.
- Max Suppression (**MaxSup**) is proposed, which modifies the regularization by penalizing the model's *top-1 logit* (zmaxz_{max}zmax) instead of the ground-truth logit (zgtz_{gt}zgt).
- This approach consistently suppresses the most confident prediction, preventing the error amplification seen in LS when the model misclassifies, and maintaining the ground-truth logit's value.

## Results

- MaxSup consistently yielded higher top-1 accuracy across diverse image classification benchmarks, including a **0.41%** improvement for DeiT-Small over LS on ImageNet-**1K**, and performed best in fine-grained and long-tailed classification.
- MaxSup preserved intra-class variation and improved inter-class separability in feature spaces, leading to more robust and transferable features evidenced by enhanced linear-probe transfer accuracy and higher mIoU (**42.8%** vs. **42.4%** for LS) in semantic segmentation on ADE20K.
- MaxSup demonstrated improved calibration in out-of-distribution settings, enhanced model interpretability via Grad-CAM, and achieved these benefits with negligible computational overhead and robustness to hyperparameter changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Label Smoothing's (LS) overall benefits stem solely from its regularization component, while a distinct "error amplification term" causes its detrimental effects, such as overconfidence in misclassifications.
> - The "error amplification term" arises when the ground-truth logit is not the maximum, inadvertently reinforcing incorrect top-1 logits by penalizing the actual ground-truth logit.
> - By consistently penalizing the *top-1 logit* rather than the ground-truth logit, MaxSup prevents this error amplification and maintains richer, more discriminative feature representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-class classification, a foundational task in machine learning, typically employs one-hot encoding for labels, which assumes mutual orthogonality between classes. However, real-world classes often share features or exhibit semantic similarities, rendering the one-hot assumption potentially suboptimal. This mismatch can lead to models that are overly confident in their predictions, potentially degrading generalization performance.

Label Smoothing (LS), introduced by Szegedy et al. (2016), is a widely adopted regularization technique designed to mitigate this overconfidence. It achieves this by softening the target distribution, combining a uniform distribution with the hard ground-truth label. LS has demonstrated benefits in various applications, including image recognition and neural machine translation, often leading to improved accuracy and better calibration of model predictions.

Despite its benefits, recent research has identified two primary drawbacks of LS. Firstly, it has been observed to paradoxically induce overconfidence in misclassified samples (Zhu et al., 2022). Secondly, LS is associated with an over-compression of feature representations, leading to overly tight clusters in the feature space. This compaction diminishes intra-class diversity, which can negatively impact the transferability of learned features to downstream tasks (Kornblith et al., 2021; Sariyildiz et al., 2022; Xu and Liu, 2023). The precise mechanisms underlying these issues have remained areas of active investigation.

This work contributes to the ongoing research by offering a deeper analytical understanding of LS's internal mechanisms, specifically identifying an "error amplification term" within its loss formulation. Building on this understanding, the paper proposes a modification, Max Suppression (MaxSup), which aims to retain the beneficial aspects of LS while eliminating its detrimental effects on feature representation and misclassification confidence. This positions the work within the broader effort to develop more robust, generalizable, and transferable deep learning models by refining fundamental regularization strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations of this research are:

1. **Analytically Decompose Label Smoothing Loss:** The authors aim to precisely understand the underlying mechanisms of Label Smoothing (LS) at the logit level. Previous studies noted LS's negative effects, such as overconfidence in misclassified samples and compression of intra-class feature representations, but a detailed analytical explanation of *why* these phenomena occur was lacking. The objective is to identify specific components within the LS-induced loss that contribute to these observed shortcomings.

2. **Identify the Root Cause of LS's Detrimental Effects:** Through this decomposition, the motivation is to pinpoint the exact term(s) responsible for inducing overconfidence in misclassified samples and driving the collapse of intra-class diversity. The hypothesis is that LS's uniform penalization of the ground-truth logit, even when it is not the top prediction, has unintended consequences. The paper specifically seeks to characterize "overconfidence" through the model’s top-1 prediction.

3. **Propose a Refined Regularization Technique:** Based on the analytical insights, a key objective is to design a new regularization method that overcomes the identified limitations of LS while preserving its beneficial effects on generalization and calibration for correctly classified samples. This new method should prevent the amplification of errors in misclassifications and maintain richer intra-class variability.

4. **Demonstrate Improved Feature Representations and Performance:** The authors aim to empirically validate that the proposed method, Max Suppression (MaxSup), yields more robust feature representations characterized by restored intra-class variation and sharpened inter-class boundaries. This objective includes demonstrating superior performance across a range of tasks, including large-scale image classification, fine-grained classification, long-tailed classification, corrupted image classification, and downstream tasks like semantic segmentation.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond merely observing the problems with LS to providing a theoretical explanation for them and then developing a principled, effective solution that enhances model robustness and transferability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research consists of both analytical decomposition and the proposal of a novel regularization technique, followed by extensive empirical validation.

#### 3.1. Analytical Decomposition of Label Smoothing Loss

The authors begin by revisiting the Cross-Entropy (CE) loss function when combined with Label Smoothing (LS). They analytically decompose the LS-induced loss term (L_LS) into two primary components at the logit level (Theorem 3.3, Corollary 3.4):

1. **Regularization Term:** This component penalizes the gap between the ground-truth logit ($z_{gt}$) and any logits that are smaller than it. Its function is to moderate overconfidence when the prediction is correct, pushing $z_{gt}$ closer to other logits.
2. **Error Amplification Term:** This term emerges specifically when $z_{gt}$ is *not* the maximum logit, i.e., when the model misclassifies. It penalizes the gap between $z_{gt}$ and *larger* (incorrect) logits. The analysis indicates that this term inadvertently increases overconfidence in incorrect predictions by pushing $z_{gt}$ further away from the *correct* (but not top-1) logit, thus reinforcing the erroneous top-1 logit. The paper also notes that LS's inconsistent regularization further impairs learning in misclassified cases by lowering $z_{gt}$ without penalizing $z_{max}$.

An ablation study using a DeiT-Small model on ImageNet-1K (Table 1) was conducted to empirically validate this decomposition. This study isolated the effects of each term, showing that performance gains from LS stemmed solely from the regularization term, while the error amplification term consistently degraded accuracy.

#### 3.2. Max Suppression (MaxSup) Regularization

Building upon the insights from the analytical decomposition, the authors propose Max Suppression (MaxSup). The core idea of MaxSup is to modify the regularization strategy by penalizing the model’s *top-1 logit* ($z_{max}$) rather than the ground-truth logit ($z_{gt}$).

The MaxSup loss component ($L_{MaxSup}$) is formulated as:
$L_{MaxSup} = \alpha \left( z_{max} - \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K z_k \right)$
where $z_{max}$ is the largest logit, and $\frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K z_k$ is the mean logit.

This formulation ensures a consistent regularization signal regardless of whether the current prediction is correct or misclassified.
- **When $z_{gt} = z_{max}$ (correct prediction):** MaxSup behaves similarly to LS, reducing overconfidence by pulling the top logit towards the mean.
- **When $z_{gt} \neq z_{max}$ (incorrect prediction):** MaxSup penalizes the incorrect top-1 logit ($z_{max}$), effectively suppressing its confidence. Crucially, it leaves the ground-truth logit ($z_{gt}$) unpenalized (except for the general mean shift), preventing the error amplification observed in LS.

A gradient analysis of MaxSup reveals that the top-1 logit $z_{max}$ is reduced by $\alpha(1 - \frac{1}{K})$, while all other logits are slightly increased by $\frac{\alpha}{K}$. This dynamic penalization focuses on the most confident prediction, promoting robust learning from misclassified samples.

#### 3.3. Experimental Validation

The proposed MaxSup method was evaluated across various tasks and architectures:

- **Feature-space Analysis:** Using metrics like intra-class variation ($\bar{d}_{within}$) and inter-class separability ($R^2$), MaxSup's effect on feature representations was quantitatively assessed on ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50 (Table 2). Linear-probe transfer accuracy on CIFAR-10 (Table 3) was used to evaluate feature transferability. Visualizations of penultimate-layer activations (Figures 3, 4) and Grad-CAMs (Figure 2) were utilized to qualitatively illustrate feature organization and attention.
- **Large-scale Image Classification:** MaxSup was benchmarked against LS and its variants (Zipf-LS, OLS) on ImageNet-1K using ResNet, MobileNetV2, and DeiT-Small architectures (Tables 4, 5).
- **Fine-Grained Classification:** Performance was evaluated on CUB-200-2011 and Stanford Cars datasets (Table 6).
- **Long-Tailed Classification:** Experiments on CIFAR-10-LT with varying imbalance ratios were conducted using ResNet-32 (Table 7).
- **Corrupted Image Classification:** Robustness to out-of-distribution data was assessed on CIFAR-10-C using ResNet-50 (Table 8).
- **Semantic Segmentation:** MaxSup-pretrained models were fine-tuned for semantic segmentation on ADE20K using UperNet with a DeiT-Small backbone (Table 9).
- **Ablation Studies:** Further ablations investigated MaxSup's robustness under different training recipes (Table 10) and the impact of the smoothing weight schedule (Table 11). Computational efficiency was also analyzed (Table 16).

All experiments maintained consistent training protocols and hyperparameters for fair comparison, with results reported as mean ± standard deviation over multiple runs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings, supported by both theoretical analysis and extensive empirical validation:

#### 4.1. Analytical and Ablation Studies of Label Smoothing

- **Decomposition of LS Loss:** The analytical decomposition revealed that Label Smoothing's loss comprises a beneficial regularization term, which reduces overconfidence for correct predictions, and a detrimental "error amplification" term.
- **Error Amplification Mechanism:** This error amplification term arises during misclassifications (when the ground-truth logit is not the highest). It penalizes the ground-truth logit more severely, inadvertently increasing the gap between it and the incorrect top-1 logit, thereby reinforcing overconfidence in erroneous predictions.
- **Empirical Validation of Decomposition:** An ablation study (Table 1) confirmed that the overall performance gain from LS is solely attributable to its regularization component. The isolated error amplification term was shown to consistently decrease accuracy, supporting the claim that it represents a fundamental shortcoming of LS. MaxSup, by removing this error amplification, achieved higher accuracy than standard LS.

#### 4.2. Improved Feature Representations and Transferability

- **Preserved Intra-Class Variation:** MaxSup led to a smaller reduction in intra-class variation ($\bar{d}_{within}$) compared to LS and other regularizers (Table 2). This indicates that MaxSup better retains subtle within-class diversity, which is beneficial for generalization.
- **Enhanced Inter-Class Separability:** Quantitatively, MaxSup achieved competitive or improved $R^2$ scores, suggesting good inter-class separation. Qualitatively, visualizations of penultimate-layer activations (Figures 3, 4) showed that MaxSup-trained models exhibited more distinct class clusters and richer intra-class diversity compared to LS-trained models.
- **Robust Transferability:** MaxSup-trained features demonstrated stronger transfer performance in linear-probe evaluations on CIFAR-10 (Table 3), preserving near-baseline accuracy, unlike LS and Logit Penalty which showed degradation. This suggests that MaxSup maintains more discriminative and transferable features.

#### 4.3. Superior Performance Across Diverse Classification Tasks

- **Large-Scale Image Classification (ImageNet-1K):** MaxSup consistently achieved the highest top-1 accuracy across various convolutional (ResNet, MobileNetV2) and transformer (DeiT-S) architectures (Tables 4, 5). For instance, MaxSup improved DeiT-Small accuracy by 0.41% over standard LS. It also outperformed other LS variants (Zipf-LS, OLS).
- **Fine-Grained Classification:** MaxSup yielded the best performance on fine-grained recognition datasets like CUB-200-2011 and Stanford Cars (Table 6), indicating its ability to learn more discriminative representations for subtle inter-class differences.
- **Long-Tailed Classification:** On the long-tailed CIFAR-10-LT dataset, MaxSup combined with Focal Loss consistently outperformed Focal Loss alone and Focal Loss with LS in overall accuracy across different imbalance ratios (Table 7). While not fully resolving minority class issues, it improved the trade-off between many- and medium-shot classes.
- **Corrupted Image Classification (OOD Robustness):** On CIFAR-10-C, MaxSup achieved comparable error rates to LS and better Expected Calibration Error (ECE), indicating improved confidence calibration in out-of-distribution settings (Table 8).

#### 4.4. Enhanced Downstream Task Performance

- **Semantic Segmentation:** Models initialized with MaxSup-pretrained weights achieved higher mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on the ADE20K semantic segmentation benchmark (42.8% vs. 42.4% for LS) (Table 9), indicating that MaxSup fosters more discriminative features suitable for dense prediction tasks.

#### 4.5. Improved Model Interpretability

- **Grad-CAM Visualizations:** Grad-CAM analysis (Figure 2) showed that MaxSup-trained models more effectively focused on class-discriminative regions and reduced attention to irrelevant background elements compared to LS. This suggests MaxSup leads to more accurate and consistent predictions by leveraging fine-grained object cues.

#### 4.6. Robustness and Efficiency

- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** MaxSup maintained high accuracy across different α scheduling strategies (Table 11) and alternative training recipes (Table 10), indicating its robustness to hyperparameter changes and training conditions.
- **Negligible Computational Overhead:** The computational cost of MaxSup was found to be minimal, introducing negligible overhead compared to the overall training time of deep networks (Table 16).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work stems from its dual contribution of providing a deeper theoretical understanding of Label Smoothing (LS) and proposing a practical, effective alternative.

**Theoretical Significance:**
The analytical decomposition of the LS-induced loss into a "regularization term" and an "error amplification term" offers a specific, logit-level explanation for previously observed phenomena such as intra-class feature collapse and overconfidence in misclassified samples. This clarifies *why* LS, despite its general benefits, has specific drawbacks. This theoretical insight advances the understanding of how label regularization techniques impact representation learning dynamics.

**Practical Significance and Potential Impact:**
- **Improved Model Robustness and Generalization:** MaxSup consistently demonstrates superior performance across a wide array of image classification tasks, including large-scale, fine-grained, and long-tailed benchmarks. Its ability to maintain richer intra-class diversity and sharper inter-class boundaries leads to more robust feature representations that generalize better. This is crucial for real-world applications where models encounter diverse data.
- **Enhanced Transfer Learning:** By preventing the over-compression of features, MaxSup yields representations that are more transferable to downstream tasks, as evidenced by improved performance in semantic segmentation and linear-probe evaluations. This has significant implications for resource-constrained scenarios where pre-training on large datasets followed by fine-tuning on smaller, task-specific datasets is common. MaxSup can enable more efficient and effective transfer of learned knowledge.
- **Better Calibrated and Interpretable Models:** The improved Expected Calibration Error (ECE) on corrupted datasets suggests that MaxSup can lead to better calibrated confidence scores, which is important for critical applications where trust in model predictions is paramount. Furthermore, the Grad-CAM visualizations indicate that MaxSup-trained models attend more accurately to salient regions, potentially leading to more interpretable decision-making processes.
- **Ease of Integration and Efficiency:** MaxSup is designed to be a computationally light and easily integrable method, requiring minimal changes to existing training pipelines. Its negligible computational overhead makes it a practical drop-in replacement for LS.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By highlighting the specific pitfalls of LS, MaxSup provides a blueprint for developing more sophisticated regularization techniques. The identified limitations of LS in knowledge distillation and its interaction with $\ell_2$ regularization, as mentioned by the authors, suggest avenues for future research to further refine regularization strategies and understand their impact on training dynamics and feature properties.

In summary, MaxSup offers a robust, generalizable, and interpretable alternative to Label Smoothing, directly addressing its core limitations. This has the potential to guide practitioners and researchers in developing more reliable deep learning models for a wide range of applications. The authors note no additional adverse effects or trade-offs have been observed, further reinforcing its practical utility.
