# Enhancing Spatial Understanding in Image Generation via Reward Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and ByteDance Seed developed SpatialScore, a reward model achieving 95.77% accuracy in evaluating spatial correctness, surpassing proprietary models like GPT-5. They then leveraged this model to fine-tune generative models, resulting in substantial improvements in compositional accuracy for complex spatial relationships in text-to-image generation.

## Problem

- Text-to-image generation models frequently fail to accurately depict complex spatial relationships among multiple objects specified in intricate text prompts, leading to compositional errors.
- Existing reward models are insufficient for evaluating fine-grained spatial understanding in reinforcement learning (RL) due to limitations in reasoning, cost, or susceptibility to hallucinations.
- The absence of a reliable, specialized reward signal for spatial understanding has hindered the effective application of RL to improve compositional accuracy in generative AI.

## Method

- A SPATIALREWARD-DATASET was constructed, comprising over 80,000 human-verified ==adversarial preference== pairs created by perturbing complex spatial prompts.
- SpatialScore, a specialized reward model utilizing a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, was trained on the SPATIALREWARD-DATASET using LoRA and a Bradley-Terry loss to accurately assess spatial correctness.
- The SpatialScore model was then used within an online reinforcement learning framework (GRPO) with a novel top-k filtering strategy to fine-tune the FLUX.1-dev image generation model.

## Results

- SpatialScore achieved a pairwise preference prediction accuracy of **95.77%** on its dedicated spatial evaluation benchmark, outperforming GPT-5 (**89.0%**) and Gemini-2.5 Pro (**95.1%**) for this task.
- RL fine-tuning with SpatialScore substantially increased the in-domain SpatialScore of the Flux.1-dev model from 2.18 to 7.81 and showed consistent improvements across out-of-domain spatial benchmarks.
- The RL-enhanced model produced images that qualitatively and quantitatively demonstrated more accurate depiction of complex spatial relationships, while a baseline trained on a simpler reward model degraded on intricate prompts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A specialized reward model trained on meticulously curated adversarial preference data can achieve superior performance in complex spatial understanding compared to general-purpose and proprietary large vision-language models.
> - Online reinforcement learning, when guided by a high-fidelity and specific spatial reward signal, effectively enhances the compositional accuracy of advanced text-to-image models.
> - A top-k filtering strategy applied to advantage estimation in GRPO improves the stability and efficiency of RL training for generative models by balancing sampling diversity and reward distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-image (T2I) generation has witnessed remarkable progress in recent years, with diffusion models and large generative models achieving unprecedented levels of visual fidelity, diversity, and creativity. Models such as Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, and Midjourney have democratized image creation, allowing users to generate complex visuals from simple text prompts.

However, despite these advancements, a significant challenge persists: the accurate depiction of scenes involving intricate spatial relationships among multiple objects. As prompts become more complex, requiring specific placements, orientations, and relative positions (e.g., "a red ball *to the left of* a blue cube, *behind* a green cylinder"), current T2I models often struggle, leading to "compositional errors" where objects are present but incorrectly arranged. This limitation often necessitates multiple sampling attempts and meticulous prompt engineering to achieve satisfactory results, hindering user experience and efficiency.

In parallel, reinforcement learning (RL) has revolutionized large language models (LLMs) by aligning them more closely with human preferences and instructions. This success has spurred research into applying similar RL techniques, particularly Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) styles, to diffusion models for T2I generation. The potential of RL lies in its ability to fine-tune generative models based on explicit feedback, thereby improving specific aspects of generation quality or alignment.

The critical bottleneck for effectively applying RL to enhance spatial understanding in T2I models has been the lack of a reliable and effective reward model. Existing reward models designed for general aesthetic quality (e.g., HPSv2, Aesthetic Score), overall text-image alignment (e.g., PickScore, ImageReward, UnifiedReward), or even those relying on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have proven insufficient for evaluating complex spatial relationships. They either lack the fine-grained reasoning capability, suffer from hallucinations (common in open-source VLMs when confronted with complex reasoning tasks), are prohibitively expensive for online RL (proprietary VLMs like GPT-5, Gemini-2.5 Pro), or are too simplistic and sensitive to visual factors (rule-based methods like GenEval, which rely on object detectors and struggle with generalization to long, complex prompts).

This work positions itself precisely to address this critical gap. By introducing a specialized, high-fidelity reward model specifically tailored for spatial understanding, it unlocks the potential of online reinforcement learning to truly enhance compositional accuracy in T2I generation. It moves beyond generic alignment and aesthetic evaluation to tackle a core challenge in generative AI: making models understand and execute complex spatial instructions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the spatial understanding of current image generation models**, enabling them to accurately depict scenes that involve complex spatial relationships among multiple objects as described in intricate text prompts.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations observed in the current state of text-to-image generation:
1. **Struggle with Complex Spatial Prompts:** Despite significant advancements in visual fidelity and creativity, state-of-the-art T2I models frequently fail to accurately render images according to complex spatial arrangements specified in prompts. This leads to compositionally incorrect outputs, forcing users to generate numerous images or drastically simplify prompts.
2. **Inadequacy of Existing Reward Models:** The application of reinforcement learning, a promising avenue for improving model alignment, has been hampered by the absence of a robust reward signal for spatial understanding.
 - **General-purpose reward models** (e.g., HPSv3, Pickscore) are good for overall quality but lack the fine-grained capability to assess specific spatial relationships. They might assign high scores to aesthetically pleasing images that are spatially incorrect (as demonstrated in Figure 1).
 - **Proprietary VLM APIs** (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini-2.5 Pro) show strong zero-shot spatial understanding but are too costly for the frequent querying required by online RL.
 - **Open-source VLMs** (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL series), while powerful, exhibit substantial hallucinations and unreliability when reasoning about complex multi-object spatial relationships, making them unsuitable as reward providers.
 - **Rule-based benchmarks** (e.g., GenEval) are limited to simple, template-based prompts, fail to generalize to longer, more complex descriptions, and are susceptible to visual ambiguities like occlusion (as shown in Figure 2).
3. **Need for Reliable Feedback:** The authors argue that effective online RL for spatial understanding fundamentally relies on constructing a highly reliable and accurate reward model that can precisely evaluate the correctness of spatial relationships. Without such a model, RL optimization can be misguided or unstable.

Therefore, the core motivation is to develop a specialized, high-fidelity reward model capable of discerning subtle spatial inaccuracies, and subsequently leverage this model within an online reinforcement learning framework to fine-tune image generation models for superior spatial reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is structured into three main components: dataset construction, specialized reward model training, and reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning of generative models.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction: SPATIALREWARD-DATASET

To address the lack of reliable spatial preference data, the authors introduce **SPATIALREWARD-DATASET**, comprising over 80,000 adversarial preference pairs.
- **Adversarial Pair Generation:** Each pair consists of a "perfect image" and a "perturbed image" generated from distinct but related prompts. GPT-5 is used to create initial complex prompts describing multi-object spatial relationships. These "perfect prompts" are then subtly perturbed by GPT-5 to modify one or more spatial relations (e.g., "left" to "right," swapping object positions) while maintaining other relationships.
- **Image Generation:** State-of-the-art T2I models (Qwen-Image, HunyuanImage-2.1, Seedream 4.0) are used to generate images from these perfect and perturbed prompts. Crucially, both images in a preference pair are generated by the *same* T2I model to minimize aesthetic or style biases, ensuring the comparison focuses purely on spatial accuracy.
- **Human Verification:** All 80,000 pairs undergo rigorous human expert review and filtering. This step ensures that: 1) the "perfect image" genuinely aligns with all spatial constraints in its prompt, and 2) the "perturbed image" truly exhibits the intended spatial deviation from the perfect image, discarding cases where the perturbation didn't manifest visually or the images were spatially identical.
- **Complexity:** The dataset features significantly longer prompts with multiple spatial relationships compared to existing simple benchmarks like GenEval, ensuring it covers a wide range of real-world, complex scenarios.

#### 3.2. Reward Model Training: SpatialScore

Building on the SPATIALREWARD-DATASET, the authors train **SpatialScore**, a specialized reward model.
- **Architecture:** SpatialScore utilizes Qwen2.5-VL-7B, an advanced Visual Language Model (VLM), as its backbone (H_phi). The VLM's original language modeling head is replaced with a new linear reward head (R_phi), implemented as a multilayer perceptron (MLP). This leverages the VLM's strong representational power while tailoring its output for reward prediction.
- **Fine-tuning Strategy:** The model is fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) to efficiently adapt the VLM's parameters while preserving its pre-trained knowledge.
- **Reward Modeling:** Instead of directly outputting a deterministic score, SpatialScore models the reward as a Gaussian distribution (s ~ N(mu, sigma^2)), inspired by HPSv3. This provides a more robust representation for ranking. A special token `<reward>` is inserted into the VLM instruction, and its final-layer embedding is mapped to the mean (mu) and standard deviation (sigma) of this distribution.
- **Loss Function:** The model is optimized using the Bradley-Terry model with a binary cross-entropy loss. For each triplet (prompt c, preferred image y_w, less preferred image y_l), the loss minimizes `–log P(y_w > y_l | c)`, where P is derived from the sigmoid of the difference between the scores of y_w and y_l. This trains SpatialScore to consistently assign higher scores to spatially correct images.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning for Image Generation

With SpatialScore providing a reliable reward signal, the authors employ it for online RL fine-tuning of an image generation model.
- **Base Model:** FLUX.1-dev is chosen as the base generation model due to its advanced performance and strong support for long, complex text inputs, which aligns with the dataset's characteristics.
- **RL Algorithm:** The GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm, adapted from Flow-GRPO, is utilized. GRPO requires diverse samples for policy optimization.
- **Stochastic Sampling:** Flow models inherently use deterministic ODEs for sampling. To enable policy exploration necessary for RL, the deterministic ODE is converted into an equivalent Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE), discretized using the Euler–Maruyama scheme, which injects Gaussian noise.
- **Advantage Estimation:** For each prompt, the policy generates a group of G images. SpatialScore evaluates each image, assigning a reward score. The advantage (A_i) for each image is computed by normalizing its reward against the group's mean and standard deviation.
- **Top-k Filtering Strategy:** To mitigate "advantage bias" (where easy prompts yield many high-reward samples, or hard prompts yield consistently low rewards, leading to biased group-wise normalization), a novel top-k filtering strategy is introduced. Images within a group are ranked by SpatialScore. Only the top-k most accurate and bottom-k least accurate samples are selected to compute the group mean and standard deviation for advantage calculation. These selected 2k samples are then used for policy updates, balancing the reward distribution and ensuring more stable and efficient training with fewer NFEs (Number of Function Evaluations) during the policy update stage.
- **Policy Optimization:** The GRPO algorithm minimizes an objective that involves the policy ratio and the calculated advantages, with an additional KL-divergence penalty to prevent the fine-tuned policy from deviating too much from the reference (base) policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields significant findings across reward model performance and the efficacy of RL-enhanced image generation.

#### 4.1. SpatialScore Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Accuracy:** SpatialScore achieves a pairwise preference prediction accuracy of 95.77% on the authors' curated reward evaluation benchmark, specifically designed for complex spatial reasoning.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** It significantly outperforms existing open-source VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B, which only reaches 76.4% accuracy) and general-purpose image reward models (e.g., PickScore, ImageReward, HPSv3).
- **Surpassing Proprietary Models:** Remarkably, SpatialScore even surpasses leading proprietary models like GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5 Pro (which achieve 89.0% and 95.1% respectively) in spatial understanding on this benchmark, despite SpatialScore being a 7B-parameter model compared to potentially much larger proprietary models.
- **Robustness:** Its performance is robust across different levels of spatial perturbation (1-perturbation vs. 2-3 perturbations), indicating its ability to discern varied degrees of spatial inaccuracy.
- **Ablation on Model Size:** The performance of SpatialScore improves with larger backbone sizes (e.g., 3B to 7B to 32B), demonstrating good scalability. The 7B variant offers an excellent balance of performance and computational efficiency.

#### 4.2. RL-enhanced Image Generation Performance

- **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - **In-domain SpatialScore:** The base model Flux.1-dev, after RL fine-tuning with SpatialScore, shows a substantial increase in its SpatialScore from 2.18 to 7.81, directly confirming the effectiveness of the reward-guided training.
 - **Out-of-domain Benchmarks:** Consistent and significant improvements are observed across various spatial-aware sub-dimensions of out-of-domain benchmarks like DPG-Bench, TIIF-Bench (short and long prompts), and UniGenBench++ (short and long prompts). This demonstrates the generalization capabilities of the fine-tuned model.
 - **Comparison to GenEval-based RL:** A baseline model trained with Flow-GRPO on the rule-based GenEval benchmark shows some improvements on simple short prompts but *degrades markedly* on long prompts and complex spatial relationships, highlighting the limitations of simplistic reward models and their inability to generalize.
 - **Overall Alignment:** A full-spectrum evaluation on DPG-Bench reveals that the RL-enhanced model achieves consistent and substantial improvements across all five major dimensions (Global, Entity, Attribute, Relation, Other), with overall performance approaching that of proprietary models like GPT-Image-1.
- **Qualitative Improvements:**
 - The RL-trained model with SpatialScore consistently produces images that more accurately reflect complex spatial relationships across multiple objects described in the prompts. Visual examples (Figure 6, Figure 11) vividly demonstrate its ability to correctly position objects, adhere to relative alignments, and maintain compositional integrity.
 - In contrast, the Flux.1-dev model and the GenEval-trained variant frequently fail to follow complex spatial instructions, often missing key objects, misplacing items, or generating visually implausible artifacts (e.g., floating objects due to object detector reliance in GenEval).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Top-k Filtering

- **Training Acceleration and Stability:** The top-k filtering strategy demonstrably accelerates RL training compared to the baseline GRPO setup without filtering. It helps maintain a better trade-off between sampling balance and diversity (with k=6 identified as an optimal configuration).
- **Efficiency:** Top-k filtering achieves comparable or even superior performance while significantly reducing the number of function evaluations (NFEs) required during the policy update stage (e.g., from 24*6 to 12*6 NFEs per prompt), leading to more efficient training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions and opens avenues for future impact in the field of generative AI:

1. **Fills a Critical Research Gap:** The paper successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of reliably evaluating and enhancing complex spatial understanding in text-to-image generation. By developing **SpatialScore**, a specialized and highly accurate reward model, it provides a missing piece essential for advanced compositional control.

2. **Enables Effective Reinforcement Learning:** The creation of SpatialScore unlocks the full potential of online reinforcement learning for fine-tuning generative models on nuanced spatial tasks. Previously, RL efforts were bottlenecked by inadequate reward signals; this work demonstrates how a high-fidelity reward model can lead to substantial and consistent performance gains. This methodology can be generalized to improve other specific aspects of T2I generation that require precise evaluation.

3. **Advances Generative Model Capabilities:** The RL-enhanced T2I models exhibit significantly improved spatial reasoning. This means generative models can better understand and execute intricate user instructions, moving beyond producing merely "plausible" images to generating "correct" compositions. This is crucial for applications demanding high fidelity to textual prompts.

4. **Open-Source Contribution:** The introduction of the **SPATIALREWARD-DATASET** (80K preference pairs) and the strong **SpatialScore** reward model as open resources provides invaluable tools for the research community. This will undoubtedly accelerate further research into compositional generation, reward modeling, and RL for generative AI.

5. **Broad Practical Implications:** Improved spatial understanding has wide-ranging practical impacts:
 - **Professional Content Creation:** Designers, artists, and marketers can generate images with greater precision, reducing iterative adjustments and saving time.
 - **Virtual World and Game Development:** Creating environments and scenes with accurate object placement becomes more feasible, enhancing realism and consistency.
 - **E-commerce and Product Visualization:** Generating product images with specific arrangements and contexts can be done more effectively.
 - **Accessibility:** Making T2I models more obedient to detailed prompts can improve their utility for a broader range of users.
 - **Reduced Sampling Costs:** By generating more accurate images on the first attempt, the need for multiple sampling runs is reduced, leading to cost savings in API usage and computational resources.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The work points towards exciting future endeavors, particularly extending spatial understanding to **video generation** and **sim-to-real embodied simulation**. In these domains, consistent spatial and temporal dynamics are paramount, and the methodology developed here provides a strong foundation for tackling these complex challenges, potentially impacting fields like robotics, autonomous systems, and advanced virtual reality.

In conclusion, this research marks a significant step forward in making generative AI models more intelligent and controllable, specifically in the critical area of spatial reasoning. By establishing a robust framework for spatial reward modeling and RL, it paves the way for a new generation of T2I models that are not only creative but also highly precise in their compositional abilities.
