# Emerging Properties in Self-Supervised Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Facebook AI Research (FAIR), Inria, and Sorbonne University investigates self-supervised learning for Vision Transformers (ViTs) and introduces DINO, a self-distillation framework. DINO-trained ViTs learn rich semantic features, demonstrating emergent properties like explicit object segmentation in self-attention maps and providing highly effective off-the-shelf features for k-NN classification, outperforming other self-supervised methods on ImageNet by significant margins.

## Problem

- Supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs) are computationally demanding, require vast datasets, and had not yet demonstrated clear benefits or unique emergent properties compared to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs).
- The challenge of replicating the success of self-supervised Transformers in Natural Language Processing (NLP) for the computer vision domain.
- A lack of understanding if self-supervised pretraining could uncover inherently different and more valuable visual representations in ViTs beyond simple performance gains.

## Method

- Introducing ==DINO== (DIstillation with NO labels), a self-supervised learning framework that trains a student ViT to match the output of a ==momentum-updated teacher ViT==.
- Employing a ==multi-crop strategy== to generate diverse views of an image, where the student processes all views while the teacher focuses on global views, ensuring robust feature learning.
- Preventing model collapse using a combination of ==centering (mean normalization)== and ==sharpening (low temperature softmax)== on the teacher's output, eliminating the need for explicit predictors, contrastive loss, or batch normalization in the heads for ViTs.

## Results

- DINO, particularly with ViT architectures, achieved state-of-the-art self-supervised performance, outperforming other methods by **+3.5%** (linear classification) and **+7.9%** (k-NN) on ImageNet for ViT-Small.
- DINO-trained ViTs automatically learn explicit semantic segmentation information, demonstrably capturing object boundaries in self-attention maps and showing strong performance on PASCAL VOC and DAVIS 2017 without fine-tuning.
- The learned ViT features are highly effective for simple k-NN classification on ImageNet (e.g., **77.4%** top-1 for ViT-B/8), indicating high-quality, semantically rich embeddings that transfer well to various downstream tasks like image retrieval and classification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning is critical for unlocking unique capabilities in Vision Transformers (ViTs) not observed with supervised training, particularly regarding spatial understanding.
> - DINO-trained ViTs intrinsically develop emergent semantic segmentation capabilities, visible in their self-attention maps, without explicit pixel-level supervision.
> - The momentum teacher within DINO consistently outperforms the student during training, acting as a 'better role model' and guiding the learning process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial juncture in the evolution of deep learning for computer vision: the convergence of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and self-supervised learning (SSL).

**Vision Transformers (ViTs):** Transformers, initially developed for Natural Language Processing (NLP) and known for their success in models like BERT and GPT, have recently been adapted for computer vision. Vision Transformers (ViTs) have shown competitive performance against traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). However, a key limitation noted by the authors is that supervised ViTs are often computationally more demanding, require significantly more training data (e.g., JFT-300M dataset), and, crucially, have not yet delivered clear benefits or exhibited unique properties compared to CNNs in the visual domain. This raises the question of whether their full potential in vision remains untapped.

**Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** In NLP, self-supervised pretraining (e.g., masked language modeling in BERT or language modeling in GPT) has been a primary driver of Transformer success, allowing models to learn rich, general-purpose representations from vast amounts of unlabeled text. Inspired by this, the computer vision community has developed numerous self-supervised methods (e.g., contrastive learning, knowledge distillation-based methods) to learn visual representations without explicit human annotations. These methods have significantly closed the performance gap with supervised learning for CNNs and offer advantages like reduced annotation costs and improved transferability.

**Bridging the Gap:** This paper proposes to bridge these two powerful paradigms. The authors question whether the "muted success" of ViTs in vision can be attributed to their reliance on *supervised* pretraining. By applying self-supervised learning to ViTs, they aim to uncover unique properties and advantages that have not emerged with supervised training. They build upon the principles of recent successful SSL methods, particularly those involving momentum encoders and multi-crop augmentations, to design DINO. DINO simplifies some existing approaches by interpreting self-supervised learning as a form of "knowledge distillation with no labels" and demonstrating that it can work effectively without components often considered critical in other SSL frameworks (e.g., predictors, batch normalizations in heads for ViTs).

In essence, this work seeks to replicate the success story of self-supervised Transformers in NLP for the visual domain. It pushes the boundaries of ViT capabilities by showing that SSL can unlock inherent properties like object segmentation and highly effective k-NN classification, which are less pronounced in supervised ViTs or CNNs. This positions the paper as a significant step towards developing a "BERT-like model based on ViT" for vision, capable of learning powerful, generic visual features from uncurated data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether **self-supervised learning (SSL) can endow Vision Transformers (ViTs) with new, distinctive properties that are not clearly observed with supervised ViTs or convolutional networks (convnets).**

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Supervised ViTs:** The authors note that while ViTs are competitive with convnets, they are often more computationally demanding, require larger datasets for effective training, and have yet to demonstrate unique advantages or emergent properties when trained in a supervised manner. This raises the question of whether their full potential in vision is being stifled by traditional supervised pretraining.

2. **Drawing Inspiration from NLP's Success:** A major motivation comes from the success of Transformers in Natural Language Processing (NLP). Models like BERT and GPT achieved their impressive capabilities largely through self-supervised pretraining on vast amounts of unlabeled text. The authors hypothesize that a similar paradigm shift could unlock ViTs' true potential in computer vision, where image-level supervision can simplify rich visual information into a single category.

3. **Uncovering Emergent Visual Properties:** Beyond mere performance gains, a core motivation is to discover whether self-supervised pretraining, with its richer learning signal derived from the data itself, can lead to inherently different and more valuable representations in ViTs. Specifically, they aim to observe:
 - **Explicit Semantic Segmentation Information:** Whether self-supervised ViT features, particularly their self-attention maps, automatically capture information about the semantic layout of an image, including object boundaries, without any explicit segmentation labels.
 - **High-Quality Features for Nearest Neighbor Search:** Whether the learned features are so semantically meaningful that they can serve as excellent off-the-shelf features for simple nearest-neighbor classification (k-NN) without the need for additional fine-tuning or complex classifiers.

4. **Developing a Simple and Effective SSL Framework for ViTs:** To explore these hypotheses, the authors aim to design a straightforward self-supervised approach. This leads to DINO, which is conceptualized as a form of "knowledge distillation with no labels." A key motivation here is to demonstrate that powerful self-supervised learning can be achieved with a simplified setup, without relying on complex mechanisms like contrastive loss queues, explicit predictors, or batch normalization in certain parts of the architecture.

In summary, the paper is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of ViTs in computer vision by leveraging self-supervised learning, not just for performance, but to reveal novel, intrinsically useful properties that could fundamentally change how visual representations are learned and applied.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces **DINO (DIstillation with NO labels)**, a self-supervised learning framework built on the principles of knowledge distillation and momentum encoders, specifically tailored for Vision Transformers.

**Core Framework: Self-Distillation with No Labels**
DINO's core idea is to train a **student network** ($g_{\theta_s}$) to match the output of a **teacher network** ($g_{\theta_t}$). Unlike traditional knowledge distillation, there are no predefined labels; instead, the teacher generates its own "soft targets" based on different views of the same image.

1. **Multi-Crop Strategy:** Given an input image, DINO generates a set of diverse views ($V$). This typically includes two "global" views (e.g., 224x224 pixels, covering a large area) and several "local" views (e.g., 96x96 pixels, covering smaller, arbitrary regions).
2. **Student and Teacher Networks:** Both student and teacher networks share the same architecture, composed of a backbone (ViT or ResNet) and a projection head (a 3-layer MLP followed by L2 normalization and a weight-normalized fully connected layer). Critically, for ViTs, the entire DINO system is designed to be **BN-free (Batch Normalization-free)**, which simplifies training by avoiding complexities associated with synchronized BNs in distributed setups.
3. **Loss Function:** The student is trained to minimize the cross-entropy loss between its output probability distribution ($P_s$) for *all* generated views and the teacher's output probability distribution ($P_t$) for *global* views. This "local-to-global" correspondence encourages robustness and rich feature learning. The gradient is "stopped" on the teacher's output, meaning gradients only flow through the student network.
4. **Teacher Update Mechanism:** The teacher network's parameters ($\theta_t$) are updated using an **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** of the student's parameters ($\theta_s$). This is akin to a momentum encoder. The authors observe that this momentum teacher consistently outperforms the student during training, acting as a "better role model" and providing higher-quality targets, similar to Polyak-Ruppert averaging for model ensembling.
5. **Avoiding Model Collapse:** A crucial aspect of self-supervised learning is preventing trivial solutions (collapse). DINO achieves this with a combination of two simple operations applied to the teacher's output:
 - **Centering:** A bias term is added to the teacher's output, which is an EMA of the teacher's output mean over the batch. This prevents one dimension from dominating the output.
 - **Sharpening:** A low temperature parameter ($\tau_t$) is used in the teacher's softmax normalization, making its output distribution sharper (more peaked). This counteracts the centering's tendency towards a uniform distribution.
 The complementarity of centering and sharpening is sufficient to avoid collapse without the need for contrastive loss, clustering constraints, or explicit predictors (which are critical in BYOL).

**Implementation Details:**
- **Pretraining Data:** ImageNet dataset, *without* labels.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW.
- **Batch Size:** Large, typically 1024, distributed across multiple GPUs.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** Linear warm-up followed by cosine decay.
- **Data Augmentations:** Similar to BYOL's augmentations (color jittering, Gaussian blur, solarization) combined with multi-crop.
- **ViT Specifics:** Utilizes standard ViT architecture (e.g., ViT-S/16, ViT-S/8, ViT-B/16, ViT-B/8), with the `[CLS]` token's output used for the projection head. Patch sizes (e.g., 16x16, 8x8, 5x5) are explored as a key hyperparameter.

**Evaluation Protocols:**
The paper employs standard evaluation protocols for self-supervised learning:
1. **Linear Classification:** Training a linear classifier on frozen features from the pretrained backbone.
2. **Fine-tuning:** Initializing a model with pretrained weights and fine-tuning all parameters on a downstream task.
3. **Weighted k-Nearest Neighbor (k-NN) Classification:** A novel emphasis is placed on k-NN evaluation. This involves freezing the pretrained model, computing features for the training data, and then classifying test images by finding their k-nearest neighbors among the training features. This protocol is highlighted for its simplicity, as it requires no hyperparameter tuning, no data augmentation for evaluation, and only one pass over the dataset, making it a robust measure of feature quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several compelling findings regarding the properties of ViTs trained with DINO, alongside comprehensive ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Performance on ImageNet Benchmark

- **Comparable with CNNs:** DINO achieves state-of-the-art performance on ImageNet with a ResNet-50 backbone, validating its effectiveness in a standard setting.
- **Superior with ViTs:** When applied to ViT architectures (ViT-Small), DINO significantly outperforms other leading self-supervised methods (BYOL, MoCov2, SwAV) by a notable margin: +3.5% in linear classification and an even more substantial +7.9% in k-NN evaluation. This highlights a strong synergy between DINO and ViTs.
- **Impact of Patch Size:** A crucial finding is the importance of using smaller patch sizes (e.g., 8x8 instead of 16x16) for ViTs. Reducing patch size significantly boosts performance, particularly k-NN accuracy, even without increasing model parameters. For instance, a ViT-Base with 8x8 patches (ViT-B/8) achieves 80.1% top-1 accuracy in linear classification and 77.4% with a k-NN classifier, outperforming previous state-of-the-art models (even larger ones) with fewer parameters and faster inference.

#### 4.2. Emergent Properties in Self-Supervised ViTs

- **Explicit Semantic Segmentation from Self-Attention:**
 - **Qualitative:** DINO-trained ViTs automatically learn features containing explicit information about the semantic segmentation of an image, which is visible in the self-attention maps of the `[CLS]` token (Figure 1, Figure 3, Figure 10). These maps effectively highlight object boundaries and different semantic regions, even for occluded or small objects.
 - **Quantitative:** This emergent segmentation capability is quantitatively supported by comparing Jaccard similarity between ground truth masks and masks derived from self-attention maps on PASCAL VOC. DINO-trained ViTs show a clear advantage over supervised ViTs in this regard. Performance on the DAVIS 2017 video instance segmentation benchmark, without fine-tuning, further demonstrates the retained spatial information.
- **Excellent k-NN Classification Performance:** DINO-trained ViT features are exceptionally good for simple nearest-neighbor classification. Their k-NN accuracy on ImageNet is remarkably close to their linear classification performance (e.g., 74.5% vs. 77.0% for ViT-S, 77.4% vs. 80.1% for ViT-B/8). This property is particularly strong with DINO + ViT and is not as pronounced with other SSL methods or ResNet backbones, indicating the high quality and semantic richness of the learned embeddings.

#### 4.3. Transferability and Retrieval

- **Image Retrieval and Copy Detection:** DINO ViT features demonstrate strong performance in image retrieval (revisited Oxford and Paris datasets) and copy detection (INRIA Copydays), often outperforming supervised features. When DINO is pretrained on a domain-specific dataset like Google Landmarks v2 (GLDv2), its performance is "remarkably good," surpassing previously published off-the-shelf methods.
- **Downstream Task Transfer Learning:** Self-supervised pretraining with DINO consistently leads to better transfer learning performance (fine-tuning) on various downstream classification datasets (e.g., Cifar-10/100, iNaturalist, Flowers, Cars) compared to supervised ViT pretraining.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Methodological Insights

- **Importance of Momentum Encoder:** The momentum encoder (EMA teacher) is *critical* for DINO's success. Without it, the model either collapses or performs very poorly.
- **Teacher Outperforms Student:** A key observation is that the momentum teacher consistently outperforms the student throughout training, acting as a "better role model" and guiding the student to higher quality representations. This behavior is distinct from other momentum-based SSL methods.
- **Centering and Sharpening are Complementary:** Both centering and sharpening are necessary to prevent model collapse; removing either leads to distinct forms of collapse (uniform output vs. single-dimension dominance).
- **Multi-crop Effectiveness:** Multi-crop training significantly improves DINO's performance and training efficiency, offering a better accuracy/running-time trade-off. It's not just an "add-on" but a crucial component for DINO's success with ViTs.
- **BN-Free Training:** DINO works effectively without any Batch Normalization layers in the projection heads or backbone (for ViTs), simplifying the architecture and distributed training.
- **Predictor Not Essential:** Unlike BYOL, DINO does not require an explicit predictor in the student network, simplifying the model.
- **Small Batch Training:** DINO can be trained effectively with relatively small batch sizes (e.g., 128), making it accessible for users with limited GPU resources.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this paper carry significant implications for the future of computer vision research and the practical application of Vision Transformers.

1. **Unlocking the Full Potential of Vision Transformers:** This work definitively demonstrates that self-supervised learning is a critical ingredient for unlocking the unique capabilities and full potential of Vision Transformers. While supervised ViTs have shown competitiveness, DINO reveals properties (like inherent semantic segmentation and strong k-NN performance) that are not as clearly evident in their supervised counterparts or even in CNNs. This challenges the notion that ViTs are merely alternative architectures, suggesting they possess distinct representational power when trained appropriately.

2. **Paving the Way for "BERT-like" Models in Vision:** By showing that self-supervised ViTs can learn rich, general-purpose visual features from unlabeled data, DINO positions ViTs as strong candidates for becoming the "BERT" or "GPT" of computer vision. This could lead to a paradigm where very large ViT models are pre-trained on massive, uncurated image datasets, and then fine-tuned for a wide array of downstream tasks, significantly reducing the reliance on costly, hand-labeled data. This is particularly impactful for applications where labeled data is scarce or expensive to acquire.

3. **Practical Advantages for Training and Deployment:**
 - **Computational Efficiency (Relative):** DINO enables ViTs to achieve state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters and often faster training times compared to some large CNN-based self-supervised systems.
 - **Simplified Architecture:** The BN-free nature of DINO, especially with ViTs, simplifies the training pipeline, particularly for large-scale distributed setups, as it avoids the complexities of synchronized batch normalizations.
 - **Highly Usable Features:** The strong performance of DINO-trained ViT features in k-NN classification is a major practical advantage. It means these features can be used "off-the-shelf" for tasks like image retrieval or content-based image search without any further fine-tuning or training of complex classifiers, making them highly efficient and adaptable for real-world applications.

4. **New Research Directions:** The emergent semantic segmentation capabilities in self-attention maps open up exciting new research avenues for weakly supervised or unsupervised segmentation. Understanding how these intrinsic properties arise and how to further leverage them for dense prediction tasks without explicit pixel-level annotations could be transformative. The discovery that the momentum teacher consistently outperforms the student also provides a new perspective on the dynamics of self-supervised learning and model ensembling.

In conclusion, "Emerging Properties in Self-Supervised Vision Transformers" is a landmark paper that not only pushes the state-of-the-art in self-supervised learning for computer vision but fundamentally alters our understanding of Vision Transformers. It demonstrates their unique representational power when paired with appropriate self-supervision, setting the stage for a new era of robust, generalized, and efficient visual feature learning.
