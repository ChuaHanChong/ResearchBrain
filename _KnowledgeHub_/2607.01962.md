# NeoMap: Training-free Novel-View Synthesis from Single Images and Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> NeoMap is a training-free framework that synthesizes high-fidelity, view-consistent novel views from single images or monocular videos. It achieves this by optimizing the initial noise latent of pre-trained video diffusion models, outperforming baselines on NVS benchmarks like Tanks-and-Temples, LLFF, and DAVIS, with an overall computational cost of 0.6 times its backbone model.

## Problem

- Generating photorealistic and geometrically consistent unseen views from monocular input is challenging due to occlusions, incomplete scene information, and depth ambiguity.
- Existing Novel View Synthesis (NVS) methods often rely on task-specific training, leading to overfitting to limited camera trajectories or depth estimation noise, or heuristic guidance that introduces artifacts.
- Current approaches either require extensive fine-tuning of video generation models or struggle with disrupting the native generation process when applying warping-and-inpainting techniques.

## Method

- **NeoMap** optimizes the ==initial noise latent== of pre-trained, general video generation models (e.g., ==diffusion models==, ==Flow Matching==/==PF-ODE==) rather than retraining or explicitly guiding their denoising process.
- It employs an iterative ==alternating projection== scheme between an ==Anchored Manifold Projection (AMP)== module and a ==Pixel-Constrained Projection (PCP)== module to ensure both semantic realism and precise geometric alignment.
- A ==novel view prior== is constructed using foundation models for depth and pose, then refined through a ==near-noise starting state== and an ==auxiliary trajectory re-anchoring== strategy to maintain camera alignment.

## Results

- NeoMap achieved superior performance across perceptual metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, FID, CLIP-Similarity) and camera control accuracy (ATE, RPE-T, RPE-R) on static (Tanks-and-Temples, LLFF) and dynamic (DAVIS) NVS benchmarks.
- Qualitative results showed robust correction of visible artifacts and seamless hallucination of geometrically coherent content in unobserved areas, outperforming baselines that often retained warping artifacts.
- The framework's effectiveness was validated through ablation studies, confirming the critical roles of both AMP and PCP modules and demonstrating applicability to video-to-video future extrapolation with smooth motion prediction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality NVS solutions are inherently encoded within the natural video data manifold learned by pre-trained, large-scale video generation models.
> - The core challenge of NVS can be reframed as locating the optimal initial noise latent that decodes into the desired photorealistic and geometrically consistent novel view video.
> - Alternating projection between a module that pulls towards the data manifold (AMP) and one that enforces strict pixel-level geometric constraints (PCP) can effectively converge to a robust solution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of monocular novel view synthesis (NVS), which involves generating photorealistic unseen views from a single image or an unconstrained monocular video. This task is foundational in computer vision, with applications in augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), 3D/4D content creation, and robotic perception. Traditional reconstruction-based methods often face challenges due to severe occlusions, incomplete scene information, and inherent depth ambiguity from monocular inputs.

Recent advancements in large-scale video generation models, particularly diffusion models, have introduced new avenues for NVS. These models exhibit an understanding of spatio-temporal relationships, which can be leveraged for generating new views. Existing approaches generally fall into two categories:

1. **Camera-Controlled Video Generation**: This line of work involves training or fine-tuning video generation models using 3D/4D data with camera pose annotations. Methods like TrajectoryAttention and ReCamMaster inject camera trajectories or depth-based warped images as conditions to guide the denoising process. While showing promise, these methods often suffer from overfitting to limited training distributions of camera trajectories and depth estimation noise, leading to generalization issues in unconstrained or novel scenes.
2. **Warping and Inpainting**: This paradigm utilizes depth-based image warping to create an initial geometric prior for the target view, followed by an inpainting network to fill in occluded or unobserved regions. Examples include ViewCrafter and FlexWorld, which may require significant fine-tuning of task-specific inpainting models to handle warping artifacts. Training-free variants, such as NVS-Solver, attempt to modulate the score function of pre-trained models with guidance strategies, but can disrupt the native generation process, leading to artifacts and compromised content.

The current work, NeoMap, positions itself by challenging a fundamental assumption common to both categories: that a valid and geometrically consistent NVS solution necessitates modifying or redefining the mapping relation between the noise space and the video latent space in a task-specific manner. Instead, NeoMap operates on the insight that high-quality NVS solutions are inherently encoded within the natural video data manifold learned by pre-trained, general video generation models. The core challenge, from this perspective, is not to engineer a new mapping, but to *locate* this optimal solution within the existing manifold. This aligns NeoMap with methods focused on controlling generation via noise manipulation, but specifically for NVS problems with novel view priors and adaptive trust in visible regions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve high-fidelity, view-consistent novel view video synthesis from single images or monocular videos using a completely training-free framework. This objective aims to overcome limitations of existing NVS methods, specifically their reliance on task-specific training, which can lead to overfitting, or heuristic guidance strategies, which often introduce artifacts.

The motivation for NeoMap stems from a critical re-evaluation of how pre-trained video generation models can be leveraged for NVS. The authors identify a fundamental assumption in current NVS paradigms: that in order to produce geometrically consistent novel views, the underlying video generation model must be adapted or retrained for the specific task, or its denoising trajectory must be explicitly guided. The paper argues that this assumption is flawed, proposing an alternative insight:

- **Existence of Solutions in the Data Manifold**: Large-scale video generation models, trained on extensive and diverse real-world video datasets, implicitly learn a rich output data manifold that encompasses a wide range of 3D scenes and natural camera motions. The authors hypothesize that promising novel view solutions, which are both photorealistic and geometrically consistent, are already embedded within this native data manifold.
- **Reframing the Problem**: Consequently, the core challenge is not to teach the model a new NVS capability or to force it to generate out-of-distribution content. Instead, it is to effectively *locate* the specific point in the model's output latent space that corresponds to the desired novel view video. Since video diffusion models establish a deterministic mapping from a regular Gaussian noise space to the video data manifold, locating a valid video point on the manifold is equivalent to finding the optimal initial noise latent that decodes into the target result.

This motivation directly addresses the shortcomings of previous approaches:
- By avoiding task-specific retraining, NeoMap aims to generalize better to in-the-wild scenes and unseen camera motions, sidestepping the overfitting issues of camera-conditioned models.
- By not imposing hard, stepwise denoising guidance, it seeks to maintain the continuity and coherence of the pre-trained model's native generation process, thereby mitigating the severe artifacts (e.g., incomplete content, corrupted scene structure) often seen in warping-and-inpainting methods.
- The framework is designed to exploit the inherent capabilities of powerful foundational video models without requiring expensive computational resources for fine-tuning or training new modules.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NeoMap (NoisE initializatiOn by Manifold Alternating Projection) is a training-free framework designed to locate high-fidelity, view-consistent novel view solutions from general pre-trained video models by optimizing the initial noise. It operates on deterministic flow-based video generation models, such as Flow Matching or Probability Flow ODE (PF-ODE), which map from a standard Gaussian noise space to a video data manifold.

The framework comprises three main modules:

#### 3.1. Novel View Prior Module

This module constructs an initial geometric prior for the target novel views. Given a monocular reference video (or a single image), foundation models (e.g., VGGT for depth and pose estimation, VIPE for dynamic scenes) are used to recover per-frame depth maps and the source camera trajectory. A warping operator then projects the source video content, depth, and poses to user-specified target camera trajectories, generating a partial warped target frame (X_c,s) and a binary visibility mask (M_s) for each target view. Stacking these yields a warped video prior (X_c) and its aligned visibility mask (M_c). This pixel-space prior is then encoded into the latent space (z_c = Enc(X_c)) using a pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE).

However, this latent prior z_c is inherently flawed due to depth noise, occlusions, and view-dependent appearance changes, particularly in visible regions, and is uninformative in unobserved areas. Directly deriving initial noise from z_c would lead to corrupted generation. To mitigate this, NeoMap defines a *near-noise starting state* z_τ by applying the forward flow from z_c to an early timestep τ (e.g., τ = 1 - ∆t), which is then iteratively refined.

#### 3.2. Anchored Manifold Projection (AMP) Module

The AMP module aims to project plausible semantic and geometric information into unconstrained regions while correcting artifacts in visible areas, ensuring the state aligns with the video data manifold. In each iteration *k*, the module performs two steps:

1. **Reverse Flow Approximation**: The current initial state z^k_τ is projected towards the data manifold using a one-step large-stride Euler approximation of the generative reverse flow (z^k_M ≈ z^k_τ - τ * v_θ(z^k_τ, τ)). This step leverages the pre-trained model's spatio-temporal prior to hallucinate content in unseen regions and move the state closer to the natural video manifold.
2. **Adaptive Latent Blending**: To prevent the reverse flow from corrupting highly confident visible regions and to strictly enforce the view prior, a harmonized state ˆz^k_M is created by blending z^k_M with the initial latent prior z_c. This blend uses a feature-level similarity mask (S_λ), which is binary (1 if CosSim(z^k_M, z_c) > λ, 0 otherwise). This mechanism adaptively anchors features where z^k_M is similar to z_c (indicating stable, high-quality prior), while allowing the model to hallucinate or correct in dissimilar regions. Finally, this harmonized latent ˆz^k_M is mapped back to the near-noise timestamp τ via a direct forward process to obtain z^(k+1)_τ.

#### 3.3. Pixel-Constrained Projection (PCP) Module

The PCP module complements AMP by enforcing strict geometric boundaries in pixel space, addressing potential spatial bleeding or structural shifts that can occur with latent-level blending. After obtaining ˆz^k_M from the AMP module, instead of directly mapping it back to noise space, PCP first decodes it to pixel space (Dec(ˆz^k_M)). It then blends this decoded image with the *original warped pixel prior* X_c, using the precise binary visibility mask M_c. This ensures that every observable pixel from the source view remains geometrically immutable. The resulting blended image is then encoded back into the latent space (˜z^k_M = Enc(M_c ⊙ X_c + (1 - M_c) ⊙ Dec(ˆz^k_M))). Finally, ˜z^k_M is mapped back to the noise space using the same forward flow as in AMP to produce z^(k+1)_τ.

#### 3.4. Alternating Projection Formulation

The core of NeoMap lies in the iterative alternating projection between the AMP and PCP modules. AMP pulls the latent state towards the natural video manifold (M), while PCP forcefully projects it onto the strict geometric constraint subspace (V) defined by the warped prior. This iterative process is mathematically guaranteed to converge to the intersection of M and V, ensuring that the final optimized initial noise latent produces a generation that simultaneously satisfies global semantic realism and precise 3D view alignment. A formal convergence proof is provided in the appendix.

#### 3.5. Auxiliary Trajectory Re-anchoring

To counter integration errors introduced by the large-stride Euler approximation during initial noise optimization, which can lead to trajectory drift during the full denoising process, an auxiliary trajectory re-anchoring strategy is employed. During the initial steps of the final ODE solving process, the AMP module is re-applied to the current intermediate latent states. This re-anchors the denoising trajectory to the NVS constraints, primarily affecting the global structure and low-frequency content, thereby maintaining view alignment without significantly impacting visual quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NeoMap's performance was evaluated extensively on three standard NVS benchmarks, comparing it against both camera-controlled video generation and warping-and-inpainting baselines, as well as noise handling methods. The underlying video generation model for NeoMap and adapted training-free baselines was Wan2.2-I2V-A14B. The total denoising steps for NeoMap were set to 15, along with 15 alternating projection steps, resulting in a total of 30 forward passes, which is 0.6 times the computational cost of the original backbone model's 50 denoising steps.

#### 4.1. Static Single-Image Novel View Synthesis

For static single-image NVS, NeoMap was tested on the Tanks-and-Temples (TNT) and LLFF datasets.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 1)**: NeoMap demonstrated superior performance across almost all perceptual metrics, including PSNR, LPIPS, FID (both FID-192 and FID-2048), and CLIP-Similarity, compared to all baselines. Its camera control accuracy, measured by ATE, RPE-T, and RPE-R, was competitive with or better than other methods, including those with heavy pose constraints. Methods that solely manipulated noise for visible regions (e.g., Reversed Noise, Flow Warped Noise) showed substantial performance drops, indicating the insufficiency of such approaches for comprehensive NVS.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4)**: The qualitative comparisons indicated that baseline models often retained warping artifacts and noise due to their reliance on flawed geometric priors. NeoMap, however, was observed to robustly correct visible artifacts and seamlessly hallucinate geometrically coherent content in unobserved areas.

#### 4.2. Dynamic Monocular Video Novel View Synthesis

For dynamic monocular video NVS, NeoMap was evaluated on the DAVIS dataset.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 2)**: NeoMap achieved state-of-the-art performance, securing optimal results in visual quality metrics (FID, FVD) and camera control metrics (ATE, RPE-T, RPE-R). In dynamic scenes, where complex object motion and camera trajectories frequently push baselines into out-of-distribution states, NeoMap exhibited a more pronounced advantage. Its camera control accuracy showed significant leads in ATE and RPE, demonstrating robust and precise conditioning even in complex scenarios.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 5)**: In dynamic scenes, where depth estimation is often less accurate, baseline methods frequently suffered from object distortion and semantic deviation. NeoMap effectively mitigated these geometric artifacts, preserving the structural integrity of moving subjects.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the Tanks-and-Temples dataset investigated the contribution of each component and hyperparameter:
- **Removing all modules**: Degraded performance significantly (akin to the Reversed Noise baseline), confirming the necessity of NeoMap's framework.
- **Number of alternating projection iterations (K)**: Visual metrics steadily improved and converged as K increased, with K=15 yielding optimal results.
- **Similarity mask threshold (λ)**: A value of λ=0.8 was identified as optimal; lower values introduced artifacts, while higher values discarded reliable priors, leading to geometric decoupling.
- **Removing AMP or PCP**: Both modules proved critical. Removing AMP (Anchored Manifold Projection) led to reduced performance. Removing PCP (Pixel-Constrained Projection) caused substantial degradation, indicating that enforcing absolute pixel-space boundaries is essential to prevent spatial bleeding and camera decoupling.
- **PCP in latent space vs. pixel space**: Applying PCP directly in latent space was inferior to its pixel-space implementation, reinforcing the importance of precise pixel-level constraints.
- **Auxiliary trajectory re-anchoring**: Omitting it resulted in the highest raw image quality but noticeable trajectory drift. Applying it for an optimal 3 denoising steps effectively calibrated camera alignment with minimal visual loss. Excessive re-anchoring (e.g., 5 or 8 steps) harmed image quality without providing further trajectory benefits. Adding PCP to the re-anchoring stage also negatively impacted performance.

#### 4.4. Further Application: Video-to-Video Future Extrapolation

NeoMap demonstrated capabilities beyond NVS by applying its framework to video-to-video (V2V) future extrapolation. By marking existing video frames as visible and future, unobserved frames as invisible, the method could provide smooth motion prediction based on the given video priors on datasets like DAVIS and PhysInOne.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NeoMap framework represents a significant advancement in novel view synthesis, primarily due to its novel, entirely training-free approach. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift**: By reframing NVS as an optimal initial noise localization problem within a pre-trained model's data manifold, NeoMap introduces a new conceptual paradigm. This contrasts with existing methods that either train new models for NVS or guide the generation process with heuristics that can compromise model integrity. This shift allows for leveraging the full capabilities of large foundational video models without task-specific modifications.
- **High Fidelity and Geometric Consistency**: The framework achieves state-of-the-art visual quality and geometric consistency across diverse and challenging datasets, including static single-image and dynamic monocular video inputs. This is critical for practical applications requiring realistic and structurally accurate novel views.
- **Robustness to Challenges**: NeoMap demonstrates robustness in handling severe occlusions, depth ambiguity, and the complexities of dynamic scenes, areas where many existing methods struggle due to reliance on imperfect geometric priors. The alternating projection mechanism effectively corrects artifacts and plausibly hallucinates content.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility**: Being training-free, NeoMap eliminates the need for expensive data collection, retraining, or fine-tuning, making advanced NVS capabilities more accessible and efficient. This also reduces the computational resources typically required for developing task-specific models.
- **Broad Applicability**: The method's ability to handle both static and dynamic scenes, coupled with its demonstrated extension to video-to-video future extrapolation, indicates its potential for broad application in various domains. These include AR/VR content creation, 3D/4D asset generation, and enhancing robotic perception systems that require understanding and predicting unseen views.
- **Theoretical Foundation**: The mathematical formulation and convergence proof for the alternating projection scheme provide a strong theoretical grounding for the practical approach, contributing to the understanding of how such iterative optimization can yield robust solutions.
- **Future Research Direction**: The work opens avenues for future research into further enhancing the learned data manifold of foundational models and improving robustness to even more severely corrupted geometric priors, pushing the boundaries of what training-free generative models can achieve.
