# Vision Transformers Need Registers

> [!summary] Summary
> Vision Transformers learn to repurpose redundant patch tokens for global information, leading to high-norm artifacts that degrade local feature quality and hinder dense visual tasks. Researchers at FAIR, Meta and Univ. Grenoble Alpes propose adding explicit learnable 'register tokens' to ViT inputs, effectively eliminating these artifacts and improving performance on dense prediction and unsupervised object discovery tasks by restoring smooth feature maps.

## Problem

- DINOv2, despite strong dense prediction performance, surprisingly lost the interpretable attention maps and object discovery capabilities seen in its predecessor, DINO.
- Investigation revealed the presence of 'artifacts' (high-norm outlier tokens) in feature maps and attention maps across various state-of-the-art ViTs, including supervised (DeiT-III) and text-supervised (OpenCLIP) models.
- These artifacts were hypothesized to negatively impact the quality of local features, hindering tasks requiring smooth, consistent local representations like object discovery.

## Method

- The authors first characterized artifacts as ==high-norm outlier tokens== that appear in low-informative background areas and carry more global than local information.
- They hypothesized that large ViTs implicitly repurpose these redundant patch tokens as internal 'registers' for global computations.
- The proposed solution involves explicitly adding a small number of learnable **register tokens** to the input sequence of the ==Vision Transformer==, which are discarded at the output, allowing them to absorb global information without distorting patch features.

## Results

- Register tokens completely eliminate high-norm outlier tokens from patch feature maps, leading to visually cleaner attention and feature maps.
- Performance on dense prediction tasks (semantic segmentation, depth estimation) improved (e.g., DINOv2 mIoU from **46.6** to **47.9**; RMSE from **0.378** to **0.366**), while global classification performance was maintained or slightly improved.
- Unsupervised object discovery (LOST) performance significantly increased (DINOv2 corloc on PASCAL VOC 2007 from **35.3** to **55.4**), restoring crucial capabilities for larger ViTs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large, well-trained Vision Transformers spontaneously repurpose redundant patch tokens from low-information areas to aggregate global image information, manifesting as high-norm outliers in feature maps.
> - These 'implicit registers' within patch tokens degrade local feature quality, hindering performance on dense prediction and unsupervised object discovery tasks.
> - Explicitly providing dedicated, learnable 'register tokens' to the ViT input effectively offers separate memory slots for global computation, preventing the model from 'hijacking' image patch tokens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Vision Transformers (ViTs), self-supervised learning (SSL), and the broader quest for robust, generic visual feature embeddings.

Traditionally, computer vision relied on handcrafted features (e.g., SIFT) or supervised training on massive datasets like ImageNet. However, the paradigm shifted with deep learning, leading to end-to-end training. ViTs have recently emerged as a powerful alternative to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for various vision tasks, demonstrating remarkable performance and scalability. This paper builds on the success of ViTs while addressing a subtle yet significant issue in their learned representations.

The motivation for generic feature embeddings remains strong, as collecting annotated data for many specific tasks (e.g., medical imaging, remote sensing) is expensive or requires specialized expertise. Pretraining models on large, readily available datasets and then extracting features for downstream tasks has become a common practice. This pretraining can be:
- **Supervised:** Models like DeiT-III are trained on large classification datasets (e.g., ImageNet-22k).
- **Text-supervised:** Models like CLIP and OpenCLIP learn visual representations by aligning images with their natural language descriptions, enabling powerful zero-shot capabilities.
- **Self-supervised:** This paradigm has gained significant traction by learning from data without human annotations, using pretext tasks. DINO (Caron et al., 2021) and its successor DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) are prominent examples within the ViT context. DINO was particularly notable for its ability to produce models that intrinsically contained semantic layout information, yielding interpretable attention maps and enabling unsupervised object discovery methods like LOST.

This paper specifically addresses an anomaly observed in DINOv2: despite its superior performance on dense prediction tasks, it surprisingly loses the interpretability and object discovery capabilities that made its predecessor, DINO, so compelling. The authors discover that this degradation is due to "artifacts"—high-norm tokens appearing in feature maps, particularly in less informative background areas. Crucially, they find these artifacts are not unique to DINOv2 but are also present in other popular supervised (DeiT-III) and text-supervised (OpenCLIP) ViTs, suggesting a more general phenomenon within Vision Transformers.

By identifying and proposing a solution to these artifacts, "VISIONTRANSFORMERSNEEDREGISTERS" contributes to:
- **Improving the fundamental understanding of ViTs:** It sheds light on how these models internally process and aggregate information, particularly highlighting a "recycling" mechanism where redundant patch tokens are repurposed.
- **Enhancing the robustness and interpretability of foundation models:** By making ViT features smoother and attention maps cleaner, the work improves their utility for tasks requiring dense, locally consistent representations, and makes their internal workings more transparent.
- **Advancing self-supervised learning:** It pushes the state of the art for self-supervised visual models on dense prediction and object discovery tasks, bridging the performance gap that emerged with DINOv2.

The paper connects to the broader discussion on the role of special tokens (like `[CLS]`) in Transformers and how models allocate "computational budget" or "memory slots" for different types of information (local vs. global). It draws parallels with "Memory Transformers" from NLP, suggesting a shared underlying computational principle across modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **identify, characterize, and effectively mitigate undesirable artifacts** observed in the feature maps and attention maps of Vision Transformers.

The motivation stems from several key observations:
1. **Divergence between DINO and DINOv2:** The original DINO algorithm (Caron et al., 2021) was lauded for its ability to produce highly interpretable attention maps that naturally highlighted semantic objects, enabling unsupervised object discovery methods like LOST. However, DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023), while excelling at dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth estimation, segmentation), surprisingly failed to yield similar interpretable attention maps and showed disappointing performance when used with LOST. This "incompatibility" suggested a fundamental difference in how DINOv2 processed visual information compared to its predecessor.
2. **Ubiquitous Artifacts:** Initial investigation revealed that the issue was not isolated to DINOv2. Similar "peaky outlier values" or "artifacts" were observed in the attention maps of other state-of-the-art ViTs, including supervised models like DeiT-III and text-supervised models like OpenCLIP. This indicated a pervasive problem, with DINO appearing to be an exception rather than the norm.
3. **Impact on Downstream Tasks:** These artifacts were hypothesized to negatively impact the quality of local features, hindering performance on tasks that rely on smooth, consistent local representations, such as object discovery and dense prediction.
4. **Lack of Understanding:** The underlying mechanism causing these artifacts was not well understood. The authors aimed to investigate *why* and *when* these high-norm tokens appear and what kind of information they encode.

In summary, the core motivation was to diagnose a critical issue affecting the quality and interpretability of representations learned by powerful ViTs, especially for dense visual understanding, and to devise a simple, generalizable solution to restore and even enhance their capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research follows a systematic approach of problem identification, characterization, hypothesis formulation, solution proposal, and rigorous experimental validation.

#### 3.1. Problem Characterization and Analysis

- **Quantitative Detection:** The authors quantitatively characterize the artifacts as "high-norm outlier tokens." They observe a bimodal distribution of feature norms at the model's output, with a small percentage (around 2%) of tokens having significantly higher norms (e.g., >150) compared to regular tokens. This norm-based criterion becomes the basis for identifying outliers.
- **Conditions for Appearance:** They investigate the training conditions under which these outliers emerge:
 - **Model Depth:** Outliers differentiate in norms around the middle layers (e.g., layer 15 of a 40-layer ViT).
 - **Training Duration:** They appear only after a sufficiently long training period (e.g., after one-third of DINOv2 training).
 - **Model Size:** Only larger models (ViT-L, ViT-H, ViT-g) exhibit these outliers, suggesting a scaling-dependent phenomenon.
- **Spatial Location:** Outliers are found to correspond to input patches that are highly similar to their neighbors, typically in low-informative background areas or near the image borders. This suggests the model discards redundant local information in these patches.
- **Information Content Probing:** To understand what information these outlier tokens carry, the authors perform linear probing experiments:
 - **Local Information:** They train linear models to predict original patch position and reconstruct pixel values from outlier vs. normal tokens. Outlier tokens show significantly lower accuracy, indicating they contain less local information.
 - **Global Information:** They train linear classifiers on standard image classification benchmarks, using either a randomly selected normal or outlier patch token as the image representation. Outlier tokens yield much higher accuracy than normal tokens, indicating they aggregate more global image information.

#### 3.2. Hypothesis Formulation

Based on their characterization, the authors hypothesize that **large, sufficiently trained Vision Transformers learn to identify redundant tokens (from low-informative background areas) and repurpose them as implicit "registers" to store, process, and retrieve global image information.** While this behavior is not inherently bad for global tasks, the fact that it happens *within* patch tokens is undesirable, as it causes the model to discard local patch information, leading to degraded feature maps and hindering dense prediction/object discovery tasks.

#### 3.3. Proposed Solution: Register Tokens

The proposed solution is simple yet elegant: **Explicitly provide additional, learnable tokens, termed "registers," to the input sequence of the Vision Transformer.**
- These register tokens are independent of the input image, initialized as learnable embeddings, similar to the `[CLS]` token.
- They are appended to the sequence after the patch embedding layer.
- Crucially, at the output of the ViT, these register tokens are *discarded* and not used as part of the final image representation. Only the `[CLS]` token and patch tokens are used, as is standard practice.
- The idea is that by providing dedicated "slots" or "memory units," the model can utilize these registers for internal global computations, preventing it from "hijacking" the image's own patch tokens for this purpose.

#### 3.4. Experimental Validation

The authors validate their solution across three state-of-the-art ViT training paradigms:
- **DeiT-III (Supervised):** Trained on ImageNet-22k.
- **OpenCLIP (Text-supervised):** Trained on a text-image-aligned corpus.
- **DINOv2 (Self-supervised):** Trained on ImageNet-22k, the primary motivation for this work.

Evaluation is performed using both qualitative and quantitative metrics:
- **Artifact Removal:** Visual inspection of attention and feature maps, and quantitative analysis of output token norm distributions.
- **Performance Evaluation (Frozen Features):** Linear probing on:
 - ImageNet classification (global task).
 - ADE20k semantic segmentation (dense task).
 - NYUd monocular depth estimation (dense task).
 - OpenCLIP also evaluated with zero-shot classification.
- **Object Discovery:** Performance evaluation using the LOST (Localizing Objects with Self-supervised Transformers and no Labels) algorithm on PASCAL VOC and COCO datasets, which directly relies on the quality and smoothness of local feature maps.
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigate the impact of the number of register tokens (0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16) on artifact removal and downstream performance.
- **Qualitative Analysis of Registers:** Visualize attention maps of the register tokens themselves to understand their learned behavior and whether they specialize.
- **Complexity Analysis:** Measure the negligible increase in parameters and FLOPs introduced by the registers.

This comprehensive experimental setup allows the authors to demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of their proposed register tokens in addressing the identified artifacts and improving ViT performance across diverse tasks and training schemes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, confirming the hypothesis and validating the proposed solution:

- **Characterization of Artifacts Confirmed:**
 - The artifacts are definitively identified as **high-norm outlier tokens** in the output feature maps, appearing primarily in larger, well-trained ViT models (ViT-L, ViT-H, ViT-g) and emerging during the middle layers of the network.
 - These outliers are consistently found in **low-informative background areas** of images, specifically in patches that are highly similar to their neighbors.
 - Probing experiments confirm that outlier tokens contain **less local information** (e.g., unable to accurately predict patch position or reconstruct pixels) but **more global image information** (e.g., significantly higher accuracy in image classification tasks). This supports the "recycling" hypothesis that models repurpose these tokens for global computations.

- **Effectiveness of Register Tokens in Artifact Removal:**
 - Training ViT models with additional register tokens **completely eliminates the high-norm outlier tokens** from the *patch feature maps*. This is evident qualitatively from cleaner attention and feature maps (Fig. 1, 19, 20) and quantitatively from the normalized distribution of patch token norms (Fig. 7, 15). The high-norm behavior is effectively absorbed by the newly introduced register tokens themselves.

- **Improved or Maintained Downstream Performance:**
 - **Dense Prediction Tasks:** The presence of register tokens leads to **improved performance** on dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation (ADE20k) and monocular depth estimation (NYUd). For example, DINOv2 with registers improves mIoU from 46.6 to 47.9 and reduces RMSE from 0.378 to 0.366 (Table 2a). This validates that removing the artifacts from patch tokens enhances their local information quality.
 - **Image Classification:** Performance on ImageNet classification (a global task) is **maintained or slightly improved** (Table 2a).
 - **Zero-Shot Classification:** For OpenCLIP, zero-shot classification performance is also **maintained** (Table 2b).

- **Enabling Unsupervised Object Discovery:**
 - Crucially, adding register tokens **significantly improves the performance of unsupervised object discovery methods** like LOST. For DINOv2, corloc scores on PASCAL VOC 2007 jump from 35.3 to 55.4 (Table 3), closing a critical gap that existed between DINO and DINOv2. Similar significant improvements are observed for DeiT-III. While OpenCLIP shows a slight decrease in LOST performance with registers, the qualitative analysis suggests its original value projection already mitigates some outlier effects, making it less dependent on this fix.

- **Optimal Number of Registers:**
 - Ablation studies show that even **one register token is sufficient to remove the visual artifacts**. However, using a small number of registers (e.g., 4) leads to optimal or improved performance on downstream dense tasks (Fig. 8). On ImageNet, performance continues to improve with more registers, suggesting an trade-off depending on the task type. The authors standardize on 4 register tokens for their experiments.

- **Behavior of Register Tokens:**
 - Qualitative analysis of the attention maps of the register tokens reveals that they do not behave identically. Some registers exhibit **diverse attention patterns**, sometimes focusing on different objects or specific parts of the image (Fig. 9, 16), hinting at a natural specialization similar to "slot attention." This emergent specialization was not explicitly enforced by the training objective.
 - Register tokens, like the `[CLS]` token, tend to have **broader attention maps** than typical patch tokens, further supporting their role in aggregating global information.

- **Minimal Computational Overhead:** The proposed solution introduces a **negligible increase in model parameters** and a **slight increase in FLOPs** (less than 2% for 4 registers), making it highly efficient (Fig. 12).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "VISIONTRANSFORMERSNEEDREGISTERS" have profound significance and potential impact across multiple facets of computer vision and deep learning:

1. **Fundamental Understanding of Vision Transformers:** The paper offers crucial insights into the internal workings of ViTs. It rigorously demonstrates that these models, when sufficiently large and trained, spontaneously develop a mechanism to repurpose "redundant" patch tokens for global information aggregation. This "register" behavior, previously an implicit artifact, is now explicitly characterized, providing a deeper understanding of how ViTs manage and prioritize information.

2. **Enhanced Model Interpretability and Reliability:** By identifying and eliminating the artifacts that caused "peaky" attention and feature maps, the work significantly improves the interpretability of ViT models. Cleaner attention maps are vital for understanding model decisions, debugging, and building trust in AI systems. This is particularly important for sensitive applications where explainability is critical. The smoother feature maps directly benefit downstream visual processing, leading to more robust and reliable features.

3. **Improved Performance for Dense Visual Tasks:** The study's most immediate practical impact is the improved performance on dense prediction tasks (segmentation, depth estimation) and, more dramatically, on unsupervised object discovery. The previous incompatibility of powerful models like DINOv2 with object discovery methods was a significant limitation. This paper provides a simple fix that not only restores but enhances these capabilities, effectively unlocking the potential of larger, more performant ViTs for tasks previously dominated by their predecessors or requiring supervised signals. This pushes the state-of-the-art in self-supervised visual models.

4. **Generalizable Architectural Improvement:** The effectiveness of register tokens across diverse ViT training paradigms (supervised, text-supervised, self-supervised) suggests that the proposed solution addresses a fundamental architectural behavior rather than a specific training recipe artifact. This implies that "registers" could become a standard architectural component in future Vision Transformers, much like the `[CLS]` token, leading to more robust and versatile models by design.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:**
 - **Optimal Register Design:** The observation that registers can specialize their attention patterns opens new avenues for research into designing more sophisticated or task-specific register mechanisms, potentially inspired by concepts like slot attention.
 - **Diagnostic Tool:** The norm-based artifact detection method can serve as a valuable diagnostic tool for researchers developing new ViT architectures or training methods, allowing them to quickly identify and address similar representational issues.
 - **Theoretical Implications:** The paper's findings could stimulate theoretical research into why Transformers spontaneously develop these "register-like" behaviors and how to formally characterize and control them.

In essence, "VISIONTRANSFORMERSNEEDREGISTERS" not only solves a practical problem observed in modern ViTs but also contributes significantly to our fundamental understanding of these models' internal mechanisms. By providing a simple, efficient, and universally applicable solution, it enhances the utility, interpretability, and performance of Vision Transformers, solidifying their role as powerful foundation models for a wide array of computer vision tasks.
