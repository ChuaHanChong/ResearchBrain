# VL-Nav: A Neuro-Symbolic Approach for Reasoning-based Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> VL-Nav is a neuro-symbolic framework enabling autonomous robots to interpret abstract human instructions and efficiently locate multiple targets in unseen, large-scale environments. The system achieved success rates of 79.2% in simulation and 86.3% in real-world scenarios, outperforming prior methods in implicit semantic inference and exploration efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language navigation (VLN) systems struggle with interpreting abstract human instructions that contain implicit semantics (e.g., inferring "rain jacket" from "rain").
- Current approaches lack robust capabilities for decomposing complex, multi-target instructions into atomic, manageable subtasks.
- Inefficient exploration strategies in large, unknown environments lead to redundant travel and poor coverage, especially when searching for widely separated targets.
- Computational intensity of end-to-end models and continuous execution of large foundation models pose real-time operational bottlenecks for resource-constrained robotic platforms.

## Method

- Developed a ==NeSy Task Planner== utilizing a unified symbolic memory (3D scene graph, object-centric image memory) and a VLM (Qwen3-VL) for logical decomposition of abstract instructions, implicit semantic inference, and fine-grained target verification.
- Introduced a NeSy Exploration System that fuses neural semantic cues from lightweight open-vocabulary detectors with symbolic geometric heuristics and curiosity terms for efficient, goal-directed navigation.
- Implemented a hybrid scoring policy for goal selection, combining vision-language detection confidence, distance weighting, and unknown-area weighting to guide exploration.
- Strategically offloaded heavy VLM computations to a remote GPU for high-level planning while using lightweight models on onboard edge devices for real-time exploration, mitigating computational bottlenecks.

## Results

- VL-Nav achieved success rates of **79.2%** across DARPA TIAMAT simulation environments and **86.3%** in diverse real-world scenarios, consistently outperforming baseline methods like SG-Nav, ApexNav, and VLFM.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that Instance-Based Target Points (IBTP) are critical for preventing missed targets and improving path efficiency, while curiosity cues are vital for efficient exploration in expansive, open environments.
- The system showed robust generalization capabilities with effective sim-to-real transfer, successfully performing complex multi-floor demonstrations and long-range trajectories up to 483 meters in real-world deployments.
- VL-Nav significantly reduced mean time usage and achieved higher success weighted by path length (SPL) compared to baselines, indicating more efficient and non-redundant navigation in challenging tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining neural reasoning with symbolic guidance provides both accurate task reasoning (implicit semantics, decomposition) and efficient exploration strategies for complex, multi-target VLN tasks.
> - A unified symbolic memory system, including a 3D scene graph and object-centric image memory, is crucial for grounding VLM decisions and enabling effective coarse-to-fine target verification.
> - Fusing lightweight semantic detections with frontier-based exploration and curiosity cues (distance and unknown area weighting) significantly improves the efficiency and robustness of target discovery in large-scale environments.
> - Distributing computational load by using a VLM for asynchronous high-level reasoning and lightweight detectors for real-time on-device perception enables practical deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms without sacrificing performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous mobile robotics, particularly in vision-language navigation (VLN), seeks to enable robots to understand and execute complex human instructions within diverse environments. This work addresses challenges in reasoning-based VLN, which requires robots to infer implicit semantics and efficiently explore large, unseen spaces to locate multiple targets.

Existing approaches to robot navigation can be categorized into several paradigms:
- **Classical Approaches:** Methods like SLAM and frontier-based exploration are effective for geometric mapping and navigation but lack the linguistic reasoning capabilities required for abstract human instructions.
- **End-to-End Learning:** These approaches, including RL-based and VLA-based models, directly map sensory inputs and instructions to actions. While they can learn semantic priors, they are typically data-hungry, computationally intensive, and exhibit poor generalization during sim-to-real transfer.
- **Modular Learning:** This category combines classical pipelines with learned modules, integrating semantic priors while maintaining interpretability and efficiency. However, many existing modular methods still rely heavily on real-world training data and may not fully leverage vision-language features.
- **Foundation Model-Based Modular Learning:** Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) have enhanced modular approaches by incorporating natural language understanding. Methods like VLFM, SG-Nav, and ApexNav utilize VLMs for zero-shot semantic navigation. However, these systems primarily operate under explicit instruction following (e.g., "go to the L-shape sofa") and often struggle with reasoning-based VLN, where targets are implicitly described (e.g., "rain" implies "rain jacket"). Additionally, the continuous execution of heavy foundation models can pose real-time operational bottlenecks on resource-constrained robotic platforms.

This research positions itself as an advancement in reasoning-based VLN by proposing a neuro-symbolic (NeSy) approach. It aims to bridge the gap left by current methods, which often lack the ability to decompose complex tasks, infer implicit semantics, or employ efficient exploration strategies in large-scale, unseen environments. By intertwining neural reasoning with symbolic guidance, VL-Nav seeks to provide both accurate task reasoning and efficient exploration, addressing the limitations of prior work in processing abstract, multi-target instructions and navigating effectively in complex scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable autonomous mobile robots to navigate unseen, large-scale environments and fulfill complex, abstract human instructions. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in existing VLN systems:

1. **Implicit Semantic Inference:** Current navigation systems frequently struggle with instructions that contain implicit semantics, requiring the robot to reason beyond literal interpretations. For instance, an instruction like "Today’s weather report indicates rain. Help Rob find an umbrella, an appropriate jacket, and shoes" necessitates the robot to infer that "rain" implies "waterproof gear," leading to the identification of a rain jacket and rain boots, rather than generic clothing items. Existing methods often fail to bridge these logical gaps, leading to incorrect target identification.

2. **Task Decomposition for Multi-Target Instructions:** Complex tasks often involve multiple targets and sub-goals distributed across an environment. Existing end-to-end or foundation model-based approaches frequently lack the robust capability to decompose such abstract, multi-target instructions into atomic, manageable subtasks. This deficiency can result in aimless wandering or an inability to systematically address all components of a complex task.

3. **Efficient Exploration in Large-Scale Environments:** Navigating large, unknown environments within limited time constraints requires efficient exploration strategies. Many current methods over-rely on neural semantic cues, neglecting the potential of geometric frontiers or heuristic guidance. This can lead to redundant travel, local minima, or inefficient coverage of the environment, particularly when searching for multiple items that are widely separated.

4. **Real-Time Performance and Generalization:** Deploying navigation systems on real-world, resource-constrained robotic platforms demands computational efficiency and strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities. End-to-end learning models are often computationally expensive and struggle with generalization, while continuous execution of large foundation models can introduce significant latency, hindering real-time operation.

To address these limitations, the paper identifies two critical capabilities lacking in existing approaches that motivate the development of VL-Nav:
- **Accurate Task Reasoning:** The ability to parse and decompose abstract, multi-target instructions, infer implicit semantics, and perform object disambiguation.
- **Efficient Exploration Strategies:** The capability to rapidly locate multiple targets in large-scale environments while minimizing unnecessary repeat travel and optimizing information gathering.

The authors aim to seamlessly integrate neural reasoning with symbolic guidance to achieve these capabilities, proposing a neuro-symbolic system that can ground VLM decisions in precise spatial awareness and historical context, while also guiding efficient exploration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VL-Nav is designed as a neuro-symbolic (NeSy) vision-language navigation system that integrates neural semantic understanding with symbolic guidance. Its architecture comprises two primary modules: a NeSy Task Planner and a NeSy Exploration System.

#### 3.1. NeSy Task Planner

The NeSy Task Planner is responsible for logically decomposing complex, abstract instructions into atomic subtasks ("exploration" or "go to") and dynamically managing the task flow.

##### 3.1.1. Unified Memory System

The planner's reasoning is grounded in a unified symbolic memory system consisting of:
- **3D Scene Graph:** This graph contains two types of nodes: object nodes and room nodes. Room node masks are generated via a room segmentation algorithm using morphological operations. Room labels are inferred by a Large Language Model (LLM) based on connected object nodes. Edges between object and room nodes are established through spatial inclusion, specifically if an object's centroid falls within a room's segmentation mask.
- **Object-Centric Image Memory:** Object nodes, generated by an open-vocabulary detector, store attributes including the object centroid, detection confidence score, the robot's pose at the detection viewpoint, and the corresponding best-viewpoint RGB image. This memory provides spatial and relational context for task decomposition and replanning.

##### 3.1.2. Task Decomposition and Replanning

- The planner utilizes Qwen3-VL as its VLM backbone to decompose complex task descriptions. It maintains a dynamic target object list and queries the memory module to track potential candidates.
- Upon completion of each subtask, a replanning phase is triggered, generating updated subtasks based on the updated symbolic memory.
- For target acquisition, a coarse-to-fine verification strategy is employed:
 1. **Symbolic Filtering:** The 3D scene graph filters object nodes to propose a top-k list of candidates (e.g., top-3) based on detection confidence.
 2. **Neural Verification:** The VLM performs fine-grained reasoning on the saved best-view images and neighboring scene graph nodes of these candidates to determine the best semantic match for the abstract instruction.
- Once a target is selected, its best-view pose is published as the navigation goal, directing the exploration system.

#### 3.2. NeSy Exploration System

The NeSy Exploration System operationalizes high-level directives from the task planner by fusing neural semantic cues from lightweight open-vocabulary detection models (OVDMs) with symbolic geometric heuristics.

##### 3.2.1. Frontier-based Target Points

- Frontier cells are identified in the robot's occupancy map, defined as free cells adjacent to unknown cells, within the robot's forward field of view (FoV) and maximum sensor range.
- These frontier cells are clustered using breadth-first search (BFS). Each cluster is represented by a centroid or multiple sampled points, serving as frontier-based target points.

##### 3.2.2. Instance-Based Target Points (IBTP)

- A vision-language detector (VL Module) periodically reports candidate instance centers, including estimated global coordinates (q_x, q_y) and a confidence score.
- Candidates with confidence scores above a detection threshold (τ_det) are retained. If multiple candidates are in close proximity, they are downsampled using a voxel-grid filter.
- These instance-based points allow the robot to approach and verify potential targets, mimicking human search behavior. If a detection is incorrect, the robot continues exploration.

##### 3.2.3. NeSy Scoring Policy

A hybrid scoring policy assigns a NeSy score to each candidate goal (g) to guide selection. The policy considers the robot's current position (x_r, y_r) and heading (θ_r).

- **VL Score (S_VL(g)):** This score translates fine-grained vision-language features from YOLO-World and FastSAM models into a Gaussian-mixture distribution over the robot's horizontal FoV. It combines contributions from K detected regions, each with a mean offset angle (μ_k), angular uncertainty (σ_k, fixed at 0.1), and a confidence-based weight (α_k). A "view confidence" term C(Δθ) downweights detections with large angular offsets from the central FoV, calculated as cos²(Δθ / (θ_fov/2) * π/2). The accumulated score is clipped to. This biases the robot towards goals in the direction of high-confidence semantic detections.

- **Curiosity Cues:** Two terms encourage exploration of unknown areas and minimize redundant travel:
 1. **Distance Weighting (S_dist(g)):** Defined as 1 / (1 + d(x_r, g)), where d is the Euclidean distance. This prioritizes nearer goals, reducing energy consumption and travel time.
 2. **Unknown-Area Weighting (S_unknown(g)):** Measures the ratio of unknown cells around g (ratio(g)) within a local BFS. A larger ratio indicates more unexplored space. An exponential mapping, 1 - exp(-k * ratio(g)), translates this into a score, encouraging the robot to move towards areas with high information gain.

- **Combined NeSy Score (S_NeSy(g)):** For frontier-based goals, the final score combines these components: S_NeSy(g) = w_dist * S_dist(g) + w_VL * S_VL(g) * S_unknown(g), where w_dist and w_VL are scalar weights. For instance-based goals, curiosity cues are omitted as their primary purpose is verification rather than general exploration.

#### 3.3. Goal Selection & Path Planning

- The system prioritizes high-confidence semantic matches. It iterates through instance-based target points exceeding a minimum distance threshold (δ_reached). If valid instances exist, the one with the highest S_VL is selected for verification.
- If no valid instance targets are available (or all are too close), the system defaults to frontier-based target points, selecting the one with the highest combined S_NeSy score to maximize information gain.
- If no candidate exceeds δ_reached, the robot holds its position.
- Once a goal is selected, the FAR Planner is used to generate a collision-free path, enabling dynamic obstacle avoidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VL-Nav system was validated through experiments in both high-fidelity simulations and diverse real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments: DARPA TIAMAT Challenge

- **Environments:** Apartment 1 & 2 (indoor, HabitatSim), Camping Site & Factory (outdoor, IsaacSim).
- **Tasks:** 8 abstract navigation tasks per environment, requiring multi-step reasoning, implicit semantic inference (e.g., "rain" → "rain jacket"), hierarchical searching, and grounding abstract social concepts. Each task involved 3 trials.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR) and Max Time Usage Ratio (MTUR, average execution time / maximum time limit).
- **Baselines:** Closest frontier exploration, VLFM, SG-Nav, ApexNav, VL-Nav w/o IBTP (ablation), VL-Nav w/o Curiosity (ablation).
- **Results:**
 - VL-Nav achieved SRs ranging from 75.0% (Camping Site & Factory) to 87.5% (Apartment 1), consistently outperforming baselines.
 - Baselines exhibited significantly lower SRs (e.g., SG-Nav: 0.0-8.3%, ApexNav: 12.5-25.0%) and higher MTURs (closer to 1.0), indicating frequent timeouts and failures due to computational latency (SG-Nav's LLM-based reasoning) or lack of consistent symbolic memory (ApexNav for long-horizon, multi-goal tasks).
 - Qualitative results (Figures 5 and 6) demonstrated VL-Nav's ability to infer implicit semantics, perform hierarchical task decomposition in indoor settings, and effectively guide search using projected value maps in unstructured outdoor environments.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Environments:** Hallway (Medium & Big), Office (High & Mid), Outdoor (Low & Big), Apartment (High & Small).
- **Tasks:** 9 distinct human-described instances (e.g., "tall white trash bin") serving as targets. 3-5 trials per task.
- **Metrics:** SR and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).
- **Baselines:** Closet frontier-based exploration, VLFM (adapted with YOLO-World and FastSAM for real-time edge deployment), VL-Nav w/o IBTP, VL-Nav w/o curiosity, and full VL-Nav.
- **Results:**
 - VL-Nav achieved high SRs (ranging from 77.8% in Outdoor to 91.7% in Office) and significantly higher SPL scores compared to baselines (e.g., 0.812 in Office for VL-Nav vs. 0.317 for Frontier Exploration and 0.556 for VLFM). This indicated efficient, non-redundant path navigation.
 - Consistent performance across simulation and real-world scenarios demonstrated VL-Nav's generalization capabilities, confirming effective sim-to-real transfer.
 - The system successfully performed complex multi-floor demonstrations and long-range trajectories up to 483 meters.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis

- **Impact of IBTP (Instance-Based Target Points):** The "VL-Nav w/o IBTP" variant showed notable performance degradation in semantically complex and cluttered environments (e.g., Apartment: SR decreased from 87.5% to 70.8% in simulation, and from 88.9% to 70.2% in real-world). This indicated that IBTP provides a crucial "short-cut" mechanism for verifying tentative detections, preventing missed targets and improving path efficiency (SPL).
- **Impact of Curiosity Cues:** The "VL-Nav w/o Curiosity" variant experienced performance drops primarily in large-scale, open environments (e.g., Simulation Factory: SR decreased from 75.0% to 66.7%; Real-world Outdoor: SR decreased from 77.8% to 55.6%). This confirmed that the distance and unknown-area weighting components of the NeSy scoring policy are important for preventing local minima and aimless wandering in expansive maps, guiding efficient exploration.

In summary, the results indicated that VL-Nav's neuro-symbolic architecture provides both the reasoning depth and execution efficiency necessary to successfully navigate abstract, multi-target tasks in large, unseen environments, outperforming prior methods which struggled with computational demands, logical complexity, or inefficient exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents VL-Nav, a neuro-symbolic framework that addresses critical limitations in reasoning-based vision-language navigation for autonomous robots. By intertwining neural semantic understanding with symbolic guidance, the work significantly advances the capability of robots to interpret and act upon complex, abstract human instructions in unseen, large-scale environments.

The key contributions and their potential impact include:

1. **Enhanced Task Reasoning and Decomposition:** VL-Nav's NeSy Task Planner, leveraging a unified symbolic memory (3D scene graph and object-centric image memory) and VLM reasoning, enables robots to infer implicit semantics and decompose abstract, multi-target instructions into executable subtasks. This capability is significant for moving beyond explicit command-following to more human-like, intuitive interaction, allowing robots to assist in complex real-world scenarios where instructions are often vague or require contextual inference.

2. **Efficient and Informed Exploration:** The NeSy Exploration System, by fusing neural semantic cues with symbolic geometric heuristics and curiosity terms, facilitates rapid and goal-directed exploration. This approach minimizes aimless wandering and redundant travel, particularly crucial in large, unknown environments. The integration of instance-based target points provides a "shortcut" for verifying potential targets, while curiosity cues prevent local minima, leading to more robust and time-efficient target discovery. This efficiency has direct implications for real-world deployments where time and energy resources are limited.

3. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer and Real-World Performance:** The rigorous validation across the DARPA TIAMAT Challenge simulation and diverse real-world environments demonstrates the system's robustness and strong generalization capabilities. Achieving high success rates (79.2% in simulation, 86.3% in real-world) in challenging scenarios, including multi-floor navigation and long-range trajectories, indicates the practical viability of the neuro-symbolic approach for deployment on physical robots. This bridges a persistent gap in robotics research where methods often perform well in simulation but fail in real-world complexity.

4. **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** By strategically using a VLM only for high-level task planning and verification (asynchronously on a remote GPU) and employing lightweight open-vocabulary detectors for real-time exploration (onboard edge devices), VL-Nav mitigates the computational bottlenecks associated with continuously running heavy foundation models. This modular and distributed computational approach makes advanced reasoning accessible for real-time robotic applications with resource constraints.

The significance of VL-Nav lies in its potential to enable a new generation of autonomous robots capable of operating more intelligently and flexibly in human-centric environments. Future work aims to extend the symbolic memory for temporal reasoning, allowing robots to track dynamic targets and adapt to changing environments, as well as incorporating lifelong learning capabilities for continuous adaptation over long-term deployments. These advancements could broaden the application of autonomous robots in fields such as logistics, search and rescue, service robotics, and planetary exploration, where understanding complex instructions and efficiently navigating dynamic, unknown terrains are critical.
