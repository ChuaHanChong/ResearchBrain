# ReST-MCTS*: LLM Self-Training via Process Reward Guided Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> ReST-MCTS*, developed by researchers at Tsinghua University and Caltech, enhances large language model self-training by iteratively improving both the generator and a process reward model, leveraging a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search guided by automatically inferred per-step quality values. The method achieves improved LLM performance on complex reasoning benchmarks by producing higher-quality reasoning traces.

## Problem

- Existing LLM self-training methods suffer from 'false positives,' where correct final answers might derive from flawed intermediate reasoning, leading to low-quality fine-tuning data.
- Training reliable per-step process reward models (PRMs) traditionally requires costly and labor-intensive human annotation for each intermediate step.
- Integrating tree-search algorithms with LLMs often relies on sparse outcome rewards, lacking fine-grained guidance for intermediate reasoning steps.

## Method

- Introduces ==ReST-MCTS*==, a reinforced self-training framework that employs a modified ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS*)== guided by a trained per-step ==process reward model (PRM)==.
- Automatically infers per-step reward labels (quality value 'v_k' and weighted reward 'w_s_k') from rollouts and verified traces, circumventing the need for manual annotation to train the PRM.
- Employs a =='MuZero-style learning' paradigm== to iteratively and mutually improve both the LLM policy (generator) and the process reward model (verifier) based on self-generated, high-quality data.

## Results

- ReST-MCTS* consistently improved LLM performance on complex reasoning benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, SciBench) across multiple models and iterations, outperforming other self-training methods.
- The autonomously trained process reward model achieved higher accuracy compared to state-of-the-art PRM training techniques (e.g., MATH-SHEPHERD) on GSM8K (**87.5%** vs **87.1%**) and MATH500 (**39.0%** vs **37.3%**) datasets.
- The MCTS* policy demonstrated superior accuracy over baselines (e.g., Self-Consistency, Tree-of-Thought) for the same search budget on challenging reasoning tasks like MATH and SciBench, achieving up to **48.5%** on MATH.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Novel definitions for quality value and weighted reward enable automated inference of per-step process rewards from oracle final answers, bypassing manual annotation.
> - A synergistic, iterative self-training loop between the LLM policy and the process reward model allows for continuous, mutual improvement of both components.
> - Guiding a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS*) with a fine-grained, learned process reward model effectively explores and selects higher-quality reasoning paths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the intersection of large language model (LLM) self-training, advanced reasoning techniques, and reinforcement learning (RL). The broader research landscape for LLMs is rapidly evolving, moving beyond training on human-generated data towards leveraging LLM-generated content for self-improvement. This shift is critical as the pool of high-quality human-produced text is finite and increasingly exhausted.

Existing LLM self-training methodologies, such as STaR, RFT, ReST_EM, V-STaR, and Self-Rewarding LM, typically rely on generating multiple responses for a given query and filtering those that yield a correct final answer as training data. While effective in improving performance, a significant limitation of these approaches is the "false positive" problem: a reasoning trace might produce a correct final output despite containing incorrect intermediate steps or flawed plans. This issue leads to a low-quality fine-tuning dataset, hindering the LLM's ability to consistently generate accurate and coherent reasoning.

To address the "false positive" problem, researchers have explored using value functions or reward models to verify reasoning traces. However, training a reliable *process reward model (PRM)* that assesses the correctness of *each step* in a reasoning trace traditionally requires costly and labor-intensive per-step human annotations (e.g., "Let's Verify Step-by-Step"). Alternative approaches, like MATH-SHEPHERD, attempt to autonomously synthesize process rewards from random rollouts or best-of-N selections, but these may still lack the granularity or reliability of human annotations. Furthermore, integrating tree-search algorithms like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with LLMs (e.g., TS-LLM, AlphaLLM, RAP) has shown promise for enhanced reasoning by exploring multiple paths, but often still rely on sparse outcome rewards or conventional value functions.

ReST-MCTS* distinguishes itself by addressing two critical gaps:
1. **Automated High-Quality Trace Acquisition:** It provides a novel mechanism to automatically infer and label per-step process rewards for training a PRM, circumventing the need for costly manual annotation. This is achieved by leveraging tree-search-based reinforcement learning with oracle final correct answers.
2. **Mutual Self-Improvement of Policy and Reward Models:** Unlike many self-training methods that focus solely on improving the policy model (generator), ReST-MCTS* proposes a "MuZero-style learning" paradigm where both the policy model (generator) and the process reward model (verifier) are continuously enhanced through iterative self-training, leading to a synergistic improvement.

By combining a sophisticated, process-reward-guided MCTS variant (MCTS*) with an iterative self-training loop that simultaneously refines both the generator and the verifier, ReST-MCTS* aims to produce demonstrably higher-quality reasoning traces, thus leading to more robust and accurate LLM reasoning capabilities. This positions the work as a significant step towards more autonomous and effective LLM self-improvement for complex reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind ReST-MCTS* stems from the inherent limitations of current LLM self-training methods, particularly the "false positive" issue in reasoning traces. The paper identifies that even when an LLM-generated reasoning trace leads to a correct final answer, the intermediate steps might be flawed, illogical, or useless. Relying on such traces for fine-tuning leads to a suboptimal self-training dataset, hindering the LLM's true reasoning capabilities for complex problems.

The key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Overcome Low-Quality Fine-Tuning Data:** To develop a method that addresses the issue of "incorrect plans or intermediate reasoning" within self-generated data, ensuring that the self-training dataset comprises higher-quality reasoning traces.
2. **Automate Process Reward Acquisition:** To circumvent the reliance on expensive and labor-intensive per-step manual annotation, which is typically required to train reliable process reward models (PRMs). The goal is to automatically infer these per-step rewards.
3. **Enhance Reasoning via Process Reward Guidance:** To integrate fine-grained process reward signals with an advanced tree search mechanism to guide the LLM's reasoning process more effectively, leading to the discovery of superior reasoning paths.
4. **Enable Mutual Self-Training:** To establish an iterative self-training framework where both the LLM policy model (generator) and the process reward model (verifier) can continuously improve by learning from self-generated, high-quality data.
5. **Improve LLM Performance on Complex Reasoning Tasks:** Ultimately, to achieve superior accuracy in complex reasoning benchmarks by producing and utilizing higher-quality training data for LLM fine-tuning.

In essence, the paper asks: "How can we automatically acquire high-quality reasoning traces and effectively process reward signals for verification and LLM self-training?" ReST-MCTS* is proposed as a comprehensive framework to answer this question.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReST-MCTS* is a reinforced self-training framework that employs a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithm, MCTS*, guided by a trained per-step process reward model (PRM). The core novelty lies in automatically inferring per-step reward labels and using them to iteratively refine both the reasoning policy and the reward model.

The methodology is structured around four main components:

1. **Value Function Definitions (Quality Value `v_k` and Weighted Reward `w_s_k`):**
- The paper introduces novel definitions for evaluating partial solutions: `v_k` (quality value) and `w_s_k` (weighted reward).
- `v_k` represents the probability that a partial solution `p_k` leads to a complete and correct answer, constrained within. It aims to reflect both cumulative progress and correctness.
- `w_s_k` is the weighted reward for a single step `s_k`. It incorporates `v_{k-1}` (previous quality value), `m_k` (reasoning distance, i.e., minimum steps to reach the correct answer from `p_k`), and `r_s_k` (a common PRM sigmoid score for `s_k` from MATH-SHEPHERD's hard estimation).
- The iterative calculation is: `v_k = max(v_{k-1} + w_s_k, 0)` and `w_s_k = (1 - v_{k-1}) / (m_k + 1) * (1 - 2r_s_k)`.
- These definitions ensure that `w_s_k` is higher for steps that make more progress or are more correct, and `v_k` converges to 1 only when a correct answer is reached. Crucially, `m_k` and `r_s_k` are *inferred* from rollouts and verified traces, eliminating manual annotation.

2. **Process Reward Model (PRM):**
- Instead of training separate models for `r_s_k` and `m_k`, ReST-MCTS* trains a single LLM-based `V_θ` to predict the quality value `v_k` for any partial solution. This `V_θ` serves as the PRM and guides the MCTS*.
- The initial training set for `V_θ` is generated autonomously using an existing dataset of questions with correct final answers (e.g., MATH dataset). For each question, a search tree is generated, and `v_k` values are inferred for all nodes based on verified correct traces.

3. **MCTS* (Search-Based Reasoning Policy):**
- MCTS* is a variant of MCTS that directly uses the trained `V_θ` to evaluate intermediate nodes, select paths, and backpropagate values. This allows for intermediate step evaluation without requiring complete trace generation for sparse outcome rewards.
- **Node Selection:** Uses an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) criterion (`v_C + ε * sqrt(ln(n_parent) / n_C)`) to balance exploration and exploitation, prioritizing high-quality nodes while still exploring new ones.
- **Thought Expansion:** If a node's quality value `v_C` is below a threshold, new solution steps are sampled from the policy model (`π_S_0`), creating new child nodes.
- **Self-Critic Mechanism:** An LLM-based self-critic generates an "End of Inference" (EoI) signal or advice *before* expansion. If EoI is signaled, further exploration is stopped, improving efficiency by pruning unproductive branches. Otherwise, the advice guides subsequent step generation.
- **Greedy MC Rollout:** A short simulation (greedy rollout) is performed from newly expanded nodes to estimate a more accurate `v_max`, which is then used to update the node's `v_C` (a form of value bootstrapping).
- **Value Backpropagation:** `n_C` and `v_C` are updated for all parent nodes on the selected path, using a weighted average of child values, reflecting the quality of discovered paths.

4. **Mutual Self-Training Pipeline:**
- This is an iterative process (Algorithm 1) that continuously improves both `V_θ` and `π_S`.
- **Initialization:** The base LLM `π` is fine-tuned (`π_S_0`), and `V_θ` is initialized using an autonomously generated dataset `D_V_0`.
- **Iteration `i`:**
- `π_S_{i-1}` and `V_{i-1}` guide MCTS* to generate new reasoning traces for a problem set `D_G`.
- **Policy Model Training Data Collection:** The generated traces are verified against ground truth (or LLM judging for final answer), and *only correct solutions* (`D_G_i(A_j=a*)`) are selected for fine-tuning `π_S_{i-1}` to obtain `π_S_i` using SFT.
- **Process Reward Model Training Data Collection:** For *all* generated traces (correct and incorrect), the `v_k` and `w_s_k` values are inferred for every node on or near correct paths (as shown in Figure 3 example). This process leverages the verified correct solutions to back-calculate `m_k` and `r_s_k` for intermediate steps. These (Q, partial_solution, v_k) triplets form `D_V_i`, which is then used to fine-tune `V_{i-1}` to obtain `V_i`.
- This iterative loop allows the improved policy to generate more diverse and potentially better traces, which in turn leads to a more accurate process reward model, and vice-versa, creating a positive feedback loop.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments validate ReST-MCTS* from three key perspectives: its effectiveness in self-training, the quality of its learned process reward model, and the performance of its tree-search policy.

1. **Performance in Self-Training Benchmarks:**
- **Continuous Self-Improvement:** ReST-MCTS* demonstrates continuous self-improvement for all three LLM backbones tested (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B: MetaMATH, SciGLM-6B) across multiple iterations (Table 2). The models consistently improve on both in-distribution (MATH, GSM8K, TheoremQA derived data) and out-of-distribution (GPQA Diamond, CEval-Hard) benchmarks.
- **Outperformance over Baselines:** ReST-MCTS* significantly outperforms other prominent self-training algorithms like ReST_EM and Self-Rewarding LM in nearly every iteration across all backbones and benchmarks. This suggests that ReST-MCTS* effectively screens out higher-quality self-generated data for better self-improvement. For example, LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct improved from 24.78% (zero-shot) to 29.02% (2nd iter) average accuracy with ReST-MCTS*, compared to 26.83% (ReST_EM) and 27.73% (Self-Rewarding).

2. **Process Reward Model Efficacy:**
- **Higher Accuracy PRM:** The process reward model (value model) trained by ReST-MCTS* achieves higher accuracy compared to state-of-the-art techniques like MATH-SHEPHERD (MS) and its variants (SC+MS) on GSM8K and MATH500 datasets (Table 3). For instance, on GSM8K, SC+ReST-MCTS* (Value) achieved 87.5% accuracy compared to MS's 87.1% and SC+MS's 86.3%. On MATH500, it reached 39.0% vs. 37.3% (MS) and 38.3% (SC+MS). This confirms the effectiveness of the proposed definitions for quality value and weighted reward in generating reliable feedback.

3. **Tree-Search Policy Performance:**
- **Superiority under Same Search Budget:** MCTS* achieves higher accuracy compared to prior LLM reasoning baselines such as Self-Consistency (SC), ORM+Best-of-N (BoN), and PRM+BoN, given the same search budget (measured in completion tokens) on MATH and SciBench (Figure 2). On MATH, ReST-MCTS* (Iter #2) achieved 48.5% accuracy, significantly exceeding Self-Consistency's 42.5%, and continuously improves with more self-training iterations. On SciBench, MCTS* also consistently outperforms other baselines even with insufficient budget.
- **Outperformance on College-Level Reasoning:** ReST-MCTS* consistently outperforms Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Tree-of-Thought (ToT) baselines across multiple large language model backbones (GLM4, GPT-3.5-turbo, LLaMA2-13B-Chat) on challenging college-level scientific reasoning benchmarks like SciBench (Table 4) and SciEval (Table 8). For example, GLM4's average accuracy on SciBench improved from 12.68% (CoT) to 16.77% (ReST-MCTS*). This demonstrates its potential in discovering accurate solutions, especially on subjects like chemistry, quantum mechanics, and statistics.
- **Efficiency Considerations:** While MCTS* consumes more time for exploration and simulation than simpler algorithms like CoT+SC or ORM+BoN (Table 6), the paper argues this extra time is acceptable given the significant accuracy gains and the fact that it can achieve higher accuracy levels that other algorithms cannot reach even with unlimited budget. The efficiency is also partially maintained by the novel value function and self-critic mechanism, which reduces the need for massive Monte Carlo estimations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReST-MCTS* framework represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model self-training and reasoning. Its core contributions address critical challenges, leading to several potential impacts:

1. **Improved LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** By generating and learning from higher-quality reasoning traces, ReST-MCTS* enables LLMs to develop more robust and accurate step-by-step reasoning abilities. This is particularly crucial for complex, multi-step problem-solving tasks in domains like mathematics, science, and potentially coding or agentic behavior.
2. **Automated Data Annotation for Process Rewards:** The ability to automatically infer per-step process rewards (`v_k` and `w_s_k`) from oracle final answers fundamentally circumvents the costly and impractical need for dense human annotations. This automation makes the training of sophisticated process reward models scalable and accessible, opening doors for more fine-grained supervision of LLM reasoning.
3. **Synergistic Self-Improvement:** The mutual self-training paradigm, akin to "MuZero-style learning," where both the LLM policy (generator) and the process reward model (verifier) are iteratively improved, creates a powerful positive feedback loop. A better policy generates more diverse data, which refines the reward model, which in turn guides the policy to even better reasoning, leading to continuous enhancement.
4. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency and Search Effectiveness:** The MCTS* algorithm, guided by the novel quality value, demonstrates superior performance compared to existing search strategies (e.g., Best-of-N, Self-Consistency) under the same computational budget. This indicates more efficient exploration of reasoning paths and a higher likelihood of finding correct solutions, even for challenging problems.
5. **Foundation for Future LLM Development:** ReST-MCTS* lays a robust foundation for building more autonomous and intelligent LLMs. By providing a scalable way to obtain high-quality reasoning supervision, it can facilitate advancements in agentic AI, complex problem-solving, and potentially lead to LLMs that can self-debug and self-correct their reasoning processes more effectively.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, primarily focusing on the generalizability and scalability of the method. These include the current reliance on ground-truth oracle labels, which limits immediate application to tasks without labels (e.g., open-ended dialogue, SWE-Bench). Future work aims to generalize ReST-MCTS* to other reasoning tasks (coding, agents), address scenarios without ground-truth answers (requiring alternative reward feedback mechanisms), and scale up the value model while improving data filtering techniques, possibly by incorporating online RL algorithms.

Overall, ReST-MCTS* presents a compelling framework for advancing LLM self-training, particularly by tackling the critical challenge of reasoning trace quality through an innovative, automated process reward mechanism and a powerful iterative self-improvement pipeline.
