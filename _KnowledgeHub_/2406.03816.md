# ReST-MCTS*: LLM Self-Training via Process Reward Guided Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> ReST-MCTS*, developed by researchers at Tsinghua University and Caltech, enhances large language model self-training by iteratively improving both the generator and a process reward model, leveraging a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search guided by automatically inferred per-step quality values. The method achieves improved LLM performance on complex reasoning benchmarks by producing higher-quality reasoning traces.

## Problem

- Existing LLM self-training methods suffer from 'false positives,' where correct final answers might derive from flawed intermediate reasoning, leading to low-quality fine-tuning data.
- Training reliable per-step process reward models (PRMs) traditionally requires costly and labor-intensive human annotation for each intermediate step.
- Integrating tree-search algorithms with LLMs often relies on sparse outcome rewards, lacking fine-grained guidance for intermediate reasoning steps.

## Method

- Introduces ==ReST-MCTS*==, a reinforced self-training framework that employs a modified ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS*)== guided by a trained per-step ==process reward model (PRM)==.
- Automatically infers per-step reward labels (quality value 'v_k' and weighted reward 'w_s_k') from rollouts and verified traces, circumventing the need for manual annotation to train the PRM.
- Employs a =='MuZero-style learning' paradigm== to iteratively and mutually improve both the LLM policy (generator) and the process reward model (verifier) based on self-generated, high-quality data.

## Results

- ReST-MCTS* consistently improved LLM performance on complex reasoning benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, SciBench) across multiple models and iterations, outperforming other self-training methods.
- The autonomously trained process reward model achieved higher accuracy compared to state-of-the-art PRM training techniques (e.g., MATH-SHEPHERD) on GSM8K (**87.5%** vs **87.1%**) and MATH500 (**39.0%** vs **37.3%**) datasets.
- The MCTS* policy demonstrated superior accuracy over baselines (e.g., Self-Consistency, Tree-of-Thought) for the same search budget on challenging reasoning tasks like MATH and SciBench, achieving up to **48.5%** on MATH.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Novel definitions for quality value and weighted reward enable automated inference of per-step process rewards from oracle final answers, bypassing manual annotation.
> - A synergistic, iterative self-training loop between the LLM policy and the process reward model allows for continuous, mutual improvement of both components.
> - Guiding a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS*) with a fine-grained, learned process reward model effectively explores and selects higher-quality reasoning paths.
