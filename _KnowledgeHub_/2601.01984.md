# Thinking with Blueprints: Assisting Vision-Language Models in Spatial Reasoning via Structured Object Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Thinking with Blueprints" framework enhances Vision-Language Models' spatial reasoning by integrating an object-centric, structured representation inspired by human cognitive maps. Developed by researchers from NUS, Microsoft Research, Tsinghua University, and the University of Toronto, the approach achieves up to a 35.9% improvement on in-distribution benchmarks and robust generalization across various out-of-distribution spatial reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with robust spatial reasoning, often compromising global spatial awareness by focusing on isolated image patches.
- Current VLM methods rely on scattered, isolated coordinates for object location, failing to capture coherent spatial relationships between objects.
- Prevailing VLM paradigms lack a principled approach to integrating holistic spatial information, leading to superficial analysis and incorrect answers in complex spatial contexts.

## Method

- The method uses a two-stage Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipeline to train VLMs to construct and analyze an object-centric, JSON-style "blueprint" of scene elements.
- Blueprint-embedded reasoning traces are generated using a strong teacher VLM (GPT-4o) and Monte Carlo Tree Search to provide structured supervision.
- Novel blueprint-aware rewards (Object Cardinality and Causal Consistency) are introduced during RL to guide appropriate blueprint construction and ensure reasoning is genuinely grounded in the blueprint, alongside anti-shortcut data augmentation (image/question perturbation) to prevent superficial learning.

## Results

- The method achieved 92.7% accuracy on the SAT-val benchmark, representing a 35.9% improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL and a ~15% gain over strong specialized spatial reasoning models.
- It demonstrated robust out-of-distribution generalization, with 79.7% accuracy on SAT-test (16.4% gain over base) and 60.7% on BLINK (4.3% gain over base), despite no task-specific fine-tuning.
- Qualitative analyses show the method enables tighter, more semantically aligned attention to relevant image regions and produces reasoning traces that correctly infer relative object movement even with camera changes, outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o and GPT-5-Thinking in many benchmarks with a smaller 7B parameter base model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Merely adding Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning does not guarantee improved performance in VLMs; an *effective* and structured reasoning strategy is crucial.
> - The structured organization of observations into an object-centric blueprint, separating perception from analysis, is more effective for spatial reasoning than relying on fragmented observations or isolated numerical cues.
> - Blueprint-aware rewards and anti-shortcut data augmentation are critical for guiding models to construct optimal blueprints, ensuring reasoning consistency, and significantly improving generalization to out-of-distribution data.
