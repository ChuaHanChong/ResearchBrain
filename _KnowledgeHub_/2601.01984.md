# Thinking with Blueprints: Assisting Vision-Language Models in Spatial Reasoning via Structured Object Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Thinking with Blueprints" framework enhances Vision-Language Models' spatial reasoning by integrating an object-centric, structured representation inspired by human cognitive maps. Developed by researchers from NUS, Microsoft Research, Tsinghua University, and the University of Toronto, the approach achieves up to a 35.9% improvement on in-distribution benchmarks and robust generalization across various out-of-distribution spatial reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with robust spatial reasoning, often compromising global spatial awareness by focusing on isolated image patches.
- Current VLM methods rely on scattered, isolated coordinates for object location, failing to capture coherent spatial relationships between objects.
- Prevailing VLM paradigms lack a principled approach to integrating holistic spatial information, leading to superficial analysis and incorrect answers in complex spatial contexts.

## Method

- The method uses a two-stage Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipeline to train VLMs to construct and analyze an object-centric, JSON-style "blueprint" of scene elements.
- Blueprint-embedded reasoning traces are generated using a strong teacher VLM (GPT-4o) and Monte Carlo Tree Search to provide structured supervision.
- Novel blueprint-aware rewards (Object Cardinality and Causal Consistency) are introduced during RL to guide appropriate blueprint construction and ensure reasoning is genuinely grounded in the blueprint, alongside anti-shortcut data augmentation (image/question perturbation) to prevent superficial learning.

## Results

- The method achieved 92.7% accuracy on the SAT-val benchmark, representing a 35.9% improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL and a ~15% gain over strong specialized spatial reasoning models.
- It demonstrated robust out-of-distribution generalization, with 79.7% accuracy on SAT-test (16.4% gain over base) and 60.7% on BLINK (4.3% gain over base), despite no task-specific fine-tuning.
- Qualitative analyses show the method enables tighter, more semantically aligned attention to relevant image regions and produces reasoning traces that correctly infer relative object movement even with camera changes, outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o and GPT-5-Thinking in many benchmarks with a smaller 7B parameter base model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Merely adding Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning does not guarantee improved performance in VLMs; an *effective* and structured reasoning strategy is crucial.
> - The structured organization of observations into an object-centric blueprint, separating perception from analysis, is more effective for spatial reasoning than relying on fragmented observations or isolated numerical cues.
> - Blueprint-aware rewards and anti-shortcut data augmentation are critical for guiding models to construct optimal blueprints, ensuring reasoning consistency, and significantly improving generalization to out-of-distribution data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Integration within the Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Current Challenges in VLM Spatial Reasoning

Spatial reasoning, which encompasses the ability to understand and interpret relationships in space, is a fundamental human cognitive capability critical for tasks ranging from navigation to object manipulation. Equipping Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with robust spatial reasoning is a crucial step towards developing more general and grounded artificial intelligence, particularly for applications in robotics and intelligent agents. However, current VLM-based approaches often struggle with rigorous spatial layout modeling. They typically adopt two main strategies:

1. **Thinking with Images**: These methods revisit local image patches (e.g., through cropping, zooming, or editing) to improve fine-grained perception. While effective for detailed analysis, this approach often compromises global spatial awareness by treating image segments in isolation, thereby weakening the understanding of overall object arrangement and interrelations.
2. **Thinking with Coordinates**: This approach involves predicting or marking object positions using points or bounding boxes. While useful for locating individual objects, it often relies on scattered and isolated coordinates rather than an organized representation that captures how objects relate to each other within a coherent spatial context.

Both existing paradigms, while making progress, lack a principled approach to integrating holistic spatial information, often leading to superficial analysis and incorrect answers when complex spatial relationships are involved.

#### 1.2. Cognitive Science Inspiration

This work draws direct inspiration from cognitive science, specifically from theories on how humans perceive and reason in space. Key cognitive concepts leveraged include:

- **Object files**: Visual signals are first bound into individuated "object files" (Kahneman et al., 1992; Egly et al., 1994), representing individual objects with their properties.
- **Object-centric blueprint (Cognitive Map)**: These object files collectively form an "object-centric blueprint" or cognitive map (Tolman, 1948; O’Keefe and Nadel, 1978), which encodes object-level layouts and relationships.
- **Blueprint scanning for reasoning**: Human reasoning then proceeds by "scanning" this internal blueprint to query spatial relations and make judgments (Kosslyn, 1973; Kosslyn et al., 1978).

The paper argues that analogous stages can be identified in prevailing VLMs: large-scale pretraining for perception, visual grounding via bounding boxes for object-file binding, and chain-of-thought reasoning for scanning. By explicitly integrating the "object-centric blueprint" concept, the authors aim to imbue VLMs with a more human-like, structured pathway for spatial understanding.

#### 1.3. Proposed Contribution

"Thinking with Blueprints" positions itself as a novel approach that bridges the gap between these cognitive insights and VLM capabilities. Instead of fragmented observations or isolated coordinates, it advocates for a unified, structured representation of scene elements. This moves beyond merely perceiving objects or their individual locations to understanding their collective spatial organization, offering a more robust foundation for complex spatial reasoning. The method explicitly separates the "observation" (blueprint construction) from "reflection" (blueprint analysis), addressing the entanglement found in prior VLM approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to significantly enhance the spatial reasoning abilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by integrating an object-centric, structured representation inspired by human cognition. The authors aim to enable VLMs to perceive, understand, and reason about spatial relationships in a more robust and coherent manner than existing methods.

The primary motivations driving this work are:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing VLM Approaches**: Current VLMs, despite advancements, often fall short in complex spatial reasoning tasks. They either focus too narrowly on local image patches, losing global context, or rely on isolated coordinate data, which fails to capture intricate spatial relationships. The authors are motivated to develop an approach that addresses these shortcomings by providing a holistic and structured understanding of spatial layouts.
2. **Emulating Human Cognitive Processes**: Insights from cognitive science suggest that humans naturally employ an object-centric "blueprint" or cognitive map for spatial reasoning. The researchers are motivated to translate this powerful cognitive mechanism into an AI model, believing that mirroring human cognitive pathways can lead to more robust and generalizable AI capabilities.
3. **Separating Observation from Reflection**: A key motivation is to decouple the process of perceiving and structuring information (observation) from the process of reasoning over that information (reflection). By first constructing a comprehensive, structured "blueprint" of relevant objects and their attributes, and then performing reasoning over this stable representation, the model can avoid the "stochastic glimpses and infer" paradigm common in existing VLMs, leading to more principled and less superficial reasoning.
4. **Enabling General and Grounded AI**: Strong spatial reasoning is fundamental for intelligent agents to interact with the physical world, especially in domains like robotics, autonomous navigation, and intelligent manipulation. The work is motivated by the broader goal of developing more generally intelligent and physically grounded AI systems.

In essence, the paper aims to introduce a principled, cognitive-inspired framework that allows VLMs to build a foundational understanding of spatial layouts, leading to superior performance in diverse spatial reasoning tasks and a step closer to human-like perception and intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed "thinking with blueprint" method integrates the cognitive concept of an object-centric blueprint into VLMs through a two-stage training pipeline: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL). This pipeline incorporates three key techniques: blueprint-embedded reasoning traces, blueprint-aware rewards, and anti-shortcut data augmentation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Object-Centric Blueprints

Given an image (I) and a question (Q), the model first generates a JSON-style blueprint (O). This blueprint is a structured list of objects relevant to the question, where each object (o_i) includes:
- `s_i`: A natural language "thought" describing how the object was identified.
- `e_i`: The entity name (e.g., "oranges", "man").
- `b_i`: A bounding box specifying its rough position and size.
- `d_i`: Natural-language attributes (e.g., "a pile of fresh oranges").

The model then performs an analysis (`z`) over this blueprint to produce the final answer (`a`). The complete reasoning trace (`τ`) thus includes the blueprint, the analysis, and the final answer, generated autoregressively by the VLM.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training: SFT and RL

1. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Stage**:
 - **Objective**: To elicit basic blueprint-based reasoning skills in the base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL).
 - **Mechanism**: The model is fine-tuned by minimizing the cross-entropy loss between its generated reasoning trace and a set of ground-truth blueprint-embedded reasoning traces.
 - **Challenge & Solution**: Existing spatial reasoning datasets lack these detailed traces. To address this, the authors construct these traces using a **stepwise collection pipeline**. A strong teacher VLM (GPT-4o) is prompted to generate atomic reasoning steps (adding objects, analyzing the blueprint, summarizing the answer), which are then assembled into coherent, goal-directed traces using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). This process creates the necessary supervision for SFT.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Stage**:
 - **Objective**: To further enhance the model's reasoning capabilities, improve generalization, and mitigate shortcut learning.
 - **Mechanism**: The model is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize an expected reward `R(τ)` over sampled reasoning traces.
 - **Key Techniques Introduced**:
 - **Blueprint-Aware Rewards**: Beyond conventional rewards (answer correctness `R_acc` and trace format `R_fmt`), two novel rewards are introduced:
 - **Object Cardinality Reward (`R_card`)**: This reward encourages the blueprint to include an *appropriate* number of objects. It uses the number of objects (K) mentioned in the question/answer as a reference. The model is rewarded for including a sufficient number of objects (`|O| <= λK`) but capped for including excessive, potentially irrelevant ones (`|O| > λK`), preventing both information sparsity and overload. Object counts are based on distinct bounding boxes (IoU <= 0.3).
 - **Causal Consistency Reward (`R_cons`)**: Inspired by RLPR, this reward encourages the final answer to be genuinely grounded in the blueprint-guided reasoning trace, rather than superficial correlations. It measures the difference between the probability of generating the correct answer conditioned on the full reasoning context (image, question, blueprint, analysis) and conditioned only on the input pair (image, question).
 - **Anti-Shortcut Data Augmentation**: To prevent the model from relying on superficial visual or linguistic cues (shortcut learning) rather than true reasoning, the RL training data is augmented:
 - **Image Perturbation**: An LLM identifies relevant objects, and an image-editing model (Flux-Kontext) removes them, creating a perturbed image (`I'`). The answer (`a'`) is altered (e.g., "0" for counting, "question and image do not match").
 - **Question Perturbation**: An LLM identifies spatial predicates and modifies them to invert the original answer (e.g., "left" to "right"), creating a perturbed question (`Q'`). The answer (`a'`) is also reversed.
 - A final LLM filter ensures the augmented examples are plausible and lead to the intended altered answer.

This comprehensive methodological approach, combining cognitive inspiration with a sophisticated two-stage training strategy and novel rewards/augmentation, forms the backbone of the "Thinking with Blueprints" system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling quantitative and qualitative results that underscore the effectiveness of the "Thinking with Blueprints" approach.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

The method, fine-tuned on Qwen2.5-VL 7B, consistently outperforms existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and specialized spatial reasoning models across various benchmarks:

- **In-Distribution (iid) Performance (SAT-val)**: Achieved a remarkable 35.9% improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL, with a score of 92.7%. This also represents a significant ~15% gain over the strongest specialized spatial-reasoning method (ViGoRL), demonstrating exceptional performance on data similar to the training set.
- **Out-of-Distribution (ood) Generalization**: The method showed robust generalization capabilities on unseen data, despite no task-specific fine-tuning:
 - **SAT-test**: 79.7% accuracy, a 16.4% gain over base Qwen2.5-VL.
 - **BLINK**: 60.7% accuracy, a 4.3% gain over base Qwen2.5-VL.
 - **RoboSpatial**: 70.2% accuracy, a 3.5% gain over base Qwen2.5-VL.
 - **VSR**: 84.8% accuracy, a 1.2% gain over base Qwen2.5-VL.
- **Superiority over Larger and Specialized Models**: "Thinking with Blueprints" surpassed proprietary models like GPT-4o and GPT-5-Thinking, and specialized models like Robix-32B, VigoRL, and ViLASR, across most benchmarks. This is particularly noteworthy given that it uses a 7B parameter base model, often outperforming much larger proprietary models.
- **Discovery 1: Reasoning is not always effective**: The results highlight that simply adding Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning does not guarantee improvement. For Qwen2.5-VL, its native CoT version underperformed the non-reasoning version. Even large models like GPT-4o showed limited or degraded performance with CoT on some benchmarks. This emphasizes that an *effective* reasoning strategy, like the blueprint-based approach, is crucial.
- **Discovery 2: Organization of perceived content matters**: The superior performance over other methods that also use numerical spatial cues (e.g., ViGoRL, ViLASR) indicates that the structured organization of observations within a blueprint, separating observation from analysis, is a key factor for enhanced reasoning.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Insights

- **Reasoning Traces**: Qualitative examples (e.g., kettle movement analysis) showcased the blueprint method's ability to identify fixed reference points (like a wall socket) and correctly infer relative object movement, even when the camera position changes. Other methods often missed movement or failed to account for camera motion.
- **Attention Maps**: Visualization of attention maps revealed that "Thinking with Blueprints" leads to a tighter, more semantically aligned focus on the true regions of interest in an image. In contrast, other reasoning-based methods often showed scattered high-attention patches. This tighter focus likely contributes to improved performance.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the critical contribution of each proposed component:

- **Anti-Shortcut Data Augmentation**: Its removal caused a modest degradation on iid benchmarks but *substantial drops* (e.g., 11.4% on SAT-test, 7.3% on RoboSpatial) on ood benchmarks, confirming its crucial role in improving generalization by preventing reliance on superficial patterns.
- **Blueprint-Aware Rewards (`R_card` & `R_cons`)**: Removing these led to clear performance declines across all benchmarks, highlighting their importance in guiding the model to construct appropriate blueprints and ensure reasoning consistency.
- **Vanilla GRPO vs. Full Method**: Vanilla GRPO (without SFT, blueprint, or blueprint-related techniques) performed significantly worse, demonstrating the blueprint-based workflow's foundational strength.
- **SFT Only vs. SFT+RL**: The RL stage consistently improved performance over SFT alone, validating its role in further refining and generalizing the reasoning capabilities.

In summary, the results strongly validate "Thinking with Blueprints" as a highly effective and robust approach for spatial reasoning in VLMs, establishing new state-of-the-art performance and providing valuable insights into the design of reasoning strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Thinking with Blueprints" research makes several significant contributions to the field of Vision-Language Models and spatial reasoning, with substantial potential impact.

#### 5.1. Contributions and Impact

1. **Novel Integration of Cognitive Science**: The most significant contribution is the pioneering integration of the object-centric blueprint concept, derived from human cognitive science, into VLMs. This provides a principled and human-inspired framework for spatial reasoning, moving beyond ad-hoc visual processing or coordinate manipulation. It validates the hypothesis that structuring perceptions into a coherent, object-centric map can dramatically enhance a model's understanding of space.
2. **State-of-the-Art Performance**: The method achieves consistent and substantial performance gains across diverse spatial reasoning benchmarks, including both in-distribution and challenging out-of-distribution datasets. Outperforming larger proprietary models (like GPT-5-Thinking) and existing specialized spatial reasoning models with a 7B parameter base model demonstrates its efficiency and effectiveness. This sets a new benchmark for spatial reasoning in VLMs.
3. **Innovative Training Techniques**: The paper introduces three novel and impactful techniques within a two-stage SFT&RL pipeline:
 - **Blueprint-embedded reasoning traces**: This method for generating structured reasoning supervision via MCTS is crucial for bootstrapping the blueprint mechanism in the absence of pre-existing annotated datasets. It offers a generalizable approach for instilling complex reasoning capabilities.
 - **Blueprint-aware rewards (Object Cardinality and Causal Consistency)**: These specially designed reinforcement learning rewards are critical for guiding the model to construct optimal blueprints (neither too sparse nor too verbose) and ensuring that its final answers are genuinely grounded in its intermediate reasoning. This addresses common failure modes in VLM reasoning, such as hallucination or shortcut learning.
 - **Anti-shortcut data augmentation**: This technique effectively mitigates shortcut learning by forcing the model to engage in deeper, blueprint-based reasoning rather than relying on superficial visual or linguistic patterns. Its impact on out-of-distribution generalization is particularly noteworthy.
4. **Advancing General and Grounded AI**: By enhancing spatial reasoning, this work takes a vital step towards developing more general and grounded artificial intelligence. Spatial intelligence is fundamental for any intelligent agent operating in the physical world.
5. **Bridging Perception and Cognition**: The approach fosters a tighter coupling between visual perception and higher-level cognitive reasoning in VLMs, enabling them to construct a mental model of the scene before making judgments, similar to human cognition.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge certain limitations:
1. **Reliance on Synthetic Data**: The primary training data (SAT) consists of synthetic indoor scene images. While effective for initial training, a more diverse dataset containing real-world images could further improve generalization and real-world applicability. An ablation study showed improvements when adding real-world images (VSR) to the training set, confirming this direction.
2. **Computational Resources**: Due to limited resources, larger VLMs (e.g., 32B parameters) were not explored. Scaling the "Thinking with Blueprints" approach to even larger base models could potentially yield further improvements.

These limitations also point to promising future directions:
- **Extension to Video and 3D Spatial Reasoning**: The paper demonstrates a potential extension to video-based spatial reasoning using a simple frame-selection module, suggesting that the blueprint concept can generalize beyond static images. Future work could fully adapt this to dynamic and three-dimensional environments, crucial for robotics.
- **Empowering Downstream Tasks**: The enhanced spatial reasoning capabilities could directly benefit downstream applications, such as robotic manipulation, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality, by providing more accurate and robust spatial understanding.
- **Integration with Real-World Priors**: Addressing failure cases related to lacking world knowledge and camera geometry sensing suggests future research into incorporating richer real-world physical priors and explicit camera model awareness.

In conclusion, "Thinking with Blueprints" represents a significant leap forward in equipping VLMs with advanced spatial reasoning abilities, driven by a principled, cognitive-inspired methodology and robust training techniques. Its impact is poised to extend across various domains of AI, contributing to the development of more intelligent, grounded, and human-like AI systems.
