# SkillFactory: Self-Distillation For Learning Cognitive Behaviors

> [!summary] Summary
> The SkillFactory framework introduces a three-stage self-distillation method to teach cognitive behaviors like verification and retrying to large language models. This approach generates structured "silver traces" from a model's own outputs, leading to superior generalization on harder tasks and robust performance on out-of-domain tasks after reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often lack consistent cognitive behaviors such as systematic exploration, output verification, and strategic retrying, which are essential for complex reasoning.
- Current methods to instill these reasoning capabilities frequently depend on access to stronger, often proprietary, teacher models for distillation or require resource-intensive data curation.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods that prioritize immediate task performance may not optimally prime models for subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) to develop robust skill utilization.

## Method

- **SkillFactory** employs a three-stage framework: ==data curation==, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL).
- Data curation involves using a base model to generate diverse solution attempts and reflections (critiques and verdicts), then structuring these into "silver traces" that explicitly demonstrate self-correction and retrying.
- SFT trains the model on these structured silver traces to internalize the *structure* of cognitive behaviors, acting as a warm-start, followed by RL (using GRPO) with sparse correctness rewards to refine skill application.

## Results

- SkillFactory consistently achieved superior performance on harder variants of Countdown tasks, outperforming distillation from stronger models (e.g., **25.1%** overall accuracy for Qwen2.5-**1.5B** vs. **21.2%** for R1 Distill on harder Countdown tasks).
- The method demonstrated competitive robustness on out-of-domain tasks, matching or exceeding distillation baselines (e.g., **35.7%** accuracy for Qwen2.5-**1.5B** on OOD tasks) and showing greater resilience to performance regression.
- SkillFactory-initialized models showed enhanced reasoning on challenging mathematical and scientific benchmarks (e.g., **42.1%** at 1k SFT rows for Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct, competitive with distillation methods and outperforming them on specific benchmarks like AMC and Math500).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training models on the explicit *structure* of cognitive behaviors through self-generated traces during SFT is more effective for subsequent RL-driven skill acquisition than solely optimizing for SFT task accuracy.
> - Robust cognitive behaviors like reflection and retrying can be instilled in LLMs through a self-contained, self-distillation process, reducing the reliance on external, more capable teacher models.
> - The learned structural biases from SkillFactory's SFT significantly enhance a model's ability to generalize reasoning skills to harder variants and maintain robustness on out-of-domain tasks, indicating effective skill transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of endowing large language models (LLMs) with cognitive behaviors or "skills," such as systematic exploration, output verification, and strategy retrying, which are considered crucial for advanced reasoning capabilities. Recent literature indicates that significant advancements in reasoning-focused LLMs are often attributed to the improved elicitation of these skills during inference.

Current approaches to developing such reasoning capabilities typically fall into three categories:
1. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with sparse rewards:** This method can reinforce latent reasoning behaviors in strong base models. However, its effectiveness is contingent on the base model already exhibiting these skills to some degree, and they may not emerge reliably if not sufficiently represented in pre-training data.
2. **Distillation from stronger models:** This involves supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on reasoning traces generated by more capable "teacher" models. While effective, this approach necessitates access to superior models, which are often proprietary or computationally expensive to acquire, and may struggle to generalize beyond the specific domains of the distilled data.
3. **Targeted data curation:** This includes strategies like continual pre-training on backtracking examples, crafting reasoning chains for in-context learning, or using Monte Carlo tree search rollouts. These methods show promise in instilling specific skills but can be resource-intensive in terms of data generation or model training.

The SkillFactory work positions itself within this landscape by offering an alternative to distillation from stronger models and a more accessible form of targeted data curation. It aims to enable models to acquire and leverage cognitive skills even when those skills are not prominently exhibited by the base model, and without requiring a more powerful external teacher. By focusing on self-distillation—where a model learns from its *own* restructured outputs—SkillFactory attempts to prime models for effective RL-based skill acquisition, emphasizing the importance of structural biases over mere content imitation from a superior model. This approach is motivated by observations that higher pre-RL performance does not always translate to better post-RL performance, suggesting that the *way* skills are learned and structured might be more impactful than initial task proficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the SkillFactory project is to develop a method for fine-tuning language models to learn and effectively utilize cognitive behaviors, such as answer verification and strategic retrying, particularly when these skills are not inherently present or consistently exhibited by the base models.

This overarching objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Bridging the Skill Gap in Base Models:** While some LLMs show incidental signs of cognitive skills, these behaviors are often inconsistent or underdeveloped, especially when not explicitly prompted. The authors aim to enable models to acquire and systematically apply skills like retrying and reflection, which are critical for robust reasoning.
- **Reducing Reliance on Superior Models:** Existing methods, particularly distillation, often require access to larger, more capable models to generate high-quality reasoning traces for SFT. This dependency can be a significant barrier due to the proprietary nature, computational cost, or data requirements of such models. SkillFactory is motivated by the desire to instill these behaviors without needing an external "teacher" model.
- **Improving Generalization and Robustness:** The research seeks to develop models that not only perform well on the specific tasks they are trained on but also generalize these acquired skills to harder variants of those tasks and to entirely novel, out-of-domain tasks. This aims to counter the limitations of some existing methods that struggle with cross-domain generalization or exhibit catastrophic forgetting.
- **Leveraging Self-Generated Data:** The core motivation behind SkillFactory's methodology is the hypothesis that a model can effectively learn complex cognitive structures by processing and restructuring its *own* generated outputs. By taking samples, reflections, and attempts (both correct and incorrect) from the base model, and then organizing them into structured "silver traces" that explicitly demonstrate self-correction, the model can be "primed" to internalize these behaviors.
- **Optimizing for RL Effectiveness:** The authors propose that directly training for task performance during SFT might not be the most effective strategy for subsequent RL. Instead, the motivation is to use SFT to teach the *structure* of cognitive behaviors, making the model a more receptive and efficient learner during the RL phase where effective skill utilization is refined through sparse correctness rewards. This challenges the notion that higher SFT accuracy directly correlates with better post-RL performance.
- **Addressing Overthinking and Underthinking:** By providing a structured framework for applying skills, SkillFactory aims to guide models to utilize cognitive behaviors appropriately, avoiding issues such as excessive verbosity without progress ("overthinking") or prematurely abandoning solution paths ("underthinking"). This involves learning when and how to apply verification and retry mechanisms effectively.

In essence, SkillFactory is motivated by the goal of democratizing access to advanced reasoning capabilities in LLMs by demonstrating that robust cognitive skill acquisition can be achieved through a self-supervised, structure-focused fine-tuning approach, followed by targeted reinforcement learning, thereby circumventing the limitations of current teacher-dependent or highly resource-intensive paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkillFactory is a three-stage framework designed to teach cognitive reasoning skills to language models. The approach centers on generating structured "silver" traces from a model's own outputs and then using these traces for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) before applying reinforcement learning (RL).

#### 3.1. SkillFactory Framework

The framework comprises three main components:

1. **Data Curation:** This stage involves using inference on a base model, combined with heuristics specific to the cognitive skills of interest (retrying and reflection), to construct the SFT dataset.
2. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The model is trained on the curated "silver" traces. The primary objective here is to teach the model the *structure* of the cognitive behaviors rather than directly improving task performance. This SFT phase serves as a warm-start for the subsequent RL.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** An off-the-shelf RL algorithm, specifically GRPO, is used to refine the skills instilled during SFT. This stage leverages sparse rewards based solely on the correctness of the final answer to hone the effective application of these skills.

#### 3.2. Detailed Data Curation Process

The data curation process, outlined in Algorithm 1, involves three steps for each question `(q_i, a_i)` in the target dataset `D_T`:

1. **Solution Generation:**
 - **Sampling:** The base model `M` is prompted to generate `N_sample` (16) diverse solution attempts for each question. To encourage variety in reasoning paths, a set of four distinct chain-of-thought prompts (`P_solve`) is utilized. This yields a total of 64 solution attempts per question.
 - **Verification:** Each generated solution `y` is automatically verified against the ground truth answer `a_i`. The final answer is extracted from the solution using `<answer>` tags (e.g., `extract(y)`). Solutions are categorized as `Y+` (correct) or `Y-` (incorrect).

2. **Reflection Generation:**
 - For each solution attempt `y` from the previous step, the base model `M` is prompted to generate a reflection `r` using a specific reflection prompt (`p_reflect`).
 - **Critique and Verdict:** The reflection is expected to critique the reasoning in `y` and provide a verdict on its correctness, enclosed in `<verdict>` tags (e.g., `verdict(r)`).
 - **Filtering:** Four reflections are sampled per solution. Only reflections where the model's `verdict(r)` accurately matches the objective `correct(y, a_i)` (i.e., the reflection correctly identifies whether the solution was truly correct or incorrect) are retained. This forms a set `R` of valid solution-reflection pairs.

3. **Trace Construction:**
 - **Pair Partitioning:** The valid solution-reflection pairs are partitioned into correct (`Y+`) and incorrect (`Y-`).
 - **Sequence Assembly:** For each training trace, `n+` correct pairs and `n-` incorrect pairs are sampled. To ensure that the final trace always concludes with a successful outcome, all but one correct pair are shuffled together with the incorrect pairs. The remaining correct pair is then appended at the very end of the sequence.
 - **Formatting:** The assembled sequence is formatted using a template (Figure 3), which involves wrapping each solution-reflection pair within `<sample>` and `<reflect>` tags, respectively, and interleaving them with transition phrases (e.g., "Let me reconsider," "I should double check my calculations again"). This structure explicitly demonstrates the retry and verification behaviors.

#### 3.3. Training Stages Details

- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** To enable the model to internalize the structured cognitive patterns (sampling, reflecting, retrying) represented in the silver traces. Performance improvement at this stage is secondary.
 - **Parameters:** Trained for 2 epochs, using a context length of 4096. A learning rate of 1e-6 with cosine annealing is applied, and full fine-tuning is performed. Training is carried out with a batch size of 1 using LlamaFactory.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Algorithm:** GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) is used.
 - **Reward Signal:** Sparse binary correctness rewards (1 for correct final answer, 0 for incorrect) are employed. This encourages the model to discover effective reasoning patterns.
 - **KL Divergence:** No KL divergence penalties are used, allowing the model to deviate significantly from its initial SFT policy.
 - **Parameters:** Learning rate 1e-6, batch size 256 (minibatches of 32), trained for 150 steps. Experiments were conducted on 4 GH200 GPUs.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Models:** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Team, 2024), and Olmo-3-7B-Instruct (Olmo Team, 2025).
- **Baselines:**
 - **RL Only:** Direct RL training on the base model.
 - **BOLT:** External data curation using a strong reasoning model (Claude Sonnet 4) for ICL examples and GPT-4o-mini to generate synthetic SFT data, followed by GRPO.
 - **Distillation (R1 Distill):** SFT on reasoning traces from a more capable model (R1), then GRPO.
 - **STaR:** Self-distillation where the base model samples responses, uses correct ones for SFT, then RL.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Countdown Family:** N-arg Countdown, Letter Countdown, Acronym Generation (testing easy-to-hard generalization).
 - **Out-of-Domain (OOD) Tasks:** Multiplication, CommonsenseQA (CSQA), GSM8K (testing robustness and generalization to unrelated domains).
 - **Challenging Math/Science:** GPQA, AIME 2025, AMC, Math500 (for OpenThoughts setting).
- **Training Settings:**
 - **Countdown-3arg Focus:** SFT data generated from 4,000 Countdown-3arg rows, followed by RL on 1,000 held-out Countdown-3arg questions.
 - **OpenThoughts Dataset:** SFT data from 1,000 or 10,000 rows, followed by RL on 10,000 held-out rows. For this setting, rationalization (hints with correct answers) was included in the context for sample generation, with filtering for mentions of "hint." Max context length 4096, evaluation at 16384.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SkillFactory was conducted across various tasks and training settings, focusing on generalization, robustness, and reasoning capability.

#### 4.1. Easy-to-Hard Generalization on Countdown Tasks

- **Superior Performance on Harder Variants:** Models initialized with SkillFactory's SFT and then trained with GRPO (SkillFactory → GRPO) demonstrated consistent superior performance when generalizing from Countdown-3arg (easier variant) to harder variants (4-, 5-, 6-arguments). For instance, on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, SkillFactory → GRPO achieved 25.1% overall accuracy, outperforming the next best baseline, R1 Distill → GRPO (21.2%), by 3.9 percentage points.
- **Impact of SFT Task Performance:** Although R1 Distill showed higher accuracy post-SFT (11.7%) compared to SkillFactory (2.8%), this trend reversed after the RL stage, with SkillFactory → GRPO surpassing R1 Distill → GRPO. This suggests that the SFT stage's role in SkillFactory is not to maximize immediate task performance but to effectively prime the model for subsequent RL-driven skill acquisition.
- **Consistency Across Model Sizes:** This generalization trend was observed across Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and OLMo-3-7B models (Figure 4, left), indicating the method's robustness to different model backbones.

#### 4.2. Robustness on Out-of-Domain (OOD) Tasks

- **Competitive OOD Performance:** On OOD tasks (Acronym Generation, Letter Countdown, Multiplication, CommonsenseQA, GSM8K) not seen during training, SkillFactory → GRPO achieved an overall accuracy of 35.7% (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct), closely matching R1 Distill → GRPO (35.9%).
- **Resilience to Regression:** SkillFactory models generally maintained performance on OOD tasks, showing greater resilience to catastrophic forgetting or regression compared to some RL-only or ablated baselines. Ablation studies confirmed that the structured SFT traces were essential for this cross-domain transfer, with variants like "No Sample Order" or "No Reflections" suffering significant performance degradation on OOD tasks.
- **Skill Transfer vs. Knowledge Transfer:** While R1 Distill → GRPO often yielded strong gains on larger models, potentially due to the breadth of knowledge in the R1 traces, SkillFactory's method closed this performance gap in two of the three models tested, indicating effective skill transfer.

#### 4.3. Improved Complex Mathematical Reasoning

- **Performance on Challenging Benchmarks:** When trained on subsets of the OpenThoughts dataset, SkillFactory-initialized Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models demonstrated enhanced reasoning on challenging math and science datasets (GPQA, AIME25, AMC, Math500).
- **Competitive with Distillation:** At 10k SFT rows, SkillFactory achieved an overall score of 40.6%, approaching QwQ distillation's 42.5%. At 1k SFT rows, SkillFactory (42.1%) performed competitively and surpassed QwQ distillation on AMC (37.5%) and Math500 (64.6%), benchmarks not explicitly targeted in the OpenThoughts curation.
- **SFT Data Scale:** Interestingly, SkillFactory's performance slightly decreased from 1k to 10k SFT examples (42.1% to 40.6%), in contrast to distillation methods that typically benefit from more data. This suggests that SkillFactory's core skill learning might be efficient and saturate early, while distillation benefits from acquiring more varied strategies and knowledge from teachers as data increases.

#### 4.4. Budget Forcing Intervention

- **Enhanced Output Context Utilization:** Applying a budget-forcing intervention at inference time (allowing models to generate more tokens for reasoning attempts) further boosted SkillFactory's performance. On Countdown tasks, SkillFactory gained +5.3 points, outperforming RL-only (+1.2) and R1 distillation (+4.7).
- **Leveraging Cognitive Behaviors:** This improvement is attributed to SkillFactory's ability to effectively utilize a larger output context, a capability enabled by its baked-in cognitive behaviors (explicit reflection and retrying mechanisms). Budget forcing also helped break degenerate output loops.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Skill Usage

- **Effective Reflection:** Analysis of SkillFactory traces showed that the explicit reflection mechanism was broadly effective. The F1 scores for correctly identifying "incorrect" answers were generally above 0.8, indicating good self-correction capabilities.
- **Scaling with Difficulty:** Reflection usage scaled with task difficulty, with harder variants like Countdown-4arg exhibiting more reflection than Countdown-3arg.
- **Generalization of Reflection:** While effective for identifying incorrect answers, the F1 for correctly identifying "correct" answers was lower on some OOD tasks (e.g., Letter Countdown, Multiplication), suggesting limitations in the model's underlying task competence rather than the reflection mechanism itself.
- **Output Lengths:** SkillFactory consistently produced responses of moderate and varied lengths for both in-domain and out-of-domain tasks, resembling R1-distilled models more than the typically short or degenerate outputs of RL-only baselines (Figure 5).
- **Qualitative Observations:** Sample traces illustrated that SkillFactory models maintained explicit skill use (e.g., explicit reflection and retrying) even for OOD tasks, a behavior less consistently observed in RL-only models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SkillFactory framework introduces a method for instilling cognitive reasoning skills in language models without requiring distillation from stronger external models, which holds several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Increased Accessibility of Advanced LLM Capabilities:** By demonstrating that models can learn complex reasoning behaviors from their own generated outputs, SkillFactory reduces the reliance on proprietary or resource-intensive "teacher" models. This could democratize access to advanced LLM reasoning capabilities, enabling a wider range of researchers and practitioners to train models with sophisticated cognitive skills.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The observed improvements in easy-to-hard generalization and robustness to out-of-domain tasks suggest that SkillFactory fosters a more fundamental acquisition of reasoning principles rather than mere task-specific memorization. This could lead to the development of more versatile and reliable LLMs capable of adapting to diverse and novel problem-solving scenarios.
- **Refined Understanding of LLM Skill Acquisition:** The finding that structural SFT (priming for *how* to use skills) can lead to superior post-RL performance, even when pre-RL task accuracy is lower than content-focused distillation, challenges existing paradigms. It emphasizes the importance of teaching inductive biases related to cognitive processes (e.g., the sequence of attempts, reflections, and retries) as a precursor to reinforcement learning, potentially guiding future research in designing more effective training methodologies.
- **Foundation for Self-Improving AI:** SkillFactory represents a step towards models that can iteratively improve their reasoning capabilities through self-reflection and self-correction. By structuring a model's own outputs to explicitly model verification and retry mechanisms, it lays a groundwork for more autonomous learning systems that can diagnose and rectify their own errors.
- **Practical Utility in Complex Domains:** The demonstrated effectiveness on challenging mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., GPQA, AIME25) and compatibility with inference-time scaling methods like budget forcing suggest practical utility in domains requiring deep, multi-step reasoning. Models trained with SkillFactory could be more effective in scientific discovery, complex problem-solving, and decision-making applications.
- **Mitigation of Reasoning Flaws:** By explicitly learning when to verify and retry, SkillFactory could help address common LLM reasoning flaws such as "overthinking" (excessive, unproductive verbosity) and "underthinking" (prematurely giving up or failing to explore alternatives). This offers a pathway to more efficient and accurate reasoning.

In summary, SkillFactory contributes to the field by offering a novel, self-contained method for instilling cognitive behaviors in LLMs. Its focus on structural learning as a precursor to RL provides new insights into the mechanisms of skill acquisition in large language models, with potential for broader impact on the development of more intelligent, robust, and accessible AI systems.
