# Robot Learning with Sensorimotor Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, Berkeley developed Robot Pre-Training (RPT), a self-supervised sensorimotor pre-training model that learns general and transferable representations from real-world multimodal robot interaction data through a masked prediction task. RPT consistently outperforms training from scratch with up to a 2x increase in success rate on manipulation tasks, scales effectively, and enables robust transfer across tasks, environments, and different robot platforms.

## Problem

- Current robot learning approaches do not fully exploit the rich multimodal data (visuals, proprioception, actions) present in robotic interaction trajectories for self-supervised pre-training.
- Existing self-supervised methods often learn task-specific policies, failing to produce general sensorimotor representations transferable across diverse robot manipulation tasks.
- Developing robust models of the physical world requires foundational learning that goes beyond task-specific supervision and integrates dynamic relationships between sensory inputs and motor outputs.

## Method

- A two-stage approach involving self-supervised pre-training of a **RPT** ==Transformer==-based model on unlabeled sensorimotor trajectories, followed by ==fine-tuning== for downstream tasks.
- The **RPT** model processes interleaved sequences of ==latent visual features==, ==proprioceptive states==, and actions, encoded into tokens with ==positional embeddings==.
- Pre-training utilizes a ==masked prediction objective==, where the model predicts randomly masked sensorimotor content across all modalities and time steps using an ==MSE loss==.

## Results

- RPT consistently surpassed training from scratch, achieving up to a **2x** increase in success rate on tasks such as block stacking.
- The model demonstrated strong transferability, with significant performance improvements (e.g., **68.8%** success in cross-lab, **50.0%** in cross-robot transfer) over scratch baselines.
- Performance scaled positively with larger pre-trained vision encoders, longer sensorimotor context lengths, and increased pre-training data, while maintaining a **10 Hz** inference speed on a physical robot.
- A high masking ratio (**0.7-0.9**) and independent token masking were critical for optimal performance, and the model sometimes exhibited emergent self-correction behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning to predict masked sensorimotor content across modalities in robot trajectories can build robust models of the physical world, forming a basis for intelligent robot action.
> - Operating on latent visual representations derived from pre-trained vision encoders makes the prediction task tractable and allows for scalable, real-time inference on physical robots.
> - Generalizable sensorimotor representations can be learned directly from raw, unlabeled real-world robot interactions, reducing reliance on extensive task-specific or human-labeled data.
