# Robot Learning with Sensorimotor Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, Berkeley developed Robot Pre-Training (RPT), a self-supervised sensorimotor pre-training model that learns general and transferable representations from real-world multimodal robot interaction data through a masked prediction task. RPT consistently outperforms training from scratch with up to a 2x increase in success rate on manipulation tasks, scales effectively, and enables robust transfer across tasks, environments, and different robot platforms.

## Problem

- Current robot learning approaches do not fully exploit the rich multimodal data (visuals, proprioception, actions) present in robotic interaction trajectories for self-supervised pre-training.
- Existing self-supervised methods often learn task-specific policies, failing to produce general sensorimotor representations transferable across diverse robot manipulation tasks.
- Developing robust models of the physical world requires foundational learning that goes beyond task-specific supervision and integrates dynamic relationships between sensory inputs and motor outputs.

## Method

- A two-stage approach involving self-supervised pre-training of a **RPT** ==Transformer==-based model on unlabeled sensorimotor trajectories, followed by ==fine-tuning== for downstream tasks.
- The **RPT** model processes interleaved sequences of ==latent visual features==, ==proprioceptive states==, and actions, encoded into tokens with ==positional embeddings==.
- Pre-training utilizes a ==masked prediction objective==, where the model predicts randomly masked sensorimotor content across all modalities and time steps using an ==MSE loss==.

## Results

- RPT consistently surpassed training from scratch, achieving up to a **2x** increase in success rate (block stacking) and strong transferability (**68.8%** cross-lab, **50.0%** cross-robot success) over scratch baselines.
- Performance scaled positively with larger pre-trained vision encoders, longer sensorimotor context lengths, and increased pre-training data, while maintaining a **10 Hz** inference speed on a physical robot.
- A high masking ratio (**0.7-0.9**) and independent token masking were critical for optimal performance, and the model sometimes exhibited emergent self-correction behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning to predict masked sensorimotor content across modalities in robot trajectories can build robust models of the physical world, forming a basis for intelligent robot action.
> - Operating on latent visual representations derived from pre-trained vision encoders makes the prediction task tractable and allows for scalable, real-time inference on physical robots.
> - Generalizable sensorimotor representations can be learned directly from raw, unlabeled real-world robot interactions, reducing reliance on extensive task-specific or human-labeled data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of robot learning by exploring self-supervised pre-training, drawing inspiration from advancements in natural language processing and computer vision. While prior robotic work has utilized self-supervised visual pre-training on large image collections, the authors identify that these approaches do not fully exploit the rich multimodal information (visual, proprioceptive, actions) inherently present in robotic trajectories.

The paper aims to bridge this gap by proposing a self-supervised sensorimotor pre-training approach. Unlike existing self-supervised methods that often focus on learning policies for specific tasks (e.g., grasping), this work seeks to learn *general sensorimotor representations* that are transferable across diverse downstream tasks. Furthermore, while other initiatives involve multi-task and large models for robotics, frequently incorporating language or expert demonstrations (e.g., BC-Z, RT-1), this study differentiates itself by employing a masked objective on latent visual representations. It focuses exclusively on self-supervised learning from raw, real-world sensorimotor trajectories, without requiring additional semantic inputs. This positions the work as an effort to build foundational models for robotics by extracting comprehensive spatio-temporal knowledge directly from robot interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that existing visual-only pre-training in robotics does not fully leverage the rich, multimodal data inherent in robotic trajectories, which includes visual observations, proprioceptive states, and executed actions. The authors investigate whether effective sensorimotor representations can be learned directly from these comprehensive robot interaction sequences.

The central hypothesis is that a robot capable of predicting masked-out sensorimotor content within a sequence will acquire a robust model of the physical world, foundational for enabling intelligent action. This implies that understanding the dynamic relationships between sensory inputs and motor outputs across time leads to a deeper comprehension of environmental physics.

Based on this, the key objectives are:
1. **Develop a Self-Supervised Sensorimotor Pre-training Approach:** Design a method to learn effective sensorimotor representations from unlabeled robot trajectories without task-specific supervision.
2. **Formulate Pre-training as a General Sequence Prediction Problem:** Conceptualize robotic pre-training as a generalized masked prediction task operating across all sensorimotor modalities and time steps.
3. **Construct a Transformer-based Model (RPT):** Design a Transformer architecture that processes interleaved sequences of camera images, proprioceptive robot states, and actions by encoding them into tokens, masking a subset, and predicting the missing content.
4. **Leverage Latent Visual Representations:** Utilize pre-trained vision encoders to process high-dimensional image inputs into a more tractable latent space, enabling scalability and fast inference on physical robots.
5. **Empirically Evaluate Performance and Transferability:** Assess RPT through real-world experiments, examining its ability to:
 - Consistently outperform training from scratch on various downstream tasks.
 - Exhibit favorable scaling properties.
 - Transfer learned representations across different tasks, environments, and robot types.
 - Demonstrate the impact of specific architectural and training choices.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employs a two-stage process: self-supervised pre-training followed by fine-tuning or linear probing for downstream tasks.

#### 3.1. Sensorimotor Pre-training (RPT)

The pre-training stage utilizes a dataset of unlabeled sensorimotor trajectories, T = (images, proprioceptive states, actions). The core task is masked prediction: given a sequence of L sensorimotor tokens, a subset M is randomly masked, and the model predicts the missing content. Masks are applied independently across all modalities and time steps with a default ratio range of [0.7, 0.9].

The RPT model is an encoder-only Transformer. High-dimensional image inputs from three RGB cameras are processed by a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) from. Visual features are extracted as mean-pooled output tokens, converting images into a latent space (e.g., 768 dimensions) to make the prediction task tractable and enable faster inference. Separate linear layers project these visual features, proprioceptive states, and actions into a shared embedding dimension of 192. Positional embeddings are added to tokens. The Transformer model, with approximately 1 million parameters, processes these embedded tokens. Linear projection layers decode the hidden representations back to their original modality dimensions. The objective minimizes mean squared error (MSE) for masked predictions only, with uniform per-modality loss weights. Pre-training is conducted for 300 epochs with a batch size of 4096.

#### 3.2. Downstream Transfer

After pre-training, evaluation occurs in two settings:
- **Fine-tuning:** The entire pre-trained RPT model is fine-tuned using behavior cloning on a specific downstream task, predicting the next 16 actions from the masked action token at the last timestep.
- **Linear Probe:** The pre-trained RPT model's weights are frozen, and its features are used as input to a newly trained linear layer for action prediction.
For inference, the fine-tuned model predicts actions autoregressively at 10 Hz on a Franka robot.

#### 3.3. Data Collection

A dataset of over 20,000 real-world trajectories was collected over nine months using a 7-DoF Franka robot with a parallel jaw gripper. Data streams from three Logitech Brio RGB cameras and proprioceptive sensors (joint positions, gripper state) were synchronized at 60 Hz and saved at 30 Hz. The dataset covers four manipulation tasks: Pick (single object), Bin Pick (from a bin), Destack, and Stack, all including variations in object pose, shape, and appearance. Trajectories were generated using a combination of motion planning and model-based grasping algorithms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation, conducted via real-world robotic trials, assessed RPT's performance, transfer capabilities, scaling properties, and design choices across 16 real-world trials.

- **Sample Complexity:** RPT consistently outperformed a scratch baseline Transformer policy across Pick, Destack, and Stack tasks. For block stacking, RPT achieved up to a 2x increase in success rate.
- **Transfer Across Tasks:** RPT learned generalizable representations; pre-training on diverse tasks yielded comparable downstream performance on stacking. Data quality was important, as pre-training on data including failure cases resulted in lower performance.
- **Scaling Studies:** Performance improved with larger pre-trained vision encoders (ViT-S to ViT-L), longer sensorimotor context lengths (1 to 16 timesteps), and increased pre-training data (480 to 1920 trajectories).
- **Transfer Across Robots:**
 - **Cross-lab (Franka → Franka):** Pre-training on one Franka robot and fine-tuning on another in a different lab yielded substantial gains (68.8% success) over scratch (25.0%).
 - **Cross-robot (xArm → Franka):** Pre-training on xArm data and fine-tuning on Franka significantly outperformed scratch (50.0% vs. 25.0%), approaching performance from Franka-only pre-training (56.3%).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Linear Probe vs. Fine-tuning:** Linear probing achieved 43.75% success for picking, significantly lower than full fine-tuning (93.8%).
 - **Partial Fine-tuning:** Performance improved with more fine-tuned Transformer blocks.
 - **Masking Type:** Independent token masking outperformed time-step or modality-wide masking.
 - **Masking Ratio:** A high masking ratio ([0.7, 0.9]) was crucial, with lower ratios significantly reducing performance.
- **Inference Speed:** The model maintained a 10 Hz system-level inference speed on a 2080 Ti GPU, even with a large ViT-L vision encoder.
- **Emergent Self-Correction:** Some models exhibited self-correction behaviors at test time (e.g., re-attempting failed grasps), despite being trained solely on successful trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a notable contribution to robot learning by demonstrating the effectiveness of self-supervised sensorimotor pre-training through a masked prediction task. The RPT model establishes a paradigm for learning general and transferable representations directly from real-world, multimodal robotic interaction data.

The findings indicate that RPT consistently improves performance over training from scratch, particularly for complex tasks, and exhibits favorable scaling properties. Its robust transfer capabilities across different tasks, lab environments, and robot types suggest that RPT learns representations that generalize well beyond the specifics of its pre-training setup, which is vital for adaptable robotic systems operating in diverse settings.

The architectural choice to operate on latent visual representations enables efficient inference on physical robots, maintaining a 10 Hz control rate even with large vision models, underscoring its practical applicability. The observation of emergent self-correction behaviors, despite training only on successful trajectories, implies that RPT acquires a deeper understanding of physical world dynamics rather than merely imitating observed actions.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. It could significantly reduce the reliance on extensive, task-specific, and human-labeled datasets, thereby accelerating the development and deployment of robotic solutions. By offering a method for robots to learn comprehensive world models from their own experiences, this research advances towards more autonomous and intelligent robotic agents. It contributes to the growing trend of foundation models in robotics, providing a pathway to build generalist robots capable of performing a wide array of tasks with minimal task-specific fine-tuning.
