# Robometer: Scaling General-Purpose Robotic Reward Models via Trajectory Comparisons

> [!summary] Summary
> ROBOMETER scales general-purpose robot reward models by integrating absolute progress and relative preference supervision within a Vision-Language Model architecture. It achieved an average VOC Pearson r of 0.95 and Kendall τa of 0.66 on out-of-distribution data, significantly improving online reinforcement learning, offline learning, and data filtering applications.

## Problem

- Manually annotating dense, absolute progress labels for millions of frames in diverse, real-world robotic datasets is labor-intensive, ambiguous for suboptimal trajectories, and does not scale efficiently.
- Existing reward models struggle to generalize a consistent notion of progress across various robot embodiments, environments, and diverse trajectory qualities (successful, suboptimal, failed).
- Large quantities of valuable 'bad behavior' data (suboptimal and failed trajectories) generated in real-world robotics are underutilized due to the difficulty of precise labeling, hindering robust policy learning.

## Method

- A ==Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone== (QWEN3-VL-4B-INSTRUCT) is trained with a novel ==dual-objective approach==, combining ==absolute progress supervision== for expert trajectories with ==relative preference supervision== for trajectory comparisons.
- ==Preference supervision== leverages pairwise comparisons between trajectories (e.g., expert vs. unlabeled failure, different tasks, original vs. synthetically rewound) to learn global ordering constraints and distinguish quality differences in unlabeled data.
- The ==RBM-1M dataset== was curated, comprising over one million trajectories from 21 robot embodiments, including significant amounts of suboptimal and failed trajectories, to support scalable reward learning.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art reward evaluation, with an average VOC Pearson r of **0.95** and Kendall τa of **0.66** on out-of-distribution data, outperforming baselines like RoboReward-8B (**0.88** r, **0.47** τa).
- Enhanced online reinforcement learning, improving success rates by an average of **2.5x** over RoboReward, achieving **85%** for single-stage and **70%** for two-stage tasks, and enabling **2.4x** higher average success rates in offline RL.
- Demonstrated effective zero-shot failure detection, achieving an average F1 score of **0.81** across seven manipulation tasks on an unseen robot, and improved imitation learning policies by **4.5x** through ROBOMETER-based data filtering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of preference learning with 'bad behavior' data (suboptimal and failed trajectories) is crucial for training robust reward models that can effectively differentiate various levels of task performance.
> - Combining precise absolute progress labels on expert data with scalable relative preference labels on diverse, unlabeled data provides a powerful and generalized approach to reward function learning.
> - Leveraging a large-scale, pre-trained Vision-Language Model backbone is essential for learning generalizable multimodal reward representations across a wide array of robotic tasks and visual inputs.
