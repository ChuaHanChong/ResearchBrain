# ContactWorld: What Matters in Vision-Tactile World Models for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue and Texas A&M Universities introduced ContactWorld, a benchmark and empirical study platform, to investigate which visual and tactile representation properties are crucial for stable, long-horizon planning in contact-rich robotic manipulation. The study found that explicit spatial structure in visual data, combined with compatible tactile force-field representations, significantly improves predictive stability and planning success.

## Problem

- Existing predictive world models often struggle with rapidly accumulating prediction errors during contact transitions in contact-rich manipulation tasks, leading to instability.
- A systematic understanding was lacking regarding which specific representation properties (spatial structure, temporal consistency, contact sensitivity) are most critical for effective world modeling and robust long-horizon planning in these complex scenarios.
- Vision-only systems face challenges like partial observability and occlusion during close-contact interactions, and the optimal integration of multimodal vision-tactile sensing for cross-modal compatibility was not well-understood.

## Method

- The authors introduced "**ContactWorld**," a benchmark and empirical study platform featuring 12 diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks (e.g., insertion, disassembly, screwing, exploration).
- They designed a planning-oriented latent world model framework based on the ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)==, trained with various synchronized visual and tactile observation modalities.
- The study systematically evaluated different representation structures based on their spatiality, continuity, and contact sensitivity, using receding-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) with CEM for long-horizon planning.

## Results

- Point-cloud visual observations consistently improved average planning success rates to **32.1%** (from **20.7%** with wrist-view images) across all tasks, demonstrating greater robustness across varying planning horizons (12 to 48 steps).
- Combining point-cloud observations with tactile force-field (TacFF) representations yielded the strongest overall planning performance, achieving an average success rate of **36.1%** compared to **32.1%** for point-cloud-only models.
- Tactile sensing provided progressively larger gains in planning success as goal offsets increased, with PointCloud+TacFF models improving success rates from **16.0%** (point-cloud only) to **20.5%** at the 48-step offset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representations that preserve explicit spatial structure (e.g., point clouds) and stable interaction continuity are fundamental for robust contact-rich manipulation, leading to more stable geometric predictions during latent rollouts.
> - The effectiveness of tactile sensing is critically dependent on its cross-modal compatibility with visual representations; tactile force-fields (TacFF) complement point-cloud visuals best by providing compatible interaction dynamics.
> - Long-horizon planning explicitly amplifies the importance of stable predictive representations and complementary multimodal information, as accumulated prediction errors necessitate robust feature encoding over extended timeframes.
