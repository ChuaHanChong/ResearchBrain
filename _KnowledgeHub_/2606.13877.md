# ContactWorld: What Matters in Vision-Tactile World Models for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue and Texas A&M Universities introduced ContactWorld, a benchmark and empirical study platform, to investigate which visual and tactile representation properties are crucial for stable, long-horizon planning in contact-rich robotic manipulation. The study found that explicit spatial structure in visual data, combined with compatible tactile force-field representations, significantly improves predictive stability and planning success.

## Problem

- Existing predictive world models often struggle with rapidly accumulating prediction errors during contact transitions in contact-rich manipulation tasks, leading to instability.
- A systematic understanding was lacking regarding which specific representation properties (spatial structure, temporal consistency, contact sensitivity) are most critical for effective world modeling and robust long-horizon planning in these complex scenarios.
- Vision-only systems face challenges like partial observability and occlusion during close-contact interactions, and the optimal integration of multimodal vision-tactile sensing for cross-modal compatibility was not well-understood.

## Method

*The JEPA-based framework. (A) Training involves encoding multimodal observations into a latent space and training a predictor P θ to model dynamics. (B) During planning, the model performs autoregressive rollouts to find the best action sequence.*

- The authors introduced "**ContactWorld**," a benchmark and empirical study platform featuring 12 diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks (e.g., insertion, disassembly, screwing, exploration).
- They designed a planning-oriented latent world model framework based on the ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)==, trained with various synchronized visual and tactile observation modalities.
- The study systematically evaluated different representation structures based on their spatiality, continuity, and contact sensitivity, using receding-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) with CEM for long-horizon planning.

## Results

- Point-cloud visual observations consistently improved average planning success rates to **32.1%** (from **20.7%** with wrist-view images) across all tasks, demonstrating greater robustness across varying planning horizons (12 to 48 steps).
- Combining point-cloud observations with tactile force-field (TacFF) representations yielded the strongest overall planning performance, achieving an average success rate of **36.1%** compared to **32.1%** for point-cloud-only models.
- Tactile sensing provided progressively larger gains in planning success as goal offsets increased, with PointCloud+TacFF models improving success rates from **16.0%** (point-cloud only) to **20.5%** at the 48-step offset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representations that preserve explicit spatial structure (e.g., point clouds) and stable interaction continuity are fundamental for robust contact-rich manipulation, leading to more stable geometric predictions during latent rollouts.
> - The effectiveness of tactile sensing is critically dependent on its cross-modal compatibility with visual representations; tactile force-fields (TacFF) complement point-cloud visuals best by providing compatible interaction dynamics.
> - Long-horizon planning explicitly amplifies the importance of stable predictive representations and complementary multimodal information, as accumulated prediction errors necessitate robust feature encoding over extended timeframes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Contact-rich manipulation, which involves sustained physical interaction between a robot and its environment, presents a significant challenge in robot learning. Unlike vision-dominant tasks, contact-rich scenarios necessitate reasoning over complex factors such as constrained geometry, localized contact events, frictional forces, partial observability, and temporally evolving multimodal sensory feedback. Existing predictive world models, while advanced in learning compact latent dynamics for future prediction and planning, often struggle with the rapid accumulation of prediction errors during contact transitions inherent in these tasks.

Recent developments in latent world models, particularly those based on Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), offer a scalable framework for robotic interaction modeling by focusing on learning action-conditioned dynamics in latent space rather than pixel-level reconstruction. Concurrently, tactile sensing has emerged as a crucial modality, providing information about local geometry, contact, and forces that is often unavailable or difficult to infer from vision alone. These advancements motivate the integration of multimodal vision-tactile sensing into world models to enhance robotic manipulation capabilities.

Despite progress in both world models and tactile sensing, a systematic understanding of which specific representation properties are most critical for effective world modeling in contact-rich manipulation has remained less explored. Prior research has frequently emphasized architectural improvements, policy optimization, or benchmark development, without a focused examination of the fundamental role of representation structure itself. This paper addresses this gap by conducting an empirical study to identify the representation properties that support stable, long-horizon planning in contact-rich environments. It aims to inform the design of future vision-tactile world models by clarifying the importance of spatial structure, temporal continuity, and cross-modal compatibility.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate which representation properties are fundamental for supporting stable, long-horizon planning in contact-rich manipulation settings. To achieve this, the authors introduce "ContactWorld," a benchmark and empirical study platform for vision-tactile world models.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and knowledge gaps in current robotic manipulation research:

1. **Complexity of Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Contact-rich tasks involve intricate physical interactions, where small prediction errors can quickly compound, leading to instability. Robots must interpret constrained geometry, frictional forces, and dynamic contact events from multimodal sensory inputs. Existing world models, often developed for less contact-intensive scenarios, may not adequately capture these nuances.
2. **Unclear Role of Representation Properties:** While world models and tactile sensors have advanced, it is not definitively understood which specific characteristics of visual and tactile representations (e.g., explicit spatial structure, temporal consistency, contact sensitivity) are most crucial for robust predictive modeling and planning in these complex scenarios. Prior work often focuses on overall system performance or novel architectures, without dissecting the impact of individual representation properties.
3. **Limitations of Vision-Only Systems:** Visual sensors can suffer from partial observability and occlusion during close-contact interactions, losing critical information about the local contact state. Tactile sensing offers complementary information, but its optimal integration with various visual modalities is not well-understood, particularly concerning "cross-modal representation compatibility."
4. **Challenges of Long-Horizon Planning:** Long-horizon tasks exacerbate prediction errors and uncertainty accumulation in world models. Understanding how different representations maintain predictive stability over extended timeframes is critical for enabling robots to perform prolonged and sequential contact operations.
5. **Need for a Standardized Benchmark:** A systematic evaluation framework is required to rigorously compare different representation choices and multimodal fusion strategies under diverse contact-rich scenarios. ContactWorld aims to fill this need by providing a unified set of tasks and evaluation protocols.

By addressing these points, the research seeks to provide empirical insights into the foundational requirements for effective vision-tactile world models, guiding the design of more capable and robust robotic manipulation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the ContactWorld benchmark and employs a systematic empirical study to evaluate vision-tactile world models. The methodology encompasses the design of contact-rich tasks, the selection of diverse sensory modalities, a planning-oriented latent world model framework, and a standardized evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. ContactWorld Benchmark Design

The benchmark consists of 12 contact-rich manipulation tasks categorized into four types:
- **Insertion:** (USB, Peg, Power Plug) Require precise geometric alignment and sustained contact under constraints, demanding accurate spatial reasoning and handling partial observability.
- **Disassembly:** (Spike Barb, Flat Barb, Loose Lid) Focus on object separation under frictional and constrained contacts, involving complex contact transitions and interaction asymmetry.
- **Screwing:** (Nut, Bulb, Valve) Involve continuous rotational interaction and long-horizon consistency, requiring stable contact dynamics over extended sequences.
- **Exploration:** (Blind Box, Normal Sorting, Dim Sorting) Emphasize active perception through physical interaction to infer properties or states difficult to discern visually, highlighting the role of tactile sensing.

All tasks are built on TacSL, a simulation framework within Isaac Gym, providing synchronized visual, tactile, and proprioceptive observations. Datasets of expert demonstrations are collected for training and evaluation. A unified control setup is used across tasks, employing relative end-effector pose control at 10 Hz.

#### 3.2. Sensory Modalities and Representation Structure

The study investigates various synchronized visual and tactile observation modalities, characterized by three properties: Spatiality (preserving structured spatial geometry), Continuity (providing temporally consistent interaction cues), and Contact Sensitivity (responding directly to physical interaction).

- **Visual Modalities:**
 - **Wrist-view RGB Images:** Local, close-range observations, prone to occlusion during close-contact interaction, potentially leading to discontinuous visual cues.
 - **Front-view RGB Images:** Global view, maintaining more consistent observability of the scene and interaction progress, less affected by local occlusion.
 - **Point-Cloud (XYZRGB):** Explicitly preserves 3D spatial structure and object-level geometric relationships, reconstructed from front-view RGB-D observations. Offers high spatiality and global continuity.

- **Tactile Modalities (from vision-based tactile sensors):**
 - **Tactile RGB Images (TacRGB):** Captures local tactile appearance and contact texture information.
 - **Tactile Depth Maps (TacDepth):** Represents local contact geometry through surface deformation measurements.
 - **Tactile Force Fields (TacFF):** Represents dense spatial force responses (normal fz, tangential fx, fy) over the tactile surface, capturing richer contact-sensitive interaction dynamics and temporal evolution.

#### 3.3. World Model Learning and Planning Evaluation

The research adopts a planning-oriented latent world model framework based on the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm.

- **Training:**
 - **Architecture:** Multimodal observations are processed by lightweight, modality-specific encoders (IMPALA-style CNNs for images, PointNet-style for point clouds, tokenized MLP for TacFF). These encoders are trained end-to-end from scratch by default.
 - **Fusion:** Visual and tactile embeddings are fused, typically via latent concatenation. The default strategy applies representation regularization only to visual latent representations *before* multimodal fusion to prevent degradation of planning performance, particularly with tactile regularization.
 - **Predictor:** A GRU-based recurrent latent dynamics model performs autoregressive future prediction in embedding space, modeling `z_t+1 = P_theta(z_t, a_t)`.
 - **Loss Functions:** The primary objective is minimizing latent prediction error (`L_pred`). Auxiliary regularization losses are also used:
 - **Variance-Covariance (VC) Regularization:** (default) Encourages batch-wise variation and reduces redundancy across latent channels.
 - **Temporal Similarity Regularization (`L_sim`):** Encourages smooth latent evolution between adjacent timesteps.
 - **Inverse Dynamics Modeling (IDM) (`L_idm`):** Predicts the executed action from consecutive latent states.

- **Planning and Evaluation:**
 - **Control Strategy:** Receding-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) optimized with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM).
 - **Process:** At each timestep, the current observation is encoded to a latent state. The CEM planner samples candidate action sequences, and the learned world model performs autoregressive latent rollouts. Planning costs are computed as the latent-space distance between the predicted future latent state and the encoded goal observation. The first optimized action is executed, and the process replans.
 - **Horizons:** The study differentiates between `rollout horizon` (number of prediction steps by the world model) and `goal-offset horizon` (temporal distance between current and target goal observation during planning).
 - **Success Metric:** An episode is deemed successful if both object-level and end-effector pose errors (position and orientation) fall below predefined geometric thresholds (e.g., 0.01m position error, 15 degrees orientation error).
 - **Evaluation:** 100 evaluation trials are run for each modality combination and goal-offset setting, with goal-offset horizons varying from 12 to 48 steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical study revealed three consistent findings regarding the influence of representation structure on predictive planning performance in contact-rich world models.

#### 4.1. Structured and Continuous Representations Improve Contact-Rich Planning

Planning performance consistently improved with visual representations that preserved stronger spatial structure and more stable interaction continuity.
- **Quantitative Results:** Averaged across all 12 tasks, wrist-view observations achieved an average success rate of 20.7%. Front-view observations improved this to 22.0%, while point-cloud observations further enhanced performance to 32.1%.
- **Explanation:** Wrist-view observations frequently suffered from target occlusion during contact, leading to discontinuous visual cues. Front-view observations maintained a more globally consistent view. Point-cloud observations, by explicitly encoding 3D geometric relationships, provided a stronger inductive bias for latent rollout prediction throughout alignment, insertion, and object separation processes, leading to improved predictive stability.
- **Long-Horizon Robustness:** Point-cloud observations consistently outperformed image-based observations across all tested goal offsets (12 to 48 steps). For instance, PointCloud-only models achieved success rates of 52.1%, 36.6%, 23.7%, and 16.0% for 12, 24, 36, and 48-step offsets, respectively, demonstrating greater robustness to increasing planning horizons. This suggests that spatial structure enhances predictive stability, and temporal continuity becomes more critical as rollout errors accumulate.

#### 4.2. Tactile Effectiveness Depends on Representation Structure and Cross-Modal Compatibility

Tactile sensing was found to be beneficial, but its effectiveness was strongly dependent on compatibility with the visual representation.
- **Quantitative Results:** For image-based visual observations (wrist-view and front-view), tactile sensing generally improved performance. For example, wrist-view models improved from 20.7% to 24.3% with TacDepth. However, for point-cloud world models, image-like tactile representations (TacRGB, TacDepth) often resulted in a slight reduction or no significant improvement in performance. In contrast, combining point-cloud observations with **tactile force-field (TacFF)** representations yielded the strongest overall performance, improving from 32.1% to 36.1%.
- **Explanation:** TacDepth and TacRGB, providing local appearance and deformation cues, often showed limited variation across interaction phases. TacFF, however, exhibited clear phase-dependent force responses, offering complementary interaction dynamics that were compatible with the explicit spatial geometry provided by point-clouds. Task-level analysis indicated that TacFF provided more consistent improvements in insertion and disassembly tasks, which rely heavily on evolving contact dynamics, whereas TacDepth sometimes performed better in screwing and exploration tasks where local geometric deformation is more informative. This highlights that specific tactile information (e.g., force fields) can better align with certain visual structures (e.g., point clouds) to provide a more stable multimodal representation.

#### 4.3. Long-Horizon Planning Reveals Representation Quality

The difficulty of long-horizon planning, characterized by increased goal offsets, amplified the importance of stable predictive representations and complementary tactile information.
- **Accumulated Errors:** All modality combinations experienced significant performance degradation as goal offsets increased (e.g., wrist-view-only dropped from 41.4% at 12 steps to 8.2% at 48 steps), reflecting the challenge of maintaining stable latent predictions over prolonged contact-rich interactions.
- **Robustness of Structured Representations:** Point-cloud world models remained the most robust across all goal offsets, maintaining a consistent advantage over image-based observations. This suggests that spatially structured representations contribute to more stable geometric predictions during extended latent rollouts.
- **Amplified Tactile Gains:** Tactile sensing provided progressively larger gains as goal offsets increased, particularly for the PointCloud+TacFF combination. For example, PointCloud+TacFF improved success rates from 52.1% to 54.4% at 12 steps and from 16.0% to 20.5% at 48 steps when compared to point-cloud-only planning. This indicates that tactile information helps improve temporal consistency in latent rollout, which becomes increasingly valuable as planning objectives extend further into the future.

#### 4.4. Additional Ablation Studies (Appendix Findings)

- **Visual Encoder Ablation:** End-to-end trained IMPALA convolutional encoders generally outperformed frozen DINOv2 features and scratch-trained ViT for image-based observations. This suggests that features learned specifically for action-conditioned dynamics prediction are more aligned with contact-rich world modeling than generic semantic features.
- **Tactile Encoder Ablation:** For point-cloud visual inputs, task-specific IMPALA tactile encoders outperformed strong pretrained tactile encoders (AnyTouch 2, Sparsh). For image-based visual inputs, pretrained tactile encoders sometimes offered slight improvements. This reinforces the importance of cross-modal compatibility, where tactile representations aligned with the visual modality's structure are more effective.
- **Regularization Ablation:** Variance-Covariance (VC) regularization consistently outperformed Sketch Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) across multimodal settings, indicating that the choice of latent regularization strategy affects the stability of predictive world models for contact-rich manipulation.
- **Cross-Modal Fusion Ablation:** Simple late concatenation with *vision-only regularization* (i.e., regularizing only the visual latent before fusion, leaving tactile unregularized) generally achieved the strongest performance. This suggests that the placement of regularization is more critical than the architectural complexity of fusion, as directly regularizing highly local and dynamic tactile features might suppress important interaction details.
- **Predictor Architecture Study:** An action-conditioned ViT predictor exhibited substantially lower planning success rates and higher computational overhead compared to the GRU-based recurrent predictor under evaluated settings.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments

- **Valve-Screwing Task:** Real-world experiments on a valve-screwing task using a Franka Emika Panda robot largely corroborated the simulation findings regarding the strength of point-cloud observations, which achieved a 90% success rate without tactile sensing.
- **Tactile Discrepancies:** The effectiveness of tactile modalities varied. TacRGB improved performance for wrist-view (70% to 90%) and front-view (70% to 80%) observations. However, the pronounced advantage of reconstructed TacDepth and TacFF observed in simulation did not fully transfer to the real world, likely due to real-world sensing noise, reconstruction errors, and sim-to-real differences in tactile signal processing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically investigating the critical representation properties for vision-tactile world models in contact-rich settings. The introduction of ContactWorld provides a standardized benchmark for rigorous evaluation and comparison, facilitating future research.

The primary significance of this work lies in its empirical insights, which challenge existing assumptions and guide future development:
1. **Emphasis on Representation Structure:** The findings demonstrate that representations preserving explicit spatial geometry and stable temporal continuity are fundamental for robust contact-rich manipulation. This suggests a shift in focus from solely increasing model complexity or data volume to prioritizing representation quality and structure during sensing and encoding.
2. **Cross-Modal Compatibility:** The study highlights that the effectiveness of multimodal sensing, particularly tactile, is not universal but depends crucially on its compatibility with the visual modality. This implies that simply adding more modalities or using stronger individual encoders is insufficient; the representations must be designed to complement each other effectively, preserving distinct yet compatible information channels (e.g., point clouds for geometry, force fields for interaction dynamics).
3. **Long-Horizon Robustness:** The amplified benefits of structured representations and compatible tactile sensing under long-horizon planning objectives underscore their importance for real-world tasks that require sustained interaction. This provides a clear direction for developing world models capable of complex, sequential manipulation.
4. **Guidance for World Model Design:** The ablation studies regarding encoder choices, regularization strategies, and fusion methods offer practical guidance for architects of future world models. The preference for end-to-end trained, task-relevant features over generic semantic features, and the efficacy of modality-specific regularization, suggest that specialized design choices are crucial for contact-rich environments.
5. **Benchmarking and Reproducibility:** By publicly releasing the benchmark environments, datasets, trained models, and evaluation code, ContactWorld directly supports community-wide efforts to advance vision-tactile world modeling, enabling reproducible research and systematic comparison of new methods.

The potential impact of this work includes the development of more robust, intelligent, and capable robotic systems that can reliably perform complex contact-rich tasks in unstructured environments, such as assembly, maintenance, and dexterous manipulation. While the study identifies limitations, such as the single-stage nature of most tasks and the persistent challenge of long-horizon prediction errors, it lays a groundwork for future research into compositional multi-stage reasoning and more stable multimodal representations under real-world noise and uncertainty.
