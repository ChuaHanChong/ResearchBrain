# COSMIK-MPPI: Scaling Constrained Model Predictive Control to Collision Avoidance in Close-Proximity Dynamic Human Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> COSMIK-MPPI, a constrained model predictive control framework, offers robust collision avoidance for robotic manipulators in dynamic human environments by integrating Model Predictive Path Integral with "Constraints as Terminations" and real-time markerless human pose estimation. This approach maintains real-time performance, with average computation times of 21-22 ms, and ensures safety by maintaining positive minimum distances to obstacles even in challenging, non-convex scenarios.

## Problem

- Ensuring safe, real-time collision avoidance for robotic manipulators in dynamic human environments with non-linear dynamics, sensing uncertainty, and unpredictable human motion.
- Gradient-Based Model Predictive Control (GB-MPC) struggles with computational scalability and local minima due to non-convex collision constraints in complex, dynamic workspaces.
- Standard sampling-based Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) uses additive penalty terms for safety, which do not guarantee constraint satisfaction and can introduce tuning complexities or numerical instability.

## Method

- A novel **COSMIK-==MPPI==** framework integrates Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) with a "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT) mechanism for principled safety enforcement.
- CaT treats constraint violations as terminal events, reducing the future utility of unsafe trajectories without relying on additive penalties or explicit human motion prediction.
- Real-time markerless human motion is estimated using RT-COSMIK, providing robust 3D human pose and shape data, which is approximated by convex primitives for collision detection.

## Results

- COSMIK-MPPI maintained real-time computation (average 21-22 ms) across all tested scenarios, unlike GB-MPC which experienced severe performance degradation (up to 1812 ms) with increasing complexity.
- It achieved **100%** task success rates in complex, non-convex simulated environments where GB-MPC failed, while maintaining positive minimum distances to obstacles (e.g., **5.28** cm in infeasible scenarios, compared to -**0.91** cm for vanilla MPPI).
- Real-world experiments using markerless human pose estimation confirmed robust, compliant, and safe robot behavior in dynamic human interaction, adapting motions and reducing speed to ensure collision-free operation in close proximity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating "Constraints as Terminations" into sampling-based MPPI provides a principled, robust method for enforcing hard safety constraints, outperforming penalty-based approaches.
> - Sampling-based predictive control maintains consistent real-time performance (21-22 ms) even as geometric complexity and collision constraints increase, a critical advantage over gradient-based methods.
> - Effective collision avoidance in dynamic human-robot interaction can be achieved without explicit human motion prediction, by combining robust real-time markerless pose estimation and continuous replanning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of robotic manipulators in human-shared environments necessitates robust and safe physical interaction, particularly for torque-controlled systems. This work addresses the ongoing challenge of trajectory planning and safe control in such dynamic settings, which are characterized by nonlinear robot dynamics, sensing uncertainty, limited reaction times, and unpredictable human motion.

Current approaches to collision avoidance for articulated manipulators broadly fall into two categories: reactive and predictive methods. Reactive methods, such as damped least-squares velocity filters or potential-field strategies, offer computational efficiency but suffer from myopia, potentially leading to oscillations, overly conservative behaviors in constrained spaces, or instability in dynamic scenarios due to their lack of explicit reasoning over a planning horizon.

Predictive control methods, specifically Model Predictive Control (MPC), provide an alternative by enabling horizon-based anticipation and systematic trade-offs between task execution, dynamics, and safety. In torque-controlled systems, MPC formulations can manage collision avoidance directly through actuator constraints. However, Gradient-Based MPC (GB-MPC) typically relies on second-order optimization solvers, which encounter several limitations. These include sensitivity to initialization, convergence to suboptimal local minima due to the non-convexity of collision constraints, and computational burdens that can hinder real-time performance, particularly when many constraints are active simultaneously. Furthermore, GB-MPC often assumes accurate estimation and prediction of obstacle motion, which is frequently unrealistic in collaborative human-robot interaction (HRI) scenarios given noisy sensing, partial observability, and non-stationary human behavior. Markerless human pose estimation, despite recent advances, compounds these issues by introducing additional noise and latency, forcing a trade-off between conservative safety margins and potentially unsafe optimistic predictions.

Sampling-based predictive control methods, such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, offer a different paradigm by evaluating numerous control perturbations through forward rollouts. This approach naturally handles non-smooth objective functions and can explore multiple candidate futures in parallel, which reduces sensitivity to local minima induced by collision constraints. MPPI can also incorporate uncertainty in human pose estimation by sampling obstacle configurations from noisy measurements. However, conventional MPPI formulations typically manage safety constraints via additive penalty terms in the running costs. This approach is inherently fragile as finite penalties do not guarantee constraint satisfaction, while large penalties introduce tuning complexities and numerical instability. Moreover, early termination of violating rollouts (often used to reduce computation) can inadvertently reduce accumulated cost, potentially incentivizing unsafe behaviors.

This research integrates the "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT) transcription, a concept previously explored in reinforcement learning and in constraint-discounted MPPI (TD-CD-MPPI), into an MPPI framework. By combining MPPI with CaT and a real-time markerless human motion estimator (RT-COSMIK), this work aims to mitigate the fragility of penalty-based MPPI and the scalability/sensitivity issues of GB-MPC, providing a robust and practical solution for collision avoidance in complex, dynamic human-shared environments without relying on explicit human motion prediction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a robust and compliant collision avoidance framework for torque-controlled robotic manipulators operating in dynamic human-shared workspaces. This framework, termed COSMIK-MPPI, aims to ensure safe physical interaction despite the inherent challenges of noisy perception and unpredictable human movement.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations observed in existing predictive control methods for human-robot interaction:

- **Computational Scalability and Robustness of Gradient-Based MPC (GB-MPC):** GB-MPC, while offering strong guarantees, exhibits significant performance degradation in complex environments. The non-convex nature of collision constraints often leads to multiple local minima, making GB-MPC solvers highly sensitive to initialization and computationally intensive. This can result in failure to meet real-time constraints, particularly when dealing with the many simultaneous collision constraints typical in multi-object environments. The inability to efficiently handle numerous, potentially saturating constraints limits its practical applicability in densely occupied or highly dynamic workspaces.
- **Reliance on Accurate Human Motion Prediction:** Both GB-MPC and some predictive approaches assume access to accurate present and future obstacle trajectories. In real-world collaborative scenarios, this assumption is often violated due to noisy sensing, partial observability, and the non-stationary, hard-to-predict nature of human behavior. The increasing use of markerless human pose estimation, despite its advancements (e.g., Neural Localizer Fields), introduces further noise and latency, which directly propagates into collision constraints. This necessitates a trade-off between overly conservative safety margins and optimistic predictions that may compromise safety.
- **Limitations of Penalty-Based Safety in Sampling-Based MPPI:** While sampling-based methods like MPPI offer advantages in exploring non-convex spaces and handling non-smooth objectives, their common practice of enforcing safety through additive penalty terms presents significant drawbacks. Finite penalties cannot reliably prevent constraint violations, potentially allowing unsafe behaviors. Large penalty terms are difficult to tune effectively and can introduce numerical instability. Additionally, a common optimization to reduce computation involves early termination of violating rollouts, which can artificially lower their accumulated cost and inadvertently encourage unsafe trajectories by making them appear more favorable.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines several key objectives:

1. **Develop a novel COSMIK-MPPI controller:** This involves integrating the Constraints-as-Terminations (CaT) transcription into the MPPI framework to provide a principled mechanism for enforcing safety. CaT treats constraint violations as terminal events, progressively diminishing the future utility of unsafe trajectories without relying on explicit penalty terms or human motion prediction.
2. **Conduct a systematic comparative analysis:** Evaluate COSMIK-MPPI against state-of-the-art GB-MPC and vanilla MPPI formulations. This comparison aims to quantify robustness to local minima, sensitivity to human motion uncertainty, trajectory compliance, and real-time feasibility in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulator tasks.
3. **Demonstrate real-world human-robot interaction capabilities:** Showcasing the system's ability to operate with noisy, markerless human pose estimates in shared workspaces, providing a practical, robust, and compliant solution for predictive collision avoidance. This includes validating the approach in scenarios with dynamic human presence and highly constrained interactions.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to overcome significant barriers to the safe and effective deployment of robots in environments shared with humans.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed COSMIK-MPPI framework integrates Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control with a "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT) mechanism for safety, operating in conjunction with a real-time markerless human motion estimation system.

**A. Real-time Human Motion Estimation:**
Human motion is estimated using RT-COSMIK, a markerless solution based on two RGB cameras. The system employs Neural Localizer Fields (NLF) for continuous 3D human pose and shape estimation, which avoids the fragility of explicit keypoint detection and is robust to occlusions. The RT-COSMIK pipeline involves three steps:
1. **Human Pose Estimation:** Utilizes a GPU TensorRT YOLO model for human detection and an NLF model to estimate a dense set of 2D body keypoint positions from each camera frame.
2. **Triangulation and Filtering:** Converts the 2D keypoints into 3D using direct linear transform and calibrated camera parameters.
3. **Inverse Kinematics:** A full-body biomechanical model is used to recover the vector of joint angles that best matches the virtual markers, solving a constrained quadratic programming problem.
This pipeline operates at 20Hz, providing real-time human pose estimates robust to noise and partial occlusions.

**B. Collision Avoidance Representation:**
For real-time collision detection, both the robot and the human body, as well as the static environment, are approximated as collections of strictly convex primitives, predominantly capsules. Capsules are chosen for their tight and smooth approximation of articulated limbs and their ability to facilitate closed-form distance computations between pairs. The system considers 11 capsules for the robot, 5 for the scene, and 8 for the human, resulting in 24 collision pairs. Signed distance functions (`d_sgn_r`) are computed, where negative values indicate free space and positive values denote overlap.

**C. COSMIK–Model Predictive Path Integral (Control Framework):**
COSMIK-MPPI builds upon the sampling-based MPPI framework but replaces traditional additive collision penalties with the CaT transcription.
- **MPPI Fundamentals:** The controller maintains a Gaussian distribution over control sequences (joint torques). At each control cycle, K control sequences are sampled and rolled out through an approximate dynamics model of the manipulator. The finite-horizon costs of these rollouts are evaluated, and the sampling distribution's mean is updated towards lower-cost trajectories using importance weights. This process occurs in a receding-horizon manner.
- **Cost Function:** The primary task-related costs include:
 - Goal reaching task cost (`ℓ_ee`): Minimizes end-effector pose error relative to a target.
 - State regularization cost (`ℓ_x`): Penalizes deviations from an initial state.
 - Control regularization cost (`ℓ_u`): Penalizes control inputs (joint torques) relative to gravity compensation.
- **Constraints as Terminations (CaT):** Instead of an additive penalty, collision avoidance is handled by treating violations as terminal events.
 - A dense, non-negative *violation magnitude* (`v_c_t,k`) is defined using the collision cost `ℓ_coll`, which is based on `max(d_sgn_r + d_th, 0)`, where `d_th` is a safety margin.
 - This violation magnitude is then mapped to a per-stage *termination hazard* (`δ_c_t,k`), a probability of the rollout terminating due to constraint violation. This hazard is adaptively normalized by `c_c_max`, an exponential moving average of the maximum observed violation, making it robust to the scale of current violations.
 - A *rollout survival factor* (`S_t,k`) is calculated as the cumulative product of `(1 - δ_c_m,k)` over the horizon, representing the probability that a rollout has remained collision-safe up to stage `t`.
 - Task costs (`ℓ_task`) are converted into positive *rewards* (`r_t,k`) by subtracting them from adaptively updated baselines (`b`, `b_T`).
 - The trajectory score is then defined as a *pseudo-cost* (`L_CaT_k`), which is the negative sum of discounted, survival-factor-weighted rewards. Rollouts that approach collisions will have a lower `S_t,k`, leading to a higher (less negative) pseudo-cost, thereby reducing their importance weights and effectively penalizing unsafe trajectories.
- **Low-level Controller:** The MPPI control input `u_t` is interpreted as a desired joint acceleration. The first element of the optimized control sequence is applied. Command joint torques (`τ`) are computed using a feedforward inverse-dynamics torque (from the Pinocchio library) and fixed state-feedback (PD) gains, acting on the tracking error between the commanded reference state and the measured state, thereby providing intrinsic compliance.

**D. Comparison Baselines:**
- **Gradient-Based MPC (GB-MPC):** An Optimal Control Problem (OCP) identical to that described in is solved using the Crocoddyl library and the constrained Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) solver from the mim solver library. This GB-MPC formulation uses explicit inequality constraints for collision avoidance. A low-level impedance controller is formed using Riccati gains for interpolation between solver iterations.
- **Vanilla Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI):** The STORM formulation of MPPI is used, which performs receding-horizon optimization in joint space. Safety constraints are handled through an additive penalty term (`ℓ_coll`) in the running costs. The low-level control is similar to COSMIK-MPPI, using fixed PD gains with feedforward inverse-dynamics torques.

**E. Simulation and Experimental Setup:**
- **Simulation:** Six scenarios of increasing geometric complexity were used, including a baseline, static obstacles, non-convex arrangements, dense obstacles, and an intentionally infeasible scenario. Performance metrics included task success rate, distance to target, distance to obstacles, trajectory smoothness (integrated squared joint torque variation), and average computation time. The evaluation used a prediction horizon of 50 steps at 50Hz, with 10 SQP iterations for GB-MPC and 1000 rollouts for MPPI variants.
- **Experimental Validation:** Five real-world pick-and-place experiments were conducted with a robotic manipulator, assessing safety, robustness, and online replanning capabilities in human-robot interaction. Scenarios included a static environment, physical perturbations, human-aware collision avoidance (human blocking), concurrent human activity, and highly constrained interactions. Qualitative behavior, execution time, and motion characteristics (e.g., minimum collision distance, Cartesian end-effector velocity) were analyzed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed simulation and experimental results to evaluate COSMIK-MPPI against state-of-the-art GB-MPC and vanilla MPPI.

**A. Simulation Benchmark:**

- **Computational Performance and Scalability:**
 - Both vanilla MPPI and COSMIK-MPPI demonstrated consistent computation times, averaging 21-22 ms across all scenarios, including those with high geometric complexity and numerous collision constraints. This illustrates a key advantage of sampling-based methods in maintaining real-time performance independent of constraint complexity.
 - GB-MPC's computation time significantly increased with scenario complexity. It took 15 ms in the baseline scenario but rose to 81-89 ms in scenarios with a few obstacles and reached 415 ms and 1812 ms in non-convex and densely constrained scenarios (Scenarios 4 and 5, respectively), indicating real-time performance degradation and eventual failure.
- **Task Success Rate:**
 - In lightly constrained environments (Scenarios 1-3), all controllers achieved a 100% success rate.
 - In complex, non-convex scenarios (Scenarios 4 and 5), GB-MPC failed to find feasible solutions within real-time limits, resulting in a 0% success rate. In contrast, both vanilla MPPI and COSMIK-MPPI maintained a 100% success rate.
 - In the intentionally infeasible Scenario 6, none of the methods reached the target, as expected.
- **Collision Avoidance:**
 - In Scenario 6 (infeasible), vanilla MPPI exhibited collision violations, with an average minimum distance of -0.91 cm. This indicates that its additive penalty approach was insufficient to guarantee safety under persistent constraint violation.
 - COSMIK-MPPI, in the same infeasible scenario, maintained a positive minimum distance of 5.28 cm, demonstrating its robustness and prioritization of collision avoidance. In other complex scenarios, COSMIK-MPPI generally achieved comparable or improved distances to obstacles relative to vanilla MPPI.
- **Trajectory Smoothness:**
 - GB-MPC generally produced smoother motions in scenarios where it successfully operated (Scenarios 1-3).
 - MPPI-based methods exhibited comparatively lower smoothness due to their stochastic nature and reliance on fixed PD gains for low-level control, but their smoothness levels were consistent between vanilla and COSMIK-MPPI variants.
- **Goal Accuracy:** When successful, all methods achieved low distances to the target.

**B. Experimental Validation:**

- **Overall Behavior:** COSMIK-MPPI consistently demonstrated safe, robust, and compliant behavior in real-world dynamic human-robot interaction tasks. A consistent observation was the robot's tendency to trade execution speed for safety as environmental and interaction complexity increased.
- **Static Environment (Baseline):** The task was completed in 6s with a smooth trajectory, serving as a reference.
- **Physical Perturbations and Compliance:** When physically perturbed with a rigid stick, the robot exhibited compliant behavior, briefly slowing down and smoothly recovering while replanning to complete the task. Execution time increased slightly to 7.5s.
- **Concurrent Human Activity:** In a scenario where a human performed an unconstrained task in the shared workspace, the robot successfully adapted its motion through frequent replanning. Execution time increased to 40s, primarily due to waiting for the human to clear the target area.
- **Human-Aware Collision Avoidance (Human Blocks Path):** The robot successfully replanned and navigated beneath an actively blocking human arm. Despite close proximity, the motion remained smooth and safe. The robot deliberately reduced speed near a kinematic singularity to reconfigure safely, trading speed for safety. Execution time was 50s.
- **Highly Constrained Human–Robot Interaction (Human Forms Narrow Passage):** In a scenario where a human created a narrow, non-convex passage with both arms, the robot successfully navigated the region without collision. The robot significantly reduced its speed when the minimum collision distance was small, demonstrating a conservative approach to safety before accelerating again. Execution time was 18s.
- **Markerless Perception:** The integration of RT-COSMIK enabled direct operation with noisy, markerless human pose estimates, demonstrating the practicality of the system in dynamic HRI scenarios. The inverse kinematics stage provided a filtering effect, reducing noise in the collision geometry.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces COSMIK-MPPI, a framework that addresses several critical limitations in ensuring safe physical human-robot interaction in dynamic, shared environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Safety and Robustness in Complex HRI:** By incorporating the Constraints-as-Terminations (CaT) transcription, COSMIK-MPPI offers a more robust and principled approach to enforcing safety than traditional penalty-based methods in sampling-based control. This eliminates the fragility associated with finite penalties and the tuning difficulties of large penalty weights, leading to more reliable collision avoidance, especially in highly constrained or geometrically infeasible scenarios where vanilla MPPI might fail or lead to collisions. The consistent generation of collision-free, conservative, and smooth motions, prioritizing safety as complexity increases, is crucial for human trust and acceptance in collaborative settings.
- **Superior Scalability and Real-time Performance:** COSMIK-MPPI demonstrates consistent real-time computation (21-22 ms) regardless of the number of collision constraints or geometric complexity. This contrasts sharply with Gradient-Based MPC (GB-MPC), which exhibits significant performance degradation and real-time failure in complex environments. This scalability is a key enabler for practical deployment of robots in dynamic, unpredictable human workspaces, allowing for continuous replanning and adaptation.
- **Practical Integration with Markerless Human Perception:** The seamless integration with RT-COSMIK, an affordable markerless human motion estimation system, allows the framework to operate directly with noisy, real-world human pose data. This reduces the need for specialized markers or high-precision sensing equipment, broadening the accessibility and applicability of safe HRI solutions in diverse settings. The filtering capabilities of the inverse kinematics stage further contribute to robust perception under realistic conditions.
- **Foundation for Future HRI Research and Applications:**
 - **Generalizable Constraint Handling:** The MPPI-CaT approach provides a generalizable method for incorporating hard constraints into sampling-based control, which could extend beyond collision avoidance to other safety-critical or task-specific constraints in robotics.
 - **Intrinsic Compliance and Adaptability:** The framework's reliance on the robot's intrinsic compliance and its continuous online replanning capabilities allow the robot to safely absorb physical perturbations and adapt to unforeseen changes in the environment or human behavior. This paves the way for more natural, flexible, and responsive human-robot collaboration.
 - **Enabling Extensive User Studies:** The demonstrated safe behavior, even under close proximity and highly constrained human-robot interactions, facilitates future large-scale evaluations with industrial operators or end-users. This capability is essential for understanding user acceptance and refining HRI systems without imposing restrictive assumptions on human behavior.
 - **Potential for Predictive Augmentation:** The framework is amenable to direct extensions to incorporate accurate predictions of human motion, which could further enhance the efficiency and fluidity of human-robot interactions.

In conclusion, this work presents a robust, scalable, and practical solution for predictive collision avoidance that addresses key limitations of existing methods. It has the potential to significantly advance the deployment of robotic manipulators in complex, dynamic, and safety-critical human-shared environments by ensuring reliable and compliant interaction.
