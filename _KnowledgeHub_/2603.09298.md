# CORAL: Scalable Multi-Task Robot Learning via LoRA Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> CORAL introduces a framework for scalable multi-task robot learning, utilizing lightweight LoRA experts on a frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model backbone. This approach effectively mitigates task interference and catastrophic forgetting, achieving state-of-the-art performance with 99.3% success on the LIBERO benchmark and reducing storage requirements by approximately 100-fold per expert.

## Problem

- Jointly fine-tuning a single VLA model for multiple, heterogeneous tasks leads to 'negative transfer' or task interference, degrading performance on some skills.
- Storing a separate, full model checkpoint for each task is prohibitive due to substantial storage (O(N × |θ|)) and deployment costs, especially for edge devices.
- Sequentially adapting a monolithic VLA model to new tasks causes catastrophic forgetting, overwriting parameters crucial for previously acquired skills.

## Method

- A ==two-stage learning paradigm==: first, general pre-training of a VLA base model which is then ==permanently frozen==, providing broad visual-linguistic generalization.
- Creation of independent, lightweight ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) experts== for each task, trained briefly on top of the frozen base model, ensuring ==strict parameter isolation==.
- A dynamic **CORAL Manager** that uses ==language instructions as a routing mechanism== to dynamically load and 'fold' the appropriate LoRA expert into the base model for real-time, low-latency task switching.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance, with **CORAL_SimVLA** reaching **99.3%** average success on the LIBERO benchmark and **CORAL_π0.5** showing a **+1.5%** absolute improvement over baselines.
- Demonstrated significant efficiency with storage savings (e.g., **40 experts** requiring **1 GB**, compared to **3 GB** for a single full model) and rapid expert switching within **100 ms** with zero additional inference FLOPs.
- Effectively mitigated multi-task interference and catastrophic forgetting in real-world scenarios, enabling new capability acquisition with performance comparable to independent full fine-tuning while requiring only a fraction of the storage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Strict parameter isolation through disjoint LoRA experts effectively prevents multi-task interference and catastrophic forgetting without requiring complex continual learning methods.
> - Leveraging the inherent task identification in language instructions eliminates the need for learned gating networks or external LLMs for expert routing, simplifying the architecture and reducing latency.
> - Disentangling general visual-linguistic grounding (frozen backbone) from task-specific control nuances (LoRA experts) enables both broad generalization and efficient, fine-grained specialization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a promising direction for achieving general-purpose robotic manipulation by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and action prediction within a unified architecture. While large-scale pre-training provides VLA models with broad visual-semantic priors, their real-world deployment often necessitates fine-tuning for specific tasks due to environmental variations, viewpoint shifts, or object appearance changes not covered by pre-training data. This requirement introduces significant challenges, particularly in multi-task settings.

A primary challenge is **task interference**, or "negative transfer," where jointly fine-tuning a single model across heterogeneous tasks can lead to conflicting gradients. This often degrades performance on some skills while improving others, especially when dealing with fine-grained instructional ambiguity. Conversely, maintaining a separate, full model checkpoint for each task, though preventing interference, is often prohibitive due to substantial storage and deployment costs. Furthermore, **catastrophic forgetting** is a concern in sequential task learning, where adapting to new tasks can overwrite parameters crucial for previously acquired skills.

The research community has approached these interconnected issues through various subfields:

- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)**: Techniques like Adapters, Prefix-Tuning, and particularly Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) have emerged to reduce computational burdens when adapting large pre-trained models. While these methods improve efficiency for static deployment, CORAL leverages LoRA not merely for compression but as a dynamic mechanism for parameter-isolated lifelong learning, specifically addressing cross-task interference without complex model merging.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)**: MoE architectures aim to scale model capacity by routing inputs to specialized experts. Internal MoE designs often involve costly joint training and complex learned gating networks, which can suffer from capacity limits or task ambiguity at scale. External routing mechanisms, while decoupling experts, can introduce inference latency. CORAL differentiates itself by exploiting the inherent task identification within language instructions in robotics, enabling deterministic routing without learned gating or external Large Language Models (LLMs), thus avoiding the associated complexities and latencies.
- **Continual Learning (CL)**: CL focuses on mitigating catastrophic forgetting during sequential skill acquisition, employing strategies like weight consolidation or data replay. Recent work has incorporated LoRA adapters into CL to reduce forgetting. However, many CL-based VLA methods still rely on learned routers, replay buffers, or partially shared updates, which can become bottlenecks as the task set grows. CORAL treats continual adaptation as a system design problem, employing strict parameter isolation via disjoint LoRA experts to mitigate forgetting by construction, thereby avoiding the complexities of traditional CL methods.

CORAL positions itself within this landscape by offering a unified, backbone- and embodiment-agnostic framework that addresses task interference, storage constraints, and catastrophic forgetting through a distinct application of LoRA for dynamic, multi-task robot learning. It proposes a scalable and practical solution for lifelong VLA deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the CORAL framework is to enable scalable multi-task robot learning for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically addressing the core challenges encountered during their real-world deployment. The motivation stems from three critical and often conflicting challenges inherent in adapting VLA models for a growing collection of tasks:

1. **Reconciling Generalization vs. Specialization**: VLA models must possess the ability to generalize across diverse scenarios, a capability often derived from large-scale pre-training. Simultaneously, they need to specialize in the specific nuances of individual tasks to achieve high success rates. The motivation is to allow for task-specific adaptation without degrading the broad visual-linguistic generalization capabilities of the foundational model, which unconstrained over-specialization can destroy.

2. **Mitigating Multi-Task Interference and Enabling Scalable Sequential Adaptation**: When multiple tasks are jointly fine-tuned within a single VLA model, gradients from different tasks can conflict, leading to "negative transfer" where improving one skill can degrade another. This issue becomes particularly acute under fine-grained instructional ambiguity. Conversely, sequentially fine-tuning a monolithic model for new tasks inevitably leads to catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired skills. The motivation for CORAL is to devise a method that avoids these multi-task optimization dilemmas, allowing for the introduction of new tasks continuously over the robot's lifetime without parameter overwriting or performance degradation on existing skills.

3. **Breaking Edge Deployment Constraints**: Traditional approaches of maintaining a separate, full model checkpoint for each task are storage- and deployment-prohibitive, especially for edge devices or systems requiring many tasks. Storing N separate full models scales linearly with the number of tasks (O(N × |θ|)), making it physically infeasible for onboard deployment as N increases. CORAL is motivated to provide a solution that strictly bounds the model footprint, enabling a vast library of task-specific knowledge within practical memory limits.

In summary, CORAL aims to create a practical and scalable system for lifelong multi-task robot learning that can achieve uniformly high success rates across a multitude of initial tasks, seamlessly and efficiently incorporate new, unseen tasks, and operate within strict onboard memory budgets. This is achieved by disentangling general visual-linguistic grounding from task-specific control nuances, thus circumventing the limitations of standard fine-tuning and the complexities of classic continual learning methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CORAL framework adopts a backbone- and embodiment-agnostic approach to scalable multi-task robot learning, primarily focused on mitigating multi-task interference and enabling continuous task acquisition efficiently. Its methodology is built around a two-stage learning paradigm and a dynamic inference engine.

**A. Two-Stage Learning Paradigm**

CORAL disentangles general embodiment learning from task-specific specialization through two distinct phases:

1. **Embodiment-Aware General Pre-training**:
 - In the initial phase, a base policy model (denoted as θ_base) undergoes training or fine-tuning on a diverse dataset encompassing all available initial tasks.
 - The objective here is to enable the model to acquire a deep understanding of the robot’s general control patterns, kinematics, and the common visual-linguistic structure pertinent to the operational environment.
 - Upon completion of this phase, the resulting base model θ_base is permanently frozen. This frozen backbone serves as a stable foundation, providing broad visual-linguistic generalization capabilities.

2. **Lightweight Task-Specific LoRA Experts**:
 - Following the pre-training of the base model, an independent, lightweight Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) expert (θ_k) is created for each of the initial N tasks and for any new tasks that emerge over the system's lifetime.
 - The training for each LoRA expert is intentionally kept brief (e.g., typically 1 to 5 epochs). This limited training acts as an implicit regularization mechanism, preventing overfitting to specific task data and ensuring that the expert primarily enhances task-specific success rates without degrading the foundational generalization abilities inherited from the base model.
 - Each LoRA expert is designed to be highly compact, fulfilling stringent memory constraints necessary for edge deployment and scalability. For instance, a rank-16 LoRA expert for a typical 0.8B-parameter VLA base model occupies approximately 26 MB, representing a roughly 100-fold compression compared to a full model checkpoint.

**B. Assumptions on the Base Policy**

CORAL is designed to be compatible with VLA backbones adhering to the common "VLM encoder + action head" architecture. This involves:
- A **Vision-Language Encoder (f_enc)** that processes image observations (I) and a language instruction (T) to generate fused multimodal representations (z).
- An **Action Head (f_act)** which takes these representations (z), and optionally proprioceptive state (s), to predict robot actions (â) over a defined action chunk horizon (H) and action dimensionality (d_a).
This general formulation allows CORAL to be applied across a wide range of existing VLA architectures and robot embodiments.

**C. LoRA Expert Injection and Training Details**

For each task k, a LoRA adapter θ_k is initialized with a specific rank (r) and scaling factor (α). During the task-specific adaptation phase, the base model θ_base remains entirely frozen. Only the parameters of the LoRA adapter θ_k are optimized. The update rule for any target weight matrix W in the base model is modified as W' = W + (α/r)BA, where B and A are trainable low-rank matrices.

CORAL employs a dual-target injection strategy, inserting LoRA modules into the attention layers of both the VLM encoder and the action head. This enables the expert to concurrently adapt visual-linguistic feature extraction and the low-level control policy to the target task. A critical aspect of this approach is the **strict parameter isolation**: during the optimization of θ_k, no other adapters (θ_j, j ≠ k) are loaded, and the base model is completely frozen. This design inherently prevents parameter overwriting, effectively mitigating interference between tasks and catastrophic forgetting in a continual learning context.

**D. Dynamic Deployment: CORAL Manager**

At inference time, the CORAL Manager orchestrates the dynamic loading, switching, and unloading of these lightweight LoRA experts on top of the single frozen base model. A key insight utilized by CORAL is that in language-conditioned robotics, the user's language instruction inherently identifies the intended task. This property eliminates the need for complex, learned gating networks typically found in standard Mixture-of-Experts architectures. Instead, the language instruction (T) directly serves as an index for routing to the appropriate expert (k = R(T)).

The **Dynamic Expert Switching Protocol** (Algorithm 1) outlines the manager's logic:
1. Upon receiving a new instruction (T) and image (I) from the robot client, the Manager determines the required expert index (k).
2. If the determined expert (k) differs from the currently active expert (k_prev), a switching procedure is initiated.
3. First, any previously active expert is "un-merged" by restoring the pristine base model (θ_base) from a cached RAM state dictionary (S_base).
4. Next, the required LoRA adapter (θ_k) is loaded from disk.
5. Its low-rank matrices are then directly "folded" or "merged" into the base model's weights. This explicit merge ensures that subsequent inference operations execute with zero additional FLOPs or latency compared to using the original base model.
6. The current active expert (k_prev) is updated, and then standard inference proceeds with the newly configured base model.
This entire switching procedure is reported to complete within 100 milliseconds, supporting real-time robotic control and seamless task transitions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of CORAL across both standard simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic environments, aiming to demonstrate its performance, generalization capabilities, efficiency, and ability to mitigate forgetting.

**A. Simulation Benchmarks**

CORAL was evaluated on three widely used simulation benchmarks: LIBERO, WidowX, and Google Robot. The framework's backbone-agnosticism was demonstrated by instantiating it with two different VLA base models: SimVLA (0.8B parameters) and π_0.5 (3B parameters). LoRA experts for these benchmarks typically used a rank of r=16 and a scaling factor α=32, trained for 1 to 5 epochs.

- **LIBERO Benchmark (40 tasks across four suites)**: CORAL demonstrated a new state-of-the-art.
 - **CORAL_SimVLA** achieved an overall average success rate of 99.3%, surpassing the previously leading X-VLA baseline.
 - **CORAL_π0.5** achieved 98.4% average success, representing a +1.5% absolute improvement over the standard π_0.5 baseline, with a notable +3.4% gain on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite.
- **WidowX Robot Tasks (Simpler-Bridge)**: Designed to assess real-to-sim transfer.
 - **CORAL_SimVLA** achieved a 97.9% average success rate, outperforming large-scale models like DD-VLA and achieving 100% success on tasks such as "Spoon" and "Carrot."
- **Google Robot Tasks (Simpler-Fractal)**: Evaluated for robustness using variant aggregation scores.
 - **CORAL_SimVLA** reached an average of 84.9%, exceeding X-VLA and RT-2-X by significant margins (e.g., +11.8% on "Move" and +8.2% on "Open").

**B. System Efficiency in Simulation**

The CORAL framework exhibited high efficiency in terms of training computation and storage.
- For the 40 tasks in the LIBERO benchmark, 40 independent LoRA experts were trained with just 50 steps each.
- A single rank-16 LoRA expert for SimVLA occupied approximately 26 MB. The entire library of 40 experts thus required about 1 GB of storage, which is roughly one-third of the approximately 3 GB needed for a single fully fine-tuned checkpoint. The paper suggests that LoRA rank can be further optimized, potentially allowing for hundreds of experts.
- Expert switching was observed to complete within 100 ms on a single GPU, with zero additional inference FLOPs, supporting real-time, seamless task transitions.

**C. Real-World Evaluation**

CORAL was deployed on a Galaxea R1 Lite dual-arm mobile manipulator. Two base policies, π_0.5 and SimVLA, were used, pre-trained on a large-scale open-world dataset. LoRA experts for new capabilities were configured with a higher rank (r=64, α=128) and trained for 10 epochs to accommodate novel dynamics.

- **Cross-Scene Zero-Shot Generalization**: Evaluated on 8 complex, bimanual manipulation tasks in unseen, held-out real-world scenes (e.g., "close the book," "uncap a pen").
 - CORAL significantly enhanced the base model's cross-scene robustness by activating task-specific LoRA experts, which mitigated the multi-task interference typically observed when relying solely on a monolithic pre-trained policy.

- **New Capability Acquisition & Forgetting Analysis**: Tested on acquiring two entirely new, out-of-domain skill suites (Open Door with 3 variants and Press Elevator Button with 5 variants), each with 20 demonstration episodes. CORAL_SimVLA was compared against two full fine-tuning baselines: Independent Full Fine-Tuning and Joint Full Fine-Tuning.
 - **Independent Full Fine-Tuning**: Achieved strong performance across all new task variants, but required storing a full model checkpoint for each, making it resource-intensive (e.g., 26.4 GB for 8 tasks).
 - **Joint Full Fine-Tuning**: Suffered from severe multi-task interference and gradient conflicts across the 8 diverse task variants, resulting in a dramatic performance collapse (averaging 24.5% success rate). This demonstrated that even simultaneous training can lead to degradation with conflicting capabilities. Moreover, sequential full fine-tuning inevitably led to complete catastrophic forgetting of previous skills.
 - **CORAL_SimVLA**: Achieved performance comparable to the resource-intensive Independent Full Fine-Tuning approach (e.g., 85.9% average for Door tasks, 92.8% for Elevator Button tasks) while requiring only a fraction of the storage space (e.g., 4.1 GB for 8 tasks). By isolating task learning into separate, lightweight LoRA experts, CORAL effectively avoided parameter overwriting and catastrophic forgetting without needing data replay. It substantially mitigated the interference seen in Joint fine-tuning.

In essence, the findings indicate that CORAL consistently improves task success rates, achieves state-of-the-art performance in various benchmarks, maintains cross-model generalization, and enables efficient acquisition of new capabilities in real-world scenarios while effectively addressing storage and catastrophic forgetting challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CORAL framework presents a significant advancement in the deployment and scaling of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for real-world robotics, with several areas of potential impact:

1. **Unified Solution for Core Multi-Task Challenges**: CORAL provides a unified solution to three critical pain points in VLA deployment: task interference, catastrophic forgetting, and storage constraints. By strictly isolating task-specific knowledge within lightweight LoRA experts, it effectively circumvents the gradient conflicts inherent in joint fine-tuning and prevents parameter overwriting typical of sequential learning. This architectural design simplifies the complex problem of multi-task optimization.

2. **Enabling Scalable Lifelong Robot Learning**: The framework's ability to seamlessly incorporate a continuous stream of new tasks without degrading previously acquired skills positions it as a practical solution for lifelong robot learning. Robots can incrementally acquire new capabilities and adapt to evolving operational requirements over their deployment lifetime, enhancing their versatility and longevity.

3. **Enhanced Deployment Efficiency**: CORAL significantly reduces the storage footprint per task (approximately 100x compression at rank 16 compared to a full model checkpoint). This efficiency is crucial for deploying VLA models on edge devices or in resource-constrained robotic systems, where maintaining multiple large models is infeasible. Furthermore, the CORAL Manager's ability to switch experts dynamically with zero additional inference FLOPs and minimal latency (under 100ms) ensures that this multi-task capability does not compromise real-time control requirements.

4. **Improved Generalization and Specialization Trade-off**: By freezing a pre-trained VLA backbone, CORAL preserves the broad visual-linguistic generalization capabilities of the foundational model. The lightweight LoRA experts then provide the necessary specialization for individual tasks without causing over-specialization that could degrade the base model's utility. This controlled adaptation leads to robust performance across diverse, and even unseen, environments.

5. **Backbone and Embodiment Agnosticism**: The design of CORAL, being agnostic to both the underlying VLA backbone and the robot embodiment, suggests broad applicability. This flexibility allows it to be integrated with various existing and future VLA models and deployed on different robotic platforms, potentially accelerating the adoption of generalist VLA policies in diverse robotic applications.

The proposed future work, including exploring hierarchical expert structures for knowledge sharing and online LoRA adaptation with reinforcement learning, indicates avenues for further enhancing the framework's capabilities and autonomy. Overall, CORAL offers a practical and scalable paradigm that moves VLA models closer to robust, adaptable, and deployable agents for complex, real-world robotic manipulation tasks.
