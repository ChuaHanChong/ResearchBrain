# Dynam3D: Dynamic Layered 3D Tokens Empower VLM for Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> National University of Singapore researchers develop Dynam3D, a framework that enhances vision-and-language navigation through dynamic 3D representations, achieving state-of-the-art performance on continuous environment benchmarks while enabling real-time adaptation to changing environments through layered 3D tokens and online instance encoding.

## Problem

- Existing video-based models struggle with 3D geometry understanding and spatial relationships in large environments
- Current approaches lack structured memory mechanisms for large-scale exploration and environmental changes
- Poor adaptability to dynamic scenes where objects and people move around

## Method

- ==Dynamic layered 3D== representation model combining patch features, instance representations, and zone-level spatial layout
- Integration with 3D-VLM using generalizable feature fields and multimodal reasoning
- Online encoding and updating of 3D instances with merging discriminator for consistency

## Results

- State-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE, REVERIE-CE, and NavRAG-CE benchmarks
- Strong performance in pre-exploration and lifelong memory settings
- Demonstrated robustness in real-world dynamic environments with moving objects
- Maintains smaller parameter footprint compared to video-based approaches while achieving superior performance

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Layered representation at multiple scales (patch, instance, zone) improves spatial understanding
> - Dynamic updating mechanisms enable adaptation to environmental changes
> - Alignment between 3D features and language semantics enhances navigation performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work sits at the intersection of several active research areas:

- **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN):** VLN is a well-established task in embodied AI, aiming to enable agents to navigate through environments based on natural language instructions. The paper acknowledges existing VLN research, highlighting the shift from discrete environments to continuous environments and real-world deployments.
- **Video-Language Models (Video-VLMs):** The paper recognizes the recent success of applying video-based large models to VLN tasks. These models, pre-trained on large datasets, demonstrate strong language understanding and multimodal reasoning. However, the paper identifies limitations of video-based representations, such as difficulty in capturing spatial geometry and semantics.
- **3D Vision-Language Models (3D-VLMs):** This is an emerging area focusing on incorporating 3D information into VLMs. This paper acknowledges existing approaches using point clouds, multi-view images, and voxel-based representations. The paper differentiates itself by addressing challenges related to large-scale, unseen, and dynamic environments, which are critical for embodied navigation.
- **Dynamic Scene Understanding:** The paper contributes to the broader field of dynamic scene understanding, where the goal is to build models that can track and reason about changes in the environment. The dynamic updating mechanism in Dynam3D aligns with this research direction.

The paper positions itself as an advancement beyond existing video-based VLN methods and addresses limitations of current 3D-VLMs in dynamic, embodied navigation scenarios. It introduces a novel approach to address shortcomings in spatial understanding, memory, and adaptability that hinder deployment in real-world conditions. The use of language-aligned 3D representations as visual input sets it apart from other 3D-VLM implementations. The paper also provides a comprehensive overview of relevant literature, citing key works in VLN, video-VLMs, and 3D-VLMs. The discussion of related works demonstrates a clear understanding of the current research landscape and how this research contributes to the existing body of knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper clearly outlines three key limitations of existing video-based VLMs for VLN tasks, which serve as the primary motivation for the proposed Dynam3D model:

1. **Insufficient understanding of 3D geometry and spatial semantics:** Video-based models struggle to capture the geometric details and spatial relationships within large 3D environments, hindering exploration and error correction.
2. **Limited capacity for large-scale exploration and long-term environmental memory:** Current models lack mechanisms for structured scene memory, preventing pre-exploration knowledge usage and lifelong learning capabilities.
3. **Poor adaptability to dynamic and changing environments:** Representations derived from historical frames are inadequate for dynamically changing 3D scenes, where object and human movements lead to performance degradation.

The paper's key objective is to develop a VLN system that overcomes these limitations by:

- Creating a dynamic, layered 3D representation of the environment that captures both fine-grained geometric details and global spatial layout.
- Enabling online encoding and localization of 3D instances, as well as dynamic updates to maintain a long-term, changing environmental memory.
- Integrating this 3D representation with a 3D-VLM to predict navigation actions based on language instructions and environmental context.
- Achieving state-of-the-art performance on VLN benchmarks, particularly in monocular settings and realistic environments.
- Demonstrating the model's effectiveness in pre-exploration, lifelong memory, and real-world deployment scenarios.

The overall motivation is to improve the robustness and applicability of VLN systems in real-world scenarios, where environments are often dynamic and complex, and robots are typically equipped with monocular cameras.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Dynam3D framework consists of two main components:

1. **Dynamic Layered 3D Representation Model:** This component is responsible for encoding and maintaining a 3D representation of the environment.
 - **Patch Feature Points:** 2D patch features are extracted from RGB images using CLIP and projected into 3D space using depth maps and camera poses. These points are dynamically updated, using a frustum culling strategy to remove outdated information and incorporate new observations.
 - **Dynamic Instance Representations:** 2D instance masks are generated using FastSAM, and patch features within each mask are aggregated into instance-level representations. A merging discriminator integrates 2D instance representations into consistent 3D instances, ensuring geometric and multi-view consistency. Contrastive learning and feature distillation are applied to align instance representations with language semantics.
 - **Zone Representations:** 3D space is partitioned into cubic zones, and instance-level features are aggregated within each zone to capture coarse-grained spatial layout information. This provides a higher-level understanding of the environment.
2. **3D Vision-Language Model (3D-VLM):** This component leverages the 3D representation to predict navigation actions.
 - **Generalizable Feature Fields:** A feature field model renders 3D patch features over an agent-centric panoramic scope, enriching local geometric and semantic perception.
 - **Multimodal Reasoning and Action Prediction:** A pre-trained LLaVA-Phi-3-mini model is integrated into the framework. The multimodal reasoning capabilities of the 2D-VLM are transferred to the 3D domain by aligning the 3D representations with the semantic space of CLIP. The 3D-VLM takes the 3D tokens (patch, instance, zone), language instructions, and action history as input and outputs atomic navigation actions (turn, move forward, stop).

The system is trained in a two-stage process: imitation learning followed by exploration and correction using the DAgger strategy to improve error recovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed Dynam3D framework.

- **State-of-the-art Performance on VLN Benchmarks:** Dynam3D achieves state-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE, REVERIE-CE, and NavRAG-CE, demonstrating its ability to understand complex instructions and navigate in continuous environments.
- **Superior Efficiency:** Despite using a large model, Dynam3D maintains a smaller parameter footprint compared to some video-based approaches, highlighting its efficient use of computational resources.
- **Effectiveness in Pre-exploration and Lifelong Memory:** Dynam3D demonstrates significant performance gains under pre-exploration and lifelong memory settings, indicating its ability to leverage prior knowledge and adapt to new environments over time.
- **Robustness in Real-World and Dynamic Environments:** The model shows strong robustness to environmental changes and achieves high success rates in real-world navigation experiments, even in dynamic settings where the target object is moved during navigation.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies confirm the importance of the 3D instance and zone representations, as well as the subspace alignment supervision, for achieving optimal performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper has significant implications for the field of embodied AI and robotics. The key contributions and potential impact include:

- **Advancement of VLN Systems:** The Dynam3D framework addresses critical limitations of existing VLN systems, paving the way for more robust and reliable navigation in real-world environments.
- **Enhanced Spatial Understanding:** The dynamic, layered 3D representation model enables a more comprehensive understanding of the environment, capturing both fine-grained details and global spatial layout.
- **Improved Long-Term Memory and Adaptability:** The model's ability to update its 3D representation in real-time and leverage prior knowledge facilitates lifelong learning and adaptation to dynamic environments.
- **Practical Real-World Deployment:** The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of Dynam3D in real-world scenarios, showcasing its potential for deployment in various applications, such as assistive robotics, autonomous delivery, and environmental monitoring.
- **Novel 3D-VLM Approach:** The integration of a dynamic 3D representation with a large language model provides a novel approach to 3D-VLM research, potentially inspiring new directions in the field.

The limitations section acknowledges the inability to explicitly output target instance coordinates and the lack of question-answering and dialogue capabilities, indicating areas for future research. Overall, this work represents a significant step forward in enabling robots to navigate complex, dynamic environments based on natural language instructions, bridging the gap between research and real-world applications.
