# ReCoDe: Reinforcement Learning-based Dynamic Constraint Design for Multi-Agent Coordination

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Cambridge developed ReCoDe, a decentralized hybrid framework that augments expert optimization-based controllers with dynamically learned constraints. This method improves multi-agent coordination, achieving higher rewards and enhanced safety with superior sample efficiency in simulated and real-world robotic environments.

## Problem

- Handcrafting adaptive constraints for classical optimization-based controllers is difficult, leading to sub-optimal coordination or deadlocks in complex, dynamic multi-agent scenarios.
- Pure multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) typically lacks formal safety guarantees and interpretability, and often suffers from slow training due to sparse rewards, limiting its use in critical applications.

## Method

- **ReCoDe** augments existing expert optimization-based controllers by introducing a dynamically learned ==quadratic constraint==, which preserves the original objective function and safety constraints.
- A ==Graph Neural Network (GNN)== policy learns to generate parameters for this quadratic constraint, defining a feasible action region for the agent's underlying convex optimization problem.
- The framework employs a ==Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution (CTDE)== setup using ==MAPPO==, allowing agents to learn coordination based on local observations and communication while operating autonomously.

## Results

- **ReCoDe** achieved an average of **18%** higher reward than the next-best baseline across diverse multi-agent coordination tasks while maintaining near-zero collision rates throughout training and deployment.
- It demonstrated significantly higher sample efficiency, converging to high performance within approximately **5%** of the training steps required by pure MARL methods.
- The learned policy successfully transferred to real-world robot platforms, enabling robust multi-agent coordination and deadlock avoidance in scenarios where expert controllers failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting expert controllers with dynamic learned constraints, rather than modifying or replacing the objective, leverages expert knowledge for data-efficient and robust learning.
> - A dynamically adjusted uncertainty radius within the learned quadratic constraint enables fluid balancing of control authority between the learned policy and the expert controller based on situational complexity.
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates the framework's ability to enforce precise tracking of safe trajectories and to dynamically mitigate uncertainty by adjusting the learned constraint's influence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent coordination in shared environments presents a long-standing challenge in robotics, requiring systems such as autonomous vehicle fleets or warehouse robots to operate safely and efficiently. Existing research primarily explores two distinct paradigms: classical optimization-based control and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), alongside a developing field of hybrid approaches.

Classical optimization-based controllers offer a structured methodology that encodes mission objectives and safety requirements (e.g., collision avoidance, kinematic limitations, formation adherence) into tractable optimization problems. These methods typically provide analytical guarantees for safety and offer interpretability. However, their primary limitation lies in the difficulty of handcrafting constraints that can adapt to the complexities of dynamic, multi-agent scenarios, such as preventing congestion or exploiting subtle coordination opportunities that evolve with changing conditions.

Conversely, multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) provides an adaptive framework where agents learn behaviors through experience without explicit task-specific design. While MARL can address complex interactions, it generally lacks the analytical structure and safety assurances inherent in optimization-based methods. This often results in decisions that are difficult to predict, verify, or trust in safety-critical applications, and training can be slow, particularly when safe, high-reward actions are sparse.

Hybrid methods aim to combine the strengths of both paradigms. Prior work in this area includes using MARL to optimize parameters of existing control barrier functions (CBFs) within constrained optimization problems for multi-agent navigation, or learning the objective function of a constrained optimization problem. Another approach, "shielding," involves a safety filter that intervenes when a learned policy's action violates safety constraints.

ReCoDe contributes to this hybrid landscape by introducing a decentralized framework that *augments* existing expert optimization-based controllers with *additional, dynamically learned constraints*. Unlike methods that modify existing constraints or learn objectives, ReCoDe preserves the original expert controller's objective and safety constraints. The proposed method hypothesizes that learning a new, situation-dependent constraint is more data-efficient and robust because it leverages the expert's foundational knowledge while enabling adaptable, context-based coordination. This approach seeks to provide the adaptability of MARL without sacrificing the safety guarantees and interpretability offered by an optimization-based core.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address the limitations of current multi-agent control strategies and to propose a hybrid framework that integrates their respective strengths.
The key motivations driving the development of ReCoDe are as follows:

1. **Enhance Adaptability of Optimization-Based Controllers**: Handcrafted constraints, while ensuring safety in many cases, often fail in complex multi-agent scenarios demanding intricate coordination. For instance, in a narrow passage, basic collision avoidance might be encoded, but agents may still deadlock due to congestion or reciprocal blocking, as the handcrafted rules do not prescribe yielding or nuanced cooperative maneuvers. The objective is to enable controllers to adapt to evolving conditions and exploit coordination opportunities that static constraints cannot capture.

2. **Impart Safety and Interpretability to Reinforcement Learning**: Pure multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) offers adaptability but typically lacks the formal safety guarantees and interpretability crucial for critical robotics applications. Its decisions can be opaque and difficult to verify. A key motivation is to harness MARL's learning capabilities without sacrificing the predictability and safety assurances of analytical methods.

3. **Develop a Decentralized, Hybrid Framework**: The authors aim to combine the reliability of optimization-based controllers with the adaptability of MARL in a decentralized manner. This means individual agents should learn to coordinate effectively using only local observations and communication, contributing to a robust and scalable system.

4. **Leverage Expert Knowledge Efficiently**: Instead of discarding well-designed expert controllers, the motivation is to *improve* them by learning *additional, dynamic constraints*. The hypothesis is that retaining a user-defined objective and existing safety constraints provides a valuable inductive bias, making the learning process more data-efficient and stable compared to learning an entire controller or an optimization objective from scratch. The learned constraints are intended to capture "subtler behaviors" such as managing congestion or enforcing implicit yielding rules.

5. **Enable Dynamic Balancing of Control Authority**: A specific motivation is to allow the system to dynamically shift reliance between the learned policy and the handcrafted controller based on the situation. For example, in crowded, complex scenarios, the learned constraints might tightly guide the agent, while in clearer situations, the agent might defer more to the default controller's efficient, greedy movements.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for multi-agent systems that are both safe and adaptive, capable of handling complex coordination challenges in real-world environments while retaining the benefits of expert-designed control principles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReCoDe (Reinforcement-based Constraint Design) is a decentralized, hybrid framework designed to augment existing optimization-based controllers with learned, dynamic constraints.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

The core idea behind ReCoDe is to preserve a given expert controller (which includes an expert-defined objective function and safety constraints) and introduce *additional* constraints that are learned through multi-agent reinforcement learning. These learned constraints dynamically modify each agent's feasible action set, thereby guiding the underlying optimization problem to achieve more nuanced coordination behaviors.

#### 3.2. Agent Control Loop and Optimization Problem

For each agent *i* at time *t*, the control process involves the following steps:
1. **Observation**: Agent *i* receives an observation, *o_i(t)*, which includes its own state, states of other visible agents (within its communication range), and environmental factors.
2. **Constraint Parameter Generation**: A learned policy, *π_i*, maps the observation *o_i(t)* to a set of constraint parameters, *θ_i(t) = (a_i(t), b_i(t))*. Here, *a_i(t) ∈ ℝ^m* represents a reference action suggested by the policy, and *b_i(t) ∈ ℝ_≥0* is an "uncertainty radius" that determines how tightly the solver must adhere to *a_i(t)*.
3. **Optimization Problem Solving**: Agent *i* then solves a constrained optimization problem to determine its control input *u_i(t)*. The problem is formulated as:
 ```
    min_{u_i(t)} J_i(o_i(t), u_i(t)) + λ_0 * s_0
    s.t. ||u_i(t) - a_i(t)||^2 <= b_i(t) + s_0  (Learned Quadratic Constraint)
         u_i(t) ∈ U_s_i(o_i(t))             (Default Expert Constraints)
    ```
 - **Objective Function**: *J_i(o_i(t), u_i(t))* is a strictly convex, quadratic cost function provided by the expert controller (e.g., minimizing energy expenditure or deviation from a desired trajectory).
 - **Learned Constraint**: A single quadratic constraint *||u_i(t) - a_i(t)||^2 <= b_i(t) + s_0* is added. This constraint defines a ball centered at the learned reference action *a_i(t)* with a radius determined by *b_i(t)*. A larger *b_i(t)* implies greater allowance for the handcrafted controller to steer away from *a_i(t)*, reflecting less strict learned guidance.
 - **Slack Variable**: *s_0* is a slack variable for the learned constraint, heavily penalized by *λ_0* during training. This ensures that the learned constraint is feasible, and *s_0* is non-zero only if no strictly feasible solution exists for the learned constraint itself, without affecting the fundamental safety guarantees of the expert constraints.
 - **Default Expert Constraints**: *U_s_i(o_i(t))* represents the set of admissible controls defined by the expert (handcrafted) controller. These include critical safety constraints (e.g., collision avoidance), kinematic limitations, and environmental boundaries.

The optimization problem is a convex Quadratic Constrained Quadratic Program (QCQP), which is known to be efficiently solvable. This efficiency is critical for both training (due to numerous solver calls) and real-time deployment.

#### 3.3. Policy Architecture and Training

- **Policy Network**: The agent's policy network, *π_i*, is implemented using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), specifically a GATv2Conv layer. This architecture is chosen for its ability to process relational information in multi-agent systems. Agents aggregate messages from neighbors within their visibility range to inform their constraint parameter decisions. GNNs enable decentralized execution at inference time, as each agent relies only on local information and messages from direct neighbors. They also ensure permutation invariance (the order of neighbors does not affect computation) and adaptability to dynamic neighborhoods (e.g., limited sensing range).
- **Training Scheme**: ReCoDe employs a Centralized Training, Decentralized Execution (CTDE) setup using the MAPPO (Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization) algorithm.
 - During centralized training, multiple environment instances with multiple agents run in parallel, and the resulting optimization problems are batched.
 - Slack variables are also introduced in the *default expert constraints* (U_s_i) during training, again heavily penalized, to ensure the overall optimization problem always remains feasible, even if an agent's learned parameters push towards an infeasible state. This helps in robust training by flagging problematic parameter configurations without crashing the solver.
 - At deployment, each agent operates fully decentralized, solving its local optimization problem based on its local observations and communications with its neighbors.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Foundations

The paper provides theoretical analysis to support ReCoDe's design principles:
- **Adaptability (Proposition 3.1)**: This proposition demonstrates that ReCoDe can enforce precision. Given a sufficiently large slack penalty *λ_0* and strict feasibility of a desired action *u*(t) within the handcrafted constraints, ReCoDe's learned constraints can force the agent's controller to track any safe, feasible trajectory with arbitrarily small error. This highlights the framework's capacity for fine-grained control when necessary.
- **Uncertainty Mitigation (Proposition 3.2)**: This proposition validates the dynamic balancing mechanism. It suggests that when the learned policy (represented by a proxy *Q_l_i*) is locally "uncertain" (e.g., has a flat gradient) but the expert objective *J_i* is decisive (e.g., has a strong gradient), enlarging the uncertainty radius *b(o)* (thereby increasing reliance on the expert controller) can lead to a better expected reward. This provides a theoretical basis for dynamically adjusting the influence between learned and expert control based on the local reliability of the learned policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ReCoDe involved both theoretical analysis and empirical studies in various multi-agent scenarios, including real-robot deployments.

#### 4.1. Experimental Scenarios and Baselines

ReCoDe's effectiveness was assessed across four multi-agent navigation and consensus tasks designed to expose common failure modes in multi-robot control:
1. **Narrow Corridor**: Two teams of robots swap positions in a confined space, requiring yielding and precise coordination to avoid deadlocks.
2. **Connectivity**: A single team navigates with obstacles while maintaining full communication links between all agents.
3. **Waypoint Navigation**: Over-sized robots navigate to random goals in a small room, often requiring non-greedy, coordinated detours to prevent congestion.
4. **Sensor Coverage**: Mobile sensors with individual targets must collectively decide on optimal positions to maximize overall environmental coverage while maintaining formation (connectivity). This is a multi-objective consensus task.

ReCoDe was benchmarked against several baselines:
- **Handcrafted Controller**: The expert optimization-based controller used as ReCoDe's foundation.
- **Reciprocal Velocity Obstacle (RVO)**: A non-learning method for multi-agent collision avoidance.
- **Pure MARL**: An end-to-end MARL policy that directly controls agent actions.
- **Online-CBF**: A hybrid method that learns parameters for control barrier functions within an optimization problem.
- **Shielding**: A hybrid method where a policy's actions are passed through a safety filter using constrained optimization.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance

Across all four scenarios, ReCoDe consistently outperformed all baselines. On average, ReCoDe achieved 18% greater reward than the next-best method (Table 2).
- Pure MARL generally exhibited the worst performance, often yielding negative rewards, particularly in scenarios sensitive to control input changes where collisions were frequent. This highlights the difficulty of achieving safety and precision with end-to-end learning in such environments.
- Other hybrid and non-learning methods frequently encountered deadlocks or suboptimal coordination, especially in tasks requiring complex inter-agent negotiation (e.g., Narrow Corridor, Connectivity, Waypoint Navigation).

#### 4.3. Training Efficiency and Safety

- **Sample Efficiency**: ReCoDe demonstrated significantly faster training convergence compared to Pure MARL. In Waypoint Navigation, ReCoDe reached high performance within 20 training steps (approximately 2.4 million frames), whereas Pure MARL still underperformed substantially after 500 steps (60 million frames), indicating that ReCoDe required only about 5% of the samples for comparable or better performance (Figure 3b).
- **Safety During Training**: ReCoDe maintained near-zero collision rates throughout the training process, similar to other optimization-based hybrid methods like Online-CBF. In contrast, Pure MARL incurred significant collision penalties, underscoring the inherent safety advantage of incorporating expert constraints (Figure 3c).

#### 4.4. Mechanism Analysis

Empirical analysis of the learned uncertainty radius *b(t)* in the Narrow Corridor task provided evidence supporting the theoretical proposition of dynamic balancing:
- **Correlation with Environment**: The value of *b(t)* correlated negatively with the number of nearby agents (r ≈ -0.03, p < 10^-40) and positively with proximity to the goal (r ≈ 0.08, p < 10^-200).
- **Behavioral Interpretation**: This suggests that ReCoDe learns to tighten the learned quadratic constraint (shrink *b*) in congested, high-interaction situations where precise coordination (guided by the learned policy) is crucial to avoid deadlocks. Conversely, *b* expands when the path is clearer (approaching the goal), indicating a greater reliance on the efficient, greedy movements of the handcrafted controller (Figure 3d-3e). This behavior aligns with the theoretical prediction that the system leverages expert knowledge when the learned policy is less certain or less critical.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

An ablation study in the Narrow Corridor scenario compared three variants of ReCoDe:
1. **ReCoDe (Constraint Only)**: The proposed method, learning only the quadratic constraint parameters.
2. **ReCoDe (Objective Only)**: Learning the objective's goal position while keeping the constraint fixed.
3. **ReCoDe (Constraint + Objective)**: Learning both simultaneously.
The "Constraint Only" variant achieved the highest return and fastest convergence, while the variants learning the objective converged slower and plateaued at lower scores (Figure 3f). This supports the hypothesis that retaining a fixed, expert-designed objective function provides a valuable inductive bias that is diluted when it becomes a learned, moving target.

#### 4.6. Real-World Implementation

The policy trained in the Narrow Corridor simulation was directly transferred to six holonomic ground robots on a physical testbed.
- The handcrafted controller consistently failed in real-robot trials, leading to deadlocks when two teams met.
- ReCoDe's learned quadratic constraints enabled the robots to successfully complete the position swap in all trials without violating safety margins. This demonstration confirmed ReCoDe's robustness to real-world noise, including positional tracking errors, communication delays, and actuator limitations, corroborating the simulation results (Figure 1).
- Experiments with learning linear constraints instead of quadratic ones in the robot demonstration showed similar average rewards but sometimes enforced different coordination behaviors (e.g., an "all or nothing" strategy for goal attainment), though this did not affect the overall reward.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReCoDe framework represents a development in multi-agent control by combining the safety and analytical assurances of optimization-based methods with the adaptability and learning capabilities of reinforcement learning.

#### 5.1. Overall Significance

- **Enhanced Performance and Safety**: ReCoDe demonstrated superior performance across diverse multi-agent coordination tasks while maintaining robust safety guarantees throughout training and deployment, a notable improvement over both purely classical and purely learning-based approaches.
- **Data Efficiency**: The framework's ability to achieve high performance with significantly fewer samples than end-to-end MARL (5% in some cases) makes it more practical for real-world robotics where data collection can be costly and time-consuming.
- **Interpretability and Trustworthiness**: By retaining an expert-designed optimization core, ReCoDe offers a more interpretable and verifiable decision-making process compared to opaque black-box deep reinforcement learning policies, which is beneficial for safety-critical applications.
- **Dynamic Adaptability**: The mechanism of dynamically adjusting the learned constraint radius (b) allows agents to fluidly shift authority between the learned policy and the expert controller. This enables precise, learned coordination in complex situations and efficient reliance on expert knowledge when paths are clear or the learned policy is less certain.
- **General Framework**: While evaluated primarily on navigation and consensus tasks, the hybrid nature of ReCoDe suggests potential applicability to a broader range of multi-agent control problems.

#### 5.2. Identified Limitations

The authors acknowledge several limitations of the current ReCoDe framework:
- **Task Scope**: The current empirical evaluations focused exclusively on multi-agent navigation and multi-objective consensus tasks. Its performance and comparative effectiveness in other non-navigation settings, such as multi-agent manipulation, remain unexplored and are subjects for future work.
- **Convexity Assumption**: ReCoDe currently assumes that the underlying optimization problem (including the expert's and learned constraints) is convex. While the framework could theoretically be extended to non-convex optimization, this would require efficient (potentially sub-optimal) non-convex solvers that are computationally viable for data collection during training, which was not investigated in this work.
- **Computational Scalability**: Data collection, particularly the need to solve numerous optimization problems in parallel, poses a computational challenge for scaling ReCoDe to very large numbers of agents. The optimization solvers often leverage CPUs more effectively than GPUs due to complex control flows. While GPU-compatible solvers (e.g., qpth, JAXOpt) exist and show promise for larger problem batches, the authors observed minimal advantages over CPU-based solvers in their relatively smaller-scale experiments, indicating further optimization and exploration are needed for massive multi-agent systems.
