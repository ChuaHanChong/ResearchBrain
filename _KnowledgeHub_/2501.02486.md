# LLMPC: Large Language Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The LLMPC (Large Language Model Predictive Control) framework integrates Large Language Models into Model Predictive Control, formally characterizing LLMs as approximate optimizers for planning tasks. This approach uses multiple LLM-generated plans and explicit objective function evaluation to enhance planning performance, achieving substantial improvements in success rates on combinatorial problems and reducing control objective gaps in continuous systems.

## Problem

- A formal framework was missing to explain how Large Language Models (LLMs) function as planners and optimally integrate with traditional control techniques.
- The inherent approximate nature of LLM planning, stemming from its sampling process, limits its performance on complex reasoning and long-horizon tasks.
- The specific connection between LLM planning and Model Predictive Control (MPC) remained underexplored in prior research.

## Method

- **LLMPC** formally establishes LLMs as approximate optimization algorithms within a ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework.
- The framework prompts the LLM to sample multiple (K) distinct control sequences or 'plans' for a given horizon.
- Each sampled plan is simulated using a state transition model and evaluated by an explicit objective function, with the best-performing plan selected for execution and subsequent replanning, following the MPC paradigm.

## Results

- For a spring-mass system, LLMPC reduced the mean objective ratio (LLMPC cost/MPC cost) from **8.21** (single plan) to **1.30** when sampling 15 plans, demonstrating improved approximation accuracy.
- On the trip planning benchmark, LLMPC increased the success rate to **44.6%** (with 7 iterations) from **14.5%** (single-round few-shot prompting), representing a **30.1** percentage point improvement.
- In meeting planning tasks, LLMPC achieved a **67%** success rate (with 9 iterations and 3 plans per iteration), consistently outperforming the **52.5%** success rate of single-round few-shot planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models inherently operate as approximate optimization algorithms when generating sequential plans.
> - Explicitly sampling multiple plans from an LLM and evaluating them against a defined objective function significantly reduces the performance gap between LLM planning and exact optimization.
> - Iterative refinement through replanning, combined with multiple plan sampling within an MPC structure, is crucial for improving LLM planning capabilities on complex tasks with many constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) with planning and control algorithms represents an active area of research in artificial intelligence. Prior work has explored various techniques to enhance LLM capabilities, ranging from early prompting methods such as chain-of-thought [Wei et al., 2022] and self-consistency [Wang et al., 2022], to more advanced approaches combining LLMs with tree search [Wang et al., 2024a, Jiang et al., 2024] and reinforcement learning [DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025]. These methods aim to guide LLM outputs towards structured planning to improve performance on complex reasoning tasks.

In robotics and embodied AI, LLMs have been utilized for generating action plans in frameworks like SayCan [Ahn et al., 2022] and Voyager [Wang et al., 2023]. LLM-Assist [Song et al., 2023] integrated LLMs with traditional planners for autonomous vehicle planning, while knowledge-guided approaches such as KnowAgent [Zhu et al., 2024] incorporate domain expertise and constraints into LLM planning.

The field has also investigated hierarchical and multi-agent planning using LLMs. Examples include two-step goal decomposition for multi-agent tasks [Singh et al., 2024], ISR-LLM [Zhou et al., 2024], and hierarchical constraint planning [Zhang et al., 2024], which address coordination in problems with multiple constraints and objectives. Search-based methods, including various tree search algorithms [Hao et al., 2023, Putta et al., 2024] and scattered forest search [Light et al., 2024], have leveraged LLMs to guide exploration and evaluate candidate solutions. Some research has also explored the use of LLMs as world models for web agents [Gu et al., 2024, Chae et al., 2024].

Furthermore, learning-based approaches have been applied to enhance LLM planning, such as supervised fine-tuning (e.g., TravelPlanner [Chen et al., 2024]), the use of world models for structured planning [Xiong et al., 2024], and continual learning for improved exploration in reinforcement learning [Paul, 2024].

Despite these advancements and the empirical successes of hybrid LLM-planning techniques, the paper identifies a gap in the existing literature. A formal framework for understanding how LLMs function as planners and how they can be optimally integrated with traditional control techniques is largely absent. Specifically, the connection between LLM planning and Model Predictive Control (MPC) [García et al., 1989, Mayne et al., 2000] has remained underexplored. This work positions itself to bridge this gap by establishing a formal link between LLM planning and MPC.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind this research arises from the observation that while various hybrid approaches combining LLMs with planning and control have demonstrated empirical benefits, there is a lack of a formal framework to explain how LLMs operate as planners and how they can be optimally integrated with established control techniques. The paper specifically highlights the unexplored relationship between LLM planning and Model Predictive Control (MPC), a framework for generating and executing control sequences.

The key objectives of this work are:
- To establish a formal connection between LLM planning and Model Predictive Control (MPC), thereby examining LLM planning through the lens of MPC.
- To demonstrate that LLMs inherently function as approximate optimization algorithms when generating plans.
- To propose a unified MPC framework, termed LLMPC (Large Language Model Predictive Control), for planning with LLMs.
- To provide theoretical insights into the underlying mechanisms that enable structured planning with LLMs.
- To develop practical methods for enhancing the planning capabilities of LLMs.
- To empirically demonstrate improved performance of the proposed LLMPC framework compared to few-shot prompting on several planning benchmarks.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of LLMs in planning contexts and to develop more robust and effective AI planning systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, LLMPC, integrates Large Language Models into a Model Predictive Control framework. The paper first describes the general Model Predictive Control setting, where an agent selects a sequence of actions over a planning horizon *H* to minimize an objective function *C* that considers states and actions. In MPC, only the first action(s) from the optimized sequence are executed, and the planning process is repeated with updated state information.

The methodology then characterizes an LLM's role as an approximate planner. An LLM takes a prompt (an input token sequence *q_t*) derived from the current state (*s_t*) and previous prompt (*q_t-1*). The LLM generates an output token sequence, which is then mapped by a function *ϕ* to a sequence of actions over the planning horizon *H*. The paper posits that this process implicitly attempts to minimize an objective function, albeit approximately. The limitation of LLM planning performance, according to the paper, stems from the LLM's inherent sampling process being an approximate minimization of the MPC objective.

To address this approximation and enhance performance, LLMPC introduces a method that explicitly utilizes the objective function. Instead of relying on a single plan generated by the LLM, the LLMPC framework operates as follows:
1. **Multiple Plan Sampling:** The LLM is prompted to sample *K* distinct control sequences or "plans." Each plan *A^i_t* consists of actions *{a^i_t..., a^i_{t+H-1}}* for the planning horizon. The generation of these multiple plans can be done by direct sampling from the LLM output or by constructing plans one action at a time using search algorithms like beam search.
2. **System Simulation:** For each of the *K* sampled plans, the system's state transition model (*s_{t+1} = f(s_t, a_t, ε_t)*) is used to simulate the future states *S^i_t* that would result from executing that plan.
3. **Objective Function Evaluation:** The explicit objective function *C(S^i_t, A^i_t)* is then used to evaluate the cost of each of the *K* simulated plans.
4. **Optimal Plan Selection:** The plan that yields the minimum objective function value among the *K* sampled plans is selected as the best plan for the current timestep (*A_hat_t*).
5. **Execution and Replanning:** As per standard MPC, only a subset of steps (up to *H*) from the chosen optimal plan *A_hat_t* is executed. The process then re-initializes, and a new planning problem is solved from the updated state, ensuring continuous adaptation.

The experimental evaluation of LLMPC was conducted across three distinct problem types:
- **Continuous Control (Spring-Mass System):** A physical system requiring precise control inputs to reach a target state. The objective function was explicitly defined (Equation 4). Experiments compared LLMPC against an exact MPC solver and analyzed the impact of varying the number of sampled plans (*K*).
- **Discrete/Combinatorial Planning (Trip Planning):** A problem from the Natural Plan benchmark [Zheng et al., 2025], involving city visits with duration, flight, and event constraints. This task assessed LLMPC's ability to iteratively refine plans using feedback.
- **Discrete/Combinatorial Planning (Meeting Planning):** Another problem from the Natural Plan benchmark [Zheng et al., 2025], focusing on scheduling meetings with temporal and spatial constraints. This experiment explored the combined effects of iterative refinement (*T*) and multiple plan sampling (*K*).

For the Natural Plan benchmarks, LLMPC employed a system prompt and an instruction prompt to provide the LLM with the problem description, few-shot examples, and feedback on previous plan attempts. An evaluation function was used to check constraints and generate feedback for subsequent iterations. GPT-4o-mini was used for the spring-mass system, and the specific LLM for Natural Plan benchmarks is implied to be GPT-4o based on comparisons.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from three experimental scenarios, demonstrating the behavior and performance of LLMPC.

#### 4.1. Control of Spring and Mass System

For the spring-mass system, the objective was to bring the system to a goal state with zero velocity using applied forces. Both MPC (solved exactly with CVXPY) and LLMPC (using GPT-4o-mini to suggest 5 plans per step) were evaluated.
- **Control Trajectory:** Both MPC and LLMPC successfully generated control sequences to guide the spring-mass system to the desired goal state (Figure 1a, 1b).
- **Objective Values:** The objective values from LLMPC-generated plans were higher than those from exact MPC, indicating that LLMs perform approximate planning.
- **Impact of Multiple Plan Sampling:** A key finding was the significant improvement in LLMPC performance by increasing the number of plans sampled (*K*) per iteration. When only a single plan was sampled (*K=1*), the mean objective ratio (LLMPC cost/MPC cost) was 8.21. This ratio substantially decreased as *K* increased: 2.89 for *K=5*, 1.42 for *K=10*, and 1.30 for *K=15* (Table 1, Figure 2). This suggests that explicitly evaluating multiple LLM-generated plans with a cost function can reduce the performance gap between approximate LLM planning and exact optimization.

#### 4.2. Trip Planning

LLMPC was evaluated on the trip-planning task from the Natural Plan benchmark, comparing its performance against a single-round few-shot prompt solution using GPT-4o. The LLMPC approach involved sequential iterations with feedback.
- **Success Rate Improvement:** LLMPC demonstrated improved success rates compared to single-round GPT-4o planning. The single-round approach achieved a 14.5% success rate. With LLMPC, the success rate increased with more iterations (*T*): 36.3% for *T=3*, 41.3% for *T=5*, and 44.6% for *T=7* (Table 2). This represents an improvement of up to 30.1 percentage points.
- **Complexity Handling:** The performance gains of LLMPC were more pronounced for problems with a higher number of cities, indicating its ability to handle increased task complexity through iterative refinement (Figure 3).

#### 4.3. Meeting Planning

This problem, also from the Natural Plan benchmark, required scheduling meetings with multiple friends across a city, accounting for temporal and spatial constraints to maximize successful meetings. LLMPC was compared against single-round few-shot prompting, with configurations exploring varying numbers of iterations (*T*) and plans sampled per iteration (*K*).
- **Consistent Improvements:** LLMPC provided consistent improvements over single-round planning, which achieved a 52.5% success rate.
- **Combined Effect of Iteration and Sampling:** The most significant performance gains were observed when both the number of iterations (*T*) and the number of plans sampled per iteration (*K*) were increased. The highest success rate of 67% was achieved with *T=9* and *K=3* (Table 3). This indicates that both iterative refinement and exploring multiple candidate solutions per iteration contribute to improved plan quality.
- **Complexity Adaptation:** Similar to trip planning, as the complexity of the meeting planning problem (number of friends/meetings) increased, the necessity for both iterative refinement and multiple plan sampling became more apparent for LLMPC to achieve higher success rates (Figure 4).

In summary, the results indicate that LLMs function as approximate planners. The LLMPC framework, by leveraging multiple plan sampling and iterative refinement guided by an explicit objective function, systematically improves planning performance across diverse tasks, particularly as problem complexity increases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces LLMPC, a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for planning with Large Language Models, offering both theoretical insights and practical advancements in the field of AI planning.

From a theoretical perspective, the work is significant for formally characterizing LLMs as approximate optimizers of planning cost functions. This establishes a structured understanding of how LLMs generate plans, bridging the gap between the empirical successes of LLM-based planning and the rigorous foundations of classical control theory. This perspective can serve as a basis for further theoretical investigations into the capabilities and limitations of LLMs in sequential decision-making.

In terms of practical impact, the LLMPC framework offers concrete methods to enhance the performance of LLM-based planners. The core contributions—sampling multiple plans from an LLM and selecting the best one based on an explicit objective function, combined with iterative refinement through replanning—have been empirically shown to improve solution quality across a range of tasks. For instance, the ability to reduce the objective gap in a continuous control problem by a factor of over six (from 8.21x to 1.3x) by increasing the number of sampled plans demonstrates a measurable improvement in planning accuracy. Similarly, the substantial increases in success rates for complex combinatorial problems like trip planning (up to 30.1%) and meeting scheduling (up to 14.5%) highlight the framework's effectiveness in domains where LLMs traditionally struggle with constraint satisfaction and long-horizon reasoning.

The demonstrated robustness of LLMPC across diverse problem types—from continuous control to discrete, constraint-heavy planning tasks—suggests its broad applicability. This could enable more reliable and capable autonomous agents in areas such as robotics, logistics, and complex task automation, where planning under uncertainty and constraints is critical.

Future research directions suggested by this work include exploring combinations of LLMPC with other planning techniques, such as beam search, which could potentially yield further performance improvements by expanding the search space more systematically. Additionally, investigating the use of learned cost functions and state update functions, particularly when explicit models are unavailable, could extend LLMPC's applicability to scenarios with incomplete domain knowledge.

Overall, the LLMPC framework provides a valuable contribution by formalizing the role of LLMs in planning within a robust control paradigm. It offers a pathway for developing more intelligent and reliable AI planning systems that leverage the generative flexibility of LLMs while adhering to the computational rigor of traditional control algorithms, thereby fostering interdisciplinary advancements between large language models and control theory.
