# Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Autonomous Robots via Mahalanobis SVDD with Audio-IMU Fusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University developed an unsupervised anomaly detection framework for autonomous robots that combines audio-IMU sensor fusion with a novel Mahalanobis Support Vector Data Description (M-SVDD) and a reconstruction branch. The approach achieved an F1-score of 92.3% and an AUC of 97.0% on a custom mobile robot dataset, demonstrating robust detection of collisions and mechanical faults while also generalizing effectively to other public datasets.

## Problem

- Existing vision and LiDAR-based anomaly detection methods for robots often degrade in adverse conditions and may not capture internal mechanical failures, while also being computationally intensive.
- A significant challenge in anomaly detection, especially for autonomous robots, is the scarcity of labeled anomaly data, as inducing faults for data collection is impractical or unethical.
- Conventional SVDD and Deep SVDD (DSVDD) methods suffer from 'hypersphere collapse' and rely on Euclidean distance, which implicitly assumes isotropic feature distributions and neglects inter-feature correlations.

## Method

- An unsupervised anomaly detection framework was developed that fuses features extracted from ==audio and IMU== sensor data using a ==cross-attention== mechanism, leveraging their complementary nature.
- A novel **M-SVDD** (==Mahalanobis Support Vector Data Description==) model was introduced, utilizing ==Mahalanobis distance== to learn an anisotropic, ellipsoidal decision boundary that accounts for inter-feature correlations and robustly estimates parameters using ==Minimum Covariance Determinant== (MCD).
- A ==reconstruction-based auxiliary branch== was incorporated to prevent representation collapse in the latent space and provide an additional anomaly metric based on reconstruction error.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an F1-score of **92.3%** and an AUC of **97.0%** on a custom mobile robot dataset, outperforming various shallow and deep learning baselines in detecting collision and mechanical fault anomalies.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the audio modality for recall, the M-SVDD module for effective anomaly detection, and the reconstruction branch for maintaining high precision and preventing representation collapse, with cross-attention fusion proving superior.
- The framework demonstrated generalizability, achieving the best average AUC of **81.5%** on the MIMII audio dataset and the highest F1-scores on two out of three multi-dimensional time-series datasets (SWaT: **82.0%**, SMAP: **94.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mahalanobis distance significantly enhances anomaly detection by capturing inter-feature correlations and varying feature dimensions, leading to more accurate, anisotropic decision boundaries for normal data.
> - Integrating a reconstruction branch effectively prevents hypersphere collapse in deep SVDD models, forcing the network to learn diverse and meaningful representations while also serving as an independent indicator of anomalous inputs.
> - Audio and IMU sensors provide complementary and robust information for anomaly detection in robots, addressing limitations of vision-based systems in challenging environments and effectively identifying both external collisions and internal mechanical faults.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Anomaly detection is a critical component for ensuring the safety and reliability of various safety-critical systems, including industrial automation, aerospace, and especially autonomous robotic systems. The ability of autonomous mobile robots to detect unforeseen events, such as mechanical faults or unexpected collisions, is important for maintaining safe operations and mitigating potential hazards, particularly as robots are increasingly deployed in dynamic and unstructured environments.

Current advancements in anomaly detection for robotic systems have primarily leveraged vision sensors (RGB cameras) and LiDAR. While these modalities have demonstrated performance in controlled settings, their efficacy can degrade significantly under adverse environmental conditions, such as variable illumination, extreme weather, occlusions, or sensor degradation. Furthermore, vision and LiDAR-based methods often concentrate on semantic or spatial anomalies, which may not adequately capture internal system failures like mechanical issues or vibration abnormalities. These methods can also require substantial computational resources and expensive sensing equipment, limiting their applicability on platforms with restricted resources.

In response to these limitations, alternative sensing modalities are being explored. Audio and Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) sensors represent underexplored options for anomaly detection in robotic systems. Acoustic sensors can capture sound signatures indicative of abnormal events (e.g., collisions, friction, mechanical faults), while IMU sensors can detect anomalous vibrations and accelerations arising from similar incidents, providing complementary information. Both audio and IMU sensors are generally energy-efficient and cost-effective, making them suitable for widespread robotic deployment.

Another fundamental challenge in anomaly detection, particularly for autonomous robots, is the scarcity of labeled anomaly data. Most existing anomaly detection approaches rely on supervised learning, necessitating large, annotated datasets of diverse anomalies. However, collecting such datasets in robotic contexts is often impractical, costly, or unethical, as robots typically operate under normal, safe conditions. While semi-supervised and few-shot learning methods address data limitations, their generalization to entirely novel, unpredictable anomaly types remains a challenge. This underscores the need for robust unsupervised anomaly detection methods that can identify various types of anomalies without requiring prior labeled anomaly data.

This paper builds upon the foundation of Support Vector Data Description (SVDD) and its deep learning extension, Deep SVDD (DSVDD). SVDD is a classical unsupervised method that aims to enclose normal data points within a minimal hypersphere. DSVDD extends this by employing deep neural networks to learn high-level latent representations. However, conventional DSVDD methods exhibit limitations, including the issue of "hypersphere collapse," where the network may learn trivial representations, and reliance on Euclidean distance, which implicitly assumes isotropic feature distributions and neglects inter-feature correlations. This work proposes an unsupervised framework that specifically addresses these issues by introducing Mahalanobis distance and a reconstruction-based auxiliary branch, integrated with audio-IMU sensor fusion, to enhance anomaly detection capabilities in autonomous robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop a robust unsupervised anomaly detection framework for autonomous mobile robots.** The aim is to create a system that can reliably identify various anomalies, such as collisions and internal mechanical faults, using only data collected during normal robot operation, thereby circumventing the need for labeled anomaly data.
2. **Leverage audio and IMU sensor data for anomaly detection.** The motivation is to explore and utilize these alternative modalities to address the limitations of vision and LiDAR-based systems in adverse conditions (e.g., poor lighting, extreme weather, occlusions) and to capture internal system malfunctions that are not typically addressed by visual or spatial anomaly detection. Audio sensors can detect distinct sound signatures of abnormal events, while IMU sensors can capture unusual vibrations and accelerations.
3. **Propose a novel Mahalanobis Support Vector Data Description (M-SVDD) model.** This objective is driven by the limitations of conventional SVDD and DSVDD methods, which rely on Euclidean distance and assume isotropic feature distributions. The M-SVDD model aims to adaptively scale feature dimensions and capture inter-feature correlations using Mahalanobis distance, enabling the learning of more expressive and anisotropic ellipsoidal decision boundaries that better reflect the true distribution of normal data.
4. **Incorporate a reconstruction-based auxiliary branch to enhance feature representation and prevent representation collapse.** A common challenge in DSVDD-based methods is hypersphere collapse, where the network may learn degenerate feature representations that map all samples to a single point. The reconstruction branch is motivated by the need to compel the network to learn diverse and meaningful feature representations, thereby improving the robustness and accuracy of anomaly detection. It also provides an additional anomaly score based on reconstruction error, as anomalous inputs are expected to be poorly reconstructed by a model trained solely on normal data.

The overarching motivation for this work is to enhance the safety and reliability of autonomous robots operating in dynamic and unpredictable environments. By providing a robust, unsupervised, and resource-efficient anomaly detection solution that utilizes complementary sensing modalities and addresses inherent limitations of existing SVDD-based methods, the research aims to contribute to the broader deployment and trustworthy operation of autonomous robotic systems in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces an unsupervised anomaly detection framework that fuses audio and IMU sensor data. The architecture operates in two main phases: training (using only normal data) and inference.

**Overall Architecture:**
The network integrates parallel branches for feature extraction, fusion, Mahalanobis Support Vector Data Description (M-SVDD), and signal reconstruction.

**A. Audio-IMU Feature Extraction:**
1. **Input Data:** Binaural audio signals ($A \in R^{L_A \times 2}$) and the Z-axis velocity of IMU data ($I \in R^{L_I}$). The Z-axis velocity is chosen for its representativeness of anomaly features, and the signals are segmented into 2-second intervals. Audio is downsampled to 4410 Hz, and IMU data is collected at 200 Hz.
2. **Encoders:**
 - An audio encoder ($f(A; \theta_{AE})$) consisting of six convolutional layers and an LSTM layer is used to extract long-range temporal dependencies and local features from audio sequences.
 - An IMU encoder ($f(I; \theta_{IE})$) composed of two LSTM layers is used to extract temporal dynamics from IMU data.
3. **Feature Fusion:**
 - Extracted audio features ($z_A \in R^d$) and IMU features ($z_I \in R^d$) are fed into a cross-attention mechanism.
 - The cross-attention module calculates attention scores between the query, key, and value vectors of the two modalities, producing inter-modal representations ($z_{IA}$, $z_{AI}$). This aims to capture interactions and shared relevant features.
 - These cross-attended features are then summed and projected into an s-dimensional latent space ($z \in R^s$) via a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) layer, forming the fused audio-IMU feature.

**B. Mahalanobis Support Vector Data Description (M-SVDD):**
1. **Objective:** To project the fused features ($z$) of normal data into a compact ellipsoidal hyperspace with a minimum radius. Anomalous samples are expected to lie outside this boundary.
2. **Mahalanobis Distance:** Instead of Euclidean distance, Mahalanobis distance ($D_i = \sqrt{(z_i - \mu_z)^T \Sigma_z^{-1} (z_i - \mu_z)}$) is employed. This distance metric accounts for the variance of each feature dimension and the inter-dependencies (correlations) between dimensions by incorporating the inverse of the feature covariance matrix ($\Sigma_z^{-1}$). This enables the model to learn an anisotropic, ellipsoidal boundary that aligns more effectively with the actual distribution of normal data.
3. **Robust Parameter Estimation:** To mitigate the impact of outliers in the training data, the Minimum Covariance Determinant (MCD) estimator is used to estimate the mean ($\mu_z$) and covariance matrix ($\Sigma_z$) of the latent features. MCD selects a subset of samples whose covariance matrix has the minimum determinant, making the estimates robust to noise and transient disturbances. A regularization matrix ($\epsilon I$) is added to prevent singularity of the covariance matrix.
4. **M-SVDD Loss:** A soft boundary loss function is utilized for M-SVDD: $L_{M S V D D} = R^2 + \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N Max(0, D_i^2 - R^2)$, where R is a learnable radius parameter.

**C. Reconstruction and Regularization:**
1. **Reconstruction Branch:** Dedicated decoders (deconvolutional for audio, LSTM for IMU) are used to reconstruct the original input signals ($\hat{A}, \hat{I}$) from their respective latent features ($z_A, z_I$).
2. **Benefits of Reconstruction:**
 - **Mitigates Hypersphere Collapse:** By forcing the network to accurately reconstruct inputs, it is compelled to learn diverse and meaningful feature representations, preventing the issue where all latent features collapse to a single point.
 - **Additional Anomaly Metric:** Anomalous inputs, not seen during normal-data training, are expected to yield significantly higher reconstruction errors compared to normal data, providing an independent signal for anomaly detection.
3. **Reconstruction Loss:** Huber loss ($L_{Huber}$) is used for reconstruction: $L_{Rec} = L_{Huber}(A, \hat{A}) + L_{Huber}(I, \hat{I})$.
4. **Regularization Loss:** A regularization loss ($L_{Reg}$) is introduced to enhance numerical stability and feature separation. It penalizes small diagonal entries of the covariance matrix and large conditional numbers ($L_{Reg} = \sum_{i=1}^s (\frac{1}{\sigma_i} + \epsilon) + (\frac{\lambda_{max}}{\lambda_{min}} + \epsilon)$, where $\sigma$ represents diagonal values, and $\lambda_{max}$, $\lambda_{min}$ are the largest and smallest eigenvalues of $\Sigma_z$).
5. **Overall Loss Function:** The total loss for training is a weighted sum of the M-SVDD loss, reconstruction loss, and regularization loss: $L_{total} = \alpha_1 L_{M S V D D} + \alpha_2 L_{Rec} + \alpha_3 L_{Reg}$.

**D. Network Inference:**
1. **Anomaly Score Calculation:** For an input sample, the final anomaly score ($\delta_i$) is computed as a weighted sum of its Mahalanobis distance ($D_i$) and its normalized reconstruction loss ($L_{i}^{Rec}$): $\delta_i = D_i + w \frac{\mu_D^T}{\mu_{L_{Rec}}^T} L_i^{Rec}$. $\mu_D^T$ and $\mu_{L_{Rec}}^T$ are the mean distance and reconstruction loss on the training dataset for normalization.
2. **Anomaly Threshold:** The anomaly threshold ($\delta^*$) is set as the 95th percentile of the anomaly scores observed across all training samples.
3. **Classification:** A test sample is classified as anomalous if its anomaly score ($\delta_{test}$) exceeds the predefined threshold ($\delta^*$).

**Data Collection and Experimental Setup:**
A new multimodal dataset was collected using a mobile robot platform, including image, audio, LiDAR, and IMU data under both normal and two types of abnormal conditions: collisions with objects and internal mechanical wheel faults. Data preprocessing includes segmentation, audio downsampling, IMU Z-axis selection, Z-score normalization, and smoothing. The evaluation metrics used are Precision, Recall, F1-score, and Area Under the Curve (AUC), including class-specific AUCs for collision and mechanical faults.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a series of experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed Mahalanobis SVDD (M-SVDD) framework with Audio-IMU fusion on a custom-collected mobile robot dataset and four public datasets.

**Performance on Mobile Robot Dataset:**
- **Overall Performance (Table I, Fig. 8):** The proposed method achieved an F1-score of 92.3%, a recall of 97.5%, and a precision of 87.7% on the collected mobile robot dataset. This represents the highest performance among all compared methods, which included traditional shallow machine learning techniques (Isolation Forest, OCSVM, SVDD) and various deep learning approaches (DeepForest, DAGMM, DAE, VAE-SVDD, DSVDD). The method also yielded the highest overall Area Under Curve (AUC) of 97.0%, indicating a favorable balance between true positive and false positive rates across different thresholds.
- **Anomaly Type Specificity (Table I):** The proposed method demonstrated robust detection across different anomaly types, achieving the highest AUC for collision-related anomalies (98.5%) and strong performance on mechanical-failure-related anomalies (95.5%), outperforming other deep learning methods in both categories. Shallow machine learning methods struggled particularly with collision anomalies.

**Ablation Studies (Table II, Table III):**
- **Modality Contribution (Table II):**
 - Removing the audio modality resulted in a substantial decrease in recall from 97.5% to 64.4%, highlighting the critical role of audio data in detecting anomalies.
 - Excluding IMU data led to a minor reduction in recall (by 1.2%) and F1-score (by 0.7%), indicating its supportive contribution to overall performance.
- **Module Effectiveness (Table II):**
 - Excluding the M-SVDD module caused a drop in recall to 91.9%, demonstrating its importance for effective detection of complex anomalies.
 - Removing the reconstruction module resulted in a significant decrease in precision (to 81.7%) and F1-score (to 88.8%), underscoring its role in preventing representation collapse and maintaining high precision.
 - The complete model, integrating both M-SVDD and the reconstruction branch, achieved the best performance, suggesting a synergistic effect in balancing recall and precision.
- **Fusion Strategy (Table III):** The cross-attention feature fusion module outperformed other fusion strategies (concatenation, element-wise sum, element-wise product), achieving the highest F1-score (92.3%). This indicates that cross-attention effectively integrates relevant information from both audio and IMU modalities.

**Performance Analysis (Fig. 7, 10, 11):**
- **Feature Separability (Fig. 7, 10):** Comparative analysis in the feature space revealed that M-SVDD effectively concentrated normal samples within the principal components while anomalies exhibited broader variance, enhancing separability compared to DSVDD. M-SVDD successfully captured inter-feature correlations in normal data, a characteristic absent in anomaly samples. t-SNE projections further demonstrated that M-SVDD formed more compact and well-separated clusters for normal and anomalous data.
- **Hyperspace Dimension (Fig. 11):** Increasing the latent space dimension from 2 to 32 consistently improved performance (AUC and F1-score). However, further increases beyond 32 led to a slight performance degradation, possibly due to feature redundancy and increased difficulty in stable covariance matrix estimation. An optimal trade-off was observed with dimensions between 16 and 32.

**Experiments on Public Datasets:**
- **MIMII Dataset (Audio) (Table IV, Fig. 9):** On the MIMII dataset, which contains industrial machine sound recordings, the proposed M-SVDD framework achieved the best average AUC score of 81.5%. It outperformed other methods across various machine types (slide rail: 85.8%, fan: 75.6%, pump: 83.1%), demonstrating its generalization capability beyond mobile robots and its effectiveness in audio-based anomaly detection.
- **Multi-Dimensional Time-Series Datasets (Table V):** When evaluated on three multi-dimensional time-series datasets (SWaT, SMAP, MSL), the method achieved the highest F1-scores on SWaT (82.0%) and SMAP (94.8%). On MSL, it achieved a competitive F1-score of 98.1%, slightly lower than the top baseline but still outperforming other deep learning approaches. These results further support the robustness and generalizability of the proposed method across diverse anomaly detection tasks and data modalities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a significant contribution to the field of anomaly detection for autonomous robots, with several implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability of Autonomous Robots:** By enabling timely detection of critical anomalies such as collisions and internal mechanical faults, the proposed framework can substantially enhance the safety and reliability of autonomous robots. This can prevent potential hazards, reduce damage, and ensure continuous, safe operation, which is paramount for real-world deployment in sensitive or dynamic environments.
2. **Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The utilization of audio and IMU sensors provides an alternative and complementary approach to traditional vision and LiDAR-based systems. This is particularly impactful in adverse conditions (e.g., poor lighting, extreme weather, occlusions) where visual sensors may become unreliable. The cost-effectiveness and energy efficiency of audio and IMU sensors also make the solution more practical for resource-constrained robotic platforms.
3. **Addresses Data Scarcity through Unsupervised Learning:** A key challenge in anomaly detection is the limited availability of labeled anomaly data, especially in robotic systems where inducing faults for data collection is impractical, costly, or unethical. The proposed unsupervised framework circumvents this limitation by training solely on normal operational data, making it highly applicable for real-world scenarios where diverse anomaly data is difficult to acquire.
4. **Methodological Advancement in Anomaly Detection:** The introduction of Mahalanobis SVDD (M-SVDD) represents a methodological advancement over conventional SVDD and Deep SVDD (DSVDD). By using Mahalanobis distance, the model learns an anisotropic, ellipsoidal boundary that accounts for inter-feature correlations and adaptive scaling, providing a more precise and robust characterization of normal data distributions. The integration of a reconstruction branch effectively mitigates the issue of representation collapse and contributes to more diverse and discriminative feature learning.
5. **Improved Multimodal Data Fusion:** The demonstrated effectiveness of the cross-attention mechanism for fusing audio and IMU data highlights its ability to integrate complementary information from diverse sensor modalities. This enhances the system's sensitivity to anomalies that manifest across multiple sensing channels, leading to more comprehensive detection.
6. **Demonstrated Generalizability:** The successful application and competitive performance of the framework on diverse public datasets, including industrial audio (MIMII) and various multi-dimensional time-series data (SWaT, SMAP, MSL), suggest its robustness and generalizability beyond the specific domain of mobile robot anomaly detection. This indicates that the core principles of M-SVDD and the reconstruction branch are broadly applicable to other unsupervised anomaly detection tasks.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several limitations, such as susceptibility to external noise affecting both modalities, inconsistencies and timing misalignments between sensors, and lack of contextual reasoning. These limitations provide a clear roadmap for future research, including cross-modal alignment methods, context-aware anomaly detection, integration of additional sensing modalities (visual, thermal), and the potential use of large language models for zero-shot anomaly classification, which could further improve interpretability and actionability in real-world applications.

In summary, this work provides a practical, robust, and methodologically innovative approach to unsupervised anomaly detection for autonomous robots, addressing critical challenges related to environmental conditions, data availability, and the limitations of existing techniques.
