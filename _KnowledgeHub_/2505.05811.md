# Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Autonomous Robots via Mahalanobis SVDD with Audio-IMU Fusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University developed an unsupervised anomaly detection framework for autonomous robots that combines audio-IMU sensor fusion with a novel Mahalanobis Support Vector Data Description (M-SVDD) and a reconstruction branch. The approach achieved an F1-score of 92.3% and an AUC of 97.0% on a custom mobile robot dataset, demonstrating robust detection of collisions and mechanical faults while also generalizing effectively to other public datasets.

## Problem

- Existing vision and LiDAR-based anomaly detection methods for robots often degrade in adverse conditions and may not capture internal mechanical failures, while also being computationally intensive.
- A significant challenge in anomaly detection, especially for autonomous robots, is the scarcity of labeled anomaly data, as inducing faults for data collection is impractical or unethical.
- Conventional SVDD and Deep SVDD (DSVDD) methods suffer from 'hypersphere collapse' and rely on Euclidean distance, which implicitly assumes isotropic feature distributions and neglects inter-feature correlations.

## Method

- An unsupervised anomaly detection framework was developed that fuses features extracted from ==audio and IMU== sensor data using a ==cross-attention== mechanism, leveraging their complementary nature.
- A novel **M-SVDD** (==Mahalanobis Support Vector Data Description==) model was introduced, utilizing ==Mahalanobis distance== to learn an anisotropic, ellipsoidal decision boundary that accounts for inter-feature correlations and robustly estimates parameters using ==Minimum Covariance Determinant== (MCD).
- A ==reconstruction-based auxiliary branch== was incorporated to prevent representation collapse in the latent space and provide an additional anomaly metric based on reconstruction error.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an F1-score of **92.3%** and an AUC of **97.0%** on a custom mobile robot dataset, outperforming various shallow and deep learning baselines in detecting collision and mechanical fault anomalies.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the audio modality for recall, the M-SVDD module for effective anomaly detection, and the reconstruction branch for maintaining high precision and preventing representation collapse, with cross-attention fusion proving superior.
- The framework demonstrated generalizability, achieving the best average AUC of **81.5%** on the MIMII audio dataset and the highest F1-scores on two out of three multi-dimensional time-series datasets (SWaT: **82.0%**, SMAP: **94.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mahalanobis distance significantly enhances anomaly detection by capturing inter-feature correlations and varying feature dimensions, leading to more accurate, anisotropic decision boundaries for normal data.
> - Integrating a reconstruction branch effectively prevents hypersphere collapse in deep SVDD models, forcing the network to learn diverse and meaningful representations while also serving as an independent indicator of anomalous inputs.
> - Audio and IMU sensors provide complementary and robust information for anomaly detection in robots, addressing limitations of vision-based systems in challenging environments and effectively identifying both external collisions and internal mechanical faults.
