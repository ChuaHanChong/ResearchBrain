# PartInstruct: Part-level Instruction Following for Fine-grained Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PartInstruct introduces the first large-scale benchmark and dataset for vision-language policy learning in fine-grained, part-level robot manipulation. Its evaluation highlights that current end-to-end models struggle with these tasks, while bi-level planning, especially with explicit 3D part information, shows improved performance.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation benchmarks primarily focus on high-level, object-instance-level tasks and lack fine-grained, part-level interactions with natural language instructions.
- Current datasets for part-level manipulation often lack diverse 3D object instances, corresponding natural language instructions, or sufficient training data for policy learning.
- Robots struggle to understand and interact with specific sub-components of objects, which is crucial for complex, human-like manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **PartInstruct** introduces a large-scale benchmark for vision-language policy learning in fine-grained, part-level robot manipulation.
- A custom simulator, ==PartGym==, provides a 7-DoF robot, diverse 3D objects from PartNet Mobility, and multimodal observations including ==part segmentation masks==.
- The dataset includes over 10,000 expert demonstrations, each with high-level task instructions and granular part-based skill instructions, generated with ==GPT-4o paraphrasing== for linguistic diversity.

## Results

- State-of-the-art end-to-end vision-language policies achieved low success rates (**1.11%** to **15.40%**) across all test sets, struggling particularly with long-horizon and unseen object categories.
- Bi-level planning approaches significantly outperformed end-to-end methods, achieving overall success rates of **21.90%** to **31.72%**, with Gemini-2.0 Flash + DP3-S showing the highest performance.
- Low-level policies leveraging 3D part point clouds (DP3-S) achieved **20.97%** success, outperforming policies using 2D part masks or no explicit part information, highlighting the importance of 3D visual input.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bi-level planning significantly outperforms end-to-end approaches for fine-grained manipulation, indicating the benefit of decomposing complex tasks into simpler, part-level subgoals.
> - Explicit part segmentation, particularly using 3D representations (e.g., 3D point clouds), substantially improves the performance of low-level action policies.
> - Spatial part references (e.g., 'left', 'right') are considerably more challenging for models to learn than semantic part names (e.g., 'handle', 'lid') due to their dynamic and relative nature.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen significant advancements, particularly with the rise of vision-language policies that allow robots to follow natural language instructions. However, as the paper highlights, a critical gap exists in the ability of current robotic systems to perform *fine-grained* manipulation tasks involving specific object parts.

**Existing Benchmarks and Their Limitations:**
Prior benchmarks for tabletop robot manipulation, such as CALVIN, RLbench, VIMAbench, and LoHoRavens, have primarily focused on high-level, object-instance-level tasks. These often involve rearranging entire objects or performing simple interactions without requiring detailed understanding or manipulation of object sub-components. For example, a task might involve "picking up the block" rather than "grasping the block by its handle." While some benchmarks like ManiSkill, PartManip, and Open6DOR have introduced part-level manipulation, they generally fall short in one or more crucial aspects: they either lack corresponding natural language instructions (e.g., ManiSkill), do not provide training data for policy learning (e.g., Open6DOR outputs only goal positions), or have limited diversity in objects and tasks.

**Addressing the Gap:**
"PartInstruct" directly addresses this void by introducing the *first large-scale benchmark* explicitly designed for *vision-language policy learning* in the context of *fine-grained, part-level robot manipulation*. This positions the work at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields:
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Robotics:** Leveraging the power of large generative models (e.g., RT-1, Octo, OpenVLA) to interpret and execute complex instructions. PartInstruct pushes this frontier by demanding fine-grained semantic and spatial reasoning from these models.
- **Part-aware Robotics:** Moving beyond whole-object manipulation to enable dexterous interaction with specific components, which is crucial for real-world tasks. This aligns with recent efforts like Composable Part-based Manipulation (CPM) and SAGE, but PartInstruct uniquely integrates this with language instruction following on a large scale.
- **Hierarchical Planning:** The paper explores bi-level planning approaches, a common strategy in robotics to break down complex tasks into manageable subgoals. By providing part-level skill annotations, PartInstruct facilitates the development and evaluation of such hierarchical systems, where high-level VLMs handle task decomposition and low-level policies execute specific part-based skills.

In essence, PartInstruct acts as a crucial stepping stone, bridging the gap between general language-guided object manipulation and the precise, part-aware dexterity needed for more complex and human-like robotic operations. It sets a new standard for evaluating the capabilities of embodied AI models in understanding and interacting with the nuances of object geometry and functionality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PartInstruct stems from the observation that while general-purpose robot manipulation policies guided by language instructions have advanced, they notably lack the capability for *fine-grained manipulation* of diverse everyday objects. Such tasks frequently require robots not only to identify a target object but also to understand, reason about, and interact with specific *parts* of that object. For instance, instructing a robot to "rotate the bottle to show me the cap" necessitates identifying the cap, understanding its current orientation, and executing precise movements to reorient it without occluding it, potentially by grasping another part of the bottle.

The **key objectives** of this research are threefold:

1. **Develop a comprehensive benchmark for fine-grained robot manipulation:** To address the notable absence of large-scale datasets that integrate part-level instructions with diverse 3D object instances annotated with detailed part-level labels. This benchmark, named PartInstruct, aims to facilitate both the training and rigorous evaluation of robot manipulation models in this challenging domain.
2. **Provide a rich, large-scale training dataset:** To support the development of sophisticated vision-language policies, PartInstruct offers over 10,000 expert demonstrations synthesized in a 3D simulator (PartGym). Each demonstration is uniquely paired with a high-level task instruction, a granular chain of base part-based skill instructions (e.g., "grasp the handle," "touch the left side"), and ground-truth 3D information about objects and their parts. This rich annotation aims to enable models to learn both long-horizon planning and precise base manipulation skills.
3. **Evaluate and reveal limitations of current state-of-the-art methods:** To establish baselines and identify the shortcomings of existing end-to-end vision-language policy learning and bi-level planning models when confronted with part-level manipulation tasks. This involves designing a comprehensive test suite to evaluate generalizability across novel states, objects, and tasks, thereby pinpointing specific areas for future research and improvement in robot perception, reasoning, and planning.

The overarching motivation is to push the boundaries of robot capabilities from simple object-level interactions to more complex, dexterous, and semantically-aware manipulation, moving closer to robots that can seamlessly operate in human environments and follow nuanced instructions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PartInstruct introduces a structured approach to benchmarking fine-grained robot manipulation, encompassing a novel problem setup, a custom simulation environment, a large-scale dataset, and a multi-faceted evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The core of the problem setup defines an "object part" as a functionally manipulable or spatially distinct sub-component (e.g., handle, left side). A "part-level instruction following task" ($I_{task}$) requires a robot to interact with a list of object parts to achieve a goal ($g$). Goals are defined using a set of "goal predicates" (e.g., `ON`, `TOUCHING`, `GRASPING`, `FACING`, `AT_POSITION`), specifying the states and relationships of objects, parts, and the robot's end-effector. Critically, relevant parts and interaction methods are often implicit in $I_{task}$, necessitating robust reasoning.
To tackle complex tasks, the authors hypothesize that decomposing them into "base skills" is beneficial. Five base skills are defined: `grasp_obj`, `move_gripper`, `rotate_obj`, `touch_obj`, and `release_gripper`. Each skill is parameterized by object parts, interaction types, and spatial directives, and associated with a "skill instruction" ($I_{skill}$). During training, tasks are provided as sequences of these $I_{skill}$, while during evaluation, only the high-level $I_{task}$ is given.

#### 3.2. Simulation Environment (PartGym)

PartGym is a custom robot simulator built upon Pybullet, designed for fine-grained, part-level instruction following. It features:
- **Robot:** A 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robot with a two-finger parallel gripper.
- **Objects:** Rich 3D assets of everyday objects from the PartNet Mobility dataset, annotated with part-level information.
- **Observations:** Multimodal observations including RGB images, depth maps, scene point clouds, 2D part segmentation masks, and 3D part point clouds. Proprioceptive robot states (joint states, end-effector poses) are also available.
- **Action Space:** 7D vector for the end-effector's Cartesian pose (position + roll/pitch/yaw) and gripper position.

#### 3.3. Dataset Generation

The PartInstruct dataset is a cornerstone of the benchmark:
- **Assets:** 513 object instances across 14 categories, totaling 4,653 part labels. Visual diversity across objects and parts is emphasized.
- **Demonstrations:** Over 10,000 training demonstrations and 1,800 evaluation episodes. Each episode comprises an observation set, an expert action trajectory, an $I_{task}$, and a sequence of $I_{skill}$ (for training).
- **Task Categories:** 16 task categories (10 seen for training, 6 unseen for testing), ranging from direct part interaction (e.g., "Hold [part] the object...") to complex tasks requiring state changes (e.g., "Rotate the object such that [part] is facing [direction]") and long-horizon plans with multiple parts.
- **Generation Process:**
 - Expert trajectories are generated by an oracle high-level planner for base skills, utilizing BiRRT for motion planning and a grasping point detection method.
 - $I_{task}$ are initially template-based and then paraphrased by GPT-4o to increase natural language diversity (3-8 variants per template). $I_{skill}$ follow fixed templates.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol

A rigorous five-level evaluation protocol assesses model generalizability:
- **Test 1 (OS - Object States):** Novel object positions and rotations.
- **Test 2 (OI - Object Instances):** Novel object instances within seen categories.
- **Test 3 (TP - Part Combinations):** Novel part combinations within seen task categories.
- **Test 4 (TC - Task Categories):** Novel part-level manipulation task categories (unseen tasks).
- **Test 5 (OC - Object Categories):** Novel object categories (unseen objects).
Success is binary at the skill level; task completion requires all skills in the chain to succeed.

#### 3.5. Evaluated Approaches

The paper evaluates two main types of robot manipulation approaches:
- **End-to-End Policy Learning:** Direct mapping from observation and instruction to actions. Baselines include Octo (fine-tuned), Act3D, RVT2, 3D Diffuser Actor (3D-DA), Diffusion Policy (DP), and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3). DP and DP3 were modified to accept language inputs via a T5 language encoder.
- **Bi-Level Planning:** Decomposes a task into high-level subgoals and low-level action plans.
 - **High-Level Task Planner:** Utilizes large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5 Flash, Gemini-2.0 Flash) to infer the next $I_{skill}$ (subgoal) given $I_{task}$, current observations, and executed skill chains. Subgoals are updated periodically.
 - **Low-Level Action Policy:** Vision-language policies (variants of DP and DP3, termed DP-S and DP3-S) trained on $I_{skill}$ to generate low-level actions. These variants incorporate explicit part segmentation as additional visual input:
 - **DP-S:** Uses a 2D part segmentation mask generated and tracked by Grounded-SAM-2 (leveraging Florence-2 for grounding).
 - **DP3-S:** Uses a 3D part point cloud, obtained by lifting 2D SAM2 masks to 3D via depth maps.
 - **Code-as-Policies (CaP):** An alternative bi-level approach where an LLM (GPT-4o) composes API calls (skill primitives) for an oracle motion planner.

The methodology provides a robust framework for understanding the challenges and potential solutions for fine-grained robot manipulation, systematically evaluating various policy architectures and the role of part-level visual information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on PartInstruct reveal significant challenges for current robot manipulation models in handling fine-grained, part-level instructions.

#### 4.1. Performance of End-to-End Policy Learning

- **Low Success Rates:** All evaluated state-of-the-art end-to-end vision-language policies (Octo, Act3D, RVT2, 3D-DA, DP, DP3) achieved notably low success rates, ranging from 1.11% to 15.40% across all test sets.
- **Generalization Challenges:** These models particularly struggled with long-horizon tasks (Test 4: novel task categories, often multi-step) and generalizing to entirely unseen object types (Test 5: novel object categories).
- **Zero-Shot Failure:** Zero-shot inference using generalist policies like RT-1, Octo, and OpenVLA resulted in near-zero success rates, highlighting the critical absence of part-level skills and detailed spatial reasoning in their pre-training data. This underscores the value of PartInstruct's annotated dataset.

#### 4.2. Performance of Bi-Level Planning

- **Significant Outperformance:** Bi-level planning baselines significantly outperformed end-to-end learning approaches, achieving overall success rates ranging from 21.90% to 31.72%. This demonstrates the effectiveness of decomposing complex tasks into simpler, part-level subgoals.
- **Effectiveness of Low-Level Action Policies with Part Information:**
 - When low-level action policies were paired with *ground-truth skill instructions* (bypassing the high-level planner's inference challenges), explicit part segmentation provided a substantial performance boost.
 - **DP3-S** (3D Diffusion Policy with 3D part point clouds) emerged as the best-performing low-level policy, achieving 20.97% success, significantly higher than vanilla DP3 (12.98%). This highlights the superiority of 3D representations and the benefit of explicit part information.
 - **DP-S** (Diffusion Policy with 2D part masks) also showed improvements over vanilla DP.
 - The performance gap between ground-truth mask-based policies (DP-S GT, DP3-S GT) and SAM2-based mask policies (DP-S SAM2, DP3-S SAM2) suggests room for improvement in general-purpose part segmentation and grounding models.
- **Performance of High-Level Task Planners (VLMs):**
 - When paired with the best low-level policy (DP3-S), **Gemini-2.0 Flash + DP3-S** achieved the highest overall success rate (31.72%), demonstrating its superior high-level planning capabilities compared to GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5 Flash.
 - Despite improvements, bi-level planning still struggled with very long-horizon tasks (Test 4), where cumulative errors from the VLM's high-level planning or the low-level policy's execution become more pronounced.
- **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **VLM + Oracle Motion Planner vs. VLM + Learned Policy:** Interestingly, VLMs paired with an *oracle motion planner* (which completes each skill perfectly) performed worse than when paired with a *learned low-level policy*. This counter-intuitive result is attributed to the learned policy's shorter execution windows for subgoals, allowing the VLM more frequent opportunities to correct mistaken instructions before errors accumulate significantly. The oracle, conversely, commits to finishing potentially incorrect subgoals.
 - **Impact of Visual Inputs:** Explicit part segmentation (2D or 3D masks) consistently and significantly boosted low-level policy performance. 3D representations (point clouds) proved more effective than 2D images for these precise manipulation tasks.

#### 4.3. Challenges in Learning Part-Level Skills

An impact study on skill types and object parts revealed:
- **Skill Difficulty:** "Grasp" and "Touch" skills achieved over 50% success rates, likely because they involve direct physical contact. In contrast, "Rotate" skills achieved only 18.2%, as they often require indirect manipulation (e.g., grasping a different part to rotate the target part) and inferring complex goal orientations.
- **Part Difficulty:** Spatial parts (e.g., "left," "right") were much more challenging to learn than semantic parts (e.g., "handle," "lid"). This is because spatial references are relative and change dynamically as the object moves, demanding a sophisticated understanding of spatial context and persistent tracking of a moving frame of reference.

In summary, PartInstruct clearly demonstrates that fine-grained, part-level robot manipulation remains a formidable challenge. While bi-level planning offers a more promising pathway than pure end-to-end learning, both high-level VLM planning and robust low-level part grounding in 3D are critical areas requiring further research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PartInstruct represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, with far-reaching implications for future research and the development of more capable robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Benchmark Contribution

The most immediate impact of PartInstruct is its establishment as the *first large-scale benchmark* that meticulously integrates part-level semantics with language-guided robot manipulation for both training and evaluation. This fills a crucial void in existing datasets and provides a standardized, challenging environment to measure progress in this domain. The dataset's diversity in objects, part annotations, and task complexities (including long-horizon and complex reasoning tasks) makes it a robust testbed for next-generation robot policies.

#### 5.2. Highlighting Current Limitations and Guiding Future Research

The comprehensive evaluation of state-of-the-art models vividly exposes their current limitations in fine-grained manipulation. The low success rates of end-to-end policies, especially on generalization tasks, clearly indicate that these models struggle with:
- **Robust Part-Level Grounding:** Accurately identifying and localizing specific object parts based on language cues and maintaining this understanding as the robot interacts with the object.
- **Fine-Grained Spatial Reasoning:** Understanding relative spatial directions (e.g., "left," "front") that change dynamically with object movement.
- **Long-Horizon Planning:** Decomposing complex, multi-step tasks into executable sub-skills and maintaining coherence over extended sequences of actions.

By pinpointing these deficiencies, PartInstruct serves as a clear roadmap for future research, urging the community to focus on perception models that can provide more precise 3D part information, and planning algorithms that can robustly reason over complex, multi-stage interactions.

#### 5.3. Validation of Bi-Level Planning and Perception Importance

The superior performance of bi-level planning models over end-to-end approaches suggests that a hierarchical decomposition of the control problem is a more effective strategy for complex manipulation tasks. This validates the intuition that separating high-level task planning (handled by VLMs) from low-level skill execution (handled by learned policies) can simplify the learning problem. Furthermore, the ablation studies powerfully demonstrate the critical role of *explicit visual representations* of object parts (especially 3D part segmentations) in boosting low-level action policy performance. This emphasizes that advancements in 3D vision and part segmentation are paramount for robust fine-grained manipulation.

#### 5.4. Implications for Foundation Models in Robotics

The findings highlight the potential and current limitations of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robotic planning. While VLMs show promise in abstract reasoning and generating subgoals, their performance on very long-horizon tasks and their inability to recover effectively from early planning mistakes indicate areas for significant improvement. This work will stimulate research into more robust VLM-based planners capable of real-time adaptation, error detection, and recovery.

#### 5.5. Enabling More Dexterous and Human-like Robot Interaction

Ultimately, the development of robots capable of fine-grained, part-level manipulation is crucial for their integration into human-centric environments. Tasks like assembling components, preparing food, or handling delicate objects all require an understanding of and interaction with object parts. PartInstruct accelerates this development by providing the necessary tools and challenges, paving the way for robots that are more adaptable, precise, and capable of understanding nuanced human instructions, thereby significantly broadening the scope of what robots can achieve in real-world scenarios.

#### 5.6. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge limitations such as the focus on a simulated environment (sim-to-real gap), reliance on oracle demonstrations (limited behavioral diversity), and single-object manipulation. These limitations naturally point to important future research directions, including real-world deployment, human demonstration collection, multi-object scenarios, and incorporating articulated objects, ensuring that PartInstruct will remain a relevant and evolving benchmark for years to come.
