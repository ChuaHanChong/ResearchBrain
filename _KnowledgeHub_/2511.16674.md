# Dataset Distillation for Pre-Trained Self-Supervised Vision Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Massachusetts Institute of Technology developed Linear Gradient Matching (LGM), a dataset distillation method for pre-trained self-supervised vision models. This approach synthesizes a single, highly discriminative synthetic image per class, enabling efficient training of linear classifiers and providing insights into model representations and biases.

## Problem

- Traditional dataset distillation (DD) methods primarily focus on training models from scratch, which is misaligned with the modern computer vision paradigm of using large, pre-trained self-supervised models.
- There was a lack of DD techniques specifically designed to efficiently train lightweight linear probes on top of these frozen pre-trained vision models for downstream tasks, which can still be computationally intensive.
- Scaling existing DD methods to achieve high performance with ultra-small datasets (e.g., one image per class) for large-scale tasks like ImageNet has been challenging due to memory constraints and instability.

## Method

- Introduced ==Linear Gradient Matching (LGM)==, a method that distills synthetic images by aligning the gradients of a randomly initialized linear classifier trained on synthetic features with those derived from real data, using a frozen pre-trained backbone.
- Employed a ==pyramid representation== for each synthetic image, optimizing multi-scale components progressively to prevent adversarial patterns, improve realism, and enhance generalization across models.
- Incorporated ==multi-round differentiable augmentations== during distillation, applying transformations like cropping, flipping, and noising to multiple copies of synthetic data per iteration to boost robustness and cross-model transferability.

## Results

- LGM achieved test accuracy up to **75%** on ImageNet-1k with DINO-v2 using just one synthetic image per class, closely approaching the **83%** accuracy from the full **1.3 million** real images, and consistently outperformed real-image baselines.
- Distilled images revealed that MoCo-v3's poor performance on adversarial datasets like Spawrious stemmed from prioritizing spurious background elements over foreground objects, unlike robust models like DINO-v2.
- The method's regularization techniques, especially differentiable augmentations and pyramid representation, were critical for achieving high performance and ensuring good generalization across different pre-trained backbone models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LGM distills highly discriminative synthetic images that effectively train linear probes, often representing extreme class features rather than averages, which is particularly effective for fine-grained classification.
> - The distilled images serve as an interpretability tool, visually diagnosing model biases (e.g., reliance on spurious correlations on datasets like Spawrious) and offering insights into the distinct representational strategies of different pre-trained backbones.
> - Distilled datasets exhibit cross-model transferability, and this transferability correlates with the alignment of embedding spaces between different pre-trained models, supporting the 'Platonic Representation Hypothesis'.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and evolving challenge at the intersection of **Dataset Distillation (DD)** and **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)**, two rapidly developing fields in machine learning.

**Dataset Distillation (DD)**, first introduced in 2018, aims to synthesize a tiny set of artificial images such that a model trained on this synthetic set achieves performance comparable to a model trained on a much larger, real dataset. The core motivation is efficiency: reducing storage, communication, and training time. Traditional DD methods, as highlighted by the authors, primarily focus on scenarios where models are trained *from scratch* on these distilled datasets. Various techniques have emerged, including matching gradients, feature distributions, or training trajectories of models trained on real data. However, these methods often struggle to scale to large models or maintain performance in the ultra-small data regime (e.g., one image per class) due to memory constraints and instability.

Concurrently, **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)** has revolutionized computer vision. The dominant paradigm now involves pre-training large neural networks on vast amounts of unlabeled data, often using methods like contrastive learning (e.g., MoCo, DINO) or masked autoencoders (e.g., MAE, EVA). These pre-trained models serve as powerful feature extractors or foundation models, which are then adapted for downstream tasks by either fine-tuning the entire model or, more commonly, by attaching and training a lightweight "linear probe" (a simple linear classifier) on top of their frozen features. This paradigm leverages the rich, generalizable representations learned by SSL models, significantly improving performance and efficiency for a wide array of vision tasks. Notable examples include CLIP, DINO-v2, EVA-02, and MoCo-v3, all utilized in this paper.

The critical gap this paper addresses is the **disconnect between traditional DD and the modern SSL paradigm**. Existing DD methods were not designed for the scenario where a pre-trained feature extractor is already available. Training a model *from scratch* on a distilled dataset becomes less relevant when state-of-the-art performance relies on leveraging powerful, pre-trained backbones. This work bridges this gap by proposing a method for distilling datasets specifically to train *linear probes* on top of such pre-trained self-supervised vision models. This shifts the focus from distilling data for *full model initialization* to distilling data for *efficient adaptation* of pre-trained models.

Furthermore, the paper ties into the emerging concept of the **"Platonic Representation Hypothesis,"** which posits that different large models, even when trained on diverse modalities or objectives, might converge to similar underlying representations of the world. By investigating how distilled datasets transfer across different pre-trained backbones, the authors contribute to understanding model alignment and the universality of these learned representations.

By innovating at this crucial intersection, the paper offers a novel approach to making the increasingly powerful SSL models even more efficient and interpretable, aligning dataset distillation with the current trends in large-scale vision model development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a method for dataset distillation tailored for the modern paradigm of pre-trained self-supervised vision models**. Specifically, the authors aim to synthesize a small set of synthetic images that can optimally train *linear probes* on top of these large, pre-trained feature extractors. This is a significant departure from traditional dataset distillation, which focuses on training models from random initialization.

The core motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

1. **Addressing the Paradigm Shift in Computer Vision:** State-of-the-art computer vision increasingly relies on large, pre-trained self-supervised models. Existing dataset distillation methods, designed for training models from scratch, are ill-suited for this new reality. There is a pressing need for distillation techniques that can leverage and enhance the efficiency of these foundation models.
2. **Enhancing Efficiency for Downstream Tasks:** Training linear probes on large real datasets can still be computationally intensive. If a small, synthetic dataset can achieve comparable performance, it would drastically reduce training time, memory footprint, and computational resources required for adapting pre-trained models to specific downstream classification tasks. The paper highlights that training a linear probe on their distilled datasets takes "just a few minutes" compared to "up to an entire day" on the full dataset.
3. **Scalability to Ultra-Small Data Regimes:** The goal is to distill datasets to an extremely compact form, ideally just one synthetic image per class, while maintaining high performance. This ultra-small data regime has been challenging for prior methods, especially with large-scale datasets like ImageNet.
4. **Improving Interpretability of Large Vision Models:** The authors hypothesize that the process of distilling data in the context of specific pre-trained backbones can offer valuable insights into how these models "see" the world. The synthetic images might reveal the key features, biases, and representational priorities of different models, thereby serving as a powerful interpretability tool. This includes understanding model alignment, susceptibility to spurious correlations, and out-of-distribution generalization capabilities.
5. **Generalization Across Architectures:** A key motivation is to investigate whether distilled datasets can transfer effectively across different pre-trained backbone architectures. If a dataset distilled using one model (e.g., DINO) can effectively train a linear probe for another (e.g., CLIP), it speaks to the robustness and universality of the distilled representations, as well as the alignment between the models themselves.

In essence, the paper seeks to modernize dataset distillation to be relevant to current deep learning practices, making large models more efficient to adapt and providing a novel lens through which to interpret their complex internal workings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **Linear Gradient Matching (LGM)**, a novel method for dataset distillation specifically designed for pre-trained self-supervised vision models. Their approach draws inspiration from previous gradient-matching and trajectory-matching techniques but adapts them to the unique context of training linear probes.

#### 3.1. Linear Gradient Matching (LGM)

- **Core Idea:** LGM aims to synthesize a small set of synthetic images ($D_{syn}$) such that when passed through a pre-trained feature extractor ($\phi$), they induce gradients in a randomly initialized linear classifier ($W$) that are similar to those produced by a batch of real images ($D_{real}$).
- **Formalization:**
 - Given a pre-trained feature extractor $\phi$, real data $(X_{real}, Y_{real})$, and synthetic data $(X_{syn}, Y_{syn})$.
 - At each distillation step, a new linear classifier $W$ (and bias $b$, omitted in the main text for simplicity) is randomly initialized.
 - The features for real and synthetic images are extracted: $\phi(X_{real})$ and $\phi(X_{syn})$.
 - Classification losses are computed using cross-entropy (CE) for both real ($\ell_{real}$) and synthetic ($\ell_{syn}$) data with respect to $W$:
 $\ell_{real} = \text{CE}(W \phi(X_{real}); Y_{real})$
 $\ell_{syn} = \text{CE}(W \phi(X_{syn}); Y_{syn})$
 - The gradients of these losses with respect to the linear classifier $W$ are calculated: $\partial\ell_{real}/\partial W$ and $\partial\ell_{syn}/\partial W$.
 - The meta-loss ($L_{meta}$) for optimizing the synthetic images is defined as the cosine distance between the vectorized real and synthetic gradients:
 $L_{meta} = 1 - \text{cos}\left(\text{vec}\left(\frac{\partial\ell_{real}}{\partial W}\right), \text{vec}\left(\frac{\partial\ell_{syn}}{\partial W}\right)\right)$
- **Optimization:** This $L_{meta}$ is then back-propagated through the inner gradient computation, the linear classifier, and the feature extractor to update the synthetic images $X_{syn}$. This process is iterated for a fixed number of steps (e.g., 5000 iterations).

#### 3.2. Implicit Regularization Techniques

To combat overfitting, improve realism, and enhance generalization, the authors incorporate two key regularization strategies:

- **Pyramid Representation:** Instead of optimizing raw pixel values, each synthetic image is represented as a multi-scale pyramid of images (e.g., $1 \times 1, 2 \times 2, \dots, 256 \times 256$). Before each optimization step, the final image $X$ is "rendered" by upsampling and summing all levels of the pyramid, followed by a sigmoid activation for pixel clamping. This progressive optimization (starting with low-resolution components and adding more tiers) significantly reduces high-frequency adversarial patterns, leading to more realistic and robust distilled images that generalize better across models.
- **Decorrelated Color Space Optimization:** Inspired by feature visualization techniques, synthetic images are optimized in a decorrelated color space. A fixed linear transform is applied after pyramid reconstruction to convert them back to the standard correlated color space. This helps prevent the distilled images from developing color-based biases induced by the distillation model.

#### 3.3. Differentiable Augmentations

Following advancements in dataset distillation, differentiable augmentations are crucial for the efficacy of the synthesized data.

- **Application:** Standard augmentations like horizontal flipping, random resized cropping, and Gaussian noising are applied to the synthetic images during the distillation process.
- **Multi-Round Augmentation:** The authors find that applying *multiple rounds* of these augmentations to different copies of the synthetic data in each iteration, and then concatenating the results, significantly boosts distillation quality. This encourages the optimization to find images that are robust to variations and that contain enough information to be effective across different augmented views.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Backbone Models:** The method is evaluated using four prominent pre-trained self-supervised vision models as feature extractors: CLIP, DINO-v2, EVA-02, and MoCo-v3 (all "ViT-B" versions).
- **Datasets:** Experiments are conducted on diverse datasets: ImageNet-1k, ImageNet-100 (standard classification), Spawrious, Waterbirds (adversarial datasets with spurious correlations), Stanford Dogs, CUB-200-2011 (fine-grained classification), and ArtBench (out-of-distribution art styles).
- **Evaluation:** Linear probes are trained to convergence on the distilled (or baseline-selected) images and evaluated on test sets. A simplified evaluation protocol (last layer features, consistent hyperparameters) is used for all models.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against real-image baselines: "Neighbors" (real images closest to distilled images in embedding space), "Centroids" (real images closest to class mean embeddings), and "Random" (randomly selected real images).

This comprehensive methodology, combining a targeted gradient-matching objective with advanced regularization and augmentation, forms the backbone of their successful dataset distillation approach for modern vision models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, both quantitative and qualitative, underscoring the effectiveness and interpretative power of Linear Gradient Matching (LGM).

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Distillation for Linear Probes

- **Outperformance of Baselines:** LGM consistently and convincingly outperforms all real-image baselines (Neighbors, Centroids, Random) when distilling to just one image per class. For instance, a linear probe trained on DINO-v2 features using an ImageNet-1k dataset distilled with LGM achieves 75% test accuracy, remarkably close to the 83% achieved with the full 1.3 million real images. Similar substantial gains are observed across all tested backbones and datasets (ImageNet-100, ImageNet-1k, Flowers-102, Food-101).
- **Discriminative Features:** Visualizations of distilled image embeddings (e.g., for ImageNet-Fruits) show that synthetic images often lie on the periphery or even outside their class's real data cluster. This suggests that LGM distills highly discriminative features into single images, pushing them to be maximally informative rather than representative of an average.

#### 4.2. Generalization Across Different Pre-Trained Models

- **Cross-Model Transferability:** Distilled datasets, when equipped with pyramid representation and differentiable augmentations, demonstrate good generalization capabilities across different pre-trained backbone models (e.g., a dataset distilled with DINO-v2 performs well when used to train a linear probe on CLIP features).
- **Model Quality Correlation:** The study notes that the model achieving the highest accuracy on the full dataset (DINO-v2) also produces distilled datasets with the best average cross-model performance, suggesting a link between the quality of the original backbone and the transferability of its distilled data.
- **Outlier Behavior:** An outlier pair (CLIP and MoCo) shows poorer cross-model generalization, hinting at potential misalignments in their learned embedding spaces.

#### 4.3. Crucial Role of Regularization and Augmentation

- **Differentiable Augmentations:** This is identified as the most critical component, dramatically improving both single-model and cross-model performance. Without augmentations, distilled images lack meaningful structure, appearing as "blobs of color with just a hint of geometry." Increasing the number of augmentation rounds per batch further enhances effectiveness.
- **Pyramid Representation:** This technique is vital for preventing adversarial, high-frequency patterns in the synthetic images, especially when distilling for higher resolutions. It significantly improves cross-model generalization by mitigating overfitting to the distillation model. Images distilled without it are visually degraded and perform poorly.
- **Color Decorrelation:** While less impactful than augmentations or pyramid representation, it contributes to the realism of distilled images and offers slight improvements, particularly in cross-model performance when distilling with CLIP.

#### 4.4. Interpretability Insights from Distilled Images

- **Revealing Model Biases (Spurious Correlations):** On adversarial datasets like "Spawrious" (dog breeds with background correlations), distilled images provide strong interpretative clues. DINO-v2's distilled images clearly depict dog breeds, reflecting its robustness. In contrast, MoCo-v3's distilled images for the same classes are dominated by spurious background elements (e.g., desert, forest), explaining its catastrophic performance drop on the adversarial test set. This highlights how distilled images can reveal what a model prioritizes.
- **Excellence in Fine-Grained Classification:** LGM achieves even larger performance gaps over real-image baselines on fine-grained datasets (Stanford Dogs, CUB-200-2011). The ability to compress didactic, highly discriminative information into a single image per class is particularly effective when distinguishing between very similar classes.
- **Predicting Model Alignment:** The cross-model performance of distilled datasets (normalized k-NN accuracy) strongly correlates with quantitative measures of alignment between different model embedding spaces (mutual k-nearest neighbors). This positions LGM as a visual diagnostic tool for understanding the "Platonic Representation Hypothesis" and how different models' representations align.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** Even DINO-v1, trained exclusively on "real-world" ImageNet images, can effectively distill out-of-distribution datasets like ArtBench (different art styles). The synthetic images for art styles are distinct from their nearest real ImageNet neighbors, demonstrating that the distillation is not merely copying and highlights the backbone's latent ability to generalize to abstract concepts.
- **Distinct "Styles" of Distilled Images:** Visually, images distilled using different backbones (e.g., CLIP, DINO-v2, EVA-02, MoCo-v3) exhibit unique stylistic qualities (e.g., focus on texture, color, or structure, as seen in Figure 1). This offers qualitative insights into the distinct representational strategies of each backbone.

In summary, LGM successfully achieves its goal of distilling highly effective and transferable datasets for training linear probes on pre-trained SSL models. Beyond quantitative performance, its strength lies in providing a new, visually intuitive tool for understanding the complexities, biases, and alignment properties of large foundation models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant contribution to the fields of dataset distillation and self-supervised learning, with far-reaching potential impact across various domains.

#### 5.1. Modernizing Dataset Distillation

The most immediate significance is the successful adaptation of dataset distillation to the prevailing paradigm of pre-trained self-supervised vision models. By focusing on training linear probes, the authors make DD directly relevant to how most state-of-the-art computer vision is done today. This shift ensures that DD remains a viable and valuable research direction in the era of large foundation models.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability

The ability to train linear classifiers to competitive performance using just a single synthetic image per class (even for large datasets like ImageNet-1k) drastically reduces training time, computational costs, and storage requirements for deploying pre-trained models. This efficiency gain is crucial for:
- **Resource-constrained environments:** Enabling high-performance classification on devices or in settings with limited compute.
- **Faster experimentation:** Accelerating research and development cycles for new downstream tasks.
- **Environmental sustainability:** Reducing the energy footprint associated with training large models repeatedly.

#### 5.3. Powerful Interpretability Tool

Perhaps one of the most exciting aspects of this work is its contribution to model interpretability. The distilled images act as a "visual diagnostic" for understanding complex black-box models:
- **Revealing Model Biases:** By showing what features models prioritize (e.g., foreground object vs. spurious background), distilled images can help diagnose and potentially mitigate biases or vulnerabilities in robust AI development.
- **Understanding Model Alignment:** The correlation between cross-model performance of distilled datasets and quantitative measures of model alignment offers a novel, intuitive way to visualize and understand the "Platonic Representation Hypothesis." This can guide the design of models that learn more universal and transferable representations.
- **Probing Generalization Capabilities:** The successful distillation of out-of-distribution datasets showcases the latent generalization abilities of pre-trained backbones, providing insights into their robustness beyond their training data.
- **Qualitative Insights into "Model Vision":** The distinct "styles" of images distilled by different backbones offer a qualitative window into how these models process and represent visual information, potentially informing future architectural choices or training objectives.

#### 5.4. Implications for Data Privacy and Security

While not explicitly focused on privacy, dataset distillation inherently offers privacy benefits by using synthetic data instead of real, sensitive samples. This could open avenues for training robust models on sensitive data without directly exposing the original data.

#### 5.5. Foundation for Future Research

The paper introduces a new and challenging task, inviting further exploration. Future research could investigate:
- Scaling to even larger distillation ratios or higher resolutions without bottleneck issues.
- Applying similar principles to other downstream tasks beyond linear classification (e.g., segmentation, object detection).
- Developing more advanced meta-learning objectives for even stronger generalization and robustness.
- Using distilled datasets for privacy-preserving or federated learning scenarios.
- Further leveraging distilled images to actively steer model development or improve fairness.

In conclusion, "Dataset Distillation for Pre-Trained Self-Supervised Vision Models" is a timely and impactful contribution that not only provides a highly effective and efficient method for adapting modern vision models but also unlocks powerful new avenues for understanding their internal representations. This dual impact on efficiency and interpretability positions the work as a significant step forward in making advanced AI systems more accessible, understandable, and robust.
