# Gaussian Joint Embeddings For Self-Supervised Representation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A probabilistic framework, Gaussian Joint Embeddings (GJE) and its extension Gaussian Mixture Joint Embeddings (GMJE), is introduced for self-supervised representation learning. This framework addresses limitations of deterministic methods by modeling multi-modal conditional distributions, intrinsically preventing representation collapse, and enabling unconditional latent space sampling for image synthesis.

## Problem

- Deterministic self-supervised learning (SSL) architectures struggle with multi-modal inverse problems, collapsing predictions to conditional means and failing to capture diverse target views.
- Existing predictive SSL methods often require ad-hoc architectural heuristics (e.g., EMA, stop-gradients) to prevent representation collapse.
- Many SSL approaches yield discriminative latent spaces that lack an explicit, continuous probabilistic density, thus hindering direct generative applications like unconditional sampling.

## Method

- Introduces ==Gaussian Joint Embeddings== (**GJE**) framework, modeling concatenated context and target representations via a joint density function, p(z_c, z_t).
- Extends GJE to Gaussian Mixture Joint Embeddings (GMJE) to explicitly model complex, multi-modal conditional distributions and achieve symmetric training without architectural heuristics.
- Develops multiple GMJE variants, including GMJE-MDN for instance-specific mixture parameters and SMC-GMJE for dynamic memory bank optimization, while systematically addressing the "Mahalanobis Trace Trap" for stable learning.

## Results

- On synthetic multi-modal datasets, GMJE-MDN and GMJE-GNG accurately tracked and recovered complex non-linear multi-modal structures and conditional uncertainty, outperforming deterministic and unimodal GJE baselines.
- For vision benchmarks, SMC-GMJE demonstrated improved linear-probe accuracy (**81.99%** vs **78.08%** for MoCo v2) and stability under severely memory-constrained conditions (M=256) over 1000 epochs.
- GMJE effectively learns a generative latent space, enabling unconditional image synthesis from sampled latent vectors on MNIST, yielding sharper and more diverse images compared to post-hoc GMMs on discriminative embeddings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current contrastive learning methods can be theoretically interpreted as degenerate non-parametric cases of GMJE, unifying different self-supervised learning paradigms.
> - Modeling joint embeddings with Gaussian Mixtures intrinsically prevents representation collapse through explicit covariance-aware geometric regularization, reducing reliance on ad-hoc architectural asymmetries.
> - The probabilistic formulation naturally endows the latent space with a continuous, normalized density function, enabling native unconditional sampling and generative capabilities beyond discriminative tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL), specifically focusing on representation learning for high-dimensional data. It addresses a prominent area of inquiry concerning joint-embedding architectures, which aim to map different views or augmentations of an input into a latent space where semantic relationships can be captured.

The broader landscape of SSL is commonly divided into two main paradigms:
- **Contrastive Learning**: Methods like SimCLR and MoCo learn by attracting positive pairs (different augmented views of the same instance) and repelling negative pairs (views of different instances). These often rely on L2-normalized embeddings and are frequently analyzed on the unit hypersphere. A common challenge is their computational demand due to the need for numerous negative samples, often achieved through large batch sizes or memory queues.
- **Non-Contrastive/Predictive Learning**: Approaches such as BYOL and Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) aim to align representations across views directly, without explicit negative samples. While these methods mitigate the computational burden of negative sampling, they often face challenges such as representation collapse (where all inputs map to a trivial constant vector) and limitations in handling genuinely multi-modal conditional distributions, as deterministic prediction tends to converge to conditional averages. These methods frequently employ architectural asymmetries like Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target networks, stop-gradients, or predictor asymmetry to prevent collapse.

This work proposes a probabilistic alternative to the predominantly deterministic predictive architectures in SSL. It aims to bridge the gap between contrastive and non-contrastive methods by offering a unified probabilistic framework, addressing the limitations of existing deterministic and unimodal models, and reducing the reliance on architectural heuristics for collapse prevention. It also connects representation learning with generative modeling, a capability often distinct from purely discriminative SSL approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce a probabilistically grounded framework for self-supervised representation learning that overcomes identified limitations of existing methods. The key motivations and objectives include:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Deterministic Predictive Architectures**: Current joint-embedding methods often use deterministic regression objectives (e.g., Mean Squared Error) to align context and target representations. In multi-modal inverse problems, where a single context can correspond to multiple plausible targets, such deterministic predictors collapse to the conditional mean, potentially yielding predictions that do not correspond to any typical data sample. The research aims to provide principled uncertainty estimates and avoid this "averaging effect."

2. **Mitigating Representation Collapse without Architectural Asymmetries**: Deterministic predictive methods frequently require architectural heuristics (e.g., EMA target networks, stop-gradients, predictor asymmetry) to prevent representations from collapsing to trivial solutions. This work seeks to develop a probabilistic alternative that inherently provides variance-covariance control, thereby reducing or eliminating the need for such explicit collapse-prevention mechanisms.

3. **Modeling Multi-Modal Conditional Structures**: The unimodal nature of single-Gaussian models (even in advanced settings like Gaussian Processes) restricts their ability to represent complex, multi-modal relationships where a context view might be compatible with several distinct target views. A key objective is to extend the framework to handle genuinely multi-modal latent structures.

4. **Providing a Unified Probabilistic Perspective for SSL**: The paper aims to establish a theoretical connection between existing SSL paradigms (contrastive and predictive) under a common probabilistic umbrella, specifically through Gaussian Mixture Models. This unification is intended to offer a deeper understanding of these methods and facilitate the development of more principled architectures.

5. **Enabling Generative Latent Densities**: Beyond learning discriminative representations, a core motivation is to equip the latent space with an explicit, continuous probabilistic density function. This would allow for downstream generative tasks, such as unconditional sampling and image synthesis, directly from the learned latent manifold, which is often not a native capability of purely discriminative SSL models.

6. **Developing Robust Optimization Strategies**: The work identifies a specific failure mode ("Mahalanobis Trace Trap") that arises from naive empirical batch optimization of probabilistic joint embedding objectives. A significant objective is to develop and implement several robust remedies for this issue, spanning various parametric and non-parametric settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a shift from deterministic latent prediction to generative joint modeling using Gaussian distributions.

**Gaussian Joint Embeddings (GJE)**
The GJE framework models the concatenated context and target representations, $Z = [z_c^T, z_t^T]^T$, through a joint density $p(z_c, z_t)$. The objective is to maximize the expected joint log-likelihood over matching pairs. This objective naturally factorizes into a conditional matching term, $\log p(z_t|z_c)$, and a marginal density term, $\log p(z_c)$, offering inherent covariance-dependent volume regularization.

1. **Dual Formulation: Sample-Space Gaussian Process Joint Embeddings (GPJE)**
 - **Concept**: This formulation operates in the "sample space" by modeling $z_t$ as a realization of a Gaussian Process (GP) function of $z_c$, defined by a positive-definite kernel $k(z_c, z'_c)$. It focuses on similarities between individual data points.
 - **Training**: The objective minimizes a conditional negative log-likelihood (NLL) of the form $L \propto \text{Tr}(Z_t^T K_{cc}^{-1} Z_t) + (d_t/2) \log|K_{cc}|$, where $K_{cc}$ is the Gram matrix over context embeddings.
 - **Collapse Mitigation**: Due to the inherent asymmetry of the conditional likelihood (it regularizes the context space but not the target space), symmetric optimization leads to catastrophic representation collapse. Therefore, the target encoder $E_{\theta'}$ is updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) with a stop-gradient on $Z_t$, making $Z_t$ a fixed topological reference.
 - **Inference**: Given a new test context $x_c^*$, the predictive distribution for $z_t^*$ is Gaussian, $N(\mu_{t|c}, \Sigma_{t|c})$, with $\mu_{t|c}$ as a non-parametric weighted average of training $Z_t$ and $\Sigma_{t|c}$ as a distance-aware variance.
 - **Scalability**: Exact GPJE involves $O(N^3)$ computation for matrix inversion. Random Fourier Features (RFF) are employed to approximate the kernel, reducing complexity to $O(ND^2)$ by reformulating operations in a lower-dimensional Fourier feature space ($D$).
 - **Limitation**: Both the exact and RFF-approximated dual GJE are unimodal, meaning they can only output a single predictive distribution and cannot route predictions to distinct multi-modal target branches.

2. **Primal Formulation: Feature-Space Covariance (Primal-GJE)**
 - **Concept**: This formulation operates in the "feature space" by directly modeling a single, zero-mean multivariate Gaussian distribution $N(0, C_{\text{joint}})$ over the concatenated joint embedding $Z = [z_c^T, z_t^T]^T$. The empirical covariance $C_{\text{joint}} = (1/N)Z^T Z$.
 - **Training and the Mahalanobis Trace Trap**: The objective is to minimize the joint NLL: $L \propto (1/2N) \sum_i z_i^T C_{\text{joint}}^{-1} z_i + (1/2) \log|C_{\text{joint}}|$. However, a critical "Mahalanobis Trace Trap" is identified: when the covariance $C_{\text{joint}}$ is empirically computed from the same batch $Z$ it evaluates, the data-fit term $(1/N) \sum_i z_i^T C_{\text{joint}}^{-1} z_i$ algebraically cancels to a constant scalar ($d$, the embedding dimension). This eliminates the expansive gradient, leaving only the volume penalty $\log|C_{\text{joint}}|$, which leads to catastrophic dimensional collapse as the model shrinks representations to the origin.
 - **Remedy for Primal-GJE**: To maintain stable representations and ensure symmetric training, the regularizer is structurally inverted, explicitly maximizing differential entropy by minimizing $L = -(1/2)\log|C_{\text{joint}}|$. This forces features to span the available volume, promoting a non-degenerate latent geometry without relying on EMA or stop-gradients.
 - **Inference**: The conditional distribution $p(z_t^*|z_c^*)$ is analytically derived as $N(\mu_{t|c}, \Sigma_{t|c})$, where $\mu_{t|c} = C_{tc}C_{cc}^{-1}z_c^*$ and $\Sigma_{t|c} = C_{tt} - C_{tc}C_{cc}^{-1}C_{ct}$. The prediction is a linear projection, and the predictive covariance $\Sigma_{t|c}$ is constant, making it homoscedastic.
 - **Limitation**: Similar to Dual GJE, this formulation is strictly unimodal and homoscedastic, limiting its ability to capture complex, multi-modal, or dynamically varying conditional structures.

**Gaussian Mixture Joint Embeddings (GMJE)**
To overcome the unimodality and homoscedasticity of GJE, the framework is extended to GMJE, which models $p(z_c, z_t)$ as a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM). This leverages the universal approximation properties of GMMs to represent complex, multi-modal semantic structures. A key aspect is that the marginal $p(z_t)$ and conditional $p(z_t|z_c)$ distributions also remain GMMs with closed-form parameters, allowing $z_c$ to act as a "router" for selecting appropriate mixture components. The GMJE framework is explicitly designed to be symmetric, propagating gradients through both encoders without asymmetric heuristics.

The paper introduces several GMJE variants, each addressing aspects of optimization and adaptivity:

1. **GMJE with Learnable Prototypes (Parametric GMJE)**
 - **Concept**: Maintains $K$ learnable joint mixture means $\mu_k$ (global cluster prototypes) and a shared, parameterized covariance structure $\Sigma$.
 - **Optimization**: Minimizes the NLL of the joint mixture using a differentiable, relaxed EM approach with the Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) trick for numerical stability. The objective involves a data-fit term (pulling embeddings toward prototypes) and a geometric regularization term ($(1/2)\log|\Sigma|$, pushing prototypes apart to cover the data manifold). This balance intrinsically prevents collapse.
 - **Mahalanobis Trace Trap Remedy**: This approach avoids the trap by treating $\Sigma$ as a standalone, learnable parameter, distinct from the empirical batch covariance.

2. **GMJE-MDN (Mixture Density Network for GMJE Learning)**
 - **Concept**: Integrates Mixture Density Networks (MDN) to allow instance-specific, dynamic prediction of mixture parameters. To prevent "Identity Collapse" (where the network trivially outputs the input as the mean if it has access to the target), a strict "Information Bottleneck" is enforced.
 - **Architecture**: A parameter network takes *only* the context embedding $z_c$ as input to predict the conditional mixture parameters ($\mu_k(z_c), \Sigma_k(z_c), \pi_k(z_c)$) for the target $z_t$.
 - **Objective**: The conditional MDN objective ($-\log p(z_t|z_c)$) is coupled with a marginal regularization loss on $z_c$ (e.g., $-(1/2)\log|\Sigma_c|$) to maintain a well-conditioned context space and prevent encoder collapse.
 - **Mahalanobis Trace Trap Remedy**: The parameters $\Sigma_k(z_c)$ are dynamically generated functions of $z_c$, not empirical batch averages, thus avoiding the algebraic cancellation.

3. **GMJE-GNG (Dynamic Prototype Discovery via Growing Neural Gas)**
 - **Concept**: Addresses the challenge of pre-defining $K$ by using Growing Neural Gas (GNG) to dynamically discover the optimal number and placement of mixture components (prototypes). GNG treats prototypes as nodes in a dynamic graph, spawning new prototypes in regions of high latent error and adapting to manifold topology.
 - **Process**: Samples joint embeddings $Z$, finds the two nearest prototypes, updates the winner and its neighbors towards $Z$, and accumulates local quantization error. Periodically, new prototypes are inserted between high-error nodes.
 - **Complexity**: Per-sample time complexity is $O(K_{max} \cdot D)$, where $D$ is embedding dimensionality.
 - **Mahalanobis Trace Trap Remedy**: The prototypes are dynamically updated based on global errors, not through direct empirical batch covariance calculations, thus avoiding the trap.

4. **Data as Mode GMJE (DaM-GMJE) & Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC-GMJE)**
 - **Contrastive Learning Bridge**: The paper establishes a theoretical connection: standard contrastive learning (e.g., InfoNCE) can be interpreted as a degenerate, non-parametric limiting case of GMJE. It treats every data point in a memory bank of size $M$ as an equiprobable mode in an isotropic (spherical, fixed-variance $\tau I$) GMM, with zero cross-covariance. The InfoNCE loss is derived from the conditional NLL of this non-parametric, isotropic GMM under specific assumptions (L2-normalization, low temperature limit).
 - **Limitations of Contrastive Learning**: This interpretation highlights that contrastive methods assume isotropic, independent covariances (setting $\Sigma_{ct}=0$, thus acting as invariance learners), and use uninformative, uniform-prior FIFO queues for memory banks, leading to "class collision" problems and computational demands for large $M$.
 - **SMC-GMJE**: To address the uniform-prior limitation of FIFO queues, Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) particle filtering is introduced. The memory bank is treated as a probability-weighted particle system, where each particle (key) is a mixture component.
 - **Dynamic Optimization**: During each training iteration, particles' importance weights are dynamically updated based on their likelihood under the current query batch (Equation 34), and resampling replaces the FIFO mechanism by sampling particles proportional to their weights. Highly informative "hard negatives" are retained or duplicated, while redundant samples are discarded.
 - **Mahalanobis Trace Trap Remedy**: In this non-parametric setting, the "components" are historical representations (keys) in a memory bank, and the covariance is fixed (e.g., $\tau I$). The current batch evaluates its distance against the historical bank, not against its own empirically calculated covariance, thus avoiding the trap.
 - **Generative GMJE**: A direct consequence of GMJE's probabilistic formulation is that it learns a continuous, closed-form generative probability density function over the latent manifold, $p(z_t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k N(z_t|\mu_{t,k}, \Sigma_{tt,k})$. This enables unconditional sampling from the learned latent space, facilitating tasks like unconditional image synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence across synthetic and vision tasks to support the GMJE framework's claims.

1. **Ambiguous Alignment of Synthetic Embedding Representations**:
 - **Task**: Two synthetic datasets (separated and intersecting non-linear branches) were created where $x_c \to x_t$ mapping is multi-modal.
 - **Baselines vs. GMJE**:
 - **Classic JEPA (MSE)** and **Unimodal Dual-space RBF GJE** failed to recover the multi-branch structure, collapsing predictions to the conditional mean or producing broad, unimodal uncertainty bands that averaged across modes.
 - **GMJE-EM (K=1)**, a single global Gaussian, heavily over-smoothed the manifold.
 - **GMJE-EM (K=3)**, a fixed-K parametric mixture, improved mode separation but remained geometrically rigid due to fixed global covariance ellipses.
 - **GMJE-GNG** adaptively placed prototypes along data manifold ridges, providing a more faithful topological approximation.
 - **GMJE-MDN** provided the most expressive conditional generative representation. By predicting instance-dependent mixture parameters, it closely tracked non-linear multi-modal structures, producing tight, localized uncertainty bands around valid target regions, and accurately recovering conditional means, standard deviations, and mixing weights.

2. **Representation Learning on Vision Benchmarks (CIFAR-10)**:
 - **SMC Memory Banks vs. FIFO**:
 - **Setup**: MoCo v2 (FIFO) vs. SMC-GMJE, both with ResNet-18, trained for 1000 epochs with a severely constrained memory bank size (M=256).
 - **Results**: SMC-GMJE demonstrated greater training stability and achieved a higher linear-probe accuracy (81.99%) compared to MoCo v2 (78.08%). The Effective Sample Size (ESS) of the SMC filter decreased significantly after initial warmup, indicating concentration on informative (hard negative) particles, which contributed to stable learning under memory constraints.
 - **SMC-GMJE vs. Standard Baselines (200-Epoch Benchmark)**:
 - **Setup**: SMC-GMJE compared with SimCLR, BYOL, and MoCo v2 (and others mentioned but not explicitly detailed in results) over a shorter 200-epoch pre-training.
 - **Results**: SimCLR (81.50%), BYOL (79.04%), and MoCo v2 (79.02%) showed higher linear probing accuracies than SMC-GMJE (74.52%) in this short-horizon setting.
 - **Analysis**: While SMC-GMJE improved over FIFO in long-run, memory-constrained scenarios, its performance against mature, highly tuned baselines in shorter benchmarks suggests further optimization and larger-scale tuning are required for it to fully match them.

3. **Generative GMJE: Unconditional Image Synthesis via Latent Sampling (MNIST)**:
 - **Objective**: Evaluate if GMJE learns a suitable latent space for unconditional image generation.
 - **Setup**: Three latent spaces were compared: SimCLR (post-hoc GMM fit to its embeddings), Unimodal Primal-GJE, and Parametric GMJE (natively learned K=50 GMM). A decoder was trained to reconstruct images from these latent spaces.
 - **Results**:
 - **SimCLR (Post-Hoc GMM)**: Samples often fell into low-density "empty regions" between semantic clusters in t-SNE visualizations, leading to blurry and class-ambiguous decoded images (chimeras).
 - **Unimodal Primal-GJE**: Samples broadly covered the latent space but lacked clear multi-cluster organization, resulting in over-smoothed and less diverse decoded images (mean collapse).
 - **Parametric GMJE**: Latent samples aligned closely with clustered semantic regions in t-SNE. Decoded images were sharper and more visually diverse. Geometric properties (prototype spread, variance) indicated a structured, multi-modal latent space.
 - **Conclusion**: GMJE demonstrated a substantially better latent generative model than post-hoc density fitting on discriminative spaces or single-Gaussian approximations, suggesting that explicit multi-modal latent density modeling directly within representation learning is effective for unconditional generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of self-supervised learning by introducing Gaussian Joint Embeddings (GJE) and its multi-modal extension, Gaussian Mixture Joint Embeddings (GMJE). Its significance and potential impact stem from several key areas:

1. **Unified Probabilistic Framework for SSL**: The work provides a common probabilistic lens through which to understand various SSL paradigms. By demonstrating that contrastive learning can be interpreted as a degenerate non-parametric GMJE, it offers a theoretical bridge between traditionally distinct approaches. This unification can foster more principled design choices and facilitate cross-pollination of ideas across SSL methodologies.

2. **Principled Collapse Prevention**: A major challenge in non-contrastive SSL methods is preventing representation collapse, often necessitating architectural hacks like EMA target networks or stop-gradients. GMJE is designed to intrinsically mitigate dimensional collapse through its explicit covariance-aware geometric regularizer (e.g., the log-determinant term). This reduces reliance on heuristic asymmetric interventions, potentially leading to more robust and theoretically sound training procedures.

3. **Enhanced Multi-Modal Representation Learning**: Deterministic predictors struggle with multi-modal inverse problems. GMJE's ability to model complex, multi-modal conditional distributions (especially with GMJE-MDN and GMJE-GNG) represents an advancement in learning richer, more flexible semantic alignments between different views of data. This is particularly relevant for ambiguous prediction tasks common in real-world scenarios.

4. **Generative Latent Densities for Downstream Tasks**: Unlike many discriminative SSL methods that yield feature spaces unsuitable for direct sampling, GMJE natively learns a continuous, normalized probabilistic density over the latent manifold. This inherent generative capability enables direct unconditional sampling from the latent space, opening avenues for generative replay in continual learning, latent data augmentation, and unconditional image synthesis. This blurs the line between representation learning and generative modeling, offering more versatile learned representations.

5. **Dynamic Adaptivity and Scalability**: The introduction of GMJE-GNG addresses the challenge of pre-defining the number of mixture components ($K$) by enabling dynamic, topology-adaptive prototype discovery. This adaptive complexity allows the model to map intricate data manifolds without rigid architectural constraints. The SMC-GMJE variant also demonstrates improved efficiency and stability in memory-constrained settings compared to traditional FIFO queues, by adaptively prioritizing informative samples.

6. **Addressing Optimization Pitfalls**: The identification and systematic remediation of the "Mahalanobis Trace Trap" highlight a crucial, often overlooked, challenge in implementing probabilistic objectives with empirical batch statistics. The proposed architectural interventions provide practical solutions for stable optimization of these models.

In summary, GMJE offers a robust, probabilistically grounded alternative to current SSL paradigms, capable of learning rich, multi-modal representations while inherently guarding against collapse and enabling diverse generative applications. Future work extending GMJE to sequential settings (e.g., video, LLMs) and integrating neuro-symbolic constraints could further broaden its impact in developing more interpretable and causally informed AI systems.
