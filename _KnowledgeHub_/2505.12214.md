# Behavior Synthesis via Contact-Aware Fisher Information Maximization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Yale University developed a principled framework for robots to actively acquire information about their environment through physical contact, formalizing a contact-aware Fisher information measure to synthesize optimal object learning behaviors. This method generated emergent human-like tactile exploratory actions, leading to more precise parameter estimates and faster convergence for identifying object characteristics like mass, friction, stiffness, and shape, even under high sensor noise.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to actively acquire rich information about their environment through physical contact, despite contact dynamics being a valuable data source.
- Existing methods for learning from contact often rely on passive observation, leading to sparse and "information-poor" contact data that is inefficient for comprehensive parameter learning.
- Accurate identification of specific object properties like shape or material stiffness requires deliberate, dynamic physical interactions involving making and breaking contact, which passive approaches cannot provide.

## Method

- The authors formulated a ==Contact-Aware Maximum A-Posteriori (CA-MAP)== estimation problem, embedding unknown object parameters directly within the robot's dynamics and explicit contact constraints.
- They derived a ==Contact-Aware Fisher Information Matrix (CA-FIM)== from the Lagrangian of the CA-MAP problem, quantifying the information content of contact interactions for parameter estimation.
- An optimal control approach, **Contact-Aware Optimal Experimental Design (CA-OED)**, was developed to synthesize robot trajectories that maximize the CA-FIM, generating information-rich contact behaviors in a closed-loop iterative process.

## Results

- The approach consistently generated distinct, information-seeking contact behaviors (hefting for mass, rubbing for friction, pinching for stiffness, contouring for shape) specifically tailored to the parameter being estimated.
- Parameter learning performance was improved across all evaluated scenarios, with the proposed method converging to more precise parameter estimates and achieving higher **Fisher information gains** compared to a baseline.
- The method demonstrated robustness to high contact force sensor noise and diverse initial parameter priors by enabling robots to actively seek contacts that yielded readings outside a defined contact uncertainty envelope.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating contact mechanics into the Fisher information calculation enables robots to make deliberate, information-seeking decisions about physical interactions, significantly enhancing data quality.
> - Maximizing contact-aware Fisher information naturally leads to the emergence of complex, human-like tactile exploratory behaviors (e.g., hefting, rubbing, pinching, contouring) that are optimal for identifying specific object properties.
> - The information landscape for parameter estimation, when properly quantified through contact-aware Fisher information, reveals optimal kinematic inputs (e.g., penetration depth, velocity) that excite the most informative contact dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of robotics, specifically addressing the challenge of enabling robots to actively acquire information about their environment through physical contact. Historically, contact dynamics have been utilized for robot manipulation and locomotion. However, the utility of contact extends to providing rich information for environmental characterization and object parameter learning.

Prior research has recognized the information-richness of contact for parameter estimation, as evidenced by works. However, much of this work relies on acquiring contact data as a byproduct of implicit or unintentional interactions. The current paper identifies a critical gap: information-rich contact interactions are scarce through passive observation, necessitating active exploration. While active exploration has been shown to improve data collection for learning, there remains a need to enhance the quality of *contact-specific* data collected during such exploration, particularly for parameters like shape and inertial properties that require continuous contact making and breaking.

The paper builds upon several established areas:
- **Parameter Estimation:** It acknowledges various techniques like Bayesian inference, least squares, and factor graph-based approaches. The authors specifically choose a maximum likelihood formulation due to its compatibility with optimal experimental design, recognizing that not all interactions are equally useful for parameter identification.
- **Optimal Experimental Design (OED) and Fisher Information:** This field provides methods to optimize data sampling for parameter estimation. Fisher information, a metric for parameter sensitivity to sensor measurements, is commonly employed in OED to maximize information richness, and has been applied in robotics for active data acquisition.
- **Contact Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO):** This area focuses on synthesizing robot trajectories where contact forces, robot states, and control inputs are simultaneously optimized. The paper integrates these ideas, embedding object parameters within the contact model and optimizing contact modes to refine parameter estimates, without needing explicit contact mode sequences.
- **Belief Space Planning (BSP):** BSP enables robots to plan under parameter uncertainty by actively gathering information to refine their belief. Recent applications extend BSP to contact-based tasks. This work shares similarities, aiming to improve task performance based on parameter uncertainty.

The key distinction of this research is its explicit consideration of contact mechanics within the computation of the Fisher information. Unlike some prior belief space planning methods that reason about contact more naively, this approach directly optimizes for information-rich contact interactions using a contact-aware Fisher information measure. This allows for the synthesis of emergent contact behaviors in real-time settings through sample-based control, aiming for broader generalization across contact-based robotic tasks without necessarily resorting to computationally intensive reinforcement learning. Thus, the work advances active learning in robotics by providing a principled framework for synthesizing contact-rich behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop theoretical foundations and a practical methodology for optimizing robot behaviors to actively acquire information-rich contact data for learning about objects in their environment.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and observations in robotic interaction:
- **Information in Contact Dynamics:** Contact dynamics are recognized as a rich source of information that can significantly enhance a robot's ability to characterize and learn about objects. However, this potential is often underexploited.
- **Sparsity and Non-Smoothness of Contact Data:** Obtaining high-quality, information-rich contact data is inherently difficult because contact events are often sparse and non-smooth. This characteristic makes passive data collection inefficient for comprehensive parameter learning.
- **Need for Active Exploration:** Most existing methods that leverage contact for learning rely on data gathered implicitly or as a byproduct of other interactions. This often leads to "information-poor" contacts, as valuable interactions are scarcely found unintentionally. To overcome this, there is a clear need for robots to actively and deliberately explore contact.
- **Specific Parameter Identification Challenges:** Certain physical properties of objects, such as shape, mass, inertia, and material stiffness, can only be accurately estimated through specific, often dynamic, physical interactions that may involve making and breaking contact. Passive observation is insufficient for these tasks.
- **Optimizing Robot Behaviors:** The core motivation is to move beyond passive data collection by enabling robots to *synthesize* behaviors that are specifically designed to excite informative contact interactions. This involves developing a mechanism to quantify what constitutes "information-rich" contact and then using this quantification to drive optimal robot actions.

To achieve this, the authors aim to:
1. Formulate a contact-aware Fisher information measure that can explicitly identify and characterize contact interactions that are rich in information for improving parameter learning.
2. Develop an optimal control approach that plans sequences of contact interactions (trajectories) to maximize this contact-aware Fisher information.
3. Demonstrate that this approach can generate emergent robot behaviors that effectively acquire data for learning various object parameters across diverse robotic scenarios.

Ultimately, the goal is to enhance a robot's ability to learn about its environment by making its physical interactions more purposeful and data-efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper integrates concepts from maximum a-posteriori estimation, optimal experimental design, and contact-implicit trajectory optimization to synthesize contact-seeking robot behaviors for parameter learning.

**A. Contact-Aware Maximum A-Posteriori Estimation (CA-MAP)**
The problem of parameter learning is framed as a maximum a-posteriori (MAP) estimation problem for parameters θ ∈ Θ.
- **Robot Model:** A robot's state x_t and control input u_t are considered at discrete time t. The dynamics are defined as x_{t+1} = f_θ(x_t, u_t, λ_t), where λ_t represents contact forces and constraints. The sensor measurement model is y_t = g_θ(x_t). Crucially, the unknown parameters θ are embedded within the dynamics (f_θ), sensor model (g_θ), and contact constraints (C_θ(x_t)), where C_θ(x_t) denotes contact conditions like friction, non-penetration, and complementarity.
- **CA-MAP Formulation (Eq. 8):** The objective is to find the parameter estimate ˆθ and a trajectory τ = {(x_t, λ_t)∀t ∈ [0, T − 1]} that maximize the log posterior probability log p(D|τ, θ)p(θ), where D is the dataset of measurements. This maximization is subject to initial conditions, the robot's dynamics, contact constraints, and parameter feasibility. This formulation explicitly includes contact forces λ as part of the trajectory optimization, making contact an implicit variable for optimization.

**B. Contact-Aware Fisher Information Matrix (CA-FIM)**
To guide the robot towards information-rich contacts, a contact-aware Fisher information matrix is derived.
- **Lagrangian Formulation:** The Lagrangian L(D|τ, θ, α) of the CA-MAP problem is defined, incorporating the log-likelihood and penalty terms for dynamic and contact constraints via Lagrange multipliers α.
- **CA-FIM Definition (Eq. 10):** The contact-aware Fisher information matrix F(D|τ, θ, α) is defined as the negative Hessian of this Lagrangian with respect to the parameters θ (i.e., −∇²_θ L(D|τ, θ, α)). This definition links the Fisher information to the conditioning of the steepest ascent problem for parameter estimation (Eq. 11), specifically for Newton's method.
- **Cramér-Rao Lower Bound:** The Fisher information matrix establishes a lower bound on the covariance of any unbiased estimator (Cramér-Rao Lower Bound, Eq. 5). Maximizing the Fisher information directly contributes to reducing the variance of parameter estimates, indicating increased precision.

**C. Contact-Aware Optimal Experimental Design (CA-OED)**
The CA-FIM is then used within an optimal experimental design framework to synthesize robot behaviors.
- **Optimization Problem (Eq. 12):** The CA-OED problem seeks to find an optimal predicted dataset ˆD and a reference trajectory ˆτ that maximize a scalar measure ψ(F(D|τ, θ)) of the contact-aware Fisher information matrix, while penalizing trajectory cost J(D, τ). The optimization is constrained by the robot's dynamics, contact model, and sensor model.
- **Solution Method:** This optimization problem is solved using predictive sampling techniques, where contact constraints are handled implicitly through the dynamics model.
- **Metric (ψ):** The trace of the Fisher information matrix is chosen as the scalar metric ψ (T-optimality). Maximizing the trace contributes to improving the condition number of the Fisher information matrix (Appendix B), which is beneficial for the numerical stability and convergence speed of parameter estimation.

**D. Closed-Loop Behavior Synthesis (Algorithm 1)**
The approach operates in a closed-loop iterative manner:
1. **Initialization:** The robot starts with an initial state and a prior distribution for the unknown parameters p(θ).
2. **Behavior Planning (CA-OED):** Using the current parameter estimate, the CA-OED problem (Eq. 12) is solved to predict an information-rich trajectory (robot behavior) and corresponding sensor measurements.
3. **Execution:** The planned trajectory is executed on the physical robot (or in simulation), and actual sensor data D is collected.
4. **Parameter Update (CA-MAP Belief Update):** The collected data D is then used to update the parameter estimate ˆθ via the CA-MAP problem (Eq. 8). The posterior covariance Σ⁺_θ is also updated using an approximate formula (Eq. 13), which effectively reduces parameter uncertainty.
5. **Iteration:** Steps 2-4 are repeated for a specified number of experiments or until a desired parameter confidence is achieved.

**E. Contact Model:** The paper employs a soft contact model (Eq. 18), which defines normal (λ_n) and tangential (λ_t) contact forces based on material stiffness (K), dampening (C), friction coefficient (μ), friction resistance (R), signed distance function (ϕ_n), and normal/tangent velocities (v_n, v_t). This model explicitly incorporates the parameters to be estimated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates its contact-aware experimental design approach through a series of simulated and experimental robot scenarios, comparing its performance against a baseline method that employs Fisher information maximization but without explicit reasoning about contact dynamics. The evaluation addresses four key questions related to emergent behaviors, convergence, and information landscapes.

**A. Emergent Contact Behaviors (Q1 & Q2)**
The approach generates distinct, information-seeking contact behaviors tailored to the parameter being estimated:

- **Compounding/Constant Contact (A1):**
 - **Mass Estimation (Hefting):** For learning the mass of a grasped object, the robot performs a "hefting" motion (up-and-down movement) that continuously perturbs the normal contact force without losing contact. This behavior is optimal because maintaining constant contact avoids data sparsity and excites terms (signed distance ϕ_n and normal velocity v_n) in the contact sensor model that are critical for mass identification.
 - **Friction Estimation (Rubbing):** To estimate the friction coefficient with a wall, the robot exhibits a "rubbing" behavior. This involves maintaining a non-zero normal force while simultaneously exerting a non-zero tangential velocity against the wall. This action excites both normal force and tangent velocity in the contact sensor model, which are essential for identifying the friction coefficient.
- **Making and Breaking Contact (A2):**
 - **Stiffness/Dampening Estimation (Pinching):** When learning the material properties (stiffness and dampening) of an object with a gripper, the robot performs a "pinching" behavior. This involves making and breaking contact and pressing into the object, thereby significantly perturbing the normal forces between the gripper and the object. These excitations are crucial for extracting information about material properties from the contact sensor model.
 - **Shape Estimation (Contouring):** For estimating the length and width of a box, the robot employs a "contouring" behavior with its end-effector. This involves making and breaking contact across different regions of the object's surface, actively exciting contact modes to gather shape information online.

**B. Parameter Convergence (Q3)**
The contact-aware experimental design consistently demonstrates improved parameter learning performance:

- **Higher Precision and Information Gains:** Across all evaluated robot scenarios (mass, friction, stiffness, shape), the proposed approach converges to a more precise parameter estimate with higher Fisher information gains compared to the baseline. This indicates that explicitly incorporating contact interactions into the optimization leads to more effective data collection.
- **Deliberate Decision-Making:** The enhanced performance is attributed to the robot's ability to make deliberate decisions about contact-seeking behaviors, guided by a structured contact measurement model within the Fisher information calculation. The baseline, which approximates gradients via finite differencing, reasons about contact more naively.
- **Scenario-Specific Performance:** While generally superior, the improvement can be marginal in highly constrained scenarios (e.g., pinching for stiffness with limited 1D motion and high sensor noise) where the robot's exploratory options are limited. However, the approach exhibits stronger advantages in more complex and unconstrained environments.
- **Robustness to Initialization:** The method demonstrates robustness to diverse initial parameter priors. For instance, it is robust to initial mass estimates within approximately 30% for hefting and even more robust for other scenarios, as shown in Figure 7.

**C. Information Loss Landscape (Q4)**
The paper provides visualizations of the information landscape that the robot optimizes over, demonstrating how it shapes the emergent behaviors:

- **Weight Estimation:** The information landscape for hefting (Figure 8) is smooth and linear with respect to kinematic inputs like penetration depth (ϕ_n) and normal velocity (v_n). The robot's strategy involves exciting these parameters at velocity boundaries, naturally resulting in the up-and-down hefting motion.
- **Stiffness Estimation:** The landscape for pinching (Figure 9a) reveals a locally optimal trade-off between the penetration length (ϕ_n) and the rate of penetration (∂ϕ_n/∂t or normal velocity v_n). The robot optimizes for trajectories that maximize both, leading to the observed squeezing and contracting behavior.
- **Friction Estimation:** The information landscape for rubbing (Figure 9b) shows that information about the friction coefficient is maximized by simultaneously exciting both normal contact velocity (v_n) and tangent contact velocity (v_t) during interaction with the wall. The robot seeks contact velocities near the peaks of this landscape (within physical constraints), which generates the rubbing behavior.

These visualizations provide intuitive evidence that the optimized behaviors directly correspond to regions in the information landscape where parameter sensitivity is highest.

**D. Robustness to Noise:** The experiments were conducted in the presence of high contact force sensor noise. The approach demonstrated robustness by enabling robots to actively seek information-rich contacts that yield readings outside a defined contact uncertainty envelope, thus mitigating the impact of noise on parameter identification.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research offers several significant contributions and has notable potential impacts within robotics and active learning:

**A. Significance**
- **Principled Active Learning for Contact:** The core significance lies in providing a principled, theoretically grounded framework for robots to actively seek out and leverage contact interactions for parameter learning. By formally deriving a contact-aware Fisher information measure, the work quantifies "information-richness" in the context of physical interaction.
- **Emergent Human-Like Tactile Behaviors:** The methodology naturally synthesizes complex, emergent robot behaviors (e.g., hefting, rubbing, pinching, contouring) that resemble human tactile exploratory procedures. These behaviors are shown to be optimal for efficiently identifying various physical properties like mass, friction, stiffness, and shape.
- **Improved Parameter Estimation Accuracy and Efficiency:** The explicit consideration of contact mechanics within the information maximization objective leads to more precise parameter estimates and faster convergence compared to methods that treat contact implicitly or naively. This addresses the challenge of acquiring high-quality data from inherently sparse and non-smooth contact dynamics.
- **Generalizability Across Contact Tasks:** The approach demonstrates utility across a diverse set of robotic contact scenarios, suggesting its potential applicability to a wider range of manipulation and interaction tasks.
- **Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The demonstrated robustness to high sensor noise and diverse initial parameter priors enhances the practical applicability of the method in real-world, uncertain robotic environments.

**B. Potential Impact**
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy and Environmental Understanding:** By enabling robots to actively learn about their environment through tactile exploration, this work can lead to more autonomous and adaptive robotic systems. Robots could independently characterize unknown objects and materials, improving their ability to plan and execute tasks in unstructured or changing environments.
- **Foundation for Advanced Manipulation:** A deeper, active understanding of object properties through contact can underpin more sophisticated and robust manipulation capabilities, especially for tasks requiring fine motor skills or interaction with novel objects.
- **Real-time Adaptive Control:** The closed-loop nature and the use of predictive sampling for behavior synthesis suggest potential for real-time adaptation of robot actions as knowledge about the environment improves.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The work lays a foundation for investigating how contact-aware information maximization can influence downstream robotic capabilities, such as policy learning, trajectory optimization, and state estimation, particularly in systems with process noise or higher-dimensional complexities.
- **Insights for Human-Robot Interaction and Haptics:** Understanding how robots can optimally acquire tactile information could also provide insights relevant to human haptics and the design of more intuitive human-robot interfaces.

In summary, this research advances the state of robotic active learning by providing a principled method for robots to intelligently engage with their environment through contact, leading to more efficient and accurate parameter identification.
