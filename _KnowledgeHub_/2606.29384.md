# Event-VLA: Action-Conditioned Event Fusion for Robust Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ShanghaiTech, HKUST(GZ), UMich, and SJTU developed Event-VLA, a framework that enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' robustness to degraded visual input by integrating event camera streams through an action-conditioned fusion interface. This approach maintains performance in normal conditions while significantly improving success rates in low-light and near-dark environments, achieving 95.6% average success in severe low-light simulations compared to 69.6% for RGB-only baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are fragile to degraded visual inputs (e.g., low light, motion blur, sensor noise), which are common in real-world robotic environments.
- The reliance on high-quality RGB observations leads to unreliable semantic grounding and unstable action prediction, limiting the robustness of robotic manipulation.
- Integrating event camera data into large, pretrained VLA models without disrupting their established RGB-language semantic priors is a challenge.

## Method

- **Event-VLA**'s ==Physical Residual Event Integration (PREI)== layer converts sparse event streams into dense, multi-channel physical residual maps (instantaneous, salient, persistent) for VLA compatibility.
- An ==action-conditioned event interface==, using ==gated cross-attention== and ==query-guided token routing==, selectively injects event information into action-related pathways *after* the pretrained VLA backbone processes RGB-language-proprioceptive context.
- A ==multi-objective training loss== includes a primary action loss and auxiliary future-event prediction losses to regularize event-routed representations without interfering with the VLA's core semantic understanding.

## Results

- Event-VLA achieved an average success rate of **96.5%** under normal visibility, demonstrating comparable performance to state-of-the-art RGB-based VLA policies without negative impact.
- Under severe low-light conditions (LL-Severe), Event-VLA maintained an average success rate of **95.6%** in simulation, significantly outperforming the best RGB-only baseline (**69.6%**).
- On a real Franka robot, Event-VLA showed enhanced stability and higher success rates in low-light (**70.0%** SR vs. **~55-57%** for baselines) and near-dark conditions (**52.5%** SR vs. **~12-15%** for baselines).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing event streams into action-time physical residuals (PREI) provides more effective and stable cues for manipulation than simpler event representations under varying illumination.
> - Introducing event information through an action-conditioned interface, rather than directly into the VLA's global semantic space, is crucial for preserving pretrained RGB-language semantic priors while enhancing robustness.
> - Event streams provide critical instantaneous motion, salient activity, and persistent structural cues that effectively compensate for the loss of information in degraded RGB observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI has seen significant advancements through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which facilitate general-purpose robotic manipulation by interpreting language instructions and visual observations to generate robot actions. Recent developments in action generation paradigms, such as flow matching-based policies, and unified multimodal representation learning, have expanded the capabilities of VLAs in complex task planning and continuous action generation.

However, a prevalent limitation in many existing VLA models is their implicit reliance on idealized visual observations. These models typically assume well-illuminated scenes, stable imaging conditions, and high-quality RGB inputs. In practical real-world robotic deployments, this assumption is frequently violated by factors such as illumination changes, sensor noise, and motion blur. Such conditions can lead to degraded semantic grounding and unstable action prediction, posing challenges for robust manipulation in uncontrolled environments.

Concurrently, event cameras offer an alternative sensing paradigm. Unlike traditional frame-based cameras that capture absolute brightness, event cameras asynchronously encode per-pixel changes in log intensity. This temporal-contrast mechanism provides several advantages, including high dynamic range, low latency, and enhanced sensitivity to motion and illumination variations. Consequently, event cameras are capable of capturing stable dynamic visual cues under challenging lighting and fast-motion conditions, making them a potential complement to conventional RGB sensors.

This work addresses the intersection of these two areas, specifically the challenge of integrating the illumination-robust and motion-sensitive event stream data with existing RGB-centric VLA models. The core problem this paper investigates is how to effectively incorporate asynchronous, high-frequency event streams into pretrained VLA models without disrupting their established RGB-language semantic priors, thereby enhancing their robustness to degraded visual input. Previous research has explored event-based perception for various robotic tasks, but the specific problem of an efficient and non-disruptive interface for event data into large, pretrained VLA backbones for robust manipulation under varying light conditions represents a targeted research gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an event-enhanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that enables robust and generalizable robotic manipulation across diverse and challenging illumination conditions. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing VLA Fragility to Degraded Visual Input:** Existing VLA models, while powerful, are predominantly built upon frame-based RGB observations. This makes them susceptible to performance degradation when visual inputs are compromised by real-world factors such as illumination shifts (e.g., low light, darkness), sensor noise, and motion blur. Such degraded visibility leads to unreliable texture, color, and boundary cues, which are critical for semantic grounding and accurate action prediction in RGB-centric policies. The researchers aim to mitigate this fragility.

2. **Leveraging Event Cameras as a Robust Complement:** Event cameras provide a sensing modality that is inherently robust to illumination changes and highly sensitive to motion. By encoding relative brightness changes rather than absolute intensity, event streams maintain clarity and provide critical motion and edge cues even when RGB observations become severely degraded. This makes event cameras a suitable complementary observation source to compensate for the loss of action-time dynamic cues in challenging visual environments.

3. **Integrating Events with Pretrained VLAs without Disruption:** A significant challenge is how to effectively incorporate event information into the sophisticated, often large-scale, pretrained VLA models. Directly merging event tokens into the global semantic token space of a VLA model could potentially disrupt its extensively learned RGB-language semantic priors, which are foundational for complex task understanding and generalization. Therefore, a key motivation is to design an interface that leverages event-based robustness while preserving the valuable existing knowledge within the pretrained VLA backbone. The goal is to allow event information to enhance robustness without fundamentally altering the VLA's core semantic reasoning capabilities.

4. **Focusing on Action-Time Physical Residuals:** The paper motivates the treatment of event streams not as another global semantic modality, but rather as sources of "action-time physical residuals." This perspective suggests that event data is most valuable for capturing instantaneous, salient, and persistent local changes around the robot, objects, and contact regions—information crucial for manipulation that is often lost in degraded RGB frames. The research aims to design an interface that specifically routes these physical cues to the action prediction pathway.

In summary, the researchers are motivated to bridge the gap between powerful VLA models and the unpredictable nature of real-world visual environments by strategically integrating event camera data to enhance manipulation robustness, particularly under low-light and degraded visibility conditions, while maintaining the integrity of the pretrained VLA's semantic understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Event-VLA, aims to enhance the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models under degraded visibility by integrating event camera streams. The core design principle involves preserving the pretrained VLA as the semantic backbone and selectively injecting event information into the action-related pathways. The methodology consists of three main components:

#### 3.1. PREI: Decomposing Events into Action-Time Physical Residuals

Raw event streams are sparse, asynchronous, and irregularly sampled. To convert this data into a format compatible with VLA models, the authors introduce **PREI (Physical Residual Event Integration)**. This lightweight representation layer transforms an event history into compact, dense, three-channel physical residual maps:
- **Instantaneous Channel (E_ins):** Emphasizes recent local changes with a short decay constant, capturing immediate motion.
- **Salient Channel (E_sal):** Highlights locally prominent event activity over a slightly longer timescale, useful for identifying objects undergoing manipulation. This involves local smoothing and normalization of activity.
- **Persistent Channel (E_per):** Preserves accumulated event structures and short-term contours over the full event window, which can provide object boundary information when RGB textures are degraded.

These channels are designed to capture complementary temporal aspects of physical residuals relevant to manipulation. PREI is contrasted with simpler representations like event count images and time surfaces, arguing that it offers a more stable and structured event representation across varying illumination conditions.

An event encoder then maps each PREI map into VLA-compatible event tokens, yielding both global and spatial event tokens. To ensure compatibility with the VLA's hidden space, the event encoder undergoes feature-level distillation. This process uses a frozen visual teacher (specifically, the DINO branch from the VLA's visual encoder) and native RGB-event pairs (from N-ImageNet) as well as synthetic RGB-event pairs (generated from LIBERO videos via v2e). This distillation aligns the event features without embedding them directly into the VLA's global semantic context.

#### 3.2. Action-Conditioned Event Interface

The core of Event-VLA's integration strategy lies in its action-conditioned event interface, which introduces event information *after* the pretrained VLA has established its initial RGB-language-proprioceptive-action context.
1. **VLA Input and Backbone Processing:** The VLA backbone receives standard inputs: RGB visual tokens, a proprioceptive token, language tokens, action placeholders, and *learnable query tokens*. Crucially, event tokens are explicitly *excluded* from this initial input sequence to the VLA backbone. The learnable query tokens are categorized into common queries (for task-level context), action queries (for action-oriented routing), and event queries (for event-oriented routing and auxiliary tasks). The VLA backbone then processes these inputs to produce contextualized hidden states and contextualized query states.
2. **Gated Cross-Attention Fusion:** After the VLA backbone has generated its contextualized RGB-language-proprioceptive representations, the encoded PREI event tokens are introduced. They are fused with the VLA's hidden tokens through a gated cross-attention mechanism. Specifically, the VLA's hidden tokens act as queries to attend to the event tokens (which serve as keys and values, augmented with positional encodings). A fusion gate, computed from the VLA hidden tokens and all contextualized query tokens, controls the influence of the cross-attention output on the VLA's hidden states, producing fused hidden tokens.
3. **Query-Guided Token Routing:** These fused tokens are then channeled to downstream heads via query-guided routing.
 - **Action Head:** Action-relevant tokens are routed to the action head, leveraging both the contextualized common queries and action queries. This head is responsible for predicting the future action chunk.
 - **Auxiliary Event Head:** Event-relevant tokens are routed to an auxiliary event head, guided by the contextualized common queries and event queries. This head predicts future PREI event targets.

This structural separation ensures that event information does not participate in the global semantic self-attention mechanism of the pretrained VLA backbone. Instead, its influence is limited to the post-backbone gated fusion and query-guided routing, primarily affecting downstream action and future-event prediction pathways.

#### 3.3. Training Objective and Inference

The training of Event-VLA involves a multi-objective loss function:
- **Primary Action Loss (L_act):** An ℓ1 loss is applied between the predicted action chunk and the ground-truth action chunk over the action horizon.
- **Auxiliary Event Loss (L_evt):** An ℓ1 loss measures the difference between the predicted future PREI event targets and the ground-truth future PREI maps. A content mask is used to focus this loss only on valid content regions.
- **Derivative Consistency Loss (L_deriv):** This term imposes a first-order derivative consistency loss between the predicted and ground-truth future PREI maps, encouraging the preservation of local event structures.

The full training objective is a weighted sum of these three losses. The auxiliary event head losses (L_evt and L_deriv) serve as regularization terms during training, encouraging the fused and event-routed representations to retain transient physical information. Importantly, these auxiliary losses and the event head are optional during inference, meaning action prediction solely relies on the action head.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Event-VLA was conducted through comprehensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, addressing questions regarding robustness, preservation of pretrained VLA pathways, optimal event representation and interface, and the contribution of individual components.

#### 4.1. Performance Under Progressive Visibility Degradation (LIBERO and LIBERO-Cross)

- **Normal Visibility (LIBERO):** Event-VLA demonstrated performance comparable to state-of-the-art RGB-based VLA policies (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT, MM-ACT). The "Ours" variant achieved an average success rate (SR) of 96.5%, while "Ours w/o event" (Event-VLA with the event pathway disabled during inference) achieved 96.2% average SR. This indicates that the event interface does not negatively impact performance under ideal lighting and successfully preserves the pretrained RGB-language action pathway.
- **Progressive Degradation (LIBERO-Cross):** Under controlled low-visibility conditions (LL-Mild, LL-Dark, LL-Severe), Event-VLA significantly improved robustness.
 - As RGB-based policies (π0, OpenVLA-OFT, MM-ACT) experienced substantial performance drops, particularly at LL-Severe (e.g., MM-ACT dropped from 95.9% at LL-Mild to 69.6% at LL-Severe), Event-VLA consistently maintained high success rates.
 - At LL-Severe, Event-VLA achieved an average SR of 95.6%, which was markedly higher than the best RGB-only baseline (MM-ACT at 69.6%). This finding supports that the action-conditioned event routing effectively leverages physical cues from event streams to compensate for unreliable RGB observations.

#### 4.2. Event-to-VLA Interface Ablation (LIBERO-Cross LL-Severe)

Ablation studies on LIBERO-Cross under LL-Severe degradation investigated the impact of event representation, fusion interface, query design, and event regularization.
- **Event Representation:**
 - Without any event input, the average SR was 60.6%.
 - Using Time Surface representation improved SR to 91.2%.
 - Utilizing PREI representation further enhanced SR to 95.6%, demonstrating its superiority in providing useful physical cues for action prediction under severe degradation.
- **Fusion Interface:**
 - Unified encoding (directly merging event tokens into the VLA backbone) achieved 95.1% SR but incurred a high latency overhead (+62.874 ms).
 - RGB/Event adapter fusion yielded 94.2% SR with low latency (+1.909 ms).
 - Query routing (the proposed method) attained the highest SR of 95.6% with minimal latency overhead (+2.157 ms). This result supports the efficiency and effectiveness of the action-conditioned query-guided routing.
- **Query Design and Regularization:** Full query routing (common, action, and event queries) and the complete auxiliary future-event supervision objective provided consistent performance gains, emphasizing their roles in effectively routing task-relevant event information and preserving physical residual information.

#### 4.3. Real-World Deployment (Franka Research 3 Robot)

Event-VLA was evaluated on a real Franka Research 3 robot across four manipulation tasks under normal, low-light, and near-dark conditions.
- **Normal Light:** Event-VLA (72.5% average SR) maintained competitive performance compared to RGB-based policies (e.g., π0 at 75%, OpenVLA-OFT at 70%).
- **Low-Light and Near-Dark Conditions:** Event-VLA demonstrated enhanced stability and higher success rates.
 - Under low-light, Event-VLA achieved 70.0% average SR, outperforming π0 (55.0%) and OpenVLA-OFT (57.5%).
 - Under near-dark conditions, Event-VLA achieved 52.5% average SR, significantly surpassing π0 (15.0%) and OpenVLA-OFT (12.5%).
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Real-world sequences illustrated that Event-VLA effectively utilized event-side observations to recover object and motion cues despite severely degraded RGB frames. However, failure cases occurred when target objects generated insufficient discriminative event activations, leading to ambiguous localization.

In summary, the main findings indicate that Event-VLA substantially improves robustness in degraded visual environments while preserving the performance and semantic priors of pretrained VLA models under normal conditions. This robustness is attributed to the effectiveness of the PREI event representation and the efficient action-conditioned event interface.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Event-VLA, an event-enhanced VLA framework, demonstrating a method for robust robotic manipulation under challenging visual conditions. The significance and potential impact of this research are multi-faceted.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addressing a Critical Robustness Gap:** The research directly tackles a known limitation of current VLA models: their reliance on high-quality RGB inputs, which renders them fragile in real-world scenarios characterized by variable illumination, noise, and motion blur. By integrating event cameras, Event-VLA offers a practical solution to enhance system robustness in uncontrolled environments.
- **Efficient Multimodal Fusion Strategy:** The proposed action-conditioned event interface, particularly the query-guided routing and gated cross-attention, represents a novel and efficient strategy for integrating event data. This approach avoids directly merging event tokens into the global semantic token space, thereby preserving the valuable RGB-language semantic priors learned by pretrained VLA backbones. This is crucial for retaining existing capabilities while extending them.
- **Novel Event Representation (PREI):** The introduction of PREI (Physical Residual Event Integration) as a lightweight, multi-timescale event representation (instantaneous, salient, persistent) is significant. It demonstrates that decomposing event activity into specific physical residuals can provide more effective and stable cues for robotic manipulation compared to traditional event representations.
- **Empirical Validation in Simulation and Real-World:** The comprehensive evaluation, spanning both a controlled simulated environment (LIBERO-Cross) with progressive degradation levels and real-world robot deployment, provides substantial empirical evidence for the method's effectiveness and practical utility.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** Event-VLA can contribute to the development of more autonomous robotic systems capable of operating reliably in a wider range of challenging conditions, such as industrial settings with fluctuating light, search-and-rescue operations in low-light, or domestic environments with varied lighting. This broadens the applicability of VLA-driven robots beyond idealized lab settings.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability:** By reducing the sensitivity of VLA models to visual degradation, Event-VLA could enhance the safety and reliability of robotic manipulation tasks, especially in scenarios where consistent performance is critical despite environmental fluctuations.
- **Guidance for Multimodal VLA Design:** The findings, particularly from the ablation studies, offer insights into effective strategies for multimodal sensor fusion within VLA architectures. The emphasis on "action-conditioned" injection and the benefits of PREI representation can guide future research in designing robust VLA models that integrate diverse sensing modalities.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future investigations into other forms of visual degradation (e.g., heavy occlusion), the integration of additional complementary sensors (e.g., tactile, depth), and the development of VLA models that are inherently robust from their inception, rather than requiring post-hoc enhancement. Further work could explore how to ensure "sufficiently discriminative event responses" for all relevant objects, addressing one of the identified limitations.
- **Practical Deployment Considerations:** While acknowledging the increased complexity of additional hardware and synchronization, the low latency overhead of the proposed interface suggests a pathway for practical deployment, particularly in applications where robustness under varying light conditions is a priority.

Overall, Event-VLA represents a step towards making advanced robotic manipulation policies more resilient and broadly applicable in the complex and unpredictable visual environments of the real world.
