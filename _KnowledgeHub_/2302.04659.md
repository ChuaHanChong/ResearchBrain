# ManiSkill2: A Unified Benchmark for Generalizable Manipulation Skills

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiSkill2, developed by researchers primarily from UCSD and Tsinghua University, presents a unified benchmark for generalizable robotic manipulation skills, featuring real-time, two-way coupled rigid-MPM soft-body simulation and achieving approximately 2000 FPS for visual reinforcement learning sample collection.

## Problem

- Existing manipulation benchmarks offer limited object diversity and often rely on simplified physics or abstract grasps, hindering the development of generalizable policies.
- Prior environments are inefficient for visual reinforcement learning due to high computational demands and memory usage, slowing down large-scale experimentation.
- Current soft-body simulators struggle with large deformations, complex materials, and effective rigid-robot coupling, limiting realistic interaction with deformable objects.

## Method

- Introduces 20 diverse manipulation task families, including novel real-time rigid-MPM soft-body environments with robust two-way coupling.
- Provides multi-controller support and a novel closed-loop method for converting large-scale demonstration actions across different action spaces.
- Optimizes for visual reinforcement learning efficiency through asynchronous rendering and an RPC-based render server, enabling high sample collection speeds and reduced GPU memory.

## Results

- Achieves approximately 2000 FPS for visual reinforcement learning sample collection with RGBD input on rigid-body tasks, notably surpassing other benchmarks.
- Soft-body environments achieve 80-84 FPS with 16 parallel environments, demonstrating efficient, real-time simulation of deformable objects with two-way rigid-soft coupling.
- State-of-the-art imitation and reinforcement learning algorithms exhibit low success rates on many high-precision assembly and complex soft-body tasks, revealing current algorithmic limitations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art imitation learning and reinforcement learning algorithms struggle with the precision and contact-rich control demanded by many ManiSkill2 tasks.
> - Visual reinforcement learning efficiency can be dramatically improved by decoupling rendering from CPU tasks and centralizing GPU resource management, significantly reducing computational barriers.
> - Sim-to-real transfer is achievable on certain tasks, but the domain gap, especially in depth sensing, remains a challenge for full generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

ManiSkill2 is positioned at the intersection of Embodied AI, robotic manipulation, computer vision, and machine learning, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL). The overarching goal of the paper is to advance research into "generalizable manipulation skills"—the ability of robots to interact with a wide variety of unseen objects in novel configurations, akin to human capabilities. This is recognized as a "cornerstone of Embodied AI," essential for tackling long-horizon and complex daily chores.

The authors meticulously detail the shortcomings of existing benchmarks, which motivate the creation of ManiSkill2. Prior benchmarks are critiqued for:
- **Lack of object-level variations:** Many benchmarks offer limited diversity in object topology and geometry, hindering the development of generalizable policies.
- **Insufficient dynamic simulation:** Some rely on abstract grasps or simplified physics, failing to capture the complexities of real-world interactions.
- **Limited task diversity:** Benchmarks often focus on a single type of manipulation, making cross-algorithm comparisons difficult and requiring significant customization efforts from researchers.
- **Inefficiency for visual RL:** High computational demands and memory usage in visual learning environments often impede rapid experimentation and large-scale training.
- **Limitations in soft-body simulation:** Existing soft-body simulators struggle with large deformations, elasto-plastic materials, or effective rigid-robot coupling, which are crucial for many real-world tasks.

ManiSkill2 directly addresses these critical pain points. It aims to provide a unified, versatile, high-performance, and realistic benchmark that supports diverse tasks, algorithms, and observation modalities. By building upon the group's prior work (SAPIEN ManiSkill and SAPIEN simulator), ManiSkill2 represents a significant evolution, offering enhanced capabilities in soft-body physics, controller support, and training efficiency. In doing so, it seeks to become a standard platform that can "push cutting-edge research works" and foster "interdisciplinary and reproducible research" on one of the most challenging problems in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ManiSkill2 is to establish a comprehensive and unified benchmark that accelerates research into generalizable manipulation skills, which are crucial for embodied AI systems. The authors are motivated by the clear deficiencies of existing benchmarks, which fail to provide the necessary realism, diversity, efficiency, and flexibility required to train and evaluate advanced robotic manipulation policies.

The key objectives of ManiSkill2 can be summarized as follows:

1. **Develop a Unified and Versatile Benchmark:** To create a standard platform that can measure the performance of different algorithms across a broad spectrum of generic and generalizable manipulation tasks. This involves providing a rich set of diverse assets, supporting various manipulation types (rigid/soft-body, stationary/mobile-base, single/dual-arm), and offering a unified interface (OpenAI Gym) and evaluation protocol.
2. **Incorporate Real-time, Realistic Soft-body Environments:** To overcome the limitations of current soft-body simulators by introducing real-time, continuum-mechanics-based material point method (MPM) simulation for elasto-plastic materials. This includes enabling robust two-way coupling between rigid robots and deformable soft bodies, a feature largely absent or inefficient in existing benchmarks.
3. **Provide Multi-Controller Support and Demonstration Action Space Conversion:** To offer a wide array of robotic controllers, allowing researchers to choose the most suitable action space for their algorithms. Crucially, ManiSkill2 aims to enable the conversion of large-scale demonstrations generated with one controller type to any other desired action space, maximizing the utility of valuable demonstration data.
4. **Optimize for Fast Visual Reinforcement Learning Experiments:** To design the environment for maximum throughput and efficiency in visual RL training. This involves implementing strategies like asynchronous rendering and a render server infrastructure to significantly reduce sample collection time and GPU memory footprint, thereby lowering computational barriers for researchers.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from the belief that without such a benchmark, progress in generalizable manipulation will be severely constrained. Researchers often face the burden of customizing environments, leading to difficulties in reproducible comparisons and a fragmented research landscape. ManiSkill2 aims to provide a robust, accessible, and challenging environment to consolidate efforts and drive innovation in perception, planning, and learning for complex robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ManiSkill2's development methodology is characterized by a "verification-driven iterative development process," akin to agile software development, ensuring high-quality and verified environments. The benchmark leverages a sophisticated technological stack and innovative engineering solutions to meet its ambitious objectives.

**Environment and Task Design:**
- **Heterogeneous Task Families:** ManiSkill2 comprises 20 diverse manipulation task families, categorized into rigid-body and soft-body tasks. These cover stationary/mobile-base, single/dual-arm, and various object types.
 - **Soft-body Manipulation (6 tasks):** Examples include Fill (clay into beaker), Hang (noodle on rod), Excavate (scooping clay), Pour (liquid), Pinch (deforming plasticine), and Write (drawing on clay). These tasks emphasize reasoning about action influence on soft bodies, large deformations, and topological changes.
 - **Precise Peg-in-hole Assembly (3 tasks):** Tasks like PegInsertionSide, PlugCharger, and AssemblingKits demand high precision (millimeter-level clearance) and focus on contact-rich insertion processes, setting a higher bar than prior benchmarks.
 - **Stationary 6-DoF Pick-and-place (5 tasks):** PickCube, StackCube, PickSingleYCB, PickSingleEGAD, PickClutterYCB, designed with large workspaces and diverse object geometries from YCB and EGAD datasets to challenge grasp generalization.
 - **Mobile/Stationary Manipulation of Articulated Objects (5 tasks):** Inherited mobile tasks from ManiSkill1 (OpenCabinetDrawer/Door, PushChair, MoveBucket) and added stationary tasks like TurnFaucet and AvoidObstacles.
- **Assets and Demonstrations:** Includes 2000+ object models and 4M+ demonstration frames, generated through Task and Motion Planning (TAMP), Model Predictive Control (MPC), or Reinforcement Learning (RL), serving as a byproduct to verify environment quality.
- **Unified Interface:** All tasks are implemented with a unified OpenAI Gym interface, supporting various observation modes (state dict, RGBD, point cloud, with/without robot segmentation) and evaluation protocols (seen/unseen assets/configurations, cloud-based system).

**Multi-Controller Support and Demonstration Conversion:**
- **Diverse Controllers:** ManiSkill2 supports a wide range of controllers (e.g., joint position, delta joint position, delta end-effector pose, velocity controllers), allowing flexible robot actuation. It also allows combining different controllers for different robot components (e.g., base, arm, gripper).
- **Action Space Conversion:** A novel closed-loop method converts demonstration actions from a source controller's action space to a target controller's action space while preserving the kinematic and dynamic process. This allows broad utility of generated demonstrations across different learning paradigms.

**Real-time Soft-body Simulation and Rendering:**
- **Custom GPU MPM Simulator:** Implemented from scratch using Nvidia's Warp framework and native CUDA, enabling high efficiency and customizability for elasto-plastic materials.
- **Two-way Rigid-Soft Coupling:** A key innovation is the 2-way dynamic coupling interface that allows any rigid-body simulator (SAPIEN) to interact seamlessly with the soft-body MPM simulator. Rigid body poses are transferred to the soft-body simulator, and accumulated soft-body forces/torques are transferred back to rigid bodies.
- **Performance Optimization:** Includes optimized data transfer between CPU/GPU via Warp, reduced compilation/startup times, and a fast screen-space splatting algorithm for rendering MPM particles to provide visually consistent output for learning.

**Parallelizing Physical Simulation and Rendering (Performance Optimization):**
- **Python-scripted, Multi-process Vectorization:** Environments are Python-scripted for customizability and vectorized via multiple CPU processes, balancing efficiency and flexibility.
- **Asynchronous Rendering:** Decouples GPU rendering from CPU-bound tasks like reward computation, improving CPU utilization.
- **RPC-based Render Server:** A novel server-client architecture centralizes rendering on the main Python process. This allows sharing GPU resources (meshes, textures) across all environments, significantly reducing GPU memory usage, eliminating redundant GPU-CPU-GPU data copies, and enabling faster visual input processing (~2000 FPS for RGBD-input PPO).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ManiSkill2's evaluation demonstrates its capabilities as a benchmark and the challenges it poses to current state-of-the-art algorithms.

**Performance and Efficiency:**
- **High Throughput for Visual RL:** ManiSkill2 achieves an impressive sample collection speed of approximately 2000 FPS for an RGBD-input PPO policy with 1 GPU and 16 CPU processes on rigid-body environments. This significantly outperforms other benchmarks like Habitat 2.0, RoboSuite, and Isaac Gym, largely due to asynchronous rendering and the render server.
- **Reduced GPU Memory Usage:** The render server effectively shares GPU resources across multiple environments, preventing linear growth in memory usage seen in other frameworks, which is critical for large-scale experiments.
- **Real-time Soft-body Simulation:** Soft-body environments achieve 80-84 FPS with 16 parallel environments on a single GPU (4x real-time), demonstrating the efficiency of the custom MPM simulator.
- **Effective Demonstration Conversion:** The action space conversion method successfully converts demonstrations between different controller types with high fidelity (e.g., 99% for PickSingleYCB, 98% for AssemblingKits), maximizing data utility.

**Benchmarking of Algorithms:**
- **Sense-Plan-Act (SPA):**
 - **Contact-GraspNet on PickSingleYCB:** Achieved 43.24% success. Failures were attributed to low-confidence grasp predictions for complex object geometries (e.g., thin objects) and kinematically unreachable or low-quality grasp poses.
 - **Transporter Networks on AssemblingKits:** Showed 18% success rate under ManiSkill2's stringent precision metrics, although achieving 99% under the original Transporter Networks' looser success criteria. This highlights ManiSkill2's demanding nature for precise manipulation.
- **Imitation Learning (IL) with Behavior Cloning (BC):**
 - **Rigid-body tasks:** Generally very low or zero success rates, indicating that BC alone is insufficient for the complexity and precision required by many ManiSkill2 tasks.
 - **Soft-body tasks:** Mixed results. Tasks like Fill and Hang (less precision-demanding) saw moderate success (0.45-0.62 for Fill, 0.20-0.35 for Hang). Highly precise soft-body tasks like Excavate, Pour, Pinch, and Write had very low or zero success rates, suggesting BC agents struggle with precise estimation of action influence on soft-body deformation.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Demonstrations (DAPG+PPO):**
 - **Pick-and-place tasks:** Achieved high success rates for point cloud-based agents (e.g., PickCube 0.94, StackCube 0.95, PickSingleYCB 0.51), often outperforming SPA methods (e.g., on PickSingleYCB). This demonstrates the potential of learning generalizable pick-and-place policies. RGBD-based agents performed notably worse (e.g., PickSingleYCB 0.18).
 - **High-precision assembly and other complex tasks:** Showed near-zero success rates across the board (e.g., PegInsertionSide, PlugCharger, AssemblingKits, TurnFaucet, AvoidObstacles), even with demonstration guidance. This emphasizes that these tasks present significant, unsolved challenges for current RL algorithms regarding precision and contact-rich control.
 - **Ablation Studies:** Confirmed the critical impact of controller choice (delta end-effector pose was superior to delta joint position) and input representation (end-effector frame for point clouds, visual goal cues) on agent performance.
 - **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** Performance typically dropped on held-out test objects, highlighting the generalization gap that still needs to be addressed (e.g., PickSingleYCB success rate dropped from 0.51 to 0.36).
 - **Difficulty Scaling:** Increasing clearance in assembly tasks significantly improved success rates for some agents, confirming that the default tasks' difficulty is largely due to their high precision requirements.

**Sim2Real Transferability:**
- **PickCube:** A policy trained in simulation achieved a 60% success rate on a real Franka Emika robot, demonstrating promising sim2real potential despite a performance drop attributed to the domain gap in depth sensing.
- **Pinch (Soft-body):** Executing a motion plan in both simulation and reality for plasticine deformation showed reasonable agreement, validating the fidelity of the 2-way coupled rigid-MPM simulator.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManiSkill2 represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation benchmarks, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Accelerating Generalizable Manipulation Research:** By explicitly focusing on generalizable manipulation skills and providing diverse object-level variations, ManiSkill2 directly addresses one of the most critical and challenging problems in robotics. The benchmark will drive the development of novel algorithms that can learn robust policies capable of interacting with unseen objects and configurations, moving beyond task-specific solutions.

2. **Setting a New Standard for Realism and Complexity:** The integration of real-time, 2-way coupled rigid-MPM soft-body simulation is a groundbreaking feature. This allows researchers to explore complex interactions with deformable materials in a performant and realistic manner, opening new avenues for research that were previously inaccessible or inefficient. The emphasis on fully dynamic simulation and high-precision tasks (e.g., millimeter-level peg-in-hole assembly) pushes the boundaries of what robot learning algorithms must achieve.

3. **Enhancing Accessibility and Reproducibility:** ManiSkill2's unified OpenAI Gym interface, open-source code, extensive documentation, large-scale demonstrations, and cloud-based evaluation system significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers. This fosters greater collaboration, ensures reproducibility of results, and enables fair comparisons across diverse algorithmic approaches, accelerating collective progress.

4. **Challenging Current State-of-the-Art:** The consistently low success rates of even advanced SPA, IL, and RL algorithms on many of ManiSkill2's tasks, particularly those requiring high precision or complex soft-body interactions, underscore that the benchmark poses "meaningful challenges" for the community. This will catalyze innovation in areas such as robust perception for varied observations (point clouds, RGBD), advanced motion planning in contact-rich environments, and more sample-efficient and precise reinforcement learning techniques.

5. **Boosting Visual Reinforcement Learning Efficiency:** The novel performance optimizations, including asynchronous rendering and the RPC-based render server, dramatically improve sample collection speed and reduce GPU memory consumption. This is a critical enabler for visual RL, allowing researchers to conduct larger-scale experiments and iterate faster on complex vision-based policies, ultimately speeding up the development cycle.

6. **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** The design philosophy of full dynamic simulation and the demonstrated potential for sim2real transfer (e.g., PickCube policy success in real world, fidelity of soft-body deformation) make ManiSkill2 a valuable platform for developing policies that can be directly deployed on physical robots. This brings research closer to real-world robotic applications.

In conclusion, ManiSkill2 is not just another benchmark; it is a meticulously crafted, technically advanced, and strategically motivated platform designed to serve as a cornerstone for the next generation of embodied AI research in robotic manipulation. Its comprehensiveness, realism, and efficiency, coupled with its open-source nature and challenge infrastructure, are poised to significantly influence the trajectory of robot learning for years to come.
