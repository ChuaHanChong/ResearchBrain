# Predicting Gradient is Better: Exploring Self-Supervised Learning for SAR ATR with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture

> [!summary] Summary
> SAR-JEPA, a self-supervised learning framework developed at NUDT, utilizes a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture to predict robust SAR-domain gradient representations, rather than raw pixels, for Synthetic Aperture Radar Automatic Target Recognition (SAR ATR). This approach achieved an average fine-tuning accuracy of 91.6% for 40-shot learning on MSTAR, outperforming MAE's 69.5% by learning more generalized features from unlabeled SAR data.

## Problem

- SAR ATR suffers from limited labeled data due to high acquisition and annotation costs, hindering robust deep learning model development.
- SAR images present unique challenges, including small targets within vast scenes and pervasive multiplicative speckle noise, which degrade image quality and impact feature extraction.
- Traditional self-supervised learning methods, often designed for natural images, struggle to adapt effectively to the specific characteristics of SAR data.

## Method

- **SAR-JEPA** (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for SAR ATR) is proposed as a self-supervised learning framework that adapts Masked Image Modeling (MIM) for SAR imagery.
- The method employs local masked patches to focus on contextual information around small targets and uses a SAR-domain-specific target encoder based on Gradient-by-Ratio (GR) to predict multi-scale ==gradient== features, which are robust to speckle noise.
- A Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture is utilized, with an 8-layer ViT decoder and an MSE loss function, to ensure the model learns semantically meaningful representations guided by robust gradient information.

## Results

- SAR-JEPA consistently outperformed ImageNet pre-training, MAE, FG-MAE, I-JEPA, and LoMaR across MSTAR, FUSAR-Ship, and SAR-ACD datasets in both few-shot fine-tuning and linear probing settings.
- On the MSTAR dataset, SAR-JEPA achieved average fine-tuning accuracies of **70.3%** (10-shot), **82.1%** (20-shot), and **91.6%** (40-shot), demonstrating substantial improvements over baseline methods.
- Scaling experiments showed improved performance with increased pre-training data and model parameters up to ViT-Base, supporting its potential for foundation models, but indicated that very large models (ViT-Large) showed overfitting with current dataset sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting SAR-domain-specific gradients, particularly via the Gradient-by-Ratio method, is more effective for self-supervised learning in noisy SAR imagery than reconstructing raw pixels.
> - Local masking strategies are crucial for learning effective contextual information around small targets embedded within large remote sensing scenes.
> - A robust, deeper decoder (e.g., an 8-layer ViT) is essential for effectively handling speckle noise during masked image modeling in SAR ATR, enabling the encoder to learn richer semantic features.
