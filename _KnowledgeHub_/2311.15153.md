# Predicting Gradient is Better: Exploring Self-Supervised Learning for SAR ATR with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture

> [!summary] Summary
> SAR-JEPA, a self-supervised learning framework developed at NUDT, utilizes a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture to predict robust SAR-domain gradient representations, rather than raw pixels, for Synthetic Aperture Radar Automatic Target Recognition (SAR ATR). This approach achieved an average fine-tuning accuracy of 91.6% for 40-shot learning on MSTAR, outperforming MAE's 69.5% by learning more generalized features from unlabeled SAR data.

## Problem

- SAR ATR suffers from limited labeled data due to high acquisition and annotation costs, hindering robust deep learning model development.
- SAR images present unique challenges, including small targets within vast scenes and pervasive multiplicative speckle noise, which degrade image quality and impact feature extraction.
- Traditional self-supervised learning methods, often designed for natural images, struggle to adapt effectively to the specific characteristics of SAR data.

## Method

- **SAR-JEPA** (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for SAR ATR) is proposed as a self-supervised learning framework that adapts Masked Image Modeling (MIM) for SAR imagery.
- The method employs local masked patches to focus on contextual information around small targets and uses a SAR-domain-specific target encoder based on Gradient-by-Ratio (GR) to predict multi-scale ==gradient== features, which are robust to speckle noise.
- A Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture is utilized, with an 8-layer ViT decoder and an MSE loss function, to ensure the model learns semantically meaningful representations guided by robust gradient information.

## Results

- SAR-JEPA consistently outperformed ImageNet pre-training, MAE, FG-MAE, I-JEPA, and LoMaR across MSTAR, FUSAR-Ship, and SAR-ACD datasets in both few-shot fine-tuning and linear probing settings.
- On the MSTAR dataset, SAR-JEPA achieved average fine-tuning accuracies of **70.3%** (10-shot), **82.1%** (20-shot), and **91.6%** (40-shot), demonstrating substantial improvements over baseline methods.
- Scaling experiments showed improved performance with increased pre-training data and model parameters up to ViT-Base, supporting its potential for foundation models, but indicated that very large models (ViT-Large) showed overfitting with current dataset sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting SAR-domain-specific gradients, particularly via the Gradient-by-Ratio method, is more effective for self-supervised learning in noisy SAR imagery than reconstructing raw pixels.
> - Local masking strategies are crucial for learning effective contextual information around small targets embedded within large remote sensing scenes.
> - A robust, deeper decoder (e.g., an 8-layer ViT) is essential for effectively handling speckle noise during masked image modeling in SAR ATR, enabling the encoder to learn richer semantic features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) is an indispensable technology for Earth observation due to its ability to acquire imagery under various weather and lighting conditions. SAR Automatic Target Recognition (SAR ATR), which involves localizing and classifying targets within SAR images, is a crucial component with widespread applications in civilian sectors (e.g., transportation, autonomous driving) and military surveillance. Over the past decade, deep learning has revolutionized SAR ATR, leading to significant advancements in accuracy and robustness.

Despite these successes, SAR ATR faces persistent challenges. Foremost among them are the high costs associated with data collection and expert annotation, which severely limit the diversity and scale of available labeled datasets. This has led to specialized approaches for different target types (e.g., vehicles, ships, aircraft), hindering the development of generalized, versatile models. The unique properties of SAR images, such as multiplicative speckle noise, phase errors, and the frequent presence of small targets within large scenes, further complicate interpretation and feature extraction. Traditional deep learning often relies on vast amounts of labeled data, a luxury SAR ATR rarely affords.

In this context, Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) has emerged as a promising paradigm shift. SSL aims to construct supervisory signals directly from unlabeled data, thereby minimizing the reliance on expensive annotations and efficiently leveraging the rapidly expanding pool of unlabeled data. This approach is instrumental in the development of "foundation models" – large models pre-trained on extensive datasets that can then be fine-tuned for various downstream tasks with minimal labeled data. Such models have demonstrated remarkable success in general computer vision, remote sensing, and even medical imaging.

This paper positions itself directly at the intersection of deep learning, SSL, and SAR ATR. It addresses the critical need for an effective SSL method tailored for SAR images, explicitly aiming to pave the way for a foundation model in SAR ATR. By overcoming the data scarcity and unique image property challenges, the authors seek to enable the development of a generic representation and method applicable across diverse SAR target recognition tasks, moving beyond the current fragmented, task-specific approaches. The work builds upon recent advancements in Masked Image Modeling (MIM) from general computer vision, adapting and innovating them for the specific demands of SAR data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and propose an effective Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) method specifically designed for Synthetic Aperture Radar Automatic Target Recognition (SAR ATR). This method, named SAR-JEPA (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for SAR ATR), aims to enable the development of a foundational model capable of unifying various SAR ATR tasks.

The motivation for this research stems from two critical challenges in current SAR ATR methodologies:

1. **Lack of Large-Scale Pretraining Datasets:** Unlike natural image processing, where datasets like ImageNet provide massive, diverse labeled data for pre-training, SAR ATR lacks a comparable large-scale, unified dataset. The prohibitive costs of SAR data acquisition and expert annotation result in limited dataset diversity, often containing only a few thousand images and a handful of target categories. This scarcity hinders the development of generalized, robust deep learning models and prevents the full exploration of SSL's potential for foundation models in SAR ATR.
2. **Special Properties of SAR Images:** SAR images possess unique characteristics that pose significant difficulties for traditional SSL methods:
 - **Small Targets in Remote Sensing:** Remote sensing images typically feature vast scenes containing relatively small targets. Learning effective contextual information around these small targets, which are crucial for recognition, is challenging for global masking strategies common in SSL.
 - **Speckle Noise:** SAR images are inherently affected by multiplicative speckle noise, phase errors, and other interferences. This noise degrades image quality and directly impacts the quality of learned representations. Reconstructing raw pixels, as done in some SSL approaches, becomes unreliable under such noise, leading to suboptimal feature learning.

To address these motivations, the authors propose SAR-JEPA, which leverages SAR domain features to generate high-quality self-supervised signals and incorporates specific architectural designs to accommodate the challenges of small targets and speckle noise.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for SAR ATR (SAR-JEPA)** is a novel SSL framework that adapts Masked Image Modeling (MIM) principles to the unique characteristics of SAR imagery. The overall objective is to predict feature representations in the SAR domain for unseen image contexts, enabling the model to learn contextual relationships crucial for target recognition.

The methodology can be broken down into three core components:

1. **Local Masked Patches:**
 - **Challenge Addressed:** Small targets in large remote sensing scenes.
 - **Approach:** Inspired by LoMaR (Local Masked Reconstruction), SAR-JEPA randomly selects multiple square windows (local patches) from the input SAR image. Within each local patch, a fixed percentage of "mask tokens" are added. This local masking strategy focuses the learning on contextual information within smaller, relevant regions around potential targets, which is more effective for small objects in vast scenes compared to masking the entire image. The use of relative positional encoding and inputting mask tokens into the encoder is adopted from LoMaR to prevent feature collapse.

2. **SAR-Domain-Specific Target Encoder (Gradient-by-Ratio - GR):**
 - **Challenge Addressed:** Multiplicative speckle noise in SAR images and the need for high-quality target features.
 - **Approach:** Instead of reconstructing noisy raw pixel values, SAR-JEPA predicts a SAR-domain-specific gradient representation. The authors adopt the **Gradient-by-Ratio (GR)** method, which is known to be more robust to multiplicative noise than traditional differential gradients.
 - **Gradient Computation:** GR calculates gradients based on the ratio of average (ROA) values in opposite directions around a pixel, followed by a logarithmic transformation. This approach effectively suppresses speckle noise while highlighting target shapes and edges.
 - **Multi-Scale Feature:** To accommodate the dynamic range of target sizes (small to large) in remote sensing, the target encoder generates multi-scale GR features. This is achieved by using convolution kernels of various sizes (e.g., r=5, 9, 13, 17) to compute ROA. The resulting four gradient features are then concatenated to form a rich multi-scale SAR feature representation ($f_{SAR}$). Smaller kernels capture fine contours of small targets, while larger kernels are better for larger targets, scene edges, and broader noise suppression.

3. **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture:**
 - **Core Structure:** SAR-JEPA utilizes a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture, similar to MAE (Masked Autoencoder), for its encoder and predictor. The encoder processes the visible patches of the local masked regions, while the DNN predictor is tasked with predicting the multi-scale GR features of the *unseen (masked)* patches.
 - **Loss Function:** The loss function computes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the deep features ($f_{Deep}$) extracted by the DNN encoder/predictor for the masked patches and the corresponding multi-scale SAR gradient features ($f_{SAR}$) derived from the target encoder. This design ensures that the model learns to extract features that are semantically meaningful in the SAR domain, guided by robust gradient information.
 - **Decoder Design:** Unlike some MIM methods that use simple linear decoders, SAR-JEPA employs an 8-layer ViT decoder. This deeper decoder is found to be more resilient to SAR image noise, allowing the encoder to learn richer, deeper semantic features without being overly perturbed by noisy reconstruction targets.
 - **Data Augmentation:** In addition to standard MAE augmentations (ResizedCrop, HFlip), a random contrast adjustment is applied to SAR magnitude images, which helps increase data diversity by accounting for strong and weak scattering points.

In essence, SAR-JEPA's approach combines a sophisticated MIM framework with domain-specific knowledge injection (GR features and multi-scale processing) to create a powerful self-supervised learning mechanism for SAR ATR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents a comprehensive evaluation of SAR-JEPA through ablation studies, comparisons with other SSL methods, and scaling experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in SAR ATR.

**Ablation Study:**
- **MIM Framework:** The study compared MAE (global masks) and LoMaR (local masks) and found that for remote sensing images with small targets, local masking (LoMaR) was more effective for learning contextual relationships. However, LoMaR's linear decoder was susceptible to SAR speckle noise. The proposed **LoMaR-SAR**, which replaces LoMaR's linear decoder with an 8-layer ViT decoder (inspired by MAE), showed significant performance improvement across MSTAR, FUSAR-Ship, and SAR-ACD datasets, confirming the importance of a robust decoder for noisy SAR data. LoMaR-SAR serves as the backbone MIM module for SAR-JEPA.
- **Target Feature Selection:** This was a critical ablation.
 - **Pixel-level (LoMaR-SAR baseline):** Performed reasonably well but was still affected by noise.
 - **Low Pass Filter (LPF):** Showed marginal improvement or even slight degradation in some cases (e.g., MSTAR linear probing), indicating that simple filtering isn't sufficient.
 - **HOG (Histogram of Oriented Gradients):** Surprisingly, HOG features, while useful for SAR scene classification (FG-MAE), performed poorly for target-level SSL. This was attributed to speckle noise creating virtual gradient points in small target regions.
 - **SAR-HOG:** A variant of HOG using ratio gradients (similar to GR) significantly improved over standard HOG, validating the principle of ratio-based gradient computation for SAR.
 - **Gradient-by-Ratio (GR):** Both linear kernel (GR_lin) and Gaussian kernel (GR_Gau) GR methods outperformed all other target features, particularly on MSTAR. GR_lin was chosen for SAR-JEPA due to its simpler implementation and comparable performance to GR_Gau without extensive hyperparameter tuning.
 - **Multi-Scale Features:** An ablation on single-scale vs. multi-scale GR features demonstrated that concatenating features from various kernel sizes (multi-scale) consistently yielded better performance than any single-scale, confirming its importance for handling diverse target sizes in remote sensing.
- **Combination of MIM and PGCA:** SAR-JEPA integrates MIM (LoMaR-SAR) with the idea of Physics-Guided Contrastive Architecture (PGCA) by using SAR-domain features as supervision. The ablation showed that both MIM and PGCA components are mutually reinforcing and crucial for SAR-JEPA's superior performance, confirming that combining a challenging pretext task with high-quality domain-specific signals leads to robust feature learning.

**Comparisons with Other Methods:**
SAR-JEPA was benchmarked against ImageNet pre-training, MAE, FG-MAE, I-JEPA, and LoMaR on three diverse SAR ATR datasets: MSTAR (vehicles), FUSAR-Ship (ships), and SAR-ACD (aircraft) under few-shot fine-tuning and linear probing settings.
- **Outperformance:** SAR-JEPA consistently outperformed all baseline methods across all three datasets and both evaluation settings. For instance, on MSTAR, SAR-JEPA achieved an average fine-tuning accuracy of 70.3% (10-shot), 82.1% (20-shot), and 91.6% (40-shot), significantly higher than MAE (50.6%, 61.3%, 69.5%) and other methods. Similar improvements were observed for FUSAR-Ship and SAR-ACD.
- **Impact of SAR-specific Design:** The results highlighted the limitations of methods not tailored for SAR (ImageNet, I-JEPA, standard HOG in FG-MAE), which often suffered from noise sensitivity or feature collapse. LoMaR performed better than MAE but was still constrained by pixel-level reconstruction. SAR-JEPA's domain-informed design was key to its success.
- **Aircraft Recognition Challenge:** While SAR-JEPA performed best on SAR-ACD, the absolute accuracy was lower than for vehicles and ships, and 10-shot fine-tuning showed signs of overfitting. This was attributed to the smoother surface of aircraft (leading to sparse strong scattering points) and limited aircraft samples in the pre-training dataset, suggesting areas for future improvement.

**Visualization (Attention Distance):**
Analysis of ViT attention distance revealed that:
- MAE's deep layers primarily focused on global information.
- LoMaR-SAR, with local reconstruction, effectively modeled local information.
- PGCA (pixel-level contrastive learning with SAR-domain features) also captured local information.
- SAR-JEPA, by integrating MIM and PGCA principles, demonstrated the ability to model both local and global features, with MIM pushing for more high-level global contextual information. This validated the hybrid approach for SAR image interpretation.

**Scaling Experiment:**
SAR-JEPA's scalability was assessed with increasing dataset size, model parameters, and training epochs.
- **Dataset Size:** Performance significantly improved as the volume of pre-training data increased, demonstrating that SAR-JEPA effectively leverages more (even noisy) unlabeled SAR images. This supports its potential for foundation models.
- **Model Parameters:** Larger models (up to ViT-Base) generally led to better representation quality. However, the largest model (ViT-Large) showed reduced performance due to overfitting, indicating that the current pre-training dataset size is still insufficient to fully exploit very large models without overfitting.
- **Training Epoch:** Similarly, excessively long training epochs led to overfitting for ViT-Base, suggesting an optimal training duration for the given data scale.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of Synthetic Aperture Radar Automatic Target Recognition (SAR ATR) by effectively adapting and innovating Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) for SAR imagery. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paving the Way for SAR ATR Foundation Models:** The most profound impact of this work is its explicit demonstration of SSL's potential to create a *foundation model* for SAR ATR. By showing that a generalized feature representation can be learned from large-scale unlabeled SAR data, SAR-JEPA offers a viable path to unify various SAR ATR tasks (vehicles, ships, aircraft, etc.) and potentially tasks across different sensors and scenes. This moves the field away from specialized, data-hungry models towards more versatile and efficient ones.

2. **Addressing Core SAR Image Challenges:** SAR-JEPA directly tackles two fundamental challenges in SAR image interpretation:
 - **Speckle Noise Robustness:** By proposing to predict SAR-domain gradient representations (Gradient-by-Ratio) instead of noisy raw pixels, the method provides a robust self-supervised signal that effectively mitigates the pervasive issue of multiplicative speckle noise. This innovation is crucial for reliable feature learning from inherently noisy SAR data.
 - **Small Target Learning:** The adoption of local masked patches and multi-scale features specifically addresses the challenge of identifying and understanding small targets within large remote sensing scenes, enabling the model to focus on relevant contextual information.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Generalizability:** The consistently superior performance of SAR-JEPA over existing SSL methods across diverse target categories (vehicles, ships, aircraft) and under few-shot learning conditions highlights its effectiveness. This indicates that the learned representations are more generalized and semantically richer, allowing for better transferability to downstream tasks with limited labeled data.

4. **Stimulating Future Research in SAR SSL:** The study's detailed ablation analysis and scaling experiments provide valuable insights into designing effective SSL strategies for SAR. It clearly identifies the need for larger, more diverse SAR pre-training datasets to unlock the full potential of very large models. This will likely stimulate further efforts in SAR data collection, synthesis, and the exploration of novel SSL architectures and pretext tasks. The finding that pixel-level pretext tasks can induce both local and global attention ranges in ViT offers a new perspective on model behavior in SAR contexts.

5. **Practical Implications for Applications:** For military surveillance, disaster monitoring, and environmental observation, the ability to train robust SAR ATR models with minimal labeled data will significantly reduce operational costs and deployment time. A foundation model could enable rapid adaptation to new target types or operational environments, providing critical intelligence more efficiently.

In conclusion, "Predicting Gradient is Better" is a landmark contribution that moves SAR ATR closer to the era of foundation models. By meticulously addressing the unique complexities of SAR data through innovative SSL design, it not only pushes the state-of-the-art in SAR target recognition but also lays down a robust framework for future research in self-supervised learning for remote sensing.
