# DeltaWAM: Change-Centric Visual Foresight via Delta Tokens for an Efficient World-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> DeltaWAM is a lightweight World-Action Model that predicts the future as change rather than scenes: a language-conditioned DeltaWorld predictor autoregressively rolls out one compact delta token per frame, encoding DINOv3 feature differences, and a flow-matching Action DiT conditions on these plus current DINO features. At 0.725B parameters and 142 ms per chunk it reaches 92.8% on LIBERO and 17.85% on LIBERO-Pro task perturbations.

## Problem

- Pixel-space WAMs repaint entire future scenes with video diffusion, spending capacity and latency on task-irrelevant static background repeated across frames, which bottlenecks real-time closed-loop control.
- Latent WAMs avoid pixel synthesis but still forecast absolute full-scene latent states per timestep, wasting representation on shared static context and obscuring the action-relevant dynamics needed for control.
- Efficiency-oriented WAMs relegate future prediction to an auxiliary training loss, while the original delta-token world model is unconditional and can hallucinate plausible futures misaligned with the instruction.

## Method

- ==Language-conditioned DeltaWorld==: a Kinetics-pretrained predictor autoregressively generating one 768-d ==delta token== per view per frame, steered by frozen T5 instructions through zero-initialized ==Gated AdaLN== in every block.
- ==Best-of-Many training==: K Gaussian queries produce candidate futures and only the lowest ==Smooth-L1== candidate is supervised, making the predictor generative in a single forward pass without iterative denoising.
- Delta-conditioned ==flow-matching== **Action DiT** with ==layer-wise KV adapter==, conditioned on current DINO patches as spatial anchor and predicted delta tokens as dynamic subgoal; trained on frozen DeltaWorld rollouts using ==oracle-branch selection== with probability 0.5.

## Results

- LIBERO average **92.8%** with **0.725B** parameters, **142.1 ms** per chunk, **3.86 GB** peak memory and **256 GPU hours**, below LaWAM's **98.6%** but far cheaper than Cosmos-Policy's **1,413 ms**.
- LIBERO-Pro task perturbation average **17.85%** (**42.2%** on Spatial) versus **7.1%** for Fast-WAM and under 1% for pi0 and pi0.5; LIBERO-Plus camera **66.9%** versus pi0-FAST **65.1%** and Fast-WAM **16.4%**.
- Ablations: conditioning on decoded future DINO drops success to **79.0%** and current-DINO-only to **73.9%**; zero future tokens gives 0% on all suites, and an 8-step horizon underperforms 6-step (**90.0%** vs 92.8%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting what changes, not whole scenes, can be a stronger policy signal: compressed transition tokens beat 1,024x-larger full-future latents, so foresight compactness and action relevance need not trade off.
> - Factoring foresight into a static spatial anchor plus dynamic change tokens yields representations more invariant to lighting and task-layout perturbations, offering robustness without collecting more diverse data.
> - Caution: heavy delta compression filters fine spatial detail, causing off-center placements in precision contact, and autoregressive error accumulation means longer foresight horizons can hurt rather than help.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot is told to pick up the black bowl on the wooden cabinet and place it on the plate. A forecast made without that instruction heads toward the stove instead. DeltaWAM forecasts a compact signal of **what will change** and steers that forecast with the instruction. It then gives the action policy both the forecast and the current view. The authors report that language steers the prediction toward the requested task. This is a predicted representation, not evidence that the robot completed the pick-and-place in this example.

Wrist-view predicted rollouts from unconditional and language-conditioned DeltaWorld, from the current RGB observation through +0.8 seconds. The unconditional prediction approaches the stove rather than the cabinet where the bowl is. Both rows are forecasts.

### 2. From scenes to changes

A robot needs visual foresight to plan manipulation. Predicting full frames or full-scene latent states repeatedly spends capacity on context that often stays the same.

DeltaWAM instead uses a "delta token." This is a learned, single-vector summary of the change between consecutive dense DINO visual features, not a pixel difference. The tokenizer is trained so that the previous feature map and the token can reconstruct the current one. At inference, DeltaWorld predicts a sequence of these tokens autoregressively, one per future step and camera view.

The action generator combines that forecast with dense features of the current view. The forecast says, roughly, *how things should move*, and the current features preserve *where things are*.

The delta-token idea is not new here: [[2604.04913|DeltaWorld]] introduced it for video world modeling. DeltaWAM applies it to robot control. It adds task-instruction conditioning through a frozen text encoder and Gated AdaLN, and it passes predicted transitions to a flow-matching action expert. Training happens in two stages. The predictor is first adapted on LIBERO demonstrations. It is then frozen while the action expert and its KV adapter are trained.

### 3. Does compression help the policy?

The LIBERO average is the arithmetic mean of terminal success rates on four suites: Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long. It is evaluated over 50 trials per task and three random seeds.

The key ablation holds the training and inference configuration fixed and changes what the action expert conditions on. With compressed delta tokens, the average reaches 92.8%. With decoded future DINO features, it reaches 79.0%, even though the future portion of the delta-token representation is about 1,024 times smaller. The current-DINO-only variant averages 73.9%.

This supports the claim that delta tokens are a better conditioning signal in this setup. It does **not** prove that token compression alone caused the entire performance difference.

A longer horizon is not always better. The default six-step model averages 92.8%, compared with 90.0% when trained with eight future steps. The authors cite accumulating autoregressive prediction error as the trade-off.

### 4. Strong results, bounded claims

DeltaWAM's 92.8% is not a claim of best-in-class success, since LaWAM's reported table entry is 98.6%. DeltaWAM's reported advantage is a smaller model and lower model-only latency. It has 0.725B parameters and takes 142.1 ms per action-chunk query, versus LaWAM's 2.3B and 187 ms. These baseline figures are reported from their source papers and do not come from a matched experiment. DeltaWAM has no embodied action pretraining in that table, unlike LaWAM. Its latency excludes robot execution time.

LIBERO is simulation, and high compression may discard spatial detail needed for precise contact. The authors report off-center placement failures in Spatial and Long and attribute them to lost fine-grained alignment cues. Under LIBERO-Pro task perturbations, DeltaWAM averages 17.85%, led by 42.2% on Spatial. Object, Goal, and Long reach only 10.2%, 9.2%, and 9.8%. This is uneven robustness, and the comparison does not isolate delta tokens as its sole cause.

### 5. A forecast of change

DeltaWAM's distinctive interface forecasts task-conditioned transitions and anchors them to what is currently visible. The action policy does not have to work from a full reconstructed future scene.
