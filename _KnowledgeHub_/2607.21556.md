# Visual Contrastive Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Visual Contrastive Self-Distillation (VCSD) introduces a method for vision-language models to create a self-distillation signal by contrasting predictions under original and content-erased visual conditions. This approach consistently improved aggregate accuracy across seven benchmarks by up to 4.77% for Qwen3-VL 2B and 4.27% for Qwen3.5 9B models, without requiring external teachers or privileged information.

## Problem

- On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) for vision-language models often requires auxiliary information (e.g., privileged answers, visual evidence signals) to establish effective asymmetry between the self-teacher and student.
- Relying on auxiliary information introduces complexities such as task-specific data, additional processing pipelines, external annotations, or specific localization models.
- Vision-language models can exhibit a tendency to over-rely on linguistic priors, potentially leading to factual hallucinations or responses not strongly grounded in visual evidence.

## Method

- **VCSD** employs an ==Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher== to produce two next-token distributions: one conditioned on the original image and another on a content-erased control image (e.g., a black image).
- A ==vocabulary-level log-probability contrast== is computed by subtracting the content-erased prediction's log-probability from the original image's log-probability for each token, highlighting image-dependent changes.
- This contrast refines the original-image teacher distribution into a ==contrast-shaped target==, which is then distilled into the student model using a ==full-distribution forward KL divergence== loss, with a ==plausibility restriction== to stabilize learning.

## Results

- VCSD consistently outperformed both base models and standard OPSD baselines, achieving aggregate accuracy improvements up to **+4.77%** for Qwen3-VL 2B and **+4.27%** for Qwen3.5 9B models across seven vision-language benchmarks.
- The method demonstrated broad improvements across diverse tasks, including general perception, visual mathematics, high-resolution perception, and reduced hallucinations, with notable gains (e.g., **+4.32%** on HallusionBench for Qwen3-VL 2B).
- Ablation studies confirmed the benefits of contrastive shaping (yielding a **+2.33%** gain over no shaping), the use of forward KL divergence, and the plausibility restriction, while showing the method's robustness to different content-erased control image constructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective teacher-student asymmetry for on-policy self-distillation can be generated purely through input conditioning, by contrasting predictions made with and without instance-specific visual content.
> - The contrastive shaping acts as an implicit visual-evidence reward, guiding the teacher's policy to emphasize tokens strongly supported by the image and de-emphasize those favored by linguistic priors.
> - Applying a plausibility restriction during target shaping and using an original-image anchor are crucial for preventing target distortions, maintaining long-horizon self-distillation stability, and ensuring language consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of knowledge distillation, specifically focusing on its application to large vision-language models (VLMs) in an on-policy setting. Knowledge distillation involves transferring knowledge from a teacher model to a student model. On-policy distillation (OPD) is a training paradigm where a student model is trained on prefixes generated by its own policy, receiving token-level supervision from a teacher model. While OPD aligns training with the student's inference-time behavior, it typically requires a stronger, external teacher, which can increase computational cost and complexity.

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) addresses this by using the same underlying model to derive targets, often through an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher. However, a key challenge in OPSD is establishing an asymmetry between the self-teacher and the student. Without this asymmetry, if the teacher and student receive identical information at the same prefix, the teacher's target distribution may not provide a sufficiently stronger or more informative learning signal than the student's current prediction.

Existing OPSD methods create this asymmetry through auxiliary information provided to the self-teacher but not the student. For language reasoning tasks, this often includes privileged answers, reasoning traces, expert demonstrations, or rich textual feedback. For vision-language tasks, the teacher might receive visual evidence signals such as crops, regions, or other visual conditions that highlight relevant image features. These approaches, while effective, often rely on task-specific information, require additional processing pipelines, external annotations, localization models, or manually designed image transformations, which can increase complexity and dependency on external resources.

This research aims to simplify OPSD by exploring whether the necessary teacher-student asymmetry can be constructed purely from input conditioning, without relying on external teachers, privileged answers, or specific visual evidence signals. It proposes a method that leverages a contrast between predictions under original and content-erased visual conditions to generate a refined self-distillation signal, thereby contributing to the development of more generalizable and resource-efficient self-distillation techniques for VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether the asymmetry required for effective on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) can be generated purely through input conditioning, thereby eliminating the need for external teachers, privileged answers, or explicit visual evidence signals. This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **Simplification of OPSD:** Existing OPSD methods, while removing the need for an entirely separate external teacher, still often rely on auxiliary information (e.g., privileged answers, reasoning traces, visual evidence signals like crops or regions) to create a meaningful asymmetry between the self-teacher and student. This auxiliary information introduces dependencies on task-specific data, additional processing pipelines, annotations, or external models, increasing overall system complexity. The research seeks to develop a simpler OPSD paradigm that removes these dependencies.

2. **Increased Generality and Reduced Resource Dependence:** Task-specific auxiliary information may not always be available or easy to acquire across diverse applications. For instance, privileged answers or reasoning traces are often specific to certain language reasoning tasks, and generating detailed visual evidence signals can require costly annotations or sophisticated external localization models. By deriving asymmetry solely from input conditioning, the proposed method aims to be more broadly applicable to various vision-language tasks without requiring bespoke data or architectural modifications.

3. **Enhancement of Visual Grounding:** Vision-language models (VLMs) sometimes exhibit a tendency to rely more on linguistic priors than on fine-grained image evidence when generating responses. This can lead to issues like hallucination or inaccurate descriptions if the model prioritizes language fluency over visual fidelity. The motivation includes developing a self-distillation signal that encourages stronger dependence on instance-specific visual evidence. By contrasting predictions made with and without specific visual content, the method aims to inherently highlight and amplify the significance of visual cues, guiding the student to produce responses that are more firmly grounded in the observed image.

4. **No Additional Inference-Time Cost:** A practical motivation is to achieve performance improvements without incurring additional computational overhead during the inference phase. Many decoding strategies or external verifiers used to improve VLM outputs introduce extra forward passes or complex computations at inference time. The proposed method aims to distill improved visual grounding into the student model during training, allowing the trained student to operate with its standard inference procedure.

In summary, the primary motivation is to advance OPSD by making it more self-contained and less dependent on external forms of supervision or complex auxiliary inputs, ultimately leading to more robust, visually grounded, and efficient VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Visual Contrastive Self-Distillation (VCSD), establishes teacher-student asymmetry in OPSD by contrasting the EMA teacher's next-token predictions under two matched visual conditions: the original image and a content-erased control image.

#### 3.1. On-Policy Self-Distillation Setup

The foundation is a standard OPSD framework.
- **Student Model ($\pi_\theta$):** The trainable model whose parameters ($\theta$) are updated.
- **EMA Teacher Model ($\pi_\phi$):** An exponential moving average of the student's parameters ($\phi \leftarrow \mu\phi + (1-\mu)\theta$). The teacher acts as a stop-gradient target, meaning its parameters are not updated directly by the loss.
- **On-Policy Rollout:** For each prompt-image pair (P, J) from the dataset, the student samples a response $y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot | P, J)$. At each generation step $t$, the student produces a next-token distribution $p_{\theta,t}(v) = \pi_\theta(v | P, J, y_{<t})$. This ensures that supervision occurs along the student's own generated trajectories.

#### 3.2. Visual Conditioning Contrast

To create target asymmetry, VCSD introduces a content-erased control image, $J_{ctrl}$, which is a black RGB image of the same size as the original image $J$. This control image preserves input interface properties (resolution, visual-token count, preprocessing path) but removes instance-specific visual content.

At each step $t$ along the student-generated prefix $y_{<t}$, the EMA teacher produces two next-token distributions:
- $p^J_{\phi,t}(v) = \pi_\phi(v | P, J, y_{<t})$: Teacher's prediction conditioned on the original image.
- $p^0_{\phi,t}(v) = \pi_\phi(v | P, J_{ctrl}, y_{<t})$: Teacher's prediction conditioned on the content-erased image.

The core of the asymmetry is the **vocabulary-level log-probability contrast**:
$\Delta_t(v) = \log p^J_{\phi,t}(v) - \log p^0_{\phi,t}(v)$.
This value indicates how much a token $v$'s likelihood changes when the instance-specific visual content is present compared to when it's absent. A positive $\Delta_t(v)$ suggests the token is strongly supported by the original image, while a negative value implies its likelihood increases when visual content is removed (e.g., due to reliance on linguistic priors). The control distribution $p^0_{\phi,t}(v)$ serves as a reference, not a direct distillation target.

#### 3.3. Contrast-Shaped Teacher Target

The raw contrast $\Delta_t(v)$ is not directly used as a target. Instead, it is employed to refine the original-image teacher distribution. This refinement incorporates two complementary roles:
1. **Plausibility Anchor:** The original-image teacher distribution $p^J_{\phi,t}(v)$ is used to identify a plausible set of candidate tokens. This is defined by a relative plausibility support $S_t(\beta) = \{v \in V : p^J_{\phi,t}(v) \ge \beta \max_{u \in V} p^J_{\phi,t}(u)\}$, where $\beta \in$ is a threshold. This ensures that shaping only occurs among tokens the teacher already considers reasonably likely under the original image.
2. **Contrastive Shaping:** Within this plausible set, the contrast $\Delta_t(v)$ sharpens the relative preferences. The contrast-shaped teacher target, $q^\star_t(v)$, is constructed as:
 $q^\star_t(v) \propto \mathbf{1}[v \in S_t(\beta)] p^J_{\phi,t}(v) \exp(\alpha \tilde{\Delta}_t(v))$,
 where $\alpha \ge 0$ controls the strength of shaping, and $\tilde{\Delta}_t(v)$ is $\Delta_t(v)$ but with sequence-termination tokens' contrast set to zero for stability.
 This formulation implies that the unnormalized log score for tokens in $S_t(\beta)$ is $(1 + \alpha) \log p^J_{\phi,t}(v) - \alpha \log p^0_{\phi,t}(v)$. When $\alpha=0$, the target reduces to the original-image teacher distribution renormalized over $S_t(\beta)$.

#### 3.4. On-Policy Distillation and EMA Update

The contrast-shaped target $q^\star_t(v)$ is distilled into the student using a full-distribution forward KL divergence loss:
$L_{VCSD} = T_{KD}^2 \mathbb{E}_{(P,J) \sim D, y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|P,J)} \left[ \frac{1}{|y|} \sum_{t=1}^{|y|} D_{KL}(\text{sg}[q^\star_t] \parallel p_{\theta,t}) \right]$
where $\text{sg}[\cdot]$ denotes stop-gradient, and $T_{KD}$ is a distillation temperature. Gradients only flow through the student's distribution.
After each student update, the EMA teacher's parameters are updated using $\phi \leftarrow \mu\phi + (1-\mu)\theta$, with $\mu$ being the EMA decay coefficient.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Perspective

The methodology is supported by two interpretations:
- **One-Step KL-Regularized Policy Update:** The contrast-shaped target $q^\star_t(v)$ is mathematically equivalent to the unique solution of an optimization problem. This problem maximizes an implicit visual-evidence reward, $r^{vis}_t(v) = \tilde{\Delta}_t(v)$, while being regularized by the KL divergence from the support-normalized original-image teacher distribution. This framing suggests that the contrast effectively defines a reward signal that guides the teacher's policy update.
- **Approximation to Conditional Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI):** The contrast $\Delta_t(v)$ can be viewed as an approximation to $\text{PMI}(v; J | H_t) = \log \frac{p(v | J, H_t)}{p(v | H_t)}$, where $H_t=(P, y_{<t})$ is the textual context. By using $p_\phi(v | J_{ctrl}, H_t)$ as a surrogate for the model's prediction without instance-specific visual information $p_\phi(v | H_t)$, $\Delta_t(v)$ measures how much the token's log-likelihood is specifically influenced by the observed image beyond content-independent factors. Upweighting tokens with large positive contrast encourages the student to generate image-dependent evidence.

The method requires only the original prompt-image pair and the student's on-policy response for training, and introduces no additional inference-time cost as only the updated student is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VCSD was conducted on the ViRL39K dataset using Qwen3-VL and Qwen3.5 models (2B to 9B parameters) across seven vision-language benchmarks: BLINK, MMStar, MathVista, V$^\ast$Bench, HRBench4K, HRBench8K, and HallusionBench.

#### 4.1. Performance Improvements

- **Consistent Outperformance:** VCSD consistently outperformed both the base models and the matched On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) baselines (which use privileged answers) across all tested Qwen3-VL and Qwen3.5 model scales.
- **Qwen3-VL Models:**
 - **2B Scale:** VCSD improved the seven-benchmark aggregate accuracy from 62.27% (Base) to 67.04% (a +4.77% gain), surpassing OPSD's 64.89%. VCSD improved every individual benchmark over OPSD (e.g., BLINK +1.27%, MMStar +3.00%, MathVista +1.40%, HallusionBench +4.32%).
 - **4B Scale:** Aggregate accuracy improved from 71.30% (Base) to 73.16% (a +1.86% gain), over OPSD's 71.40%.
 - **8B Scale:** Aggregate accuracy improved from 72.51% (Base) to 76.26% (a +3.75% gain), over OPSD's 73.72%.
- **Qwen3.5 Models:**
 - VCSD consistently improved these models by 2.9% to 4.3% over their corresponding base models, whereas OPSD showed no consistent gain over the base models in this family. For example, at 9B, VCSD achieved 79.24% aggregate accuracy, compared to 74.97% (Base) and 74.73% (OPSD), representing a +4.27% gain.
- The improvements spanned general perception, visual mathematics, fine-grained/high-resolution perception, and hallucination reduction, suggesting a broad enhancement of visual capabilities.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Plausibility Restriction:** Restricting target shaping to tokens within the plausible support (using $\beta=0.1$) stabilized long-horizon self-distillation. Without this restriction ($\beta=0$), performance steadily degraded with continued training, suggesting that plausibility support prevents accumulation of target distortions.
- **Contrastive Strength ($\alpha$):** Performance was maximal and locally robust for $\alpha \in [1, 1.5]$. Setting $\alpha=0$ (removing contrastive shaping) reduced accuracy by 2.33% compared to $\alpha=1$. Increasing $\alpha$ too much (e.g., to 2) also reduced performance, indicating that a moderate level of contrastive shaping is beneficial.
- **Distillation Divergence:** Forward KL divergence performed best for distilling the contrast-shaped target (67.04% aggregate accuracy), outperforming JSD (66.25%) and reverse KL (64.77%). This suggests that preserving coverage of the full target distribution is advantageous.
- **Control-Image Construction:** The exact nature of the content-erased control image (black, Gaussian noise, Gaussian blur, no image) had a minimal effect on aggregate accuracy. All variants produced similar results, indicating that content removal itself, rather than a specific degradation process, is the critical factor.
- **Original-Image Anchor:** The original-image anchor primarily regularized generation stability rather than significantly improving aggregate accuracy. While aggregate accuracy was comparable with and without the anchor, the anchor substantially reduced "language drift" (rollouts containing non-target-language tokens) throughout training, improving language consistency.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics

- VCSD maintained a consistent advantage over OPSD throughout training, exhibiting less late-stage performance degradation on the aggregate accuracy as well as on individual benchmarks like MMStar and MathVista. This suggests that VCSD is more robust to continued self-distillation.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

- **MathVista Example:** A case study on a MathVista question involving visual counting demonstrated VCSD's ability to correctly interpret image evidence. While the base model and OPSD miscounted the number of "thousand-cubes," VCSD accurately identified the correct quantity, leading to the correct answer. This indicates improved image grounding.
- **Token-Level Contrast Scores:** Visualization of learned image-dependent contrast scores ($\Delta(v)$) showed that VCSD concentrated additional contrast on semantically meaningful visual concepts (e.g., "roof," "shingles," "food," "sign") compared to the base model and OPSD. This pattern supports the hypothesis that contrastive shaping reallocates probability mass toward image-dependent tokens, rather than merely amplifying existing linguistic preferences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Visual Contrastive Self-Distillation (VCSD) method introduces a novel approach to generate asymmetry in on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), offering several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Reduced Dependency on External Supervision:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating that effective teacher-student asymmetry for OPSD can be constructed purely from matched input conditioning. This eliminates the need for external teachers, privileged answers, reasoning traces, or specific visual evidence signals (like object crops or regions). This reduction in external dependencies simplifies the OPSD pipeline, potentially making it more cost-effective and easier to implement for a broader range of vision-language tasks where such auxiliary information may be scarce or expensive to acquire.

2. **Enhanced Visual Grounding and Reduced Hallucinations:** The core mechanism of VCSD—contrasting predictions under original and content-erased images—explicitly encourages the student model to develop stronger reliance on instance-specific visual evidence. By shaping the target distribution to amplify tokens strongly supported by the image and de-emphasize those favored by linguistic priors alone, VCSD promotes more accurate and visually grounded responses. This has a direct impact on mitigating issues such as factual hallucination, which is a common challenge in current large vision-language models (VLMs). The consistent improvements observed across benchmarks, including HallusionBench and visual reasoning tasks like MathVista, support this claim.

3. **Improved Robustness and Stability in Self-Distillation:** The ablation studies and training dynamics show that VCSD offers enhanced stability during extended self-distillation training. The plausibility restriction helps prevent target distortions from accumulating over recursive updates, and the original-image anchor regularizes generation consistency. This contributes to more robust VLM training processes, allowing models to benefit from longer distillation periods without performance degradation.

4. **Zero Inference-Time Overhead:** A practical advantage of VCSD is that the knowledge is distilled into the student model during training, and the trained student operates without any additional computational cost at inference time. This is a crucial benefit for deploying large models, as many methods for improving VLM output (e.g., contrastive decoding, external verifiers) introduce extra forward passes or complex procedures during inference, increasing latency and resource consumption.

5. **Generalizability Across Models and Scales:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across different Qwen model families (Qwen3-VL and Qwen3.5) and various model scales (2B to 9B parameters) suggest the method's broad applicability and effectiveness. This indicates that VCSD is a generalizable technique that can benefit a range of multimodal large language models.

In conclusion, VCSD presents a resource-efficient and effective method for improving the visual grounding and overall performance of VLMs through a simplified self-distillation process. Its ability to create informative self-supervision solely from input conditioning represents a significant step towards more autonomous and robust training paradigms for multimodal AI.
