# Disentangling Length Bias In Preference Learning Via Response-Conditioned Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Science and Technology of China developed a Response-conditioned Modeling framework to explicitly disentangle length bias in preference learning and enhance Large Language Models' adherence to explicit length instructions. This approach, utilizing a Response-conditioned Bradley-Terry model, demonstrated improved semantic quality and precise length control for generated text across various models and datasets.

## Problem

- Reward models for Large Language Models (LLMs) often exhibit 'length bias,' assigning higher scores to longer responses regardless of semantic quality, leading to reward overoptimization.
- LLMs struggle to adhere to explicit numerical length instructions despite implicit sensitivity to length, with current methods often compromising semantic quality to enforce length constraints.
- Existing strategies either inadequately mitigate length bias or achieve length instruction following at the cost of semantic quality, generally viewing length information as inherently detrimental.

## Method

- Introduced a ==Response-conditioned== Modeling framework, built upon a novel Response-conditioned Bradley-Terry (**Rc-BT**) model, to explicitly differentiate human semantic preferences from response length requirements.
- Created an augmented length instruction dataset (D_Rc) by generating preference pairs where responses either violate or satisfy specific length constraints, conditioning the model on how responses adhere to these conditions.
- Integrated the Rc-BT model into both reward modeling (Rc-RM) and direct preference optimization (Rc-DPO) algorithms to simultaneously mitigate length bias and promote adherence to length instructions during LLM alignment.

## Results

- Rc-RM significantly improved Quality Eval Accuracy (e.g., **69.55%** for Qwen2-**1.5B**-Base, compared to **59.14%** baseline) and achieved high Length Eval Accuracy (e.g., **92.31%** for Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct), outperforming baselines in both length bias mitigation and instruction adherence.
- Rc-DPO effectively reduced response length while improving semantic quality (Quality Win Ratio) on AlpacaEval, and demonstrated strong adherence to both 'or less' and 'or more' length instructions (e.g., **82.04%** to **100%** Length Acc) without sacrificing semantic coherence.
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining both chosen and rejected response augmentations is essential for effectiveness, with the augmented dataset showing high sample efficiency, and the framework generalizing well to out-of-distribution datasets and other biases like format bias.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling length information from semantic quality in preference learning allows LLMs to develop a controllable understanding of response length, moving beyond mere suppression of length as a confounder.
> - Augmenting preference data with conditions that specify length violations or satisfactions is essential for training models that can balance both semantic quality and precise length instruction following.
> - The proposed framework is generalizable across different foundational models, diverse datasets (including out-of-distribution scenarios), and effective in mitigating other types of superficial biases beyond just length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has achieved considerable success in aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences across various tasks, including conversation, code generation, and reasoning. A core component of RLHF is preference learning, which involves training a reward model to approximate human preferences. However, a significant challenge in this paradigm is "reward overoptimization" or "reward hacking," where LLMs exploit superficial confounding factors in the preference model to achieve higher scores without truly meeting the intended objective.

Among these confounding factors, length bias is identified as a prevalent and challenging issue. Reward models often assign higher scores to longer responses, regardless of their semantic quality. Existing strategies to mitigate length bias fall into two main categories:
1. **Rectifying policy optimization**: This involves techniques such as increasing KL penalty coefficients, applying length penalty rewards, or using reward clipping. The paper notes that these methods are often sensitive to hyperparameter changes and foundational model variations, and their effectiveness has been demonstrated to be limited.
2. **Disentangling length information from quality in reward modeling**: Approaches include length balancing in datasets, reward data augmentation, confidence-based truncation, and sophisticated training objectives (e.g., ODIN). While generally showing superior performance to the first category, these methods face issues such as unstable optimization due to overparameterization or the inability of regularization techniques to guarantee true independence between length and quality. Furthermore, the paper highlights that these methods generally regard length information as inherently harmful, aiming to minimize its influence. This perspective can be problematic in contexts where response length is a legitimate aspect of response quality, such as explicit length instructions.

Another related area is length instruction following, where current LLMs struggle to adhere to explicit numerical length constraints (e.g., "150 words or less"), despite exhibiting an implicit sensitivity to length perception. Prior work, such as LIFT, has attempted to improve length instruction adherence through specialized datasets but has been observed to compromise semantic quality.

This work addresses the limitations of existing approaches by proposing a framework that explicitly differentiates between human semantic preferences and response length requirements. Instead of suppressing length information, it aims to leverage it to foster explicit length understanding, thereby mitigating length bias and improving adherence to length instructions without sacrificing semantic quality. It positions itself as an improvement over methods that either ignore the nuanced role of length or compromise semantic performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research is motivated by two primary observations and associated challenges within the RLHF paradigm:
1. **Susceptibility of Reward Models to Length Bias**: Reward models, crucial for aligning LLMs, are prone to exploitation by superficial factors, with length bias being particularly significant. This phenomenon causes LLMs to favor longer responses even when they lack true semantic quality, leading to suboptimal alignment. Preliminary investigations conducted by the authors confirmed the widespread existence of length bias in reward models, showing a strong linear correlation between response length and reward score, even when prompts and responses were semantically mismatched or when prompts were empty. This indicated that models were relying on length as a proxy for quality.
2. **LLMs' Inability to Follow Explicit Length Instructions**: Despite the pronounced impact of length bias suggesting an inherent sensitivity to length perception in LLMs, fine-tuned models consistently struggle to adhere to explicit numerical length instructions (e.g., "generate a 100-word response"). Further preliminary studies revealed that standard reward models, despite exhibiting length bias, performed no better than random chance (approximately 50% accuracy) when evaluated on their ability to follow explicit length instructions. Additionally, straightforward approaches to train models on length instructions (e.g., LIFT) showed a tendency to overfit to these instructions, leading to a degradation of semantic understanding and overall response quality. This highlights a disconnect between implicit length perception and explicit length control.

Based on these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:
- **Develop a novel framework** that enables reward models to explicitly differentiate between human semantic preferences and response length requirements. The aim is to transform the implicit and often harmful length bias into a conscious and controllable understanding of response length.
- **Introduce a Response-conditioned Bradley-Terry (Rc-BT) model**: This model is designed to enhance the ability of preference learning systems to mitigate length bias and follow explicit length instructions.
- **Propose Rc-RM and Rc-DPO algorithms**: These algorithms are intended to integrate the Rc-BT model into both reward modeling and direct policy optimization (DPO) pipelines, allowing for simultaneous mitigation of length bias and promotion of adherence to length instructions during LLM alignment.
- **Demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability** of the proposed approach through extensive experimentation across various foundational models and datasets.

The underlying motivation is to move beyond simply suppressing length information, recognizing that length can be a legitimate instruction, and instead to leverage this information to build more robust and aligned LLMs that can handle complex instructions involving both semantic content and structural constraints like length.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a Response-conditioned Modeling framework built upon a novel Response-conditioned Bradley-Terry (Rc-BT) model. This framework is then integrated into both reward modeling (Rc-RM) and direct preference optimization (Rc-DPO) for LLM alignment.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Explorations

Before introducing the core method, the authors conducted several preliminary investigations to characterize length bias and LLM behavior with length instructions.
- **Dataset**: The OpenAssistant dataset (first turn English conversations) was used, partitioned into D_sft (for supervised fine-tuning), D_rm (for reward model training), and D_eval (for reward model evaluation, with chosen y_w and rejected y_l responses).
- **Models**: Qwen2-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.1-8B (Base and Instruct variants) were utilized.
- **Existence of Length Bias**: Reward models were evaluated on D_eval, an empty prompt dataset (D_e_eval), and a random prompt dataset (D_r_eval). Accuracy and consistency of preferences were measured, and the correlation between response length and reward score was plotted.
- **Length Bias in Evaluation Data**: The original D_eval was found to have a length bias (59.78% of chosen responses were longer). To address this, a "quality evaluation dataset" (D_q_eval) was constructed using GPT-4o to rewrite, expand, or compress responses, ensuring semantic similarity while balancing length differences between chosen and rejected responses.
- **Limited Length Instruction Adherence**: A "length evaluation dataset" (D_l_eval) was constructed from D_q_eval, where one response adhered to a length constraint (x_l) and the other did not, but both were semantically similar. Models were evaluated for adherence.
- **Limitations of Direct Length Instruction Learning**: LIFT (Yuan et al., 2024) was extended to LIFT-plus (including "or more" constraints) and variants (reverse preference, no-reverse preference, empty prompt) were created. These were used to assess how models learn length instructions and their impact on semantic quality.

#### 3.2. Response-Conditioned Modeling Framework

The core of the method is to explicitly condition preference modeling on both the prompt and how a response adheres to specific length conditions.
- **Augmented Length Instruction Dataset (D_Rc)**: From an original preference pair (x, y_w, y_l):
 - **Chosen Response Augmentation**: For the chosen response y_w, a length-augmented instruction x_1_l is created by adding a length constraint to x that y_w *violates*. A preference pair (x, x_1_l, y_w) is formed, signifying that (x, y_w) is preferred over (x_1_l, y_w) – meaning the original prompt is better than the prompt with a *violating* length constraint for that response.
 - **Rejected Response Augmentation**: For the rejected response y_l, a length-augmented instruction x_2_l is created such that y_l *satisfies* this constraint. A preference pair (x_2_l, x, y_l) is formed, signifying that (x_2_l, y_l) is preferred over (x, y_l) – meaning the prompt with a *satisfying* length constraint is better than the original prompt for that response.
 - The combined dataset D_Rc = {(x, x_1_l, y_w)} ∪ {(x_2_l, x, y_l)}.

#### 3.3. Response-Conditioned Bradley-Terry (Rc-BT) Model

The standard Bradley-Terry (BT) model is adapted to this response-conditioned format. The preference probabilities are modeled as:
- For chosen responses: p*(x ≻ x_1_l | y_w) = exp(r*(x, y_w)) / (exp(r*(x, y_w)) + exp(r*(x_1_l, y_w)))
- For rejected responses: p*(x_2_l ≻ x | y_l) = exp(r*(x_2_l, y_l)) / (exp(r*(x_2_l, y_l)) + exp(r*(x, y_l)))
The Rc-BT objective function (L_Rc) is derived through maximum likelihood estimation based on these two types of preference pairs, aiming to make the model perceive response lengths systematically.

#### 3.4. Response-Conditioned Reward Model (Rc-RM)

- The reward model r_phi is initialized from a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) LLM.
- It is optimized using the L_Rc objective (Eq. 6), which involves log-sigmoid terms of reward differences for the augmented pairs. A weighting factor λ balances the contributions of the chosen and rejected response augmentations.
- Training is performed on the combined dataset D_rm ∪ D_Rc.

#### 3.5. Response-Conditioned Direct Preference Optimization (Rc-DPO)

- Rc-DPO extends the DPO algorithm by incorporating the Rc-BT modeling into the DPO framework.
- The derivation involves reformulating the optimal reward in terms of policy probabilities (log-ratios of the optimal policy to a reference policy) and substituting this into the Rc-BT objective.
- The final Rc-DPO objective function (Eq. 8) directly optimizes the policy based on log-ratios of policy probabilities for the response-conditioned preference pairs.

#### 3.6. Experimental Settings

- **Training Details**: Reward models and DPO models were trained for 5 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-5 (RM) or 1e-6 (DPO), a cosine learning rate schedule, warmup steps, and a batch size of 64. Experiments used DeepSpeed and Transformers on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
- **Comparison Methods**: Baseline (RM/DPO on D_rm), ODIN (RM comparison, length regularization), LIFT-plus (RM/DPO comparison, direct length instruction learning), R-DPO (DPO comparison, length penalty).
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **RM**: Quality Eval Acc (accuracy on D_q_eval for semantic quality and length bias mitigation) and Length Eval Acc (accuracy on D_l_eval for length instruction adherence).
 - **DPO**: Model-based evaluation using GPT-4o as a judge. Benchmarks included AlpacaEval, AlpacaEval-LI-plus-less (for "or less" instructions), and AlpacaEval-LI-plus-more (for "or more" instructions). Metrics were Length Acc (response length adherence), Length Win Ratio (semantic quality with length-augmented instructions), and Quality Win Ratio (semantic quality with original prompts).
- **Ablation Studies**: Investigated the necessity of both chosen (D_c_Rc) and rejected (D_r_Rc) response augmentation, the efficiency of D_Rc, optimal mixing ratios, and generalizability across different datasets (HH-RLHF for OOD evaluation) and different biases (format bias).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated the effectiveness and generalizability of the Response-conditioned Modeling approach across various tasks and models.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Explorations

- **Length Bias Confirmation**: All baseline reward models exhibited significant length bias. They achieved over 60% accuracy on semantically mismatched or empty-prompt evaluation datasets (D_e_eval, D_r_eval), close to their performance on the original D_eval. A strong linear correlation between response length and reward score was observed, and accuracy increased with the length difference between chosen and rejected responses.
- **Evaluation Dataset Bias**: The original D_eval was found to be length-biased, with a majority of chosen responses being longer. The reconstructed D_q_eval effectively mitigated this bias, leading to reduced performance for baseline models (mostly below 60% accuracy), suggesting a more objective assessment of semantic quality.
- **Limited Length Instruction Adherence**: Despite exhibiting length bias, baseline reward models showed an accuracy near 50% on D_l_eval, indicating they could not effectively follow explicit length instructions.
- **LIFT Limitations**: Models trained with LIFT-plus variants, while achieving high Length Eval Acc, consistently showed similar or worse Quality Eval Acc compared to baselines. Training trajectories confirmed rapid overfitting to length instructions at the expense of semantic understanding.

#### 4.2. Results of Reward Models (Rc-RM)

- **Length Bias Mitigation**: Rc-RM significantly outperformed both Baseline and ODIN in Quality Eval Acc across all tested models (Qwen2-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B). For instance, Rc-RM achieved 69.55% on Qwen2-1.5B-Base, surpassing Baseline (59.14%) and ODIN (56.12%).
- **Policy Training Improvement**: Policies trained with PPO using Rc-RM showed higher semantic quality (Quality Win Ratio) and reduced response length on AlpacaEval compared to those trained with Baseline or ODIN reward models.
- **Stability Against Length Variation**: When evaluated on D_ml_eval (length-varied response pairs), Rc-RM demonstrated more stable reward scores with respect to response length (smaller slopes) compared to Baseline, LIFT-plus, and ODIN, indicating effective length bias mitigation.
- **Length Instruction Following**: Rc-RM achieved high Length Eval Acc, comparable to or exceeding LIFT-plus, demonstrating effective adherence to length instructions. For Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Rc-RM reached 92.31%, outperforming LIFT-plus (83.97%). Rc-RM also exhibited consistent and expected behavior in score differences across varying word_num constraints, unlike LIFT-plus which showed overfitting.

#### 4.3. Results of DPO Models (Rc-DPO)

- **Balanced Response Length and Quality**: On AlpacaEval, Rc-DPO effectively reduced response length while significantly improving Quality Win Ratio compared to Baseline, LIFT-plus, and R-DPO. LIFT-plus was observed to negatively impact semantic quality, often producing excessively short responses (a "short bias") or excessively long ones.
- **Adherence to "Or Less" Instructions**: On AlpacaEval-LI-plus-less, Rc-DPO achieved high Length Acc (e.g., 82.04% to 100%) and maintained strong Length Win Ratios (e.g., 44.14% to 64.96%), indicating successful adherence to length constraints while preserving semantic quality. LIFT-plus, in contrast, achieved high Length Acc by generating extremely short, semantically poor responses (Length Win Ratio near 0%).
- **Adherence to "Or More" Instructions**: On AlpacaEval-LI-plus-more, Rc-DPO maintained high Length Acc (e.g., 97.51% to 100%) and outperformed other methods in Length Win Ratio, indicating effective adherence to minimum length requirements without generating redundant content.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Complementarity of D_c_Rc and D_r_Rc**: Training with only one part of the augmented dataset (D_c_Rc or D_r_Rc) led to significant drops in both Quality Eval Acc and Length Eval Acc (near Baseline performance or random chance), and introduced new biases. The combination of both types of augmentation was found to be essential.
- **Efficiency of D_Rc**: The augmented dataset D_Rc demonstrated high sample efficiency, with even a small proportion (e.g., 40% of D_Rc) yielding substantial performance improvements (approximately 70% of the maximum improvement observed).
- **Mixture Ratios**: Increasing the proportion of D_r_Rc (rejected response augmentation) while keeping D_c_Rc (chosen response augmentation) constant showed improvements in both Length Eval Acc and Quality Eval Acc, underscoring the complementary effect.
- **Generalizability Across Datasets**: Rc-RM exhibited strong generalizability, maintaining significant performance improvements in Quality Eval Acc and comparable Length Eval Acc on the HH-RLHF dataset, an out-of-distribution scenario.
- **Generalizability Across Biases**: The framework was also found to be effective in mitigating "format bias" on the FormatBiasEval dataset, achieving superior accuracy over other methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in addressing the problem of length bias and explicit length instruction following in the context of LLM alignment via human feedback. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Principled Approach to Length Bias**: The work moves beyond treating length information as a purely harmful confounder. By introducing "response-conditioned modeling," it offers a principled method to disentangle semantic intent from length requirements. This allows LLMs to develop an explicit understanding of response length, rather than implicitly relying on it as a spurious correlation. This reframing has implications for managing other superficial biases beyond length.
- **Improved LLM Alignment and Reliability**: By mitigating length bias in reward models, the framework contributes to the training of LLMs that are more truly aligned with human semantic preferences. This reduces the risk of reward hacking, where models exploit easily optimizable metrics (like length) at the expense of actual quality, leading to more reliable and predictable LLM outputs.
- **Enhanced Instruction Following Capabilities**: A key impact is the substantial improvement in LLMs' ability to adhere to explicit numerical length instructions (e.g., "summarize in 50 words"). Unlike previous methods (like LIFT) that often achieved length adherence by compromising semantic quality or generating overly short responses, Rc-BT enables models to balance length constraints with high semantic quality. This is crucial for real-world applications requiring precise output specifications.
- **Generalizability and Robustness**: The demonstrated effectiveness across multiple foundational models (Qwen, Llama), diverse datasets (OpenAssistant, HH-RLHF as OOD), and even other types of biases (format bias) highlights the robustness and generalizability of the Rc-BT framework. This suggests that the core principles could be extended to a broader range of instruction-following tasks and confounding factors.
- **Efficiency in Data Utilization**: The ablation studies revealed that the augmented dataset (D_Rc) is highly sample-efficient, achieving significant performance gains with relatively small proportions of additional data. This is an important consideration for computational resource management in large-scale LLM training.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research into explicitly modeling and disentangling other complex aspects of human preference and instruction following. It provides a methodological blueprint for transforming implicit biases into explicit, controllable dimensions of LLM behavior, potentially leading to more granular control over generative models. The identified limitations regarding computational costs for larger models and further empirical validation suggest clear directions for ongoing development.

In summary, this research offers a practical and theoretically grounded solution to a persistent problem in LLM alignment, paving the way for more controllable, semantically coherent, and instruction-adherent generative AI systems.
