# Towards Bridging the Gap between Large-Scale Pretraining and Efficient Finetuning for Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The LIFT framework addresses challenges in humanoid robot control by integrating large-scale SAC pretraining with a physics-informed world model, enabling robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and safe, data-efficient finetuning on physical robots within minutes of interaction.

## Problem

- On-policy Reinforcement Learning methods for humanoid control suffer from low sample efficiency, making real-world adaptation costly and time-consuming.
- Off-policy RL's stochastic exploration methods pose significant safety risks for inherently unstable humanoid robots during real-world data collection.
- Existing approaches struggle to bridge robust large-scale policy pretraining in simulation with safe and data-efficient finetuning on physical humanoids.

## Method

- Utilizes a three-stage pipeline: large-scale ==Soft Actor-Critic== (SAC) policy pretraining in parallel simulation, offline physics-informed world model pretraining, and iterative finetuning.
- Employs a hybrid world model that combines known Lagrangian dynamics with a learned neural network residual, trained on logged pretraining data.
- Confines stochastic exploration for policy improvement entirely to rollouts within the learned world model during finetuning, using only deterministic actions in the real environment for safety.

## Results

- LIFT's SAC pretraining achieves fast wall-clock convergence and enables robust zero-shot deployment of policies to physical humanoids like Booster T1, demonstrating stable locomotion on unseen real-world surfaces.
- The framework achieves superior sim-to-sim finetuning, adapting to new velocity targets within approximately 4x10^4 environment steps (**800** seconds of interaction) while significantly outperforming other RL baselines.
- Real-world finetuning on the Booster T1 robot improves gait patterns and velocity tracking with only **80**-590 seconds of collected data, demonstrating practical and data-efficient adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale SAC pretraining, when optimized with JAX for massively parallel simulation, yields robust policies capable of zero-shot transfer to physical humanoids.
> - Incorporating physics priors into the world model significantly improves its predictive accuracy, generalization, and stability for humanoid dynamics, which is vital for reliable synthetic rollouts.
> - Confining stochastic exploration to a learned physics-informed world model enables safe and sample-efficient policy adaptation for humanoids without risky real-world random actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robot control through Reinforcement Learning has seen significant advancements, largely propelled by the development of high-fidelity, parallelizable physics simulators like IsaacGym, MuJoCo Playground, and Brax. **On-policy methods, particularly Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, have emerged as a dominant baseline due to their robustness and fast wall-clock convergence in these massively parallel simulation environments. PPO-trained policies can even achieve zero-shot deployment to physical robots, demonstrating impressive sim-to-real transfer capabilities. However, a major limitation of on-policy methods is their **low sample efficiency**, meaning they require vast amounts of data and discard off-policy experiences. This makes them less suitable for scenarios where real-world data collection is expensive, time-consuming, or risky, and for efficiently adapting policies to new environments or out-of-distribution tasks.

To address sample efficiency, **off-policy RL algorithms like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3)** offer an appealing alternative by effectively reusing past experiences. While these methods have shown success in training quadruped robots and some sim-to-real transfers, they face their own challenges. They can be prone to overfitting to replayed data, which biases exploration during fine-tuning. More critically, their reliance on **stochastic exploration through injected action noise in the environment poses significant safety risks for humanoid robots**. Humanoids, with their smaller and more dynamic support polygons, are inherently less stable than quadrupeds, making random exploration potentially damaging to the robot or its surroundings. Furthermore, prior large-scale off-policy efforts have either not fully utilized massively parallel simulations (leading to high wall-clock times) or lacked comprehensive sim-to-real validation and finetuning capabilities in new environments.

**Model-based RL techniques** represent another frontier for improving sample efficiency by learning a world model of the environment dynamics. Methods like MBPO and Dreamer leverage synthetic rollouts to generate additional training data. However, these often still necessitate some degree of stochastic exploration in the real environment. A significant hurdle for model-based approaches is the potential for **model bias**, where inaccuracies in the learned world model can destabilize finetuning. **Physics-informed world models**, which embed structural priors from physics (e.g., Lagrangian dynamics), have shown promise in improving predictive accuracy and reducing model bias, as demonstrated by SSRL for quadruped robots. However, training such sophisticated model-based systems from scratch is often extremely slow and susceptible to local minima, particularly for the more complex dynamics of humanoids.

The work by Huang et al. positions itself squarely in this landscape by recognizing and explicitly aiming to **bridge "the gap between large-scale pretraining and efficient finetuning on humanoids."** It synthesizes the strengths of these diverse approaches: the wall-clock efficiency of large-scale simulation during pretraining, the sample efficiency of off-policy learning, and the stability and data efficiency afforded by physics-informed model-based learning during fine-tuning. The paper specifically addresses the safety concerns of exploration for humanoids by confining stochasticity to the world model during real-world adaptation. By building upon and extending prior work (e.g., SSRL's physics-informed models, but scaling for humanoids), LIFT attempts to provide a comprehensive, practical, and safe framework for continuous humanoid learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the ambition to create **generalist physical agents**, with humanoid robots serving as an embodiment offering broad task coverage. The authors identify a critical bottleneck in current reinforcement learning paradigms for humanoids: the disconnect between robust large-scale policy pretraining and efficient, safe adaptation in new or real-world environments with limited data.

The paper outlines several key objectives to address this gap:

1. **Bridging Large-Scale Pretraining and Efficient Finetuning:** The primary objective is to develop a unified framework that combines the speed and robustness of large-scale pretraining in simulation with the sample efficiency required for effective finetuning in novel environments. This aims to overcome the "low sample efficiency of on-policy algorithms" that limits safe adaptation.
2. **Enabling Robust Zero-Shot Deployment:** A critical goal is to ensure that the pretrained policies are robust enough for direct, zero-shot transfer to real humanoid robots, laying a solid foundation for subsequent real-world learning.
3. **Facilitating Safe and Data-Efficient Adaptation:** For humanoids, whose instability makes random exploration risky, a key objective is to design a finetuning strategy that minimizes unsafe interactions in the real environment while still enabling significant policy improvement. This necessitates a mechanism for "confining stochastic exploration to a physics-informed world model."
4. **Leveraging Physics Priors for World Models:** The work aims to demonstrate that incorporating structural priors from physics into the world model significantly improves its predictive accuracy and generalization, thereby reducing the amount of data needed for reliable policy finetuning, especially in out-of-distribution scenarios.
5. **Providing a Practical and Open-Source Solution:** The authors commit to releasing an open-source pipeline that serves as a practical baseline for the robotics community, fostering further research and development in humanoid control.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to develop a practical, safe, and efficient continuous learning pipeline for humanoids that can scale from massive simulations to real-world deployment and adaptation, thereby advancing the state of the art towards truly intelligent and adaptable robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Large-scale pretraIning and efficient FineTuning (LIFT)" framework is a three-stage pipeline designed to achieve its objectives: (i) large-scale policy pretraining, (ii) physics-informed world model pretraining, and (iii) efficient finetuning of the policy and world model.

#### 3.1. Large-Scale Policy Pretraining

- **Algorithm Choice**: LIFT adopts **Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)** as its backbone policy optimization algorithm. A preliminary study (Appendix A.1) revealed that SAC integrates more naturally with model-based methods and offers stable convergence with limited samples compared to PPO, making it better suited for finetuning.
- **Scalable Implementation**: The SAC implementation is developed in **JAX**, leveraging its capabilities for efficient large-scale parallel simulation. It uses fixed tensor shapes, operation fusion, and compiled kernels to enable fast, robust convergence in massively parallel environments like MuJoCo Playground (thousands of environments on a single GPU).
- **Exploration Strategy**: Unlike some previous large-scale off-policy methods, LIFT relies entirely on SAC's **stochastic policy** (which produces state-dependent action mean and variance) for exploration during pretraining, without additional mixed Gaussian noise. The entropy coefficient (α) controls exploration.
- **Performance Optimization**: Systematic **hyperparameter tuning using Optuna** significantly reduces pretraining convergence time. The authors find that **large-batch updates and a high Update-To-Data (UTD) ratio** (e.g., UTD=10, batch size 1,024, 10^6-transition replay buffer) improve sample efficiency without needing auxiliary stabilizers, attributing this to the massively parallel, domain-randomized regime suppressing early overfitting.

#### 3.2. World Model Pretraining

- **Offline Training**: To maintain wall-clock efficiency under massive parallelism, world model training is decoupled from policy pretraining. All transitions collected during SAC pretraining are logged to disk. After the policy converges, the world model is trained entirely **offline** from these logged records.
- **Physics-Informed World Model**: The world model uses a hybrid architecture that combines known **Lagrangian dynamics** (mass, Coriolis, gravity terms) with a **learned residual network**.
 - The residual network approximates unknown contributions, specifically external contact forces and dissipative torques, as well as predicting per-feature predictive uncertainty (log σ^2_t).
 - The model is implemented using **Brax's differentiable rigid-body primitives**, ensuring the entire rollout and loss pipeline is end-to-end differentiable.
 - Crucially, specific adjustments were made for humanoid stability, such as correcting the mapping from privileged state to Brax generalized state, aligning observation dimensions between MuJoCo and Brax, and including **base height** in the privileged state – which was found to be pivotal for stable humanoid rollouts.
- **Loss Function**: The world model minimizes the **negative log-likelihood of a Gaussian predictive distribution** (Eq. 5), which encourages the model to predict small variance for states it has observed well and large variance for uncertain or unseen states.

#### 3.3. Policy and World Model Finetuning

- **Iterative Process**: Finetuning occurs in an iterative loop: (1) deploy policy in target environment, collect data; (2) finetune world model on new data; (3) generate synthetic rollouts in the world model for policy updates; (4) update SAC actor-critic; (5) repeat.
- **Safe Data Collection**: During finetuning in the target environment (real world or sim-to-sim transfer), the policy executes **deterministic actions** (mean action only), explicitly *avoiding stochastic exploration noise*. This is a critical safety measure for humanoids. Strict physics-informed termination conditions (e.g., base height, velocities, joint limits) are applied.
- **World Model Finetuning (Online)**: The pretrained world model is continuously fine-tuned on the deterministically collected real-world data. An **auto-regressive training objective** (Eq. 19) over a loss horizon (e.g., H=4) is used to enhance sample efficiency and improve long-term prediction accuracy.
- **Stochastic Exploration in the World Model**: To drive policy improvement while maintaining safety, stochastic exploration is **confined entirely to rollouts within the learned physics-informed world model**.
 - The stochastic SAC policy interacts with the world model to generate diverse imaginary trajectories.
 - **Asymmetric Observation Generation** is maintained: the actor receives proprioceptive states, while the critic uses privileged states. Proprioceptive states are derived from the model's predicted privileged states via a fixed projection.
 - **Safety Resets** are applied during world model rollouts, terminating imaginary trajectories if physically abnormal states are predicted, preventing NaNs and stabilizing training.
 - **Physics-Consistent Rewards** are computed deterministically from the world model's predicted privileged state, leveraging the model's kinematic and dynamic signals, avoiding unstable learned reward predictors.
- **Policy Update**: The synthetic trajectories generated by rolling out the stochastic policy in the world model are added to the replay buffer, which is then used to update the SAC actor-critic network.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments validate LIFT's effectiveness across pretraining, sim-to-real transfer, and efficient finetuning for humanoid locomotion, primarily on Booster T1 and Unitree G1 robots.

#### 4.1. Pretraining Experiments

- **Competitive Pretraining Performance**: LIFT's SAC pretraining module achieves comparable or superior evaluation returns and significantly faster convergence (in wall-clock time) compared to established baselines like PPO and FastTD3, especially on challenging rough terrain environments (Figure 7, Appendix A.2). It demonstrates effective learning across diverse humanoid configurations (low-dimensional Booster T1, full Booster T1, Unitree G1) and terrain types.
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Deployment**: A policy pretrained on the low-dimensional Booster T1 successfully transfers zero-shot to the physical robot, demonstrating stable locomotion on various unseen real-world surfaces, including grass, uphill, downhill, and mud (Appendix A.6). This confirms LIFT's ability to produce deployable humanoid controllers.

#### 4.2. Finetuning Experiments

- **Superior Sim-to-Sim Finetuning**: LIFT consistently converges across a range of finetuning tasks in Brax, including in-distribution, long-tail, and challenging out-of-distribution velocity targets (e.g., 0.6 m/s to 1.5 m/s forward speed for Booster T1). Policies finetuned with LIFT exhibit stable walking, precise velocity tracking, and reduced body oscillations (Figure 2).
 - **Baseline Failures**: In contrast, SAC (without exploration noise) rapidly diverges. PPO's performance degrades and ultimately collapses. FastTD3 shows strong oscillations and fails to converge. SSRL (trained from scratch) struggles to reach target speeds, especially at higher velocities, highlighting the critical role of pretraining for humanoids.
 - **Sample Efficiency**: LIFT achieves robust adaptation within 4x10^4 environment steps (approximately 800 seconds of real-robot interaction), underscoring its practical feasibility for real-world scenarios. Similar improvements are observed for Unitree G1 finetuning (Figure 15, Appendix A.7).
- **Successful Real-World Finetuning**: LIFT successfully finetunes a initially weak sim-to-real policy on the physical Booster T1 robot. With only 80-590 seconds of real-world data collection, the robot's behavior significantly improves, demonstrating a more upright posture, smoother gait patterns, and stable forward velocity (Figure 3, Appendix A.3). The authors acknowledge practical limitations (Vicon reliance, IMU drift, sequential process) but frame them as engineering challenges rather than conceptual flaws.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Criticality of Pretraining Stages**: Both large-scale SAC policy pretraining and physics-informed world model (WM) pretraining are essential. Removing WM pretraining noticeably slows down finetuning convergence. Removing both stages (reducing LIFT to SSRL from scratch) results in policy failure, with the robot barely learning to stand (Figure 4). This confirms that SAC pretraining prevents poor local minima, and WM pretraining significantly boosts finetuning efficiency.
- **Advantage of Physics-informed World Model**: LIFT's physics-informed world model significantly outperforms an MBPO-style ensemble of purely neural network models. The MBPO model fails to converge, exhibiting substantially worse test MSE and leading to exploding critic loss due to physically implausible predictions from out-of-distribution actions during model-based rollouts (Figure 5). This highlights the crucial role of physics priors for robust generalization with limited data.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**:
 - **Pretraining**: High UTD ratios, larger replay buffer sizes, and larger batch sizes generally accelerate convergence, though benefits plateau beyond certain thresholds (Figure 9, Appendix A.4).
 - **Finetuning**: A smaller entropy coefficient (α) for SAC leads to more stable convergence during finetuning. A multi-step autoregressive loss horizon (e.g., 2 or 4 steps) for world model training consistently ensures stable learning, outperforming single-step predictions and improving policy stability (Figure 10, Appendix A.4).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LIFT framework represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robot control, offering a practical and robust solution to the long-standing challenge of bridging large-scale simulation training with efficient real-world adaptation.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Unified and Practical Framework**: LIFT provides a comprehensive, three-stage pipeline that effectively combines the wall-clock efficiency of massively parallel simulation for pretraining with the sample efficiency and safety requirements of finetuning humanoid robots. This integrated approach addresses a critical gap in existing RL methods.
2. **Safe and Sample-Efficient Finetuning**: The most impactful aspect of LIFT is its strategy for safe and data-efficient adaptation. By restricting stochastic exploration to a learned physics-informed world model while executing deterministic actions in the real environment, LIFT dramatically mitigates the inherent risks of random exploration for fragile humanoid robots. This enables practical adaptation with minimal real-world data collection (demonstrated with minutes of real data).
3. **Robust Pretraining and Zero-Shot Transfer**: LIFT demonstrates that large-scale SAC pretraining can yield highly capable policies that achieve robust zero-shot deployment on physical humanoids across diverse, unseen terrains. This establishes a strong foundation for subsequent real-world learning.
4. **Power of Physics-Informed Models**: The research unequivocally highlights the superiority of physics-informed world models over purely neural network-based approaches, especially for complex systems like humanoids. Integrating structural priors significantly improves model generalization and stability, which is crucial for reliable synthetic rollouts and policy improvement.
5. **Open-Source Contribution**: By releasing an open-source pipeline, the authors provide a valuable baseline for the robotics community, fostering reproducibility and accelerating future research in humanoid control.

**Potential Impact:**

The LIFT framework has the potential to significantly accelerate the development and deployment of humanoid robots in various real-world applications. It moves beyond tedious manual engineering and unsafe trial-and-error learning, enabling robots to continuously adapt to new environments, tasks, and unforeseen conditions with greater autonomy and safety. This could unlock new capabilities for humanoids in areas such as logistics, assistance, disaster response, and exploration.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as noted by the authors):**

Despite its achievements, the paper acknowledges several limitations and outlines promising avenues for future work:

1. **Enhanced Real-World Safety Management**: While LIFT improves safety, current real-world finetuning still relies on strict manual termination conditions and human supervision. Future work should integrate more automated safety mechanisms, such as uncertainty-aware exploration, learned recovery policies, or robot-assisted resetting, to further reduce human intervention and enhance robustness.
2. **Integration of High-Dimensional Sensory Inputs**: LIFT currently operates solely on proprioceptive observations. Extending it to incorporate high-dimensional external sensor data (e.g., cameras) would be crucial for tasks involving dexterous manipulation, object interaction, or complex navigation. This would likely necessitate the development of latent world models capable of capturing dynamics beyond the robot's body state.
3. **Hierarchical and Object-Centric Tasks**: For more complex tasks involving external objects or high-level navigation, the framework would need extensions to model object dynamics or integrate with hierarchical RL approaches, where LIFT's low-level controller could provide robust motion primitives.
4. **Engineering Refinements**: Practical limitations observed during real-world finetuning, such as reliance on external motion capture (Vicon), IMU drift for velocity estimation, and sequential training iterations, are recognized as engineering challenges. Adopting asynchronous pipelines (e.g., like SERL) and developing onboard perception solutions for state estimation could substantially improve the practicality and wall-clock efficiency of real-world finetuning.

In conclusion, LIFT marks a substantial step forward in making advanced humanoid control a more practical, efficient, and safe endeavor, paving the way for truly adaptive and generalist humanoid robots.
