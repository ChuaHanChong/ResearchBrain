## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses foundational challenges within Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), a critical component in aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences. RLHF methods, particularly those based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), have been successful in developing LLMs that exhibit human-level performance across various tasks. However, the field has identified two persistent issues: reward hacking and stable policy optimization.

Reward hacking refers to the phenomenon where a policy over-optimizes for the assigned reward signal, leading to high expected rewards but undesirable actual performance. Stable policy optimization, conversely, focuses on preventing drastic policy shifts and model collapse that can arise from unstable gradient estimations during RL training.

Current online RLHF approaches typically address these challenges through separate regularization strategies. Reward hacking is commonly mitigated by incorporating a KL-divergence penalty against an initial supervised fine-tuned model (π₀). Stable optimization is often achieved using policy ratio clipping relative to the current policy (πₜ), as seen in PPO.

This paper identifies a limitation in these independent regularization strategies: they can create an overly restrictive optimization framework. By enforcing constraints independently, the learning policy is confined to the intersection of trust regions around both π₀ and πₜ. This can prevent the exploration of high-reward policies that might lie outside this intersection but still offer stable optimization guarantees. The authors argue that this implicit trade-off between regularizing towards π₀ (for reference) and πₜ (for stability) remains under-explored.

The presented work aims to unify these two regularization objectives, proposing an explicit balancing mechanism. It builds upon existing policy optimization methods like PPO, REINFORCE variants, and Direct Alignment from Preference (DAP) methods, but distinguishes itself by systematically analyzing and addressing the conflict arising from dual regularization. It also extends the family of weighted regression algorithms, such as Reward Weighted Regression (RWR), Advantage Weighted Regression (AWR), and Critic Regularized Regression (CRR), by incorporating reference regularization into this framework for LLM alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

1. **Address the limitations of independent regularization strategies in RLHF:** The authors identify that current online RLHF methods treat reference regularization (against π₀ to prevent reward hacking) and stable optimization (against πₜ to prevent policy collapse) as separate components. This independent application leads to an overly restrictive optimization landscape where the learning policy is constrained to the intersection of two trust regions. This setup can prevent the discovery of optimal policies that require substantial behavioral changes and lie outside the limited intersection, leading to suboptimal alignment performance.

2. **Propose a unified regularization approach:** To overcome the identified limitation, the paper aims to introduce a unified regularization strategy that explicitly balances the objectives of preventing reward hacking and maintaining stable policy updates. This unified approach is hypothesized to enlarge the search area and allow the policy to explore high-reward regions that were previously inaccessible.

3. **Theoretically analyze the benefits of dual-KL regularization:** The research seeks to provide a theoretical foundation for the unified approach, specifically by demonstrating that a dual-KL objective can be mathematically equivalent to optimizing against a single, dynamically constructed reference policy. This dynamic reference is expected to be an interpolation of π₀ and πₜ, gradually moving closer to the optimal distribution.

4. **Reformulate the RL alignment objective into a stable and simple framework:** Building on theoretical insights, a key objective is to transform the complex RL alignment objective into a more stable and implementable form. The authors aim to reformulate it as a weighted supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss, thereby simplifying implementation and potentially improving learning stability compared to PPO-based methods.

5. **Empirically validate the proposed method's superiority:** The paper aims to conduct extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks to demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms existing RLHF and online preference learning methods in terms of alignment performance, stability, and efficiency. This validation is designed to cover both direct AI alignment settings and standard RLHF pipelines using human preference data.

In essence, the motivation stems from the observed conflict between existing regularization methods in RLHF, leading to suboptimal exploration and alignment. The authors propose a principled unification to resolve this, aiming for both theoretical clarity and practical advantages in LLM alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper involves a multi-pronged approach: identifying a core limitation in existing RLHF, proposing a theoretical unification, deriving a practical algorithm, and conducting extensive empirical validation.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The paper first establishes the mathematical framework for RLHF. The standard RLHF objective is presented as maximizing reward expectation while applying a KL-divergence penalty to a reference policy π_ref_ (Equation 1). PPO is introduced as a common method for RLHF, optimizing a clipped objective that constrains policy updates to ensure stability (Equation 2). The authors highlight that stable policy optimization can also be achieved via a KL-divergence penalty with respect to the current policy πₜ (PPO-Penalty formulation), thereby conceptually bridging stable optimization and reference regularization as dual-KL penalties.

#### 3.2. Dual-KL Alignment Objective

The core of the proposed method is a unified dual-KL regularization objective (Equation 3):
$J_{\text{dual KL}}(\pi_{\theta}; \pi_{\text{ref}}, \pi_{t}) = \max_{\pi_{\theta}} E_{x \sim D, y \sim \pi_{\theta}(y|x)}[A(x, y)] - \beta \left[ \alpha D_{\text{KL}}[\pi_{\theta}(y|x) \parallel \pi_{0}(y|x)] + (1 - \alpha)D_{\text{KL}}[\pi_{\theta}(y|x) \parallel \pi_{t}(y|x)] \right]$
Here, $A(x, y)$ is the advantage function, $\pi_0$ is the initial model, $\pi_t$ is the current policy, $\beta > 0$ controls the overall regularization strength, and $\alpha \in$ balances the influence of $\pi_0$ and $\pi_t$.

#### 3.3. Understanding the Limitation of PPO-based RLHF

The paper argues that PPO-based RLHF methods enforce a stable constraint (e.g., policy ratio clipping) outside the primary optimization objective. This confines policy updates to the intersection of trust regions around $\pi_0$ (for reward hacking) and $\pi_t$ (for stability). As $\pi_t$ diverges from $\pi_0$ during training, this intersection becomes increasingly restrictive, hindering exploration of high-reward regions. Empirical validation using "Dual-PPO" and "Dual-PPO-Clip" variants, which incorporate dual-KL penalties, showed superior Pareto frontiers in reward-KL trade-offs compared to standard PPO, supporting this hypothesis.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis (Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2)

- **Proposition 4.1:** This proposition mathematically demonstrates that the dual-KL objective is equivalent to optimizing against a single, dynamically constructed reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}} = \frac{1}{C(x)} \pi_{0}(y|x)^{\alpha} \pi_{t}(y|x)^{1-\alpha}$. This interpolated reference target in log-space moves closer to the optimal policy distribution as $\pi_t$ aligns with human preferences, providing better support for high-reward regions.
- **Theorem 4.2:** This theorem provides a closed-form solution for the optimal policy $\pi^*$ under the dual-KL advantage maximization objective: $\pi^*(y|x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)} \pi_0(y|x)^\alpha \pi_t(y|x)^{1-\alpha} \exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta} A(x, y)\right)$, where $Z(x)$ is a partition function. This derivation forms the basis for the proposed algorithm.

#### 3.5. Dual-Regularized Advantage Regression (DAR)

Leveraging Theorem 4.2, the paper reformulates the RL alignment objective as an iterative policy regression problem. The improved policy $\pi_{t+1}$ is obtained by minimizing the KL-divergence between the learning policy $\pi_\theta$ and the optimal policy $\pi^*$. This optimization simplifies into a weighted supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss (Equation 4):
$\pi_{t+1} = \text{arg max}_{\pi_{\theta}} E_{x \sim D, y \sim \pi_t(y|x)} \left[ \left(\frac{\pi_0(y|x)}{\pi_t(y|x)}\right)^\alpha \exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta} A(x, y)\right) \log \pi_\theta(y|x) \right]$
This objective assigns higher log probabilities to responses based on a "Regularization Weight" (penalizing divergence from the interpolated reference) and an "Advantage Weight" (reflecting expected policy improvement).

#### 3.6. Practical Implementation (Algorithm 1)

The DAR algorithm incorporates several practical considerations:
- **Value Model Elimination:** Monte Carlo sampling is used to estimate expected returns, avoiding the need for a separate value model.
- **Stability Enhancements:** Batch-based advantage normalization is applied to improve learning stability.
- **Gradient Explosion Mitigation:** A clipping mechanism (with threshold $w_{\text{clip}}$) is applied to the product of regularization and advantage weights to prevent gradient explosions from exponential operations.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted in two settings:
1. **Direct AI Alignment:** LLMs (Qwen2-7B/Qwen2.5-7B) are aligned using an LLM annotator (Qwen2-72B-Instruct/Qwen3-32B) as the reward judge. Datasets include Reddit TL;DR, Anthropic Helpfulness, and Anthropic Harmlessness. Baselines include offline DAP (DPO, SimPO), online DAP (DPO, IPO, SLiC), and online RLHF (PPO, RLOO, GRPO). Evaluation uses win rates over $\pi_0$ and reward scores judged by GPT-4-Turbo/GPT-5.1.
2. **Standard RLHF:** Qwen2-7B-Instruct is aligned using a state-of-the-art reward model (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct fine-tuned on Helpsteer2). Baselines are RLOO, GRPO, and iterative supervised fine-tuning (Iter-SFT) with best-of-n sampling. Performance is assessed using MT-Bench and AlpacaEval 2.0.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the proposed Dual-regularized Advantage Regression (DAR) method consistently outperforms existing RLHF and online preference learning approaches across various benchmarks and evaluation metrics.

#### 4.1. Empirical Validation of Dual-KL Regularization

- **PPO Variants Comparison (Table 1, Figure 1c):** Initial experiments showed that Dual-PPO variants, which incorporate two KL divergence penalties with respect to $\pi_0$ and $\pi_t$, achieved superior Pareto frontiers in the reward-KL trade-off compared to standard PPO. LLM-judged evaluations on the Anthropic Helpfulness dataset indicated that Dual-PPO (9.522 mean reward, 88.40% win rate over $\pi_0$) and Dual-PPO-Clip (9.581 mean reward, 90.37% win rate over $\pi_0$) consistently outperformed standard PPO (9.070 mean reward, 80.87% win rate over $\pi_0$). This suggests that the dual-KL approach effectively addresses the limitations of standard PPO's restrictive trust regions.

#### 4.2. Performance in Direct AI Alignment (Figure 3, Table 2 & 3)

- **Comparison to DAP Methods:** All online methods generally outperformed offline DAP methods. DAR demonstrated superior sample efficiency, converging with approximately half the annotations required by DAP methods. Crucially, DAR consistently outperformed all three online DAP methods (DPO, IPO, SLiC) across all tasks (Reddit TL;DR, Anthropic Helpfulness, Anthropic Harmlessness). For instance, on Reddit TL;DR, DAR achieved a 98.27% win rate, significantly higher than online SLiC (78.29%). This suggests that online preference learning algorithms, which lack explicit stable optimization guarantees, may suffer from insufficient exploration.
- **Comparison to Online RLHF Methods:** DAR showed clear advantages over established online RLHF baselines (PPO, RLOO, GRPO). DAR achieved a mean reference win rate of 92.42% across the three tasks (Qwen2-7B/GPT-4-Turbo setting), substantially outperforming GRPO, the strongest baseline, at 85.15%. This 7.27% improvement validates the benefits of unifying stable optimization and reference regularization. This performance advantage was consistent in a more powerful setting (Qwen2.5-7B/Qwen3-32B annotator/GPT-5.1 judge), where DAR again led with an 82.60% mean win rate compared to RLOO's 76.65%.

#### 4.3. Improved KL/Reward Pareto Frontier (Figure 4)

- DAR consistently dominated the Pareto frontier across all datasets, achieving higher rewards with reduced KL regularization compared to online RLHF baselines (GRPO, RLOO, PPO). This empirical evidence supports the theoretical claim that DAR's dynamically interpolated reference target leads to a more favorable reward/KL trade-off and better exploration of high-reward regions.

#### 4.4. Performance in Standard RLHF (Table 4)

- In standard RLHF settings using a pre-trained reward model (Llama-3.1-Nemotron-70B-Reward on Helpsteer2), DAR demonstrated superior alignment capabilities. On MT-Bench, DAR achieved the highest scores (GPT-4: 8.538, GPT-4-Turbo: 7.931). On AlpacaEval 2.0, DAR exhibited the highest win rate (LC%) over $\pi_0$ at 54.17%. These results indicate that DAR's benefits extend to realistic RLHF scenarios.

#### 4.5. Importance of Regression Transformation (Figure 5a, 5b)

- A comparison with DAO (Direct Alignment Optimization, a dual-KL RL-based variant without regression) and Dual-PPO showed that DAR achieved superior final performance and maintained consistent training stability. DAO suffered from training instability and reward curve collapse, while Dual-PPO exhibited high variance. This highlights the critical role of the regression transformation in DAR for both implementation simplicity and learning stability.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies (Figure 5c-f)

- **$\alpha$ trade-off and $\beta$ regularization (Figure 5c, 5d):** Decreasing the $\alpha$ coefficient (shifting regularization closer to $\pi_t$) progressively improved performance and generation length. However, setting $\alpha=0$ (reference-free) led to reward hacking, manifested as unnecessarily long responses and missing-EOS rates. DAR demonstrated robust performance across different $\beta$ values, indicating stability.
- **N-shot sampling and Weight Clipping (Figure 5e, 5f):** DAR showed robustness even with one-shot sampling, attributed to effective weight clipping that minimized gradient estimation variance. An optimal weight clip threshold of 20 was found to balance reward signal preservation and prevention of outlier rewards.

#### 4.7. Computational Complexity (Table 8)

- DAR demonstrated computational efficiency, achieving a GPU time of 12.54 minutes per online batch, which was competitive with Iterative SFT (11.79 minutes) and superior to all other online RLHF baselines (PPO: 14.10, RLOO: 12.69, GRPO: 12.85).

#### 4.8. Forward KL vs. Reverse KL for Stability (Figure 8 in Appendix C.4)

- An ablation study comparing Dual-PPO (using reverse KL for stable optimization) with Dual-Mix-PPO (using forward KL for stable optimization) on the Helpfulness task showed that Dual-Mix-PPO consistently yielded lower final performance despite more stable learning curves. This suggested that while mode-covering (forward KL) prevents catastrophic updates, it may overly restrict exploration, whereas mode-seeking (reverse KL) allows for more aggressive optimization towards high-reward regions, resulting in better alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for Large Language Models (LLMs) by addressing a fundamental limitation in current alignment methodologies.

The primary significance lies in its **unification of stable optimization and reference regularization**. By explicitly balancing the KL-divergence penalties with respect to both the initial model ($\pi_0$) and the current policy ($\pi_t$), the work resolves the inherent conflict and overly restrictive nature of separately applied regularization strategies. This unified framework, through a dynamically interpolated reference target, effectively expands the policy search space, enabling the exploration of high-reward regions that were previously inaccessible due to constrained trust regions.

The **theoretical analysis** supporting this approach, particularly Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2, provides a principled foundation. It demonstrates that the dual-KL objective constructs a dynamic reference policy that gradually moves closer to the optimal distribution, enhancing the effectiveness of regularization throughout the alignment process.

A key practical impact is the introduction of **Dual-regularized Advantage Regression (DAR)**. By reformulating the RL alignment objective as an iterative weighted supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss, DAR offers several advantages:
- **Enhanced Learning Stability:** The regression-based approach inherently contributes to more stable training dynamics compared to traditional RL algorithms, as evidenced by the contrast with DAO and Dual-PPO.
- **Reduced Implementation Complexity:** DAR's RL-free nature simplifies the training pipeline, eliminating the need for a separate value model and complex PPO-specific components like clipped objectives. This makes it more accessible for practitioners.
- **Superior Alignment Performance:** Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that DAR consistently outperforms state-of-the-art online RLHF and preference optimization methods across diverse alignment tasks and evaluation metrics, including direct AI alignment and standard RLHF settings.
- **Improved Efficiency:** DAR exhibits better sample efficiency compared to online DAP methods and competitive computational efficiency among RL approaches.

The **generalizability and robustness** of DAR across different base models (Qwen2-7B, Qwen2.5-7B), LLM annotators (Qwen2-72B-Instruct, Qwen3-32B), and evaluation judges (GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-5.1) underscore its broad applicability in advancing LLM alignment techniques. The ablation studies further reinforce the robustness of the method to various hyperparameters and sampling strategies.

In summary, this work provides both a theoretical explanation and a practical solution to a critical issue in RLHF, leading to LLMs that are not only better aligned with human preferences but are also achieved through a more stable, efficient, and simpler training process. The potential impact is significant for improving the safety, helpfulness, and overall quality of future LLMs. However, the limitation regarding its applicability to purely offline RLHF settings, due to the requirement for online data collection and current policy distribution access, highlights an important direction for future research.
