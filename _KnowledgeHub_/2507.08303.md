# Keep on Going: Learning Robust Humanoid Motion Skills via Selective Adversarial Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed the Selective Adversarial Attack for Robust Training (SA2RT) framework, which significantly improves the robustness and real-world performance of RL-learned motion policies for humanoid robots. This method enabled a Unitree G1 robot to achieve a 40% higher success rate on complex terrains and 32% reduced tracking error for long-horizon dynamic movements.

## Problem

- Humanoid robot motion policies trained in simulation are often fragile and fail when deployed in real-world environments due to domain distribution shifts and susceptibility to perturbations.
- Existing robustness methods like Domain Randomization (DR) struggle to efficiently target specific policy vulnerabilities, while traditional adversarial training often leads to conservative or overfit policies due to the "weak adversary dilemma."
- RL-based neural controllers lack systematic robustness design, hindering their ability to reliably execute long-horizon tasks on physical humanoid robots.

## Method

- The proposed ==Selective Adversarial Attack== for Robust Training (SA2RT) framework integrates a learnable motion policy with a learnable attack policy through a non-zero-sum alternating optimization.
- A Selective Attack Policy (SAP) is introduced, which strategically identifies and sparsely perturbs the most vulnerable states and actions under an attack-budget constraint, rather than applying indiscriminate continuous attacks.
- The adversarial training is formulated as a non-zero-sum game, where the attacker's objective is to maximize its own reward (robot failure) while negatively impacting the motion policy, ensuring continuous, monotonic improvement of both policies.

## Results

- SA2RT policies (PL-SAP) achieved a **40%** higher success rate on average over complex terrains compared to DR-trained policies (PL-DR) on the real Unitree G1 robot, with **90%** success on slopes versus **54%**.
- For whole-body trajectory imitation, SA2RT policies (WBC-SAP) successfully completed an entire 120-second dynamic dance, reducing trajectory tracking error by **32%**, whereas DR-trained policies became unstable and failed prematurely.
- In simulation, SA2RT-trained policies (PL-SAP) consistently outperformed DR-trained policies (PL-DR) in both clean and DR environments, for example reducing linear velocity error by **24%** (**0.19** vs **0.25** m/s) in clean environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The effectiveness of adversarial training is maximized when attacks are selective and targeted at true policy vulnerabilities, preventing conservative overfitting.
> - Introducing an attack-budget constraint forces the adversary to be efficient, identifying critical states and actions where perturbations have the most impact.
> - A non-zero-sum game formulation, coupled with alternating optimization, allows for continuous strengthening of the motion policy against increasingly sophisticated and targeted threats.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots represent a grand challenge in robotics, aiming to replicate human-like versatility in diverse environments. Recent advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL) have enabled these robots to learn complex motion skills autonomously in simulation. However, a major hurdle remains the **sim-to-real transfer problem**, where policies trained in idealized simulations often fail in the real world due to domain distribution shifts caused by environmental variations, sensor noise, actuator imperfections, and external disturbances. Furthermore, RL-based neural controllers are inherently sensitive to perturbations, lacking systematic robustness design, which hinders their ability to execute long-horizon tasks reliably.

The broader research landscape for enhancing RL policy robustness in robotics encompasses several approaches:
- **Domain Randomization (DR)**: Widely used to introduce diverse perturbations (e.g., observation noise, environmental variations) during training, forcing the policy to generalize to a wider range of conditions. However, DR struggles to target specific policy vulnerabilities efficiently.
- **Regularization Constraints**: Techniques like Lipschitz regularization (limiting output variation to small input perturbations) or symmetry regularization (leveraging robot morphology) indirectly improve robustness by guiding policy learning. These methods often involve trade-offs between exploration and constraint adherence.
- **Residual Action Models**: Employing models trained on real-world data to compensate for simulation-reality dynamic mismatches, improving sim-to-real transfer. These are data-intensive and can be inefficient.
- **Adversarial Training**: Directly exposing the RL agent to "worst-case" perturbations during training, often framed as a zero-sum game between an agent and an adversary. This field has explored attacks in observation, action, reward, and environmental state spaces. Previous work, however, often suffers from the "weak adversary dilemma" in zero-sum games, leading to conservative overfitting without truly strengthening against diverse attacks.

This paper positions itself by addressing a critical gap in existing adversarial training methods: the lack of targeted identification and selective perturbation of policy vulnerabilities. While traditional adversarial training (e.g., Persistent Attack Policy, PAP) might continuously attack, leading to conservative policies, this work introduces a "selective" approach. By developing a **Selective Adversarial Attack for Robust Training (SA2RT)**, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of untargeted DR and the "weak adversary" problem in conventional adversarial training. It moves beyond simply introducing noise to *strategically* identifying and exploiting weak points, thereby driving more efficient and effective robustness learning, particularly for complex, high-dimensional humanoid control. The Unitree G1 humanoid robot serves as a tangible platform to validate these advancements, pushing the boundaries of reliable long-horizon humanoid operation in challenging real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the inherent fragility of Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based motion policies when deployed on physical humanoid robots. Despite their ability to learn complex skills in simulation, these policies frequently lose stability under real-world conditions characterized by prolonged operation, sensor/actuator noise, and external disturbances. This vulnerability leads to the inability of humanoid robots to reliably complete long-horizon motion tasks and effectively bridge the "sim-to-real" gap.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Enhance Robustness of Humanoid Motion Skills**: The primary goal is to develop a method that significantly improves the resilience of RL-learned motion policies for humanoid robots against various perturbations and uncertainties encountered in real-world deployment.
2. **Develop a Selective Adversarial Attack Mechanism**: To overcome the limitations of existing robustness methods (like untargeted Domain Randomization or conservative persistent adversarial attacks), the authors aim to design an adversarial training strategy that can intelligently identify and sparsely perturb the most vulnerable states and actions. This "selective" aspect is crucial to expose true weaknesses without inducing overly conservative and sub-optimal policy behavior.
3. **Drive Robustness through Game-Theoretic Optimization**: To ensure continuous improvement of the motion policy against increasingly sophisticated threats, the objective is to formulate the interaction between the motion policy and the adversary as a non-zero-sum, alternating optimization problem. This framework is intended to continually strengthen the motion policy against the strongest discovered attacks.
4. **Validate on Real Humanoid Robots Across Diverse Tasks**: The research aims to empirically demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1) across challenging tasks, specifically perceptive locomotion over complex terrains and agile whole-body trajectory tracking, showcasing improved success rates, reduced errors, and sustained long-horizon performance.

In essence, the authors seek to move beyond brute-force perturbation methods by introducing a strategic, "smart" adversary that pinpoints and targets critical vulnerabilities, thereby enabling humanoid robots to perform complex and durable motion skills in unstructured and unpredictable real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the **Selective Adversarial Attack for Robust Training (SA2RT)** framework, which integrates a learnable motion policy with a learnable attack policy through a non-zero-sum alternating optimization.

#### 3.1. Selective Adversarial Attacks

The paper identifies that previous adversarial approaches, particularly those employing a Persistent Attack Policy (PAP), apply perturbations indiscriminately across all states, leading to easily detectable attacks and potentially overly conservative victim policies. To address this, SA2RT introduces a **Selective Attack Policy (SAP)**.

- **Attack Space Definition**: The SAP's action space is defined as the joint space of the motion policy's state and action spaces. This means the adversary can perturb both the observed state ($s$) (e.g., sensor noise, state estimation errors) and the executed action ($a$) (e.g., actuator noise, execution deviations). Perturbations ($\delta_s, \delta_a$) are bounded by $\epsilon_s, \epsilon_a$.
- **Attack-Budget Constraint**: A key innovation is the introduction of an attack-budget constraint ($N_a$). Instead of continuously attacking, SAP strategically selects a *subset* of timesteps ($T_{adv}$) to apply perturbations. This makes the attacks stealthier and more efficient, aiming to cause significant performance degradation with minimal intervention.
- **Optimization Formulation**: The SAP is formulated as an optimization problem (Equation 3) to maximize an adversarial reward ($\hat{R}$) while respecting the budget constraint. This hard constraint is transformed into a soft penalty term using a Lagrange multiplier ($\lambda$) (Equation 4), allowing $\lambda$ to control the "cost" of each attack and thus the overall attack ratio. A higher $\lambda$ encourages sparser, more targeted attacks.
- **SAP Training**: The SAP itself is trained using Reinforcement Learning within a modified MDP, where its goal is to maximize the modified adversarial reward, balancing attack efficacy with the budget penalty. The appendix provides specific details on the attack space, including additive and multiplicative attacks on proprioceptive information, elevation maps, policy output actions, and PD controller parameters (Kp, Kd), as well as joint torques. Lipschitz regularization is also applied to the adversarial policy to ensure smoother, more realistic perturbation characteristics.

#### 3.2. Non-Zero-Sum Adversarial Training

Unlike traditional zero-sum games where the adversary's loss is the agent's gain (and vice-versa), this work formulates the adversarial training as a **non-zero-sum game**.

- **Objective Function**: The attack policy's objective is to maximize its own reward associated with robot safety failure *while negatively impacting* the motion policy's performance. This is formalized as maximizing $(V_{\alpha}^{\pi}(s) - V_{m}^{\pi}(s))$, where $V_{\alpha}^{\pi}(s)$ is the attacker's value function and $V_{m}^{\pi}(s)$ is the motion agent's value function. This encourages the adversary to find weaknesses that genuinely destabilize the motion policy, rather than merely driving it into a local optimum that makes it robust to easy, constant attacks.
- **Monotonic Improvement**: The paper adopts an optimization objective (Equation 7), inspired by prior work, that ensures monotonicity in the value functions, preventing the "weak adversary dilemma" and robust overfitting seen in some zero-sum frameworks. This objective uses a clipped PPO-like formulation to update the attacker policy.
- **Alternating Optimization**: The training process is iterative and alternating (Algorithm 1).
 1. The motion policy (initialized from a pre-trained state) is fixed, and the attack policy is optimized for $N_{adv}$ iterations to discover and exploit vulnerabilities.
 2. The attack policy is then fixed, and the motion policy is optimized for $N_m$ iterations to become resilient against the strongest attacks discovered by the adversary.
 This dynamic, online process ensures that both policies continuously adapt and improve against each other, leading to a truly robust motion policy.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Training Details (from Appendix)

- **Robot Platform**: Unitree G1 humanoid robot with 29 degrees of freedom.
- **Simulation Environment**: NVIDIA IsaacGym and LeggedGym frameworks.
- **Tasks**: Perceptive Locomotion (PL) over unstructured terrain using LiDAR-based elevation maps and Whole-Body Control (WBC) for agile trajectory tracking (dynamic dance motions).
- **Observation/Action Spaces**: Detailed definitions for both PL and WBC, including proprioceptive states, perceptive information (elevation maps for PL), velocity commands, joint positions/velocities, and previous actions.
- **Reward Functions**: Task-specific rewards for velocity tracking, orientation, stability, joint limits, action smoothness, and regularization terms. The SAP reward function specifically penalizes robot falling, high roll/pitch angles, and high angular/linear velocities.
- **Symmetry Regularization**: Incorporated into motion policy training to encourage human-like, coordinated movements.
- **Network Architecture**: All policies (actor-critic) and value functions are implemented using Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs).
- **Hyperparameters**: Standard RL hyperparameters (Adam optimizer, PPO clip range, GAE $\lambda$, learning rates) are used, along with specific coefficients for symmetry regularization and Lipschitz constraints.

This comprehensive approach combines intelligent attack generation, a robust game-theoretic training scheme, and meticulous engineering of observation spaces and reward functions to yield highly robust humanoid motion skills.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SA2RT on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot yielded significant findings across simulation and real-world tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Effectiveness of Adversarial Perturbations (Table 1)**:
 - Domain Randomization (DR) significantly reduced the success rate of policies trained in clean environments (PL/WBC-CE), confirming DR's ability to introduce perturbations.
 - However, DR-trained policies (PL/WBC-DR) achieved high success rates when tested in DR environments, but completely failed (0.0% success) when tested under Selective Adversarial Policy (SAP) attacks.
 - Conversely, SA2RT-trained policies (PL/WBC-SAP) maintained a high success rate even under SAP attacks (95.7 ± 6.4%), demonstrating that SAP induces stronger, more targeted perturbations that expose vulnerabilities beyond what DR can achieve.
- **Robustness of Motion Policies (Table 2, Fig. 3)**:
 - For Perceptive Locomotion (PL), PL-SAP consistently outperformed PL-DR in both clean and DR environments across all metrics (linear velocity error $E_{vel}$, angular velocity error $E_{ang}$, and gravitational projection component $E_g$). For example, in clean environments, PL-SAP reduced $E_{vel}$ by 24% (0.19 vs 0.25 m/s) compared to PL-DR. This indicates that SA2RT effectively addresses policy vulnerabilities and enhances robustness.
 - For Whole-Body Control (WBC), WBC-SAP showed superior trajectory tracking performance compared to WBC-DR across all evaluation metrics, especially velocity tracking, maintaining body stability without compromising accuracy in perturbed environments.
- **Ablation Study (Fig. 4)**:
 - Comparing Persistent Attack Policy (PAP) and SAP under varying perturbation levels ($L_p$), results showed that adversarial training generally surpasses DR in robustness.
 - As $L_p$ increased, PL-SAP's normalized rewards decreased less sharply than PL-PAP's, suggesting that SAP's selective attacks prevent conservative overfitting from unnecessary attacks on stable states.
 - In unperturbed environments, PL-SAP showed slower performance degradation with increasing $L_p$ compared to PL-PAP, while PL-PAP's degradation even exceeded that of PL-DR. This highlights that PAP's full-time attacks can cause significant state distribution shifts, leading to performance drops in low-perturbation settings, whereas SAP intelligently balances performance and robustness.
- **Analysis of Selective Attack Policy (Fig. 5, Fig. 6)**:
 - SAP's attack ratio increased with task difficulty (e.g., higher on stairs/dynamic dancing than flat ground/simple walking), confirming its ability to learn and execute targeted attacks based on perceived vulnerability.
 - Visualizations (Fig. 6) demonstrated SAP's strategic behavior, selectively attacking vulnerable transition phases (e.g., preparing to traverse stairs) to induce falls, proving its capability to distinguish critical motion states and apply targeted perturbations efficiently.
- **Analyzing Hyperparameter $\lambda$ (Fig. 7)**:
 - $\lambda$ (Lagrange multiplier for the attack budget) effectively regulated the attack ratio. Increasing $\lambda$ significantly reduced the attack ratio.
 - Motion performance initially improved as $\lambda$ increased (from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-1}$), indicating that SAP became more efficient by identifying true vulnerabilities. However, further increasing $\lambda$ (to $10^1$) overly penalized attacks, weakening the adversary and hindering robustness improvements. This confirms that $\lambda$ is crucial for balancing attack effectiveness and policy agility.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Complex Terrain Traversability (Table 3, Fig. 8)**:
 - PL-SAP significantly improved the terrain traversal success rates of the real Unitree G1 robot by 40% (average over terrains) compared to PL-DR. For instance, on slopes, PL-SAP achieved 90% success vs. PL-DR's 54%.
 - Qualitative observations (Fig. 8) showed PL-SAP exhibiting fewer foot trips and slips on challenging terrains like stairs and slopes. It also successfully navigated thin, sparse ridges often missed by elevation maps, where PL-DR frequently fell. This demonstrates SA2RT's strong enhancement of motion policy robustness and practical terrain adaptability.
- **Whole-Body Trajectory Imitation Performance (Fig. 1, Fig. 9)**:
 - When tracking a 120-second dynamic dance motion, WBC-DR policies exhibited accumulating tracking errors, leading to instability and premature termination after only 65 seconds.
 - In contrast, WBC-SAP policies successfully completed the entire 120-second trajectory, reducing trajectory tracking error by 32%. Snapshots (Fig. 1) illustrated the robot's ability to dynamically regulate whole-body balance during complex tracking. This highlights SA2RT's effectiveness in bridging the sim-to-real gap for dynamic, long-horizon motion control.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that SA2RT's selective adversarial training approach is highly effective in developing more robust, stable, and long-horizon motion policies for humanoid robots in both simulation and challenging real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Keep on Going" paper introduces a highly significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics and Reinforcement Learning, particularly regarding the crucial aspect of **robustness for real-world deployment**. The proposed **Selective Adversarial Attack for Robust Training (SA2RT)** framework, with its novel Selective Attack Policy (SAP) and non-zero-sum game formulation, addresses fundamental limitations of existing methods.

#### 5.1. Significance of Contributions

- **Targeted Vulnerability Identification**: Unlike broad-spectrum Domain Randomization (DR) or untargeted adversarial attacks, SAP intelligently identifies and exploits specific vulnerabilities in the motion policy. This precision prevents conservative overfitting, leading to policies that are robust where it matters most, without sacrificing performance in benign conditions. This "smart adversary" paradigm marks a significant conceptual advancement in adversarial training for robotics.
- **Efficient Robustness Learning**: By operating under an "attack-budget constraint," SAP optimizes for maximal impact with minimal perturbations. This efficiency is critical for learning in high-dimensional, complex systems like humanoid robots, ensuring that training resources are focused on exposing true weaknesses rather than generating redundant attacks.
- **Overcoming the "Weak Adversary Dilemma"**: The non-zero-sum game formulation between the motion policy and the SAP ensures that the adversary continuously strengthens, driving the motion policy to develop genuinely robust defenses. This iterative, competitive process yields policies that are resilient to diverse and challenging real-world disturbances.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Efficacy**: The extensive validation on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot across demanding tasks like perceptive locomotion over complex terrains and agile whole-body trajectory imitation provides strong empirical evidence of SA2RT's practical value. The 40% improvement in terrain traversal success rates and 32% reduction in trajectory tracking errors are remarkable, directly tackling the long-standing sim-to-real transfer challenge.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enabling Reliable Humanoid Deployment**: The most immediate impact is on the practicality of humanoid robots. By learning robust, long-horizon motion skills, humanoid robots become more reliable for real-world applications in domains such as domestic service, industrial production, disaster response, and healthcare, where prolonged operation in uncertain environments is essential. This work brings the vision of versatile, autonomous humanoids closer to reality.
- **Advancing RL for Robotics**: The SA2RT framework offers a new paradigm for enhancing robustness in RL-based robotic control. Its principles of selective, budget-constrained, non-zero-sum adversarial training could be adapted and applied to other complex robotic platforms (e.g., quadrupedal robots, manipulation arms) and various motor skills, pushing the boundaries of what RL can achieve in dynamic, unstructured settings.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The results directly contribute to bridging the sim-to-real gap. By creating policies that are inherently resilient to discrepancies between simulation and reality, SA2RT reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection for fine-tuning, accelerating the development cycle for new robotic capabilities.
- **Inspiring Future Research**: The paper opens several avenues for future work, as highlighted by the authors: exploring perturbation attacks in robot-environment interaction to enhance adaptability to dynamic environments, and optimizing the attack-budget constraint for a better trade-off between attack effectiveness and strategy agility. These directions will further refine the robustness and adaptability of autonomous robots.

In conclusion, "Keep on Going" provides a robust, principled, and empirically validated method for learning highly resilient humanoid motion skills. Its impact will be felt across academic research in AI and robotics, and in the practical deployment of next-generation humanoid platforms, making them more capable, reliable, and trustworthy in diverse real-world scenarios.
