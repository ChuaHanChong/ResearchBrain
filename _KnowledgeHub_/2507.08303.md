# Keep on Going: Learning Robust Humanoid Motion Skills via Selective Adversarial Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed the Selective Adversarial Attack for Robust Training (SA2RT) framework, which significantly improves the robustness and real-world performance of RL-learned motion policies for humanoid robots. This method enabled a Unitree G1 robot to achieve a 40% higher success rate on complex terrains and 32% reduced tracking error for long-horizon dynamic movements.

## Problem

- Humanoid robot motion policies trained in simulation are often fragile and fail when deployed in real-world environments due to domain distribution shifts and susceptibility to perturbations.
- Existing robustness methods like Domain Randomization (DR) struggle to efficiently target specific policy vulnerabilities, while traditional adversarial training often leads to conservative or overfit policies due to the "weak adversary dilemma."
- RL-based neural controllers lack systematic robustness design, hindering their ability to reliably execute long-horizon tasks on physical humanoid robots.

## Method

- The proposed ==Selective Adversarial Attack== for Robust Training (SA2RT) framework integrates a learnable motion policy with a learnable attack policy through a non-zero-sum alternating optimization.
- A Selective Attack Policy (SAP) is introduced, which strategically identifies and sparsely perturbs the most vulnerable states and actions under an attack-budget constraint, rather than applying indiscriminate continuous attacks.
- The adversarial training is formulated as a non-zero-sum game, where the attacker's objective is to maximize its own reward (robot failure) while negatively impacting the motion policy, ensuring continuous, monotonic improvement of both policies.

## Results

- SA2RT policies (PL-SAP) achieved a **40%** higher success rate on average over complex terrains compared to DR-trained policies (PL-DR) on the real Unitree G1 robot, with **90%** success on slopes versus **54%**.
- For whole-body trajectory imitation, SA2RT policies (WBC-SAP) successfully completed an entire 120-second dynamic dance, reducing trajectory tracking error by **32%**, whereas DR-trained policies became unstable and failed prematurely.
- In simulation, SA2RT-trained policies (PL-SAP) consistently outperformed DR-trained policies (PL-DR) in both clean and DR environments, for example reducing linear velocity error by **24%** (**0.19** vs **0.25** m/s) in clean environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The effectiveness of adversarial training is maximized when attacks are selective and targeted at true policy vulnerabilities, preventing conservative overfitting.
> - Introducing an attack-budget constraint forces the adversary to be efficient, identifying critical states and actions where perturbations have the most impact.
> - A non-zero-sum game formulation, coupled with alternating optimization, allows for continuous strengthening of the motion policy against increasingly sophisticated and targeted threats.
