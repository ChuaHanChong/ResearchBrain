# Are Your Reasoning Models Reasoning or Guessing? A Mechanistic Analysis of Hierarchical Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute conducted a mechanistic analysis of the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), uncovering that its impressive performance stems from a "scaled guessing" dynamic, characterized by fixed-point violations and spurious attractors, rather than gradual reasoning. This understanding led to an "Augmented HRM" achieving 96.9% accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme, a substantial increase from 54.5%.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex "System-2 thinking" tasks like Sudoku, often achieving 0% success rates.
- Latent-space reasoning models, such as the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), demonstrate high accuracy on these tasks, but the precise mechanisms behind their success and failure modes remain unclear.
- A need exists to mechanistically scrutinize the nature of impressive performance in advanced AI reasoning models to determine if they are truly 'reasoning' or employing other strategies.

## Method

- A mechanistic analysis of the ==Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM)== was performed using the challenging Sudoku-Extreme dataset to probe its internal dynamics.
- The methodology involved examining the ==fixed-point property==, analyzing segment-wise loss for =='grokking' patterns==, and visualizing latent state trajectories via PCA to classify reasoning modes.
- Three performance enhancement strategies were developed and combined: ==data augmentation, input perturbation (token relabeling), and model bootstrapping==, culminating in =="Augmented HRM"==.

## Results

- Data augmentation (data mixing) restored the fixed-point property, boosting HRM's accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme from **54.5%** to **59.9%**.
- Input perturbation (token relabeling) alone improved accuracy from **54.5%** to **73.2%**, demonstrating the effectiveness of diversifying input perspectives for generating 'guesses.'
- Combining data augmentation, input perturbation, and model bootstrapping in 'Augmented HRM' achieved a new state-of-the-art accuracy of **96.9%** on the Sudoku-Extreme dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - HRM frequently violates the 'fixed-point property,' failing on extremely simple puzzles despite solving complex ones, indicating a lack of stability in its solutions.
> - The model exhibits a 'grokking' dynamic in its reasoning steps, where solutions are found abruptly rather than through gradual refinement, suggesting recursive segments scale 'guessing attempts.'
> - Latent space analysis reveals 'spurious fixed points' as misleading attractors, causing non-trivial failures where the model gets trapped in incorrect solutions corresponding to local minima of a heuristic error function.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current landscape of artificial intelligence models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) based on the transformer architecture, has demonstrated considerable success across various tasks. However, these models frequently encounter difficulties in tasks that demand systematic, "System-2" reasoning, such as solving complex Sudokus or mazes, often achieving 0% success rates on challenging benchmarks.

To address these limitations, a significant body of research has focused on enhancing LLMs' reasoning capabilities. Approaches include prolonging the reasoning process through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting or fine-tuning with reinforcement learning. These methods typically operate at the token level, which can impose inherent limitations on the potential of deep neural networks.

An alternative paradigm that has emerged is latent-space reasoning models. These models, exemplified by the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) (Wang et al., 2025a), process intermediate computations in a continuous hidden trajectory rather than explicit token sequences. HRM has achieved high accuracy on various reasoning-intensive tasks, significantly outperforming LLM-based reasoners.

This paper positions itself within this context by focusing on a mechanistic analysis of HRM. While HRM's superior performance is established, the underlying reasons for its success and its potential failure modes remain less understood. This research aims to open the "black box" of HRM, scrutinizing its internal reasoning patterns to provide a scientific understanding of how such models operate, thereby contributing to the broader effort of developing more competent reasoning AI. It also extends the understanding of reasoning dynamics observed in other models, such as "grokking" in CoT-based models and the quantization model of neural scaling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a detailed mechanistic study of the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) to understand its reasoning patterns, identify its strengths, and uncover its potential failure modes. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Understand HRM's Superior Performance:** HRM has demonstrated exceptional performance on complex reasoning tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art LLMs. The researchers are motivated to understand the fundamental mechanisms that contribute to this success, moving beyond empirical observations to a deeper, scientific comprehension of its internal workings.
2. **Identify and Analyze Theoretical-Practical Discrepancies:** A core motivation is to examine whether HRM adheres to its foundational theoretical assumptions in practice. Specifically, the paper investigates the "fixed point property," a crucial assumption that justifies the use of one-step gradients in HRM's training and enables reasoning depth scaling. Probing this property for potential violations is key to understanding model stability and training efficacy.
3. **Investigate Recursive Reasoning Dynamics:** The study aims to analyze how HRM processes information across its recursive segments. The intuitive expectation is a gradual, monotonic refinement of the solution. The researchers are motivated to ascertain if this expectation holds true or if alternative, less straightforward dynamics, such as sudden "grokking" or periods of stagnation, characterize the reasoning process.
4. **Characterize Latent Space Behavior:** A significant motivation is to understand the trajectory and convergence behavior of HRM's latent states. The research seeks to determine if the model consistently converges to correct solutions or if it can get "lost" in the latent space, potentially trapped by incorrect or "spurious" attractors. This involves mapping and classifying different reasoning modes based on latent state dynamics.
5. **Develop Practical Improvements from Mechanistic Insights:** Beyond scientific understanding, a practical motivation is to leverage the insights gained from the mechanistic analysis to develop concrete strategies. These strategies aim to address identified weaknesses, mitigate failure modes, and enhance HRM's performance on challenging benchmarks like Sudoku-Extreme. The goal is to translate scientific understanding into tangible performance improvements.
6. **Inform Future Reasoning Model Design:** Ultimately, the work aims to provide a new perspective on how reasoning models "reason," challenging conventional notions and offering a "guessing" framework. This understanding is intended to serve as a foundation for developing more robust, efficient, and interpretable recursive reasoning models in the future.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology combining detailed architectural analysis, empirical observation, latent space visualization, and the development of targeted intervention strategies.

**Model under Investigation:**
The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) (Wang et al., 2025a) serves as the primary subject. HRM is a recursive latent-space model where an input sequence is first mapped to an embedding (˜x). A latent state (z_i), deterministically initialized, is recurrently updated by an HRM segment (F): z_{i+1} = F(z_i, ˜x; θ). After multiple segments, a final prediction (ˆy) is extracted. The model incorporates an Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) mechanism to halt computation once a plausible solution is reached.
Training involves deep supervision, where loss is computed for each segment's output, and a one-step gradient approach, which detaches z_i from the computational graph, theoretically ensuring training costs scale linearly with reasoning depth. This one-step gradient relies on the assumption that if HRM finds a correct solution (z*), it becomes a fixed point (z* = F(z*, ˜x; θ)).

**Datasets:**
The primary benchmark utilized is **Sudoku-Extreme**, a dataset of highly challenging Sudoku puzzles. The model is trained on augmented versions of 1000 samples of similar difficulty to the test set. Additionally, some experiments are conducted on the **Maze-Hard** dataset to demonstrate generalizability.

**Analysis Techniques:**

1. **Probing Fixed Point Property:**
 - **Method:** HRM is presented with "extremely simple puzzles" (e.g., Sudokus with only one unknown cell or one masked row) where the correct solution is trivial or quickly found.
 - **Observation:** The behavior of the latent state and output is monitored across segments to see if the model maintains the correct solution or makes unnecessary, destabilizing updates.
 - **Purpose:** Directly test the fundamental "fixed point property" assumption in a controlled setting.

2. **Analysis of Segment-wise Loss Dynamics:**
 - **Mean-field Analysis:** The average segment-wise loss is calculated across all test samples to observe overall performance trends as reasoning depth increases.
 - **Single-Sample Analysis:** The loss curve for individual samples is examined over segments.
 - **Purpose:** Determine if reasoning progress is gradual and monotonic at both aggregate and individual levels, challenging or confirming the intuitive view of incremental refinement.

3. **Latent Space Trajectory Visualization:**
 - **Method:** Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to the sequence of latent states (z_i) for individual puzzles. The trajectories are then projected onto the first two principal components.
 - **Categorization:** Reasoning patterns are visually classified into four modes: Trivial Success, Non-trivial Success, Trivial Failure, and Non-trivial Failure.
 - **Purpose:** Mechanistically understand how HRM navigates its latent space during problem-solving, identifying distinct successful and unsuccessful pathways, including instances of "getting lost" or converging to incorrect states.

4. **Characterization of Spurious Fixed Points:**
 - **Method:** For cases exhibiting non-trivial success or failure, the latent space around identified attractors is further analyzed by observing the initial update directions of latent states (z_i) from various starting points.
 - **Hypothesis:** Spurious fixed points might correspond to local minima of an "energy function." An **error metric E(ˆy)** is defined to quantify Sudoku rule violations (counting conflicts in rows, columns, and boxes, Equation 7).
 - **Purpose:** Understand the nature of "false attractors" (spurious fixed points) and their competitive interaction with true solutions, and explore whether these points represent plausible local minima in a label-agnostic error landscape.

**Proposed Practical Strategies (Augmented HRM):**
Based on the mechanistic insights, the authors propose three strategies to improve HRM performance by scaling "guessing attempts":

1. **Data Augmentation (Scaling Quality of Guesses):**
 - **Method:** For each training puzzle, a simplified replicate is created by revealing a random portion of previously hidden tokens.
 - **Purpose:** Explicitly train HRM on "easier" sub-problems and nearly-solved puzzles to enforce the acquisition of the fixed point property and enhance stability.

2. **Input Perturbation (Scaling Number of Guesses via Inference Randomness):**
 - **Method:** Input Sudoku puzzles are transformed using equivalent operations (e.g., token relabeling, swapping rows/columns/bands, reflection/rotation) to generate multiple semantically identical but syntactically distinct inputs.
 - **Inference:** Multiple forward passes are performed with these perturbed inputs. A majority vote is used among successful runs.
 - **Purpose:** Leverage HRM's deterministic nature by presenting it with diverse "perspectives" of the same problem, increasing the chance of one perturbation leading to a correct solution.

3. **Model Bootstrapping (Scaling Number of Guesses via Training Randomness):**
 - **Method:** An ensemble of multiple model checkpoints from different stages of the later half of the training phase (e.g., 10 checkpoints separated by ~1000 steps) is used.
 - **Inference:** Each model in the ensemble performs a forward pass, and a majority vote is taken from successful runs.
 - **Purpose:** Exploit the inherent parameter diversity among adjacent training checkpoints, even if highly correlated, as they may exhibit different success patterns on various test samples.

**Evaluation:**
The effectiveness of these strategies is evaluated by measuring the exact accuracy on the Sudoku-Extreme test set, comparing the baseline HRM performance with models trained and inferred using the proposed augmentation methods, individually and in combination.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The mechanistic analysis of HRM revealed three principal findings that challenge conventional understandings of its reasoning process and inform new practical strategies.

#### 4.1. Violation of the Fixed Point Property on Simple Puzzles

- **Observation:** Contrary to the theoretical assumption that a correct solution constitutes a fixed point for HRM's latent state updates, the model exhibits instability when presented with "extremely simple puzzles" (e.g., Sudokus with only one missing cell or one masked row). HRM frequently corrupts its answer by making unnecessary updates to its latent state even after initially reaching the correct solution. In some cases, it fails completely on such simple puzzles. On challenging Sudoku-Extreme puzzles, if HRM finds a solution, it maintains it; however, this observation alone does not confirm the fixed-point property for arbitrary inputs.
- **Explanation:** The researchers attribute this to the one-step gradient training combined with the focus on Sudoku-Extreme's extreme difficulty. HRM is not explicitly trained to solve easier problems or to ensure stability around already correct solutions. This leads to a postponement of stability acquisition during training.
- **Impact:** This violation causes tangible performance loss and indicates a theory-practice mismatch in HRM's foundational assumptions.

#### 4.2. "Grokking" Dynamics in Reasoning Steps

- **Observation:** While the average segment-wise loss across all test samples demonstrates a smooth and gradual decrease, suggesting incremental refinement, individual samples exhibit different behavior. For a single sample, the error reduction is often not gradual. Instead, the loss typically decreases quickly to a relatively low value, then enters a prolonged plateau before abruptly dropping to zero ("groks" the answer) in a few segments. Alternatively, it may remain on the plateau and fail.
- **Interpretation:** This "grokking" dynamic suggests that HRM's recursive steps do not primarily serve as a mechanism for incremental refinement. Instead, the recursion appears to function as a way of scaling "guessing attempts" for a plausible latent state, challenging the notion of sequential, deliberative reasoning.

#### 4.3. Existence of Multiple Fixed Points and Misleading Attractors

- **Observation:** Visualization of latent state trajectories through PCA reveals four distinct reasoning modes: Trivial Success, Non-trivial Success, Trivial Failure, and Non-trivial Failure. Of particular interest are the non-trivial modes where HRM gets "lost." In these cases, the latent state can converge to "spurious fixed points" that do not correspond to the correct solution, or it may linger around such points for many segments before eventually escaping to the true solution (Non-trivial Success) or remaining trapped indefinitely (Non-trivial Failure).
- **Characterization:** These spurious fixed points function as "rival attractors" in the latent space. The initial latent state's proximity to a true or false attractor dictates the convergence path. The researchers propose an "error metric" E(ˆy), which quantifies Sudoku rule violations. Analysis shows that these misleading attractors often correspond to shallow local minima of this metric, creating an "energy barrier" that discourages HRM from exploring further towards the true solution.

#### 4.4. Performance Enhancement via "Augmented HRM" Strategies

Leveraging these insights, the researchers developed three strategies to "scale guesses," resulting in significant performance improvements:

- **Data Augmentation (for Fixed Point Restoration):**
 - **Method:** Introducing simplified puzzle replicates during training.
 - **Result:** Increased accuracy from 55.0% to 59.9% on Sudoku-Extreme. Critically, it eradicated the failure on simple puzzles and eliminated unfavored drifts after finding solutions, restoring the fixed point property. It also led to symmetric latent-state trajectories for similarly structured simple puzzles, indicating more generalized rule learning.

- **Input Perturbation (Token Relabeling):**
 - **Method:** Applying equivalent Sudoku transformations (e.g., relabeling tokens) to generate multiple input variants during inference, with majority voting.
 - **Result:** Boosted accuracy from 55.0% to 73.2% when applied independently. This demonstrates that HRM treats syntactically distinct but semantically equivalent inputs as novel problems, leveraging their diversity for better outcomes.

- **Model Bootstrapping:**
 - **Method:** Ensembling predictions from multiple model checkpoints from later stages of a single training run, with majority voting.
 - **Result:** Improved accuracy from 55.0% to 64.2% when applied independently. This indicates that even highly correlated checkpoints possess distinct strengths, capturing different successful reasoning pathways for various samples.

- **Combined Augmented HRM:**
 - **Result:** The combination of all three strategies (data augmentation, input perturbation, and model bootstrapping) achieved an accuracy of **96.9%** on Sudoku-Extreme. This constitutes a substantial improvement over the baseline HRM (55.0%) and surpasses existing variants, establishing a new state-of-the-art for this benchmark without altering the core HRM architecture.

- **Generalizability (Maze-Hard):** Similar augmentation techniques were also effective on the Maze-Hard dataset, improving baseline accuracy from 74.5% to 93.3% with the combined approach, indicating the general applicability of these findings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions on both scientific and practical fronts, with potential implications for the development and understanding of advanced reasoning models.

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Mechanistic Understanding of Recursive Reasoning:** The paper provides a detailed mechanistic account of how Hierarchical Reasoning Models operate, moving beyond black-box performance analysis. It systematically investigates HRM's internal dynamics, shedding light on the actual process of "reasoning" in these architectures.
- **Challenging the Gradual Refinement Paradigm:** A core scientific contribution is the challenge to the intuitive notion that recursive reasoning models achieve solutions through gradual, incremental refinement. The observation of "grokking" dynamics and the "guessing" interpretation suggests a different operational mode, where recursive steps might function more as repeated attempts to find a correct latent state, rather than a continuous optimization process.
- **Identification of Fixed Point Violation:** The discovery of the fixed point property violation is scientifically important as it highlights a critical mismatch between theoretical assumptions (justifying one-step gradients) and practical model behavior. This uncovers a fundamental instability that impacts model reliability and performance on certain inputs.
- **Characterization of Spurious Fixed Points:** The identification and characterization of "spurious fixed points" as "rival attractors" in the latent space provides a novel insight into failure modes. Interpreting these attractors as local minima of a label-agnostic "error metric" opens new avenues for understanding the implicit objectives guiding latent space navigation during inference.
- **Establishment of a Taxonomy for Recursive Reasoners:** The categorization of reasoning patterns into four distinct modes (Trivial/Non-trivial Success/Failure) offers a valuable qualitative taxonomy. This framework can serve as a common vocabulary for analyzing and comparing the behavior of emerging recursive reasoning models across different tasks and architectures.

#### 5.2. Potential Practical Impact

- **Significant Performance Enhancement:** The development of "Augmented HRM," leveraging data augmentation, input perturbation, and model bootstrapping, leads to a substantial improvement in accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme (from 55.0% to 96.9%). This practical outcome demonstrates that a deeper mechanistic understanding can directly translate into state-of-the-art performance gains without requiring fundamental architectural changes.
- **Generalizable Optimization Strategies:** The proposed augmentation techniques are simple yet effective and are shown to be generalizable to other complex reasoning tasks like Maze-Hard. This suggests that these methods could be widely applicable to a broader class of recursive latent-space models, offering readily deployable strategies for performance enhancement.
- **Informing Future Model Design:** The insights into HRM's "guessing" nature, the fixed point violation, and the existence of spurious attractors can guide the design of future reasoning models. Future architectures and training regimes could explicitly aim to enforce stability, prevent convergence to false attractors, and promote more efficient exploration of the latent space, potentially leading to more robust and reliable AI reasoners.
- **Improved Debugging and Interpretability:** A mechanistic understanding of reasoning models can aid in debugging and improving their interpretability. By understanding *why* a model succeeds or fails on specific inputs, researchers can diagnose issues more effectively and build models that are not only powerful but also more transparent in their decision-making processes.
