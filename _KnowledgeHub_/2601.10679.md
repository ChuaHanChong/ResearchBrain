# Are Your Reasoning Models Reasoning or Guessing? A Mechanistic Analysis of Hierarchical Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute conducted a mechanistic analysis of the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), uncovering that its impressive performance stems from a "scaled guessing" dynamic, characterized by fixed-point violations and spurious attractors, rather than gradual reasoning. This understanding led to an "Augmented HRM" achieving 96.9% accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme, a substantial increase from 54.5%.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex "System-2 thinking" tasks like Sudoku, often achieving 0% success rates.
- Latent-space reasoning models, such as the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), demonstrate high accuracy on these tasks, but the precise mechanisms behind their success and failure modes remain unclear.
- A need exists to mechanistically scrutinize the nature of impressive performance in advanced AI reasoning models to determine if they are truly 'reasoning' or employing other strategies.

## Method

- A mechanistic analysis of the ==Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM)== was performed using the challenging Sudoku-Extreme dataset to probe its internal dynamics.
- The methodology involved examining the ==fixed-point property==, analyzing segment-wise loss for =='grokking' patterns==, and visualizing latent state trajectories via PCA to classify reasoning modes.
- Three performance enhancement strategies were developed and combined: ==data augmentation, input perturbation (token relabeling), and model bootstrapping==, culminating in =="Augmented HRM"==.

## Results

- Data augmentation (data mixing) restored the fixed-point property, boosting HRM's accuracy on Sudoku-Extreme from **54.5%** to **59.9%**.
- Input perturbation (token relabeling) alone improved accuracy from **54.5%** to **73.2%**, demonstrating the effectiveness of diversifying input perspectives for generating 'guesses.'
- Combining data augmentation, input perturbation, and model bootstrapping in 'Augmented HRM' achieved a new state-of-the-art accuracy of **96.9%** on the Sudoku-Extreme dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - HRM frequently violates the 'fixed-point property,' failing on extremely simple puzzles despite solving complex ones, indicating a lack of stability in its solutions.
> - The model exhibits a 'grokking' dynamic in its reasoning steps, where solutions are found abruptly rather than through gradual refinement, suggesting recursive segments scale 'guessing attempts.'
> - Latent space analysis reveals 'spurious fixed points' as misleading attractors, causing non-trivial failures where the model gets trapped in incorrect solutions corresponding to local minima of a heuristic error function.
