# ViTaS: Visual Tactile Soft Fusion Contrastive Learning for Visuomotor Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ViTaS is a visuo-tactile representation learning framework for visuomotor policies. It pairs soft fusion contrastive learning, which treats the tactile duals of the top-K most similar visual frames as positives while alternating which encoder updates, with a conditional VAE that reconstructs images from fused visuo-tactile embeddings. Across 12 simulated and 3 real robot tasks it averages 91.4% success, beating six baselines.

## Problem

- Prior visuo-tactile methods fuse modalities by direct concatenation or simple patching, failing to exploit the structural correspondence between touch and vision.
- Existing work emphasizes alignment while ignoring modality complementarity, so performance degrades in self-occluded scenes where vision alone is insufficient.
- Conventional instance-discrimination contrastive learning marks resembling samples from different timesteps as negatives, discarding key shared cross-modal information.

## Method

- **ViTaS**'s ==soft fusion contrastive learning==: retrieve the top-K (K=10) most similar visual frames, use their dual tactile embeddings as positives, and freeze one encoder per stage.
- A ==periodic switching schedule== alternates the two contrastive objectives across modality roles, preventing ==modality imbalance== so both visual and tactile CNN encoders update.
- A ==CVAE== conditioned on the concatenated visuo-tactile feature reconstructs the current frame, enforcing ==cross-modal consistency==; total loss combines contrastive, VAE, and ==PPO== or diffusion-policy losses.

## Results

- Averages **91.4%** success over 12 simulated tasks versus **71.5%** for the best baseline, with large gains on rotation tasks such as Egg Rotate (**85.7%** vs **71.6%**).
- Substituting ViTaS encoders into Diffusion Policy gives **60.4%** average across three tasks versus **38.2%** for CNN and **33.4%** for transformer encoders.
- Real-world Galaxea-R1 tasks average **46.0%** versus **30.0%** for Diffusion Policy using only a head camera plus touch; ablations drop to **54.7%** without contrastive, **63.2%** without CVAE.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Similarity-retrieved positives beat both single-timestep pairing and temporal-neighbor heuristics, showing the nearest frames in feature space are not always the temporally adjacent ones.
> - Separate per-modality encoders matter: a shared unified encoder collapses performance to 27.1%, since touch and image statistics differ too much to merge early.
> - Reconstructing vision from fused embeddings lets touch compensate for occlusion and camera loss, but high-dynamics and deformable-object manipulation remain out of reach.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has long sought to mimic human dexterity and adaptability in complex tasks. While traditional visuomotor learning, primarily relying on visual input, has achieved considerable success, its limitations become apparent in scenarios involving occlusions, transparent objects, or nuanced contact interactions. This is where tactile information, a crucial component of human manipulation, enters the picture.

The paper "ViTaS" positions itself squarely within the rapidly evolving landscape of multimodal (vision and touch) representation learning for visuomotor control. Previous works, such as M3L and VTT, have attempted to integrate visual and tactile information, but ViTaS identifies critical shortcomings in these existing approaches:
1. **Suboptimal Feature Alignment:** Many methods use direct concatenation or simple patching of visual and tactile features, failing to fully exploit the inherent structural correspondence between the two modalities.
2. **Neglect of Complementarity:** In challenging situations like self-occlusion (e.g., the robot's gripper obscuring the object), tactile information becomes complementary to vision, providing crucial data that vision alone cannot. Existing methods often overlook this complementary nature.

ViTaS aims to address these limitations by proposing a more sophisticated fusion mechanism inspired by human physiology. It builds upon established machine learning paradigms like contrastive learning and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), adapting them for the specific challenges of visuo-tactile integration. Contrastive learning (e.g.,–) has been successful in learning robust representations by bringing similar samples closer and pushing dissimilar ones apart. ViTaS extends this to a cross-modal "soft fusion" approach. Conditional VAEs (CVAEs) are employed to leverage the complementary nature of modalities by reconstructing one modality conditioned on the fused features of both, a strategy aimed at overcoming visual ambiguities in occluded settings.

The paper directly contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in multimodal robotic learning, especially in tasks requiring fine motor control and robust perception in challenging visual conditions. By explicitly addressing both feature alignment and complementarity, ViTaS seeks to overcome the performance ceilings of prior methods, thereby paving the way for more capable and reliable robotic systems in real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the ViTaS research is to **develop a more effective framework for fusing visual and tactile information to enhance the performance of visuomotor algorithms**, particularly in complex and challenging manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is motivated by several key observations and limitations in the current state of robotic manipulation:

1. **Human Inspiration:** Humans naturally integrate visual and tactile senses for highly dexterous manipulation. While vision guides gross movements, touch provides critical feedback for fine control, object properties, and interactions, especially when vision is ambiguous or blocked. The paper cites psychological research on how touch can enhance visual perception, motivating a similar synergy in robotics.
2. **Limitations of Vision-Only Approaches:** Many past and even some current visuomotor learning methods rely solely on vision. While powerful, these systems struggle with scenarios where visual information is inherently limited, such as transparent objects, poor lighting, or self-occlusion by the robot's own body parts.
3. **Inadequacies of Current Multimodal Fusion:** Existing visuo-tactile fusion methods are often simplistic, employing direct concatenation or basic patching of features. The authors contend that these methods "fail to exploit the inherent correspondence between visual and tactile information," leading to "suboptimal performance." They also "overlook the complementary nature of visual and tactile modalities," which is crucial for handling occluded scenarios and limits real-world deployment.
4. **Addressing Alignment and Complementarity:** The authors identify the specific gaps in prior work: insufficient exploitation of the *alignment* between modalities (how similar they are) and neglecting their *complementarity* (how they provide different, yet useful, information). ViTaS is explicitly designed to tackle both these aspects.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond superficial multimodal integration towards a deeper, more biologically inspired fusion that leverages the full potential of both vision and touch, making robotic manipulation more robust, generalizable, and adaptable to real-world complexities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViTaS proposes a novel visuo-tactile representation learning framework built upon two core mechanisms: **Soft Fusion Contrastive Learning** and **Conditional VAE (CVAE) Visuo-Tactile Feature Integration**. The overall framework processes visual and tactile inputs through separate CNN encoders, then fuses their embeddings for policy learning, incorporating both contrastive and VAE-based losses.

**A. Soft Fusion Contrastive Learning:**
This module is designed to exploit the *alignment* or structural similarity between visual and tactile representations, especially in non-occluded scenarios. It extends conventional contrastive learning in a cross-modal, "soft" manner:
1. **Separate Encoders:** Visual observations ($o_i$) and tactile observations ($t_i$) at timestep $i$ are first mapped to latent representations by dedicated encoders $f_o(\cdot)$ and $f_t(\cdot)$ respectively.
2. **Cross-Modal Positive Sample Mining:** Instead of using temporally adjacent frames as positives (a common heuristic), ViTaS identifies positives based on feature similarity.
 - For a given visual observation $o_i$, the system retrieves its top-K most similar *visual* images ($o_{i1}, \ldots, o_{iK}$). The *corresponding tactile embeddings* ($t_{i1}, \ldots, t_{iK}$) for these top-K visual images are then designated as positive samples for $t_i$. All other tactile maps are considered negatives.
 - A contrastive loss ($L_{CON,1,i}$) is applied, but crucially, only the *tactile encoder* ($f_t$) is updated, while the visual encoder ($f_o$) remains frozen. This ensures that the tactile representations are pulled closer to the visual context.
3. **Modality Role Swapping:** To prevent modality imbalance and ensure both encoders are optimized, the roles are periodically swapped. The visual encoder is then updated based on similarities found using tactile features, and the corresponding visual positive samples are designated. This defines $L_{CON,2,i}$.
4. **Alternating Training Schedule:** A switching schedule (coefficient $u_i$) alternates between optimizing $L_{CON,1,i}$ and $L_{CON,2,i}$, ensuring balanced updates for both encoders. The total contrastive objective is $L_{CON}$. This "soft fusion" approach, by finding top-K similar samples across modalities, is argued to be more robust than simple instance discrimination or temporal proximity.

**B. Conditional VAE Visuo-Tactile Feature Integration:**
This module targets the *complementarity* of modalities, especially vital in self-occluded scenarios where visual information might be partial or ambiguous.
1. **Fused Embedding as Condition:** A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) is employed. The key idea is to reconstruct the current visual image ($o_{cur}$) conditioned on a concatenated, *fused* visuo-tactile feature ($c$). This fused feature leverages both modalities.
2. **Reconstruction and Consistency:** The CVAE consists of an encoder, decoder, and a visuo-tactile embedding projector. It attempts to reconstruct the visual frame from the fused embedding. This process enforces cross-modal consistency: the model learns to generate a coherent visual observation even when actual visual input might be compromised, by drawing upon the tactile context.
3. **Joint Optimization:** The CVAE components (encoder, decoder, projector) and the image/tactile encoders are jointly optimized with a VAE loss ($L_{VAE}$), which includes a reconstruction term and a KL divergence term. For inference, only the trained image and tactile encoders are retained.

**Overall Objective:**
The final objective function combines the soft fusion contrastive loss ($L_{CON}$), the CVAE loss ($L_{VAE}$), and a policy-specific loss ($L_{policy}$), balanced by coefficients $\lambda$ and $\mu$:
$L = \lambda L_{CON} + \mu L_{VAE} + L_{policy}$
$L_{policy}$ can be PPO for Reinforcement Learning (RL) or Diffusion Policy (DP) for Imitation Learning (IL). The parameters $\lambda=1, \mu=0.1$ were found empirically optimal.

This integrated approach aims to leverage both the explicit alignment and the implicit complementarity of vision and touch, creating robust and information-rich multimodal representations for visuomotor control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for ViTaS span a wide range of simulated and real-world tasks under both Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL) paradigms, rigorously evaluating the method's performance, generalization, robustness, and the contribution of its individual components.

**A. Simulation Experiment Results (RL and IL):**
- **Performance (Table I & II):**
 - **RL Tasks:** ViTaS consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance across 9 primitive RL tasks (e.g., Insertion, Door, Lift, Pen Rotate, Dual Arm Lift, Mobile Catch, Egg Rotate, Block Rotate, Block Spin). It significantly outperformed 6 baselines (M3L, VTT, PoE, Concat, MViTac, ConViTaC), particularly in "tough tasks" like Egg Rotate and Block Rotate, where many baselines struggled. ViTaS maintained high success rates (e.g., 85.7% for Egg Rotate, 93.3% for Block Rotate) where baselines scored as low as 0.5% to 15%.
 - **IL Tasks:** When integrated with Diffusion Policy (DP) for imitation learning (3 tasks: Box Stack, Wiping, Assembly), ViTaS (DP w/ ViTaS) achieved an average success rate of 60.4%, significantly outperforming DP with default CNN (38.2%) or Transformer (33.4%) encoders. Notably, ViTaS used only a single head camera and tactile sensors, whereas baseline DP used three cameras, highlighting ViTaS's ability to compensate for reduced visual input with tactile features.
- **Generalization and Robustness (Table I & III):**
 - **Object Geometry Changes:** In "Lift w/ Cap" and "Lift w/ Can" tasks (variations of the Lift task with different object shapes), ViTaS exhibited only a "negligible decrease" in performance compared to baseline models, which experienced significant drops. This demonstrates resilience to object geometry variations.
 - **Randomized Targets:** For "Pen Rotate w/ Random Target," ViTaS achieved a 78.4% success rate, substantially higher than the best baseline (MViTac at 47.5%), showcasing superior generalization to varied target conditions.
 - **Noise Robustness:** In "Insertion Noisy" (Gaussian noise added to observations), ViTaS maintained an 89.2% success rate, far exceeding baselines (e.g., MViTac 51.6%), proving its robustness to sensory noise.

**B. Real-World Experiment Results (IL):**
- **Tasks:** Evaluated on 3 challenging real-world tasks using a Galaxea-R1 Humanoid Robot: Dual Arm Clean (DAC), Table Pick Place (TPP-1 for one object type, TPP-3 for three object types), and Fridge Pick Place (FPP).
- **Performance (Table IV):** ViTaS consistently outperformed DP across all real-world tasks, achieving an average success rate of 46.0% compared to DP's 30.0%. This 16% improvement was achieved despite ViTaS using fewer cameras (head camera only) than DP (head + two wrist cameras).
- **Generalization in Real-World:** The smaller performance drop between TPP-1 and TPP-3 for ViTaS compared to DP demonstrated its superior real-world generalization.
- **Occlusion Handling:** Robust performance in tasks like FPP, where robotic arm self-occlusion is common, indicated that tactile sensing effectively compensates for visual limitations, validating the CVAE mechanism's role in handling such scenarios.

**C. Ablation Study (Table V & VI):**
- **Cruciality of Tactile Information:** Removing tactile information (TA column) led to a 32% average performance drop, confirming its critical role. Using a unified encoder for both modalities (U column) performed poorly, demonstrating the necessity of separate encoders to handle inherent discrepancies between visual and tactile data.
- **Contribution of CVAE and Soft Fusion Contrastive:** Ablating either the CVAE (C column) or Soft Fusion Contrastive (S column) module resulted in significant performance drops, highlighting the necessity of both designs for the method's success.
- **Impact of K in Soft Fusion Contrastive:** The optimal K value was found to be 10 (ViTaS's default). K=1 (conventional contrastive learning) showed respectable performance but was inferior to K=10. Too large K (K=20, K=50) also led to performance degradation, suggesting that selecting an appropriate number of "soft" positives is crucial.
- **Soft Fusion vs. Time Contrastive:** Soft fusion contrastive (ViTaS) consistently outperformed a time-contrastive approach (TC column), confirming the hypothesis that explicit similarity-based positive sampling is more effective than relying on temporal proximity alone.
- **Weight Ablation ($\lambda, \mu$):** Varying the weights for $L_{CON}$ and $L_{VAE}$ confirmed that the chosen values ($\lambda=1, \mu=0.1$) were optimal for the challenging Egg Rotate and Block Rotate tasks.

**D. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 6):**
- **Reconstruction Quality:** ViTaS surpassed M3L's token-based MAE in reconstructing critical interaction details (e.g., egg's location) under varying noise levels, maintaining robustness even with high noise.
- **Visuo-Tactile Complementarity:** Applying heavy noise to one modality (e.g., visual) still allowed for better reconstructions than noise on both, visually confirming the complementary nature exploited by ViTaS.
- **Occlusion Compensation:** When core image regions were masked, ViTaS reliably reconstructed observations guided by tactile and masked visual features, visually validating CVAE's role in handling self-occlusion.

In summary, the findings overwhelmingly demonstrate ViTaS's superior performance, generalization, and robustness in a wide array of visuomotor tasks, both simulated and real. The ablation and qualitative studies provide strong evidence for the effectiveness and necessity of its core components, particularly the innovative soft fusion contrastive learning and the CVAE-based integration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViTaS represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic visuomotor learning, particularly for tasks requiring dexterous manipulation and robust perception in complex, real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robotic Manipulation Capabilities:** By effectively fusing visual and tactile information, ViTaS allows robots to perform tasks that are challenging for vision-only systems, such as handling transparent objects, operating in self-occluded scenarios, or precisely interacting with objects that require subtle force feedback. This directly translates to more capable and versatile robots for industrial, service, and even domestic applications.
2. **Addressing Core Multimodal Fusion Challenges:** The paper directly tackles two critical issues in multimodal learning: effectively aligning cross-modal features and leveraging their complementarity. The proposed Soft Fusion Contrastive Learning provides a refined method for alignment, moving beyond simple concatenation. The CVAE-based integration mechanism specifically exploits complementarity, which is crucial for robustness against sensory ambiguities like occlusions or noise. This methodological contribution can inspire further research in multimodal representation learning beyond just vision and touch.
3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The comprehensive experiments demonstrate ViTaS's ability to generalize to novel object shapes, randomized target positions, and noisy sensory inputs. This is a crucial step towards deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured environments where conditions are rarely perfectly controlled. Its superior performance in real-world settings, even with reduced visual input, further underscores its practical viability and robustness.
4. **Inspiration from Human Physiology:** By explicitly drawing inspiration from how humans integrate vision and touch, ViTaS offers a biologically plausible approach to sensory fusion. This connection can foster further interdisciplinary research between cognitive science, neuroscience, and robotics.
5. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Consistently outperforming numerous established baselines in a diverse set of tasks (both RL and IL, simulation and real-world) firmly establishes ViTaS as a new state-of-the-art method in visuo-tactile visuomotor learning. This benchmark sets a new standard for future research in the field.
6. **Practical Deployment Potential:** The successful real-world deployment on a humanoid robot (Galaxea-R1) for tasks like cleaning, table pick-and-place, and fridge manipulation demonstrates ViTaS's readiness for practical application. This is a critical validation, as bridging the sim-to-real gap remains a significant challenge in robotics.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its successes, the authors candidly acknowledge two main limitations:
1. **High Dynamic Accurate Manipulation:** While ViTaS performs well, complex high-dynamic tasks like real-world pen spinning remain challenging due to physical robot capabilities and the inherent bottleneck of RL in high-dimensional continuous control.
2. **Deformable Object Manipulation:** The potential of visuo-tactile sensing for manipulating deformable objects (e.g., cloth, soft foods) warrants further exploration. This area presents unique challenges for both perception and control.

Future work will focus on exploring the capabilities of fused visuo-tactile features in even more complex scenarios, leveraging advanced simulation and real-world platforms to push these boundaries. Overcoming these limitations would significantly broaden the applicability of ViTaS and similar multimodal learning frameworks, paving the way for robots that can truly interact with the world with human-like dexterity and intelligence.
