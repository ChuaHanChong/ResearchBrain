# Continuum Robot Modeling with Action Conditioned Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> An action-conditioned flow matching framework is introduced for quasi-static self-modeling of tendon-driven continuum robots (TDCRs), predicting full 3D geometry from actuation states using dense point clouds. This data-driven approach achieved significantly lower geometric errors, with up to 96% reduction in Chamfer Distance in simulation and 33.5% reduction on physical robots compared to baselines, accurately capturing complex deformations.

## Problem

- Accurately modeling the continuous deformation of tendon-driven continuum robots (TDCRs) is challenging due to their compliance, complex tendon routing, friction, hysteresis, and fabrication variations.
- Existing classical kinematic models lack the detail and physical realism needed for complex deformations, while mechanics-based models require extensive and difficult parameter calibration.
- Many robotic tasks require dense, full-body 3D geometry, which sparse state estimations or implicit representations often fail to provide adequately.

## Method

- A data-driven framework utilizes **action-conditioned flow matching** to learn the mapping from robot actuation commands to its settled 3D geometry.
- Robot shape is represented by dense, ==permutation-invariant 3D point clouds==, collected via a multi-camera RGB-D setup for physical robots or simulated in MuJoCo.
- A neural network (either an MLP or a ==Hybrid PVConv== architecture) learns a ==conditional velocity field== that transports points from a simple Gaussian prior to the target robot shape distribution.

## Results

- In simulated environments, the proposed Hybrid velocity network achieved a **64-96%** reduction in Chamfer Distance and a **50-83%** reduction in Earth Mover's Distance compared to the best baselines.
- On real 2- and 3-module TDCRs, the method reduced Chamfer Distance by **20-33.5%** and Earth Mover's Distance by **24-36.9%** compared to the next best baseline (PointFlow).
- Qualitatively, the method produced visually cleaner and more accurate full-body reconstructions, especially near the distal tip, outperforming baselines which often generated diffuse point clouds or struggled with thin structures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-conditioned flow matching effectively learns complex, high-dimensional, and continuous mappings from robot actuation to dense 3D shapes, overcoming limitations of traditional models.
> - Representing robot shape as dense point clouds allows for direct supervision from RGB-D sensors and captures intricate deformations better than sparse or implicit representations.
> - The generative nature of flow matching, by learning a conditional velocity field, yields more robust and realistic shape generation compared to direct regression or other generative baselines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Continuum robots, particularly tendon-driven continuum robots (TDCRs), are a class of manipulators known for their high dexterity and inherent compliance, making them suitable for applications in environments that are confined, cluttered, or unstructured. However, accurately modeling the shape of TDCRs presents challenges due to their continuous deformation, complex tendon routing, compliance characteristics, internal friction, hysteresis, variations in fabrication, and the influence of external loading.

Existing approaches to TDCR modeling can be categorized into several areas:

- **Classical Kinematic Models:** Methods such as constant curvature (CC) and piecewise constant curvature (PCC) models offer compact and interpretable descriptions of continuum robot shape. However, these models typically do not account for material stress, tendon forces, friction, or external loads, and may miss detailed shape variations critical for specific tasks.
- **Mechanics-Based Formulations:** More physically-grounded approaches, including Cosserat rod models, aim to model distributed deformation, tendon routing, actuation, and external loads explicitly. While expressive, these models often require precise parameter identification and can be computationally expensive or difficult to calibrate accurately for specific physical platforms.
- **Shape Sensing and Learning-Based State Estimation:** Techniques like fiber Bragg grating (FBG) sensors provide embedded sensing for curvature or shape. External sensing methods, such as cameras and RGB-D sensors, offer non-invasive observation but are subject to depth accuracy, calibration errors, and segmentation quality. Learning-based visual methods have been used for control and inverse kinematics, but often target low-dimensional state estimation or image-space objectives rather than full 3D geometry prediction.
- **Robot Self-Modeling and Neural 3D Reconstruction:** Recent developments in visual self-modeling (e.g., VSM, NeRF-inspired methods, 3D Gaussian Splatting) aim to learn internal representations of robot morphology from sensory observations. These methods are powerful for appearance modeling and novel view rendering, but their primary objectives are not always aligned with predicting full 3D body geometry directly from an actuation state. They may also struggle with thin structures or require specific input modalities like silhouettes or 2D masks.
- **Point Cloud Generative Modeling:** Advances in deep learning for 3D data have led to generative models for point clouds, such as continuous normalizing flows (e.g., PointFlow) and diffusion/score-based models. These methods can generate high-quality 3D representations but have not been extensively applied to the specific problem of action-conditioned shape prediction for continuously deformable robots like TDCRs.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing the limitations of existing approaches. It focuses on developing a data-driven model that learns the mapping from robot actuation to its dense, settled 3D geometry, overcoming the calibration difficulties of mechanics-based models and providing more comprehensive shape information than classical kinematic models. By utilizing action-conditioned flow matching with a dense point cloud representation, it aims to achieve higher accuracy in shape prediction compared to existing robot self-modeling methods that may prioritize rendering or implicit representations, while also directly addressing the continuous and high-dimensional nature of TDCR deformation. The use of a generative flow matching model for point clouds is distinct from direct regression approaches, providing a method to transport a simple prior to a complex robot shape distribution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a data-driven framework for action-conditioned quasi-static self-modeling of tendon-driven continuum robots (TDCRs). Specifically, the goal is to predict the robot’s settled 3D geometry (shape) from its actuation state (motor commands) after the robot has reached a steady configuration.

The motivations driving this objective are multifaceted:

- **Challenges in Traditional TDCR Modeling:** Traditional analytical models for TDCRs face significant difficulties. Classical kinematic models, such as constant curvature (CC) or piecewise constant curvature (PCC) approximations, offer simplicity but may not accurately capture the complex, continuous deformation, especially in multi-module robots or under varying conditions. More sophisticated mechanics-based models, like Cosserat rod models, can represent distributed deformation and external loads but require extensive and often complex calibration to identify material parameters, tendon forces, and friction characteristics specific to a physical robot. Furthermore, fabrication variability (e.g., in 3D printed components) can introduce discrepancies that are difficult to account for with purely analytical models.
- **Need for Dense, Full-Body Shape Information:** Many robotic tasks, including path planning, collision avoidance, and visual servoing, require detailed and accurate full-body 3D geometry of the robot. Predicting only a sparse set of backbone states or a limited number of geometric parameters often proves insufficient for these applications, potentially leading to inaccurate planning or collisions. A dense representation, such as a point cloud, can capture the intricate bending and twisting of the entire robot body without imposing pre-defined geometric constraints.
- **Data-Driven Solution for Complex Mappings:** The intricate interplay of tendon routing, backbone compliance, friction, hysteresis, and manufacturing imperfections makes the relationship between motor actuation and resulting robot shape highly nonlinear and difficult to model analytically. A data-driven approach allows the system to learn this complex input-output mapping directly from observations of the physical robot's behavior, thereby absorbing these difficult-to-model effects.
- **Focus on Quasi-Static Behavior:** The research specifically targets the quasi-static settled shape rather than transient deformation trajectories or underlying physical parameters like material stress or tendon forces. This focus simplifies the problem significantly while still providing the most relevant information for steady-state applications, where the robot has reached its final configuration after an actuation command.
- **Leveraging RGB-D Sensing:** The use of dense point clouds as the shape representation is motivated by its direct compatibility with RGB-D camera systems, which are increasingly common for real-world robotic perception. This allows for direct supervision of the learned model with high-fidelity 3D observational data.
- **Generative Modeling for Robustness:** Instead of directly regressing point coordinates, the use of an action-conditioned flow matching model is motivated by its ability to learn a conditional velocity field. This approach aims to transport samples from a simple prior distribution to the complex distribution of observed robot shapes for a given actuation state, which can lead to more robust and realistic shape generation compared to deterministic regression models that might struggle with the multi-modality or complexity of the output space.

A secondary objective, explored as an extension in simulation, is to demonstrate the framework's ability to incorporate additional quasi-static state variables, such as scalar tip payload magnitude, into the conditioning input. This aims to show the potential for predicting robot shape under varying external conditions beyond just actuation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on an action-conditioned flow matching framework for learning a quasi-static self-model of Tendon Driven Continuum Robots (TDCRs). The approach involves custom hardware, a multi-camera data collection pipeline, and a generative neural network model trained to predict dense 3D point clouds.

**Problem Definition:**
The problem is defined as learning a mapping from an actuation command (condition vector `c`) to the robot’s settled 3D geometry, represented as a permutation-invariant point cloud `X1` (N points, d channels – XYZ or XYZ+RGB). The condition vector `c` primarily consists of normalized motor actuation states (`m`). For an extended application, `c` can be augmented to include normalized scalar tip payload magnitudes (`p`).

**TDCR Hardware Platform and RGB-D Observation:**
A lightweight, configurable TDCR platform was developed using 3D printing. This platform was instantiated as 2- and 3-module physical robots for real-world experiments. Each module is actuated by three tendons positioned at 120° intervals around a PLA structural backbone. The design features specific spring geometries and ball joint interfaces for tuneable compliance, with tendon routing that assigns tendons to specific modules. DYNAMIXEL servos were used for actuation.

For data collection, four calibrated Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D cameras were arranged around the robot. For each sampled quasi-static configuration (i.e., after a motor command and subsequent settling), synchronized RGB-D views were captured. These views were then back-projected using camera intrinsics, transformed into a shared world coordinate frame using calibrated extrinsics, and fused into a single colored point cloud. These raw point clouds were then voxel downsampled and either randomly sampled or padded to a fixed count of `N_train = 20,000` points for training. Point clouds were globally scaled to a normalized space during training, with metric scale recovered during inference.

**Action Conditioned Flow Matching in Point Space:**
The core of the modeling approach is learning a conditional velocity field `u_θ` that transports points from a simple prior distribution `p0` to the target distribution of robot shapes associated with a specific condition `c`.
1. **Prior Distribution:** A Gaussian point cloud `X0` is sampled from `p0 = N(0, σ^2*I)`. This `X0` has the same `N × d` shape as the ground truth data sample `X1`.
2. **Interpolation Path:** For a randomly sampled flow time `t` between 0 and 1, a straight interpolation path `X_t = (1 - t)X0 + tX1` is constructed between the prior `X0` and the ground truth `X1`.
3. **Target Velocity:** The target velocity field `u_t` for this path is defined as `X1 - X0`, representing the direct displacement vector from the prior point cloud to the ground truth point cloud.
4. **Learning Objective:** A neural network `u_θ(X_t, t | c)` parameterizes the conditional velocity field. The model is trained to minimize the `L_FM` loss, which is the expected Frobenius norm squared between the predicted velocity `u_θ` and the target velocity `u_t`:
 `L_FM = E_[t,X0,X1,c] ||u_θ(X_t, t | c) - (X1 - X0)||^2_F`.
 For colored point clouds, the loss includes separate terms for XYZ coordinates (`L_xyz`) and RGB colors (`L_rgb`), with a weighting factor `λ_rgb = 0.05`. Flow time `t` is sampled from a Beta distribution (`Beta(α, 1)` with `α=3.0`) to emphasize later interpolation states closer to the data distribution.
5. **Inference (Shape Prediction):** To predict a robot shape for a new query condition `ˆc`, an initial point cloud `ˆX0` is sampled from the prior `p0`. The learned velocity field `u_θ` is then integrated from `t=0` to `t=1` using an ODE solver (Heun's method, RK2, with `S=100` steps) to generate the final predicted point cloud `ˆX_⋆`. The integration variable `t` is a generative transport parameter and does not correspond to the physical deformation time of the robot.

**Condition Injection and Velocity Network Architectures:**
To integrate the condition `c` and flow time `t` into the neural network, sinusoidal features embed `t`, and a linear layer processes `c`. These embeddings are additively fused to form a global embedding `e(t, c)`. This embedding is then injected into the neural network's intermediate features using FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) layers, which apply affine transformations based on `e` to normalize and scale features.

Two permutation-equivariant velocity network architectures for `u_θ` were evaluated:
1. **MLP Velocity Network:** A lightweight architecture where each point `x_i` is processed independently. Its coordinates/features are concatenated with the global embedding `e(t, c)` and passed through a shared residual MLP block with FiLM modulation to predict the per-point velocity. This architecture has O(N) complexity.
2. **Hybrid PVConv Velocity Network:** This architecture extends the MLP by incorporating a multi-scale point-voxel convolutional (PVConv) pyramid. This pyramid extracts local geometric context features (`C_pv`) from the point cloud. These context features are then concatenated with each point's features and the global embedding before being processed by the FiLM MLP head. A time-gated blend (sigmoid function `α(t)`) is used to smoothly transition from global conditioning to incorporating local geometric context as `t` increases, acknowledging that the initial state `X0` is unstructured noise.

**Datasets and Evaluation:**
- **Simulation Datasets:** A family of MuJoCo simulators were developed for 2-, 3-, and 5-module TDCRs, modeled as chains of rigid disks with ball joints and spatial tendons. Datasets included variants "with base" (WB) and "no base" (NB) to analyze the effect of rigid scene geometry. Data sizes ranged from 5,000 to 10,000 configurations with an 80/10/10 train/val/test split.
- **Payload Conditioned Simulation:** An extension where the condition `c` included a scalar tip payload mass `p` applied gravitationally.
- **Real Hardware Datasets:** Collected from the physical 2- and 3-module TDCRs using the described multi-camera setup.
- **Metrics:** Squared L2 Chamfer Distance (CD) and Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) were used to quantify geometric error between predicted and ground truth point clouds. Metrics were computed on `N_eval = 10,000` points sampled uniformly after denormalizing to metric scale.
- **Baselines:** The method was compared against Visual Self-Modeling (VSM), conditional continuous normalizing flows (PointFlow), NeRF-inspired self-simulation (FFKSM), and articulated 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). Foreground masks for view-based baselines were obtained from MuJoCo (simulation) or SAM 2 (real hardware).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results comparing the proposed action-conditioned flow matching framework against several baselines in both simulated and real-world TDCR environments.

**Quantitative Results (Simulation):**
Table I details the performance of the models on simulated 2-, 3-, and 5-module TDCRs, both with and without the rigid base in the observations.
- The proposed action-conditioned flow matching method, particularly the Hybrid velocity network variant, consistently achieved the lowest Chamfer Distance (CD) and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) across all six simulation settings.
- Relative to the strongest non-ours baseline in each setting (typically PointFlow or NeRF), the Hybrid model demonstrated significant reductions in error: approximately 64% to 96% reduction in CD and 50% to 83% reduction in EMD.
- Among the baselines, PointFlow generally performed better than VSM, NeRF, and 3DGS, especially on datasets without the rigid base. However, it was still substantially outperformed by the proposed method.
- 3DGS showed inconsistent performance, sometimes struggling significantly in more complex scenarios (e.g., 5m-WB).

**Quantitative Results (Real Hardware):**
Table II presents the results from experiments on physical 2-module and 3-module TDCRs.
- On real hardware, the proposed method again yielded lower CD and EMD values compared to all baselines.
- The best performing velocity network (Hybrid for 2m, MLP for 3m) reduced CD by 33.5% (Real-2m) and 20.3% (Real-3m) relative to PointFlow. EMD reductions were 36.9% (Real-2m) and 24.6% (Real-3m).
- View-based baselines (NeRF, 3DGS) exhibited lower accuracy on real data compared to their simulation performance, potentially due to challenges with mask quality and the accurate reconstruction of thin, deformable robot bodies in real-world conditions.

**Qualitative Results:**
Figure 5 and 7 illustrate representative predictions.
- The proposed method, especially with the Hybrid velocity network, produced visually cleaner and more accurate reconstructions of the robot's full body geometry, including the distal tip.
- PointFlow, while capturing global pose, sometimes generated diffuse point clouds, particularly near the robot's end effector.
- View-based baselines like NeRF (FFKSM) and 3DGS showed sensitivity to mask quality and often struggled with the accurate representation of thin continuum bodies, degrading as the number of modules and complexity increased. For instance, 3DGS sometimes reconstructed the rigid base more reliably than the deformable robot body itself.

**Payload Conditioned Simulation Extension:**
Table III and Figure 6 demonstrate the capability of the framework to incorporate additional quasi-static state variables.
- When the condition vector was augmented with a scalar tip payload mass, the model successfully predicted the loaded steady-state shapes for 2-, 3-, and 5-module TDCRs.
- Both MLP and Hybrid velocity network variants maintained predictive accuracy under payload conditioning.
- Qualitative overlays in Figure 6 showed that the model accurately captured the load-induced deflection of the robot's tip.
- In this specific extension, the Hybrid model did not uniformly outperform the MLP, indicating context-dependent performance for the architectural choices.

**Computational Efficiency:**
The proposed Hybrid model also demonstrated improved computational efficiency, with substantially faster training times on simulated datasets compared to PointFlow (e.g., 8.8 hours versus 21.5 hours for the 2-module with base dataset).

**Discussion Takeaways:**
The findings indicate that action-conditioned flow matching is effective for learning accurate quasi-static TDCR self-models within a fixed morphology and actuation range. The dense point cloud representation aligns well with RGB-D observation, and the payload extension highlights the framework's extensibility. The generative nature of flow matching appears to yield better performance than comparable point-based and view-based methods, which struggled with output diffuseness or sensitivity to input mask quality and thin geometries.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for action-conditioned quasi-static self-modeling of tendon-driven continuum robots (TDCRs) that has several implications for the field of robotics.

**Advancement in TDCR Modeling Accuracy:** The primary significance lies in providing a data-driven method that achieves improved accuracy in predicting the dense 3D geometry of TDCRs from actuation states. This addresses limitations inherent in classical kinematic models (which often lack detail or physical realism) and mechanics-based models (which demand extensive and challenging parameter calibration). By learning directly from observations, the model can account for complex real-world phenomena such as friction, hysteresis, and fabrication variabilities that are difficult to model analytically.

**Enabling Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Performance:** Accurate and detailed 3D shape prediction is fundamental for numerous robotic applications.
- **Improved Planning and Control:** A reliable forward model of robot shape can enable more precise collision detection, path planning in cluttered environments, and advanced control strategies that depend on accurate knowledge of the robot's configuration.
- **Adaptation to Morphology:** The data-driven nature of the framework facilitates adapting the model to specific physical robot instances or new designs without requiring re-derivation of physics equations or complex analytical parameter adjustments, thereby supporting rapid prototyping and deployment of new TDCRs.

**Contribution to Generative Modeling in Robotics:** The work demonstrates the effective application of flow matching, a modern continuous-time generative modeling technique, to the domain of complex robotic body modeling, specifically for continuously deformable structures. This suggests a valuable approach for handling high-dimensional and continuous robot state spaces, moving beyond simpler regression or classification tasks. The use of a dense point cloud representation ensures that the full geometric detail of the robot's deformation is captured and predicted, directly complementing real-world RGB-D sensing capabilities.

**Extensibility to Complex Conditions:** The successful demonstration of a payload-conditioned extension in simulation indicates the framework's potential to incorporate other quasi-static environmental or interaction variables. This capability is significant for developing more comprehensive robot models that can predict shape under varying external loads or environmental interactions, paving the way for more robust and versatile robot operation.

**Foundation for Future Research:** The authors position this work as a foundation for several future research directions. These include conducting real-world payload experiments, developing models that are aware of historical states and contact interactions, exploring morphology-conditioned transfer learning across different robot designs, and integrating these accurate self-models into closed-loop planning and control systems. This suggests the work can serve as a building block for addressing more advanced challenges in continuum robotics and autonomous systems.

In summary, this research contributes a robust and accurate data-driven method for modeling TDCR shape, which can enhance the capabilities of continuum robots in complex tasks and environments, while also demonstrating the applicability of advanced generative modeling techniques to challenging problems in robotic self-perception.
