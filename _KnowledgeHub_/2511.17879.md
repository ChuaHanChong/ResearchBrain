# Generative Adversarial Post-Training Mitigates Reward Hacking in Live Human-AI Music Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mila, MIT, and collaborating institutions developed Generative Adversarial Post-Training (GAPT) to counteract reward hacking in reinforcement learning for real-time human-AI music interaction. GAPT recovered output diversity while maintaining harmonic coherence, leading to higher perceived adaptation quality and control in a user study with expert musicians.

## Problem

- Generative AI models fine-tuned with reinforcement learning (RL) often suffer from "reward hacking," producing repetitive or unintelligible outputs despite high reward scores.
- RL post-training typically leads to "diversity collapse," where models converge to a narrow set of outputs, hindering creative expression in generative sequence models.
- Existing RL post-training methods and supervised learning struggle with real-time, co-adaptive human-AI interactions like live music jamming, due to exposure bias and a lack of dynamic adaptation.

## Method

- Introduces ==Generative Adversarial Post-Training== (**GAPT**), augmenting RL post-training with a Transformer encoder discriminator that distinguishes policy-generated chord trajectories from real data.
- Incorporates an adversarial reward, derived from the discriminator's output, into the policy's reward function, which encourages data-like outputs and complements task-specific coherence rewards and rule-based penalties.
- Employs a two-phase adaptive update schedule for the discriminator, including a confidence-gated phase, to ensure training stability and prevent it from overpowering the policy.

## Results

- GAPT demonstrated higher output diversity (e.g., Vendi Score of 26.645 vs. ReaLchords' 20.968) while maintaining strong harmonic coherence in fixed-melody simulations and model-model co-adaptation.
- In a user study with 12 expert musicians, GAPT received the highest mean ratings across adaptation quality, adaptation speed, and perceived control/agency, significantly exceeding baselines in adaptation speed and control (p < **0.05**).
- The method consistently performed robustly across fixed-melody simulations, model-model co-adaptation, and live human interaction, generalizing to unseen data and handling input perturbations effectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial training acts as an effective regularizer in RL post-training, successfully mitigating reward hacking and restoring output diversity without compromising task-specific coherence.
> - The combination of coherence-based rewards, rule-based penalties, and adversarial rewards creates complementary learning pressures that guide the policy towards diverse, well-formed, and realistic outputs.
> - Integrating a lightweight adversarial component into RL fine-tuning can significantly enhance user experience in real-time interactive AI systems by improving perceived adaptation speed, control, and agency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges within the field of generative artificial intelligence, particularly concerning real-time human-AI interaction and the limitations of reinforcement learning (RL) post-training for generative sequence models.

Modern generative AI, powered by large-scale transformer models and RL post-training (e.g., Large Language Models), has achieved widespread adoption. However, current applications often involve sequential, non-real-time interactions where user input is followed by a potentially delayed response. This paradigm is insufficient for dynamic, collaborative tasks such as live music jamming, which demand real-time coordination, adaptation to unforeseen inputs, and creative diversity.

Reinforcement learning post-training is recognized for its ability to enable models to adapt through on-policy interaction, mitigating "exposure bias" encountered by purely supervised models. However, a significant drawback of RL post-training is its susceptibility to "reward hacking." This phenomenon occurs when a policy exploits weaknesses in a learned reward signal, leading to the generation of trivial, repetitive, or otherwise undesirable outputs that score highly on the reward function but lack utility or realism. In the context of music, reward hacking manifests as harmonically coherent but simplistic, non-varying accompaniment that diminishes musical creativity and user engagement. Existing mitigation strategies for reward hacking often involve Kullback-Leibler (KL) constraints, reward shaping, or elastic resets, but these have shown limitations in preserving diversity in complex generative tasks.

This paper positions itself by tackling reward hacking specifically within the demanding domain of real-time human-AI music accompaniment. It builds upon previous research in real-time music systems, which have evolved from non-neural, rule-based or score-following methods to deep learning approaches utilizing LSTMs and Transformers. Recent efforts, such as RL-Duet and ReaLchords, have incorporated RL post-training to enhance adaptability in music generation. However, these RL-tuned models, while improving adaptation, have demonstrated a tendency towards diversity collapse due to reward hacking.

The current work reintroduces and adapts principles from generative adversarial learning, which was prominent in generative models (images, audio, text) from 2014-2020 but has seen reduced application in the recent post-LLM era. Specifically, it draws inspiration from Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) to apply adversarial training as a policy regularization mechanism *during* RL post-training. By doing so, it aims to preserve the benefits of RL-driven adaptation while explicitly mitigating diversity collapse, a problem that traditional KL constraints have not fully resolved in this interactive music setting. This represents an extension of adversarial training into the domain of RL post-training for generative sequence models, demonstrating its renewed utility for complex, real-world interactive tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and evaluate a novel adversarial training method designed to mitigate reward hacking in reinforcement learning post-training of generative sequence models, specifically applied to the challenging context of live human-AI music interaction.

The motivation for this work arises from several key observations and challenges:

- **Mismatch between Current Generative AI and Real-time Interaction:** Most prevalent applications of generative AI (e.g., LLMs) are designed for sequential, asynchronous interactions. In contrast, live music jamming is a highly collaborative activity that necessitates real-time coordination, immediate adaptation to a partner's moves without foreknowledge of their future actions, and the continuous generation of diverse outputs to maintain creative flow and emotional connection.
- **Reward Hacking in RL Post-training:** While RL post-training enables models to adapt effectively through on-policy interaction, it frequently suffers from "reward hacking." This occurs when the policy learns to exploit the reward function, leading to outputs that score highly but are trivial, repetitive, or lack desired complexity and diversity. In music accompaniment, this manifests as harmonically coherent but monotonous progressions, diminishing the user's experience and perceived agency.
- **Limitations of Existing Mitigation Strategies:** Common methods to mitigate reward hacking, such as KL constraints, have proven insufficient to prevent diversity collapse in the online music accompaniment setting. This highlights a need for more robust mechanisms to ensure realism and diversity alongside task coherence.
- **The Demands of Live Music Jamming:** A model designed for live music jamming must not only produce harmonically coherent accompaniment but also maintain dynamic variation, respond quickly to changes, and recover from errors. Repetitive outputs, even if harmonically correct, hinder creative expression and user engagement.
- **Need for Robust Adaptation:** Supervised maximum likelihood training often results in models that fail during deployment because curated datasets lack the errors, corrective maneuvers, and co-adaptive behaviors typical of live interaction. RL post-training is crucial for learning these adaptive skills, but only if the inherent diversity problem can be addressed.

In response to these motivations, the research aims to:

- Develop Generative Adversarial Post-Training (GAPT) as a mechanism to augment existing RL post-training pipelines.
- Integrate a co-evolving discriminator that distinguishes policy-generated musical trajectories from real data.
- Utilize the discriminator's realism estimation as an additional reward signal, alongside coherence-based task rewards, to prevent the policy from collapsing to trivial outputs.
- Introduce a two-phase training schedule for the discriminator to ensure stable and effective learning, balancing its evolution with the policy's optimization.
- Demonstrate that this approach improves output diversity, harmonic coherence, adaptation speed, and user agency in live human-AI music interaction, specifically for melody-to-chord accompaniment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Generative Adversarial Post-Training (GAPT) to enhance reinforcement learning post-training for real-time melody-to-chord accompaniment. The methodology integrates adversarial training into a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework, building upon a pre-trained generative Transformer model.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Pre-training

The core of the system is a decoder-only Transformer, similar to LLaMA-style models, which serves as the chord-generation policy (πθ). This policy is initially pre-trained using maximum-likelihood estimation (MLE) on a dataset of paired melody and chord sequences. This supervised pre-training establishes a foundational ability to generate chords given a melody history. The model is designed to operate online, generating the current chord token (yt) based only on the shared history (x<t, y<t), ensuring real-time applicability.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Post-Training

Following MLE pre-training, the policy undergoes RL post-training using PPO. This phase aims to improve adaptive capabilities by learning from on-policy rollouts. During training, the policy generates chord trajectories (y) in response to sampled melodies (x). The policy is updated by optimizing a KL-regularized objective:
`maxθ E_x∼D, y∼πθ(·|x) [R(x, y) − β D_KL(πθ( · | x ) ∥ φω( · | x )) + γ Σ_t H( πθ( · | x<t, y<t) )]`
where R(x,y) is the combined scalar reward, D_KL is a KL divergence term regularizing the policy towards an offline KL anchor (φω) to prevent excessive drift, and H is an entropy regularization term promoting output diversity.

#### 3.3. Reward Model Components

The total scalar reward R(x,y) for each generated trajectory is an ensemble of three types of rewards:

1. **Self-supervised Coherence Rewards (R_coh):** This component assesses the harmonic and temporal alignment between the generated chords and the input melody. It comprises:
 - **Contrastive Model:** Encodes melody and chord sequences into embeddings, trained with an InfoNCE objective to align true pairs. Cosine similarity between embeddings provides a global harmonic-alignment signal. Rhythm-only variants are included to mitigate pitch-shift bias.
 - **Discriminative Model:** A classifier that takes a (melody, chord) pair and predicts if it's a real pair or a randomly re-paired negative, providing a temporal-coherence signal. Rhythm-only variants are also used here.
 Scores from multiple seeds and variants are normalized and averaged.

2. **Rule-based Penalties (R_rules):** These penalties discourage musically undesirable or structurally invalid outputs:
 - Invalid output penalty for format violations.
 - Silence penalty for excessive silence when the melody is active.
 - Early stop penalty if an end-of-sequence token is emitted prematurely.
 - Repetition penalty for chords repeating more than four consecutive times.

3. **Adversarial Reward (R_adv):** This is the core contribution of GAPT. A discriminator (Dψ), implemented as a Transformer encoder, is trained to distinguish policy-generated chord trajectories (ŷ) from real chord sequences (y) sourced from the dataset. The adversarial reward is defined as `R_adv = − log(1 − Dψ(y))`, which rewards sequences that the discriminator deems "data-like." The discriminator is trained only on the model's outputs (y) to capture an input-agnostic prior. This creates complementary pressure: outputs exploiting coherence rewards with low realism are penalized by R_adv, while realistic but incoherent outputs receive low R_coh.

#### 3.4. Discriminator Training Schedule

To ensure stable co-evolution of the discriminator and policy, a two-phase update schedule with adaptive discriminator updates is employed:

1. **Phase 1 (Warmup):** For the initial 200 steps, the discriminator is updated at a fixed interval (one Dψ update for every five PPO policy updates). This allows Dψ to roughly match the policy's learning speed.
2. **Phase 2 (Adaptive, Confidence-Gated):** After warmup, Dψ is updated only when the moving average of the adversarial reward (¯R_adv) over the most recent three PPO updates exceeds a threshold (τ = 1.0). If ¯R_adv is below this threshold, Dψ remains frozen. This gating mechanism prevents Dψ from becoming too powerful or receiving unstable gradients, which could impede policy optimization. Label smoothing (α = 0.1) is applied to the discriminator's binary cross-entropy targets to reduce overfitting.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Settings and Metrics

The effectiveness of GAPT is evaluated across three interactive settings:

1. **Fixed Melody Simulation:** The policy accompanies held-out melodies online. This evaluates adaptation to static inputs.
2. **Model-Model Interaction:** A learned melody jamming agent (trained with its own RL objective and adversarial reward) co-adapts with the chord policy. This simulates interaction with an adaptive partner.
3. **Real-time User Study:** Expert musicians interact live with the GAPT-trained model (and baselines) deployed in a real-time system. Participants provide Likert-scale ratings and qualitative feedback.

Performance is assessed using:

- **Adaptation Quality (Harmony):** Measured by the note-in-chord ratio, which is the proportion of frames where the melody pitch class is part of the concurrent chord.
- **Diversity:** Quantified by the Vendi Score, which reflects the effective number of distinct patterns in the generated chord sequences. Higher Vendi Scores indicate greater diversity.

#### 3.6. Baselines

The performance of GAPT is compared against:
- **Online MLE:** The policy trained solely with maximum-likelihood estimation.
- **ReaLchords:** A reproduction of a prior RL post-training method using coherence rewards and penalties, but without the entropy term or adversarial reward.
- **GAPT w/o Adv. Training:** An ablation study where the adversarial reward is removed, but all other GAPT components (including entropy regularization) are retained.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evidence demonstrating that Generative Adversarial Post-Training (GAPT) effectively mitigates reward hacking in real-time melody-to-chord accompaniment, improving both output diversity and interaction quality.

#### 4.1. Fixed Melody Simulation

- **Harmony vs. Diversity Trade-off:** Across both the test set and an out-of-distribution Wikifonia dataset, GAPT achieved higher diversity while preserving strong harmonic coherence compared to baselines.
 - **Online MLE** yielded high diversity but low harmony, indicating its failure in harmonic coherence during interactive generation.
 - **ReaLchords** and the **GAPT w/o Adv. Training** ablation achieved strong harmony but at the cost of reduced diversity (e.g., ReaLchords had a Vendi Score of 20.968 on the test set, while GAPT had 26.645). This supports the notion of diversity collapse under coherence-only RL.
 - **GAPT** advanced the Pareto frontier, attaining both higher harmony (e.g., 0.497 note-in-chord ratio on test set vs. 0.484 for ReaLchords) and higher diversity.
- **Visual Representation:** t-SNE projections of generated chord sequences showed that GAPT spanned a broader region of the accompaniment space, indicating more varied outputs compared to the non-adversarial ablation.
- **Adaptation to Perturbation:** GAPT adapted quickly and maintained good harmony when a melody input was transposed mid-sequence, demonstrating robustness to dynamic changes.

#### 4.2. Co-adaptation with Melody Jamming Agent

- In model-to-model interaction, GAPT consistently outperformed ReaLchords and the non-adversarial ablation on both harmony and diversity metrics (e.g., GAPT had a Vendi Score of 12.914 compared to ReaLchords' 7.480, and similar harmony scores). This indicates that the adversarial reward effectively regulates diversity during mutual adaptation.
- Online MLE exhibited strong performance when paired with a melody agent specifically trained to accommodate it, suggesting that its limitations become apparent when faced with less predictable or "out-of-distribution" partners.

#### 4.3. Real-time Interactive System and User Study

- **Participant Ratings:** GAPT received the highest mean ratings from 12 expert musicians across all three Likert-scale questions: "Adaptation quality," "Adaptation speed," and "Control and Agency."
 - GAPT significantly improved "Adaptation speed" and "Control and Agency" compared to ReaLchords (p < 0.05).
- **Harmony and Diversity in Live Sessions:** Analysis of human interaction trajectories confirmed GAPT's superior balance, positioning it on the empirical Pareto frontier of harmony and diversity, consistent with simulation results.
- **Qualitative Feedback:** Musicians' comments reinforced the quantitative findings:
 - GAPT was perceived as "listening," "catching key and chord changes faster," and "adapting faster."
 - ReaLchords was described as "dumb," "boring," and repetitive ("keeps giving me the same two chords").
 - Online MLE was noted for "sometimes tried novel progressions" but also "took some time to adapt" and had "chords not really matching."
 These qualitative insights underscore that the increased diversity from GAPT leads to a more responsive, collaborative, and engaging user experience.

#### 4.4. Ablation Experiments

- **Reward Weighting:** Varying the coefficients of coherence, rule-based, and adversarial rewards showed that the equal weighting (α=β=γ=1) used in GAPT provided a balanced performance. Overemphasizing rules or adversarial signals could degrade harmony or diversity. Critically, excluding rule-based rewards led to severe reward hacking and degenerate outputs, highlighting their essential role, particularly the invalid sequence penalty.
- **RL Objective:** The benefits of adversarial training (diversity restoration and harmony maintenance) were observed even when using an alternative RL objective like GRPO instead of PPO, indicating the robustness of the GAPT approach.
- **Discriminator Input:** A discriminator conditioned *only* on the chord trajectory (Dψ(y)) performed better than one conditioned on both melody and chord (Dψ(x,y)). This suggests that an input-agnostic discriminator is more effective for generalization, likely by preventing memorization of specific melody-chord pairs and encouraging a broader understanding of natural chord progressions.

#### 4.5. Additional Findings

- **Long Sequences:** GAPT and baselines maintained stable note-in-chord ratios on long sequences (T=512) when using a sliding window inference strategy, demonstrating sustained harmonic coherence beyond the training horizon.
- **Chord Complexity:** The average number of pitch classes in generated chords was consistent across all models and the ground truth, indicating that models did not exploit unusually dense chords to artificially inflate harmony metrics.

In summary, GAPT successfully mitigates reward hacking, specifically diversity collapse, in RL post-trained generative sequence models for live music accompaniment. It achieves this by combining coherence-based rewards and rule-based penalties with a dynamically updated adversarial reward. This results in superior harmonic adaptation, restored output diversity, and enhanced user perception of adaptability and agency in interactive settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the development of adaptive generative AI, particularly for real-time human-computer interaction in creative domains. Its primary significance lies in addressing the pervasive problem of "reward hacking" that often plagues reinforcement learning post-training of generative sequence models. By effectively mitigating diversity collapse, GAPT enables RL post-training to realize its full potential for fostering dynamic, creative, and engaging interactions.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Enhanced Human-AI Collaboration in Creative Arts:** The demonstrated ability of GAPT to maintain both harmonic coherence and output diversity in live music accompaniment facilitates more natural and creatively satisfying human-AI jamming experiences. This could extend beyond music to other collaborative creative domains like storytelling, visual art generation, or real-time design, where diversity and responsiveness are paramount for user engagement and creative exploration.
- **Robustness of RL Post-training:** The methodology provides a practical and effective solution to a fundamental limitation of RL post-training. By incorporating a lightweight adversarial training mechanism, GAPT offers a generalizable approach to regularize generative models, steering them towards realistic and varied outputs while still optimizing for task-specific objectives. This could make RL post-training more reliable for a wider array of generative applications that require both high performance and output quality.
- **Improved User Experience and Agency:** The user study results directly demonstrate that GAPT leads to higher perceived adaptation speed and a greater sense of control and agency for expert musicians. This is critical for fostering trust and engagement in human-AI systems. When users feel heard and responded to creatively, they are more likely to adopt and benefit from AI tools.
- **Foundation for Future Interactive AI Systems:** The paper establishes a robust framework for training generative agents in interactive environments. The insights from the two-phase adaptive discriminator update and the importance of input-agnostic adversarial regularization could inform the design of future learning algorithms for complex multi-agent systems and personalized AI experiences.
- **Practical Resources for the Research Community:** The release of training datasets, model checkpoints, and an RL training infrastructure for Transformer-based music agents provides valuable resources. This will enable other researchers to build upon this work, replicate findings, and explore new directions in human-AI music interaction and generative modeling.

In conclusion, GAPT offers a critical step forward for generative AI by enabling models to be not just proficient, but also creative and adaptive in real-time. This has implications for transforming how humans interact with AI in dynamic, collaborative, and artistically rich contexts, pushing the boundaries of what AI can achieve in partnership with human creativity.
