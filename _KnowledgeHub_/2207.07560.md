# Skill-based Model-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Skill-based Model-based Reinforcement Learning (SkiMo) integrates abstract skill learning and a skill dynamics model trained from offline data to enable efficient and accurate long-horizon planning. The framework consistently solved complex tasks, achieving over 3 sub-tasks in Kitchen manipulation with approximately 5 times fewer interactions than skill-based model-free methods, and accurately predicted trajectories over 500 timesteps.

## Problem

- Traditional Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) struggles with long-term tasks due to accumulating prediction errors in single-step dynamics models and computationally expensive planning over vast action spaces.
- Skill-Based Reinforcement Learning (SBRL), while addressing long horizons, remains sample-inefficient, requiring excessive environment interactions to learn complex behaviors.
- Existing hybrid approaches combining MBRL and SBRL often fail to fully resolve these issues, either by still relying on single-step models or being constrained by fixed skill sets.

## Method

- Develops **SkiMo**, a framework that jointly learns a reusable ==skill repertoire== (low-level policy) and a ==skill dynamics model== from task-agnostic offline data.
- The ==skill dynamics model== directly predicts the H-step outcome of executing an entire skill, reducing prediction error accumulation compared to conventional single-step dynamics models.
- Employs ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== with the ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)== for efficient long-horizon planning in the abstract skill space, leveraging imaginary rollouts from the skill dynamics model.

## Results

- SkiMo consistently solved challenging long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards, such as Maze Navigation and Kitchen Manipulation, significantly outperforming model-based and skill-based baselines.
- The framework achieved notably higher sample efficiency, completing over **3 sub-tasks** in Kitchen with approximately **5x** fewer environment interactions compared to the skill-based model-free method SPiRL.
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated the skill dynamics model accurately predicted ground truth trajectories over **500 timesteps**, showcasing improved long-term forecasting accuracy compared to flat single-step dynamics models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying temporal abstraction to dynamics models, through a skill dynamics model that predicts skill outcomes, substantially enhances long-term prediction accuracy by mitigating error compounding.
> - Jointly training abstract skills and their corresponding skill dynamics model from diverse offline data is crucial for creating a robust, adaptable latent skill space and state embedding.
> - High-level planning in the skill space using a learned skill dynamics model enables significantly more sample-efficient and robust policy learning for complex, sparse-reward tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents are capable of learning complex behaviors, but often require extensive interaction with their environment, particularly for tasks that extend over long time horizons. Two primary paradigms address these challenges: Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and Skill-Based Reinforcement Learning (SBRL).

**Model-Based RL** methods aim to improve sample efficiency by learning a dynamics model of the environment. This model predicts the next state given the current state and an action, allowing the agent to simulate "imaginary" trajectories and plan actions without physical interaction. While this approach can significantly reduce the number of real-world interactions, its effectiveness is often limited in long-horizon tasks. This limitation stems from two main issues: the accumulation of prediction errors over many steps, leading to inaccurate long-term forecasts, and the computational expense associated with searching through a vast action space for extended sequences. Existing MBRL methods typically rely on flat, single-step dynamics models, which predict only the immediate next state.

**Skill-Based RL (also known as Hierarchical RL)** addresses long-horizon tasks by introducing temporal abstraction. Instead of planning with primitive actions, agents learn and execute multi-action subroutines, or "skills." These temporally extended actions reduce the effective horizon of the task, simplify policy optimization, and enable more directed exploration. However, SBRL methods can still be sample-inefficient, requiring millions to billions of environmental interactions to learn complex behaviors, which restricts their applicability in real-world scenarios.

Previous attempts to combine MBRL and SBRL have been made. Some approaches integrate skill learning with single-step dynamics models, which do not fully mitigate the error accumulation problem inherent in long-term predictions. Others propose learning temporally extended dynamics models but often condition these on low-level actions rather than abstract skills, or are limited to a fixed, manually defined set of skills.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing a novel framework, Skill-based Model-based RL (SkiMo), that integrates the sample efficiency benefits of model-based methods with the long-horizon capabilities of skill-based approaches. It addresses the limitations of prior work by learning a *skill dynamics model* that directly predicts the outcome of executing a skill, rather than modeling every intermediate step with primitive actions. This approach aims to reduce error accumulation and improve long-term planning, thereby extending the practical horizon of model-based RL while enhancing the sample efficiency of skill-based RL. The work uniquely focuses on the joint learning of a skill repertoire and its corresponding skill dynamics model from task-agnostic offline data, a distinction from methods that learn these components separately.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reinforcement learning framework that can efficiently learn and execute complex, long-horizon tasks, particularly those with sparse rewards. The authors identify specific limitations in existing RL paradigms that motivate their proposed approach:

1. **Addressing Inaccurate Long-Term Prediction in Model-Based RL:** Traditional model-based RL approaches learn a "flat" single-step dynamics model, predicting only the immediate next state. While effective for short horizons, this leads to a compounding of prediction errors over longer sequences of actions. As the imagined trajectory extends, the predicted states increasingly diverge from reality, rendering long-term planning unreliable. The motivation is to overcome this by introducing a mechanism that can predict outcomes over a longer temporal scale more accurately.

2. **Mitigating Computationally Expensive Search in Model-Based RL:** Planning every primitive action for long-horizon tasks using a single-step dynamics model is computationally intensive. The search space for optimal action sequences grows exponentially with the planning horizon, making it impractical for tasks requiring thousands of steps. The goal is to reduce this search complexity by abstracting actions.

3. **Improving Sample Efficiency in Skill-Based RL:** While skill-based RL enables agents to tackle long-horizon tasks by reducing the effective horizon and allowing directed exploration, it often still requires a substantial number of environment interactions (millions to billions) to learn effectively. This high sample complexity limits its application in real-world scenarios where data collection is costly or time-consuming. The motivation is to enhance the sample efficiency of skill-based approaches.

4. **Leveraging Human Intuition for Abstract Planning:** The authors draw inspiration from human cognition, noting that humans plan complex tasks (e.g., cooking) by considering high-level skills (e.g., washing vegetables) rather than individual muscle movements. This abstract planning ability scales intelligence to long-horizon tasks by reducing the complexity of the internal model and search space. The objective is to translate this intuition into an artificial intelligence framework.

5. **Developing a Synergistic Combination of Model-Based and Skill-Based RL:** The core motivation is to combine the strengths of both MBRL (sample efficiency) and SBRL (long-horizon planning and temporal abstraction) while mitigating their individual weaknesses. This involves creating a skill dynamics model that directly predicts skill outcomes, thereby reducing the number of predictions needed for a given temporal span compared to flat dynamics models.

6. **Utilizing Offline Task-Agnostic Data:** A further objective is to effectively leverage readily available, task-agnostic offline datasets. Such data can be used to pre-train a reusable set of skills and a corresponding skill dynamics model, making the subsequent downstream task learning more efficient without requiring task-specific demonstrations or rewards.

In summary, the key objective is to propose a novel framework, SkiMo, that enables accurate and efficient long-term planning in complex, sparse-reward tasks by jointly learning abstract skills and a skill-level dynamics model from diverse prior experience, significantly improving the sample efficiency of both model-based and skill-based RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkiMo operates in two distinct phases: (1) Pre-training the skill dynamics model and skills from an offline task-agnostic dataset, and (2) Downstream task learning using the learned skill dynamics model and skill repertoire. The approach aims to leverage temporal abstraction in both the policy and the dynamics model to enable efficient and accurate long-horizon planning.

#### 3.1. Model Components

SkiMo utilizes several interconnected components:
- **State Encoder (Eψ):** Maps raw observations (s) to a latent state (h). Parameters ψ are finetuned during downstream RL.
- **Observation Decoder (Oθ):** Reconstructs observations from latent states. Parameters θ are fixed after pre-training.
- **Skill Prior (pθ):** Predicts the distribution of skills from a given state, guiding exploration. Parameters θ are fixed after pre-training.
- **Skill Encoder (qθ):** Embeds a sequence of states and actions into a skill latent (z). Parameters θ are fixed after pre-training.
- **Skill Policy (πLθ):** Decodes a skill latent (z) and current state (s) into a sequence of low-level actions. Parameters θ are fixed after pre-training.
- **Skill Dynamics Model (Dψ):** Predicts the resultant latent state (h_t+H) after executing a skill (z) from a starting latent state (h_t), where H is the skill horizon. Parameters ψ are finetuned during downstream RL.
- **Task Policy (πφ):** A high-level policy that outputs a skill latent (z) given a latent state (h). Parameters φ are learned during downstream RL.
- **Reward Function (Rφ):** Predicts the sum of H-step rewards for a skill. Parameters φ are learned during downstream RL.
- **Value Function (Qφ):** Predicts the Q-value for a given latent state and skill. Parameters φ are learned during downstream RL.

#### 3.2. Pre-training Skill Dynamics Model and Skills from Task-agnostic Data

This phase focuses on jointly learning reusable skills and a skill dynamics model from a large, reward-free, task-agnostic offline dataset. The joint training is designed to shape a latent skill space and state embedding that are conducive to accurate future prediction.

1. **Skill Repertoire Learning (Variational Autoencoder - VAE based):** A conditional VAE is trained to learn a low-dimensional skill latent space (Z) and a low-level skill policy (πLθ). Given H consecutive states and actions, the skill encoder (qθ) maps them to a skill embedding (z). The skill policy (πLθ) then reconstructs the original action sequence from z. The training objective (L_VAE) combines:
 - **Behavioral Cloning Loss:** Minimizes the squared difference between the actions predicted by πLθ and the ground-truth actions.
 - **Embedding Regularization:** A KL divergence term regularizes the skill latent distribution qθ(z|...) towards a prior (e.g., tanh-transformed unit Gaussian), ensuring a well-behaved latent space.

2. **Skill Dynamics Model Learning:** The skill dynamics model (Dψ), state encoder (Eψ), and observation decoder (Oθ) are jointly trained with the VAE. The objective (L_REC) includes:
 - **Observation Reconstruction Loss:** Ensures the state encoder produces rich information by requiring the observation decoder to reconstruct the original observation from the encoded latent state.
 - **Latent State Consistency Loss:** Encourages the skill dynamics model to accurately predict the latent state H-steps ahead after a skill execution. This loss minimizes the difference between Dψ(h_t, z_t) and the ground-truth latent state Eψ(s_t+H). Gradients are back-propagated through multiple sequential skill transitions (N sequential skill transitions) for stable training, often using a target network.

3. **Skill Prior Learning:** A skill prior (pθ(z|s)) is extracted from the offline data, predicting the skill distribution for any given state. This prior guides exploration in downstream tasks by encouraging the high-level policy to select plausible skills. It is trained by minimizing the KL divergence between the output of the skill encoder and the skill prior (L_SP).

The overall pre-training objective is a combination of L_VAE, L_REC, and L_SP.

#### 3.3. Downstream Task Learning with Learned Skill Dynamics Model

In this phase, the pre-trained components (θ parameters) are mostly fixed, while the state encoder and skill dynamics model (ψ parameters) are finetuned, and new components for the specific task (φ parameters: task policy, reward, value functions) are learned.

1. **High-Level Task Policy Learning:** A high-level task policy (πφ(z_t|h_t)) is trained to output skill latent embeddings (z_t) based on the current latent state (h_t). These skill latents are then executed by the pre-trained low-level skill policy (πLθ) in the environment.

2. **Model-Based RL in Skill Space:** The central aspect is leveraging the skill dynamics model (Dψ) to generate *imaginary rollouts* in the skill space. This process involves:
 - Sampling a skill (z_t) from the task policy (πφ).
 - Predicting the H-step future latent state (h_t+H) using Dψ(h_t, z_t).
 This allows for long-horizon simulations with significantly fewer dynamics predictions (1/H compared to flat models), leading to more efficient and accurate imaginary trajectories.

3. **Policy Optimization and Planning:**
 - **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** At each step, MPC is performed by re-planning using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) in the skill space. CEM samples candidate skill sequences, evaluates their N-step returns using the learned reward and value functions, and refines the skill distribution, ultimately selecting the first skill in the optimized sequence for execution.
 - **Component Finetuning and Learning:** The skill dynamics model (Dψ) and state encoder (Eψ) are finetuned on downstream task data to adapt to new state distributions. A reward function (Rφ) learns to predict the sum of H-step rewards, and a Q-value function (Qφ) learns to estimate the expected future return. These are trained using an objective (L'_REC) that includes:
 - Latent state consistency loss (similar to pre-training).
 - Reward prediction loss: Minimizes the difference between predicted and actual H-step rewards.
 - Value prediction loss: Uses a TD-style objective to train the Q-function.
 - **Task Policy Optimization (L_RL):** The high-level task policy (πφ) is trained to maximize the estimated Q-value, while being regularized by the pre-trained skill prior (pθ) to ensure it selects plausible skills.

The overall downstream learning process minimizes L'_REC and L_RL through back-propagation over N consecutive skill-level transitions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated SkiMo against various model-based and skill-based RL baselines across four long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards: Maze Navigation, two Kitchen Manipulation tasks (one standard, one mis-aligned), and a CALVIN tabletop manipulation task.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison with Baselines

- **Maze Navigation (Sparse Reward, Long Horizon):** SkiMo consistently achieved the goal, demonstrating robust performance in a challenging exploration scenario where primitive actions require over 1,000 steps for completion. Baselines like Dreamer, TD-MPC, DADS, and LSP largely failed to learn effective policies or accurate models. SPiRL, while showing good exploration, did not consistently find the goal due to difficulties in long-horizon policy optimization. SPiRL + Dreamer/TD-MPC also struggled with model accuracy and often resulted in collisions.
- **Kitchen Manipulation (Sequential Sub-tasks):** SkiMo reached performance levels above 3 sub-tasks with approximately 5 times fewer environment interactions than SPiRL. Other baselines (Dreamer, TD-MPC, DADS, LSP) rarely completed the first sub-task due to sparse rewards and the need for complex, sequential manipulation. SPiRL + Dreamer/TD-MPC showed improvement over flat model-based RL but did not consistently achieve more than two sub-tasks, indicating issues with independently trained models and policies.
- **Mis-aligned Kitchen (Challenging Skill Transitions):** In this task, where the target sub-task sequence deviated significantly from the offline data distribution, SkiMo maintained strong performance. This suggests that the skill dynamics model can adapt to novel behavioral distributions, even when the skill prior provides less direct guidance.
- **CALVIN (Highly Task-Agnostic Offline Data):** SkiMo learned faster than the model-free baseline, SPiRL, highlighting the benefit of the skill dynamics model in environments with very sparse task-specific transitions in the offline data. Dreamer performed better here than in Kitchen, possibly due to the more compact object arrangements, but still struggled with composing long, coherent action sequences.

**Summary of Baseline Comparison:** SkiMo demonstrated a synergistic benefit of temporal abstraction in both the policy and the dynamics model. It was the only method that consistently solved these long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards, and it achieved this with significantly improved sample efficiency compared to both model-free skill-based methods and flat model-based methods. The results also indicated that naive combinations of existing approaches (e.g., SPiRL + Dreamer) were not sufficient, underscoring the importance of SkiMo's specific algorithmic design, particularly the joint training of skills and skill dynamics.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Model-based vs. Model-free (SkiMo vs. SkiMo + SAC):** SkiMo, utilizing its model-based approach, achieved both better asymptotic performance and higher sample efficiency across all tasks compared to SkiMo + SAC (where a model-free SAC agent trains the high-level policy). This indicates that leveraging long-term predictions from the skill dynamics model enables more effective policy decisions.
- **Joint Training of Skills and Skill Dynamics Model (SkiMo vs. SkiMo w/o joint training):** The ablation showed that joint training is crucial, especially in tasks requiring accurate long-term plans (Maze) or involving diverse skill sets (CALVIN). Without joint training, where the skill latent space is optimized only for action reconstruction, performance was degraded.
- **CEM Planning (SkiMo vs. SkiMo w/o CEM):** CEM planning significantly improved learning in Kitchen, Mis-aligned Kitchen, and CALVIN, suggesting its effectiveness in finding better plans in these complex manipulation tasks. In Maze, SkiMo w/o CEM learned faster, which the authors attributed to action noise from CEM potentially hindering exploration by causing the agent to get stuck at walls.
- **Skill Dynamics Model vs. Single-Step Dynamics (SkiMo vs. SkiMo w/ single-step dynamics):** Replacing the skill dynamics model with a conventional single-step dynamics model resulted in significantly worse performance across all tasks. This ablation confirmed that the skill dynamics model's ability to make accurate long-horizon predictions, by reducing compounding errors, is critical for effective planning.

#### 4.3. Long-Horizon Prediction Accuracy

A qualitative analysis visualizing imagined trajectories showed that SkiMo's skill dynamics model could reproduce ground truth trajectories over 500 timesteps with minimal error, even when navigating complex environments like mazes. In contrast, flat single-step dynamics models (e.g., Dreamer) exhibited rapid accumulation of prediction errors, leading to substantial deviations from the ground truth. This directly supports the claim that temporal abstraction in the dynamics model enhances long-term prediction accuracy.

#### 4.4. Additional Ablations (Appendix)

- **Skill Horizon (H):** A very short skill horizon (H=1, 5) was insufficient for temporal abstraction. A very long skill horizon (H=15, 20) could degrade performance, especially in Maze, due to increased inaccuracy and stochasticity of skill dynamics predictions, making long-term planning harder. An intermediate horizon (H=10) generally performed well.
- **Planning Horizon (N):** A short planning horizon led to slower learning initially. An overly long planning horizon also degraded performance, particularly when approaching the full episode length, likely due to difficulties in accurately modeling such extended sequences.
- **Fine-Tuning Model:** Freezing the skill dynamics model and state encoder after pre-training resulted in worse performance. This indicates that finetuning these components during downstream RL is necessary for adapting to task-specific states and transitions that may differ from the pre-training data distribution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Skill-based Model-based RL (SkiMo) framework demonstrates several significant advancements with potential implications for the field of reinforcement learning:

1. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency for Long-Horizon Tasks:** SkiMo significantly improves sample efficiency in complex, long-horizon tasks, particularly those with sparse rewards. By enabling planning in a high-level skill space using a skill dynamics model, the agent requires fewer interactions with the environment to learn effective policies. This addresses a major bottleneck in applying RL to real-world problems where data collection is often costly, time-consuming, or even dangerous.

2. **Improved Long-Term Prediction Accuracy:** The core innovation of the skill dynamics model, which predicts the outcome of an entire skill rather than individual primitive actions, substantially reduces the accumulation of prediction errors over extended horizons. This improved accuracy in long-term forecasting is critical for robust planning in complex environments.

3. **Effective Temporal Abstraction in Dynamics:** The work validates the concept of "saltatory" model-based hierarchical RL, demonstrating that temporal abstraction can be effectively applied not just to policies but also to dynamics models. This extends the temporal reasoning capabilities of RL agents beyond the limitations of single-step predictions.

4. **Leveraging Offline Task-Agnostic Data:** SkiMo's ability to jointly learn a skill repertoire and a skill dynamics model from diverse, reward-free offline data is a practical advantage. This allows for the pre-training of foundational capabilities without requiring task-specific demonstrations, maximizing the utility of readily available datasets.

5. **Robustness to Diverse Task Settings:** The framework's performance across varied domains (navigation, different manipulation tasks) and its adaptability to mis-aligned task distributions (Mis-aligned Kitchen) suggest a degree of generality. This indicates that skills and skill dynamics learned from broad experience can be recombined and adapted to solve new tasks effectively.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Real-World Robotics:** The improved sample efficiency and ability to handle long-horizon tasks make SkiMo a promising candidate for applications in real-world robotics, where direct robot interactions are expensive. The framework's design, which leverages offline data and reduces online interactions, aligns with practical constraints of robotic deployment.
- **Autonomous Systems:** For complex autonomous systems operating in dynamic environments (e.g., self-driving cars, industrial automation), the capacity for accurate long-term planning with abstract skills could enhance reliability and adaptability.
- **Foundation for Future Hierarchical RL Research:** The work establishes a strong baseline for future research into model-based hierarchical RL, particularly concerning how to jointly learn and leverage different levels of abstraction in both planning and control.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations and suggest future research directions. These include:
- **Variable-Length Skills:** The current method extracts fixed-length skills. Learning variable-length semantic skills could further improve long-term prediction and planning by adapting skill granularity to context and goals.
- **High-Dimensional Observations:** While tested on state-based inputs, extending SkiMo to handle high-dimensional observations like RGB images, depth maps, or tactile sensor data would be a logical next step, especially for broader real-world applications.
- **Real Robot Deployment:** Directly applying and validating the approach on physical robots, beyond simulation, remains an important area for future investigation to fully assess its practical utility and robustness.
