# Learning to Focus and Precise Cropping: A Reinforcement Learning Framework with Information Gaps and Grounding Loss for MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage reinforcement learning framework enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to genuinely leverage cropped regions for fine-grained perception and precise object localization in high-resolution visual question answering. The approach yields improved accuracy on VQA benchmarks while reducing inference time by focusing on crucial visual information.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with fine-grained perception and reasoning, especially for small or obscured objects in complex visual scenes.
- Existing agent-based MLLMs with cropping tools often perform "perfunctory tool calls," relying on global input rather than genuinely utilizing information from cropped regions to form answers.
- Prior methods either depend on costly trajectory supervision from powerful teacher models or fail to ensure MLLMs truly integrate cropped details into their reasoning.

## Method

- A novel ==two-stage== reinforcement learning framework, built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, is introduced, operating without requiring trajectory supervision.
- Stage 1, "Learn to Focus," creates an information gap by feeding the MLLM a strategically downsampled global image (offline resolution selection) while extracting cropped regions from the original high-resolution image, compelling reliance on cropped information.
- Stage 2, "Learn to Crop Precisely," refines cropping accuracy using a composite grounding reward, incorporating IoU and L1 distance, guided by hierarchical ground-truth bounding box annotations on a small dataset.

## Results

- The method achieved state-of-the-art overall accuracy, scoring **72.1%** on HR-Bench **8K** (1,024 visual tokens) compared to **62.0%** for DeepEyes and **66.0%** for Mini-o3.
- Stage 1 (information gap) improved overall accuracy by **8.9%** on HR-Bench **8K**, and Stage 2 (grounding reward) further improved accuracy by an average of **2.2%** across benchmarks while increasing cropped image IoU by 5-9 percentage points.
- The framework demonstrated improved computational efficiency, achieving higher accuracy with an inference time of 2.8 seconds per question on HR-Bench **8K** (1,024 tokens) compared to other models using up to 16,384 tokens (e.g., DeepEyes at 12.4 seconds).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current agent-based MLLMs often exhibit an "answer first, crop later" behavior, formulating responses before genuinely using cropped content, even when a full high-resolution image is available.
> - Introducing an "information gap" by providing a low-resolution global view and high-resolution crops forces the model to actively seek and utilize fine-grained details from the cropped regions.
> - A composite grounding reward, combining conditional IoU and L1 distance, effectively guides the model to predict precise and non-redundant bounding boxes, addressing the issue of oversized crops and improving efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have advanced significantly in understanding and reasoning across various vision-language tasks. However, a persistent challenge remains in their capacity for fine-grained perception and reasoning, particularly when dealing with small or obscured objects within complex visual scenes. Traditional MLLMs often struggle to accurately locate targets and extract detailed cues from a single, high-resolution input.

To address this, recent research has explored agent-based workflows, where MLLMs function as active agents capable of interacting with external tools. A prominent approach involves equipping MLLMs with a "cropping tool," enabling them to autonomously identify and zoom into specific regions of interest to acquire detailed information necessary for complex question answering. These agentic MLLMs typically employ two main training paradigms: a hybrid of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), or a pure RL approach.

The SFT+RL hybrid methods, such as those used by Mini-o3 and Pixel Reasoner, rely on generating extensive reasoning trajectories from powerful proprietary MLLMs, which incurs high computational costs and establishes an inherent performance ceiling tied to the teacher model. Pure RL methods, exemplified by DeepEyes, bypass the need for a teacher model but, as observed by the authors of this paper, often exhibit a tendency to predict answers before or without genuinely leveraging the information within the cropped regions. This leads to what the authors term "perfunctory tool calls," where the model’s reasoning process is not significantly influenced by the cropped content. This work positions itself by identifying and directly addressing this limitation in existing agent-based MLLMs, proposing a novel RL framework designed to compel genuine reliance on tool-based observations without requiring trajectory supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an observed limitation in current agent-based MLLMs when performing high-resolution visual question answering: their tendency to rely heavily on global input rather than genuinely utilizing information from cropped regions. Through empirical analysis, the authors identified that models like DeepEyes often exhibit an "answer first, crop later" behavior. This means the model frequently formulates a final answer before executing a cropping action, subsequently using the cropped region merely to confirm its pre-existing conclusion rather than integrating new, fine-grained details into its reasoning.

The paper quantitatively supports this hypothesis by demonstrating that, even when substituting the model's predicted bounding boxes with ground truth bounding boxes or replacing cropped regions with random noise, the performance of existing models (e.g., DeepEyes, CoF-sft) remains largely unchanged, particularly when the full input image retains rich information (Table 1a). This suggests that the cropping tool is often invoked superficially, without the model truly attending to the detailed content within the cropped area. Even when the full image is heavily downsampled to 1,024 visual tokens, forcing a greater information gap, the reliance on cropped regions for performance improvement remains insignificant (Table 1b), indicating a fundamental failure to learn effective utilization.

The key objectives are therefore to:
1. **Induce genuine reliance on cropped regions:** Develop a mechanism that forces the MLLM to actively seek out and utilize detailed information from cropped regions, rather than relying on the low-detail global view.
2. **Enhance cropping precision:** Refine the agent's ability to precisely locate and crop relevant regions, minimizing redundant information and computational overhead.
3. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance without trajectory supervision:** Develop a reinforcement learning framework that can overcome the limitations of existing methods, leading to improved performance on high-resolution VQA benchmarks, without the costly and teacher-bound process of generating reasoning trajectories.
4. **Improve computational efficiency:** Enable MLLMs to process high-resolution visual inputs more efficiently by focusing on crucial regions rather than processing the entire high-resolution image.

These objectives are pursued through a novel two-stage reinforcement learning framework, specifically designed to address the identified "information gap" and "cropping precision" challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces a novel two-stage reinforcement learning framework that does not require trajectory supervision. The underlying MLLM is Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

**Overall Framework:**
The model is designed to predict coordinates for a critical region, which is then cropped and used to answer a given question. The action at step *t* is formulated as $a_t \sim \pi_{\theta}(a | I_0, q, [r_1, I_{\text{crop}_1}], \dots, [r_m, I_{\text{crop}_m}])$, where $I_0$ is the full input image, $r_i$ is a text response, and $I_{\text{crop}_i}$ is a cropped image. The training proceeds in two stages.

**Stage 1: Learn to Focus (Information Gap Mechanism)**
The primary goal of this stage is to compel the model to genuinely focus on and extract information from cropped regions.
- **Problem Identification:** Previous methods fed the MLLM high-resolution global images and cropped regions from the same high-resolution source, offering no new information in the crop. This led to "shortcut learning" where the model relied on the global image.
- **Offline Resolution Selection:** To create an "information gap," the initial input image ($I_0$) to the MLLM is strategically downsampled, while cropped regions ($I_{\text{ori}}$) are extracted from the original, full-resolution image. This downsampling is performed offline. For each training instance, the original high-resolution image is iteratively downsampled. The process stops when the MLLM's answer based on the downsampled image diverges from the answer obtained using the original image. This "optimally low" resolution $I_0$ provides just enough context for the model to identify relevant regions but insufficient detail to answer without cropping.
- **Reward Formulation:** The total reward $r$ for an output trajectory is a sum of three components:
 - $r_{\text{acc}}$: Accuracy reward, based on matching the ground-truth response.
 - $r_{\text{format}}$: Formatting reward, penalizing structurally deficient outputs.
 - $I(r_{\text{acc}} > 0) \cdot r_{\text{tool}}$: A tool usage bonus, granted only if a correct solution is provided and at least one external perception tool (cropping) is used. This encourages tool invocation when beneficial.
- **Optimization:** Advantages $A$ are computed by normalizing rewards, and the policy is updated using the GRPO (Generalized Advantage Estimation for Policy Optimization) objective (Equation 3) over mini-batches.
- **Training Data:** The first stage utilizes high-resolution VQA data from Pixelreasoner and CoF, along with a small set from ThinkLite-VL, using only image-question-answer pairs.

**Stage 2: Learn to Crop Precisely (Grounding Loss)**
This stage refines the model's cropping precision, addressing the tendency for oversized crops that occur when only accuracy rewards are used in Stage 1.
- **Problem Identification:** Maximizing accuracy reward alone in Stage 1 can lead to the model predicting progressively larger bounding boxes to ensure full coverage of necessary details, resulting in redundant information and computational inefficiency.
- **Hierarchical Bbox Annotation:** A small dataset (256 instances from VisualProbe datasets from Mini-o3) is manually annotated with hierarchical ground-truth (GT) bounding boxes ($B_1, B_2, \dots, B_n$). These range from the minimal necessary region ($B_1$) to a larger, contextual area, providing flexible targets for precision.
- **Grounding Reward Formulation ($r_{\text{geo}}$):** A composite reward signal guides the agent towards precise and sufficient crops.
 - **IoU Reward ($r_{\text{IoU}}$):** Encourages high overlap between the predicted box ($B_p$) and one of the GT boxes. To prevent excessively tight crops that might miss crucial details, this reward is conditional: it's granted only if the overlap ratio of $B_1$ by $B_p$ (termed 'overlap') exceeds a threshold (0.9 in experiments).
 - **L1 Distance Reward ($r_{\text{l1}}$):** Provides a denser signal, especially early in training when IoU rewards might be sparse. It measures the normalized L1 distance between the corners of $B_p$ and the closest GT box.
 - **Combined Grounding Reward:** $r_{\text{geo}} = \omega \cdot r_{\text{IoU}} + (1 - \omega) \cdot r_{\text{l1}}$.
- **Training Data:** The second stage uses the manually annotated VisualProb dataset.

**Training Details and Benchmarks:**
- **Implementation:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model, trained on 8 A100 GPUs for 80 steps with GRPO.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** HR-Bench 8K (Fine-grained Single-instance Perception - FSP, Fine-grained Cross-instance Perception - FCP), HR-Bench 4K, and V* (Attribute recognition - Attr, Spatial reasoning - Spatial).
- **Visual Token Budgets:** Models are evaluated under two visual token limits for the complete input image: 1,024 and 16,384. Cropped regions are always extracted from the original, uncompressed image.
- **Compared Methods:** CoF (SFT-stage), Pixel Reasoner, Mini-o3 (SFT+RL), and DeepEyes (pure RL).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Preliminary Analysis:**
The preliminary analysis (Table 1) revealed that existing agent-based MLLMs exhibit weak reliance on cropped regions. When the full input image had a high visual token budget (16,384), replacing the model’s predicted crops with ground truth or random noise resulted in only marginal performance changes for DeepEyes and CoF-sft. This indicated that these models primarily depend on the global image, performing perfunctory cropping. Even with a reduced visual token budget for the full image (1,024), which increased the information gap, the performance change, though more pronounced, remained insignificant, suggesting persistent failure to effectively leverage cropped information.

**Overall Performance:**
The proposed method achieved state-of-the-art results across HR-Bench 8K, HR-Bench 4K, and V* benchmarks (Table 2).
- **1,024 Visual Tokens:** Under the 1,024-token constraint for the full image, the method significantly outperformed all other approaches. For instance, on HR-Bench 8K, it achieved an overall accuracy of 72.1%, compared to 62.0% for DeepEyes and 66.0% for Mini-o3. This performance advantage was more evident as the input image resolution increased, indicating effective utilization of information from cropped regions in high-resolution scenarios.
- **16,384 Visual Tokens:** Even when compared to models utilizing a higher 16,384 visual token budget, the proposed method maintained competitive or superior performance, achieving 75.4% overall accuracy on HR-Bench 8K and 76.4% on HR-Bench 4K. Notably, its performance at 1,024 visual tokens could even surpass some methods at 16,384 tokens on certain benchmarks.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Two-Stage Training (Table 3):**
 - **Stage-I (Information Gap):** Introduction of the information gap mechanism in Stage 1 improved overall accuracy by 8.9% on HR-Bench 8K, 6.5% on HR-Bench 4K, and 5.7% on V* compared to a baseline model. Further analysis (Table 5) showed that Stage-I trained models exhibited a larger accuracy delta when cropped images were replaced with ground truth or random noise (e.g., 18.5% on HR-Bench 8K vs. 4.5% for baseline), confirming increased attention to cropped regions.
 - **Stage-II (Grounding Reward):** The second stage, focusing on cropping precision, further improved overall accuracy by an average of 2.2% across benchmarks and significantly enhanced the Intersection over Union (IoU) metric for cropped images by 5-9 percentage points.
- **Effect of Data Selecting (Table 4):** Training Stage 2 with challenging VisualProb (VP) datasets (Mini-o3) and the IoU reward was more effective (72.7% overall on HR-Bench 8K) than using data from the same source as Stage 1 (68.1% overall), as the Stage 1 model could already correctly answer most of the initial training data.
- **Effect of L1 Reward (Table 4):** Incorporating the L1 distance reward alongside the IoU reward in Stage 2 further improved response accuracy on all three benchmarks (e.g., 72.1% overall on HR-Bench 8K), demonstrating its utility in guiding more accurate localization, especially for difficult samples.
- **Resolution Selection Strategy (Table 9):** The offline resolution selection strategy in Stage 1, which creates an "optimal" information gap, outperformed simple baselines like consistently using the most downsampled image ("Hard") or randomly selecting a downsampled image ("Random") for multi-target tasks, confirming its effectiveness in encouraging reliance on crops.
- **Reward Design in Stage-II (Table 10):** The conditional IoU reward, applied only when the overlap with the ground truth exceeded a threshold, led to higher IoU and accuracy compared to removing this condition. This demonstrated that the condition effectively guided more precise cropping.

**Efficiency Analysis:**
The method demonstrated substantial efficiency advantages (Table 6). When processing full images with 1,024 visual tokens, it achieved higher accuracy while incurring significantly lower inference time per question (e.g., 2.8 seconds on HR-Bench 8K) compared to other models using up to 16,384 tokens (e.g., DeepEyes at 12.4 seconds, Mini-o3 at 27.8 seconds). This indicates a more efficient utilization of visual tokens by enabling the model to focus on accurately cropped regions.

**Training Dynamics and Visualization Analysis (Supplementary Material Figures 5-8):**
- **Stage 1:** Training dynamics showed that the information gap mechanism in Stage 1 led to a higher number of tool calls and an increased overlap between predicted and ground-truth regions, even though IoU did not significantly improve. This indicated that the model learned to actively explore and identify relevant regions, despite initial difficulty.
- **Stage 2:** The introduction of the grounding reward in Stage 2 led to a distinct upward trend in IoU, a decrease in the predicted bounding box ratio and token number, and a moderate increase in overlap. This confirmed that the grounding reward effectively encouraged more precise and compact cropping, leading to improved inference efficiency without losing crucial information.
- **Visualizations:** Comparative examples (Figure 7) showed Stage-I correctly answering questions where DeepEyes failed, despite both cropping correctly. Another example showed Stage-I successfully performing a second, corrective crop to find a missing detail, unlike DeepEyes. Figure 8 illustrated that Stage-II produces more accurate and less redundant crops compared to Stage-I, leading to correct answers in challenging scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research identifies a critical limitation in current agent-based MLLMs: their tendency to perform superficial tool invocation rather than genuinely leveraging detailed information from cropped regions. This finding provides a specific area for improvement in the development of more capable MLLMs for complex visual understanding.

The proposed two-stage reinforcement learning framework offers a method to address this limitation without relying on costly trajectory supervision from powerful teacher models.
- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Perception:** The "Information Gap" mechanism, by strategically downsampling the global image input, compels the MLLM to actively utilize cropped regions for fine-grained details, leading to more robust perception.
- **Improved Cropping Precision:** The introduction of hierarchical bounding box annotations and a composite grounding reward effectively guides the model to produce precise and non-redundant crops, which is crucial for efficient and accurate reasoning.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The method achieves state-of-the-art results on challenging high-resolution visual question-answering benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in practical applications.
- **Computational Efficiency:** By enabling models to focus on relevant, accurately cropped regions, the framework reduces the number of visual tokens required for input, significantly lowering inference time and computational cost, which is a key advantage for real-world deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Agentic MLLMs:** This work contributes a novel training paradigm that encourages MLLMs to become more discerning and active agents in visual exploration. This could serve as a foundation for developing more intelligent multimodal agents capable of complex interactive reasoning.

Potential future work, as suggested by the authors, includes leveraging synthetic data to overcome the scarcity of bounding box annotations for high-resolution VQA and integrating more advanced segmentation models as callable tools for agents, further enhancing the precision and flexibility of visual grounding. These directions indicate avenues for continued advancement in robust multimodal understanding.
