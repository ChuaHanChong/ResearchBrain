# Trimming the Long-Tail of Visual World Modeling Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces TailOR, a new benchmark to evaluate visual generative models' physical reasoning capabilities, particularly in less common 'long-tail' interaction scenarios. The study reveals a significant gap in models' ability to generalize physical understanding beyond typical interactions, with video models exhibiting more pronounced difficulties than image models.

## Problem

- Current multimodal generative models show high realism but their internalization of underlying physical principles, beyond statistical regularities, remains unverified.
- Existing benchmarks for visual generative models primarily assess perceptual quality and physical correctness in common ('head-distribution') scenarios.
- There is a lack of systematic evaluation for 'long-tail scenarios' involving unconventional or abstract physical interactions, which are underrepresented in training data.

## Method

- A multi-dimensional benchmark, **TailOR**, is introduced with ==three scenario modes==: Regular (common tool-task), Unconventional (attribute-compatible substitute tools), and Impossible (attribute-violating tools).
- The benchmark evaluates both image and video generation models under two settings: ==Predictive Generation== (inferring outcomes) and ==Descriptive Generation== (realizing explicit instructions).
- A structured ==data curation pipeline== leverages ==Large Language Models== and human verification to generate diverse tasks, tool variants, prompts, and detailed evaluation rubrics.

## Results

- All state-of-the-art models evaluated exhibited a consistent **'long-tail gap,'** with performance degrading significantly across metrics like **Interaction Accuracy** and **Physical Realism** when moving from Regular to Unconventional and Impossible scenarios.
- Video generation models consistently scored **lower** than image generation models across all evaluation metrics, highlighting the increased complexity of simulating dynamic physical interactions.
- Failure mode analysis showed image models struggle with instruction adherence and outcome correctness in long-tail cases, while video models primarily suffer from implausible dynamics and temporal inconsistencies, often reverting to common training patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical interactions, like real-world phenomena, follow a long-tailed distribution, where models struggle to generalize beyond statistically frequent behaviors.
> - Models appear to internalize high-frequency interaction templates as holistic visual patterns rather than learning transferable physical primitives such as rigidity or sharpness.
> - The generation of temporally coherent physical dynamics in videos poses a greater challenge than static image generation, as evidenced by lower performance scores for video models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in multimodal generative models, particularly in generating realistic images and videos from text prompts. These "world models" are increasingly capable of synthesizing complex scenes and simulating physical interactions. Existing benchmarks for evaluating these models primarily focus on perceptual quality, prompt faithfulness, and physical correctness within conventional or "head-distribution" scenarios. Examples include T2I-CompBench, GenEval, GenAI-Bench for images, and VideoPhy, PhyWorldBench, and MMGR for videos, which assess aspects like compositionality, object-level correctness, temporal consistency, and physical commonsense.

However, a fundamental question remains regarding whether these models truly internalize underlying physical principles and object affordances, or if they primarily rely on statistical regularities observed in vast training datasets. Current evaluations often do not sufficiently distinguish between genuine physical understanding and sophisticated pattern reproduction. The paper identifies a gap in existing evaluation methodologies: a lack of systematic assessment for "long-tail scenarios"—physical interactions that are less common, unconventional, or require abstract reasoning about object attributes beyond typical usage.

TailOR addresses this gap by introducing a benchmark specifically designed to probe models' generalization capabilities to these irregular physical interactions. By moving beyond common scenarios, this work aims to provide a diagnostic tool to assess whether generative models can reason about object properties, causal dynamics, and physical constraints in novel contexts. This approach contrasts with prior work that largely operates under "standard world assumptions" where objects and tools behave in familiar ways, thereby evaluating a deeper level of physical intelligence in generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central question motivating this research is: "Do current world models internalize the underlying physical principles of object interactions, or do they primarily rely on statistical regularities observed in training data?"

The authors argue that physical interactions in the real world follow a long-tailed distribution. Common, regular interactions (e.g., cutting bread with a knife) form the "head" of this distribution and are well-represented in visual data. In contrast, a vast spectrum of "long-tail scenarios" involves less typical, improvised, or context-dependent interactions (e.g., using a coin as a screwdriver). While these long-tail interactions are physically valid, they are underrepresented in training data. Current world models demonstrate impressive performance on head scenarios, but their success in these common settings might be attributable to pattern recognition rather than genuine physical understanding.

The key objectives of this work are to:
1. **Introduce TailOR:** A benchmark specifically designed to challenge world models to simulate irregular physical interactions, moving beyond common, head-distribution scenarios.
2. **Systematically Evaluate Generalization:** Investigate whether models can generalize based on functional attributes and physical affordances, rather than merely relying on memorized tool-task associations or statistical co-occurrences.
3. **Probe Constraint Awareness:** Determine if models can recognize and depict physical limitations, such as when an interaction should fail due to attribute-violating tools.
4. **Disentangle Capabilities:** Evaluate two complementary capabilities:
 - **Implicit Physical Reasoning:** A model's ability to anticipate outcomes without explicit guidance.
 - **Faithful Realization:** A model's ability to adhere to explicitly specified physical instructions and maintain consistency.
5. **Diagnose Failure Modes:** Identify specific types of errors (e.g., perceptual issues, lack of affordance understanding, temporal inconsistencies) that emerge when models attempt to simulate long-tail physical interactions, for both image and video generation.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to highlight the limitations of current visual generative models in understanding object attributes, causal dynamics, and constraint-aware interactions, thereby guiding future research towards more physically grounded world modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TailOR is designed as a multi-dimensional benchmark framework to systematically evaluate visual generative models under irregular physical interactions.

#### 3.1. Scenario Modes

The benchmark defines three scenario modes that progressively increase the challenge to models' physical reasoning:
- **Regular Scenario:** Involves common tool–task pairs (e.g., using a hammer to hit a nail). This mode assesses a model's ability to reproduce frequent, in-distribution interactions.
- **Unconventional Scenario:** Replaces regular tools with attribute-compatible substitutes (e.g., using a heavy book to crack a walnut). This mode tests the model's ability to generalize based on functional attributes (e.g., rigidity, mass) rather than memorized tool-task associations.
- **Impossible Scenario:** Introduces attribute-violating tools (e.g., using a soft marshmallow to crack a walnut). This mode evaluates whether models can respect physical constraints and generate outcomes that accurately reflect failure or incompatibility.

#### 3.2. Evaluated Model Types

The benchmark evaluates both:
- **Image Generation Models:** Required to produce a single, spatially coherent final state, primarily testing representation of plausible configurations and outcomes.
- **Video Generation Models:** Additionally required to model temporal dynamics, including motion trajectories, contact events, force application, and state transitions over time.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Settings

Two complementary generation settings are used to disentangle models' predictive and realization capabilities:
- **Predictive Generation:** Models are given a task and a tool (X to Y for task Z) and must infer the outcome without guidance. This evaluates implicit physical reasoning and reliance on internal representations of functional attributes.
- **Descriptive Generation:** Models are provided with an explicit description of the desired outcome (e.g., a specific final state or interaction dynamics). This evaluates controllability and the ability to faithfully realize specified physical results.

#### 3.4. Benchmark Design and Curation Pipeline

The data curation pipeline for TailOR consists of structured steps leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) and human verification:
1. **Raw Data Sources:**
 - **Common Action Ontology:** 18 manually verified tool-use actions are selected from HICO-DET, each with a description, underlying physics, and core affordance requirements.
 - **Object–Affordance Graph:** Built from ConceptNet, extracting object concepts and properties (e.g., "HasProperty sharp").
2. **Evaluation Instance Generation:**
 - **Step 1: Action-driven Task Generation:** LLMs generate 20 diverse, realistic tool-use tasks for each action, including `task_goal`, `expected_outcome`, `original_tool`, and `required_tool_attributes`. These form the `Regular Scenario` instances.
 - **Step 2: Unconventional Tool Generation:** LLMs propose 4-5 uncommon but attribute-compatible substitute tools for each task, yielding `Unconventional Scenario` instances.
 - **Step 3: Impossible Tool Generation:** LLMs generate opposite affordances (e.g., soft vs. rigid) and identify tools from the object–affordance graph that possess these contradictory attributes, creating `Impossible Scenario` instances with expected failure outcomes.
 - **Step 4: Human Verification and Filtering:** Human annotators review and revise generated tasks and tools to ensure physical plausibility for unconventional cases, unambiguous impossibility for negative cases, and visual clarity. Each task is finalized with one regular, two unconventional, and two impossible tools.
 - **Step 5: Prompt Generation:** For each task-tool pair, four prompts are generated: Predictive Image, Descriptive Image, Predictive Video, and Descriptive Video.
 - **Step 6: Evaluation Rubric Generation:** An automatic LLM-based process generates a checklist-based evaluation rubric for each prompt, grounded in prompt and task specifications.
 - **Step 7: Human Verification and Finalization:** A second round of human verification ensures prompt and rubric quality.

#### 3.5. Dataset Statistics

The final TailOR benchmark comprises 80 tool-use tasks across diverse action categories (e.g., block, break, cut, hammer, loosen, tighten) and environments. With five tool variants per task (1 regular, 2 unconventional, 2 impossible) and two generation settings (predictive, descriptive) for both image and video, this yields 1600 generation prompts in total (400 evaluation tasks).

#### 3.6. Evaluation Metrics

Four complementary dimensions are evaluated:
- **Instruction Adherence (0–100%):** Measures whether the generated scene correctly instantiates required entities, functional properties, and scene constraints. Sub-scores: Entity Completeness, Attribute Fidelity, Scene Validity.
- **Interaction Accuracy (0–100%):** Measures whether the interaction outcome and dynamics are correctly realized. Sub-scores: State Change Correctness, Affordance Grounding, Motion Plausibility (video only).
- **Physical Realism (0–5):** An open-ended rating assessing consistency with fundamental physical principles (gravity, contact mechanics, material constraints, causality).
- **Perceptual Quality (0–5):** An open-ended rating assessing visual clarity, rendering fidelity, and overall visual quality, including cross-frame identity consistency for video.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Protocol

Both human and automatic evaluations are conducted:
- **Human Evaluation:** 9 annotators (with prior experience) independently assess each generated sample using standardized forms, checklist-based questions, and 0-5 rating scales. Each sample is evaluated by three annotators, and scores are averaged. Krippendorff’s α (0.72) and Spearman correlation (0.68) indicate substantial inter-annotator agreement.
- **Automatic Evaluation:** Gemini-2.5-pro is used as a VLM judge to answer the same rubric questions and provide scores, with explicit reasoning steps encouraged. A subset of 80 high-quality prompts is sampled for evaluation, covering different scenarios and settings across 1080 generated data points.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art image models (Z-Image, Qwen-Image, GPT-Image-1, Nano-Banana-2) and video models (HunyuanVideo-1.5, Wan-2.2, Sora-2, Veo-3.1) on TailOR yielded several observations.

#### 4.1. Long-Tail Gap in Physical World Modeling

Performance consistently degraded across all evaluated models and metrics as tasks progressed from Regular to Unconventional and then to Impossible scenarios. This indicates a systematic "long-tail gap," demonstrating limited generalization beyond common, head-distribution patterns. The most significant performance drops were observed in "Interaction Accuracy" and "Physical Realism," suggesting that failures are primarily due to an insufficient understanding of affordance-level reasoning rather than merely perceptual issues.

#### 4.2. Greater Difficulty for Video Models

Video generation models consistently achieved lower scores than image generation models across all evaluation metrics. This performance disparity highlights the additional complexity of forward physical simulation in video, which requires maintaining temporally coherent state transitions, motion trajectories, and consistent dynamics across frames, beyond depicting a plausible static interaction. Video models often exhibited a bias towards familiar training patterns over instruction adherence, especially for impossible interactions, leading to generated dynamics that reverted to conventional behaviors. Errors in early frames also tended to propagate, causing cascading failures that compromised causal consistency and physical plausibility over time.

#### 4.3. Consistency Between Automatic and Human Evaluation

The automatic evaluation using a VLM judge showed strong agreement with human evaluations in model rankings across different scenarios and metrics. Most models identified as top-performing by the automatic system were also highly ranked by human assessors. Specifically, Sora-2 was consistently identified as the best video model and Nano-Banana-2 as the best image model across various scenarios and evaluation metrics.

#### 4.4. Failure Mode Analysis

- **Image Generation Models:**
 - **Regular Scenarios:** Failures were often characterized by incorrect outcomes (e.g., nail not driven in) and inaccurate attribute depiction (e.g., deformed hammer), alongside minor physical violations like object intersections.
 - **Unconventional Scenarios:** Dominated by incorrect outcomes, followed by affordance misgeneralization (e.g., book not applying force correctly) and physical violations (e.g., book unrealistically bending).
 - **Impossible Scenarios:** Primarily suffered from instruction adherence failures (models ignoring constraints and treating impossible tools as functional), leading to incorrect outcomes (e.g., sponge cutting a carrot) and physical violations.
- **Video Generation Models:**
 - **Regular Scenarios:** Failures included implausible dynamics (e.g., sudden contact, lack of force progression), interaction misexecution (action not completed), and temporal inconsistency (e.g., object jitter or discontinuity).
 - **Unconventional Scenarios:** Characterized by affordance misgeneralization (e.g., book behaving like a soft object despite rigidity), implausible dynamics, and interaction misexecution.
 - **Impossible Scenarios:** Dominated by temporal inconsistency (e.g., walnut cracking without visible force), physical violations (e.g., soft tool not deforming), and interaction misexecution.

#### 4.5. Sensitivity to Generation Setting (Predictive vs. Descriptive)

Across most models, predictive generation generally achieved comparable or sometimes higher performance than descriptive generation, particularly for video models. This suggested that when explicitly instructed (descriptive setting), models frequently reverted to familiar training patterns rather than faithfully following the prompt, especially in unconventional scenarios. The gap was larger in unconventional settings, indicating a struggle to reconcile explicit instructions with learned visual priors.
A sub-metric breakdown showed that descriptive generation improved "Entity Completeness" and "Scene Validity" for image models, suggesting that explicit outcomes aid object grounding. However, video models benefited less, with "Attribute Fidelity" remaining low even with descriptive prompts, indicating a reliance on training data biases over object attribute grounding.

#### 4.6. Reasons for Struggle

- **Predictive Generation:** Failures stem from a lack of underlying physical knowledge for reasoning about object interactions. Models appear to internalize high-frequency interaction templates as holistic visual patterns rather than decomposing them into transferable physical primitives (rigidity, sharpness, leverage). This results in template interpolation or outcome completion instead of compositional reasoning, leading to visually plausible but physically inconsistent results. In video, temporal dynamics amplify these inconsistencies.
- **Descriptive Generation:** Failures suggest a limitation in physically grounded simulation, attributed to a bias toward perceptual coherence over causal consistency. Models default to common interaction patterns from training data even when the prompt specifies an unconventional outcome. For video, this issue is compounded by forward propagation, where initial object states maintain their "regular" roles, hindering the simulation of intended causal dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TailOR provides a systematic and comprehensive benchmark that addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of visual generative models. By focusing on long-tail physical interactions, the benchmark shifts the assessment from merely reproducing common visual patterns to evaluating genuine physical reasoning and generalization capabilities.

The findings from TailOR have several significant implications:
1. **Exposing Fundamental Limitations:** The benchmark definitively reveals a "long-tail gap" in current world models, demonstrating that their impressive realism on common scenarios does not equate to a robust understanding of underlying physical principles, object attributes, or causal dynamics. This suggests that models primarily rely on statistical regularities and memorized templates rather than compositional physical reasoning.
2. **Diagnostic Tool for Research:** TailOR offers a diagnostic testbed that identifies specific failure modes (e.g., weak affordance-level understanding, temporal inconsistencies, instruction adherence failures) for both image and video generation. This diagnostic capability is crucial for guiding future research efforts.
3. **Highlighting the Need for Deeper Physical Understanding:** The consistent degradation in "Interaction Accuracy" and "Physical Realism" underscores the necessity for models to move beyond superficial visual patterns and develop a deeper, transferable understanding of physical properties and their implications for interaction.
4. **Informing Model Development:** The benchmark highlights key areas for improvement, such as incorporating stronger inductive biases for object attributes, affordances, and causal dynamics during training. This could lead to models that learn reusable physical primitives rather than holistic interaction templates.
5. **Advancing Video World Modeling:** The observation that video models struggle more significantly, particularly with temporal coherence and propagating errors, indicates a need for advancements in long-horizon state tracking, force-consistent motion modeling, and constraint-aware temporal planning.
6. **Potential for Future Expansion:** TailOR establishes a framework that can be extended to more complex embodied settings, including multi-step manipulation, multi-object causal chains, and more intricate environment dynamics, further pushing the boundaries of physically grounded AI.

Ultimately, TailOR contributes to the development of more intelligent and robust world models that can reason about a broader spectrum of physical interactions, thereby moving towards a more complete and generalizable understanding of the physical world.
