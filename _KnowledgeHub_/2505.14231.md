# UniVG-R1: Reasoning Guided Universal Visual Grounding with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> UniVG-R1 introduces a reasoning-guided multimodal large language model for universal visual grounding, employing a two-stage training approach combining Chain-of-Thought supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning with a difficulty-aware weighting strategy. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on multi-image grounding benchmarks while demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization across diverse reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing visual grounding methods struggle with implicit and complex instructions, particularly when information is spread across multiple images.
- Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) lack explicit reasoning processes tailored for deep multimodal grounding challenges in multi-image settings.
- Reinforcement learning for MLLMs faces a 'cold-start' problem, struggling to generate correct reasoning without initial guidance, and can suffer from a 'difficulty bias' where training over-emphasizes easier samples.

## Method

- UniVG-R1 employs a ==two-stage training protocol== built on Qwen2-VL, starting with ==Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-tuning (CoT-SFT)== using a 90k curated dataset of reasoning chains to address the RL 'cold-start' problem.
- The second stage refines reasoning capabilities through rule-based ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, which uses accuracy and format rewards to guide the model towards correct reasoning and bounding box predictions.
- A ==difficulty-aware weight adjustment strategy== is introduced into the GRPO objective function to mitigate the 'difficulty bias,' dynamically increasing the influence of harder samples during training based on their mean Intersection over Union (IoU).

## Results

- UniVG-R1 achieves state-of-the-art performance on the MIG-Bench for multi-image grounding, surpassing the previous best model by **9.1%** average accuracy while using considerably less data (approx. **8.3%** of the data size).
- It demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization, achieving an average improvement of **23.4%** across four diverse reasoning-guided grounding benchmarks (LISA-Grounding, LLMSeg-Grounding, ReasonVOS-Grounding, ReVOS-Grounding).
- Ablation studies confirm that both the Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-tuning stage and the difficulty-aware Group Relative Policy Optimization stage contribute to performance gains, with the latter providing an additional **2.0%** improvement and showing greater benefit for smaller models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A supervised 'cold-start' with high-quality reasoning chains is necessary to effectively initiate reinforcement learning for complex multi-image reasoning tasks.
> - Explicitly training models to generate and follow Chain-of-Thought processes significantly enhances performance on reasoning-heavy visual grounding tasks.
> - Addressing difficulty bias in reinforcement learning through dynamic weighting improves model learning, particularly for challenging samples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of **visual grounding**, **multimodal large language models (MLLMs)**, and **reinforcement learning (RL) for reasoning**. It addresses a critical gap in the field by pushing the boundaries of visual grounding towards more complex, real-world scenarios.

**Visual Grounding Evolution:**
- **Traditional Visual Grounding:** Initially, visual grounding focused on localizing objects in a *single image* based on *explicit and simple textual instructions* (e.g., "the blue shirt"). Datasets like RefCOCO/+/g are prime examples. Early methods leveraged vision-language pre-trained models like CLIP.
- **MLLM Era for Grounding:** With the advent of MLLMs, the field progressed to handle more complex single-image instructions requiring some level of reasoning (e.g., "find the food rich in vitamins"). Datasets like LISA-Grounding and LLMSeg-Grounding emerged, and models like Shikra and GroundingGPT were developed.
- **Multi-Image Grounding:** More recently, the "Migician" benchmark introduced the challenge of free-form *multi-image grounding*, requiring models to understand correlations across multiple visual contexts. This was a significant step towards real-world complexity.

**The Gap Addressed by UniVG-R1:**
Despite these advancements, the authors identify a crucial limitation: **a lack of advanced reasoning ability, especially for implicit and complex instructions across diverse multi-modal contexts.** Existing methods, including Migician, do not explicitly incorporate a reasoning process during training, leading to suboptimal performance when nuanced queries or cross-image comparisons are required (e.g., "Which furniture in Image-2 can deal with the objects in Image-1?").

**Leveraging Reasoning-Chain Guided Reinforcement Learning:**
The paper draws inspiration from the success of large reasoning models in the LLM domain, such as DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI-o1, which use rule-based reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance complex reasoning. This trend is already extending to MLLMs for tasks like visual reasoning. UniVG-R1 pioneers the application of this powerful paradigm to the challenging "universal visual grounding" task, which combines the complexity of multi-image inputs with the need for deep, implicit reasoning.

In essence, UniVG-R1 aims to bridge the gap between simple visual grounding and complex human-like visual reasoning by explicitly training an MLLM to generate and follow "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) reasoning processes, guided by reinforcement learning, across both single and multiple image scenarios. This positions it as a significant step towards more intelligent and versatile multimodal AI systems capable of truly understanding and interacting with complex visual information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **address the "universal visual grounding" task**, which involves recognizing and localizing target regions in images based on implicit, complex instructions, often in conjunction with multiple visual inputs.

The motivations driving this objective are multi-faceted:
1. **Bridging the Reality Gap:** Traditional visual grounding methods are primarily designed for single-image scenarios with straightforward textual references. However, real-world applications frequently involve nuanced, implicit instructions and require understanding information spread across multiple images. Current methods struggle significantly with this complexity.
2. **Enhancing Reasoning Capabilities:** To effectively tackle universal visual grounding, models must possess advanced reasoning capabilities to comprehend complex instructions and infer correspondence across diverse visual contexts. Existing MLLMs, while strong in text comprehension, often lack explicit reasoning processes tailored for this multimodal grounding challenge, particularly in multi-image settings (as seen with Migician).
3. **Leveraging RL for Reasoning:** Recent breakthroughs in LLMs have demonstrated that reinforcement learning, especially rule-based methods like GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), can significantly enhance complex reasoning abilities. The authors are motivated to explore and adapt this powerful paradigm for MLLMs to improve visual reasoning.
4. **Addressing Cold-Start Problem in RL:** Initial experiments showed that pure RL on advanced MLLMs (like Qwen2-VL) for multi-image contexts struggles to generate correct reasoning due to limited intrinsic knowledge. This highlights the need for a "cold-start" initialization to guide the model, motivating the creation of a high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset.
5. **Mitigating Difficulty Bias in GRPO:** The GRPO algorithm, while effective, was observed to suffer from a "difficulty bias" where easier samples receive disproportionately high attention during training, hindering the model's ability to learn from more challenging instances. This motivated the development of a dynamic weighting strategy to address this imbalance.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond superficial visual grounding towards a deeper, reasoning-driven understanding of visual queries in highly complex and practical scenarios, enabled by a novel two-stage training approach combining CoT supervised fine-tuning with targeted reinforcement learning enhancements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniVG-R1 proposes a powerful reasoning-guided MLLM for universal visual grounding, built upon a two-stage training protocol. The base model used is Qwen2-VL-7B (and also tested with 2B).

**Overall Problem Formulation:**
Given a textual instruction $T$, a target image $I$, and several additional images $V$, the model $M$ is expected to output a bounding box $B$, defined as $B = M(T, I, V)$. Crucially, the model is guided to output not just the bounding box, but also a detailed reasoning chain.

**Stage 1: Cold Start Data Construction and Chain-of-Thought Supervised Fine-tuning (CoT-SFT)**
The purpose of this stage is to provide the model with an initial understanding of structured reasoning trajectories, addressing the "cold-start" problem where pure RL alone might struggle.

1. **High-Quality CoT Grounding Dataset Construction:**
 - **Source Data:** Randomly sampled items from the MGrounding-630k dataset, which is designed for multi-image grounding.
 - **CoT Generation:** The advanced MLLM Qwen-VL-MAX is used to generate detailed reasoning processes. For each (question, bounding box coordinates) pair, the model is prompted with a predefined CoT prompt to generate three reasoning chains in the format: `<think>thinking process here</think><answer>(x1, y1), (x2, y2)</answer>`.
 - **Best CoT Selection:** Qwen-VL-MAX evaluates and selects the best of the three generated reasoning chains.
 - **Verification:** A total of 76k samples are collected, with a manual verification of 10% achieving a 99.87% acceptance rate, ensuring high quality.
 - **Combined Data:** This 76k CoT dataset is augmented with 14k samples from RefCOCO/+/g (a single-image dataset) for the first stage.
2. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The collected CoT dataset is used to supervise fine-tune the Qwen2-VL-7B model. This trains the model to produce final bounding box predictions through a step-by-step reasoning process, mimicking the generated CoTs.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning for Enhancing Reasoning Capability**
This stage refines the model's reasoning abilities using rule-based reinforcement learning, building upon the CoT-SFT model from Stage 1.

1. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - **Algorithm Choice:** GRPO is chosen over methods requiring an additional critic model (like PPO), as it uses a direct verification function to assess response correctness, simplifying the training process.
 - **Reward Functions:** For a given question $q$ and sampled response $o_i$ (which includes both reasoning chain and bounding box):
 - **Accuracy Reward ($r_{acc}$):** Calculated as the Intersection over Union (IoU) between the model's predicted bounding box ($B_{pred}$) and the ground truth bounding box ($B_{GT}$). This encourages precise localization.
 - **Format Reward ($r_{format}$):** A binary reward (1 or 0) that ensures the model's output strictly adheres to the required format: `<think>...</think><answer>(x1, y1), (x2, y2)</answer>`.
 - **Total Reward:** $r_i = r_{acc,i} + r_{format,i}$.
 - **Advantage Calculation:** For a group of $N$ responses $\{o_1..., o_N\}$ sampled from the policy model $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$, the advantage $A_i$ for each response $o_i$ is computed by normalizing its reward relative to the group: $A_i = (r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_1..., r_G\})) / \text{std}(\{r_1..., r_G\})$.
 - **Objective Function:** GRPO updates the policy $\pi_\theta$ to maximize an objective function that incorporates this advantage and a KL divergence regularization term to prevent large policy shifts: $J_{GRPO}(\theta) = E_{q, \{o_i\}} \left[ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\pi_\theta(o_i|q)}{\pi_{\theta_{old}}(o_i|q)} A_i - \beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta||\pi_{ref}) \right]$.
 - **Prompting:** A specific prompt is added: "First output the thinking process in <think> </think> tags and then output the bounding box in <answer> </answer> tags."
 - **Data Mix:** For stage 2, a smaller set of 7k MGrounding-630k samples is mixed with 3k RefCOCO samples.

2. **Difficulty-Aware Weight Adjustment Strategy:**
 - **Problem Identification:** During GRPO training, a "difficulty bias" emerges where easier samples (high mean IoU across responses) become predominant, leading to most policy updates being derived from these instances and less focus on harder samples.
 - **Proposed Solution:** A dynamic weighting strategy is introduced to adjust the influence of each sample based on its difficulty.
 - **Difficulty Coefficient ($\phi$):** A coefficient $\phi$ is defined to be negatively correlated with the sample's mean IoU ($mIoU = \text{mean}(r_{acc,1}..., r_{acc,G})$). Harder samples (lower $mIoU$) receive higher weights.
 - **Modified Objective:** The GRPO objective is modified to include $\phi(mIoU)$ as a multiplier for each sample: $J_{GRPO}(\theta) = E_{q, \{o_i\}} \left[ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \phi(mIoU) \frac{\pi_\theta(o_i|q)}{\pi_{\theta_{old}}(o_i|q)} A_i - \beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta||\pi_{ref}) \right]$.
 - **Function Forms:** The authors experimented with various functions for $\phi$, including $-\log(mIoU)$, $(1.0-mIoU)^2$, and $\exp(1-mIoU)$, with $\exp(1-mIoU)$ proving most effective.

This two-stage approach, combining initial supervised learning of reasoning paths with subsequent reinforcement learning focused on selecting correct reasoning (and mitigating difficulty bias), aims to imbue UniVG-R1 with robust reasoning capabilities for universal visual grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the superior performance and generalizability of UniVG-R1 across various visual grounding benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on MIG-Bench (Multi-Image Grounding)

- UniVG-R1 achieves **state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance** on the revised MIG-Bench, surpassing the previous leading model, Migician, by a significant margin of **9.1%** (average accuracy).
- It excels across all 10 subtasks within MIG-Bench (Spontaneous Grounding: Static, Robust, Common; Referential Grounding: OTMV, Region, Refer, GG, Reason, Co-Re, Textual, Visual+Textual).
- **Data Efficiency:** UniVG-R1 achieves these SOTA results using a considerably smaller dataset: 100k curated CoT samples (90k for Stage 1, 10k for Stage 2), compared to Migician's 1.2 million samples (roughly 8.3% of Migician's data size).
- **Model Efficiency:** UniVG-R1 (based on Qwen2-VL-7B) outperforms Qwen2-VL-72B by 75.12%, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed training methodology even with a much smaller parameter count.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Performance on Reasoning Grounding Benchmarks

- UniVG-R1 demonstrates strong generalizability, achieving an **average improvement of 23.4%** in zero-shot performance across four diverse reasoning-guided grounding benchmarks.
- It consistently outperforms baselines (Qwen2-VL-7B and Migician) on:
 - **Single-image reasoning grounding:** LISA-Grounding (+27.8%) and LLMSeg-Grounding (+15.9%).
 - **Video reasoning grounding:** ReasonVOS-Grounding (+25.3%) and ReVOS-Grounding (+20.3%).
- This indicates that the learned reasoning capabilities are transferable to novel scenarios and different modalities (images and videos) without further fine-tuning.

#### 4.3. Performance on RefCOCO/+/g (Conventional Single-Image Grounding)

- UniVG-R1 achieves the **best average performance of 88.20%** on the conventional RefCOCO/+/g datasets, slightly outperforming Migician (88.16%) and Qwen2-VL-7B (87.96%).
- Notably, it performs exceptionally well on **RefCOCOg**, which is known for its more complex and ambiguous referring expressions, further validating the model's enhanced ability to comprehend intricate instructions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Training Stages:**
 - **Cold-Start Necessity:** Pure RL training on 21k data samples significantly underperforms CoT-SFT (by 7.07%), confirming the necessity of a supervised cold-start for effective reasoning exploration, especially in multi-image contexts.
 - **Data Scaling:** Increasing CoT-SFT data from 21k to 90k samples improves average performance by 4.5%.
 - **CoT-SFT vs. Standard SFT:** CoT-SFT with reasoning chains (69.00%) outperforms standard SFT (67.30%) trained only with coordinate annotations, particularly on reasoning-heavy subtasks like "Reason" (+11.34%) and "Co-Re" (+10.27%). This confirms the value of explicit reasoning guidance.
 - **GRPO vs. CoT-SFT (Stage 2):** Applying GRPO in Stage 2 improves performance by 1.88% over merely continuing CoT-SFT, demonstrating the additional benefit of RL in guiding the model towards optimal reasoning paths.
 - **Difficulty-Aware Weight Adjustment:** The proposed strategy provides an additional ~2.0% improvement over standard GRPO, proving its effectiveness in addressing the difficulty bias and focusing training on challenging samples.
- **Different Difficulty Functions:** Among the tested functions for the difficulty coefficient $\phi$, $\exp(1-\text{mIoU})$ yielded the highest average performance (72.62%), making it the default choice.
- **Model Size Impact:** For the smaller Qwen2-VL-2B model, GRPO training yielded a more substantial performance boost (from 41.09% to 56.57%) compared to the 7B model. This suggests that GRPO is particularly beneficial for less capable models, helping them to develop logical reasoning abilities. The difficulty-aware strategy further amplifies this gain for the 2B model.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Observations

- Visualizations (Figure 4) demonstrate that UniVG-R1 provides more accurate bounding box predictions and clear, explicit reasoning explanations compared to Qwen2-VL-7B and Migician. This underscores its superior comprehension of implicit and complex multi-context instructions.

In summary, UniVG-R1 sets a new benchmark for universal visual grounding, not just in performance but also in data efficiency and generalizability, attributed to its innovative reasoning-guided RL approach and the effective mitigation of training biases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniVG-R1 represents a significant step forward in visual grounding and multimodal AI, with considerable potential impact across research and real-world applications.

#### 5.1. Academic Significance

- **Advancing Universal Visual Grounding:** The paper rigorously defines and effectively tackles the challenging "universal visual grounding" task, moving beyond the limitations of single-image, simple instruction scenarios. This pushes the research frontier towards more human-like, nuanced understanding of visual queries.
- **Pioneering Reasoning-Guided RL in MLLMs for Grounding:** UniVG-R1 is among the first to successfully integrate rule-based reinforcement learning (GRPO) with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to enhance complex reasoning capabilities specifically for multimodal grounding tasks. This validates the transferability of successful LLM reasoning paradigms to the visual domain.
- **Novel Data Contribution:** The construction of a high-quality 90k CoT grounding dataset, meticulously annotated with detailed reasoning chains and validated by MLLMs and human review, is a valuable public resource for future research in reasoning-guided visual tasks.
- **Methodological Innovation for RL Training:** The identification and effective mitigation of the "difficulty bias" in GRPO training through a difficulty-aware weight adjustment strategy is a notable methodological contribution. This strategy can be generalized to improve other RL algorithms facing similar challenges, enhancing training efficiency and model robustness.

#### 5.2. Potential Real-world Impact

- **Enhanced Human-AI Interaction:** UniVG-R1's ability to handle implicit and complex instructions across multiple images significantly improves the naturalness and effectiveness of human-AI communication for visual tasks. This can lead to more intuitive visual assistants, smart home devices, and interactive AR/VR experiences.
- **Robust Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** For robots and autonomous vehicles, understanding commands that refer to objects across different camera views or require inferring functionality (e.g., "find the tool that can fix this part") is crucial. UniVG-R1's enhanced reasoning and multi-image capabilities could lead to more robust perception and instruction following in these systems.
- **Advanced Content Understanding and Retrieval:** In vast visual databases (e.g., e-commerce, media archives), the ability to perform complex searches like "find an item in image B that relates to the activity shown in image A" could revolutionize content organization, discovery, and targeted advertising.
- **Improved Accessibility and Support Tools:** For visually impaired users, tools that can accurately identify and describe objects based on complex verbal cues, potentially integrating information from multiple visual inputs (e.g., from different cameras or historical views), would be transformative.
- **Foundation for Future MLLM Development:** The success of UniVG-R1 in achieving SOTA results with significantly less data (compared to previous methods) suggests a path towards more data-efficient and performant MLLMs. This could accelerate the development and deployment of larger, more capable multimodal models.

In conclusion, UniVG-R1 addresses a critical bottleneck in visual grounding by imbuing MLLMs with explicit, reasoning-guided capabilities. Its impressive performance, generalizability, and data efficiency, coupled with its methodological innovations, position it as a foundational work that will likely inspire further research and drive the development of more intelligent and practical multimodal AI systems.
