# Text2Grasp: Grasp synthesis by text prompts of object grasping parts

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Dalian University of Technology developed Text2Grasp, a method for generating human-like hand grasps on objects controlled by precise text prompts specifying object grasping parts. The framework achieves high grasp quality, diversity, and 87.76% grasp part accuracy on the OakInk dataset by combining a text-guided diffusion model with a specialized contact optimization process and semi-automatic text generation via Large Language Models.

## Problem

- Existing controllable grasp synthesis methods often use ambiguous high-level intentions or task descriptions, leading to multiple possible grasps for one intention or the same grasp for different intentions.
- This ambiguity complicates data annotation for grasp datasets and hinders the precise convergence of grasp generation models.
- Many current methods prioritize maximizing hand-object contact, sometimes resulting in less natural, overly generic, or "closed-finger" grasps, lacking diversity and specific part-alignment.

## Method

- A two-stage approach combines **TextGraspDiff**, a ==text-guided conditional diffusion model==, for initial coarse grasp generation, and a subsequent ==text-guided contact optimization== for refinement.
- The method employs a ==semi-automatic text generation== process using ==Large Language Models (LLMs)== to expand template text prompts into diverse, personalized, and task-level descriptions, reducing manual annotation effort.
- A custom ==multi-modal attention module== fuses object point cloud features with text features (from ==CLIP==) within the diffusion model to enable precise part-level control, while optimization focuses on specific active fingers and weighted contact towards text-segmented object parts.

## Results

- Text2Grasp achieved superior grasp stability and reduced interpenetration, with lower penetration depth (**0.40**) and simulation displacement (**2.49**) on OakInk compared to GrabNet (**0.48** and **2.84**, respectively).
- The method demonstrated high grasp part accuracy of **87.76%** with template text and **82.32%** with personalized text on OakInk, a metric not addressed by previous methods.
- Qualitative results and diversity metrics showed Text2Grasp generates a wider variety of natural hand poses tailored to specific object shapes and text prompts, unlike methods that often produce generic grasps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Controlling grasp synthesis with precise text prompts specifying object grasping parts effectively resolves ambiguities inherent in intention- or task-level guidance, leading to more accurate and controllable grasps.
> - A two-stage pipeline, leveraging a diffusion model for diverse initial poses and a targeted, text-guided optimization, yields physically plausible grasps that align precisely with specified object parts while maintaining diversity.
> - Integrating Large Language Models for semi-automatic text generation significantly enhances the flexibility and scalability of training data and control inputs, allowing for personalized and task-level grasp synthesis without extensive manual labeling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The modeling and synthesis of hand grasps have garnered attention due to their applications in fields such as human-computer interaction, virtual reality, and robotic imitation learning. Previous research in grasp synthesis can be broadly categorized into uncontrolled and controllable methods.

Uncontrolled grasp synthesis typically focuses on generating stable hand poses for objects, often leveraging large-scale datasets of hand-object interactions (e.g., ContactDB, GrabNet, OakInk, AffordPose) and generative models like Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) or Conditional Variational Auto-Encoders (CVAEs). More recent advancements in this area have explored diffusion models (e.g., SceneDiffuser, UGG, DexDiffuser) to address limitations of CVAEs, such as posterior collapse and limited diversity. These methods produce stable grasps but do not account for task-specific requirements or human manipulation habits, which may necessitate particular grasp types for different tasks.

Controllable grasp synthesis aims to generate grasps that align with specific intentions or tasks. Existing approaches in this domain often employ datasets annotated with various human intentions (e such as use, pass, twist) or utilize task-level text descriptions as control signals. Some methods translate intentions into one-hot embeddings or word embeddings to condition grasp generation networks, while others use task-level text to predict gripper poses, primarily for parallel jaw grippers.

However, a significant challenge in existing controllable grasp synthesis methods is the inherent ambiguity associated with using broad intentions or task-level language. This ambiguity manifests in two main ways:
1. **Same intention, different grasps:** A single intention, such as "lifting a mug," might correspond to multiple distinct grasps, like holding the handle or the body.
2. **Different intentions, same grasps:** Various intentions, such as "lifting" or "twisting" a bottle, could commence with an identical initial grasping pose (e.g., holding the bottle's neck).

Such ambiguities complicate data annotation and hinder the convergence of grasp generation models. The current work addresses these limitations by shifting the control paradigm from general intentions or task descriptions to precise textual prompts specifying object grasping parts. This approach aims to provide a more direct and unambiguous control signal for dexterous human-like grasp synthesis, distinguishing it from prior methods that often rely on coarser controls or simpler gripper models. Furthermore, the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) represents an advancement in generating diverse textual inputs and deriving task-level grasps without extensive manual annotation, contributing to the broader trend of leveraging powerful language models in robotics and embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for controllable grasp synthesis that can generate physically plausible, diverse, and human-like hand grasps on objects, guided by precise textual instructions. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

First, a core motivation stems from the limitations of existing controllable grasp synthesis methods. Current approaches often rely on human intentions (e.g., "use," "pass") or high-level task descriptions (e.g., "lift a bottle") as control signals. While these offer a degree of control, they frequently suffer from ambiguity. As noted in the broader research landscape, a single intention can lead to multiple possible grasps, and conversely, different intentions might require the same initial grasp. This ambiguity complicates the training of models, leads to less precise outcomes, and increases the difficulty and cost of annotating large datasets. The authors propose to overcome this by introducing a more granular control mechanism: text prompts that explicitly specify *object grasping parts*. This part-level guidance is hypothesized to reduce uncertainty and facilitate better convergence of grasp generation networks.

Second, the motivation includes ensuring the physical plausibility and diversity of the synthesized grasps. Many existing grasp synthesis methods prioritize maximizing hand-object contact for stability, which can sometimes lead to less natural or overly generic "closed-finger" grasps. The authors aim to develop a refinement process that not only ensures stability but also maintains diversity and accurately aligns the grasp with the specific object part indicated by the text prompt. This requires moving beyond global optimization strategies to more targeted contact optimization.

Third, a significant motivation involves enhancing the practicality and flexibility of controllable grasp synthesis. Manual annotation of grasp datasets, especially with detailed text descriptions, is labor-intensive and time-consuming. The researchers are motivated to explore how Large Language Models (LLMs) can be leveraged to generate various text prompts (including personalized and task-level descriptions) semi-automatically, thereby expanding training datasets and enabling more flexible control without requiring extensive additional manual labeling. This addresses the challenge of scaling up data and application scenarios for controllable grasping.

In summary, the key objectives are:
1. To develop a grasp synthesis method (Text2Grasp) guided by text prompts of object grasping parts, offering more precise and unambiguous control than intent-based or task-level guidance.
2. To design a two-stage process, incorporating a text-guided diffusion model for coarse grasp generation and a targeted hand-object contact optimization, to ensure both plausibility and diversity while maintaining part-level accuracy.
3. To integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) to facilitate semi-automatic text generation, enabling grasp synthesis guided by personalized and task-level descriptions without requiring further manual annotations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Text2Grasp method is a two-stage approach designed to generate controllable, plausible, and diverse human-like grasps guided by text prompts specifying object parts.

#### 3.1. Semi-automatic Text Generation for Grasp

To mitigate the labor-intensive nature of manual text annotation, the authors developed a semi-automatic approach to generate text prompts for existing hand grasp datasets.
- **Template Definition:** A predefined text template, "Grasp the [Object Part] of the [Object Category]," is used.
- **Object Category Extraction:** The object category is directly obtained from existing datasets.
- **Object Part Determination:** For each grasp in a dataset, the contact between the hand mesh and the object's point cloud is calculated. Each point on the object is assigned a contact label. The "Object Part" for that specific grasp is then identified as the part with the highest number of contact points. This allows for the automatic generation of a template text prompt for each grasp.
- **Personalized Text Generation via LLM:** A Large Language Model (LLM) is utilized to expand these template texts into more diverse and personalized descriptions. Given a prompt like "Please write [N] sentences with the same meanings as [template]," the LLM infers a list of plausible alternative text descriptions. During training, one description is randomly selected from this list for each grasp. This process aims to enhance the flexibility of grasp synthesis control and enables task-level grasp synthesis by allowing the LLM to interpret task descriptions into specific grasping actions (e.g., "grasping the mug’s handle for drinking").

#### 3.2. Text to Grasp via Diffusion Model (TextGraspDiff)

The core of the initial grasp generation is TextGraspDiff, a conditional diffusion model.
- **Input and Output:** It takes an object point cloud and a part-level text prompt as input. The output is a 66-dimensional hand grasp vector `g`. This vector comprises:
 - MANO model pose parameters (`g_θ` ∈ R^48^)
 - MANO shape parameters (`g_β` ∈ R^10^)
 - Distance between object and hand centroids (`g_dis` ∈ R^3^)
 - Finger vector (`g_f` ∈ R^5^), which indicates which of the five fingers are involved in the grasp (e.g., for thumb, index, middle finger).
- **Forward Process:** This process progressively adds infinitesimal Gaussian noise to a ground-truth grasp vector `g_0` over `T` steps, resulting in a sequence of noised data `g_t`. If `T` is sufficiently large, `g_T` approximates a standard Gaussian distribution.
- **Reverse Process:** A neural network `G_θ` is trained to reverse this process, predicting `g_0` from `g_t` conditioned on the object point cloud `o` and text prompt `l`.
- **Denoising Network Architecture (G_θ):** The network employs a Transformer backbone.
 - **Encoders:** PointNet++ is used to extract features from the object point cloud (`f_p`), and a pretrained CLIP model extracts features from the text prompt (`f_l`).
 - **Multi-Modal Attention Module:** A Transformer-based Multi-Modal Attention Module is designed to fuse these features. The point cloud feature `f_p` acts as the query, while the text feature `f_l` provides the key and value, enabling precise control over grasp locations.
 - **Timestep and Condition Fusion:** A timestep-residual block and cross-attention mechanism integrate the diffusion step `t` and the combined condition `c` (from `o` and `l`) into the network.
 - **Loss Function:** The training objective minimizes the L2 norm between the predicted `g_0` and the ground-truth `g_0`.
After training, the denoising network can generate a coarse grasp vector for new objects and text descriptions, which is then used to create a hand mesh via the MANO model.

#### 3.3. Text-guided Contact Optimization

The coarse grasp pose from TextGraspDiff is refined through a contact optimization process to ensure physical plausibility, stability, diversity, and alignment with the specified object part. This differs from methods that globally optimize for maximum contact.
- **Hand Finger Perception:** Instead of considering all fingers, the optimization focuses on the specific fingers indicated as active by the `g_f` vector generated by TextGraspDiff. The loss `L_hc` minimizes the distance between the hand vertices belonging to these active fingertips and the closest points on the object.
- **Text-guided Object Part Perception:** A pretrained text-guided segmentation network, TextSegNet, is used to segment the input object point cloud into targeted (`O_c`) and non-targeted (`O_nc`) parts based on the text prompt. During optimization, the contact loss `L_c` assigns higher weights to the targeted part (`O_c`), directing the active fingers towards the specified region. TextSegNet itself uses PointNet++ and CLIP for encoding and a multi-layer fully connected network for segmentation, trained with negative log-likelihood loss.
- **Additional Losses:**
 - `L_ptr`: Penalizes hand-object interpenetration by minimizing the distance of object points inside the hand to the closest hand surface points.
 - `L_angle`: Enforces realistic joint angle limitations for the hand.
 - `L_self`: Penalizes self-collisions within the hand.
- **Overall Optimization Objective:** The final optimization minimizes a weighted sum of these four loss components (`L_c`, `L_ptr`, `L_angle`, `L_self`) to refine the MANO pose (`g_θ`), shape (`g_β`), and hand-object distance (`g_dis`).

This two-stage methodology, combining a conditional diffusion model with a specialized, text-guided optimization, aims to generate high-quality, controllable grasps that accurately reflect human manipulation intent expressed through specific object part descriptions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate Text2Grasp focused on assessing its performance in grasp quality, diversity, and, critically, grasp part accuracy, contrasting it with state-of-the-art methods and analyzing its individual components through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison with State-of-the-Art

The method was evaluated on two public datasets, OakInk (in-domain) and AffordPose (out-of-domain), against GrabNet, a prominent method in grasp generation.
- **Grasp Quality:** On the OakInk dataset, Text2Grasp demonstrated lower penetration depth (0.40 vs. 0.48 for template text, 0.41 vs. 0.48 for personalized text) and lower simulation displacement (2.49 vs. 2.84 for template, 2.49 vs. 2.84 for personalized) compared to GrabNet. This indicates superior grasp stability and reduced hand-object interpenetration. On the out-of-domain AffordPose dataset, Text2Grasp showed comparable generalization ability, with lower simulation displacement (2.93 for template, 3.00 for personalized vs. 3.09 for GrabNet) and comparable penetration.
- **Diversity:** Text2Grasp achieved entropy and cluster size values for diversity that were comparable to and, in some cases, outperformed the ground truth on OakInk (e.g., cluster size of 4.70/4.74 vs. GT 4.11 and GrabNet 2.57), suggesting the generation of more diverse and natural grasps.
- **Grasp Part Accuracy:** A novel metric introduced to evaluate control, Text2Grasp achieved a grasp part accuracy of 87.76% with template text and 82.32% with personalized text on the OakInk dataset. This demonstrates the method's capability for precise part-level grasp control, a feature absent in GrabNet.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Visualizations confirmed the quantitative findings:
- **Plausibility and Naturalness:** Text2Grasp generated plausible hand grasps for a variety of objects, including those with simple and complex shapes. For specific parts (e.g., bottle cap), the generated grasps showed smaller penetration and more natural contact compared to GrabNet.
- **Diversity:** Text2Grasp produced a wider variety of hand poses for objects, being better suited to specific object shapes (e.g., eyeglasses, knife), in contrast to GrabNet's tendency to generate predominantly closed-hand, five-finger grasps.
- **Controllability:** The method successfully generated grasps precisely guided by various types of text inputs, including template text (e.g., "Grasp the cap of the cylinder bottle") and personalized text (e.g., "Clasp the bottle's cylindrical body firmly"). This showcased its ability to enable fine-grained object part grasping.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of individual components:
- **VAE vs. Diffusion Model:** Replacing the diffusion model with a VAE for grasp synthesis (without optimization) resulted in lower grasp quality (higher penetration and displacement) and reduced grasp part accuracy (77.38% for VAE vs. 85.25% for Diffusion). The diffusion model also produced more diverse hand poses.
- **Multi-Modal Attention:** The custom Multi-Modal Attention module, designed for fusing text and object point features, outperformed a simpler feature addition approach across all metrics, particularly in grasp part accuracy (85.25% vs. 83.44%), confirming its effectiveness for precise control.
- **Optimization Strategy:**
 - Adding a global optimization (where all fingers are directed toward the object) significantly improved grasp quality (e.g., 35.46% reduction in penetration volume, 20.33% reduction in simulation displacement) but limited diversity and could lead to inaccuracies in contact parts.
 - The proposed optimization, based on finger perception and text-guided object part perception, maintained grasp quality while achieving greater diversity.
 - Crucially, the text-guided object part perception specifically enhanced grasp part accuracy.
 - When compared to GrabNet's RefineNet, the proposed optimization demonstrated better performance in detail and achieved a superior balance between penetration and simulation displacement.

These results collectively indicate that Text2Grasp achieves both high grasp quality and accurate part-level control through its two-stage architecture and novel text-guided optimization strategy, while also demonstrating robust generalization and diversity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Text2Grasp method presents a significant advancement in the field of controllable grasp synthesis, primarily by addressing the inherent ambiguities of previous intention- or task-level guidance systems. Its primary significance lies in providing a more precise and natural mode of interaction for generating human-like grasps.

**Enhanced Precision and Control:** By introducing text prompts that specify exact object grasping parts, the method moves beyond abstract intentions or generalized task descriptions. This part-level control offers a higher degree of granularity, enabling the synthesis of grasps that are not only stable but also accurately aligned with specific interaction requirements. This precision is critical for applications where the exact point of contact matters, such as delicate manipulation tasks or specific tool usage.

**Improved Plausibility and Diversity:** The two-stage approach, which combines a text-guided diffusion model with a specialized hand-object contact optimization, contributes to generating grasps that are both physically plausible (low penetration, high stability) and diverse. The optimization, tailored to specific fingers and text-described object parts, prevents the generation of generic, maximum-contact grasps, leading to more natural and varied hand configurations.

**Reduced Annotation Burden and Increased Flexibility:** The leveraging of Large Language Models (LLMs) for semi-automatic text generation is a practical innovation. This capability allows for the expansion of training data with personalized and task-level text descriptions without requiring extensive manual annotations. This directly tackles a major bottleneck in developing robust, data-driven grasp synthesis systems, making the method more scalable and adaptable to new scenarios and user preferences.

**Broad Applicability in Embodied AI:** The potential impact of Text2Grasp extends to various domains:
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** Enables more intuitive and expressive control over virtual hands in VR/AR environments, allowing users to specify exactly how they want to interact with virtual objects.
- **Robotics:** Provides a robust foundation for imitation learning and task planning in dexterous robotics, allowing robots to learn and execute human-like manipulation strategies based on natural language instructions. This could lead to more adaptable and intelligent robotic assistants.
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Enhances realism in virtual environments by allowing characters or avatars to perform nuanced and context-aware grasps, improving immersion and interaction fidelity.

**Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a precedent for integrating deep semantic understanding from text (via LLMs) with geometric and physical reasoning (through diffusion models and contact optimization) for complex embodied interactions. It opens avenues for future research into dynamic manipulation, generalization to novel object categories, and more complex multi-step tasks, which the authors acknowledge as current limitations. The ability to articulate desired manipulation through natural language at a fine-grained level offers a promising direction for creating more intelligent and user-friendly autonomous systems.
