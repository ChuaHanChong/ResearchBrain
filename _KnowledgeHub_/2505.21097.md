# Thinker: Learning to Think Fast and Slow

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Cambridge, The University of Hong Kong, and Shanghai AI Lab introduce the "Thinker task," a structured approach to Large Language Model reasoning that decomposes problem-solving into distinct stages for intuition, verification, deliberation, and summarization. This method improved accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks by 6.7% to 11.1% (relative) for 1.5B and 7B parameter models, demonstrating enhanced efficiency and more direct reasoning.

## Problem

- Current Reinforcement Learning-trained LLMs exhibit inefficient and verbose Chain-of-Thought reasoning, often with redundant self-correction steps.
- Standard RL reward mechanisms struggle with precise temporal credit assignment, often rewarding both useful and futile search paths equally within long reasoning sequences.
- LLMs frequently lack explicit training signals to develop "intuition" for quickly identifying promising solution paths and "verification" for confidently evaluating correctness.

## Method

- Proposed the "**Thinker** task," a four-stage, multi-turn Reinforcement Learning environment for LLMs, inspired by human dual-process cognition.
- Each stage (Fast Thinking, Verification, Slow Thinking, Summarization) receives specific, isolated reward signals to train distinct cognitive functions like intuition and deliberation.
- The approach utilizes Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) fine-tuning on LLMs, with pre-defined token budgets for each reasoning stage.

## Results

- The Thinker agent demonstrated relative accuracy improvements of **6.7%** (Qwen2.5-**1.5B**) and **11.1%** (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-**1.5B**) on mathematical reasoning benchmarks compared to baseline models.
- "Thinker-Fast" mode achieved accuracy competitive with or exceeding baselines using significantly fewer tokens, offering improved inference efficiency.
- Training with the Thinker task led to a decrease in "reflection patterns" and a reduction in average token length for larger models, indicating more streamlined and confident reasoning processes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex reasoning into distinct, rewarded stages enables more precise temporal credit assignment, fostering the development of specific cognitive abilities in LLMs.
> - Training for separate "fast" (intuitive) and "slow" (deliberative) thinking processes enhances both reasoning accuracy and token efficiency.
> - The "Summarization" stage is essential for distilling correct, extensive reasoning into concise patterns, thereby improving the model's intuition for subsequent fast thinking.
