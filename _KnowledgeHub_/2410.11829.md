# MMFuser: Multimodal Multi-Layer Feature Fuser for Fine-Grained Vision-Language Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> MMFuser is a module designed to enhance fine-grained visual understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by efficiently fusing multi-layer features from a single Vision Transformer. It achieved average score increases of 1.5 points for 7B models and 0.9 points for 13B models across 12 diverse benchmarks, particularly improving performance on OCR tasks and reducing object hallucinations.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs primarily rely on deep features from vision encoders, leading to a loss of low-level, fine-grained visual details necessary for tasks like OCR or precise object identification.
- This reliance results in performance deficiencies such as Optical Character Recognition (OCR) errors and object hallucinations.
- Existing solutions like integrating multiple vision encoders are computationally intensive and redundant, while simple multi-layer feature fusion methods show limited success due to semantic misalignment.

## Method

- **MMFuser** is proposed as a ==plug-in module== within the standard ==ViT-MLP-LLM== architecture to efficiently integrate ==multi-layer features== from a single Vision Transformer (ViT).
- It employs a dynamic, ==attention-based mechanism== where deep, semantically aligned features act as ==queries== to extract fine-grained details from shallow, detail-rich features.
- The fusion mechanism combines ==cross-attention== for detail extraction, ==self-attention== for feature interaction, and final integration with learnable weights, utilizing ==deformable attention== for efficiency.

## Results

- MMFuser-enhanced LLaVA-1.5-7B and 13B models consistently surpassed their baselines, showing average score increases of **1.5 points** and **0.9 points** respectively across **12** diverse multimodal benchmarks.
- The models demonstrated substantial improvements in fine-grained tasks, with an **18-point** increase on OCRBench for the 7B model and a **12-point** increase for the 13B model, along with improved CIDER scores for region captioning and better referring expression comprehension.
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining features from multiple layers and the specific cross- and self-attention mechanisms are crucial for MMFuser's effectiveness, with deformable attention providing optimal performance and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Significant fine-grained visual details from a single Vision Transformer can be effectively harnessed by strategically fusing features from multiple layers, obviating the need for multiple vision encoders.
> - Simple concatenation or averaging of multi-layer features is inadequate due to the semantic misalignment between shallow and deep features; a more sophisticated, attention-driven fusion is essential.
> - Deformable attention provides an efficient and effective mechanism for integrating diverse multi-layer features, contributing to improved performance and computational practicality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen substantial progress, enabling models to interact with and understand complex human intentions across visual and linguistic modalities. These models typically integrate pre-trained vision encoders with Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform a range of vision-language tasks.

A common approach in current MLLMs involves using a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT) or its variants as the vision encoder, with features from its final or penultimate layer serving as the visual representation for the LLM. While these deep features capture high-level semantic information effectively, they often lack the low-level, fine-grained details necessary for tasks requiring precise visual understanding, leading to issues such as Optical Character Recognition (OCR) errors and object hallucinations.

To address this limitation, some researchers have explored two main strategies:
1. **Integrating Multiple Vision Encoders:** This approach combines features from several diverse pre-trained vision encoders (e e.g., CLIP, DINOv2, ConvNeXt) to provide complementary visual information. While demonstrating improved performance in fine-grained tasks, this method introduces model redundancy and increases computational overhead, leading to questions regarding its efficiency and necessity.
2. **Scaling Up Vision Encoders:** This involves increasing the parameter count of the vision encoder to enhance its representational capacity.

The presented work, MMFuser, identifies an alternative, underutilized aspect within the current MLLM landscape: the potential of multi-layer features from a *single* vision encoder. While classic image and video processing tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation commonly leverage multi-layer features to combine shallow (detail-rich) and deep (semantically rich) visual information, this practice is not widespread in MLLMs. Prior attempts at simple fusion methods (e.g., element-wise averaging, concatenation) of multi-layer features in MLLMs have shown limited success due to semantic misalignment between shallow features and the text feature space.

MMFuser positions itself as a solution to this identified gap. It aims to efficiently integrate multi-layer features from a single ViT to enhance fine-grained visual understanding without the computational burden and redundancy of multi-encoder ensembles. By focusing on a structured fusion mechanism that maintains semantic alignment, MMFuser seeks to maximize the expressive potential of existing vision encoders, thereby offering a more lightweight and flexible alternative within the MLLM ecosystem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the fine-grained visual understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by more effectively utilizing the internal representations of single vision encoders.

The motivation for this objective stems from several observations and identified limitations within existing MLLMs:

- **Loss of Fine-Grained Details:** Current mainstream MLLMs primarily rely on the final-layer feature map of their vision encoders (e.g., Vision Transformers or ViTs) for visual representation. While these deep features are rich in high-level semantic information and are well-aligned with the text feature space, they inherently lose much of the low-level, fine-grained image details (e.g., edges, textures, small text). This loss directly contributes to performance deficiencies in tasks requiring precise visual perception, such as accurately reading text (OCR errors) or identifying specific objects (object hallucinations).
- **Underutilization of Single Vision Encoder Potential:** The authors observe that even a single ViT generates diverse visual representations across its different layers. Shallow layers are noted for capturing richer low-level details, while deeper layers excel at high-level semantics. However, current MLLMs largely ignore these shallow features, thus underutilizing the full expressive potential of their chosen vision encoder.
- **Inefficiency of Existing Solutions:** While integrating multiple pre-trained vision encoders (ensemble methods) has been explored to enhance fine-grained visual representations, these approaches introduce significant model redundancy and increase computational overhead. The authors question the necessity of employing multiple vision encoders if the potential within a single encoder is not fully exploited.
- **Challenges of Simple Feature Fusion:** Initial experiments with straightforward methods to combine multi-layer features (e.g., concatenation, averaging) from a single ViT yielded minimal performance improvements. This is attributed to a crucial issue: shallow features, despite their detail richness, exhibit poor semantic alignment with the text feature space, making it difficult for LLMs to interpret them effectively when simply merged with deep, semantically aligned features.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives of the MMFuser project are:
- To **reveal and address the underutilization** of expressive potential within single vision encoders in MLLMs, particularly concerning the fine-grained information present in shallow features.
- To **design and develop MMFuser**, a multi-layer feature fuser that efficiently integrates deep and shallow features from Vision Transformers.
- To ensure that this fusion process **dynamically extracts missing fine-grained details from shallow features** using semantically aligned deep features as queries, thereby preserving semantic coherence while enriching the overall visual representation.
- To provide a **flexible and lightweight solution** for enhancing MLLM visual understanding that avoids the redundancy and computational complexity of multi-encoder ensemble methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MMFuser methodology focuses on enhancing visual representations in MLLMs by strategically fusing multi-layer features from a single Vision Transformer (ViT). This approach aims to combine the fine-grained details from shallow layers with the semantic coherence of deep layers, addressing the limitations of using only the final-layer features.

**A. Core Architecture and Integration:**
MMFuser is designed as a plug-in module within the standard "ViT-MLP-LLM" architecture commonly found in open-source MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5. It is positioned between the frozen ViT and the trainable MLP projector. This allows MMFuser to process the multi-layer visual features extracted by the ViT before they are aligned with the text embedding space by the MLP projector and subsequently fed into the Large Language Model (LLM).

**B. Multi-Layer Feature Extraction:**
From the pre-trained ViT, MMFuser extracts $L$ feature maps, denoted as $F = [F_1, F_2..., F_L]$. Each feature map $F_i$ has dimensions $R^{N \times D}$, where $N$ represents the number of ViT patches and $D$ is the dimension of the image feature embeddings. The choice of $L$ is configurable, with a default setting of 5 layers in the experiments.

**C. Dynamic Feature Fusion Mechanism (MMFuser Module):**
The MMFuser module employs an attention-based mechanism to integrate features. The process involves three main steps:

1. **Cross-Attention for Detail Extraction:**
 - The deepest feature map, $F_L$, which possesses strong semantic alignment with the text space, is designated as the **query** input.
 - The shallow and intermediate feature maps ($F_1$ to $F_{L-1}$), which are rich in fine-grained details but less semantically aligned, are concatenated along the channel dimension to form a combined feature map $X = \text{Concat}(F_1, F_2..., F_{L-1})$. This $X$ serves as the **key and value** input.
 - A cross-attention operation is then performed: $F_{ca} = \text{Attention}(\text{norm}(F_L), \text{norm}(X))$. This mechanism dynamically extracts fine-grained details from the shallow features ($X$) that are relevant to the high-level semantics of the deep features ($F_L$), producing a feature map $F_{ca}$ that is enriched with details while maintaining semantic guidance. Layer normalization ($\text{norm}(\cdot)$) is applied before the attention mechanism.

2. **Self-Attention for Feature Interaction:**
 - To further enhance feature interaction and emphasize salient information within the newly detailed representation, a self-attention mechanism is applied to $F_{ca}$: $F'_{sa} = \text{Attention}(\text{norm}(F_{ca}), \text{norm}(F_{ca}))$.
 - The output $F'_{sa}$ is then combined with $F_{ca}$ using a learnable vector $\gamma_2 \in R^D$: $F_{sa} = F_{ca} + \gamma_2 F'_{sa}$. This step allows for adaptive weighting of the self-attention output.

3. **Final Feature Integration:**
 - The refined feature map $F_{sa}$ is integrated with the original deep feature $F_L$ using another learnable vector $\gamma_1 \in R^D$: $F_{visual} = F_L + \gamma_1 F_{sa}$. This final step ensures that the enriched fine-grained features are blended with the foundational high-level semantic features, resulting in the enhanced visual feature $F_{visual}$ that serves as the input to the MLP projector. Both $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are initialized to 0.

**D. Attention Mechanism:**
For the attention operations within MMFuser, deformable attention is employed as the default mechanism. This choice is based on its ability to provide superior performance and computational efficiency compared to global attention, particularly in handling dense feature maps. The deformable attention is configured with 4 sampling points and 16 heads.

**E. Training Strategy:**
The training of MMFuser follows a two-stage process, consistent with the LLaVA-1.5 baseline to ensure fair comparison:

1. **Pre-training Stage:**
 - **Dataset:** LLaVA-LCS-558K dataset (558K image-caption pairs).
 - **Frozen Components:** The Vision Encoder (ViT) and the Large Language Model (LLM) are kept frozen.
 - **Trainable Components:** Only the MLP projector and the MMFuser module are trained.
 - **Objective:** To align the enhanced visual features generated by MMFuser with the input embedding space of the LLM.

2. **Fine-tuning Stage:**
 - **Dataset:** LLaVA-Instruct-665K dataset (665K instruction-following data from various sources like GQA, TextCaps, ShareGPT).
 - **Frozen Components:** The Vision Encoder (ViT) remains frozen.
 - **Trainable Components:** The MLP projector, MMFuser, and the LLM are trained end-to-end.
 - **Objective:** To enable the MLLM to follow instructions and perform complex vision-language tasks using the enriched visual representations.

**F. Implementation Details:**
- **Baseline Model:** LLaVA-1.5, which uses CLIP-ViT-L-336px as the vision encoder and Vicuna-v1.5 (7B or 13B) as the LLM, connected by a two-layer MLP projector.
- **Hyperparameters:** Consistent with LLaVA-1.5, including AdamW optimizer, cosine decay learning rate scheduler with a 0.03 warmup ratio, and no weight decay. Global batch sizes are 256 for pre-training and 128 for fine-tuning. Learning rates are 1e-3 for pre-training and 2e-5 for fine-tuning. Both stages are trained for one epoch.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MMFuser involved a series of experiments comparing its performance against the LLaVA-1.5 baseline across various benchmarks, along with ablation studies to understand its design choices.

**A. Initial Feature Fusion Experiments:**
Prior to MMFuser, the authors explored simple feature fusion methods (concatenation, element-wise average, weighted average, and Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) integration) using the LLaVA-1.5-13B model. These methods showed limited or inconsistent performance improvements over the baseline. For instance, concatenation yielded an average score of 63.5, slightly above the baseline's 63.0, but other methods showed marginal or even decreased performance on some tasks. This confirmed the hypothesis that simple fusion strategies are insufficient due to semantic misalignment between shallow and deep features. In contrast, MMFuser achieved an average score of 64.9, indicating its effectiveness.

**B. General Multimodal Benchmarks:**
MMFuser demonstrated consistent performance enhancements across a suite of 12 academic and comprehensive multimodal benchmarks.
- **7B Models:** The MMFuser-enhanced LLaVA-1.5-7B model surpassed the original LLaVA-1.5-7B on 11 out of 12 benchmarks, achieving an average score of 61.8 compared to 60.3.
- **13B Models:** Similarly, the MMFuser-enhanced LLaVA-1.5-13B model outperformed its baseline on 10 out of 12 benchmarks, with an average score of 64.1 versus 63.2.
- **Notable improvements (13B model):** MMFuser achieved a 3.8-point increase on VizWiz, a 53.9-point increase on MME (reaching 1585.2), and a 2.2-point increase on MMBench-EN (reaching 69.9). Performance on other benchmarks like POPE, SEED-Bench, and MMVet also showed improvement.

**C. OCRBench Results:**
On the OCRBench, a specialized benchmark for OCR-related Visual Question Answering, MMFuser models showed substantial improvements.
- The 7B MMFuser model improved the overall score by 18 points (from 297 to 315) over LLaVA-1.5-7B.
- The 13B MMFuser model improved the overall score by 12 points (from 331 to 343) over LLaVA-1.5-13B.
These results indicate MMFuser's enhanced capability in processing fine-grained visual details crucial for accurate text recognition.

**D. Region-Level Benchmarks (Region Captioning and Referring Expression Comprehension):**
MMFuser's ability to capture fine-grained information was further validated on regional-level tasks.
- **Region Captioning:** MMFuser demonstrated improved CIDER scores. The 7B model improved by an average of 2.5 points, and the 13B model by 3.9 points, indicating more accurate and descriptive captions for specific image regions.
- **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC):** MMFuser consistently outperformed LLaVA-1.5 in locating target objects based on descriptions (Precision@0.5). The 7B model showed an average improvement of 5.7 points. This highlights MMFuser's superior spatial localization and grounding capabilities due to its detailed visual representations.

**E. Ablation Studies:**
- **Layer Combination:** An ablation study on the MMFuser-13B model revealed that incorporating features from multiple layers significantly improved visual representation. Uniformly sampling features from diverse layers (e.g.,) achieved the highest average performance (65.4), underscoring the benefit of combining information from various receptive fields.
- **Attention Mechanisms:** MMFuser's effectiveness was consistent across different attention mechanisms. Deformable attention, with its linear complexity, not only outperformed global attention (quadratic complexity) but also yielded the most significant performance gains (average score 65.4), confirming its suitability for efficient and effective feature fusion.
- **Internal Module Designs:** Incremental additions to the LLaVA-1.5-13B baseline demonstrated the contribution of each MMFuser component:
 - Adding cross-attention alone (to extract fine-grained details) improved performance (e.g., 1.3 points on POPE).
 - Further adding self-attention (to refine feature interaction) led to additional gains (e.g., 2.8 points on VizWiz, 28.1 points on MME). This confirmed the critical role of each design element in enhancing visual representation.

**F. Qualitative Comparisons and Visualization:**
Qualitative examples illustrated MMFuser's superior performance in complex captioning (identifying small, blurred text and objects), OCR (reducing hallucinations and improving small text recognition), and visual grounding (more accurate bounding box predictions with higher Intersection over Union (IoU), especially for small objects). Feature map visualizations further showed that MMFuser effectively highlighted foreground objects and aligned semantic information from shallow features with deep feature semantics, enriching visual representations for the LLM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMFuser research contributes significantly to the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and holds several potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Understanding:** The primary significance of MMFuser lies in its demonstrated ability to improve MLLMs' perception of fine-grained image details. By effectively integrating shallow, detail-rich features with deep, semantically aligned features, MMFuser directly addresses a known bottleneck in current MLLMs, which often struggle with tasks like Optical Character Recognition (OCR), precise object recognition, and detailed captioning. This enhancement is crucial for real-world applications where subtle visual cues are vital.

- **Efficient and Lightweight Solution:** MMFuser offers an efficient alternative to existing methods that aim to improve fine-grained visual understanding. Unlike multi-encoder ensemble approaches that introduce redundancy and increase computational overhead by integrating multiple vision encoders, MMFuser maximizes the potential of a *single* Vision Transformer (ViT). This efficiency makes it a more practical and scalable solution for integrating into various MLLM architectures, potentially reducing resource requirements for deployment and training.

- **Generalizability and Adaptability:** The modular design of MMFuser, which fits within the common "ViT-MLP-LLM" architecture, indicates its generalizability across different MLLMs. Its successful application to LLaVA-1.5 suggests it can be readily adopted by other similar frameworks. The paper also notes its potential adaptability for processing video data, expanding its impact beyond static images.

- **Improved Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** The consistent performance gains observed across a wide array of benchmarks—including general VQA, comprehensive multimodal benchmarks, OCR, region captioning, and referring expression comprehension—highlight MMFuser's broad applicability. This suggests that the improved visual representations benefit a spectrum of vision-language tasks, making MLLMs more robust and versatile.

- **Guidance for Future MLLM Development:** MMFuser challenges the prevailing trend of solely scaling up models or combining multiple complex components. By demonstrating the significant untapped potential within a single, existing vision encoder, the research encourages a re-evaluation of how visual features are extracted and utilized in MLLMs. This could inspire future research to focus on more sophisticated internal feature processing mechanisms rather than relying solely on external scaling or ensemble strategies.

In summary, MMFuser represents a step forward in making MLLMs more perceptive and efficient by intelligently leveraging the hierarchical nature of visual features. Its design provides a flexible, high-performing, and resource-conscious method for improving fine-grained vision-language understanding, with implications for a wide range of applications requiring detailed visual interpretation.
