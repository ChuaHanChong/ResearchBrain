# Synthesizing Diverse Human Motions in 3D Indoor Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> DIMOS is a reinforcement learning framework that populates 3D indoor scenes with virtual humans who navigate and interact with objects. Policies act in the latent space of a CVAE motion-primitive model learned from mocap, using a local walkability map for collision-aware locomotion and marker plus signed-distance-field goals for sitting and lying. It beats SAMP and GAMMA on contact, penetration and perceptual naturalness.

## Problem

- Learning-based interaction synthesis needs paired human-motion and scene capture, which is costly, hard to record, and covers only a narrow slice of plausible interactions.
- RL motion-synthesis policies such as GAMMA produce natural goal-reaching locomotion but ignore scene geometry, so bodies frequently inter-penetrate furniture and cannot sit or lie.
- Conditional-VAE methods trained on paired data generalize poorly to unseen object shapes, orientations and body initializations, and show artifacts like visible foot-ground skating.

## Method

- Latent motion-primitive action space: a ==CVAE== trained on AMASS plus SAMP decodes each sampled latent into a 0.25s marker motion clip, with policies learned by actor-critic ==PPO== plus a KL prior term.
- Scene-aware locomotion policy whose state carries a 16x16 binary ==walkability map== over a local 1.6m x 1.6m area, paired with a penetration reward counting non-walkable cells overlapping the body box.
- Fine-grained interaction policy conditioned on ==signed-distance-field== values and gradients at body markers, with goal guidance from COINS-generated static interaction bodies, trained jointly on sit/lie-down and stand-up tasks.

## Results

- Locomotion in cluttered synthetic scenes reaches foot-contact score **0.99** against GAMMA's **0.94** and SAMP's **0.84**, with the best penetration score **0.95** but slower completion (6.43s vs 3.87s).
- On 10 unseen ShapeNet objects, sitting takes 4.09s vs SAMP's 8.63s with contact **0.97** vs **0.89** and mean penetration **1.91** vs **11.91**; lying penetration drops **44.77** to **9.90**.
- In Replica scene sequences, **85%** of perceptual-study comparisons favor the method over SAMP; ablations show removing the penetration reward raises mean penetration from **1.91** to **16.80**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Casting motion synthesis as RL over a generative model's latent space removes the need for paired motion-scene capture while keeping mocap-level naturalness.
> - Compact scene encodings, an occupancy grid for navigation and signed distances at body surface markers, suffice to teach collision avoidance without explicit physics simulation.
> - Reward-only penetration avoidance is a soft constraint: contact artifacts persist, and skills with scarce mocap coverage, like lying, stay noticeably less natural.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in computer graphics, animation, and artificial intelligence: **simulating realistic and autonomous human-scene interactions in complex 3D environments.** This capability is fundamental for a wide array of applications, including:
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Populating virtual worlds with believable, interactive characters.
- **Computer Games:** Enhancing character AI and animation realism.
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** Creating diverse, high-quality training data for machine learning tasks like human pose estimation, action recognition, and scene understanding, especially where real-world data is scarce or expensive to collect.
- **Robotics:** Understanding human behavior for better human-robot collaboration and navigation.
- **Architectural Design:** Simulating human flow and interaction within proposed spaces.

The research landscape for human motion synthesis can be broadly categorized:

- **Traditional Character Animation:** Methods like motion graphs and motion matching produce high-quality animations. However, they are typically manual, labor-intensive, and lack the autonomy and spontaneity required for simulating diverse, natural behaviors in dynamic environments. They struggle to generalize to unseen interaction scenarios.

- **Learning-Based Human Motion Synthesis:** Recent advancements leverage deep learning, including CVAEs, Transformers, and Diffusion Models, to generate diverse and controllable motions conditioned on various inputs (e.g., text, audio, goals). While impressive for general motion, many of these focus on isolated actions or simpler scenes.

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based Motion Synthesis:** RL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training autonomous agents to perform complex tasks, including generating physically plausible character movements. These methods learn control policies to achieve goals, but often face limitations in complex environments:
 - **Collision Avoidance:** Characters often inter-penetrate with scene geometry.
 - **Interaction Complexity:** Limited to basic locomotion or simple interactions (e.g., hitting a box), rather than fine-grained human-object interactions like sitting or lying. For example, GAMMA, a related work from the same research group, excels at generalizable locomotion across diverse human body shapes but often collides with complex scene objects and cannot perform specific object interactions.

- **Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) Synthesis:** This area specifically focuses on how humans interact with objects and environments.
 - **Static HSI:** Works like COINS and PLACE focus on generating plausible static human poses in interaction with scene objects, often based on semantics or geometry. These provide crucial *target states* but don't generate dynamic motion.
 - **Dynamic/Motion HSI:** Directly relevant to this paper. Existing methods often rely on costly and difficult-to-capture paired human motion and 3D scene data. This data scarcity limits the diversity and generalization capability of the learned models. SAMP is a notable baseline that synthesizes stochastic, scene-aware motions, but the authors highlight its limitations in terms of motion artifacts (foot-ground skating) and challenges in handling unseen scenarios.

This paper explicitly positions itself to bridge the gap between general RL-based motion synthesis (like GAMMA) and specific human-object interaction synthesis (like SAMP). It aims to overcome the data scarcity issue by decoupling the learning process, using a generative motion model trained on general motion capture data, and then leveraging RL with novel scene and interaction-aware policies guided by easily generatable static interaction poses. This approach seeks to combine the strengths of previous works while mitigating their respective weaknesses (e.g., GAMMA's collision issues and lack of interaction; SAMP's artifacts and data dependency).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, reinforcement learning-based framework that enables virtual humans to **autonomously navigate and interact with objects in complex 3D indoor scenes in a realistic and diverse manner.**

The key specific objectives and motivations underpinning this work are:

- **Overcoming Data Scarcity for Interaction:** Existing methods for dynamic human-scene interaction often rely on high-quality, simultaneously captured human motion and 3D scene data. Such data is expensive, difficult to acquire, and inherently limited in covering the full spectrum of plausible human-scene interactions in complex real-world environments. The authors are motivated to bypass this dependency by using a more autonomous learning approach.

- **Enabling Autonomous and Spontaneous Motion:** Traditional character animation workflows, while producing high-quality results, lack the autonomy to generate spontaneous and diverse human motions that adapt to varying environments. The goal is to create virtual humans that can react and interact with their surroundings naturally.

- **Generalization to Complex and Unseen Scenes:** Previous RL-based motion synthesis methods, like GAMMA, often perform well for locomotion in simple environments but struggle with collision avoidance and object interaction in cluttered, complex scenes with functional furniture. A core objective is to allow virtual humans to navigate and interact plausibly in environments significantly different from their training data.

- **Achieving Fine-Grained Human-Object Interactions:** Beyond basic locomotion, the paper aims to synthesize specific, detailed interactions such as sitting on a chair, getting up, or lying on a sofa. This requires precise control over the human body's pose and contact points with objects. The motivation here is to provide more granular control and realism than prior methods.

- **Ensuring Physical Plausibility:** A common challenge in synthesized motions is the occurrence of artifacts like foot-ground skating or body-scene inter-penetration. The authors aim to minimize these artifacts to produce more physically plausible and visually natural motions.

- **Composing Diverse Sequences of Activities:** Real human behavior involves continuous sequences of actions (e.g., walk to a chair, sit, then get up and walk to a sofa, lie down). The method seeks to seamlessly combine locomotion and various object interactions into coherent and diverse activity sequences.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the gap between highly realistic, but manually crafted, animations and autonomous, but often less sophisticated or generalizable, learned behaviors. It seeks to bridge this by creating a robust, learning-based framework that allows virtual humans to truly "inhabit" complex digital environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method is a novel **reinforcement learning (RL)-based framework** designed to synthesize continuous, stochastic, and plausible human motions in 3D scenes, including both navigation and fine-grained object interactions.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Core Components

- **Body Representation:** The SMPL-X body model is used, represented by 67 body surface markers (SSM67). This marker-based representation is crucial for both the motion model and the fine-grained interaction goals.
- **Motion Model / Actions:** The framework leverages a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)-based generative motion primitive, as introduced in GAMMA. This model, trained on a combination of AMASS and SAMP motion capture datasets, learns a latent motion action space. Each latent variable `z` from this CVAE acts as an "action" that decodes into a short (0.25 seconds) motion clip.
- **RL Framework:** An actor-critic architecture with the PPO algorithm is used to train the policy network. The policy learns to map perceived states to latent actions that guide the human's movement.

#### 3.2. State Design (Key Innovation for Scene and Interaction Awareness)

The state `s_t` provided to the RL agent at each time step is critical for scene and interaction awareness: `s_t = (X_s, I, G)`.
- `X_s`: Represents the motion history (M past frames of body markers).
- `I` (Person-Scene Interaction Feature): This is dynamically designed based on the task:
 - **For Locomotion:** A `2D occupancy-based local walkability map` (1.6m x 1.6m, 16x16 grid) centered at the human's pelvis and aligned with their forward orientation. Each cell indicates whether the area is walkable or occupied by an obstacle. This allows the policy to "sense" nearby obstacles for collision avoidance.
 - **For Fine-grained Interaction:** Features derived from the `Signed Distance Field (SDF)` of the scene object. Specifically, the signed distance from each body marker to the object surface, and the gradient direction of the SDF at each marker location. This compactly encodes the precise proximity and contact relationship between the human body and the interaction object.
- `G` (Goal-Reaching Feature): Also task-dependent:
 - **For Locomotion:** Normalized direction vectors from each body marker to the target pelvis location.
 - **For Fine-grained Interaction:** Normalized direction vectors and distances from current body markers to the target goal body markers (e.g., the specific pose for sitting on a chair).

#### 3.3. Reward Design (Key Innovation for Physical Plausibility and Goal Attainment)

The reward function `r = r_goal + r_contact + r_pene` drives the learning process:
- `r_goal`: Task-specific reward for reaching the defined goal (e.g., closeness to target pelvis for locomotion, closeness to target markers for interaction).
- `r_contact`: Encourages realistic foot-ground contact and penalizes foot skating. This is essential for preventing common animation artifacts.
- `r_pene` (Penetration Avoidance): This is another crucial novel component.
 - **For Locomotion:** Penalizes the overlap between the human's 2D bounding box and non-walkable areas on the walkability map.
 - **For Fine-grained Interaction:** Penalizes negative SDF values for body vertices within the interaction object, effectively penalizing inter-penetration.

#### 3.4. Goal Guidance for Object Interaction

A significant innovation for fine-grained interaction is the use of `marker-based interaction goals`. Instead of requiring dynamic interaction data, the method leverages **COINS** to generate plausible *static* human bodies interacting with objects given semantic labels (e.g., "sit on chair"). These generated static body poses provide the detailed marker targets (`g_m`) that the interaction policy aims to achieve. This decouples the need for dynamic interaction data from the training process, addressing a major data bottleneck.

#### 3.5. Navigation and Composition

- **Path-finding:** For long-term navigation in complex 3D scenes, the system integrates `navigation mesh-based path-finding algorithms` (like A*). This allows generating intermediate waypoints that guide the human agent through the environment collision-free, before triggering specific object interactions.
- **Composition:** The framework allows for the continuous synthesis of sequences of activities by seamlessly switching between the learned locomotion policy (to reach an object) and the object interaction policy (to interact with it), and then potentially switching back to locomotion.

#### 3.6. Training Strategy

- **Modular Training:** Separate policies are trained for locomotion and interaction.
- **Locomotion Training:** Trained in synthetic cluttered scenes with randomized objects (from ShapeNet) and random collision-free initial-target location pairs.
- **Interaction Training:** Uses static human-object interaction data from PROX, retargeted to diverse ShapeNet objects for generalization. Crucially, the training environment randomly swaps initial and goal bodies to enable learning both "sit/lie down" and "stand up" motions.
- **Tree Sampling for Inference:** During testing, due to the stochastic nature of the policies, tree-based sampling (similar to GAMMA) is employed. Multiple latent actions are sampled, and only the best K are preserved based on the defined rewards, ensuring high-quality output motions.

This comprehensive methodology, particularly the innovative state and reward designs tailored for scene awareness and fine-grained interaction, allows the system to learn generalizable and physically plausible human behaviors without relying on extensive dynamic interaction datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical evaluations demonstrating the effectiveness of their proposed method across locomotion, fine-grained object interaction, and continuous interaction sequences. The results consistently highlight improvements in motion naturalness, diversity, and physical plausibility compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Locomotion in 3D Scenes

- **Baselines:** SAMP and GAMMA.
- **Metrics:** Time to target, average distance to target, foot contact score, and locomotion penetration score (percentage of body vertices inside walkable areas).
- **Results:**
 - **Foot Contact:** Our method achieved a significantly higher contact score (0.99) compared to GAMMA (0.94) and SAMP (0.84), indicating better foot-floor contact and less foot skating.
 - **Locomotion Penetration:** Our method also showed improved collision avoidance with a higher penetration score (0.95) than GAMMA (0.94) and SAMP (0.94).
 - **Time:** While our method took slightly longer to reach targets (6.43s) than GAMMA (3.87s), the authors attribute this to a "more conservative behavior" learned by the scene-aware policy, which prioritizes collision avoidance, similar to how humans might navigate carefully in complex environments.
 - **Qualitative:** Visual demonstrations confirmed that our scene-aware locomotion policy successfully avoided collisions with obstacles, unlike GAMMA which frequently inter-penetrated.

#### 4.2. Fine-Grained Human-Object Interaction

- **Baseline:** SAMP.
- **Test Scenario:** Interactions (sitting, lying) with 10 unseen ShapeNet objects of various shapes and orientations, with random initial body placements.
- **Metrics:** Time to complete, foot contact score, and interaction penetration score (mean and maximum negative SDF values for body vertices).
- **Results:**
 - **Completion Time:** Our method completed interaction tasks much faster (sit: 4.09s, lie: 4.20s) than SAMP (sit: 8.63s, lie: 12.55s). This indicates better generalization to unseen initial body configurations and objects, allowing the policy to initiate interactions more promptly.
 - **Foot Contact:** Consistently higher contact scores for our method (sit: 0.97, lie: 0.78) compared to SAMP (sit: 0.89, lie: 0.73), leading to more natural-looking motions.
 - **Interaction Penetration:** Our method exhibited significantly lower mean and maximum penetration values (e.g., sit mean: 1.91 vs. SAMP's 11.91), demonstrating superior physical plausibility by minimizing body-object inter-penetration.
 - **Qualitative:** The method successfully generalized to diverse object shapes and initial body configurations, producing varied and realistic interactions (e.g., sitting from different angles, lying on L-shaped sofas).

#### 4.3. Interaction Sequences in 3D Scenes

- **Test Scenario:** Continuous sequences of locomotion and object interactions in real scene scans (Replica, Paris street).
- **Metrics:** Foot contact, locomotion penetration, interaction penetration (mean/max), and perceptual study (user preference for naturalness).
- **Results:**
 - Across all physical metrics, our method consistently outperformed SAMP (e.g., contact: 0.96 vs. 0.87; mean interaction penetration: 3.40 vs. 15.61).
 - **Perceptual Study:** In side-by-side comparisons, users chose our method as perceptually more natural 85% of the time, significantly outperforming SAMP's 15%.
 - **Qualitative:** The system demonstrated the ability to synthesize coherent, long-term sequences of activities in complex real-world environments.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Purpose:** To evaluate the importance of the novel foot-ground contact reward (`r_contact`) and penetration avoidance reward (`r_pene`).
- **Results:**
 - Removing `r_pene` led to a significant increase in human-scene penetration.
 - Removing `r_contact` resulted in notably inferior contact and penetration scores, accompanied by "erratic synthesized motions that either remain suspended in the air or penetrate the floor."
- **Conclusion:** These studies definitively underscore the critical role of the carefully designed reward terms in achieving physically plausible and natural motions.

In summary, the experimental results robustly support the paper's claims, demonstrating that the proposed RL-based framework successfully synthesizes diverse, physically plausible, and perceptually natural human motions across various tasks in complex 3D indoor scenes, outperforming existing baselines in key metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of 3D human motion synthesis and human-scene interaction, offering a robust and generalizable framework for populating virtual environments with intelligent, interactive agents.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Autonomous and Diverse Human-Scene Interaction:** The most significant contribution is enabling virtual humans to autonomously navigate and perform diverse, fine-grained interactions (locomotion, sitting, lying, standing up) in complex 3D indoor scenes. This moves beyond traditional methods that are either manual or limited to simple motions/environments.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** By decoupling the need for costly dynamic human-scene interaction motion capture data, the method offers a scalable approach. It cleverly leverages general motion capture data for motion primitives and static interaction poses (generated by COINS) as fine-grained goals. This is a crucial step towards synthesizing behaviors for situations where dynamic interaction data is impractical to obtain.
- **Enhanced Physical Plausibility:** The novel scene-aware policies and reward designs, particularly for collision avoidance (using walkability maps and SDF) and foot-ground contact, significantly reduce common animation artifacts like penetration and foot skating. This leads to more believable and high-quality synthesized motions.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The training approach, using randomized synthetic scenes and retargeted interaction data, allows the learned policies to generalize well to out-of-distribution scenarios, including unseen objects of various shapes and orientations, and different initial body positions and poses. This is vital for real-world application where environments and interactions are highly varied.
- **Coherent Activity Sequences:** The ability to seamlessly compose locomotion and interaction policies into continuous sequences of activities (e.g., walking to a chair, sitting, then standing up and walking to a sofa) makes the generated behaviors much more realistic and useful for complex simulations.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** The method can vastly improve the realism and immersion of virtual experiences by populating them with intelligent, interactive non-player characters (NPCs) that react realistically to the environment. This is especially valuable for training simulations, virtual tours, and social VR applications.
- **Computer Games:** Game developers can leverage this framework to create more sophisticated and believable character AI, leading to dynamic gameplay where characters intelligently navigate and interact with game environments without extensive hand-authored animations.
- **Synthetic Data Generation for AI:** This is a powerful tool for generating large-scale, diverse, and annotated synthetic datasets. Such data can be used to train and validate various machine learning models (e.g., for human pose estimation, action recognition, scene understanding, or even robotic imitation learning), particularly in scenarios where real-world data collection is challenging or biased.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** The realistic simulation of human behavior and interaction with environments can provide valuable insights for developing robots that can safely and effectively operate alongside humans in complex spaces. It can inform robot navigation and manipulation planning.
- **Architectural Design and Urban Planning:** Architects and urban planners can use this technology to simulate human flow and interaction patterns within proposed designs, allowing for pre-visualization and optimization of spaces based on realistic human behavior.
- **Daily-Living Activity Simulation:** Beyond specific applications, the framework provides a foundation for simulating complex daily activities for research in ergonomics, biomechanics, or even social sciences.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as residual penetration issues (due to reward-based optimization rather than hard constraints), challenges with limited data for certain complex motions (e.g., lying), and current restrictions to flat-floor scenes, this work establishes a robust foundation for the next generation of realistic, autonomous virtual human agents. The potential for impact across multiple scientific and industrial domains is substantial.
