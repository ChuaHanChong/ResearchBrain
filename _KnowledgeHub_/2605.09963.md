# Learning to Perceive "Where": Spatial Pretext Tasks for Robust Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University introduced Spatial Prediction (SP), a plug-in pretext task that explicitly models geometric relationships in self-supervised learning, enabling models to perceive relative positions and scales between image regions. This approach consistently enhanced spatial reasoning capabilities and improved performance across various computer vision tasks, including a +25.0% gain on fine-grained classification for MoCo v3.

## Problem

- Existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods primarily focus on object-invariant representations or local reconstruction, often neglecting the explicit modeling of spatial structure and relationships.
- Invariance-based methods can inadvertently suppress spatial variation, reducing the learned representations' sensitivity to relative position and scale.
- Reconstruction-based methods typically operate at a patch level without an explicit global coordinate system, limiting their ability to encode structured spatial dependencies.

## Method

- Introduced Spatial Prediction (SP), a plug-and-play pretext task that trains models to predict the relative position and scale between two local views sampled from the same input image.
- Implemented a rejection sampling strategy for view curation to ensure semantically rich, balanced, and non-trivial spatial target distributions.
- Developed a parameter-free ==cross-attention==-like mechanism that interacts the classification token from the reference view with patch tokens from both views, followed by an MLP to regress predicted relative position and scale.

## Results

- SP consistently improved Top-1 classification accuracy across MAE, MoCo v3, and DINO on ImageNet-1K (e.g., MAE improved by +**8.0%**, from **44.6%** to **52.6%**).
- Enhanced robustness to out-of-distribution conditions, reducing mean Corruption Error on ImageNet-C and showing gains on ImageNet-R (+**2.8%** for MoCo v3) and ImageNet-Sketch, indicating a reduction in texture bias.
- Significantly improved performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks, including a reduction in L2 error for position prediction (e.g., MAE from 0.92 to 0.61) and substantial gains in jigsaw permutation prediction accuracy (e.g., MoCo v3 from **69.87%** to **90.24%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling continuous geometric relationships, such as relative position and scale, provides a critical inductive bias to enhance both semantic quality and geometric awareness in self-supervised representations.
> - The proposed parameter-free cross-attention mechanism effectively enforces stronger supervision on token interactions for spatial reasoning without introducing additional learnable parameters.
> - Joint supervision of both relative position and scale provides complementary information, leading to more comprehensive learning of spatial structure than supervising either objective alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of self-supervised learning (SSL) in computer vision aims to learn robust visual representations from unlabeled data. Current dominant SSL paradigms primarily focus on two aspects: learning object-invariant representations and local reconstruction. Invariance-based methods, such as contrastive learning and self-distillation (e.g., MoCo, DINO), train models to produce consistent representations across different augmented views of an image, promoting robustness to transformations like cropping and scaling. Reconstruction-based methods, such as Masked Image Modeling (MIM) approaches (e.g., MAE, BEiT), learn by recovering masked or corrupted image regions, often focusing on low-level appearance statistics at a patch level.

However, a recurring limitation across these approaches is their tendency to neglect the explicit modeling of spatial structure and relationships among object parts or image regions. Invariance-based methods, by design, often suppress spatial variation, potentially reducing sensitivity to relative position and scale. Reconstruction methods, while capturing fine-grained details, typically operate on local patches without a direct global coordinate system or explicit encoding of part-to-part geometry. This can result in learned representations that are biased towards low-level textures rather than holistic scene structure and exhibit limited sensitivity to geometric relationships.

This limitation contrasts with human visual perception, which strongly relies on spatial reasoning, including inferring relative positions, scales, and arrangements of objects and their parts. Such spatial understanding is fundamental for various real-world computer vision tasks, including object detection, scene understanding, 3D reconstruction, and visual navigation.

Earlier attempts at incorporating spatial cues into SSL involved heuristic pretext tasks like relative patch position prediction or jigsaw puzzle solving. While these methods injected spatial information, they were often handcrafted and less aligned with modern, large-scale Vision Transformer (ViT)-based SSL frameworks. More recent works, such as I-JEPA and DropPos, integrate spatial awareness via positional embeddings, treating position as a conditioning factor rather than explicitly modeling geometric relations.

This paper addresses the gap by introducing Spatial Prediction (SP), a method designed to explicitly model geometric relationships in a continuous space, serving as a plug-in for existing SSL frameworks. It contrasts with concurrent work like PART, which also predicts relative position and scale via regression but as a standalone objective, while SP is designed as a regularizer. A key distinction highlighted by the authors is SP's use of a rejection sampling strategy for view curation, aiming for balanced and informative spatial distributions, which is not explicitly controlled in some related methods. By addressing the explicit modeling of spatial information, this work seeks to provide a more balanced inductive bias for SSL, leading to representations that are both semantically robust and geometrically aware.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while contemporary self-supervised learning (SSL) models have achieved considerable success in learning semantic representations, they frequently overlook the explicit modeling of spatial structure and relationships within visual scenes. Humans possess a robust capacity for spatial perception, enabling them to comprehend the relative positions, scales, and arrangements of objects and their constituent parts. This capacity for reasoning about part-to-part relationships is foundational for a comprehensive understanding of scene composition and is critical for real-world tasks such as object detection, 3D reconstruction, depth estimation, and visual navigation.

The authors identify specific limitations in existing SSL approaches:
1. **Invariance-driven methods**: Techniques like contrastive learning and self-distillation enforce consistency across augmented views, which can inadvertently suppress spatial variation by treating transformations (e.g., cropping, scaling) as nuisances. This reduces the learned representations' sensitivity to relative position and scale.
2. **Reconstruction-based methods**: Approaches such as Masked Image Modeling (MIM) focus on recovering masked regions and capturing local appearance statistics. However, they typically operate at a patch level without an explicit global coordinate system, thereby limiting their ability to encode structured spatial dependencies.
These limitations lead to representations where the geometric structure of visual scenes remains under-constrained, potentially biasing models towards low-level texture cues rather than holistic shape and compositional understanding.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the paper are:
1. **Introduce Spatial Prediction (SP)**: To propose a novel, spatially-aware pretext task that explicitly models geometric relationships between pairs of local regions from the same image. This is achieved through regression in a continuous geometric space, predicting relative position and scale.
2. **Enhance Spatial Reasoning in SSL**: To encourage SSL models to capture fine-grained spatial dependencies and the compositional structure of visual scenes, moving beyond object-invariant categorical semantics. This aims to provide a more balanced inductive bias that integrates both semantic robustness and geometric sensitivity.
3. **Develop a Flexible Integration Mechanism**: To design SP as an architecture-agnostic, decoupled plug-in that can be seamlessly integrated into diverse existing SSL frameworks (e.g., MoCo v3, DINO, MAE) without modifying their core architectures or objectives, thereby introducing an auxiliary spatial reasoning branch.
4. **Comprehensive Evaluation**: To rigorously evaluate the effectiveness of representations learned with SP on a broad range of standard computer vision tasks, including image recognition, fine-grained classification, semantic segmentation, and depth estimation, as well as assessing robustness to out-of-distribution conditions like occlusion and corruption.
5. **Establish Diagnostic Benchmarks for Spatial Awareness**: To introduce and utilize two novel diagnostic benchmarks specifically designed to probe a model's spatial reasoning abilities: (a) a position and scale prediction task between image patch pairs, and (b) a jigsaw understanding task requiring patch reordering, image reconstruction, and subsequent object recognition. These benchmarks aim to provide direct evidence of improved spatial structure and geometric awareness beyond conventional SSL metrics.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to advance SSL towards learning more structured, geometrically informed, and generalizable visual representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Spatial Prediction (SP)**, a plug-and-play pretext task designed to augment existing SSL frameworks with explicit geometric supervision. SP operates by training a model to predict the relative position and scale between two local views sampled from the same input image.

#### 3.1. Curating Pairs of Augmented Local Views

To generate training data for SP, two local views are extracted from a single source image, denoted as a reference view ($I_r$) and a target view ($I_t$). This sampling process incorporates a **rejection sampling strategy** guided by three criteria:
1. **Semantic Content**: Views must be sufficiently large to retain semantic information but local enough to represent parts rather than the whole image.
2. **Target Distribution**: The ground-truth relative position and scale targets are sampled to be approximately uniform, mitigating bias towards trivial solutions (e.g., frequently predicting "center" or "same size").
3. **Non-trivial Displacement**: A minimum spatial displacement between views is enforced to prevent excessive overlap, which could lead to easy "shortcut" predictions.

After sampling, both $I_r$ and $I_t$ are resized to a fixed resolution, and independent color augmentations are applied. Crucially, no additional spatial transformations (e.g., rotation) are used to preserve the ground-truth spatial relationships. The ground-truth relative position ($p$) and scale ($s$) are defined and normalized with respect to the reference view's dimensions ($H_r, W_r$), ensuring scale-invariance of the geometric targets.

#### 3.2. Spatial Representation Learning

The SP module shares the encoder ($\psi(\cdot)$) of the underlying SSL framework. Given $I_r$ and $I_t$, the encoder produces their respective patch tokens ($Z_r, Z_t$) and classification tokens ($z_r, z_t$). The process is designed to mimic reference-conditioned spatial inference, where $I_r$ acts as an anchor for localizing $I_t$.

A **parameter-free cross-attention-like mechanism** is introduced for this interaction:
- The classification token $z_r$ from the reference view serves as the query.
- The patch tokens $Z_r$ and $Z_t$ from both views act as keys and values.
- This computes reference-aware ($h_r$) and target-aware ($h_t$) features. Unlike standard cross-attention, this mechanism directly uses the raw token embeddings without additional linear projections, which the authors suggest enforces stronger supervision on token interactions for spatial reasoning and avoids additional learnable parameters.
- The resulting features $h_r$ and $h_t$ are concatenated and passed through a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), termed the spatial predictor ($\theta(\cdot)$), to regress the predicted relative position ($\hat{p} \in \mathbb{R}^2$) and scale ($\hat{s} \in \mathbb{R}^2$).

#### 3.3. Training Objectives and Implementation Details

SP is integrated as an auxiliary task, meaning its loss ($L_{SP}$) is added to the base SSL objective ($L_{base}$):
$L_{total} = L_{base} + L_{SP}$
The spatial loss $L_{SP}$ is an $\ell_2$ regression loss for both position and scale:
$L_{SP} = \lambda_p \| \hat{p} - p \|_2^2 + \lambda_s \| \hat{s} - s \|_2^2$
The weights $\lambda_p$ and $\lambda_s$ are set to 0.1 to balance the two terms.

**Integration into Existing SSL Frameworks:**
- **MAE + SP**: The SP branch shares the base MAE encoder. The MAE reconstruction loss ($L_{MAE}$) and $L_{SP}$ are computed concurrently.
- **MoCo v3 + SP**: The online encoder of MoCo v3 is shared with the SP branch. Gradients from both $L_{MoCo}$ and $L_{SP}$ update this online encoder.
- **DINO + SP**: The student encoder of DINO is shared with the SP branch. Critically, gradients from $L_{SP}$ only update the student encoder, while the teacher encoder is updated solely via exponential moving average (EMA), preserving DINO's self-distillation mechanism.

**Experimental Setup:**
- Models are pre-trained from scratch on CIFAR-100 or ImageNet-1K.
- Hardware: Two NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs, batch size 256.
- Optimizer: AdamW with cosine learning-rate schedule.
- Hyperparameters: Default configurations of original SSL implementations are maintained for fairness.
- Backbones: ViT-S/16 for MoCo v3 and DINO; ViT-B/16 for MAE.
- Evaluation: Linear probing for most downstream tasks, fine-tuning for semantic segmentation.

#### 3.4. Diagnostic Benchmarks

Two novel tasks are introduced to specifically evaluate spatial reasoning:
1. **Position and Scale Prediction Task**: Directly measures the model's ability to infer relative geometry between two image patches (evaluated using L2 distance).
2. **Jigsaw Understanding Task**: An image is partitioned into a 3x3 grid, and patches are shuffled using a predefined set of 1,000 permutations. Models must predict the permutation index (1,000-way classification), reconstruct the image, and then perform recognition on the restored input. This assesses the ability to recover spatial layouts and leverage them for semantic understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Spatial Prediction (SP) consistently enhances both the spatial reasoning capabilities and the semantic quality of representations learned by various self-supervised learning (SSL) models.

#### 4.1. Performance on Standard Computer Vision Tasks (Table 1)

- **In-Domain Image Classification**: SP consistently improved Top-1 classification accuracy across all evaluated SSL models (MAE, MoCo v3, DINO) on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K. For instance, MAE improved by +8.0% on IN-1K (from 44.6% to 52.6%), and MoCo v3 improved by +5.0% (from 61.1% to 66.1%). This indicates that explicit spatial supervision can enhance semantic representation learning. Qualitative visualizations of attention maps showed SP-trained models exhibiting more focused attention on semantically meaningful object regions compared to their baselines.
- **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution Data**: SP enhanced robustness across various ImageNet-derived benchmarks.
 - On ImageNet-C (common corruptions), SP reduced the mean Corruption Error (mCE) for all backbones (e.g., MAE: 108.5 → 104.8; MoCo v3: 85.6 → 81.4), suggesting improved resilience to noise and blur.
 - On ImageNet-R (artistic renditions) and ImageNet-Sketch (sketch-based images), which emphasize shape over texture, SP yielded notable gains for MAE and MoCo v3. For MoCo v3, accuracy improved by +2.8% on IN-R and +2.3% on Sketch. This indicates SP introduces a structural inductive bias, reducing models' reliance on texture cues.
 - On ImageNet-Occlusion (synthetic occlusion), SP improved accuracy for all models (e.g., MAE: 36.7% → 41.6%), demonstrating better spatial reasoning under partial visibility.
- **Transfer Learning for Fine-grained Classification**: SP consistently improved transfer learning performance, with particularly substantial gains on fine-grained datasets. MoCo v3 with SP, for example, achieved a +25.0% improvement on Flowers-102 (from 63.7% to 88.7%). This suggests that modeling part-level spatial relationships benefits tasks requiring subtle visual distinctions.
- **Semantic Segmentation**: SP consistently improved mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on PASCAL VOC for all SSL methods (e.g., MoCo v3: 58.5 → 60.2). This finding indicates that SP enhances spatial grounding in learned features, leading to better pixel-level predictions and improved object boundaries.
- **Depth Estimation**: SP consistently reduced Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) on NYU v2 (e.g., DINO: 57.3 → 55.8). This suggests that SP encourages representations that capture 3D geometric information, beneficial for tasks involving spatial inference from images.

#### 4.2. Performance on Spatial Reasoning Benchmarks (Table 2)

- **Spatial Prediction (Position and Scale)**: SP consistently reduced L2 errors for both relative position and scale prediction across all SSL backbones. For MAE, position L2 error decreased from 0.92 to 0.61, and scale L2 error from 0.39 to 0.35. These results directly indicate that SP provides explicit geometric supervision, enabling models to encode more precise spatial relationships than those relying on implicit positional embeddings alone. Qualitative visualizations confirmed that SP-trained models produced spatial predictions closely matching ground truth.
- **Jigsaw Understanding**: SP substantially improved performance on the jigsaw understanding task.
 - **Permutation Prediction Accuracy**: Large gains were observed for permutation classification accuracy (e.g., MoCo v3: 69.87% → 90.24%; MAE: 77.95% → 98.58%). This demonstrates a significant strengthening of the models' ability to infer global spatial configurations from disorganized local patches.
 - **Recognition on Reconstructed Images**: The improved spatial reasoning translated to higher object recognition accuracy on images reconstructed according to the predicted permutations (e.g., MAE: 39.19% → 48.21%). This suggests that the recovered spatial structures are both geometrically accurate and semantically meaningful.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

Ablation studies using MoCo v3 on CIFAR-100 provided insights into SP's design choices:
- **Joint Supervision**: Jointly supervising both relative position and scale outperformed individual supervision terms (position-only or scale-only), indicating that these objectives provide complementary information essential for comprehensive spatial structure learning.
- **Projection-free Cross-Attention**: The proposed parameter-free cross-attention mechanism for spatial feature extraction yielded superior performance compared to variants using linear projections for queries, keys, and values, or simple concatenation of features. This suggests that preserving raw token interactions and enforcing structured cross-view attention is more effective for learning spatial relationships.

Overall, the findings indicate that SP provides an effective mechanism to inject explicit spatial inductive bias into SSL, leading to more structured, robust, and generalizable visual representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Spatial Prediction (SP) as a spatially aware pretext task represents a notable contribution to the field of self-supervised learning (SSL). Its significance lies in addressing a previously under-constrained aspect of visual representation learning: the explicit modeling of geometric relationships and spatial structure.

The research demonstrates that SP, designed as a simple and architecture-agnostic plug-in, consistently enhances the performance of diverse SSL frameworks (MoCo v3, DINO, MAE) across a broad spectrum of computer vision tasks. These improvements are not limited to spatial reasoning alone but extend to fundamental areas such as:
- **Improved Semantic Representations**: The observed gains in in-domain image classification suggest that explicit spatial supervision contributes to higher-quality semantic features, with attention maps showing more focused and object-centric representations.
- **Enhanced Robustness**: SP-trained models exhibit increased robustness to common corruptions, domain shifts, and occlusions. The reduction in texture bias and improved performance on shape-dominant tasks (like ImageNet-R and Sketch) indicates that SP encourages models to rely more on structural and compositional cues.
- **Better Transferability**: Significant improvements in fine-grained classification and dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation and depth estimation highlight that SP yields spatially grounded features that transfer effectively to downstream applications. This suggests the learned representations better capture both local detail and global context necessary for precise pixel-level tasks and subtle visual distinctions.
- **Direct Spatial Reasoning Capabilities**: The newly introduced diagnostic benchmarks, spatial prediction and jigsaw understanding, provide empirical evidence that SP directly improves a model's ability to infer geometric relationships and reconstruct coherent spatial layouts. This capability goes beyond what implicit positional embeddings in standard SSL typically achieve.

The work effectively bridges the gap between learning semantic invariance and geometric awareness in self-supervised representations. By explicitly modeling "where" parts are located relative to each other, SP provides a crucial inductive bias that leads to more structured and comprehensive visual features.

The potential impact of this research is substantial. More capable vision foundation models, equipped with stronger spatial reasoning, could benefit various downstream applications:
- **Robotics and Embodied AI**: Agents operating in physical environments require robust spatial understanding for navigation, manipulation, and interaction with objects. SP could contribute to more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Assistive Technologies**: Improved spatial perception could enhance technologies for visually impaired individuals or systems requiring advanced scene understanding.
- **3D Reconstruction and Scene Understanding**: Representations that inherently capture 3D geometric structure could lead to more accurate and robust models for complex scene analysis and reconstruction.

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as SP's current focus on 2D space, suggesting extensions to 3D or temporal constraints as future work. Further exploration of advanced local view sampling strategies is also identified as a direction for optimizing SP's performance. Overall, this research provides a practical and effective mechanism for enriching self-supervised representations with explicit spatial knowledge, paving the way for more holistic and robust visual intelligence.
