# Tra-MoE: Learning Trajectory Prediction Model from Multiple Domains for Adaptive Policy Conditioning

> [!summary] Summary
> Tra-MoE trains a trajectory-prediction model for robot manipulation by jointly learning from small-scale in-domain demonstrations and large-scale out-of-domain, action-free videos via a sparsely-gated Top-1 Mixture-of-Experts architecture, paired with an adaptive policy-conditioning technique that maps predicted 2D trajectories onto image observations as learnable embeddings, outperforming dense baselines on LIBERO and real-world dual-arm tasks.

## Problem

- Naively adding large-scale out-of-domain video data to train a single dense trajectory model degrades in-domain manipulation performance rather than improving it.
- It is unclear how to fuse complementary information across environments, objects, skills, and embodiments within one unified trajectory model without optimization conflicts.
- Given a pretrained trajectory model, how to condition a downstream policy on predicted 2D trajectories flexibly and with accurate spatial alignment remains unresolved.

## Method

- ==Sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts== (**Tra-MoE**) replaces select transformer layers of the ATM track transformer with MoE blocks using ==Top-1 token-choice gating==, keeping FLOPs per token constant while scaling capacity.
- Training combines the trajectory MSE loss with an auxiliary ==router z-loss== for gating stability and an optional ==load-balancing loss==, with an empirical study of which auxiliary losses help versus hurt.
- ==Adaptive policy conditioning== encodes each predicted trajectory point as a learnable embedding in an extra image-channel mask, explicitly aligning 2D trajectories with visual observations for the downstream policy.

## Results

- On LIBERO, adding out-of-domain data raises Tra-MoE's average success rate from **51.8%** to **61.4%**, while the dense baseline drops from **57.6%** to **52.0%** under the same data.
- Dense baselines expanded in width or depth to match Tra-MoE's parameter count only reach **48.4%** and **52.5%**, both well below Tra-MoE's **61.4%**.
- Adaptive policy conditioning improves real-world dual-arm task success from **38%** (MoE only) to **56%**, and lifts LIBERO average success to **66.8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparsely-gated MoE, not just added data, is what lets a trajectory model benefit from out-of-domain video; naive dense scaling degrades in-domain performance.
> - Router z-loss stabilizes MoE gating and helps, but load-balancing loss and gating noise can hurt when domains are highly heterogeneous.
> - Explicitly aligning predicted 2D trajectories with image space via learnable per-point embeddings gives more flexible policy conditioning than hand-drawn masks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tra-MoE comes from a team spanning Nanjing University, Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, the University of Science and Technology of China, and Tongji University, whose prior work spans video-based robot policy learning and predictive pre-training. Robot learning has historically relied on small, in-domain, action-labeled demonstration sets due to data scarcity, in contrast to computer vision and NLP where models routinely pretrain on broad out-of-domain data before fine-tuning. A promising alternative paradigm — exemplified by [[2401.00025|Any-Point Trajectory Modeling (ATM)]] and [[2405.01527|Track2Act]] — learns a trajectory-prediction model from action-free internet or human videos, then uses small-scale action-labeled data only to learn a trajectory-guided policy, improving sample efficiency. However, it remained unexplored whether naively expanding the out-of-domain training pool for this trajectory model actually helps: the authors show it can hurt, motivating an architecture that captures out-of-domain benefit without out-of-domain harm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper pursues two goals: (i) design a trajectory-prediction architecture that benefits from jointly training on large-scale, heterogeneous out-of-domain video alongside small-scale in-domain demonstrations, avoiding the optimization conflicts and performance regressions that a dense model suffers under the same data mixture; and (ii) design a mechanism to condition a downstream manipulation policy on the predicted 2D trajectories more flexibly and with better spatial grounding than prior hand-crafted trajectory representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Following the ATM setup, Tra-MoE predicts any-point 2D trajectories for the next H timesteps given an image observation, a set of query points, and a language instruction, then a separate policy module consumes the predicted trajectory to output executable robot actions. The core contribution replaces select transformer blocks of the ATM track transformer with sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts blocks using a fixed Top-1 gating strategy, so each token activates exactly one of several expert feedforward networks — keeping FLOPs per token constant regardless of how many experts are added. Training uses ground-truth tracks generated by [[2307.07635|CoTracker]] and an MSE trajectory loss, augmented with a router z-loss (penalizing large gating logits for training stability, following ST-MoE) and an optional load-balancing loss (encouraging even expert utilization, following [[1701.06538|the original sparsely-gated MoE layer]]). For policy conditioning, the paper introduces an adaptive technique that instantiates each point of the predicted trajectory as a learnable embedding within an extra mask channel concatenated to the image before encoding — explicitly mapping the 2D trajectory into image space rather than relying on raw coordinates or hand-drawn strokes (as in RT-Trajectory-style conditioning).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

A comprehensive ablation on LIBERO isolates each design choice. The router z-loss meaningfully improves performance (a 4.5-point gain at the best weight), while the load-balancing loss and injected gating noise both tend to hurt under highly heterogeneous domain mixtures. Most centrally, when out-of-domain data is added to joint training, the dense Tra-baseline's average success rate drops from 57.6% to 52.0%, while Tra-MoE's rises from 51.8% to 61.4% under the identical data — demonstrating that the architecture, not merely the data, is what unlocks the benefit of out-of-domain video. Expanding the dense baseline's width or depth to match Tra-MoE's parameter count still only reaches 48.4% and 52.5% respectively, confirming the gain is not just about capacity. The adaptive policy conditioning technique adds a further 5.4-point improvement over the trajectory-only baseline (66.8% vs. 61.4%), and real-world dual-arm experiments across five manipulation tasks show the same pattern: MoE alone lifts success from 38% to 46%, and adding adaptive conditioning lifts it further to 56%.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Tra-MoE offers empirical evidence that architectural sparsity, specifically parameter cooperation-and-specialization via MoE, is a more effective vehicle than dense scaling for absorbing large, heterogeneous out-of-domain video into a trajectory-prediction model without harming in-domain manipulation performance. This has direct implications for the broader "learn trajectories from internet video, then a lightweight policy from robot demonstrations" paradigm shared by ATM, Track2Act, and related methods: the bottleneck is not the data itself but how the trajectory model is architected to consume it. The adaptive policy conditioning technique further offers a general, learnable alternative to hand-crafted trajectory-to-image mappings that other trajectory-guided policy pipelines could adopt directly.
