# RoboGene: Boosting VLA Pre-training via Diversity-Driven Agentic Framework for Real-World Task Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Peking University and Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics developed RoboGene, an agentic framework that autonomously generates diverse and physically plausible robotic manipulation tasks. This framework expands data coverage to 719 distinct objects and 118 skills, achieving 0.99 physical feasibility and enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model generalization across unseen real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- A scarcity of diverse, real-world robotic interaction data hinders the training of general-purpose Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Manual task curation is unscalable and introduces human biases, resulting in long-tail distributions in datasets where common objects and skills are over-represented.
- Existing automated task generation methods using Large Language Models (LLMs) often lack physical grounding, suffer from hallucinations, and fail to address dataset diversity effectively.

## Method

- **RoboGene**, an agentic framework, employs a ==closed-loop process== for synthesizing diverse and physically valid robotic manipulation tasks suitable for real-world data collection.
- A ==diversity-driven task space sampling== strategy utilizes a ==Least Frequently Used (LFU)== approach to prioritize under-explored scenarios, objects, and skills based on historical usage statistics.
- A ==multi-faceted self-reflection mechanism==, powered by ==LLM-based evaluators==, scrutinizes tasks for physical feasibility, novelty, and constraint adherence, while a ==memory-augmented refinement== process incorporates human feedback via a ==long-term memory module==.

## Results

- RoboGene-generated tasks achieved high physical feasibility (**0.99**) and strong semantic validity, outperforming both human-designed tasks and direct applications of Large Foundation Models (LFMs).
- The framework produced significantly more diverse datasets, covering **719** distinct objects (a **1.7x** expansion over the strongest baseline) and **118** distinct skills (**91.5%** coverage of the skill space) with a balanced distribution.
- VLA models pre-trained on RoboGene data demonstrated superior generalization, with success rates up to **40%** on unseen dual-arm tasks and maintaining **35%** success in unseen environmental conditions (e.g., illumination changes), tripling baseline LFM performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining diversity-driven sampling with multi-faceted self-reflection significantly improves the quality and breadth of generated robotic tasks, ensuring physical plausibility and mitigating dataset biases.
> - Integrating a human-in-the-loop memory module, which consolidates human feedback into generalized heuristics, enables continuous refinement and adaptation, enhancing task generation quality over time.
> - VLA models pre-trained on datasets generated by this framework acquire more robust and transferable features, leading to superior generalization performance in unseen real-world scenarios compared to baseline methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robotic manipulation systems, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, relies heavily on extensive and diverse real-world interaction data. This work is positioned within the broader effort to overcome the "embodied data bottleneck" in robotics. Unlike data collection in fields like computer vision or natural language processing, which often leverage vast web-scale datasets, robotic data acquisition is an active, physically demanding, and costly process.

Existing approaches to robotic data collection primarily fall into two categories. First, manual task specification, where human operators define tasks for data collection, is a prevalent method. However, this approach is limited by scalability and human cognitive biases, leading to datasets with skewed distributions that over-represent simple, repetitive tasks and common objects, thereby hindering the generalization capabilities of learned policies. Second, the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has offered a potential avenue for automated task generation, addressing some scalability concerns. However, naive application of LLMs for this purpose often results in significant challenges, including a lack of physical grounding, susceptibility to hallucinations (generating physically infeasible or non-existent instructions), and an inability to perceive global dataset statistics to mitigate long-tail effects. Furthermore, these LLM-based methods typically operate in an open-loop manner, lacking mechanisms to incorporate real-world execution feedback for continuous refinement.

RoboGene addresses these specific limitations by proposing an agentic framework that automates the generation of diverse, physically plausible, and high-quality manipulation tasks. It aims to bridge the gap between the need for scalable data generation and the requirements for physical grounding and diversity, which are crucial for pre-training robust and generalist VLA models. By focusing on automated task curation that maximizes data value, RoboGene contributes to the foundational capabilities of general-purpose embodied agents by providing a more balanced and comprehensive training distribution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing RoboGene stems from several critical challenges in advancing general-purpose robotic manipulation:

- **Scarcity of Diverse Real-World Robotic Data:** General-purpose robots require training on a vast and diverse array of interaction data to develop robust manipulation skills in unstructured environments. However, collecting such data is an active process that incurs significant physical and financial costs, leading to a scarcity of truly diverse real-world datasets.
- **Limitations of Manual Task Curation:** Current methods often rely on human operators to design tasks. This approach is inherently unscalable and introduces cognitive biases. Human designers tend to gravitate towards familiar, simple, and repetitive tasks, resulting in datasets with a severe long-tail distribution where a narrow subset of objects and skills is over-represented. This lack of diversity prevents VLA models from acquiring broad physical understanding and limits their generalization to novel scenarios.
- **Deficiencies of Existing Automated Generation Methods:** While Large Language Models (LLMs) offer scalability for task generation, their direct application presents notable impediments. LLMs typically lack perception of global dataset statistics, failing to address the long-tail problem. Moreover, they often suffer from hallucination, producing physically infeasible instructions, referencing non-existent objects, or proposing actions that exceed a robot's kinematic capabilities. These open-loop generation processes also fail to incorporate valuable feedback from real-world execution.

In response to these motivations, the key objectives of RoboGene are:

- **Automate Diverse and Physically Plausible Task Generation:** To create an agentic framework capable of autonomously generating a wide range of manipulation tasks for various robot types, including single-arm, dual-arm, and mobile robots, while ensuring physical feasibility.
- **Mitigate Long-Tail Distribution Problems:** To employ strategies that actively guide the task generation process towards under-explored regions of the task space, prioritizing interactions with rare objects and skills, thereby producing a more balanced dataset distribution.
- **Ensure Robust Physical Grounding and Suppress Hallucinations:** To integrate mechanisms that rigorously scrutinize generated task proposals against physical constraints, kinematic feasibility, and logical consistency, preventing the creation of impractical or impossible tasks.
- **Incorporate Continuous Improvement via Human Feedback:** To establish a closed-loop system that assimilates feedback from human operators regarding task modifications and execution failures, leveraging this knowledge to progressively refine the system's understanding of physical constraints and enhance generation quality over time.
- **Enhance Generalization Capabilities of VLA Models:** To provide high-quality, diverse pre-training datasets that enable VLA models to acquire a sufficiently broad physical understanding, leading to superior generalization performance in unseen real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboGene is introduced as an agentic framework that employs a closed-loop process for synthesizing diverse, physically valid, and high-quality robotic manipulation tasks for real-world data collection. The methodology is structured around three core components: diversity-driven sampling, self-reflective generation, and memory-augmented refinement.

The task space `T` is formally defined as a tuple `⟨r, e, c, O, S⟩`, where `r` is the robot type, `e` is the scenario category, `c` is the task description context, and `O` and `S` are sets of objects and manipulation skills, respectively. The overall task generation is conceptualized as `T = Φ_refine(Φ_gen(Φ_sample(E, O, S | H), R) | M)`, where `H` represents historical usage statistics and `M` is the long-term memory.

#### 3.1. Diversity-Driven Task Space Sampling

To address the problem of long-tail distributions and ensure broad coverage of the task space, RoboGene implements a Least Frequently Used (LFU) sampling strategy. This strategy is guided by historical usage statistics `H`, which maintains counters `u(·)` for scenarios, objects, and skills, incrementing them upon successful task generation.

The sampling process is hierarchical:
1. **Scenario Selection:** The system first selects the scenario category `e_t` with the minimum usage count (`e_t = arg min_e∈E u(e)`), prioritizing less-explored environments.
2. **Object and Skill Filtering:** To maintain semantic coherence, a relevant subset of objects `O_e_t ⊂ O` and skills `S_e_t ⊂ S` is filtered based on semantic similarity to the selected scenario `e_t`.
3. **Candidate Set Sampling:** From these filtered subsets, fixed-size candidate sets `O_t` and `S_t` are sampled, again using the LFU strategy to favor objects and skills with lower usage frequencies.
The resulting tuple `(e_t, O_t, S_t)` is then integrated into the prompt template for task generation, providing the generative agent with flexibility while maintaining diversity.

#### 3.2. Self-Reflection and Task Generation

Once sampling constraints `(e_t, O_t, S_t)` are established, the framework enters a generator-evaluator-improvement loop:

- **Task Proposal Generation:** An initial task `T_raw` is generated by a Proposal Generator `G(·)`, implemented using a Large Language Model (LLM). For robots requiring spatial awareness (e.g., mobile manipulators), `G(·)` is instantiated as a Vision-Language Model (VLM) that processes scene images as supplementary input to ensure spatial plausibility. The output `T_raw` is a standardized JSON containing robot type, scenario, task description, objects, and skills.
- **Multi-Faceted Self-Reflection:** `T_raw` is then rigorously scrutinized by three specialized LLM-based evaluators:
 - **Physical Feasibility Evaluator (E_phy):** Assesses kinematic feasibility (e.g., robot reachability, dual-arm workspace overlap), logical consistency of actions, compliance with physical laws, and synchronization control. It outputs a critique `f_phy`.
 - **Novelty Evaluator (E_nov):** Measures task complexity and novelty across dimensions like object complexity (e.g., deformable, fluid), precision of contact, temporal/logical depth (e.g., long-horizon tasks), and unstructured environment features. It outputs `f_nov` to ensure sufficient task novelty.
 - **Constraint Adherence Evaluator (E_con):** Verifies that the task description aligns with the assigned scenario type and uses objects and skills *strictly* from the sampled sets `O_t` and `S_t`, detecting hallucinations. It outputs `f_con` with specific modification suggestions.
These natural language critiques `{f_phy, f_nov, f_con}` are then passed to the Self-Reflection Refiner `E_ref(·)` to produce the revised task `T_ref`.

#### 3.3. Integrating Human Feedback via Memory

To enable continuous system evolution and prevent recurring errors, RoboGene integrates a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) mechanism with a semantic Long-Term Memory module `M`.

- **Human-in-the-Loop Feedback Consolidation:** During real-world task execution, human operators provide natural language feedback detailing infeasibility reasons or necessary modifications. An LLM-based summarizer distills these specific feedback instances into generalized heuristic knowledge.
- **Long-Term Memory Module (M):** This module stores the consolidated heuristics as key-value pairs `(K, V)`, where `K` is the semantic embedding of the task context and `V` is the actionable guideline.
- **Memory-Augmented Refinement:** In the refinement phase, the system retrieves relevant knowledge from `M` by computing the embedding of the current task proposal and finding the top-k most similar heuristic guidelines via cosine similarity. These retrieved insights are injected into the context window of the refiner `E_ref(·)`. This Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach allows the agent to dynamically access a growing knowledge base, continuously enhancing the quality of generated tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of RoboGene was evaluated through extensive quantitative analysis and large-scale real-world experiments, addressing the validity, diversity, and utility of the generated tasks. RoboGene was benchmarked against human experts, a rule-based method, and state-of-the-art Large Foundation Models (LFMs) like GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Pro. A suite of 900 tasks (300 for single-arm, dual-arm, and mobile manipulation each) was generated for each method.

#### 4.1. Individual Task Evaluation

Six metrics were introduced to assess semantic and physical validity: Task Clarity, Type Consistency, Logical Validity (averaged ratings from human evaluators, GPT-4o, and Gemini 2.5 Pro), Object Coverage, Skill Coverage (ratio of generated entities present in valid configuration sets), and Physical Feasibility (average success rate of tasks executed via human teleoperation over 5 trials).

- **Performance:** RoboGene consistently achieved high scores across all metrics. For instance, it recorded 0.9910 for Task Clarity, 0.9876 for Type Consistency, 0.9899 for Logical Validity, 0.6323 for Object Coverage, 0.9152 for Skill Coverage, and 0.9899 for Physical Feasibility.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Pro exhibited high task clarity but suffered from hallucination, showing low object coverage (0.3105 and 0.2172, respectively) and skill coverage (0.2542 and 0.2458). Their physical feasibility was 0.7467 and 0.6790.
 - Human-generated tasks achieved high clarity (0.9644) and physical feasibility (0.9478) but had low object (0.3580) and skill coverage (0.1769), indicating bias.
 - The rule-based method demonstrated low scores across all metrics.
- **Conclusion:** RoboGene effectively grounds generation within available assets and ensures physical grounding, outperforming both human-designed tasks and direct LFM applications.

#### 4.2. Dataset Diversity Analysis

Diversity was assessed across scenarios, objects, skills, and task semantic descriptions.

- **Scenario Diversity:** LFMs, particularly GPT-4o, showed a strong bias towards domestic settings, with almost 90% of tasks concentrated in home, kitchen, and office scenarios. RoboGene achieved a more uniform distribution, with no single category exceeding 20%, by explicitly sampling under-represented environments.
- **Skill Diversity:** RoboGene generated tasks encompassing 118 distinct skills, achieving 91.5% coverage of the skill space. In contrast, GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Pro achieved only 25.4% and 24.6% coverage, respectively, with baseline methods heavily skewed towards simple primitives like pick and place. RoboGene maintained a balanced distribution where top skills constituted less than 5% each.
- **Object Diversity:** Human-curated datasets exhibited a long-tail distribution. While LLMs partially mitigated this, they plateaued at approximately 350 (GPT-4o) and 250 (Gemini 2.5 Pro) unique objects. RoboGene covered 719 distinct objects, a 1.7× expansion over the strongest baseline, resulting in a significantly flatter distribution.
- **Task Semantic Diversity:** RoboGene consistently recorded the lowest similarity scores across BLEU, ROUGE-L, and cosine similarity metrics (e.g., BLEU-4 score of 1.75 compared to 2.71 for GPT-4o). This indicates less lexical overlap and higher diversity in task descriptions compared to the repetitive nature of rule-based and human-designed tasks.

#### 4.3. Real-World Manipulation Task Evaluation

The practical utility of RoboGene was validated through real-world robotic experiments.

- **Validation of Generated Tasks:** RoboGene-generated tasks were successfully executed on physical robots (single-arm UR-5e, dual-arm Franka, AgileX mobile robot). Training ACT for stationary robots and π_0.5 for mobile robots on three representative tasks achieved robust success rates, including 90% for the SUR-SortButton task, confirming the physical executability of the tasks.
- **Effectiveness of Pre-training Datasets:** The π_0 model, pre-trained on RoboGene-generated datasets (150 single-arm, 150 dual-arm tasks) and then fine-tuned on five unseen dual-arm tasks with 15 demonstrations each, exhibited superior generalization. RoboGene pre-training significantly outperformed LLM-based baselines, which often yielded success rates below 20%. For instance, RoboGene achieved 40% success on the DFR-GrillSkewers task.
- **Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** Policies pre-trained on RoboGene data demonstrated superior generalization in five unseen environmental conditions (novel objects, background variations, static distractors, illumination changes, instruction changes) for the DFR-HangCups task. RoboGene maintained a 35% success rate in illumination changes, approximately tripling Gemini 2.5 Pro's performance (10%), suggesting the acquisition of robust, transferable features.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Appendix F)

An ablation study demonstrated the individual contributions of RoboGene's components:

- **Self-Reflection Mechanism:** Integrating self-reflection significantly improved physical feasibility (from 0.75 to 0.88) and nearly doubled the number of unique skills (from 30 to 58) compared to a direct LFM baseline.
- **Diversity-Driven Sampling (LFU):** Incrementally adding LFU strategies for skills and objects further expanded diversity. Skill sampling increased unique skills to 101, and object sampling raised unique objects to 685. However, aggressively maximizing object diversity without context led to a slight decline in physical feasibility (from 0.91 to 0.85).
- **Long-Term Memory Module:** Incorporating long-term memory, which leverages human feedback, addressed the feasibility drop. RoboGene achieved near-perfect physical feasibility (0.99) while maintaining the broadest coverage (719 unique objects and 108 unique skills).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboGene addresses a fundamental barrier in the development of general-purpose embodied intelligence: the scarcity of diverse, physically grounded, and high-quality real-world robotic interaction data. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Enabling Generalist Robotics:** By automating the generation of diverse and physically plausible manipulation tasks, RoboGene provides a scalable solution to the embodied data bottleneck. This allows for more comprehensive pre-training of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which is crucial for endowing robots with the ability to perform a wide array of tasks in unstructured, real-world environments.
- **Mitigating Data Collection Challenges:** The framework reduces the prohibitive physical costs, manual labor, and cognitive biases associated with traditional human-driven data collection methods. This automation makes the process of acquiring foundational robotic datasets more efficient and accessible.
- **Improving VLA Model Generalization:** The empirical results demonstrate that VLA models pre-trained on RoboGene-generated data exhibit superior generalization capabilities across unseen objects, scenarios, and instructions. This suggests that RoboGene facilitates the learning of more robust and transferable features, which is critical for real-world deployment of robotic policies.
- **Overcoming LLM Limitations in Robotics:** RoboGene effectively addresses the limitations of directly applying Large Language Models (LLMs) for task generation, specifically their propensity for hallucination and lack of physical grounding. By integrating diversity-driven sampling, multi-faceted self-reflection, and human-in-the-loop memory, it ensures generated tasks are not only novel but also executable and meaningful.
- **Advancing Research in Robotic Learning:** The proposed framework and the resulting high-quality task datasets provide a valuable resource for the research community. By open-sourcing these resources, RoboGene aims to accelerate future research in areas such as curriculum learning, sim-to-real transfer, and the development of increasingly capable embodied agents.

Overall, RoboGene offers a promising pathway towards constructing scalable, automated pipelines for robotic data curation, contributing significantly to the progression of general-purpose robotic manipulation and embodied AI.
