# TouchWorld: A Predictive and Reactive Tactile Foundation Model for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harbin Institute of Technology and PHANES AI developed TouchWorld, a hierarchical tactile foundation model for dexterous manipulation that separates semantic planning, tactile world-model prediction, and rapid tactile-conditioned refinement. This architecture consistently improved task success rates in real-robot manipulation, achieving an average of 65.0% in clean settings and 53.7% under human perturbations, by enabling both proactive anticipation and reactive adjustment of contact.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language-action (VLA) policies often lack reliable information about hidden contact states (e.g., force, slip, grasp stability) crucial for precise physical interaction.
- Monolithic VLA policies integrate all information (slow semantic task reasoning, intermediate action generation, fast contact feedback) into a single control loop, making them inefficient for rapid, tactile-based error correction.
- Dexterous manipulation requires both anticipating future contact states (prediction) and rapidly correcting local deviations (reaction), a dual capability often conflated or inadequately addressed by current systems.

## Method

- **TouchWorld** introduces a hierarchical manipulation policy with three distinct layers operating at different frequencies: a high-level planner (1 Hz), an intermediate visuo-tactile goal-conditioned policy (10 Hz), and a high-frequency tactile-conditioned refinement policy (30 Hz).
- The system explicitly decouples functionalities into a **Subtask Planner** for long-horizon task decomposition, a ==Tactile World Model== for predicting future contact subgoals, and a **Tactile Residual Transformer** for rapid, local action correction based on real-time tactile feedback.
- A four-stage sequential training strategy is employed, allowing each module to be optimized for its specific role and timescale, with the refinement policy attached to a frozen ==VLA== backbone.

## Results

- TouchWorld achieved an average success rate of **65.0%** in clean settings and **53.7%** under human perturbations across six real-robot tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline (FTP-1) by **15.7** and **18.5 percentage points**, respectively.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all hierarchical components are essential for performance, with the tactile-conditioned refinement policy being particularly crucial for handling human perturbations.
- The Tactile World Model accurately predicted future contact subgoals, achieving **86.3%** temporal contact accuracy and **52.7%** contact IoU, significantly improving upon baseline prediction methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling control aspects across multiple timescales (semantic planning, tactile prediction, action generation, reactive refinement) is critical for achieving robust contact-rich manipulation.
> - Treating tactile sensing as both a proactive predictive cue for contact-aware goal generation and a reactive feedback signal for online error correction significantly enhances robotic dexterity.
> - A memory-augmented vision-language subtask planner, fine-tuned with task-phase supervision, proves more effective for semantic task decomposition than larger zero-shot models, suggesting that structured supervision is more impactful than scale alone for this interface.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous robotic manipulation in dynamic, unstructured environments necessitates capabilities for both anticipating future contact states and reacting to unexpected deviations during execution. While advancements in vision-language-action (VLA) policies have enabled robots to understand semantic and geometric aspects of tasks, these systems often struggle with tasks requiring precise physical interaction because they lack reliable information about hidden contact states such as force, slip, and grasp stability. Tactile sensing offers direct access to these physical cues, making it a critical modality for robust contact-rich manipulation.

Existing VLA approaches, when incorporating tactile sensing, frequently integrate tactile observations as additional input tokens into a single, monolithic action model. This design processes all information—slow semantic task reasoning, intermediate action-chunk generation, and fast contact feedback—within a unified control loop. This monolithic structure is often poorly suited for tasks demanding rapid tactile-based corrections, leading to failures in scenarios requiring stable grasping, precise force regulation, slip recovery, or accurate insertion, even when the policy demonstrates high-level task understanding.

TouchWorld addresses this limitation by proposing a hierarchical policy that explicitly decouples these multi-timescale control aspects. It positions itself as a predictive-and-reactive tactile foundation model, departing from monolithic VLA structures. By separating semantic planning, tactile world-model prediction, visuo-tactile action generation, and high-frequency tactile residual refinement, TouchWorld aims to overcome the conflict between high-level task generalization and low-level contact adaptation. This work extends the capabilities of current VLA policies by integrating tactile intelligence in a structured manner, allowing for both proactive anticipation of contact and rapid reactive adjustments, thereby enhancing robustness in contact-rich, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a tactile foundation model that enhances the robustness and adaptability of dexterous manipulation in everyday environments, particularly for tasks involving significant physical contact. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in existing robotic manipulation policies:

1. **Inadequate Contact State Information:** Robots operating in real-world settings require intricate knowledge of contact dynamics, including grasp stability, object slip, insertion alignment, and applied force. Vision and language models, while providing high-level semantic and geometric understanding, do not reliably infer these critical, often hidden, physical contact states. This information gap restricts a robot's ability to perform precise and stable contact-rich operations.

2. **Mismatched Control Timescales in Monolithic Policies:** Current robotic policies, including advanced VLA models, typically process all sensory inputs and generate actions within a single, unified control loop. When tactile observations are included, they are often treated as a low-frequency stream, integrated at the same rate as slower semantic reasoning and action-chunk generation. This monolithic approach fails to differentiate between the slow timescale of task planning, the intermediate timescale of action sequencing, and the fast timescale required for immediate, local contact feedback and error correction. As a result, such policies can exhibit plausible high-level behavior but fail when rapid adjustments are needed for stable contact, leading to task failure due to issues like unstable grasping, slip, or misalignment.

3. **Need for both Prediction and Reaction:** Dexterous manipulation fundamentally demands a dual capability: anticipation of how contacts should evolve throughout a task (prediction) and rapid correction of local errors when actual contact deviates from expectations (reaction). Existing systems often emphasize one aspect over the other or conflate them, leading to suboptimal performance in complex scenarios.

To address these challenges, the research poses a central question: "whether a tactile foundation policy can become more robust in contact-rich, long-horizon manipulation by explicitly separating semantic planning, tactile world-model prediction, visuo-tactile action generation, and tactile-conditioned feedback refinement."

To answer this question, TouchWorld was designed with three specific objectives:
- **Semantic Decomposition for Long-Horizon Tasks:** To enable the robot to break down complex, multi-step manipulation tasks into a sequence of executable subtasks, providing a structured approach to long-horizon planning.
- **Predictive Tactile-Goal Conditioning for Action Generation:** To anticipate future contact configurations and generate contact-aware subgoals that guide the robot's actions, ensuring that nominal movements are planned with expected physical interactions in mind.
- **Reactive Tactile Correction under Local Contact Perturbations:** To implement a mechanism for rapid, online adjustment of actions based on high-frequency tactile feedback, allowing the robot to correct local execution errors such as slip or misalignment caused by internal factors or external disturbances.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to develop a robotic system that maintains the generalization capabilities of VLA policies while significantly enhancing its robustness and adaptability in physical interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TouchWorld is implemented as a hierarchical manipulation policy designed to leverage tactile sensing in both a predictive and reactive capacity across multiple timescales.

**Overall Architecture:**
The system comprises three main layers operating at distinct frequencies:

1. **High-Level Planning Layer (Slow Semantic Rate, 1 Hz):**
 - **Subtask Planner (π_subtask):** This component receives the global task instruction (ℓ), current multi-view images (I_t), and a high-level memory (m_t) that stores previous subtasks, predicted subgoals, and execution status. Its function is to produce an executable subtask (ℓ_sub_t) at a slow rate, maintaining a compact task state. The output is a structured object {ℓ, ℓ_sub_t, r_t}, where r_t is optional free-form reasoning.
 - **Tactile World Model (π_world):** This model acts as the predictive component. Conditioned on the task instruction (ℓ), current subtask (ℓ_sub_t), current images (I_t), and tactile observations (X_t), it predicts future visual-tactile subgoals (g_t). These predictions describe the expected contact outcome for the current subtask and are refreshed only when the Subtask Planner indicates a new subtask or a significant task-state change, promoting stability by reusing predictions during stable phases.

2. **Visuo-Tactile Goal-Conditioned Policy Layer (Intermediate Action-Chunk Rate, 10 Hz):**
 - **π_goal:** This layer consists of a tactile diffusion Transformer policy. It receives the composed language prompt ("Task: ℓ ⊕ Current subtask: ℓ_sub_t"), predicted visual-tactile subgoals (g_t), multi-view RGB observations (I_t), image-form tactile observations (X_t), and proprioceptive state (s_t). From these inputs, it generates a *nominal action chunk* (Â_t:t+H-1) with a horizon H (H=32 in experiments) and a context token (c_t).
 - **Tactile Input Representation:** Raw tactile readings are converted into a unified image representation, allowing the VLA backbone to process touch data consistently with visual observations, maintaining compatibility with image-language pretraining.
 - **Action Generation:** The policy utilizes flow matching (a diffusion Transformer) to generate the nominal action sequence.

3. **Tactile-Conditioned Refinement Policy Layer (High-Frequency Feedback Rate, 30 Hz):**
 - **π_tactile (Tactile Residual Transformer, TRT):** This component operates at a faster rate than the nominal policy. At each refinement step (τ), TRT takes a sliding nominal-action lookahead window (Â_τ:τ+W-1, with W=16 in experiments), recent proprioceptive (s_τ-k:τ) and tactile (X_τ-k:τ) histories, and the VLA context token (c_t). It predicts a *residual action window* (ΔA_τ:τ+W-1).
 - **Action Refinement:** The residual action window is added to the nominal lookahead window to produce a *corrected action window* (Ã_τ:τ+W-1 = Â_τ:τ+W-1 + ΔA_τ:τ+W-1).
 - **Execution:** The robot controller executes the first C actions (C=4 in experiments) from this corrected window before refreshing the residual prediction with new feedback. This design restricts the refinement policy to local error correction, allowing the VLA policy to maintain responsibility for semantic and geometric task progression.
 - **Tactile Input Processing:** Unlike the VLA policy, TRT directly processes high-frequency tactile histories, employing modality-specific lightweight encoders for various tactile signal types (e.g., image-like maps, matrices, low-dimensional states).

**Training Strategy:**
TouchWorld is trained in four sequential stages without end-to-end backpropagation across the entire hierarchy, allowing each module to optimize for its specific role and timescale:

1. **Stage 1: Subtask Planner Training:** The Subtask Planner is instantiated from Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and fine-tuned using LoRA. It is trained on 128,866 annotated teleoperation traces, where each sample includes a global task instruction, current camera observation, high-level memory, and the corresponding executable subtask label. This stage focuses on learning a policy-compatible semantic interface.

2. **Stage 2: Tactile World Model Training:** Instantiated from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B.
 - **Pretraining:** Initially pretrained on large-scale EgoTouch human interaction video data, which provides synchronized egocentric/wrist videos, bimanual hand pose, and dense bilateral palm pressure maps. This exposes the model to diverse human-object contact patterns and establishes a general visual-to-tactile dynamics prior.
 - **Fine-tuning:** Subsequently fine-tuned on 10 hours (~1.08 million frames at 30 FPS) of robot demonstrations. Given a current observation grid and executable subtask, it predicts a future 17-frame visual-tactile clip or terminal subgoal state at 384x224 resolution.

3. **Stage 3: Visuo-Tactile Goal-Conditioned Policy Training:** Trained as a standalone nominal action policy using imitation learning and flow matching. Each training sample includes the global task instruction, current executable subtask, RGB and image-form tactile observations, proprioceptive state, and the demonstrated future action chunk. Predicted visual-tactile subgoals are incorporated as additional goal context when available.

4. **Stage 4: Integrated VLA-Refinement Training:** The Tactile-Conditioned Refinement Policy (TRT) is attached to the *frozen* trained VLA policy. TRT is trained to predict residual actions by minimizing the masked Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between its corrected action window and the demonstrated high-frequency action window. This residual prediction is applied only to a 58-dimensional tactile-sensitive action subspace (wrist pose and selected hand joints), allowing the VLA policy to maintain control over other dimensions.

**Experimental Setup:**
The hardware platform consists of a teleoperation setup (Meta Quest headset, Touch Plus controllers, Wuji Glove) for human input and a robot platform (humanoid with Wuji dexterous hands and a JQ-Industries tactile glove) for execution and sensing. The system was evaluated on a suite of six real-robot manipulation tasks: Water Flower, Tabletop Clearing, Cup Insertion, Power Plug Insertion, Pot Wiping, and Tissue Pulling. These tasks cover long-horizon planning, precision insertion, continuous contact regulation, and soft-object handling. Each task was evaluated in both a "clean setting" and a "human perturbation setting" (involving disturbances like target displacement or grasp interference). For each task, 200 teleoperated training trajectories and 100 real-robot evaluation rollouts were conducted. Performance was measured by success rate. Baselines included Pi-0.5 (VLA), FTP-1 (monolithic tactile policy), and GR00T N1.7 (generalist robot policy).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TouchWorld on a real-robot benchmark, comprising six diverse dexterous manipulation tasks, yielded several findings regarding its performance and the contribution of its hierarchical components.

**Overall Performance Against Baselines:**
TouchWorld consistently demonstrated improved task success rates compared to existing baselines across both clean and human-perturbed scenarios (Table 1).
- **Clean Setting:** TouchWorld achieved an average success rate of 65.0%, surpassing the strongest baseline (FTP-1, 49.3%) by 15.7 percentage points.
- **Human Perturbation Setting:** Under human perturbations, TouchWorld attained an average success rate of 53.7%, outperforming the strongest baseline (FTP-1, 35.2%) by 18.5 percentage points.
The gains were particularly noticeable in tasks requiring precise contact and rapid adaptation, such as Power Plug Insertion, Pot Wiping, and Tissue Pulling. Improvements were also observed in tasks like Water Flower and Tabletop Clearing, suggesting that the hierarchical structure maintained high-level task execution while enhancing contact-aware manipulation.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation experiments (Figure 5) were conducted to assess the individual contributions of TouchWorld's key components:
- **Tactile input removal:** The most significant performance degradation occurred when tactile observations were removed, confirming their essential role in the benchmark tasks.
- **Tactile-Conditioned Refinement Policy removal:** Removing this layer caused a substantial decrease in success rates, particularly in the human perturbation setting, indicating its importance for online local error correction.
- **Subtask Planner removal:** This led to a reduction in long-horizon consistency, underscoring its contribution to structuring complex tasks.
- **Tactile World Model removal:** Removing the predictive tactile subgoal generation weakened contact-aware goal conditioning, impacting performance where foresight of contact was crucial.
These findings suggest that each component of TouchWorld contributes positively to its overall robustness and performance, particularly in their respective functional domains (e.g., reactive control for perturbations, planning for long-horizon tasks).

**Tactile World Model Prediction Analysis:**
The Tactile World Model's ability to predict future tactile subgoals was quantitatively evaluated on held-out trajectories (Table 2). The model achieved:
- Temporal Contact Accuracy: 86.3%
- Contact IoU: 52.7%
- Volumetric IoU: 43.8%
These metrics represent a substantial improvement over baseline methods, such as copying the current tactile observation (e.g., 70.4% temporal accuracy, 31.8% contact IoU) or using nearest-neighbor subgoal retrieval (e.g., 77.5% temporal accuracy, 39.2% contact IoU). This supports the model's efficacy in providing accurate predictive contact references for downstream policies.

**Vision-Language Subtask Planner Analysis:**
The Vision-Language Subtask Planner was analyzed for its ability to generate semantically correct and executable subtasks (Table 3):
- Supervised fine-tuning of the Qwen3-VL-4B model improved performance over zero-shot planning.
- The memory-augmented SFT Qwen3-VL-4B planner, as used in TouchWorld, achieved higher metrics (Subtask Accuracy: 88%, Execution Success: 65%, Transition F1: 82%) compared to a larger zero-shot Qwen3-VL-32B planner (Subtask Accuracy: 69%, Execution Success: 54%, Transition F1: 71%). This indicates that task-phase supervision and the integration of execution history are more influential than model scale alone for this specific interface.

**Qualitative Inference Analysis:**
Qualitative demonstrations (Figure 6) illustrated TouchWorld's inference process, showing its capacity to decompose long-horizon instructions into a sequence of executable subtasks. The predicted tactile subgoals were observed to provide contact-aware references that guided subsequent manipulation actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TouchWorld represents an advancement in robotic dexterous manipulation by introducing a predictive-and-reactive tactile foundation model. This work's significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**Novel Hierarchical Architecture for Robust Manipulation:** The core contribution is the development of a multi-timescale hierarchical policy that explicitly separates high-level semantic planning, predictive tactile-goal generation, intermediate action-chunk generation, and high-frequency tactile residual refinement. This architectural design addresses a fundamental challenge in robot control: effectively integrating slow cognitive processes with rapid physical feedback. By decoupling these elements, TouchWorld maintains the semantic generalization capabilities of vision-language-action policies while enhancing local contact adaptation, leading to more robust and reliable manipulation.

**Enhanced Performance in Contact-Rich Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate consistent and notable improvements in task success rates across a diverse set of six real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks, particularly under human perturbations. This improved robustness is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic environments where unexpected contact deviations are common. The gains observed in tasks like power plug insertion, pot wiping, and tissue pulling highlight the efficacy of tactile prediction and fast local correction for precise physical interactions.

**Foundational Framework for Tactile AI:** TouchWorld establishes a framework where tactile sensing is treated as both a proactive predictive cue for contact-aware goal generation and a reactive feedback signal for online error correction. This dual role elevates the utility of touch in robot intelligence, moving beyond its traditional use as a purely reactive input. Such a framework is foundational for developing more physically intelligent robots capable of nuanced interactions with their environment.

**Contribution to Real-World Benchmarking:** The research introduces a six-task real-robot benchmark, featuring both clean and human-perturbation settings. This benchmark provides a standardized and challenging evaluation platform for future research in tactile manipulation, robust control, and hierarchical robot policies, enabling direct comparison and driving further innovation in the field.

**Potential for Broader Application:** The demonstrated ability to handle long-horizon tasks, precision insertions, continuous contact regulation, and soft-object handling suggests that TouchWorld's principles could be extended to a wider array of domestic, industrial, and service robotics applications requiring fine motor skills and adaptive physical interaction.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its contributions, the paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Task Diversity:** The evaluation on six representative tasks, while comprehensive, does not cover the full spectrum of household manipulation or complex deformable object interactions.
- **Tactile World Model Prediction Horizon:** The current Tactile World Model predicts short-horizon subgoals, and extending this to longer horizons while accounting for uncertainty and multiple plausible futures remains a challenge.
- **Sensor and Hand Morphology Dependence:** The policy is tied to the specific sensing layout of the robot platform. Transferring to different tactile sensors or hand morphologies would likely necessitate calibration and potentially additional adaptation data.
- **Fixed Scheduling Hyperparameters:** The use of fixed scheduling rates for the hierarchical layers is an implementation choice. Adaptive scheduling could potentially optimize computation and responsiveness based on varying contact dynamics.

Future work will focus on scaling the task suite, improving uncertainty-aware tactile goal prediction, and investigating broader transferability across different tactile sensors and robot hand designs to further generalize TouchWorld's capabilities.
