# Hierarchical Diffusion Policy: manipulation trajectory generation via contact guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Hierarchical Diffusion Policy (HDP) introduces a structured approach to robot manipulation by decomposing tasks into high-level contact planning and low-level trajectory generation, achieving a 20.8% average higher success rate compared to Diffusion Policy across various contact-rich tasks in simulation and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation in contact-rich environments is challenging for traditional imitation learning due to the need for precise interaction with objects.
- Existing end-to-end learning approaches often lack explicit contact modeling, leading to limited interpretability and poor generalization in scenarios with varying contact patterns.
- Current policies struggle to incorporate human guidance or intervention to specify desired contact strategies during manipulation.

## Method

- A ==Hierarchical Diffusion Policy (HDP)== is proposed, decomposing manipulation into a high-level "Guider" and a low-level "Actor", both implemented as conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic models.
- The Guider predicts optimal contact positions using 3D point clouds and robot state, while the Actor generates action sequences to achieve these predicted contacts.
- HDP employs a hybrid training strategy combining behavioral cloning from demonstration data with Q-learning for fine-tuning, and introduces snapshot gradient optimization for training efficiency.

## Results

- Hierarchical Diffusion Policy achieved an average of **20.8%** higher success rate compared to Diffusion Policy across six different simulation tasks.
- Specifically, HDP demonstrated an **88%** success rate in the Tilt task (vs. **78%** for Diffusion Policy) and an **86%** success rate in the PushT task (vs. **72%** for Diffusion Policy).
- Real-world experiments on a UR5 robotic arm validated HDP's effectiveness in tasks such as Move-T and handling rigid and deformable objects, while demonstrating robustness to perturbations and enabling human prompt guidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling contact as an intermediate representation between perception and action improves robustness and interpretability in contact-rich manipulation.
> - Decomposing manipulation tasks into high-level contact planning and low-level trajectory generation allows for more structured learning and execution.
> - Integrating human prompt guidance for contact positions enables real-time human-robot collaboration and corrective intervention in complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot manipulation in contact-rich environments remains one of the most challenging problems in robotics. Traditional imitation learning approaches struggle when robots need to interact with objects through complex contact patterns, as these methods often fail to explicitly model the crucial relationship between the robot and the object being manipulated.

The paper "Hierarchical Diffusion Policy: Manipulation Trajectory Generation via Contact Guidance" introduces a structured approach to address these limitations by decomposing manipulation tasks into two levels: high-level contact planning and low-level trajectory generation. This hierarchical decomposition enables robots to better understand and execute contact-rich manipulation tasks while maintaining interpretability and allowing for human intervention.

The authors propose Hierarchical Diffusion Policy (HDP), which combines the generative capabilities of diffusion models with a principled hierarchical structure. Unlike existing end-to-end approaches that directly map observations to actions, HDP explicitly predicts contact positions as an intermediate representation, guiding the subsequent action generation process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Contact-rich manipulation tasks present unique challenges for learning-based robotics approaches. When a robot needs to push, tilt, or manipulate objects, the success of the task often depends on making contact at the right location with appropriate force and timing. Existing imitation learning methods, particularly Diffusion Policy, have shown promising results in various manipulation tasks but struggle with contact-rich scenarios.

The key limitations of current approaches include:

1. **Lack of explicit contact modeling**: End-to-end policies don't explicitly reason about where and how contact should occur
2. **Limited interpretability**: It's difficult to understand why a policy makes certain decisions
3. **Poor generalization**: Policies trained on specific scenarios often fail when contact patterns change
4. **Inability to incorporate human guidance**: Most systems don't allow human operators to intervene or guide the contact strategy

The authors identify that contact information serves as a natural bridge between high-level task understanding and low-level motor control. By predicting where contact should occur before determining how to achieve that contact, the system can make more informed and robust decisions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HDP decomposes the manipulation policy into two interconnected levels, each implemented as a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model (CDDPM):

#### 3.1. High-Level Policy (Guider)

The Guider operates at a strategic level, taking as input:
- 3D point cloud representation of the scene
- Current robot state
- Optional human prompts for contact positions

The Guider's objective is to predict the optimal contact position $C_t$ for the next manipulation step. This prediction is formulated as:

$$C_t \sim \text{Guider}(\text{PointCloud}, \text{RobotState}, t)$$

The use of 3D point clouds enables the system to maintain spatial awareness and reason about object geometry, which is crucial for determining appropriate contact locations.

#### 3.2. Low-Level Policy (Actor)

The Actor operates at a tactical level, generating action sequences to achieve the contact position predicted by the Guider. The Actor takes as input:
- Current observation (images and robot state)
- Target contact position from the Guider
- Prediction horizon $T_p$

The Actor generates a sequence of actions $A_t = \{a_t, a_{t+1}..., a_{t+T_p-1}\}$ using:

$$A_t \sim \text{Actor}(\text{Observation}, C_t, T_p)$$

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

HDP employs a hybrid training approach combining behavioral cloning and reinforcement learning. The Actor is jointly optimized using:

1. **Behavioral Cloning Loss**: Learning from demonstration data
2. **Q-Learning Loss**: Reinforcement learning to guide actions toward predicted contacts

The total loss function combines both objectives:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{BC} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{QL}$$

where $\lambda$ balances the contribution of each loss component.

#### 3.4. Snapshot Gradient Optimization

Traditional diffusion model training requires computing gradients for all timesteps in the denoising process, which is computationally expensive. HDP introduces snapshot gradient optimization, which calculates gradients for only one randomly selected timestep per iteration. This approach significantly improves training efficiency while maintaining performance.

#### 3.5. 3D Point Cloud Conditioning

To enhance spatial awareness, HDP incorporates 3D point clouds as input to the Guider. The system uses a modified PointNet architecture to process point cloud data, enabling better understanding of object geometry and spatial relationships. This 3D conditioning allows the policy to reason about contact locations in three-dimensional space rather than relying solely on 2D image representations.

#### 3.6. Prompt Guidance

HDP supports human intervention through prompt guidance, where human operators can manually specify desired contact positions. This capability enables:
- Corrective guidance when the policy makes suboptimal contact predictions
- Task specification through contact point designation
- Interactive manipulation planning

The prompt guidance is seamlessly integrated into the Guider's prediction process, allowing for real-time human-robot collaboration.

#### 3.7. Data Augmentation Strategy

To improve robustness and generalization, the authors employ data augmentation techniques that create variations in object poses and configurations during training. This helps the policy generalize to novel situations and reduces overfitting to specific demonstration scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluate HDP across six different manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments:

#### 4.1. Simulation Tasks

1. **Can**: Picking and placing cylindrical objects
2. **Square**: Manipulating square-shaped objects
3. **Tilt**: Tilting objects to specific orientations
4. **PushT**: Pushing T-shaped objects along specified paths

#### 4.2. Performance Comparison

HDP demonstrates significant improvements over Diffusion Policy across all tested tasks:

- **Average improvement**: 20.8% higher success rate
- **Tilt task**: 78% vs 88% success rate for HDP
- **PushT task**: 72% vs 86% success rate for HDP

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The paper includes comprehensive ablation studies examining:

**Q-learning Integration**: The analysis shows that Q-learning action steps $T_r$ between 2-4 provide optimal performance, with diminishing returns beyond this range.

**Snapshot Gradient Optimization**: Comparing oneshot denoising (snapshot) versus iterative denoising shows that snapshot optimization maintains performance while significantly reducing computational cost.

**Contact Representation**: Different objective contact schemes are evaluated, with phased contact prediction showing superior performance compared to nearest contact or no contact guidance.

#### 4.4. Robustness Analysis

The system demonstrates robustness to various perturbations:
- **Object occlusion**: HDP maintains performance even when objects are partially occluded
- **Dynamic perturbations**: The policy adapts to external disturbances during manipulation
- **Stage-specific perturbations**: Robustness is maintained across different phases of manipulation

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Real-World Experimental Setup

The authors validate HDP on a real UR5 robotic arm equipped with:
- RealSense D435i camera for RGB-D perception
- Custom end-effector for manipulation tasks
- Real-time control system running at 10 Hz

#### 5.2. Task Demonstrations

Real-world experiments demonstrate HDP's effectiveness across multiple manipulation scenarios:

1. **Move-T Task**: Successfully manipulating T-shaped objects with complex contact requirements
2. **Rigid Object Manipulation**: Handling solid objects with precise contact control
3. **Deformable Object Handling**: Adapting to soft materials that deform under contact

#### 5.3. Human-Robot Collaboration

The prompt guidance capability enables effective human-robot collaboration:
- **Corrective guidance**: Humans can intervene when the policy predicts suboptimal contacts
- **Task specification**: Complex manipulation goals can be communicated through contact point designation
- **Adaptive execution**: The system adapts its trajectory based on human input while maintaining safety

#### 5.4. Current Limitations

Despite its achievements, HDP has several limitations that present opportunities for future research:

1. **Contact Representation**: The current approach uses simplified contact point representations that may not capture complex contact geometries
2. **Scalability**: The method has been tested primarily on single-arm, single-object scenarios
3. **Training Data Requirements**: The system still requires substantial demonstration data for effective learning
4. **Real-time Performance**: Computational requirements may limit real-time performance in complex scenarios

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The authors identify several promising avenues for extending this work:

**Visual Language Models Integration**: Incorporating vision-language models could enable more natural human-robot interaction and task specification through natural language commands.

**Multi-Robot Extension**: Extending HDP to multi-robot scenarios where multiple agents need to coordinate contact strategies.

**Multi-Finger Manipulation**: Adapting the approach for dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands, where contact planning becomes significantly more complex.

**Advanced Contact Modeling**: Developing more sophisticated contact representations that capture force, torque, and contact geometry information.

#### 5.6. Conclusion

Hierarchical Diffusion Policy represents a significant advancement in learning-based robot manipulation, particularly for contact-rich tasks. By explicitly modeling contact as an intermediate representation between perception and action, HDP achieves superior performance compared to end-to-end approaches while maintaining interpretability and enabling human guidance.

The key contributions of this work include:

1. **Principled Hierarchical Decomposition**: Separating contact planning from trajectory generation provides a more structured approach to manipulation learning
2. **Technical Innovations**: Snapshot gradient optimization, 3D conditioning, and prompt guidance improve both efficiency and capability
3. **Demonstrated Effectiveness**: Comprehensive evaluation shows consistent improvements across multiple tasks and conditions
4. **Real-World Validation**: Successful deployment on real robotic systems demonstrates practical applicability

The work opens new avenues for research in hierarchical robot learning and provides a foundation for more capable and collaborative robotic manipulation systems. As robots increasingly work alongside humans in complex environments, approaches like HDP that combine learning efficiency with interpretability and human guidance capabilities will become increasingly important for practical deployment.
