# GRUtopia: Dream General Robots in a City at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> GRUtopia introduces a comprehensive, large-scale simulated 3D city environment featuring diverse scenes, LLM-driven non-player characters, and a physically grounded benchmark for embodied AI. The platform provides realistic low-level robot control APIs and exposes challenges in integrating high-level planning with low-level physical actions.

## Problem

- The high cost and logistical difficulty of collecting large-scale, diverse real-world robot data for training embodied AI agents.
- Existing embodied AI simulators often lack diverse environments (beyond homes), complex task generation capabilities, and realistic social interaction for general robots.
- A significant gap exists between high-level planning in simulation and low-level physical control in real-world or physically realistic settings, hindering sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- ==Develops GRScenes==, a dataset of approximately 100,000 interactive 3D scenes across 89 diverse functional categories, enabling the construction of city-scale environments.
- Integrates GRResidents, an LLM-driven non-player character system, for realistic social interactions and dynamic, context-aware task generation.
- Provides GRBench, a multi-faceted benchmark that includes robust, pre-trained reinforcement learning-based low-level robot control APIs for physically realistic locomotion and manipulation tasks.

## Results

- The LLM-driven NPCs (GRResidents) demonstrate high accuracy, achieving over **83%** in object referring, language grounding, and object-centric question answering.
- While provided low-level robot controllers achieve **100%** success rates on flat terrain, their performance significantly drops in cluttered, realistic environments (e.g., H1 to **58%**, Aliengo to **14%** for locomotion), primarily due to high-level planning failures.
- Even the best LLM agent baselines achieve low success rates on GRBench tasks, with a maximum of **22%** for Object Loco-Navigation, **14%** for Social Loco-Navigation, and **0%** for Loco-Manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating high-level planning with robust low-level physical actions remains a core challenge, as evidenced by significant performance drops in cluttered environments despite robust low-level controllers.
> - Complex embodied tasks require structured agent frameworks, as zero-shot large language or vision-language models alone show limited performance.
> - Current large models and planning methods struggle with intricate motion planning for mobile manipulation in realistic, cluttered environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI is rapidly evolving, driven by the aspiration to develop intelligent agents capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting within physical environments. A significant inspiration for this field comes from the "scaling laws" observed in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV), where increasing model size and data volume have led to remarkable performance gains. The robotics community is keen to explore whether similar scaling principles apply to robot learning.

However, a major bottleneck for scaling robot learning in the real world is the **prohibitive cost and logistical challenges of collecting large-scale, diverse, and finely-annotated real-world robot action trajectories**. Initiatives like Open X-Embodiment and DROID exemplify efforts in real-world data collection but face limitations in data acquisition costs and generalization across heterogeneous hardware platforms.

In this context, the **Simulation-to-Real (Sim2Real) paradigm** emerges as a crucial alternative. Simulation offers a controlled, cost-effective, and scalable environment for training and testing embodied agents, circumventing many real-world constraints. Prior work has demonstrated the utility of simulation for learning specific low-level policies (e.g., Legged Gym for locomotion, ManiSkill for manipulation, Orbit for general interactive environments). Many embodied AI research efforts already rely on simulators like VirtualHome, ALFRED, Habitat, and iGibson.

Despite these advancements, existing simulation platforms exhibit notable limitations that GRUtopia aims to address:

- **Limited Diversity and Complexity of Scenarios:** Many platforms (e.g., ALFRED, Habitat 2.0, iGibson) predominantly focus on household environments, hindering the development of "general robots" capable of operating in a wider array of real-world settings. While Behavior-1K offered 8 scene types, this is still limited compared to real-world complexity.
- **Insufficient Task Diversity:** While some platforms offer language-conditioned tasks, the generation of truly diverse and open-ended tasks that reflect real-world human-robot interaction remains a challenge.
- **Lack of Realistic Social Interaction:** Most simulators lack sophisticated Non-Player Characters (NPCs) or robust frameworks for natural, LLM-driven social interactions, which are critical for human-robot collaboration. Recent works like Habitat 3.0 and Generative Agents hint at this direction but are often confined to home settings.
- **Gap Between High-Level Planning and Low-Level Control:** Many benchmarks abstract physical interactions or rely on idealized actions, creating a significant "sim-to-real gap" when policies are deployed on physical robots.

**GRUtopia positions itself as a pioneering effort** to overcome these limitations by introducing a novel simulated interactive 3D society at an unprecedented scale and diversity. It directly confronts the challenges of data scarcity and policy generalization by:
1. **Vastly expanding environmental scope** beyond homes to a city scale with diverse service-oriented scenarios.
2. **Integrating sophisticated LLM-driven NPCs** for dynamic social interaction and task generation.
3. **Providing physically realistic low-level control APIs** for legged robots, allowing researchers to focus on high-level perception and planning while ensuring sim-to-real transferability.

By tackling these critical aspects, GRUtopia seeks to provide a more comprehensive and realistic platform for scaling embodied AI research, thereby bridging the gap between current simulation capabilities and the demands of general-purpose real-world robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of the GRUtopia project is to contribute to the understanding and exploration of scaling laws in Embodied AI by providing a rich, interactive, and scalable simulated environment. The primary motivations stem from the inherent challenges of real-world robot learning, specifically:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** Collecting high-quality, diverse real-world data for training embodied agents is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. GRUtopia aims to alleviate this by offering a high-fidelity, scalable simulation environment where data can be generated efficiently and at a massive scale.
- **Facilitating Generalization Across Environments:** Existing platforms often confine agents to limited, homogeneous environments (e.g., homes). A key motivation for GRUtopia is to enable the development of "general robots" that can operate effectively across a wide spectrum of real-world scenarios, particularly service-oriented environments like hospitals, supermarkets, and offices, where general robots are likely to be initially deployed.
- **Enabling Realistic Human-Robot Interaction:** For robots to be truly useful, they must interact seamlessly with humans. This includes understanding ambiguous instructions, clarifying user intent through dialogue, and adapting to dynamic social contexts. GRUtopia is motivated to simulate such realistic social interactions, moving beyond mere physical interaction.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Many simulated environments abstract away the complexities of low-level physical control, leading to policies that do not transfer well to the real world. GRUtopia aims to provide a more physically genuine setting by offering robust, learning-based low-level control policies as APIs, allowing researchers to focus on high-level perception and planning without sacrificing physical realism.
- **Providing a Comprehensive Benchmark:** There is a need for robust and progressively challenging benchmarks to comprehensively assess the capabilities of embodied AI agents. GRUtopia seeks to fulfill this need by introducing tasks that test not only navigation and manipulation but also social interaction, with well-defined difficulty levels.

In summary, GRUtopia's key objectives are to:
1. **Develop GRScenes:** A large-scale, interactive, and finely annotated 3D scene dataset spanning 89 diverse functional categories, capable of constructing city-scale environments.
2. **Implement GRResidents:** An LLM-driven NPC system for realistic social interaction, dynamic task generation, and task assignment.
3. **Establish GRBench:** A multi-faceted benchmark focusing on "moderately challenging" tasks, including Object Loco-Navigation, Social Loco-Navigation, and Loco-Manipulation, primarily for legged robots, with integrated low-level control APIs.
4. **Offer a comprehensive assessment tool:** For embodied AI research, guiding the development of more general, capable, and socially intelligent robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GRUtopia is conceived as a comprehensive simulated interactive 3D society, built upon three core components: GRScenes (the environment), GRResidents (the NPC system), and GRBench (the benchmark). The platform is stated to be built with NVIDIA Omniverse, leveraging its X-form for annotations and physical realism.

#### 3.1. GRScenes: Fully Interactive Environment at Scale

- **Data Collection & Diversity:** The foundation is a massive collection of ~100k high-quality synthetic 3D scenes sourced from designer websites. These prototypes undergo cleaning and semantic annotation, and can be combined to form city-scale environments. Crucially, GRScenes expands beyond typical home environments to include 89 diverse categories such as hospitals, supermarkets, offices, libraries, museums, and amusement parks, addressing the gap in service-oriented scenarios.
- **Realism and Interactivity:** Scenes feature physically realistic materials, detailed external appearances, and accessible internal structures with realistic furniture arrangements. A professional team modified and created complete internal models for objects to ensure they are physically interactable, unlike many static 3D assets.
- **Hierarchical Multi-modal Annotations:** To support complex interactions, GRScenes provides fine-grained, hierarchical, and multi-modal annotations:
 - **Scene-level:** Overall scene understanding.
 - **Region-level:** UI-based annotation of indoor regions with polygons, including object-region relationships.
 - **Object-level:** Attributes like category, position, size, appearance.
 - **Part-level:** Fine-grained labels attached to interactive parts (e.g., "handle" of a drawer).
 - **Language Annotations:** Initialized using powerful Visual Language Models (VLMs) like GPT-4V on multi-view images, followed by human manual checks, enabling rich textual descriptions for objects and scenes.
 - **Scene Graph:** A structural representation where nodes represent object instances with attributes, and edges denote spatial relationships (e.g., "on," "near," "in," "out of"), persistently managed and updated.

#### 3.2. GRResidents: Generative NPCs in 3D Environments

- **LLM-Driven System:** GRResidents integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) to create generative NPCs that simulate social interactions, task generation, and assignment. This system addresses the challenge of providing context-aware and dynamic human-robot interaction within the simulation.
- **World Knowledge Manager (WKM):** The WKM is central to the NPCs' perception capabilities. It manages dynamic knowledge of the real-time world state, constructing a comprehensive scene graph from the dataset's hierarchical annotations and the simulator's backend information.
 - **Core APIs:** WKM exposes three key data interfaces for knowledge retrieval and object grounding:
 - `find_diff(target, objects)`: Compares differences to narrow down candidate objects.
 - `get_info(object, type)`: Retrieves specific attributes of an object.
 - `filter(objects, condition)`: Filters objects based on specified conditions.
- **LLM Planner:** This is the decision-making module for NPCs, comprising:
 - **Memory Module:** Stores chat history with other agents.
 - **LLM Programmer:** Iteratively calls WKM's data interfaces to query necessary scene knowledge, effectively performing fine-grained object grounding through parameterized function calls.
 - **LLM Speaker:** Generates natural language responses based on chat history and queried knowledge.
- **Evaluation of NPCs:** Experiments validate the NPCs' capabilities in object referring (describing an object for human identification), language grounding (locating objects from descriptions), and object-centric Question Answering (providing information during navigation tasks). This is done by cross-verification with humans or other LLMs (like GPT-4o as an "annotator").

#### 3.3. Robot Control APIs

- **RL-based Controllers:** Unlike approaches that use simplified animations or direct position setting, GRUtopia provides pre-trained, robust, RL-based low-level control policies as APIs. This allows high-level agents to leverage realistic movements without needing to train low-level controllers from scratch.
- **Robot Embodiments:** The platform supports various robots, including humanoid robots (Unitree H1, Unitree G1, Fourier GR-1) and quadrupeds (Unitree Aliengo with Unitree Z1 Arm).
- **Performance Evaluation:** Extensive evaluations demonstrate the robustness of these controllers on flat terrain but also highlight significant performance drops in cluttered, realistic environments due to failures in path or motion planning, emphasizing the need for combined high-level and low-level research. A reset function is introduced to evaluate robustness by counting fall recoveries.

#### 3.4. GRBench: A Benchmark for Assessing Embodied Agents

- **Benchmark Setups:** GRBench features three progressively challenging benchmarks:
 - **Object Loco-Navigation:** Agent navigates to a uniquely identified target object based on a given language goal (e.g., "find the bowl in the dining room that is on the table").
 - **Social Loco-Navigation:** Agent must interact with an NPC via dialogue (max three rounds) to clarify ambiguous instructions and identify the target object (e.g., "Can you find the bowl on the table?").
 - **Loco-Manipulation:** Agent performs mobile manipulation tasks, picking up a handheld object and placing it in a target receptacle, often with multiple valid solutions (e.g., "Can you pick the bowl from the dining room... and place it on the table near the sofa?").
- **Episode and Instruction Generation:**
 - **Solvability Guarantee:** Episodes are generated ensuring collision-free paths from start to target, using occupancy maps and path planning (RRT*).
 - **GPT-4-based Instruction Generation:** Instructions are dynamically generated using GPT-4, leveraging the WKM's `find_diff` and `filter` APIs to ensure non-ambiguity (Object Loco-Navigation) or controlled ambiguity (Social Loco-Navigation, by coarse-to-fine information revelation) and multi-solution conditions (Loco-Manipulation).
- **Robot Setups for Benchmarking:** Unitree H1 humanoid for navigation tasks; Unitree Aliengo with Unitree Z1 arm for mobile manipulation tasks. Both use RGB-D cameras.
- **Baselines:** The paper evaluates performance using Zero-Shot VLM Baselines (InternVL-chat-1.5, GPT-4o, Qwen-VL) and LLM Agent Baselines (Qwen, Llama-3, InternLM-2-Chat, ChatGLM3, GPT-4o). The LLM agent features a grounding module (semantic segmentation, 3D point cloud, bounding boxes), a memory module (BEV map, action-observation, dialogue history), a decision module (LLM for reasoning/speaking), and an action module (navigation via RRT*, manipulation via IK solver).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Navigation:** Success Rate (SR), Path Length (PL), Success rate weighted by normalized inverse Path Length (SPL), and Reset Times (RT, for low-level policy robustness).
 - **Social Loco-Navigation:** Adds Excluded Candidate Rate (ECR) to quantify dialogue efficiency in narrowing down object candidates.
 - **Loco-Manipulation:** Adds Satisfied Condition Rate (SCR) for multi-condition tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted within GRUtopia reveal several key insights into the capabilities of both the platform's components and existing embodied AI models.

#### 4.1. GRResidents (NPC) Performance

- The LLM-driven NPC system demonstrates high accuracy in **object referring (95.9%-100%)** and **language grounding (83.3%-93.2%)** across different LLM backends (GPT-4o, InternLM-2-Chat, Llama-3). This validates the ability of GRResidents to generate precise object descriptions and accurately locate objects based on language.
- In **object-centric QA tasks**, NPCs achieved high scores (88.7%-95.8%), indicating their capability to provide relevant and helpful information for navigation, thus simulating realistic human-robot dialogue.

#### 4.2. Robot Control API Performance and Challenges

- The provided RL-based low-level locomotion policies for Unitree H1 and Aliengo exhibit excellent performance on flat terrain (100% success rate, low trajectory error).
- However, a significant **performance degradation is observed in cluttered, realistic "House" environments**. Success rates drop drastically (H1 to 58%, Aliengo to 14% for locomotion; manipulation to 8%). This drop is attributed primarily to failures in high-level path or motion planning, rather than the robustness of the low-level controllers themselves. This finding highlights a crucial challenge in deploying policies trained in simple environments to complex, real-world-like scenarios, underscoring the "sim-to-real gap."

#### 4.3. GRBench Baseline Performance (VLM and LLM Agents)

- A **random strategy yields near 0% performance**, confirming the non-trivial nature and challenge of the tasks posed by GRBench.
- **LLM agent baselines consistently outperform zero-shot VLM baselines**, showing better overall performance across all three benchmarks. This suggests that while multimodal large models are powerful, their direct application in complex embodied tasks without a structured agent framework (like the grounding, memory, and decision modules in the LLM agent baseline) is limited.
- **Qwen LLM demonstrated relatively superior performance in dialogue-heavy tasks** (Social Loco-Navigation) compared to GPT-4o, indicating nuances in LLM capabilities for specific embodied interaction types.
- **Overall success rates remain low** even for the best LLM agent baselines (e.g., maximum SR of 22% for Object Loco-Navigation, 14% for Social Loco-Navigation, and 0% for Loco-Manipulation). This suggests that complex embodied tasks, even those like navigation that have been studied extensively, are far from solved when integrated with physically realistic constraints.
- **Incremental Difficulty:** The experimental results align with the design intuition that the tasks are incrementally difficult: Object Loco-Navigation < Social Loco-Navigation < Loco-Manipulation.
 - **Loco-Manipulation specifically registered a 0% success rate.** Analysis revealed two main failure causes:
 1. **Locomotion failures and high reset counts:** The Aliengo robot's larger turning radius and base size compared to the H1 humanoid led to more frequent collisions in cluttered environments, highlighting the agility advantage of humanoids.
 2. **Manipulation planning difficulties:** The robot arm frequently collided with environmental objects during manipulation, indicating that current multimodal large models struggle with the intricate motion planning required for such complex tasks.

#### 4.4. Diagnostic Study

- **Impact of Perception Module:** Replacing GPT-4o (used for perception) with QwenVL in the agent framework showed that perception performance significantly impacts overall task success, with QwenVL leading to superior results in some cases.
- **RL Controller vs. Oracle Actions:** A comparison with "oracle action" settings (where positions are directly set, bypassing the low-level controller) revealed a substantial performance increase. This strongly validates the hypothesis that there is a significant gap between idealized simulation assumptions and real-world application scenarios, re-emphasizing the challenge of integrating robust low-level control.

In conclusion, the findings highlight that GRUtopia provides a challenging and realistic testbed that exposes the current limitations of large models in integrating high-level planning with low-level, physically constrained actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GRUtopia represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI, addressing several critical bottlenecks that have hindered progress towards truly general and capable robots. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Accelerating Embodied AI Research:** By providing a highly scalable, diverse, and interactive simulation platform, GRUtopia can significantly accelerate the development and evaluation of embodied AI agents. It mitigates the prohibitive costs and logistical complexities associated with real-world robot training, allowing researchers to rapidly iterate on new algorithms and train models on massive datasets.

2. **Enabling Research in General-Purpose Robotics:** Current embodied AI research is often confined to simplified or single-domain (e.g., home) environments. GRUtopia's expansive GRScenes dataset, with its 89 diverse functional categories and city-scale landscapes, opens up unprecedented opportunities for developing "general robots" capable of operating in a wide array of service-oriented environments like hospitals, supermarkets, and offices. This aligns with a critical real-world need for versatile robotic assistants.

3. **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction:** The innovative LLM-driven GRResidents NPC system introduces a new dimension to simulated human-robot interaction. By enabling dynamic dialogue, task generation, and clarification of ambiguous instructions, GRUtopia provides a testbed for developing socially intelligent agents that can effectively collaborate with humans, a crucial step towards seamless integration of robots into human society.

4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Benchmarking:** GRBench offers a comprehensive set of "moderately challenging" tasks (Loco-Navigation, Social Loco-Navigation, Loco-Manipulation) that are designed to be physically genuine. By providing robust, learning-based low-level control APIs and evaluating with metrics like "reset times," the platform encourages the development of algorithms that are not only capable of high-level planning but also robust to the complexities of real-world physics and robot locomotion/manipulation. This helps identify and address the persistent sim-to-real transfer challenges.

5. **Identifying Key Research Challenges:** The experimental results, particularly the low success rates of even state-of-the-art baselines in complex tasks like loco-manipulation and the performance gap between RL-controlled and oracle-controlled agents, clearly highlight the current limitations of large models and the significant challenges in integrating high-level planning with realistic low-level physical actions. This diagnostic capability is invaluable for guiding future research directions in embodied AI, focusing efforts on areas that require the most significant breakthroughs.

6. **Fostering Community Collaboration:** The open-sourcing of GRUtopia (including the initial scenes and the ongoing development plan) is a substantial contribution to the research community. It provides a standardized, high-quality platform and dataset for benchmarking, facilitating comparative studies, and fostering collaborative research efforts across institutions.

In conclusion, GRUtopia is more than just a simulation platform; it is a comprehensive ecosystem designed to push the boundaries of embodied AI. By providing an unprecedented scale of diverse environments, sophisticated social interaction capabilities, and a rigorous, physically-grounded benchmark, it sets a new standard for research in general robotics and intelligent agents, promising to significantly impact the development of future autonomous systems.
