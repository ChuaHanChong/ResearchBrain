# RelAfford6D: Relational 6D Affordance Graphs for Constraint-Driven Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RelAfford6D introduces a training-free framework for robotic manipulation that bridges the perception-action gap by utilizing a Relational 6D Affordance Graph. This graph explicitly encodes relative rigid-body geometry and kinematic constraints between object parts, enabling robust zero-shot task execution. The system achieved a 74% average success rate on articulated manipulation tasks, outperforming data-driven baselines, and demonstrated robust generalization in real-world environments.

## Problem

- Bridging the "perception-action gap" to translate abstract natural language into precise, low-level robotic control commands in open-world environments.
- Limitations of existing object representations, where 2D affordances lack depth, sparse 3D representations miss continuous relative geometry, and dense fields lack explicit kinematic constraints for articulated objects.
- Data-driven methods often require extensive task-specific training, limiting zero-shot generalization and robustness against environmental disturbances.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Relational 6D Affordance Graph (R6DAG)== as an intermediate representation to explicitly model dynamic SE(3) relationships and kinematic constraints between object parts.
- Leverages a three-module framework: Semantic Topology Generation (using a kinematic knowledge base and LLM), Metric Visual Grounding (with open-world segmentation and 6D pose estimation), and Constraint-Driven Kinematic Execution (formulating manipulation as an SE(3) kinematic constraint satisfaction problem).
- Employs a closed-loop execution strategy with dynamic replanning, continuously tracking object poses and recalculating constraint manifolds in real-time to ensure robustness against disturbances.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **74%** on ten articulated object categories in simulation, outperforming four data-driven baselines that were trained on 10,000 successful trajectories.
- Demonstrated **100%** success on classical rigid object manipulation tasks (PickCube, StackCube, PushCube) across 250 trials, highlighting broad generalizability without re-training.
- Maintained strong performance under visual corruptions (e.g., Stack Cube **85%** with **30%** occlusion) and successfully deployed on a robotic arm in real-world environments, showcasing robust zero-shot generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly embedding rigid-body and articulation constraints into an SE(3) manifold representation offers a mathematically rigorous and robust foundation for manipulating articulated objects, surpassing methods relying on implicit or isolated affordances.
> - A hybrid approach that combines LLM semantic understanding with structured kinematic priors and precise VFM-based 6D pose estimation effectively translates abstract language instructions into physically valid robotic actions.
> - Dynamic, closed-loop tracking and real-time manifold recalculation are critical for maintaining physical consistency and task success in unpredictable real-world environments with potential disturbances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces a fundamental challenge in bridging the "perception-action gap," which refers to the difficulty of translating high-level, abstract natural language instructions into precise, low-level physical control commands for robots operating in open-world environments. Existing approaches have explored various strategies to address this challenge, each with inherent limitations that RelAfford6D aims to overcome.

Traditional methods for affordance learning and object representation can be broadly categorized. Two-dimensional (2D) pixel-level affordances, while effective in grounding semantics, intrinsically lack metric depth, limiting their applicability for precise 3D physical execution. Moving into three-dimensional (3D) space, recent zero-shot paradigms leverage vision foundation models to extract spatial affordances. Some represent affordances as sparse 3D point clouds or keypoints, providing isolated contact coordinates. However, these methods often struggle to explicitly encode the continuous relative geometry, such as precise rotational axes, which is crucial for manipulating articulated objects. Other advanced paradigms utilize dense 3D descriptor fields, treating the workspace as an implicit energy field to guide robotic trajectories. While offering high expressiveness for generic spatial reasoning or deformable objects, these fields typically lack explicit, hard kinematic constraints (e.g., rigid-body joint limits) essential for structured articulated manipulation. Even 6D affordance representations, which encode both position and orientation, have traditionally treated affordances as isolated actionable poses rather than structured relationships.

For articulated object manipulation specifically, early approaches relied on geometric fitting or model-based estimation of articulation parameters, often requiring extensive knowledge or task-specific training. Primitive-based frameworks decompose manipulation into predefined actions (e.g., pulling, rotating), with parameters optimized through learning or planning. However, these methods often exhibit brittleness under distribution shifts and require task-specific data. More recent data-driven policies, including flow-based, reinforcement learning (RL)-based, and diffusion-based algorithms, have shown progress in simulated environments. Nevertheless, their generalization to open-world settings is often limited by the size and diversity of real-world datasets used for training.

RelAfford6D positions itself by introducing the "Relational 6D Affordance Graph" as a novel intermediate representation. This representation directly models the relative rigid-body geometry between object parts, explicitly encoding rigid-body and articulation constraints that are often overlooked or implicitly handled by other methods. By mathematically formulating manipulation as an SE(3) kinematic constraint satisfaction problem, the work fundamentally deviates from relying on learned end-to-end latent features or open-loop heuristic planners. This approach aims to provide robust, training-free, and generalizable manipulation capabilities by grounding abstract semantics in rigorous physical control through explicit kinematic constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the persistent gap between abstract semantic understanding and precise physical control in open-world robotic manipulation. The authors identify a critical bottleneck in existing object representations for manipulation, particularly for complex articulated objects, where the lack of rigorous kinematic constraints hinders robust and generalized performance.

The key motivations driving the development of RelAfford6D are:

1. **Addressing the limitations of current object representations**: Traditional 2D affordances lack 3D physical meaning, while sparse 3D point clouds or keypoints only provide isolated contact points without encoding the continuous relative geometry vital for articulated objects. Dense descriptor fields, despite their expressiveness, typically formulate manipulation as navigating an implicit energy field, lacking explicit, hard kinematic constraints like rigid-body joint limits. The motivation is to overcome these deficiencies by introducing a representation that explicitly captures continuous relative geometry and kinematic constraints.
2. **Establishing rigorous kinematic constraints**: The authors observe that successful manipulation of articulated objects (e.g., opening a microwave) is strictly governed by the rigid-body relative geometry between interacting parts. Current data-driven policies often rely on isolated contact points or latent affordance embeddings, which lack the mathematical rigor required for such tasks. A core motivation is to embed these strict, mathematically grounded physical boundaries directly into the manipulation framework.
3. **Achieving zero-shot generalization**: Many existing methods require extensive task-specific training data or demonstrations, limiting their ability to generalize to novel objects or unseen manipulation tasks in open-world scenarios. A key objective is to develop a training-free framework that can infer manipulation strategies and execute tasks on unseen objects directly from natural language instructions.
4. **Enhancing robustness against disturbances**: Open-world environments are dynamic and unpredictable. Manipulators must contend with external disturbances that can shift objects or alter their configurations. The authors aim to develop a closed-loop system that can continuously track object states and dynamically replan trajectories to maintain task success in the face of such perturbations, thereby ensuring unprecedented robustness.
5. **Integrating semantic reasoning with physical control**: The work aims to create a principled pipeline that seamlessly translates abstract natural language instructions into precise physical actions. This involves leveraging the semantic understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the robust visual grounding abilities of Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), and then translating these into physically valid kinematic control strategies.
6. **Developing a novel intermediate representation**: The central objective is to propose the "Relational 6D Affordance Graph," which explicitly models the manipulated object as a dynamic spatial relationship in SE(3) between a primary interacting part and a physical anchor part. This representation is intended to preserve 3D metric precision while naturally mapping semantic operations into relative geometric constraint manifolds.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RelAfford6D is a training-free robotic manipulation framework built upon the Relational 6D Affordance Graph, comprising three core modules: Semantic Topology Generation, Metric Visual Grounding, and Constraint-Driven Kinematic Execution.

#### 3.1. Semantic Topology Generation

This module addresses the challenge of deducing the underlying kinematic chain and relational node topology of unseen objects from open-world language instructions, compensating for LLMs' lack of inherent physical priors.
1. **Structured Kinematic Knowledge Base**: A kinematic relation base, `K`, is constructed from the PartNet-Mobility dataset. This base abstracts articulated objects into sets of interacting nodes (primary interacting part `s_p`, physical anchor part `s_a`) linked by specific kinematic constraint types (`a`, e.g., `pull` or `rotate`). Each entry in `K` is a tuple `(s_pi, s_ai, a_i)`, encoding fundamental kinematic chains and relative geometric constraints (e.g., a "rotate" token defines a revolute constraint between a laptop screen and base).
2. **Zero-Shot Topology Retrieval**: A user query `q` is processed by an LLM to generate a concise semantic query. This query is embedded using a language encoder `ϕ(·)`, and the top-k most relevant kinematic chain candidates are retrieved from `K` based on cosine similarity. This step provides explicit relational pairs and corresponding kinematic constraint types relevant to the query.
3. **Relational Topology Inference**: The retrieved candidates serve as structured kinematic priors, augmenting the prompt given to the LLM. The LLM is guided to output a rigorous relational node topology `((s_p, s_a), a)`. Here, `s_p` and `s_a` are the precise semantic nodes for the primary and anchor parts, and `a` acts as the Kinematic Manifold Selector, dictating the specific topological edge (e.g., revolute or prismatic) connecting them. This hybrid conditioning combines LLM generalization with physically valid kinematic structures.

#### 3.2. Metric Visual Grounding

This module elevates the abstract semantic nodes (primary part `s_p` and anchor part `s_a`) into precise, geometry-aware SE(3) coordinates from raw visual observations.
1. **Language-Guided Metric Grounding (f_sem)**: Given an RGB-D observation `{I, D}` and the relational node pair `(s_p, s_a)` from the previous module, an open-world segmentation model (SAM3) generates independent binary segmentation masks `{M_p, M_a}` for both parts. This step converts abstract semantic nodes into spatially structured visual extents guided by the kinematic intent.
2. **Rigid-Body 6D Pose Reasoning (f_geo)**: The masked RGB-D regions, camera intrinsics `K`, and an object-specific mesh `M_obj` (reconstructed from RGB-D reference views, not category-level CAD models) are fed into a category-agnostic pose estimator (FoundationPose). This process yields high-fidelity 6D poses `{T*_P, T*_A}` (3D rotation and translation) for both the primary and anchor parts in the world coordinate system. By constraining pose inference within the specific topological masks, the module provides the exact SE(3) boundary conditions for control.

#### 3.3. Constraint-Driven Kinematic Execution

This module converts the instantiated spatial graph `Ψ = {(T*_P, T*_A)}` and the kinematic manifold selector `a` into a continuous 6-DoF robotic trajectory by formulating manipulation as an SE(3) Kinematic Constraint Satisfaction Problem.
1. **Kinematic Constraint Manifolds**: The semantic token `a` defines a specific geometric constraint manifold `M_a` that governs the relative spatial relationship between `T*_P` and `T*_A`. This reduces the feasible motion space to a 1D subspace aligned with the physical joint.
 - **Grasp**: The robot's end-effector `T_ee` first establishes a rigid grasp on the primary part `T*_P`, defining an initial offset `T_offset = (T*_P)^-1 T_ee(0)`.
 - **Prismatic Constraint Manifold (a = pull)**: The motion is restricted to a 1D translation along an axis `v` defined in the local coordinate frame of the anchor part. The axis `v` is inferred from the relational topology. The manifold is defined as `M_pris(q) = T*_A · Trans(vq) · (T*_A)^-1 T*_P · T_offset`, where `q` is the scalar displacement.
 - **Revolute Constraint Manifold (a = rotate)**: The motion follows a 1D rotational orbit around a hinge axis `u` anchored at `T*_A`. The axis `u` is inferred from the part-level spatial configuration. The manifold is `M_rev(q) = T*_A · Rot(u, q) · (T*_A)^-1 T*_P · T_offset`, where `q` is the rotation angle. The joint axes are analytically computed.
2. **Trajectory Generation via Manifold Tracking**: This involves a two-stage strategy:
 - **Global reaching**: An RRT-Connect motion planner guides the end-effector to the initial grasp pose `T_ee(0)`, avoiding environmental obstacles.
 - **Local execution**: The manipulation trajectory is analytically synthesized by discretizing a 1D state interval `[0, q_target]` (where `q_target` is inferred from the LLM or set as an exploration horizon) into `N` steps, generating a sequence of valid SE(3) waypoints `τ* = {M_a(q_k)}`.
3. **Closed-Loop Tracking and Replanning**: To ensure robustness, the trajectory is executed in a closed-loop fashion. FoundationPose continuously tracks the live poses of active parts (`^T_P(t)` and `^T_A(t)` at 5 Hz). If the deviation between the live tracked anchor pose and its initially planned state exceeds a geometric threshold (`δ` = 5 cm), the system re-instantiates the constraint manifold using live coordinates and analytically re-synthesizes the valid SE(3) waypoints for the remaining horizon. This dynamic recalculation maintains physical consistency against disturbances.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments to evaluate the performance, generalization, and robustness of RelAfford6D.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison

RelAfford6D was evaluated on two task suites: articulated object manipulation and rigid object manipulation, without requiring any task-specific training or demonstrations.

- **Articulated Object Manipulation**: The framework was compared against four data-driven baselines (Where2act, FlowBot3D, ManipLLM, RoboMamba) on ten articulated object categories (e.g., opening a drawer, closing a laptop, screwing a bottle). All baselines were trained on a dataset of 10,000 successful trajectories. RelAfford6D achieved an average success rate of 74%, outperforming all data-driven baselines. The authors attribute this superior performance to their method's explicit extraction of the metric instantiated graph `Ψ` and enforcement of strict SE(3) manifold constraints, which ensure generated trajectories strictly adhere to articulated joint geometry, unlike baselines that infer isolated contact points or regress end-effector poses without global joint awareness.

- **Rigid Object Manipulation**: On classical rigid-object tasks (PickCube, StackCube, PushCube), RelAfford6D achieved a 100% success rate across 250 trials (10 random seeds, 25 trials/seed). This performance surpassed other rigid object manipulation methods like RDT, OpenVLA, Octo, and Diffusion-Policy, demonstrating the framework's broad generalizability even for tasks with different control objectives, without any re-training.

#### 4.2. Ablation and Robustness Analysis

Ablation studies were conducted across ten manipulation tasks (averaged over 30 trials per task) to quantify the contribution of core components and assess robustness.

- **Effect of Kinematic Knowledge Retrieval**: Disabling the kinematic knowledge base (forcing the LLM to infer purely from raw textual prompts) resulted in a notable performance drop (65.0% success rate compared to 74.4% for the full system). This indicated that the structured kinematic prior is crucial for the LLM to construct valid relational graphs, preventing outputs of isolated parts or physically impossible kinematic edges.
- **Precision of Metric Visual Grounding**: Replacing SAM3 with alternative detectors (Grounding DINO, T-Rex2) led to a reduced success rate (68.5% compared to 74.4%). While imprecise segmentation could degrade `T*_P` and `T*_A` estimation, the overall performance did not collapse, suggesting that the relational SE(3) constraints compensate for moderate perception errors, and visual grounding quality improves performance but is not the sole determinant of robustness.
- **Necessity of the Relational Anchor Constraint (Anchor-Free)**: An "Anchor-Free" variant, where only the primary part `T*_P` was tracked, resulted in a significant performance drop (48.5%). This confirmed that tracking both the primary and anchor parts is indispensable for dynamically establishing the screw axis and generating kinematically valid SE(3) waypoints.
- **Impact of Dynamic Manifold Replanning**: Disabling the closed-loop tracking mechanism, forcing open-loop execution, resulted in a drastic drop in success rate (35.0%). This demonstrated that continuous tracking of object poses and dynamic recalculation of the manifold are critical for maintaining physical consistency and robustness against external disturbances.
- **Robustness under Visual Corruptions**: The full system was stress-tested under physical distractors and partial occlusions (30% coverage) of target parts. RelAfford6D showed graceful degradation under severe occlusion (e.g., Open Microwave: 78% clean to 72% with occlusion; Pull Drawer: 82% to 74%; Stack Cube: 100% to 85%). This indicates that as long as the perception module can partially recover geometry to establish the spatial graph, the kinematic manifold tracking remains valid, providing a more robust execution anchor than global visual embeddings.

#### 4.3. Real-world Evaluation

RelAfford6D was deployed on a Realman RM75 robotic arm with an Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D camera to evaluate its performance in unconstrained physical environments. The system was tested on seven representative manipulation categories. Despite real-world sensor noise and uncalibrated backgrounds, the system maintained strong performance (e.g., Screw Bottle 9/10, Close laptop 8/10, Open microwave 8/10, Stack cubes 10/10). This demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization from simulation to the physical world. The system showed efficient operational timings: a one-time initialization cost of approximately 20.7 seconds, with closed-loop tracking operating at 5 Hz, replanning costing less than 0.05 seconds, and low-level control less than 0.01 seconds.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents RelAfford6D as a significant contribution to open-world robotic manipulation, primarily by offering a novel, training-free framework that addresses the perception-action gap through a physically grounded approach.

The **primary significance** lies in the introduction of the Relational 6D Affordance Graph. This intermediate representation explicitly models manipulation as part-conditioned SE(3) relations, embedding rigid-body and articulation constraints directly. This is a departure from many existing data-driven or end-to-end methods that often lack explicit kinematic awareness, thus providing a more mathematically rigorous foundation for robotic control.

The **potential impact** of RelAfford6D is multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Zero-Shot Learning**: By formulating manipulation as a kinematic constraint satisfaction problem and leveraging large language and vision foundation models for semantic and metric grounding, the framework demonstrates superior zero-shot success rates. This means robots can operate on unseen objects and execute novel tasks without requiring task-specific training data or demonstrations, substantially broadening the applicability of robotics in unstructured and dynamic environments.
2. **Increased Robustness and Reliability**: The closed-loop kinematic constraint satisfaction process, coupled with real-time pose tracking and dynamic replanning, allows the robot to adapt to external disturbances and visual corruptions. This capability is critical for deploying robots in real-world scenarios where precise initial conditions cannot always be guaranteed and environmental changes are common. The empirical evidence of graceful degradation under occlusion and successful real-world deployment highlights this resilience.
3. **Principled Integration of AI Components**: The work demonstrates a coherent pipeline that integrates high-level semantic reasoning from LLMs with precise visual grounding from VFMs, and then translates this into analytical physical control. This bridges the gap between abstract human instructions and low-level robot actions through a structured, interpretable representation, offering a path towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
4. **Foundation for Future Research**: The Relational 6D Affordance Graph and its constraint-driven execution paradigm provide a robust base for future work. The authors acknowledge current limitations, such as focusing primarily on revolute and prismatic joints. This suggests future extensions to more complex joint types (e.g., helical, multi-DoF mechanisms, or compositions of constraints) could further expand the framework's capabilities. Additionally, addressing the manipulation of deformable objects, which fall outside the current rigid SE(3) formulation, and enhancing robustness against extreme visual corruptions (e.g., through temporal 3D tracking or multi-view semantic fusion) are identified as promising avenues for future exploration.

In conclusion, RelAfford6D offers a robust and generalizable approach to robotic manipulation, moving beyond purely data-driven methods by integrating strong physical priors and mathematical rigor. Its success in zero-shot tasks and real-world environments suggests a significant step towards more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.
