# Learning-Based Collaborative Control for Bi-Manual Tactile-Reactive Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> A learning-based collaborative control framework for bi-manual tactile-reactive grasping enables robots to handle fragile, deformable, and irregularly shaped objects with enhanced stability. The multi-agent Model Predictive Controller, utilizing GelSight Mini sensors, achieved higher grasping success rates across various objects and maintained a favorable 45% average runtime increase despite doubling input data.

## Problem

- Most robotic grasping systems struggle with fragile, deformable, or irregularly shaped objects, requiring precise contact force regulation to prevent damage or slippage.
- Single-agent tactile-reactive grasping is limited in handling large, heavy, or elongated objects, which typically demand multiple contact points for stability and effective force distribution.
- Existing single-agent solutions often assume ideal object positioning and orientation, which is not representative of dynamic, unstructured real-world environments.

## Method

- A learning-based ==collaborative multi-agent Model Predictive Controller (MPC)== was developed, integrating two robotic manipulators each equipped with a ==GelSight Mini tactile sensor==.
- The system employs a modified ==ResNet-152 CNN encoder== to compress high-dimensional tactile images into low-dimensional embeddings for each agent, which then feed into a ==differentiable MPC layer==.
- The MPC optimizes a cost function over a prediction horizon, incorporating ==learnable cross-arm coupling parameters== for tactile sensory input coupling between agents and leveraging ==parameter sharing== for computational efficiency.

## Results

- The multi-agent MPC achieved superior grasping success rates (up to **10/10** for several objects like Banana, Pringles Can, PVC Pipe) across all tested objects compared to single-agent MPC and PD baselines.
- Qualitative analysis indicated that the proposed method produced substantially more stable grasps with smaller fluctuations and more consistent gripper positions, demonstrating effective collaborative control.
- Despite effectively doubling input data and increasing matrix dimensions, the multi-agent system showed an average runtime increase of only **45%** for batch sizes greater than one, attributed to efficient parameter sharing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bi-manual control, by distributing contact forces across two manipulators, significantly enhances grasping stability for soft and irregularly shaped objects, minimizing localized pressure and potential damage.
> - Integrating real-time, high-resolution tactile feedback from GelSight sensors allows the learning-based MPC to dynamically infer contact dynamics and object compliance, enabling adaptive control policies.
> - Parameter sharing across the CNN and MPC layers in a homogeneous multi-agent framework provides computational efficiency, leading to a less-than-linear increase in runtime despite increased input data and agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic grasping and manipulation represent a foundational challenge in robotics, with a significant portion of existing research focusing on rigid objects. The handling of fragile, deformable, or irregularly shaped objects presents distinct difficulties due to their compliance, varied surface properties, and the necessity for precise contact force regulation. Human manipulation intrinsically relies on tactile feedback to discern material properties such as stiffness and texture, a capability that tactile sensors aim to replicate in robotic systems. These sensors provide dense, high-dimensional data that can be leveraged by learning-based controllers to improve grasping performance.

While advancements have been made in single-agent tactile control, coordinating multiple agents for grasping and manipulation tasks, particularly in unstructured environments, remains a complex area. Previous work, such as LeTac-MPC, has explored learning-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) for single robotic manipulators using tactile feedback. However, these implementations often assume ideal object positioning and orientation, which may not be representative of real-world scenarios. Moreover, single-agent systems face inherent limitations when dealing with large, heavy, or elongated objects, where multiple contact points are beneficial for stability and force distribution.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a multi-agent, collaborative framework for tactile-reactive grasping. It seeks to expand the capabilities of robotic systems beyond single-point contact, thereby broadening the range of objects that can be manipulated and improving stability for challenging items. The paper integrates three distinct but related research areas:
1. **Tactile Grasping:** It utilizes image-based tactile sensors (GelSight Mini) to acquire rich contact information, building on the principle that tactile data can significantly enhance object perception and control compared to conventional visual or point cloud data. It acknowledges the challenge of limited tactile sensing datasets and simulation fidelity in this domain.
2. **Bi-manual Manipulation:** The work contributes to the field of bi-manual robotic manipulation by focusing on coordination between two manipulators. Unlike many approaches that employ complex multi-finger grippers and rely on Reinforcement Learning or Imitation Learning to learn collaboration, this research simplifies the action space by using parallel-jaw grippers with one degree of freedom, making the learning process more tractable.
3. **Learning-based MPC:** It extends the concept of learning-based Model Predictive Control, which incorporates real-world sensory data to refine system dynamics and enable more adaptive controllers, to a multi-agent context. This approach aims to overcome the limitations of traditional MPC in modeling complex, tactile-rich interactions by making the control layer differentiable and trainable.

By combining these elements, the proposed system aims to provide a robust, intelligent solution for collaborative grasping in complex environments, addressing the stability and applicability gaps of single-agent systems and traditional control methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in current robotic grasping systems, particularly when confronted with objects that deviate from rigid, geometrically simple forms. Specifically, the authors identify two major problem areas:

1. **Challenges with Fragile, Deformable, and Irregularly Shaped Objects:** Most robotic grasping implementations are designed for rigid items, exhibiting considerable performance degradation when handling soft, fragile, or irregularly shaped materials. These objects necessitate real-time feedback and finely tuned contact forces to prevent damage or slippage.
2. **Limitations of Single-Agent Tactile-Reactive Grasping:** While tactile-reactive grasping has advanced for single manipulators, its applicability is restricted to objects within a certain size and weight range. Manipulating large, heavy, or elongated objects often requires multiple contact points for stability and effective force distribution, which single-agent systems cannot provide. Existing single-agent solutions often assume ideal object positioning, which is not always realistic in dynamic, unstructured environments.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes a learning-based, tactile-reactive multi-agent Model Predictive Controller (MPC). The core objectives are detailed below:

- **Develop a multi-agent control system for diverse object handling:** The primary goal is to create a system capable of grasping a wide array of objects with varying softness and shapes, extending beyond the capabilities of existing single-agent implementations. This involves leveraging real-time tactile information to infer contact dynamics and object compliance.
- **Enhance grasping stability for soft objects through bi-manual control:** A specific objective is to improve the handling of soft objects. Tactile sensors can struggle with objects softer than their gel membrane, leading to minimal deformations and potential misinterpretation as no-contact scenarios. By distributing contact forces across two manipulators, the system aims to reduce localized pressure, decrease the risk of object damage, and achieve more stable manipulation through increased contact points.
- **Improve computational efficiency in multi-agent learning-based control:** Learning-based control methods are computationally intensive, especially at high frequencies and when dealing with large datasets. A key objective is to design a collaborative learning approach that mitigates the computational burden associated with adding an extra manipulator. This involves leveraging parameter sharing across learnable MPC and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) layers to make the multi-agent system more efficient than a linear increase in resources might suggest.
- **Incorporate real-time tactile feedback for adaptive control:** The system is motivated by the need to adapt control policies dynamically. Using two GelSight Mini tactile sensors, the system aims to extract real-time information on object texture and stiffness. This rich tactile feedback is critical for estimating contact dynamics and object compliance, enabling the controller to adjust force and position based on real-time grasp stability predictions.
- **Validate the approach through rigorous real-world experimentation:** A critical objective is to conduct an extensive comparative evaluation of the proposed pipeline against established baselines (independent PD and single-agent MPC controllers) using a variety of real-world objects. This is intended to demonstrate the practical advantages in terms of success rates for achieving and maintaining stable grasps across diverse object properties.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between single-agent robotic grasping capabilities and the complex demands of real-world manipulation tasks involving diverse, non-rigid, or large objects, by developing an efficient and robust collaborative tactile-reactive control framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system integrates a learning-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) layer with tactile sensing for collaborative bi-manual grasping. The methodology involves data collection, a neural network architecture for tactile encoding, and a differentiable MPC formulation.

#### 3.1. Data Collection for Bi-manual Tactile Grasping

The data collection pipeline utilizes two Robotiq 2f-series grippers, each equipped with a GelSight Mini tactile sensor. This setup enables simultaneous data acquisition from both manipulators during contact interactions.

- **Materials:** Standardized blocks made of wood, soft gel, soft foam, and hard rubber surfaces were used to capture tactile images under various contact conditions. No-contact baseline images were also collected.
- **Objective:** The process aims to record tactile images and identify the transition between firm contact and tactile slippage. "Slippage opening" is defined as the minimum gripper width (in millimeters) at which an object begins to slip due to insufficient friction.
- **Procedure:**
 1. Manual measurement of initial contact width and slippage threshold. To enhance dataset diversity and generalization, small random variances (±0.7 mm for initial width, ±0.35 mm for slippage opening) were introduced.
 2. Each data collection trial comprises four sub-trials:
 - Both grippers initially grasp the target object.
 - One gripper (designated "active") gradually opens until it reaches the predefined slippage opening, while the other remains stationary. Tactile images are recorded continuously from both sensors.
 - After the active gripper returns to its initial position and the stationary gripper is slightly opened, the process is repeated with the roles of active and stationary grippers reversed.
 3. Post-sub-trial, 25 pairs of tactile images are saved with metadata including trial number, sub-trial index, and measured slippage opening. Filenames indicate the sensor, current gripper opening, and frame index for precise alignment. Agent roles are alternated to ensure balanced data representation.

#### 3.2. Collaborative Learning-Based Multi-Agent MPC Architecture

The system pipeline consists of a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder for tactile image processing and an MPC layer for control.

- **Tactile Image Encoder (CNN):**
 - A modified ResNet-152 architecture is employed.
 - The convolutional backbone is frozen to retain pre-trained visual features.
 - The original classification head is replaced with a two-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) using ReLU activations.
 - This MLP compresses high-dimensional tactile images into low-dimensional embeddings `f ∈ R^M`, where `M = 20`. Hidden layers of the MLP have a dimensionality of 128.
- **Multi-agent Learning-Based MPC Layer:**
 - **Inputs:** For each forward pass, the MPC layer takes a pair of tactile embeddings (one per agent), along with the current position and velocity values for both agents. A fixed agent ordering is maintained to ensure consistent interpretation due to sensor variability. Positions and velocities are converted to metric units (millimeters and mm/s).
 - **Gripper Motion Model:** Agents are assumed to have one degree of freedom (open/close). Gripper motion is modeled as a discrete-time double integrator:
 `[p^(i)_n+1, v^(i)_n+1]^T = A_g [p^(i)_n, v^(i)_n]^T + B_g a^(i)_n`, for each agent `i = 1, 2`.
 Where `p`, `v`, `a` are position, velocity, and acceleration, respectively; `A_g` and `B_g` are standard double integrator matrices.
 - **Optimization Problem:** The core of the MPC layer is an optimization problem that minimizes a cost function over a prediction horizon `N`:
 `min_(a^(1)_n, a^(2)_n) sum_(k=n)^(n+N-1) [f_k^T Q_f f_k + g^(1)_k + g^(2)_k] + P[f_n+N^T Q_f f_n+N + g^(1)_n+N + g^(2)_n+N]`
 Where:
 - `f_k` is the concatenation of tactile embeddings from both agents `[f^(1)_k, f^(2)_k]^T`.
 - `f_k^T Q_f f_k` is a learnable cost term for the tactile sensory input, capturing the coupling between the two grippers. `Q_f` is a learnable quadratic penalty matrix.
 - `g^(i)_k = Q_v (v^(i)_k)^2 + Q_a (a^(i)_k)^2` penalizes velocity magnitude and control effort (acceleration) for agent `i`.
 - `P` is a scalar that amplifies the terminal cost weight.
 - **Constraints:** The optimization is subject to the gripper motion dynamics (Equations 6 and 7) and a tactile hidden state dynamic (Equation 5):
 `f^(i)_k+1 = f^(i)_k + A_f V^(i)_k + C_f V^(-i)_k`
 Where `A_f` represents how an agent's velocity influences its own hidden state, and `C_f` is a coupling term encoding how the velocity of one agent affects the hidden state of the other. Position, velocity, and acceleration are bounded within `min` and `max` values (Equation 10).
 - **Differentiability:** The MPC layer is fully differentiable, enabling its integration into end-to-end learning pipelines. It utilizes OSQP, a solver for convex Quadratic Programs (QPs), allowing gradients to backpropagate through the solution, which is crucial for gradient-based optimization of controller parameters.

#### 3.3. Training

- **Loss Function:** The overall loss function `L` is a combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) terms:
 `L = MSE(hat_Y_1, Y_1) + MSE(hat_Y_2, Y_2) + MSE(S * hat_y^(T)_1, S * y^(T)_1) + MSE(S * hat_y^(T)_2, S * y^(T)_2)`
 Where `hat_Y` and `Y` are predicted and ground-truth trajectories (all timesteps), `hat_y^(T)` and `y^(T)` are predictions and ground-truths for the last timestep. `S = 3` is a scalar weight to emphasize the importance of the final timestep (slippage opening).
- **Learnable Parameters:** The controller is trained end-to-end by optimizing:
 - `A_f ∈ R^(N×1)`: Component of the primary state transition matrix in the tactile dynamics.
 - `C_f ∈ R^(1×N)`: Coupling term in the tactile dynamics, influencing cross-agent hidden states.
 - `Q_f ∈ R^(2(N+2)×2(N+2))`: Learnable quadratic penalty matrix for the tactile state embeddings.
 - `α`: A learned scalar coefficient for scaling coupling blocks within the `Q` matrix to maintain positive semi-definiteness.
- **Non-learned Parameters:** `Q_v`, `Q_a`, and `P_q` are manually tuned positive scalars for numerical stability and regularization.
- **Parameter Selection:** The embedding size `M` and prediction horizon `N` are selected carefully. Excessively large `N` led to high initial losses and convergence issues during experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed multi-agent tactile-reactive MPC system involved training the model for 200 epochs and comparing its real-world grasping performance against single-agent MPC and single-agent PD baselines.

- **Training Performance:**
 - The model demonstrated convergence during training, with both training and validation losses decreasing over 200 epochs (Figure 7).

- **Grasping Success Rates (Table III):**
 - The multi-agent MPC (Our method) achieved higher success rates across all tested objects compared to both single-agent baselines.
 - For objects like the Banana, Pepperoni Stick, Pringles Can, and PVC Pipe, the proposed method achieved a perfect 10/10 success rate. For the Coffee Beans Bag, it achieved 9/10.
 - In contrast, the single-agent MPC baseline showed success rates ranging from 7/10 (Pepperoni Stick, Coffee Beans Bag) to 10/10 (PVC Pipe). The single-agent PD baseline generally performed lower, with success rates between 5/10 (Pringles Can) and 7/10 (Banana).
 - A trial was considered successful if a stable grasp was achieved and maintained without object slippage for at least 15 seconds, and without visible damage to the object.

- **Grasp Stability (Figure 6):**
 - Qualitative analysis of gripper opening over time indicated that the proposed multi-agent MPC layer yielded substantially smaller differences in opening between agents and maintained more consistent gripper positions once a grasp was established.
 - Baseline methods, even in "successful" trials, often exhibited pronounced fluctuations and inconsistent behavior in gripper opening, suggesting less stable grasps. The multi-agent approach demonstrated a steady grasp throughout the trial due to its collaborative design.

- **Impact of Tactile Data Consistency:**
 - The proposed model and the LeTac-MPC baseline utilize RGB tactile images, which are reported to be more consistent across runs. This consistency facilitates repeatable behavior for the controller.
 - The baseline PD model, which relies on binary depth images from tactile sensors, often required recalibration between runs due to inconsistent depth sensor quality.

- **Runtime Efficiency (Table IV):**
 - Despite processing effectively twice the input data and performing matrix operations with doubled dimensions compared to the single-agent case, the multi-agent implementation exhibited an average runtime increase of approximately 45% for batch sizes greater than one.
 - This represents a favorable computational trade-off, as it significantly outperforms a 100% slowdown that would be expected under linear scaling assumptions.
 - The efficiency improvement is attributed to parameter sharing across the learnable MPC and CNN layers, leading to more efficient construction of state transition matrices.
 - For a batch size of one, a higher increase (136.56%) was observed, which the authors attribute primarily to computational overhead from the PyTorch framework rather than core inefficiency.

In summary, the main findings indicate that the learning-based collaborative multi-agent MPC significantly enhances grasping success rates and stability for a variety of objects, particularly elongated and varied-stiffness items. This performance is achieved with a computationally efficient multi-agent design, leveraging homogeneous parameter sharing and consistent tactile sensory input.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly within the domain of multi-agent systems and tactile sensing. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several key aspects:

- **Enhanced Grasping Capability for Complex Objects:** The primary impact is the extended capability of robotic systems to effectively grasp and manipulate objects that are fragile, deformable, or of irregular shapes and sizes. By moving beyond the limitations of single-agent systems, the proposed bi-manual, tactile-reactive approach offers a solution for objects that are challenging due to their compliance, weight, or extended dimensions. This is particularly relevant for scenarios requiring precise force distribution and multiple contact points to ensure stability and prevent damage.
- **Improved Grasp Stability and Reliability:** The experimental results demonstrate that the collaborative multi-agent MPC leads to significantly more stable grasps compared to single-agent baselines. This enhanced stability reduces the likelihood of object slippage or failure during manipulation, contributing to greater reliability in robotic tasks. The ability to maintain stable grasps even with asymmetric gripper openings further broadens its practical utility.
- **Efficient Multi-Agent Control Framework:** The implementation of parameter sharing within a homogeneous multi-agent framework provides a computationally efficient solution. The observed runtime increase, which is less than proportional to the increase in agents and data, indicates that the system offers substantial performance benefits without a prohibitive computational cost. This efficiency is crucial for real-time robotic applications where rapid control adjustments are necessary.
- **Leveraging Rich Tactile Feedback:** The system's reliance on high-resolution tactile images from GelSight Mini sensors, coupled with a learning-based approach, enables real-time estimation of contact dynamics and object compliance. This rich sensory information allows the controller to adapt its policy to diverse object geometries and stiffness profiles, mimicking a fundamental aspect of human manipulation. The use of consistent RGB tactile images, as opposed to less reliable depth images, also contributes to the system's robustness and reduces the need for frequent recalibration.
- **Foundational for Advanced Robotic Applications:** This work introduces a framework for successfully integrating tactile sensing into robotic manipulation at a multi-agent scale, which is a relatively unexplored area. This lays foundational groundwork for a wide range of future applications where collaborative, dexterous manipulation is critical. Potential domains include:
 - **Construction Sites:** Manipulating large, heavy, and irregularly shaped structural materials.
 - **Search and Rescue:** Delicate handling of patients or debris in hazardous environments.
 - **Food Preparation:** Grasping and manipulating varied food items with different textures and fragilities.
 - **Robotic Surgery:** Precision handling of tissues and instruments in confined spaces.
- **Directions for Future Research:** The paper also delineates clear pathways for future work, addressing current limitations such as sensor-to-sensor variability, computational demands, and challenges with extremely soft or damaging objects. Avenues like transfer and continual learning for cross-sensor robustness, decentralized learning for scalability, and human-in-the-loop collaboration indicate the potential for further advancements building upon this core framework.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the capabilities of multi-agent robotic systems by providing a robust, intelligent, and computationally efficient solution for collaborative, tactile-reactive grasping. Its contributions extend beyond theoretical formulation to validated real-world performance, paving the way for more versatile and reliable robotic manipulators in complex, unstructured environments.
