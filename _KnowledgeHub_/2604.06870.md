# RefineAnything: Multimodal Region-Specific Refinement for Perfect Local Details

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Zhejiang University and Harvard University developed RefineAnything, a multimodal diffusion framework for region-specific image refinement. This framework addresses "local detail collapse" by restoring fine-grained details within user-specified regions using a Focus-and-Refine strategy and a Boundary Consistency Loss, achieving near-perfect background preservation and up to a 50% reduction in MSE for edited regions compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Image generation and editing models frequently suffer from "local detail collapse," where fine-grained elements like text or logos become distorted or inconsistent.
- Existing image editing methods, designed for coarse semantic changes, struggle with precise local refinement due to weak region controllability, insufficient micro-detail recovery, and unwanted background alterations (background drift).
- Modern diffusion models operating in fixed-resolution latent spaces often allocate insufficient pixel budget to small target regions, hindering the recovery of subtle details.

## Method

- A multimodal diffusion framework, **RefineAnything**, was developed, utilizing a VLM encoder, VAE, and an ==MMDiT backbone==, specifically tailored for precise, region-specific image refinement.
- The ==Focus-and-Refine strategy== crops and resizes the target region to concentrate computational capacity with blended-mask paste-back for background preservation, while a ==Boundary Consistency Loss== increases supervisory signal at edit boundaries to reduce seam artifacts.
- The Refine-30K dataset (20K reference-based, 10K reference-free) and RefineEval benchmark (67 real-world cases) were constructed to enable large-scale training and rigorous evaluation.

## Results

- RefineAnything demonstrated superior edited-region fidelity, achieving a **50**% reduction in MSE (**0.020** vs. **0.040**) and a **41**% reduction in LPIPS (**0.155** vs. **0.264**) compared to the best open-source baseline on the reference-based RefineEval.
- The model achieved near-perfect background preservation, with MSE_bg and LPIPS_bg scores of **0.000**, significantly outperforming baselines that exhibited background drift.
- In reference-free scenarios, RefineAnything consistently ranked first in VLM-based subjective evaluations across visual quality, naturalness, aesthetics, fine-detail fidelity, and instruction faithfulness, surpassing baselines by over **0.7** points in several categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reallocating a fixed-resolution model's capacity by cropping and resizing a small target region significantly improves local reconstruction quality, highlighting the importance of focused attention for fine details.
> - Effective integration of multimodal conditioning (VLM-derived tokens and VAE latents) is crucial for precise control over both semantic and fine-grained visual aspects of the refinement process.
> - Dedicated training with a boundary-aware loss function is essential for achieving seamless and natural integration of refined regions, preventing visible seam artifacts at edit boundaries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image generation has made substantial progress, with diffusion models and multimodal generative models demonstrating improved fidelity, controllability, and instruction following. Models such as the Stable Diffusion family (SD1.5, SDXL) and more recent Diffusion Transformers (Hunyuan-DiT, PixArt, SD3, FLUX) have significantly advanced the state of the art. Concurrently, image editing models (e.g., FLUX Kontext, OmniGen2, BAGEL, Qwen-Edit) have emerged, leveraging these powerful generators to perform manipulations based on text instructions or other inputs.

Despite these advancements, a persistent challenge in real-world applications is the frequent occurrence of "local detail collapse." This phenomenon involves the distortion or inconsistency of fine-grained elements such as printed text, logos, or thin structures within an otherwise plausible image. This issue is particularly problematic in applications where small details convey critical information, such as e-commerce, advertising, or user interfaces.

Existing image editing paradigms primarily focus on coarse-grained semantic edits. When applied to local detail refinement, they often exhibit several limitations: weak region controllability, insufficient micro-detail recovery, and unintended alterations to non-target areas (background drift). These shortcomings indicate a gap in current generative models' ability to perform high-precision, localized corrections without affecting the surrounding context.

RefineAnything addresses this specific gap by formally introducing and tackling the problem of "region-specific image refinement." This dedicated problem setting aims to restore fine-grained details within a user-specified region while ensuring that all non-edited pixels remain strictly unchanged. By focusing on this precise task, the work positions itself as a specialized solution to overcome a significant practical barrier in the deployment of advanced image generation and editing technologies. It seeks to provide a tool that is simultaneously accurate in its regional targeting, effective in detail recovery, and strictly preserving of the background, thereby complementing the broader capabilities of existing generative and editing models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recurring failure mode observed in contemporary image generation and editing models: the inability to consistently produce or restore fine-grained local details without distortion or inconsistency. This "local detail collapse" manifests in issues such as malformed text, corrupted logos, or unnatural thin structures, even when the overall image composition is visually coherent. Such defects are deemed critical in practical scenarios where precise details carry significant information, including e-commerce product imagery, advertising materials, and user interface graphics. A single error in text or a flawed stroke can diminish trust and usability.

To address this challenge, the authors define a new problem setting: **region-specific image refinement**. The objective is to take an input image and a user-specified region (e.g., via a scribble mask or bounding box) and enhance the local details within that region, while strictly ensuring that all pixels outside the designated region remain entirely unaltered.

The detailed motivations for proposing this dedicated problem setting and developing the RefineAnything framework are:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Editing Models:** Current instruction-driven image editing models are not well-suited for high-precision local refinement due to three specific issues:
 1. **Weak Region Controllability:** It is often challenging for users to precisely indicate the exact area to be refined, leading to edits that are not perfectly localized.
 2. **Poor Micro-Detail Recovery:** Existing models frequently fail to resolve subtle defects, such as broken text strokes or intricate patterns, leaving the target region still imperfect.
 3. **Background Drift:** A significant drawback is the unintentional alteration of non-target regions, which can occur even when the region of interest is small. This background drift undermines the utility of the tool for precise edits.

- **Meeting User Requirements for Practicality:** In practical applications, users require a refinement tool that exhibits three key characteristics simultaneously:
 1. **Region Accuracy:** The refinement must be confined precisely to the user-specified area.
 2. **Detail Effectiveness:** The tool must be capable of accurately restoring or enhancing fine-grained details.
 3. **Background Preservation:** The rest of the image, outside the edited region, must remain absolutely unchanged.

- **Overcoming Resolution Allocation Challenges:** The authors identify that modern diffusion models, which operate in a fixed-resolution VAE latent space, allocate a limited "pixel budget" across the entire image. When a target region is small, it receives insufficient effective pixel information, hindering micro-detail recovery. A key motivation emerges from the counter-intuitive observation that simply cropping a small target region and resizing it to the full VAE input resolution significantly improves reconstruction quality within that region, even though no new information is introduced. This implies that the problem is often one of attention and resolution allocation rather than inherent information loss.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the RefineAnything project are:

1. To develop a multimodal diffusion-based refinement model that effectively restores fine-grained local details in user-specified regions.
2. To ensure strict preservation of non-edited regions, eliminating background drift.
3. To support both reference-based and reference-free refinement scenarios.
4. To introduce a novel strategy, Focus-and-Refine, which leverages the insight about resolution allocation to enhance refinement quality and efficiency.
5. To develop a Boundary Consistency Loss to further improve the seamless integration of refined regions.
6. To construct a dedicated dataset (Refine-30K) and an evaluation benchmark (RefineEval) to facilitate training and rigorous assessment of region-specific refinement models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RefineAnything is designed for localized image refinement, taking an input image (I), an optional reference image (I_ref), a user-provided scribble mask (M) indicating the edit region, and a text instruction (y). The goal is to refine the specified region while strictly preserving the remainder of the image.

#### 3.1. Architecture

The overall framework builds upon the Qwen-Image model and comprises three main components:

- **Frozen Multimodal Encoder (Qwen2.5-VL):** This encoder processes the input image, optional reference image, region cue, and text instruction to generate high-level multimodal conditioning tokens. These tokens provide semantic and instructional guidance to the denoiser through joint-attention mechanisms.
- **VAE (Variational Autoencoder):** A VAE maps both the input and optional reference images into a compact latent space, providing low-level, fine-grained visual context. These latents serve as additional conditioning branches for the diffusion backbone.
- **Diffusion Backbone (MMDiT blocks):** The core denoiser is an MMDiT (MultiModal Diffusion Transformer) backbone, adapted from Qwen-Image. It iteratively removes noise from a target latent representation, conditioned on both the multimodal tokens from the VLM and the VAE latent branches. For training, only LoRA parameters on attention projections are optimized.

#### 3.2. Focus-and-Refine Strategy

This strategy is motivated by the observation that cropping a small target region and resizing it to a fixed input resolution (e.g., 1024x1024) significantly improves VAE reconstruction quality within that region, even without introducing new information. This suggests that explicitly focusing the model's fixed-resolution capacity on the target area is beneficial. Focus-and-Refine consists of three steps:

- **Region Localization and Focus Crop:**
 1. A tight bounding box (B) is computed around the user-provided scribble mask (M).
 2. This bounding box is expanded by a margin (m = 64 pixels) to create a focus crop box (C), which is clipped to the image boundaries. The margin provides essential local context.
 3. The input image (I) and corresponding mask (M) are cropped and resized according to (C) to obtain a focused view (I_c) and cropped mask (M_c).
- **Focused Generation with Spatial Conditioning:**
 1. The cropped view (I_c), optional reference image (I_ref), and cropped mask (M_c) are used as inputs for conditional generation.
 2. The RefineAnything model (G) generates a refined crop (e_I_c) based on these inputs and the text instruction (y).
- **Seamless Paste-Back via a Blended Mask:**
 1. To prevent visible seams at the crop boundary, a blended mask (f_M_c) is created by morphologically dilating (kernel size r=7) and then Gaussian smoothing (kernel size k=11) the cropped mask (M_c).
 2. The refined crop (e_I_c) is composited with the original cropped image (I_c) using the blended mask: `b_I_c = f_M_c ⊙ e_I_c + (1 − f_M_c) ⊙ I_c`.
 3. Finally, `b_I_c` is resized and pasted back onto the original full canvas at the location (C) to produce the final output image (b_I). This construction inherently ensures strict background preservation.

#### 3.3. Boundary Consistency Loss

To enhance the naturalness of the paste-back and reduce seam artifacts, a boundary-aware loss is introduced during training.
- A "boundary band" (B_c) is defined as the region between a dilated and an eroded version of the cropped mask (M_c), with dilation/erosion kernel sizes r_out = r_in = 16.
- During training, the base flow-matching denoising objective (`ℓ_base`) is weighted more heavily in this boundary band. The boundary-weighted objective `L_boundary` incorporates `(1 + αB_c)` (with α = 9) multiplied element-wise with `ℓ_base`, effectively upweighting supervision near the edit boundary.

#### 3.4. Refine-30K Dataset Construction

To enable large-scale training and evaluation, the Refine-30K dataset was constructed, comprising 20K reference-based and 10K reference-free samples. The construction pipelines are designed to generate challenging refinement tasks while preserving background integrity:

- **Reference-Based Refine Data:**
 1. **Cross-image grounding:** A VLM (Gemini3) identifies a salient subject in a reference image (I_ref) and localizes it with a bounding box (B) in a target image (I_⋆). Strict subject-consistency checks are applied.
 2. **Mask generation with SAM:** SAM3, conditioned by the VLM box and textual description, generates an object mask (M_obj) for the subject in I_⋆, ensuring a single-instance mask.
 3. **Scribble degradation via inpainting:** Random scribble strokes are generated within a dilated M_obj to create an inpainting mask (M). The target image (I_⋆) is then inpainted using (M) to produce a degraded input image (I) (`e_I = Inpaint(I_⋆, M)`). A light paste-back blending ensures differences only within the edited region.
 4. **Instruction and outputs:** The sample stores (I, I_ref, I_⋆, M, y), where (y) is a VLM-derived instruction referring to the localized region.
- **Reference-Free Refine Data:**
 1. **Salient object localization:** A VLM (Gemini3) detects salient objects in a single image (I_⋆) and provides candidate bounding boxes. One object (B) is randomly sampled.
 2. **Masking and degradation:** Similar to the reference-based process, SAM3 generates M_obj, random scribbles form M within a dilated M_obj, and I is synthesized via inpainting from I_⋆.
 3. **VLM-based defect validation:** A VLM (Gemini3-Pro) judges if the degraded image (I) has noticeable, plausible defects. Samples without obvious defects or with semantic inconsistencies are discarded.
 4. **Instruction and outputs:** The sample stores (I, I_⋆, M, y), where (y) is a reference-free instruction generated from the VLM description.

#### 3.5. RefineEval Benchmark

A benchmark named RefineEval was curated to evaluate the model's performance in both reference-based and reference-free settings. It comprises 67 manually annotated cases (31 reference-based, 36 reference-free) from open-source websites. Degraded inputs are synthesized using multiple inpainting methods (Flux-fill, SDXL, Qwen-Edit) and seeds, resulting in 402 degraded inputs.

- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Reference-Based:** Edited-region fidelity is measured by MSE, SSIM, LPIPS, VGG, DINO (dino-v2 large), and CLIP (clip-vit-large-patch14-336) similarities between the refined image and the ground truth. Background preservation is quantified by MSE_bg, LPIPS_bg, and SSIM_bg, comparing the refined image to the input image outside the edit region.
 - **Reference-Free:** A VLM-based evaluator (Gemini2.5-Pro) scores the expanded foreground crop on five subjective dimensions: visual quality (VQ), naturalness (Nat.), aesthetics (Aes.), fine-detail fidelity (Det.), and instruction faithfulness (Faith.), on a scale.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RefineAnything model, alongside its proposed methodologies, was evaluated against various competitive baselines using the RefineEval benchmark.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results on Reference-Based Image Refinement

On the reference-based subset of RefineEval, RefineAnything demonstrated improved performance across both edited-region fidelity and background preservation metrics (Table 1).
- **Edited-Region Fidelity:** RefineAnything achieved lower reconstruction errors compared to the best open-source baseline (Kontext), with MSE reduced by 50% (0.020 vs. 0.040), LPIPS by 41% (0.155 vs. 0.264), and VGG by 26% (0.401 vs. 0.540). Semantic alignment was also improved, with DINO similarity increasing by +0.108 (0.793 vs. 0.685) and CLIP similarity by +0.100 (0.885 vs. 0.785), and SSIM improved from 0.538 to 0.591.
- **Background Preservation:** The model achieved near-perfect background consistency, registering MSE_bg of 0.000, LPIPS_bg of 0.000, and an SSIM_bg of 0.9997. This performance notably surpassed baselines which exhibited background drift (e.g., Kontext with MSE_bg = 0.011, Qwen-Edit with MSE_bg = 0.454).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results on Reference-Free Image Refinement

For reference-free refinement, where evaluation relied on VLM-based subjective scores across five dimensions (Table 2), RefineAnything consistently ranked first:
- The model surpassed the strongest open-source baseline (Qwen-Edit) across all criteria: Visual Quality (+0.725), Naturalness (+0.758), Aesthetics (+0.771), Fine-Detail Fidelity (+0.745), and Instruction Faithfulness (+0.430). These results suggest that RefineAnything produces more natural, detailed, and instruction-adherent refinements even without an explicit visual reference.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons (Figures 6 and 7) illustrate the model's capabilities. Prior methods often showed issues with background preservation, inconsistent responsiveness to instructions or references, and limited capacity to restore fine details. RefineAnything, enabled by its Focus-and-Refine strategy, demonstrated effective restoration of subtle details while maintaining the background unchanged, addressing these limitations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to ascertain the contribution of key components (Table 3, Figures 8 and 9):
- **Focus-and-Refine:** Removing the Focus-and-Refine step led to weaker refinements, frequently leaving subtle errors unaddressed and occasionally introducing artifacts. Its inclusion resulted in sharper local details and background preservation. Quantitatively, removing Focus-and-Refine increased MSE to 0.021 (from 0.020), LPIPS to 0.177 (from 0.155), and reduced SSIM to 0.578 (from 0.591), while also impairing background consistency (e.g., MSE_bg 0.005 vs. 0.000).
- **Boundary Consistency Loss:** The absence of the Boundary Consistency Loss during training resulted in reduced coherence between the refined region and its surroundings. This manifested as visible seams, color discrepancies, and structurally implausible stitching along object boundaries. While background consistency metrics remained near-perfect without this loss (MSE_bg and LPIPS_bg 0.000), other metrics indicated a degradation in quality (e.g., MSE increased to 0.023, LPIPS to 0.191, and SSIM decreased to 0.563), suggesting an impact on the seamless integration of the refined region.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a dedicated framework for region-specific image refinement, addressing a practical limitation in current image generation and editing systems. The work makes several contributions that have both methodological and practical significance.

#### 5.1. New Problem Formulation and Solution

The paper formally defines "region-specific image refinement" as a distinct problem setting, emphasizing the restoration of local details within a user-defined area while strictly preserving the integrity of all non-edited pixels. By explicitly formulating this problem, the authors highlight a critical gap where existing instruction-driven editing models often fall short due to weak region controllability, poor micro-detail recovery, and unintended background modifications. RefineAnything offers a practical solution to this problem, designed to be simultaneously region-accurate, detail-effective, and background-preserving.

#### 5.2. Methodological Innovations

The paper proposes two key methodological contributions:
- **Focus-and-Refine Strategy:** This strategy leverages an empirical observation that reallocating the fixed resolution budget of VAE-based models to a cropped region significantly improves local reconstruction quality. By concentrating computational and representational capacity on the target area, the method enhances refinement effectiveness and efficiency. The integrated blended-mask paste-back mechanism is designed to guarantee strict background preservation.
- **Boundary Consistency Loss:** This boundary-aware loss function addresses the challenge of seamless integration by specifically increasing supervisory signal at the edit boundaries during training. This directly contributes to reducing seam artifacts and improving the naturalness of the paste-back operation.

#### 5.3. Resource Contributions

To support the development and evaluation of models in this new problem setting, the authors constructed two significant resources:
- **Refine-30K Dataset:** A large-scale dataset comprising 20,000 reference-based and 10,000 reference-free samples. This dataset, built using VLM grounding, SAM-based segmentation, and controlled inpainting degradations, provides a comprehensive training ground for region-specific refinement models.
- **RefineEval Benchmark:** An evaluation benchmark curated with 67 real-world cases, including both reference-based and reference-free scenarios. This benchmark, alongside its detailed evaluation metrics (edited-region fidelity and background preservation, including VLM-based subjective scores), provides a standardized tool for rigorous assessment and comparison of future research.

#### 5.4. Potential Impact

The significance of RefineAnything lies in its ability to address a critical practical failure mode of existing generative AI models. The capacity to accurately and seamlessly correct fine-grained details in specific regions, such as distorted text, logos, or faces, without altering the surrounding context, has broad implications across various high-stakes applications:
- **E-commerce and Advertising:** Enables precise correction of product images, ensuring accurate branding and text, which can directly influence consumer trust and sales.
- **User Interface (UI) and Infographics:** Facilitates fixing minor textual errors or graphical inconsistencies in digital content, enhancing usability and professional appearance.
- **Creative Content Generation:** Provides artists and designers with a tool for detailed post-generation adjustments, improving the quality and realism of AI-generated assets.
- **General Image Editing Workflows:** Integrates as a high-precision tool for localized touch-ups, complementing existing coarse-grained editing functionalities.

By establishing a practical solution and providing foundational datasets and benchmarks, RefineAnything is expected to facilitate future research in high-precision local image refinement, contributing to the development of more robust and reliable generative AI systems for real-world deployment.
