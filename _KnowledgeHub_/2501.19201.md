# Efficient Reasoning with Hidden Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework called Heima allows Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform complex reasoning efficiently by compressing verbose Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps into single "thinking tokens." This approach reduces token generation to as low as 6% of original counts while maintaining or improving zero-shot accuracy on diverse benchmarks and enabling interpretation of the hidden reasoning process through a dedicated decoder.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting generate verbose textual reasoning steps.
- This verbosity leads to high computational costs, increased inference latency, and significant memory footprint, hindering practical deployment.
- Existing CoT compression or efficiency methods often result in performance degradation or lack generalizability to large-scale multimodal models.

## Method

- Introducing ==Heima==, a novel framework comprising a ==Heima Encoder and Heima Decoder==, for efficient multimodal reasoning.
- The Heima Encoder compresses verbose Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps into single, compact =="thinking tokens"== through a progressive encoding fine-tuning strategy.
- The Heima Decoder, a separate LLM, reconstructs textual CoTs from the ==hidden states of thinking tokens==, validating the encapsulation of reasoning.

## Results

- Heima achieved substantial token reduction, generating as little as **6%** of the original token count on various multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- The framework consistently outperformed baseline models like Llama3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct and LLaVA-CoT in both efficiency and zero-shot accuracy across 6 diverse benchmarks, notably improving MMBench accuracy from **70.7%** to **72.8%**.
- The Heima Decoder successfully reconstructed textual Chain-of-Thought steps from the hidden thinking tokens, confirming that complex multimodal reasoning information is effectively encapsulated in the compressed latent representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex Chain-of-Thought reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can be effectively compressed into compact hidden representations (single "thinking tokens") without sacrificing accuracy.
> - A progressive fine-tuning strategy is essential for MLLMs to internalize verbose textual reasoning into these hidden states while maintaining or improving performance.
> - Separately trained decoders can successfully reconstruct high-quality textual explanations from these hidden thinking tokens, validating that genuine, interpretable reasoning is encapsulated in the latent space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which integrate visual processing with traditional Large Language Models (LLMs), has highlighted the utility of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. CoT methods enhance problem-solving by generating intermediate textual reasoning steps, thereby improving interpretability and enabling multi-step reasoning for complex tasks, particularly those involving intricate visual information.

However, the generation of verbose textual CoTs, especially for challenging problems, leads to substantial inference costs and computational inefficiencies in large generative models. This has driven research into methods for improving reasoning efficiency in MLLMs.

Previous efforts in this direction include:
- **Latent Reasoning Methods:** Some works explore compressing CoTs for smaller, text-only models, such as GPT-2 on specific tasks like math datasets (Hao et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2024b). However, these approaches often do not directly address the challenges of large-scale MLLMs handling general reasoning tasks with multimodal inputs.
- **Visual Decoding of Latent Information:** Other studies investigate latent reasoning in MLLMs by employing visual decoders for tasks like segmentation, detection, and recognition (Pi et al., 2023; Yan et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2024a). These works demonstrate the potential for decoding visual information from MLLM-generated tokens but primarily focus on downstream visual tasks rather than the construction of efficient internal feature representations for higher-level reasoning.
- **Textual Efficient Reasoning:** Approaches in this category aim to accelerate reasoning by parallel generation (Ning et al., 2023), contextual compression (Chevalier et al., 2023; Qin et al., 2023), or compressing CoTs into continuous embeddings (Cheng & Van Durme, 2024). The latter, while directly attempting CoT compression, has shown limitations with significant accuracy degradation on some datasets, indicating the challenge of maintaining performance during such compression.

The work presented in this paper introduces Heima as a CoT compression framework specifically designed for MLLMs, aiming to bridge the identified gaps by performing reasoning in a compact latent space using "thinking tokens." This differentiates it from prior work by focusing on general multimodal reasoning, addressing large-scale models, and striving to preserve reasoning accuracy effectively during compression.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent inefficiency associated with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). While CoT enhances MLLMs' ability to solve complex problems and improves interpretability, it often necessitates the generation of extensive textual reasoning steps. This verbosity leads to high inference costs, increased memory usage, and slower generation times, particularly for intricate multimodal tasks. The goal is to develop methods that enable MLLMs to retain the benefits of CoT reasoning—such as multi-step logical understanding and contextual coherence—without incurring prohibitive computational expenses.

The specific objectives of this work are:

- **Develop an Efficient CoT Compression Framework:** To propose and implement "Heima," a novel framework that compresses verbose textual CoTs into a reduced set of abstract "thinking tokens." This framework is designed to operate in the latent space of MLLMs, aiming to significantly reduce the number of tokens generated during reasoning while preserving essential reasoning capabilities.
- **Theoretically Quantify Compression-Induced Information Gap:** To provide a formal information-theoretic analysis of CoT compression. This involves quantifying the potential loss of task-relevant information when verbose textual CoTs are condensed into compact thinking tokens and establishing conditions under which reasoning effectiveness is maintained (i.e., when non-trivial mutual information is retained).
- **Empirically Validate Information Preservation through Reconstruction:** To design and implement "adaptive interpreters" that can map the compact thinking tokens back into variable-length textual reasoning sequences. This empirical reconstruction process serves to assess the practical magnitude of the information gap induced by compression and to verify that the thinking tokens encapsulate sufficient information to reconstruct coherent and semantically aligned reasoning processes.
- **Demonstrate Improved Reasoning Efficiency with Maintained Accuracy:** To experimentally demonstrate that Heima can achieve substantial improvements in reasoning efficiency, characterized by a significant reduction in generated tokens, while maintaining or even surpassing the zero-shot accuracy of MLLMs employing traditional verbose CoTs across a range of multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- **Enhance Interpretability of Latent Reasoning:** To show that the compressed "thinking tokens" are not merely opaque numerical representations but carry interpretable reasoning information, as evidenced by the successful reconstruction of coherent textual reasoning traces by the interpreters, even without direct visual input to the interpreters themselves.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Heima, focuses on efficient reasoning by compressing verbose Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanations into compact "thinking tokens" within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The methodology consists of three main components: the Heima framework for CoT compression, an information-theoretic analysis, and the design of interpreters for validating the compression.

#### 3.1. Heima Framework

Heima is designed to distill textual CoTs into latent thinking tokens to accelerate reasoning.

- **Dataset Preparation:** The initial CoT training dataset, `D = {(X, CoTs, Y)}`, where `X` represents visual and query inputs, `CoTs` is a sequence of textual CoT stages, and `Y` is the final answer, is transformed. Each textual CoT stage `CoT(k)` is replaced with a unique special thinking token, `<CoT>(k)`. For example, `<Thinking_of_Summary>` replaces the summary stage. These special tokens are added to the model's vocabulary. The modified dataset, `D_H = {(X, <CoTs>, Y)}`, is then used for training Heima.
- **Distillation Objective:** The MLLM (Heima) is fine-tuned to predict these thinking tokens and the final answer. The training objective is to maximize the likelihood `log P_θ(<CoTs>, Y | X)`. This distillation aims to teach the model to internalize reasoning processes and represent them compactly.
- **Progressive Distillation:** To ensure a smooth transition from textual to latent reasoning and preserve performance, a progressive distillation strategy is employed. Instead of distilling all CoT stages simultaneously, the process occurs in `M` stages (where `M` is `max{K_i} + 1`, with `K_i` being the number of CoT stages in sample `i`). In each stage `s` (from `0` to `M-1`), the first `s` CoT stages are represented by thinking tokens, while the remaining stages `(k > s)` are still textual CoTs. The model is fine-tuned on this progressively updated dataset: `max_θ E log P_θ({<CoT>(k)}s k=1, {CoT(k)}K_i k=s+1, Y | X)`. This gradual approach allows the model to incrementally learn and integrate reasoning into thinking tokens.
- **Additional Recovering Stage:** After the progressive distillation completes, an extra recovering stage is introduced. In this stage, the model is further optimized using only thinking tokens (Equation 3) across all CoT stages. This stage aims to consolidate the learned information, optimize transitions between thinking tokens, and enhance overall performance and robustness.
- **Efficient Reasoning (Inference):** During inference, Heima generates a sequence of `K_i` thinking tokens, `<CoTs>`, followed by the `T` tokens of the final answer `Y`. The total number of generated intermediate tokens is `K_i`, which is significantly less than the sum of token lengths of the original verbose textual CoTs, `sum(|CoT(k)|)`, thereby achieving efficiency.

#### 3.2. Information-Theoretic Compression

The paper provides a theoretical analysis to quantify the information gap induced by CoT compression, drawing from information theory.

- **Formalization:** Given an input `X`, verbose CoTs, and task output `Y`, Heima compresses `CoTs` into ` <CoTs> = f(X, CoTs)`, where `|<CoTs>|` is much smaller than `|CoTs|`. The core question is whether ` <CoTs>` retains sufficient task-relevant information about `Y`.
- **Data-Processing Inequality:** By the data-processing inequality, the Markov chain `Y - (X, CoTs) - <CoTs>` holds, leading to `I(Y; <CoTs> | X) ≤ I(Y; CoTs | X)`. This implies that thinking tokens carry no more information than full CoTs but can still preserve non-trivial information for reasoning.
- **Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2:** These formalize the information preservation. They state that the conditional entropy `H(Y | X, CoTs) ≤ H(Y | X, <CoTs>) ≤ H(Y | X)`. The difference `I(Y; CoTs | X) − I(Y; <CoTs> | X) = I(Y; CoTs | X, <CoTs>) ≥ 0` quantifies the reasoning details lost in compression. The effectiveness of reasoning is preserved as long as `I(Y; <CoTs> | X) > 0`, meaning compressed reasoning captures non-trivial task-relevant information. The information gap `I(Y; CoTs | X, <CoTs>)` becomes zero if `<CoTs>` is sufficient for `CoTs` with respect to `Y`.

#### 3.3. Interpreter Design

To empirically assess and quantify the information gap, corresponding interpreters are designed.

- **Purpose:** These interpreters reconstruct textual reasoning traces from thinking tokens, providing an empirical proxy for evaluating the information gap and verifying the practical validity of latent reasoning. This also helps confirm if the model genuinely learns reasoning in the latent space.
- **Adaptive Interpretation:** For each reasoning stage (summary, caption, reasoning), a separate interpreter `I_θ_k(·)` is fine-tuned using a pretrained LLM.
- **Interpreter Training Data:** The training set `D_I = {(X_e, X_q, <CoT>(k), H_<CoT>(k), CoT(k))}` is used. `X_e` refers to explanatory prompts, `X_q` is the textual question, `<CoT>(k)` is the k-th thinking token, `H_<CoT>(k)` is the *last hidden state* of `<CoT>(k)` generated by the frozen Heima model, and `CoT(k)` is the original textual CoT at stage `k`. Crucially, interpreters are pure-text LLMs and do not receive visual inputs; they infer visual features from the thinking token's hidden state.
- **Input Substitution:** During interpreter training, the symbol `<CoT>(k)` is not directly fed. Instead, its corresponding last hidden state `H_<CoT>(k)` from Heima replaces the word embedding of the special token. This ensures that the interpreter decodes information encapsulated in the latent representation.
- **Explanatory Prompts:** Prompts like "According to question: `X_q`, can you explain the thinking progress `<CoT>(k)`?" are used to guide the interpreter, ensuring reconstructed reasoning aligns with the query and the hidden representations.
- **Training Objective:** The interpreter is fine-tuned with a standard next-token prediction loss: `max_θ_k E log P_θ_k(CoT(k) | X_e, X_q, H_<CoT>(k))`.
- **Validation:** By comparing the reconstructed reasoning with the original CoTs using metrics like BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE, BERTScore, and GPT-4o similarity, the study evaluates the semantic alignment and, consequently, the magnitude of the information gap.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the Heima framework across multiple dimensions.

#### 4.1. Efficiency and Accuracy of Heima

- **Reduced Token Generation:** Heima significantly reduces the number of generated tokens during inference. For instance, on some datasets, it uses as little as 6% of the tokens compared to the baseline LLaVA-CoT, which generates verbose textual reasoning.
- **Comparable or Superior Zero-Shot Accuracy:** Despite the drastic reduction in tokens, Heima maintains or achieves better zero-shot accuracy across six diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MMStar, MMBench V1.1, MMVet, MathVista, AI2D, HallusionBench).
 - Compared to the original Llama3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct model, Heima demonstrates substantial improvements in average accuracy while using fewer tokens.
 - Compared to the LLaVA-CoT baseline, Heima performs comparably or better, notably achieving higher accuracy on MMBench while using significantly fewer tokens.
- **Generalization Across Architectures:** The consistent performance is observed not only with the Llama3 model family but also with the LLaVA-Next-Vicuna-7B model family, indicating the framework's generalization capability across different MLLM architectures.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Progressive Distillation and Recovering Stage

- **Impact of Progressive Distillation:** Ablation studies reveal that the progressive distillation strategy is critical for performance. When all CoT stages are distilled in a single shot ("Heima w/o progressive"), the accuracy notably degrades compared to the progressive approach, confirming the benefit of gradual learning.
- **Necessity of Recovering Stage:** The additional "recovering stage" further optimizes the model after progressive distillation. Experiments without this stage ("Heima w/o recover") show a decline in performance, underscoring its role in consolidating learning and refining transitions between thinking tokens.

#### 4.3. Interpretability Analysis and Information Gap

- **Successful Reasoning Reconstruction:** The trained interpreters successfully reconstruct coherent and meaningful textual reasoning processes from the compact thinking tokens. This provides empirical evidence that thinking tokens encapsulate essential reasoning information.
- **Quantitative Similarity Metrics:** Evaluation using metrics such as BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, and BERTScore demonstrates a high degree of semantic alignment between the interpreter-reconstructed reasoning sentences and the original ground-truth textual CoTs.
 - Reconstruction is most successful for the "summary" stage, followed by "caption," and then "reasoning."
- **GPT-4o Similarity Evaluation:** A human-like evaluation using GPT-4o on a 5-point ranking scale further corroborates these findings, indicating that all reasoning stages are effectively reconstructed with high similarity scores.
- **Minimal Information Gap:** These results collectively suggest that the information gap introduced by compressing verbose CoTs into thinking tokens is minimal. The preserved non-trivial mutual information within the thinking tokens is sufficient to sustain effective reasoning and to allow for the reconstruction of understandable explanations.
- **Multimodal Signal Preservation:** An important finding is that thinking tokens preserve multimodal signals. Even when interpreters (which are pure LLMs) receive no visual input, they can reconstruct textual reasoning that accurately captures key insights from the original image, validating the robustness of latent reasoning in Heima.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on Thinking Token Configuration

- **Optimal Number of Thinking Tokens:** An ablation study on the number of thinking tokens per CoT stage indicates that a single thinking token per stage yields the best overall reasoning performance.
- **Adaptive Distillation and Retention Ratio:** Experiments with varying retention ratios of original CoT token length show that simply retaining a percentage of original tokens (adaptive distillation) is not an effective compression strategy. Accuracy fluctuates irregularly, and the number of generated tokens increases without a clear performance benefit, reinforcing the efficacy of the discrete thinking token approach.
- **Number of Interpreters:** An ablation study on the number of interpreters suggests that having distinct interpreters for the "summary" and "caption" stages is crucial for accurate reconstruction, while a single interpreter might perform adequately for the "reasoning" stage, highlighting task-specific reconstruction requirements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in the field of efficient reasoning within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), addressing a critical challenge related to the computational cost of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

The significance and potential impact include:

- **Enhanced MLLM Efficiency and Scalability:** Heima demonstrates a method for drastically reducing the number of tokens generated during MLLM reasoning (up to 94% reduction) without compromising, and in some cases even improving, zero-shot accuracy. This efficiency gain translates directly into faster inference times, lower computational resource consumption (e.g., memory, energy), and thus improved scalability for MLLMs in real-world applications. This can make complex multimodal reasoning more accessible and deployable, especially in resource-constrained environments or for applications requiring rapid responses.
- **Theoretical Foundation for Latent Reasoning:** The paper provides a formal information-theoretic analysis that quantifies the information retained and lost during CoT compression. This theoretical framework contributes to a deeper understanding of how reasoning capabilities are preserved in compressed latent representations and establishes conditions for effective compression. It offers a principled approach to analyzing and developing future latent reasoning models.
- **Interpretability of Compressed Representations:** The successful design and application of interpreters to reconstruct coherent textual reasoning from abstract "thinking tokens" validate that these compressed representations are not opaque but semantically meaningful. This interpretability enhances trust in latent reasoning processes and provides a tool for researchers to understand what information is encapsulated within these hidden states, particularly for multimodal inputs where visual cues are implicitly processed.
- **Advancement in Multi-Modal Reasoning:** By demonstrating that thinking tokens can preserve multimodal signals and allow for reconstruction of reasoning that effectively utilizes visual information (even when interpreters themselves do not see the image), the work contributes to the understanding and development of MLLMs capable of truly integrating and abstracting information across modalities in an efficient manner.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work paves the way for further exploration into scalable latent reasoning models. It encourages research into optimizing the compression of reasoning processes, exploring different latent representations, and extending these methods to larger and more complex MLLMs. The findings on progressive distillation and the importance of specific stages for reconstruction also offer insights for future training strategies.
- **Broad Applicability:** The ability to perform efficient, accurate, and interpretable reasoning has broad implications for various applications, including advanced virtual assistants, automated content analysis, medical diagnostics, and educational tools, where complex multimodal inputs require sophisticated yet timely responses.
