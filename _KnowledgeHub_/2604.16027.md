# Where does output diversity collapse in post-training?

> [!summary] Summary
> This study from the University of Sheffield empirically investigates the factors contributing to output diversity collapse in large language models after post-training, identifying that data composition, particularly narrow supervised fine-tuning data, is a primary driver, and that the Chain-of-Thought format does not inherently reduce diversity at inference. It reveals that models undergoing direct reinforcement learning without prior supervised fine-tuning or direct preference optimization retain substantially more original diversity, while aligned models show reduced benefits from inference-time sampling due to genuine narrowing among correct outputs.

## Problem

- Post-training techniques like SFT, DPO, and RLHF, while improving LLM capabilities, consistently lead to a reduction in output diversity, undermining inference-time scaling methods.
- Previous research often failed to isolate whether diversity collapse stems from specific post-training algorithms or from the composition of the training data itself.
- It remained unclear if the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation format itself constrains diversity during inference, or if the collapse is already embedded within the model's learned weights.

## Method

- Conducted a controlled experimental study across **13 Olmo 3 (7B) model checkpoints** representing different post-training lineages (==Base==, ==Think==, ==Instruct==, ==RL-Zero==) and stages (SFT, DPO, RL).
- Output diversity quantified using four complementary metrics: ==lexical (EAD)==, ==semantic (SBERT)==, ==logical (NLI)==, and ==effective number of modes (Vendi Score)==, across 15 diverse tasks.
- Experiments with ==Think-not-thinking== models isolated the effect of the CoT format, and diversity loss was decomposed into a quality-control component and genuine narrowing among correct outputs.

## Results

- Think models, using narrow CoT distillation, experienced an average of **62% SBERT diversity collapse** at the SFT stage, compared to **38%** for Instruct models trained on multi-source data.
- **RL-Zero** models retained a median of **94%** of the Base model's semantic diversity, while SFT/DPO-aligned models reached similar low diversity floors.
- Suppressing CoT at inference time in 'Think-not-thinking' models **did not recover diversity** but caused accuracy drops of up to **48%**, confirming diversity loss is in model weights.
- Post-trained models show reduced benefits from majority voting (**+0.4% on GSM8K for Think vs. +24% for Base**) due to persistent narrowing among correct outputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The composition of post-training data dictates the timing and severity of diversity collapse; narrow distillation data causes steep collapse at SFT, while broader multi-source data shifts it to DPO.
> - Diversity collapse is primarily embedded in the model weights during training, not a consequence of the Chain-of-Thought generation format at inference time.
> - RL-Zero models, bypassing SFT and DPO, retain significantly more of the Base model's diversity, indicating that SFT and DPO are the primary stages where diversity is lost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has increasingly relied on post-training techniques such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO), and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to enhance capabilities like helpfulness, safety, and instruction compliance. However, this post-training often leads to a reduction in output diversity, a phenomenon referred to as "output diversity collapse." This collapse is observed across various tasks including summarization, reasoning, and open-ended generation, and has implications for inference-time scaling methods such as self-consistency, pass@k sampling, and test-time compute scaling, which rely on generating varied samples. Prior research has identified diversity as a mechanism underlying inference scaling laws.

Existing literature has attributed diversity collapse to specific post-training algorithms, such as DPO in narrative generation or SFT in reasoning tasks. However, these studies have not consistently disentangled the influence of the training data's composition from the specific post-training method, nor have they separated the impact of the generation format (e.g., chain-of-thought) from the learned model weights. This paper seeks to address these gaps by conducting a controlled investigation into the factors contributing to diversity collapse, thereby providing a more nuanced understanding of its origins and persistence. The work contributes to the ongoing discussion about the reliability-diversity tradeoff inherent in LLM alignment and post-training processes, building on studies that observe homogeneity in aligned models, reduced diversity in human-AI co-creation, and the limitations of current mitigation strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research aims to investigate the factors that contribute to output diversity collapse in post-trained language models. Specifically, the authors identify two primary open questions that motivate their study:
1. **Does output diversity collapse co-vary with the post-training method or with the post-training data composition?** Prior work often attributes collapse to a specific algorithm without fully separating the role of the data used in that algorithm. The authors seek to determine if the nature of the training data (e.g., narrow distillation vs. broad multi-source data) dictates *when* and *how severely* diversity is lost during the post-training pipeline.
2. **Does the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation format itself constrain diversity at inference, or is the collapse embedded in the model weights by training?** Many advanced LLMs utilize CoT reasoning, and it is unclear whether the structured format of CoT intrinsically limits the variety of final answers, or if the underlying model distribution, shaped by training, is already collapsed irrespective of the explicit reasoning path.

The motivation for answering these questions stems from the practical consequences of diversity collapse. Reduced output diversity can:
- **Undermine inference-time scaling methods:** Techniques like self-consistency, pass@k sampling, and test-time compute scaling rely on generating multiple distinct outputs to improve performance. If outputs are highly uniform, these methods lose their effectiveness.
- **Risk homogenizing model outputs:** In creative tasks, where variety is inherently desirable, or in value-laden tasks, where diverse perspectives are crucial, a lack of output diversity can lead to formulaic or biased responses, potentially reinforcing majority preferences or pre-existing misconceptions.
- **Limit understanding of algorithmic causes and mitigations:** Without a clear understanding of whether data composition or generation format is the primary driver, efforts to mitigate diversity collapse might be misdirected or ineffective. Identifying the precise stage and cause of collapse is essential for developing targeted interventions during the training process.

By addressing these questions, the research aims to provide insights that can inform the design of more diversity-preserving post-training strategies and help practitioners make informed decisions about model selection and application in scenarios where output variation is important.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study employs a controlled experimental design to trace output diversity across different stages and lineages of post-training for the Olmo 3 model family (7B scale).

**Models and training lineages:** The research investigates 13 Olmo 3 checkpoints, originating from a common Base model and undergoing up to three post-training stages: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Three main lineages are examined:
- **Base (1 model):** Pretrained on Dolma 3 Mix (6T tokens) and midtrained on Dolmino Mix (100B tokens).
- **Think (3 models: Think-SFT, Think-DPO, Think):** This lineage focuses on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
 - **SFT:** Trained on approximately 2.3 million synthetic CoT reasoning traces using (prompt, completion) pairs from two teachers (QwQ-32B and DeepSeek-R1).
 - **DPO:** Utilizes approximately 200,000 Delta Learning pairs.
 - **RL:** Uses a variation of GRPO with verifiable rewards and no KL penalty, trained on approximately 105,000 prompts, resulting in the final Think model.
 - **Think-not-thinking:** To isolate the effect of the CoT format, all three Think checkpoints are additionally evaluated with CoT suppressed at inference by prefilling an empty `<think>\n</think>\n` block, forcing direct answers.
- **Instruct (3 models: Instruct-SFT, Instruct-DPO, Instruct):** This lineage emphasizes broad instruction following.
 - **SFT:** Initializes from Think-SFT, then trained on approximately 2.2 million examples, including function-calling and drawing from multiple sources (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4.1), with reasoning traces stripped.
 - **DPO:** Uses approximately 260,000 pairs from the same pool as Think-DPO but with thinking mode disabled, adding multi-turn and GPT-judged preference pairs.
 - **RL:** The same RL stage as Think is applied to produce the final Instruct model.
- **RL-Zero (6 models):** These models apply RL training directly to the Base model, bypassing SFT and DPO. Variants include RL-Zero-Math, RL-Zero-Code, RL-Zero-IF, RL-Zero-General (each on approximately 105,000 prompts), and two additional Olmo 3.1 variants (RL-Zero-Math$_{3.1}$, RL-Zero-Code$_{3.1}$) trained for more steps.

**Tasks and Data:** The models are evaluated across 15 tasks categorized into:
- **Summarization:** TL;DR, CNN/DailyMail, XSum.
- **Code:** HumanEval, MBPP, CRUXEval.
- **Reasoning:** GSM8K, MATH-Algebra, MATH-Geometry, TruthfulQA.
- **Instruction following:** Alpaca, IFEval.
- **Creative writing:** WritingPrompts.
- **Value pluralism:** PRISM, WildBench.
Output length is controlled for summarization tasks. Training-evaluation overlap is measured using C$_{13}$ 13-gram matching, with most datasets showing negligible overlap (≤ 2%), but HumanEval, CRUXEval, IFEval, MATH-Algebra, MATH-Geometry, and WildBench showing elevated overlap (7–30%) attributed to shared upstream data sources.

**Metrics:** Diversity is measured along four complementary axes:
- **EAD (Expectation-Adjusted Distinct n-grams):** Captures lexical diversity by counting unique n-grams (1-5 grams) normalized by expected counts.
- **SBERT (Semantic diversity):** Measures mean pairwise cosine distance of sentence embeddings (all-mpnet-base-v2), capturing semantic similarity. For code tasks, UniXcoder embeddings are also used.
- **NLI (Logical diversity):** Scores output pairs with an NLI classifier (roberta-large-mnli) to capture logical entailment, neutrality, or contradiction. Excludes code tasks.
- **Vendi Score:** Measures the effective number of dissimilar outputs using eigenvalue entropy of the SBERT similarity kernel, providing an "effective number of modes."
- **AST subtree diversity (Code only):** For code tasks, measures structural diversity via mean pairwise Jaccard distance on AST subtree multisets (height ≤ 4) on *correct* outputs.

#### 3.1. Quality Evaluation

- For six verifiable tasks (GSM8K, MATH-Algebra, MATH-Geometry, HumanEval, MBPP, IFEval), standard metrics like accuracy@1 (greedy decoding), majority vote@16 (most frequent answer among K=16 samples), and pass@16 (at least one correct among K) are used. Unbiased pass@k estimator for code.
- For eight tasks without verifiable answers, LLM-as-judge (gpt-4.1-mini) is employed following established protocols (e.g., pairwise win-rate, checklist-guided scores).

**Quality-filtered diversity:** Diversity is decomposed into a quality-control component (removal of incorrect outputs) and a residual component (genuine narrowing among correct outputs). This involves comparing SBERT diversity on all K outputs ($D_a$) with SBERT diversity on only the correct outputs ($D_c$).

**Generation Parameters:** For each model-task pair, K=16 outputs are generated per prompt (N=500 prompts, or full dataset for some tasks) with a 32,768-token generation length. Sampling parameters are T=0.6, top-p=0.95. For Think-lineage models, `<think>...</think>` reasoning traces are stripped before metric computation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's findings are organized around three core questions, revealing distinct patterns of diversity collapse across different post-training lineages and task types.

#### 4.1. Lineage-dependent diversity collapse

- **SFT Asymmetry:** The location of diversity collapse varies with data composition. Think models, which use narrow CoT distillation from two teachers, experience a substantial collapse at the SFT stage, losing an average of 62% of Base model SBERT diversity across 15 tasks. This is significantly more than Instruct models (38%), which use multi-source data. Instruct-SFT, despite initializing from Think-SFT, shows a median 40% recovery of lost diversity, suggesting multi-source data can partially restore diversity. The magnitude of collapse at SFT correlates with task difficulty; easier tasks with dominant solution strategies collapse more.
- **DPO Asymmetry:** Instruct models show a larger drop in diversity during DPO (23% on average) compared to Think models (4% on average). This is attributed to Think models having already collapsed significantly at SFT, leaving less diversity for DPO to remove. On some math/code tasks, Think-DPO and Instruct-DPO show a slight increase in diversity, indicating DPO can sometimes partially correct collapsed SFT distributions.
- **RL Reversal:** The RL stage in Think models modestly increases semantic diversity on most tasks (roughly 5% of total lost diversity), particularly for code and summarization. This suggests that if a model enters RL already at a diversity floor, there might be room for exploration. In contrast, Instruct models, which enter RL with more residual diversity, continue to experience compression. On GSM8K, Instruct RL results in a 37% loss of Base diversity, the largest single-stage loss after SFT, as verifiable rewards concentrate probability on dominant correct strategies. While semantic diversity can increase or stabilize, lexical diversity (EAD) generally decreases in RL, indicating standardization of surface form.
- **Convergence and RL-Zero:** Despite different trajectories, both supervised lineages (Think and Instruct) converge to similar final diversity floors (approximately 1.3–1.6 Vendi modes on verifiable tasks, ~2 modes overall), with Think slightly higher on 11/15 tasks. RL-Zero models, which bypass SFT and DPO, retain significantly more Base diversity (median 94%, range ≥ 71%), confirming that SFT and DPO are the primary bottlenecks for diversity.
- **Semantic vs. Lexical Collapse:** The collapse is predominantly semantic, not lexical. SBERT diversity drops sharply (e.g., from 0.32 for Base to 0.12 for Think), while EAD (lexical diversity) remains stable or even increases. This indicates that post-trained models use varied vocabulary and phrasing to express semantically identical content. NLI diversity generally drops, but post-trained models can still make logically distinct claims, especially Think models where CoT helps preserve logical structure.
- **Value-Pluralism and Creative Tasks:** Value-pluralism tasks (PRISM, TruthfulQA) show the steepest collapse in Think models (-78% and -79% SBERT), as narrow two-teacher distillation struggles to represent diverse perspectives. On creative writing tasks (WritingPrompts), both Think and Instruct models experience sharp diversity drops (-63% Vendi Score) but achieve high LLM-judge quality scores (>97% win rate against Base), suggesting a trade-off where better stories come at the cost of formulaic variation. RL-Zero retains near 100% Base diversity on these tasks but shows lower quality (50% win rate).

#### 4.2. Think-not-thinking: CoT as reliability, not diversity

- **No Diversity Recovery:** Suppressing CoT generation at inference ("Think-not-thinking" models) does not recover diversity on any task-stage combinations. SBERT diversity for Think-not-thinking models remains comparable to Think models, and Instruct models show similar collapsed diversity. This holds true across SFT, DPO, and RL stages.
- **Accuracy Cost:** CoT suppression negatively impacts accuracy, particularly for harder tasks (e.g., -32% on MATH-Geometry, -20% on MBPP). The quality cost is task-dependent, severe for summarization (-48% on CNN/DM) and complex helpfulness (WildBench Score 4.6 → 1.4), but negligible for open-ended generation.
- **Implication:** The findings suggest that diversity collapse is embedded in the model weights through training data, rather than being imposed by the CoT generation format itself. CoT appears to improve reliability by aiding strategy execution, not by broadening the answer-level diversity distribution. This means practitioners cannot recover diversity by simply switching Think models to direct-answer mode; the diversity cost is incurred during training.

#### 4.3. Quality-filtered diversity decomposition

- **Task-Dependent Split:** The observed aggregate diversity reductions consist of two components: elimination of incorrect outputs (quality control) and genuine narrowing among correct outputs. The proportion attributable to each varies by task. For instance, on IFEval, 83.4% of the diversity drop is genuine narrowing among correct outputs, while on MBPP, only 38% is genuine, and on HumanEval, less than 10%. Math reasoning tasks fall in between (57–64% genuine narrowing).
- **Implications for Sampling:** Even among correct outputs, a narrowing persists. Base models maintain 1.7 effective Vendi modes among correct answers, while post-trained models converge to 1.3–1.6 modes. This limits the effectiveness of majority voting. For example, Think models gain only +0.4% on GSM8K from majority voting, whereas Base gains +24% and RL-Zero +22–26%. Models with more diverse correct outputs consistently benefit more from sampling, even at matched accuracy levels.
- **Amplified Errors:** On TruthfulQA, majority voting can actually hurt performance (majority vote@16 < accuracy@1) for all models, as diversity collapse can cause models to confidently converge on common misconceptions, amplifying errors when the dominant mode is incorrect.

#### 4.4. Cross-cutting patterns

- **General Ordering:** The general ordering of diversity (Base > RL-Zero > Final aligned models) holds across tasks, although specific RL-Zero variants can exceed Base on tasks aligned with their reward signals.
- **Quality Gains:** LLM-as-a-judge evaluations confirm that post-training generally improves quality across non-verified task categories, even as diversity decreases. For example, summarization win rates increase significantly (26–48% Base to 83–95% Think/Instruct).
- **Reward Signal Specificity:** Among RL-Zero variants, the type of reward signal predicts diversity preservation. Instruction-following rewards (RL-Zero-IF) retain more diversity (99% of Base) than code execution rewards (RL-Zero-Code, 88%), suggesting that pass/fail execution rewards narrow the solution space more aggressively. However, RL-Zero's diversity advantage comes at a steep quality cost compared to SFT/DPO aligned models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a detailed empirical analysis of output diversity collapse in large language models during post-training, offering several significant insights and potential impacts:

**1. Re-evaluating the Causes of Diversity Collapse:** The study demonstrates that diversity collapse is not solely attributable to specific post-training algorithms but is strongly influenced by the composition of training data. The finding that different data compositions for SFT (narrow two-teacher distillation vs. broader multi-source data) lead to diversity collapse at different stages (SFT vs. DPO) highlights the critical role of data in shaping the model's output distribution. This challenges simpler explanations and indicates that data-level interventions are as crucial as algorithmic ones.

**2. Clarifying the Role of Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** The "Think-not-thinking" experiment definitively shows that the CoT generation format does not intrinsically constrain final-answer diversity at inference time. Instead, the collapse is embedded within the model's learned weights during training. This has practical implications, as it means practitioners cannot simply "switch off" CoT to recover diversity; the cost is incurred during the training phase. CoT primarily serves to improve reliability and strategy execution, especially on complex tasks, rather than diversifying the output space.

**3. Decomposing Diversity Loss into Actionable Components:** By separating diversity reduction into a quality-control component (removing incorrect outputs) and a genuine narrowing among correct outputs, the research offers a more nuanced understanding of the phenomenon. This decomposition reveals that the balance between these two components is task-dependent. This is significant because it provides a framework for evaluating whether diversity loss is "beneficial" (by filtering errors) or "detrimental" (by homogenizing correct responses). For instance, in tasks like code generation, much of the diversity loss is beneficial quality control, while in creative writing or value-pluralism, genuine narrowing is a concern.

**4. Implications for Inference-Time Scaling and Robustness:** The observed narrowing of diversity even among *correct* outputs significantly impacts the effectiveness of inference-time techniques like majority voting and pass@k sampling. If multiple samples yield near-identical correct answers, the benefits of repeated sampling are limited. Furthermore, the finding that diversity collapse can amplify errors when the dominant mode is incorrect (e.g., on TruthfulQA) underscores risks to model robustness and truthfulness. This has direct implications for how models are deployed in high-stakes applications.

### 6. Guiding Future Post-Training Strategies

- **Data Sourcing:** For practitioners, the results strongly suggest that broadening the source distribution for SFT data (e.g., using more teachers, diverse styles, or larger, varied datasets) can mitigate the initial, steepest diversity collapse. Avoiding single- or dual-teacher distillation when output diversity is valued is a clear actionable insight.
- **Algorithmic Choices:** While data composition shapes the trajectory, algorithmic changes appear necessary to fundamentally raise the "diversity floor." The higher diversity retained by RL-Zero models (which bypass SFT/DPO) points to the potential of RL without KL penalties to partially reverse DPO-induced semantic narrowing, although at a potential cost to quality. This indicates a need for further research into diversity-aware preference optimization objectives.

**6. Future Research Directions:** The paper also opens avenues for future work, including investigating reasoning-path diversity (distinct from final-answer diversity), direct empirical testing of data-composition interventions, and exploring whether the observed diversity floor can be shifted by modifying preference-optimization objectives. Addressing the distinction between distributional and representational diversity, especially in value-pluralism tasks, is also a critical long-term impact, requiring targeted probes beyond current metrics to understand which perspectives are lost.
