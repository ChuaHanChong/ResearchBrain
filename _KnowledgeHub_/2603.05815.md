# Hierarchical Latent Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> HiLAM provides a hierarchical latent action model for robots that automatically extracts variable-length, high-level skills from unlabelled video data. By leveraging these discovered skills for pretraining, it achieves a 94% success rate on the LIBERO-Long benchmark and demonstrates substantial data efficiency, reducing reliance on costly action-labeled demonstrations.

## Problem

- High cost and effort of collecting action-labeled data for robot training, limiting scalability and diversity for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) only capture short-horizon, low-level motions, failing to extract long-term temporal structure or higher-level skills from observation-only videos.
- Traditional hierarchical skill discovery methods often assume fixed skill primitives or fixed-length action sequences, which are not suitable for dynamic, variable-duration real-world skills.

## Method

- **HiLAM** utilizes a two-phase framework: first, learning ==latent skills== from observation-only videos, then training ==hierarchical policies==.
- Employs a pre-trained ==Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM)== to infer low-level latent actions and an ==H-Net== architecture for ==dynamic chunking== to discover variable-length, high-level latent skills.
- Pretrains a hierarchical policy (high-level predicting skills, low-level predicting primitive actions) using the discovered latent skills and actions as ==pseudo-labels== from large-scale actionless videos, followed by fine-tuning on expert demonstrations.

## Results

- HiLAM consistently outperformed the BAKU baseline across all four challenging LIBERO benchmark suites.
- Demonstrated a **94%** success rate on the LIBERO-Long benchmark, achieving **84%** success with only **50%** of expert demonstrations, indicating strong data efficiency compared to the baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that hierarchical policy learning and pretraining on large-scale actionless videos are crucial for performance, with human videos slightly outperforming robot videos as pretraining data.
- Qualitative evaluation revealed that the dynamic chunking mechanism successfully segments complex tasks into semantically meaningful, variable-length skill segments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Long-term temporal information can be effectively modeled by dynamically chunking low-level latent actions into variable-length, high-level latent skills from unlabelled video data.
> - Hierarchical policies, pre-trained using these automatically discovered latent skills and actions, achieve superior performance and data efficiency compared to flat policies or those without large-scale pretraining.
> - The learned latent actions maintain their dynamic motion properties, enabling consistent future-frame prediction and serving as reliable pseudo-labels for robot control.
