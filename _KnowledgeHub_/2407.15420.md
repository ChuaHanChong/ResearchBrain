# Local All-Pair Correspondence for Point Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> LocoTrack tracks arbitrary points across video by reframing tracking as local all-pair correspondence: a local 4D correlation volume (not a 2D point-to-region map) supplies bidirectional matching and smoothness priors, refined by a length-generalizable Transformer. It sets new state-of-the-art accuracy on all TAP-Vid benchmarks while running about 6x faster than prior best methods.

## Problem

- Prior TAP methods build local 2D correlation maps between a query point and a target region, which struggle in homogeneous or repetitive-texture regions.
- Point-to-region 2D correlation lacks bidirectional consistency and smoothness priors that dense correspondence methods exploit, causing matching ambiguity.
- Existing temporal modeling (1D convolutions, MLP-Mixers, sliding-window Transformers) needs constrained windows or extra query points, adding overhead or limiting sequence length.

## Method

- ==Local 4D correlation==: computes all-pair similarities within local radius-3 neighborhoods around the current track estimate and the query point, yielding a 7x7x7x7 volume for bidirectional, smoothness-aware matching.
- Local 4D correlation encoder: two symmetrical branches of stacked ==2D convolutions== and pooling compress the high-dimensional correlation volume into a compact embedding per frame.
- **LocoTrack**'s length-generalizable ==Transformer== refinement: replaces sinusoidal position encoding with a ==directional relative position bias== so iterative track/occlusion updates generalize to unseen video lengths without sliding windows.

## Results

- LocoTrack-S beats TAPIR by **+5.6 AJ** and CoTracker by **+2.5 AJ** on DAVIS strided-query at 256x256; LocoTrack-B adds a further **+0.9 AJ**.
- LocoTrack-S runs at **7,244** points/sec versus TAPIR's **2,097** and CoTracker's **1,146**, using **1.08** GFLOPs per point (**4.3-4.7x** lower) with only **8.2M** parameters (**5.5x** fewer than CoTracker).
- Ablations show local 4D correlation beats 2D correlation and outperforms random/line-shaped neighbor sampling; relative position bias is essential for length generalization, and even a single refinement iteration beats TAPIR at roughly **9x** its speed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense all-pair (4D) correlation's disambiguation power can be recovered cheaply by localizing it to a small neighborhood instead of computing it globally.
> - Restricting a historically expensive representation to a local window is a general recipe for importing rich-context methods into real-time pipelines.
> - Position-encoding choice determines length generalization: relative bias transfers to unseen sequence lengths where sinusoidal encoding degrades sharply.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Point correspondence, the task of identifying matching points across different views or frames, is a cornerstone problem in computer vision, underpinning applications such as 3D reconstruction, autonomous driving, and pose estimation. More recently, the "tracking any point" (TAP) task has emerged, extending point correspondence to long video sequences by requiring models to track an arbitrary query point on a physical surface across all subsequent frames, along with its visibility status.

Previous state-of-the-art methods in point tracking, such as TAPIR and CoTracker, primarily rely on local 2D correlation maps. These maps establish correspondences by comparing features of a query point with a local region in the target frame. While effective in many scenarios, this point-to-region approach inherently struggles with challenges posed by homogeneous regions, repetitive patterns, or the need to differentiate between co-occurring objects. In such ambiguous situations, precise point localization becomes difficult. Existing solutions often attempt to integrate temporal context using various architectures like MLP-Mixers, 1D convolutions, or Transformers. However, these approaches often either rely on constrained temporal windows with sliding inference for longer videos or incur significant computational overhead, as seen in CoTracker's strategy of tracking additional query points to gather spatial context.

This paper addresses a fundamental limitation in the current paradigm by drawing inspiration from dense correspondence methods. Traditional dense correspondence techniques compute similarities for every pair of points across two images, forming a 4D correlation volume. This 4D correlation provides rich spatial context, including dense bidirectional correspondence and matching smoothness priors, which are highly effective at disambiguating matches. However, the computational expense and high dimensionality of global 4D correlation have historically prevented its direct application to point tracking.

LocoTrack aims to bridge this gap. It introduces a novel approach that reinterprets point tracking as a *local all-pair correspondence problem*. By leveraging the strengths of 4D correlation—specifically, its ability to provide richer spatial context and enforce bidirectional consistency and smoothness—but restricting its application to local regions, LocoTrack seeks to overcome the matching ambiguities of 2D correlation-based methods while maintaining computational efficiency. The paper therefore represents a significant step towards more robust and efficient "tracking any point" solutions, integrating a historically powerful correspondence mechanism into a modern video tracking framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of LocoTrack can be summarized as follows:

1. **Resolve Matching Ambiguities:** The central motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing point tracking methods that rely on local 2D correlation maps. These methods frequently struggle to accurately identify points within homogeneous areas, regions with repetitive patterns, or when distinguishing between visually similar, co-occurring objects. LocoTrack aims to provide a more robust solution in these challenging scenarios.

2. **Enhance Spatial Context for Correspondence:** The authors observed that prior works lacked sufficient spatial context, leading to ambiguities. They are motivated to leverage the "rich spatial context" provided by dense correspondence methods, specifically the benefits derived from 4D correlation volumes. This includes obtaining bidirectional correspondence for match verification and enforcing matching smoothness across neighboring points.

3. **Maintain Computational Efficiency:** While acknowledging the robustness of global 4D correlation, the authors recognize its computational intractability for point tracking. A key objective is to integrate the advantages of 4D correlation while ensuring the model remains computationally efficient and capable of real-time performance. This is achieved by formulating the problem as *local* all-pair correspondence, restricting the 4D correlation computation to specific local regions.

4. **Effective Integration of Long-Term Temporal Information:** The model seeks to incorporate long-term temporal context from video sequences effectively. It aims to achieve this with a compact architecture, leveraging the global receptive field capabilities of Transformers to model dependencies over extended periods with minimal layers.

5. **Generalizability to Variable Sequence Lengths:** A practical goal is to design a model that can handle videos of arbitrary lengths at inference time without requiring hand-designed chaining processes or sliding window inference strategies that introduce computational inefficiencies and complexity.

In essence, LocoTrack is motivated by the desire to build a highly accurate and exceptionally efficient point tracking model that leverages the disambiguating power of dense spatial correspondence (4D correlation) within a local, computationally feasible framework, while also effectively modeling long-range temporal dependencies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LocoTrack employs a two-stage approach for point tracking: an initialization stage followed by an iterative refinement stage.

#### 3.1. Stage I: Track Initialization

The first stage aims to provide a rough initial estimate of the query point's position and its occlusion probability across all target frames.
1. **Feature Extraction:** A ResNet18 backbone, modified with instance normalization, extracts hierarchical feature maps ($F^l_t$) at three pyramidal levels (strides 2, 4, and 8) from the input video frames.
2. **Global 2D Correlation:** For each target frame $t$, a query feature vector $F^l(q)$ (sampled from the query frame at position $q$) is compared with the entire feature map $F^l_t$ to compute a global 2D correlation map $C^l_t$. These multi-level correlation maps are then resized and concatenated.
3. **Initial Track and Occlusion Prediction:** The concatenated correlation map $C_t$ is processed by a single 2D convolution and a Softargmax function (with a Gaussian kernel) to predict the initial 2D position $T^0_t$. Initial occlusion probabilities $O^0_t$ are derived from the correlation map using global max and average pooling followed by a linear projection, inspired by CBAM.

#### 3.2. Stage II: Track Refinement

This stage iteratively refines the initial track $T^0$ and occlusion $O^0$ to correct jitter and matching noise. This is achieved through the integration of local 4D correlation and a length-generalizable Transformer.

1. **Local 4D Correlation:**
 - **Motivation:** Unlike the global 2D correlation in initialization, which struggles with ambiguities, the refinement stage uses a richer correlation type. Inspired by dense correspondence, LocoTrack utilizes a 4D correlation volume to provide robust matching priors like bidirectional correspondence and matching smoothness.
 - **Localized Computation:** To overcome the computational burden of global 4D correlation, the computation is restricted to local neighborhoods. For each predicted track position $p = T^k_t$ in the target frame and the query point $q$, a local 4D correlation volume $L_t$ is constructed. This volume computes all-pair similarities between features within a local region of radius $r_p$ around $p$ in the target frame and a local region of radius $r_q$ around $q$ in the query frame. The default radii are set to $r_p = r_q = 3$, resulting in a $7 \times 7 \times 7 \times 7$ correlation volume. This local all-pair formulation provides dense correspondence information within a constrained search space, thus improving robustness against ambiguities while maintaining efficiency.

2. **Local 4D Correlation Encoder:**
 - **Processing High-Dimensional Volume:** The obtained 4D correlation volume $L_t$ is a high-dimensional tensor. An efficient encoding strategy is proposed to process it.
 - **Symmetrical Branches:** The encoder processes the 4D correlation in two symmetrical branches (as shown in Fig. 5 of the paper). One branch treats the target dimensions as spatial and the flattened query dimensions as channels, while the other branch reverses this, treating query dimensions as spatial and flattened target dimensions as channels.
 - **Compact Embedding:** Each branch consists of stacked 2D convolutions, group normalization, ReLU activations, and a final average pooling layer. This progressively reduces the spatial dimensions of the correlation, yielding two compact vectors which are then concatenated to form the correlation embedding $E^k_t$.

3. **Temporal Modeling with Length-Generalizable Transformer:**
 - **Architecture Choice:** A Transformer architecture is chosen for temporal modeling due to its ability to handle arbitrary sequence lengths and form a global receptive field with few layers, unlike 1D convolutions or MLP-Mixers.
 - **Relative Position Bias:** To enable generalization to videos of unseen lengths at inference time, sinusoidal position encoding is replaced with a novel relative position bias mechanism. The attention heads are divided into two groups: one for tokens on the left (causal attention for previous frames) and one for tokens on the right (causal attention for future frames), each with learnable scaling factors. This allows the Transformer to adjust attention based on relative distance and direction.
 - **Inputs:** The Transformer receives the sequence of correlation embeddings $\{E^k_t\}$, along with sinusoidal encodings of the current track $T^k_t$, its differences from adjacent frames ($T^k_t - T^k_{t-1}$ and $T^k_{t+1} - T^k_t$), and the current occlusion status $O^k_t$.

4. **Iterative Update:** The Transformer outputs residuals $\Delta T^k$ and $\Delta O^k$. These residuals are then added to the current track and occlusion estimates: $T^{k+1} := T^k + \Delta T^k$ and $O^{k+1} := O^k + \Delta O^k$. This refinement process is performed for $K=4$ iterations to yield the final track $T^K$ and occlusion $O^K$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LocoTrack demonstrates significant advancements in both accuracy and efficiency for the point tracking task, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Accuracy

- **Superior Performance:** LocoTrack, even its small variant (LocoTrack-S), achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on all TAP-Vid benchmarks.
 - On the DAVIS dataset in strided query mode (256x256 resolution), LocoTrack-S achieves a +5.6 AJ (Average Jaccard) increase compared to TAPIR and a +2.5 AJ increase compared to CoTracker.
 - The larger LocoTrack-B variant further improves performance, achieving an additional +0.9 AJ over LocoTrack-S in the same DAVIS benchmark.
- **Resolution Dependence:** While excelling at 256x256 resolution, the paper notes a slight degradation in performance on some datasets when moving to 384x512 resolution. This is attributed to a diminished effective receptive field of the local correlation when the input resolution is increased, suggesting an area for future improvement.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Throughput

- **Unmatched Efficiency:** LocoTrack exhibits exceptionally high throughput and low computational cost.
 - LocoTrack-S is approximately 6 times faster than CoTracker and 3.5 times faster than TAPIR (measured in points per second). Specifically, LocoTrack-S achieves 7,244 points/sec compared to TAPIR's 2,097 points/sec and CoTracker's 1,146 points/sec at 256x256 resolution.
 - In terms of FLOPs per point, LocoTrack-S requires 1.08 GFLOPs, which is 4.7 times lower than TAPIR (5.12 GFLOPs) and 4.3 times lower than CoTracker (4.65 GFLOPs).
 - The model is also remarkably compact, with LocoTrack-S having only 8.2 million parameters, 5.5 times fewer than CoTracker (45.5 million parameters).
- **Real-time Potential:** The high efficiency enables LocoTrack-S to process 10,000 points in approximately one second, implying real-time performance for near-dense query points (e.g., a 64x64 grid) on a 24-frames-per-second video.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- **Enhanced Smoothness and Robustness:** Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 6, 8, 9) demonstrate that LocoTrack produces tracks with superior smoothness and spatial coherence compared to TAPIR and CoTracker. It effectively handles challenging scenarios, including long-range tracking sequences, occlusions, homogeneous regions, and deforming objects, where other methods often show jitter or drift.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Local 4D Correlation:** Ablations confirm the superiority of the local 4D correlation over traditional 2D correlation, showing a significant performance gain. The study also highlights the importance of dense all-pair correlations within the local region, where regular grid sampling of neighbors (the proposed 4D approach) outperforms random or line-shaped sampling.
- **Importance of Relative Position Bias:** The use of relative position bias in the Transformer is crucial for enabling the model to generalize to arbitrary video lengths at inference time. Sinusoidal encoding, in contrast, leads to significant performance degradation when tested on sequences of different lengths than trained.
- **Transformer vs. Convolutional Architectures:** The Transformer-based refinement model consistently outperforms 1D Convolutional Mixer architectures (like that in TAPIR) even when parameter counts are matched. This is attributed to the Transformer's ability to establish a global receptive field with fewer layers.
- **Iterative Refinement Efficiency:** LocoTrack surpasses TAPIR in performance even with a single iteration of refinement, achieving approximately 9 times faster inference speed. It reaches comparable performance to CoTracker while being about 9 times faster.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LocoTrack presents a significant leap forward in the field of point tracking, offering a potent combination of high accuracy and exceptional efficiency. Its significance and potential impact can be highlighted in several key areas:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Dense Correspondence and Point Tracking:** The most profound contribution of LocoTrack is its innovative integration of local all-pair 4D correlation into the point tracking pipeline. Historically, the rich spatial context and disambiguating power of 4D correlation were largely confined to dense correspondence tasks due to computational complexity. LocoTrack successfully brings these benefits to the "tracking any point" problem, resolving long-standing ambiguities in homogeneous regions and repetitive patterns that plagued 2D correlation-based methods, without incurring the prohibitive costs of global 4D correlation.

2. **Setting New Performance Benchmarks:** LocoTrack achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all TAP-Vid benchmarks, consistently outperforming leading methods like TAPIR and CoTracker. This establishes a new high bar for point tracking performance, demonstrating that substantial gains are still possible.

3. **Enabling Real-time, Near-Dense Tracking:** The model's unparalleled computational efficiency is a game-changer. Being orders of magnitude faster (e.g., 6x faster than CoTracker, 3.5x faster than TAPIR) and significantly more compact (5.5x fewer parameters than CoTracker), LocoTrack enables real-time processing of thousands of query points. This capability opens doors for applications requiring dense, high-frequency tracking, which was previously computationally prohibitive.

4. **Practical Generalizability:** The development of a length-generalizable Transformer, utilizing relative position bias, eliminates the need for complex, hand-designed chaining processes or sliding window inference for videos of arbitrary lengths. This greatly simplifies deployment and enhances the robustness and practicality of the model for real-world scenarios.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** LocoTrack's success with local 4D correlation may inspire further exploration of localized high-dimensional correlation volumes in other computer vision tasks beyond tracking, such as video object segmentation, optical flow, or few-shot learning, where rich local context can improve performance. The observed performance degradation at higher resolutions also points to an interesting research direction for optimizing local correlation receptive fields.

In conclusion, LocoTrack represents a substantial advancement for computer vision. By ingeniously combining robust local 4D correspondence with efficient deep learning architectures, it delivers a high-performance, real-time solution for point tracking. Its implications span from enhancing professional video editing and visual effects workflows to improving the perception systems in robotics and autonomous driving, making precise and efficient point tracking more accessible and powerful than ever before.
