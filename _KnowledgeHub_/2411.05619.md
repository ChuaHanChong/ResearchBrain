# WHALE: Towards Generalizable and Scalable World Models for Embodied Decision-making

> [!summary] Summary
> WHALE is a world-model learning framework combining behavior-conditioning (embedding inferred policy intent as a latent covariate) and retracing-rollout (ensemble-free uncertainty via action-equivalent backtracked rollouts). Instantiated as Whale-ST and scaled to a 414M-parameter Whale-X pre-trained on 970K Open X-Embodiment trajectories, it beats prior world models on value accuracy, video fidelity, and real-robot OOD generalization.

## Problem

- World models trained via teacher-forcing MLE suffer compounding rollout errors amplified quadratically by horizon, worsened when target policies diverge from narrow near-expert training data.
- Prior generalization fixes (adversarial counterfactual learning, energy-based models, reward-consistent dynamics) rely on adversarial training that does not scale to large visual datasets.
- Ensemble-based uncertainty estimation for offline model-based RL is computationally expensive at scale, and existing efficient alternatives like any-step uncertainty only work for RNNs, not transformers.

## Method

- **Behavior-conditioning**: an ==ELBO==-based posterior/prior encoder-decoder learns a ==latent behavior embedding== from trajectory history, injected as a covariate into the dynamics model to adapt predictions to the rollout policy's distribution shift.
- **Retracing-rollout**: defines a ==retracing-action== equivalent to an action sequence via end-effector pose summation, backtracks k steps and re-executes it, then multiplies disagreement with the non-retraced output by ==predictive entropy== for uncertainty, with no extra training cost.
- **Whale-ST**: a ==spatial-temporal transformer== instantiation with a CNN visual encoder, ==VQ-VAE== video tokenizer, and ==ST-transformer dynamics model==, scaled to **Whale-X** (414M parameters) pre-trained on 970K Open X-Embodiment demonstrations.

## Results

- On Meta-World at 256x256, Whale-ST beats DreamerV3 with value gap **9.7** vs **11.7**, return correlation **0.86** vs **0.72**, and lowest FVD of **28.2** vs **112.4**.
- Behavior-conditioning ablation cuts value gap by **18%** and regret by **22%** versus the same model without it, at matched parameter count.
- Retracing-rollout improves model-error correlation by **500%** over ensemble-based and **50%** over entropy-based uncertainty baselines, averaging **0.55** versus **0.10** and **0.35**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting inferred behavior/policy identity as a conditioning variable is a general, non-adversarial way to counter the policy-divergence term that dominates world-model generalization error.
> - Uncertainty estimation need not require costly model ensembles; exploiting an action space's semantic structure (equivalent action sequences) can substitute for ensemble disagreement at near-zero extra training cost.
> - Even at 414M parameters and 970K real demonstrations, generalization to truly novel real-world tasks remains limited, suggesting data-driven scaling alone cannot fully solve OOD world-model transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models are a component in embodied decision-making systems, designed to simulate real-world dynamics and enable agents to explore hypothetical scenarios without direct environmental interaction. This capability facilitates tasks such as counterfactual prediction, off-policy evaluation, and offline reinforcement learning by generating synthetic data.

Prior research on world models has explored various architectural approaches, including recurrent neural networks (RNNs), diffusion models, and transformer-based models, often focusing on high-dimensional visual observations. While these models have achieved success in specific decision-making tasks, they frequently encounter challenges related to generalizability and reliable uncertainty estimation.

A fundamental challenge for world models is their ability to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) regions, where the agent's actions or environmental states differ significantly from the training data. This OOD generalization is crucial for evaluating diverse policies and making effective decisions in novel situations. Existing scalable methods have not adequately addressed this issue, often suffering from generalization errors exacerbated by policy distribution shifts and compounding errors during long-horizon rollouts.

Another area of concern is the accurate quantification of prediction uncertainty. When world models generate data in OOD regions, their predictions can become unreliable. For applications like offline policy optimization, it is important to estimate this uncertainty to prevent policies from exploiting erroneous model predictions. Traditional methods for uncertainty estimation, such as model ensembles, can be computationally intensive, particularly for large-scale visual world models.

This work positions itself within this landscape by proposing a framework that specifically addresses these two challenges: enhancing OOD generalizability and providing efficient uncertainty estimation in a scalable manner. By integrating techniques suitable for transformer-based architectures and large-scale datasets, the authors aim to advance the development of world models for real-world embodied control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop generalizable and scalable world models that can support effective decision-making in embodied environments. This overarching goal is motivated by two specific, interconnected challenges:

#### 2.1. Enhancing Generalizability of World Models

The first key objective is to improve the generalizability of world models, particularly their ability to make accurate predictions in out-of-distribution (OOD) regions. World models are expected to answer "what-if" questions, necessitating faithful imagination beyond the data distributions observed during training. The paper identifies policy distribution divergence as a primary source of generalization error. When a world model is trained on data collected by a narrow range of policies (e.g., expert trajectories), it may not accurately predict outcomes for novel or suboptimal policies. This distribution shift, combined with compounding errors in autoregressive rollouts, leads to an amplification of evaluation inaccuracy.

The motivation for addressing this challenge stems from the need for world models to facilitate robust decision-making across a wider spectrum of possible actions and environmental states. Without strong OOD generalization, agents may perform poorly when encountering scenarios slightly different from their training experiences, or when attempting to explore new strategies. Existing solutions like distribution matching or adversarial learning are noted as difficult to scale to high-dimensional visual data. Therefore, a key motivation is to introduce a scalable method that mitigates policy divergence and its impact on model generalization, enabling models to adapt to target policy behaviors dynamically.

#### 2.2. Enabling Efficient and Reliable Uncertainty Estimation

The second objective is to provide a method for reliable uncertainty estimation for imagined visual experiences, without incurring substantial computational overhead. When world models generate data, especially in OOD regions, their predictions are not always reliable. Using such unreliable synthetic data without assessing its credibility can lead to the exploitation of model prediction errors during offline policy optimization, potentially undermining policy performance.

The motivation for efficient uncertainty estimation arises from the limitations of prior approaches. Ensemble-based methods, which involve training multiple models in parallel, are computationally expensive for large visual world models. Simpler indicators like predictive entropy may not always be sufficiently reliable for robust control. The goal is to develop an uncertainty quantification technique that is computationally efficient, scalable to large transformer-based models, and provides trustworthy estimates of model prediction errors, thereby enabling conservative and robust policy learning in fully offline scenarios. This is critical for the trustworthy utilization of synthetic data in decision-making processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce WHALE, a framework designed to learn generalizable world models, incorporating two primary techniques: behavior-conditioning and retracing-rollout. These techniques are described as universal and applicable with various neural network architectures.

#### 3.1. Behavior-conditioning for Generalization

To address generalization issues arising from policy distribution shifts, WHALE employs a behavior-conditioning technique. This approach aims to embed information about the policy generating a trajectory into the world model, allowing it to adapt to different behavior patterns.
The methodology involves:
1. **Behavior Embedding Learning:** A behavior-conditioning model is trained to extract "behavior embeddings" (latent variable `z`) from training trajectories. This model comprises a CNN-based visual encoder, and ST-Transformer-based posterior (`q_ϕ`), prior (`p_ψ`), and reconstruction (`π_ω`) models.
2. **Objective Function:** The learning objective for the behavior-conditioning model maximizes an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) of the trajectory likelihood conditioned on history. Specifically, it involves minimizing the negative log-likelihood of actions reconstructed by the decoder (`π_ω(a_h|o_h, τ_{h-1}, z)`) and minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the posterior encoder (`q_ϕ(z|τ_H)`) and the prior predictor (`p_ψ(z|τ_h)`). The KL terms are applied for each decision step to encourage policy-consistent representations across sub-trajectories.
3. **Integration with World Model:** The learned prior predictor (`p_ψ`) is then used to infer behavior embeddings (`z_h`) from the interaction history (`τ_h`). These `z_h` are passed as additional covariates to the dynamics model during world model learning. This process enables the autoregressive generation process to adapt to the target distribution during rollouts, thereby mitigating extrapolation errors caused by policy divergence.

#### 3.2. Retracing-rollout for Uncertainty Estimation

To provide efficient uncertainty estimation without relying on computationally expensive model ensembles, WHALE introduces the retracing-rollout technique. This method is inspired by variable-length history approaches but is adapted for transformer-based architectures.
The methodology involves:
1. **Retracing-Action Definition:** For embodied control tasks, the action space typically consists of delta changes in end-effector pose (e.g., ∆x, ∆y, ∆z, ∆roll, ∆pitch, ∆yaw) and gripper state. A "retracing-action" (`a^r_k`) for an action sequence `a_{1:k}` is defined as the cumulative sum of the first six dimensions (position/orientation changes) and the final gripper state of the last action. This cumulative action aims to achieve the same outcome as the original sequence from the same starting point.
2. **Retracing-Rollout Process:** Given an observation `o_t` and an action `a_t`, and a chosen "retracing step" `k`, the process backtracks to an earlier observation `o_{t-k}`. The corresponding retracing-action `a^r_k` for the sequence `a_{t-k:k}` is computed. To prevent out-of-range actions, `a^r_k` is divided into `k` smaller steps, and the dynamics model performs a multi-step rollout from `o_{t-k}` using these scaled retracing-actions. This yields a retracing-rollout prediction.
3. **Uncertainty Quantifier:** Uncertainty for `(o_t, a_t)` is quantified by combining two measures:
 - **Feature-level disagreement:** The perceptual loss between the output generated by the standard rollout at `t` and the output from the retracing-rollout starting from `t-k`.
 - **Predictive entropy:** The entropy of the dynamics model's categorical token prediction distribution (`entropy(T(·|o_t, a_t))`).
 The final uncertainty estimation is derived by multiplying the average disagreement and predictive entropy. This method is designed to be plug-and-play, requiring no modifications during the model training phase.

#### 3.3. Instantiation with ST-transformer Architecture: Whale-ST

The WHALE framework is instantiated as Whale-ST, a scalable world model utilizing a spatial-temporal transformer (ST-transformer) architecture. This architecture processes tokens by attending to other tokens in the current frame and corresponding positions in prior frames, enabling parallel generation of next-frame tokens.
Whale-ST comprises three main components:
1. **Video Tokenizer:** A VQ-VAE-based tokenizer encodes raw observations into discrete tokens and reconstructs videos from these tokens, trained with a standard VQ-VAE loss.
2. **Behavior-conditioning Model:** As described in Section 4.1, this model learns behavior embeddings.
3. **Dynamics Model:** An ST-Transformer-based model predicts future observation tokens (`x_{h+1}`) conditioned on history tokens (`x_{1:h}`), actions (`a_{1:h}`), and the behavior-conditioning embedding (`z_h`) inferred by the prior predictor. Its objective is to maximize the log-likelihood of the next frame's tokens.

#### 3.4. Pre-training on Real-world Robot Demonstrations: Whale-X

As a further step, Whale-X is presented as a 414M-parameter world model pre-trained on 970K real-world robot demonstrations from the Open X-Embodiment (OpenX) dataset.
1. **Data Selection:** The full OpenX dataset (27 datasets, 970K demonstrations) is used for pre-training the video tokenizer and behavior-conditioning model. A subset focusing on tabletop tasks from OpenX is used for pre-training the dynamics model.
2. **Purpose:** Whale-X is designed as a foundational embodied world model to evaluate real-world behaviors and generate controllable video trajectories that align with specified actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental evaluations on simulated and real-world tasks to assess the effectiveness of WHALE, Whale-ST, and Whale-X.

#### 4.1. Whale-ST on Simulated Tasks (Meta-World)

Experiments were conducted on the Meta-World benchmark, using 60k trajectories from 20 tasks, with models trained from scratch.

##### 4.1.1. Model Evaluation (Value Estimation Accuracy and Video Generation Fidelity)

- **Value Estimation Accuracy:** Whale-ST demonstrated performance in value prediction accuracy, as measured by Value Gap, Return Correlation, and Regret@5. At 64x64 resolution, Whale-ST achieved a value gap comparable to DreamerV3 and surpassed other models in return correlation (0.77 vs. 0.62-0.72) and regret@5 (7.3 vs. 7.2-22.0). At 256x256 resolution, Whale-ST achieved the smallest value gap (9.7) and highest return correlation (0.86), alongside the lowest regret (3.9).
- **Video Fidelity:** Whale-ST consistently outperformed baselines (FitVid, MCVD, DreamerV3, iVideoGPT) across metrics including FVD, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with a notable advantage in FVD (e.g., 38.5 vs. 115.7-272.8 at 64x64).
- **Ablation Study on Behavior-conditioning:** The inclusion of behavior-conditioning (comparing Whale-ST with and without it) resulted in improved value prediction accuracy (e.g., 18% reduction in value gap, 22% reduction in regret at 256x256) and enhanced video fidelity across all metrics.

##### 4.1.2. Uncertainty Estimation (Retracing-rollout)

- **Model Error Prediction:** Retracing-rollout exhibited a stronger correlation between estimated uncertainty and perceptual model error compared to entropy-based and ensemble-based methods. On average across five tasks, retracing-rollout achieved a correlation of 0.55, compared to 0.35 for entropy-based and 0.10 for ensemble-based methods. This represents a 500% improvement over the ensemble method and a 50% increase over the entropy-based method in predicting model error.
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning:** In offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) scenarios, retracing-rollout contributed to superior convergence performance and training stability in three of five tasks (plate-slide, button-press-wall, faucet-close), and comparable performance in the remaining two. It was the only method capable of stable convergence in faucet-close and plate-slide tasks where other methods experienced performance decline. The results also indicated that all uncertainty estimation methods improved performance compared to learning without an uncertainty penalty.

#### 4.2. Whale-X on Real-world Tasks

Evaluation of Whale-X was conducted on an ARX5 robotic platform using minimal demonstrations (60 trajectories per task for fine-tuning) for four tasks. The model was tested on challenging unseen scenarios related to visual generalization, motion generalization, and task generalization.

##### 4.2.1. Model Evaluation (Consistency Rate and Video Fidelity)

- **Consistency Rate:** Evaluated using GPT-4o, the "consistency rate" assessed whether reconstructed object positions, interactive object states, and robot arm positions were within an acceptable range of ground truth.
 - Whale-X improved consistency by 63% compared to models without behavior-conditioning.
 - Pre-training on 970k samples resulted in a higher consistency rate than training from scratch.
 - Larger models (Whale-X-base with 203M parameters) achieved approximately three times higher consistency than smaller models (Whale-X-medium with 77M parameters) across the unseen tasks.
- **Video Fidelity:** Video generation quality on real-world tasks aligned with consistency rate findings, showing that Whale-X, with behavior-conditioning and pre-training, achieved better PSNR (22.3), SSIM (80.5), and LPIPS (29.6) compared to ablations.

##### 4.2.2. Uncertainty Estimation

- **Consistency Rate with Uncertainty Filtering:** By filtering trajectories based on their cumulative uncertainty (using retracing-rollout), a clear relationship was observed: reducing the proportion of selected trajectories (i.e., selecting those with lower uncertainty) led to a marked increase in consistency rate. Specifically, when selecting the top 5% of trajectories with the lowest uncertainty, the filtered trajectories aligned with real outcomes with 100% accuracy.

#### 4.3. Scaling Experiments

Whale-X's scaling behavior was investigated by varying model size and pre-training data size for dynamics models, while freezing the tokenizer and behavior-conditioning model.
- **Pre-training Scaling:** Whale-X exhibited strong scalability, with increasing pre-training data or model parameters reducing training loss. The training loss followed a log-linear relationship with FLOPs.
- **Fine-tuning Scaling:** Larger models demonstrated lower test loss after fine-tuning on new tasks, indicating that increased model capacity translates to improved performance post-adaptation.

#### 4.4. Visualization Results

- **Qualitative Evaluation:** Whale-ST and Whale-X were shown to generate high-fidelity, long-horizon video trajectories that maintained visual quality and consistency across simulated (Meta-World) and real-world (Open X-Embodiment, ARX5 tasks) environments.
- **Controllable Generation:** Whale-X demonstrated strong controllability, generating videos that semantically aligned with scaled actions (e.g., executing "1.2 * a_0:t" resulted in increased movement, while "0.8 * a_0:t" resulted in decreased movement).
- **Behavior-conditioning Visualization:** t-SNE visualizations of learned behavior embeddings indicated that they capture distinct policy modes. Embeddings could differentiate between various policies for the same task and between expert policies for different tasks, but not between random policies for different tasks, suggesting a focus on policy representation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces WHALE, a framework addressing challenges in world model generalization and uncertainty estimation, which are critical for robust embodied decision-making. The proposed techniques, behavior-conditioning and retracing-rollout, are presented as universal solutions that can be integrated with existing neural network architectures.

The development of Whale-ST demonstrates improved performance in simulated tasks across both value estimation accuracy and video generation fidelity, suggesting enhanced model reliability. Whale-X, a large-scale world model pre-trained on extensive real-world robot demonstrations, exhibits scalability and generalization capabilities. Its ability to perform in unseen real-world manipulation scenarios with minimal fine-tuning positions it as a foundational model for physical robot control. This is relevant for practical applications where data collection in new environments is costly or time-consuming.

The efficient uncertainty estimation method, retracing-rollout, offers a computationally feasible alternative to ensemble-based methods. Its effectiveness in enhancing model-based policy optimization in offline scenarios suggests that it can contribute to the development of more trustworthy and conservative learning agents, preventing the exploitation of model errors in critical applications.

The work's impact extends to advancing the state of the art in robotics and reinforcement learning by providing a pathway toward more autonomous, reliable, and adaptable embodied agents. By improving a world model's capacity to generalize and to quantify its predictive confidence, this research contributes to the broader objective of deploying AI systems in complex, dynamic real-world environments with greater safety and effectiveness. This framework could potentially facilitate breakthroughs in areas such as robot teleoperation, task planning, and simulation-to-reality transfer.
