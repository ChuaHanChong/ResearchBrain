# SpatialAfford: Teaching Compact VLMs Where to Look and Where to Ground for Affordance

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialAfford introduces a two-stage framework for compact Vision-Language Models to improve affordance grounding by first aligning cross-modal attention to functional regions and then refining spatial predictions. The framework enables 4B models to surpass larger 7B and 8B baselines, achieving 86.57% P@50 on PartAfford and 74.92% gIoU on ReasonAff.

## Problem

- Affordance grounding, which localizes specific functional regions for interaction, is more challenging than general object detection and critical for precise robotic manipulation.
- Compact autoregressive Vision-Language Models often suffer from an "attention-output mismatch," where internal cross-modal attention remains diffuse despite accurate bounding box predictions.
- Existing VLM training methods primarily apply supervision at the output level, failing to explicitly guide the model's visual focus to affordance-relevant features before coordinate decoding.

## Method

- **SpatialAfford** employs a two-stage coarse-to-fine framework, first establishing visual focus and then refining spatial predictions.
- Stage 1, ==Spatial Attention Alignment (SAA)==, uses a ==KL divergence loss== to explicitly align the VLM's cross-modal attention map with a smoothed ground-truth affordance mask, teaching the model "where to look."
- Stage 2, Spatial-Aware ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, refines coordinate generation by optimizing a policy with a composite reward function (IoU, L1 proximity, format) applied to the rectified features from Stage 1, enabling "where to ground."

## Results

- SpatialAfford (4B model) consistently outperformed larger 7B+ and 8B baselines across ShareRobot-Bench, ReasonAff, and PartAfford datasets.
- Achieved **86.57%** P@50 and **76.37%** gIoU on PartAfford, significantly surpassing Affordance-R1 (7B) which scored **60.90%** P@50, and obtained **74.92%** gIoU on ReasonAff.
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization on the AGD20K dataset, scoring the highest P@50 (**37.44%**) and NSS (**1.05**), with ablation studies confirming the synergistic contribution of both SAA and GRPO components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly regularizing a VLM's internal cross-modal attention towards ground-truth affordance regions is a necessary prerequisite for accurate and robust spatial grounding.
> - The identified "attention-output mismatch" is a critical limitation in compact VLMs, where models can predict correct coordinates without genuinely focusing their visual attention on the functional parts.
> - Decoupling attention alignment from spatial prediction and training them synergistically allows compact models to achieve superior affordance grounding performance and better generalization by fostering more visually grounded reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of embodied intelligence, specifically focusing on affordance grounding. Affordance grounding is a task that aims to localize the functional region of an object that supports a specific interaction, rather than identifying the entire object. This capability is considered fundamental for embodied agents, such as robots, as precise interaction requires identifying the actionable parts (e.g., a handle to grasp, a button to press).

The research landscape for affordance grounding has evolved from foundational convolutional neural network architectures (e.g., Myers et al., 2015; Sawatzky et al., 2017) to more recent decoder-only Vision-Language Models (VLMs) (e.g., Liu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). These modern VLMs often frame affordance region localization as an autoregressive coordinate generation task, building upon advancements in open-vocabulary detection and general embodied foundation models. Techniques like supervised instruction tuning followed by policy optimization (e.g., in RoboBrain, RynnBrain) and reinforcement learning (e.g., RLHF, GRPO) have been extended to spatial grounding tasks, with methods like Affordance-R1 leveraging GRPO for coordinate token generation.

However, the paper identifies a limitation in existing VLM training paradigms, particularly for compact autoregressive models. While sequence-level supervision and reinforcement learning have improved the quality of coordinate generation, these approaches primarily apply supervisory signals at the output level (i.e., the bounding box coordinates). They often do not provide explicit supervision on how the model's internal cross-modal attention aligns language queries with localized visual evidence *before* decoding the coordinates. This can lead to an "attention–output mismatch," where a model might predict an accurate bounding box but its internal visual attention remains diffuse or anchored to non-functional or task-irrelevant regions, such as the entire object rather than the specific affordance. This phenomenon has been observed in other multimodal reasoning contexts where visual-token attention can decay or be dominated by language priors (Yang et al., 2026; Zou et al., 2025).

SpatialAfford addresses this gap by proposing a framework that explicitly decouples visual evidence alignment from spatial prediction. It aims to introduce direct supervision for the model's internal cross-modal attention to ensure that the model first learns "where to look" (i.e., focuses on the correct affordance-relevant visual features) before optimizing "where to ground" (i.e., refining the precise coordinate prediction). This approach seeks to enhance the reliability and precision of affordance grounding, especially for compact VLMs with limited representational capacity, which are more susceptible to the identified attention-output mismatch.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the accuracy and reliability of affordance grounding in compact Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by addressing a specific internal limitation: the attention–output mismatch.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent challenges of affordance grounding and the observed shortcomings of current VLM training methodologies when applied to this task, particularly in compact models.
1. **Complexity of Affordance Grounding:** Affordance grounding is inherently more demanding than generic object grounding. The target regions are typically smaller, visually subtle, and highly dependent on the task context. An imprecise prediction, even if generally covering the object, may render it unusable for robotic manipulation. Therefore, accurate localization of the *functional* region is critical.
2. **Limitations of Existing VLM Training:** Current VLM training, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) methods like GRPO, predominantly focuses on optimizing the final bounding box coordinate tokens. While these methods can improve the quality of the output, they often lack direct supervision on the internal mechanism of how the model aligns textual queries with visual evidence (i.e., cross-modal attention) *prior* to generating coordinates.
3. **The "Attention–Output Mismatch" in Compact VLMs:** The core motivation arises from the observation that compact autoregressive VLMs frequently exhibit an "attention sink phenomenon" or diffuse attention. This means that despite producing a correct bounding box, the model's internal cross-modal attention may not be concentrated on the actual affordance-relevant visual evidence. Instead, it might be spread across the entire object, background clutter, or driven by global object-level shortcuts or language priors. The paper formalizes this by noting that the normalized attention density within the ground-truth region (M_gt) is often suppressed compared to task-irrelevant regions. This disconnect, where spatial decoding proceeds without proper visual anchoring, makes the prediction process less robust and generalizable, especially for models with limited representational capacity.
4. **Need for Explicit Visual Focus:** The authors argue that this lack of explicit spatial regularization in cross-modal attention leads to "shortcut learning," where the model finds ways to predict coordinates without truly grounding its visual focus on the functional affordance evidence. For compact models, which are more susceptible to such issues, this bottleneck is particularly severe.

To address these issues, the key objective is to decompose the affordance grounding problem into two distinct, yet synergistically trained, stages:
- **Stage 1: Establishing Affordance-Aware Visual Focus:** Teach the model "where to look" by explicitly aligning its internal cross-modal attention with the ground-truth affordance region. This aims to create a rectified visual feature space.
- **Stage 2: Refining Spatial Prediction:** Optimize the coordinate generation "where to ground" by applying policy optimization techniques on top of these pre-aligned, affordance-aware features, ensuring precise bounding box output.

By explicitly teaching the model where to focus visually before optimizing the output coordinates, the research aims to transform affordance grounding from a purely output-constrained task into a more visually grounded reasoning process, thereby enhancing performance and robustness in compact VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatialAfford is a two-stage framework designed to address the attention–output mismatch in compact VLMs for affordance grounding. It adopts a coarse-to-fine training strategy, first aligning visual attention and then refining coordinate prediction.

**A. Stage 1: Spatial Attention Alignment (SAA) – Fixing “Where to Look”**

This stage focuses on explicitly aligning the model's text-to-image cross-modal attention with the ground-truth affordance region.

1. **Constructing the Spatial Target Distribution (P_M):**
 - **Bbox to Grid Mask:** The ground-truth affordance bounding box `B = (x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2)` is first mapped onto the visual feature grid (`H_g × W_g`) of the vision encoder, creating a binary mask `M`. Pixels within the bounding box are set to 1, and others to 0.
 - **Box Blur Smoothing:** To create a soft, localized target distribution, the binary mask `M` is smoothed using a uniform average filter. The kernel size (`k`) of this filter is adaptive, scaling with the bounding box dimensions, and constrained to an odd-sized range of 3 to 7. This smoothing operation (2D convolution) produces a smoothed mask `M_tilde`.
 - **Normalization:** `M_tilde` is then normalized into a probability distribution `P_M`, ensuring that it sums to 1. This `P_M` serves as the explicit supervisory target for the model's attention.

2. **SAA Loss Formulation:**
 - The model's cross-modal attention map (`P_S`) is extracted, aggregated across text instruction tokens and specified transformer layers. This attention is specifically from the instruction prompt positions to prevent "teacher-forcing shortcut leakage."
 - The SAA loss (`L_SAA`) is formulated as the KL divergence `KL(P_S || P_M)`. This penalizes the model if its attention distribution `P_S` assigns significant probability mass to regions where the target distribution `P_M` is low (i.e., task-irrelevant areas). A small constant `ϵ` is added for numerical stability.

3. **Overall Stage 1 Objective:**
 - The SAA loss is combined with the standard negative log-likelihood loss for sequence-level supervision (`L_SFT`) via a weighted sum: `L_Stage1 = L_SFT + α · L_SAA`. This balances conventional coordinate prediction with explicit attention alignment.

**B. Stage 2: Spatial-Aware GRPO – Perfecting “Where to Ground”**

Following the attention alignment in Stage 1, this stage refines the coordinate generation using policy optimization. This optimization operates on the rectified, affordance-aware features (`F_SAA`) produced by Stage 1.

1. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - The methodology adopts Group Relative Policy Optimization (Shao et al., 2024). The policy (`π_θ`) samples a group of `G` candidate output bounding boxes (`{o_1, ..., o_G}`).
 - Each sampled output is evaluated by a spatial reward function (detailed below).
 - A group-relative advantage (`A_hat_g`) is computed for each sample, normalizing its reward (`R_g`) relative to the mean (`R_bar`) and standard deviation (`σ_R`) of rewards within its group.
 - The policy parameters are updated using a clipped surrogate objective, similar to PPO, regularized by a KL divergence penalty to keep the optimized policy (`π_θ`) close to a reference policy (`π_ref`). The BNPO loss variant with adaptive KL control is used for training stability.

2. **Spatial Reward Engine:**
 - A composite reward function `R` is designed to guide fine-grained coordinate adjustments, consisting of three components:
 - **Spatial Overlap Reward (R_IoU):** Measures the Intersection over Union (IoU) between the predicted bounding box (`b(o_g)`) and the ground-truth annotation (`b_gt`). For multiple ground-truth boxes, it considers the optimal assignment.
 - **Coordinate Proximity Reward (R_L1):** Calculates the mean absolute distance between the predicted and ground-truth bounding box coordinates in the normalized (norm1k) coordinate space. It is normalized to be between 0 and 1, with `R_L1 = max(0, 1 - d_bar_1 / τ_L1)`, where `d_bar_1` is the mean absolute coordinate error and `τ_L1` is a scaling factor.
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** A binary reward (0 or 1) that checks if the predicted output strictly adheres to the required coordinate format `[(x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2)]`, ensuring syntactic validity.
 - **Combined Reward:** The overall reward `R` is a combination of these components: `R = (R_IoU + R_L1) / 2 + R_format`. For the single-bbox annotations used in this work, `(R_IoU + R_L1) / 2` serves as the primary learning signal.

**C. Synergistic Training: Asymmetric Two-Stage Dependency**

- **Stage 1 as Prerequisite:** The policy optimization in Stage 2 is explicitly conditioned on the rectified feature space (`F_SAA`) produced by Stage 1. Without `F_SAA`, the reward signal would be applied to scattered or task-irrelevant visual evidence, reducing the effectiveness of policy optimization.
- **Stage 2 as Refinement:** While SAA establishes affordance-aware visual focus, it does not directly determine precise box boundaries. GRPO further optimizes the mapping from these aligned features to precise coordinate outputs, completing the transition from "where to look" to "where to ground."
- Both stages involve full-parameter optimization of the VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL-4B).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of SpatialAfford, particularly for compact VLMs in affordance grounding tasks. The evaluations were conducted across multiple benchmarks, including ShareRobot-Bench, ReasonAff, and PartAfford, with zero-shot generalization assessed on AGD20K.

**A. Overall Performance and Comparison with Baselines:**
- **Consistent Superiority:** SpatialAfford consistently achieved superior performance across all evaluated benchmarks compared to various baseline models, including general-purpose VLMs, embodied VLMs, and other affordance-specific VLMs.
- **Compact Model Outperformance:** A notable finding is that SpatialAfford, when instantiated with a compact 4B VLM (Qwen3-VL-4B), frequently outperformed larger 7B+ and 8B baselines.
 - **ShareRobot-Bench and PartAfford (Table I):** On ShareRobot-Bench, SpatialAfford (4B) achieved 55.00% P@50 and 47.80% gIoU. This performance surpassed RoboBrain2.5-8B in P@50 (51.80%) and closely matched its gIoU (48.07%). On PartAfford, SpatialAfford (4B) recorded 86.57% P@50 and 76.37% gIoU, significantly outperforming all baselines, including Affordance-R1 (7B) which achieved 60.90% P@50 and 57.24% gIoU.
 - **ReasonAff (Table II):** For instruction-dependent affordance reasoning, SpatialAfford (4B) achieved 74.92% gIoU and 82.50% P@50. This represented a substantial improvement over the affordance-specialized Affordance-R1 (7B), which scored 67.41% gIoU and 74.50% P@50, demonstrating the framework's efficacy even for complex reasoning tasks.

**B. Generalization and Attention Analysis (OOD on AGD20K):**
- **Zero-Shot Generalizability (Table III):** On the AGD20K dataset, which serves as an out-of-distribution (OOD) testbed, SpatialAfford (4B) demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization. It achieved the highest P@50 (37.44%) and Normalized Scanpath Saliency (NSS) scores (1.05), while ranking second only to the 8B RoboBrain2.5 in gIoU and cIoU. These results indicate that the explicit attention alignment (SAA) contributes to guiding models towards relevant visual focus even in novel scenarios.
- **Qualitative Attention Maps (Figure 5):** Visualizations of cross-modal attention maps confirmed that SpatialAfford effectively concentrated its visual focus on functionally relevant affordance regions. In contrast, competing methods often exhibited diffuse attention over task-irrelevant background areas or entire objects, supporting the claim of an attention–output mismatch in baseline models.

**C. Ablation Studies: Component Synergy (Table IV):**
- **Baseline (Qwen3-VL-4B):** The unmodified baseline model achieved 17.50% P@50.
- **Standard SFT:** Applying standard supervised fine-tuning alone resulted in a modest improvement to 22.50% P@50.
- **SFT + SAA (Spatial Attention Alignment):** Incorporating SAA during SFT led to a substantial increase in performance, with P@50 reaching 40.50% and gIoU 42.59%. This finding supports that explicit cross-modal attention regularization is critical for anchoring visual focus onto affordance regions.
- **GRPO (Standalone):** Applying Group Relative Policy Optimization alone improved P@50 to 45.55% and gIoU to 42.45%, primarily by refining coordinate prediction.
- **SpatialAfford (SAA + GRPO):** The full two-stage SpatialAfford framework achieved the best overall performance (47.80% gIoU and 55.00% P@50). This indicates a synergistic relationship: reward-driven boundary refinement (GRPO) is most effective when executed over an already established and aligned visual feature space (SAA).
- **SAA Loss Sensitivity (Table V):** Further analysis on the SAA loss weight (α) and function type showed that KL divergence with α=0.1 achieved the optimal balance, enhancing attention alignment while maintaining strong grounding ability. Using a pure coverage loss, despite improving attention alignment, severely degraded coordinate prediction accuracy, justifying the choice of KL divergence.

**D. Qualitative Visualizations (Figures 3 & 4):**
- Qualitative comparisons of predicted bounding boxes and affordance masks illustrated that SpatialAfford consistently produced precise and accurate localizations of functional regions. Baseline models, conversely, frequently made object-level predictions or suffered from diffuse bounding boxes that included non-functional parts.

In summary, the results consistently support the paper's central hypothesis: explicitly aligning a compact VLM's visual attention to affordance regions (Stage 1) before refining coordinate generation through policy optimization (Stage 2) leads to significant improvements in affordance grounding performance and generalizability, allowing smaller models to outperform larger, less focused baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "SpatialAfford" contributes to the field of embodied intelligence and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by addressing a core challenge in affordance grounding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Affordance Grounding:** The work provides an effective methodology for improving the precision and reliability of affordance grounding, a crucial capability for robotic manipulation and human-robot interaction. By enabling models to identify specific functional regions for interaction, SpatialAfford supports more accurate and context-aware robotic actions.

2. **Addressing VLM Limitations for Embodied AI:** The paper effectively diagnoses and mitigates the "attention–output mismatch" prevalent in compact autoregressive VLMs. This is particularly relevant for embodied systems, where models often need to operate under computational constraints, and precise understanding of visual cues is paramount. By demonstrating that teaching a model "where to look" is a necessary prerequisite to "where to ground," the research offers a pathway to more robust visual reasoning in VLMs.

3. **Efficiency and Accessibility:** SpatialAfford enables compact 4B models to achieve performance comparable to or exceeding that of larger 7B+ and 8B baselines. This is significant for practical applications, as smaller models require fewer computational resources for training and deployment, making advanced embodied intelligence capabilities more accessible and deployable on edge devices or in resource-constrained environments.

4. **Improved Generalization:** The demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization capabilities on out-of-distribution datasets (like AGD20K) suggest that SpatialAfford's approach fosters a more fundamental understanding of affordances, rather than relying on dataset-specific biases. This enhances the potential for deploying these models in novel, real-world scenarios without extensive re-training.

5. **Methodological Contribution:** The two-stage framework, explicitly separating attention alignment (SAA) from coordinate refinement (Spatial-Aware GRPO), offers a new paradigm for training VLMs for fine-grained spatial tasks. This decomposition could inspire similar approaches for other complex visual grounding problems where precise visual focus is critical. The design of the spatial reward engine further refines how policy optimization can be applied effectively in such contexts.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, particularly the extension of 2D attention alignment to 3D representations. This step is crucial for bridging the gap between 2D semantic perception and 3D physical manipulation, moving towards a more comprehensive understanding of functional focus in three-dimensional environments for embodied agents. Such advancements could lead to robots with enhanced capabilities for navigating, perceiving, and interacting with the physical world.

In conclusion, SpatialAfford contributes to the development of more capable and efficient VLMs for embodied intelligence by providing a principled approach to teach models not just what to predict, but also where to direct their visual attention for critical interaction cues.
