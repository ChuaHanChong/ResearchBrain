# Closing the Reality Gap: Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Deployment for Dexterous Force-Based Grasping and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ByteDance Seed developed a holistic sim-to-real framework enabling zero-shot deployment of reinforcement learning policies for dexterous force-based grasping and in-hand manipulation on real hardware. The approach achieved controllable grasp force tracking and robust object reorientation across diverse objects, bridging the gap between simulation and real-world robotic dexterity.

## Problem

- The significant "reality gap" often prevents zero-shot deployment of reinforcement learning policies from simulation to real multi-finger dexterous hands, especially for contact-rich interactions.
- Existing sim-to-real methods struggled with force-sensitive, closed-loop manipulation, which necessitates precise joint torque control and high-resolution tactile feedback.
- Specific practical hurdles included computationally intensive, high-fidelity tactile contact simulation and discrepancies between idealized simulated actuators and non-ideal real motor behaviors.

## Method

- A holistic sim-to-real reinforcement learning framework, trained using an asymmetric actor-critic PPO in the IsaacLab environment, was developed for full-state observation policies.
- Implemented a computationally efficient distance-field based tactile simulation that abstracts raw data into compact force and contact position features, avoiding slow soft-body physics.
- Introduced a one-time current-to-torque calibration to align real motor current signals with simulated joint torques and a randomized actuator model to robustly account for real-world motor imperfections like backlash and saturation.

## Results

- Policies demonstrated zero-shot deployment on a real 5-finger dexterous hand, achieving controllable grasp force tracking and in-hand object reorientation with high behavioral consistency between simulation and reality.
- The system successfully performed force-adaptive grasping of diverse and novel objects (e.g., angular ball, cube, apple, gamepad) across various commanded power levels (10-**100%**).
- In-hand rotation policies, leveraging tactile feedback and 6D orientation, achieved an average of 25.1 consecutive successful rotations on a real robot, significantly outperforming policies lacking contact sensing (1.1 successes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of explicit rewards for fingertip contact force and joint torque is essential for policies to learn robust, adaptive force-modulated grasping behaviors.
> - High-resolution tactile feedback (force-weighted contact center, contact force) and a continuous 6D object orientation representation are critical for stable and successful in-hand object reorientation.
> - Extensive domain randomization, especially for actuator dynamics, effectively forces policies to acquire robust strategies that are invariant to real-world hardware variations and uncertainties.
