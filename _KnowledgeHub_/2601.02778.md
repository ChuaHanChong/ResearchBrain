# Closing the Reality Gap: Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Deployment for Dexterous Force-Based Grasping and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ByteDance Seed developed a holistic sim-to-real framework enabling zero-shot deployment of reinforcement learning policies for dexterous force-based grasping and in-hand manipulation on real hardware. The approach achieved controllable grasp force tracking and robust object reorientation across diverse objects, bridging the gap between simulation and real-world robotic dexterity.

## Problem

- The significant "reality gap" often prevents zero-shot deployment of reinforcement learning policies from simulation to real multi-finger dexterous hands, especially for contact-rich interactions.
- Existing sim-to-real methods struggled with force-sensitive, closed-loop manipulation, which necessitates precise joint torque control and high-resolution tactile feedback.
- Specific practical hurdles included computationally intensive, high-fidelity tactile contact simulation and discrepancies between idealized simulated actuators and non-ideal real motor behaviors.

## Method

- A holistic sim-to-real reinforcement learning framework, trained using an asymmetric actor-critic PPO in the IsaacLab environment, was developed for full-state observation policies.
- Implemented a computationally efficient distance-field based tactile simulation that abstracts raw data into compact force and contact position features, avoiding slow soft-body physics.
- Introduced a one-time current-to-torque calibration to align real motor current signals with simulated joint torques and a randomized actuator model to robustly account for real-world motor imperfections like backlash and saturation.

## Results

- Policies demonstrated zero-shot deployment on a real 5-finger dexterous hand, achieving controllable grasp force tracking and in-hand object reorientation with high behavioral consistency between simulation and reality.
- The system successfully performed force-adaptive grasping of diverse and novel objects (e.g., angular ball, cube, apple, gamepad) across various commanded power levels (10-**100%**).
- In-hand rotation policies, leveraging tactile feedback and 6D orientation, achieved an average of 25.1 consecutive successful rotations on a real robot, significantly outperforming policies lacking contact sensing (1.1 successes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of explicit rewards for fingertip contact force and joint torque is essential for policies to learn robust, adaptive force-modulated grasping behaviors.
> - High-resolution tactile feedback (force-weighted contact center, contact force) and a continuous 6D object orientation representation are critical for stable and successful in-hand object reorientation.
> - Extensive domain randomization, especially for actuator dynamics, effectively forces policies to acquire robust strategies that are invariant to real-world hardware variations and uncertainties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL), sim-to-real transfer, and dexterous robotic manipulation. The field of sim-to-real RL has witnessed remarkable progress in dynamic control tasks, especially in legged locomotion. Pioneering works with quadruped and humanoid robots, such as ANYmal's agile locomotion and soccer-playing humanoids, have demonstrated that systematically designed domain randomization techniques, coupled with advanced RL, can bridge the "reality gap" for complex whole-body skills. This success is often attributed to physics-based simulation augmentation, learning-based actuator modeling, and sophisticated RL techniques like asymmetric actor-critic learning and curriculum learning.

However, the paper highlights that applying sim-to-real RL to dexterous in-hand manipulation remains a significant challenge. This difficulty stems from two primary distinctions: (1) the highly contact-rich physics involved in multi-point interactions, often with diverse material properties that are difficult to model accurately in rigid-body physics simulations; and (2) the complex sensorimotor coupling required to effectively fuse tactile feedback, joint positions, torques, and high-dimensional visual perception.

Prior work in dexterous manipulation has made strides, particularly with multi-finger hands. OpenAI's achievements in in-hand rotation and solving the Rubik's Cube demonstrated the potential of large-scale domain randomization and high-dimensional state spaces. More recent efforts, such as Dextreme, have further improved robust cube reorientation. A key trend in this progress has been the shift towards multimodal feedback, integrating tactile sensing to enhance precision and dexterity, as shown by works utilizing binary touch signals or fusing visuotactile and point-cloud inputs. Force-controlled dexterous manipulation, however, has largely remained an underexplored frontier compared to position-centric tasks. The explicit regulation of interaction forces, critical for handling fragile objects or precision assembly, requires policies that can leverage full-state feedback, combining high-resolution tactile sensing with joint-level torque control.

This paper directly addresses these two profound practical hurdles: the "Tactile Simulation Bottleneck" (computational cost and fidelity of high-resolution tactile contact simulation) and the "Actuation Reality Gap" (discrepancy between idealized simulated actuators and non-ideal real motors that lack direct torque sensors). By offering a holistic sim-to-real recipe for force-sensitive, closed-loop manipulation, this work pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with simulation-trained policies on real dexterous hardware, positioning itself as a crucial step towards more general-purpose, human-level manipulation capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **close the reality gap** for dexterous force-based grasping and manipulation, enabling **zero-shot deployment** of simulation-trained control policies directly onto real hardware. The authors are motivated by the gap between the remarkable success of sim-to-real reinforcement learning in areas like legged locomotion and its limited application to the highly complex, contact-rich domain of dexterous in-hand manipulation.

The paper identifies several core motivations and specific objectives to address this challenge:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Dexterous Tasks:** Despite the promise of human-like manipulation from multi-finger hands, training control policies that deploy directly to real hardware is difficult due to contact-rich physics and imperfect actuation. The authors aim to overcome these discrepancies.
- **Achieving Force-Sensitive, Closed-Loop Manipulation:** Most prior work focused on position-centric tasks. A critical unmet need is the explicit regulation of interaction forces, essential for tasks involving fragile items or precision assembly. This necessitates policies that leverage full-state feedback, fusing high-resolution tactile sensing with joint-level torque control.
- **Addressing the Tactile Simulation Bottleneck:** High-fidelity, high-resolution tactile contact simulation is computationally prohibitive for deep RL at scale. An objective is to develop a fast and efficient tactile simulation method that provides the necessary high-rate, high-resolution touch signals without sacrificing critical contact information.
- **Overcoming the Actuation Reality Gap:** Most commercially available dexterous hands lack joint-level torque sensors, relying on motor current for estimation. The gap between idealized simulated actuators and non-ideal real motors (e.g., torque-speed saturation, friction, backlash) is a major barrier. The goal is to develop a robust approach to align simulated actuator dynamics with real-world behavior, including a current-to-torque calibration and comprehensive actuator dynamics modeling with randomization.
- **Demonstrating Key Dexterous Skills:** The practical objectives include demonstrating two fundamental manipulation skills with policies trained entirely in simulation and deployed zero-shot on a real five-finger hand:
 1. **Command-based, controllable grasp force tracking:** The ability to modulate grip force according to user commands.
 2. **In-hand reorientation of objects:** The ability to reposition and rotate objects within the grasp.

Ultimately, the authors envision this work as a practical solution and an "easy-to-replicate handbook" for training full-state RL policies on dexterous hands, moving towards the grand vision of general-purpose robots with human-level manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a holistic sim-to-real reinforcement learning framework designed to achieve zero-shot deployment of dexterous manipulation policies. The core methodology integrates an asymmetric actor-critic PPO pipeline, trained exclusively in the IsaacLab simulation environment, with several key innovations to bridge the reality gap. The experimental platform used is a 12-DoF direct-drive dexterous hand, the xHand.

**Policy Formulation:**
The policies are designed to leverage a "full-state" observation space that combines proprioception (joint angles, velocities), dense tactile signals, and estimated joint torques. Actions are target joint positions for a motor-level PD controller.

**Sim-to-Real Bridging Techniques:**
The framework addresses three primary contributors to the sim-to-real gap:

1. **Computationally Efficient Tactile Simulation:**
 - **Distance-Field Based Approach:** Instead of complex finite element method (FEM) simulations, the method employs a rapid, massively parallel tactile sensor simulation. It computes the distance from each of 600 virtual tactile units (120 per fingertip) to the N-nearest surface points on the object via parallel forward kinematics.
 - **Contact Detection:** Contact is detected when a virtual sensor "penetrates" the object's surface, determined by a negative dot product between the vector from the object to the sensor and the object's surface normal (Eq. 11).
 - **Force Approximation:** Contact force at each unit is approximated using a linear spring model ($f_{ij} \approx k \cdot d_{ij}$), justified by the relatively rigid rubber material of the xHand fingertips, which operates in a near-linear force-penetration range (Fig. 3b).
 - **Tactile Feature Abstraction:** Raw tactile data is abstracted into a compact, semantically meaningful representation for each fingertip $i$: $T_i = (F_i, \mu_i)$. $F_i$ is the total contact force (sum of individual unit forces), and $\mu_i$ is the force-weighted mean contact position, calculated efficiently using penetration distances. This reduces observation dimensionality while retaining critical contact information.

2. **Current-to-Torque Calibration:**
 - Recognizing that the real xHand provides motor current but lacks direct joint torque sensors, a one-time, per-joint calibration procedure is performed.
 - It establishes a linear correlation between real-world motor current ($I_{real}$) and real-world contact force ($F_{real}$), and similarly between simulated joint torque ($\tau_{sim}$) and simulated contact force ($F_{sim}$).
 - Both signals are then normalized to a dimensionless force proxy scale of based on their maximum observed values. During real-world deployment, the policy receives this normalized current ($I_{norm}$) as the "joint torque" input, aligning it with the simulated torque it was trained on (Fig. 2).

3. **Actuator Model Randomization:**
 - To account for non-ideal real-world motor behaviors, a randomized actuator model is incorporated.
 - **PD Control with Dynamics:** Torque is computed via a PD controller ($ \tau_c = k_p (q_{ref} - q_m) + k_d (\dot{q}_{ref} - \dot{q}_m)$).
 - **Backlash Hysteresis:** A deadband function ($ \tau_b $) is introduced to simulate mechanical dead zones in gear transmissions, with a randomized threshold ($\epsilon$).
 - **Torque-Velocity Saturation:** A velocity-dependent saturation function ($ \tau_{sat}^{\pm}(\dot{q})$) models the characteristics of DC motors, with randomized stall torque ($\tau_0$) and maximum no-load velocity ($\dot{q}_{max}$).
 - **Efficiency Factor:** A randomized factor ($\eta$) accounts for variations in motor torque constant and drive efficiency.
 - All actuator model parameters are re-sampled for each actuator at the start of every training episode, forcing the policy to be robust to a wide range of hardware imperfections (Eq. 18-21).

**Training Scenarios and Reward Design:**

- **Force-Adaptive Grasping:**
 - **"Inverted Catch-the-Object" Setup:** To improve sample efficiency and prevent reward hacking, objects are procedurally dropped into a palm-up, fixed dexterous hand. This simplifies exploration by leveraging gravity to bring the object into the workspace.
 - **Randomization:** Object properties (mass, size, shape, friction) and initial drop orientations are randomized.
 - **Reward Components (Section 3.5.1):** Includes explicit rewards for target joint torque ($R_{torque}$) and fingertip contact force ($R_{force}$), with Gaussian functions for desired values and binary validity masks (Eq. 1-6). Novel consistency rewards ($R_{diff}$) promote coordinated motion among fingers (Eq. 7), and an outer finger movement penalty ($R_{outter}$) (Eq. 8). Standard penalties for action rate ($R_{action}$) and joint velocity ($R_{vel}$) are also used (Eq. 9-10).

- **In-Hand Object Rotation:**
 - **Constrained Rotation:** The task involves rotating a standardized cube around a single, predefined axis to simplify the problem and focus on fundamental dynamics.
 - **Dynamic Curriculum:** The target orientation continuously shifts by 90 degrees upon success, encouraging continuous regrasping and manipulation rather than a static reorientation.
 - **Observation Variations:** Evaluates proprioception-only vs. IMU-augmented policies. Crucially, it uses a **continuous 6D rotation representation** for orientation feedback to avoid discontinuities associated with quaternions.
 - **Reward Engineering (Table 2):** A composite reward function minimizes rotation error and position drift, with a large bonus for reaching the target, incentivizing continuous manipulation.

This integrated methodology, combining specific sensor modeling, actuator dynamics, and tailored RL training strategies, forms the "sim-to-real recipe" for dexterous manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed sim-to-real framework for dexterous force-based grasping and manipulation, achieving zero-shot deployment of simulation-trained policies on real hardware.

**Force-Adaptive Grasping:**
- **Reward Structure Efficacy:** An ablation study on reward terms (Table 3, Fig. 5) confirmed that the combination of `R_Force` (penalizing inaccurate fingertip contact forces) and `R_torque` (penalizing inaccurate joint torques) is "strictly necessary" for robust and adaptive force-modulated grasping. Policies trained with both rewards achieved significantly stronger and wider ranges for both contact forces and joint torques, indicating a synergistic effect where the policy learns to coordinate joint actuation and fingertip contact simultaneously.
- **Controllable Grasp Force Tracking:** The policies successfully tracked variable force commands, exhibiting an approximately linear relationship between the commanded input and the exerted contact force and joint torque (Fig. 5). This validated the policy's capacity for fine-grained, controllable force exertion.
- **Generalization to Diverse Objects:** The policy demonstrated robust force-adaptive grasping of a wide range of objects with varying geometries (e.g., angular ball, cube, tennis ball, milk bottle, apple) in both simulation and the real world (Fig. 6). Furthermore, it successfully grasped novel, irregularly shaped objects (e.g., gamepad, bowl) not seen during training (Fig. 9), showcasing strong generalization capabilities.
- **Modulation of Grasp Strength:** The system could effectively modulate grasp intensity across commanded power levels (10%, 50%, 100%) for different objects (Fig. 8).

**In-Hand Object Rotation:**
- **Criticality of Tactile and Orientation Feedback:** A real-world ablation study (Table 4) on sensory components unequivocally demonstrated the critical role of tactile information (force-weighted contact center and contact force) and a continuous 6D object orientation representation.
 - The complete observation configuration (contact center, contact force, 6D orientation) yielded the best performance, averaging 25.1 consecutive successful rotations.
 - Removing tactile feedback significantly degraded performance (e.g., 12.2 successes without contact center, 13.2 without contact force, 7.5 without both). The baseline without any contact sensing performed poorest (1.1 successes).
- **Importance of 6D Rotation Representation:** Replacing the continuous 6D rotation representation with quaternions severely impacted performance, reducing average consecutive successes to 2.9. This highlights the necessity of a singularity-free representation for stable gradient-based learning.
- **Continuous Manipulation:** The dynamic curriculum, where the target rotation shifted by 90 degrees upon success, enabled the policy to learn continuous regrasping behaviors, effectively re-stabilizing the object for subsequent manipulation steps (Fig. 7).

**Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
- A high degree of behavioral consistency was observed between the simulated policies and their real-world execution for both tasks. Motion trajectories, contact patterns, and overall task performance were remarkably similar across both domains (Fig. 6, Fig. 7). This visual and quantitative evidence strongly supports the claim of effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieved without any fine-tuning on the physical robot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for dexterous hands operating in contact-rich environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **First-of-its-Kind Demonstration:** The paper claims the "first demonstration of controllable grasping on a multi-finger dexterous hand trained entirely in simulation and transferred zero-shot on real hardware." This achievement is a critical milestone, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with sim-to-real reinforcement learning for fine-grained manipulation.
- **Practical Sim-to-Real Recipe:** The integrated approach, detailed as a "holistic solution" and an "easy-to-replicate handbook," provides a clear and practical pathway for researchers and practitioners to tackle complex dexterous tasks. It offers concrete solutions to long-standing challenges in tactile simulation and actuator modeling that have historically hindered progress.
- **Lowering Barriers to Entry:** By providing a robust method for current-to-torque calibration and eliminating the need for expensive, miniaturized joint-level torque sensors, the research significantly lowers the financial and engineering barriers to conducting advanced force-based manipulation research. Similarly, the computationally efficient tactile simulation bypasses the need for high-fidelity, but often prohibitively slow, soft-body physics engines.
- **Enabling Force-Sensitive Applications:** The demonstration of force-adaptive grasping and controllable force tracking is crucial. This capability is indispensable for real-world applications such as handling fragile objects (e.g., in manufacturing, medical, or food industries), precision assembly, and safe human-robot interaction where regulating interaction forces is paramount.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive domain randomization, especially of actuator dynamics (e.g., torque-speed saturation, backlash), forces the policies to learn robust strategies that are invariant to real-world hardware variations and uncertainties. This results in policies that generalize well to unseen objects and maintain stability even with non-ideal actuators, a key requirement for reliable deployment.
- **Advancement in Multimodal Perception for Robotics:** The explicit incorporation and proven efficacy of full-state tactile-torque observations underscore the importance of multimodal sensing for complex manipulation. This work encourages further development and integration of high-resolution touch and proprioception in future robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Dexterity:** While the current tasks are structured, the robust foundation laid here for force-controlled, zero-shot transfer opens doors for more complex, long-horizon manipulation skills. It provides a stepping stone towards integrating these dexterous hand policies with mobile manipulators, tackling tasks requiring whole-body coordination, and eventually learning directly from raw visual and tactile sensory inputs without privileged state information.

In essence, this research not only solves critical technical challenges in sim-to-real transfer for dexterous manipulation but also provides a pragmatic framework that can accelerate the development and wider deployment of dexterous robotic hands in diverse, human-centric environments.
