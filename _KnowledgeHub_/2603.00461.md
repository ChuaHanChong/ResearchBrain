# ReMoT: Reinforcement Learning with Motion Contrast Triplets

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified framework, ReMoT, enhances Vision-Language Models' spatio-temporal reasoning by leveraging a large-scale motion-contrast triplet dataset and a novel Reinforcement Learning training paradigm. It improves fine-grained motion understanding, achieving 38.0% overall accuracy on its custom ReMoT-16K-Test benchmark and outperforming significantly larger models on existing spatial-temporal evaluation sets.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently exhibit poor spatio-temporal consistency, misinterpreting 3D object motion, dynamic inter-frame changes, and global orientation in real-world applications.
- Existing VLM training data lacks explicit, fine-grained supervision for motion attributes, relying on static image-text pairs or coarse video-text captions.
- Current solutions for spatio-temporal reasoning are often piecemeal (e.g., architectural tweaks, data augmentation) rather than providing a systematic framework for comprehensive motion understanding.

## Method

- Developed ==ReMoT-16K==, a large-scale (**16.5K** triplets) motion-contrast dataset, explicitly designed to model inter-frame motion attributes and generate hard negatives through a multi-expert collaborative pipeline.
- Implemented a hybrid training paradigm combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, using a composite reward function (task accuracy, logical consistency, CoT length regularization) in an alternating SFT↔GRPO scheme.
- Constructed ==ReMoT-16K-Test==, the first benchmark for fine-grained motion contrast triplets, featuring visually similar samples with opposing motion attributes to rigorously evaluate VLM spatio-temporal reasoning.

## Results

- The alternating SFT↔GRPO training achieved **38.0%** Overall Accuracy and **64.0%** Partial Accuracy on the ReMoT-16K-Test benchmark, a **+18.7%** relative gain in Overall Accuracy over the Qwen3-VL-CoT baseline.
- ReMoT-4B-CoT reached state-of-the-art performance on existing spatial-temporal benchmarks (VLM2, VSI, MMSI), outperforming models like the **7.5 times** larger Qwen3-VL-30B-CoT by **+1.8%** to **+2.7%** and matching GPT-4o.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the logical consistency reward improved manipulation accuracy by **+10.6%** and reduced reasoning errors from approximately **60%** in the base model to **12%** in ReMoT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling motion-contrast triplets with synthesized hard negatives is crucial for teaching VLMs fine-grained spatio-temporal reasoning, outperforming binary contrast approaches.
> - An alternating hybrid SFT↔GRPO training strategy, guided by a composite reward including a logic consistency component, effectively balances linguistic fluency and reward alignment, leading to superior performance.
> - Using rule-based geometric verification for constructing logical consistency rewards provides more effective and efficient supervision for learning spatial facts compared to learned reward models or DPO.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have advanced significantly, evolving into versatile perception systems capable of unifying visual understanding and language reasoning. These models are increasingly integrated into critical applications such as generative AI (AIGC), embodied intelligence (e.g., robotics), and autonomous driving. Such applications necessitate reasoning beyond static, single-frame perception, requiring models to understand how physical scenes evolve in space and time, including tasks like 3D object motion tracking, interpreting dynamic changes across frames, and inferring global orientation.

Despite their progress, current mainstream VLMs exhibit limitations in spatio-temporal consistency. While proficient in aligning visual semantics, they often struggle with the fundamental capability of maintaining coherent spatial and temporal reasoning. Empirical observations indicate that even advanced general-purpose models like Qwen3-VL, Claude-Sonnet-4.5, and the GPT series can confound camera rotation with real object motion, misinterpret object states (e.g., gripper status), or incorrectly infer movement directions.

Existing methodologies, including architectural modifications and data augmentation techniques, have provided partial solutions. However, these approaches are often piecemeal and have not offered a systematic framework to address this fundamental limitation across the paradigms of data generation, model training, and evaluation.

The broader research landscape for VLM spatio-temporal reasoning reveals several gaps:
- **Data Granularity**: Current VLM training data largely relies on static image-text pairs or coarse clip-level captions from video datasets, lacking explicit, fine-grained supervision for motion attributes. Embodied datasets offer structured metadata but are limited in semantic annotations for motion. Methods based on self-supervised learning or temporal proximity often focus on spatial geometry over motion semantics or use weak motion proxies, failing to explicitly model contrastive motion attributes.
- **Training Consistency**: Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for VLMs often struggles with achieving long-horizon reasoning consistency due to limited reasoning-specific data. While reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) have shown promise in enhancing chain-of-thought generation, systematic explorations of their application with diverse data sources, particularly hybrid strategies, have been limited.
- **Evaluation Nuance**: Existing benchmarks, while valuable, may not rigorously measure a VLM's ability to discriminate subtle motion attributes (e.g., opposing directions) or reason about complex inter-frame dynamics consistently.

ReMoT positions itself within this landscape by offering a systematic and unified training paradigm designed to address these fundamental shortcomings. It contributes by:
1. Developing a rule-based automatic framework for large-scale generation of motion-contrast data, explicitly encoding directional semantics.
2. Investigating and validating Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), including hybrid SFT-GRPO strategies, for efficient learning of contrastive reasoning.
3. Constructing a novel benchmark focused on fine-grained motion contrast to measure subtle motion discrimination.
This comprehensive approach aims to fill the identified gaps in data, training, and evaluation for VLM spatio-temporal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of contemporary Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robustly understanding spatio-temporal dynamics and maintaining consistency in reasoning about physical changes within multi-image or video sequences. Despite their advanced capabilities in general visual understanding and language reasoning, VLMs frequently fail in tasks requiring an accurate interpretation of motion, relative positioning, and temporal evolution, which are critical for real-world applications such like autonomous driving, robotics, and embodied AI.

The key objectives of the ReMoT project are:

1. **Systematically Address Spatio-Temporal Consistency Deficiencies**: The overarching objective is to provide a unified training paradigm that overcomes the "fundamental shortcomings" of VLMs in spatio-temporal consistency. This includes rectifying errors such as confusing camera movement with object motion, misinterpreting gripper states, or inaccurately describing character trajectories.

2. **Develop a Scalable Motion-Contrast Dataset**: To address the scarcity of training data that explicitly models fine-grained motion attributes, the objective is to construct ReMoT-16K, a large-scale (16.5K triplets) motion-contrast dataset. This dataset is designed to provide explicit supervision for inter-frame motion attributes (e.g., "camera rotates left" vs. "camera rotates right"). A critical motivation for its generation is to surpass the scale, consistency, and cost-efficiency of manual annotation or direct VLM-based generation, which was found to be prone to errors and expensive.

3. **Investigate and Optimize Training Paradigms**: The research aims to systematically evaluate various optimization strategies for learning contrastive motion reasoning. Beyond standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), a key objective is to explore the effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and various hybrid strategies (sequential and alternating SFT-GRPO integration). This includes designing a composite reward mechanism that integrates task accuracy, reasoning compactness, and logical consistency to guide the learning process. The motivation here is to identify a training approach that yields optimal performance and data efficiency for this specific type of reasoning.

4. **Establish a Fine-Grained Motion Contrast Benchmark**: To enable rigorous and nuanced evaluation of VLM capabilities in discriminating subtle motion attributes, an objective is to construct the first benchmark specifically for fine-grained motion contrast triplets. This benchmark is designed to differentiate models based on their ability to reason about complex inter-frame dynamics, even when visual differences are subtle but motion attributes are opposing.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to deliver a VLM that demonstrates improved spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities, validated by state-of-the-art performance on both the newly constructed benchmark and existing VLM benchmarks, thereby providing a robust solution for a critical failure point in VLM deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ReMoT framework integrates a multi-faceted approach involving data construction, training paradigm selection, and dedicated evaluation.

#### 3.1. ReMoT-16k Data Construction

The core of the data approach is the creation of ReMoT-16K, a large-scale motion-contrast triplet dataset. Each triplet consists of (I\_anchor, I\_pos, I\_neg), where the anchor-positive pair exhibits a specific motion property (m), and the anchor-negative pair visually resembles the positive but violates the property (m). This design aims to compel models to learn fine-grained motion discrimination.

Initial attempts to generate these triplets using state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) like Qwen-3-VL and Gemini-2.5-Pro proved inefficient, yielding only 632 valid triplets after significant filtering due to 55% format errors and high operational costs. This motivated the development of a more systematic, rule-based pipeline.

The multi-expert collaborative construction pipeline operates on structured meta-annotations (e.g., camera pose matrices, robot action logs) derived from video datasets:

1. **Motion Estimation Experts (g : (I\_t, I\_t', A) → m)**: These domain-specific components extract precise geometric and physical motion properties. For instance, navigation experts compute rigid transformations from SE(3) pose matrices to describe camera rotation, while manipulation experts extract end-effector trajectories from robot telemetry to describe robot actions. The output (m) encodes compositional motion semantics and attributes.

2. **Triplet Construction Expert (ϕ, N)**:
 - **Positive Pair Selection**: This expert identifies perceptible and coherent transitions by filtering for motion properties (m) within specific magnitude thresholds (T\_m). For example, camera rotations might be selected if they fall within [10°, 50°], balancing discriminability and visual coherence.
 - **Hard Negative Synthesis**: For each (I\_anchor, I\_pos, m) pair, a hard negative (I\_neg) is generated. This involves two strategies:
 - **Geometric Synthesis (T\_geo)**: Applying spatial transformations to I\_pos that simulate the opposite or conflicting motion (e.g., rotating an image left when the positive motion was right).
 - **Retrieval (R)**: Searching the original video for frames that are visually similar to I\_pos but possess contradictory motion properties (¯m).

3. **VQA Formulation Expert**: This expert designs comprehensive question-answer pairs for each constructed triplet. Instead of isolated questions, it generates multi-question reasoning chains that probe motion understanding from various perspectives and granularities, examining logical dependencies. Question formats vary, including multiple-choice, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, and comparative reasoning, requiring models to infer object motion, camera rotation, and physical continuity.

#### 3.2. ReMoT-16k-Test Benchmark Construction

A dedicated benchmark was created for rigorous evaluation of fine-grained spatio-temporal reasoning. It comprises 600 evaluation triplets (1776 questions total), combining 500 multi-expert generated triplets (covering navigation, robotic manipulation, and other scenarios) with 100 VLM-generated triplets (focusing on object tracking and game scenarios). This benchmark is designed with high visual hardness, featuring sample pairs that are visually similar but possess opposing motion attributes.

#### 3.3. Training Variants

The base model for ReMoT is Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking, chosen for its balance of performance, computational feasibility, and intrinsic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) capabilities. The training loss for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is computed solely on tokens within the `<answer>` tag.

The research systematically investigates various optimization paradigms:
1. **Pure Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Standard training with cross-entropy loss.
2. **Pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: GRPO is adopted as the state-of-the-art RL paradigm. It optimizes the policy by comparing sampled responses with group-normalized advantages derived from a composite reward.
3. **Hybrid Optimization Schemes**:
 - **Sequential Hybrid (SFT→GRPO)**: An initial SFT phase provides a stable policy initialization, followed by a complete switch to GRPO for refinement.
 - **Alternating Hybrid (SFT↔GRPO)**: SFT and GRPO steps alternate every few updates. This allows for the joint evolution of linguistic fluency (from SFT) and reward alignment (from GRPO).

#### 3.4. Composite Reward Design for GRPO

To guide the policy effectively in GRPO, a composite reward function is designed:
- **Task Accuracy Reward (R\_task)**: Directly reflects the correctness of the generated answer against ground truth.
- **CoT Length Regularization (R\_length)**: A penalty `R_length(o_i) = -max(0, |o_think_i| - L_target)` is applied to discourage excessively verbose and repetitive reasoning traces, promoting conciseness.
- **Logical Consistency Refinement (R\_logic)**: This novel reward component, formalized as `R_logic(o)`, assigns +1 if answers satisfy logical constraints (e.g., transitivity in comparative reasoning), -1 if contradictions are detected (even without ground truth), and 0 if no verifiable relation exists. This aims to foster self-consistent reasoning.
- **Format Reward**: An internal reward from the `ms-swift` library encouraging valid answer syntax.
These components are weighted (3.5:3.5:1.3:1.7 for format, accuracy, length, and logic, respectively) to calculate the total reward, which then influences the group-normalized advantages in GRPO. A KL regularization term with coefficient 0.01 is also used to constrain reward drift from the SFT distribution.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Training was conducted for 2 epochs on 8x A800 GPUs using mixed precision, with a batch size of 16 for both SFT and RL. AdamW optimizer with a cosine decay schedule was used for SFT. RL employed 4 rollouts per sample. Evaluations utilized the VLMEvalKit toolkit, with unified prompting and answer extraction rules, measuring both Overall Accuracy (all sub-questions correct) and Partial Accuracy (proportional score for correct sub-questions).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the ReMoT framework in enhancing spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models.

#### 4.1. Performance on ReMoT-16k-Test Benchmark (Table 1)

- **Baseline Performance**: State-of-the-art VLMs, including InternVL3-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieved low overall accuracies (below 15%) on the ReMoT-16k-Test benchmark. Performance on multi-step tasks like Composite Manipulation was near zero (0.0%–4.8%). Even the reasoning-enhanced Qwen3-VL-CoT-4B showed limited performance (20.7% Overall, 38.9% Partial).
- **ReMoT Improvements**:
 - **GRPO (Reinforcement Learning)**: Significantly improved performance across categories, achieving 27.0% Overall for Navigation, 94.1% for Perceptual Grounding (Counting), and 61.3% for Manipulation (Gripper-Move). The overall macro-averaged performance was 33.6% (Overall) and 61.6% (Partial).
 - **SFT→GRPO (Sequential Hybrid)**: Showed a marginal improvement over pure GRPO, reaching 35.0% (Overall) and 63.3% (Partial).
 - **SFT↔GRPO (Alternating Hybrid)**: Achieved the highest performance across all tested paradigms, with 38.0% Overall Accuracy and 64.0% Partial Accuracy. This represents a relative gain of +18.7% and +25.1% in overall and partial accuracy, respectively, compared to the base Qwen3-VL-CoT-4B model. These results indicate that the alternating hybrid strategy is the most effective for integrating supervised learning and reinforcement learning for this task.

#### 4.2. Ablation and Comparative Studies

- **Training Data Composition (Table 2)**:
 - Starting from the Qwen3-VL baseline (20.7% Overall Accuracy), adding manipulation data provided modest gains (+3.25%).
 - Including navigation data led to a substantial improvement, raising overall accuracy to 32.4% (+8.4%), indicating the critical role of spatial relation reasoning.
 - Further incorporating simulation data boosted performance to 38.0% (+5.65%), demonstrating the cumulative benefit of diverse spatio-temporal data types.
- **Logic Reward Decoupling (Table 3)**:
 - The base Qwen3-VL-4B-CoT model exhibited low logical correctness (46.6%) on the manipulation subset, which specifically tests consistency.
 - GRPO without an explicit logic reward improved logical consistency to 98.6% but achieved an overall accuracy of 68.6%.
 - GRPO with the decoupled logic reward further elevated logical correctness to 99.3% and significantly boosted overall accuracy to 78.0% (+10.6%). This strong correlation highlights that explicitly enforcing self-consistent reasoning chains is vital for performance improvement.
- **Data Scaling Analysis (Figure 3)**:
 - The multi-expert collaborative pipeline for data construction demonstrated smooth scaling of performance from 0.42 to 0.66 (accuracy) without saturation, suggesting high construction quality and scalability.
 - In contrast, VLM-generated data exhibited volatile scaling behavior and plateaued at approximately 0.49, indicating that data quality fundamentally limits achievable performance.
 - Both pipelines showed a trend of declining response verbosity (word counts) as the amount of training data increased, suggesting models learn to generate more concise reasoning.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Other VLM Benchmarks (Table 4)

- **Spatio-Temporal Reasoning Benchmarks (VLM2, VSI, MMSI)**: ReMoT-4B-CoT achieved state-of-the-art performance among all evaluated models. It surpassed the much larger Qwen3-VL-30B-CoT (7.5x parameter count) by +1.8% on VLM2, +2.7% on VSI, and +2.3% on MMSI. Notably, ReMoT-4B-CoT matched GPT-4o on these tasks despite being orders of magnitude smaller.
- **General Multimodal Benchmarks (BLINK, MUIR, MMStar, MMMU)**: ReMoT-4B-CoT maintained comparable or improved performance relative to other open-source models, and was competitive with proprietary models. For instance, it outperformed Qwen3-VL-30B-CoT on BLINK by +2.7%. This indicates that the enhanced spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities do not compromise the model's general multimodal understanding, suggesting good generalizability.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4)

Qualitative comparisons demonstrated that Qwen3-VL frequently misinterpreted ambiguous scenarios and produced contradictory conclusions. In contrast, ReMoT, leveraging its structured reasoning chains, accurately resolved fine-grained distinctions by effectively integrating temporal dynamics and spatial relationships.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReMoT framework offers a systematic solution to a fundamental limitation in Vision-Language Models (VLMs): their deficiency in spatio-temporal consistency. This work contributes to the field in several significant ways and carries substantial potential impact across various domains.

#### 5.1. Systematic Solution for VLM Spatio-Temporal Consistency

ReMoT presents a unified training paradigm that systematically addresses the challenges of spatio-temporal reasoning across data, training, and evaluation. This is an advancement beyond fragmented or ad-hoc solutions, offering a cohesive framework for improving VLM performance in dynamic environments. The empirical validation of this approach, demonstrating a significant performance leap (25.1% relative gain) on spatio-temporal tasks, suggests a robust method for integrating dynamic understanding into VLMs.

#### 5.2. Novel and Scalable Data Generation Methodology

The development of ReMoT-16K through a rule-based, multi-expert collaborative pipeline is a significant contribution. This methodology enables the large-scale generation of high-quality motion-contrast triplets, explicitly modeling subtle inter-frame motion attributes. By automating data construction from video meta-annotations, ReMoT bypasses the prohibitive costs and quality issues associated with manual annotation or less reliable VLM-based generation, offering a scalable solution for creating fine-grained motion supervision data. This data construction approach can serve as a blueprint for generating similar high-quality, targeted datasets for other complex reasoning tasks.

#### 5.3. Advanced Training Paradigms for Reasoning Enhancement

The systematic exploration and validation of reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), especially the alternating hybrid (SFT↔GRPO) strategy, provide insights into effective VLM training. The integration of composite rewards, including novel logical consistency and CoT length regularization, demonstrates a method for guiding policy learning toward not only correct answers but also coherent, concise, and self-consistent reasoning chains. This advancement in training methodology can lead to more reliable and interpretable VLM outputs in reasoning-intensive tasks.

#### 5.4. Establishment of a Rigorous Fine-Grained Benchmark

The creation of the ReMoT-16K-Test benchmark addresses a gap in existing VLM evaluation protocols. By focusing on fine-grained motion contrast triplets with visually similar but semantically opposing motion attributes, the benchmark provides a stringent measure of a VLM's ability to discriminate subtle temporal dynamics and spatial relationships. This benchmark will be a valuable tool for future research, enabling more precise evaluation and comparison of VLM progress in spatio-temporal understanding.

#### 5.5. State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalizability

ReMoT achieves state-of-the-art performance on its new benchmark and multiple existing spatio-temporal reasoning benchmarks. Notably, the 4B model variant achieves results comparable to or superior to much larger proprietary and open-source models (e.g., outperforming Qwen3-VL-30B-CoT and matching GPT-4o on specific tasks). Furthermore, these gains in spatio-temporal reasoning are achieved without compromising general multimodal understanding capabilities, indicating robust generalizability and avoidance of catastrophic forgetting. This suggests that the learned motion understanding is deeply integrated and beneficial across a broader range of visual-linguistic tasks.

#### 5.6. Potential Impact on Real-World Applications

The improved spatio-temporal consistency imparted by ReMoT has direct and significant implications for critical real-world applications:
- **Embodied Intelligence and Robotics**: Enhanced understanding of robotic actions, gripper states, and environmental changes will enable more reliable task execution and human-robot interaction.
- **Autonomous Driving**: A VLM with better spatio-temporal reasoning can more accurately interpret dynamic road scenes, track object movements, and predict future states, contributing to safer and more robust autonomous systems.
- **Navigation Systems**: Improved reasoning about ego-motion, camera rotation, and relative object positions will facilitate more intuitive and accurate navigation instructions.
- **Video Understanding and AIGC**: The ability to discern fine-grained motion attributes can lead to more nuanced video analysis and the generation of more physically consistent and logically sound dynamic content.

In conclusion, ReMoT provides a foundational advancement in VLM capabilities by systematically addressing spatio-temporal consistency. Its contributions in data generation, training methodologies, and evaluation protocols offer a comprehensive framework with substantial potential to impact the development and deployment of more reliable and intelligent multimodal AI systems.
