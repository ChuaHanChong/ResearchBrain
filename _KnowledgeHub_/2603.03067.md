# CMoE: Contrastive Mixture of Experts for Motion Control and Terrain Adaptation of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The CMoE framework introduces a contrastive learning approach to enhance Mixture of Experts (MoE) for humanoid robot motion control and terrain adaptation, directly addressing the "lazy gating problem." This enables a Unitree G1 robot to autonomously traverse extreme mixed terrains, including 20 cm steps and 80 cm gaps, with a single, generalized policy through successful Sim-to-Real transfer.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with robust navigation across diverse and abruptly changing complex terrains using a single control policy.
- Existing Mixture of Experts (MoE) frameworks in robotics suffer from a "lazy gating problem," where experts fail to specialize in distinct terrain types.
- Two-phase training methods for multi-terrain adaptation are inefficient and prone to overfitting, hindering robust, end-to-end learning.

## Method

- Introduces CMoE, a single-stage reinforcement learning framework integrating a VAE for proprioceptive state estimation and an AE for terrain perception from elevation maps.
- Utilizes a Mixture of Experts (MoE) policy with a novel contrastive learning objective, inspired by SwAV, to encourage expert specialization.
- Applies contrastive constraints to maximize consistency of expert activations within the same terrain and minimize similarity across different terrains, directly addressing the "lazy gating problem."

## Results

- CMoE achieves significantly higher success rates (e.g., 0.886 on stairs up, 0.974 on gaps) and longer travel distances in simulation compared to baseline and vanilla MoE strategies.
- T-SNE visualization confirms contrastive learning leads to distinct expert activation clustering based on terrain type, demonstrating effective specialization.
- A Unitree G1 robot successfully performs zero-shot Sim-to-Real transfer, traversing physical steps up to 20 cm high and gaps up to 80 cm wide, surpassing prior benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contrastive learning effectively forces the MoE gating network to produce terrain-specific expert activations, overcoming the "lazy gating problem" and enabling true specialization.
> - Explicit expert specialization leads to higher learning ceilings, faster training convergence, and more robust skill acquisition for diverse terrain adaptation.
> - Integrating a VAE for proprioceptive history and an AE for elevation map encoding provides comprehensive multimodal input, crucial for adaptive and generalized locomotion.
