# CMoE: Contrastive Mixture of Experts for Motion Control and Terrain Adaptation of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The CMoE framework introduces a contrastive learning approach to enhance Mixture of Experts (MoE) for humanoid robot motion control and terrain adaptation, directly addressing the "lazy gating problem." This enables a Unitree G1 robot to autonomously traverse extreme mixed terrains, including 20 cm steps and 80 cm gaps, with a single, generalized policy through successful Sim-to-Real transfer.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with robust navigation across diverse and abruptly changing complex terrains using a single control policy.
- Existing Mixture of Experts (MoE) frameworks in robotics suffer from a "lazy gating problem," where experts fail to specialize in distinct terrain types.
- Two-phase training methods for multi-terrain adaptation are inefficient and prone to overfitting, hindering robust, end-to-end learning.

## Method

- Introduces CMoE, a single-stage reinforcement learning framework integrating a VAE for proprioceptive state estimation and an AE for terrain perception from elevation maps.
- Utilizes a Mixture of Experts (MoE) policy with a novel contrastive learning objective, inspired by SwAV, to encourage expert specialization.
- Applies contrastive constraints to maximize consistency of expert activations within the same terrain and minimize similarity across different terrains, directly addressing the "lazy gating problem."

## Results

- CMoE achieves significantly higher success rates (e.g., 0.886 on stairs up, 0.974 on gaps) and longer travel distances in simulation compared to baseline and vanilla MoE strategies.
- T-SNE visualization confirms contrastive learning leads to distinct expert activation clustering based on terrain type, demonstrating effective specialization.
- A Unitree G1 robot successfully performs zero-shot Sim-to-Real transfer, traversing physical steps up to 20 cm high and gaps up to 80 cm wide, surpassing prior benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contrastive learning effectively forces the MoE gating network to produce terrain-specific expert activations, overcoming the "lazy gating problem" and enabling true specialization.
> - Explicit expert specialization leads to higher learning ceilings, faster training convergence, and more robust skill acquisition for diverse terrain adaptation.
> - Integrating a VAE for proprioceptive history and an AE for elevation map encoding provides comprehensive multimodal input, crucial for adaptive and generalized locomotion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous humanoid robots capable of navigating complex and diverse terrains is a long-standing challenge in robotics. Traditional control methods often struggle with the inherent instability and high degrees of freedom of bipedal systems, especially when encountering varied surfaces or unexpected obstacles. This paper directly addresses the critical need for humanoid robots to operate effectively in real-world, unstructured environments characterized by abrupt terrain transitions, such as gravel paths leading to slopes or stepping stones amidst deformable surfaces.

**Evolution of Legged Locomotion:**
Initially, research in legged locomotion saw significant advancements with quadruped robots due to their inherent stability. Humanoid robots, while offering advantages in human-designed environments, present greater control complexities dueating to the challenge of maintaining balance on two legs, particularly on uneven ground. Early efforts in humanoid locomotion often focused on maintaining stability on a single terrain type, with limited adaptability to variations.

**Learning-Based Approaches and Their Limitations:**
The emergence of learning-based methods, particularly deep reinforcement learning (RL), has shown immense promise in enabling robots to acquire complex locomotion skills.
- **Two-phase training approaches** (e.g., Robot Parkour Learning, Humanoid Parkour Learning) were introduced to handle multiple terrains by training models on single terrains and then distilling them. While effective, these methods suffer from increased training time and the risk of overfitting.
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures** gained traction as a way to simultaneously consider features from multiple tasks or environments by dynamically activating specialized "experts." In robotics, MoE has been applied to quadruped and bipedal locomotion (e.g., MoE-Loco, MoRE) to alleviate gradient conflicts in multi-task learning and improve gait learning. However, a significant limitation identified in previous MoE implementations for locomotion is the **"lazy gating problem"** or a lack of effective expert specialization. The gating networks often exhibit nearly uniform expert activations across different terrains (as shown in Fig. 2), failing to truly leverage the modularity of MoE for specific terrain adaptation. This hinders the model's expressive power and adaptability.

**Perception Systems for Terrain Awareness:**
The ability of robots to perceive their environment is crucial for adaptive locomotion.
- Early RL models for locomotion often relied solely on **proprioceptive information** (e.g., joint angles, velocities, IMU data). Some works (e.g.,) used autoencoders (AE) or variational autoencoders (VAE) to encode historical proprioceptive data and predict states, but these lacked direct environmental sensing.
- Later, **external perception systems** like depth cameras and LiDAR were integrated. Depth cameras offer limited field of view, restricting robots mostly to forward movement. LiDAR, with its wider field of view and higher accuracy, proved more effective for generating elevation maps, providing rich terrain information for RL policies. This work builds upon the use of elevation maps for environmental perception.

**CMoE's Contribution to the Landscape:**
CMoE directly addresses the shortcomings of existing MoE-based locomotion systems by introducing **contrastive learning** to refine the expert activation distributions. It tackles the "lazy gating problem" head-on, ensuring that experts truly specialize in distinct terrain types. By achieving this, CMoE pushes the boundaries of single-stage, end-to-end learning for humanoid locomotion. It aims to integrate robust state estimation (VAE), advanced terrain perception (AE for elevation maps), and an innovatively refined MoE architecture, positioning itself as a state-of-the-art solution for agile and robust humanoid navigation across extremely challenging and varied terrains. Its emphasis on a single policy for diverse, extreme parkour maneuvers (e.g., large gaps, high steps) represents a significant advancement over previous benchmarks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to **autonomously and robustly navigate a diverse range of complex terrains with abrupt transitions** in real-world environments using a single, generalized control policy.

This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in existing humanoid locomotion systems:
1. **Limited Terrain Adaptability:** Current humanoid robots often struggle with heterogeneous terrains that demand rapid and distinct behavioral adaptations. While they might excel on a single terrain, their performance degrades significantly when faced with abrupt changes.
2. **Ineffective Expert Specialization in MoE Frameworks:** Although the Mixture of Experts (MoE) framework is theoretically well-suited for modeling diverse features, in practice, prior implementations in robotics suffer from a "lazy gating problem." The gating network tends to activate experts uniformly across different terrains, preventing experts from developing specialized skills and thus limiting the overall expressive power and adaptability of the model. This lack of specialization results in less efficient skill activation and suboptimal terrain handling.
3. **Computational and Training Inefficiency:** Two-phase training approaches, which involve training on single terrains followed by distillation, are time-consuming and prone to overfitting. There is a clear motivation to develop a more efficient, single-stage training paradigm that can handle multiple terrains simultaneously.
4. **Lack of End-to-End Multimodal Integration:** Many existing methods do not seamlessly integrate multimodal sensory inputs (proprioception, visual/depth perception) into a unified framework that directly maps to robot actions, hindering comprehensive environmental understanding and responsive action generation.
5. **Benchmarking and Performance Limitations:** Existing methods often struggle with extreme terrain conditions, such as very high steps or wide gaps, failing to achieve human-like agility and robustness in such challenging scenarios.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the CMoE framework are:
- **To introduce a novel single-stage reinforcement learning framework (CMoE)** that integrates the Mixture of Experts architecture with contrastive learning.
- **To refine expert activation distributions:** This is achieved by imposing contrastive constraints that maximize the consistency of expert activations within the same terrain while minimizing their similarity across different terrains.
- **To encourage true expert specialization:** By doing so, experts are guided to specialize in distinct terrain types, overcoming the "lazy gating problem" and enhancing the robot's ability to adapt its response strategy.
- **To achieve robust and natural gait:** Enable the robot to traverse a variety of challenging terrains, including continuous steps up to 20 cm high and gaps up to 80 cm wide, surpassing the limits of existing methods.
- **To develop an end-to-end system:** The framework should directly map multimodal sensory inputs (historical proprioception, elevation maps) to robot actions.
- **To contribute to the research community:** By publicly releasing the code, the authors aim to support further research and foster community development in humanoid locomotion.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CMoE framework is a novel single-stage, end-to-end reinforcement learning architecture designed to enable humanoid robots to adapt to diverse terrains. It synergistically integrates three core components: a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for state estimation, an Autoencoder (AE) for terrain perception, and a Mixture of Experts (MoE) policy augmented with a unique contrastive learning objective.

**A. System Overview:**
The CMoE framework aims to improve multi-terrain adaptability by learning a generalized policy that maps multimodal sensory inputs directly to robot actions. It leverages Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) as its underlying reinforcement learning algorithm to find an optimal policy that maximizes expected discounted return in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) setting.

**B. Information Encoding:**
The framework processes two primary types of sensory information:
1. **Proprioceptive Information:** The robot's internal state `o_t` includes angular velocity, gravity direction, velocity command, joint angles, joint velocities, and the previous action. This historical proprioceptive data `o_t^H` is fed into a **VAE-based estimator**. This estimator predicts the robot's body velocity `v_t` and generates a latent representation `z_t^H`. The VAE is trained with a hybrid loss `L_CS` comprising an MSE loss for body velocity prediction and a standard VAE reconstruction loss `L_VAE` (MSE for observation reconstruction and a KL divergence term for latent space regularization).
2. **Environmental Perception:** An **Autoencoder (AE)** is used to extract features from an elevation map `e_t`, which represents the surrounding terrain. This AE is trained with an MSE reconstruction loss `L_AE` to ensure accurate feature extraction `z_t^E`.

These encoded representations (`v_t`, `z_t^H`, `z_t^E`) are fused with the current observation `o_t^c` and the raw elevation map `e_t` to form the comprehensive system observation.

**C. Mixture of Experts (MoE) Policy:**
To address gradient conflicts and enable specialized skill learning across diverse terrains, the CMoE framework incorporates an MoE architecture into both the actor and critic networks of the PPO agent.
- **Expert Modules:** The system employs `N` expert modules (5 in the reported experiments), each consisting of a dedicated actor-critic pair. Each critic evaluates only its corresponding actor, using privileged observations.
- **Inputs to Experts:** Each expert receives the estimated body velocity `v_t^p`, implicit contextual state variables (`z_t^E`, `z_t^H`), the current observation `o_t^c`, and the elevation map `e_t`.
- **Gating Network:** A single shared gating network dynamically determines the activation level `g_i` for each expert `i`. This network is crucial for deciding which experts are most relevant for the current terrain.
- **Action Generation:** The final action `μ_weighted` is a softmax-normalized weighted sum of the outputs `μ_i` from each expert, based on their activation levels `g_i`.

**D. Terrains Contrastive Learning (The Core Novelty):**
This is the key innovation that distinguishes CMoE from traditional MoE implementations. The goal is to explicitly encourage the gating network to produce terrain-specific expert activations.
- **Transformation:** The gate output `g_t` and the elevation map `e_t` are transformed into a common latent dimension using two separate Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), resulting in `g_z_t` and `e_z_t`.
- **Positive/Negative Samples:** In the contrastive learning setup, pairs `⟨g_z, e_z⟩` originating from the *same robot trajectory* (implying the same terrain context) are treated as positive samples. All other pairs are considered negative.
- **SwAV-inspired Optimization:** Inspired by the SwAV (Swapping Assignments for Views) self-supervised learning method, the system predicts cluster assignment probabilities (`p_g_t`, `p_e_t`) for both `g_z_t` and `e_z_t` using a set of learnable prototypes.
- **Sinkhorn-Knopp Algorithm:** To avoid trivial solutions (e.g., all samples mapping to a single cluster), the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm is applied to obtain balanced cluster assignments (`q_g_t`, `q_e_t`).
- **Contrastive Loss (`J_SwAV`):** The objective is to maximize the agreement between the cluster assignments derived from the gate output and the elevation map. This effectively forces the gating network to produce activations that are consistent within a given terrain type and distinct across different terrain types. This directly addresses the "lazy gating problem" by making expert activations truly reflective of terrain features.

**E. Implementation Details:**
- **Training Environment:** Simulated on IsaacGym using an NVIDIA RTX 4090, with 4096 parallel environments for 20,000 epochs.
- **Terrain Variety:** Trained on eight diverse terrains (slopes, stairs, gaps, hurdles, discrete, and two mixed terrains) with varying parameters (e.g., slope angle 0-20°, step height 0.05-0.23m, gap width 0.1-0.8m).
- **Curriculum Learning:** Employed to progressively increase task difficulty (e.g., velocity commands on complex terrains) and balance learning across different terrain categories.
- **Domain Randomization:** Crucial for robust Sim2Real transfer. Parameters such as joint mass, friction, motor strength, and k_p/k_d were randomized. Perturbations were applied to the robot. Elevation map noise (delay, Gaussian, non-linear salt-and-pepper) and chamfering of right-angled edges were introduced to mimic real-world sensor limitations and terrain characteristics.
- **Reward Function:** A comprehensive reward function (Table II) combined standard locomotion rewards (velocity tracking, orientation, base height, etc.) with specialized, discontinuous rewards (e.g., 'foot edge' penalty for hurdles/gaps) to fine-tune behavior for specific terrain challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the superior performance and effectiveness of the CMoE framework through a series of quantitative and qualitative experiments in both simulation and real-world scenarios.

**A. Quantitative Motion Control Performance (Simulation):**
- **Superior Performance:** CMoE consistently outperforms both the "Base strategy" (actor-critic without MoE) and the "Vanilla MoE strategy" (MoE without terrain encoder and contrastive learning) across all eight diverse training terrains (Table III).
- **Higher Success Rates:** CMoE achieves significantly higher success rates, particularly on challenging terrains such as "stairs up" (0.886 vs. 0.798 for Vanilla MoE), "gaps" (0.974 vs. 0.818), and complex mixed terrains "mix1" (0.767 vs. 0.605) and "mix2" (0.747 vs. 0.662).
- **Longer Travel Distances:** Correspondingly, CMoE enables robots to traverse longer average distances, demonstrating its enhanced stability and continuous locomotion capabilities on varied terrains.
- **Higher Learning Ceiling:** The results indicate that CMoE achieves a higher "learning ceiling" on each terrain, suggesting a more thorough and robust acquisition of skills.

**B. Effect of Contrastive Learning (Expert Specialization):**
- **Clear Terrain Clustering (t-SNE):** A t-SNE visualization (Fig. 2) vividly demonstrates the impact of contrastive learning. CMoE's expert activations clearly cluster based on terrain similarity, with distinct boundaries between dissimilar terrains. For example, "ascending steps," "simple steps," and "uphill terrain" are grouped but also stratified by difficulty, and "ascending steps" are markedly separated from "descending steps," reflecting a nuanced understanding of terrain demands.
- **Lack of Specialization in Vanilla MoE:** In stark contrast, the Vanilla MoE model shows dispersed expert activations without distinct clustering, indicating its inability to develop terrain-specific expert specializations. This confirms the "lazy gating problem" and highlights how CMoE addresses it.

**C. Expert Behavior Analysis:**
- **Dynamic Expert Activation:** Visual analysis of expert activation levels over mixed terrains (Fig. 5) reveals that CMoE exhibits dynamic, distinct jumps in expert activation as the robot transitions between terrains. This is unlike Vanilla MoE, where activations remain relatively flat and unresponsive to terrain changes.
- **Observable Specialization:** CMoE demonstrates clear expert specialization. For instance, "Expert 1" shows high activation specifically on ascending terrains (uphill, hurdles), which require significant foot lifting. Conversely, "Expert 2" is more active on descending terrains.
- **Validation of Specialization:** To further validate, removing Expert 1's output in an experimental setup caused the robot to fail on ascending stairs (unable to lift its leg properly) but still successfully navigate downhill scenarios, confirming Expert 1's specialized role in ascending steps.

**D. Training Performance:**
- **Improved Efficiency and Learning Rate:** Training curves (Fig. 4) show that CMoE achieves the maximum terrain level faster and reaches higher reward values compared to the Base method. This indicates that the gating network's effective terrain classification, facilitated by contrastive learning, reduces learning difficulty and improves overall training efficiency.

**E. Real-world Experiment (Sim-to-Real Transfer):**
- **Successful Deployment:** The CMoE policy, trained purely in simulation with extensive domain randomization, was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot using LiDAR for elevation map observations, demonstrating effective Sim2Real transfer.
- **State-of-the-Art Terrain Traversal:** The robot achieved remarkable performance on various physical terrains:
 - **Gaps:** Traversed up to 80 cm wide, identified as the largest among existing methods.
 - **Steps:** Successfully ascended continuous steps up to 20 cm high, surpassing the 15 cm limit of prior research.
 - **Hurdles:** Crossed obstacles up to 30 cm high.
 - **Slopes:** Easily navigated 17-degree slopes.
- **Complex Mixed Terrains:** The Unitree G1 robot successfully traversed a combination of steps, gaps, slopes, and hurdles (Fig. 1, 6), showcasing its ability to adapt rapidly to challenging, heterogeneous environments and perform "extreme parkour maneuvers."
- **Robustness:**
 - Maintained excellent stability and continuous movement on untrained, irregular step structures with varying widths and protruding edges (Fig. 7a).
 - Demonstrated resilience against external disturbances, such as rope dragging and object collisions, while crossing a 30 cm obstacle (Fig. 7b), maintaining a stable posture.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CMoE framework represents a substantial advancement in the field of humanoid robot locomotion, offering significant contributions and potential impact across several dimensions:

**1. Breakthrough in Terrain Adaptability and Robustness:**
The ability of CMoE to enable the Unitree G1 robot to traverse continuous steps up to 20 cm high and gaps up to 80 cm wide with a single policy significantly pushes the state-of-the-art in humanoid locomotion. This level of agility and robustness on extreme and mixed terrains makes humanoid robots far more capable of operating in unstructured, real-world environments that closely mimic human living and working spaces.

**2. Novelty of Contrastive Learning for Expert Specialization:**
The core innovation of integrating contrastive learning into the Mixture of Experts architecture directly addresses the long-standing "lazy gating problem" in MoE. By explicitly forcing the gating network to differentiate and specialize experts for distinct terrain types, CMoE unlocks the full potential of modular learning. This methodological breakthrough can inspire similar approaches in other multi-task or multi-environment learning problems beyond robotics.

**3. Enhanced Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
The combination of robust information encoding, contrastive learning, and extensive domain randomization results in a highly generalized policy. The successful deployment on a physical Unitree G1 robot without further real-world training ("zero-shot Sim2Real transfer") is a critical indicator of the method's practical viability and robustness against the reality gap, which is a major hurdle in robotics research. This paves the way for faster deployment of sophisticated robot behaviors.

**4. Improved Training Efficiency and Interpretability:**
CMoE's single-stage training approach, coupled with its improved learning efficiency (faster convergence and higher reward ceiling), offers practical benefits for robot development. Furthermore, the ability to clearly observe and even validate the specialization of individual experts provides a level of interpretability that is often lacking in complex deep learning models. This can aid in debugging, understanding learned behaviors, and guiding future policy improvements.

**5. Broader Implications for Humanoid Robot Applications:**
The enhanced capabilities conferred by CMoE have profound implications for various applications:
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** Humanoid robots could more effectively navigate complex factory floors or warehouses with varying floor surfaces, ramps, and small obstacles.
- **Disaster Response and Exploration:** Robots could be deployed in hazardous environments with unpredictable and damaged terrain, such as disaster sites or rough exploration zones, to perform search-and-rescue or reconnaissance missions.
- **Assistance in Human Environments:** Future humanoid assistants could more naturally and safely navigate homes, offices, and public spaces, which are inherently filled with diverse floorings, steps, and obstacles.

**6. Contribution to Open Science:**
The public release of the code is a valuable contribution to the research community. It promotes reproducibility, allows other researchers to build upon this work, and accelerates the pace of innovation in humanoid locomotion and intelligent robotics.

**Future Directions:**
The paper itself points to future work extending this approach to whole-body control, enabling coordinated full-body parkour. This indicates a clear path for CMoE to evolve from leg-centric locomotion to more complex, human-like acrobatic maneuvers, further broadening the scope of what humanoid robots can achieve.

In conclusion, CMoE marks a significant step forward in making humanoid robots truly adaptive and robust in complex, dynamic real-world environments. Its novel integration of contrastive learning with Mixture of Experts provides a powerful paradigm for learning highly specialized and generalized locomotion policies, with far-reaching implications for the future of robotics.
