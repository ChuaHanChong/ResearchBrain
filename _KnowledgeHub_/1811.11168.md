# Deformable ConvNets v2: More Deformable, Better Results

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Microsoft Research Asia introduce Deformable ConvNets v2 (DCNv2), an enhanced adaptive feature learning framework that expands the use of deformable layers and incorporates a novel modulation mechanism. The framework also employs an R-CNN feature mimicking scheme to guide training, resulting in a 1.4% improvement in AP_bbox for Faster R-CNN on the COCO dataset compared to its DCNv1 predecessor, and demonstrating improved generalization across diverse object scales and backbones.

## Problem

- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) struggle with significant geometric variations in objects (e.g., scale, pose, deformation) due to their fixed geometric sampling structures.
- Previous Deformable ConvNets (DCNv1) adaptively sampled features but often included irrelevant background context in their spatial support, potentially degrading performance.
- DCNv1's adaptive capabilities were limited by applying deformable layers only to a small number of top network stages and lacking a mechanism for finer control over feature focus.

## Method

- The number of ==deformable convolution== layers was increased by applying them to all 3x3 convolutions in `conv3`, `conv4`, and `conv5` stages of the backbone, leading to `conv4``conv5` deformable layers.
- A ==modulation mechanism== was introduced for deformable convolutions and RoIpooling, allowing the network to learn both spatial offsets and scalar weights (0-1) for sampled features, thus controlling their influence.
- An ==R-CNN feature mimicking== scheme was developed, where a teacher R-CNN trained on tightly cropped object patches guides the **DCNv2** (student) to learn object-focused features for positive regions through a ==cosine distance loss==, without adding inference overhead.

## Results

- DCNv2 improved Faster R-CNN AP_bbox on COCO by **5.1%** over the regular ConvNet baseline (from **38.0%** to **43.1%** with ResNet-50) and **3.0%** over DCNv1.
- For Mask R-CNN, DCNv2 achieved **44.3%** AP_bbox and **38.3%** AP_mask, showing consistent gains over baselines across detection and segmentation tasks.
- The model achieved **44.8%** AP_bbox for Faster R-CNN with a ResNet-101 backbone on COCO test-dev, surpassing DCNv1 (**41.4%**) and demonstrating robust performance across various resolutions and stronger backbones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visualizations confirmed that prior deformable networks, while adaptive, still processed features from irrelevant background areas, highlighting the need for finer spatial control.
> - Enriching the deformation modeling by integrating deformable layers across more stages of the CNN backbone significantly boosts the network's capacity to adapt to object geometry.
> - Introducing a learnable modulation mechanism alongside spatial offsets provides an attention-like ability, allowing the network to explicitly weight and focus on pertinent sampled features, leading to more precise object localization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Object recognition and detection in computer vision face challenges due to significant geometric variations such as changes in scale, pose, viewpoint, and object part deformation. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have shown robust performance, but their fixed geometric structure (e.g., regular grid sampling in convolution, fixed binning in RoI pooling) limits their ability to model these variations adaptively.

The prior work, Deformable Convolutional Networks (DCNv1), introduced deformable convolution and deformable RoIpooling modules. These modules allow CNNs to adaptively sample features by learning offsets for sampling locations, thereby enabling the network to conform its feature representation to object structures. DCNv1 demonstrated substantial improvements in object detection accuracy by allowing the receptive fields to dynamically adjust to the geometry of objects.

This paper builds upon DCNv1 by addressing observed limitations in its adaptive behavior. Through visualization, the authors noted that while DCNv1’s spatial support for neural features adapts better than regular ConvNets, it often extends beyond the object of interest. This suggests that features can still be influenced by irrelevant image content, potentially degrading performance.

The work aims to further enhance the adaptive capabilities of deformable ConvNets, particularly in improving their ability to focus on pertinent image regions. It integrates concepts from knowledge distillation (feature mimicking) and introduces a new modulation mechanism, positioning it as an evolution of adaptive feature learning in deep networks. It also relates to broader themes in computer vision, such as spatial support manipulation, effective receptive field analysis, and attention mechanisms, by proposing a mechanism that acts as a sparse, learned attention.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the performance of Deformable Convolutional Networks for tasks such as object detection and instance segmentation by enhancing their ability to adapt to object geometry and focus on relevant image content.

The motivation stems from an analysis of the behavior of DCNv1. Specifically, the authors conducted visualizations of the spatial support of DCNv1, employing three modalities: effective receptive fields, effective sampling/bin locations, and error-bounded saliency regions. These analyses revealed that while DCNv1 adaptively deforms its sampling patterns to fit object structures, its spatial support may still extend beyond the precise boundaries of an object. This means that features intended for an object might be influenced by extraneous background information, which could be detrimental to accurate detection and segmentation.

The authors identified two main areas for improvement based on this observation:
1. **Limited Modeling Power:** The original DCNv1 typically replaced a limited number of convolutional layers (e.g., in the `conv5` stage) with deformable versions. The analysis suggested that greater potential exists for learning deformable convolutions if their integration within the network is more comprehensive.
2. **Inexact Spatial Support/Focus:** Even with learnable offsets, the network's spatial support was not always precisely confined to the object. This indicated a need for a mechanism that not only shifts sampling locations but also controls the *influence* of sampled features, effectively allowing the network to filter out irrelevant information.
3. **Training Signal:** To effectively leverage increased modeling power, a stronger training signal is needed to guide the network to learn more focused and task-relevant feature representations. The conventional Faster R-CNN training loss alone may not sufficiently drive the learning of such refined adaptive behaviors, particularly for excluding redundant context.

Thus, the key objectives were to reformulate Deformable ConvNets by:
- Increasing their geometric modeling capacity through more extensive use of deformable layers.
- Introducing a mechanism to modulate the influence of sampled features, enabling finer control over spatial support.
- Proposing a training strategy to effectively guide the network to learn object-focused features, thereby mitigating the impact of irrelevant context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Deformable ConvNets v2 (DCNv2) introduces three main contributions to address the identified limitations:

#### 3.1. Stacking More Deformable Convolution Layers

The authors expanded the application of deformable convolution layers beyond the `conv5` stage, which was typically used in DCNv1. In DCNv2, deformable convolutions are applied to all 3x3 convolution layers in the `conv3`, `conv4`, and `conv5` stages of the ResNet backbone. This results in 12 deformable convolution layers, in contrast to the three layers in DCNv1. The rationale is that a more widespread integration of deformable layers allows the network to control feature sampling over a broader range of feature levels, thereby strengthening its overall geometric transformation modeling capability. Experiments confirmed that applying deformable layers in `conv3-conv5` stages offered the best trade-off between accuracy and efficiency on the COCO benchmark.

#### 3.2. Modulated Deformable Modules

To provide DCNs with finer control over their spatial support, a modulation mechanism was introduced alongside the learnable offsets. This mechanism allows deformable modules to not only adjust the spatial locations from which features are sampled but also to modulate the *amplitude* or *weight* of these sampled features.

- **Modulated Deformable Convolution:** For a given convolutional kernel with $K$ sampling locations, the output $y(p)$ at location $p$ is calculated as:
 $y(p) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k \cdot x(p + p_k + \Delta p_k) \cdot \Delta m_k$
 Here, $\Delta p_k$ is the learned offset for the $k$-th sampling location (as in DCNv1), and $\Delta m_k$ is a newly introduced learnable modulation scalar for the $k$-th location. $\Delta m_k$ is constrained to the range via a sigmoid activation, effectively acting as an attention mechanism that can reduce or eliminate the influence of a particular sampled location. Both $\Delta p_k$ and $\Delta m_k$ are predicted by an auxiliary convolutional layer operating on the same input feature maps.
- **Modulated Deformable RoIpooling:** Similarly, for RoIpooling, each bin $k$ has a learnable offset $\Delta p_k$ and a modulation scalar $\Delta m_k$. The output binning feature $y(k)$ is computed by averaging sampled values within the bin, where each sample $x(p_{kj} + \Delta p_k)$ is also multiplied by $\Delta m_k$:
 $y(k) = \frac{1}{n_k} \sum_{j=1}^{n_k} x(p_{kj} + \Delta p_k) \cdot \Delta m_k$
 The offsets and modulation scalars for RoIpooling bins are generated by a sibling branch consisting of RoIpooling, two fully-connected layers, and an additional fully-connected layer.

These modulation scalars provide an additional dimension of freedom, allowing the network to explicitly deemphasize or ignore image content from irrelevant spatial locations/bins, leading to a more focused feature representation.

#### 3.3. R-CNN Feature Mimicking

To effectively train the enhanced DCNv2 and encourage it to focus on pertinent object regions, a feature mimicking scheme was introduced, inspired by knowledge distillation. The motivation for this is that R-CNN, which is trained on tightly cropped image patches, inherently learns features that are less affected by extraneous context outside the object. The goal is to imbue DCNv2 with this characteristic.

- **Teacher Network:** An R-CNN branch serves as a teacher. Given an RoI, the corresponding image patch is cropped and resized to a fixed size (e.g., 224x224 pixels). The R-CNN backbone processes this patch, and a (modulated) deformable RoIpooling layer, followed by two fully-connected layers, produces an R-CNN feature representation, $f_{RCNN}(b)$.
- **Student Network:** The DCNv2 Faster R-CNN network generates its own per-RoI feature representation, $f_{FRCNN}(b)$, from its Fast R-CNN head.
- **Feature Mimic Loss:** An auxiliary loss, $L_{mimic}$, is applied during training to minimize the cosine distance between $f_{RCNN}(b)$ and $f_{FRCNN}(b)$. This loss is defined as:
 $L_{mimic} = \sum_{b \in \Omega} [1 - \cos(f_{RCNN}(b), f_{FRCNN}(b))]$
 where $\Omega$ is the set of sampled RoIs.
- **Selective Mimicking:** The feature mimic loss is enforced only on *positive* RoIs (those with sufficient overlap with ground-truth objects). This is because a tightly focused feature representation might not be optimal for background (negative) RoIs, where considering more context could help avoid false positives.
- **Training Integration:** The DCNv2 network is trained with its original Faster R-CNN losses, augmented by the feature mimic loss and a classification loss from the R-CNN teacher branch. Network parameters between corresponding modules in the two branches are shared. Critically, the R-CNN branch is only used during training and is detached during inference, ensuring no additional computational overhead at test time.

The combination of these approaches aims to create a more powerful and accurately focused deformable network.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the COCO 2017 dataset for object detection and instance segmentation, using Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN as baseline systems with various ResNet and ResNeXt backbones. The results are primarily reported using mean Average Precision (AP).

#### 4.1. Analysis of Deformable ConvNet Behavior (Visualizations)

- **Spatial Support:** Visualizations (Figure 1) using effective sampling locations, effective receptive fields, and error-bounded saliency regions confirmed that while DCNv1 improved adaptation over regular ConvNets, its spatial support for foreground objects could still include irrelevant background areas.
- **DCNv2 Improvement:** DCNv2, with modulated deformable convolutions and R-CNN feature mimicking, exhibited spatial support that better conformed to object boundaries and was more focused on pertinent image regions (Figure 1c, Figure 2d). The modulation mechanism allowed for weighted influence of samples, and feature mimicking helped the network learn features less affected by context outside the RoI.

#### 4.2. Enriched Deformation Modeling (Ablation Studies)

- **Baseline (DCNv1):** Replacing the last three 3x3 convolutions in `conv5` and aligned RoIpooling with their deformable counterparts (DCNv1) yielded significant gains over the regular ConvNet baseline. For Faster R-CNN (ResNet-50, shorter side 1000), DCNv1 achieved 38.0% AP_bbox, an increase of 3.3% over the 34.7% of the regular baseline. For Mask R-CNN, similar gains were observed (40.4% AP_bbox, 35.3% AP_mask).
- **Stacking More Deformable Layers:** Progressively replacing more 3x3 convolutional layers (from `conv5` to `conv4-c5` and then `conv3-c5`) with deformable convolutions consistently improved accuracy. For Faster R-CNN, applying deformable convolutions in `conv3-c5` stages and deformable RoIpooling increased AP_bbox to 41.0%, a 3.0% gain over DCNv1. Mask R-CNN showed comparable improvements (42.4% AP_bbox, 37.0% AP_mask).
- **Modulated Deformable Modules:** Further upgrading deformable modules to *modulated* deformable modules (e.g., `mdconv@c3-c5 + mdpool`) yielded additional performance gains. For Faster R-CNN, AP_bbox increased to 41.7% (a 0.7% gain over unmodulated DCNv2 with more layers), and for Mask R-CNN, AP_bbox reached 43.1% and AP_mask 37.3%. These improvements were achieved with minor increases in parameters and FLOPs (Table 1).

#### 4.3. R-CNN Feature Mimicking (Ablation Studies)

- **Effectiveness with Modulated DCN:** When combined with modulated deformable modules, R-CNN feature mimicking further improved performance. For Faster R-CNN, AP_bbox increased from 41.7% to 43.1% (a 1.4% gain), and for Mask R-CNN, AP_bbox increased to 44.3% and AP_mask to 38.3% (Table 3).
- **Mimicking Strategy:** The most effective strategy was mimicking features of *positive* RoIs only. Mimicking all regions (foreground & background) or only background regions resulted in lower performance, supporting the hypothesis that focused features are most beneficial for objects.
- **Limited Impact on Regular ConvNets:** Applying R-CNN feature mimicking to regular ConvNets (without any deformable layers) yielded almost no accuracy gains, indicating that the enhanced modeling power of DCNv2 is crucial to effectively utilize the mimicking signal. Visualizations showed that regular ConvNets could not learn focused features even with the auxiliary loss (Figure 2e).

#### 4.4. Application on Stronger Backbones and Various Resolutions

- **ResNet-101 and ResNeXt-101:** DCNv2 consistently outperformed both regular ConvNets and DCNv1 across stronger backbones (ResNet-101 and ResNeXt-101) on the COCO 2017 test-dev set (Table 4). For instance, with ResNet-101, DCNv2 achieved 44.8% AP_bbox for Faster R-CNN, compared to 39.2% for regular and 41.4% for DCNv1. With ResNeXt-101, DCNv2 achieved 45.3% AP_bbox.
- **Resolution Adaptability:** DCNv2 demonstrated robust performance across various input image resolutions (400 to 1400 pixels shorter side), outperforming regular ConvNets at all scales. Notably, DCNv2 maintained its performance at higher resolutions where regular ConvNets showed a decrease in AP_bbox for large and medium objects, attributed to DCNv2's ability to adapt its spatial support to objects of varying sizes (Figure 4, Figure 5).

#### 4.5. ImageNet Pre-training

- **Classification:** DCNv2 pre-trained on ImageNet-1K achieved higher top-1 and top-5 classification accuracies (e.g., 78.2% top-1 for ResNet-50) compared to regular ConvNets and DCNv1, indicating that the enriched deformation modeling is also beneficial for classification (Table 6).
- **Fine-tuning:** Pre-training offsets and modulation scalars on ImageNet generally improved fine-tuned performance on tasks like PASCAL VOC object detection and semantic segmentation. However, the effect was minor for COCO detection, suggesting that the larger COCO dataset is sufficient for learning these parameters from scratch (Table 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in adaptive feature learning within Convolutional Neural Networks, specifically for challenging computer vision tasks involving geometric variations.

The significance of DCNv2 lies in its comprehensive approach to enhancing the adaptive capabilities of deformable networks. By expanding the integration of deformable layers and introducing a novel modulation mechanism, the network gains a finer level of control over its receptive field, moving beyond mere spatial shifting to also weighting the influence of sampled features. This enables the network to more precisely focus on object-relevant information and disregard irrelevant context, directly addressing a limitation of prior deformable network versions.

The proposed R-CNN feature mimicking scheme demonstrates a method for effectively guiding the training of these complex adaptive networks. By distilling the object-focused characteristics of features from cropped regions into the main detection network, DCNv2 learns more robust and discriminative representations for foreground objects. The ability to perform this mimicking during training without incurring additional computational cost during inference makes it a practical solution.

The consistent and substantial performance improvements observed across various object detection and instance segmentation benchmarks (COCO, PASCAL VOC, ImageNet VID), different network backbones (ResNet-50, ResNet-101, ResNeXt-101), and diverse image resolutions indicate the general applicability and robustness of DCNv2. The improved adaptability, especially at varying object scales and resolutions, suggests that DCNv2 can process images more effectively in diverse real-world scenarios.

The potential impact of this research is primarily in pushing the state-of-the-art for object detection and instance segmentation. The methodologies introduced offer refined tools for researchers and practitioners working with CNNs to build more accurate and geometrically adaptive models. The concept of modulated feature sampling could also inform future work on attention mechanisms in vision, providing a computationally efficient alternative to dense pairwise attention models. Furthermore, the detailed visualization and analysis techniques employed in this paper provide a framework for understanding and debugging complex adaptive network behaviors.
