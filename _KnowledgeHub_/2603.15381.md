# Why AI systems don't learn and what to do about it: Lessons on autonomous learning from cognitive science

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at META, NYU, and UC Berkeley propose a conceptual architecture for autonomous learning systems in AI, drawing inspiration from cognitive science. Their framework integrates learning from observation (System A), learning from action (System B), and a meta-control system (System M) to enable continuous, self-improving AI capable of adapting to dynamic, real-world environments without constant human intervention. They also outline an Evolutionary-Developmental (Evo/Devo) framework for bootstrapping this interdependent architecture.

## Problem

- Current AI systems are largely static post-deployment, requiring extensive human intervention for data curation, model re-training, and adaptation.
- Existing AI models suffer from an over-reliance on linguistic data and a lack of direct environmental interaction, limiting their ability to learn novel information beyond pre-existing human knowledge.
- AI research approaches to learning are conceptually fragmented, often siloed into passive (e.g., self-supervised) and active (e.g., reinforcement learning) paradigms, hindering synergistic development.

## Method

- A three-component architecture is proposed: ==System A== for learning from observation, ==System B== for learning from action, and ==System M== for meta-control, inspired by human and animal cognition.
- The framework emphasizes deep, synergistic interactions where System A provides abstract representations and world models for System B, and System B actively selects and generates grounded data for System A.
- An ==Evolutionary-Developmental (Evo/Devo)== framework, based on ==bilevel optimization==, is introduced to address the bootstrapping problem by co-evolving the components (A, B, M) across simulated 'life cycles'.

## Results

- The paper delineates the limitations of current AI, particularly the fragmentation of learning paradigms and the externalization of critical learning functions to human experts.
- It articulates System A (observation-based), System B (action-based), and System M (meta-control) as a comprehensive, biologically-inspired blueprint for an autonomous learning architecture.
- A high-level Evolutionary-Developmental (Evo/Devo) framework is proposed, leveraging bilevel optimization, to enable the co-evolution and bootstrapping of these complex, interdependent learning components in AI.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous learning, rather than being outsourced, must be an intrinsic capability for AI systems to achieve robustness and operate flexibly in complex, unpredictable real-world environments.
> - Truly autonomous learning requires a dynamic integration of passive observation and active interaction, orchestrated by an internal meta-control system that automates MLOps functions.
> - The complex interdependencies within an autonomous learning architecture (A, B, M) necessitate an evolutionary-developmental strategy that shapes innate biases and learning programs over species and individual timescales.
