# Balanced Thinking: Improving Chain of Thought Training in Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> IBM Research introduces SCALe-SFT, a supervised fine-tuning framework for Vision-Language Models that enhances multimodal reasoning by addressing token imbalance in reasoning-oriented data. This method employs a scheduled adaptive loss, leading to improved answer accuracy and more structured reasoning outputs, with gains of up to 3% over vanilla SFT on various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) uses a uniform cross-entropy loss, which creates an optimization imbalance in reasoning-oriented tasks.
- Reasoning segments (``) are typically much longer than critical answer segments (``), causing models to prioritize verbose reasoning over factual precision.
- This imbalance can lead to models generating stylistically fluent but factually inaccurate reasoning traces, potentially undermining the effectiveness of subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) phases.

## Method

- **SCALe-SFT** (Scheduled Curriculum Adaptive Loss Supervised Fine-Tuning) replaces the standard ==uniform cross-entropy loss== with a dynamic, ==segment-aware weighting strategy==.
- A ==segment-aware loss== computes the mean ==cross-entropy== independently for reasoning and answer spans, ensuring each segment contributes equally to the loss regardless of its token length.
- A ==cosine annealing schedule== dynamically adjusts the weight assigned to reasoning tokens (decaying from 1.0 to 0.5) while keeping answer token weight constant (1.0), forming a ==curriculum== that shifts focus from reasoning structure to answer accuracy.

## Results

- SCALe-SFT consistently achieved higher answer accuracy than vanilla SFT across models and datasets, with improvements up to **3%** (e.g., LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B on ScienceQA at **82.6%** vs **80.17%**).
- SCALe-SFT alone often outperformed or matched the full SFT+GRPO pipeline, while requiring approximately **one-seventh** of the training time, demonstrating its computational efficiency as a lightweight alternative.
- When used as initialization for GRPO (SCALe-GRPO), it consistently yielded the highest overall performance, with gains ranging from **0.87%** to **5.4%** over vanilla GRPO (e.g., Gemma-3-4B on IconQA: **67.8%** vs **64.33%**).
- SCALe-SFT substantially reduced invalid generations (missing `` tags) by **8.62%** to **87.40%** compared to vanilla SFT, with SCALe-GRPO nearly eliminating such structural inconsistencies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly rebalancing the loss between verbose reasoning segments and concise answer segments during SFT significantly improves a VLM's ability to generate accurate and coherent multimodal reasoning.
> - A dynamic curriculum schedule that gradually shifts training focus from reasoning structure to answer precision proves more effective than fixed weighting for establishing robust reasoning capabilities.
> - Encouraging explicit termination of reasoning (``) and adherence to answer tags through segment grouping in the loss calculation helps produce structurally consistent and well-formed model outputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is undergoing a transition from models that merely generate direct answers to those capable of structured reasoning, often producing intermediate chains of thought (CoT) to connect visual evidence with textual conclusions. This evolution is crucial for enhancing accuracy, interpretability, and reliability in real-world applications. The standard approach to developing these multimodal reasoning capabilities involves a two-phase training pipeline: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on reasoning-annotated data, followed by reinforcement learning (RL) using methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to refine behavior based on answer accuracy and desired response styles.

While significant advancements have been made in the RL stage, particularly in developing improved reward functions and optimized algorithms, the initial SFT stage—where models first establish a coherent reasoning prior—has received comparatively less attention. Existing SFT methodologies typically employ a uniform cross-entropy loss across all tokens. This uniform weighting, however, overlooks a fundamental imbalance in reasoning-oriented data: reasoning segments (`<think>`) are often substantially longer and more descriptive than the concise, yet task-critical, answer segments (`<answer>`). This imbalance can lead models to generate verbose reasoning traces that are stylistically fluent but lack factual precision, essentially "getting lost in thought" and failing to yield accurate answers. This research positions itself to address this underexplored limitation by proposing an enhancement to the SFT stage, aiming to establish a more balanced and effective reasoning foundation prior to, or in some cases as an alternative to, the subsequent RL refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from an identified optimization imbalance within the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) trained for multimodal reasoning. Standard SFT employs a uniform cross-entropy loss, where every token contributes equally to the training objective. This approach, while generally effective for language modeling, is suboptimal for reasoning-oriented tasks due to the inherent structural asymmetry of reasoning data. Specifically, reasoning segments, often delimited by `<think>` tags, tend to be verbose and lengthy, providing detailed thought processes. In contrast, the final answer segments, enclosed within `<answer>` tags, are typically concise but bear the primary responsibility for task correctness and evaluation.

The consequence of this uniform weighting is that the extensive reasoning segments dominate the loss computation, effectively overshadowing the critical signal from the shorter answer segments. This phenomenon can cause models to prioritize generating lengthy and fluent reasoning narratives, even if these narratives do not culminate in factually accurate answers. The models may become proficient at producing "thought-like" text without necessarily grounding it in correctness. As an example, the authors cite reasoning-oriented datasets where the average reasoning length can be up to 150 times longer than the average response length, exacerbating this issue. This bias can lead to models that are stylistically coherent but factually imprecise, potentially undermining the effectiveness of subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) phases, such as those employing GRPO, which are designed to align model outputs with desired behaviors.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:
- To address the token imbalance in reasoning-oriented SFT by developing an adaptive loss mechanism.
- To propose a Scheduled Curriculum Adaptive Loss Supervised Fine-Tuning (SCALe-SFT) framework that explicitly differentiates and separately supervises reasoning and answer segments.
- To implement a dynamic, length-independent weighting scheme for these segments, preventing longer reasoning traces from disproportionately influencing the loss.
- To introduce a cosine scheduling policy that progressively shifts the training focus from establishing reasoning structure (initially higher weight on `<think>`) to emphasizing answer accuracy (gradual increase in `<answer>` weight).
- To encourage the generation of concise, coherent, and well-grounded reasoning traces that lead to accurate final answers.
- To evaluate whether SCALe-SFT can consistently improve accuracy over vanilla SFT across diverse VLM architectures and benchmarks.
- To investigate if SCALe-SFT can achieve performance comparable to, or even surpass, the full two-phase SFT + GRPO pipeline while being more computationally efficient.
- To determine if SCALe-SFT can serve as a stronger and more stable initialization for subsequent GRPO optimization, leading to enhanced overall performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Scheduled Curriculum Adaptive Loss Supervised Fine-Tuning (SCALe-SFT), targets the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage within the conventional two-phase training pipeline for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which typically includes SFT followed by reinforcement learning (RL) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This work primarily focuses on enhancing the SFT component to provide a more effective inductive bias for reasoning-oriented tasks.

**Reasoning Dataset:**
The research utilizes reasoning-focused VLM datasets, specifically the Vision-R1 corpus, which explicitly couples visual understanding with structured reasoning traces. Each sample in these datasets comprises an image, a textual question, a reasoning trace delimited by `<think>...</think>`, and a final answer enclosed in `<answer>...</answer>`. These datasets are designed to instill reasoning abilities in models during the SFT stage.

**Scheduled Weighted Loss (SCALe-SFT):**
The core of SCALe-SFT lies in replacing the standard uniform cross-entropy loss with a dynamic, segment-aware weighting strategy.

1. **Standard SFT Loss (Baseline):** In conventional SFT, the loss `L_vanilla` is computed as the average cross-entropy over all predicted tokens, where each token contributes equally: `L_vanilla = (1/N) * sum(CE(y_i, ŷ_i))`. The authors identify this uniform weighting as suboptimal due to the length disparity between reasoning and answer segments, which allows verbose reasoning to overshadow factual correctness.

2. **Segment-Aware Loss:** To address this, the output sequence is semantically segmented into a reasoning span (`<think>` tags) and an answer span (`<answer>` tags). The loss for each segment, `L_seg`, is computed in a length-independent manner as the *mean* cross-entropy for tokens belonging to that specific segment: `L_seg = (1/N_seg) * sum(CE(y_i, ŷ_i))` for tokens `i` where `e_i = seg`. This formulation ensures that each segment contributes equally to the loss, regardless of its token length, preventing longer reasoning traces from dominating.

3. **`</think>` Tag Grouping:** To encourage deliberate termination of reasoning and a clear transition to the answer, the closing `</think>` tag is explicitly grouped with the subsequent `<answer>` segment during loss computation. This design choice aims to mitigate the generation of excessively long or non-terminating CoTs.

4. **Cosine Annealing Scheduling:** A cosine annealing schedule is employed to dynamically adjust the weights assigned to the reasoning and answer segments throughout training. For a training step `τ` over a total of `T` steps, the weight `w(τ)` is defined as: `w(τ) = w_end + (1/2)*(w_start - w_end)*(1 + cos(π*τ/T))`.
 - **Weight Configuration:** In the experiments, the reasoning segment weight `w_t` is initialized at `w_start = 1.0` and decays to `w_end = 0.5`. The answer segment weight `w_a` is kept constant at `w_start = w_end = 1.0`.
 - **Rationale:** This curriculum-like schedule initially (when `τ` is small) gives a higher relative weight to reasoning tokens, encouraging the model to develop structured reasoning patterns during the early, unstable phases of learning. As training progresses (when `τ` approaches `T`), the reasoning weight decreases, shifting the emphasis towards factual accuracy and answer alignment, once a stable reasoning structure has emerged.

5. **Total Loss:** The final loss for SCALe-SFT is a weighted sum of the segment-specific losses: `L = w_t * L_think + w_a * L_answer`, where `L_think` and `L_answer` are the mean cross-entropy losses for the reasoning and answer segments, respectively, and `w_t`, `w_a` are their scheduled weights.

**GRPO Reinforcement Refinement:**
After the SFT stage (either vanilla or SCALe-SFT), an optional GRPO phase can be applied for further refinement. The GRPO stage utilizes a combined reward signal:

1. **Format-Structure Reward (`r_tag`):** This reward component assesses the correctness of reasoning-answer tag placement (`<think>`, `</think>`, `<answer>`, `</answer>`). A partial score of 0.25 is awarded for each correctly placed tag, up to a maximum of 1.0 (`r_tag = 0.25 × n_correct`). This encourages the model to maintain proper structural boundaries.

2. **Answer-Accuracy Reward (`r_ans`):** This component evaluates the exact match between the model's generated answer and the reference answer.

The total reward `r` is a weighted combination of these two signals: `r = λ_tag * r_tag + λ_ans * r_ans`. In the experiments, equal weights were used (`λ_tag = λ_ans = 1`). The GRPO phase, when initialized from a SCALe-SFT checkpoint, aims to further fine-tune the model for reasoning faithfulness and answer accuracy, building upon the stable foundation provided by the weighted SFT.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SCALe-SFT across various Vision-Language Models (LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B, Gemma-3-4b, and Qwen2.5-VL-3B) and reasoning-oriented multimodal benchmarks (ScienceQA and IconQA). The performance is measured using exact match accuracy for the extracted answers. Five configurations are compared: the base model (Base), vanilla Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), SCALe-SFT (the proposed method), GRPO initialized from SFT (GRPO), and GRPO initialized from SCALe-SFT (SCALe-GRPO).

**Performance Improvements:**
- **SFT vs. Base:** Vanilla SFT on reasoning-oriented datasets consistently improves performance compared to the pre-trained base models, demonstrating the value of explicit reasoning training.
- **SCALe-SFT vs. Vanilla SFT:** SCALe-SFT consistently achieves higher accuracy than vanilla SFT across all models and datasets. The improvements in the SFT phase alone reached up to 3% (e.g., LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B on ScienceQA, 82.6% vs 80.17%). This indicates that rebalancing the loss between reasoning and answer segments leads to more effective supervision.
- **SCALe-SFT as a Lightweight Alternative:** In several instances, SCALe-SFT alone outperformed the full SFT+GRPO pipeline. For example, LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7B on ScienceQA achieved 82.6% with SCALe-SFT compared to 80.86% with GRPO. Similarly, Qwen2.5-VL-3B on ScienceQA showed 85.52% for SCALe-SFT versus 85.08% for GRPO. This finding suggests that SCALe-SFT can serve as an efficient alternative when computational resources are limited, delivering competitive results with significantly less training time (approximated as one-seventh of the full SFT+GRPO pipeline).
- **SCALe-GRPO vs. Vanilla GRPO:** When GRPO is initialized using the improved SCALe-SFT models (SCALe-GRPO), it consistently achieves the highest overall performance. SCALe-GRPO demonstrates improvements ranging from 0.87% to 5.4% compared to vanilla GRPO (e.g., Gemma-3-4B on IconQA, SCALe-GRPO 67.8% vs GRPO 64.33%, a 5.4% gain). This highlights that a reasoning-aware SFT provides a stronger, more stable foundation for subsequent reinforcement refinement.

**Reasoning Termination and Structural Consistency:**
The study also quantifies the percentage of generations that fail to produce a closing `<answer>` tag, indicating incomplete or unbounded reasoning.
- **SCALe-SFT reduces invalid outputs:** SCALe-SFT significantly reduces the rate of invalid generations, showing a relative reduction between 8.62% and 87.40% compared to vanilla SFT.
- **SCALe-GRPO near elimination of invalid outputs:** SCALe-GRPO further improves structural consistency, nearly eliminating invalid outputs in some cases and achieving up to a 63.5% relative reduction from vanilla GRPO. This demonstrates that explicitly emphasizing tag tokens within the weighted loss effectively trains the model to close its reasoning process, yielding structured and well-formed outputs.

**Ablation Study on Scheduling Mechanism:**
An ablation study on the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model investigates the contribution of the cosine scheduling mechanism.
- **Fixed Weights (SFT-FW) vs. SCALe-SFT:** A variant with fixed segment weights (SFT-FW, where both reasoning and answer weights are constant at 1.0) showed some improvement over vanilla SFT, but the full SCALe-SFT with dynamic scheduling achieved an additional average improvement of 0.45%.
- **GRPO-FW vs. SCALe-GRPO:** Similarly, GRPO initialized from SFT-FW showed smaller gains compared to GRPO initialized from SCALe-SFT (SCALe-GRPO), which had an average improvement of 1.92%. These results confirm that the gradual, dynamic scheduling of reasoning weight acts as a beneficial curriculum mechanism, guiding the model to progressively balance structured reasoning and answer precision.

**Qualitative Observations:**
Qualitative examples illustrate distinct behaviors:
- Vanilla SFT models often produce plausible reasoning that is either factually incorrect (e.g., miscounting objects), logically inconsistent (e.g., correct intermediate steps but wrong final conclusion), or gets stuck in reasoning loops, demonstrating a bias towards verbose output over factual accuracy.
- SCALe-SFT models, by contrast, generate more structured, concise, and perceptually grounded reasoning, maintaining logical consistency between the thought process and the final, accurate answer. They also demonstrate better adherence to the expected tag format.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SCALe-SFT represents a notable advancement in the training of Vision-Language Models for multimodal reasoning. Its significance lies in several key areas:

**Enhanced Reasoning Quality and Accuracy:** The primary impact is the consistent improvement in answer accuracy and the quality of generated reasoning traces. By explicitly addressing the token imbalance in reasoning-oriented SFT, SCALe-SFT guides models to produce more concise, coherent, and factually grounded reasoning. This leads to better alignment between the model's thought process and its final answer, which is crucial for reliable and interpretable AI systems.

**Improved Model Reliability and Robustness:** The significant reduction in invalid generations (outputs that fail to close reasoning or answer tags) is a critical practical benefit. In real-world applications, non-terminating or ill-formed responses can lead to system failures, require costly re-inference, or necessitate manual intervention. SCALe-SFT's ability to instill a stronger sense of structural consistency directly improves the robustness and deployment readiness of reasoning VLMs.

**Computational Efficiency and Accessibility:** SCALe-SFT offers a lightweight yet effective alternative to computationally intensive reinforcement learning phases. The demonstration that SCALe-SFT can match or even surpass the performance of the full SFT+GRPO pipeline while requiring only a fraction of the training time (approximately one-seventh) is a significant advantage. This makes high-performing reasoning models more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited computational resources, democratizing the development of advanced VLMs.

**Stronger Foundation for Reinforcement Learning:** When used in conjunction with GRPO, SCALe-SFT provides a more stable and effective initialization. The improved reasoning structure and answer alignment established during the SCALe-SFT phase lead to better performance in subsequent RL optimization, resulting in the best overall accuracy. This highlights its value not only as a standalone method but also as a fundamental enhancement for multi-stage training pipelines.

**Generalizability and Future Applications:** The token-level supervision approach of SCALe-SFT is inherently architecture-agnostic and modality-independent. The authors suggest its direct applicability to language-only reasoning datasets and potential extensions to broader multimodal scenarios, such as multi-image cases. This broad applicability indicates that the benefits of improved reasoning structure, reduced invalid terminations, and enhanced answer alignment could generalize across diverse reasoning tasks, offering a unified approach to structured reasoning.

In summary, SCALe-SFT addresses a fundamental limitation in current VLM training, leading to more accurate, reliable, and efficient reasoning agents. Its potential to streamline training pipelines and improve the foundational reasoning capabilities of models is substantial, influencing the development and deployment of next-generation AI systems.
