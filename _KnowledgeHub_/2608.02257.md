# Learning Panorama-Aware VLA for Mobile Manipulation with Whole-Body Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Beihang University, BIGAI, and collaborators developed PanoVLA, a framework for mobile bimanual robots that combines a whole-body teleoperation system for data collection with a panorama-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy. PanoVLA achieved an average stage completion rate of 91.3% and an end-to-end success rate of 73.4% across four complex real-world mobile manipulation tasks, outperforming local-view baselines by a large margin.

## Problem

- Collecting high-quality, fluid whole-body demonstration data for mobile bimanual manipulation is challenging due to complex and disjointed teleoperation interfaces.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often rely on local camera observations, which provide a limited field of view, hindering global spatial understanding for long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks.
- Integrating panoramic observations into VLA policies is difficult due to the unique non-perspective distortions in panoramic images, which can degrade feature quality with conventional visual encoders.

## Method

- A ==General Motion Retargeting (GMR)==-based whole-body teleoperation system was developed, enabling operators to control a mobile base and dual arms simultaneously through a single VR interface with sparse body tracking.
- Introduced **PanoVLA**, a panorama-aware ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== VLA policy, which incorporates a dedicated panorama expert to process omnidirectional observations.
- Utilized ==MTPano==, a foundation model specialized for panoramic scene parsing, as the visual encoder for the panorama expert to effectively extract geometry-aware spatial context from equirectangular projection images.

## Results

- PanoVLA achieved an average stage completion rate of **91.3%** and an end-to-end success rate of **73.4%** across four real-world mobile manipulation tasks, which is a substantial improvement over the standard π0.5 baseline (**58.6%** SCR, **30.0%** SR).
- The policy demonstrated robust performance across diverse tasks, achieving **93.3%** end-to-end success rate on 'Move Block' and **46.7%** on 'Wipe Table', showcasing its ability to handle multi-stage state tracking and long-range spatial consistency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the **100M**-parameter panorama expert and the MTPano encoder are crucial for performance, with MTPano improving the success rate on 'Move Pen' by over **40 percentage points** compared to a general-purpose SigLIP encoder.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fluid and coordinated whole-body teleoperation significantly reduces operator load and improves the quality of demonstration data for complex mobile manipulation.
> - Integrating a dedicated panorama expert with a specialized panoramic encoder provides superior global spatial understanding compared to relying solely on local views or simple concatenation of panoramic inputs.
> - Employing geometry-aware panoramic representations (e.g., from MTPano) is critical for effectively capturing the spatial continuity and layout information in distorted panoramic images, leading to improved policy performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mobile manipulation is a foundational capability for embodied intelligence, enabling robots to perform complex tasks that require both navigation and object interaction in diverse, open-world environments. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often trained on large-scale robot data and leveraging pre-trained vision-language representations, have demonstrated progress in robot manipulation, particularly for tabletop scenarios. However, extending these capabilities to mobile bimanual robots operating in larger, dynamic environments introduces specific challenges that this research addresses.

The current research landscape for robot learning faces two primary obstacles relevant to this work:
1. **Data Collection for Whole-Body Control:** Acquiring high-quality demonstrations for mobile bimanual manipulation is challenging. Existing teleoperation systems, while improving accessibility for fixed-base manipulation, often struggle to provide fluid, coordinated control of both a mobile base and multiple manipulator arms. Many methods decouple locomotion from manipulation, relying on separate interfaces (e.g., joysticks for base control), which can increase operator load and reduce the fluidity of demonstrations, especially for tasks involving movement across large workspaces. This paper contributes a unified teleoperation system that aims to simplify whole-body demonstration collection.
2. **Global Spatial Understanding from Visual Perception:** Current VLA models predominantly rely on local camera observations (e.g., front-facing, wrist-mounted cameras). These views possess a limited field of view, which can hinder a robot's global spatial understanding. In long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks, target objects, landmarks, or goal regions can frequently disappear from these local views due to robot motion, self-occlusion, or changes in environmental context. While some systems integrate multi-view sensing, simply aggregating multiple image streams does not necessarily establish a coherent, robot-centric global spatial understanding. Panoramic imaging offers an alternative by providing omnidirectional observations; however, panoramic images present unique non-perspective distortions that can create a domain gap for conventional visual encoders, potentially degrading feature quality and semantic information preservation. This work aims to overcome these visual perception limitations by explicitly incorporating panorama-aware representations into VLA policies.

This paper positions itself within the evolving field of general-purpose robot manipulation by developing both a data collection methodology and a policy learning approach specifically tailored for mobile bimanual platforms. It seeks to bridge the gap between fixed-base manipulation success and the demands of mobile, open-world scenarios by addressing the coupled challenges of coordinated whole-body control data acquisition and robust, global visual perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to advance the capabilities of mobile bimanual robots in performing complex, multi-stage tasks in open-world environments by enhancing their Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. To achieve this, the authors identify and address two key challenges:

1. **Objective 1: Facilitate Efficient Whole-Body Demonstration Collection.**
 - **Motivation:** Acquiring high-quality demonstration data for mobile bimanual manipulation is currently labor-intensive and technically complex. Existing teleoperation systems often require operators to manage separate control interfaces for the mobile base and robotic arms, leading to disjointed movements and reduced fluidity, especially for tasks that require continuous coordination between mobility and manipulation. This lack of fluid, holistic control hinders the generation of diverse and effective training data for learning robust policies. The goal is to reduce operator load and improve the naturalness of demonstrations.

2. **Objective 2: Enhance Global Spatial Understanding in VLA Policies.**
 - **Motivation:** Conventional VLA models rely heavily on local camera observations, which inherently have a limited field of view. In dynamic mobile manipulation scenarios, target objects or relevant environmental features can frequently move out of frame or become occluded by the robot's own body. This limited perception capability prevents the robot from maintaining a coherent global understanding of its environment, which is crucial for long-horizon planning, target search, mobile approach, and multi-stage task execution. While panoramic cameras offer a solution for omnidirectional views, their non-perspective distortions pose a challenge for standard visual encoders, making direct integration difficult without specialized processing. The aim is to overcome these visual perception limitations to enable robust decision-making in open environments.

3. **Objective 3: Develop a Panorama-Aware VLA Policy for Mobile Bimanual Manipulation.**
 - **Motivation:** Building upon the solutions for data collection and panoramic perception, the overarching goal is to integrate these advancements into a comprehensive policy framework. This policy needs to effectively combine local, fine-grained manipulation cues with global, robot-centric spatial context derived from panoramic observations. Such a policy is hypothesized to lead to improved performance in tasks requiring navigation, spatial reasoning, and coordinated whole-body actions over extended periods, directly addressing the limitations of current local-view VLA approaches in complex mobile manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a unified framework comprising a whole-body teleoperation system for data collection and a panorama-aware VLA policy, PanoVLA, for closed-loop control.

#### 3.1. Whole-Body Teleoperation System and Dataset

The system is designed for wheeled bimanual robots to enable efficient collection of coordinated mobile manipulation demonstrations.

- **Operator Interface and Motion Capture:** The operator wears a VR headset for head tracking, holds two controllers for hand poses and gripper commands, and wears three body trackers (waist, ankles). This sparse tracking data is used to reconstruct a real-time SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear Model) body state, from which pelvis motion, foot motion, and bilateral hand poses are extracted.
- **Robot Embodiment:** The robot platform consists of a RANGER MINI 3 mobile base, two 6-DoF Agilex PIPER arms, and parallel grippers. Its sensor suite includes a top-mounted 360-degree panoramic camera and three local RGB cameras (one front-facing, two wrist-mounted).
- **Motion Retargeting (GMR-based):**
 - **Formulation:** Human motion features are mapped to robot whole-body commands through a General Motion Retargeting (GMR) optimization. The robot configuration `q` includes planar base pose `(xb, yb, θb)` and left/right arm joint configurations `(qL, qR)`.
 - **Optimization Objective:** At each control step, the system minimizes a weighted objective function (Equation 1) to determine `q*`. This objective includes terms for aligning hand and gripper positions (`L_pos`), aligning hand and end-effector orientations (`L_rot`), coupling planar base motion to the operator's pelvis displacement (`L_base`), and regularizing joint configurations to avoid limits and excessive changes (`L_reg`).
 - **Mobile Manipulation Adaptation:** The mobile base is modeled as a 3-DoF planar virtual floating base, with its pose optimized jointly with the dual-arm joint configurations. This allows the optimizer to adjust base translation and yaw if an end-effector target is outside the current arm workspace, reducing retargeting error.
 - **Cost-Driven Mode-Switching:** The optimized base motion is converted into a velocity command (v_x, v_y, ω). A strategy evaluates matching costs against three base motion modes (spin, ackermann-steering, diagonal-translation) and selects the lowest-cost mode for execution, allowing dynamic switching.
 - **Synchronous Data Output:** The system synchronously sends base velocity commands, dual-arm joint-position targets, and gripper commands, producing a time-aligned whole-body action sequence.
- **Dataset Collection:** Using this system, 800 successful demonstration trajectories were collected across four real-world mobile manipulation tasks (Move Pen, Move Block, Open Curtain, Wipe Table), totaling approximately 5.5 hours and 500,000 synchronized frames at 25 Hz.
- **Data Modalities:** Each data record includes local RGB views, a 360-degree panoramic observation (stored as an equirectangular projection, ERP image), proprioceptive states (arm joint positions, gripper states, planar base pose), and 17-dimensional whole-body actions (14 for arms/grippers, 3 for base velocity).

#### 3.2. PanoVLA: Panorama-Enhanced Mobile Manipulation Policy

PanoVLA is a panorama-aware Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) VLA policy designed to integrate global spatial context.

- **Problem Formulation:** The policy `πθ` predicts a horizon-H continuous action sequence given an observation `o_t = {I_loc_t, I_pano_t, s_t}` (local views, panoramic view, proprioceptive state) and a language instruction `l`.
- **Model Architecture:** PanoVLA is built upon a dual-expert π0.5 backbone, augmented with a dedicated panorama expert. It employs three expert blocks:
 - **VLM Expert:** Encodes local visual observations (`I_loc_t`), language instructions (`l`), and robot proprioceptive states (`s_t`). It computes a VLM cache (`C_vlm_t`) using a block-wise causal attention mask.
 - **Panorama Expert:** Processes the panoramic observation (`I_pano_t`) to generate task-conditioned spatial context.
 - **Action Expert:** Predicts continuous action chunks based on the combined semantic and spatial context.
- **Fusion Mechanism:** The transformer key–value (KV) cache serves as the fusion interface. The panorama expert performs joint self-attention using its panoramic tokens (`Z_pano_t`) and the VLM cache (`C_vlm_t`), producing a panorama cache (`C_pano_t`). The action expert then predicts actions using a concatenated cache `C_t = [C_vlm_t; C_pano_t]`, integrating both fine-grained manipulation cues and global spatial context.
- **Panorama Encoder:** This module processes raw panoramic observations into a dense, tokenized representation:
 - **Spherical Projection:** Dual-fisheye observations are reprojected onto a common viewing sphere and rasterized into an equirectangular projection (ERP) image.
 - **Panoramic Feature Extraction:** The ERP image is fed into a panoramic visual encoder `E_p`, instantiated with MTPano, a foundation model for dense panoramic scene parsing. `E_p` extracts intermediate features from its segmentation and depth branches, which are then concatenated to form a unified dense panoramic representation encoding semantic and geometric cues.
 - **Token Adaptation:** A lightweight token adapter `A_ψ` transforms the dense feature map into the VLA backbone's token embedding space. This involves adaptive average pooling to a fixed spatial resolution, followed by an MLP layer to map tokens to the panorama expert's hidden dimension, yielding `Z_pano_t`.
- **Training Objective:** The action expert predicts continuous action chunks using conditional flow matching. The model learns a conditional velocity field by minimizing the squared L2 norm difference between its predicted velocity field and the target velocity field, conditioned on a noisy action sample, flow-matching timestep, and the combined context `C_t`.
- **Training Configuration:** The model is built on the π0.5 framework, using Gemma-2B as the language backbone and SigLIP for local camera observations. The pretrained MTPano encoder and language model are frozen during training. The remaining modules, including a 100M-parameter panorama expert, are optimized using AdamW with gradient norm clipping for 30K steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PanoVLA was conducted through closed-loop real-robot trials on four mobile manipulation tasks, comparing its performance against three π0.5-based baselines.

#### 4.1. Real-World Closed-Loop Evaluation

- **Overall Performance:** PanoVLA achieved an average stage completion rate (SCR) of 91.3% and an end-to-end success rate (SR) of 73.4% across the four tasks. In contrast, the standard π0.5 baseline (using only local views) attained 58.6% SCR and 30.0% SR, indicating a substantial performance improvement by PanoVLA.
- **Task-Specific Performance (Table II):**
 - **Move Pen:** PanoVLA achieved 95.6% SCR and 86.7% SR, matching the performance of π0.5 w/ Pano. This task primarily involves single-target localization and short-horizon manipulation, where even raw panoramic input provides sufficient spatial information.
 - **Move Block:** PanoVLA demonstrated significantly higher performance with 96.7% SCR and 93.3% SR, outperforming the next best baseline (π0.5 w/ Pano) by a notable margin in SR (93.3% vs. 73.3%).
 - **Open Curtain:** PanoVLA maintained the highest performance, achieving 88.3% SCR and 66.7% SR.
 - **Wipe Table:** PanoVLA also led with 84.4% SCR and 46.7% SR, demonstrating a substantial advantage over baselines which achieved SRs of 20.0% (π0.5 w/ Pano) and 40.0% (π0.5 w/ Stacked Pano).
- **Impact of Panoramic Observations:** The results indicate that incorporating panoramic observations generally improves performance over policies relying solely on local views. However, the method of incorporating panoramic information influences the extent of these gains.
 - **Raw Panoramic Input (π0.5 w/ Pano):** While effective for tasks like Move Pen, this baseline showed limitations in multi-stage tasks. For instance, on Wipe Table, it achieved a 74.4% SCR but only a 20.0% SR, suggesting failures in later stages despite initial success.
 - **Stacked Panoramic Input (π0.5 w/ Stacked Pano):** Reprojecting panoramas into multiple perspective views for input improved SR on Wipe Table to 40.0% compared to raw panoramic input. However, this approach generally underperformed the raw panoramic baseline on other tasks, suggesting potential weakening of cross-view continuity.
- **Role of Panorama Expert:** PanoVLA's consistent performance across all tasks, particularly its ability to handle multi-stage state tracking and long-range spatial consistency (e.g., in Wipe Table), suggests that its dedicated panorama expert effectively integrates robot-centric global spatial context. This enables improved spatial awareness and more stable closed-loop policy execution over extended task horizons.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (on Move Pen task)

- **Panorama Expert Scale (Table III):**
 - A 50M-parameter panorama expert resulted in 84.4% SCR and 60.0% SR.
 - A 100M-parameter expert yielded 95.6% SCR and 86.7% SR, which was the selected configuration.
 - Experts with 200M and 300M parameters did not show consistent closed-loop improvements, suggesting that a 100M-parameter expert offers a favorable empirical trade-off between representational capacity and data-efficient fine-tuning.
- **Panorama Visual Encoder (Table III):**
 - Using SigLIP (a vision encoder primarily pre-trained on perspective images) as the panorama encoder resulted in 75.6% SCR and 40.0% SR.
 - Using MTPano (designed for panorama-specific spatial structure) as the panorama encoder significantly improved performance to 95.6% SCR and 86.7% SR.
 - This finding supports the design choice of employing geometry-aware panoramic representations for constructing effective spatial context, as MTPano is better suited to capture the horizontal continuity and global layout inherent in equirectangular observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses key challenges in enabling mobile manipulation for embodied intelligent agents, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Advancement in Mobile Manipulation Capabilities:** The PanoVLA framework directly contributes to the development of more capable general-purpose robots. By integrating global spatial context through panoramic vision, robots can now effectively perform complex, multi-stage tasks that involve both navigation and fine-grained manipulation across larger, open-world environments, moving beyond the limitations of tabletop or static scenarios. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in diverse real-world applications, such as logistics, household assistance, or industrial settings.
- **Improved Spatial Understanding and Robustness:** The study empirically demonstrates that incorporating panorama-aware spatial context significantly improves a robot's ability to maintain awareness of its environment, locate targets, and track task states over long horizons. This enhanced spatial reasoning contributes to more robust closed-loop policy execution, reducing failures related to objects leaving the field of view or changes in the robot's pose. The ability to generalize across varying spatial layouts and task complexities, as shown by PanoVLA's consistent performance, suggests a step towards more reliable autonomous operation.
- **Efficient Data Collection Paradigm:** The developed GMR-based whole-body teleoperation system offers a practical and efficient methodology for acquiring high-quality demonstration data for complex mobile bimanual tasks. By enabling coordinated control of the mobile base and dual arms through natural human body movements via a single VR interface, the system reduces operator burden and facilitates the collection of fluid, holistic demonstrations. This is important for scaling up data collection efforts, which are essential for training data-hungry VLA models and accelerating robot learning research.
- **Specialized Vision for Embodied AI:** The work highlights the importance of using specialized visual encoders (like MTPano) for specific sensor modalities, such as panoramic cameras. It demonstrates that simply feeding raw panoramic images into conventional perspective-image encoders may not fully leverage the information contained within these unique views. This insight can guide future research in designing or fine-tuning vision models for diverse robotic sensing modalities.
- **Implications for Embodied AI and Generalist Robots:** This research contributes to the broader goal of developing generalist robots by endowing them with better environmental awareness and the capacity to handle tasks requiring dynamic interaction with their surroundings. The principles demonstrated in PanoVLA could be extended to other embodied AI tasks that benefit from global context, such as long-term navigation, reconnaissance, or complex assembly in large workspaces.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The dataset collected and the architectural design of PanoVLA provide a foundation for further research. This includes exploring more advanced methods for integrating multimodal sensor streams, investigating the transferability of panorama-aware policies to new environments or tasks, and potentially integrating active perception strategies that leverage the panoramic view for intelligent decision-making about where to focus local attention.
