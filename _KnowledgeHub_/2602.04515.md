# EgoActor: Grounding Task Planning into Spatial-aware Egocentric Actions for Humanoid Robots via Visual-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoActor, developed by BAAI, introduces a unified Vision-Language Model framework enabling humanoid robots to translate natural language instructions into a range of spatially-aware, egocentric actions. It achieves high success rates in real-world human-robot interaction and mobile manipulation tasks, consistently outperforming navigation-only baselines.

## Problem

- Integrating perception, locomotion, and manipulation into a cohesive system for humanoid robots in dynamic, real-world settings.
- Coordinating diverse humanoid behaviors (movement, head orientation, manipulation, human interaction) from high-level natural language instructions.
- Developing advanced spatial understanding and reasoning for robots to perform contextually appropriate and precise actions.

## Method

- Instantiates "EgoActing," a task formulated for a unified VLM (based on Qwen3-VL) that directly predicts low-level, egocentric actions in textual format (==Structured Language Actions== and Natural Language Actions).
- Employs a diverse and scalable data acquisition strategy, including real-world demonstrations, spatial reasoning datasets, and virtual environment trajectories, to train the VLM.
- Integrates with existing, calibrated low-level skills for manipulation, locomotion, and human interaction, allowing the VLM to coordinate these capabilities in real-time.

## Results

- Achieved high success rates (11/12 to 12/12) in real-world human-robot interaction and mobile manipulation tasks, successfully handling multi-person disambiguation and unseen objects.
- Demonstrated superior traversability in narrow real-world spaces, with 7/8 to 8/8 successes for entering/leaving rooms in unseen environments, significantly outperforming navigation-only baselines (0/8 to 5/8 successes).
- Exhibited strong generalization in unseen virtual environments, with the 8B model achieving **51.4%** success at <0.5m distance and **89.9%** at <3.0m, dramatically surpassing navigation-only baselines (**6.3%** to **8.8%** success at <0.5m).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single Vision-Language Model can unify the prediction of diverse humanoid actions, including locomotion, active perception, manipulation, and human interaction, by grounding high-level instructions into egocentric, textual commands.
> - Extensive and varied supervision, drawing from real-world demonstrations and synthetic data, is critical for achieving robust generalization and fluent action inference in dynamic, unseen environments.
> - Directly predicting spatially-aware, low-level actions from egocentric observations allows for superior collision avoidance, adaptive movements, and human-like behaviors, enhancing robot robustness and interaction quality.
